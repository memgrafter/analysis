# Evaluating Monolingual and Multilingual Large Language Models for Greek Question Answering: The DemosQA Benchmark
## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2602.16811
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.16811
- Reference count: 15
- Primary result: Among open-weight models, the monolingual Greek Llama Krikri 8B consistently outperformed most multilingual counterparts; GPT-4o mini achieved the highest overall accuracy, especially on domain-specific datasets.

## Executive Summary
This study addresses the gap in comprehensive evaluation of Greek Question Answering with Large Language Models. It introduces DemosQA, a novel QA dataset sourced from the Greek subreddit r/greece, and a memory-efficient 4-bit quantized evaluation framework enabling inference on commodity hardware. The empirical evaluation of 11 monolingual and multilingual LLMs across 6 human-curated Greek QA datasets reveals that monolingual Greek models excel among open-weight options, while GPT-4o mini leads overall. Prompt analysis shows role-based prompts benefit open-weight models, whereas zero-shot Chain-of-Thought often reduces accuracy due to hallucinations.

## Method Summary
The research evaluates multiple-choice QA performance using 6 Greek datasets: the new DemosQA (600 community-reviewed QA pairs from r/greece) and five existing benchmarks (Greek Truthful QA, BELEBELE-GR, Greek Medical MCQA, Greek ASEP MCQA, INCLUDE-GR). Models are tested with three prompt templates (instruction, role, zero-shot Chain-of-Thought) under greedy decoding (temperature=0). A 4-bit quantization framework via the bitsandbytes library allows inference on a single 12 GB GPU. Accuracy is computed after extracting answer letters using a regular expression, with outputs failing to match any option treated as incorrect. Proprietary GPT-4o mini is evaluated via API under comparable settings.

## Key Results
- Monolingual Llama Krikri 8B outperformed most multilingual open-weight models on Greek QA tasks.
- GPT-4o mini achieved the highest overall accuracy, particularly on Greek Medical MCQA, Greek ASEP MCQA, and INCLUDE-GR.
- Role-based prompts improved open-weight model performance, while zero-shot Chain-of-Thought often reduced accuracy due to hallucinations.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
**Mechanism 1: Monolingual Advantage**  
Monolingual LLMs like Llama Krikri achieve higher accuracy because continued pre‑training on large Greek corpora aligns internal representations with Greek linguistic structures and cultural context, reducing the distribution shift between training and evaluation data. Evidence: The paper reports Llama Krikri 8B's consistent outperformance; Meltemi 7B and Llama Krikri are created through additional pre‑training on Greek data. Break condition: if the Greek training corpus is not representative of target distributions, the performance gain may diminish.

**Mechanism 2: 4‑bit Quantization Efficiency**  
Bitsandbytes reduces weight precision from 16‑bit to 4‑bit, lowering VRAM consumption while preserving most predictive capability. This enables inference on 12 GB GPUs with minimal accuracy loss. Evidence: Abstract and Section 4.1 quantify memory reduction (e.g., a 7B model from ~14 GB to 3.5 GB). Break condition: further bit‑depth reduction (e.g., 2‑bit) or hardware incompatibility may increase errors or cause failure.

**Mechanism 3: Prompt Strategy Impact**  
Role‑based prompts improve open‑weight model accuracy by providing clear contextual cues, while zero‑shot Chain‑of‑Thought often harms due to hallucinations in smaller models. Evidence: Abstract and Section 4.3 show open‑weight models benefit from role specification; CoT reduces accuracy for most open weights. Break condition: fine‑tuning for robustness or using self‑consistency verification may mitigate CoT issues.

## Foundational Learning
- **4-bit Model Quantization**: Lowers weight precision to reduce memory usage. *Why needed*: Enables evaluation on commodity GPUs. *Quick check*: What memory reduction occurs from 16-bit to 4-bit for a 7B model, and what is the typical accuracy impact?
- **Continued Pre‑training for Monolingual Adaptation**: Further pre‑training on language‑specific corpora adapts a base model. *Why needed*: Explains monolingual model superiority. *Quick check*: How does continued pre‑training differ from original pre‑training and instruction tuning?
- **Prompt Engineering (Instruction, Role, Chain‑of‑Thought)**: Templates that guide model behavior. *Why needed*: Critical for interpreting performance differences. *Quick check*: What structural difference exists between role‑based and simple instruction prompts, and why might the former help smaller open‑weight models?
- **Social Media QA Dataset Curation**: Building QA pairs from community discussions. *Why needed*: Understands DemosQA's origin and potential biases. *Quick check*: Which criteria determined the "best" answer in DemosQA, and why is shuffling answer order important?
- **Evaluation Metrics for Multiple‑Choice QA**: Accuracy calculation with handling of invalid outputs. *Why needed*: Essential for fair comparison. *Quick check*: If a model fails to produce a valid answer for 10% of samples, how should those be treated in accuracy?

## Architecture Onboarding
Component map: Data Loader → Prompt Formatter → Quantized Model Wrapper (HuggingFace + bitsandbytes) → Answer Extractor (regex) → Metrics Calculator → Results Aggregator. For GPT‑4o mini, an API Client replaces the Quantized Model Wrapper.

Critical path: Model generation in the Quantized Model Wrapper dominates end‑to‑end processing time per sample.

Design tradeoffs: 4‑bit quantization vs. higher precision (memory vs. accuracy), greedy vs. sampling decoding, local open‑weight vs. proprietary API, rule‑based vs. LLM‑based answer extraction.

Failure signatures: Out‑of‑memory errors (quantization misconfiguration), low accuracy on domain‑specific datasets (insufficient specialized knowledge), high "No match" rate (rigid extraction or unexpected output format), large variance across prompt strategies (model instability).

First 3 experiments:
1. Baseline evaluation: Run all models on 6 datasets using instruction prompt with 4‑bit quantization; record accuracy and "No match" rates.
2. Role prompt impact: Repeat with role‑based prompt; compare delta over baseline for open‑weight models; note GPT‑4o mini's stability.
3. CoT prompt assessment: Repeat with zero‑shot CoT; check for accuracy drops and increased "No match" in open‑weight models; see if any larger model improves.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- Does instruction‑tuning with domain‑specific corpora improve Greek LLM performance on specialist QA? (Unresolved due to lag on medical/ASEP benchmarks.)
- How does expanding DemosQA with additional social media sources affect its representativeness? (Currently only Reddit r/greece.)
- Can hybrid human‑automatic evaluation provide deeper insights than accuracy alone for Greek QA? (Accuracy may miss cultural nuance, hallucinations, partial correctness.)
- Does training a Greek LLM from scratch on inclusive corpora (dialects, polytonic) improve performance on linguistically diverse tasks? (Existing models are adaptations and may not capture full diversity.)

## Limitations
- Dataset curation fidelity: Exact "best" answer selection criteria and Reddit biases are not fully detailed, potentially affecting representativeness.
- Oracle API conditions: GPT‑4o mini evaluation lacks transparency on system prompts and versioning, limiting comparability with open‑weight models.
- Prompt engineering specificity: Exact string concatenation and preprocessing for prompt templates are inferred, risking replication gaps.

## Next Checks
1. **Quantization Accuracy Sanity Check**: Re‑evaluate a subset of models in full 16‑bit and compare accuracy delta against reported 4‑bit results; >1% deviation may indicate issues.
2. **Prompt Robustness Test**: For top 3 open‑weight models, re‑evaluate each prompt with multiple seeds and phrasing variations; measure accuracy variance; >2% variance suggests prompt fragility.
3. **DemosQA Curation Audit**: Inspect released DemosQA metadata or sample 100 items to verify "best" answer correlates with highest comment score and that topics are evenly distributed across common Greek Reddit themes.
