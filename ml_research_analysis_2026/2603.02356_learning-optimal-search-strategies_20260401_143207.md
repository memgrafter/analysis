# Learning Optimal Search Strategies

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2603.02356
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.02356
- **Reference count:** 15
- **Primary result:** The Indifference Level Updating (ILU) algorithm achieves logarithmic regret growth (O(ln T)) uniformly over a broad class of smooth intensity functions, with a matching minimax lower bound proving optimality.

## Executive Summary
This paper addresses the problem of learning an optimal stopping rule for a parking problem where free spots arrive according to an unknown inhomogeneous Poisson process. The optimal policy is a threshold rule defined by an indifference position. The proposed ILU algorithm estimates the integrated jump intensity rather than the intensity function itself, leading to a mean squared error that converges at O(1/n). This efficiency translates to a cumulative regret that grows only logarithmically with the number of rounds, O(ln T). A minimax lower bound confirms that no algorithm can achieve sub-logarithmic growth, establishing the optimality of ILU.

## Method Summary
ILU maintains a set of past rounds where the stopping time occurred after position zero (full-information rounds). From these, it computes two estimators: the average first jump time after zero (ˆφ) and the integrated intensity function ˆΛ(y) via the empirical average of jump counts on intervals [y,0]. The current threshold ˆb is obtained by solving the integral equation ∫_{ˆb}^0 e^{ˆΛ(y)} dy = ˆφ, mirroring the optimality condition for the true threshold b*. If no solution exists, it defaults to the leftmost position S. After each round, if the observed stopping time exceeds zero, the round is added to the full-information set. This approach leverages the Poisson structure to estimate a cumulative quantity with fast convergence, enabling logarithmic regret.

## Key Results
- ILU algorithm achieves cumulative regret O(ln T) over the class M(L) of smooth intensity functions.
- A matching minimax lower bound proves that no algorithm can achieve sub-logarithmic regret over M(L).
- The core innovation is estimating the integrated jump intensity Λ(y) instead of λ(y), yielding an estimator with MSE Λ(y)/n.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- **Claim:** Estimating the integrated jump intensity Λ(y) yields an estimator with MSE converging at O(1/n), faster than possible for direct intensity estimation.
- **Mechanism:** ILU uses the empirical average of jump counts on [y,0] across past full-information rounds as an unbiased estimator for Λ(y). For a Poisson process, the variance of this count estimator is exactly Λ(y), so MSE = Λ(y)/n.
- **Core assumption:** λ is continuously differentiable and bounded (class M(L)), ensuring Λ(y) is well-behaved and its estimator's variance is finite.
- **Evidence:** [abstract] states ILU estimates integrated intensity and achieves logarithmic regret; [section 2, Algorithm] defines ˆΓ(y) as average of (Nⁱ₀ - Nⁱʸ); [section 4, Lemma 4.4] gives MSE(ˆΛₙ(y)) = 1/n Λ(y).
- **Break condition:** If λ is not smooth enough (e.g., discontinuous), the integrated estimator's MSE may degrade, harming regret.

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim:** Small MSE in estimating Λ(y) and E[τ₀] leads to small bias in the estimated threshold ˆbₙ, and via a second-order Taylor expansion of the optimality gap Δ(b), yields per-round regret O(1/n) and thus accumulated regret O(ln T).
- **Mechanism:** The optimal threshold b* satisfies ∫_{b*}^0 e^{Λ(y)} dy = E[τ₀]. The estimated threshold ˆbₙ solves the same with ˆΛₙ and ˆτ₀,ₙ. For small errors, |ˆbₙ - b*| is proportional to the estimation errors (Proposition 4.7). Since Δ(b) is twice differentiable with Δ(b*)=Δ'(b*)=0, a second-order Taylor expansion gives Δ(ˆbₙ) ≤ (c/2)(ˆbₙ - b*)². Thus E[Δ(πₙ)] ∝ E[(ˆbₙ - b*)²] = O(1/n).
- **Core assumption:** λ is twice continuously differentiable (so Δ is C²) and estimation errors stay small enough for the Taylor approximation to hold (enforced by event Cₙ in Lemma 4.10).
- **Evidence:** [section 1, Lemma 1.2] provides Δ''(b); [section 4, Proposition 4.7] bounds E[(ˆbₙ - b*)²] by O(1/n); [section 5, proof of Theorem 3.3] uses this to get r_λ(n)=O(1/n).
- **Break condition:** If λ is not C² or if MSE rates are slower than O(1/n), the per-round regret decay worsens.

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim:** A logarithmic minimax regret lower bound exists over M(L), proving ILU's O(ln T) growth rate is optimal.
- **Mechanism:** Reduce the problem to estimating a constant intensity λ (subclass H(L) ⊆ M(L)). For constant λ, b* = -ln(2)/λ and Δ(b) is lower bounded by c(b - b*)² (Proposition 6.1). Thus cumulative regret is at least a constant times cumulative MSE of any estimator for λ. The van Trees inequality applied to a Beta(3,3) prior on λ∈[a,b] yields a logarithmic lower bound on total MSE, hence on regret.
- **Core assumption:** M(L) contains constant intensity functions and the van Trees conditions (prior density vanishes at boundaries, smoothness) are satisfied.
- **Evidence:** [section 6, proof of Theorem 3.4] states the reduction and applies van Trees; [section 6, Proposition 6.1] gives quadratic lower bound for constant λ.
- **Break condition:** If the prior doesn't vanish at boundaries or M(L) excludes constant functions, the lower bound may weaken.

## Foundational Learning

1. **Inhomogeneous Poisson Process**
   - **Why needed here:** Models the arrival of free parking spots with unknown intensity λ(t). Its properties (independent increments, jump count distribution) are crucial for deriving the optimal stopping rule and designing the estimator.
   - **Quick check:** If the integrated intensity Λ(1)=2, what is the probability of at least one jump in [0,1]? (Answer: 1 - e^{-2}.)
2. **Optimal Stopping (Threshold Rules)**
   - **Why needed here:** The optimal policy is a threshold rule: accept the first free spot after some b*. The learning task is to estimate b*. The derivations rely on the first-order condition ∂/∂b E[|τ_b|] = 0.
   - **Quick check:** What does the indifference equation (1.1) intuitively represent about the agent's decision at b*? (Answer: The expected cost of stopping at b* equals the expected cost of continuing to 0, making the agent indifferent between these actions.)
3. **Nonparametric Estimation & MSE Rates**
   - **Why needed here:** The core innovation is estimating Λ(y) instead of λ(y). Knowing that count-based estimators for Poisson intensities have MSE O(1/n) while kernel density estimators for λ have slower rates (e.g., O(n^{-4/5})) explains the efficiency of ILU.
   - **Quick check:** Why does estimating a cumulative quantity (Λ) from Poisson counts lead to faster convergence than estimating the derivative (λ) directly? (Answer: Because Λ(y) is a linear functional of the Poisson process's cumulative hazard; the count estimator has variance Λ(y) independent of smoothness, while estimating λ involves differentiating noisy data, incurring a bias-variance tradeoff that slows convergence.)

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map:** 
  Information Set Manager (maintains I) -> Intensity Integrator (computes ˆΛₙ(y) from jump counts) & Expectation Estimator (computes ˆτ₀,ₙ from first jump times) -> Threshold Solver (solves ∫_{b}^0 e^{ˆΛₙ(y)} dy = ˆτ₀,ₙ for ˆbₙ) -> Policy Dispatcher (returns πₙ = ˆbₙ). After each round, if τₙ^{πₙ} > 0, Information Set Manager updates I ← I ∪ {n}.
- **Critical path:** 
  1. Initialize I ← {0}, π₀ = 0.
  2. For n ≥ 1: fetch I → compute ˆΛₙ and ˆτ₀,ₙ → solve for ˆbₙ → set πₙ = ˆbₙ (or S if no solution) → simulate round n with threshold πₙ → observe τₙ^{πₙ} → if τₙ^{πₙ} > 0, update I.
  3. Repeat until T rounds.
- **Design tradeoffs:**
  - *Estimator choice:* Integrated vs. intensity estimation. Integrated yields O(1/n) MSE, enabling logarithmic regret; intensity estimation would yield slower MSE (e.g., O(n^{-4/5})), leading to polynomial regret.
  - *Information usage:* Using only full-information rounds (I) simplifies analysis but discards data from other rounds. Tradeoff between statistical efficiency and bias correction.
  - *Threshold solving:* Numerical root-finding for equation (2.3) adds computational cost; assumes smoothness of e^{ˆΛₙ} guarantees uniqueness/solvability.
- **Failure signatures:**
  - No solution to ∫_{b}^0 e^{ˆΛₙ(y)} dy = ˆτ₀,ₙ (Algorithm sets πₙ = S too often). Indicates noisy or misspecified ˆΛₙ.
  - Set I remains small (few stops after 0). Leads to high variance in ˆΛₙ and ˆτ₀,ₙ.
  - Violation of λ smoothness (e.g., sharp peaks). The linear approximation for Λ(y) from count data assumes λ is reasonably smooth; high-frequency variation biases Λ estimates.
- **First 3 experiments:**
  1. **MSE Validation:** For synthetic λ ∈ M(L), simulate many independent Poisson paths. Compute ˆΛₙ(y) for a grid of y∈[S,0]. Plot log(MSE) vs. log(n) for various y; slope should be ≈ -1, confirming Lemma 4.4.
  2. **Regret Curve:** Run ILU for increasing T under a fixed λ∈M(L). Plot cumulative regret vs. T on log-log scale; slope should approach 1 (logarithmic). Compare to a naive policy (e.g., always pick b=0).
  3. **Lower Bound Tightness:** For constant λ, compare ILU's regret to the minimax lower bound via van Trees (Theorem 3.4). Compute ILU_regret / (c ln T) and check convergence to a constant ≤ the theoretical upper-bound constant.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

**Open Question 1: Generalization to Other Search Problems**
Can the ILU algorithm be adapted to general timing and search problems beyond the parking problem, such as optimal stopping with different reward structures or arrival mechanisms? The authors state that the methods should apply more broadly but no formal extension is provided. The analysis relies on the threshold structure and specific optimality condition for Poisson processes; other problems may not admit such a clean characterization. Evidence: The paper suggests extensions but lacks proofs. Resolved by proving logarithmic regret for another search problem or finding a counterexample.

**Open Question 2: Minimal Smoothness Assumptions**
What are the minimal smoothness assumptions on λ that still allow logarithmic regret? The environment class M(L) requires λ to be C¹ and bounded. The necessity of C¹ is not discussed. The proof uses Taylor expansion, so it's unclear if weaker conditions (e.g., Lipschitz) suffice. Evidence: The analysis in Proposition 4.7 uses C² for Δ(b). Resolved by extending the analysis to a larger function class or proving that without C¹, regret grows faster than logarithmic.

**Open Question 3: Robustness to Model Misspecification**
How robust is ILU if the arrival process is not an inhomogeneous Poisson process? The entire analysis assumes Poisson arrivals; real-world arrivals may have dependencies (clustering, repulsion). The ILU estimator may become biased or inconsistent under misspecification, leading to suboptimal regret. Evidence: No results for other point processes. Resolved by theoretical analysis under an alternative model (e.g., inhomogeneous renewal) or empirical simulations showing degradation and suggesting robust modifications.

**Open Question 4: Extension to Multi-Dimensional Search**
Can ILU be extended to multi-dimensional search problems, such as parking in a 2D grid or planar search? The parking problem is 1D; multi-dimensional search introduces complications like non-threshold optimal policies and more complex Poisson geometries. Evidence: The authors suggest extensions but only within 1D. Resolved by formulating a multi-dimensional analogue, characterizing the optimal policy (possibly numerically), and developing an ILU-inspired algorithm with sublinear regret.

## Limitations
- The analysis assumes λ is smooth (C²) and belongs to class M(L); real-world intensities may violate these, potentially harming performance.
- The ILU estimator for Λ(y) is claimed unbiased with MSE Λ(y)/|I|, but using only full-information rounds may introduce selection bias, undermining the key MSE guarantee.
- The minimax lower bound is derived by reducing to constant λ; while valid, it does not prove that constant functions are the hardest case, leaving tightness uncertain.

## Confidence
- ILU achieves O(ln T) regret over M(L): Medium (depends on unbiasedness of integrated estimator and C² smoothness).
- No algorithm attains sub-logarithmic regret over M(L): High (van Trees reduction is robust).
- Integrated estimator has MSE Λ(y)/|I|: Medium (rate holds for Poisson averages, but selection bias might inflate error).
- Uniformity over all smooth λ: Medium (uniform convergence plausible but not fully elaborated).
- Algorithmic feasibility (threshold solving, updates): High (steps are well-defined).

2. **Robustness to Non-Smooth Intensities:** Test ILU with piecewise-constant or discontinuous λ; compare cumulative regret to O(ln T) benchmark. Degradation would indicate fragility to smoothness.
