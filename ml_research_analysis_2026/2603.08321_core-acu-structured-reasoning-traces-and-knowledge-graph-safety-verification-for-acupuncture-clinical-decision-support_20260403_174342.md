# CORE-Acu: Structured Reasoning Traces and Knowledge Graph Safety Verification for Acupuncture Clinical Decision Support

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2603.08321
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.08321
- Reference count: 40
- Primary result: Achieved 0% safety violations on 1,000 held-out cases vs GPT-4o's 8.5%, with Entity-F1 0.4612 and superior reasoning quality.

## Executive Summary
CORE-Acu addresses the black-box reasoning and safety hallucinations of large language models in high‑stakes acupuncture clinical decision support. The framework integrates a Structured Chain‑of‑Thought (S‑CoT) dataset and schema‑constrained fine‑tuning to enforce explicit causal reasoning, a Lexicon‑Matched Entity‑Reweighted Loss (LMERL) to correct terminology drift for critical entities, and a symbolic knowledge graph safety verification within a Generate–Verify–Revise loop. On 1,000 held‑out clinical cases, CORE‑Acu achieved a 0% observed safety violation rate (95% CI: 0–0.37%) compared to GPT‑4o’s 8.5%, while demonstrating superior entity fidelity (Entity‑F1 0.4612) and multidimensional reasoning quality in automated and expert evaluations.

The system’s modular design ensures auditable reasoning traces and deterministic safety checks, making it suitable for deployment in real‑world medical settings. Ablation studies confirm the necessity of each component, with the full configuration repairing all first‑pass violations.

## Method Summary
CORE‑Acu employs a multi‑stage pipeline. First, a Structured Chain‑of‑Thought dataset (Acu‑Reasoning) with 42,512 samples is constructed, each entry containing a patient complaint followed by causal fields: diagnosis, pathology, treatment principle, and acupoints. The model (initialized from a base LLM like Qwen3‑8B) is fine‑tuned using LoRA with a custom Lexicon‑Matched Entity‑Reweighted Loss (LMERL), which amplifies gradient contributions for tokens matching the domain lexicon extracted from a Traditional Chinese Medicine Knowledge Graph (TCM‑KG). The TCM‑KG encodes 4,628 nodes and 12,500 edges, including hard safety constraints such as `ProhibitedFor` and `Incompatible` relations. During inference, a Generate–Verify–Revise loop runs: the model generates a JSON output adhering to the S‑CoT schema; a verifier extracts conditions and acupoints, checks against the KG’s unsafe relation set, and on detecting violations, provides feedback and retries up to five times. If violations persist, a fallback “Human Confirmation Required” is returned. The system optimizes for safety (zero contraindication violations) and entity fidelity while maintaining reasoning quality, as measured by Entity‑F1, BLEU‑4, and LLM‑as‑a‑judge scores.

## Key Results
- 0% safety violations on held‑out test (95% CI: 0‑0.37%) vs GPT‑4o (8.5%).
- Entity‑F1 score of 0.4612, significantly higher than baselines.
- Superior reasoning quality across five dimensions in LLM‑as‑a‑judge and expert assessments.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1
- **Claim:** Explicit schema‑constrained S‑CoT forces traceable, causal reasoning from symptoms to acupoints, mitigating black‑box generation.
- **Mechanism:** The model is fine‑tuned to produce outputs adhering to a fixed JSON schema (`diagnosis` → `pathology` → `principle` → `acupoints`), making intermediate reasoning states observable and verifiable.
- **Core assumption:** Implicit TCM reasoning can be decomposed into human‑interpretable causal steps; enforcing this structure prevents shortcut learning (symptom → acupoint).
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract] "enforcing an explicit causal chain from pattern identification to treatment principles, treatment plans, and acupoint selection"
  - [section 3.1] "factorize the generation probability... P(D|C)P(P|C,D)P(T|C,D,P)P(A|D,P,T)"
- **Break condition:** If the schema constraints are violated during generation (e.g., missing `diagnosis`), the reasoning trace becomes non‑auditable and the mechanism fails.

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim:** LMERL corrects terminology drift by adaptively up‑weighting gradients for safety‑critical entities (e.g., acupoint names), countering the frequency–importance mismatch.
- **Mechanism:** During fine‑tuning, a piecewise weight `ω(yt)=α` (α>1) is applied to tokens matching the domain lexicon (`V_dom` from the KG), amplifying their gradient contribution relative to high‑frequency function words.
- **Core assumption:** Uniform cross‑entropy loss under‑penalizes errors on rare but clinically crucial entities; gradient focusing optimizes for clinical importance.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract] "corrects terminology drift caused by the frequency–importance mismatch"
  - [section 3.3] "LLMERL... adaptively amplifying gradient contributions of high‑risk entities"
- **Break condition:** If `α` is set too high (e.g., α=3.0 in sensitivity analysis), it disrupts semantic coherence and degrades overall performance.

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim:** The Symbolic Veto Mechanism via a KG‑grounded Generate–Verify–Revise loop enforces hard safety boundaries by intercepting contraindications deterministically.
- **Mechanism:** After initial generation, the acupoints are checked against safety relations (`R_unsafe`) in the TCM‑KG. If violations `V(C,A)≠∅` are found, a bounded revision loop injects negative feedback (e.g., "LI4 is ProhibitedFor Pregnancy") to guide regeneration.
- **Core assumption:** Deterministic symbolic rules can reliably intercept rule‑violating outputs, and the model retains sufficient flexibility to revise prescriptions under explicit feedback.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract] "Symbolic Veto Mechanism... employing deterministic rules to intercept hallucinations"
  - [section 3.4] "V(C,A)= [(A × R_unsafe × A)∩E] ∪ [(C × R_unsafe × A)∩E]"
- **Break condition:** If the KG lacks coverage for a relevant contraindication or the model fails to revise within `T_max` retries, the fallback ("Human Confirmation Required") is triggered, indicating the safety envelope could not be closed.

## Foundational Learning
### Structured Chain‑of‑Thought (S‑CoT) Schema Design
- **Why needed here:** Acupuncture reasoning requires explicit causal steps (syndrome → pathology → principle → acupoints). Without a forced schema, LLMs shortcut to plausible but unauditable prescriptions.
- **Quick check:** Can you decompose a given acupuncture case into the four S‑CoT fields without omitting causal links?

### Knowledge Graph (KG) Safety Relation Modeling
- **Why needed here:** Safety rules (e.g., "LI4 ProhibitedFor Pregnancy") must be computable as graph edges for symbolic veto. This requires transforming clinical guidelines into `(subject, predicate, object)` triples.
- **Quick check:** How would you represent the rule "Hegu (LI4) is contraindicated in pregnancy" as a directed edge in the KG?

### Gradient Reweighting for Entity Importance (LMERL)
- **Why needed here:** Standard CE loss treats all tokens equally, but missing a rare acupoint name (e.g., "Taiyuan") is clinically more severe than a stopword error. LMERL’s `α` up‑weighting addresses this.
- **Quick check:** If `α=1.5` and the loss for a general token is `L_gen`, what is the effective loss for a high‑risk entity token? How does this affect LoRA’s low‑rank updates?

## Architecture Onboarding
### Component Map
S‑CoT Dataset & Schema → Schema‑constrained Fine‑Tuning (with LMERL) → Generate–Verify–Revise Loop (using TCM‑KG) → Safe Output.

### Critical Path
The pipeline must successfully: (1) produce a schema‑compliant JSON output; (2) have sufficient KG coverage of safety constraints; (3) allow the model to revise within the retry budget (`T_max=5`). Failure in any stage breaks the safety guarantee.

### Design Tradeoffs
- *Schema strictness vs. flexibility:* Rigid JSON schema ensures auditability but may reject valid non‑standard reasoning.
- *KG completeness vs. maintainability:* More safety edges reduce violations but increase curation cost and risk of over‑constraining.
- *Retry budget vs. latency:* Higher `T_max` improves repair chances but increases inference time; paper uses 5.

### Failure Signatures
- **Schema violation:** Model outputs non‑compliant JSON (missing fields, duplicate acupoints) → reasoning untraceable.
- **Entity drift:** High Entity‑Hallucination Rate despite S‑CoT → LMERL `α` insufficient or lexicon mismatch.
- **Unresolved violations:** GVR loop exhausts `T_max` without repair → fallback triggered, indicating KG rule conflict or model inability to revise.

### First 3 Experiments
1. **S‑CoT parsing validation:** Test if generated JSON adheres to schema on 100 validation samples; measure parse error rate.
2. **KG constraint coverage:** Sample 50 held‑out cases, manually verify if all contraindications present in clinical notes are encoded in `R_unsafe`.
3. **LMERL sensitivity sweep:** Run validation with `α ∈ {1.0, 1.5, 2.0, 3.0}`; pick `α` that maximizes Entity‑F1 without degrading BLEU/ROUGE (paper selects α=1.5).

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
### Open Question 1
- **Question:** How does the optimal α hyperparameter for the LMERL objective scale with the size of the base model or the diversity of the fine‑tuning corpus?
- **Basis in paper:** [explicit] The authors fixed α=1.5 after a sensitivity analysis on their internal validation set (Table 5) and noted that higher values degraded performance, but this was only tested with a Qwen3‑8B base model and their specific Acu‑Reasoning dataset.
- **Why unresolved:** The optimal reweighting intensity likely depends on the model’s pre‑training distribution and the prevalence of safety‑critical entities in the task data. Without testing across diverse model architectures and dataset scales, the generalizability of α=1.5 is uncertain.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** A systematic study varying base model size (e.g., 1B, 8B, 70B parameters) and dataset characteristics (e.g., entity density, long‑tail distribution) while measuring downstream entity fidelity and safety violation rates.

### Open Question 2
- **Question:** To what extent does the structured S‑CoT schema (symptom→diagnosis→pathology→principle→acupoints) generalize across different schools of thought or regional variations within Traditional Chinese Medicine?
- **Basis in paper:** [inferred] The S‑CoT dataset and schema were constructed from aggregated “authoritative” sources aligned with national standards (GB/T 12346‑2006) and expert consensus. The paper presents results on a single, curated dataset but does not discuss potential schema limitations for representing divergent TCM philosophies (e.g.,regional schools like “Warm School” vs. “Cool School”).
- **Why unresolved:** A schema that successfully encodes one clinical reasoning paradigm may inadvertently constrain or misrepresent alternative, clinically valid diagnostic pathways. This risks reducing the model’s real‑world applicability.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Curating and evaluating an S‑CoT dataset from explicitly contrasting TCM doctrinal sources, then testing whether a model trained on one schema can accurately reason with cases from another without schema‑conflict errors.

### Open Question 3
- **Question:** Can the binary “Symbolic Veto” mechanism be extended to model graded clinical risk or context‑dependent contraindications where absolute prohibition is inappropriate?
- **Basis in paper:** [explicit] The authors acknowledge in the Conclusion that “the binary veto mechanism may overlook nuanced, context‑dependent clinical trade‑offs” and propose “graded risk modeling” as future work. Their current KG encodes hard `ProhibitedFor` relations.
- **Why unresolved:** Clinical practice often involves managing relative risks (e.g., cautions vs. absolute禁忌). A system that only enforces hard stops may betoo conservative or fail to support nuanced decision‑making in complex, multi‑morbidity cases.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Developing a formal framework for probabilistic or utility‑based reasoning over the KG (e.g., annotating edges with risk scores and patient condition modifiers) and evaluating it on a test set of complex, multi‑factor cases against expert judgments on acceptable risk.

### Open Question 4
- **Question:** What are the primary failure modes and error patterns within the “Generate–Verify–Revise” loop that lead to the 40 violations detected (and successfully repaired) in the ablation study?
- **Basis in paper:** [inferred] The ablation study (Table 8) shows the full CORE‑Acu system repaired 40/40 first‑pass violations. However, the paper does not categorize these violations (e.g., were they due to incorrect condition extraction, rare acupoint misnaming, or novel contraindication patterns not in the KG?) or analyze the revision strategies that succeeded.
- **Why unresolved:** Understanding the nature of these residual errors is critical for improving the symbolic verifier’s rule completeness and the model’s ability to self‑correct. It also indicates the limits of the current KG’s coverage.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** A qualitative error analysis of the 40 violation cases, classifying the root cause (e.g., KG coverage gap, extraction error, model hallucination type) and analyzing the content of the successful revision prompts to identify effective correction patterns.

### Open Question 5
- **Question:** How robust is the LMERL‑driven entity fidelity improvement to distribution shifts, such as encountering novel acupoint names, synonyms, or non‑standardized clinical terminology in real‑world, unstructured practitioner notes?
- **Basis in paper:** [explicit and inferred] LMERL reweights loss based on a domain lexicon anchored to the KG’s standardized acupoint codes and names (Section 3.3.1, “Lexical Anchors”). The evaluation uses a held‑out test set where entities are covered by the KG. The paper notes the KG covers “approx. 90% of routine clinical pathways,” implying 10% are out‑of‑coverage.
- **Why unresolved:** The model’s precision on in‑vocabulary entities may not transfer to out‑of‑vocabulary or synonym‑rich inputs. A clinical system must handle the linguistic variability of real‑world notes without failing catastrophically on unseen entity forms.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Evaluating CORE‑Acu on a test set deliberately augmented with non‑standard synonyms, misspellings, or rare clinical terms for acupoints and syndromes, measuring the degradation in Entity‑F1 and SVR compared to the standard held‑out set.

## Limitations
- KG construction pipeline (sources, curation of safety edges) remains undisclosed.
- S‑CoT dataset generation method (semi‑automated inference‑and‑verify) lacks prompt and verification details.
- Entity‑to‑KG mapping for violation detection is underspecified; mapping heuristics unknown.
