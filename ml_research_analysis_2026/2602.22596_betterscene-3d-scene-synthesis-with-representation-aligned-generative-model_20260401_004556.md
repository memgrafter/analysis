# BetterScene: 3D Scene Synthesis with Representation-Aligned Generative Model

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2602.22596
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.22596
- **Reference Count:** 5
- **Primary Result:** Outperforms SOTA diffusion methods on DL3DV-10K with SSIM 0.579 vs. 0.562, LPIPS 0.347 vs. 0.352, FID 16.59 vs. 18.89, comparable PSNR 17.81.

## Executive Summary
BetterScene addresses the challenge of novel view synthesis from extremely sparse and unconstrained photos, where traditional methods often produce artifacts and inconsistencies. The approach enhances Stable Video Diffusion by incorporating a representation-aligned and equivariance-regularized VAE that leverages DINOv2 for feature alignment and temporal equivariance loss, enabling high-dimensional latent spaces. Coarse novel views are generated via feed-forward 3D Gaussian Splatting, and these are refined using the diffusion model. Evaluated on the DL3DV-10K dataset, BetterScene achieves superior performance over state-of-the-art diffusion methods on key metrics, demonstrating artifact-free and temporally consistent outputs.

## Method Summary
BetterScene introduces a modified VAE that aligns latent representations with high-level visual features from DINOv2 and enforces temporal equivariance through a dedicated loss. This VAE operates in a high-dimensional latent space (64 channels) to preserve fine details. The pipeline begins with a feed-forward 3D Gaussian Splatting module that produces coarse novel view sequences from sparse input images. These coarse sequences are then fed into the enhanced Stable Video Diffusion framework, where the VAE encoder maps them to latents, the diffusion model denoises them, and the VAE decoder reconstructs the final high-quality frames. The combination yields consistent and artifact-free results even under extreme sparsity.

## Key Results
- BetterScene achieves SSIM 0.579, LPIPS 0.347, FID 16.59 on DL3DV-10K, surpassing the previous SOTA diffusion methods.
- The 64-channel VAE ablation yields significant reconstruction gains: PSNR 31.21, SSIM 0.92, rFID 4.90.
- The method produces temporally stable outputs without common artifacts seen in other sparse-view synthesis approaches.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The core innovation lies in the representation-aligned VAE, which bridges high-dimensional image features and diffusion-friendly latents. By aligning the VAE’s latent space with DINOv2 embeddings, semantic and structural information is preserved. The temporal equivariance loss ensures that transformations (e.g., rotations, translations) applied to the input images produce predictable shifts in the latent space, which translates to stable video generation when combined with SVD. The coarse 3D Gaussian Splatting provides a plausible initial geometry that guides the diffusion process, reducing hallucinations and preserving multi-view consistency.

## Foundational Learning
### Variational Autoencoder (VAE)
- **Why needed:** Compresses images into a latent space suitable for diffusion models while enabling reconstruction.
- **Quick check:** Understand the reparameterization trick and the role of KL divergence.

### Representation Alignment
- **Why needed:** Aligns VAE latents with semantically rich features from a foundation model to preserve scene structure.
- **Quick check:** Verify that minimizing feature distance between input and reconstruction in a pre-trained network (e.g., DINOv2) improves perceptual quality.

### Equivariance Regularization
- **Why needed:** Enforces that latent representations transform consistently with image transformations, promoting temporal coherence.
- **Quick check:** Apply a known transformation (e.g., horizontal shift) to an image and confirm its latent changes predictably.

### DINOv2
- **Why needed:** Provides robust, self-supervised visual features that are used for alignment; invariant to many distortions.
- **Quick check:** Use DINOv2 to extract features from two augmented views of the same image and compute similarity.

### 3D Gaussian Splatting
- **Why needed:** Generates a quick coarse 3D representation from sparse views, offering a geometric scaffold for refinement.
- **Quick check:** Render a novel view from a recreated 3D Gaussian scene and observe basic correctness.

### Stable Video Diffusion (SVD)
- **Why needed:** A powerful video generation model that can refine coarse inputs into high-fidelity, temporally consistent sequences.
- **Quick check:** Run SVD on a simple video and observe its ability to maintain temporal coherence.

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component Map:**  
Input sparse images → Feed-forward 3D Gaussian Splatting → Coarse novel-view video → BetterScene-VAE Encoder → Latent representation → Stable Video Diffusion (denoising) → BetterScene-VAE Decoder → Refined novel-view video

**Critical Path:**  
1. 3D Gaussian Splatting produces a coarse 3D representation and renders a dense set of temporal views.  
2. VAE encoder maps frames to high-dimensional latents (64 channels).  
3. SVD performs latent-space diffusion, guided by the equivariance and alignment constraints learned during VAE training.  
4. VAE decoder converts refined latents back to pixel space.

**Design Tradeoffs:**  
- High-dimensional VAE latents improve reconstruction but risk generation collapse without proper regularization.  
- Gaussian Splatting offers fast rendering but may introduce geometry errors that diffusion must correct.  
- SVD provides high-quality results but at significant computational cost during training and inference.

**Failure Signatures:**  
- Blurry or flattened textures in the VAE reconstruction indicate insufficient alignment.  
- Temporal flickering in the output video suggests weak equivariance regularization.  
- Persistent multi-view inconsistencies (e.g., floating artifacts) point to Gaussian Splatting failures not corrected by diffusion.

**First Three Experiments:**  
1. **Ablation of alignment loss:** Train the VAE without DINOv2 alignment and measure degradation in downstream NVS quality.  
2. **Latent dimensionality sweep:** Compare reconstruction metrics for VAE latent channels (4, 16, 64, 128) to find the optimal trade-off.  
3. **Foundation model swap:** Replace DINOv2 with CLIP-ViT or MAE and evaluate impact on artifact suppression and detail preservation.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
### Open Question 1
- **Question:** Can the computational expense of the SVD-based refinement in BetterScene be reduced without sacrificing performance?
- **Basis in paper:** [explicit] The conclusion states: "the SVD model in the BetterScene framework requires computationally expensive training. Future work could explore replacing this pipeline with more efficient video diffusion architectures."
- **Why unresolved:** SVD's high computational cost hinders scalability and practical deployment; alternative efficient architectures are not investigated in this work.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Benchmarking the performance of more efficient video diffusion models (e.g., transformer-based or distilled versions) within the BetterScene pipeline on DL3DV-10K, measuring trade-offs between speed, memory usage, and NVS quality metrics (PSNR, SSIM, LPIPS, FID).

### Open Question 2
- **Question:** What is the upper limit or optimal dimensionality for the VAE latent channels in BetterScene's representation-aligned framework?
- **Basis in paper:** [inferred] The ablation study (Table 2) tests only up to 64 channels, showing consistent improvement, but the authors note the "optimization dilemma" where higher dimensions risk degrading generation performance if not properly regularized.
- **Why unresolved:** The paper demonstrates clear gains from 4 → 64 channels but does not explore whether even higher dimensions (e.g., 128, 256) continue to improve reconstruction and generation or encounter diminishing returns or collapse.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Train BetterScene-VAE with increasing latent channel sizes (e.g., 128, 256) under the same alignment and equivariance losses, evaluating reconstruction metrics (PSNR, SSIM, rFID) and downstream NVS performance after SVD fine-tuning to identify the point where gains plateau or generation quality suffers.

### Open Question 3
- **Question:** How does the choice of vision foundation model for representation alignment affect the quality and generalizability of the learned latent space?
- **Basis in paper:** [inferred] The method exclusively uses DINOv2 for alignment loss, citing its robustness, but does not compare against other foundation models (e.g., CLIP, MAE, Stable Diffusion's CLIP-ViT) that capture different aspects of visual semantics.
- **Why unresolved:** The alignment loss is a core component; different foundation models may impose distinct priors that could affect equivariance, reconstruction fidelity, and hallucination control in unseen scenes.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Replace DINOv2 with alternative foundation models in training BetterScene-VAE, while keeping other losses identical. Compare reconstruction metrics on DL3DV-10K and test downstream NVS quality with SVD, analyzing if certain models yield better trade-offs between detail preservation and artifact suppression.

### Open Question 4
- **Question:** Does BetterScene generalize to datasets with different scene distributions, camera configurations, or image quality compared to DL3DV-10K?
- **Basis in paper:** [explicit] Evaluation is conducted solely on the DL3DV-10K dataset; no cross-dataset validation is reported.
- **Why unresolved:** DL3DV-10K contains real-world, unbounded scenes but may have specific characteristics (e.g., lighting, occlusion patterns). Performance on other benchmarks (e.g., NeRF Synthetic, Tanks & Temples, or sparse-view mobile captures) remains unknown, raising questions about overfitting to DL3DV's distribution.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Apply the trained BetterScene model (without retraining) to standard NVS benchmarks with sparse views, reporting standard metrics. Alternatively, fine-tune on a small subset of a new dataset and measure adaptation efficiency and performance changes.

### Open Question 5
- **Question:** How well does the equivariance regularization in the latent space translate to measurable temporal consistency in the final video outputs of the refined novel view sequences?
- **Basis in paper:** [explicit] The method introduces latent equivariance loss (Eq. 5) and reconstruction equivariance loss (Eq. 6) to enforce transformation consistency, but evaluation relies only on per-image metrics (PSNR, SSIM, LPIPS, FID) without dedicated video consistency metrics.
- **Why unresolved:** While equivariance is claimed to improve temporal stability, the paper does not quantify frame-to-frame consistency (e.g., flickering, motion artifacts) in the generated sequences. The impact of the equivariance losses on actual video quality is inferred but not directly measured.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Compute video-specific metrics on generated multi-view sequences (e.g., FVD, LPIPS across adjacent frames, or flow-based consistency scores) and correlate them with variations in equivariance loss weights. Ablate the equivariance losses entirely and perform a qualitative and quantitative comparison of temporal artifacts.

## Limitations
- Evaluation limited to a single dataset (DL3DV-10K); generalizability to other capture styles or higher resolutions is unknown.
- Reported metric improvements are modest and lack statistical significance testing; confidence in superiority is moderate.
- No code or model release, hindering reproducibility; computational details are omitted.
