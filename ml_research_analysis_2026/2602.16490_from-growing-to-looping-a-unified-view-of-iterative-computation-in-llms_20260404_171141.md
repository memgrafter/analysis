# From Growing to Looping: A Unified View of Iterative Computation in LLMs
## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2602.16490
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.16490
- Reference count: 40
- Primary result: Looping and depth growth unify as mechanisms to build iterative computation into LLM depth, improving reasoning efficiency; combined use yields up to 2x gains on primitives.

## Executive Summary
Looping (weight‑tied recurrent blocks) and depth growth (middle‑layer duplication during training) are two techniques that inject iterative computation into a transformer’s depth. The paper unifies them mechanistically, showing both produce convergent depth‑wise signatures: greater reliance on later layers and periodic patterns aligned with the repeated/grown block size. Compositionally, applying inference‑time looping to the middle block of a depth‑grown model doubles accuracy on reasoning primitives without any loop‑specific training. Both approaches adapt more efficiently than baselines with extra in‑context examples or fine‑tuning data, and depth‑grown models gain especially from math‑heavy cooldown data, with further improvements from middle‑block looping.

## Method Summary
Experiments use SmolLM backbones (360M/1.7B) trained on 200–400B tokens from SmolLM‑Corpus. Looped models tie weights across repetitions of an L‑layer block (e.g., Loop(4×6)), optionally with unique outer layers (Loop(e‑L×k‑d)). Depth‑grown models (LIDAS) duplicate a middle block during training via a PROP‑α schedule (α=1) while copying optimizer states. Evaluation covers 22 benchmarks, emphasizing reasoning primitives, GSM8K, and math word problems. Mechanistic analyses include depth score, Tuned Lens early‑exit, residual norm growth, local‑effects interventions, and layer‑swap robustness. A “cooldown” phase with increased math tokens (e.g., Nemotron‑CC‑Math‑4+) may follow, and retrofitted looping (repeat a middle block once during cooldown) tests composition.

## Key Results
- Both looping and depth growth shift indispensable computation to later layers and induce periodic (sub)layer patterns with a period matching the block size (B=4), as shown by depth scores, residual norms, and attention contribution cycles.
- Combining them—looping the middle block of a depth‑grown model—improves reasoning primitive accuracy by up to 2× even though the model never saw loops during training.
- Both methods adapt more efficiently than baselines that rely on additional in‑context examples or fine‑tuning data; depth‑grown models benefit most from math‑heavy cooldown, and this boost is further amplified by middle‑block looping.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1: Depth-utilization shift to later layers
**Claim:** Both looped and depth‑grown models concentrate indispensable computation in later layers, contrasting with standard transformers where later layers contribute less.  
**Mechanism:** Training with either weight tying (looping) or depth growth (duplicating middle layers) counteracts the “Curse of Depth” by making the network rely more on late layers for final predictions. This is evidenced by multiple diagnostics: higher depth scores, slower residual norm growth, and lower early‑exit vocabulary overlap in later layers.  
**Core assumption:** The increased reliance on later layers reflects iterative refinement where earlier layers set up computations that later layers finalize.  
**Evidence anchors:** [abstract] “both create convergent depth‑wise signatures: greater reliance on late layers”; [Section 4.1] Depth score, Tuned Lens early‑exit accuracy, and residual norm growth analyses across Baseline, LIDAS, Loop(4×6), Loop(4‑4×4‑4) show consistent late‑layer usage; [corpus] Weak direct anchor; neighboring work on depth diagnostics (e.g., Csordás et al. [9] cited) provides context but the specific signature is from this paper.  
**Break condition:** If interventions show that removing later layers does not disproportionately degrade performance compared to standard models, the mechanism would be questioned.

### Mechanism 2: Periodic (sub)layer patterns aligned with block size
**Claim:** Both architectures induce recurring computational patterns with periodicity matching the looped block size or grown block size (B=4).  
**Mechanism:** The repeated structure (either explicit via weight tying or implicit via initialized duplication) causes periodic behavior in residual stream updates and sublayer contributions. Specifically, attention contributions show a 4‑layer cycle, and local effect heatmaps reveal “aggregation” layers every B layers that integrate information broadly from preceding layers.  
**Core assumption:** The periodicity indicates that computation proceeds in discrete iterative steps, with each block‑sized segment performing a similar transformation function.  
**Evidence anchors:** [Section 4.1] Fig. 3 shows periodic attention contributions; Fig. 4 shows aggregation‑layer pattern every 4 layers for LIDAS and Loop(4‑4×4×4); [Section B.3] Fig. 14 shows mean relative layer contribution with periodic local maxima.  
**Break condition:** If the periodic patterns disappear under different block sizes or if the period does not match the architectural repetition, the link to iterative computation weakens.

### Mechanism 3: Middle-block robustness and order‑sensitivity trade‑off
**Claim:** Fully looped models (no unique encoder‑decoder layers) are more sensitive to layer order interventions than depth‑grown models, but introducing unique outer layers (Loop(e‑L×k‑d)) recovers robustness similar to growth.  
**Mechanism:** Depth‑growth by middle‑block duplication seems to allow the model to naturally develop unique encoding/decoding layers while the middle becomes a flexible, robust “looping” region. Fully tied loops force exact repetition, making the entire sequence order‑sensitive. Partially looped designs with untied boundaries balance iteration with structural flexibility.  
**Core assumption:** Robustness to local reordering indicates that the computational role of layers within a block is more interchangeable, consistent with a repeated iterative process.  
**Evidence anchors:** [Section 4.1] Fig. 5: swapping a single layer degrades Loop(4×6) much more than LIDAS or Loop(4‑4×4‑4); [Section 4.1] “depth‑growing mechanism … allows the model to flexibly learn unique encoder–decoder layers, with the middle of the network acting as a relaxed version of a fully looped model.”  
**Break condition:** If all architectures showed similar sensitivity to layer swaps, the connection between robustness and iterative middle blocks would be undermined.

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component map:**  
Standard Transformer: [ℓ0, ℓ1, ..., ℓL‑1] (all untied)  
Looped: Loop(L×k) – Unique block of L layers repeated k times → effective depth L·k  
Looped (encoder‑decoder): Loop(e‑L×k‑d) – Unique encoding block, recurrent middle block, unique decoding block  
Depth‑grown (MIDAS/LIDAS): Start shallow, duplicate middle block/layer during training stages; all layers remain untied at inference.

**Critical path:**  
1. Choose target effective depth (e.g., 24 layers).  
2. For looping: Design recurrent block size (L). Determine k. Decide on unique outer layers.  
3. For growing: Choose block size B. Apply PROP‑α schedule for growth stages (α=1 used).  
4. Implement training loop that at each growth stage deep‑copies middle block/layer, continues training.  
5. For inference‑time looping on grown models: Identify candidate middle 4‑layer block; at inference, repeat that block’s forward pass.

**Design tradeoffs:**  
- Params: Looping minimizes unique parameters; growing keeps all parameters unique (higher param count but same training compute savings).  
- Inference FLOPs: Both increase compute via repetition; looped does this by design, grown via retrofitting.  
- Training FLOPs: Growing reduces training FLOPs; looping does not (trains full depth each step unless adapted).  
- Robustness: Fully looped (no unique outer layers) is order‑sensitive; partially looped or grown are more robust.

**Failure signatures:**  
- Looped model fails to extrapolate to more recursions at inference (performance degrades after optimal k).  
- Grown model shows no benefit from middle‑block looping → may indicate poor block choice or that growth did not create reusable structure.  
- Depth diagnostics show no late‑layer shift or periodicity → growth/looping may have been ineffective.

**First 3 experiments:**  
1. Depth‑utilization comparison: Compute depth score and early‑exit accuracy for Baseline vs. your grown/looped model. Expect greater late‑layer reliance for iterative models.  
2. Repeat‑block ablation (no adaptation): For a grown model, select each 4‑layer block, repeat it during inference (e.g., 2×). Measure accuracy on a reasoning primitive. Expect middle blocks (especially 3rd/4th) to help; baseline should not.  
3. Layer swap intervention: Swap two consecutive layers in the middle of the network. Compare accuracy drop between fully looped (Loop(L×k)), partially looped (Loop(e‑L×k‑d)), and grown model. Expect fully tied looped to degrade most.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
#### Open Question 1
Why do depth‑grown models, particularly LIDAS, exhibit larger reasoning gains from math‑heavy cooldown mixtures compared to looped and standard models?  
*Basis in paper:* Table 3 shows LIDAS improves more on Math Word Problems and GSM8K after adding Nemotron‑CC‑Math‑4+ during cooldown, while baseline and looped models show smaller relative gains. The paper does not analyze this differential.  
*Why unresolved:* The paper presents the observation empirically but does not examine how the weight structure from depth growth interacts with math‑focused data during fine‑tuning. The underlying mechanism—whether related to optimization, representation learning, or inductive bias—remains unclear.  
*What evidence would resolve:* Compare training trajectories, gradient norms, or representation quality (e.g., on math‑specific features) across LIDAS, looped, and baseline models during cooldown. Ablating the proportion of math tokens and measuring corresponding changes in depth‑utilization diagnostics could reveal the source of the difference.

#### Open Question 2
What causes fully looped models to be more sensitive to layer order interventions (e.g., swapping layers) compared to depth‑grown models and partially looped models with unique encoder‑decoder blocks?  
*Basis in paper:* Figure 5 reveals that swapping a single layer in Loop(4×6) drastically reduces performance on Lambada and Variable Assignment Math, while LIDAS and Loop(4‑4×4‑4) remain robust. The paper notes this but does not explain why.  
*Why unresolved:* The authors hypothesize that depth growth allows flexible learning of unique encoder‑decoder layers with a relaxed middle, but this is not tested. The exact property—such as weight redundancy, gradient flow, or attention specialization—that confers robustness is not identified.  
*What evidence would resolve:* Perform ablations by gradually introducing unique layers into a fully looped model and measuring the resulting robustness. Analyze weight statistics, attention entropy, or neuron activation patterns across models to pinpoint what makes the middle block tolerant to reordering.

#### Open Question 3
Do the benefits of depth growth and looping generalize to larger model scales (e.g., 10B+ parameters) and different transformer architectures (e.g., encoder‑decoder, MoE, alternative normalization)?  
*Basis in paper:* All experiments use SmolLM decoder‑only models up to 1.7B parameters with pre‑LayerNorm. The paper does not explore whether the observed depth‑wise signatures and reasoning gains hold at larger scales or with architectural variations.  
*Why unresolved:* Scaling laws and architectural choices can fundamentally alter model behavior. Without empirical validation beyond the studied regime, the claim that these techniques improve reasoning universally is unsupported.  
*What evidence would resolve:* Train and evaluate depth‑grown and looped models at scales like 10B, 70B, or 100B across diverse architectures (e.g., decoder‑only with RMSNorm, encoder‑decoder, Mixture‑of‑Experts). Measure depth‑utilization diagnostics and reasoning performance to test if the inductive biases persist.

#### Open Question 4
Is there a theoretical foundation that formally relates depth growth and looping, beyond the empirical similarity in depth‑wise signatures?  
*Basis in paper:* The paper unifies the two techniques mechanistically by showing convergent patterns (e.g., increased late‑layer reliance, periodic sublayer usage) but provides no mathematical derivation or formal equivalence.  
*Why unresolved:* A formal theory would enable principled design choices—e.g., predicting when to grow versus loop—and clarify whether one method subsumes the other. The current unification remains descriptive.  
*What evidence would resolve:* Derive analytical expressions for the forward pass of a depth‑grown model and show it approximates a looped model under certain conditions (e.g., as growth steps become infinitesimal). Alternatively, prove in a simplified setting (e.g., linear transformers) that both minimize a similar regularized loss, or that their gradient dynamics are equivalent.

## Limitations
- No public implementation or checkpoints; exact training hyperparameters (batch size, learning rate schedule) and cooldown data mixing ratios are unspecified, hindering faithful reproduction.
- Generalizability beyond SmolLM (≤1.7B) and GSM8K‑focused diagnostics is untested; benefits may not hold for larger scales or different architectures.
- The observed mechanistic links (late‑layer shift, periodicity) are correlational; causal role of these patterns has not been directly ablated.
