# Evaluating GFlowNet from partial episodes for stable and flexible policy-based training
## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID**: 2603.01047
- **Source URL**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.01047
- **Reference count**: 40
- **Primary result**: The Subtrajectory Evaluation Balance (Sub-EB) objective enables stable, fast convergence and flexible policy-based training for GFlowNets, matching or outperforming baselines across multiple tasks.

## Executive Summary
GFlowNets aim to sample combinatorial objects with probability proportional to a reward function, but policy-based training suffers from unreliable estimation of the divergence between forward and backward distributions. This paper introduces the Subtrajectory Evaluation Balance (Sub-EB) objective, which learns an evaluation function \(V(s)\) from partial episodes to measure that divergence. Sub-EB unifies value- and policy-based approaches, leading to stable and rapid convergence while supporting advanced features such as parameterized backward policies and offline data collection. Experiments on hypergrids, biological sequence design, Bayesian network structure learning, and molecular graph generation demonstrate that Sub-EB achieves performance equal to or better than existing methods (Sub-TB, Q-Much, CV) across metrics including total variation, Jensen–Shannon divergence, mode accuracy, and diversity.

## Method Summary
Sub-EB operates in an online policy-based loop. A batch of forward trajectories is sampled using the current forward policy \(\pi_F\). The evaluation function \(V\) is updated by minimizing the Sub-EB loss, which sums over all subtrajectories \(\tau_{i:j}\) within each trajectory:
\[
\mathcal{L}_V(\phi) = \mathbb{E}_{P_F(\tau)}\left[ \sum_{i<j} w_{j-i} \, \delta_V(\tau_{i:j};\phi)^2 \right],
\]
with \(\delta_V(\tau_{i:j};\phi) = \log\frac{P_F(\tau_{i:j}|s_i) \exp(V(s_i;\phi))}{P_B(\tau_{i:j}|s_j) \exp(V(s_j;\phi))}\). Weights \(w_{j-i} = \lambda^{j-i} / \sum_{i<j} \lambda^{j-i}\) use \(\lambda=0.9\). After updating \(V\), the forward policy is updated using a gradient estimator derived from \(V\): the advantage-like term
\[
A_\gamma(s_h \to s_{h+1}) = \sum_{i=h}^H \gamma^{i-h} \bigl( R(s_i \to s_{i+1}) + V(s_{i+1}) - V(s_h) \bigr),
\]
where \(R(s_i \to s_{i+1}) = \log\frac{\pi_B(s_i|s_{i+1})}{\pi_F(s_{i+1}|s_i)}\). This update does not require a separate value function stage. The method extends to parameterized backward policies and offline regimes. Baselines include Sub-TB (value-based), RL with \(\lambda\)-TD, and Q-Much.

## Key Results
- Sub-EB provides stable and fast convergence, overcoming the instability of standard policy-based GFlowNet training.
- It supports flexible configurations, including learned backward policies and offline data collection, without sacrificing performance.
- Across hypergrid, sequence design, Bayesian network, and molecular graph tasks, Sub-EB matches or surpasses baselines (Sub-TB, Q-Much, CV) in total variation, Jensen–Shannon divergence, mode accuracy, and top-reward diversity.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
Flow balance conditions on a DAG imply a relationship between forward and backward probabilities that can be expressed via an evaluation function \(V(s)\). Sub-EB learns \(V\) to satisfy this relationship on subtrajectory pairs, yielding a consistent estimate of the divergence between the forward and backward distributions. This estimate serves as a regularizer for \(V\) and as a critic for policy improvement. The policy gradient for \(\pi_F\) uses an advantage signal derived from \(V\), approximating the optimal policy’s performance without explicit likelihood‑ratio gradients. By leveraging partial episodes, Sub-EB reduces variance and enables use of any trajectory segment, which is key for both online and offline training. The framework also accommodates parameterized backward policies by treating them symmetrically in the loss.

## Foundational Learning
- **GFlowNet**: A framework for generative modeling of discrete combinatorial objects, with the goal of sampling objects with probability proportional to a given reward. *Why needed*: It defines the task of matching a target distribution over a DAG of states. *Quick check*: What property must the forward policy satisfy to achieve \(P^*(x) \propto R(x)\)? (Flow balance at every state.)
- **Flow Balance**: The condition that for every state, incoming flow equals outgoing flow, and terminal flow equals the reward. *Why needed*: It is necessary and sufficient for the forward policy to produce the desired distribution. *Quick check*: How is flow defined at the source state? (Typically \(F(s_0)=1\).)
- **Subtrajectory**: A contiguous segment of a full trajectory. *Why needed*: Sub-EB uses subtrajectories to evaluate flow balance locally, enabling learning from partial episodes and improving gradient signal. *Quick check*: How does subtrajectory length affect bias/variance? (Longer subtrajectories capture global consistency but increase variance.)
- **Evaluation Function \(V(s)\)**: A scalar approximating the log‑flow (or divergence) at each state. *Why needed*: It transforms flow balance into a differentiable loss. *Quick check*: In the optimal solution, what value does \(V(s)\) take? (Often \(V(s) = \log Z(s)\), where \(Z(s)\) is the total flow out of \(s\).)
- **Sub‑EB Loss**: \(\mathcal{L}_V = \mathbb{E}\left[\sum_{i<j} w_{j-i} \delta_V(\tau_{i:j})^2\right]\) with \(\delta_V = \log\frac{F(\tau_{i:j}|s_i)e^{V(s_i)}}{B(\tau_{i:j}|s_j)e^{V(s_j)}}\). *Why needed*: It directly enforces flow balance on subtrajectories, providing a stable learning objective. *Quick check*: What does \(\delta_V = 0\) imply? (Exact flow balance for that subtrajectory.)
- **Policy Gradient via \(V\)**: The forward policy is updated using \(\nabla_\theta V(s_0)\) approximated by an advantage sum over the trajectory. *Why needed*: It yields an unbiased gradient estimate for maximizing expected reward without explicit likelihood‑ratio gradients. *Quick check*: How is the advantage \(A_\gamma(s_h \to s_{h+1})\) computed? (Discounted sum of \((R + V(\text{next}) - V(\text{current}))\).)

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component map** (plain text):
Forward policy π_F(s→s'; θ) → Trajectory Sampler → Batch of trajectories τ
Backward policy π_B(s|s') (uniform or parameterized) 
Evaluation network V(s; φ)
Loss module: computes Sub-EB loss over all subtrajectories of each τ using π_B and V
Optimizer: updates φ (minimize loss), then computes advantages and updates θ (and ψ for π_B if used)

**Critical path**:
1. Sample a batch of forward trajectories using current \(\pi_F\).
2. For each trajectory, enumerate all subtrajectories \(\tau_{i:j}\) and compute \(\delta_V\) with weights \(w_{j-i}\).
3. Average the squared errors to obtain \(\mathcal{L}_V\) and perform a gradient step on \(\phi\).
4. Using the updated (or same) \(V\), compute the advantage \(A_\gamma\) for each transition in the batch.
5. Form the policy gradient estimator \(\hat{g} = \frac{1}{K}\sum_{\tau \in D}\sum_{h=0}^{H-1} A_\gamma(s_h \to s_{h+1}) \nabla_\theta \log \pi_F(s_{h+1}|s_h)\) and update \(\theta\) (and \(\psi\) if applicable).
6. Repeat from step 1.

**Design tradeoffs**:
- *Subtrajectory sum vs. cost*: Using all subtrajectories minimizes bias but yields \(O(L^2)\) complexity per trajectory; efficient vectorization (via cumulative sums) is essential.
- *Discount \(\lambda\)*: Higher \(\lambda\) (e.g., 0.9) emphasizes long‑range consistency but can increase gradient variance.
- *Network sharing*: Sharing layers between \(\pi_F\) and \(V\) reduces parameters but may cause interference; the paper uses separate networks (details unspecified).
- *Uniform vs. learned \(\pi_B\)*: Uniform \(\pi_B\) simplifies training; a parameterized \(\pi_B\) can improve sample efficiency but requires careful joint optimization.

**Failure signatures**:
- **High total variation** after many iterations: likely incorrect sign for the \(\theta\) update or poor \(V\) learning.
- **Memory explosion** for long trajectories: indicates naive subtrajectory enumeration; should switch to cumulative‑sum vectorization.
- **Oscillating or increasing \(\mathcal{L}_V\)**: learning rates too high, or improper handling of terminal states in \(\delta_V\).
- **Policy collapse** to deterministic actions early: missing action masking for invalid moves or lack of entropy regularization.

**First three experiments**:
1. **Sanity check on tiny hypergrid** (e.g., \(8 \times 8\)): Train Sub‑EB with both signs for the \(\theta\) update. The correct sign yields decreasing \(D_{\text{TV}}\) and increasing \(V(s_0)\).
2. **Loss implementation verification**: For a fixed short trajectory (length ≤ 5), compute Sub‑EB loss by explicit enumeration of subtrajectories and compare with the codebase’s vectorized version; they must match exactly.
3. **Parameterized backward policy test**: On a \(32 \times 32\) hypergrid, replace uniform \(\pi_B\) with a learned network and train jointly. Compare total variation against the uniform baseline; Sub‑EB should at least match performance, confirming its flexibility.

## Limitations
- **Gradient sign ambiguity**: The theoretical direction for the policy gradient update is unclear; the paper’s description may contain a typo, requiring empirical validation.
- **State encoding details**: The exact “K‑hot” encoding and network architecture are unspecified, which can hinder exact reproduction.
- **Computational complexity**: Summing over all subtrajectories leads to \(O(L^2)\) cost per trajectory; without efficient vectorization, training on large grids becomes infeasible.
- **Evaluation surrogates**: For large tasks the paper uses indirect metrics (mode accuracy, FCS) whose correlation with true distributional divergence remains unverified.

## Confidence
- Theoretical validity of Sub‑EB: **High**
- Stability and fast convergence: **Medium**
- Flexibility (parameterized backward, offline): **Medium**
- Empirical superiority over Sub‑TB/RL: **Medium**
