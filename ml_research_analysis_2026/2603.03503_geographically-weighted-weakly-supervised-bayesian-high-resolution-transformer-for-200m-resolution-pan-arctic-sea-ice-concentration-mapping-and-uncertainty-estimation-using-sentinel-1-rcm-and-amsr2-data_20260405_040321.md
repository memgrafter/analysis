# Geographically-Weighted Weakly Supervised Bayesian High-Resolution Transformer for 200m Resolution Pan-Arctic Sea Ice Concentration Mapping and Uncertainty Estimation using Sentinel-1, RCM, and AMSR2 Data

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2603.03503  
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.03503  
- **Reference count:** 38  
- **Primary result:** Achieves 200-meter resolution pan-Arctic SIC maps with calibrated uncertainty (BBB ECE 0.0018–0.0035), improving feature detection (0.70 overall accuracy) and preserving large-scale patterns (Sentinel-1 R² = 0.90 vs. ASI).

## Executive Summary
This study presents a method to map pan-Arctic sea ice concentration (SIC) at 200-meter resolution with robust uncertainty quantification. It addresses four key challenges: resolving subtle ice features (e.g., leads, floes), learning from inexact low-resolution NASA Team labels, quantifying model uncertainty, and fusing heterogeneous sensor data (Sentinel-1/RCM SAR, AMSR2 passive microwave). The core innovation is a Bayesian High-Resolution Transformer with global (GloFormer) and local (LoFormer) attention modules, a geographically-weighted weakly supervised L1 loss to prioritize reliable training regions, and Bayes by Backpropagation for uncertainty. Decision-level fusion prioritizes Sentinel-1’s high-resolution detail, then RCM and AMSR2. Results show improved SIC accuracy and well-calibrated uncertainty estimates, enabling more reliable operational sea ice monitoring.

## Method Summary
The method trains three independent Bayesian Transformer models on Sentinel-1, RCM, and AMSR2 data. The architecture uses a hierarchical attention design: GloFormer applies multi-head self-attention across all spatial tokens to model pan-Arctic context, while LoFormer applies attention within each token’s internal patches to capture fine-scale details (repeated four times). Training employs a weakly supervised L1 loss that aggregates predictions and NASA Team SIC labels at the cluster level (e.g., 20-30% SIC bin) and scales each cluster’s loss by a geographic weight derived from NIC ice charts—higher for pure ice/water, lower for the marginal ice zone (MIZ). Bayesian inference via Bayes by Backpropagation treats model weights as random variables, adding a KL divergence regularizer to the loss. For fusion, the three models’ SIC and uncertainty maps are combined via a priority overlay: AMSR2 provides full coverage, RCM fills gaps where available, and Sentinel-1 overwrites both where available, selecting the pixel with the lowest uncertainty at each location.

## Key Results
- The model achieves 0.70 overall feature detection accuracy on Sentinel-1 data and preserves large-scale SIC patterns (R² = 0.90 relative to ASI).
- Bayesian Deep Learning with Bayes by Backpropagation yields the lowest, best-calibrated uncertainty (ECE 0.0018–0.0035) compared to MC Dropout and epoch ensembles, with low standard deviations (0.01%–0.33% for Sentinel-1).
- Decision-level fusion successfully integrates SAR and PM data, maintaining Sentinel-1’s high-resolution detail while filling coverage gaps, and produces uncertainty maps consistent with sensor reliability.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1: Dual-Scale Transformer Architecture
The GloFormer (global attention across tokens) and LoFormer (local attention within token patches) enable extraction of both pan-Arctic patterns and subtle local features (e.g., leads). Sequential processing first establishes coarse spatial relationships then refines details. Ablation shows removing LoFormer reduces overall accuracy (e.g., Sentinel-1 Ice Acc 0.83 → 0.80), confirming its role in capturing high-frequency information.

### Mechanism 2: Geographically-Weighted Weak Supervision
The L1-GW loss aggregates predictions and NASA Team labels at the cluster level and weights each cluster by a geographic weight (GW) from NIC charts. This reduces the impact of noisy labels in the MIZ by assigning lower weights to ambiguous regions, while prioritizing learning from more reliable pure ice/water areas. Using an unweighted L1 loss decreases water detection accuracy (e.g., Sentinel-1 Water Acc 0.57 → 0.54), indicating increased false positives in the MIZ.

### Mechanism 3: Bayesian Uncertainty Quantification
Bayes by Backpropagation (BBB) models weights with Gaussian variational posteriors, combining task loss with KL divergence to the prior. This explicitly captures epistemic uncertainty and yields better calibration than heuristic methods. BBB shows lower Expected Calibration Error (ECE) across all sensors than MC Dropout or epoch ensembles and produces more consistent uncertainty ranges (e.g., lower in ice pack, higher in MIZ).

### Mechanism 4: Decision-Level Sensor Fusion
Training separate models per sensor and fusing via priority overlay (Sentinel-1 > RCM > AMSR2) leverages each sensor’s strengths: Sentinel-1’s high-resolution detail, RCM’s intermediate coverage, and AMSR2’s complete spatial context. The order respects Sentinel-1’s superior resolution and lower uncertainty (justified by Table 4), while filling gaps with coarser data. Visual results confirm fused maps retain fine SAR detail where available and use AMSR2 elsewhere.

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map:** Input (200m SAR/PM chip) -> Patch Merging -> [GloFormer (AmongAttn) -> LoFormer (WithinAttn) -> MLP] x4 -> Output Head (interpolation) -> Bayesian Wrapper (Gaussian weights) -> (Inference: 30 stochastic passes) -> Decision Fusion (Priority: S1 > RCM > AMSR2)
- **Critical path:** Data Preprocessing -> Patch Merging -> [GloFormer Block -> LoFormer Block] x4 -> Output Head -> (Bayesian sampling) -> Decision Fusion.
- **Design tradeoffs:** 
  1. LoFormer is critical for SAR’s 200m detail (ablation shows performance drop) but may overfit AMSR2’s coarser, noisier channels.
  2. BBB provides best calibration but requires multiple stochastic forward passes (~30), increasing inference compute.
  3. Decision-level fusion is simple and respects sensor reliability but prevents cross-sensor feature learning, potentially causing seam artifacts.
- **Failure signatures:** 
  1. Smoothed/blocky outputs in ice pack: LoFormer ineffective or removed.
  2. Overestimation in MIZ: GW incorrectly implemented (e.g., MIZ weights too high).
  3. Homogeneous uncertainty maps (esp. AMSR2): BBB misconfigured (prior/sigma) or using poorly calibrated MC Dropout.
  4. Seams in fused map: Decision fusion order logic flawed or availability mask error.
- **First 3 experiments:**
  1. Ablate LoFormer block and validate feature detection accuracy on high-resolution SAR tiles; expect drop in Ice/Water accuracy.
  2. Compare BBB, MC Dropout, and Epoch Ensemble uncertainty calibration (ECE) on Sentinel-1; expect BBB lowest and most consistent across ice classes.
  3. Test fusion orders (S1>RCM>AMSR2 vs. reverse vs. RCM-centric) against ASI; expect proposed order best (highest R², lowest MAE).

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
### Open Question 1
- **Question:** How does the model generalize across seasons (melt vs. freeze-up) beyond September minimum extent?
- **Basis:** Conclusion states future work needed to assess cross-seasonal performance; current evaluation limited to September.
- **Why unresolved:** September has distinct ice conditions (expanded MIZ, melt ponds); performance in dynamic spring/fall unknown.
- **Resolution evidence:** Evaluate on multi-season satellite data (e.g., March, June, December) and compare accuracy/uncertainty seasonally.

### Open Question 2
- **Question:** Can LoFormer be optimized for AMSR2’s coarser 89 GHz channels to improve resolution without overfitting atmospheric noise?
- **Basis:** Ablation (Table 7) shows LoFormer removal *increased* AMSR2 ice detection accuracy, suggesting overfitting to atmospheric interference.
- **Why unresolved:** LoFormer designed for SAR detail; no tailored variant for PM tested.
- **Resolution evidence:** Redesign LoFormer for AMSR2 (e.g., adjust patch size, add atmospheric correction) and compare against original on held-out PM test set.

### Open Question 3
- **Question:** Would late-stage feature-level fusion yield more consistent cross-sensor SIC and uncertainty than decision-level fusion?
- **Basis:** Authors note decision fusion “does not allow cross-sensor perception” and suggest future feature-level fusion.
- **Why unresolved:** Feature-level fusion might improve coherence but risks noise propagation; not implemented or compared.
- **Resolution evidence:** Implement multimodal encoder with cross-attention fusion and compare spatial continuity, ECE, and seam artifacts against decision-level fusion.

### Open Question 4
- **Question:** How sensitive are results to the definition of geographical weights (GW), particularly reliance on NIC charts?
- **Basis:** GW uses NIC to weight MIZ lower; assumes NIC accuracy uniform, but NIC itself has uncertainties.
- **Why unresolved:** GW improvement shown (Table 7) but robustness to alternative weightings (e.g., SAR texture, ASI confidence) not tested.
- **Resolution evidence:** Ablate with alternative GW formulations (e.g., distance-decay, learned spatial attention) and measure impact on MIZ error and calibration.

## Limitations
- **Missing hyperparameters:** Token dimensions, patch size, hidden dimension `F` unspecified; critical for replication.
- **Ambiguous GW derivation:** Mapping from NIC polygons to per-pixel weights unclear; implementation guidance lacking.
- **BBB training details:** KL scaling, prior variance, and `sigma` initialization omitted; risks incorrect uncertainty calibration.

## Confidence
- **Multi-scale Transformer (GloFormer+LoFormer):** High (ablation confirms LoFormer’s contribution to local feature capture).
- **Geographically-weighted weak supervision:** Medium (loss concept clear, but GW derivation ambiguous; ablation shows benefit but MIZ handling uncertain).
- **Bayesian uncertainty (BBB):** Medium (BBB shows lower ECE, but training details lacking; computational cost unreported).
- **Decision-level fusion:** High (visual/quantitative results support priority order and improved maps).

## Next Checks

2. **GW sensitivity:** Vary GW formulations (binary MIZ mask, distance-decay from ice edges) and measure impact on MIZ SIC bias and ECE; expect significant variation in MIZ performance.
3. **Fusion comparison:** Implement uncertainty-weighted blending (e.g., inverse variance fusion) and compare against hard-overlay using R², MAE, and seam artifact metrics; expect decision-level priority to perform best but with potential seams.
