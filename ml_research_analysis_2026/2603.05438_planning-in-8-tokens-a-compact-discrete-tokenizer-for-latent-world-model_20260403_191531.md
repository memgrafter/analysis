# Planning in 8 Tokens: A Compact Discrete Tokenizer for Latent World Model
## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID**: 2603.05438
- **Source URL**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.05438
- **Reference count**: 40
- **Primary result**: CompACT compresses images to 8–16 tokens and achieves planning accuracy comparable to a 784-token baseline with a ~40× speedup in planning latency.

## Executive Summary
World model-based planning is computationally expensive because conventional tokenizers produce hundreds of latent tokens, leading to quadratic attention costs. This paper introduces CompACT, a compact discrete tokenizer that reduces each image to only 8 or 16 tokens. It uses a frozen pretrained vision encoder (DINOv3) for semantic resampling and a generative decoder that synthesizes fine details from a pretrained high-token tokenizer. On navigation planning with RECON, CompACT matches the Absolute Trajectory Error (ATE) and Relative Pose Error (RPE) of a 784-token baseline while providing a ~40× speedup. On RoboNet action-conditioned video prediction, CompACT (16 tokens) yields 3× lower action prediction error and 5.2× faster generation than a 256-token baseline. An inverse dynamics model trained on CompACT tokens also outperforms one trained on 16× more tokens.

## Method Summary
CompACT consists of three main components: an encoder that maps an image to a small set of discrete tokens, a generative decoder that reconstructs high-fidelity images from these tokens, and a world model that operates on the compact latent space. The encoder (Ecompact) uses a frozen DINOv3-B vision backbone to extract semantically rich patch embeddings. A learnable latent resampler (a 5-layer transformer decoder) attends to these embeddings via cross-attention, producing a small number of continuous feature vectors that are discretized using Finite Scalar Quantization (FSQ) into 8–16 tokens. The decoder (Dcompact) is a masked generative model (MM-DiT) that predicts the latent tokens of a pretrained high-token tokenizer (MaskGIT-VQGAN) from the compact tokens; this two-stage approach avoids the ill-posed direct pixel reconstruction. The pretrained tokenizer (Eψ, Dψ) remains fixed throughout. For planning, a world model (fϕ) – either a DiT for navigation or a block-causal transformer for manipulation – is trained on sequences of compact tokens to predict future tokens given actions and history. At test time, a Cross-Entropy Method (CEM) optimizer rolls out the world model in latent space, evaluating trajectories with a cost function that can be computed in latent space or after pixel reconstruction.

## Key Results
- CompACT (8/16 tokens) achieves ATE/RPE on RECON comparable to a 784-token SD-VAE baseline while reducing planning latency by ~40×.
- On RoboNet video prediction, CompACT (16 tokens) reduces action prediction error by a factor of 3 and speeds up generation by 5.2× compared to a 256-token baseline; an inverse dynamics model trained on CompACT tokens outperforms one trained on 256 tokens.
- Reconstruction quality on ImageNet-1K: rFID ≈ 2.4 for 16 tokens, comparable to much higher-token tokenizers.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1: Semantic prioritization via frozen pretrained encoder
CompACT preserves planning-critical semantics by using a frozen DINOv3 encoder to guide latent resampling. The frozen backbone provides object-level and spatial information while discarding transient details like textures and lighting. A learnable resampler cross-attends to the patch embeddings, distilling high-level semantics into a compact set of tokens. Evidence: The paper states that the frozen foundation model abstracts away low-level details, allowing selective focus on semantic information. Ablation shows finetuning the encoder degrades reconstruction (rFID 5.22 vs. 2.40), confirming the benefit of freezing.

### Mechanism 2: Generative decoding for detail synthesis
Directly reconstructing pixels from 8–16 tokens is ill-posed; instead, CompACT’s decoder synthesizes high-frequency details by predicting the latent tokens of a pretrained high-token tokenizer (MaskGIT-VQGAN). The compact tokens provide semantic guidance, while the generative process fills in details consistent with that semantics. Evidence: Ablation without generative decoding yields rFID 28.80 vs. 2.40, demonstrating its necessity.

### Mechanism 3: Discrete latent space for single-step sampling
CompACT’s discrete tokens (via FSQ) enable the world model to predict future states with a single forward pass, unlike continuous diffusion models that require iterative denoising. This reduces per-step rollout cost dramatically, leading to order-of-magnitude speedups in planning latency. Evidence: Table 4 shows CompACT (16 tokens) provides ~40× speedup vs. 784-token SD-VAE while maintaining planning accuracy. The discrete space also supports masked generative modeling with parallel unmasking.

Break conditions: If the frozen encoder’s semantics are misaligned with the task, if the target VQGAN latent space lacks sufficient detail, or if quantization error accumulates catastrophically over long horizons, the method may fail.

## Foundational Learning
- **Vision Transformer (ViT) encoders and self-supervised pretraining**
  - *Why needed*: DINOv3, a ViT-based model pretrained via self-distillation, provides stable patch embeddings that capture high-level semantics (objects, layout) needed for planning.
  - *Quick check*: How does cross-attention in the latent resampler differ from the ViT’s self-attention, and why does freezing the encoder encourage semantic over texture learning?

- **Finite Scalar Quantization (FSQ)**
  - *Why needed*: FSQ discretizes the resampler’s output into a fixed-size codebook without learned embeddings, preserving geometric structure and enabling efficient masked generative modeling.
  - *Quick check*: What are the trade-offs between FSQ and vector quantization (VQ) regarding codebook utilization, gradient flow, and suitability for parallel token prediction?

- **Masked generative modeling (non-autoregressive)**
  - *Why needed*: Both the decoder and world model use masked generative modeling (e.g., MaskGIT) to predict multiple tokens in parallel per step, enabling fast sampling and single-step prediction.
  - *Quick check*: How does the cosine masking schedule during training and inference affect the quality and speed of token prediction, and why is it preferred over autoregressive decoding here?

- **Cross-attention for latent resampling**
  - *Why needed*: The latent resampler uses cross-attention to map a small set of learnable query tokens to the frozen DINOv3 patch tokens, achieving extreme compression.
  - *Quick check*: Why is cross-attention with frozen features more effective for extreme compression than a standard transformer encoder trained end-to-end?

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component map**:  
Image → DINOv3-B (frozen) + Latent Resampler (transformer decoder) + FSQ → Compact Tokens (8/16).  
Compact Tokens → Decoder (Dcompact, MM-DiT) → Predict MaskGIT-VQGAN latents → Decoded image (via fixed Dψ).  
Compact Tokens + Action → World Model (fϕ) → Next Compact Tokens.  
Planner (CEM) uses World Model rollouts to optimize actions.

**Critical path**:
1. Train CompACT tokenizer on ImageNet: freeze DINOv3-B, train latent resampler and decoder with reconstruction loss via generative decoding.
2. Encode all training videos (e.g., RECON, RoboNet) into compact token sequences.
3. Train world model fϕ on (compact_t, action, history) → compact_{t+1} using masked generative objective.
4. At test time, run CEM to propose action sequences, roll out fϕ in latent space, compute cost (latent L1 or LPIPS after decoding), and execute the first action.

**Design tradeoffs**:
- Compression vs. reconstruction: Lower token counts degrade rFID but planning may tolerate it; CompACT prioritizes semantics.
- Frozen vs. finetuned encoder: Freezing prevents overfitting to pixel details and preserves semantics; finetuning hurts rFID and planning.
- Discrete vs. continuous: Discrete enables single-step prediction, continuous (e.g., SD-VAE) requires iterative diffusion, increasing latency.
- Target tokenizer choice: The pretrained VQGAN must provide rich latent detail; MaskGIT’s VQGAN is a good default, but alternatives may affect fidelity and decoder speed.

**Failure signatures**:
- Poor planning accuracy (high ATE/RPE): Check resampler attention maps for semantic loss; world model may underfit.
- Reconstruction artifacts (blurry, missing objects): Indicates decoder failure; monitor unmasking confidence and adjust masking schedule.
- Slow decoding latency: If Dcompact requires many sampling steps, reduce steps or use a more efficient architecture; generative decoding contributes non-trivial overhead.
- Quantization collapse: Monitor FSQ level usage distribution; if some levels are rarely used, adjust quantization temperature or add regularization.
- Low IDM R²: Compact tokens may have lost action-relevant information; verify modular token structure (Fig. 4) and consider increasing token count.

**First 3 experiments**:
1. **Tokenizer validation**: Train CompACT-16 on ImageNet-1K and compute rFID/IS; expect rFID ~2.4 (Table 1) and IS comparable to baselines.
2. **Inverse Dynamics Model (IDM) test**: Train IDM on CompACT latents vs. target VQGAN latents on RoboNet; measure L1 error and R²; expect CompACT-16 to match or exceed 256-token baseline.
3. **Planning latency & accuracy**: Run CEM planning on RECON with CompACT (8/16 tokens) vs. SD-VAE (784 tokens); measure ATE/RPE and wall-clock time per trajectory; expect ~40× speedup with minimal ATE increase.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
1. **Is emergent modularity necessary?** The paper shows CompACT tokens exhibit modular, object-centric structure and links it to IDM accuracy, but no ablation isolates modularity from compression. A tokenizer that matches compression but discourages modular allocation (e.g., via spatial entropy regularization) would test causality.
2. **Impact of frozen encoder choice**: While DINOv3 is used, other foundation models (MAE, SigLIP-2) may encode different feature distributions affecting planning across domains. Evaluating CompACT with alternative backbones on multiple datasets (RECON, RoboNet, real robots) is needed.
3. **Lower bound on token count for planning**: The paper cites a theoretical minimum H(a*) but does not systematically study token count vs. planning error across tasks of varying complexity. Training CompACT variants with 4–32 tokens on a suite of tasks and comparing to estimated action entropy would locate the practical lower bound.
4. **Decoder efficiency**: The generative decoder (MM-DiT) may be overkill if pixel-level fidelity is unnecessary when using latent-space cost functions. Could a deterministic or shallower decoder preserve planning accuracy while reducing latency? Testing alternatives with latent-space cost would answer this.

## Limitations
- Frozen DINOv3 may not capture domain-specific semantics in novel environments, risking misalignment with task-critical features.
- The generative decoder relies on a pretrained VQGAN; if that tokenizer’s latent space lacks sufficient detail or is misaligned, reconstruction artifacts may impair planning or evaluation.
- Discrete FSQ quantization may accumulate errors over long planning horizons; the paper only evaluates horizons up to H=8, leaving long-term stability unverified.
- Incomplete reporting of CEM parameters, RECON preprocessing, and VQGAN checkpoint hinders exact replication and may mask subtle implementation choices.

## Confidence
- Semantic preservation via frozen encoder: Medium
- Generative decoding for detail synthesis: High
- Discrete latent space for single-step sampling: Medium
- Overall planning accuracy and speedup: Medium
