# Causal Reconstruction of Sentiment Signals from Sparse News Data

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID**: 2603.23568
- **Source URL**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.23568
- **Reference count**: 40
- **Primary result**: Robust three-week lead-lag pattern between reconstructed sentiment signal and stock prices, consistent across all pipeline configurations and aggregation regimes.

## Executive Summary
The paper tackles the challenge of transforming sparse, noisy article-level sentiment scores into a stable, causally coherent temporal signal for financial analysis. It introduces a three-stage pipeline: (1) aggregating sentiment onto a regular time grid with weights that account for classifier uncertainty, article redundancy, and recency; (2) filling missing periods via strictly causal forward projection; and (3) applying a causal smoother (e.g., a weighted arctanh Kalman filter). A comprehensive label-free evaluation framework uses internal stability metrics and counterfactual tests to ensure causal integrity. Applied to AI-related news for 43 banks from November 2024 to February 2026, the reconstructed signal consistently leads stock prices by about three weeks—a pattern that persists across all aggregation regimes and smoothing choices. This demonstrates that producing deployable sentiment indicators requires careful reconstruction of the latent sentiment process, not merely better per-article classifiers.

## Method Summary
The pipeline operates per bank in three strictly causal stages. Stage 1 (Aggregation) pools article-level sentiment scores onto a weekly grid; each article is weighted by a composite of uncertainty (e.g., classifier entropy), redundancy (deduplication or corroboration via embedding similarity), and recency (exponential decay within the bin). The weighted mean yields a sparse series \(S_A(k)\). Stage 2 (Filling) imputes missing bins using only past information: constant forward carry for global aggregation or linear-decay carry-forward (with horizon \(H=60\)) for local. Stage 3 (Smoothing) applies a causal filter—preferably a count‑weighted arctanh Kalman filter—to reduce high‑frequency noise while respecting the no‑future‑leakage constraint. Hyperparameters (e.g., Kalman process noise variance) are not fully disclosed; defaults are inferred. Evaluation relies on label‑free internal diagnostics (total variation reduction, gap drift, lag proxy) and counterfactual tests (impulse causality, duplicate robustness), plus external market consistency via prewhitened cross‑correlation, rolling Granger causality, and spectral coherence.

## Key Results
- The reconstructed sentiment signal exhibits a robust three‑week lead over stock prices, confirmed by prewhitened cross‑correlation, rolling Granger causality, and spectral coherence across all tested pipeline configurations and aggregation regimes.
- Internal diagnostics show significant total variation reduction and strict causality (no leakage), validating the reconstruction approach.
- Ablation indicates that raw article‑level aggregation (without reconstruction) fails to produce stable, consistent signals, underscoring the need for dedicated causal reconstruction beyond classifier improvements.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1: Uncertainty- and Redundancy-Aware Aggregation
- **Claim**: Aggregating article-level sentiment onto a regular grid with composite weights mitigates sparsity and noise while accounting for classifier ambiguity and duplicate coverage.
- **Mechanism**: For each time bin, articles are weighted by \( w_{p,i} = W_{\text{unc}}(p,i) \times W_{\text{dup}}(p,i) \times W_{\text{time}}(p,i) \). \( W_{\text{unc}} \) downweights titles with high classifier uncertainty (e.g., high normalized entropy). \( W_{\text{dup}} \) adjusts for near‑duplicates via embedding clustering, either downweighting (deduplication) or upweighting (corroboration). \( W_{\text{time}} \) gives recency preference. The weighted mean (or other reducer) yields \( S_A(k) \).
- **Core assumption**: The classifier's probability vector provides a meaningful uncertainty estimate; embedding similarity correctly identifies near‑duplicate articles; within‑bin recency correlates with relevance.
- **Evidence anchors**:
  - [abstract] "aggregating scores onto a regular grid with uncertainty-aware and redundancy-aware weights"
  - [section 3.2] Formal definitions of uncertainty functionals (entropy, top‑two margin, polarity‑conflict), redundancy control via \( \phi(n) \), and recency weighting; Tables 5–6 show empirical impact on stability.
  - [corpus] Neighboring papers (e.g., "Beyond Polarity: Multi‑Dimensional LLM Sentiment Signals") explore multi‑dimensional sentiment, but the specific composite weighting scheme is a contribution of this work.
- **Break condition**: If the classifier is miscalibrated (high uncertainty does not imply low accuracy) or the duplicate detection produces many false positives/negatives, aggregation may mis‑weight articles and destabilize the signal.

### Mechanism 2: Strictly Causal Gap Filling
- **Claim**: Missing bins are filled using only past information via forward‑carry projections with optional staleness decay, preserving strict causality.
- **Mechanism**: For a missing bin \( k \), find \( k^* = \max\{u \le k : S_A(u) \neq \text{NA}\} \). Set \( S_F(k) = g(\Delta_k) \times S_A(k^*) \), where \( \Delta_k = k - k^* \) and \( g \) is non‑increasing with \( g(0)=1 \). Choices include constant fill (\( g=1 \)) or linear decay (\( g(\Delta)=\max(0,1-\Delta/H) \)).
- **Core assumption**: In the absence of new articles, the latent sentiment state persists unchanged or decays gradually toward a neutral baseline. The fill rule encodes this prior.
- **Evidence anchors**:
  - [abstract] "filling gaps via strictly causal projection"
  - [section 3.3] Definition of the fill operator; Table 6 shows gap‑drift diagnostics and stability trade‑offs for different \( g \).
  - [corpus] Related work on causal time‑series (e.g., "Time Series Domain Adaptation via Latent Invariant Causal Mechanism") addresses causality, but gap‑filling for sparse news is specific to this pipeline.
- **Break condition**: If the true sentiment shifts rapidly during a gap (e.g., due to an event not captured by the scraper), the fill rule may introduce a temporary bias that only corrects when new data arrives.

### Mechanism 3: Causal Smoothing for Noise Reduction
- **Claim**: Applying strictly causal smoothing filters reduces high‑frequency noise while respecting the no‑future‑leakage constraint.
- **Mechanism**: The smoothed value \( S_S(k) = \mathcal{F}(S_F(1:k)) \) uses only present and past. Variants include EWMA, Kalman filter (standard, arctanh‑transformed, count‑weighted), and Beta‑Binomial conjugate smoother. Count‑weighted versions incorporate article count \( t_k \) as a proxy for observation reliability.
- **Core assumption**: The latent sentiment process is a smoothed version of the noisy filled series; a causal filter can estimate it without future data. Heteroscedastic methods assume article count inversely relates to observation variance.
- **Evidence anchors**:
  - [abstract] "applying causal smoothing"
  - [section 3.4] Full specification of smoothers; Table 7 shows TV reduction; Table 8 shows lag trade‑off; weighted arctanh Kalman is preferred.
  - [corpus] Neighbors like "From News to Returns: A Granger‑Causal Hypergraph Transformer" use causal attention, but the smoothing methods here are classical time‑series adaptations.
- **Break condition**: Over‑smoothing (e.g., low process noise variance in Kalman) introduces significant lag and amplitude attenuation, weakening responsiveness to genuine changes.

## Architecture Onboarding

**Component map:**
- Input: Sparse article records \( (t_{p,i}, s_{p,i}, \pi_{p,i}) \) per bank.
- Stage 1 (Aggregation): Bin articles into fixed intervals (e.g., weekly). Compute per‑article weights (\( W_{\text{unc}}, W_{\text{dup}}, W_{\text{time}} \)). Reduce bin to \( S_A(k) \) via weighted mean. Optional global vs local aggregation.
- Stage 2 (Filling): Identify missing bins; for each, compute \( \Delta_k \) and apply decay \( g(\Delta_k) \) times last observed value; output complete grid \( S_F \).
- Stage 3 (Smoothing): Apply causal filter to \( S_F \) to produce final \( S_S \). Choices: EWMA, Kalman (standard/arctanh), Beta‑Binomial, with optional count‑weighted observation variance.
- Output: Regular‑grid sentiment series \( S_S \) (or earlier stage) per bank.

**Critical path:**
1. Articles arrive with timestamps and classifier probabilities.
2. Bin into fixed intervals.
3. Compute article weights using configured functions.
4. Aggregate to get \( S_A \) (may contain NAs).
5. Fill NAs using last observed value and decay \( g \).
6. Apply chosen smoother to filled series.
7. Evaluate using label‑free metrics (TV, gap drift, lag proxy, counterfactual tests) and external market consistency.

**Design tradeoffs:**
- Aggregation: Global mixes categories but benefits from corroboration; local preserves thematic separation but may be sparser per category.
- Uncertainty weighting: Choice of functional (entropy, top‑two margin, polarity‑conflict) affects how ambiguous titles are down‑weighted.
- Redundancy control: Deduplication (\( \phi(n)=1/n \)) vs corroboration (\( \phi(n)=\log(n) \)). Deduplication is conservative; corroboration treats multi‑source coverage as salience.
- Time decay within bin: Exponential/linear decay gives more weight to recent articles; may improve responsiveness but reduce stability.
- Fill rule: Constant vs decayed carry‑forward. Short decay horizons add instability; long horizons approach constant.
- Smoother: Light (EMA) preserves responsiveness but less TV reduction; heavy (Kalman) reduces TV more but introduces lag. Weighted arctanh Kalman balances boundary enforcement and heteroscedasticity.

**Failure signatures:**
- Impulse causality test: \( D_{\text{pre}} > 0 \) indicates leakage; pipeline must be fixed.
- Duplicate robustness test: Without redundancy control, \( D_{\text{no detect}} \gg D_{\text{detect}} \). If both are high, signal is unstable even with deduplication.
- Excessive lag: \( |\ell^*| \) large (e.g., >2 weeks) indicates over‑smoothing.
- Gap drift high: Fill rule deviates substantially from hold baseline within gaps; consider longer \( H \) or constant fill.
- Market consistency: If the three‑week lead‑lag pattern disappears across configurations, reconstruction may have failed to recover a meaningful signal.

**First 3 experiments:**
1. Run impulse causality test: perturb an article at \( t_0 \) (or add synthetic) and ensure no pre‑\( t_0 \) changes in output.
2. Quantify duplicate impact: inject synthetic near‑duplicates; run with redundancy control on/off; compute \( \ell_1 \) distance to baseline (without injection). Expect large distance when off, small when on.
3. Compare smoothing methods: Fix aggregation/filling; test EMA, simple Kalman, weighted arctanh Kalman. Compare TV reduction and lag proxy \( |\ell^*| \). Choose best trade‑off.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

1. **How does using full article content instead of titles affect the stability, lead‑lag pattern, and market consistency of the reconstructed sentiment signal?**  
   Basis: The paper’s data limitation states titles were used, not full content; conclusion proposes full‑text incorporation as future work.  
   Why unresolved: Full text could improve sentiment extraction but also introduce different sparsity and redundancy patterns; current evaluation is titles‑only.

2. **Can the sentiment classifier and reconstruction pipeline be jointly optimized to reduce error propagation and improve the latent signal?**  
   Basis: The limitations note the classifier is fixed and errors propagate uncontrollably; conclusion cites joint estimation as an open problem.  
   Why unresolved: Joint training might align classifier calibration with reconstruction needs but breaks modularity and may require new loss functions.

3. **Does the three‑week lead‑lag pattern between AI news sentiment and stock prices generalize across longer time periods and different market regimes?**  
   Basis: The paper presents a three‑week lead‑lag as a key finding but data spans only Nov 2024–Feb 2026 (16 months) and a small panel of banks; the authors caution about generalizability.  
   Why unresolved: The pattern could be specific to the AI news surge and market conditions in that short window; longer data might reveal different lags or breakdowns.

4. **What theoretical guarantees can be provided for the reconstruction pipeline components, such as optimal weighting under sparsity or the effect of smoothing on lag introduction?**  
   Basis: The paper uses heuristic choices for weights (uncertainty, redundancy, decay) and smoothing; no theoretical analysis is provided, only empirical comparisons.  
   Why unresolved: Without theory, practitioners cannot know if chosen parameters are optimal or well‑calibrated for their data characteristics.

5. **How valid is the label‑free evaluation framework at predicting the quality of the reconstructed signal when ground truth longitudinal sentiment is available?**  
   Basis: The framework is introduced because ground truth is usually unavailable; it is used for internal comparison but never validated against true labels.  
   Why unresolved: It is unclear whether metrics like total variation reduction and causality tests correlate with reconstruction accuracy; they might accept unstable but accurate signals.

## Limitations
- Undisclosed details of the sentiment classifier and embedding model (e.g., architecture, training data, similarity threshold) hinder exact replication and sensitivity analysis.
- Missing specifications for key hyperparameters (Kalman process noise variance, initial state variance) and duplicate injection protocol introduce uncertainty in result robustness.
- Lack of ground‑truth longitudinal sentiment labels forces reliance on indirect stability and market consistency metrics; absolute accuracy remains unvalidated.
- Empirical findings are restricted to AI‑related news for 43 banks over a 15‑month window; broader generalizability is uncertain.
