# Learning functional components of PDEs from data using neural networks

## Quick Facts
- **ArXiv ID:** 2602.13174
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.13174
- **Reference count:** 40
- **Primary result:** Unknown functional components (spatially dependent kernels or potentials) in a PDE can be recovered from observational steady-state data by embedding neural networks as approximators into the PDE structure and minimizing a steady-state residual loss.

## Executive Summary
This paper addresses the inverse problem of identifying unknown functional components—such as interaction kernels or potentials—within partial differential equations (PDEs) using observational data. The core method integrates neural networks directly into the PDE as universal function approximators, enabling simultaneous fitting of these functions and scalar parameters via optimization of a steady-state residual loss. Using nonlocal aggregation-diffusion equations as a testbed, the authors demonstrate that recovery is achievable from exact steady-state solutions, and remains feasible under sparse or moderately noisy conditions. Key findings reveal that identifiability hinges on the spectral richness of the observed solution profiles: a single profile can suffice for one unknown function, but recovering multiple functions (e.g., both kernel and potential) fundamentally requires at least two distinct profiles. Performance degrades with higher noise, and non-identifiability can be structural (due to insufficient data) or practical (due to poor data quality).

## Method Summary
The approach reformulates the inverse problem as a neural PDE (UPDE): unknown functions (e.g., kernel \(W(x)\), potential \(V(x)\)) are replaced by neural networks \(NN_W(x;\theta_W)\), \(NN_V(x;\theta_V)\), with output transformations enforcing physical constraints (e.g., \(W \leq 0\), \(\int V = 0\)). The steady-state condition of the PDE defines a fixed-point operator \(T\); the loss \(R(u) = \|T(u) - u\|_{L^2}\) measures deviation for observed solution profiles \(u(x)\). Optimization over the combined parameter set (scalar \(\kappa\), network weights) proceeds via Adam followed by L-BFGS, often with multi-start initialization to avoid local minima. Data may be exact, downsampled, or noise-corrupted. Once trained, the PDE can generate forward predictions.

## Key Results
- A single steady-state profile can uniquely determine one unknown function if its Fourier spectrum is sufficiently rich; however, identifiability of both \(W\) and \(V\) simultaneously requires at least two distinct solution profiles.
- Recovery accuracy strongly depends on the spectral content of the observed solutions: profiles with broader frequency support provide more information and yield better identifiability.
- Non-identifiability arises structurally (e.g., from a single profile when multiple functions are unknown) or practically (due to sparse/noisy data), manifested as wide ensembles of low-loss fits or regeneration failures.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
Neural networks serve as differentiable, universal approximators for unknown functions. Embedding them into the PDE’s fixed-point formulation yields a smooth loss landscape amenable to gradient-based optimization. Physical constraints are enforced via output transformations, narrowing the feasible function space and ensuring interpretability. The steady-state residual directly penalizes mismatches between the model’s predicted steady states and the data. Multi-start optimization helps navigate the non-convex loss. Crucially, the observed solution(s) must contain enough independent information to constrain all unknowns; spectrally rich profiles excite more Fourier modes of the functions, making them disentanglable.

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map:** Data (steady-state profile \(u(x)\)) -> Neural networks (\(NN_W, NN_V\)) embedded in PDE -> Fixed-point map \(T\) -> Loss \(R(u)\) -> Optimizer (Adam + L-BFGS) -> Fitted parameters (\(\kappa, \theta_W, \theta_V\)).
- **Critical path:** (1) Define NNs with constraint layers. (2) Implement \(T\) using NNs. (3) Compute loss on \(u(x)\). (4) Optimize parameters. (5) Validate by regenerating solutions.
- **Design tradeoffs:** Deeper/wider NNs increase expressivity but risk overfitting and slower optimization; constraint enforcement adds complexity but improves physical consistency; multi-start boosts robustness at computational cost.
- **Failure signatures:** (a) Low-loss ensemble of fitted functions with high spread → structural non-identifiability. (b) Loss stagnates above zero, regenerated solutions deviate → practical non-identifiability (bad data/hyperparameters). (c) Constraint violations in NN outputs → inadequate transformation.
- **First experiments:** (1) Reimplement steady-state solver from [15] and reproduce Fig. 2/S4 solutions. (2) Baseline: recover \(W\) (with \(V=0\)) from single full-resolution noiseless profile using 3-layer 32-unit softplus NN, Adam (lr=1e-3, 50k) + L-BFGS; target L2 error <1e-3. (3) Recover both \(W\) and \(V\) from two distinct profiles; verify minimal ensemble spread and accurate regeneration. Also test with 20% sparsity and Gaussian noise (σ=0.05).

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- **Question 1:** Under what conditions do distinct PDEs (different \(W, V, \kappa\)) generate identical or overlapping sets of steady-state solutions? *Basis:* Discussion calls for theoretical framework linking PDE properties to solution-set uniqueness. *Unresolved:* Lacks general classification; numerical evidence shows overlaps. *Evidence needed:* Theoretical characterization for aggregation-diffusion; systematic numerical exploration of solution-set overlaps.
- **Question 2:** Can time-dependent observational data recover functional PDE components in cases where steady-state data fails due to structural non-identifiability? *Basis:* Explicit suggestion that time-series may be more informative; study focused solely on steady states. *Unresolved:* UPDE framework for temporal data underdeveloped. *Evidence needed:* Extend method to time-dependent residual loss; test on known non-identifiable steady-state cases.
- **Question 3:** How can experimental design be optimized to maximize information content for functional parameter recovery, given constraints on data sparsity and noise? *Basis:* Call for systematic design strategies; paper shows success depends on spectral richness of profiles. *Unresolved:* No practical design principles. *Evidence needed:* Information-theoretic metric (e.g., Fisher information) predicting recovery success across PDE families.
- **Question 4:** Can incorporating qualitative prior knowledge (e.g., unimodality, monotonicity) into the neural network architecture or loss function improve identifiability and robustness to noise? *Basis:* Mention of benefits in non-spatial UDEs; not tested for spatial PDEs. *Unresolved:* Baseline uses unconstrained networks; prior knowledge unused. *Evidence needed:* Implement constrained architectures/regularizers and compare performance on noisy/sparse data.

## Limitations
- The steady-state solver (Newton-Krylov from [15]) code is unavailable; rebuilding it is necessary for exact benchmark generation.
- Optimization hyperparameters (learning rates, L-BFGS settings) and precise neural network architectures are only given as ranges; figure-specific configurations are unknown.
- Data generation details (sparsity ratios, noise levels) for each experiment are omitted, requiring inference from supplementary figures.
- Methodology is demonstrated only on 1D periodic domains; generalizability to other geometries is not established.
- Framework assumes perfect steady-state observations, which may be unrealistic for many real datasets.

## Confidence
- Recovery from clean steady-state data: **Medium** (numerical evidence but no theoretical guarantees or full implementation transparency).
- Identifiability conditions (profile richness, dual-profile necessity): **Medium-High** (supported by experiments and analytical arguments).
- Robustness to sparsity/noise: **Medium** (expected degradation shown, but error distributions and sensitivity not fully characterized).
