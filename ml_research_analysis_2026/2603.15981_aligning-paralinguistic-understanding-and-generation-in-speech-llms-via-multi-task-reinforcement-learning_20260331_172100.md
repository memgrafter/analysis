# Aligning Paralinguistic Understanding and Generation in Speech LLMs via Multi-Task Reinforcement Learning

## Quick Facts
- **ArXiv ID:** 2603.15981
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.15981
- **Reference count:** 11
- **Primary result:** PALLM achieves state-of-the-art response appropriateness on Expresso, IEMOCAP, and RAVDESS, improving 8-12% absolute over SFT baselines and proprietary models (e.g., 77.0% vs. GPT-4o-audio 67.4% on Expresso); human evaluation confirms the trend.

## Executive Summary
Speech LLMs often ignore paralinguistic cues like tone and emotion, relying on textual shortcuts. This paper introduces PALLM, a two-stage training pipeline that first performs supervised fine-tuning (SFT) on sentiment classification and synthetically generated tone-conditioned responses, then applies multi-task reinforcement learning (RL) with chain-of-thought (CoT) reasoning to jointly optimize both tasks. The CoT requirement forces explicit grounding in audio evidence. On benchmark datasets (Expresso, IEMOCAP, RAVDESS), PALLM significantly outperforms SFT baselines and strong proprietary models, demonstrating the effectiveness of multi-task RL for aligning understanding and generation in speech LLMs.

## Method Summary
PALLM uses Llama 4 Scout (17Bx16E) with a frozen audio encoder. Stage 1 (SFT) jointly trains on two objectives: (1) sentiment classification (cross-entropy loss) using labels mapped from fine-grained tones; (2) response generation (cross-entropy) where target responses are synthesized by prompting a text LLM with transcripts and ground-truth tones. Stage 2 (RL) employs GRPO: for each task, the model generates a chain-of-thought (CoT) reasoning trace followed by the final output (sentiment or response). Rewards are binary: classification correctness via a rule-based judge; generation appropriateness via GPT-4o. Tasks are sampled uniformly, and the policy is updated using group-relative advantages. This pipeline encourages the model to use acoustic evidence rather than lexical shortcuts.

## Key Results
- **SOTA on paralinguistic appropriateness:** PALLM (CLS+GEN) reaches 77.0% on Expresso, 8-12% absolute above SFT baselines and proprietary models like GPT-4o-audio (67.4%).
- **Human evaluation:** 76% of PALLM responses judged appropriate vs. 68% for GPT-4o-audio on 100 Expresso samples.
- **Robustness across datasets:** Significant gains on IEMOCAP and RAVDESS, though out-of-domain performance remains lower, highlighting generalization challenges.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
**Mechanism 1: SFT Warm Start Provides a Capable Initial Policy**
- **Claim:** SFT on sentiment classification and synthetic tone-conditioned responses establishes a foundational policy that can recognize paralinguistic cues and generate tone-matching responses, enabling subsequent RL refinement.
- **Mechanism:** Classification loss maps acoustic features to coarse sentiment; generation loss associates those sentiments with response styles. The joint SFT objective (`L_SFT = L_cls + L_gen`) initializes parameters away from text-only heuristics.
- **Core assumption:** Paralinguistic information is present in training data and captured through direct supervision.
- **Evidence:** Ablation: `SFT (CLS+GEN)` outperforms `SFT (GEN ONLY)`. Formal loss equations in Section 3.2.1.
- **Break condition:** If synthetic responses are generic or ignore subtle cues, the policy may still rely on text patterns, limiting RL recovery.

**Mechanism 2: RL with CoT Forces Explicit Audio Grounding**
- **Claim:** Multi-task RL requiring CoT traces before outputs compels the model to verbalize acoustic evidence, discouraging lexical shortcuts.
- **Mechanism:** For each task, the policy generates a reasoning trace `c`/`c'` then output `ŝ`/`r̂`. Rewards (`r_cls`, `r_gen`) are binary based on final correctness/appropriateness. The model must cite features like "hesitant prosody" to succeed.
- **Core assumption:** CoT trace is a trainable proxy for internal grounding; optimizing final output reward induces audio-grounded reasoning.
- **Evidence:** CoT prompts (Figures 5,7), GRPO implementation, reward definitions (Section 3.2.2).
- **Break condition:** If the judge fails to penalize ungrounded reasoning, the model may learn to produce plausible but post-hoc rationalizations.

**Mechanism 3: Joint Optimization Yields Synergistic Gains**
- **Claim:** Simultaneous RL on classification and generation creates mutual reinforcement: classification improves generation by providing an affect signal; generation reinforces which acoustic cues matter.
- **Mechanism:** Uniform task mixing with shared parameters allows co-adaptation of representations serving both objectives.
- **Core assumption:** The two tasks are aligned enough that joint training benefits both; sentiment labels (`positive/neutral/negative`) guide generation appropriateness.
- **Evidence:** `PALLM (CLS+GEN)` outperforms `PALLM (GEN ONLY)` (Table 5, Section 4.4.1).
- **Break condition:** Task misalignment could cause interference, degrading one or both tasks.

## Foundational Learning
**Concept: Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT) as a Warm Start**
- *Why needed here:* Paralinguistic cues are sparse; RL from a random/purely text-pretrained policy would struggle to discover them. SFT on available labels and synthetic targets provides a baseline capability.
- *Quick check question:* Why is SFT with synthetic responses (from a text LLM) insufficient for robust paralinguistic awareness, necessitating the RL stage?

**Concept: Reinforcement Learning for Behavioral Alignment**
- *Why needed here:* SFT minimizes token-level cross-entropy, which can be achieved via lexical shortcuts. RL optimizes for a downstream outcome metric (response appropriateness) that cannot be gamed by text-only patterns when combined with CoT constraints.
- *Quick check question:* How does using a binary reward (`-1/1`) based on a judge differ from token-level likelihood training in forcing audio grounding?

**Concept: Chain-of-Thought (CoT) Reasoning as an Explicit Reasoning Regularizer**
- *Why needed here:* Requiring an intermediate reasoning trace makes the decision process interpretable and (hypothesized) forces the model to rely on acoustic evidence to achieve correct final outputs.
- *Quick check question:* What is the hypothesized causal link between generating a CoT trace that mentions acoustic features and the model's actual internal use of those features?

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component Map:**
Frozen Audio Encoder → LLM Backbone (Llama 4 Scout 17Bx16E) → Task-specific outputs (sentiment or response). No separate heads; tasks distinguished by instruction prompts. Rewards: rule-based judge (classification) and GPT-4o judge (generation).

**Critical Path:**
1. *Data preprocessing:* For each audio sample, obtain transcript (ASR), ground-truth tone, map to sentiment. For SFT generation, synthesize target responses using an external text LLM conditioned on transcript and tone.
2. *Stage 1 SFT:* Train on `(audio, sentiment)` for classification and `(audio, transcript, synthetic response)` for generation, using combined loss `L_cls + L_gen`. Audio encoder frozen.
3. *Stage 2 RL:* Initialize from SFT checkpoint. For each batch, sample tasks uniformly. For each of K=4 completions per audio, generate CoT then output. Compute rewards: `r_cls` (binary sentiment match), `r_gen` (binary appropriateness via LLM judge). Update policy with GRPO using group-relative advantages.

**Design Tradeoffs:**
- *Frozen vs. Fine-tuned Audio Encoder:* Freezing preserves pretrained acoustic features but prevents adaptation. Fine-tuning might improve task-specific representations but risks overfitting and higher compute.
- *Binary vs. Granular Reward:* Binary reward simplifies RL but provides sparse signal; a scalar judge score could give richer gradients but may be less reliable.
- *Synthetic vs. Human Responses:* Synthetic responses are scalable but may lack true paralinguistic nuance; human responses are more authentic but costly.

**Failure Signatures:**
- *Lexical Shortcut Persistence:* Model performance does not degrade when audio is removed (transcript-only input); indicates it ignores audio.
- *Reasoning-Output Mismatch:* CoT trace cites acoustic features but final output is inappropriate; suggests reward model not back-propagating through reasoning quality.
- *Catastrophic Forgetting:* Validation performance on SFT tasks drops sharply during RL.
- *Poor OOD Generalization:* Large performance drop on RAVDESS (out-of-domain) vs. in-domain datasets.

**First 3 Experiments:**
1. *SFT Baseline Verification:* Train `SFT (CLS+GEN)` and `SFT (GEN ONLY)`. Verify joint SFT improves sentiment accuracy and appropriateness over generation-only SFT, establishing a better warm start.
2. *RL Stage Ablation:* Take `SFT (CLS+GEN)` checkpoint and apply only generation RL (`PALLM (GEN ONLY)`). Measure improvement in appropriateness over SFT baseline to isolate RL benefit on generation.
3. *Full Multi-task RL Evaluation:* Train `PALLM (CLS+GEN)` and compare against `PALLM (GEN ONLY)` and proprietary models (GPT-4o-audio, Gemini-2.5-Pro) on all benchmarks. Validate synergistic gain and SOTA performance. Also conduct human evaluation on a subset to corroborate LLM-judge results.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
1. **Domain adaptation:** What techniques best reduce the performance gap between in-domain (Expresso, IEMOCAP) and out-of-domain (RAVDESS) datasets?
2. **Leveraging unlabeled audio:** How can training be modified to use large-scale unlabeled audio, moving beyond reliance on emotion labels?
3. **CoT grounding genuineness:** To what extent does CoT reasoning genuinely force audio grounding versus learning to generate plausible but textually shortcutted rationales?
4. **LLM judge biases:** What failure modes and biases are introduced by using GPT-4o as the reward model for paralinguistic awareness?
5. **Synthetic response impact:** How do the synthetic, text-LLM-generated responses from Stage 1 impact final paralinguistic awareness, and can this data be improved?

## Limitations
- **Judge-centric evaluation:** Response appropriateness relies entirely on GPT-4o judge; its biases and subjectivity may not reflect human judgment perfectly.
- **Synthetic data dependency:** Stage 1 uses LLM-synthesized responses that lack fine-grained paralinguistic details (e.g., hesitations, laughter), potentially limiting the ceiling of the initial policy.
- **Generalization gap:** Performance drops significantly on out-of-domain datasets (e.g., RAVDESS 48.0% vs. Expresso 77.0%), indicating overfitting to training distribution.

## Next Checks
1. **Audio-Conflict Probe:** Construct a test set where transcript sentiment conflicts with ground-truth paralinguistic tone (e.g., "I'm fine" with sad tone). Compare performance with full audio vs. transcript-only inputs for PALLM and baselines. Robust audio reliance would show minimal drop when audio is removed only if the model truly used audio.
2. **CoT Ablation & Faithfulness:** Ablate CoT requirement in RL (train without reasoning traces). Compare performance to full PALLM. Then manually annotate 200 CoT traces for evidence specificity (mentions pitch/pauses vs. vague "tone") and correlate with appropriateness scores. Low correlation would suggest reward hacking.
3. **Speaker & Domain Generalization:** Re-evaluate on Expresso with a stricter speaker-held-out split and on a completely held-out corpus (e.g., MSP-Podcast). Measure performance degradation relative to in-domain sets to quantify overfitting.
