# Switch-Hurdle: A MoE Encoder with AR Hurdle Decoder for Intermittent Demand Forecasting
## Quick Facts
*   **arXiv ID:** 2602.22685
*   **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.22685
*   **Reference count:** 5
*   **Primary result:** Achieved WRMSSE 0.6307 on M5, beating TFT (0.6932) and PatchTST (1.0393); on proprietary data, WAPE 53.99% and MASE 0.5865.

## Executive Summary
Switch-Hurdle is a novel architecture for probabilistic forecasting of intermittent (sparse, zero-inflated) demand time series. It combines a Mixture-of-Experts (MoE) encoder with a specialized autoregressive decoder. The encoder uses sparse Top-1 expert routing with a straight-through estimator for efficient, stable training. The decoder employs a hurdle model that separately predicts the binary occurrence of a sale and the positive demand magnitude using a Negative-Binomial distribution. This design explicitly addresses the two-state nature of intermittent data. Evaluated on the M5 competition benchmark and a large proprietary retail dataset, Switch-Hurdle significantly outperforms strong baselines like TFT and DeepAR, demonstrating the effectiveness of its component choices.

## Method Summary
The method has two main stages. First, a two-layer Switch MoE encoder processes the input sequence of embedded static and dynamic features. Each MoE layer uses SwiGLU experts and a Top-1 router. During the forward pass, each token is sent to only one expert (sparse), but gradients flow to all experts via a straight-through estimator using the router's soft pre-gate probabilities (backward-dense). A KL-divergence regularizer encourages balanced expert utilization.

Second, an autoregressive decoder generates forecasts step-by-step for the horizon `T`. For each step `t`, a query is built from the previous forecast `log(1+ ŷ_{t-1})`, future covariates, and a positional embedding. This query attends via cross-attention to the encoder's output memory to produce a hidden state `h_t`. A shared "hurdle head" MLP takes `h_t` and outputs three values: `p⁺` (probability of a sale occurring), `μ` (mean of positive demand), and `α` (dispersion) for a Negative-Binomial distribution. The final forecast distribution is a mixture: `P(Y=0)=1-p⁺` and `P(Y>0)=p⁺ * NB(Y|μ,α)`. Training uses a composite loss of hurdle negative log-likelihood (or a hybrid with MAE) plus the expert load balancing term, with teacher forcing scheduled over epochs.

## Key Results
*   On the M5 benchmark, Switch-Hurdle achieves a WRMSSE of **0.6307**, substantially lower (better) than TFT (0.6932) and PatchTST (1.0393).
*   On a proprietary dataset of ~40,000 series, it attains WAPE **53.99%** and MASE **0.5865**, outperforming TFT (55.60%, 0.5803) and DeepAR.
*   Ablations confirm the importance of SwiGLU experts and discrete Top-1 routing with STE; soft gating degrades performance (WRMSSE +0.05), and the hurdle structure is core to handling intermittency.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1
*   **Claim:** Sparse expert routing (Top-1 with STE) enables efficient, specialized pattern recognition without destabilizing training.
*   **Mechanism:** Each input token is assigned to a single expert in the forward pass (sparse computation). Gradients flow to all experts in the backward pass via the soft pre-gate probabilities using a straight-through estimator (STE). This maintains stable gradient updates while ensuring inference efficiency.
*   **Core assumption:** Expert specialization emerges from discrete, non-uniform token assignments, and dense backward gradient flow prevents router collapse.
*   **Evidence anchors:** Paper describes STE in Section 3.2 and Eq. 2. Expert specialization patterns are visualized in Figures 4 & 5. The design choice is internal to this work; neighboring MoE papers (GateTS, Time-MoE) use different routing strategies.
*   **Break condition:** If gradient noise from STE causes training instability, or if the KL-to-uniform regularizer fails, leading to expert collapse (all tokens routed to one expert).

### Mechanism 2
*   **Claim:** The autoregressive hurdle decoder explicitly decouples zero-demand occurrence from positive magnitude, aligning with the statistical structure of intermittent data.
*   **Mechanism:** The shared hurdle head produces a binary probability `p⁺` for "sale occurs" and a Negative-Binomial distribution `(μ, α)` for positive demand. The final forecast is a mixture: `P(Y=0)=1-p⁺`, `P(Y>0)=p⁺ * P_NB(Y|μ,α)`.
*   **Core assumption:** Intermittent demand is generated by two distinct processes (occurrence and magnitude); modeling them jointly but separately yields better calibration than a single distribution.
*   **Evidence anchors:** Defined in Section 3.4 (Eqs. 12-14). The paper justifies NB over Poisson for overdispersion. Comparative results against TFT/DeepAR (single distribution) support the claim, though a direct head-to-head ablation of the hurdle vs. single distribution is not the primary experiment.
*   **Break condition:** If the correlation between occurrence and magnitude is high and non-separable, or if the `log(1+ ŷ_{t-1})` conditioning fails to capture essential lagged dependencies for magnitude.

### Mechanism 3
*   **Claim:** KL-to-uniform load balancing regularizes expert utilization, preventing collapse while allowing regime-specific specialization.
*   **Mechanism:** The term `L_balance` minimizes KL divergence between the empirical expert assignment distribution and a uniform prior. This discourages any single expert from dominating while permitting non-uniform, structured specialization.
*   **Core assumption:** A uniform prior on expert usage is a suitable proxy for encouraging balanced capacity utilization without forcing uniform assignments.
*   **Evidence anchors:** Section 3.2 defines `L_balance`. Section 4.4 (Figures 2, 4, 5) shows balanced utilization and conditional specialization. Ablations in Tables 6 & 7 imply its necessity, though a direct isolation of *only* this regularizer's effect is not shown.
*   **Break condition:** If the optimal expert assignment for different regimes is intrinsically imbalanced (e.g., 90% of tokens naturally to one regime), the uniform prior may penalize useful, natural specialization.

## Foundational Learning
*   **Concept: Mixture-of-Experts (MoE) with discrete routing**
    *   **Why needed:** The MoE encoder is the core capacity-scaling mechanism. Understanding Top-1 routing, the Straight-Through Estimator, and load balancing is essential to grasp how the model handles many series efficiently.
    *   **Quick check:** "How does the Straight-Through Estimator allow for sparse forward passes but dense gradient updates, and why is this important for training sparse models?"
*   **Concept: Hurdle models for zero-inflated count data**
    *   **Why needed:** The decoder's probabilistic output is a hurdle model. This is the statistical innovation that directly addresses intermittent demand's two-state nature.
    *   **Quick check:** "Why is a Negative-Binomial distribution used for the positive-demand component instead of a Poisson, and how does the hurdle formulation combine the two parts?"
*   **Concept: Autoregressive decoding with covariate conditioning**
    *   **Why needed:** The decoder generates multi-step forecasts conditioned on previous predictions and future covariates (e.g., promotions). This is the inference-time dynamic mechanism.
    *   **Quick check:** "What is the role of the `log(1+ ŷₜ₋₁)` transformation in the decoder's input projection, and why is teacher forcing with a schedule used?"

## Architecture Onboarding
*   **Component map:** Input Embedding (static + dynamic features) -> Switch MoE Encoder (2 layers, Top-1 STE routing, SwiGLU experts) -> AR Hurdle Decoder (cross-attention loop) -> Shared Hurdle Head (`p⁺`, `μ`, `α`) -> Loss (Hurdle NLL/MAE + `λ_aux * L_balance`)
*   **Critical path:** Data pipeline -> Token embedding -> Encoder training (with STE & KL-balance) -> Autoregressive loop (predict `ŷ_t`, feed into next step's query) -> Hurdle distribution -> Loss computation & backward pass.
*   **Design tradeoffs:**
    *   **Sparsity depth vs. width:** 2 layers with 4 experts each outperforms 1 layer with more experts, enabling hierarchical specialization.
    *   **STE vs. Soft gating:** Hard Top-1 routing (+STE) yields better accuracy than soft mixture weights (Table 7: +0.05 WRMSSE) by promoting expert diversity.
    *   **SwiGLU vs. GELU:** SwiGLU's multiplicative gate improves specialization and gradient flow (consistent ~1-2% WAPE/MASE improvement in ablations).
    *   **Hurdle vs. single distribution:** Explicit separation handles zero-inflation better; NB captures overdispersion.
*   **Failure signatures:**
    *   **Expert collapse:** One expert gets >90% of tokens. Check per-expert routing histograms (like Figure 2). Likely `λ_aux` too low or STE broken.
    *   **Degenerate hurdle head:** `p⁺` → 0 or 1 for all series, or `α` → ∞/0. Indicates hurdle head not training; check output scaling (use `softplus`).
    *   **Autoregressive drift:** Forecasts explode/decay to zero. Check `log(1+ ŷ)` stabilization and teacher forcing schedule.
*   **First 3 experiments:**
    1.  **Routing sanity check:** Train on a tiny subset, visualize expert assignment vs. demand regime (zero/low/normal/spike) per layer. Expect to see patterns like Figure 4/5; if all random, STE or gating is broken.
    2.  **Hurdle ablation:** Train with hurdle head disabled (predict positive demand from `h_t` directly with NB, ignore `p⁺`). Expect significant WRMSSE/WAPE degradation on intermittent series.
    3.  **Ablation of STE:** Replace STE gate with soft routing (use `p_t` as weights). Replicate Table 7: expect measurable drop in WRMSSE (~+0.05) confirming hard routing benefit.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
*   **Adaptive Expert Specialization:** How can routing become adaptive to evolving demand regimes (seasonality, promotions) rather than static? Current model uses fixed Top-1 routing.
*   **Cross-Domain Pretraining:** Would pretraining on a large multi-domain dataset improve zero-shot generalization and calibration for new intermittent series?
*   **Stability of Routing Patterns:** Are the regime-specific expert routing patterns (Zero/Low/Normal/Spike) stable across different retail contexts, or do they overfit to dataset-specific quirks? Analysis is only on two datasets.
*   **Alternative Count Distributions:** Would other distributions (e.g., Poisson, ZINB, or neural density estimators) in the hurdle head improve calibration? The head is modular but only NB is tested.

## Limitations
*   **Implementation unknowns:** Exact input feature engineering (categorical embedding sizes, price/event transformations), critical hyperparameters (`d_model`, expert width, dropout, teacher forcing schedule), and point forecast definition are not disclosed.
*   **Data split ambiguity:** The precise train/val/test date splits for M5 and the rolling window sampling strategy during training are unclear.
*   **Ablation gaps:** While ablations show SwiGLU and STE are better, a direct, isolated ablation of *only* the KL-to-uniform load balancing regularizer's impact is not provided.

## Confidence
*   **Hurdle decoder design (binary + NB):** **High**. The statistical formulation is standard for intermittent data and strongly supported by comparative results against single-distribution models.
*   **MoE routing mechanism (Top-1 with STE):** **Medium**. The design is clearly described and justified, but the evidence that *this specific STE implementation* drives gains relies solely on the paper's own internal ablations compared to soft routing.
*   **Load balancing via KL-to-uniform:** **Medium**. The objective is defined and utilization plots appear balanced, but an ablation isolating *only* this term's contribution is absent; its success may be entangled with other architectural choices.

## Next Checks

Confirm patterns similar to Figures 4–5 emerge (e.g., distinct experts for zeros vs. spikes). Failure suggests STE routing or `λ_aux` misconfiguration.
2.  **Hurdle necessity test:** Ablate the hurdle head by removing the binary `p⁺` output and modeling all demand with a single NB distribution. Expect significant WRMSSE degradation (>0.05) on intermittent series, validating the decoupled design.
3.  **Hierarchical WRMSSE audit:** Recompute WRMSSE per aggregation level (1–12) using the official M5 hierarchy script. Identify if errors concentrate at specific levels (e.g., item-store vs. total). Discrepancies may indicate prediction aggregation issues or metric implementation errors.
