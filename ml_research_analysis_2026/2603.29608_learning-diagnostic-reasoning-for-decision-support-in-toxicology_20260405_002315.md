# Learning Diagnostic Reasoning for Decision Support in Toxicology
## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2603.29608
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.29608
- **Reference count:** 18
- **Primary result:** DeToxR, a GRPO-finetuned LLM, achieves superior multi-label F1 for poly-substance intoxication prediction, outperforming its base model, classical ML, a supervised fine-tuned variant, and a single expert toxicologist in a clinical validation.

## Executive Summary
Clinical diagnosis of emergency poly-substance intoxication requires rapid synthesis of heterogeneous data, including structured vitals and unstructured narratives. This paper introduces DeToxR, a system that fine-tunes a lightweight LLM using Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO) with a reward directly based on the multi-label F1 score. This approach explicitly optimizes for accurate substance prediction, penalizing both missed co-ingestions and hallucinated poisons. On a single-center dataset of 870 cases, DeToxR significantly outperforms baseline models. In a validation study against an expert toxicologist, DeToxR demonstrated higher Micro-F1 (0.644 vs. 0.473) and Macro-F1 (0.488 vs. 0.398), suggesting a potential diagnostic advantage through improved recall while maintaining competitive precision.

## Method Summary
DeToxR processes each case by serializing all available evidence—structured clinical variables (demographics, vitals, symptoms) and unstructured free-text fields (paramedic reports, history, exam)—into a single Markdown-formatted prompt. A lightweight instruction-tuned LLM (Qwen3 4B Instruct) is then fine-tuned using GRPO. The training uses a composite reward: a sample-level multi-label F1 score (`R_F1`) that directly optimizes for the clinical metric, and a format reward (`R_format`) that ensures the output adheres to a structured schema containing a reasoning block and a JSON object with 14 binary substance predictions. This decouples structural compliance from task accuracy. The model is trained on a retrospective dataset of 870 cases from one hospital, with iterative stratification for a 50:20:30 train/validation/test split.

## Key Results
- DeToxR significantly outperforms its base LLM, classical ML baselines (MLP, XGBoost), and a supervised fine-tuned (SFT) variant on test set Micro-F1 and Macro-F1.
- Against an expert toxicologist on 25 held-out cases, DeToxR achieves higher Micro-F1 (0.644 vs. 0.473) and Macro-F1 (0.488 vs. 0.398), primarily by improving recall.
- The SFT baseline shows higher precision but much lower recall, indicating difficulty in predicting positive cases; DeToxR’s GRPO fine-tuning better balances the trade-off.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1: Reward Aligned to Clinical Utility
- **Claim:** GRPO finetuning with a sample-level F1 reward directly optimizes the model for the clinical utility metric (balancing recall and precision), reducing both false negatives (missed co-ingestions) and false positives (hallucinated poisons).
- **Mechanism:** The scalar reward `R_F1` is computed per completion by comparing the predicted multi-label vector to the gold vector. GRPO updates the generation policy to increase the probability of outputs that maximize this F1 score. The harmonic mean of precision and recall in F1 penalizes missing true substances and predicting absent ones symmetrically.
- **Core assumption:** Optimizing for the end-task metric at the sequence level, rather than token-level cross-entropy (SFT), will induce outputs that better align with the clinical objective of comprehensive yet accurate toxin identification.
- **Evidence anchors:** Abstract states the model is "explicitly penalized for missing co-ingested substances and hallucinating absent poisons." Section 4 notes DeToxR improves F1 and recall with a smaller precision drop compared to SFT.
- **Break condition:** If the F1 reward surface is too sparse or noisy (e.g., due to label imbalance or ambiguous ground truth), the RL signal may be ineffective, leading to instability or failure to surpass SFT.

### Mechanism 2: Heterogeneous Data Fusion via Serialization
- **Claim:** Serializing all clinical evidence (structured and unstructured) into a single natural-language prompt enables a single lightweight LLM to fuse modalities without specialized multimodal architectures.
- **Mechanism:** The input processor converts structured key-value pairs and free-text sections into a Markdown prompt. The decoder-only LLM processes this unified stream, using attention to implicitly correlate information across different sections (e.g., linking a symptom to a vital sign).
- **Core assumption:** A capable instruction-tuned LLM, given a well-structured serialization, can perform implicit data fusion by treating all prompt content as a single contextual sequence.
- **Evidence anchors:** Section 2.1 describes serializing "all available evidence into a single Markdown-formatted natural-language prompt." Section 2.2 notes this provides both parseable predictions and readable rationales.
- **Break condition:** If serialization loses critical relational/temporal information (e.g., negations, uncertainty) or exceeds the context window, fusion is impaired, leading to omissions or misinterpretations.

### Mechanism 3: Composite Reward Enforces Structured Output and Elicits Reasoning
- **Claim:** A composite reward including `R_format` enforces a structured output schema (reasoning block + JSON), yielding parseable predictions and implicitly encouraging intermediate diagnostic reasoning traces without requiring ground-truth rationales.
- **Mechanism:** `R_format` (max 1.0) is awarded only if the output contains a `<reasoning>` block, valid JSON, and all 14 substance keys. This shapes the generation policy to produce a valid, complete prediction vector. The F1 reward is only computable on the JSON, so format compliance is a prerequisite for learning. The reasoning tag incentivizes the model to generate step-by-step text before finalizing predictions.
- **Core assumption:** Decoupling structural requirements from task accuracy via a separate reward allows GRPO to learn the prediction task while ensuring outputs are usable and inspectable.
- **Evidence anchors:** Section 2.3 states the approach drives "accurate multi-label predictions, parseable outputs, and diagnostic reasoning traces without requiring ground-truth rationales." Qualitative analysis shows reasoning traces that correctly analyze clinical findings.
- **Break condition:** If `R_format` dominates `R_F1`, the model may game the format (e.g., valid JSON with random values and placeholder reasoning) without improving diagnostic performance.

## Foundational Learning
- **Concept:** Multi-label classification with sparse, imbalanced labels.
  - **Why needed here:** The task predicts up to 14 substances simultaneously, with low average prevalence (2.7 per case) and many rare toxins. Standard binary loss would be dominated by negative labels.
  - **Quick check:** Why is a sample-level F1 score a more appropriate reward than token-level cross-entropy or instance-level accuracy for this specific multi-label, imbalanced clinical prediction problem?

- **Concept:** Reinforcement Learning from Environmental Feedback (RLAIF) using a scalar reward derived from a verifiable metric.
  - **Why needed here:** GRPO fine-tunes the LLM directly on the end-task metric (F1), bypassing the need for ground-truth reasoning chains by using lab-confirmed labels as feedback.
  - **Quick check:** How does using a reward that is a function of the final multi-label prediction (F1) enable the model to learn better reasoning compared to supervised fine-tuning (SFT) which only sees (input, target label) pairs?

- **Concept:** Serialization of multimodal/heterogeneous data for decoder-only LLMs.
  - **Why needed here:** The model must process both structured (vitals) and unstructured (narratives) data. Serialization converts all inputs into a single text sequence, avoiding complex multimodal fusion modules.
  - **Quick check:** What are the key risks of losing information or introducing misleading context when converting structured data tables and free-text narratives into a linear text prompt, and how might the prompt design in this paper mitigate them?

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component map:** Input Processor -> LLM Backbone (Qwen3 4B Instruct) -> GRPO Trainer -> Output Parser
**Critical path:** Raw clinical data (dict + text) → Serialized Markdown prompt → LLM generation (GRPO-guided during training) → Structured output (reasoning + JSON) → Evaluation/computation of F1 reward (training) or clinician consumption (inference).
**Design tradeoffs:**
- *Lightweight model vs. capability:* 4B parameter model reduces cost but may limit raw reasoning power; RL fine-tuning aims to compensate.
- *Reward sparsity:* F1 is sparse/non-differentiable at token level; GRPO handles this but may need many steps. Format reward is dense, ensuring early compliance.
- *Generalization vs. specificity:* Training on one hospital's data (870 cases) risks overfitting to local styles and prevalence; out-of-distribution performance is unknown.
**Failure signatures:**
- High precision but very low recall: model overly conservative; likely from reward imbalance or label sparsity.
- Valid JSON but reasoning contradicts/ignores input: format reward works but F1 reward fails to guide coherent reasoning.
- Poor handling of missing data fields (e.g., "N/A"): degrades performance on incomplete real-world reports.
**First 3 experiments:**
1. **Reproduce SFT baseline:** Train Qwen3 4B Instruct with cross-entropy loss on the 14-label vector to establish the performance gap RL aims to close.
2. **Ablate reward components:** Train variants with `R_F1` only, `R_format` only, and combined. Expect `R_F1` only to fail format learning, `R_format` only to yield random predictions in valid format, and combination to work best.
3. **Test base model generalization:** Zero-shot evaluate other base LLMs (Llama 3.1 8B, MedGemma 4B) on serialized prompts to confirm inherent inability to solve the task, justifying adaptation.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- How can DeToxR be modified to reduce hallucinations in its diagnostic reasoning traces without compromising predictive accuracy? The paper notes the model sometimes "rationalizes its correct predictions by hallucinating non-existent findings."
- Does DeToxR's performance generalize to external clinical settings beyond the single-center dataset? The dataset stems from one hospital's toxicology department; no external validation is reported.
- How robust is the reported superiority over an expert toxicologist when assessed with a larger panel of experts and a larger, more diverse case set? Validation involved one toxicologist on 25 cases.
- Can clinical utility be enhanced by reformulating the reward to reflect differential costs of false negatives versus false positives for specific substances? The symmetric F1 reward treats all errors equally, but missing a life-threatening toxin may be more consequential.

## Limitations
- Single-institution dataset (n=870) may limit generalizability to other hospitals or populations.
- GRPO implementation details (e.g., KL penalty, reward scaling, group size) are not fully specified, hindering exact replication and potentially affecting stability.
- Serialization of heterogeneous data could lose critical relational/temporal cues (e.g., negations, uncertainty); no analysis of such losses is provided.

## Confidence
- **GRPO with F1 reward:** High confidence. Strong empirical gains on test set and clinical validation against an expert directly support the claim.
- **Text serialization for multimodal fusion:** Medium confidence. The approach yields good results but lacks an ablation against alternative fusion methods, so necessity is not proven.
- **Format reward producing reasoning traces:** Medium confidence. The reward reliably enforces structured output, but quality and clinical utility of reasoning remain anecdotal without systematic evaluation.

## Next Checks
1. **Reward ablation study:** Train models with `R_F1` only, `R_format` only, and their combination. Compare Micro/Macro-F1, recall, precision, and output validity to quantify each reward’s contribution and detect reward hacking.
2. **External validation:** Evaluate DeToxR on a separate hospital’s poly-substance cases or a public toxicology dataset to test generalizability across documentation styles and case distributions.
3. **Clinician assessment of reasoning:** Have expert toxicologists blind-rate reasoning traces from DeToxR vs. SFT for clinical coherence, support by input data, and diagnostic usefulness to check if the format reward elicits meaningful reasoning.
