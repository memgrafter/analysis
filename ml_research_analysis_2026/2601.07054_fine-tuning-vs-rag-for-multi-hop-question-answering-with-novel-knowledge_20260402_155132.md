# Fine-Tuning vs. RAG for Multi-Hop Question Answering with Novel Knowledge
## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2601.07054
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.07054
- Reference count: 32
- Primary result: Supervised fine-tuning achieves the highest multi-hop QA accuracy; RAG yields substantial gains on temporally novel knowledge; unsupervised fine-tuning provides only marginal improvements.

## Executive Summary
Multi-hop question answering requires integrating multiple pieces of information to arrive at a correct answer. This paper compares three approaches to injecting knowledge into language models for this task: unsupervised fine-tuning (continual pretraining), supervised fine-tuning on labeled QA pairs, and retrieval-augmented generation (RAG). The study uses two datasets: the science-focused QASC and a novel 2024 Events dataset containing questions about recent events, ensuring that the knowledge is temporally outside typical pretraining corpora.

Results show that unsupervised fine-tuning provides only marginal improvements over base models, suggesting that standard language modeling objectives are insufficient for multi-hop reasoning. In contrast, RAG yields substantial and consistent gains, more than doubling accuracy on the 2024 Events dataset by dynamically retrieving relevant context. Supervised fine-tuning achieves the highest overall accuracy across both datasets, demonstrating the superiority of task-specific supervision for multi-hop reasoning.

## Method Summary
The study evaluates three knowledge injection methods on two multi-hop QA datasets (QASC and 2024 Events) using 7B-parameter language models (Mistral-7B, Llama2-7B, Llama2-7B-Instruct). Unsupervised fine-tuning involves continued pretraining on raw text from the target knowledge corpus (science sentences for QASC, chunked Wikipedia 2024 text for Events) using the causal language modeling objective, with LoRA adapters. Supervised fine-tuning trains on the labeled multiple-choice QA pairs, optimizing a cross-entropy loss over answer options via a classification head. RAG employs a dense retriever (BGE-large-en) to fetch relevant text chunks from the knowledge corpus (indexed with FAISS), reranks them with a cross-encoder, and provides the top four chunks as context to a frozen generator model; the model is prompted to answer using only the context, with answer selection performed by comparing next-token probabilities for option labels. All experiments use a common setup: LoRA on query/value projections (rank 16, alpha 32), AdamW optimizer (learning rate 1e-5), 20 epochs, batch size 16, and a 90/10 train/evaluation split.

## Key Results
- Unsupervised fine-tuning yields only marginal accuracy improvements over base models.
- RAG provides substantial, consistent gains, more than doubling accuracy on the temporally novel 2024 Events dataset.
- Supervised fine-tuning achieves the highest overall accuracy across both datasets.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1
- **Claim:** Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG) provides substantial and consistent accuracy gains for multi-hop question answering, especially with temporally novel knowledge, by dynamically accessing an external, up-to-date knowledge corpus at inference time.
- **Mechanism:** A dense retriever (BGE-large-en) identifies top-*k* relevant text chunks from a knowledge base (e.g., a 2024 Wikipedia dump). These chunks are reranked and provided ascontext to a frozen generator LLM. The model is prompted to answer using only this context, with answer selection via next-token probability scoring over option labels (A, B, C, D). This decouples knowledge storage from model parameters.
- **Core assumption:** The retriever can successfullyfetch all necessary facts for the multi-hop reasoning chain. If any supporting fact is missing from the top retrieved chunks, the generator cannot compose a correct answer.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract] "retrieval-augmented generation yields substantial and consistent improvements, particularly when answering questions that rely on temporally novel information."
  - [section V.B.2] Details the complete RAG pipeline: chunking (1000-token overlap), embedding (BGE), FAISS indexing, top-20 retrieval with cross-encoder reranking to top-4, and MMLU-style prompt formatting.
  - [corpus] Neighbor papers (e.g., "EfficientRAG," "Reasoning in Trees") focus on improving retrieval for multi-hop QA, providing indirect corroboration that retrieval quality is a critical bottleneck for this task.
- **Break condition:** If the multi-hop question's supporting facts are distributed across chunks with low semantic overlap, the retriever may fail to return a complete evidence set within the top-*k*, causing the generator to produce an unsupported or incorrect answer.

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim:** Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT) achieves the highest overall accuracy by explicitly training the model on the task distribution (multiple-choice QA format) using labeled question-answer pairs, which teaches it both the required knowledge and the specific compositional reasoning pattern.
- **Mechanism:** The model is trained from a base checkpoint on a dataset of (question, candidate answers, correct label) tuples. A classification head predicts the correct option. The cross-entropy loss directly optimizes for selecting the right answer from the given choices, implicitly encouraging the model to learn the multi-hop reasoning steps present in the training data.
- **Core assumption:** The training data contains sufficiently many examples that capture the multi-hop reasoning structures and factual combinations needed for the test distribution. SFT effectiveness is-task-specific and does not guarantee generalization to novel facts or unseen reasoning chains.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract] "Supervised fine-tuning achieves the highest overall accuracy across models and datasets."
  - [section V.A] "Supervised Fine-tuning... We considered C∈ {4,8} answer options... The training objective minimizes the cross-entropy loss over the C answer classes."
  - [corpus] Corpus evidence is weak; neighbor papers primarily address RAG optimization. The claim is anchored in this paper's own comparative results.
- **Break condition:** Performance degrades sharply on questions requiring knowledge not present in the SFT training set (e.g., the 2024 Events dataset if trained only on QASC) or involving reasoning chains not represented in the training QA pairs.

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim:** Unsupervised Fine-Tuning (Continual Pretraining) provides only marginal accuracy improvements over base models because the standard language modeling objective (next-token prediction) does not effectively teach models to perform explicit multi-hop composition or internalize sparse, factual knowledge for recall.
- **Mechanism:** The base model is further trained on raw, unlabeled text from the target knowledge corpus (e.g., science sentences from QASC or Wikipedia event articles) using the causal language modeling loss. This theoretically updates the model's parametric memory to include new facts, but without task-specific supervision, the model does not learn to actively retrieve and combine them for question answering.
- **Core assumption:** Continued exposure to relevant factual sentences during pretraining will lead to the model internalizing and being able to recall those facts upon demand. This assumes knowledge is stored and can be activated without explicit retrieval or reasoning supervision.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract] "Unsupervised fine-tuning provides only limited gains over base models, suggesting that continual pretraining alone is insufficient for improving multi-hop reasoning accuracy."
  - [section V.A] "Unsupervised Finetuning... we minimized: L_CLM = -Σ log p(x_t | x_<t)."
  - [results Tables I & II] Show minimal absolute accuracy gains (e.g., ~2-4%) for unsupervised FT across all models and datasets.
- **Break condition:** When required knowledge is temporally novel (2024 Events) or requires integrating facts that are rarely co-occurring in the pretraining text, the model fails to form reliable parametric representations for recall, leading to performance near the base model.

## Foundational Learning
- **Concept: Multi-Hop Reasoning**
  - **Why needed here:** The core task is multi-hop QA, which requires retrieving and composing *multiple* distinct facts (e.g., "A is B" and "B is C" to answer "What is A?"). A learner must understand that the answer depends on a chain of dependencies, not a single fact.
  - **Quick check question:** Given facts "The capital of France is Paris" and "Paris is in the EU," what is the capital of France that is in the EU? What intermediate step connects the question to the answer?

- **Concept: Parametric vs. Non-Parametric Knowledge Access**
  - **Why needed here:** The paper's central comparison hinges on this dichotomy. Parametric (FT) encodes knowledge into weights; non-parametric (RAG) reads from an external store. Understanding this distinction is crucial to interpret *why* RAG succeeds on novel knowledge while FT (especially unsupervised) struggles.
  - **Quick check question:** If a model knows "Thomas Jefferson was born in 1743" via pretraining, is that knowledge parametric or non-parametric? How would RAG answer a question about a 2024 event?

- **Concept: Evaluation via Multiple-Choice Classification**
  - **Why needed here:** All results are reported in accuracy for multiple-choice QA. The method of scoring (log-prob of option tokens vs. classification head) is a key experimental control. Misunderstanding this metric could lead to misinterpreting "accuracy" as free-text generation quality.
  - **Quick check question:** For a 4-option question, what is random chance accuracy? If a model outputs "A" for every question, what accuracy would it achieve on a balanced test set?

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map:**
  1. **Knowledge Corpus:** Source text (e.g., 2024 Wikipedia chunks). This is the ground-truth evidence source.
  2. **Retriever:** Dense model (BGE-large-en) + FAISS index. Takes a query, returns top-*k* text chunks.
  3. **Reranker:** Cross-encoder (MiniLM-L6-v2). Re-scores retrieved chunks for semantic precision.
  4. **Generator (LLM):** Frozen base model (Mistral-7B, Llama2-7B). Consumes prompt = [context + question + options].
  5. **Scoring Head:** For RAG/base models: computes next-token log-prob for each option letter. For SFT: a trained classification head.
  6. **Training Loop:** For SFT: (question, options, label) → cross-entropy loss. For unsupervised FT: text chunks → causal LM loss.

- **Critical path:**
  1. For **RAG**: Question → Retriever → Top chunks → Reranker → Final context → Prompt construction → Generator → Option log-probs → Answer.
  2. For **SFT**: (Question, Options) → Model with classification head → Logits → Cross-entropy loss (train) / Argmax (eval).
  3. For **Unsupervised FT**: Text chunks → Causal LM loss (train). At eval: same as base model (no context).

- **Design tradeoffs:**
  - **Retrieval scope vs. noise:** Top-*k* retrieval (k=4 post-rerank) balances evidence completeness with irrelevant context. Larger *k* risks noise; smaller *k* risks missing hops.
  - **Parametric update vs. flexibility:** SFT gives high task accuracy but is brittle to novel facts. RAG generalizes to new data via corpus update but depends on retriever quality.
  - **Compute cost:** RAG adds retrieval latency and memory (FAISS index). Unsupervised FT requires full-model training. SFT uses parameter-efficient LoRA but still requires labeled data.
  - **Prompt design:** The prompt must instruct the model to "use only the provided context." Without this, the generator may ignore retrieval and fall back to parametric memory.

- **Failure signatures:**
  - **RAG failure:** Low retrieval recall (e.g., >1 key fact missing from top-4 chunks). Evidenced by correct answer not supported by provided context. Can be diagnosed by manually inspecting retrieved chunks for gold facts.
  - **SFT failure:** High performance on training distribution but large drop on test sets with different fact combinations or temporal scope (e.g., trained on QASC, tested on 2024 Events). Signals overfitting or lack of generalization.
  - **Unsupervised FT failure:** Minimal accuracy delta over base model. Suggests language modeling objective did not implant recallable factual representations for the multi-hop task.
  - **Base model failure:** Accuracy near random (1/4 or 1/8). Indicates parametric knowledge is insufficient or inaccessible for the task format.

- **First 3 experiments:**
  1. **Retriever isolation audit:** For a sample of 100 multi-hop questions, manually check if all supporting facts are present within the top-20 retrieved chunks (pre-rerank). Compute recall per fact and per question. This establishes the upper bound for RAG if the generator were perfect.
  2. **Base model + RAG vs. Base model alone:** Run the full RAG pipeline with the frozen base generator and compare to base model zero-shot accuracy on the same multiple-choice prompts *without* context. This isolates the contribution of provided evidence.
  3. **SFT data ablation:** Train supervised models on varying proportions (e.g., 25%, 50%, 100%) of the SFT training set. Plot accuracy vs. data size. This tests the sample efficiency of SFT and whether performance saturates, indicating sufficient task supervision.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
### Open Question 1
- **Question:** Why does unsupervised fine-tuning via continual pretraining yield only marginal improvements in multi-hop question answering accuracy?
- **Basis in paper:** [inferred] From Tables I and II and statements in the abstract and Section VI, unsupervised fine-tuning shows minimal gains over base models across datasets.
- **Why unresolved:** The paper empirically demonstrates this outcome but does not investigate underlying causes, such as whether pretraining data lacks multi-hop compositional structure or models fail to internalize such knowledge.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Ablation studies varying pretraining data composition, probing model representations for multi-hop reasoning, or comparing with models trained on explicit multi-hop objectives.

### Open Question 2
- **Question:** How does retrieval quality and context integration affect RAG performance on multi-hop questions, and what are the key failure modes?
- **Basis in paper:** [inferred] The related work section notes that "less is known about how retrieval quality and context integration affect performance on multi-hop questions," and the paper uses a fixed retriever without analyzing retrieval metrics.
- **Why unresolved:** No detailed analysis of retrieval recall for multi-hop queries or examination of cases where retrieved evidence is insufficient for composition.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Error analysis of retrieval results, experiments with diverse retrieval strategies (e.g., iterative retrieval), and correlating retrieval quality metrics (e.g., hit rate) with final accuracy.

### Open Question 3
- **Question:** To what extent do the findings generalize to larger language models and different architectures beyond the 7B-parameter models studied?
- **Basis in paper:** [explicit] Section V.A explicitly states: "We evaluate three open-source large language models with approximately 7 billion parameters," limiting the scope to this scale.
- **Why unresolved:** Model scale and architecture significantly impact reasoning capabilities; results may not hold for larger or differently designed models.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Replicating the experiments with models of varying sizes (e.g., 13B, 70B) and different families (e.g., GPT, Claude) to assess trend consistency.

### Open Question 4
- **Question:** Can hybrid approaches combining parametric fine-tuning and non-parametric RAG further improve multi-hop question answering, particularly for temporally novel knowledge?
- **Basis in paper:** [inferred] The paper compares methods in isolation (Section III) and does not explore combinations, such as fine-tuning with RAG-augmented data or jointly optimizing retrievers and generators.
- **Why unresolved:** Potential synergies between knowledge injection methods remain untested, leaving gaps in understanding optimal integration.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Implementing and evaluating hybrid setups, like supervised fine-tuning on RAG-generated contexts or fine-tuning retrievers on multi-hop queries, and comparing accuracy to standalone methods.

## Limitations
- Exact train/test splits and RAG in-context examples are not disclosed, blocking precise replication.
- Evaluation is confined to two datasets (QASC, 2024 Events); generalizability to other multi-hop domains is unknown.
- No quantification of retrieval completeness for multi-hop chains—RAG’s gains assume all necessary facts are retrieved, which may not hold systematically.

## Confidence
- RAG superiority on novel knowledge: Medium – strong empirical results but confined to specific retriever/generator setups; retrieval failures could critically limit gains.
- Supervised FT highest accuracy: High – direct, consistent observation across models and datasets; task-specific supervision demonstrably effective.
- Unsupervised FT marginal gains: High – repeated minimal improvements indicate a robust limitation of continual pretraining for multi-hop reasoning.

## Next Checks
1. **Retrieval chain recall audit:** For a stratified sample of test questions, manually verify whether all gold supporting facts appear in the top-4 retrieved chunks. Compute per-question chain recall to estimate RAG’s upper bound.
2. **SFT temporal generalization test:** Train supervised FT models only on QASC, then evaluate on 2024 Events without further adaptation. A severe accuracy drop would confirm brittleness to novel facts.
3. **Retriever robustness check:** Replace BGE-large-en with alternative dense retrievers (e.g., Sentence-BERT, Contriever) while keeping all other RAG components fixed. Compare accuracy to assess sensitivity to retriever choice.
