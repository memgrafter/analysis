# Robust Embodied Perception in Dynamic Environments via Disentangled Weight Fusion

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2604.01669
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.01669
- **Reference count:** 26
- **Primary result:** The framework achieves state-of-the-art average accuracies on CDDB (86.35%), CORe50 (92.36%), and DomainNet (71.12%) without exemplars or domain IDs.

## Executive Summary
The paper tackles catastrophic forgetting and environmental noise overfitting in domain incremental learning (DIL) for embodied perception. Unlike existing approaches that rely on domain IDs or exemplar buffers, the proposed method disentangles intrinsic semantic features from domain style using a two-stream encoder with cross-gradient blocking, and employs a QR-decomposition-based weight fusion strategy to merge old and new knowledge in parameter space without storing historical data. Additionally, counterfactual feature augmentation breaks spurious correlations. Evaluated on CDDB, CORe50, and DomainNet, the method sets new state-of-the-art average accuracies (86.35%, 92.36%, and 71.12%) in a fully exemplar-free and domain-ID-free setting, significantly reducing forgetting compared to exemplar- and prompt-based baselines.

## Method Summary
The method leverages a pre-trained CLIP backbone to extract image features. A disentanglement module projects these features into two latent spaces: an intrinsic stream (E_i) capturing class-discriminative, domain-invariant semantics, and a domain stream (E_d) capturing environmental style. Cross-gradient blocking ensures that gradients from the domain loss do not update E_i and vice versa, enforcing orthogonality. A relative difficulty score re-weights samples to focus on cases where domain information is insufficient, guiding the intrinsic branch. Counterfactual augmentation swaps domain features between samples within a batch, using a weighted loss to force the intrinsic classifier to ignore swapped domain cues. After each domain is learned, the weights of E_i are fused with the previous domain's weights via QR decomposition: the old weight matrix is factorized as Q·R; the new initialized weights are projected onto Q; a fusion mask is computed from the discrepancy between the projection and R, determining an element-wise interpolation between old and new weights. This preserves critical prior knowledge while allowing adaptation. Training uses a combination of disentanglement loss (weighted CE for intrinsic branch, GCE for domain branch) and swap loss, with hyperparameters tuned (e.g., GCE bias q=0.7, augmentation weight λ=5).

## Key Results
- **State-of-the-art accuracy:** Average accuracies of 86.35% (CDDB), 92.36% (CORe50), and 71.12% (DomainNet) under exemplar-free, domain-ID-free conditions.
- **Reduced catastrophic forgetting:** The method substantially outperforms prior exemplar-based and prompt-based approaches on incremental learning benchmarks.
- **Robustness to noise:** Disentanglement effectively isolates domain-invariant semantics, mitigating overfitting to environmental style variations.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
**Mechanism 1: Disentangled representation learning** isolates invariant features from style noise. The two-stream encoder (E_i, E_d) with cross-gradient blocking forces orthogonal optimization. The relative difficulty score S(x) re-weights samples where domain features are insufficient, steering E_i toward class-discriminative, domain-invariant representations. The core assumption is that domain-specific correlations are spurious and can be structurally separated. Evidence includes the abstract and section II.A; a break condition arises if domain features leak into the intrinsic branch.

**Mechanism 2: QR-decomposition-based weight fusion** prevents catastrophic forgetting without exemplars. After learning task t-1, the weights of E_i are factorized W_{t-1}=QR. At task t, the new initialization W_{t} is projected onto Q producing P_t; the discrepancy ∥P_t - R∥ (normalized) yields an adaptive fusion mask M, which element-wise interpolates between old and new weights. The assumption is that Q captures a stable subspace for intrinsic features; freezing projections there preserves old knowledge while allowing adaptation in the orthogonal complement. Evidence in section II.B; break conditions include misidentification of critical subspaces due to non-linear interactions.

**Mechanism 3: Counterfactual feature augmentation** breaks spurious correlations within a task. Domain features are swapped between samples to create synthetic cross-domain combinations, forcing the intrinsic classifier to ignore domain cues while the domain classifier learns new domain labels. The assumption is that random domain assignment destroys statistical correlations. Evidence from abstract and section II.C; break conditions involve insufficient domain diversity or inadequate loss weighting.

## Foundational Learning
- **Domain Incremental Learning (DIL):** Defines the problem of sequentially adapting to new environmental domains while keeping the classification task fixed. It is needed because embodied agents operate in changing conditions; DIL is harder than class-incremental learning because covariate shifts (sensor noise, textures) induce subtle forgetting and overfitting. ## Architecture Onboarding
**Component map:**  
Pre-trained CLIP backbone → Disentanglement module (E_i and E_d MLPs) → Intrinsic and domain features (x_i, x_d) → Classifiers C_i (intrinsic, CE) and C_d (domain, GCE). Counterfactual augmentor swaps x_d to create x_sp for swap loss. QR fusion module operates on E_i weights after each task: decomposes old weights, projects new initialization, computes mask, fuses. Loss aggregator combines disentanglement loss (weighted by S(x)) and swap loss.

**Critical path:** Input image → CLIP feature → split via E_i and E_d → (x_i, x_d) → C_i, C_d produce losses. During training, x_i is paired with a domain feature from another sample (or batch) to form x_sp → C_i(x_sp) contributes to swap loss. After completing a domain task, QR fusion updates E_i weights using knowledge from all previous domains.

**Design tradeoffs:**
- Orthogonality constraint vs. expressiveness: Strict cross-gradient blocking may limit synergistic learning; too loose reintroduces entanglement.
- Fusion rigidity vs. plasticity: The fusion mask's clamp parameter β controls preservation vs. adaptation; extremes cause forgetting or under-adaptation.
- Augmentation scope: Swapping assumes domain features are independent of class; strong domain-class correlations may yield unrealistic composites confusing the intrinsic classifier.

**Failure signatures:**
- **Forgetting old domains:** Accuracy drops on earlier tasks; inspect fusion mask M—if too close to 1 on critical weights, old knowledge is overwritten.
- **Overfitting new domain:** High in-domain accuracy but low generalization to unseen domains; check S(x) distribution—if many samples have low S(x), the domain branch is too weak.
- **Domain ID dependency creeping in:** If domain classifier accuracy becomes excessively high, the model may indirectly rely on domain cues; monitor GCE loss.

**First 3 experiments:**
1. **Baseline replication & sanity check:** Train on the first domain only, then sequentially add others. Verify that the intrinsic branch alone (without fusion) already shows some cross-domain transfer, confirming disentanglement yields invariance.
2. **Ablation of fusion:** Disable QR fusion (train E_i normally with disentanglement and swap losses). Expect significant forgetting compared to the full method, isolating fusion's contribution.
3. **Sensitivity tuning:** Vary the GCE bias q and augmentation weight λ (as in Figure 3). Identify optimal values (e.g., q=0.7, λ=5); performance should degrade if q is too low (domain branch not robust) or λ is too high (synthetic features dominate).

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- **Extension to class-incremental or joint class-domain shifts:** The current method assumes a fixed class set; extending it to handle new object classes over time without sacrificing domain adaptation is an open challenge.
- **Enhancing disentanglement under style-semantic correlation:** When style variations are inherently predictive of class (e.g., snow only for certain animals), disentanglement may fail; improving robustness to such confounders remains unresolved.
- **Scalability to lifelong learning with many domains:** The QR fusion strategy is validated on up to 8 domains; its efficiency, computational cost, and accuracy in open-world settings with dozens of domains need investigation.

## Limitations
- **Implementation details missing:** Exact MLP architectures, training hyperparameters (learning rate, epochs), and precise definitions (e.g., ∥R∥max) are not fully specified, hindering faithful reproduction.
- **Feature-swapping mechanism unclear:** The method for obtaining and swapping domain features without exemplars is ambiguous—whether from a buffer, current batch, or synthetic generation—and its impact on robustness is unclear.
- **Insufficient evidence for core mechanisms:** The disentanglement and QR fusion gains are demonstrated only through final accuracy; no direct probing of latent space invariance or fusion subspace stability is provided.

## Confidence
- **Disentanglement mitigates noise:** Medium (mechanism sound, but no direct latent analysis).
- **QR fusion prevents forgetting:** Low (novel but unablated; risk of subspace misidentification).
- **Counterfactual augmentation helps:** Medium-Low (conceptually valid but depends on correct weighting and independence assumptions).

## Next Checks
1. **Ablate QR fusion:** Train with disentanglement and swap losses but without the QR weight fusion step. Expect significant forgetting compared to the full method, confirming fusion's necessity.
2. **Probe disentanglement strength:** After training, evaluate the intrinsic classifier on synthetic test domains where domain features (e.g., texture, lighting) are artificially correlated with class labels. High accuracy would indicate residual reliance on domain cues.
3. **Analyze feature-swapping buffer:** Inspect the source of domain features used for counterfactuals (e.g., buffer vs. batch). If a buffer is used, measure its diversity; if from the batch, quantify how often x_i and x̃_d belong to the same true domain. Validate that swaps indeed break spurious correlations by measuring C_i accuracy on swapped vs. unswapped inputs.
