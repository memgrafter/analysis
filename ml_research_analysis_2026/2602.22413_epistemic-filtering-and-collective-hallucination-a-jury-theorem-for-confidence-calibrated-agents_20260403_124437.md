# Epistemic Filtering and Collective Hallucination: A Jury Theorem for Confidence-Calibrated Agents

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2602.22413  
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.22413  
- **Reference count:** 34  
- **Primary result:** Confidence‑gated majority voting with heterogeneous agents achieves asymptotically perfect collective decisions when average competence exceeds 0.5, and a non‑asymptotic bound quantifies the error probability.

## Executive Summary
The paper studies collective decision‑making by heterogeneous agents who may abstain based on confidence in their own reliability. Each agent has a fixed competence level and calibrates it using a Beta posterior over \(T-1\) preliminary rounds. At the final round, an agent votes only if the posterior probability that its competence exceeds a critical value \(p_{\text{crit}}\) is above an abstention threshold \(\tau\). The authors derive a non‑asymptotic lower bound on the probability that the majority of the published votes is correct. They prove that if the average competence exceeds 0.5 and the abstention gate is uniformly nondegenerate, the collective success probability converges to 1 as the number of agents \(N\) grows. An analogous upper bound controls collective hallucination. Monte Carlo simulations confirm the theoretical bound and show that confidence‑gated voting significantly outperforms always‑voting baselines, especially when agent competencies are diverse.

## Method Summary
Agents are modeled with an inherent but unknown competence \(p_i \in [0,1]\). Over \(T-1\) calibration rounds, each agent observes its binary decisions and outcomes, maintaining a Beta posterior distribution over \(p_i\). At decision time (round \(T\)), each agent computes the posterior probability \(P(p_i > p_{\text{crit}} \mid \text{history})\). If this confidence exceeds threshold \(\tau\), the agent publishes its vote; otherwise it abstains. The final collective decision is the majority sign among all published votes. The analysis uses concentration inequalities to bound the probability that the majority errs, yielding an expression involving each agent’s expected publishing probability \(E[D_i]\) and competence \(p_i\). The bound depends on \(T\) and the heterogeneity of competences. The authors also define a “uniformly nondegenerate” gate condition to ensure that a nontrivial fraction of agents publishes votes, enabling asymptotic unanimity.

## Key Results
- Derivation of a non‑asymptotic lower bound on majority correctness:  
  \[
  1 - \exp\left( -\frac{(\sum_i (2p_i-1)E[D_i])^2}{2\sum_i \big((T-1)(2p_i-1)^2 + 4\big)} \right).
  \]
- Proof that if \(\frac{1}{N}\sum_i p_i > 0.5\) and the gate is uniformly nondegenerate, the success probability tends to 1 as \(N\to\infty\); an upper bound analogous controls collective hallucination.
- Monte Carlo validation shows the confidence‑gated mechanism substantially improves success rates compared to always‑voting, with greater gains under heterogeneous competencies.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The mechanism leverages self‑assessment: agents use Bayesian inference to quantify their own competence. Those with low confidence (likely due to low competence or insufficient calibration data) abstain, thus filtering out unreliable votes. The remaining pool of voters has an effective competence distribution shifted upward relative to the original average. The derived bound shows that the majority error decays exponentially with the squared sum of centered competences weighted by publishing probabilities, explaining the convergence when average competence \(>0.5\). The “uniformly nondegenerate” condition ensures that enough agents publish so that the law of large numbers applies to the majority vote. Essentially, epistemic filtering turns heterogeneous groups into a more homogeneous high‑competence jury, beating the classic Condorcet jury theorem’s i.i.d. assumption.

  *Why needed:* Provides a finite‑sample guarantee on collective accuracy, not just limiting behavior.

## Architecture Onboarding
### Component Map
Agent -> BetaCalibration -> ConfidenceEstimation -> AbstentionGate -> VotePublisher -> MajorityAggregator -> FinalDecision

### Critical Path
1. For each agent: over \(T-1\) rounds, collect outcomes and update Beta posterior.
2. At round \(T\): compute posterior probability that competence \(> p_{\text{crit}}\).
3. Compare to threshold \(\tau\); if above, publish vote; else abstain.
4. Collect all published votes.
5. Take majority sign; if tie, break arbitrarily (e.g., random).
6. Output final decision.

### Design Tradeoffs
- More calibration rounds (\(T-1\)) sharpens posterior but delays decision.
- Lower \(\tau\) increases participation but risks including low‑confidence (low‑competence) agents.
- Choice of \(p_{\text{crit}}\) influences the confidence measure; it sets the benchmark for “acceptable” competence.
- Heterogeneity: gating may disenfranchise many low‑competence agents, reducing total votes; bound still holds if gate is nondegenerate.
- Computation: Beta updates are \(O(1)\) per agent; overall linear in \(N\) and \(T\).

### Failure Signatures
- **Gate degeneracy:** \(\tau\) too high or \(p_{\text{crit}}\) too high → \(E[D_i] \to 0\), violating nondegenerate condition, leading to random majority or no votes.
- **Overconfident low‑competence agents:** Posterior miscalibrated (small \(T-1\) or prior mismatch) → agents with low true \(p_i\) publish and harm accuracy.
- **Average competence ≤ 0.5:** Even perfect filtering cannot make success converge to 1.
- **Violation of independence:** Correlated posteriors could break the bound (analysis assumes conditional independence).

### First Experiments
1. **Threshold Sweep:** Vary \(\tau\) across \([0,1]\) and measure success probability, average number of published votes, and compare empirical error to theoretical bound. Identify region where bound is tight and where it becomes loose.
2. **Competency Distribution Test:** Compare performance under different heterogeneity patterns (e.g., uniform, bimodal, heavy‑tailed) while keeping average competence constant. Assess how distribution affects bound tightness and benefit of gating.
3. **Nondegenerate Gate Stress Test:** Construct gate functions that intentionally violate uniform nondegeneracy (e.g., \(\tau\) such that only a vanishing fraction of agents publish as \(N\) grows). Observe whether convergence fails and how quickly the bound degrades.

## Limitations
- The “uniformly nondegenerate” gate condition is not explicitly defined in the summary, leaving its precise mathematical statement and necessity unclear.
- The non‑asymptotic bound may be loose; the report does not quantify its tightness relative to empirical performance across parameter regimes.
- The model assumes fixed agent competence and ideal Beta calibration; it is unknown how robust the results are to non‑stationary agents (learning, drift) or model misspecification.
- The abstention thresholds \(\tau\) and \(p_{\text{crit}}\) are critical but lack a sensitivity analysis in the provided summary.
