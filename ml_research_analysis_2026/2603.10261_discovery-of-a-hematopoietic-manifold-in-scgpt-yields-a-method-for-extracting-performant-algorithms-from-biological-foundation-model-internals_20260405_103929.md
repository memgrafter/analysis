# Discovery of a Hematopoietic Manifold in scGPT Yields a Method for Extracting Performant Algorithms from Biological Foundation Model Internals

## Quick Facts
*   **arXiv ID:** 2603.10261
*   **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.10261
*   **Reference count:** 40
*   **Primary result:** The first competitive, biologically useful algorithm extracted from a foundation model via mechanistic interpretability, achieving state-of-the-art pseudotime-depth ordering and subtype classification while being dramatically more efficient.

## Executive Summary
This paper investigates whether meaningful biological algorithms can be extracted from a single-cell foundation model (scGPT) using mechanistic interpretability. It introduces a three-stage extraction pipeline: (1) directly exporting a frozen attention operator, (2) training a lightweight adaptor to map its features to a low-dimensional manifold, and (3) attaching a task-specific readout. This yields a standalone algorithm that generalizes without retraining on the target dataset. Applied to hematopoietic development, the extracted algorithm sets new performance benchmarks for pseudotime-depth correlation and immune cell subtype classification across numerous donor-holdout splits, while being vastly more parameter-efficient and faster than standard probing methods. The work demonstrates that core biological geometries are encoded in specific transformer components and can be compressed into interpretable, reusable algorithms.

## Method Summary
The core method is a three-stage pipeline for extracting a standalone algorithm from a frozen single-cell foundation model. First, **direct operator export** isolates specific value-projection matrices from scGPT's attention mechanism, constructing a "drift" feature that captures changes in gene embeddings across transformer layers. Second, a **lightweight learned adaptor** is trained on a subset of internal data using a Latent Embedding Transfer (LET) objective to align these drift features with a low-dimensional (e.g., 10D) manifold that preserves biologically meaningful pairwise developmental distances from a stage ontology. Third, a **task-specific readout** (a linear probe or small MLP) is trained on this manifold for a downstream task like pseudotime inference or cell type classification. Critically, after the adaptor is trained, both the operator and adaptor are frozen; the algorithm generalizes to new datasets without any retraining on target data. The pipeline includes compaction steps (selecting a single attention head or applying low-rank factorization) to yield smaller, interpretable operators.

## Key Results
*   The extracted algorithm achieves the strongest pseudotime-depth ordering (|ρ| = 0.439) and leads on key subtype classification tasks (CD4/CD8 AUROC 0.867, mono/macro AUROC 0.951) across 88 donor-holdout splits, significantly outperforming comparators.
*   It is Benjamini-Hochberg significantly better than probing frozen scGPT embeddings with a 3-layer MLP on 6/8 endpoints, while completing evaluations 34.5× faster and using ~1000× fewer parameters.
*   Mechanistic interpretability reveals a concentrated four-factor core within the compacted operator that explains 66.2% of ablation impact, resolving into explicit T/lymphoid, B/plasma, granulocytic, and monocyte/macrophage gene programs.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1
-   **Claim:** The attention value-projection operators in scGPT encode a geometric manifold that reflects hematopoietic developmental proximity.
-   **Mechanism:** Successive differences of cell representations across transformer layers (the drift operator) capture representational changes that correlate with biological stage distances. A lightweight learned adaptor then aligns this drift space to a low-dimensional manifold while preserving those distances.
-   **Core assumption:** Developmental stage distance is encoded in how gene embeddings evolve through transformer layers.
-   **Evidence anchors:** The recovered manifold shows high trustworthiness (0.993) and strong correlation with biological distances (0.835), with branch structure aligning to established hematopoiesis. External validation on a strict non-overlap panel confirms transferability.
-   **Break condition:** If the drift operator fails to produce high trustworthiness (≥0.80) and holdout correlations (≥0.20) on internal data, or if external transfer fails.

### Mechanism 2
-   **Claim:** A three-stage pipeline (frozen operator export, lightweight learned adaptor, task probe) produces a standalone algorithm that generalizes without target-dataset retraining.
-   **Mechanism:** The frozen operator provides transferable geometry; the small adaptor (trained only on internal data) maps to a task-agnostic manifold; the task probe then maps to outputs. No target data is used in adaptor training.
-   **Core assumption:** The geometry encoded in the operator is dataset-agnostic for the same biological system.
-   **Evidence anchors:** Zero-shot tests on an independent multi-donor immune panel confirm transferability. The extracted head is BH-significantly better on 6/8 endpoints vs. standard probing, with 34.5× faster execution and ~1000× fewer parameters.
-   **Break condition:** If zero-shot external transfer yields low alignment metrics or if the extracted algorithm does not outperform baselines on target tasks.

### Mechanism 3
-   **Claim:** The hematopoietic signal is localized to a few attention heads and factors, enabling compression and mechanistic interpretation.
-   **Mechanism:** Head attribution identifies top heads (e.g., L2H5) that retain most transferable geometry. Truncated SVD and sparse pruning compress the operator. Factor ablation reveals a small set of factors that explain most performance, with gene loadings mapping to biological programs.
-   **Core assumption:** The manifold is encoded by a sparse subnetwork of the attention mechanism.
-   **Evidence anchors:** A single head (L2H5) retains classification utility without BH-significant loss. Factor ablation of the compact operator reveals a four-factor core (66.2% of impact) mapping to explicit hematopoietic gene programs.
-   **Break condition:** If compaction degrades performance beyond BH-significant thresholds, or if factor ablation yields no interpretable biological enrichment.

## Foundational Learning
*   **Geometric Manifold Learning and Validation:** Needed to learn and validate that the drift features form a low-D space preserving developmental proximities. Uses metrics like trustworthiness and correlation with held-out biological distances.
    *   Quick check: "Given a high-dimensional dataset and a distance matrix of ground-truth relationships, how would you quantitatively test if a 2D t-SNE embedding preserves the neighborhood structure?"
*   **Transformer Attention Operators:** Needed to understand that the value-projection matrix is a linear transformation applied to token embeddings, and that differences between such transformations across layers can encode meaningful biological change.
    *   Quick check: "In a transformer, what is the mathematical role of the value projection matrix (`W_v`), and how does its output differ from that of the key (`W_k`) and query (`W_q`) projections?"
*   **Probing and Transfer Learning:** Needed to understand the paradigm of using a frozen feature extractor and training only a small downstream probe, assuming the features are linearly related to the task.
    *   Quick check: "When probing a frozen neural network for a downstream task, under what condition would a linear probe be sufficient, and what might indicate that a more complex (non-linear) probe is required?"

## Architecture Onboarding
Input -> scGPT frozen checkpoint -> Drift Feature Extractor (f_drift: x*A_early - x*A_mid; x*A_mid - x*A_late) -> Learned Adaptor (W_enc, b via LET loss) -> Task-Agnostic Manifold (z) -> Task Readout (h_φ) -> Output (predictions)

Critical path: (1) Identify top attention heads with transferable geometry via attribution. (2) Construct drift operator (pooled or single-head). (3) Train LET adaptor on internal anchors using stage-distance targets. (4) Freeze operator and adaptor. (5) Train task probe. (6) Evaluate on external donor-holdout splits.

Design tradeoffs:
*   Head selection: Pooling multiple heads improves subtype separation but increases model size; a single top head (L2H5) retains global geometry at lower cost.
*   Adaptor objective: Including topology preservation yields cleaner branch geometry; pure distance-matching may better preserve exact stage proximities.
*   Compression: Low-rank (SVD) vs. sparse pruning: rank-64 surrogate retains most performance; aggressive sparsity aids interpretability at some accuracy cost.

Failure signatures:
*   Internal manifold validation fails: trustworthiness <0.80, holdout correlation <0.20.
*   External zero-shot transfer fails: low trustworthiness/correlation on non-overlap panel.
*   Extracted algorithm does not BH-significantly outperform baselines on key endpoints (pseudotime ρ, subtype AUROC).
*   Compaction (single-head, low-rank, sparse) causes BH-significant drop on multiple metrics.
*   Factor ablation shows no concentrated core (impact distributed evenly across factors).

First 3 experiments:
1.  **Internal manifold validation:** Train LET adaptor on internal anchors, compute trustworthiness and holdout correlations on internal splits. Must pass quality gates (≥0.80, ≥0.20).
2.  **External non-overlap zero-shot transfer:** Apply frozen adaptor to external anchors, evaluate same metrics. Must pass gates and show positive margin vs. PCA-2 baseline.
3.  **Benchmark against baselines:** Run extracted algorithm (with task probe) on 88 donor-holdout splits, compute pseudotime-depth Spearman and classification AUROCs. Compare to raw, PCA, SVD, CellTypist, scVI, DPT, Palantir. Check BH-significant improvements.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
### Open Question 1
**Question:** Does the three-stage extraction pipeline generalize to other single-cell foundation models (e.g., scBERT, Geneformer) and other biological systems beyond hematopoietic development?
**Basis in paper:** The study applies the method only to scGPT and primarily to hematopoiesis. The authors explicitly pose this as a key next question and note that a second case (intercellular communication) required rescue, limiting certainty about full generality.
**Why unresolved:** No systematic evaluation across different model architectures or diverse biological manifolds (e.g., neurogenesis) has been performed.
**What evidence would resolve it:** Successful replication with comparable fidelity and performance on multiple foundation models and multiple, distinct biological developmental processes.

### Open Question 2
**Question:** What biological processes or gene regulatory programs underlie the statistically significant residual ripple structure detected in the manifold after accounting for the dominant planar geometry?
**Basis in paper:** The authors report detecting structured residual geometry but mapping it to specific curated programs did not survive multiple-testing correction. This is stated as an open limitation.
**Why unresolved:** Despite significant periodic patterns in residuals, no gene sets or pathways correlate robustly (after FDR correction) with the residual axes.
**What evidence would resolve it:** Identification of gene sets/pathways that significantly correlate with residual axes across multiple independent datasets, surviving multiple-testing correction, potentially validated by perturbation experiments.

### Open Question 3
**Question:** Does the fixed-probe leave-one-factor-out ablation truly reflect causal necessity of each factor for the algorithm's performance, or could the same performance be recovered by retraining the readout probes without that factor?
**Basis in paper:** The authors acknowledge the protocol measures what the current model uses, not whether performance could be recovered after retraining without a given factor.
**Why unresolved:** Zeroing a factor at inference with fixed probes shows reliance, but retraining the probe from scratch in the absence of that factor might re-weight remaining factors to restore performance.
**What evidence would resolve it:** Re-running ablation while allowing task probes to be retrained from scratch without each factor. Persistent degradation would support necessity; recovery would suggest the factor was merely a convenient feature.

### Open Question 4
**Question:** Can the extracted algorithm achieve robust performance across a broader range of donors and datasets, given the limited donor diversity and potential leakage in the current 88-split benchmark?
**Basis in paper:** The authors note substantial donor reuse across splits and that external validation covered only three panels (two passing, one failing), calling for broader cross-cohort replication.
**Why unresolved:** Donor reuse may inflate variability estimates, and one external panel (lung) failed transfer, raising questions about robustness to new tissues/platforms.
**What evidence would resolve it:** A new benchmark using completely held-out donors from many more independent cohorts (different studies, platforms, tissues) with zero overlap between training and test sets.

## Limitations
*   **Reproducibility gaps:** Critical LET training hyperparameters (learning rate, α, epochs) and precise details of the stage-distance matrix construction are not specified.
*   **Generalizability scope:** The method's success is demonstrated only for scGPT on hematopoietic development; its applicability to other foundation models or biological systems remains unevaluated.
*   **Interpretation depth:** While a four-factor core is identified, a significant residual geometric structure remains biologically uninterpreted.

## Confidence
*   **Manifold existence in scGPT:** High (supported by strong internal trustworthiness 0.993, external transfer success, and blocked-permutation significance).
*   **Standalone extraction pipeline efficacy:** Medium (zero-shot performance demonstrated, but exact replication is hindered by missing hyperparameters and layer-grouping specifics).
*   **Downstream outperformance & efficiency gains:** High (BH-significant improvements across 88 splits on multiple key endpoints, with clear speed/parameter advantages).
*   **Localization & compression:** High (single-head L2H5 retains performance, factor ablation yields a concentrated, biologically coherent core).

## Next Checks
1.  **Re-produce LET adaptor training with hyperparameter search:** Systematically vary learning rate, α, and training epochs to determine if the reported internal manifold metrics (trustworthiness ≥0.80, holdout ρ≥0.20) are stable or sensitive to these choices.
2.  **Test on an independent hematopoietic dataset:** Apply the complete extraction and evaluation pipeline to a bone marrow scRNA-seq dataset (e.g., from a different study/platform) with no donor overlap to Tabula Sapiens. Re-evaluate pseudotime-depth correlation and subtype AUROC.
3.  **Sensitivity analysis of layer partitioning:** Assess the robustness of the drift operator and downstream performance to different definitions of early/middle/late layer blocks (e.g., 3-3-6 vs. 4-4-4 vs. 2-8-2) to confirm the chosen grouping is not a critical tuning artifact.
