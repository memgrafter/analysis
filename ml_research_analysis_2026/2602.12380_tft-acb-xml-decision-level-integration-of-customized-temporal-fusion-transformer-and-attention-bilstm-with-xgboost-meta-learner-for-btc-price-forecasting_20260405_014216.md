# TFT-ACB-XML: Decision-Level Integration of Customized Temporal Fusion Transformer and Attention-BiLSTM with XGBoost Meta-Learner for BTC Price Forecasting
## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2602.12380
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.12380
- Reference count: 0
- Primary result: Achieved a MAPE of 0.65%, MAE of 198.15, and RMSE of 258.30 on a walk-forward one-step-ahead test spanning the 2024 BTC halving and spot ETF period, outperforming 15 baseline models.

## Executive Summary
Bitcoin price forecasting is hindered by market non-linearity, volatility, and regime shifts such as the 2024 halving and spot ETF introduction. This study proposes TFT-ACB-XML, a stacked generalization framework that integrates a customized Temporal Fusion Transformer (TFT) to capture long-range structural dependencies with an Attention-Customized BiLSTM (ACB) to model short-term liquidity-driven volatility. Base predictions are dynamically weighted via an error-reciprocal strategy derived from validation performance and then refined by an XGBoost meta-learner to correct residual biases. Under walk-forward evaluation on a challenging test block, the model achieved a MAPE of 0.65%, demonstrating robust performance across volatile regimes and significantly surpassing 15 recent baselines.

## Method Summary
The framework processes daily OHLCV data (2014–2026) split chronologically (80% train, 10% validation, 10% test). In parallel, a customized TFT (60-day window, single-head attention, variable selection network) and an ACB (BiLSTM with feature-level attention on closing price and volume) are trained on the training split. Validation predictions from both are combined using weights computed as `W = (1/Validation_MAPE) / Σ(1/Validation_MAPE)`. The weighted predictions form a 2D meta-feature vector `[W_bl×P_bl, W_tft×P_tft]`, which trains an XGBoost regressor on the validation set. For walk-forward testing, all components (base models, weights, XGBoost) remain fixed; each test day's forecast uses the last 60 observed days to generate base predictions, apply fixed weights, and output via the trained XGBoost.

## Key Results
- Achieved MAPE of 0.65%, MAE of 198.15, and RMSE of 258.30 on a test block covering the 2024 halving and ETF period.
- Outperformed 15 baseline models, including recent transformers and hybrid ensembles.
- Demonstrated robustness during high-volatility regime shifts, with tight alignment to actual prices even around key events.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
**Mechanism 1: Complementary temporal dynamics.** The customized TFT uses variable selection and single-head self-attention over a fixed 60-day window to model global, cross-regime patterns (e.g., halving cycles), while the ACB’s BiLSTM with a custom attention gate prioritizes closing price and volume to capture immediate liquidity shocks. Their error-weighted combination allows the meta-learner to correct regime-specific biases. *Evidence:* The abstract states TFT handles long-range dependencies and ACB captures short-term dependencies; Section 3.1 details TFT’s variable selection and temporal attention; Section 3.2 describes ACB’s attention gate and feature-level focus. *Break condition:* If validation error poorly correlates with test error across regimes, the fixed weighting misattributes reliability, leading to meta-learner overfitting.

**Mechanism 2: Error-reciprocal weighting.** Weights `W = (1/Validation_MAPE) / Σ(1/Validation_MAPE)` assign dynamic influence to base predictions without retraining. The model with lower validation MAPE receives higher weight in the stacked feature vector. *Evidence:* Abstract mentions error-reciprocal weighting; Section 3.3 defines weight computation from validation MAPE; Section 4.3 notes weights prevent data leakage. *Break condition:* A structural break between validation and test error distributions (e.g., validation in low-volatility, test in high-volatility) causes miscalibrated fixed weights.

**Mechanism 3: XGBoost residual correction.** XGBoost learns a non-linear mapping from the weighted base predictions to the true price, capturing residual interactions that persist after weighting (e.g., correlated under-predictions during volatility). *Evidence:* Abstract states XGBoost captures non-linear residuals; Section 3.4 describes gradient-boosted trees modeling residuals; Section 4.6.3 notes XGBoost refines stacked inputs. *Break condition:* If residuals are primarily stochastic or regime-dependent in unseen ways, XGBoost may overfit or fail to generalize.

**Concept: Attention-Customized BiLSTM (ACB)**  
*Why needed here:* To process the 60-day window but emphasize short-term sequential patterns and liquidity signals (volume/price) that drive intra-regime volatility, which TFT’s coarser attention might dilute.

**Concept: Stacked Generalization with Error-Weighted Inputs**  
*Why needed here:* To avoid simple averaging; instead, let validation performance dynamically scale each base learner’s contribution before a meta-learner captures residual structure.  
*Quick check:* Why is the error-reciprocal weight computed solely on the validation split (not training or test), and what leakage risk would using test error introduce?

## Architecture Onboarding
Component map: Inputs (OHLCV + calendar for TFT; OHLCV for ACB) -> Base learners (customized TFT, ACB) -> Weighting (compute W_tft, W_bl from validation MAPE) -> Meta-features `[W_bl×P_bl, W_tft×P_tft]` -> Meta-learner (XGBoost) -> Walk-forward test predictions.

Critical path: Train base models on training split -> Generate validation predictions -> Compute weights from validation MAPE -> Train XGBoost on weighted validation features -> Freeze all components -> Walk-forward test with sliding 60-day window, fixed weights and models.

Design tradeoffs: Fixed 60-day look-back vs. adaptive context (simplicity but may miss longer cycles); single-head TFT attention vs. multi-head (reduced complexity but limited pattern diversity); OHLCV-only ACB vs. adding calendar (focused on microstructure but ignores seasonality encoded in TFT).

Failure signatures: High validation vs. low test error (or vice versa) indicates weighting miscalibration; XGBoost training loss much lower than base validation loss suggests overfitting; weight collapse (one weight ~1.0, other ~0) signals one learner dominates, defeating ensemble purpose.

First 3 experiments:
1. Train TFT and ACB separately; plot validation vs. training loss to confirm no severe overfitting (as in Figure 12).
2. Recompute validation MAPE using rolling windows (last 30, 60, 90 days of validation); assess weight volatility—high variance implies unstable error signal.
3. Ablate XGBoost: compare weighted average `[W_bl*P_bl, W_tft*P_tft]` vs. XGBoost output on validation; if XGBoost improves >1% MAPE, residuals are learnable.

## Limitations
- Architectural details (ACB’s attention gate, TFT’s simplified config) are underspecified, risking replication drift.
- Error-reciprocal weighting assumes validation error stability across the halving/ETF regime break—a strong, unvalidated assumption for non-stationary BTC data.
- Minimal external corpus corroboration (0 references, FMR≈0.47) limits confidence in the claimed synergy.

## Confidence
- Mechanism 1 (TFT/ACB complementarity): Medium (plausible but no BTC-specific evidence).
- Mechanism 2 (error-weighted fusion): Low (regime-shift vulnerability untested).
- Mechanism 3 (XGBoost residual correction): Medium-High (aligned with ensemble literature).
