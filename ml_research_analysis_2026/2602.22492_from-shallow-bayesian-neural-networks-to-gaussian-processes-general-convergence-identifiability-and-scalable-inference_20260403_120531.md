# From Shallow Bayesian Neural Networks to Gaussian Processes: General Convergence, Identifiability and Scalable Inference

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2602.22492
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.22492
- **Reference count:** 16
- **Primary result:** Proves general convergence of shallow BNNs to GPs under relaxed assumptions, introduces a mixed kernel combining four activation functions, and presents a scalable MAP inference algorithm using Nyström approximation.

## Executive Summary
The paper contributes to the theoretical understanding and practical scalability of Bayesian neural networks. It shows that shallow BNNs converge to Gaussian processes under broader conditions than previously known, extending the classical infinite‑width limit. The authors derive a new kernel that unifies kernels from tanh, sigmoid, ReLU, and Leaky ReLU activations via a convex combination; this mixed kernel is positive definite and identifiable. For inference, they develop a maximum a posteriori approach that exploits the Nyström method to approximate large kernel matrices, enabling training on datasets with tens of thousands of points. Experiments on synthetic and real tabular datasets confirm that the method provides stable hyperparameter estimates and competitive predictive accuracy, with the mixing weight staying balanced and the nugget robust to approximation rank.

The work bridges theory and computation, delivering a practical Bayesian model that retains the non‑parametric flexibility of GPs while scaling to large‑n regimes. The trade‑off between Nyström rank and accuracy is documented, showing diminishing returns beyond a moderate rank.

## Method Summary
The authors specify a GP with kernel  
`K_mix(x,x′;θ) = σ²_b + σ²_v [ w A(x,x′) + (1−w) B(x,x′;α) ]`,  
where `A` and `B` are analytic functions derived from the arcsin and rectangular kernels respectively, and include per‑input variance terms `σ²_a + σ²_u ||x||²`. The parameters `θ` also include a nugget `σ²_ε`. For scalable MAP estimation, they employ the Nyström approximation: select `m` anchor points `S`, compute `C = K(X_train, S)` and `W = K(S,S)`, then approximate `K ≈ C W⁻¹ Cᵀ`. The marginal likelihood of the GP under this approximation is expressed via Woodbury identities to avoid explicit handling of the `n×n` matrix. The objective  
`L(θ) = ½ yᵀ K̃⁻¹ y + ½ log det K̃ − log p(θ)`  
(with `K̃ = K + σ²_ε I`) is minimized using Adam for 50 epochs with full‑batch gradients. Hyperparameters are constrained to valid ranges via transformations (e.g., softplus for variances, sigmoid for weights). For prediction, the same Nystr factors are used to compute the predictive mean and variance efficiently.

## Key Results
- The mixed kernel is provably positive definite for all interior parameter values and strictly identifiable, meaning distinct parameter sets produce distinct covariance functions.
- Nyström‑based MAP inference scales to `n ≈ 500 000` with rank up to 5000, offering a controllable trade‑off between computational cost and predictive accuracy.
- Empirical findings: the mixing weight `w` remains well‑behaved (balanced), the nugget `σ²_ε` is stable across ranks, and increasing rank beyond a moderate size yields only marginal error reduction while significantly increasing runtime.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The convergence of shallow BNNs to GPs follows from extending the classic result by Neal (1996) to a broader class of activation functions and weight priors; the proof relies on the Central Limit Theorem for sums of independent random variables and the continuity of the activation function. The mixed kernel arises by analytically computing the limiting covariance between two BNN outputs under i.i.d. Gaussian weights; each activation contributes a specific kernel (tanh → arcsin, sigmoid/ReLU/Leaky ReLU → rectangular), and their convex combination preserves positive definiteness because each component kernel is positive definite. The Nyström approximation works because the kernel matrix often has rapidly decaying eigenvalues; selecting a subset of anchor points captures most of the spectral energy, and the Woodbury identity allows the marginal likelihood to be computed using only the low‑rank factors without forming the full matrix. MAP estimation with a Gaussian likelihood then becomes tractable even for large `n`.

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component map:**  
Training Data (X, y) → Anchor Selection (S) → Compute Cross‑kernel `C = K(X, S)` and anchor kernel `W = K(S, S)` → Form Woodbury matrix `M = W + (1/σ²_ε) Cᵀ C` → Compute log‑det(`K̃`) and quadratic term via Woodbury → Evaluate MAP loss `L(θ)` + priors → Optimize `θ` with Adam → Store `C, W, θ̂`.  
Prediction: `k_* = K(X_train, X_*)` → Solve linear system using stored factors → Compute predictive mean `μ` and variance `σ²_*` → De‑standardize.

**Critical path:** The most demanding operations are (i) computing the kernel matrix between all training points and the anchors (`O(n m I)` for dot products), (ii) Cholesky decomposition of `W` (`O(m³)`), and (iii) solving the `m×m` linear system during each gradient step. Memory usage is dominated by `C` (`n × m`).

**Design tradeoffs:**  
- **Nyström rank `m`**: Larger `m` improves approximation accuracy but increases memory `O(n m)` and time `O(m³)`. The paper reports diminishing returns beyond a few thousand.  
- **Anchor selection**: k‑means++ yields better coverage than first‑`m` (random if ordered) but adds `O(n I m)` clustering cost.  
- **Prior specification**: Stronger priors can prevent hyperparameter collapse but risk bias if mis‑specified. The paper uses vague defaults, leaving robustness to be tested.  
- **Full‑batch vs. mini‑batch**: The method relies on all training points to form `C`; mini‑batching would require recomputing `C` or using a fixed `C`, which may bias gradients.

**Failure signatures:**  
- Non‑PD `W` ➔ Cholesky fails; remedy: add jitter (e.g., `1e‑6 * max(diag(W))`).  
- MAP loss becomes `NaN` ➔ check `σ²_ε` positivity (use softplus), clip gradients, reduce learning rate.  
- Mixing weight `w → 0` or `1` ➔ indicates identifiability issues; try stronger Beta prior or re‑initialize.  
- Out‑of‑memory: `C` too large; switch to float32 or reduce `m`.  
- Poor test performance: verify preprocessing (min‑max on train only), check that `ρ` is clipped to `[−1,1]`, inspect anchor diversity.

**First experiments:**  
1. **Kernel positive definiteness**: Sample `θ` from priors, generate 200 random points in `[0,1]^d`, compute `K_mix`, and confirm all eigenvalues ≥ `-1e‑6`.  
2. **MAP convergence**: On a small dataset (`n=500, d=5`), train for 50 epochs; plot `L_MAP` vs. epoch; expect monotonic decrease.  
3. **Rank‑accuracy curve**: On a medium dataset (`n=5000`), train with `m ∈ {100, 300, 500, 1000}` and record test RMSE and training time; observe diminishing returns.

## Limitations
- Prior hyperparameters are not specified; default choices may influence results and reproducibility.
- Anchor selection details (number of k‑means++ restarts, whether to cluster on full data) are omitted, leaving ambiguity.
- Initialization of non‑nugget parameters is unspecified; reproducibility may depend on random seeds.
- Theoretical convergence claims lack a full proof sketch in the paper, making independent verification difficult.
- Scalability experiments are limited to tabular datasets; performance on high‑dimensional or image data remains unknown.

## Confidence
- General convergence of shallow BNNs to GPs: **Low** (theoretical result not fully verifiable here).
- Mixed kernel positive definite and strictly identifiable: **Medium** (can be numerically tested but proof is theoretical).
- Scalable MAP inference via Nyström: **High** (algorithm clearly described and implementable).
- Experimental results (stable hyperparameters, competitive performance): **Medium** (implementation dependent on missing details).
