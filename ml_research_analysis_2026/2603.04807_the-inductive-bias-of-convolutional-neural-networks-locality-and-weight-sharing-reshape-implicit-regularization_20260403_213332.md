# The Inductive Bias of Convolutional Neural Networks: Locality and Weight Sharing Reshape Implicit Regularization

## Quick Facts
- ArXiv ID: 2603.04807  
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.04807  
- Reference Count: 40  
- Key Result: Weight sharing in convolutional architectures couples filters to a low-dimensional patch manifold, enabling favorable generalization under the edge-of-stability (BEoS) regime, while fully connected networks provably fail.

## Executive Summary
This paper investigates how the inductive biases of locality and weight sharing in convolutional neural networks (CNNs) alter the implicit regularization induced by gradient descent (GD) when operated at the edge-of-stability. The authors prove that for locally connected networks with weight sharing (LCN-WS) on hyperspherical data, the generalization rate scales as \(n^{-1/6+O(m/d)}\), improving with ambient dimension \(d\), whereas fully connected networks cannot generalize. They demonstrate that weight sharing is essential by showing that without it, even locally connected networks overfit. Empirically, natural image patches exhibit geometry (low intrinsic dimension, high half-space depth) that aligns with the theory, and LCN-WS generalizes under label noise while FCN memorizes. The work reveals the precise mechanism: the BEoS condition imposes a weighted path norm penalty whose weights depend on patch geometry; weight sharing ensures that this penalty couples across all spatial locations, amplifying the regularization effect when patches concentrate.

## Method Summary
The authors combine theoretical analysis with empirical validation. Theoretically, they characterize the set of parameters satisfying the BEoS condition (bounded Hessian eigenvalue \(\leq 2/\eta\)) and show that for a locally connected network with weight sharing, this set implies a bound on a weighted variation norm. The weight function depends on the patch distribution’s geometry (e.g., half-space depth and concentration). For data uniformly distributed on a hypersphere with small patches (\(m \ll d\)), they derive a polynomial generalization rate. Conversely, they construct examples where BEoS holds but overfitting occurs, highlighting the necessity of favorable patch geometry. Empirically, they compare LCN-WS against fully connected networks on synthetic hyperspherical data (varying \(d, n\)), a clustered-patch synthetic benchmark, and CIFAR-10 with noisy labels. They measure train/test loss, compute empirical slopes of generalization gap vs. sample size, and analyze patch statistics (PCA, half-space depth). Implementation uses full-batch GD with large learning rate (\(\eta=0.2\)), wide networks (\(K=1024\)), and ReLU activations.

## Key Results
- For hyperspherical data with patch size \(m \ll d\), LCN-WS achieves generalization rate \(n^{-1/6+O(m/d)}\) that improves with dimensionality \(d\); fully connected networks provably fail.
- Weight sharing is essential: in a clustered-patch model, LCN-WS generalizes while locally connected networks without sharing and FCN memorize.
- Natural image patches (CIFAR-10) have low intrinsic dimension (~3) and high half-space depth, making isolation difficult; LCN-WS generalizes under label noise (test loss near \(\sigma^2\)), while FCN overfits.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
**Claim:** Weight sharing reduces effective optimization dimensionality by tying filter activations to a low-dimensional patch manifold, bypassing ambient curse of dimensionality.  
- **Mechanism:** In LCN-WS, gradients for shared filters aggregate over spatial locations. The BEoS constraint then imposes a weighted path norm penalty whose weights depend on patch geometry. When \(m \ll d\), patches concentrate near the origin in patch space, making the activation-mass term large across most data, tightening the penalty.  
- **Core Assumption:** Uniform input on \(S^{d-1}\); small fixed patch size \(m\).  
- **Evidence:** Theorem 4.2 gives rate \(n^{-1/6+O(m/d)}\); CIFAR-10 patches show low intrinsic dimension.  
- **Break Condition:** If patches can be nearly isolated (e.g., on \(S^{m-1}\)), flat interpolation satisfies BEoS yet overfits (Theorem 4.3).

### Mechanism 2
**Claim:** The BEoS condition induces implicit regularization via a stability-to-regularity implication that penalizes neurons active on many patches.  
- **Mechanism:** For parameters in \(\Theta_S^{BEoS}\), Theorem 4.1 bounds a weighted path norm. The weight \(g_{D,S}(u,t)\) is proportional to \(P(u^T X_S^D > t)\) times conditional ReLU margin. Patch concentration increases \(g_{D,S}\) for most directions, strengthening the penalty.  
- **Core Assumption:** ReLU, twice-differentiable loss, Hessian bound \(\leq 2/\eta\).  
- **Evidence:** Proposition B.9 shows \(g_{P,S}\) scales like \(\varepsilon^d\) for small offsets; synthetic experiments yield negative log-log slopes for LCN-WS vs. ~0 for FCN.  
- **Break Condition:** If patches are boundary-aligned and isotropic, \(g_{D,S}\) can be made arbitrarily small per neuron (Theorem 4.3).

### Mechanism 3
**Claim:** Locality alone insufficient; weight sharing is essential to couple a filter to the global patch distribution, enabling the stability penalty to act across all locations.  
- **Mechanism:** Without sharing (LCN), each spatial location has independent filters, allowing a neuron to be active on a tiny subset at one location without affecting others. Sharing forces a filter to be compatible with patches from all locations, increasing activation mass and stability cost for memorizing hyperplanes.  
- **Core Assumption:** Patch distribution has a dominant noise cluster and rare signal clusters.  
- **Evidence:** Clustered-patch experiment: only LCN-WS generalizes; LCN and FCN memorize.  
- **Break Condition:** If patches are globally separable, sharing provides less benefit; natural patches are clustered.

## Foundational Learning

### Concept: Edge-of-Stability (BEoS)
- **Why needed:** The paper analyzes generalization via the set of parameters satisfying \(\lambda_{\max}(\nabla^2 L) \leq 2/\eta\) during large-step GD, providing a data-dependent complexity proxy without tracking dynamics.
- **Quick check:** For a trained network with \(\eta=0.1\), how would you empirically verify BEoS (e.g., via Hessian eigenvalue estimation)?

### Concept: Path Norm and Variation Semi-norm
- **Why needed:** Theorem 4.1 translates BEoS into a weighted path norm bound. The (unweighted) variation norm \(|f|_V\) characterizes minimal \(L1\) path norm over finite decompositions and controls generalization via Rademacher complexity.
- **Quick check:** Why is the integral representation (16) introduced (width-agnostic relaxation), and how does it relate to finite-width networks?

### Concept: Patch Geometry (Half-space Depth, Concentration)
- **Why needed:** The weight \(g_{D,S}(u,t)\) depends on \(P(u^T X_S^D > t)\) and conditional margin. High half-space depth means typical patches lie deep inside convex hull, making isolation hard, forcing larger \(g_{D,S}\) and stronger regularization. Low-dimensional manifolds concentrate patches, increasing depth.
- **Quick check:** For a patch cloud with PCA elbow at dimension \(k\), what does that imply about feasibility of isolating individual patches via a half-space?

## Architecture Onboarding

### Component Map
Input \(\mathbf{x}\) → Patch Extractors \(\{\pi_j\}\) → Patch Multiset \(\{\pi_j(\mathbf{x})\}_{j=1}^J\) → Shared ReLU Filters \(\{\psi_k(\mathbf{w}_k^\top \mathbf{z} - b_k)\}\) → Global Average Pooling over Locations → Linear Readout \(\sum_k v_k (\text{avg}_j \psi_k(\cdot)) + \beta\).

### Critical Path
1. **Patch extraction design:** Choose receptive field size \(m\) and stride/padding. Disjoint coordinate patches (theory) vs. overlapping (practice) affect patch statistics.
2. **Weight sharing enforcement:** Ensure filter parameters \((\mathbf{w}_k, b_k)\) are identical across all spatial locations \(j\).
3. **Stability monitoring:** Track \(\lambda_{\max}(\nabla^2 L)\) during training to confirm BEoS regime (hover near \(2/\eta\)). Also monitor neuron activation rates (fraction of patches where ReLU > 0).
4. **Regularization proxy:** Compute weighted path norm (or surrogate) using estimated \(g_{D,S}\) from batch statistics.

### Design Tradeoffs
- **\(m\) vs. \(d\):** Smaller \(m\) relative to \(d\) improves theoretical rate but may lose necessary receptive field.
- **Number of filters \(K\):** Overparameterization (\(K \gg n\)) required to approximate weighted variation norm; too few may alter dynamics.
- **Pooling strategy:** Theory uses global average pooling; max pooling or strided convs require separate analysis.

### Failure Signatures
1. **Flat interpolation:** Train loss → 0, excess risk ≈ \(\sigma^2\), activation rates near 0 (each neuron active on <2 patches). Indicates memorization via patch isolation.
2. **BEoS with poor generalization:** \(\lambda_{\max}\) saturates at \(2/\eta\), but test loss does not plateau above \(\sigma^2\). Likely adversarial patch geometry.
3. **Diverging sharpness:** \(\lambda_{\max}\) grows beyond \(2/\eta\) → instability, cannot reach BEoS.
4. **FCN comparison:** FCN should show no decay in generalization gap vs. \(n\) (slope ≈0), while LCN-WS shows negative slope.

### First 3 Experiments
1. **Synthetic spherical data sweep:** \(\mathbf{x} \sim \text{Uniform}(S^{d-1})\), \(y = f_{\text{true}}(\mathbf{x}) + \mathcal{N}(0,1)\). Vary \(n\) and \(d\) (fixed \(m=10\)). Train LCN-WS and FCN with \(\eta=0.2\). Plot \(\log(\text{gap})\) vs \(\log(n)\); slopes negative for LCN-WS (more negative with larger \(d\)), ~0 for FCN.
2. **Patch geometry audit:** Extract CIFAR-10 patches (3×3). Compute PCA explained variance curve and half-space depth concentration. Compare to uniform \(S^{m-1}\) patches; expect lower intrinsic dimension and higher depth.
3. **Clustered-patch ablation:** Synthetic distribution: one signal patch \(\{v_+,v_-\}\) per image, others Gaussian. Compare FCN, LCN (no sharing), LCN-WS. Train loss curves and activation rates; only LCN-WS should converge to noise floor.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Question 1
What is the exact implicit bias of GD under BEoS for CNNs? Which stable solution does GD converge to?  
- **Basis:** "A more detailed dynamical account of the implicit bias is left for future work." (Sec. 6)  
- **Why unresolved:** Stability set defines admissible solutions but doesn’t explain which one GD selects; crucial to understand learned features.  
- **Resolution path:** Derive dynamical analysis (e.g., continuous-time) revealing an implicit minimization principle or characterizing the limit point within BEoS set.

### Question 2
How does self-attention in Vision Transformers affect generalization under BEoS compared to CNNs?  
- **Basis:** "For an attention block in a ViT, as training set scales, what representation geometry does attention induce that allows its generalization to remain competitive with CNNs? Left for future investigation." (Sec. 6)  
- **Why unresolved:** Interplay between attention weights, patch embeddings, and stability-induced regularization unknown.  
- **Resolution path:** Derive stability-to-regularity result for attention models, expressing weighted path norm in terms of attention geometry; validate on large-scale vision data.

### Question 3
What are necessary and sufficient geometric conditions on empirical patch multiset to guarantee generalization under BEoS?  
- **Basis:** Theorem 4.3 shows BEoS alone insufficient without favorable geometry; Sec. 5.1 shows CIFAR-10 patches benefit but precise threshold unknown.  
- **Why unresolved:** Weight function \(g_{D,S}\) depends on entire cloud; we lack simple testable conditions (e.g., minimum half-space depth) ensuring non-vacuous rates.  
- **Resolution path:** Prove that if patch cloud has a certain statistic (e.g., all points have half-space depth \(\geq \tau\)) then \(g_{D,S} \geq \text{poly}(\tau)\) yielding polynomial bound; construct counterexamples when condition violated.

## Limitations
- The proven rate \(n^{-1/6+O(m/d)}\) yields empirical slopes an order of magnitude steeper (e.g., -0.86 vs -0.167), indicating bounds may be loose or unmodeled factors; confidence in exact rate is medium.
- The analysis assumes uniform hyperspherical data; natural image patches may deviate (e.g., correlation structure), limiting direct rate transfer.
- The necessity of weight sharing is shown only for specific synthetic clustered-patch distributions; extension to real-world tasks remains inferential.

## Confidence
- Weight sharing is essential for generalization under BEoS: **High** (clustered-patch ablation).
- BEoS yields weighted path norm bound translating stability to regularity: **High** (Theorem 4.1).
- Favorable patch geometry (low PCA elbow, high half-space depth) holds for CIFAR-10: **Medium** (heuristic assessment).
- Natural patches exhibit the required concentration for improved rates with \(d\): **Medium** (empirical trends support but no direct proof).

## Next Checks
1. **Rate reconciliation:** Vary \(d\) and \(m\) broadly in hyperspherical experiments, fit slopes of \(\log(\text{gap})\) vs \(\log(n)\), estimate \(O(m/d)\) term, compare with theoretical bound.
2. **Activation-rate profiling:** For all models and datasets, compute distribution of neuron activation rates; LCN-WS should show no concentration near 0 when patch geometry favorable.
3. **Adversarial geometry test:** Generate patches as unit-norm points on \(S^{m-1}\) (uniform). Train LCN-WS with large \(\eta\) to achieve BEoS; verify test error remains high despite near-zero train loss, confirming geometry necessity.
