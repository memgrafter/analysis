# Ensemble of Small Classifiers For Imbalanced White Blood Cell Classification

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID**: 2603.20856
- **Source URL**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.20856
- **Reference count**: 23
- **Primary result**: Macro F1-score of 0.67726 on the WBCBench 2026 competition test set.

## Executive Summary
Leukemia diagnosis from white blood cell (WBC) images faces severe class imbalance and domain shifts. This paper presents a lightweight ensemble of three pretrained vision transformers (SwinV2-Tiny, DinoBloom-Small, ConvNeXT-V2-Tiny) augmented with external data and a weighted sampler. Nine models are trained via stratified 3‑fold cross‑validation and combined using test‑time augmentations and logit averaging. The approach yields a macro F1‑score of 0.67726 on the official WBCBench test set, with errors concentrated among biologically similar cell types in granulopoiesis and lymphopoiesis.

## Method Summary
The authors expand the WBCBench training set (38,535 images) with three external sources—Acevedo‑20, Blood dataset, and CellWiki—totaling 51,580 images across all 13 haematopoiesis classes. For each of three backbone architectures, three models are trained on separate stratified folds, each using a four‑layer MLP classification head. Training employs α‑balanced focal loss (α=0.25, γ=2), an AdamW optimizer with cosine learning‑rate decay, exponential moving average, and a weighted random sampler defined by effective numbers (β=0.9999). Strong augmentations including cutmix and mixup are applied. At inference, test‑time augmentations (random flips and rotations) generate multiple views; logits from all nine models and views are averaged to produce the final prediction.

## Key Results
- Macro F1‑score on test set: **0.67726**.
- Primary error pattern: misclassifications occur between biologically similar classes within granulopoiesis and lymphopoiesis.
- Ensemble of nine models with TTA outperforms single‑model baselines (exact numbers not provided but implied).

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The ensemble leverages architectural diversity to capture complementary features, while lightweight backbones keep inference feasible despite the 9‑model combination. The weighted sampler and focal loss directly address class imbalance by boosting the learning signal for rare cell types. External data expansion alleviates data scarcity for minority classes and improves generalization across staining and scanner variations. Test‑time augmentations further enhance robustness to image transformations, and logit averaging smooths individual model biases. Together, these components mitigate the challenges of severe imbalance, domain shift, and biological similarity among classes, leading to strong macro‑averaged performance.

## Foundational Learning
**Imbalanced Classification**  
*Why needed*: The dataset has a long‑tail distribution (e.g., plasma cells extremely rare), causing models to ignore minority classes.  
*Quick check*: Visualize class frequencies; ensure minority classes have representative samples after expansion and sampling.

**Ensemble Learning**  
*Why needed*: Single models may overfit or have high variance; combining multiple models improves generalization and stabilizes predictions.  
*Quick check*: Train multiple models on same data; verify diversity of errors (confusion matrices differ).

**Transfer Learning with Pretrained Backbones**  
*Why needed*: Medical image datasets are relatively small; pretrained weights on large natural or medical corpora provide robust feature extractors.  
*Quick check*: Load pretrained weights (e.g., from timm) and compare convergence speed vs. random initialization.

**Focal Loss**  
*Why needed*: Down‑weights easy examples, forcing the model to focus on difficult/rare classes.  
*Quick check*: Replace cross‑entropy with focal loss and monitor per‑class F1 on validation.

**Weighted Random Sampler**  
*Why needed*: Prevents the sampler from being overwhelmed by majority classes; ensures each batch sees balanced representation.  
*Quick check*: Inspect batch class distribution; verify minority class sampling rate matches desired effective number.

**Test‑TimeAugmentation (TTA)**  
*Why needed*: Reduces sensitivity to small image transformations at inference, effectively ensembling multiple views of the same image.  
*Quick check*: Apply TTA to a single model and measure consistency of predictions across augmented views.

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component Map**  
Data (WBCBench + External) → WeightedSampler → Augmentation (train) → Model (Backbone + MLP Head) → Training (Focal Loss, AdamW, EMA) → 3‑Fold CV → Ensemble (9 models) → Inference (TTA + Logit Averaging)

**Critical Path**  
The success hinges on: (1) correct data merging and label mapping, (2) proper implementation of weighted sampling to balance batches, (3) training each model to convergence without overfitting, and (4) correct aggregation of logits during inference. Failure in any of these steps degrades the macro F1, especially for rare classes.

**Design Tradeoffs**  
- *Number of models*: Using 9 models (3 architectures × 3 folds) improves accuracy but increases training cost and inference time (~30 minutes for 16k images). Fewer models would reduce overhead but may lose performance.  
- *Uniform vs. weighted logit averaging*: Uniform averaging is simple and fair, but weighting by per‑class validation performance could further boost rare‑class recall at the cost of additional tuning.  
- *External data quality*: Adding external datasets increases sample size but introduces potential label noise and domain shift; careful mapping and filtering are needed.  
- *TTA complexity*: More augmentation views yield marginal gains but linearly increase inference time; the optimum depends on computational budget.

**Failure Signatures**  
- Poor scores on rare classes (e.g., plasma cells, prolymphocytes) in validation.  
- Large gap between training and validation macro F1 indicates overfitting.  
- Inconsistent predictions across CV folds suggest data leakage or insufficient model diversity.  
- Out‑of‑memory errors during training due to large batch size or memory‑hungry backbones; adjust batch size or use gradient accumulation.  
- Slow inference time prohibits clinical use; may require model compression.

**First Experiments**  
1. **Baseline single model**: Train one SwinV2‑Tiny on one fold without weighted sampler or TTA; record macro F1.  
2. **Weighted sampler vs. uniform**: Add weighted sampler and focal loss; compare per‑class F1 and macro score to baseline.  
3. **Ensemble and TTA**: Combine the three models from one fold (SwinV2, DinoBloom, ConvNeXT) and apply TTA; evaluate macro F1 gain over best single model.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
1. **Lineage‑specific ensembles**: Would training separate expert ensembles for granulopoiesis, lymphopoiesis, and monocytopoiesis reduce confusion between biologically similar cell types? The authors hypothesize stronger inter‑class representations could be learned, but no experiments were conducted.  
2. **Generative augmentation for rare classes**: Can GANs or diffusion models generate high‑fidelity synthetic images for extremely rare types (e.g., plasma cells, prolymphocytes) to boost their classification accuracy? The expanded dataset still shows severe imbalance; synthetic data might help but risks artifacts.  
3. **Model distillation/pruning**: Can the 9‑model ensemble be distilled into a single lightweight model that maintains the macro F1 (~0.677) while cutting inference time by ≥50%? The current 30‑minute test runtime is impractical for clinics; compression remains unexplored.  
4. **Confidence‑weighted logit averaging**: Would weighting models by validation confidence or using a meta‑learner to combine logits outperform the current uniform averaging? Uniform averaging may not reflect varying model reliability across classes.

## Limitations
- **Implementation ambiguities**: MLP head dimensions, dropout placement, TTA transforms, and weighted sampler formula are not fully specified, hindering exact replication.  
- **External label mapping**: The mapping of external dataset classes to the 13 WBCBench categories is undefined, risking label noise and domain mismatch.  
- **Potential data leakage**: Stratified splitting across expanded data must ensure no duplicate cells appear in train/val; not explicitly validated.

## Confidence
- Macro F1‑score (0.67726) on WBCBench test set: **Medium** confidence due to missing hyperparameters and potential data handling uncertainties.  
- Error pattern (confusion among similar classes): **Medium** confidence; plausible but not independently verified.  
- Overall efficacy of ensemble + data expansion + weighted sampler: **Medium** confidence; the approach is sound but lacks ablations to isolate contributions.

## Next Checks

2. **TTA parameter sweep**: Compare performance using 1, 4, and 8 augmentation views (including rotations) to find optimal trade‑off between accuracy and inference time.  
3. **External label audit**: For each external dataset, manually inspect a random sample of images per class to verify correct mapping to WBCBench taxonomy; quantify misalignment rate.
