# Exons-Detect: Identifying and Amplifying Exonic Tokens via Hidden-State Discrepancy for Robust AI-Generated Text Detection

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID**: 2603.24981
- **Source URL**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.24981
- **Reference count**: 18
- **Primary result**: Exons-Detect achieves an average AUROC of 92.14% across multiple benchmarks, outperforming DNA-DetectLLM by 2.2% relative on DetectRL, while remaining robust to paraphrasing attacks and short inputs.

## Executive Summary
Exons-Detect is a training-free AI-generated text detector that addresses the fragility of existing methods on short or manipulated texts. It introduces an "exon-aware" reweighting strategy: by comparing hidden-state representations from two proxy LLMs, it identifies tokens that carry strong discriminative signals ("exonic" tokens) and amplifies their contribution when computing a translation score. This approach yields state-of-the-art detection accuracy (92.14% average AUROC) and robustness against adversarial paraphrasing and polishing, with fast inference times under 0.8 seconds per sample.

## Method Summary
The method runs the input through two proxy LLMs (M and M̃) to extract per-token hidden states from all layers. For each token, it computes cosine discrepancy between the aggregated hidden representations from both models. Tokens whose discrepancy exceeds a threshold θ are labeled exonic. A nonlinear mapping assigns additional weight Δwₜ to exonic tokens, producing normalized weights wₜ. The final translation score R(s) combines weighted log-perplexity (under M), weighted cross-perplexity (between M and M̃), and a mutation-repair term based on an "ideal AI sequence." A threshold τ derived from validation data determines the final label.

## Key Results
- Average AUROC of 92.14% on M4, DetectRL, and RealDet benchmarks.
- Relative improvement of 2.2% over DNA-DetectLLM on DetectRL.
- Maintains high accuracy under paraphrasing and polishing attacks; robust to short inputs.
- Inference time under 0.8 seconds per sample.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- **Claim**: Tokens with high hidden-state discrepancy between two proxy LLMs carry stronger discriminative signals about text origin (AI vs. human).
- **Mechanism**: For each token, the cosine distance between its aggregated hidden representations across all layers from models *M* and *M̃* is computed (δₜ). Tokens where δₜ exceeds a threshold θ are flagged as "exonic."
- **Core assumption**: Hidden-state representations encode provenance-related signals; discrepancy magnitude correlates with token informativeness for detection.
- **Evidence anchors**:
  - [abstract]: "Exons-Detect identifies and amplifies informative exonic tokens by measuring hidden-state discrepancy under a dual-model setting."
  - [section 3.1]: "Tokens whose hidden-state discrepancy between model *M* and *M̃* exceeds a predefined discrepancy threshold θ are identified as exonic tokens."
  - [corpus]: Neighbor papers (e.g., "Not all tokens are created equal") explore token-level importance, but direct evidence for hidden-state discrepancy as a signal is not corroborated in the provided corpus.
- **Break condition**: If the proxy LLMs are highly similar or misaligned, hidden-state discrepancies may not systematically correlate with text origin, causing exonic identification to fail.

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim**: Amplifying exonic tokens via importance weighting improves detection robustness, especially for short or adversarially manipulated texts.
- **Mechanism**: A nonlinear mapping *g*(δₜ) = 1 − exp(−α(δₜ − θ)⁺) assigns additional weight Δwₜ to exonic tokens. Final normalized weights wₜ = (1 + Δwₜ) / Σ(1 + Δwᵢ) reweight token contributions in the score.
- **Core assumption**: Uniform token averaging (in baseline methods) dilutes critical signals; selective amplification enhances class separability by focusing on provenance-rich tokens.
- **Evidence anchors**:
  - [abstract]: "Exons-Detect introduces an exon-aware reweighting strategy... assigns them higher weights."
  - [section 3.4]: "To further emphasize the contribution of exonic tokens, we remap their hidden-state discrepancies into importance weights... This nonlinear mapping smoothly amplifies the weights of exonic tokens."
  - [corpus]: Weak; corpus mentions token reweighting ("Perplexity Attention Weighted Networks") but not specifically via hidden-state discrepancy.
- **Break condition**: Over-amplification (α too high) or noisy exonic identification (θ poorly set) can overweight irrelevant tokens, degrading performance on clean texts and increasing sensitivity to spurious correlations.

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim**: The translation score—a ratio of weighted log-perplexity to weighted cross-perplexity, augmented with a mutation-repair term—produces a robust, interpretable detection metric.
- **Mechanism**: Score *R(s)* = [log PPLW_M(s) + log PPLW_M(ŝ)] / log X-PPLW_{M, M̃}(s), where ŝ is the "ideal AI sequence" (max-probability tokens). The mutation-repair component captures deviation from expected AI generation patterns under the same weighting.
- **Core assumption**: AI-generated text exhibits lower relative perplexity under its own model (*M*) compared to cross-perplexity with a paired model (*M̃*), and this gap is more pronounced when exonic tokens are emphasized.
- **Evidence anchors**:
  - [abstract]: "computes an interpretable translation score from the resulting importance-weighted token sequence."
  - [section 3.5]: "We introduce a translation score by aggregating token contributions according to their weights and probability distributions... Incorporating this mechanism, the final translation score is formulated as..."
  - [section 4.2]: "Exons-Detect achieves an average AUROC of 92.14%... outperforming... DNA-DetectLLM by 1.4%."
- **Break condition**: If the proxy models *M* and *M̃* are not properly paired (e.g., vastly different architectures or training data), cross-perplexity may not meaningfully contrast AI vs. human patterns, undermining the score's discriminative power.

## Foundational Learning
- **Concept: Hidden-State Representations in Transformer LLMs**
  - *Why needed here*: Exons-Detect operates on token-level hidden states (outputs of transformer layers). Understanding that these vectors encode contextual, model-internal information is prerequisite to interpreting discrepancy as a signal.
  - *Quick check question*: What might a large hidden-state discrepancy between two LLMs at a given token position suggest about that token's role in the text's generation?

- **Concept: Perplexity as a Generative Statistic**
  - *Why needed here*: The translation score builds on log-perplexity (under *M*) and cross-perplexity (between *M* and *M̃*). These measure how "surprised" a model is by the token sequence—a core signal in training-free detection.
  - *Quick check question*: Why might AI-generated text have lower perplexity under its own model compared to human-written text?

- **Concept: Token-Level Contribution and Aggregation**
  - *Why needed here*: The method challenges the uniform aggregation baseline. Grasping that not all tokens equally indicate origin (some are "noisy") is key to understanding the reweighting innovation.
  - *Quick check question*: If only 20% of tokens are truly informative for detection, what risk does averaging over 100% of tokens pose?

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map**: Input tokenization -> concurrent forward passes through M and M̃ -> per-token hidden-state aggregation -> discrepancy calculation -> thresholding -> weight mapping -> weighted probability summation -> score computation -> threshold comparison -> output.
- **Critical path**: The sequence above; discrepancy calculation and weight mapping are central to the exon-aware reweighting.
- **Design tradeoffs**:
  - Accuracy vs. Speed: Using all 32 layers maximizes signal but doubles memory/computation vs. fewer layers.
  - Generality vs. Specificity: Method is training-free and model-agnostic, but performance varies with LLM pair choice.
  - Stability vs. Sensitivity: Threshold θ and slope α are low-sensitivity but extreme values cause failure (θ too small → mostly exonic; α too low → insufficient emphasis).
- **Failure signatures**:
  1. Collapse on short texts: If θ is too high for short sequences, few/no tokens are labeled exonic → reverts to uniform weighting → baseline performance.
  2. High variance across datasets: Indicates poor proxy model pairing; cross-perplexity distributions may not separate AI/human.
  3. Paraphrasing/polishing attack vulnerability: If g(·) is linear or α is too high, localized token changes cause large weight swings.
  4. Memory/time blowup: Processing long sequences (>1024 tokens) with 32-layer extraction may exceed GPU memory.
- **First 3 experiments**:
  1. Ablate g(·) and mutation-repair: Run with Δwₜ = δₜ (linear) and without log PPLW_M(ŝ) term. Expect ~1% and ~4% AUROC drop respectively (Table 2).
  2. Hyperparameter sweep on θ and α: For fixed LLM pair (e.g., Falcon-7B/Instruct), vary θ ∈ {0.05, 0.10, 0.15, 0.20} and α ∈ {2, 6, 10, 20} on validation set. Expect performance peak around θ=0.15, α≥10; identify failure at extremes.
  3. Cross-LLM pairing robustness: Test 4 pairings from Table 2 (Falcon, LLaMA, Mistral, LLaMA-3.2). All should exceed 90% avg AUROC, but note dataset-specific variations. If one pairing underperforms, investigate model similarity.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- **Question 1**: How can discrepancy evaluation methods be enhanced to more finely capture source-related information from hidden states for improved token importance weighting in Exons-Detect?
- **Question 2**: What principles should guide the selection of proxy LLM pairs in Exons-Detect to maximize detection accuracy and robustness across diverse text domains?
- **Question 3**: Can the hyperparameters discrepancy threshold θ and mapping slope α be automatically tuned based on input text properties to enhance adaptability and reduce manual configuration?

## Limitations
- No clear theoretical or empirical framework for selecting optimal proxy LLM pairs; performance varies across pairings.
- Memory scaling with 32-layer hidden-state extraction may limit deployment on very long contexts.
- May fail on very short texts if no tokens exceed discrepancy threshold θ, reverting to uniform weighting.
- Potential to amplify spurious correlations (e.g., domain artifacts) instead of generation origin; out-of-distribution robustness not tested.
