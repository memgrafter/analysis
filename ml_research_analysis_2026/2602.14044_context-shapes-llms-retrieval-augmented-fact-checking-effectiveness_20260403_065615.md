# Context Shapes LLMs Retrieval-Augmented Fact-Checking Effectiveness

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2602.14044
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.14044
- Reference count: 40
- Primary result: Context length and evidence placement significantly impact LLM fact-checking accuracy; mid-context evidence is less effective and longer contexts reduce accuracy, with Qwen3-32B showing robustness.

## Executive Summary
This paper examines how the length of the input context and the positioning of evidence documents influence the performance of large language models (LLMs) on retrieval-augmented fact-checking tasks. Using three fact-checking datasets—HOVER, FEVEROUS, and ClimateFEVER—the authors evaluate five models ranging from 7B to 70B parameters. They compare parametric-only performance (no external evidence) against scenarios where gold-standard evidence is inserted at various positions within prompts of 2k–16k tokens. Results indicate that LLMs possess substantial parametric knowledge, achieving up to 83% accuracy without evidence. Adding gold evidence improves accuracy by up to 20 percentage points. However, accuracy tends to decrease as context length grows, and evidence placed in the middle of the prompt consistently yields lower performance compared to evidence at the beginning or end. Notably, the Qwen3-32B model maintains performance above the parametric baseline across all tested lengths and positions, suggesting greater robustness.

## Method Summary
The study employs three fact-checking benchmarks (HOVER, FEVEROUS, ClimateFEVER) and tests five LLMs (7B–70B parameters). For each claim, the authors construct prompts by concatenating the claim with gold evidence documents, controlling for total context length (2k–16k tokens). Evidence is placed at one of three depth positions: beginning, middle, or end of the prompt. Two conditions are evaluated: (1) parametric-only (no evidence) and (2) with evidence. Performance is measured by accuracy of the model’s verdict. The experimental design isolates the effects of context length and evidence placement by fixing the evidence content and only varying its position and the amount of surrounding text (padding). The authors report accuracy scores across models, context lengths, and positions.

## Key Results
- LLMs achieve up to 83% accuracy using only parametric knowledge; adding gold evidence improves accuracy by up to 20 points.
- Accuracy declines as context length increases (2k–16k tokens) and mid-context evidence underperforms evidence at the beginning or end.
- Qwen3-32B shows robustness across all conditions, maintaining above-baseline performance.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The paper does not provide a theoretical analysis of why these effects occur. The authors present empirical trends but do not investigate underlying mechanisms such as attention dilution, positional bias, or the interplay between context length and evidence salience. Future work is needed to explain the observed patterns.

## Foundational Learning
- **Retrieval-Augmented Fact-Checking**: Combining LLM parametric knowledge with external evidence to verify claims. *Why needed:* Core paradigm of the study. *Quick check:* What is the purpose of retrieval in fact-checking?  
- **Context Length**: Total number of tokens in the input prompt. *Why needed:* Determines the amount of information the model processes; key independent variable. *Quick check:* How might increasing context length affect model performance?  
- **Evidence Placement**: Position of the relevant evidence within the context (beginning, middle, end). *Why needed:* Explores positional bias in transformer attention. *Quick check:* Why might mid-context evidence be less effective?  
- **Parametric Knowledge**: Facts stored within the model’s parameters, accessed without external evidence. *Why needed:* Baseline to measure benefit of retrieval. *Quick check:* What does “parametric-only” condition mean?  
- **Gold Evidence**: Perfectly relevant and accurate documents provided for verification. *Why needed:* Isolates the effect of evidence content without retrieval noise. *Quick check:* How does gold evidence differ from retrieved evidence?  

## Architecture Onboarding
Component map:
Claim -> Prompt Construction (with evidence at specified depth) -> LLM -> Verdict -> Accuracy Calculation

Critical path: The claim is combined with gold evidence to form a prompt of fixed length; the LLM processes the prompt and outputs a verdict; the verdict is compared to the gold label to compute accuracy.

Design tradeoffs:
- Longer contexts provide more background but risk attention dilution.
- Evidence placed at extremes may be more salient due to recency/primacy effects.
- Model size vs. robustness: larger models may have larger effective context windows but also may be more sensitive to noise.

Failure signatures:
- Sharp drop in accuracy when context length exceeds the model’s optimal window.
- Mid-context evidence consistently yields lower accuracy across models.
- Smaller models (e.g., 7B) show larger performance swings with length/position changes.

First three experiments:
1. Measure parametric-only accuracy across models to establish baseline knowledge.
2. Introduce gold evidence at beginning, middle, and end within a 4k-token context to assess positional effects.
3. Vary context length (2k, 4k, 8k, 16k) with evidence at a fixed position (e.g., beginning) to isolate length effects.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
None explicitly identified; the study focuses on empirical patterns without outlining future research questions.

## Limitations
- Limited scope: only three datasets and five models (7B-70B), restricting generalizability.
- Evidence was gold (perfect); no evaluation with realistic noisy retrieval.
- Single evaluation runs; no statistical confidence intervals provided.

## Confidence
- Parametric knowledge up to 83%: **High**
- Gold evidence gains (+20%): **High**
- Accuracy declines with longer contexts: **Medium**
- Mid-context evidence underperformance: **Medium**
- Qwen3-32B robustness: **Low** (needs replication)

## Next Checks
1. Test on additional datasets (e.g., SciFact, PubHealth) and with >70B parameter models.
2. Introduce realistic retrieval noise (e.g., top-k passages with relevance scores) to evaluate robustness of positional/length effects.
3. Perform multiple random seeds (n≥5) and report confidence intervals to assess statistical reliability.
