# MERIT: Multi-domain Efficient RAW Image Translation

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2603.20836
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.20836
- Reference count: 40
- Primary result: MERIT achieves state-of-the-art performance, improving PSNR by +5.56 dB and reducing MAE by 0.008 on average, while scaling to multiple domains with only ~58.7M parameters and 80% fewer training iterations.

## Executive Summary
MERIT presents the first unified framework for multi-domain RAW-to-RAW translation. Traditional approaches require a separate translator for each camera pair, which does not scale across many devices. MERIT addresses this by using a single generator conditioned on domain-specific style embeddings extracted from a reference RAW of the target camera. Two key technical contributions are a Sensor-Aware Noise Modeling (SANM) loss that aligns signal-dependent noise statistics between translated and real target images, and a Conditional Multi-Scale Large Kernel Attention (MS-LKA) module that enhances spatial perception and adapts features to the target domain. Evaluated on the new MDRAW dataset spanning five cameras, MERIT sets new benchmarks, reducing MAE by 0.008 and increasing PSNR by 5.56 dB over prior methods. Moreover, it demonstrates strong scalability: as the number of domains grows, its parameter count remains constant (~58.7M) and it requires far fewer training iterations (180K vs. up to 901K), while competitors scale linearly.

## Method Summary
MERIT’s architecture consists of three main components: a transformer-based style encoder that maps a reference RAW from the target domain into a low-dimensional embedding; a U-Net-like generator that incorporates MS-LKA blocks in its upsampling path to fuse multi-scale context while being modulated by the style embedding; and a patch-based discriminator that, together with the generator, is trained with adversarial, cycle-consistency, and style-reconstruction losses. The generator takes a source RAW and the target domain embedding to produce a translated RAW. The SANM loss operates by selecting low-texture patches, estimating their mean intensity and variance, binning these into histograms, and enforcing that the generated image’s noise statistics match the precomputed target domain noise profile via a masked L1 distance. The MS-LKA module aggregates features from three depthwise convolution branches with dilation rates 1, 4, and 9, then applies channel-wise attention weights derived from the style embedding. Training uses a combined loss: L_total = λ₁L_noise + λ₂L_D^adv + λ₃L_G^adv + λ₄L_cycle-L1 + λ₅L_cycle-SSIM + λ₆L_style. This unified formulation allows arbitrary source-target pairs within the same model without retraining.

## Key Results
- Average improvement: +5.56 dB PSNR, -0.008 MAE compared to baselines.
- Scalability: Constant ~58.7M parameters and 180K training iterations for 5 domains, while competitors (e.g., UVCGAN) scale to >600M parameters and >900K iterations.
- Ablation gains: Adding SANM yields +0.34 dB PSNR gain; adding MS-LKA on top yields additional +0.49 dB.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1: Explicit Sensor Noise Alignment via SANM
The SANM loss explicitly aligns the signal-dependent noise statistics (following a Poisson-Gaussian model) between generated and target-domain RAW images. It identifies low-texture patches using gradient magnitude, computes per-patch mean intensity and a robust variance estimate (MAD), bins these into histograms, and compares the generated histogram `H_fake` with a precomputed target histogram `H_real` using a masked L1 loss. This enforces that the noise variance as a function of signal intensity matches the target sensor’s statistical profile, reducing domain shift in noise characteristics. Ablation results confirm its benefit: adding SANM improves average PSNR from 32.37 to 32.71 dB and reduces MAE from 0.0221 to 0.0210.

### Mechanism 2: Multi-Scale Domain-Adaptive Feature Aggregation via MS-LKA
MS-LKA captures both fine local details and long-range spatial dependencies through three parallel depthwise convolution branches with dilation rates 1, 4, and 9. The fused features are modulated by channel-wise attention weights generated from the target domain style embedding via a feed-forward network. This allows the generator to emphasize domain-specific channels (e.g., noise patterns, color response) adaptively. The multi-scale design addresses variations in spatial patterns across cameras, such as local demosaicing artifacts and global illumination gradients. Ablation shows that adding MS-LKA on top of SANM further increases PSNR to 33.20 dB.

### Mechanism 3: Unified Multi-Domain Conditioning with Style Embeddings
A single generator can learn mappings across arbitrary camera pairs by conditioning on domain-specific style embeddings. The style encoder extracts an embedding from any reference RAW of the target domain. During training, random source-target pairs from all domains are processed jointly. This disentangles domain-invariant content (preserved by the backbone) from domain-variant "style" (noise, color, tone) captured in the embedding. The approach scales gracefully: increasing the number of domains does not increase model size or training cost, as evidenced by constant parameters and iterations in experiments.

## Foundational Learning
### Conditional GANs & Cycle Consistency
**Why needed:** MERIT builds on CycleGAN’s adversarial training and cycle consistency to learn unsupervised mappings. Cycle losses (`L_cycle-L1`, `L_cycle-SSIM`) preserve source-domain content (geometry, layout) during translation, preventing the generator from ignoring the input and collapsing to a target-domain prior.  
**Quick check:** Why is a simple L1 cycle loss insufficient for RAW translation, necessitating an SSIM term?

### Poisson-Gaussian Noise Model
**Why needed:** RAW sensor noise comprises signal-dependent shot noise (Poisson) and signal-independent read noise (Gaussian). SANM explicitly aligns this physical model across domains; ignoring it yields unrealistic noise patterns in translated RAWs.  
**Quick check:** How does SANM’s flat-patch selection ensure the computed variance reflects sensor noise rather than scene texture?

### Style-Based Modulation & Domain Embeddings
**Why needed:** To scale to more than two domains, the model must condition on a target domain identifier. Style encoders (inspired by StarGAN v2, UVCGAN) extract domain statistics from a reference image; MS-LKA uses these to modulate channels, enabling sensor-aware feature scaling.

## Architecture Onboarding
### Component map
Style Encoder E -> style embedding s_b -> Generator G -> translated RAW Î_b; Discriminator D -> patch scores; Loss Orchestrator -> total loss = L_noise + L_adv + L_cycle + L_style.

### Critical path for inference
Source RAW `I_a` and a cached style embedding `s_b` (from a reference target RAW) are fed into generator `G` to produce `Î_b`. The style encoder can be run once per target domain to precompute and store `s_b`.

### Design tradeoffs
- **SANM complexity vs. reliability:** Histogram binning and flat-patch detection add computational overhead but are crucial for noise fidelity. If scenes lack flat areas, the SANM gradient weakens; too many bins yields noisy estimates.
- **MS-LKA receptive field vs. efficiency:** Larger dilation rates capture broader context but may blur fine details. Fixed dilations (1,4,9) offer a compromise; adaptive rates per dataset could improve results at the cost of tuning.
- **Shared generator capacity:** A single model risks underfitting highly diverse domains. Style conditioning allows specialization, but extreme heterogeneity may demand more capacity or a mixture of experts.

### Failure signatures
- **High SANM loss:** Noise histograms of generated images fail to match target profiles, visible as unnatural grain (over-smoothed or excessively noisy outputs).
- **Style collapse:** Style embeddings from different domains become nearly identical, causing the generator to ignore target style and produce average-domain outputs.
- **Cycle inconsistency:** `L_cycle` remains high after translation and back-translation, indicating content distortion; may require stronger cycle constraints.
- **Discriminator saturation:** D loss collapses to 0 or 1, often because G cannot produce realistic samples; adjust adversarial weights, increase capacity, or use regularization.
- **MS-LKA ineffectiveness:** No performance improvement over baseline; check that style modulation is active and that dilation rates are appropriate.

## Limitations
- Missing architectural details: generator backbone configuration, style encoder transformer parameters, MS-LKA kernel size, discriminator specifics, exact SANM hyperparameters (flat-patch threshold, histogram bins), and loss weighting coefficients (λ₁-λ₆) limit faithful replication.
- SANM's reliance on flat patches may fail in highly textured scenes; the reported PSNR gain of +0.34 dB is modest, suggesting limited robustness.
- Style collapse risk if the style encoder is not trained on sufficiently diverse references; extreme domain heterogeneity may exceed the capacity of a single unified generator.
