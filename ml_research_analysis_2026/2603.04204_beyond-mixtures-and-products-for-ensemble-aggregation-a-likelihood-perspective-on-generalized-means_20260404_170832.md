# Beyond Mixtures and Products for Ensemble Aggregation: A Likelihood Perspective on Generalized Means

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2603.04204
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.04204
- **Reference count:** 40
- **Primary result:** The normalized generalized mean of order \(r\) unifies linear (\(r=1\)) and geometric (\(r=0\)) pooling; only \(r \in [0,1]\) guarantees that the aggregated log-likelihood is at least the average of individual models for all data points, explaining the reliability of these common methods.

## Executive Summary
This paper investigates density aggregation for ensembles through the lens of the normalized generalized mean (power mean). It provides a theoretical guarantee that for any ensemble of predictive densities, choosing an aggregation order \(r\) within \([0,1]\) ensures the aggregated model's log-likelihood never falls below the average individual log-likelihood on any data point—a formal "wisdom of crowds" effect. This result explains why linear (\(r=1\)) and geometric (\(r=0\)) pooling are consistently effective. For \(r\) outside this interval, the authors prove aggregation can actively degrade performance, identifying distinct failure modes for negative (pessimistic) and large positive (optimistic) orders. Empirical evaluations on image and text classification tasks confirm a U-shaped relationship between test cross-entropy and \(r\), with optimal performance typically within or near \([0,1]\), and show variance reduction across all \(r\).

## Method Summary
The paper studies aggregating \(k\) predictive densities \(p^{(i)}(x)\) via the normalized generalized mean of order \(r\):
\[
\circ p_{k,r}(x) = \frac{M_{k,r}(x)}{Z_{k,r}}, \quad M_{k,r}(x) = \left( \frac{1}{k} \sum_{i=1}^k [p^{(i)}(x)]^r \right)^{1/r}, \quad Z_{k,r} = \int M_{k,r}(x) \,dx.
\]
For \(r=0\), this reduces to the geometric mean. The core evaluation metric is the log-likelihood \(\log \circ p_{k,r}(x)\). Theoretically, the authors prove that \(\log \circ p_{k,r}(x) \ge \frac{1}{k}\sum_{i=1}^k \log p^{(i)}(x)\) for all \(x\) holds **if and only if** \(r \in [0,1]\). Empirically, they train multiple deep ensembles (10 models each) on CIFAR-100, DermaMNIST, and IMDb, sweeping \(r\) over a wide range and measuring test cross-entropy and variance. Computation uses a stable log-domain implementation to avoid numerical issues.

## Key Results
- The interval \(r \in [0,1]\) is the **only** range guaranteeing the aggregated log-likelihood is at least the average individual log-likelihood for every data point, providing a theoretical foundation for the success of linear and geometric pooling.
- For \(r < 0\), aggregation can catastrophically underperform at points of strong model disagreement, as the power mean behaves like a smooth minimum.
- For \(r > 1\), aggregation can degrade performance at points of strong model agreement (consensus), because the normalization constant \(Z_{k,r} > 1\) shifts probability mass away.
- Empirically, test cross-entropy follows a U-shaped curve across \(r\), with minima inside or near \([0,1]\) on all tested datasets; extreme \(r\) (e.g., \(-5\), \(5\), \(10\), \(\pm\infty\)) worsen performance.
- All values of \(r\) reduce the variance of ensemble predictions relative to individual models, with stronger reduction for \(r \ge 0\).

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- **Claim:** For any positive densities and any data point, the aggregated log-likelihood under the normalized generalized mean of order \(r\) is at least the average individual log-likelihood if and only if \(r \in [0,1]\).
- **Mechanism:** The log-likelihood is \(\log \circ p_{k,r}(x) = \log M_{k,r}(x) - \log Z_{k,r}\). For \(0 \le r \le 1\), Jensen's inequality applied to the concave \(\log\) function yields \(\log M_{k,r}(x) \ge \frac{1}{k}\sum_{i=1}^k \log p^{(i)}(x)\). Simultaneously, Proposition 3.1 shows \(\log Z_{k,r} \le 0\) for \(r \le 1\). The combination guarantees the desired inequality.
- **Core assumption:** All densities \(p^{(i)}(x) > 0\) for all \(x\).
- **Evidence anchors:** Theorem 3.1 in the paper provides the formal proof; the abstract states the [0,1] guarantee.
- **Break condition:** Fails for \(r \notin [0,1]\). Breakdown occurs at strong disagreement (\(r < 0\)) or strong agreement (\(r > 1\)) points.

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim:** For \(r > 1\), the aggregated log-likelihood can be worse than the average at consensus points where all models assign high probability to the true outcome.
- **Mechanism:** While Jensen still gives \(\log M_{k,r}(x) \ge \frac{1}{k}\sum \log p^{(i)}(x)\), the normalization constant satisfies \(Z_{k,r} > 1\) for non-identical densities. This makes \(\log Z_{k,r} > 0\), so subtracting it can make \(\log \circ p_{k,r}(x)\) fall below the average log-density.
- **Core assumption:** Ensembles have diversity; individual densities are not almost everywhere equal.
- **Evidence anchors:** Section 3.3 and Appendix C.1 present a two-Gaussian counterexample where \(\log \circ p_{2,r}(0) < \log p^{(i)}(0)\) for \(r>1\) at the consensus point \(x=0\).
- **Break condition:** Occurs when \(r > 1\) and there is a region where multiple (but not all) models concentrate probability.

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim:** For \(r < 0\), the aggregated log-likelihood can be worse than the average at disagreement points where one model assigns very low probability.
- **Mechanism:** Negative \(r\) causes the power mean to behave like a smooth minimum. If one density \(p^{(j)}(x)\) is much larger than another \(p^{(i)}(x)\), then \([p^{(i)}(x)]^r\) becomes huge relative to \([p^{(j)}(x)]^r\), dragging \(M_{k,r}(x)\) down. This can overwhelm the Jensen bound, causing \(\log M_{k,r}(x)\) to drop below the average log-density.
- **Core assumption:** There exists at least one point with orders-of-magnitude density differences among models.
- **Evidence anchors:** Section 3.3 and Appendix C.1 analyze the two-Gaussian case at \(x=m\) (mode of one, tail of the other), showing the gap tends to \(-\infty\) as separation grows for \(r<0\).
- **Break condition:** Triggered when \(r < 0\) and the ensemble exhibits strong, asymmetric disagreement.

## Foundational Learning
- **Probability densities and log-likelihood:**  
  *Why needed:* The entire analysis is framed in terms of log-likelihood of predictive densities, the standard criterion for evaluating probabilistic models (e.g., cross-entropy loss).  
  *Quick check:* Why does minimizing negative log-likelihood correspond to maximizing the probability of the observed data under the model?
- **Generalized (power) mean:**  
  *Why needed:* The aggregation operator \(\circ p_{k,r}\) is defined as the normalized power mean. Understanding its monotonicity in \(r\) and limiting cases (\(r \to \pm\infty\) yield min/max) is essential to interpret results.  
  *Quick check:* What are the limits of the power mean as \(r \to -\infty\) and as \(r \to +\infty\)?
- **Jensen's inequality and concavity of \(\log\):**  
  *Why needed:* The key theorem's proof relies on Jensen's inequality applied to the concave \(\log\) function to lower-bound \(\log M_{k,r}(x)\).  
  *Quick check:* For a concave function \(f\), what is the direction of Jensen's inequality: \(f(\sum w_i x_i) \ge \text{or} \le \sum w_i f(x_i)\)?
- **Normalization of unnormalized densities:**  
  *Why needed:* The power mean \(M_{k,r}(x)\) is not necessarily normalized; dividing by \(Z_{k,r} = \int M_{k,r}(x)dx\) yields a valid density. The sign and magnitude of \(\log Z_{k,r}\) directly affect the final log-likelihood.  
  *Quick check:* If a function \(f(x)\) integrates to a constant \(c \neq 1\), what operation turns it into a probability density?
- **Deep ensembles:**  
  *Why needed:* The empirical validation uses deep ensembles as the source of predictive densities. Understanding that they produce approximate Bayesian posteriors via finite model averaging is context.  
  *Quick check:* How do deep ensembles typically reduce variance and improve calibration compared to a single model?

## Architecture Onboarding
Input models (k predictive densities) -> Aggregation core (compute M_{k,r}(x)) -> Normalization (compute Z_{k,r}) -> Output aggregated density.

Critical path: The log-likelihood improvement for \(r \in [0,1]\) hinges on two properties holding simultaneously: (a) Jensen's inequality provides a non-negative bound on \(\log M_{k,r}(x) - \frac{1}{k}\sum \log p^{(i)}(x)\), and (b) \(\log Z_{k,r} \le 0\). For \(r \notin [0,1]\), either (a) reverses sign (\(r<0\)) or (b) becomes positive (\(r>1\)), breaking the guarantee.

Design tradeoffs:
- Safety vs optimality: \(r \in [0,1]\) guarantees non-worsening log-likelihood pointwise (safety). However, empirical optimum may lie slightly outside (e.g., \(r \approx 1.4\)), offering potential average gains at the risk of localized failures.
- Optimism-pessimism axis: \(r\) controls emphasis on high-density regions (\(r>1\), optimistic) versus low-density regions (\(r<0\), pessimistic).
- Variance reduction: All \(r\) reduce prediction variance, but stronger reduction occurs for \(r \ge 0\).

Failure signatures:
- \(r < 0\): Catastrophic drop on data points where at least one model assigns very low probability to the true label. Manifests as sensitivity to minority classes or extreme disagreement.
- \(r > 1\): Localized degradation around points where models strongly agree on the true label, due to normalization mass being diverted to regions of higher disagreement.
- Numerical instability: Extreme \(|r|\) causes underflow/overflow in \([p^{(i)}(x)]^r\). Always use log-domain computation.

First 3 experiments:
1. Synthetic Gaussian sanity check: Two 1D Gaussians \(N(-m,1)\) and \(N(m,1)\). Plot NLL vs \(r\) for data from \(N(0,\sigma^2)\). Expect U-shape with minimum in \([0,1]\); verify failure at \(x=m\) for \(r<0\) and at \(x=0\) for \(r>1\).
2. Controlled near-consensus experiment: Duplicate a trained classifier and slightly perturb its logits on non-true-class dimensions. This should make \(r>1\) harmful, illustrating the consensus-failure mode.
3. Real dataset sweep (CIFAR-100): Train 10-model Deep Ensembles (WideResNet-28-10). Sweep \(r\) on test cross-entropy; verify U-shaped curve with all \(r \in [0,1]\) below individual model uncertainty band.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- How can we determine the optimal aggregation order \(r\) for a given ensemble and dataset? The paper only shows the U-shaped curve and notes empirical optima may exceed 1, but offers no principled selection method.
- Does dataset imbalance systematically influence the optimal \(r\)? The paper observes arithmetic mean (\(r=1\)) appears less favorable on the imbalanced DermaMNIST, aligning with prior work favoring logarithmic pooling on long-tailed data, but this is based on a single dataset.
- Can the optimal aggregation order \(r\) be learned directly from data within the generalized mean framework? The paper does not explore adaptive \(r\); related work learns parameters for other divergences.
- How does the diversity of ensemble members affect the optimal \(r\)? Ensembles in the study have diversity from random initialization only; systematic variation (e.g., different architectures) is not examined.
- Beyond log-likelihood, how does \(r\) affect classification accuracy, calibration error, and robustness to distribution shift? The evaluation is solely on test cross-entropy; impact on other practical metrics is unknown.

## Limitations
- Theoretical guarantee is pointwise and requires strictly positive densities; it does not guarantee lower *average* NLL, and empirical optima can lie outside [0,1], indicating potential average gains from localized violations.
- Evaluation is limited to image and text classification on three datasets; generality to regression, generative modeling, or Bayesian ensembles is unclear.
- Reproducibility is hindered by unspecified training hyperparameters (exact learning rates, augmentation details) and preprocessing choices, which could shift the NLL‑\(r\) curve and affect ensemble diversity.

## Confidence
- Theorem 3.1 (characterization of safe \(r\)): **High** (rigorous proof under clear assumptions).
- Empirical U‑shaped cross‑entropy curve: **Medium** (consistent across three datasets but limited task diversity).
- Variance reduction across all \(r\): **Low** (observed empirically, not systematically analyzed).
- Failure modes for \(r \notin [0,1]\): **High** (supported by both theory and counterexamples).

## Next Checks
1. Out‑of‑distribution (OOD) NLL: Evaluate aggregated models on OOD data (e.g., ImageNet for CIFAR‑100‑trained ensembles) to test whether failure modes for \(r<0\)/\(r>1\) are exacerbated under distribution shift.
2. Small ensembles: Repeat key experiments with only 2–3 models per ensemble to verify the U‑shape persists and whether the pointwise guarantee still yields average NLL improvements with fewer models.
3. Calibration analysis: Generate reliability diagrams and compute expected calibration error (ECE) across \(r\) values, especially for \(r>1\), to link theoretical pointwise properties to uncertainty quantification quality.
