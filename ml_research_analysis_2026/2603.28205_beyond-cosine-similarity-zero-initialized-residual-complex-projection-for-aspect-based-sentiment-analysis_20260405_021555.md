# Beyond Cosine Similarity: Zero-Initialized Residual Complex Projection for Aspect-Based Sentiment Analysis

## Quick Facts
*   **arXiv ID:** 2603.28205
*   **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.28205
*   **Reference Count:** 24
*   **Primary Result:** Achieves state-of-the-art Macro-F1 of 0.8851 on the ASAP dataset by expanding the inter-polarity discriminative margin by over 50% via complex-space disentanglement and contrastive false-negative correction.

## Executive Summary
This paper introduces a framework for Aspect-Based Sentiment Analysis (ABSA) that tackles two core problems: the entanglement of aspect-specific semantics with subjective sentiment polarity, and the presence of false-negative collisions in standard contrastive learning. The proposed solution features a Zero-Initialized Residual Complex Projection (ZRCP) that maps text embeddings into a complex space, using phase to represent sentiment polarity and amplitude for intensity. An anti-collision mask is applied within a contrastive loss to prevent the erroneous repulsion of samples sharing the same aspect and sentiment. Evaluated on a fine-grained Chinese restaurant review dataset (ASAP), the model sets a new state-of-the-art in Macro-F1. Ablation studies confirm the contribution of both the complex projection and the masking mechanism, while a geometric analysis reveals that constraining amplitude harms the representation of subjective intensity.

## Method Summary
The framework builds upon a RoBERTa backbone. For each aspect-specific text segment, the [CLS] token embedding is passed through the ZRCP module, which uses two zero-initialized residual linear layers to split the representation into real (aspect) and imaginary (polarity) components, forming a complex-valued vector. The training objective combines three losses: (1) a masked in-batch negative loss that applies a binary mask to prevent treating samples with identical aspect and sentiment labels as negatives, (2) an angle loss that explicitly maximizes the phase difference between opposing polarities, and (3) a standard cosine similarity loss for stability. This design encourages phase to encode polarity separation while allowing unconstrained amplitude to capture expressive intensity. The model is trained with standard optimizers and evaluated using Macro-F1 and Accuracy.

## Key Results
*   Achieves a state-of-the-art Macro-F1 score of **0.8851** on the ASAP dataset with 18 fine-grained aspects.
*   Expands the **inter-polarity discriminative margin by over 50%** compared to baselines, while preserving intra-polarity cohesion.
*   Ablation studies confirm that both the ZRCP projection and the anti-collision mask are critical components, with their removal causing significant performance drops.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1
*   **Claim:** Zero-Initialized Residual Complex Projection (ZRCP) disentangles objective aspect semantics and subjective sentiment polarity by mapping real-valued embeddings into a complex space where phase encodes polarity and amplitude encodes intensity.
*   **Mechanism:** ZRCP uses zero-initialized residual linear layers to split the RoBERTa hidden state into real (aspect) and imaginary (polarity) components, preserving pre-trained continuity while learning a disentangling delta. The complex phase (angle) is optimized to separate opposing sentiments, while unconstrained amplitude naturally captures expressive intensity.
*   **Core Assumption:** Complex geometry provides orthogonal degrees of freedom (phase vs. amplitude) that align with the orthogonal nature of aspect (what is talked about) and sentiment (how it is talked about) in fine-grained ABSA.
*   **Evidence Anchors:**
    *   Abstract statement on utilizing phase for polarity and amplitude for intensity.
    *   Section 4.2 describes zero-initialized residual projection and its role in smooth disentanglement.
    *   Corpus support is weak; neighboring papers focus on general ABSA or multimodal sentiment, not complex-space disentanglement.
*   **Break Condition:** If phase-amplitude decoupling fails empirically (e.g., ablation shows no gain over real-valued baselines), the core geometric assumption is invalid.

### Mechanism 2
*   **Claim:** Anti-collision Masked Angle Loss resolves false-negative collisions in contrastive learning by dynamically masking intra-polarity pairs within the batch, preventing erroneous repulsion of same-aspect/same-sentiment samples.
*   **Mechanism:** A binary mask matrix zeros out the negative log-likelihood penalty for pairs sharing identical aspect and sentiment labels in the in-batch negative loss. This preserves intra-polarity cohesion while the angle loss expands inter-polarity margin.
*   **Core Assumption:** Standard contrastive learning's instance discrimination objective is mismatched to fine-grained ABSA, where multiple sentences share identical aspect-sentiment pairs and should not be pushed apart.
*   **Evidence Anchors:**
    *   Abstract reference to the mask preserving intra-polarity cohesion and expanding margin.
    *   Section 4.3 formal definition of masked in-batch negative loss.
    *   Corpus evidence is absent; neighbors discuss contrastive learning for ABSA but not false-negative collisions via masking.
*   **Break Condition:** If the mask inadvertently suppresses useful inter-class variation (e.g., different expressions of "positive taste"), cohesion gains trade off with discriminative power.

### Mechanism 3
*   **Claim:** Explicitly penalizing complex amplitude catastrophically over-regularizes subjective representations because amplitude encodes intensity and lexical richness of subjective descriptions, not just objective aspect.
*   **Mechanism:** Adding an amplitude penalty forces vectors of the same aspect to have identical magnitudes. This collapses semantic variance for subjective aspects (e.g., "very good" vs. "somewhat good") where intensity differences are critical.
*   **Core Assumption:** Amplitude is a meaningful channel for subjective intensity variance; constraining it removes essential signal for fine-grained sentiment.
*   **Evidence Anchors:**
    *   Abstract statement on catastrophic over-regularization.
    *   Section 5.6 ablation with AmpLoss shows performance drops for subjective aspects (Decoration, Taste) while minor gains occur for factual aspects (Discount, Price Level).
    *   Geometric analysis linking amplitude to sentiment intensity, not text length.
*   **Break Condition:** If amplitude variance is shown to be noise rather than signal, unconstrained amplitude might be suboptimal.

## Foundational Learning
*   **Concept:** Complex number geometry (phase/amplitude).
    *   *Why needed here:* The entire ZRCP design relies on mapping sentiment to phase (angular separation) and intensity to amplitude (magnitude). Misunderstanding this leads to incorrect implementation.
    *   *Quick check question:* If two sentences have opposite sentiments about the same aspect, what should their phase difference be in the ideal complex space? (Answer: near π radians).
*   **Concept:** Contrastive learning false negatives.
    *   *Why needed here:* Standard in-batch negatives treat all non-query samples as negatives. In ABSA, same-aspect/same-sentiment samples are false negatives; without masking, they harm cohesion.
    *   *Quick check question:* Why does masking same-polarity pairs *within an aspect* not collapse the aspect cluster? (Answer: Aspect is encoded in real component; masking preserves intra-aspect polarity cohesion while inter-polarity margin is expanded via angle loss.)
*   **Concept:** Residual connections and zero initialization.
    *   *Why needed here:* ZRCP starts as an identity mapping to avoid catastrophic degradation from random complex projection; residual deltas learn disentanglement gradually.
    *   *Quick check question:* What would happen if ZRCP projections were randomly initialized instead of zero-initialized? (Answer: Likely immediate spatial collapse or entanglement, losing pre-trained semantic structure.)

## Architecture Onboarding
*   **Component Map:** Input text -> Context-Aware Aspect Extraction -> RoBERTa encoder ([CLS] token h) -> ZRCP (zero-init residual split into real/imaginary) -> Complex embedding z -> Combined Losses (Masked In-Batch Negative, Angle, Cosine).
*   **Critical Path:** 1) Extract aspect-specific context windows. 2) Encode segments with RoBERTa to get [CLS] embedding. 3) Apply ZRCP to obtain complex representation. 4) Compute masked contrastive loss (Libn) using mask M derived from aspect-sentiment labels. 5) Compute angle loss (Langle) on phase difference after amplitude normalization. 6) Combine with cosine loss (Lcos) and train.
*   **Design Tradeoffs:**
    *   ZRCP residual vs. hard chunking: residual preserves pre-trained semantics but slightly lowers intra-polarity cohesion for greater inter-polarity margin.
    *   Mask vs. no mask: mask rescues high-frequency aspects but may slightly reduce intra-polarity similarity (acceptable trade-off).
    *   Loss weights (1.0, 1.0, 0.1) balance stability and disentanglement.
*   **Failure Signatures:**
    *   Without mask: false-negative collisions cause spatial collapse, visible as chaotic similarity matrices.
    *   Without ZRCP (hard chunking): pre-trained continuity broken, performance drops.
    *   With amplitude penalty (AmpLoss): subjective aspect performance drops, amplitude distribution collapses.
*   **First 3 Experiments:**
    1.  Ablate ZRCP: replace with hard chunking (direct bisection), measure Macro-F1 drop.
    2.  Ablate anti-collision mask: remove mask matrix M from Libn, evaluate on high-frequency aspects like Taste.
    3.  Validate amplitude hypothesis: add AmpLoss, measure ΔF1 on subjective vs. objective aspects.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
*   Should explicit amplitude penalties be enforced to coerce identical magnitudes for identical aspects? The paper shows a uniform penalty harms subjective representations but does not explore nuanced, aspect-adaptive constraints.
*   What is the internal mechanism by which complex amplitude naturally encodes semantic intensity and subjectivity without explicit supervision? The paper empirically links amplitude to subjectivity but does not explain the training dynamics.
*   Does the proposed ZRCP and anti-collision mask framework generalize effectively beyond Chinese restaurant reviews to other languages, domains, or aspect taxonomies? Experiments are limited to a single Chinese dataset.
*   Is zero-initialized residual projection optimal, or could alternative initialization schemes (identity, random, aspect-aware) improve disentanglement and convergence? No comparative ablation over initialization strategies is provided.

## Limitations
*   Missing critical implementation details (exact mask formulation, triplet generation thresholds, ZRCP chunk dimension).
*   The ASAP dataset is not publicly referenced, preventing independent replication.
*   The complex-space disentanglement relies on internal geometric analysis without external corroboration.
*   The over-regularization claim is based on a few aspects; other subjective domains remain unexplored.

## Confidence
*   ZRCP disentangles aspect semantics and sentiment polarity via phase/amplitude: **Medium** (ablation gains positive, but core geometric assumption untested).
*   Anti-collision mask resolves false-negative collisions: **Medium** (mask effect evident, but precise formulation ambiguous).
*   Amplitude penalty catastrophically harms subjective representations: **High** (consistent drop on Taste/Decoration).
*   Reported Macro-F1 0.8851 on ASAP: **Low** (dataset inaccessible, no external verification).
