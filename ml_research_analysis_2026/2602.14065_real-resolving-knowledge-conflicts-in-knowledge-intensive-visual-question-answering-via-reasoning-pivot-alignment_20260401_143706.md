# REAL: Resolving Knowledge Conflicts in Knowledge-Intensive Visual Question Answering via Reasoning-Pivot Alignment

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2602.14065
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.14065
- **Reference count:** 40
- **Primary result:** REAL achieves state-of-the-art accuracy on E-VQA (+3.8%) and InfoSeek (+1.6%) by redefining and resolving knowledge conflicts at the reasoning-pivot level.

## Executive Summary
Knowledge-intensive visual question answering (KI-VQA) requires models to answer questions by reasoning over retrieved external knowledge, but noisy or contradictory passages often lead to failures. This paper introduces REAL, a framework that redefines and resolves knowledge conflicts at the level of reasoning-pivots—atomic logical units essential for answering a query. REAL comprises two components: Reasoning-Pivot Aware Supervised Fine-Tuning (RPA-SFT) trains a discriminator to align conflict detection with explicit pivot extraction, and Reasoning-Pivot Guided Decoding (RPGD) applies contrastive decoding at inference to suppress conflict-aligned token probabilities. Experiments on E-VQA and InfoSeek show REAL achieves state-of-the-art accuracy, with absolute gains of +3.8% and +1.6% respectively. Moreover, conflict discrimination improves by 14.68% on average over base models, with F1 scores above 95% on the REAL-VQA test set. The approach demonstrates that precise, pivot-based conflict handling significantly advances KI-VQA reliability.

REAL's contributions include a novel definition of reasoning-pivots, a multi-stage supervised fine-tuning recipe that avoids shortcut learning, and a training-free decoding strategy that leverages contrastive principles. The method's strong empirical results across multiple benchmarks highlight its potential for real-world KI-VQA applications where reliable evidence integration is critical.

## Method Summary
REAL operates in two stages. First, Reasoning-Pivot Aware Supervised Fine-Tuning (RPA-SFT) trains a multimodal large language model (e.g., Qwen3-VL) on the REAL-VQA dataset. This dataset annotates reasoning-pivots—atomic units such as entities or relations—in both questions and retrieved passages. The model is fine-tuned to extract these pivots using special tokens `<RPivot>` and `</RPivot>`, producing three-stage outputs: question pivots, passage pivots, and a binary conflict label. This multi-stage supervision forces the model to learn logical verification rather than superficial pattern matching.

Second, at inference time, Reasoning-Pivot Guided Decoding (RPGD) is applied to the fine-tuned model without additional training. For each token generation step, RPGD runs two forward passes: one with the original image and one with randomly shuffled image patch embeddings. The shuffled pass generates conflict-dominant logits. Using the discriminator's identification of pivot tokens from the first two stages, an adaptive gate α is computed per token based on the conflict logits. The standard logits are then projected onto the conflict direction via Gram-Schmidt orthogonalization, and the scaled projection is subtracted: `L_final = L_std - α ⊙ L_proj`. Finally, a cutoff τ filters extreme values before sampling. This contrastive process suppresses tokens that align with contradictory evidence while preserving correct reasoning pathways.

## Key Results
- State-of-the-art accuracy on E-VQA with an absolute gain of +3.8% over prior methods.
- State-of-the-art accuracy on InfoSeek with an absolute gain of +1.6%.
- Conflict discrimination (F1) improves by 14.68% on average over base models, exceeding 95% on REAL-VQA.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1
- **Claim:** Redefining knowledge conflicts at the reasoning-pivot level (atomic nodes/edges in a reasoning chain) yields more precise conflict detection than entity/keyword mismatches.
- **Mechanism:** A reasoning-pivot is an indispensable unit for answering a query. A conflict exists only when external evidence provides mutually exclusive assertions about the same pivot (e.g., differing nationalities for the same artist). This filters out irrelevant variations (e.g., location differences when the question targets nationality).
- **Core assumption:** That KI-VQA failures are primarily caused by contradictions on these specific pivots rather than general noise. Model reasoning can be decomposed into a discrete set of such pivots.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract]: "REAL defines reasoning-pivots as atomic logical units in a reasoning chain and resolves conflicts..."
  - [section 3.2]: Formalizes Reasoning-Pivot Conflict with Equation 1: `Kconflict = {u∈P | ∃a_i, a_j ∈ I_u, s.t. a_i ∧ a_j → ⊥}`.
  - [corpus]: The corpus shows prior work (e.g., entity mismatch, keyword mismatch) but does not provide direct evidence for this redefinition; this is an internal conceptual shift proposed by the paper.
- **Break condition:** If the reasoning chain cannot be correctly segmented into discrete pivots, or if answer errors stem from non-pivot-related issues (e.g., visual misperception).

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim:** RPA-SFT trains a generalizable conflict discriminator by aligning conflict detection with explicit reasoning-pivot extraction, avoiding "shortcut learning."
- **Mechanism:** The model is fine-tuned on REAL-VQA (with pivot annotations) to perform a three-stage process: 1) extract question pivots, 2) locate paragraph pivots, 3) verify conflict by comparing pivot consistency. Special tokens (`<RPivot>`) anchor attention to pivot spans.
- **Core assumption:** That multi-stage supervision on pivot extraction forces the model to learn logical verification rather than dataset-specific artifacts.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract]: "RPA-SFT trains a discriminator to align conflicts with pivot extraction."
  - [section 4.2]: Details token-level pivot perception and multi-stage reasoning training. Table 4 shows RPA-SFT achieves high Reasoning-Pivot F1 (e.g., 79.4% on Qwen3-VL-8B), unlike few-shot.
  - [corpus]: No direct external validation; performance is measured on internal and external benchmarks (E-VQA, ScienceQA, MMKC).
- **Break condition:** If pivot extraction is consistently inaccurate (low Rea. F1), conflict discrimination will degrade. Performance depends on the quality of REAL-VQA pivot annotations.

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim:** RPGD mitigates conflicts during inference by contrasting standard logits with conflict-dominant logits (induced via patch shuffle), selectively suppressing pivot-token probabilities using an adaptive gate and Gram-Schmidt orthogonalization.
- **Mechanism:** Two forward passes: standard (original image) and conflict-dominant (shuffled patches). The latter's logits are projected onto the standard logits to isolate conflict-aligned components. An adaptive gate (based on the discriminator's confidence on pivot tokens) scales the suppression strength before subtraction: `L_final = L_std - α ⊙ L_proj`.
- **Core assumption:** That patch shuffle sufficiently disrupts visual verification to force reliance on textual conflicts, and that the discriminator's pivot identification accurately highlights conflict-relevant tokens.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract]: "RPGD uses contrastive decoding with patch shuffle and adaptive gating to mitigate conflicts."
  - [section 4.3 & Algorithm 1]: Details the three components. Table 6 ablations show each component (Patch Shuffle, Adaptive Gating, Gram-Schmidt) contributes to performance.
  - [corpus]: The corpus lists related contrastive decoding methods (CAD, VCD) but RPGD is a novel variant; its superiority is shown in Table 7.
- **Break condition:** If patch shuffle fails to construct a valid conflict pathway, or if the adaptive gate mis-targets non-pivot tokens, valid reasoning may be suppressed. Gram-Schmidt may also over-remove shared structure.

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component map:** Data Processing -> RPA-SFT -> RPGD Inference

**Critical path:**
- *Training:* Prepare REAL-VQA → Initialize model with `<RPivot>` tokens → Multi-stage SFT on (image, question, passages, pivot labels, conflict label) → Save checkpoint.
- *Inference:* Retrieve k passages → For each generation step: standard forward pass → Shuffle image patches → conflict forward pass → Identify pivot token indices from model's pivot extraction output → Compute adaptive gate α from conflict logits on pivot tokens → Compute projection `L_proj` and final logits `L_final` → Sample token.

**Design tradeoffs:**
- *Patch shuffle vs. masking:* Shuffle preserves feature magnitude but disrupts topology; masking discards information entirely. Shuffle is chosen to maintain distribution while breaking spatial coherence.
- *Adaptive gating:* Requires a reliable discriminator (from RPA-SFT). If pivot detection is poor, gating may suppress non-pivot tokens or miss pivot tokens.
- *Orthogonalization:* Gram-Schmidt ensures only conflict-aligned components are removed, preserving robust structures. However, it adds computational overhead and may be sensitive to numerical stability (δ).

**Failure signatures:**
- *Discriminator failure:* Low pivot extraction F1 (<70%) on validation sets (Table 4) indicates the model cannot identify reasoning units, making RPGD unreliable.
- *RPGD degradation:* Ablation in Table 6 shows removing any component hurts performance. If patch shuffle leads to incoherent text, the conflict pathway is invalid. If adaptive gate values are uniformly high/low, the discriminator is not providing useful signals.
- *Wrapper token `<RPivot>` causing misinterpretation:* The special tokens might not be properly handled by the base model's tokenizer or could be treated as normal text during generation, leading to failure in extracting clean pivot spans.
- *Inconsistent retrieval between training and evaluation:* The RPA-SFT model is trained on REAL-VQA passages, which are synthesized from Wikipedia with controlled conflicts. If the evaluator uses a different retriever (e.g., EVA-CLIP-8B) that returns passages with different style or noise patterns, the discriminator's performance may degrade.

**First 3 experiments:**
1. Reproduce RPA-SFT discrimination: Train on REAL-VQA training set, evaluate on REAL-VQA test and E-VQA conflict subsets. Aim for MCC >0.9 on REAL-VQA (Table 3).
2. Run full REAL vs. ablations: Compare REAL (RPA-SFT + RPGD) against RPA-SFT without RPGD and zero-shot on E-VQA/InfoSeek. Expect gains as in Table 5.
3. Component-wise RPGD ablation: Disable each RPGD component (Patch Shuffle, Adaptive Gating, Gram-Schmidt) to verify individual contributions as in Table 6. Expect full RPGD to be best.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
**Open Question 1:** How can we improve the robustness of conflict detection and resolution when retrieval evidence is sparse, biased, or noisy?  
*Basis in paper:* [explicit] "sparse, biased, or noisy evidence may still compromise the accuracy of conflict detection and resolution."  
*Why unresolved:* REAL's effectiveness depends on high-quality retrieval and precise reasoning-pivot extraction; it does not address how to maintain performance under imperfect real-world retrieval conditions.  
*What evidence would resolve:* Developing and evaluating noise-robust training protocols, retrieval denoising methods, or synthetic benchmarks with controlled noise levels to measure resilience.

**Open Question 2:** What techniques can reduce the computational overhead of the RPGD decoding strategy while preserving its conflict mitigation efficacy?  
*Basis in paper:* [explicit] "the approach incurs additional computational overhead due to the retrieval and pivot-based decoding processes."  
*Why unresolved:* RPGD requires dual forward passes (standard and shuffled) and extra arithmetic, increasing latency and resource usage; the paper does not explore optimization or approximation methods.  
*What evidence would resolve:* Proposing and benchmarking efficient variants (e.g., single-pass approximations, cached projections) on edge devices or under tight latency budgets.

**Open Question 3:** How does REAL generalize across diverse domains (e.g., scientific, legal) and multilingual contexts, and what adaptations are required?  
*Basis in paper:* [explicit] "model performance may exhibit variability across diverse domains and linguistic contexts."  
*Why unresolved:* Evaluation focused on English VQA benchmarks (E-VQA, InfoSeek, A-OKVQA) with limited domain diversity; performance on other languages or specialized fields remains unknown.  
*What evidence would resolve:* Systematic evaluation on multilingual VQA datasets (e.g., XM-VQA) and domain-specific benchmarks (e.g., medical VQA), coupled with error analysis to guide cross-domain fine-tuning.

**Open Question 4:** How can KI-VQA systems effectively communicate uncertainty to users to prevent over-reliance on answers, especially when external evidence is incomplete or conflicting?  
*Basis in paper:* [explicit] "user over-reliance on model outputs even when evidence is incomplete."  
*Why unresolved:* REAL focuses on resolving conflicts but does not provide calibrated confidence scores or user-facing uncertainty indicators, which could foster blind trust.  
*What evidence would resolve:* Integrating uncertainty quantification (e.g., ensemble methods, conflict-aware confidence) into the output and conducting user studies to evaluate how different presentations affect trust and decision-making.

**Open Question 5:** What mechanisms can ensure open-domain retrieval in KI-VQA avoids biased, unsafe, or copyrighted material and maintains reliable data provenance?  
*Basis in paper:* [explicit] "open-domain retrieval may inadvertently expose biased, unsafe, or copyrighted material. Therefore, responsible deployment necessitates robust source filtering, clear data provenance mechanisms, and the incorporation of uncertainty-aware response protocols."  
*Why unresolved:* REAL assumes the retrieved content is given and does not incorporate content filtering or provenance tracking, leaving ethical and legal risks unaddressed.  
*What evidence would resolve:* Implementing and evaluating content moderation filters, logging retrieval sources with cryptographic provenance, and auditing the system on datasets with known safety or copyright issues.

## Limitations

- The cutoff threshold τ in RPGD is omitted; its value could affect decoding stability and performance.
- Prompt templates for the multi-stage RPA-SFT output (exact separators and ordering) are not disclosed, risking training divergence.
