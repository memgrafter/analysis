# DMESR: Dual-view MLLM-based Enhancing Framework for Multimodal Sequential Recommendation

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID**: 2602.13715
- **Source URL**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.13715
- **Reference count**: 37
- **Primary result**: DMESR achieves average improvements of 31.21% in H@10 and 35.68% in N@10 over baselines, winning 17 of 18 settings.

## Executive Summary
DMESR introduces a novel dual-view framework for multimodal sequential recommendation that effectively leverages large multimodal models (MLLMs) while addressing their limitations of misaligned cross-modal semantics and loss of fine-grained textual details. By combining coarse-grained semantics extracted via three-way MLLM prompting with contrastive alignment and fine-grained semantics preserved from original texts fused through bidirectional cross-attention, the method substantially enhances recommendation accuracy. Extensive experiments on MovieLens, Yelp, and Amazon Games datasets with three backbone models (SASRec, BERT4Rec, GRU4Rec) demonstrate strong effectiveness and generalizability, with average gains exceeding 31% in Hit@10 and wins in 17 of 18 configurations.

## Method Summary
DMESR consists of two parallel branches. The coarse-grained branch uses a three-way prompting strategy with an MLLM to extract high-level semantic representations of items and employs contrastive learning to align representations across modalities. The fine-grained branch directly encodes raw textual item descriptions to preserve detailed lexical information. The outputs of both branches are fused using bidirectional cross-attention, enabling the model to integrate both abstraction levels. The fused item representations are then fed into a sequential recommendation backbone (e.g., SASRec, BERT4Rec, GRU4Rec) to capture user sequential patterns and predict the next item.

## Key Results
- Average H@10 improvement: 31.21%; N@10 improvement: 35.68% over the None baseline.
- Achieves state-leading performance in 17 of 18 tested configurations.
- Validated across three datasets (MovieLens, Yelp, Amazon Games) and three sequential backbones, demonstrating broad applicability.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The dual-view design effectively addresses two key weaknesses of directly applying MLLMs to recommendation: cross-modal misalignment and loss of fine-grained textual details. The MLLM provides rich, abstract concepts that capture high-level item characteristics, while the text branch retains precise lexical information. Contrastive alignment ensures that the coarse representations from different modalities are harmonized in a shared space. Bidirectional cross-attention then allows each view to attend to the other, creating a fused representation that combines the strengths of both. This synergy yields richer item embeddings, leading to more accurate sequential recommendation.

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component map**:
- Coarse path: Multimodal input -> MLLM (three-way prompting) -> Coarse embeddings -> Contrastive alignment -> Cross-attention fusion
- Fine path: Text input -> Text encoder -> Fine embeddings -> Cross-attention fusion
- Sequential path: Cross-attention fusion -> Sequential backbone (SASRec/BERT4Rec/GRU4Rec) -> Prediction

**Critical path**: Raw multimodal item data is processed by an MLLM to generate coarse semantic vectors; simultaneously, raw text is encoded into fine-grained vectors. The coarse vectors undergo contrastive alignment to synchronize cross-modal representations. Both aligned coarse and fine vectors are then fused via bidirectional cross-attention. The fused item representations are integrated with the user’s interaction sequence and fed into a sequential backbone to produce next-item scores.

**Design tradeoffs**:
- Added complexity: The dual-branch design with cross-attention introduces extra parameters and computational cost compared to a single-branch baseline, but this is justified by performance gains.
- Contrastive alignment sensitivity: The choice of temperature and negative sampling in contrastive learning can significantly affect convergence and final performance; careful tuning required.
- Potential overfitting: The increased model capacity may overfit on smaller datasets; regularization or early stopping is necessary.

**Failure signatures**:
- If contrastive loss fails to converge (e.g., remains high), cross-modal alignment may be inadequate, leading to poor fusion and performance.
- Removing either the coarse or fine branch causes a significant drop in metrics, indicating reliance on both views.
- On datasets with limited textual content, the fine branch may contribute little, and the model might degrade to a coarse-only system.

## Limitations
- No statistical significance tests or variance estimates are reported, so the observed improvements may not be statistically reliable.
- Baseline definition unclear ("None baseline") and questionable comparison with state-of-the-art multimodal recommenders.
- Limited scope: Evaluation covers only three datasets (MovieLens, Yelp, Amazon Games), restricting claims about generalizability across domains.
