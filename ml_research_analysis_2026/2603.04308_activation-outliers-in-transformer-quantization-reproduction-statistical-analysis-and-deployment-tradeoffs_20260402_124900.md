# Activation Outliers in Transformer Quantization: Reproduction, Statistical Analysis, and Deployment Tradeoffs

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2603.04308  
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.04308  
- **Reference count:** 9  
- **Primary result:** Global W8A8 PTQ collapses BERT-base (QNLI) accuracy from 89.66% to 54.33% due to structured activation outliers.

## Executive Summary
The paper identifies structured activation outliers as the primary cause of failure in post-training quantization (PTQ) for transformer models. Using BERT-base fine-tuned on QNLI, they demonstrate that uniform min-max scaling (W8A8) leads to catastrophic accuracy loss (~35 points). Statistical analysis reveals heavy-tailed activation distributions, with kurtosis reaching 271 in later layers and ~55% of activation energy concentrated in the top 1% of channels. To mitigate these outliers, the authors evaluate mixed precision, per-embedding-group (PEG) quantization, and percentile-based calibration. Mixed precision restores near-baseline accuracy (89.42%), while PEG with 4 groups achieves 86.18%. In contrast, percentile calibration fails (50.54%) because it clips structurally important channels. Deployment profiling on an RTX 3050 GPU shows minimal latency and memory differences between quantized and full-precision models, suggesting that without hardware-specific INT8 kernels, quantization offers no tangible speedup.

## Method Summary
The authors fine-tune BERT-base (12 layers, embedding dim=768) on the GLUE QNLI task using AdamW (batch=8, seq_len=128, lr=5e-5, seed=1000) to obtain an FP32 checkpoint. They then apply static post-training quantization to weights (INT8 in all cases) and activations using several strategies: global min-max (W8A8), mixed precision (FP16 for FFN outputs, residual summation inputs, and attention outputs), per-embedding-group (PEG) with K=3 or 4 groups (includes channel permutation and per-group scales), and percentile-based calibration (99.9% threshold). Activation statistics (mean, variance, kurtosis, top-1% energy) are collected from 64 validation samples. Accuracy is evaluated on the QNLI validation set. Deployment profiling runs inference with batch=8, seq_len=128 on an RTX 3050, measuring median/p95 latency (500 iterations after 100 warm-ups), peak VRAM, and model size. The one-click script `oneclick/run_all.py` orchestrates these steps, writing results to `runs/results/`.

## Key Results
- Global W8A8 quantization reduces accuracy from 89.66% (FP32) to 54.33%, confirming the failure mode.  
- Mixed precision (FP16 on select activations) restores accuracy to 89.42%; PEG with K=4 achieves 86.18%; percentile calibration (99.9%) fails at 50.54%.  
- On an RTX 3050, both FP32 and quantized models exhibit nearly identical latency (~58–59 ms) and VRAM usage (~484–486 MB), indicating that standard PyTorch kernels do not leverage INT8 acceleration.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

**Mechanism 1: Structured Channel Dominance**  
The quantization scale for a tensor is set by the maximum absolute value (`max(|x|)`). A small subset of embedding dimensions consistently exhibits large magnitudes (outliers), so this extreme value determines the global scale. Consequently, the majority of channels, whose values are much smaller, are mapped to only a few integer bins, drastically increasing quantization error. Evidence: ~55% of activation energy is concentrated in the top 1% of channels; the fraction of energy held by top channels increases with depth, reaching 55% at Layer 11.

**Mechanism 2: Residual Error Amplification**  
In transformer residual blocks (`h_{l+1} = h_l + f_l(h_l)`), quantization errors from each layer accumulate. If the quantization error is biased due to poor scaling (as in Mechanism 1), the accumulated error grows linearly with depth. The paper shows increasing variance (0.25 to 0.58) and exploding kurtosis (9 to 271) across layers, confirming that distortion amplifies. Mixed precision protects summation points and FFN outputs, mitigating this accumulation and restoring accuracy.

**Mechanism 3: Calibration Misalignment**  
Percentile-based calibration (e.g., clipping at 99.9%) attempts to remove outliers by discarding the largest values. However, because the highest-magnitude channels encode structured, semantically meaningful information (as evidenced by their dominance in activation energy), clipping them destroys critical signal. In contrast, channel-aware methods (PEG, mixed precision) preserve these channels by assigning them separate scales or higher precision, explaining why percentile calibration catastrophically fails (~50.54%).

## Architecture Onboarding
Component map: Calibration dataset -> Activation statistics (per layer) -> Scale computation (per method) -> Fake-quant insertion -> Model evaluation -> Deployment profiling

Critical path:
1. Fine-tune FP32 BERT-base on QNLI to obtain a checkpoint.
2. Run the calibration dataloader (training split) through the model to collect activation statistics.
3. For each PTQ method, compute quantization parameters (scales, zero-points) based on the collected statistics and the method's rules.
4. Insert fake-quant nodes (or modify kernels) using these parameters into the model graph.
5. Run the validation set to compute top-1 accuracy.
6. Profile inference on an RTX 3050 GPU (batch=8, seq_len=128) with warm-up and timed iterations to measure latency, VRAM, and model size.

Design tradeoffs:
- Accuracy vs. Compression: Mixed precision yields highest accuracy but retains some FP16 activations, reducing theoretical memory savings.
- Accuracy vs. Implementation Complexity: PEG achieves good accuracy (86.18% with K=4) but requires channel reordering and per-group scale handling, increasing runtime complexity.
- Simplicity vs. Efficacy: Percentile calibration is conceptually simple but catastrophically fails, showing that scalar clipping is insufficient.
- Theoretical vs. Actual Efficiency: All INT8 methods show negligible latency/VRAM improvement on RTX 3050 because standard kernels lack INT8 optimizations; real speedups require hardware-specific kernels.

Failure signatures:
- Accuracy collapse: W8A8 and percentile methods drop to ~50-54%, near random on binary QNLI.
- Statistical signature: Per-layer kurtosis >100 in deeper layers; top-1% of channels hold >40% of activation energy.
- Deployment signature: No meaningful reduction in latency or VRAM compared to FP32 on consumer GPU without INT8 acceleration.

First 3 experiments:
1. Reproduce W8A8 collapse: Apply uniform W8A8 PTQ to the FP32 checkpoint and measure accuracy; expect ~54%.
2. Depth-wise statistical profiling: Extract activations from each layer on 64 validation samples; compute mean, variance, kurtosis, and top-1% energy fraction; verify increasing kurtosis and energy concentration with depth.
3. Ablation: PEG group count: Run PEG with K=2, 3, 4 groups; expect accuracy to improve with larger K (e.g., ~49%, 66%, 86%), demonstrating the importance of isolating dominant channels.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- How do activation outliers scale with model size and architecture (e.g., encoder vs. decoder)? The paper only studied BERT-base; larger models and decoder-only architectures may exhibit different patterns.
- Do quantization methods provide runtime efficiency benefits on hardware with native INT8 support? The study used an RTX 3050 lacking such kernels; profiling on NPUs, mobile SoCs, or data center GPUs (e.g., A100) is needed.
- Can we formulate a theoretical model to predict error accumulation due to residual connections under affine quantization? An analytical framework would help generalize findings and derive stability bounds.
- How consistent are activation outlier statistics and PTQ collapse across different fine-tuning seeds, tasks, and data domains? The paper used a single seed and QNLI task; robustness checks across seeds and tasks are required.

## Limitations
- Calibration dataset size (full training set vs. subset) is unspecified, potentially affecting reproducibility of scale factors.
- The experimental scope is limited to BERT-base fine-tuned on QNLI and profiling on an RTX 3050; broader model/task/hardware generalization is unknown.
- Activation statistics are computed on only 64 validation samples, which may not fully capture the distribution.

## Confidence
- Structured activation outliers cause global scaling collapse: High
- Residual error amplification explains depth sensitivity: Medium
- Mixed precision restores accuracy by protecting summation points: High
- PEG (K≥4) recovers most accuracy: Medium
- Percentile calibration fails due to clipping structured signal: High
- No latency/VRAM gain on RTX 3050: High
