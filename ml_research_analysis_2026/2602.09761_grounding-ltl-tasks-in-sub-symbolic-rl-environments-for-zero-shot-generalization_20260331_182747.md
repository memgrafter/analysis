# Grounding LTL Tasks in Sub-Symbolic RL Environments for Zero-Shot Generalization

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2602.09761
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.09761
- **Reference count:** 25
- **Primary result:** Achieves performance comparable to an oracle with true grounding and significantly outperforms the state-of-the-art baseline, with average total returns near 1.0 on training tasks and substantial gains in zero-shot generalization; grounder accuracy converges to near 100%.

## Executive Summary
This paper addresses the challenge of multi-task reinforcement learning (RL) with Linear Temporal Logic (LTL) instructions in sub-symbolic environments where the mapping from raw observations to LTL symbols is unknown. The authors propose a method that jointly trains a policy and a symbol grounder using Neural Reward Machines (NRMs) for semi-supervised grounding from sparse rewards. The agent conditions its actions on the progression of the LTL formula, leveraging a pretrained and frozen LTL module to encode task semantics. Experiments on Minecraft-like and FlatWorld environments show that the method attains near-oracle performance on training tasks and exhibits strong zero-shot generalization, with the symbol grounder reaching near-perfect accuracy.

## Method Summary
The approach tackles the problem of unknown symbol grounding by jointly learning a grounder network and an RL policy across multiple LTL-specified tasks. For each training task, an NRM—derived from the LTL formula as a differentiable probabilistic Moore machine—provides indirect supervision: the shared grounder predicts symbol distributions from raw observations, which drive the NRM’s state transitions and predicted rewards. By minimizing cross-entropy between NRM-predicted rewards and the environment’s sparse binary rewards, gradients update the grounder without requiring ground-truth labels. Meanwhile, a progression operator updates the current LTL formula based on the most likely symbol, and a frozen LTL module (a relational GNN pretrained on a symbolic bootcamp) encodes the progressed formula. This encoding is concatenated with environment features and fed to a PPO policy. The entire system is trained end-to-end, enabling the agent to interpret and execute diverse LTL instructions directly from raw observations.

## Key Results
- Achieves performance comparable to an oracle with true grounding on training tasks.
- Significantly outperforms the state-of-the-art baseline (Kuo et al., 2020) in zero-shot generalization.
- Symbol grounder accuracy converges to nearly 100% during training.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1
- **Claim:** Semi-supervised symbol grounding via Neural Reward Machines (NRMs) allows learning the mapping from raw observations to LTL symbols without explicit labels, using only sparse task rewards.
- **Mechanism:** For each training LTL task, an NRM (a differentiable probabilistic Moore machine) is constructed from the formula. The shared grounder network predicts symbolic probabilities from observations. These predictions drive the NRM’s state transitions and reward predictions. By minimizing cross-entropy between NRM-predicted rewards and actual sparse rewards during episodes, gradients flow back through the NRM to train the grounder. Multiple tasks provide diverse learning signals, mitigating the "reasoning shortcuts" problem common with single tasks and sparse rewards.
- **Core assumption:** The structure of LTL formulae (as Moore Machines) provides sufficient inductive bias to constrain the grounder’s learned mapping, even with ambiguous raw observations.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract] "The symbol grounder is trained only from raw observations and sparse rewards via Neural Reward Machines in a semi-supervised fashion."
  - [section 4.4] Detailed NRM formulation and training loop: "the grounder module... cannot be trained via standard supervised learning... Instead, the agent learns the grounding indirectly from the environment through the sequence of rewards... via Neural Reward Machines."
  - [corpus] Neighbors (e.g., "Neural Reward Machines" paper ID 163263) confirm the NRM framework but the joint multi-task extension is novel.
- **Break condition:** If training tasks do not produce enough non-zero reward episodes (e.g., all tasks are extremely long-horizon with rare outcomes), the grounder receives insufficient gradient signal and may fail to converge.

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim:** Progression-based policy conditioning enables a single policy to generalize across LTL tasks by dynamically encoding task state from the (noisy) grounder’s output.
- **Mechanism:** At each step, the most likely symbol from the grounder updates the current LTL formula via the progression operator. This progressed formula, encoded by a pretrained LTL module (GNN over AST), is concatenated with environment features and fed to the policy. The policy thus conditions on a compact representation of "what remains to be done" for the current task, independent of the specific formula.
- **Core assumption:** Even if the grounder’s output is imperfect, the progression algorithm and LTL module are robust enough to maintain a useful task state representation for decision-making.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract] "Jointly trains a policy and symbol grounder... The resulting agent can then follow many different LTL instructions by keeping track of task progression directly from raw observations."
  - [section 4.3] Pipeline description: "The most likely symbol p = arg max Lθ(s(t)) progresses φ(t−1) to φ(t), which is mapped into a vector by the LTL module. The concatenated features are passed to the RL module."
  - [section 4.2] Review of progression: "progression-based approaches... are most effective for multi-task RL with temporal goals."
- **Break condition:** If grounder errors systematically corrupt the progression state (e.g., consistent false negatives on a critical symbol), the policy may be conditioned on a permanently incorrect task state and fail catastrophically.

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim:** Pretraining and freezing the LTL module on a simple symbolic bootcamp environment stabilizes joint training by providing a robust, transferable encoding of LTL semantics, preventing destructive early updates from the uncertain grounder.
- **Mechanism:** The LTL module (GNN) is first trained on a toy environment where actions correspond to symbols and rewards directly indicate formula satisfaction. This imbues it with an understanding of LTL structure. During main training, these weights are frozen, so the LTL representation is fixed even as the grounder and policy jointly adapt. This prevents the LTL module from overfitting to early noisy grounder outputs.
- **Core assumption:** LTL semantics are environment-agnostic and can be learned in a simple setting then applied unchanged to complex vision-based tasks.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [appendix A.4] Ablation study: "using the pretrained and frozen LTL module performs better... during the first training steps... backpropagation partially disrupts the pretraining."
  - [section 4.5] "pretraining imbues the module with task semantics before environment interaction... a fixed LTL module stabilizes learning in the presence of the uncertain grounder."
  - [corpus] Limited direct evidence; neighbors focus on LTL encoders but not the specific pretraining-freezing strategy.
- **Break condition:** If the target tasks involve LTL constructs or proposition interactions absent in the bootcamp distribution, the frozen LTL module may fail to represent required progression states accurately.

## Foundational Learning
- **Concept: LTL Progression & Moore Machines**
  - *Why needed here:* The entire approach relies on transforming an LTL formula into a finite-state monitor (Moore Machine) that updates its state based on symbol inputs. This provides the Markovian representation necessary for RL. The agent must approximate this monitor using the learned grounder’s outputs.
  - *Quick check question:* If your grounder outputs symbol probabilities, how would you compute the expected next state of the Moore Machine for a given nominal machine state?
- **Concept: Semi-Supervised Symbol Grounding (SSSG)**
  - *Why needed here:* The core challenge is learning Lθ (mapping images to symbols) without ground-truth labels. NRMs are a specific SSSG technique that uses the formula’s automaton to provide indirect supervision via rewards.
  - *Quick check question:* Why might standard supervised learning fail for the grounder in this setting, and how does an NRM provide a training signal instead?
- **Concept: Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO)**
  - *Why needed here:* The policy and value function are trained with PPO, a standard on-policy RL algorithm. The non-Markovian task is made Markovian by the state representation (env features + LTL state).
  - *Quick check question:* How does the use of a learned, progressing task state (φ(t)) instead of raw history affect the assumptions of PPO’s clipped objective?

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map:**
  1. **Grounder (Lθ):** CNN (for images) or MLP (for features) outputting distribution over |P|.
  2. **Env Module (f_imgθ):** Shared feature extractor (e.g., CNN) producing state embedding.
  3. **LTL Module (f_LTLθ):** Relational GCN over formula AST; pretrained then frozen.
  4. **RL Module (πθ, Vθ):** MLP policy and value head taking concatenated [env_feat, ltl_feat].
  5. **NRM (per training task):** Constructed from formula’s Moore Machine; uses grounder output; trained only during grounder updates.
- **Critical path:** 
  Observation s(t) → Grounder (Lθ(s)) & Env Module (f_imgθ(s)) → Symbol prediction p (argmax) → LTL progression: φ(t) = prog(p, φ(t-1)) → LTL Module (f_LTLθ(φ(t))) → Concatenation with env feat → RL Module (πθ) → Action a(t).
  Grounder update: sample episode (s, r, φ) → build task-specific NRM → compute ˜r via NRM using Lθ(s) → minimize cross-entropy(˜r, r) → update only Lθ.
- **Design tradeoffs:**
  - *Shared grounder vs per-task:* Shared grounder enables transfer but risks negative transfer if symbols look different across tasks. Per-task grounders would be simpler but lose generalization.
  - *Frozen vs fine-tunable LTL module:* Frozen provides stability but cannot adapt to novel LTL constructs. Fine-tuning risks catastrophic forgetting of bootcamp semantics.
  - *NRM construction cost:* Precomputing Moore Machines for 10k training formulae is expensive but done offline; online training uses these fixed structures.
- **Failure signatures:**
  - *Grounder:* Accuracy plateaus below 80% and does not improve; NRM loss stagnates. Likely cause: insufficiently diverse training tasks or overly ambiguous observations.
  - *Policy:* Discounted return oscillates or collapses to near zero; progression state rarely reaches terminal (⊤/⊥). Likely cause: grounder errors causing irrecoverable progression mistakes, or LTL module mismatch.
  - *Zero-shot transfer:* Performance on complex unseen formulae is at random or negative. Indicates lack of generalization in either grounder (fails on new symbol contexts) or policy (cannot interpret progressed formula).
- **First 3 experiments:**
  1. **Baseline sanity check:** Train with known true labeling function (oracle) on base tasks. Should converge to ~1.0 total return quickly. Verifies environment and LTL module correctness.
  2. **Grounder convergence:** Train proposed method on base tasks, plot grounder accuracy vs frames. Should reach >95% within 1M frames. If not, inspect NRM gradient flow or task diversity.
  3. **Zero-shot generalization:** After training on base tasks, evaluate on +depth and +conj. tasks. Should match oracle performance (~1.0) and significantly beat baseline [Kuo et al. 2020]. If not, check if grounder generalizes to new symbol combinations or if progression state becomes invalid.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
**Open Question 1**
- **Question:** How robust is the symbol grounder to noisy observations or to symbols defined by non-iconic semantics (e.g., spatial relations, object interactions)?
- **Basis in paper:** [explicit] *"First, the robustness of the symbol grounder to raw observations, for instance by providing noisy observations or having symbols’ semantics beyond just being in a certain location."*
- **Why unresolved:** The current experimental setup uses clean, synthetic visual inputs where each symbol corresponds to a distinctive, static pattern. It remains unknown how the grounding function would perform under realistic corruption (e.g., sensor noise, occlusions) or for symbols requiring more abstract features (e.g., "near", "above"). These factors could significantly impact the semi‑supervised learning signal and downstream policy performance.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Systematically perturb observations with noise, blur, or occlusions and evaluate grounder accuracy and task returns. Additionally, design symbols that cannot be recognized from a single pixel but require relational reasoning (e.g., "two objects adjacent"). Compare to a baseline with no such perturbations. If performance decays, explore robustness methods like data augmentation, invariances, or auxiliary objectives that encourage stable grounding.

**Open Question 2**
- **Question:** Does reward shaping informed by task progression improve sample efficiency and final performance, especially during early training when the policy and grounder are both unreliable?
- **Basis in paper:** [explicit] *"Second, learning could benefit from reward shaping informed by task progression, particularly during early training when neither the policy nor the grounder produces meaningful behavior."*
- **Why unresolved:** The method uses sparse LTL3 rewards that may yield slow learning due to the scarcity of non‑zero feedback. Reward shaping could provide denser signals, but it risks altering the optimal policy (e.g., by introducing deceptive local optima) or requiring careful tuning. No experiment in the paper explores shaping, leaving its net benefit unknown.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Incorporate shaping bonuses derived from the progression state (e.g., Moore‑machine state distance to accepting states, or potential‑based shaping that respects the LTL semantics). Run controlled comparisons with and without shaping across multiple environments and task families, measuring sample complexity, asymptotic performance, and zero‑shot generalization. Analyze whether shaping consistently accelerates learning without compromising correctness.

**Open Question 3**
- **Question:** Can curriculum learning—starting with easier LTL formulae—improve convergence speed, stability, and generalization to more complex tasks?
- **Basis in paper:** [explicit] *"For the same reason, curriculum learning, where easier formulae are trained first, could also be beneficial."*
- **Why unresolved:** The training distribution mixes formulae of varying syntactic and semantic complexity uniformly. It is unclear whether a structured progression from simple to hard tasks would yield better learning dynamics, especially given the joint learning of grounding and policy. The definition of "easier" LTL tasks (e.g., shorter depth, fewer conjuncts) also needs validation.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Define a set of complexity metrics (e.g., formula depth, number of sequential dependencies, number of conjuncts). Train agents with a curriculum that gradually introduces more complex formulae compared to a random baseline. Track learning curves, final success rates, and performance on unseen complex tasks. If curriculum yields faster convergence and higher generalization, explore automated difficulty assessment and pacing schedules.

**Open Question 4**
- **Question:** Can the need for precomputed automata be eliminated by using fuzzy logic conformance checking to train the symbol grounder in a semi‑supervised manner?
- **Basis in paper:** [explicit] *"Finally, building on recent work on fuzzy logic conformance checking [Donadello et al., 2024; Andreoni et al., 2025] could allow to train the symbol grounder without explicitly constructing the automata for the LTL formulae."*
- **Why unresolved:** Precomputing Moore machines for every training formula is computationally heavy and limits scalability to large or infinite formula spaces. Fuzzy conformance checking offers a way to assess trace satisfaction without automata, but it is unknown whether this signal can effectively replace the automaton‑based supervision in the Neural Reward Machine framework, and whether it remains differentiable and equally expressive.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Replace the NRM’s automaton with a differentiable fuzzy conformance module that takes a trace and an LTL formula and outputs a satisfaction score in [0,1]. Train the grounder using this score as the target (e.g., via cross‑entropy against binary success/failure after episode termination). Compare sample efficiency, scalability (number/size of formulae), and final performance to the standard NRM approach on the same benchmark tasks. Success would demonstrate the feasibility of automata‑free grounding.

## Limitations
- Implementation details: paper lacks full specifications for grounder, environment feature extractor, RL networks, and NRM parameterization; reproduction depends on prior work (Umili et al., 2024).
- Generalization may be limited to LTL constructs present in the pretraining bootcamp; unseen operators (e.g., release, weak until) could break the frozen LTL module.
- Performance is sensitive to the diversity of training tasks; insufficient diversity can prevent the grounder from learning robust symbol mappings.

## Confidence
- Semi-supervised symbol grounding via NRMs: Medium
- Progression-based policy conditioning: Medium-High
- Pretrained frozen LTL module: Medium
