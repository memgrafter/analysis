# Not All Latent Spaces Are Flat: Hyperbolic Concept Control
## Quick Facts
- **ArXiv ID**: 2603.14093  
- **Source URL**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.14093  
- **Reference count**: 14  
- **Primary result**: HyCon achieves state-of-the-art suppression of unsafe content while maintaining competitive image quality across multiple diffusion backbones.

## Executive Summary
The paper addresses unsafe content generation in text‑to‑image models, where existing Euclidean steering methods cause unstable or abrupt semantic shifts as control strength varies. The authors propose HyCon, a concept steering method operating in a hyperbolic embedding space (HyCoCLIP) where concepts are organized as entailment cones. Control directions are defined via Fréchet means of positive and negative prompts and applied to new inputs through parallel transport along geodesics, respecting the manifold geometry. A lightweight adapter maps hyperbolic embeddings to the CLIP space of pretrained diffusion backbones without retraining. Evaluation on four benchmarks and four diffusion backbones shows HyCon significantly outperforms Euclidean baselines in safety suppression (e.g., NudeNet reduced by ~39% vs. SAFREE, GPT‑4o detection near zero) while preserving image quality (FID ~44.3, CLIPScore ~0.31). Retrieval experiments confirm edits reliably place embeddings into intended concept cones (R@1 ≥ 0.78) with minimal interference.

## Method Summary
HyCon introduces a hyperbolic concept space (HyCoCLIP) that encodes text prompts into a manifold of constant negative curvature. In this space, semantically related concepts form convex cones (entailment cones) that capture subset relationships. To steer generation away from unsafe concepts, the method computes a control direction as the difference between the Fréchet means of positive (target) and negative (undesired) prompt embeddings. This direction is used to perform parallel transport of a neutral input’s embedding along a geodesic, yielding a new hyperbolic embedding that respects the geometry. A lightweight adapter then translates this embedding into the CLIP space required by the diffusion backbone. The adapter is trained on a small dataset of paired embeddings and enables plug‑and‑play use with any CLIP‑based text‑to‑image model, avoiding costly retraining.

## Key Results
- HyCon achieves state‑of‑the‑art unsafe content suppression on four benchmarks and four diffusion backbones, reducing NudeNet scores by ~39% relative to the Euclidean SAFREE baseline and driving GPT‑4o detection to near zero.  
- Image quality remains competitive; on SD 3.5, FID is ~44.3 and CLIPScore is ~0.31, indicating only modest degradation versus unconditioned generation.  
- Retrieval experiments show edits reliably land in intended concept cones (R@1 ≥ 0.78) with minimal interference to other concepts.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
Hyperbolic space’s negative curvature naturally accommodates hierarchical relationships between concepts via entailment cones. Steering along geodesics ensures that control increments are geometrically consistent, avoiding the abrupt semantic jumps observed in linear Euclidean interpolation. Fréchet means aggregate multiple prompt embeddings in a way that respects the manifold, yielding robust central directions. Parallel transport moves embeddings while preserving their directional relationship to the manifold, guaranteeing that the effect of a control strength is uniform across different starting points. The lightweight adapter provides an efficient bridge between hyperbolic and CLIP spaces, preserving the benefits of the hyperbolic manipulation without altering the diffusion model itself.

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component map**: Prompt → HyCoCLIP → Hyperbolic Space → Fréchet Mean & Parallel Transport → Adapter → CLIP → Diffusion → Image

**Critical path**: The essential data flow starts with input text prompts encoded into hyperbolic space. The method computes a steering direction from positive/negative prompt embeddings (Fréchet mean) and moves a neutral prompt’s embedding via parallel transport. The adapter then converts this hyperbolic embedding to CLIP space, which the diffusion model uses to synthesize an image.

**Design tradeoffs**:
- *Hyperbolic vs. Euclidean*: Hyperbolic geometry yields smoother control and better alignment with concept hierarchies but introduces computational overhead and potential numerical instability.
- *Adapter size vs. fidelity*: A lightweight adapter preserves diffusion model quality but may limit expressiveness, leading to a modest increase in FID.
- *Backbone generality*: HyCon is tightly coupled to CLIP‑based models; its applicability to non‑CLIP architectures (e.g., DALL‑E 3, Imagen) remains untested.
- *Evaluation metrics*: Automated detectors (NudeNet, GPT‑4o) are efficient but imperfect proxies for human judgment; quality metrics show a small degradation.

**Failure signatures**:
- Abrupt semantic changes when varying control strength, indicating unstable hyperbolic operations or poorly chosen curvature.
- High NudeNet scores after steering, suggesting the control direction failed to move the embedding into the safe region.
- Significant drop in CLIPScore or FID compared to baseline, hinting at adapter distortion or information loss.
- Retrieval accuracy below expectations (R@1 < 0.78), implying concept cones are not well formed or the Fréchet mean is miscomputed.

**First experiments**:
1. *Adapter reconstruction*: Validate the adapter by mapping known CLIP embeddings into hyperbolic space and back; measure reconstruction error to ensure fidelity.
2. *Concept cone validation*: For a set of positive/negative prompt pairs, compute Fréchet means and verify that target embeddings fall within the expected cone using a similarity threshold.
3. *Smoothness sweep*: Incrementally vary control strength on a neutral prompt and track CLIP similarity to the target concept; compare the rate of change against a Euclidean baseline to confirm smoothness.

## Limitations
- HyCon’s adapter is restricted to CLIP‑based diffusion backbones; generalization to non‑CLIP models (e.g., DALL‑E 3, Imagen) is untested.  
- Hyperbolic operations (Fréchet mean, parallel transport) may suffer from numerical instability; sensitivity to curvature and embedding dimension remains to be fully characterized.  
- Safety evaluation relies on automated detectors (NudeNet, GPT‑4o) whose alignment with human judgment is imperfect, and image quality shows a modest trade‑off (FID ~44.3).

## Confidence
- **High**: HyCon achieves significantly better unsafe content suppression than Euclidean steering (SAFREE) across four diffusion backbones, supported by multiple detectors.  
- **Medium**: HyCon maintains competitive image quality (FID, CLIPScore) during control; modest FID values suggest some degradation versus unconditioned generation.  
- **High**: Edits reliably land in intended concept cones (R@1 ≥ 0.78) with minimal interference, validated via retrieval experiments.  
- **Medium**: The hyperbolic latent space inherently enables stable, non‑abrupt control via geodesics; evidence is empirical and could depend on embedding quality.
