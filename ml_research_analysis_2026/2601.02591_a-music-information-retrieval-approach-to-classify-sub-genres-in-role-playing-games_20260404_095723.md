# A Music Information Retrieval Approach to Classify Sub-Genres in Role Playing Games
## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2601.02591
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.02591
- **Reference Count:** 0
- **Primary Result:** KNN classifier achieved 33.3% accuracy in sub-genre classification; Adventure RPG soundtracks showed significantly wider ZCR fluctuations and broader chroma key distribution.

## Executive Summary
This study applies Music Information Retrieval (MIR) techniques to quantitatively analyze soundtracks across three RPG sub-genres: Adventure, Action, and Strategy. From 27 instrumental tracks, the authors extracted audio features like tempo, zero-crossing rate (ZCR), spectral centroid, chroma, and MFCCs. While Adventure RPGs exhibited greater variability in ZCR and chroma—interpreted as "musical robustness" linked to diverse gameplay—the classification task failed, with a KNN model performing at random chance (33.3%). The work highlights the challenge of using hand-crafted audio features for genre discrimination in video game music and calls for larger datasets and more advanced methods.

## Method Summary
The authors curated 27 instrumental soundtracks (3 games × 3 tracks each) from top-rated Adventure, Action, and Strategy RPGs on Steam. Audio was preprocessed: normalized to -5 dB, resampled to 48 kHz, and trimmed to a 15-second middle segment. Using `librosa`, they computed tempo, ZCR statistics, spectral centroid, chroma distributions, and MFCCs. A KNN classifier (hyperparameters unspecified) was trained on these features to predict sub-genre labels, achieving 33.3% accuracy. Statistical comparisons of feature distributions across sub-genres were also conducted, though testing methods were not detailed.

## Key Results
- Adventure RPG soundtracks had significantly wider fluctuations in zero-crossing rate (ZCR) and broader chroma key distributions compared to other sub-genres.
- No significant differences were found in spectral centroid or tempo.
- A KNN classifier achieved only 33.3% accuracy, indicating poor discriminative power of the selected features.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1
- **Claim:** Wider ZCR fluctuations and broader chroma distributions in Adventure RPG soundtracks correspond to greater musical robustness, mirroring the diverse gameplay and narrative elements typical of that sub-genre.
- **Mechanism:** These features reflect broader ranges of sonic texture (ZCR) and tonal centers (chroma). The authors hypothesize such variability in the audio signal parallels the variety of environments, emotions, and mechanics in Adventure RPGs.
- **Core assumption:** Quantitative audio feature variability directly correlates with perceived gameplay/narrative complexity.
- **Evidence anchors:** Abstract link between "musical robustness" and "diverse gameplay mechanics"; Section 4 explicitly ties feature robustness to gameplay robustness.
- **Break condition:** If larger datasets or different feature sets fail to show consistent variability patterns across sub-genres, the correlation hypothesis is undermined.

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim:** The 33.3% KNN accuracy indicates the hand-crafted MIR features lack sufficient discriminative power for RPG sub-genre classification.
- **Mechanism:** KNN performance reflects feature space separability. Near-chance accuracy suggests overlapping distributions for Adventure, Action, and Strategy RPGs within the chosen feature dimensions.
- **Core assumption:** Supervised classification accuracy reliably indicates the existence of latent, feature-representable genre distinctions.
- **Evidence anchors:** Abstract and Section 4 both report the 33.3% accuracy as "not significant enough."
- **Break condition:** If advanced models (e.g., CNN spectrogram classifiers) achieve high accuracy on the same or larger datasets, it confirms the insufficiency of the initial feature set rather than the absence of any signal.

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim:** Using 15-second middle clips may discard genre-discriminative information present in song boundaries or structural progression.
- **Mechanism:** Truncation aims to capture a "main theme" but could remove cues like introductory fanfares (common in Action RPGs) or slow-building developments (Strategy RPGs), homogenizing genre-specific patterns.
- **Core assumption:** Genre identity is encoded in local, mid-segment features rather than global track structure.
- **Evidence anchors:** Section 3 describes clipping to the middle to capture the "highlighted part"; no tempo differences were found, possibly influenced by preprocessing choices.
- **Break condition:** Experiments using full tracks or varied clip positions yielding different feature stats or classification accuracy would implicate truncation as an information-loss source.

## Foundational Learning
- **Concept:** Music Information Retrieval (MIR) feature extraction (e.g., ZCR, spectral centroid, chroma, MFCCs).
  - *Why needed:* All analysis depends on these descriptors to quantify sonic characteristics. Understanding their perceptual correlates (ZCR ≈ noisiness/brightness, spectral centroid ≈ brightness, chroma ≈ harmonic content) is key to interpreting results.
  - *Quick check:* What does a consistently high spectral centroid indicate about timbre, and why might that be relevant for differentiating fast-paced action music from slower strategy music?
- **Concept:** Video game genre taxonomy based on mechanics vs. narrative.
  - *Why needed:* The study's hypothesis links musical features to gameplay/narrative traits of RPG sub-genres. Disentangling these axes is crucial for formulating and testing correlation claims.
  - *Quick check:* How might the core gameplay loop of an Action RPG (real-time combat) versus a Strategy RPG (turn-based planning) plausibly demand different musical supports in terms of rhythm, harmony, or texture?
- **Concept:** Supervised machine learning evaluation (accuracy, feature separability).
  - *Why needed:* The 33.3% KNN accuracy is the primary evidence for (lack of) discriminative power. Interpreting it requires understanding baseline chance (33.3% for 3 classes), overfitting risks with small N, and what accuracy implies about feature space geometry.
  - *Quick check:* With a balanced 3-class dataset and KNN, what would a 33.3% accuracy suggest about feature distributions relative to a 90% accuracy result?

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map:** Game selection (Steam) → Soundtrack download (YouTube/VGM DB) → Preprocessing (normalize, resample, trim) → Feature extraction (`librosa`: ZCR, spectral centroid, chroma, MFCCs, tempo) → Statistical comparison (across sub-genres) → KNN classification → Interpretation.
- **Critical path:** Game selection → Soundtrack collection → Preprocessing → Feature extraction → Statistical comparison & Classification → Interpretation.
- **Design tradeoffs:**
  - *Dataset Size vs. Control:* 27 samples is tractable but weak statistically. Expanding to more games/tracks increases computational/annotation cost but improves power.
  - *Feature Complexity vs. Interpretability:* Hand-crafted MIR features are interpretable but may lack power. Deep learning (e.g., CNN spectrograms) may perform better but are less transparent for linking to concepts like "chroma key distribution."
  - *Clip Duration vs. Context:* 15s middle clip reduces cost and aims for "theme" but risks losing structural cues (intros/outros). Full-track analysis is computationally heavier.
- **Failure signatures:**
  - Statistical: Overlapping boxplots/violin plots for key features (ZCR, chroma) across sub-genres.
  - ML: Classification accuracy near random chance (33.3%) for any simple model, especially with small N.
  - Preprocessing: Inconsistent dB levels or sample rates causing extraction errors; inclusion of vocal tracks.
- **First 3 experiments:**
  1. Replicate exact pipeline on 27 tracks, documenting train/test splits and K value. Add Random Forest to test if a more flexible model finds any signal.
  2. Extract features from *entire* tracks (not 15s clips) for same set. Compare distributions and KNN accuracy to clipped version.
  3. Scale dataset: add one more popular RPG per sub-genre (total 12 games), 5 instrumental tracks each (60 total). Rerun stats and classification.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
### Open Question 1
- **Question:** How does increasing dataset size affect the statistical significance and robustness of observed audio feature differences between RPG sub-genres?
- **Basis in paper:** Explicitly stated in "LIMITATION AND FUTURE WORK": including more songs would enhance accuracy and concreteness.
- **Why unresolved:** Current sample (27 tracks from 9 games) may not capture full sub-genre variability, risking non-generalizable findings.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** A follow-up with a larger, systematically curated dataset (hundreds of tracks from dozens of games per sub-genre) performing the same analyses to see if initial patterns (wider ZCR/chroma for Adventure) hold and become significant.

### Open Question 2
- **Question:** Which ML models or feature representations (e.g., deep learning on raw waveforms, different ensemble methods) most effectively improve classification accuracy beyond 33.3%?
- **Basis in paper:** Explicitly suggests "further training using other algorithms or expanding the dataset may be necessary."
- **Why unresolved:** Only one simple classifier (KNN) on a limited hand-crafted feature set was tested; advanced models/representations were not explored.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Comparative study of multiple state-of-the-art algorithms (SVMs, Random Forests, CNNs on spectrograms, Transformers) on existing or new features, reporting accuracy, precision, recall, F1-score.

### Open Question 3
- **Question:** To what extent are observed musical feature correlations (e.g., Adventure RPG's wider ZCR/chroma) specifically tied to gameplay mechanics/narrative themes versus composer styles or era/platform?
- **Basis in paper:** Inferred: paper hypothesizes links to "storytelling elements or play mechanics" but controls for no confounders (composer, year, platform).
- **Why unresolved:** Study design is correlational without controlling for alternative explanations; link between audio features and *game-specific* genre traits is assumed, not tested.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Collect metadata (composer, year, platform, developer) and use mixed-effects models to parse variance attributable to sub-genre vs. confounders; pair audio analysis with qualitative gameplay/theme mapping.

### Open Question 4
- **Question:** Do findings change when including vocal/lyrical soundtracks, which were excluded for consistency?
- **Basis in paper:** Explicit: "We avoided lyrical tracks because they are not necessarily apparent in all the games, while instrumental tracks are the more dominant type."
- **Why unresolved:** Excluding lyrical tracks limits generalizability; vocal content (melody, timbre) is a potentially rich, unexamined dimension.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Replicate with dataset including both instrumental and lyrical tracks (or separate analysis), comparing feature distributions and classification performance against instrumental-only baseline.

### Open Question 5
- **Question:** Are low-level features (ZCR, spectral centroid, chroma, MFCCs) sufficient for characterizing high-level genre perceptions (e.g., "robustness," "diversity"), or are higher-level, semantically richer features needed?
- **Basis in paper:** Inferred: paper interprets ZCR fluctuation/chroma distribution as proxies for "musical robustness" and diversity, but this mapping from objective features to subjective constructs is unvalidated.
- **Why unresolved:** The leap from low-level signal properties to high-level perceptual/narrative constructs is based on author interpretation, without listener validation.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Perceptual study where human listeners rate/classify same soundtracks based on perceived genre alignment; correlate these judgments with extracted audio features to identify truly predictive features.

## Limitations
- Small dataset (27 tracks) limits statistical power and generalizability.
- Statistical testing methods and multiple comparison corrections are unspecified.
- KNN evaluation details (train/test splits, *k* value) are missing; accuracy near chance may stem from improper validation or overfitting.
- No exploration of alternative classifiers or feature representations (e.g., deep embeddings).
- Using 15-second middle clips may discard genre-specific structural cues (intros, outros).
- The link between audio variability and gameplay complexity remains speculative, lacking direct validation.

## Confidence
- **Adventure RPGs exhibit wider ZCR fluctuations and broader chroma distribution:** Medium (observed differences but underpowered).
- **KNN accuracy of 33.3% shows poor discriminative power of selected features:** High (directly reported and at chance level).
- **Audio "robustness" reflects gameplay robustness:** Low (interpretive, unverified hypothesis).

## Next Checks

2. Scale the dataset (e.g., 30 games, 5 tracks each) and apply a CNN on mel-spectrograms to test whether richer representations yield better discrimination.
3. Correlate audio feature variability with concrete game design metrics (e.g., number of mechanics, environment diversity) to directly evaluate the robustness hypothesis.
