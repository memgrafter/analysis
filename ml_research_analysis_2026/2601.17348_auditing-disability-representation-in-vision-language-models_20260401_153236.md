# Auditing Disability Representation in Vision-Language Models
## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2601.17348
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.17348
- Reference count: 40
- Primary result: Introducing disability context in prompts degrades interpretive fidelity across VLMs, with verbosity inflation and speculative inference as dominant failures.

## Executive Summary
The paper audits disability representation in vision-language models (VLMs) by measuring how the introduction of disability context in prompts impacts interpretive fidelity — the faithfulness of model outputs to observable evidence and normative disability standards. Using a controlled benchmark (PAIRS) of paired Neutral and Disability-Contextualized prompts across nine disability categories, the authors evaluate fifteen state-of-the-art VLMs via a hybrid framework combining text metrics (VADER sentiment, Regard, verbosity) and an LLM-as-judge protocol validated by disability-experienced annotators. Results reveal systematic degradation when disability cues are present: models produce longer, more speculative narratives, shift toward affective and deficit-oriented framing, and show intersectional amplification (e.g., white men receive the highest speculative enrichment). Statistical tests confirm significant drops in regard and increases in verbosity (p<0.05). Mitigation strategies — targeted prompting and Direct Preference Optimization (DPO) — substantially reduce these interpretive shifts, demonstrating that the biases are addressable.

## Method Summary
The evaluation employs the synthetic PAIRS dataset containing photorealistic images of individuals with balanced demographic attributes (race, gender, age). For each image, two prompt types are used: a Neutral Prompt (e.g., “Describe this person.”) and a Disability-Contextualized Prompt (e.g., “Describe this person considering they may have a ##disability##”) where “##disability##” is substituted with one of nine disability categories. Fifteen VLMs (both open- and closed-source) are run in zero-shot mode (temperature=0, max_tokens=512) to generate descriptions. Degradation is quantified using three automated text metrics: VADER (sentiment), Regard (social respect), and verbosity (token count). Additionally, a Qwen2.5-72B-Instruct judge compares the two responses across four dimensions: speculative inference (Interpret), stereotype activation (Stereotype), deficit framing (Framing), and refusal (Abstain). Statistical significance is assessed via ANOVA, and mitigation is tested by adding a “factual and neutral” instruction to the prompt and by applying DPO with neutral captions as preferred outputs. Human annotators with disability experience validate a subset of judge decisions, showing high agreement.
Assumption: The validation subset is representative of the full dataset's error profile.

## Key Results
- Across all models, disability context triggers consistent degradation in interpretive fidelity, manifested as verbosity inflation (up to ~90% in some models) and increased speculative inference.
- Affective shifts (VADER/Regard drops) and deficit-oriented framing occur more frequently; intersectional analysis reveals that white men receive the most speculative enrichment while Black women receive the most constrained descriptions.
- Both targeted prompting and DPO reduce interpretation shifts by >30 percentage points on the worst-performing models, confirming mitigability.
- Notable individual model failures include Claude-Haiku-4.5 (95.78% verbosity inflation), Gemma-3-4B (94.28% speculative inference), and GPT-4o (41.67% abstention).

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1: Asymmetric Modality Weighting
Disability cues in the prompt cause the model to rely excessively on linguistic priors over visual evidence. The textual signal activates stereotypical narratives from training data, leading to speculative inferences and narrative elaboration. This suggests that the fusion architecture does not maintain balanced evidence attribution under semantically loaded prompts.

### Mechanism 2: Training Data Correlation
Web-scale training data often associates disability terms with emotional or evaluative language (e.g., inspiration tropes, pity). When disability is mentioned, the model retrieves these correlated patterns, shifting sentiment and framing even without visual justification.

### Mechanism 3: Intersectional Amplification
Disability bias intersects with race and gender stereotypes encoded in the model. The study finds a stable ordering: white men receive the highest speculative enrichment, while Black women receive the most constrained descriptions. This reflects compounded social hierarchies present in the training corpus.

## Foundational Learning
### Interpretive Fidelity
- **Why needed:** The core metric in the study, capturing whether model outputs stay anchored to observable evidence and normative disability standards. Without it, surface sentiment analysis would miss crucial speculative inferences.
- **Quick check:** For an image of a person using a wheelchair, how would you operationalize “no inference beyond observable evidence” in a description?

### Paired Prompt Evaluation (NP vs. DP)
- **Why needed:** The methodological backbone. Comparing Neutral Prompt (NP) and Disability-Contextualized Prompt (DP) responses for the same image isolates the effect of disability framing, providing a counterfactual design that controls for image-specific characteristics.
- **Quick check:** What key threat to validity does this paired design eliminate that a between-image comparison would not?

### Normative Grounding in AI Auditing
- **Why needed:** The paper anchors evaluation in external disability rights standards (CRPD, WCAG, NCDJ) rather than purely statistical fairness. This shifts the goal from equal error rates to equitable representation as defined by affected communities.
- **Quick check:** Why might a model show low sentiment bias (VADER) yet still violate disability representation standards?

## Architecture Onboarding
### Component Map
PAIRS Dataset -> Neutral/Disability Contextualized Prompts -> VLM (15 models) -> Text Metrics (VADER, Regard, Verbosity) and LLM-Judge (Qwen2.5-72B) -> Statistical Analysis (ANOVA, significance) -> Mitigation (Targeted Prompting, DPO) -> Re-evaluation.

### Critical Path
Image selection from PAIRS → generate NP and 9 DP responses per image → compute text-based deltas and run LLM-judge pairwise comparison → identify significant degradation patterns → apply targeted prompt or DPO → repeat evaluation to measure improvement.

### Design Tradeoffs
- *Synthetic vs. real images:* PAIRS enables controlled demographics but may lack authentic disability cues and real-world complexity.
- *LLM-judge vs. human evaluation:* Scalable and shows high agreement but may miss subtle tropes; human validation is gold-standard but costly.
- *Zero-shot vs. fine-tuned:* Zero-shot reveals inherent biases; mitigation requires fine-tuning (DPO) for robust change.

### Failure Signatures
- Verbosity inflation >70% (e.g., Claude-Haiku-4.5: 95.78%).
- High “Interpret” score (e.g., Gemma-3-4B: 94.28%) indicating speculative inference.
- Significant Regard drop (p<0.05) with medium/large Cohen’s d.
- Abstention increase (e.g., GPT-4o: 41.67%) suggesting safety overblocking.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
1. **Visual Attention Patterns** – What attention distributions do VLMs exhibit under neutral vs. disability-conditioned prompts, and how do they correlate with interpretive fidelity? The current study infers linguistic dominance but lacks empirical grounding analysis.
2. **Prompt Sentiment Interaction** – How does the sentiment polarity of prompts interact with disability context to influence severity of interpretation shifts? The fixed prompt templates prevent disentangling this effect.
3. **Generalization to Real Imagery & Tasks** – Do observed biases extend to real-world images and downstream multimodal tasks (e.g., VQA, content moderation)? The synthetic PAIRS dataset and captioning-only evaluation limit external validity.
4. **Scalability of Mitigations** – How effective are targeted prompting and DPO across larger, diverse model families (e.g., GPT-4o, Gemini) and in production settings? Initial results are limited to smaller open-source models.

## Limitations
- PAIRS dataset is unreleased and synthetic, hindering exact replication and possibly omitting authentic disability cues and real-world intersectional complexities.
- LLM-as-judge dependency on Qwen2.5-72B-Instruct introduces variability; human validation covers only a subset, potentially missing subtle biases.
- Text metrics (VADER, Regard) are coarse and may not fully align with disability rights standards; zero-shot evaluation may not reflect fine-tuned deployments.
- The evaluation is limited to image captioning; generalizability to other multimodal tasks (e.g., VQA, content moderation) is unknown.

## Next Checks
1. **External validation with real-world disability images** – Source a public corpus of authentic disability imagery and repeat NP/DP evaluation on a diverse set of VLMs to test generalizability beyond synthetic data.
2. **Judge robustness audit** – Re-run LLM-as-judge using multiple LLMs (e.g., GPT-4o, Claude-3.5, Llama-3.1-70B) and compute inter-rater reliability; if agreement is low (<0.7), incorporate human adjudication to recalibrate thresholds.
3. **Mitigation portability test** – Apply DPO to a distinct model family (e.g., Phi-4) using the same preference pairs and measure improvement on held-out intersectional subgroups to confirm transferability.
