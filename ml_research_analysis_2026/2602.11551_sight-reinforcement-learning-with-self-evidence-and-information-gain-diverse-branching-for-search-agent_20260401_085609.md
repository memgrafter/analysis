# SIGHT: Reinforcement Learning with Self-Evidence and Information-Gain Diverse Branching for Search Agent

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2602.11551  
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.11551  
- **Reference count:** 40  
- **Primary result:** SIGHT achieves state-of-the-art accuracy with fewer tool calls on seven QA benchmarks by using self-evidence distillation and information-gain-driven branching.

## Executive Summary
SIGHT addresses tunnel vision in multi-turn search agents caused by noisy retrievals. It introduces Self-Evidence Support (SES) to distill raw results into clean evidence and Information-Gain (IG) scoring to identify pivotal states. IG-driven dynamic prompting (de-duplication, reflection, adaptive branching) guides exploration toward high-utility branches while masking hints during training via GRPO with hierarchical rewards. On seven QA benchmarks with Qwen2.5-3B/7B, SIGHT achieves higher exact match accuracy with significantly fewer tool calls, especially on complex multi-hop questions.

## Method Summary
The SIGHT agent interleaves reasoning with search. After each search, it generates a `<self-evidence>` statement that distills retrieved documents into noise-free evidence, conditioning subsequent thoughts. An IG score, computed as the pointwise mutual information between an observation and the ground truth given the history, determines intervention: low IG triggers reflection, high IG spawns a new branch with the SES evidence, and repeated high-similarity contexts trigger de-duplication. Branching is managed via shared prefix caching for efficiency. Training uses GRPO with hierarchical rewards: an answer reward (F1 + bonus) and an SES reward (λ=0.2 if ground truth appears in concatenated self-evidence). During loss computation, hint tokens and raw `<result>` contents are masked to prevent reliance on them.

## Key Results
- On Qwen2.5-3B: average EM 37.30% vs. ARPO 36.00% with tool calls (TC) 1.71 vs. 3.10.
- On Qwen2.5-7B: EM 40.66% vs. 39.75% with TC 1.59 vs. 2.15.
- On 3+ hop questions: EM 31.98% vs. ARPO 30.46% and Search-R1 25.36% with TC 2.21.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
SIGHT combats tunnel vision through two synergistic mechanisms. First, SES acts as a denoising filter: after retrieval, the agent generates distilled evidence that focuses on answer-relevant information, reducing the impact of noisy documents. Second, IG scoring (during training) identifies states that significantly update the answer distribution; high IG states trigger branching to explore alternatives, while low IG states trigger reflection. Dynamic prompting injects task-specific hints based on these signals, efficiently guiding exploration. GRPO with hierarchical rewards and masking ensures the policy learns to generate correct reasoning without relying on hints or raw results. This combination promotes efficient, robust multi-hop reasoning.

## Architecture Onboarding
### Component Map
Question -> Agent (Qwen2.5 policy) -> Decide (thought/search) -> If search: Search API (E5+Wikipedia) -> Raw results -> SES module (LLM distillation) -> Self-evidence -> History update; If thought: direct history update. After each step, IG scorer (oracle PMI) computes score -> Dynamic Prompter injects hint (de-dup/reflection/branch) -> Branching manager (shared prefix cache). Termination: answer extracted -> Reward (Answer F1 + SES bonus) -> GRPO update (masking hints/raw results).

### Critical Path
1. Agent generates thought or search action.  
2. If search: retrieve documents and generate SES.  
3. Append thought/SES to history.  
4. Compute IG for latest observation.  
5. Based on IG/history, decide hint injection and potential branching.  
6. Repeat until answer or max steps.  
7. Evaluate answer and compute hierarchical reward.  
8. During training, collect trajectory group and perform GRPO update.

### Design Tradeoffs
- **SES cost vs. accuracy:** Extra LLM call for distillation improves accuracy but increases latency.
- **Fixed IG thresholds:** Static thresholds (δ_low=0, δ_high=0.5) simplify but may not adapt to query difficulty.
- **Branching budget:** Partial sampling (M-N) balances exploration and compute; mis设置 leads to under-/over-exploration.
- **Hint masking:** Prevents crutch reliance but requires careful implementation to avoid masking learning signals.

### Failure Signatures
- **High TC + low accuracy:** IG miscalibration or branching on uninformative states.
- **Degraded single-hop EM:** SES over-distillation or reward misalignment.
- **Performance drop without hints:** Policy relies on hints; masking failed.
- **Early budget exhaustion:** Insufficient exploration for complex questions.

### First Experiments
1. Ablate SES reward (R_SES) and measure impact on single-hop vs. multi-hop EM.
2. Disable hints during inference rollout; quantify accuracy drop.
3. Vary IG thresholds (e.g., 0.3, 0.5, 0.7) and observe EM/TC trade-off.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
1. **Inference-time IG estimation:** How does SIGHT decide interventions during inference without ground truth, given IG depends on it?
2. **SES utility conditions:** When does SES help vs. distract, and can its generation be adaptively regulated?
3. **Threshold sensitivity:** How sensitive are fixed IG thresholds, and can they be auto-adapted?
4. **Generalizability:** Can SIGHT extend beyond QA to other tool-use tasks (e.g., web navigation, code generation)?

## Limitations
- **Score_IG ambiguity:** Unclear whether PMI uses policy or oracle probabilities; risks miscalibration.
- **Hint integration vagueness:** Template format and injection logic are unspecified; masking errors may cause leakage.
- **SES generalization:** May harm single-hop QA; evidence quality unvalidated beyond reward signal.

## Confidence
- **Results claims (EM/TC improvements):** High  
- **Methodology (GRPO masking, dynamic prompting):** Medium  
- **Theoretical justification (IG as pivotal detector):** Low
