# Drift Localization using Conformal Predictions
## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2602.19790
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.19790
- Reference count: 13
- Primary result: A conformal prediction-based method for drift localization surpasses state-of-the-art local tests in ROC-AUC across image datasets, with decision trees excelling on subtle drift.

## Executive Summary
The paper addresses drift localization—identifying samples affected by distribution change—in high-dimensional, low-signal settings like image streams where existing local statistical tests fail. It proposes a novel approach using conformal prediction: a classifier is trained to predict temporal labels from features, and prediction sets from conformal calibration indicate drift if they exclude some time points. Calibration is performed via bootstrapping with out-of-bag samples and median aggregation to stabilize p-value estimates.

Evaluated on Fashion-MNIST, NINCO, and a new Fish-Head dataset with subtle drift, the method (CP) using decision trees or multi-layer perceptrons consistently achieves higher ROC-AUC than baselines like kdq-tree and LDD-DIS. On the challenging Fish-Head benchmark, CP with decision trees performs best, and performance stabilizes with approximately 100–300 bootstraps.

## Method Summary
The method trains a supervised classifier to predict time \( T \) from features \( X \). For each sample \( x \), conformal calibration computes p-values \( p_y(x) \) for every time point \( y \) by comparing the classifier's score to a calibration set. A sample is considered drifting if its minimal p-value \( p_{\text{drift}}(x) = \min_{y} p_y(x) \) falls below a significance level \( \alpha \), meaning at least one time point is excluded from its prediction set. Calibration sets are formed using out-of-bag samples from bootstrap replicates, and final p-values are aggregated via the median across bootstraps to reduce variance from small calibration sets.

## Key Results
- CP with decision trees or MLPs outperforms baselines (kdq-tree, LDD-DIS, model-based) on Fashion-MNIST, NINCO, and Fish-Head.
- On the subtle-drift Fish-Head benchmark, CP with decision trees achieves the best ROC-AUC.
- Performance stabilizes with approximately 100–300 bootstraps.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1
- **Claim:** Conformal prediction replaces local statistical testing with a global variance analysis, avoiding suboptimal groupings in high-dimensional spaces.
- **Mechanism:** A classifier is trained to predict time \( T \) from features \( X \). For a sample \( x \), conformal calibration yields p-values \( p_y(x) \) for each time point \( y \in \mathcal{T} \). The minimal p-value \( p_{\text{drift}}(x) = \min_{y \in \mathcal{T}} p_y(x) \) tests whether \( x \) is drifting: if \( p_{\text{drift}}(x) < \alpha \), the null hypothesis (non-drifting) is rejected. This leverages the theoretical result that non-drifting points maximize conditional entropy \( H(T|X=x) \), leading to uniform p-values.
- **Core assumption:** The classifier can learn a meaningful mapping from \( X \) to \( T \), and non-drifting points yield uniformly distributed conformal p-values under the null.
- **Evidence anchors:** Abstract states replacing local testing with conformal prediction; Section 3 defines rejection when prediction set excludes a time point.
- **Break condition:** If the classifier fails to learn \( T|X \) (e.g., due to insufficient model capacity or uninformative features), p-values will not be uniform for non-drifting points, inflating false positives/negatives.

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim:** Bootstrapping with out-of-bag (OOB) samples provides a calibration set without sacrificing training data, stabilizing p-value estimation.
- **Mechanism:** For each bootstrap replicate, the model is trained on in-bag samples. OOB samples serve as a calibration set to compute nonconformity scores for each class. In-bag samples receive p-values based on their rank among OOB scores. The final p-value per sample is the median across bootstraps, aggregating evidence while mitigating variance from small calibration sets.
- **Core assumption:** OOB samples are i.i.d. from the same distribution as in-bag samples; median aggregation yields a consistent test statistic.
- **Evidence anchors:** Abstract describes bootstrapping with OOB calibration sets; Algorithm 1 lines 5–9 detail the OOB calibration loop.
- **Break condition:** If bootstraps produce highly divergent models (e.g., high variance in \( X \rightarrow T \) learning), the median p-value becomes unstable, especially with very few bootstraps.

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim:** The method is model-agnostic, unlike grouping-based local tests, allowing any classifier that outputs class-probability scores to be used.
- **Mechanism:** Any scoring function \( f(c|x,\theta) \) (e.g., from a probabilistic classifier) can define nonconformity. Conformal calibration then transforms these scores into p-values without requiring the model to induce explicit groupings (e.g., trees or neighborhoods).
- **Core assumption:** The chosen scoring function provides monotonically decreasing confidence for correct classes; poorly calibrated scores may distort p-values.
- **Evidence anchors:** Section 3 emphasizes no longer being limited to specific models; Section 4 compares decision trees vs. MLP.
- **Break condition:** If the classifier's scores are not meaningful for ranking (e.g., constant probabilities), conformal p-values will lose discrimination power.

## Foundational Learning
- **Concept: Conformal prediction calibration**  
  Why needed: The core drift test relies on p-values derived from comparison to a calibration set; understanding CP's coverage guarantees is essential to interpret \( p_{\text{drift}}(x) \).  
  Quick check: How does the choice of nonconformity score affect the validity of the p-values for drift detection?
- **Concept: Concept drift as temporal dependence**  
  Why needed: The paper formalizes drift as dependence between \( X \) and \( T \); H0: \( P_{T|X=x} = P_T \) requires estimating conditional vs. marginal time distributions.  
  Quick check: Why does maximal entropy \( H(T|X=x) \) characterize non-drifting points under uniform \( P_T \)?
- **Concept: Bootstrap aggregation for variance reduction**  
  Why needed: Bootstrapping provides multiple calibration sets; median aggregation stabilizes p-values against small-sample noise in high dimensions.  
  Quick check: What is the trade-off between number of bootstraps and computational cost versus p-value stability?

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map:** Dataset -> Classifier Module -> Conformal Wrapper -> Bootstrap Orchestrator -> Aggregator
- **Critical path:** For each bootstrap replicate: (1) split data into in-bag and OOB, (2) train classifier on in-bag, (3) compute p-values for in-bag samples using OOB scores, (4) store minimal p-value per sample. After B bootstraps, (5) take median of stored p-values as final test statistic.
- **Design tradeoffs:**  
  - *Number of bootstraps:* >300 yields diminishing returns; use 100–300 for balance.  
  - *Model choice:* Decision trees better for subtle drift; MLP for clearer drift. Match model capacity to data complexity.  
  - *Calibration set size:* OOB fraction ~0.32; more bootstraps increase coverage.
- **Failure signatures:**  
  - Low ROC-AUC on Fish-Head with MLP: switch to decision trees.  
  - High p-value variance across bootstraps: increase B or ensure classifier convergence.  
  - Many samples unassigned: too few bootstraps; use oversampling scheme.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- **Open Question 1:** How can the method be extended to maintain high detection power in extremely low-drift scenarios (<5% drifting samples)?  
  Basis: Conclusion explicitly states future work for small-sample drift.  
  Why unresolved: Experiments use ≥8% drifting samples.  
  Evidence needed: Systematic experiments with 1–2% drift, modify calibration/aggregation for severe imbalance.
- **Open Question 2:** What is the effect of pre-trained embedding architecture (DINOv2 vs. CLIP/ResNet) on reliability and performance?  
  Basis: Conclusion notes embedding relevance.  
  Why unresolved: Only DINOv2 used for NINCO and Fish-Head.  
  Evidence needed: Replicate with multiple embeddings, correlate feature quality (e.g., linear separability) with ROC-AUC.
- **Open Question 3:** What are the statistical properties (type I error, FDR) of the median-aggregated p-value test under the null?  
  Basis: Inferred; paper focuses on ROC-AUC, not error control.  
  Why unresolved: Bootstrapping and median aggregation may affect error rates; no theoretical analysis.  
  Evidence needed: Simulations under no-drift (permuted time labels), estimate FPR at various α, compare with baselines.

## Limitations
- Theoretical guarantees for p-value uniformity under non-drifting data lack rigorous justification.
- Performance is classifier-dependent; no principled model selection guidance for new contexts.
- Computational scalability with bootstrapping is not analyzed for large datasets.
