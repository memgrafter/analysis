# Bridging the Simulation-to-Reality Gap in Electron Microscope Calibration via VAE-EM Estimation

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2603.16549
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.16549
- Reference count: 24
- Primary result: The method reduces aberration estimation error by a factor of 2 compared to prior automated approaches and converges to high accuracy within approximately 20 observations (roughly 20 seconds).

## Executive Summary
This paper addresses automated calibration of scanning transmission electron microscopes (STEMs), where high-dimensional, noisy Ronchigram images must be interpreted to correct optical aberrations. Existing data-driven methods use scalar quality metrics and train on simulated data, suffering from information loss and a simulation-to-reality gap. The proposed solution uses a variational autoencoder (VAE) to learn a multi-dimensional latent representation from simulated Ronchigrams, preserving more information about the aberration state. A joint expectation-maximization (EM) algorithm then simultaneously estimates the initial aberration state and the latent mapping, explicitly accounting for the simulation-to-reality gap. Global identifiability is established by enforcing a known symmetry constraint via a symmetrized Gaussian process kernel. On real STEM data collected across different operating days, the method reduces aberration estimation error by a factor of 2 compared to prior automated approaches and converges to high accuracy within approximately 20 observations (roughly 20 seconds), outperforming commercial solutions that take about a minute.

## Method Summary
The method comprises two main stages: (1) training a VAE on a large simulated dataset of Ronchigram power spectra to learn a low-dimensional latent space that preserves aberration-relevant information while inheriting a physical evenness symmetry; (2) online calibration using a joint EM algorithm that simultaneously estimates the unknown initial aberration state and the latent mapping from simulated to real data, modeled by a Gaussian process (GP) with a symmetrized kernel to enforce evenness. The EM alternates between an E-step that computes a posterior over candidate initial states using the current GP model, and an M-step that updates the GP posterior as a mixture conditioned on those candidates. To improve sample efficiency, the candidate set is adaptively refined by retaining high-weight candidates, perturbing them with Gaussian noise, and adding uniform samples. Input selection chooses the next aberration adjustment to maximize the expected posterior variance of the latent mapping, focusing on under-modeled regions. This framework explicitly accounts for simulation-to-reality mismatch and achieves fast convergence with few observations.

## Key Results
- Reduces aberration estimation error by a factor of 2 compared to prior automated approaches on real STEM data.
- Converges to high accuracy within approximately 20 observations (roughly 20 seconds), while commercial solutions take about a minute.
- Outperforms Bayesian optimization-based methods in both accuracy and convergence speed.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1: VAE learns a low-dimensional latent representation preserving aberration information and symmetry
The VAE encoder maps high-dimensional, noisy Ronchigram power spectra (preprocessed via Hann windowing, 2D Fourier transform, and squaring) to a Gaussian distribution in a latent space whose dimensionality equals the number of aberration parameters. Training on simulated data that satisfies the evenness property \( h(-x)=h(x) \) (from Fourier optics) ensures the learned latent mapping \( f(x)=\mu_\alpha(P\{y\}) \) inherits this symmetry: \( f(-x)=f(x) \). This preserves information about the aberration state while reducing dimensionality. *Core assumption:* Simulated training data sufficiently covers the aberration space and preprocessing maintains evenness. *Break condition:* Insufficient data diversity or altered preprocessing breaks symmetry or loses information.

### Mechanism 2: Joint EM estimation with symmetry-constrained GP ensures global identifiability
The EM algorithm simultaneously estimates the initial aberration state \( x_0 \) and the latent mapping \( f \) (which accounts for simulation-to-reality gap). The GP prior on \( f \) uses a symmetrized kernel \( k(x,x')=\frac{1}{2}(k_0(x,x')+k_0(x,-x')) \) to enforce evenness. Under conditions (evenness, spectral richness, etc.), the pair \( (x_0,f) \) is globally identifiable (Theorem 2), meaning a unique optimum exists. The EM alternates: E-step computes posterior over discrete candidates \( x_0^{(i)} \) via GP marginal likelihood; M-step updates GP posterior as a mixture conditioned on candidates. The gap between simulated and real \( f \) is absorbed by the GP adapting to real observations. *Core assumption:* True \( f \) is even and well-approximated by the GP; candidate set dense enough. *Break condition:* Violation of evenness (e.g., instrument asymmetry) or GP misspecification (under/overfitting).

### Mechanism 3: Adaptive candidate refinement and variance-based input selection achieve fast convergence
After each EM update, the candidate set \( \{x_0^{(i)}\} \) is refined: high-weight candidates are retained; new candidates are generated by perturbing high-weight ones with Gaussian noise; a small fraction of uniform samples maintains global coverage. For input selection, the GP posterior variance (approximated using only the most likely candidate for efficiency) is evaluated over possible inputs \( u \); the input maximizing expected variance at the next state is chosen, steering the system to under-modeled regions to reduce uncertainty quickly. *Core assumption:* Posterior mode is accurate enough for hard EM approximation; perturbing high-weight candidates explores local basin effectively; maximizing variance is a valid information-gain heuristic. *Break condition:* Poorly tuned refinement variance \( \sigma^2 \) (premature collapse or excessive diffuseness) or non-dominant most-likely candidate leading to misguidance.

## Foundational Learning
- **Concept: Variational Autoencoders (VAEs)**  
  *Why needed:* To compress high-dimensional, noisy Ronchigram power spectra into a low-dimensional latent space that retains aberration-relevant information, avoiding the information loss of scalar metrics.  
  *Quick check:* If the VAE encoder's mean function \( \mu_\alpha(\cdot) \) is applied to two Ronchigrams from aberration states \( x \) and \( -x \), what property should their latent outputs satisfy, and why?  

- **Concept: Expectation-Maximization (EM) for Joint State-Model Estimation**  
  *Why needed:* To simultaneously estimate the unknown initial aberration state \( x_0 \) and the reality-mismatched latent mapping \( f \) from simulated training, where the joint problem is ill-posed without structural constraints.  
  *Quick check:* In the EM E-step, what quantity is computed for each candidate \( x_0^{(i)} \), and how does it depend on the current GP model of \( f \)?  

- **Concept: Symmetry-Constrained Gaussian Processes**  
  *Why needed:* To provide a flexible, non-parametric model for \( f \) that enforces the physical evenness property \( f(-x)=f(x) \), which is key to establishing global identifiability of the joint estimation problem.  
  *Quick check:* How does the symmetrized kernel \( k(x,x')=\frac{1}{2}(k_0(x,x')+k_0(x,-x')) \) ensure that any function drawn from the GP prior is even, assuming \( \mu_f \) is even?

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component map:** Preprocessing (Hann window → 2D DFT → power spectrum) → VAE encoder \( \mu_\alpha \) → latent vector \( z \) → GP model (symmetrized kernel) → candidate manager (discrete set \( \{x_0^{(i)}\} \)) → input selector (variance maximization) → EM loop (E-step: candidate weights via GP likelihood; M-step: GP posterior update) → new input application.

**Critical path:** Acquire new Ronchigram \( y(t) \) → preprocess to \( \tilde{y}(t) \) → encode to \( z(t)=\mu_\alpha(\tilde{y}(t)) \) → augment dataset \( \{z(\tau), s(\tau)\}_{\tau=0}^t \) → run EM iterations → update candidate set → select next input \( u(t) \) → apply input and repeat.

**Design tradeoffs:**
- VAE training data: Larger, more diverse simulated datasets improve latent representation but increase training cost; insufficient coverage harms generalization.
- Candidate set size \( N \): Larger \( N \) improves E-step accuracy but increases computational cost \( O(N T^3) \) per EM iteration; refinement reduces needed \( N \) over time.
- EM vs. hard EM: Full EM maintains mixture posterior, better exploration but higher memory/computation; hard EM commits to single candidate, faster but risk of local optima.
- Latent dimension \( \ell \): Setting \( \ell=n \) matches parameter count; \( \ell>n \) might capture extra variations but could introduce redundancy or noise.

**Failure signatures:**
- Latent space shows poor structure (e.g., no clustering or broken symmetry).
- EM weights \( w_i \) remain nearly uniform after many observations, indicating poor discriminability.
- Estimation error plateaus well above acceptable thresholds even after 30+ observations.
- Input selector repeatedly chooses similar inputs, failing to explore diverse aberration states.

**First 3 experiments:**
1. **VAE latent space validation:** Train VAE on simulated Ronchigrams with two aberration parameters (C1, A1x). Visualize 2D latent space to confirm that (a) similar aberrations cluster, (b) antipodal points \( (x,-x) \) map near identically, and (c) different quadrants are separated.
2. **Synthetic gap closure:** Introduce a known systematic bias (e.g., scaling) into simulated Ronchigrams to mimic reality. Run VAE-EM and verify it recovers the biased mapping \( f \) and corrects \( x_0 \) estimation, while a baseline using the simulated \( f \) directly fails.
3. **Real-data convergence:** On a real STEM dataset, run VAE-EM from random initial candidates. Plot estimation error vs. number of observations and confirm: (a) convergence within ~20 observations, (b) final error significantly lower than prior Bayesian optimization method, and (c) consistency across random initializations (narrow spread).

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
1. **Scalability to higher-order aberrations:** How can the VAE-EM framework be effectively scaled to higher-order aberrations, and what hierarchical calibration strategies best address computational challenges from GP scaling and the curse of dimensionality?
2. **EM convergence guarantees:** Under what additional conditions on the basis functions and observation noise distribution does the EM algorithm converge to the unique global optimum identified by the global identifiability theorem?
3. **Simulation data coverage:** What is the minimal required diversity and coverage of simulated aberration states needed to train a VAE that generalizes to real microscope day-to-day variations, and how does this relate to the simulation-to-reality gap?
4. **General structural constraints for identifiability:** What classes of structural constraints (beyond the evenness property) can ensure global identifiability in joint state-model estimation problems where the observation mapping is non-injective?
5. **Training on real data:** How does training the VAE on real (versus simulated) STEM data affect calibration performance, and what amount of real data suffices to make the simulation-to-reality gap negligible?

## Limitations
- Insufficient details on simulated dataset (simulator parameters, aberration ranges, noise model) hinder faithful replication.
- GP hyperparameters and prior mean fitting remain unspecified; mis-specification risks under/overfitting.
- Real instruments may violate the evenness symmetry, undermining identifiability guarantees.
- Adaptive candidate refinement and input selection rely on heuristic tuning with unclear sensitivity.

## Confidence
- **Mechanism 1 (VAE latent symmetry):** Medium. Inheritance of evenness depends on adequate VAE capacity and training diversity; break conditions include data insufficiency and preprocessing changes.
- **Mechanism 2 (Joint EM identifiability):** High. Theoretical proof under physical assumptions is rigorous; assumes GP correctly specifies the latent mapping.
- **Mechanism 3 (Adaptive sample efficiency):** High. Real-data experiments demonstrate convergence within ~20 observations, though cross-instrument robustness and tuning sensitivity require further validation.

## Next Checks
1. **VAE capacity ablation:** Train VAEs with varying latent dimensions and encoder/decoder depth; evaluate latent clustering (e.g., silhouette score) and downstream estimation error to confirm robustness.
2. **Symmetry violation stress test:** Introduce controlled odd-phase distortions into real Ronchigrams; measure degradation in estimation accuracy to quantify reliance on evenness.
3. **Hyperparameter sensitivity:** Systematically vary GP lengthscale, candidate refinement noise scale, and candidate set size; track convergence speed and final error to establish robust defaults and identify failure regimes.
