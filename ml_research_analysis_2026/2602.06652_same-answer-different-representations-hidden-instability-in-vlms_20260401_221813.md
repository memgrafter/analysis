# Same Answer, Different Representations: Hidden instability in VLMs

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID**: 2602.06652
- **Source URL**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.06652
- **Reference count**: 40
- **Primary result**: Output-level robustness in VLMs can mask substantial internal representation drift, with larger models exhibiting greater sensitivity to semantics-preserving perturbations despite higher accuracy.

## Executive Summary
This paper reveals a critical hidden flaw in Vision-Language Model (VLM) evaluation: standard accuracy metrics fail to detect significant internal representation instability. Even when models produce correct answers under natural image perturbations like translations or text overlays, their internal embeddings, spectral properties, and spatial token structures drift substantially—sometimes approaching the variability between entirely different images. The study introduces a multi-level evaluation framework measuring embedding drift, spectral sensitivity, and spatial smoothness (via Dirichlet energy) across multiple benchmarks. Key findings show that larger models, while more accurate, are not more robust internally; perturbations disrupt multi-scale visual reasoning but can paradoxically reduce hallucinations by shifting predictions toward a skeptical language prior. The work demonstrates that VLM robustness cannot be assessed by output alone and that multimodal alignment remains fragile.

## Method Summary
The paper presents a zero-shot evaluation framework applied to Qwen3-VL and LLaVA-OneVision models on SEEDBench, MMMU, and POPE benchmarks. A suite of natural, semantics-preserving perturbations (translation, pad/crop, scale, rotation, text overlays) is applied to images before standard preprocessing. Output stability is measured via Instance Flip Rate (IFR) and Image Vulnerability (IV). Internal stability is probed at five representational regimes (open/MCQ context and answer tokens) using final LLM layer embeddings, computing cosine/L2 distances and Cohen’s d against a control baseline. Spatial smoothness is quantified by ΔDirichlet Energy computed on the reshaped vision token grid. For POPE, a drift-to-prior metric isolates how internal changes affect hallucination rates. All metrics aggregate across multiple perturbation instances per image.

## Key Results
- Representation drift under text overlays approaches inter-image variability (Cohen’s d ≈ -0.34), indicating non-local internal displacement despite unchanged outputs.
- Larger models achieve higher accuracy but exhibit equal or greater sensitivity, with flip rates increasing with scale.
- Perturbations harm reasoning tasks (SEEDBench, MMMU) by disrupting multi-scale cues but reduce POPE hallucinations by shifting predictions toward a skeptical prior.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The paper does not propose a mechanistic explanation for the observed instability; it is a diagnostic study that demonstrates the phenomenon without identifying causal training or architectural factors.

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component Map**: Image -> Vision Encoder -> Vision Projector -> LLM Decoder -> Output Tokens

**Critical Path**: Input image is encoded into a grid of patch tokens by the vision encoder. These tokens are projected and interleaved with text tokens, forming a unified sequence fed to the LLM decoder. The decoder’s final hidden states at specific token positions are the probe points. Perturbations alter the visual token sequence, which propagates through the LLM, potentially changing internal states even if output distributions remain similar.

**Design Tradeoffs**: 
- Patch-based vision encoders offer scalability but introduce discrete tokenization that may amplify small spatial shifts into large representation changes.
- Decoder-only LLMs facilitate flexible generation but may lack explicit multimodal alignment mechanisms, making fused representations sensitive to input formatting.
- Using final-layer hidden states provides a consistent probe point but may miss earlier-stage instabilities.

**Failure Signatures**:
- High baseline Dirichlet Energy on clean images indicates incorrect vision token grid reconstruction.
- Unexpected stability ordering across probe regimes (e.g., ctx_open more stable than ctx_mcq) suggests prompt misalignment.
- Discrepancy between base accuracy and reported values implies mishandling of multi-image inputs (MMMU).

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
1. What training objectives or architectural modifications can mitigate representation drift in VLMs while preserving accuracy on downstream tasks?
2. Do representation drift and spectral sensitivity generalize to VLMs with alternative architectures, such as those employing early fusion, encoder-decoder LLMs, or non-patch vision backbones?
3. What is the causal mechanism by which spectral drift reduces hallucinations in POPE, and does this reveal a fundamental trade-off between robustness and task performance?
4. Why do larger VLMs exhibit increased sensitivity to semantic perturbations despite higher base accuracy, and can decision boundary sharpness be quantitatively linked to representation drift?
5. Are Dirichlet energy and spectral sensitivity sufficient to capture all dimensions of representation instability, or do additional structural metrics (e.g., topological or information-theoretic) better predict robustness failures?

## Limitations
- Prompt templates and tokenization specifics for Qwen3-VL and LLaVA-OneVision are not fully detailed, risking probe point misalignment.
- Vision token grid reconstruction for Dirichlet Energy depends on model-specific patch ordering; the exact mapping is unclear.
- Results are limited to two model families and three datasets; generalization to other architectures or domains is unknown.

## Confidence
- **C1**: Hidden representation drift exists despite output stability. *Medium*
- **C2**: Larger models exhibit equal/greater sensitivity, with flip rates increasing with scale. *Medium*
- **C3**: Perturbations harm reasoning tasks yet can reduce POPE hallucinations. *Low*
