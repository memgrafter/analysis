# Learnable Koopman-Enhanced Transformer-Based Time Series Forecasting with Spectral Control

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2602.02592  
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.02592  
- **Reference Count:** 40  
- **Primary Result:** Koopman-enhanced Transformers achieve lower and more tightly concentrated forecasting errors (MSE, MAE) than LSTM, DLinear, and diagonal SSM baselines across climate, financial, and energy datasets, with stable spectral properties.

## Executive Summary
The paper tackles the rigidity of spectral control in existing Koopman-enhanced forecasting models, which typically rely on fixed or highly constrained operators that limit synergy with expressive Transformer architectures. It introduces four learnable Koopman operator parameterizations—scalar-gated, per-mode gated, MLP-shaped spectral mapping, and low-rank—that can be integrated into Transformer backbones like PatchTST, Autoformer, and Informer. These operators are trained with a standard forecasting loss augmented by a Lyapunov stability penalty, enabling explicit regulation of the latent linear dynamics’ spectrum, stability, and rank. Large-scale experiments on climate (CMIP6, ERA5), financial (cryptocurrency), and energy (electricity) data demonstrate consistent error reduction and improved stability, with learned eigenvalues predominantly in a contractive range (0.3–0.8), avoiding instability or collapse.

## Method Summary
The proposed method augments a Transformer encoder (e.g., PatchTST) with a learnable Koopman propagator $K_\phi$ that maps latent state $z_t$ to $z_{t+1}$. The propagator is factorized as $U_\phi \text{diag}(\Sigma_\phi) V_\phi^T$, where $U_\phi, V_\phi$ are enforced to be orthogonal via QR retraction, and $\Sigma_\phi = \rho_{\text{max}} \sigma(S)$ with $\sigma$ the sigmoid function and $S$ raw parameters controlling eigenvalue magnitudes. Four parameterizations vary how $S$ is structured: global scalars, per-mode values, an MLP output, or a low-rank factorization. A Lyapunov penalty $\lambda \text{ReLU}(\|z_{t+1}\|_P^2 - \|z_t\|_P^2)$ (with $\lambda=0.1$) discourages state energy growth, using a fixed positive-definite matrix $P$. Training minimizes MSE + penalty using Adam ($lr=3e^{-4}$) for 4000 steps per configuration. Inputs are sliding windows of length $P \in \{80,100,120,140\}$ from normalized multivariate series, split chronologically 80/20, predicting horizons $H \in \{4,8,12,16\}$.

## Key Results
- Koopman-enhanced Transformers yield significantly lower and less variable test MSE and MAE compared to LSTM, DLinear, and diagonal SSM baselines across all three data domains.
- All learnable operator variants maintain eigenvalue magnitudes predominantly in the stable, contractive range of 0.3–0.8, avoiding instability ($\rho \ge 1$) or collapse ($\rho \approx 0$).
- The constrained variant (spectrally bounded) produces the most compact error violin plots, indicating heightened stability and generalization.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The core mechanism is the introduction of a structured, learnable Koopman operator that directly controls the spectral properties of latent linear dynamics. By parameterizing $K_\phi$ with orthogonal factors and a bounded diagonal matrix, the model can adapt the linear propagator while inherently limiting eigenvalue magnitudes via the sigmoid and $\rho_{\text{max}}$. The Lyapunov penalty explicitly discourages state energy growth, further enforcing contractive behavior. This combination prevents the common failure modes of unbounded spectral radius (instability) or over-damping (collapse), leading to a better bias-variance trade-off, improved numerical conditioning, and interpretable latent dynamics—all while remaining compatible with powerful Transformer encoders that capture complex input patterns.

## Foundational Learning
- **Koopman Operator Theory:** Linearizes nonlinear dynamics in a lifted space; needed to model system evolution with a linear operator. *Quick check:* Eigenvalues indicate stability (magnitude <1 contractive).  
- **Transformer Architectures:** Provide strong sequence modeling via self-attention; needed to extract rich latent representations from raw time series. *Quick check:* Attention weights should focus on relevant time patches.  
- **Spectral Control:** Directly manipulating operator eigenvalues ensures stable forward propagation; needed to avoid divergence or information loss. *Quick check:* Maximum eigenvalue magnitude should stay below $\rho_{\text{max}}$ during training.  
- **Lyapunov Stability:** A function certifying stability of dynamical systems; needed as a differentiable penalty to enforce contraction. *Quick check:* Penalty term should decrease or stay near zero over epochs.  
- **Orthogonal Parameterization:** Ensures $U_\phi, V_\phi$ remain orthonormal; needed to preserve isometry and avoid scaling artifacts. *Quick check:* $U_\phi^T U_\phi$ and $V_\phi^T V_\phi$ should be close to identity matrices.

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component Map:**  
Input Sequence -> Transformer Encoder -> Latent State $z_t$ -> Learnable Koopman Operator $K_\phi$ -> Forecast Horizon

**Critical Path:** The integration point between the Transformer encoder output ($z_t$) and the Koopman propagator ($K_\phi$) is pivotal; $K_\phi$ replaces or complements typical decoder steps, directly advancing the latent state before final projection to forecasts.

**Design Tradeoffs:**  
- *Flexibility vs. Stability:* More expressive parameterizations (e.g., MLP-shaped) risk spectral deviation but may capture richer dynamics; constrained variants trade expressivity for guaranteed stability.  
- *Rank vs. Efficiency:* Low-rank factorization reduces parameters and computation but may limit the operator’s ability to represent full coupling across latent dimensions.

**Failure Signatures:**  
- Spectral instability: max eigenvalue magnitude $\rho \ge 1$ during training.  
- Spectral collapse: all $|\lambda| \ll 1$ (e.g., $<0.2$), causing vanishing latent signals.  
- Orthogonality violation: $U_\phi^T U_\phi$ or $V_\phi^T V_\phi$ significantly deviates from identity (Frobenius norm $>10^{-5}$).

**First Three Experiments:**  
1. Train a single Koopman-enhanced PatchTST on a small subset (e.g., ERA5) with default hyperparameters; verify training loss decreases and validation MSE improves over baselines.  
2. After training, extract $K_\phi$ and compute its eigenvalue spectrum; confirm all magnitudes lie below $\rho_{\text{max}}=0.99$ and cluster in 0.3–0.8 range.  
3. Compare constrained vs. unconstrained variants on the same validation set; measure difference in error spread (e.g., standard deviation of MSE across forecast horizons) to confirm constrained variant’s stability.

## Limitations
- The precise implementation of the “constrained” Koopman variant (fixed spectrum vs. distinct parameterization) is ambiguous, hindering exact replication.  
- The positive-definite matrix $P$ used in the Lyapunov penalty is not specified (e.g., identity, data covariance), affecting the penalty’s behavior.  
- Architectural details of the MLP-shaped spectral mapping (layers, hidden units, activations) are omitted, requiring inference from code.  
- Initialization schemes for raw parameters $S$ and orthogonal matrices $U_\phi, V_\phi$ are not described, potentially impacting training dynamics and spectral outcomes.
