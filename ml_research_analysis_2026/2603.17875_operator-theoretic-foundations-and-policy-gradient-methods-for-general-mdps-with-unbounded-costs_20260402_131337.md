# Operator-Theoretic Foundations and Policy Gradient Methods for General MDPs with Unbounded Costs

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2603.17875
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.17875
- Reference count: 40
- Primary result: An operator-theoretic framework for general MDPs (Polish spaces, unbounded costs) yields a majorization-minimization policy gradient method; for finite MDPs, MM-RKHS achieves substantially faster convergence and lower sample complexity than PPO in a 1000-state GARNET experiment.

## Executive Summary
This paper develops a rigorous operator-theoretic foundation for Markov decision processes (MDPs) with general state/action spaces (Polish) and unbounded cost functions. The transition kernel and policy are treated as bounded linear operators between appropriately weighted Banach spaces of value functions. This perspective enables the application of perturbation theory to derive a policy difference lemma and the Gateaux derivative of the value function with respect to the policy operator. To enable policy gradient optimization, the policy advantage is upper-bounded using an integral probability metric (IPM), leading to a majorization-minimization (MM) algorithm. For finite MDPs, choosing the IPM as maximum mean discrepancy (MMD) yields a closed-form update rule (MM-RKHS) that avoids iterative KL-divergence projections. Empirical evaluation on a 1000-state GARNET MDP demonstrates that MM-RKHS converges significantly faster and with lower sample complexity than PPO.

## Method Summary
The core method is an MM policy gradient algorithm. At each iteration, the current policy’s advantage function \(A^\pi\) is computed. A surrogate objective is formed by upper-bounding the policy performance gain \(L^\pi(\pi')\) with a quadratic term involving an IPM \(\text{IPM}_{F_A}(\pi, \pi')\) and the `\(\infty\)`-norm of \(A^\pi\). This surrogate is minimized over the policy class \(\Pi\) to obtain the next policy. For finite MDPs, using the MMD (with a linear kernel) as the IPM leads to a closed-form per-state policy update via a mirror descent step: \(\pi_{k+1}(s,a) \propto \pi_k(s,a) \exp(-\eta_k (A^{\pi_k}(s,a) - \beta_k \pi_k(s,a)))\), where \(\beta_k = \|A^{\pi_k}\|_\infty\). This avoids the numerical optimization required by PPO/TRPO’s KL constraint. The algorithm is evaluated on a deterministic GARNET MDP with 1000 states and 50 actions, comparing exact policy evaluation and updates against a standard tabular PPO implementation.

## Key Results
- The operator-theoretic reformulation of MDPs with unbounded costs yields a policy difference lemma and Gateaux derivative via linear operator perturbation theory.
- The majorization-minimization policy gradient method, with the IPM-based majorizer, provides a unifying view that recovers trust-region methods (e.g., TRPO) for specific IPM choices.
- For finite MDPs, the MM-RKHS algorithm (using MMD) attains a closed-form update and exhibits substantially faster convergence and lower sample complexity than PPO in a large-scale GARNET experiment.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- **Claim:** MDP optimization can be formulated as an objective function over linear operators acting on structured Banach spaces of value functions.
- **Mechanism:** The transition kernel \(P\) and policy \(\pi\) are viewed as bounded linear operators between appropriately weighted normed spaces \(\mathcal{F}_S\) and \(\mathcal{F}_K\). The performance objective \(\langle v^\pi, \rho \rangle\) is minimized over policy operators. This reframing allows application of perturbation theory for linear operators to derive sensitivity results.
- **Core assumption:** Assumptions 1–3 (well-posedness, compatibility of operators with structured spaces \(\mathcal{V}, \mathcal{Q}\)). The spaces must be Banach spaces or linear manifolds, and \(c \in \mathcal{Q}\).
- **Evidence anchors:** [abstract]: "Markov decision processes (MDPs) is viewed as an optimization of an objective function over certain linear operators over general function spaces." [section 3.1.2]: Formal definitions of operators \(P, \pi, P^\pi, T^\pi\). [corpus]: Weak signal – neighbor papers on "operator-theoretic" and "general MDPs" suggest this is a novel unifying framework.
- **Break condition:** If \(\mathcal{V}\) or \(\mathcal{Q}\) are not linear manifolds or if \(P\) or \(\pi\) are not bounded linear operators w.r.t. the weighted norms, the operator framework collapses. Spectral radius conditions (Assumption 2) may fail.

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim:** The Gateaux derivative of the value function with respect to the policy operator is exactly the policy advantage function \(L^\pi(\pi')\), derived via perturbation theory of linear operators.
- **Mechanism:** Using Lemma 2.1 (perturbation formula for inverse operators), the difference \(v^{\pi+\varepsilon\Delta} - v^\pi\) is expanded. The first-order term is identified as \(\sigma^\pi \Delta q^\pi = L^\pi(\pi')\). This avoids finite-difference approximations and generalizes policy gradient theorems to infinite-dimensional spaces.
- **Core assumption:** Policies \(\pi, \pi'\) must be spectrally stable (\(\Pi_{ss}\)) so that \((\text{id} - \gamma P^\pi)^{-1}\) exists as a bounded operator (Lemma A.1). Also requires Assumption 3 (\(P\mathcal{V} \subset \mathcal{Q}\), \(\pi\mathcal{Q} \subset \mathcal{V}\)).
- **Evidence anchors:** [section 5]: Lemma 5.4 (Policy Difference Lemma) and Lemma 5.5 (Gateaux derivative) are proved using operator perturbation (Eq. 2.2). [abstract]: "Using the well-established perturbation theory of linear operators… policy difference lemma is established for general MDPs."
- **Break condition:** If \(\text{spec}(\gamma P^\pi) \geq 1\) for a candidate \(\pi\), the Neumann series \((\text{id} - \gamma P^\pi)^{-1} = \sum \gamma^t (P^\pi)^t\) may diverge, invalidating the derivative expression. The structured space \(\mathcal{V}\) must be invariant under \(P^\pi\).

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim:** A majorization-minimization (MM) policy gradient algorithm is derived by upper-bounding the policy advantage \(L^\pi(\pi')\) via an integral probability metric (IPM), leading to a closed-form update for finite MDPs using maximum mean discrepancy (MMD).
- **Mechanism:** Lemma 5.8 shows the second-order term \(\gamma \sigma^\pi P^\Delta \sigma^{\pi'} \Delta q^\pi\) is bounded by \(\beta(q,\pi') w_S(s) \text{IPM}_{\Pi,w_S}(\pi,\pi')^2\). An upper bound on \(L^\pi(\pi')\) is formed: \(\langle \pi' A^\pi + \beta w_S \text{IPM}^2, \sigma_\pi^* \rho \rangle\). Minimizing this surrogate yields the MM step (Eq. 6.1). For finite MDPs with MMD (\(\text{IPM}_{\mathcal{F}_A}(s) = \text{MMD}\)), the minimization over the simplex has a closed-form solution via mirror descent (Eq. 7.2), avoiding KL-divergence optimization.
- **Core assumption:** Assumption 8 (\(\varrho_{\mathcal{F}_Q}(Pv) \leq \kappa_P \|v\|_{\mathcal{F}_S}\)) bounds the operator norm of \(P\) w.r.t. Minkowski functional. Also requires \(\mathcal{F}_Q\) to be a norm-generating IPM class (absolutely convex, separates points). Assumption 4 ensures the minimizer of the surrogate lies in \(\Pi\).
- **Evidence anchors:** [section 5.2]: Lemma 5.8 derives the majorization bound. [section 6.2]: Eq. (6.1) gives the general MM algorithm. [section 7]: Derivation of MM-RKHS with closed-form update (Eq. 7.2) and comparison to PPO/TRPO. [abstract]: "...bounding policy advantage via the theory of integral probability metric leads to a majorization-minimization policy gradient method."
- **Break condition:** If Assumption 8 fails (e.g., \(P\) is not bounded w.r.t. \(\varrho_{\mathcal{F}_Q}\)), the quadratic majorization may not hold. The MMD kernel choice must be positive definite; poor kernel choice may yield uninformative IPM. The closed-form mirror descent solution requires the IPM to be a quadratic form (like MMD), which may not hold for arbitrary IPMs.

## Foundational Learning
- **Concept: Perturbation Theory for Linear Operators (Kato)**
  - **Why needed here:** To derive the policy difference lemma and Gateaux derivative. The core identity \((A + \varepsilon B)^{-1} = A^{-1} - \varepsilon A^{-1}B(A+\varepsilon B)^{-1}\) (Lemma 2.1) is the engine for the first-order expansion of \(v^{\pi+\varepsilon\Delta}\). Without this, the derivative would require stronger assumptions (e.g., Fréchet differentiability in a Hilbert space).
  - **Quick check question:** Why is the Neumann series argument (\(\text{spec}(\gamma P^\pi)<1\)) insufficient to guarantee the derivative formula, and how does Lemma 2.1 provide a more robust tool?
- **Concept: Integral Probability Metrics (IPM) and Minkowski Functionals (Müller)**
  - **Why needed here:** Provides a unified, computable way to measure policy divergence \(\text{IPM}_{\Pi,w_S}(\pi,\pi')\). The bound in Lemma 5.8 relies on \(\varrho_{\mathcal{F}_Q}(q)\) to scale the IPM. Specific choices (e.g., MMD for finite MDPs) yield closed-form updates.
  - **Quick check question:** How does the choice of generator set \(\mathcal{F}_Q\) (e.g., unit ball in RKHS) affect the tightness of the majorization bound and the computational form of the MM step?
- **Concept: Banach Space Contraction arguments for MDP existence**
  - **Why needed here:** The paper's existence proof (Theorem 4.1) does *not* rely on \(T^\pi\) being a contraction in a weighted norm (unlike classic proofs). Instead, it uses monotonicity of value iteration, boundedness via Lemma B.1 (which uses \(\text{spec}(\gamma P^\pi)\) on a subspace), and closure of \(\mathcal{V}\) under increasing limits.
  - **Quick check question:** What is the key difference between the classic contraction mapping proof for MDP existence and the proof in Theorem 4.1? Why is this important for unbounded costs?

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map:** Policy Operator (\(\pi\)) -> Transition Operator (\(P\)) -> Bellman Operator (\(T^\pi\)) -> Policy Advantage (\(A^\pi\)) -> Policy Advantage Functional (\(L^\pi\)) -> IPM Divergence -> Majorizer (\(M^\pi\))
- **Critical path:** Existence Theorem (4.1) -> Policy Difference Lemma (5.4) -> Majorization Bound (5.8) -> MM Algorithm (6.2) -> Closed-form for Finite MDPs (7.1). Validate at each step: ensure \(\Pi_{stable} \neq \emptyset\) (Assumption 2), verify \(\mathcal{V}\) is closed under increasing limits (Assumption 5), confirm Assumption 8 (bounded \(P\)).
- **Design tradeoffs:**
  - **Unbounded costs vs. Banach spaces:** General state/action spaces (Polish) and unbounded costs force use of weighted Banach spaces (e.g., \(w_S(s)=1+s^\top s\)) instead of \(\ell_\infty\). This complicates analysis but enables LQR-like applications.
  - **IPM choice vs. computability:** General IPMs may not be easy to compute; MMD yields closed form but requires choosing a positive definite kernel (\(R\) matrix). Total variation (TRPO) requires KL projection, which is iterative.
  - **Structured policy class (\(\Pi\)) vs. optimality:** Assumption 4 (structured selectors) is crucial to ensure \(Tv \in \mathcal{V}\) and the optimal policy lies in \(\Pi\). Violation means algorithm may converge to a suboptimal structured policy.
- **Failure signatures:**
  - **Spectral instability:** \(\text{spec}(\gamma P^\pi) \geq 1\) for all \(\pi \in \Pi\) (violates Assumption 2). Value iteration may diverge; \(v^\pi\) undefined.
  - **Non-invariance:** \(P\mathcal{V} \not\subset \mathcal{Q}\) or \(\pi\mathcal{Q} \not\subset \mathcal{V}\) (violates Assumption 3). Bellman iterate \(Tv\) may leave \(\mathcal{V}\), breaking the iterative scheme.
  - **Non-closed \(\mathcal{V}\):** \(\mathcal{V}\) not closed under bounded increasing limits (violates Assumption 5). Limit \(v^*\) from value iteration may not be in \(\mathcal{V}\), so \(\pi^*\) not guaranteed in \(\Pi\).
  - **Unbounded majorization:** \(\varrho_{\mathcal{F}_Q}(Pv)\) not bounded by \(\kappa_P \|v\|_{\mathcal{F}_S}\) (violates Assumption 8). Quadratic bound in Lemma 5.8 fails; MM step may not be a valid majorizer.
  - **Kernel mismatch:** For MM-RKHS, chosen \(R\) is not positive definite or poorly scaled, leading to degenerate or numerically unstable updates (Eq. 7.2).
- **First 3 experiments:**
  1. **Validate operator stability:** For a simple MDP (e.g., small GARNET), compute \(\text{spec}(\gamma P^\pi)\) for a range of policies \(\pi \in \Pi\). Confirm \(\Pi_{ss}\) non-empty (Assumption 2). Use finite approximations (\(|S|,|A|\) small) to compute spectra.
  2. **Verify policy difference lemma numerically:** For two policies \(\pi, \pi'\) in \(\Pi_{ss}\), compute \(v^\pi, v^{\pi'}\) via solving linear systems (Finite MDP) and compare \(v^{\pi'} - v^\pi\) to \(\sigma^\pi \pi' A^\pi\) (from Eq. 5.1). Should match within numerical tolerance.
  3. **Implement MM-RKHS vs. PPO:** Replicate the 1000-state GARNET experiment (section 7.1). Use identical random seed, initial policy, and advantage estimation. Measure sample complexity (episodes to reach \(J^\pi \geq 0.95 J^*\)) and wall-clock time. Expect MM-RKHS to converge in fewer gradient steps due to second-order majorization.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- **Open Question 1:** How can the operator-theoretic framework and majorization-minimization policy gradient method be extended to Markov decision processes with structured transition kernels (e.g., derived from elliptical PDEs or measure-preserving dynamics) and sparse reward functions?
- **Open Question 2:** For continuous action spaces, what choice of integral probability metric (e.g., kernel for maximum mean discrepancy) and policy parametrization yields a closed-form or computationally tractable solution to the per-state MM update (Equation 6.1)?
- **Open Question 3:** Do the theoretical guarantees (e.g., monotonic policy improvement, convergence) of the proposed majorization-minimization algorithm hold when the IPM and advantage function are estimated from finite sampled trajectories, and what is the resulting sample complexity?
- **Open Question 4:** Are the key assumptions (e.g., Assumption 6: IPM-continuity of the transition kernel; Assumption 8: boundedness constant \(\kappa_P\)) necessary for the main results (existence of optimal policy, policy difference lemma, MM bound)? Can they be relaxed or verified for common MDP models?

## Limitations
- **Assumption coverage:** The framework requires spectral radius <1, space invariance, and bounded \(P\) w.r.t. IPM; these may not hold for many unbounded-cost or continuous MDPs.
- **Algorithmic scope:** MM-RKHS closed-form applies only to finite MDPs with MMD; scaling to function approximation or other IPMs is unresolved.
- **Empirical validation:** GARNET experiment lacks critical details (transition probabilities, cost distribution, PPO hyperparameters). Superiority claim needs reproducible, multi-domain evaluation.

## Confidence
- Operator-theoretic foundations (existence & gradient): **Medium**
- IPM majorization improves sample efficiency: **Low–Medium**
- Closed-form MM-RKHS update for finite MDPs: **High**
