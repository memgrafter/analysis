# UQ-SHRED: uncertainty quantification of shallow recurrent decoder networks for sparse sensing via engression

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2604.01305
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.01305
- **Reference count:** 40
- **Primary result:** Extends the SHRED architecture by injecting Gaussian noise into sensor inputs and training with an energy score loss, enabling a single network to produce well-calibrated predictive distributions for sparse sensing without architectural changes.

## Executive Summary
UQ-SHRED addresses the need for reliable uncertainty quantification in sparse sensing tasks where only a few measured values are available to reconstruct full spatiotemporal fields. It augment the deterministic SHRED framework by treating the network as a distributional predictor: a fixed noise vector is concatenated to each lagged sensor input, and the model is trained with the strictly proper energy score loss. At inference, Monte Carlo sampling of the noise yields an empirical predictive distribution, from which quantiles, credible intervals, and point estimates (e.g., median) are derived. Across five diverse scientific datasets—sea-surface temperature, turbulent flow, neural activity, solar activity, and propulsion physics—UQ-SHRED achieves competitive reconstruction accuracy (low RMSE and CRPS) and well-calibrated uncertainty estimates (observed coverage close to nominal 50–99% levels). Theoretical analysis supports consistency of the learned conditional distribution and quantiles under standard assumptions.

## Method Summary
UQ-SHRED modifies the SHRED pipeline by introducing stochasticity at the input level and changing the training objective. The input is a sliding window of `L` lagged sensor measurements (shape `L×p`). During training and inference, a Gaussian noise vector `ε ~ N(0, I_{d_ε})` is sampled once per forward pass and concatenated to every sensor vector in the window, preserving temporal consistency. The augmented sequence is processed by a GRU-based temporal unit, followed by a shallow fully-connected decoder that outputs a spatial state prediction. Training employs the energy score loss: for each training sample, two independent noise draws produce two predictions `ŷ₁` and `ŷ₂`; the loss `½(‖ŷ₁−y‖ + ‖ŷ₂−y‖) − ½‖ŷ₁−ŷ₂‖` is minimized. This repulsive term prevents collapse to a single point. At inference, `K` noise samples generate an empirical predictive distribution; coordinate-wise quantiles and intervals are derived from these samples. No architectural changes to GRU or decoder are required.

## Key Results
- UQ-SHRED produces full predictive distributions for sparse sensing with minimal computational overhead relative to deterministic SHRED.
- Calibration diagrams show observed coverage near nominal levels (e.g., 95% nominal → ~90–94% empirical) across all five datasets, indicating reliable uncertainty estimates.
- Reconstruction accuracy (RMSE, CRPS) remains competitive with or superior to deterministic SHRED and other baselines.
- Ablation studies confirm robustness to hyperparameters: sufficient noise dimension (`d_ε ≥ 100` for small sensor counts, `d_ε ≥ 1000` for larger problems) and Monte Carlo samples (`K ≥ 100`) are effective.
- Theoretical guarantees: consistency of the learned conditional distribution (Theorem 1) and convergence of empirical quantiles (Theorem 2) are established under standard assumptions.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
**Claim:** Injecting Gaussian noise into the sensor input transforms the deterministic SHRED into a distributional predictor.  
**Mechanism:** A noise vector `ε ∼ N(0, I)` is concatenated to the lagged sensor window `x_t` at each time step, kept constant across the temporal window. This stochastic input propagates through the GRU and decoder, making the output `G_θ(x, ε)` a random variable conditional on `x`. Different `ε` draws yield different reconstructions, approximating `P(y|x)`.  
**Core assumption:** The GRU+decoder architecture is expressive enough that varying `ε` sufficiently covers the plausible output space for a given `x`.  
**Evidence anchors:**  
- [abstract] "injecting stochastic noise into sensor inputs ... produces predictive distributions"  
- [section 3.1] formal definition of augmented input `˜x_t = [x_t, ε]`  
**Break condition:** Under-parameterized network or insufficient training leads to underdispersed predictions.

### Mechanism 2
**Claim:** Training with the energy score loss aligns the learned predictive distribution with the true conditional distribution `P(y|x)`.  
**Mechanism:** The energy score `ES(P, z) = ½E[‖Z−Z′‖²] − E[‖Z−z‖²]` is a strictly proper scoring rule. Minimizing its empirical version over `θ` encourages `P_θ(·|x)` to match the true law `L(y|x)`. The repulsive term `‖G_θ(x,ε)−G_θ(x,ε′)‖²` pushes predictions apart for different noise draws, preventing mode collapse.  
**Core assumption:** The model class `{P_θ(·|x)}` contains or approximates the true conditional law; loss is properly minimized over the training distribution.  
**Evidence anchors:**  
- [abstract] "training with an energy score loss ... learns the full conditional distribution"  
- [section 2.2] definition and strict propriety of energy score  
- [section 3.3] Theorem 1 proves consistency under conditions (1.a)-(1.d)  
**Break condition:** Model misspecification (condition 1.c fails) leads to the best approximating distribution in `d_ES`, which may still be miscalibrated.

### Mechanism 3
**Claim:** Monte Carlo sampling at inference yields valid quantile estimates and confidence intervals.  
**Mechanism:** For a test `x`, `K` noise samples `ε_k` generate predictions `ŷ^{(k)} = G_θ(x, ε_k)`. The empirical distribution approximates `P_θ(·|x)`. Coordinate-wise α-quantiles converge to true quantiles as `K→∞` (Theorem 2) if the conditional CDF is continuous/increasing near the quantile.  
**Core assumption:**Learned `P_θ` is close to `L(y|x)` (from Mechanism 2) and `K` is sufficiently large.  
**Evidence anchors:**  
- [section 3.2] description of MC sampling and quantile estimation  
- [section 3.3] Theorem 2 on almost sure convergence of empirical quantiles  
- [section 4] calibration diagrams (Fig. 6) show coverage near nominal levels  
**Break condition:** Finite `K` causes quantile estimation error; discontinuities in the predictive CDF may cause erratic quantiles.

## Foundational Learning
- **Concept: Engression (distributional neural regression)**  
  *Why needed:* UQ-SHRED uses engression to model full conditional distributions without likelihoods or ensembles. A single network outputs distributions via noise injection and energy score minimization.  
  *Quick check:* If you replace the energy score loss with MSE in the same architecture, what property of the learned model fundamentally changes?  
- **Concept: Proper scoring rules (energy score vs CRPS)**  
  *Why needed:* The energy score is strictly proper, ensuring the population optimum corresponds to the true conditional distribution (Theorem 1). CRPS is used for evaluation but not training.  
  *Quick check:* Why is the repulsive term (`−½‖G(x,ε)−G(x,ε′)‖²`) necessary for learning a distribution rather than just the mean?  
- **Concept: Takens’ delay embedding theorem**  
  *Why needed:* Justifies using a temporal lag window of sensor measurements (`L` steps) to embed the underlying dynamical attractor, enabling reconstruction of the full state from sparse sensors. This underpins the SHRED input structure.  
  *Quick check:* What would happen if the temporal lag `L` is too short relative to the system dynamics?  

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map:**  
  Input (lagged sensor window `x ∈ ℝ^{L×p}`) → Concatenate fixed noise `ε ∼ N(0,I_{d_ε})` to each `x_t` → GRU temporal unit → Fully connected shallow decoder `f_D` → Output prediction `ŷ`  
- **Critical path:** Sensor data → sliding windows → noise-augmented batches → energy score minimization → single trained network → Monte Carlo sampling at inference → predictive distribution (mean, quantiles, intervals)  
- **Design tradeoffs:**  
  - Noise dimension `d_ε`: Larger values increase expressive capacity but slow training/inference; ablation suggests scaling with input size (e.g., `d_ε=1000` for SST).  
  - Monte Carlo samples `K`: Higher `K` reduces quantile error but increases inference cost; `≥100` recommended.  
  - Temporal lag `L`: Affects reconstruction accuracy via Takens embedding; calibration less sensitive past minimal `L`.  
- **Failure signatures:**  
  - Overdispersed predictions: very large `d_ε` or underfitting yields unnecessarily wide intervals.  
  - Underdispersed predictions: too small `d_ε` or overfitting yields overconfident, poorly calibrated intervals (coverage below nominal).  
  - Temporal extrapolation failure: deterministic SHRED diverges outside training window; UQ-SHRED stabilizes but may miscalibrate under strong shift (e.g., RDE experiment).  
- **First 3 experiments:**  
  1. **Sea-surface temperature (SST):** 3 sensors, `L=52` weeks, `d_ε=1000`, `K=200`. Shows well-calibrated intervals widening during rapid seasonal shifts.  
  2. **Isotropic turbulent flow:** 3 sensors, `L=100`, `d_ε=100`, `K=50`. Uncertainty widens at violent pressure fluctuations.  
  3. **Neural activity (LFP):** 2 sensors, `L=100`, `d_ε=50`, `K=100`. Confidence bands widen at high-frequency perturbations, reflecting sensor noise ambiguity.

## Limitations
- Hyperparameters (network architecture, training details) are not fully disclosed; reproduction relies on codebase.
- Sensor placement randomness and data splits are not exhaustively documented, causing potential metric variance.
- Theoretical assumptions (model expressivity, continuity of conditional CDF) may not hold in practice, risking miscalibration.
- Calibration degrades under strong distribution shift (e.g., RDE transient), limiting reliability in extrapolation.

## Confidence
- **Noise injection yields distributional predictions:** Medium (empirical evidence present but theoretical guarantee indirect).  
- **Energy score learning aligns predictive distribution:** High (strictly proper loss, Theorem 1 consistency, strong calibration results).  
- **Monte Carlo sampling provides valid quantiles:** High (Theorem 2 convergence, coverage near nominal levels across datasets).
