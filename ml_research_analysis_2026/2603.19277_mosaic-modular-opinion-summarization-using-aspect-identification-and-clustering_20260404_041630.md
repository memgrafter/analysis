# MOSAIC: Modular Opinion Summarization using Aspect Identification and Clustering

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2603.19277  
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.19277  
- Reference count: 40  
- Primary result: Modular pipeline yields higher faithfulness and aspect coverage; online A/B tests show +1% conversion and +1.5% revenue per visitor (p<0.1).

## Executive Summary
MOSAIC introduces a modular framework for opinion summarization that decomposes the task into theme discovery, structured opinion extraction via aspect-based sentiment analysis, and opinion-aware summarization with clustering. The system was validated offline on SPACE and PeerSum datasets, achieving state-of-the-art aspect coverage and faithfulness metrics, and online on a travel platform where surfacing intermediate outputs (themes, tips) improved conversion and revenue. The modular design enhances interpretability, allows targeted debugging, and the clustering component specifically combats redundancy and noise in user reviews, leading to more faithful summaries.

## Method Summary
The pipeline consists of four stages: (1) Theme Discovery & Standardization: raw reviews are processed by an LLM to generate an unconstrained set of themes, which are then filtered by frequency and semantically deduplicated using BERT embeddings; optional human validation can be applied. (2) Theme-Constrained Opinion Extraction: for each review, the LLM extracts (theme, aspect, opinion, sentiment) tuples using the finalized theme list; recall is improved by running k=3 prompts with shuffled review order, and precision is refined by a binary validator LLM. (3) Opinion Clustering: tuples are grouped by product-theme-sentiment, and HDBSCAN clusters semantically similar opinions; from each cluster up to three representative opinions are retained, while unclustered outliers are sampled with max-min marginal relevance to maintain diversity. (4) Hierarchical Summarization: the clustered opinions feed few-shot LLM prompts to produce theme-level summaries, which are then combined into a final product-level summary. The modular approach isolates errors and enables inspection of intermediate outputs.

## Key Results
- Offline: On PeerSum with GPT-4o, MOSAIC achieved Coverage 0.99, G-Eval 0.84, AlignScore-R/M 0.81/0.16, outperforming prior baselines.
- Online: A/B tests on a travel platform showed surfacing themes and tips increased conversion by 1% (p<0.1) and revenue per visitor by 1.5% (p<0.1).
- Clustering significantly improved summary faithfulness under noisy, redundant conditions, as demonstrated via stress tests.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Modular Decomposition into Interpretable Stages
MOSAIC breaks opinion summarization into sequential, interpretable modules: theme discovery, constrained extraction, clustering, and summarization. Each stage uses targeted prompts and post-processing, containing errors and enabling inspection. The core assumption is that complex summarization can be partitioned without catastrophic performance loss, and intermediate representations are both debuggable and valuable to users. Evidence includes the abstract’s emphasis on interpretable components and Section 3’s detailed pipeline. Break condition: incomplete or inaccurate theme discovery propagates downstream; the optional human validation mitigates but does not eliminate this risk.

### Opinion Clustering for Redundancy Management
After extraction, opinions are clustered using HDBSCAN, and up to three representative opinions per cluster are kept. This reduces prompt length, diminishes positional bias, and prevents LLMs from over-weighting dominant but non-universal opinions, thereby improving faithfulness and coverage of minority viewpoints. The mechanism assumes raw extraction yields substantial redundancy that harms summarization. Stress tests (Figures 4 & 5) show judges prefer summaries from clustered inputs, especially when opinion order is shuffled. Break condition: clustering hyperparameters may over-merge (losing nuance) or under-merge (failing to reduce redundancy); the fixed settings from SPACE might not generalize.

### Surfacing Intermediate Outputs for User Engagement
MOSAIC makes themes and extracted tips directly available on product pages as interactive filters and highlights. This transparency lets users quickly find relevant information, reducing cognitive load and boosting purchase confidence, leading to higher conversion and revenue. The mechanism assumes users value evidence and structure behind summaries. A/B test results (Section 5.1) confirm positive lifts: +1% conversion from sorting by theme/sentiment, and +1.5% revenue/visitor from interactive themes. Break condition: if intermediate outputs are inaccurate or misleading, user trust could degrade, negating gains; thus faithfulness is a prerequisite.

## Foundational Learning
- **Concept: Aspect-Based Sentiment Analysis (ABSA)**
  - Why needed: MOSAIC’s extraction phase produces structured (theme, aspect, opinion, sentiment) tuples, which are the building blocks for clustering and summarization.
  - Quick check: How does fixing a theme list after discovery change the ABSA task compared to open-domain aspect extraction?

- **Concept: Density-Based Clustering (HDBSCAN)**
  - Why needed: Used to group similar opinion phrases; its ability to handle variable cluster shapes and identify noise points suits the heterogeneous, often outlier-filled opinion data.
  - Quick check: Why might K-means be less suitable than HDBSCAN for clustering short opinion spans?

- **Concept: LLM Prompt Engineering for Structured Output**
  - Why needed: The pipeline relies on few-shot prompts to generate JSON (themes) and adhere to strict schemas (extraction, summarization); understanding prompt design, ordering, and validation is essential.
  - Quick check: What is the purpose of the separate binary validation step in opinion extraction, and why is it needed after the initial extract?

## Architecture Onboarding
Component map:  
Theme Discovery & Standardization -> Theme-Constrained Opinion Extraction -> Opinion Clustering -> Hierarchical Summarization

Critical path: Raw Reviews -> Theme Set -> Structured Tuples -> Clustered Opinions -> Final Summary. Failures in early modules propagate; clustering mitigates some noise; final summary is the deliverable.

Design tradeoffs:
- Modularity vs. Error Propagation: Clear boundaries but weak early stages doom downstream quality.
- Clustering Granularity: Hyperparameters balance deduplication (reducing noise) and nuance preservation; fixed parameters may not transfer across domains.
- Human-in-the-Loop: Optional validation improves quality but adds latency and reduces scalability.

Failure signatures:
- Theme Discovery: Excessive or overlapping themes; very low opinion counts for some themes.
- Extraction: Large recall gap between shuffled runs and single-run indicates position bias; high false-positive rate from validator.
- Clustering: Few clusters (over-merge) or many tiny/noise clusters (under-merge); check cohesion metrics.
- Summarization: Low AlignScore/G-Eval relative to coverage suggests hallucination; discrepancy between theme-level and product-level coverage.

First 3 experiments:
1. End-to-End Sanity Check: Run pipeline on 5-10 seed products; manually inspect each module’s output for coherence, correctness, representativeness, and summary fidelity.
2. Module Isolation - Extraction: Fix reviews and themes; compare recall from shuffled k=3 runs vs. single-run baseline; validate precision with binary validator on a sample.
3. Clustering Stress Test: For a high-redundancy product-theme group, vary HDBSCAN `min_samples`; evaluate cluster cohesion (intra-cluster similarity) and coverage (percentage of opinions in clusters vs. noise) to choose suitable parameters.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
1. **Agentic Framework Integration:** How can an agentic framework be integrated into MOSAIC to automate prompt selection and intermediate output validation? (Future work suggests automation; current manual prompt engineering limits scalability.)
2. **Metrics for Coverage, Balance, Fairness:** What evaluation metrics best capture trade-offs between coverage, balance, and fairness in opinion summarization? Existing metrics lack explicit quantification of viewpoint balance and fairness.
3. **Reliable Benchmark Construction:** How can benchmark datasets be constructed to ensure reliable evaluation of theme coverage and sentiment balance? SPACE shows positivity bias and incomplete coverage; TRECS improves but broader design principles are needed.
4. **Cross-Domain Transferability:** To what extent do MOSAIC’s modular components transfer across domains without domain-specific adaptation? The paper notes prompt engineering and clustering hyperparameters may not generalize uniformly.
5. **Causal Impact of Traveler Tips:** What is the causal impact of surfacing structured Traveler Tips on user purchasing decisions and revenue? Ongoing A/B tests show directional improvements but lack statistical significance and long-term data.

## Limitations
- Missing official code repository forces reimplementation, risking deviations from reported setup.
- Vague thresholds in theme discovery (frequency cutoffs, semantic similarity τ) and MMR implementation details hinder faithful reproduction.
- Clustering hyperparameters are domain-sensitive and may not generalize; SPACE benchmark reliability issues require corrected evaluation.

## Confidence
- Modular decomposition improves interpretability and debugging: **Medium** (supported by offline results but error propagation risk remains).
- Opinion clustering enhances faithfulness under redundancy: **High** (stress tests provide direct evidence, though hyperparameter-dependent).
- Surfacing intermediate outputs boosts engagement: **Medium** (A/B test shows modest lift with p<0.1; long-term trust depends on summary quality).
