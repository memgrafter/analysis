# Global Geometry Is Not Enough for Vision Representations

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2602.03282
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.03282
- **Reference Count:** 40
- **Primary Result:** Standard geometric metrics (isotropy, participation ratio) show near-zero correlation with compositional binding accuracy across 21 vision encoders, while functional sensitivity measured by Jacobian Effective Rank (JER) correlates strongly (r = 0.65, p = 0.001).

## Executive Summary
The paper investigates whether global embedding geometry predicts an encoder's ability to perform compositional binding—preserving distinct attribute assignments (e.g., shape-position pairs). Evaluating 21 pretrained vision encoders on a synthetic attribute-binding task, it finds that common geometric metrics (isotropy, participation ratio) fail to correlate with binding performance. In contrast, a functional metric, the Jacobian Effective Rank (JER), which measures the effective dimensionality of the input-output mapping, exhibits a strong positive correlation with binding accuracy. A combined model using JER and a structural discrimination probe explains 74% of the variance in binding performance. The work concludes that global geometry is insufficient for modeling composite structure and that functional sensitivity is critical.

## Method Summary
The study evaluated 21 frozen vision encoders from diverse training objectives (e.g., contrastive, variance-decorrelation, masked modeling). For each encoder, several metrics were computed: global embedding geometry (Global Participation Ratio, Global Isotropy) from 1,000 ImageNet validation embeddings; local isotropy (k=16 nearest neighbors) from 500 anchor embeddings; functional sensitivity via Jacobian Effective Rank (JER), estimated using randomized SVD with 32 Gaussian probe directions on 100 clipped Gaussian noise images; and performance on a synthetic attribute-binding task (500 trials) and a same/different control task (500 pairs). Binding accuracy was measured by selecting a target candidate via highest cosine similarity to a query. Statistical analysis involved Pearson correlation of each metric with binding accuracy and a multiple regression (binding ~ JER + same/different) with leave-one-out cross-validated R².

## Key Results
- Standard geometric metrics (Global Participation Ratio, Global Isotropy, Local Isotropy) show near-zero, non-significant correlations with binding accuracy (e.g., r = -0.00 to +0.18).
- Jacobian Effective Rank (JER) shows a strong positive correlation with binding accuracy (Pearson r = 0.65, p = 0.001).
- A linear model combining JER and same/different accuracy explains 74% of the variance in binding performance (LOO-CV R² = 0.74).

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1: Standard Geometric Metrics Fail to Predict Binding
Global embedding metrics (isotropy, participation ratio) summarize distributional properties of the embedding cloud but do not capture how the encoder's input-output mapping preserves separable feature dimensions. An isotropic embedding may still collapse distinct attributes (e.g., shape and position) into an additive mixture. Across 21 models, these metrics exhibit near-zero correlation with binding accuracy (Table 2), demonstrating that uniform geometry does not guarantee compositional competence.

### Mechanism 2: Jacobian Effective Rank Measures Functional Capacity for Binding
JER quantifies the effective dimensionality of an encoder's local input-output sensitivity. A high JER indicates the encoder uses many independent directions to transform input into representation, allowing attribute variations to remain linearly separable. Models with high JER maintain near-orthogonal sensitivity to different attributes, which is necessary for binding. The observed strong correlation (r = 0.65) between JER and binding accuracy provides evidence that functional sensitivity, not just embedding geometry, predicts compositional performance.

### Mechanism 3: Training Objectives Determine Functional Sensitivity Patterns
Differences in JER among encoders stem from their training objectives. Variance-decorrelation objectives (Barlow Twins, VICReg) penalize off-diagonal terms in a covariance-like matrix derived from the Jacobian, explicitly encouraging orthogonal rows and high effective rank. Other objectives (contrastive, clustering, masked, vision-language) constrain the Jacobian only through specific projections (e.g., onto text embeddings or prototypes), resulting in concentrated sensitivity and lower effective rank. This explains why variance-decorrelation models achieve both high JER and high binding accuracy.

## Foundational Learning
- **Jacobian Effective Rank (JER):** For singular values σ₁,…,σₖ of the input-output Jacobian, effective rank = (∑σᵢ)² / ∑σᵢ². It measures the effective dimensionality of local sensitivity. *Why needed:* It is the central predictor of compositional binding. *Quick check:* What does a high JER indicate about the encoder's Jacobian? (Answer: The Jacobian uses many nearly equally-weighted directions, implying orthogonal sensitivity to input variations.)
- **Compositional Binding:** The ability to preserve separate attribute assignments (e.g., shape A ↔ position X) when features are recombined. *Why needed:* It is the target capability evaluated in the paper's synthetic probe. *Quick check:* Why does the attribute-binding task require more than color matching? (Answer: Query and target share zero colors; the model must rely on shape-position bindings to discriminate.)
- **Variance-Decorrelation Objectives:** Losses like Barlow Twins and VICReg that explicitly reduce off-diagonal terms in a representation covariance matrix. *Why needed:* They produce high JER and binding by encouraging orthogonal Jacobian rows. *Quick check:* Which term in the Barlow Twins loss directly promotes Jacobian orthogonality? (Answer: The off-diagonal term ∑_{i≠j} C_{ij}², where C = JΣδJᵀ.)

## Architecture Onboarding
Component map: Pretrained Vision Encoders (21 models) -> Geometric Metrics Module (compute G.PR, G.Iso, L.Iso on ImageNet embeddings) -> JER Estimator (compute effective rank via randomized SVD on noise images) -> Synthetic Evaluation Suite (attribute binding, same/different tasks) -> Statistical Analysis (correlation, regression, depth analysis)

Critical path: 1) Load each frozen encoder. 2) Compute geometric metrics: extract 1,000 ImageNet embeddings; derive eigenvalues for global metrics; for local isotropy, compute covariance within 16 nearest neighbors for 500 anchors and average. 3) Estimate JER: generate 100 clipped Gaussian noise images; for each, use 32 random Gaussian probes to approximate top Jacobian singular values via randomized SVD; compute effective rank and average across images. 4) Run binding probe: generate 500 synthetic trials; encode query and 4 candidates; select candidate with highest cosine similarity; compute accuracy. Also evaluate same/different on 500 pairs by sweeping threshold. 5) Correlate all metrics with binding (Pearson r); fit linear model binding ~ JER + same/different; compute LOO-CV R². 6) (Optional) For selected models, compute JER per network block to analyze depth-wise collapse.

Design tradeoffs: • Synthetic binding probes isolate compositionality but may not capture natural image diversity. • JER estimation is computationally heavy (auto-diff on high-res images); using fewer probes/images trades off accuracy. • Variance-decorrelation objectives yield high binding but may trade off other capabilities (e.g., linear probing). • JER on noise inputs may differ from that on natural images; natural image Jacobians could provide complementary insight.

Failure signatures: • Low binding accuracy (<25%) despite high isotropy (>0.9) and strong ImageNet linear probe results. • JER far below embedding dimension (e.g., <10 for D=384) with rapidly decaying singular value spectrum. • Mid-network JER collapse: effective rank drops in intermediate layers (as seen in CLIP/DINOv2). • High same/different accuracy but near-chance binding indicates global structure sensitivity without attribute binding.

First 3 experiments: 1) Geometric vs Binding Correlation: Compute Pearson r between each geometric metric and binding accuracy across 21 models (expected: near-zero). 2) JER vs Binding Correlation: Compute JER for each model, regress against binding; report r=0.65, p=0.00; fit combined model with same/different to obtain R²=0.74. 3) Jacobian Spectra and Depth Analysis: For representative models (Barlow Twins, CLIP, DINOv2, MAE), plot normalized singular value spectra and effective rank vs. network depth to illustrate functional collapse patterns.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- **Can JER be used as a training regularizer to improve compositional binding?** The paper shows correlation but not causality; it is unknown whether directly optimizing JER improves binding.
- **Does JER generalize as a predictor of compositional competence on natural datasets?** The evaluation used a minimal synthetic task; it is unclear if the correlation holds for complex, realistic compositional benchmarks (e.g., CLEVR, COCO).
- **Are there alternative geometric metrics that can capture compositional structure when standard metrics fail?** The study tested limited geometric readouts; other measures based on manifold topology or higher-order statistics remain unexplored.
- **Can we derive a theoretical framework that formally links training objectives to the Jacobian spectrum?** The paper provides intuitive explanations but lacks rigorous conditions predicting Jacobian singular value distributions from objective functionals.

## Limitations
- Model diversity limited (21 encoders, mostly CNN/ViT); generalization to newer architectures is uncertain.
- Synthetic binding task may not reflect real-world compositionality; natural benchmarks are needed.
- JER estimation is approximate (noise inputs, randomized SVD); stability across image distributions and probe counts is unclear.
- Ambiguous protocol details (e.g., local isotropy neighbor distance, distractor color assignment) hinder reproducibility.

## Confidence
- Global geometry fails to predict binding: **Medium** (strong null correlation but narrow model/task set).
- JER predicts binding: **Medium** (robust correlation yet causal link indirect).
- Objectives drive JER differences: **Medium** (consistent patterns but no controlled ablation).

## Next Checks
1. **Expand model and task diversity:** Evaluate ~50 models (including ConvNeXt, MAE+, CLIP-ViT-L) on both the synthetic binding task and established natural composition benchmarks (e.g., CLEVR, COCO).
2. **Intervention study:** Add a variance-decorrelation term to a contrastive objective (e.g., MoCo) and measure causal effects on JER and binding accuracy.
3. **Robustness of JER estimation:** Compare JER computed on noise vs. ImageNet images; vary probe count (32/64/128); compute exact SVD on subsampled Jacobians; report coefficient of variation across seeds.
