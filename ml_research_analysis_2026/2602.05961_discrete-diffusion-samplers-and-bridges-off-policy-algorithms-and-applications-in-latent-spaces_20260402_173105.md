# Discrete diffusion samplers and bridges: Off-policy algorithms and applications in latent spaces

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2602.05961  
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.05961  
- **Reference count:** 40  
- **Primary result:** Off-policy reinforcement learning with replay buffers and MCMC-guided exploration significantly improves discrete diffusion samplers and enables robust data-to-energy bridging in latent spaces.

## Executive Summary
This paper tackles the problem of sampling from unnormalized discrete distributions, a key challenge in probabilistic inference and combinatorial optimization. The authors introduce off-policy reinforcement learning techniques—specifically replay buffers with importance weighting and MCMC-guided exploration—into the training of discrete diffusion models. This addresses common failure modes like mode collapse and poor distribution coverage. The method is further generalized to Schrӧdinger bridge problems, allowing transport from an arbitrary sample-based distribution to one defined by an energy function. Empirical results on Ising/Potts models, synthetic multimodal densities, and VQ-VAE latent spaces demonstrate consistent improvements in mode coverage and sample quality over on-policy methods and baselines like MDNS and MH MCMC. The approach successfully amortizes posterior inference in pretrained generative models, particularly where on-policy methods fail.

## Method Summary
The core method enhances discrete diffusion sampler training via off-policy reinforcement learning. A replay buffer stores past generated samples, which are reused to reduce distribution shift between training and sampling phases. Importance weighting corrects biases from off-policy data by reweighting losses based on the ratio of target and behavior policy probabilities. MCMC-guided exploration (e.g., Metropolis-Hastings proposals) actively enriches the buffer with samples from under-represented modes. The framework extends to Schrӧdinger bridge problems by learning a diffusion process that transports mass from a source distribution (given by samples) to a target specified by an energy function, using a trajectory balance objective. This enables “data-to-energy bridging” without requiring target samples.

## Key Results
- Off-policy training with replay buffers and MCMC exploration yields substantially better mode coverage (lower Sinkhorn distance) and matches ground-truth statistics (magnetisation, correlations) on Ising and Potts models versus on-policy baselines.
- On highly multimodal synthetic densities (40GMM, ManyWell), the method achieves higher ELBOs / lower EUBOs and avoids collapse; on-policy methods fail to cover all modes.
- Discrete data-to-energy bridging succeeds where on-policy approaches diverge, demonstrating robust transport from arbitrary sample-based sources to energy-defined targets.
- Amortized posterior sampling in VQ-VAE latent spaces produces higher-quality conditional samples compared to standard MCMC, validating the approach for conditioning generative models.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
Off-policy training mitigates the distribution shift that plagues on-policy discrete diffusion training, where the model’s changing distribution causes training samples to become stale. The replay buffer reuses past high-quality samples, maintaining a diverse reference distribution. Importance weighting provides unbiased gradient estimates despite off-policy data. MCMC-guided exploration injects fresh samples from low-probability regions, preventing mode collapse and ensuring the buffer remains representative of the target. For Schrӧdinger bridging, the trajectory balance objective aligns the diffusion’s marginal with the energy-defined target without requiring target samples, using the source distribution as an anchor. Together, these elements yield a stable, high-coverage amortized sampler.

## Foundational Learning
- **Discrete diffusion models:** Needed to define a Markov chain that gradually noisifies and denoises discrete data. *Quick check:* Forward process adds noise (e.g., categorical masking); reverse process is learned denoising.
- **Schrӧdinger bridge:** Needed for distribution transport between two marginals without a predetermined path. *Quick check:* Interpolates between source and target while minimizing a kinetic cost.
- **Off-policy reinforcement learning:** Needed to break the on-policy dependency and reuse experience. *Quick check:* Uses a replay buffer to store transitions for diverse updates.
- **Importance sampling:** Needed to correct distribution mismatch in off-policy learning. *Quick check:* Reweight losses by importance ratios of target over behavior policy.
- **Markov Chain Monte Carlo:** Needed to explore low-probability regions and replenish the buffer. *Quick check:* Proposals (e.g., Gibbs, Metropolis-Hastings) that asymptotically target the distribution.
- **Amortized inference:** Needed to replace per-instance optimization with a single feedforward pass after training. *Quick check:* Train a neural sampler once; condition on new energies by reusing the same model.

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component map (plain text):**  
Replay Buffer -> Sample Batch -> Importance Weighting -> Loss Computation (Trajectory Balance) -> Diffusion Model Update -> MCMC Exploration -> Update Replay Buffer -> (repeat)

**Critical path:** The training loop iterates: sample batch from replay buffer; compute importance-weighted trajectory balance loss; update diffusion model parameters; generate new samples via MCMC guided by the current model; add new samples to buffer. This cycle maintains a stable, diverse buffer while progressively improving the sampler.

**Design tradeoffs:**  
- Buffer size vs. memory: larger buffers improve diversity but increase storage/compute.  
- MCMC step size vs. acceptance rate: too small yields slow exploration; too high causes rejections.  
- Importance weight variance vs. bias: high variance destabilizes training; truncation or clipping may introduce bias.  
- Exploration frequency vs. training stability: too much MCMC can derail learning if proposals are poor.

**Failure signatures:**  
- Mode collapse: buffer lacks diversity, Sinkhorn distance rises, magnetisation errors increase.  
- Diverging loss: unweighted gradients or extreme importance weights cause instability.  
- Poor test coverage: model overfits to buffer content, fails on held-out energies.  
- High EUBO: lower bound on energy indicates sampler does not capture high-probability regions.

**First experiments (3):**  
1. **Replay buffer ablation:** Train without buffer (on-policy) and with buffer; compare mode coverage and ELBO on 40GMM.  
2. **MCMC step size sweep:** Vary step size in Metropolis-Hastings proposals; plot acceptance rate vs. final Sinkhorn distance on Ising model.  
3. **Scalability pilot:** Apply method to a 100-variable Ising model; measure training time, buffer size needed, and correlation matching error.

## Limitations
- Evaluation restricted to small-scale benchmarks (Ising, Potts, synthetic GMM/ManyWell, VQ-VAE latents); scalability to high-dimensional discrete spaces (e.g., hundreds of variables) is untested.
- Off-policy hyperparameters (buffer size, importance weight clipping, MCMC step size) require careful tuning; sensitivity analysis is lacking.
- Computational cost of MCMC-guided exploration and replay buffer management is not quantified relative to on-policy training.
- No theoretical convergence analysis for the proposed off-policy algorithm.
- Baseline comparisons may exclude recent discrete diffusion or adaptive MCMC methods, potentially overstating gains.

## Confidence
- Off-policy training significantly improves discrete diffusion samplers: **Medium** (empirical gains on selected tasks, limited benchmark diversity, possible baseline bias).  
- Discrete data-to-energy bridging succeeds where on-policy methods collapse: **Medium** (some evidence; needs validation across diverse energy functions).  
- Amortized posterior sampling in pretrained generative model latents is effective: **Medium** (validated on VQ-VAE only; generalizability unknown).

## Next Checks
1. **Scalability test:** Evaluate on high-dimensional combinatorial problems (e.g., MAX-CUT with 200+ variables) to assess performance, mode coverage, and computational scaling.
