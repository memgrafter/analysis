# Grounded Token Initialization for New Vocabulary in LMs for Generative Recommendation

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2604.02324
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.02324
- **Reference Count:** 28
- **Primary Result:** GTI achieves up to +21.63% relative Precision@5 gain over standard supervised fine-tuning on an industrial candidate retrieval dataset.

## Executive Summary
Language models extended with new tokens for generative recommendation suffer from a critical failure mode: mean initialization collapses all new tokens into a single degenerate point, erasing inter-token distinctions necessary for the model to distinguish them. This low-rank subspace is difficult for subsequent fine-tuning to recover. Grounded Token Initialization (GTI) addresses this by inserting a lightweight pre-training stage where only the new token embeddings are learned via paired linguistic supervision (e.g., mapping item descriptions to token sequences) while the LM backbone remains frozen. This grounds the tokens in semantically meaningful, distinct regions of the pretrained embedding manifold. GTI yields significant performance gains on recommendation benchmarks and produces richer, more stable spectral and geometric structure in the new token embeddings that persists through fine-tuning.

## Method Summary
GTI is a two-stage pipeline for extending a pretrained autoregressive LM with a new vocabulary of Semantic ID (SID) tokens generated by an RQ-VAE. **Stage 1 (Grounding):** The LM backbone is frozen. Only the new SID token embeddings are trained on a curated corpus of bidirectional pairs: natural language descriptions ↔ sequences of SID tokens. The loss is the standard next-token prediction NLL, which updates `E_new` to be both readable (from text) and generatable (to text) within the frozen LM’s semantic manifold. **Stage 2 (SFT):** All parameters, including the now-grounded `E_new`, are fine-tuned on the downstream generative recommendation task (e.g., candidate retrieval) using standard supervised fine-tuning with identical hyperparameters as baselines. The key contrast is against mean-initialization (all new tokens start at the vocabulary mean) and multi-task auxiliary methods like LC-Rec.

## Key Results
- On an industrial candidate retrieval dataset, GTI achieves up to **+21.63% relative Precision@5** gain over standard SFT with mean initialization.
- Gains are **consistent** across multiple public recommendation benchmarks (e.g., Vibrent rental).
- **Spectral and geometric analyses** confirm GTI yields higher effective rank and preserves the hierarchical structure of the RQ-VAE codebooks after fine-tuning, whereas mean initialization leads to rapid spectral decay and collapsed token similarity.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1: Mean Initialization Induces Token-Embedding Collapse
- **Claim:** Mean-of-vocabulary initialization forces all new tokens to an identical vector, creating a degenerate, low-rank subspace that eliminates pairwise distances and directional variance (`effective rank`) required for attention and MLP layers to distinguish tokens.
- **Mechanism:** The identical starting point gradient descent must *create* discriminative structure from scratch during SFT, which is hindered by the model’s optimization dynamics that struggle to differentiate points originating from a single location.
- **Evidence:** Diagnostic analysis shows rapid spectral decay and low effective rank in SID embeddings after SFT from a mean-initialized state (Fig 2b, Fig 7). The paper assumes this spectral decay is causally linked to performance collapse.
- **Break condition:** The effect is most severe for large vocabulary extensions (thousands of tokens); for very small sets (<5 tokens), fine-tuning may more easily separate the points.

### Mechanism 2: GTI Pre-positions Tokens in a Structured, Semantically Grounded Manifold
- **Claim:** By freezing the LM and training only `E_new` on paired linguistic supervision, GTI places each new token in a location that is (a) predictable from its associated text by the frozen LM’s pretrained semantics and (b) distinct from other new tokens to minimize grounding loss. This uses the LM as a fixed semantic oracle.
- **Mechanism:** The bidirectional prompts (text→SID and SID→text) force `E_new` to be useful for both reading and generation, aligning it with the existing embedding geometry where semantically related words are proximal.
- **Evidence:** Visualizations (Fig 5) show only GTI produces a rich, differentiated structure within the SID block and coherent cross-block affinities to relevant lexical tokens. This structure is present immediately after the grounding stage.
- **Break condition:** If the grounding corpus `D_ground` is noisy, sparse, or semantically misaligned with the downstream task, tokens may be placed in misleading locations, harming performance.

### Mechanism 3: Grounded Structure Persists Through Subsequent Fine-Tuning
- **Claim:** The high-quality, non-collapsed initialization from GTI creates a "better basin" in the optimization landscape. The model’s SFT gradients are less likely to collapse the SID subspace because it starts in a stable, expressive configuration. The pretrained LM can immediately leverage the grounded tokens’ semantic relations.
- **Mechanism:** The initial geometric prior (high effective rank, hierarchical organization) is preserved during SFT, allowing the model to build task-specific behavior on top of a robust representational foundation.
- **Evidence:** Post-SFT analyses (Fig 6, Fig 7, Fig 8) show GTI is the only method that (1) maintains clear blockwise hierarchical semantics in cosine similarity matrices, (2) exhibits slower spectral decay and higher effective rank, and (3) achieves higher Representational Similarity Analysis (RSA) scores with ground-truth RQ-VAE codebooks.
- **Break condition:** An extremely aggressive SFT learning rate or optimizer could erode the initial structural advantages quickly.

## Foundational Learning
- **Spectral Geometry of Embedding Matrices:** The singular value spectrum (effective rank) quantifies the rank and expressive capacity of an embedding matrix. A collapsed set of identical vectors has rank 1 (one non-zero singular value). GTI’s goal is to produce a higher-rank, slower-decaying spectrum for `E_SID`.
  - *Quick check:* If all row vectors in a matrix are identical, what is its rank and what does its singular value spectrum look like?
- **Autoregressive LM Forward Pass & Weight Tying:** In standard LMs, input embeddings and output lm_head weights are tied (`lm_head.weight = E^T`). Changing a token’s embedding vector `v` affects (1) its representation when read as input and (2) its contribution to output logits when generated.
  - *Quick check:* In a tied-weight LM, if you change the embedding for token "X", which two components of the model’s computation are directly altered?
- **Residual Quantization (RQ-VAE) & Semantic IDs (SIDs):** An RQ-VAE encodes an input vector into a multi-level discrete code `(c1, c2, ..., cL)`. Each code index at each level is a distinct token. This creates a hierarchical token set where `c1` is coarse and `cL` is fine-grained.
  - *Quick check:* How does an RQ-VAE’s final discrete representation differ from a VQ-VAE’s single-codebook representation?

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map:** Pretrained LM Backbone → Extended Embedding Matrix (`E_text` frozen, `E_new` trainable) → Grounding Corpus `D_ground` (paired text↔SID sequences) → Prompt Templates → RQ-VAE Codebooks (offline) → Downstream SFT Pipeline.
- **Critical path:** Load pretrained LM and extend tokenizer with SIDs → **GTI Stage:** Freeze backbone, train only `E_new` on `D_ground` → Save grounded `E_new` → **SFT Stage:** Unfreeze all, train on downstream task → Evaluate.
- **Design tradeoffs:**
  - **Grounding Data Cost vs. Benefit:** High-quality `(text, SID)` pairs are expensive to curate; benefit scales with new token count and domain complexity.
  - **Grounding Epochs vs. Efficiency:** More steps improve token differentiation but increase compute; paper uses 8k steps.
  - **Bidirectional vs. Unidirectional Grounding:** Using both `(text→SID)` and `(SID→text)` pairs aims to make `E_new` useful in both input and output directions; unidirectional may be cheaper but less effective.
- **Failure signatures:**
  1. **No spectral improvement:** Post-GTI effective rank of `E_SID` remains low and decays rapidly.
  2. **Poor cross-block affinities:** SID block in cosine similarity matrix shows no coherent proximity to relevant pretrained tokens (e.g., "shoe" tokens not near SID representing shoes).
  3. **Downstream degradation:** GTI performs worse than mean-init SFT, indicating faulty grounding (e.g., backbone not frozen, noisy `D_ground`, too high LR).
- **First 3 experiments:**
  1. **Spectral Sanity Check:** After GTI (pre-SFT), compute effective rank of `E_SID`. It should be significantly higher than mean-init baseline.
  2. **Zero-Shot Grounding Probe:** Freeze entire model after GTI. Evaluate held-out accuracy of generating correct SID sequence from a description without SFT. Tests if grounding alone made tokens decodable.
  3. **Ablation: Unidirectional Grounding:** Run GTI with only `(text→SID)` pairs or only `(SID→text)` pairs. Compare Precision@5 to full bidirectional GTI to confirm bidirectionality necessity.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
1. **Does GTI generalize beyond recommendation?** The paper tests only recommendation benchmarks; generality to other vocabulary-extension tasks (e.g., tool use, vision-language) is unknown.
2. **How does description quality affect GTI?** Real-world metadata can be noisy or sparse; the paper’s results assume high-quality text but do not test robustness to degraded descriptions.
3. **Is the two-stage training necessary?** Could joint training (backbone unfrozen during grounding) be more efficient or effective? The paper strictly separates stages.
4. **How sensitive is GTI to prompt templates?** Specific instruction prompts are used without ablation; prompt design optimal for grounding is unexplored.

## Limitations
- Grounding corpus construction is costly and not automated; its quality directly determines token semantics.
- The frozen pretrained LM’s embedding geometry may not perfectly align with the new domain, risking misgrounding.
- Evaluation is limited to Qwen3-0.6B and a few datasets; broader model and dataset generalizability remains unproven.

## Confidence
- **Mean initialization causes token collapse:** Medium‑High (strong spectral evidence, but causal link is indirect).
- **GTI enables semantic grounding:** Medium (visual/performance gains depend on data and prompt design quality).
- **Grounded structure persists through fine‑tuning:** Medium (empirical spectral stability, lacking theoretical guarantee).

## Next Checks

2. **Spectral Stress Test:** During SFT, use aggressive learning rates; track effective rank of SID embeddings to see if GTI’s advantage decays.
