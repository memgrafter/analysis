# GSI Agent: Domain Knowledge Enhancement for Large Language Models in Green Stormwater Infrastructure

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2603.15643
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.15643
- Reference count: 9
- Primary result: BLEU-4 on GSI dataset increased from 0.090 to 0.307, with stable common knowledge performance (0.304 → 0.305).

## Executive Summary
This paper introduces a three-part framework—supervised fine-tuning (SFT) on a newly created Green Stormwater Infrastructure (GSI) instruction dataset, retrieval-augmented generation (RAG) over municipal documents, and an agent-based reasoning pipeline—to enhance large language models for domain-specific GSI inspection and maintenance tasks. General-purpose LLMs lack reliable, document-grounded knowledge in this specialized area, leading to potential inaccuracies; the proposed system addresses this by combining domain adaptation, factual grounding, and coordinated reasoning.

Evaluation on the GSI dataset shows a substantial improvement: BLEU-4 rises from 0.090 (baseline) to 0.307 with the full system, while performance on a general common knowledge benchmark remains essentially unchanged (0.304 vs. 0.305). This indicates that domain-specific gains can be achieved without sacrificing general knowledge. Human expert evaluation, though limited, suggests promising correctness and utility.

## Method Summary
The authors built a curated GSI instruction dataset covering tasks such as question answering, verification, and generation in the context of green stormwater infrastructure maintenance. Using this dataset, they fine-tuned a general-purpose LLM (Qwen3-VL-2B-Instruct) via supervised fine-tuning to instill domain-specific knowledge and response patterns. To ensure factual grounding, they implemented a retrieval-augmented generation component that queries a corpus of 22 municipal documents (e.g., regulations, manuals) and supplies relevant passages to the model. An agent-based reasoning pipeline orchestrates the process: it decides when retrieval is needed, merges retrieved context with the model’s output, verifies consistency with source documents, and iteratively refines the response. The full pipeline runs autonomously, producing final answers that are both domain-informed and document-supported.

The system is evaluated against a baseline general LLM (without any adaptation) on the GSI test set using BLEU-4, as well as on a common knowledge dataset to ensure retention of general abilities. Ablation experiments isolate the contributions of SFT, RAG, and their combination. Additionally, a small human expert assessment rates outputs for correctness and helpfulness.

## Key Results
- BLEU-4 on the GSI dataset increased by over 240% relative (0.090 → 0.307) with the full GSI Agent.
- Performance on a common knowledge benchmark remained stable (0.304 → 0.305), demonstrating retention of general knowledge.
- Ablation indicates additive benefits: SFT alone (0.63) and RAG alone (0.51) both underperform the combined system (0.72) on G-Eval.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The hybrid mechanism works because each component addresses a specific limitation of general LLMs. SFT adapts the model’s internal knowledge to the GSI domain, enabling it to generate plausible domain-specific content. RAG retrieves up-to-date, authoritative information from municipal documents, grounding the model’s outputs in verifiable sources and reducing hallucination. The agent coordinates these components, dynamically determining when retrieval is necessary, merging retrieved facts with the model’s generation, and applying reasoning steps to ensure consistency and compliance. This synergy yields accurate, document-grounded responses while preserving the model’s general capabilities through careful integration.

## Foundational Learning
- **Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT)**  
  *Why needed:* To transfer the general LLM into the GSI domain by learning from expert demonstrations, capturing domain terminology, procedures, and response formats.  
  *Quick check:* Does the fine-tuned model correctly use GSI-specific terms (e.g., "bioretention", "flow drain") and follow inspection protocols without prompting?

- **Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG)**  
  *Why needed:* To provide factual grounding in municipal regulations and manuals, reducing hallucination and ensuring responses reflect current authoritative standards.  
  *Quick check:* Can the system accurately cite source documents (e.g., city code section) when answering regulatory questions?

- **Agent-Based Reasoning**  
  *Why needed:* To orchestrate the interaction between SFT generation and retrieval, perform verification, and handle complex multi-step reasoning required for GSI tasks.  
  *Quick check:* Does the agent appropriately decide to trigger retrieval for queries that appear unfamiliar, and does it revise the initial generation based on retrieved evidence?

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component map:** Query -> Agent -> (SFT Module & RAG Module) -> Fusion & Verification -> Output

**Critical path:** The agent first receives the user query and decides whether retrieval is necessary. If needed, it queries the RAG system to fetch relevant municipal document chunks. Concurrently, the SFT model generates a draft response. The agent then merges the retrieved context with the draft, checks for consistency, and performs iterative refinement to ensure compliance with regulations. Finally, the polished answer is returned.

**Design tradeoffs:**  
- The SFT component provides domain adaptation but may overfit to the limited instruction set; combining with RAG mitigates by supplying fresh factual content.  
- RAG introduces retrieval latency and depends on document quality; the agent must balance speed and accuracy.  
- The agent's reasoning steps add computational overhead but enable conflict resolution and verification.  
- The chosen base model (2B parameters) prioritizes efficiency over expressive power, possibly limiting complex reasoning.

**Failure signatures:**  
- The system produces fluent but factually incorrect answers when retrieval fails or returns irrelevant passages.  
- Conflicting information between retrieved documents can cause the agent to output inconsistent or hedging responses.  
- Over-reliance on SFT may cause the model to ignore retrieved corrections, violating the document-grounded promise.  
- High latency or timeouts when the agent engages in lengthy iterative reasoning.

**Three first experiments:**  
1. Replicate the main BLEU-4 results on the GSI test set with the full pipeline.  
2. Disable the RAG module (use SFT+agent only) and measure the drop in performance, both automatic and via a small human evaluation.  
3. Evaluate the RAG component in isolation: compute retrieval precision@5 by checking if retrieved chunks contain the gold-standard answers.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
### Open Question 1
- **Question:** How does the GSI Agent's performance scale with larger, more diverse human expert evaluations in real-world GSI inspection and maintenance scenarios?
- **Basis in paper:** [explicit] The conclusion states that "future work includes scaling human expert evaluation" (Section 6), indicating that the current human expert assessment was limited to a small sample (Table 7).
- **Why unresolved:** The paper's human evaluation was minimal, so it's unclear if the substantial BLEU-4 gains (0.090 → 0.307) translate to consistent, reliable utility and correctness in practical, high-stakes field decisions where errors could have safety or regulatory consequences. Automated metrics like BLEU may not capture real-world task success.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** A large-scale, multi-site study where domain experts (engineers, maintenance crews) rate the system's outputs on usefulness, safety, and compliance across hundreds of diverse GSI tasks (e.g., clog diagnosis, maintenance scheduling), compared against baseline LLMs and current non-AI practices.

### Open Question 2
- **Question:** What is the precise, quantifiable contribution and interaction effect of each component (SFT vs. RAG vs. Agent) in the hybrid framework, and are there diminishing returns or failure modes when they are combined?
- **Basis in paper:** [inferred] The ablation study (Table 11) shows a simple additive pattern (LLM+RAG: 0.51, LLM+SFT: 0.63, LLM+RAG+SFT: 0.72), but it does not analyze deeper interactions (e.g., does fine-tuning harm retrieval's ability to introduce new facts? Does the agent's reasoning introduce errors that offset gains?).
- **Why unresolved:** The ablation is high-level and uses a single metric (G-Eval). It doesn't test failure cases where components might conflict (e.g., fine-tuned biases overriding retrieved regulations) or identify which task types (from Table 5) benefit most from which component. This limits principled design for future systems.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** A fine-grained ablation analyzing performance per task family (QA, verification, generation, etc.), with error analysis categorizing failures by component (e.g., "incorrect fact" from SFT, "irrelevant retrieval," "agent reasoning flaw"). Controlled experiments could also test component conflict cases.

### Open Question 3
- **Question:** To what extent are the performance gains and stable general knowledge retention (Common Knowledge Dataset) generalizable beyond the specific base model (Qwen3-VL-2B-Instruct) and to other professional infrastructure domains (e.g., building codes, electrical grids)?
- **Basis in paper:** [inferred] The methodology and baselines are evaluated almost exclusively on Qwen3-VL-2B-Instruct (Section 4.3, 4.4). Other models (InternVL, etc.) are mentioned only for "feasibility comparison," not as primary test beds. The domain is uniquely specialized (GSI).
- **Why unresolved:** LLM architectures differ significantly. A framework that works for a 2B parameter model might not scale or interact similarly with larger models (e.g., GPT-4, Llama3-70B) or models with different pre-training biases. The claim that "systematic domain knowledge enhancement can effectively adapt general-purpose LLMs" needs validation across a spectrum of models and domains.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Replication studies using multiple, architecturally diverse base LLMs (different sizes, families, modalities) on the GSI dataset and analogous datasets from other infrastructure domains (e.g., a "Building Code Agent" dataset). Performance and general knowledge retention must be measured comparably.

### Open Question 4
- **Question:** How robust is the system to noise, incompleteness, or contradictions in the underlying municipal documents used for the RAG corpus, and how does the agent's reasoning pipeline handle ambiguous or conflicting retrieved passages?
- **Basis in paper:** [inferred] The RAG corpus is built from 22 specific municipal documents (Appendix A). The agent design includes a soft constraint to "avoid inventing regulations," but there is no discussion of document quality control, inter-document consistency, or the agent's conflict-resolution strategy when retrieval returns passages with differing standards or outdated information.
- **Why unresolved:** Municipal documents are known to contain inconsistencies, updates, and jurisdictional overlaps. The paper assumes the corpus is authoritative and coherent. The agent's ability to detect and resolve such conflicts is critical for real-world reliability but is untested. The "agent-based reasoning pipeline" (Section 3.3) is described conceptually without detailing conflict-handling logic.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Stress-testing with a perturbed RAG corpus containing deliberate contradictions (e.g., different maintenance schedules from different city years) or missing critical clauses. Evaluating the system's outputs for consistency, correctness, and explanation of conflicts compared to a baseline that naively uses retrieved text. Analysis of failure cases in the current dataset where source documents may conflict.

## Limitations
- Evaluation relies heavily on BLEU-4, which may not reflect factual correctness, a critical aspect for GSI domain tasks.
- Details on the newly created GSI instruction dataset (size, curation process, diversity) are missing, raising concerns about bias and generalizability.
- Retrieval performance of the RAG component is unreported; poor retrieval could undermine the document-grounded claim.

## Confidence
- Domain performance improvement: Medium
- Preservation of general knowledge: Medium
- Document-grounded generation: Low
