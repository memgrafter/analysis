# Uncertainty Quantification for Multimodal Large Language Models with Incoherence-adjusted Semantic Volume

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2602.24195
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.24195
- Reference count: 40
- Primary result: UMPIRE, a training-free uncertainty metric for multimodal LLMs, outperforms baselines on diverse benchmarks (AUROC up to 0.82, CPC up to 0.91, ECE as low as 0.039).

## Executive Summary
Multimodal large language models (MLLMs) often produce confident‑sounding but incorrect outputs, especially when faced with challenging or out‑of‑distribution inputs. Existing uncertainty quantification methods for MLLMs are either modality‑specific, require external models, or incur significant computational overhead. This paper introduces UMPIRE, a novel, training‑free framework that leverages only the MLLM’s internal embeddings and token probabilities to assess uncertainty. By repeatedly sampling responses for a given multimodal query, UMPIRE extracts their semantic embeddings and computes an incoherence score from the model‑assigned probabilities. These are combined via a determinantal point process (DPP)‑style kernel to measure the “incoherence‑adjusted semantic volume,” which captures both the global diversity of the responses and their local quality. Extensive experiments on image, audio, and video question‑answering benchmarks—including adversarial and out‑of‑distribution scenarios—and on non‑text generation tasks demonstrate that UMPIRE consistently surpasses prior uncertainty metrics, achieving AUROC up to 0.82, CPC up to 0.91, and expected calibration error (ECE) as low as 0.039, while adding negligible computational cost.

## Method Summary
UMPIRE operates in four steps:  
1. **Sampling**: Given a multimodal input \(q_t\), it draws \(k\) responses \(\{y_i\}_{i=1}^k\) using a fixed temperature (e.g., \(T=1\)) and nucleus sampling.  
2. **Representation**: For each response, it extracts the normalized last‑layer EOS token embedding \(\phi_i \in \mathbb{R}^d\) and computes the total token probability \(p_i = P_M(y_i \mid q_t)\).  
3. **Incoherence scaling**: It defines a quality weight \(c_i = \exp(\alpha(1-p_i))\) that penalizes responses the model deems unlikely.  
4. **Volume computation**: With \(\Phi = [\phi_1; \dots; \phi_k]\), it forms the Gram matrix \(G = \Phi\Phi^\top + \epsilon I_k\) and calculates the semantic volume \(U = \frac{1}{2k}\log\det(G)\). The mean incoherence \(Q = \frac{1}{k}\sum_i (1-p_i)\) estimates quadratic entropy. The final uncertainty score is \(V = U + \alpha Q\), a DPP‑quality‑weighted log determinant that balances diversity and incoherence. The method is training‑free, modality‑agnostic, and requires only standard forward passes through the MLLM.

## Key Results
- UMPIRE achieves AUROC up to 0.82, CPC up to 0.91, and ECE as low as 0.039 across image, audio, video QA and non‑text generation tasks.
- It consistently outperforms existing uncertainty baselines (e.g., LN‑Ent, Sem.Ent, Eigen, SCoOP) in discriminating correct from incorrect answers.
- The framework exhibits negligible computational overhead beyond standard MLLM inference and remains effective with as few as 5 sampled responses.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1
- **Claim**: UMPIRE captures global semantic diversity of model responses via the volume of their embeddings, which increases when the model is uncertain and generates semantically disparate answers.
- **Mechanism**: For \(k\) sampled responses, extract normalized embeddings (last layer EOS token) into matrix \(\Phi \in \mathbb{R}^{k\times d}\). Compute the Gram matrix \(G = \Phi\Phi^\top\) and add a small jitter \(\epsilon I\). The unadjusted semantic volume is \(U = (1/(2k))\log\det(G + \epsilon I)\). Larger determinant indicates greater spread of embeddings on the hypersphere, correlating with higher uncertainty.
- **Core assumption**: The MLLM’s embedding space preserves semantic similarity; diverse responses for a fixed query indicate the model lacks a confident, singular answer.
- **Evidence anchors**:  
  - [abstract]: "capturing both the global semantic diversity of samples"  
  - [section 3.1]: "Samples with greater response diversity have more widely spaced out points that enclose larger ‘semantic volume’."  
  - [corpus]: Neighbors like *SCoOP* consider diversity via opinion pooling, but UMPIRE’s geometric volume measure is distinct and training‑free.
- **Break condition**: If model embeddings are not semantically meaningful (e.g., poor multimodal alignment), \(U\) may not reflect true uncertainty. Also, responses that are lexically varied but semantically similar (e.g., synonyms) may inflate volume without real uncertainty, which the incoherence term mitigates.

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim**: UMPIRE captures local incoherence of each response using the model’s conditional probability, estimating the quadratic entropy of the response distribution; lower average probability indicates the model spreads mass across many answers, signifying higher uncertainty.
- **Mechanism**: For each sampled response \(y_i\), compute \(p_i = P_M(y_i \mid q_t)\). Define incoherence score \(c_i = \exp(\alpha(1-p_i))\). The average \(Q = (1/k) \sum (1-p_i)\) is a Monte Carlo estimate of quadratic entropy \(H_2 = 1 - \sum p^2\). Smaller \(p_i\) (higher incoherence) raises \(Q\).
- **Core assumption**: Although uncalibrated, the model’s token‑level probabilities retain relative information about multimodal coherence; confabulations produce lower‑probability responses.
- **Evidence anchors**:  
  - [abstract]: "local incoherence of responses based on internal model confidence"  
  - [section 3.2]: "Qt could be interpreted as the Monte Carlo estimate of quadratic entropy ... indicating higher uncertainty when a model spreads out probability mass"  
  - [corpus]: Works like *FESTA* use model probabilities for trust, but UMPIRE’s quadratic entropy framing is novel and does not require external verifiers.
- **Break condition**: If the model is overconfident on wrong answers (high \(p_i\) for incorrect responses), \(Q\) will be misleading. Miscalibration can be mitigated by the diversity term \(U\). For very long generations, token probabilities may underflow; length normalization could be applied but was found not to drastically change results.

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim**: UMPIRE combines semantic diversity and incoherence via a DPP‑style quality‑diversity kernel, yielding a single uncertainty metric that balances both signals and satisfies the desiderata (discrimination, calibration, multimodal generalizability).
- **Mechanism**: Build diagonal matrix \(C = \operatorname{diag}(c_1,\dots,c_k)\) with \(c_i = \exp(\alpha(1-p_i))\). Compute kernel \(L = C (\Phi\Phi^\top + \epsilon I) C\). Then \(V = (1/(2k))\log\det(L)\). This decomposes exactly to \(V = U + \alpha Q\). The kernel scales each response’s contribution to the volume by its incoherence, so uncertain responses (low \(p_i\)) enlarge the volume.
- **Core assumption**: \(U\) and \(Q\) are complementary; \(Q\) captures probability dispersion while \(U\) distinguishes between different types of dispersion (e.g., semantic ambiguity vs. lexical variation). Their weighted sum improves risk‑score quality without losing discrimination.
- **Evidence anchors**:  
  - [abstract]: "Combining these via a DPP‑style kernel"  
  - [section 3.1]: "UMPIRE incorporates both of these signals coherently ... by computing the incoherence‑adjusted semantic volume"  
  - [section 3.2]: "Ut and Qt are complementary ... Empirically, this additional information improves risk‑score quality (R2), allowing the overall UMPIRE metric Vt to better satisfy the desiderata"  
  - [corpus]: *SCoOP* uses opinion pooling; UMPIRE’s DPP‑inspired kernel is a novel way to fuse diversity and quality without external tools.
- **Break condition**: The linear weighting via \(\alpha\) may not be optimal for all tasks; an inappropriate \(\alpha\) could overemphasize one term. The approach assumes \(U\) and \(Q\) are positively correlated with uncertainty; if one term is anticorrelated, performance degrades. Additionally, the kernel assumes embeddings lie near a hypersphere; severe anisotropy might affect volume interpretation.

## Foundational Learning
- **Determinantal Point Processes (DPP)**  
  - **Why needed**: UMPIRE’s kernel \(L\) is a quality‑weighted DPP kernel; the log determinant corresponds to the volume of a parallelepiped spanned by scaled embedding vectors, promoting diversity.  
  - **Quick check**: How does a DPP kernel encourage diversity, and what does the quality matrix represent?

- **Quadratic Entropy**  
  - **Why needed**: \(Q\) estimates \(H_2 = 1 - \sum p^2\), which measures dispersion of probability mass. It is a Monte Carlo estimate from sampled responses and relates to the chance two samples differ.  
  - **Quick check**: Why might quadratic entropy be more stable than Shannon entropy for small sample sizes?

- **Gram Matrix and Volume Computation**  
  - **Why needed**: \(U\) uses \(\log\det(\Phi\Phi^\top + \epsilon I)\) to measure the spread of embeddings. This is related to the differential entropy of a Gaussian with covariance \(\Phi\Phi^\top/k\).  
  - **Quick check**: Why does the determinant of the Gram matrix reflect the volume enclosed by the response vectors?

- **Multimodal LLM Inference and Feature Extraction**  
  - **Why needed**: UMPIRE requires sampling responses, extracting embeddings (last layer EOS), and computing sequence probabilities from the MLLM.  
  - **Quick check**: How do you obtain \(p_i\) and \(\phi_i\) from a forward pass of a MLLM?

## Architecture Onboarding
1. **Component map**  
   Sampling module -> Embedding extraction -> Probability computation -> Incoherence scaling -> Gram matrix -> Log determinant -> Combine

2. **Critical path**  
   Sampling (slow) → embeddings & probabilities → matrix ops → log det → combine

3. **Design tradeoffs**  
   - **k**: Number of sampled responses; larger k reduces variance but increases cost. UMPIRE works well even with k=5.  
   - **α**: Weight balancing semantic volume U and incoherence Q; can be tuned on a small dev set or set adaptively (median ratio).  
   - **ϵ**: Small jitter (e.g., 1e‑6) to avoid singular Gram matrices; negligible impact.  
   - **Embedding layer**: Last layer recommended; other layers yield similar AUROC.  
   - **Temperature**: Use T=1 for uncertainty sampling; final answer can use low T.  
   - **Long generations**: Token probabilities may underflow; length normalization optional.

4. **Failure signatures**  
   - Degraded U when model’s multimodal alignment is poor (e.g., corrupted encoders).  
   - Low Q due to overconfident wrong answers may mask uncertainty; reliance on U may compensate but not always.  
   - Inappropriate α can imbalance signals; verify on validation set.  
   - Numerical instability for large k and nearly dependent embeddings; ensure sufficient ϵ.  
   - For black‑box models, proxy model embeddings may not align, degrading both terms.

5. **First 3 experiments**  
   1. **Baseline replication**: On VQAv2 val (3000 samples) with Llava‑v1.5‑13b, compute UMPIRE (k=50). Expect AUROC ~0.88, CPC ~0.95, ECE <0.04 (Table 1). Verify against LN‑Ent, Sem.Ent, Eigen.  
   2. **Ablation of components**: Compute U only, Q only, and V. Confirm V significantly improves CPC/ECE over each alone (Table 5, LRT Table 4).  
   3. **Multimodal coherence test (R4)**: Corrupt image input (noise, black, remove) and recompute UMPIRE. Expect AUROC to degrade monotonically as image information is removed (Fig. 2).

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
### Open Question 1
- **Question**: How to extend UMPIRE to long multimodal generation tasks (e.g., reasoning)?
- **Basis**: Conclusion mentions future work on "longer-generation multimodal tasks such as reasoning."
- **Why unresolved**: UMPIRE uses fixed last‑token embeddings; may not capture long‑range semantic diversity.
- **Evidence to resolve**: Apply UMPIRE to long‑form benchmarks; compare calibration; adjust embedding aggregation (e.g., pooled over tokens).

### Open Question 2
- **Question**: Can UMPIRE be used for active learning of MLLMs?
- **Basis**: Conclusion mentions active learning as an application.
- **Why unresolved**: Unclear if volume‑based uncertainty selects the most informative data for model updates.
- **Evidence to resolve**: Run active learning loops with UMPIRE acquisition vs. random/other on a multimodal dataset; measure model improvement per labeled sample.

### Open Question 3
- **Question**: How does sampling dependence affect UMPIRE's theoretical guarantees and empirical performance?
- **Basis**: Theoretical analysis assumes i.i.d. samples, but nucleus sampling introduces correlations.
- **Why unresolved**: Concentration bounds rely on independence; correlated samples may degrade calibration and discrimination.
- **Evidence to resolve**: Compare UMPIRE under independent sampling vs. nucleus sampling; derive new bounds for dependent samples or propose corrections.

## Limitations
- Embedding quality and multimodal alignment: If the model’s multimodal encoder is poor, the semantic volume \(U\) may not reflect true uncertainty.
- \(Q\) miscalibration: Overconfidence on wrong answers yields low incoherence scores, which can mask uncertainty unless compensated by \(U\).
- \(\alpha\) weighting: The linear combination is heuristic; inappropriate values may imbalance diversity and incoherence signals, requiring task‑specific tuning.

## Confidence
- Combined metric \(V\) achieves superior discrimination and calibration: **High**
- Semantic volume \(U\) captures global response diversity: **Medium**
- Incoherence \(Q\) estimates quadratic entropy from probabilities: **Medium**
- Modality‑agnostic, training‑free design: **Medium**

## Next Checks
1. **Stress test**: Deliberately degrade or randomize the MLLM’s multimodal encoder and measure how the correlation between \(U\) and error rates changes relative to the unmodified model.
2. **Cross‑model/task generalization**: Apply UMPIRE to large proprietary MLLMs (e.g., GPT‑4V, Gemini) and tasks beyond QA (e.g., captioning, summarization); compare AUROC and ECE against existing baselines.
3. **\(\alpha\) robustness**: Sweep \(\alpha\) across a wide range on multiple datasets; evaluate the stability of the adaptive median‑ratio method and explore learnable calibration using a tiny unlabeled set.
