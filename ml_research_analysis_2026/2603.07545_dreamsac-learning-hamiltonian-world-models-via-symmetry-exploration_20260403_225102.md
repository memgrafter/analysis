# DreamSAC: Learning Hamiltonian World Models via Symmetry Exploration

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2603.07545
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.07545
- Reference count: 40
- Primary result: DreamSAC achieves 22%-163% higher performance on out-of-distribution physics tasks and up to 10× lower image prediction error compared to DreamerV3.

## Executive Summary
DreamSAC introduces a novel model-based reinforcement learning agent that overcomes the poor extrapolation of learned world models to unseen physical parameters. It combines a Hamiltonian world model, trained with a contrastive objective to produce viewpoint-invariant representations, and an unsupervised exploration strategy driven by Hamiltonian curiosity. This synergy enables the agent to collect physically informative data and learn dynamics that generalize to novel masses, friction, and camera angles. Empirical results show substantial gains over strong baselines, demonstrating the promise of incorporating physical symmetries into world model learning.

## Method Summary
DreamSAC operates in two phases. First, an unsupervised pre‑training phase uses a Symmetry Exploration policy that generates actions guided by an intrinsic reward proportional to the predicted change in the learned Hamiltonian (i.e., the work done on the system), alongside a RND novelty bonus. The world model jointly learns: (i) an encoder that maps pixels to object‑centric slots with canonical coordinates and momenta, (ii) a Hamiltonian function implemented as a G‑invariant Lie Transformer, and (iii) a dynamics prior trained via a variational objective that includes a contrastive loss enforcing viewpoint invariance. During world model training, an explicit Euler integrator stabilizes gradients; during policy imagination, a symplectic Leapfrog integrator preserves energy conservation. Second, for each downstream task, the viewpoint‑robust encoder is frozen, the Hamiltonian is fine‑tuned, and a new actor‑critic is trained in imagination using the task’s extrinsic reward. This decoupling allows rapid adaptation while retaining the learned physical structure.

## Key Results
- Out‑of‑distribution physics tasks: 22%–163% higher success/reward compared to DreamerV3.
- Image prediction error reduced by up to 10× on challenging environments.
- Ablations confirm the necessity of the contrastive viewpoint‑robust loss and Hamiltonian‑based exploration for achieving strong generalization.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Symmetry‑Aware Exploration
The intrinsic reward $r_{sym} \approx |\Delta H_\phi|$ incentivizes the agent to select actions that maximize the predicted change in the Hamiltonian. Under continuous‑time Hamiltonian dynamics with control, $\frac{dH}{dt}$ equals external power input, so maximizing $|\Delta H|$ over discrete steps approximates maximizing work done on the system. This drives the agent to probe the system’s energy structure (potential and kinetic terms) rather than merely visiting novel states. The mechanism relies on $H_\phi$ being a differentiable proxy for true energy; annealing of $r_{sym}$ with a RND bonus stabilizes early learning when the Hamiltonian is still inaccurate. Evidence: the paper links $r_{sym}$ to Eq. (10) (work input) and shows that data collected with $r_{sym}$ yields lower world model prediction error.

### Viewpoint‑Robust Contrastive Learning
The self‑supervised contrastive loss $L_{vr}$ (InfoNCE) operates on pairs of augmented views of the same observation (e.g., crops, perspective shifts). By pulling together the corresponding latent encodings $Z^A_t$ and $Z^B_t$ while pushing apart encodings from other observations, the encoder $q_\phi$ learns to discard camera‑dependent information, producing states $Z_t$ that are invariant to nuisance viewpoint variations. This is essential because the Hamiltonian prior assumes a $G$‑invariant input; pixel reconstruction alone would embed viewpoint details. Evidence includes t‑SNE visualizations (Fig. 3a) where representations from different views cluster tightly only when $L_{vr}$ is active.

### Dual Integration Strategy
DreamSAC employs two different numerical integrators: an explicit Euler method for updating the world model during training (stable gradients) and a symplectic Leapfrog integrator during imagination and inference (energy conservation). The rationale is that the Euler‑trained vector field approximates the true continuous dynamics well enough for a small step size (Δt=0.1), and the symplectic property of Leapfrog then ensures near‑conservation of the learned Hamiltonian $H_\phi$ over long, unrolled trajectories. This hybrid approach balances training stability with physical consistency at test time. Empirical evidence (Supp. Table 10) shows that Leapfrog inference drastically reduces Hamiltonian drift compared to Euler, while maintaining similar prediction MSE.

## Foundational Learning
### Hamiltonian Mechanics & Noether’s Theorem
Hamiltonian mechanics formalizes the total energy $H(q,p)$ as a fundamental conserved quantity in closed systems. Noether’s theorem states that symmetry under time translation implies energy conservation. DreamSAC builds its world model and exploration reward on these principles; understanding $dH/dt$ is key to deriving the intrinsic reward. Quick check: given $H = p^2/(2m) + V(q)$ and an external force $F_{ext}$, what is $dH/dt$ and how does it relate to $r_{sym}$? (Answer: $dH/dt = F_{ext} \cdot dq/dt$, i.e., power input.)

### Contrastive Self‑Supervised Learning (InfoNCE)
InfoNCE reduces the mutual information between representations and nuisance factors by contrasting positive pairs (different augmentations of the same image) against negative samples from other images. The temperature $\tau$ controls the sharpness of the distribution. In $L_{vr}$, this loss forces the encoder to produce viewpoint‑invariant latents. Quick check: In the InfoNCE loss used, what are the positive pairs? (They are $(Z^A_t, Z^B_t)$ from two augmented views of the same timestep.) What role does $\tau$ play? (It scales the logits, acting like a softmax temperature; lower $\tau$ makes the loss focus more on hard negatives.)

### Model‑Based Reinforcement Learning with World Models
DreamSAC falls under the MBRL paradigm where a latent dynamics model is learned and used for planning via imagination. The standard architecture includes a stochastic state $Z_t$ and a deterministic recurrent state $h_t$. DreamSAC modifies the dynamics of $Z_t$ to follow Hamiltonian flow. Quick check: In a typical RSSM, what are the roles of $Z_t$ and $h_t$? ($Z_t$ captures latent variables; $h_t$ summarises past information for the dynamics.) How does DreamSAC change $Z_t$ dynamics? (It replaces the standard transition with a Hamiltonian system where $Z_t$ is split into position $q$ and momentum $p$, and evolves via symplectic integration of $\dot q = \partial H/\partial p$, $\dot p = -\partial H/\partial q + g(q)a$.)

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component map**  
Vision Encoder (q_ϕ) → Slots Z_t = {z_i_t} = (q_i, p_i) → Hamiltonian H_ϕ (Lie Transformer) and control matrix g_ϕ(q) → Dynamics Prior (Euler training / Leapfrog inference) → Decoder + GRU (h_{t+1}) → Reconstruction  
Policy π_θ acts on (h_t, Z_t) in imagination; intrinsic reward r_int computed from H_ϕ change and RND.

**Critical path**  
1. Policy generates action a_t from (h_t, Z_t).  
2. Environment returns next pixel observation x_{t+1}.  
3. Encoder produces Z_{t+1} (object‑centric slots).  
4. Hamiltonian H_ϕ computes r_sym = |H_ϕ(Z_{t+1}) - H_ϕ(Z_t)| minus smoothness penalty.  
5. World model updates with total loss L_total (ELBO + L_vr).  
6. Actor‑critic updates on imagined rollouts (T=64) using r_int.  
7. Annealing shifts weight from RND to r_sym over T_anneal steps.

**Design tradeoffs**  
- Training stability vs. long‑term conservation: Euler (stable) vs. Leapfrog (conservative). The dual strategy uses Euler for training updates, Leapfrog for inference/imagined rollouts.  
- Invariance vs. discriminative power: Excessive L_vr augmentation may discard physically relevant cues; the pipeline limits augmentations to spatial/chromatic only, forbidding temporal transforms.  
- Exploration efficiency vs. stability: r_sym is noisy early on; mixing with RND and annealing mitigates collapse but may reduce sample efficiency initially.

**Failure signatures**  
- Non‑conservative dynamics: Drift in H_ϕ during zero‑action rollouts (Fig. 3b) indicates untrained Hamiltonian or wrong integrator.  
- View‑dependent latents: t‑SNE shows scattered clusters for different camera views when L_vr is disabled or misconfigured.  
- Degenerate exploration: Policy outputs high‑frequency jitter when λ_s = 0 or annealing is too fast; monitor action smoothness.  
- Poor OOD adaptation despite pre‑training: Could signal insufficient expressivity in the G‑invariant architecture or over‑invariance from L_vr.

**First 3 experiments**  
1. Viewpoint Robustness Ablation: Train with γ=0 (no L_vr) and evaluate on an Unseen View task; performance drop validates L_vr.  
2. Exploration Strategy Comparison: Collect 1M steps using full DreamSAC (annealed r_sym) vs. random actions vs. RND‑only. Train a fixed Hamiltonian world model on each dataset and compare image prediction MSE on held‑out data; expect r_sym data to yield lowest MSE.  
3. Integrator Energy Conservation Test: From a pre‑trained model, run zero‑action rollouts in imagination using Euler vs. Leapfrog. Plot H_ϕ(Z_t) over 100 steps; expect Leapfrog to have near‑zero drift while Euler drifts.

## Limitations
- Implementation specifics (e.g., Lie Transformer details, SA Vi initialization) are omitted, hindering exact reproduction and potentially affecting performance.
- The assumption that $|\Delta H_\phi|$ reliably proxies external work may break down when the Hamiltonian model is inaccurate, especially early in training.
- Augmentations for contrastive learning might discard subtle but physically relevant cues if not carefully tuned; the paper restricts augmentations but does not provide a full sensitivity analysis.
- Generalization to extreme out‑of‑distribution shifts (e.g., masses an order of magnitude different, non‑rigid bodies) remains uncertain; the paper tests only moderate parameter variations.

## Confidence
- **High**: The role of the contrastive viewpoint‑robust loss in achieving invariance is strongly supported by ablation experiments and t‑SNE visualizations.  
- **Medium**: The data efficiency of symmetry exploration is backed by prediction error ablations, but it depends critically on the quality of the learned Hamiltonian.  
- **Low**: Generalization to very extreme OOD conditions (e.g., unseen material properties) is not thoroughly evaluated; results may not extrapolate beyond tested ranges.

## Next Checks
1. **Hamiltonian signal sanity**: During pre‑training, compute ground‑truth external work (if available) or energy drift in zero‑action rollouts, and correlate with $r_{sym}$. A low correlation would indicate the proxy is ineffective.  
2. **Contrastive loss ablation**: Train with γ=0 and with augmentations that alter physics (e.g., frame dropping). Measure both viewpoint clustering (t‑SNE) and downstream task performance to detect over‑invariance or failure.  
3. **Extreme OOD stress test**: After pre‑training, fine‑tune on tasks with unseen mass ratios (10× heavier objects) or non‑rigid dynamics. Compare success rates to baselines to assess the limits of the $G$‑invariant prior.
