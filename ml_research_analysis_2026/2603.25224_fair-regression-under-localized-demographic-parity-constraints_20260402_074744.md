# Fair regression under localized demographic parity constraints

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID**: 2603.25224  
- **Source URL**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.25224  
- **Reference count**: 27  
- **Primary result**: Localized demographic parity via thresholded CDF constraints achieves a more favorable fairness–accuracy trade-off than full distribution matching in regression.

## Executive Summary
Demographic parity (DP) in regression often forces strict distributional alignment between groups, leading to significant accuracy loss. This paper proposes a localized relaxation: instead of matching entire distributions, it enforces equality only at a finite set of thresholds on the cumulative distribution functions (CDFs) of predictions. The optimal predictor under these constraints is derived via Lagrangian duality, yielding a closed-form solution that discretizes the base regressor’s output. A practical post-processing method is introduced that uses a small labeled sample to train a base regressor and a larger unlabeled sample to calibrate the fairness constraints. Dithering is added to ensure continuity. Theoretically, the discretization cost and constraint violation are bounded; empirically, the approach reduces disparities at targeted quantiles while preserving much of the predictive performance, outperforming strong DP.

## Method Summary
The authors formulate a fair regression problem with constraints on the differences between groupwise and marginal CDFs at a chosen set of thresholds. Using Lagrangian duality, they characterize the optimal discrete predictor as a piecewise constant function derived from the base regressor’s output, with threshold-dependent group-specific adjustments. This solution is implemented via a model-agnostic post-processing pipeline: a base regressor is trained on labeled data; an unlabeled dataset is then used to estimate the necessary CDFs and solve for the optimal discretization mapping. To avoid discontinuous jumps in predictions, a small random dithering noise is added after discretization. Theoretical analysis provides bounds on the excess risk due to discretization and on the maximal constraint violation in finite samples, scaling with the number of thresholds and sample sizes.

## Key Results
- Localized CDF constraints allow selective targeting of fairness violations at specific quantiles or thresholds, avoiding the overly restrictive nature of full distributional parity.
- The discretization step incurs a bounded increase in risk (gap ≤ C A²/K, where K is number of thresholds), and the maximal constraint violation converges at rate Oₚ(√(1/N + K²/N)).
- Experiments on synthetic and real datasets (e.g., Adult income) show lower root mean squared error and comparable or better constraint violation (U) and Kolmogorov–Smirnov statistic than methods enforcing strong DP, demonstrating a improved fairness–accuracy trade-off.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The mechanism hinges on relaxing global distributional matching to local pointwise constraints. By limiting enforcement to a finite set of thresholds, the optimization problem becomes tractable and less demanding on the predictor. Lagrangian duality transforms the constrained problem into an unconstrained one, revealing that the optimal predictor is a thresholded and shifted version of the base regressor—effectively a discretization with group-specific offsets. This structure allows exact solutions for each threshold. Dithering then smooths the discrete steps without substantially affecting the CDFs at the constrained points. The use of unlabeled data for calibration separates the estimation of fairness constraints from the supervised learning objective, reducing the burden on the limited labeled sample.

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component map**:  
Base regressor (trained on labeled data) → CDF estimation (using unlabeled data) → Lagrangian solution (compute per-threshold offsets) → Discretization mapping → Dithering → Final predictor

**Critical path**:  
1. Train base regressor on labeled dataset.  
2. Estimate groupwise and marginal CDFs from unlabeled dataset at predetermined thresholds.  
3. Solve Lagrangian dual to obtain optimal discretization thresholds and group-specific shifts.  
4. Apply discretization and shift to base regressor outputs.  
5. Add dithering noise (uniform within bin width) to ensure continuity.

**Design tradeoffs**:  
- Number of thresholds K: Larger K captures finer distributional details but increases discretization cost and finite-sample error; smaller K improves stability but reduces locality precision.  
- Dithering magnitude: Must be small enough to preserve CDF constraints yet large enough to smooth prediction steps; typically proportional to bin width.  
- Unlabeled data size: Larger unlabeled sample yields more accurate CDF estimates and tighter constraint satisfaction, but the method remains viable even with moderate sizes due to parametric efficiency.

**Failure signatures**:  
- Constraint violation U remains high despite calibration, indicating poor CDF estimation (e.g., unlabeled data unrepresentative) or too aggressive dithering.  
- Significant drop in predictive accuracy when K is large, suggesting overfitting to noise in CDF estimates or excessive discretization.  
- Discontinuities in final predictions if dithering is omitted, violating the continuity assumption in theory.

**First experiments**:  
1. Vary K on a synthetic dataset with known group disparities; plot U and RMSE vs K to observe trade-off curve.  
2. Compare dithered vs. non-dithered predictions on a real dataset (e.g., Adult) by measuring the Kolmogorov–Smirnov statistic between prediction distributions and visual inspection of prediction continuity.  
3. Implement the post-processing pipeline and benchmark against “strong DP” (full distribution matching) on multiple datasets, reporting RMSE, U, and KS statistic.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
The paper does not explicitly enumerate open questions or future research directions. However, the limitations discussed in the paper point to several natural extensions, such as: extending the localized CDF constraint framework to other fairness notions (e.g., equalized odds), developing more robust methods for selecting the number of thresholds and dithering magnitude, and scaling the approach to high-dimensional covariates and complex model classes. These constitute potential open questions for subsequent work.

## Limitations
- Requires a large representative unlabeled sample for accurate CDF estimation; availability and potential biases in such data are practical concerns.
- Choice of number of thresholds K and dithering magnitude lacks robust tuning guidelines; these hyperparameters significantly affect the fairness–accuracy trade-off.
- Theoretical bounds on discretization cost and constraint violation may be conservative; their empirical tightness across diverse data distributions remains unclear.
- Experimental evaluation is limited to a small set of synthetic and real datasets, raising questions about scalability to high-dimensional covariates or complex, non-linear relationships.
- Framework is tailored specifically to demographic parity; extensions to other fairness notions (e.g., equalized odds) are not addressed.

## Confidence
- **High**: Theoretical characterization of the optimal discretized predictor via Lagrangian duality and the resulting closed-form solution.
- **Medium**: Finite-sample guarantees (convergence rates for constraint violation and excess risk) and empirical results on the tested datasets.
- **Low**: Broad claims of superiority over full distribution matching and scalability to very large K or high-dimensional feature spaces.
