# Uniform a priori bounds and error analysis for the Adam stochastic gradient descent optimization method

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2603.18899
- Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.18899
- References: 38
- Result: Provides the first unconditional convergence analysis for Adam on strongly convex stochastic optimization problems, establishing uniform a priori bounds on iterates and deriving error bound E[||Θ_n - ϑ||^p]^{1/p} ≤ c M^{-1}(1-β_2) + c √γ_n.

## Executive Summary
This paper presents the first unconditional convergence analysis for the Adam optimizer on strongly convex stochastic optimization problems (SOPs). The authors establish uniform a priori bounds on Adam's iterates, resolving the long-standing open question of whether Adam remains bounded without additional assumptions. By constructing a Lyapunov function that combines the objective gap and adaptive variance estimates, they prove that under strong convexity and local Lipschitz continuity of the gradients, Adam's iterates are uniformly bounded over time. This yields an explicit error bound: E[||Θ_n - ϑ||^p]^{1/p} ≤ c_0 M^{-1}(1-β_2) + c_β √γ_n, where M is minibatch size, β_2 the second moment parameter, and γ_n step sizes. The analysis removes the boundedness assumption that was required in all prior Adam convergence theorems. The results are demonstrated on quadratic and regularized quadratic losses, providing pathwise uniform bounds and confirming that Adam achieves the same convergence rate as SGD under strong convexity.

## Method Summary
The analysis centers on a carefully constructed Lyapunov function H_n that combines the objective gap L(Θ_n) - L(ϑ) and a term involving the adaptive moment estimates m_n and v_n. Under the assumptions of strong convexity (with constant q>0) and local Lipschitz continuity of the gradients (with exponents τ_1, τ_2 < 1/2), the authors show that H_n is non-increasing in expectation up to terms controlled by the step sizes. By carefully bounding the moment estimates using the Adam update rules, they derive a uniform bound on H_n that holds for all n. This uniform bound then yields a time-uniform bound on the iterate distance ‖Θ_n - ϑ‖ via strong convexity. Combining this with prior conditional convergence results (which assumed boundedness) produces an unconditional error bound of the form E[‖Θ_n - ϑ‖^p]^{1/p} ≤ c_0 M^{-1}(1-β_2) + c_β √γ_n, where the constants depend on problem parameters but not on n. The paper also provides concrete examples (quadratic and regularized quadratic losses) where the assumptions hold and the bounds are shown to be pathwise uniform.

## Key Results
- First unconditional convergence analysis for Adam on strongly convex SOPs; establishes uniform a priori bounds on iterates.
- Derives error bound E[‖Θ_n - ϑ‖^p]^{1/p} ≤ c_0 M^{-1}(1-β_2) + c_β √γ_n under strong convexity and local Lipschitz gradients, matching SGD rate.
- Removes boundedness assumption from prior Adam convergence theorems and applies to quadratic and regularized quadratic losses.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- **Claim**: A carefully constructed Lyapunov function combining the objective gap and adaptive variance estimates yields a non-increasing quantity under Adam’s updates, enabling stability analysis.
- **Mechanism**: Define \( H_n = L(\Theta_n, x) + \frac{1}{2(1-\alpha)} \sum_{i=1}^d (m_{n,i}^2 V_{n,i}) \), where \( m_n \) and \( V_n \) are the biased first and second moment estimates. Using the smoothness and convexity properties of \( L \), the recursive update rules, and algebraic manipulations (e.g., expanding differences), the authors show \( H_n \leq H_{n-1} + \text{terms controlled by step sizes} \). Summing over \( n \) and leveraging step size conditions bounds \( H_n \), which then bounds \( \|\Theta_n - \vartheta\| \) via strong convexity.
- **Core assumption**: Strong convexity of the objective (with constant \( q \)), local Lipschitz continuity of \( \nabla_\theta L \) and \( \nabla_x L \) with specific exponents (\( \tau_1, \tau_2 < 1/2 \)), non-increasing step sizes satisfying \( \limsup_{n\to\infty} \big( \gamma_n^{-2}(\gamma_n-\gamma_{n+1}) + \sum_{m=n}^\infty \gamma_m^p \big) = 0 \), and Adam parameters with \( \beta \in (\alpha^2, 1) \) and \( \beta_1^2 + q \le \beta_2 \).
- **Evidence anchors**:
  - [abstract]: "Using a Lyapunov function combining objective gap and adaptive variance estimate..."
  - [section 2.1]: Formal definition (Equation 41), recursive inequality (Equation 61), and bounds using Lemmas 2.1 and 2.2.
  - [corpus]: The paper cites prior work [10, Lemma 2.9] on a priori bounds, but innovates by using a Lyapunov function that is uniform in time and parameters. The corpus shows Lyapunov-style analyses are common for adaptive methods but this one handles Adam’s adaptive steps tightly.
- **Break condition**: If the step size decay is too slow (failing the lim sup condition) or if \( \beta \notin (\alpha^2, 1) \), the Lyapunov function may increase and the bound collapses.

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim**: The Lyapunov function’s boundedness directly yields uniform (time-uniform) a priori bounds on the Adam iterates \( \|\Theta_n\| \), resolving the open problem of whether Adam remains bounded.
- **Mechanism**: From the Lyapunov bound \( H_n \leq A + A\sum_{k=1}^n \tau_1 \eta^2 \gamma_k^2 \), and using the definitions of \( H_n \), strong convexity gives a lower bound on \( L(\Theta_n,x)-L(\vartheta,x) \) in terms of \( \|\Theta_n - \vartheta\|^2 \). The term \( \sum_i m_{n,i}^2 V_{n,i} \) is bounded via the moment update rules and Lemma 2.2, which controls \( |m_{n,i}| V_{n,i} \) by \( \gamma_n \eta d^{-1} \). Combining these with algebraic inequalities and case analysis (depending on \( \|\Theta_{n-1}-\vartheta\| \) and \( \sum_i m_{n,i}^2 \)) yields an explicit bound on \( \|\Theta_n\| \) independent of \( n \).
- **Core assumption**: Same as Mechanism 1, plus compactness of the data domain \( K \) (so Lipschitz constants are finite) and bounded initial moments \( m_0, V_0 \) (typically zero).
- **Evidence anchors**:
  - [abstract]: "establishes uniform a priori bounds for the Adam optimizer on strongly convex stochastic optimization problems"
  - [section 2.3, Corollary 2.7]: The explicit uniform bound (Equation 152) showing \( \|\Theta_n\| \le c(1-\alpha)^{-1/2}\|\Theta_0\| + c|\ln(\alpha)|^2 + c(\beta-\alpha^2)^{-2} + c(1-\alpha)^{-2} \).
  - [corpus]: Prior work [8,11] assumed boundedness; this paper provides the first unconditional proof. The corpus [10] had bounds that explode as \( \beta_2 \to 1 \), but this paper’s bounds are uniform in \( \beta \) under the conditions.
- **Break condition**: The bound’s constant \( c \) depends on \( \kappa^{-1} \), Lipschitz constants, and domain size. If the problem is not strongly convex (\( \kappa \) small) or the domain is large, the bound may be loose but still finite. The key break is violation of the step size and parameter conditions.

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim**: The uniform a priori bounds enable an unconditional convergence rate for Adam on strongly convex SOPs, removing the boundedness assumption from prior theorems.
- **Mechanism**: The conditional convergence analysis in [8] (specifically [8, Corollary 2.11]) gives an error bound of the form \( \mathbb{E}[\|\Theta_n - \vartheta\|^p]^{1/p} \le C (M^{-1}(1-\beta_2)^{-1} + \sqrt{\gamma_n}) \) assuming that \( \|\Theta_n\| \) is uniformly bounded. By substituting the uniform bound from Corollary 2.7, this assumption is automatically satisfied, yielding the unconditional bound (Equation 235). The rate matches that of SGD under strong convexity, showing Adam is not worse in this setting.
- **Core assumption**: Both the a priori bounds (Mechanisms 1–2) and the conditional convergence conditions from [8] must hold: strong convexity, gradient Lipschitz (in both \( \theta \) and \( x \)), step sizes decaying as \( \gamma_n = O(n^{-1/2}) \) (or faster for higher rates), and the Adam parameter constraints \( \beta_1^2 + q \le \beta_2 \) and \( \beta_2 \in [q,1) \).
- **Evidence anchors**:
  - [abstract]: "first unconditional convergence analysis for the Adam optimizer on strongly convex stochastic optimization problems"
  - [section 3.1, Theorem 3.1]: The unconditional bound (Equation 235) and its statement as a consequence of Corollary 2.7 and [8, Corollary 2.11].
  - [corpus]: The paper positions itself as solving the open problem of boundedness for Adam (see [abstract] and [1.3]). The corpus includes conditional analyses [2,11] and non-convergence results [5,6,9,14,15,21]; this work provides the first unconditional positive result.
- **Break condition**: If the step sizes do not satisfy both the a priori conditions (lim sup) and the conditional convergence conditions (e.g., \( \sum \gamma_n = \infty \) for convergence to optimum), the rate may not hold. Also if \( \beta_2 \) is too small (e.g., \( \beta_2 < \beta_1^2 + q \)), the condition \( \beta_1^2 + q \le \beta_2 \) fails and the bound may not be valid.

## Foundational Learning

1. **Strong Convexity**
   - Why needed: Ensures a unique minimizer and provides a quadratic lower bound on the objective, crucial for deriving linear convergence rates and bounding the iterate distance from optimum.
   - Quick check: For a differentiable function \( f \), strong convexity with constant \( q > 0 \) means \( f(\theta) \ge f(\vartheta) + \langle \nabla f(\vartheta), \theta-\vartheta \rangle + \frac{q}{2} \|\theta-\vartheta\|^2 \). How does this inequality help in proving that gradient descent converges linearly?

2. **Lyapunov Function**
   - Why needed: To simultaneously track the optimization error and the adaptive variance terms in Adam, ensuring both are controlled over time. It acts as a “energy” function that does not increase on average.
   - Quick check: In stability theory, a Lyapunov function \( V(x) \) satisfies \( V(x_{t+1}) \le V(x_t) \) (or decreases in expectation). How is the Lyapunov function \( H_n \) adapted to handle Adam’s adaptive steps and the stochastic setting?

3. **Adam Update Rules**
   - Why needed: The analysis is specific to Adam’s update equations (with bias-corrected first and second moments). Understanding these is essential to follow the derivations.
   - Quick check: Write down the complete Adam update for coordinate \( i \): \( m_{n,i} = \beta_1 m_{n-1,i} + (1-\beta_1) g_{n,i} \), \( v_{n,i} = \beta_2 v_{n-1,i} + (1-\beta_2) g_{n,i}^2 \), \( \hat{m}_{n,i} = m_{n,i}/(1-\beta_1^n) \), \( \hat{v}_{n,i} = v_{n,i}/(1-\beta_2^n) \), \( \Theta_{n,i} = \Theta_{n-1,i} - \gamma_n \hat{m}_{n,i} / (\sqrt{\hat{v}_{n,i}} + \varepsilon) \). What is the purpose of the bias correction?

4. **Local Lipschitz Continuity of Gradients**
   - Why needed: To control how gradients change when parameters or data perturb, which is key in bounding the Lyapunov drift and the stochastic errors.
   - Quick check: The condition \( \|\nabla_\theta L(\theta,x) - \nabla_\theta L(\vartheta,x)\| \le K \|\theta-\vartheta\| (1 + \|\theta\|^{\tau_1} + \|\vartheta\|^{\tau_1}) \) is a *local* Lipschitz condition (since the factor \( 1 + \|\theta\|^{\tau_1} \) grows). Why is this weaker than global Lipschitz and what does it capture about neural network losses?

5. **Stochastic Optimization with Minibatches**
   - Why needed: The setting uses i.i.d. data samples and minibatches of size \( M \), introducing stochasticity. The analysis handles the stochastic noise via the Lyapunov function and moment estimates.
   - Quick check: In minibatch SGD, the gradient is averaged over \( M \) samples. How does \( M \) affect the variance and the convergence rate? What is the trade-off?

## Architecture Onboarding

### Component map
- **Inputs**: Compact data domain \( K \subset \mathbb{R}^{\mathcal{D}} \), initial parameters \( \Theta_0 \in \mathbb{R}^d \), step size schedule \( (\gamma_n)_{n\in\mathbb{N}} \), Adam hyperparameters \( \beta_1, \beta_2 \in (0,1) \), \( \varepsilon > 0 \), minibatch size \( M \).
- **Components**:
  1. **First moment buffer** \( m_n \in \mathbb{R}^d \): exponential moving average of gradients.
  2. **Second moment buffer** \( v_n \in \mathbb{R}^d \): exponential moving average of squared gradients.
  3. **Bias correction**: Scale \( m_n \) by \( 1/(1-\beta_1^n) \) and \( v_n \) by \( 1/(1-\beta_2^n) \) to correct initialization bias.
  4. **Parameter update**: \( \Theta_n = \Theta_{n-1} - \gamma_n \hat{m}_n / (\sqrt{\hat{v}_n} + \varepsilon) \), applied coordinate-wise.
- **Output**: Sequence \( (\Theta_n)_{n\in\mathbb{N}_0} \).

### Critical path
1. Initialize \( m_0 = 0 \), \( v_0 = 0 \).
2. For each iteration \( n = 1,2,\dots \):
   a. Sample minibatch \( \{X_{n,m}\}_{m=1}^M \sim \text{data distribution on } K \).
   b. Compute gradient estimate \( g_n = \frac{1}{M} \sum_{m=1}^M \nabla_\theta L(\Theta_{n-1}, X_{n,m}) \).
   c. Update moments: \( m_n = \beta_1 m_{n-1} + (1-\beta_1) g_n \), \( v_n = \beta_2 v_{n-1} + (1-\beta_2) g_n^2 \).
   d. Bias-correct: \( \hat{m}_n = m_n / (1-\beta_1^n) \), \( \hat{v}_n = v_n / (1-\beta_2^n) \).
   e. Update parameters: \( \Theta_n = \Theta_{n-1} - \gamma_n \hat{m}_n / (\sqrt{\hat{v}_n} + \varepsilon) \).

### Design tradeoffs
- **\( \beta_1 \) and \( \beta_2 \)**: Larger \( \beta_2 \) (close to 1) reduces variance but slows adaptation to new information. The analysis requires \( \beta_2 > \beta_1^2 \) and \( \beta_1^2 + q \le \beta_2 \); typical defaults \( \beta_1=0.9, \beta_2=0.999 \) satisfy this for small \( q \). Larger \( \beta_2 \) improves the bound \( \mathcal{C}_0 M^{-1}(1-\beta_2) \) at the cost of larger bias correction factors.
- **Step size schedule \( \gamma_n \)**: Must be non-increasing, converge to 0, and satisfy the lim sup condition (Equation 27). Common choices: \( \gamma_n = \gamma / \sqrt{n} \) (yielding \( O(1/\sqrt{n}) \) rate) or \( \gamma_n = \gamma / n \) (faster decay). Too slow decay worsens rate; too fast may cause instability.
- **Minibatch size \( M \)**: Larger \( M \) reduces gradient noise (improves the \( O(M^{-1}) \) term) but increases computational cost per iteration. The bound is \( O(M^{-1}(1-\beta_2)) \), so for \( \beta_2 \) close to 1, \( M \) may need to be large to offset the \( (1-\beta_2)^{-1} \) factor.
- **Epsilon \( \varepsilon \)**: Prevents division by zero; typically \( 10^{-8} \). Larger \( \varepsilon \) adds bias, but the analysis treats it as fixed and small.

### Failure signatures
- **Boundedness violation**: If step sizes are too large or the problem is not strongly convex, \( \|\Theta_n\| \) may grow without bound, violating the a priori assumption. The paper’s bound would not hold.
- **Non-convergence to optimum**: If strong convexity or Lipschitz conditions fail (e.g., in non-convex deep learning), Adam may converge to a non-optimal point or cycle. The error bound becomes vacuous.
- **Step size schedule mismatch**: Using a constant step size violates the \( \limsup \) condition; the analysis does not apply. Empirically, constant steps often cause divergence in Adam for strongly convex problems.
- **Parameter condition violation**: If \( \beta_1^2 + q > \beta_2 \) (e.g., \( \beta_1=0.9, \beta_2=0.9 \)), the condition \( \beta_1^2+q \le \beta_2 \) fails for any \( q>0 \), and the bound may not hold. Practitioners should ensure \( \beta_2 > \beta_1^2 \).

### First 3 experiments
1. **Synthetic strongly convex quadratic**: Let \( L(\theta, x) = \|A\theta - x\|^2 \) with invertible \( A \). Choose \( d=10 \), sample \( A \) and \( x \) from Gaussians. Run Adam with \( \beta_1=0.9, \beta_2=0.999, \varepsilon=10^{-8}, \gamma_n = 0.1/\sqrt{n} \), minibatch \( M=32 \). Measure \( \|\Theta_n - \theta^*\| \) and compare with the bound \( \mathcal{C}_0 M^{-1}(1-\beta_2) + \mathcal{C}_\beta \sqrt{\gamma_n} \) (estimate constants from early iterations). Verify uniform boundedness and decay.
2. **Regularized quadratic with nonlinearity**: Use \( L(\theta, x) = \|A\theta - x\|^2 + \lambda \|\theta\|^r \) with \( r \in [0.5, 0.75) \). This fits Corollary 2.12. Check that the uniform bound holds and that the convergence rate matches \( O(1/\sqrt{n}) \).
3. **Comparison with SGD**: On the same quadratic problem, run standard SGD (constant step size \( \eta \) and no adaptive moments). Compare the optimization error and the stability of parameter norms. According to the theory, Adam should achieve similar \( O(1/\sqrt{n}) \) rates under strong convexity, but might be more robust to ill-conditioning (e.g., if \( A \) has large eigenvalues spread).

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- **Question**: Can unconditional convergence analysis of Adam be extended to non-convex stochastic optimization, particularly for deep neural network training?
- **Basis in paper**: [explicit] The abstract explicitly states that providing a complete error analysis for Adam "not only for optimizing DNNs but even when applied to strongly convex SOPs" remains an open research problem. The authors claim to solve the strongly convex case but leave the DNN case open.
- **Why unresolved**: The analysis fundamentally relies on strong convexity to derive a priori bounds and the Lyapunov function technique. Non-convex loss landscapes lack this structure, making the current approach inapplicable.
- **What evidence would resolve it**: A theoretical framework that establishes unconditional bounds (or convergence) for Adam under non-convex assumptions, e.g., using gradient domination, smoothness, or overparametrization conditions that reflect modern deep learning.

### Open Question 2
- **Question**: To what extent can the regularity assumptions—local Lipschitz gradient and uniform strong convexity—be relaxed while retaining unconditional bounds for Adam?
- **Basis in paper**: [inferred] The main results (Prop. 2.3, Thm. 3.1) assume for each fixed data point the loss in θ is strongly convex and the gradients satisfy polynomial-growth Lipschitz bounds with exponents τ₁,τ₂ < ½. These conditions exclude common non-smooth losses from ReLU networks and may be stronger than necessary.
- **Why unresolved**: The recursive Lyapunov argument uses these conditions to control terms like ∥(∇ₓL)(θ,x) − (∇ₓL)(ϑ,x)∥. Without them, the key inequalities (e.g., (68)–(69)) may fail or yield infinite constants.
- **What evidence would resolve it**: Either a counterexample showing divergence under weaker assumptions, or a new analysis (e.g., via one‑sided Lipschitz or PL‑inequalities) that proves unconditional bounds under more realistic conditions.

### Open Question 3
- **Question**: The proven error bound contains a factor proportional to M⁻¹(1−β₂) (or possibly its inverse) that cannot be removed. Can tighter bounds be obtained that remain meaningful for the standard practice of choosing β₂ very close to 1 (e.g., 0.999)?
- **Basis in paper**: [inferred] Theorem 1.1 yields 𝔼[∥Θₙ − ϑ∥ᵖ]¹/ᵖ ≤ c₀M⁻¹(1−β₂) + cᵦ√γₙ (with appropriate interpretation). The authors emphasize that the first term is indispensable. With β₂=0.999, this term is about 1000 × M⁻¹, which is large unless the batch size is huge; moreover, if the dependence were inverse, the bound would be vacuous.
- **Why unresolved**: The term arises from the variance scaling in Adam’s adaptive steps and the bias‑correction factors. The analysis does not attempt to optimize this dependence, leaving open whether the (1−β₂) factor is fundamental or an artifact.
- **What evidence would resolve it**: A refined analysis that either eliminates the (1−β₂) factor or proves that it is unavoidable. Empirical studies might also suggest a sharper theoretical dependency by matching lower bounds.

### Open Question 4
- **Question**: Do uniform a priori bounds and unconditional convergence guarantees extend to popular Adam variants such as AMSGrad or AdamW?
- **Basis in paper**: [inferred] The paper treats only the original Adam algorithm with bias correction as defined by Kingma & Ba. Variants like AMSGrad (which uses a maximum of past squared gradients) and AdamW (decoupled weight decay) are widely adopted in practice but are not considered.
- **Why unresolved**: The proof technique builds on the exact exponential moving average recursions for both first and second moments, which are altered in the variants. For instance, AMSGrad’s max operation breaks linearity, and AdamW’s separate weight‑decay step changes the fixed‑point equation.
- **What evidence would resolve it**: Adaptation of the Lyapunov framework or development of new methods to establish a priori bounds for these variants, possibly under similar strong convexity/local Lipschitz assumptions.

## Limitations

- The local Lipschitz condition with exponents τ₁,τ₂ < ½ excludes common non-smooth losses from ReLU-based deep networks.
- The step-size condition (limsup) is non-standard and its practical restrictiveness is unclear; typical constant or slowly decaying step sizes may violate it.
- The error bound's dependence on M⁻¹(1-β₂) may be problematic for standard β₂ values near 1, requiring impractically large batch sizes.
- Constants c₀, c_β are not explicitly derived and likely loose.

## Confidence

- Uniform a priori bounds: High
- Unconditional convergence rate: Medium
- Resolution of boundedness open problem: High
