# Sensor to Pixels: Decentralized Swarm Gathering via Image-Based Reinforcement Learning

## Quick Facts
*   **arXiv ID:** 2601.03413
*   **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.03413
*   **Reference Count:** 19
*   **Primary Result:** A CNN-based RL policy learns decentralized swarm gathering from bearing-only image inputs, achieving 100% connectivity on 20-agent hard cases in 506 steps, outperforming the analytical solution (702 steps) and maintaining connectivity where a vector-based method (VariAntNet) fails in marginal 30-agent scenarios.

## Executive Summary
This paper addresses the challenge of decentralized swarm gathering where agents only perceive the bearings of neighbors within a limited range. The core innovation lies in encoding each agent's unordered set of neighbor bearings as a fixed-size 75x75 binary pixel image. This spatial representation is processed by a Convolutional Neural Network (CNN) within a Centralized Training with Decentralized Execution (CTDE) Multi-Agent Reinforcement Learning (MARL) framework using Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO). A carefully shaped reward, combining a local penalty for losing any neighbor with a global incentive to shrink the swarm's bounding radius, guides learning. The method demonstrates high reliability in maintaining connectivity across challenging configurations, significantly outperforming a traditional analytical approach and showing superior robustness compared to a recent vector-based learned method in dense, marginal scenarios.

## Method Summary
The proposed system uses a CTDE MARL architecture. Each agent's observation is a 75x75 binary image: the agent is at the center, and each within-range neighbor is represented by a 3x3 pixel block placed according to its relative bearing. This image feeds a shared CNN feature extractor (three convolutional layers) whose output is processed by separate actor and critic heads. The actor outputs a continuous action (heading change and step size). The critic, which has access to global state during training, helps stabilize learning. The reward function is a weighted sum: a local component heavily penalizes the loss of any neighbor connection, and a global component rewards reduction of the swarm's overall bounding radius. Training occurs first on 10-agent constellations for 150 million steps, then on 20-agent constellations for another 150 million steps using PPO.

## Key Results
*   On 20-agent "Challenging" configurations, the RL policy (RL20) achieves 100% connectivity and converges in 506 steps, faster than the analytical solution (702 steps) though slower than VariAntNet (322 steps, 95.2% connectivity).
*   In the harder "Marginal" setting with 30 agents, RL20 preserves 91.6% connectivity in 1173 steps, while VariAntNet's connectivity drops to 45.8%, making the RL method the only viable option for such dense swarms.
*   The approach demonstrates a clear trade-off: it prioritizes guaranteed connectivity over absolute convergence speed, offering improved reliability at a moderate cost in steps.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1: Spatial Order-Invariance via Pixel Grid Encoding
*   **Claim:** Encoding unordered bearing vectors as a fixed-size binary pixel image enables a CNN to extract spatial features that are inherently permutation-invariant to the number and order of neighbors.
*   **Mechanism:** Each neighbor's bearing is mapped to a 3×3 block on a 75×75 grid centered on the agent. The CNN's convolutional kernels perform weight-sharing operations over this grid, detecting local spatial patterns (e.g., clusters, gaps) that correspond to the relational geometry of neighbors. This spatial processing is agnostic to the sequence in which neighbor bearings were provided.
*   **Core assumption:** The spatial arrangement of neighbors, captured by pixel proximity on the grid, contains the necessary information for control decisions, superseding the need for algebraic vector summaries that require explicit ordering or set-processing architectures.
*   **Evidence anchors:** The abstract states observations are "encoded as structured visual inputs" for feature extraction. Section 4.1 details the 75×75 binary matrix projection. Performance gains over vector-based VariAntNet in dense cases suggest the image encoding captures useful relational cues.
*   **Break condition:** If the sensing range is too large relative to the fixed grid size, neighbor projections overlap excessively, causing spatial patterns to become indistinguishable and degrading policy performance.

### Mechanism 2: Dual-Reward Shaping for Connectivity-Convergence Trade-off
*   **Claim:** The combination of a strong local neighbor-loss penalty and a global bounding-radius reduction reward shapes a policy that prioritizes strict connectivity preservation while still driving overall convergence.
*   **Mechanism:** The local penalty (`P_ln`) creates immediate, large negative reinforcement for any edge loss in the visibility graph, enforcing a "never lose a neighbor" principle. The global reward (`C_g`) provides a sustained positive incentive to shrink the swarm. The PPO agent learns a policy that balances these, e.g., by moving in directions that pull the swarm together without creating vulnerable gaps or isolated agents.
*   **Core assumption:** A weighted sum of local and global reward signals can be optimized via RL to achieve a Pareto-like balance between the hard constraint (connectivity) and the objective (convergence speed).
*   **Evidence anchors:** Section 4.4 defines the reward structure. Section 5.3 and Table 2 show RL achieving 100% connectivity versus VariAntNet's drop. The ablation study (Section 6) shows that setting `P_ln=0` leads to failure in marginal configurations, directly supporting the necessity of the local component.
*   **Break condition:** If `P_ln` is too high relative to `C_g`, agents become risk-averse and freeze (observed in ablation with `P_ln=-1`). If `P_ln` is too low, connectivity is sacrificed for speed.

### Mechanism 3: CTDE Enables Learning from Local Images
*   **Claim:** Centralized Training with Decentralized Execution (CTDE) allows the CNN-based policy to learn cooperative strategies from local image observations that would be unstable or impossible in a fully decentralized training setting.
*   **Mechanism:** During training, the centralized critic has access to the global state (all agent positions) to compute the global reward component and provide accurate value estimates, which stabilizes policy gradient updates. The actor only sees the local 75x75 image, forcing it to infer necessary coordination cues from the spatial pattern of its immediate neighbors. This separation mitigates the non-stationarity issues that plague fully decentralized multi-agent training.
*   **Core assumption:** A centralized critic can provide effective credit assignment and value bootstrapping even when the actors operate on partial, image-based observations, enabling the learning of a decentralized policy that implicitly coordinates.
*   **Evidence anchors:** The abstract and section 4 describe the CTDE PPO framework with shared CNN encoder and separate actor/critic heads. The successful deployment of a purely local image-based policy at execution is indirect evidence that the critic's guidance during training was effective.
*   **Break condition:** If the local image observation lacks sufficient information to infer the state of the broader swarm (e.g., an agent sees only one neighbor in a large swarm), the actor cannot learn to coordinate with unseen agents, leading to swarm fragmentation.

## Foundational Learning
*   **Concept: Multi-Agent Reinforcement Learning (MARL) & CTDE**
    *   *Why needed here:* The problem requires multiple agents to learn a cooperative behavior from their own sensory data without explicit inter-agent communication. CTDE is the specific paradigm used to make this feasible by using a centralized critic to stabilize training while keeping execution decentralized.
    *   *Quick check question:* Why is a centralized critic necessary during training if all agents will execute decentralized policies?
*   **Concept: Convolutional Neural Networks for Spatial Feature Extraction**
    *   *Why needed here:* The paper's core innovation is replacing traditional vector-based observations with pixel images. Understanding CNNs—convolutional kernels, strides, weight sharing, and activation functions—is key to grasping how spatial patterns from neighbor bearings are automatically learned.
    *   *Quick check question:* How does a convolutional layer's weight sharing contribute to permutation invariance of neighbor order?
*   **Concept: Graph Connectivity in Swarm Systems**
    *   *Why needed here:* The primary task constraint is maintaining connectivity (i.e., no edge loss) in the time-varying visibility graph. The reward function and evaluation metrics are directly derived from graph properties like node degree and bounding radius.
    *   *Quick check question:* What is the difference between a "leaf" agent and a "bridge" (cut edge) in the visibility graph, and why do they matter for conservative behavior?
*   **Concept: Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO)**
    *   *Why needed here:* PPO is the specific on-policy RL algorithm used to train the actor and critic networks. Its clipped objective helps stabilize training in the high-variance, multi-agent setting.
    *   *Quick check question:* How does PPO's clipped objective function help prevent destructive policy updates during training?
*   **Concept: Bearing-Only Sensing**
    *   *Why needed here:* The sensory constraint (only direction, not distance, to neighbors within range) defines the observation space and makes the problem geometrically and computationally distinct from full-state sensing.
    *   *Quick check question:* Why is bearing-only sensing a harder problem than having both bearing and range information, and how does the image encoding attempt to compensate?

## Architecture Onboarding
*   **Component map:**
    Bearing Vectors → 75x75 Binary Image → CNN Feature Extractor (3 Conv Layers) → Flatten → 512-D Feature Vector → [Actor Head (→ α, σ) | Critic Head (→ Value)]
    Global State (Critic Only) → Critic Head

*   **Critical path:** 1) Agent senses neighbor bearings. 2) Observation Encoder converts bearings to a 75x75 image. 3) CNN extracts a 512-dimensional spatial feature vector. 4) Actor head maps features to a continuous action (heading change α, step size σ). 5) Agent executes action in the environment. 6) Environment computes new state and the combined reward (`R_local` + `R_global`). 7) PPO algorithm updates actor and critic networks using collected experience, with the critic leveraging global state for accurate value estimation.

*   **Design tradeoffs:**
    *   *Image resolution (75x75):* Higher resolution could capture finer bearing details for larger swarms but increases CNN parameters and computational cost. The fixed size imposes a hard limit on neighbor density before pattern overlap occurs.
    *   *Reward weights (`P_ln`, `P_acc`, `C_g`):* Tuning is critical and highly sensitive. The ablation study shows extreme values break performance. This is a primary hyperparameter search burden and a key factor for transferring the method to new scenarios.
    *   *CTDE dependency:* Training requires global state access for the critic and to compute the global reward component. Performance at execution relies entirely on the actor's ability to generalize from local images, creating a potential train-execution mismatch if the local view is insufficient.

*   **Failure signatures:**
    *   **Connectivity drop:** Agent neighbor counts plummet rapidly early in episodes. Likely cause: `P_ln` weight too low, or the CNN failing to recognize threatening spatial patterns in the image. Diagnose by logging per-agent neighbor counts.
    *   **Stagnation:** Agents move very little; steps are high but bounding radius `D_global` changes minutely. Likely cause: `P_ln` too high (over-caution) or `P_acc` (step penalty) too high. Diagnose by checking average action magnitude (σ).
    *   **Fragmentation:** Swarm splits into two or more disconnected subgroups. Indicates the local image-based policy cannot learn to coordinate with distant agents it cannot see, a failure of the CTDE actor to infer global state.

*   **First 3 experiments:**
    1.  **Sanity check image encoding:** For a fixed set of synthetic bearing vectors, render the 75x75 image. Visually confirm the 3x3 neighbor blocks are placed correctly relative to the center pixel and do not catastrophically overlap at expected maximum neighbor densities.
    2.  **Verify local reward dominance:** Run a short training run with `C_g = 0` and a moderate `P_ln`. The learned policy should exhibit extreme caution, often choosing not to move to avoid any remote risk of neighbor loss, resulting in near-zero neighbor loss but minimal convergence. This isolates the behavior driven by `R_local`.
    3.  **Test generalization from 10→20 agents:** Load the best checkpoint from 10-agent training (RL10) and evaluate it on 20-agent "Marginal" test constellations. Expect a significant drop in connectivity compared to a model explicitly trained on 20 agents (RL20), as reported (RL10: 61.7% vs RL20: 93.6%). This validates the need for curriculum training on larger swarm sizes.

## Limitations
*   **Scalability of Image Encoding:** The fixed 75x75 grid with 3x3 neighbor blocks limits the maximum distinguishable neighbor density. Performance beyond 30 agents is not evaluated, and the specific point where spatial patterns become unrecognizable due to overlap is not quantified.
*   **Reward Sensitivity:** The exact tuning sensitivity of the dual-reward weights (`P_ln`, `C_g`) across diverse initial configurations and swarm sizes is not fully characterized. The paper shows key ablations but not a comprehensive sensitivity analysis, increasing replication risk for new scenarios.
*   **CTDE Necessity Gap:** The paper does not empirically test whether the identical image-based actor could be learned using a fully decentralized training regime (e.g., Independent PPO). The absolute necessity of the centralized critic for *this specific method* remains an inferred, not demonstrated, claim.

## Confidence
*   **Mechanism 1 (Spatial Order-Invariance via Pixel Grid):** **Medium Confidence.** The mechanism is theoretically sound (CNNs are permutation-equivariant on grids) and results exceed a vector-based competitor in hard cases. However, the causal contribution of the *image format itself* (vs. using any permutation-invariant neural network) is not isolated via an ablation.
*   **Mechanism 2 (Dual-Reward Shaping):** **High Confidence.** Strongly supported by the presented ablation study where removing the local penalty (`P_ln=0`) causes failure in marginal configurations. The resulting trade-off (high connectivity, moderate speed) is directly evidenced in the comparative results.
*   **Mechanism 3 (CTDE Enables Learning):** **Medium Confidence.** The architecture follows standard CTDE patterns (e.g., MADDPG) and the successful execution of a local policy implies the critic's training role was effective. However, the counterfactual—that decentralized training would fail—is not tested, so the *necessity* of CTDE for this image-based method is inferred.

## Next Checks
1.  **Grid Density Stress Test:** Systematically increase swarm size (e.g., 40, 50 agents) in fixed-area marginal configurations to measure the neighbor density at which the 75x75 image encoding fails (connectivity drops below 90%). This quantifies the break condition for spatial pattern distinguishability.
2.  **CTDE vs. Fully Decentralized Training Ablation:** Train the identical image-based actor network under two conditions: (a) the proposed CTDE with centralized critic, and (b) Independent PPO (fully decentralized training, no shared critic). Compare success rates and convergence speeds on the same 20/30-agent marginal tests to empirically validate the centralized critic's necessity.
3.  **Image Encoding Ablation vs. Permutation-Invariant Vector Baseline:** Replace the CNN image encoder with a Set Transformer or DeepSet architecture that directly processes the raw bearing vectors (guaranteeing permutation invariance). Keep all other components (reward, CTDE, PPO) identical. Compare performance to isolate the specific contribution of the *spatial image representation* versus other permutation-invariant function approximators.
