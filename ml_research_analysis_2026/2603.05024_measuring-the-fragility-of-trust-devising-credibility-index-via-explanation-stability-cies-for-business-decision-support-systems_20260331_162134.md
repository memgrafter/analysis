# Measuring the Fragility of Trust: Devising Credibility Index via Explanation Stability (CIES) for Business Decision Support Systems

## Quick Facts
- **ArXiv ID:** 2603.05024
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.05024
- **Reference count:** 0
- **Primary result:** Introduces CIES, a rank-weighted robustness metric for XAI explanations, showing significantly better discriminative power than uniform baselines in business decision support contexts.

## Executive Summary
The paper addresses the lack of quantified credibility for XAI explanations under realistic business data noise. It proposes the Credibility Index via Explanation Stability (CIES), a metric that measures explanation robustness using a rank-weighted distance function, penalizing instability in top-ranked features more heavily. Evaluated across three business datasets, four tree models, and two balancing conditions, CIES demonstrates statistically superior discriminative power over a uniform baseline metric (p < 0.01 in all 24 configurations). Key findings indicate that model complexity affects credibility, SMOTE balancing can degrade explanation stability despite improving predictive performance, and CIES scores are robust across noise levels. The metric provides a deployable "credibility warning system" for business decision support systems.

## Method Summary
CIES computes an explanation stability score by comparing the original model explanation (e.g., SHAP values) to those of perturbed instances. Perturbations are generated by adding multiplicative Gaussian noise to numerical features, simulating realistic business noise. Feature attributions are compared using a distance weighted by the rank of each feature's absolute contribution (harmonic decay). The mean distance over multiple neighbors is normalized by the weighted magnitude of the original explanation, producing a score in [0,1] where higher values indicate greater stability. The method is evaluated by training four tree-based classifiers on three business datasets, with and without SMOTE balancing, and measuring model-level mean CIES across test instances. Statistical significance is assessed via paired Wilcoxon tests against a uniform-weight baseline.

## Key Results
- CIES outperforms uniform baseline in all 24 test configurations (p < 0.01).
- Model complexity correlates with higher explanation stability; SMOTE balancing improves accuracy but may reduce explanation credibility.
- CIES scores are robust to noise level variations, supporting practical deployment.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1
- **Claim:** CIES captures business semantics by penalizing instability in top-ranked features more heavily than in marginal ones.
- **Mechanism:** It computes a rank-weighted distance between an original explanation (e.g., SHAP values) and explanations from perturbed instances. Features are ranked by absolute SHAP value (rank 1 = most important). Weights follow a harmonic decay: weight = (1/rank) / H_M, where H_M is the M-th harmonic number. This gives the most important feature M times the weight of the least important. The weighted distance is averaged over K perturbed neighbors and normalized by the weighted magnitude of the original explanation.
- **Core assumption:** In business decision-making, changes in the primary drivers (top features) are materially more consequential than changes in low-ranked features. The metric's value lies in reflecting this prioritization.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract]: "...employs a rank-weighted distance function that penalizes instability in the most important features disproportionately, reflecting business semantics where changes in top decision drivers are more consequential..."
  - [section 3.3]: Formal definition of rank-weighted distance D*(φ(x), φ(x′)) = ∑ w_j |φ_j(x) − φ_j(x′)| with w_j = (1/r_j) / H_M.
  - [corpus]: Weak direct evidence. Corpus neighbors discuss robustness and trust in XAI generally (e.g., "Robust explanations...critical to establish human trust") but do not specifically validate the rank-weighting hypothesis. The claim is therefore primarily anchored in the paper's own design rationale and empirical results showing superior discriminative power.
- **Break condition:** If the business context treats all features as equally critical (e.g., a highly regulated checklist where every feature has audit importance), the harmonic penalty may misalign with actual decision consequences.

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim:** CIES models realistic business data perturbations using multiplicative Gaussian noise proportional to feature magnitude.
- **Mechanism:** For each numerical feature j in an instance x, a perturbed value x′_j = x_j + η_j is generated, where η_j ~ N(0, σ_j^2) and σ_j = ε * |x_j|. The noise level ε is a tunable parameter (e.g., 0.03). Categorical features remain unchanged. This simulates common business noise like rounding errors, reporting delays, or measurement imprecision.
- **Core assumption:** The dominant form of data instability in business settings is small, symmetric, magnitude-proportional noise rather than systematic shifts or adversarial attacks. The perturbation neighborhood N_ε(x) thus represents plausible alternative records for the same underlying entity.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract]: "...subject to realistic business noise."
  - [section 3.2]: Complete formulation: "For each numerical feature j, we generate perturbed values by adding Gaussian noise proportional to the feature’s own magnitude: x′_j = x_j + η_j, η_j ~ N(0, σ_j^2), σ_j = ε·|x_j|."
  - [corpus]: Limited direct evidence. Neighbors focus on adversarial robustness (e.g., "adversarial attacks on post hoc explanation methods") or different stability concepts (e.g., "trust regions for explanations"). The paper's own sensitivity analysis (ε ∈ {0.01,0.03,0.05,0.10}) provides empirical support that model rankings are robust to reasonable noise magnitudes.
- **Break condition:** If real-world data errors are non-Gaussian, asymmetric (e.g., always under-reporting income), or involve categorical feature mutations (e.g., a product code change), this perturbation model may not represent true fragility.

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim:** CIES produces an interpretable, bounded score in [0,1] by normalizing the mean perturbed distance by the weighted magnitude of the original explanation.
- **Mechanism:** After computing the mean rank-weighted distance D̅_R over K neighbors and the weighted magnitude ||φ(x)||_W = ∑ w_j |φ_j(x)|, CIES(x) = max(0, 1 − D̅_R / ||φ(x)||_W). A score of 1 means all perturbed explanations are identical to the original. As the score approaches 0, the average perturbation-induced change equals or exceeds the "size" of the original explanation, indicating severe fragility.
- **Core assumption:** Expressing instability as a fraction of the original explanation's own weighted magnitude yields an intuitive ratio that non-technical stakeholders can interpret (e.g., "the explanation changed by 20% of its original importance").
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract]: "...producing an interpretable score in [0,1] where a value of 1 indicates perfect stability and values approaching 0 indicate fragile, untrustworthy explanations."
  - [section 3.4]: Full derivation and interpretation: "This yields an interpretable ratio with clear semantics."
  - [section 3.5, Theorem 1]: Proof that 0 ≤ CIES(x) ≤ 1 and CIES(x)=1 iff φ(x′_k)=φ(x) for all k.
  - [corpus]: No direct evidence; this is a novel design choice by the authors. The interpretability claim is asserted but not externally validated.
- **Break condition:** If the weighted magnitude ||φ(x)||_W is near zero for some instances (e.g., all SHAP values very small), the normalization can produce unstable or extreme CIES values even for modest absolute changes.

## Foundational Learning
- **Concept: SHAP/LIME Value Interpretation**
  - **Why needed here:** CIES operates on feature attribution vectors (φ(x)). Users must understand that SHAP/LIME outputs are contributions summing to the model's prediction (SHAP) or local surrogate coefficients (LIME). Misinterpreting these as global importance or causal effects invalidates CIES's interpretation.
  - **Quick check question:** "If a SHAP value for 'income' is +0.3 in a loan rejection, what does that mean about the model's reasoning, and how would a perturbation to 'income' affect this attribution?"

- **Concept: Lipschitz Continuity (Local Smoothness)**
  - **Why needed here:** Theorem 2 bridges CIES to Lipschitz constants, showing CIES ≥ max(0, 1 − L·||w||₂·δ̅(x)/||φ(x)||_W). Understanding that a smaller local Lipschitz constant L implies smoother model boundaries and thus higher CIES is key to interpreting what drives stability.
  - **Quick check question:** "Would a Random Forest or a pruned decision tree likely have a smaller local Lipschitz constant for explanations, and why?"

- **Concept: Rank Correlation vs. Raw Distance**
  - **Why needed here:** CIES relies on feature *ranks* (for weights) but uses raw SHAP differences in the distance. The paper shows (Proposition 4, Figure 6) that model rankings by CIES are robust to the exact weighting function. This teaches that the *relative stability of top features* is a more reliable signal than absolute attribution stability.
  - **Quick check question:** "If Model A's top 3 features are perfectly stable but features 4-10 fluctuate wildly, while Model B's top 3 features swap ranks but features 4-10 are stable, which model would likely have a higher CIES and why?"

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map:**
  1. **Data Preprocessing Module:** Handles train/test split, imputation, scaling (fit on train only), and optional SMOTE application on the training set only. Critical for preventing data leakage.
  2. **Model & Explainer Training Module:** Trains one of four tree-based classifiers (RF, XGB, LGBM, CB). Configures a SHAP TreeExplainer or LIME TabularExplainer on the *training* data.
  3. **Perturbation & Scoring Engine:** For each test instance x:
     - Compute original explanation φ(x).
     - Determine feature ranks r_j and harmonic weights w_j from |φ(x)|.
     - Generate K perturbed neighbors x′_k using multiplicative Gaussian noise on numerical features.
     - Compute φ(x′_k) for each neighbor.
     - Calculate D̅_R (mean rank-weighted distance) and ||φ(x)||_W.
     - Output CIES(x) = max(0, 1 − D̅_R / ||φ(x)||_W).
  4. **Aggregation & Validation Module:** Computes model-level mean CIES ± SD, distribution percentiles, and per-instance scores. Performs Wilcoxon signed-rank test between CIES (rank-weighted) and Baseline (uniform weights) for statistical significance.

- **Critical path:** Data splitting → Training preprocessing → Model training → Explainer fitting → For each test instance: get explanation → compute ranks/weights → generate K neighbors → get neighbor explanations → compute CIES → aggregate results → statistical test. The single point of failure is step 3's loop over test instances; if K is too small, CIES variance is high. The paper uses K=20.

- **Design tradeoffs:**
  - **Noise level (ε):** Lower ε (e.g., 0.01) tests micro-fluctuations; higher ε (e.g., 0.10) tests tolerance to larger business noise. The paper finds model rankings are robust across ε ∈ {0.01,0.03,0.05,0.10}, but absolute CIES drops linearly with ε.
  - **Neighbors (K):** More neighbors reduces Monte Carlo variance in D̅_R but increases compute time linearly. K=20 is a pragmatic default.
  - **Explainer choice:** SHAP (TreeExplainer) gives exact, lower-variance attributions; LIME gives noisier, local approximations. CIES works with both but absolute scores differ (LIME lower). The paper recommends SHAP for evaluation due to consistency.
  - **Weighting function:** Harmonic is original, but paper shows model ranks are invariant across harmonic, exponential, logarithmic, top-K binary, and uniform weights. Choice affects absolute scores but not comparative conclusions.

- **Failure signatures:**
  - **CIES ≈ 0 for many instances:** Indicates explanations are highly fragile under realistic noise; do not trust these predictions' explanations. Often seen with LightGBM+SMOTE on high-imbalance data.
  - **High variance in per-instance CIES (large SD):** Model yields stable explanations for some instances but fragile ones for others (e.g., edge cases). Check distribution plots (Figure 5).
  - **Wilcoxon test not significant (p>0.05):** Rank-weighting did not provide discriminative advantage over uniform in that config; consider if features truly have heterogeneous importance.
  - **Extreme drop when applying SMOTE:** As in RF on HR Attrition (0.936 → 0.772), indicates synthetic data degrades explanation stability; may need to avoid SMOTE or use a different balancing method if explanations are critical.

- **First 3 experiments:**
  1. **Baseline Validation (RQ4):** For each model-dataset-condition, compute both CIES (rank-weighted) and Baseline (uniform) scores on N=100 test instances. Perform paired Wilcoxon signed-rank test. Expect p<0.01 in all configurations as per paper's results. If not, re-examine if features truly have a meaningful importance hierarchy.
  2. **Noise Sensitivity Sweep (RQ5):** For a chosen model-dataset (e.g., LightGBM on Telco SMOTE), run CIES with ε ∈ {0.01,0.03,0.05,0.10}. Plot mean CIES vs. ε. Expect approximately linear decay. If rankings flip with ε, the model's stability is highly noise-dependent and may not be robust to real data variation.
  3. **SMOTE Impact Analysis (RQ2):** For a model with severe imbalance (e.g., RF on HR Attrition), compare CIES and F1 between Raw and SMOTE conditions. Expect F1 increase but CIES decrease. Quantify the "credibility cost" as ΔCIES. If ΔCIES is large (e.g., >0.15), deploying the SMOTE model may yield untrustworthy explanations despite better accuracy.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
1. How can the component of CIES variance attributable to local model smoothness be separated from explainer-specific sensitivity? The paper acknowledges the confound but provides no methodology to disentangle the two sources; experiments that hold the model constant and compare CIES across multiple explainers or interventions that smooth decision boundaries could isolate explainer-specific variance.

2. Can the CIES framework be adapted to non-tabular data domains (e.g., images, text, time series), and what modifications to the perturbation model or rank-weighting are required? The current perturbation model and rank-weighted distance assume tabular feature vectors; non-tabular data require different perturbation semantics and may lack clear feature rankings.

3. How does CIES perform under adversarial perturbation models designed to maximize explanation instability, compared to the random Gaussian noise used in the study? Random noise may underestimate worst-case explanation fragility; adversarial perturbations could reveal vulnerabilities not captured by the sensitivity analysis with increasing ε.

4. Does CIES retain its discriminative power and business semantics when applied to explanation methods that do not produce feature attributions (e.g., counterfactual or rule-based explainers)? CIES relies on ranking features by attribution magnitude; counterfactuals highlight changed features without scalar attributions, and rule-based explanations use different structures, requiring reformulation.

## Limitations
- **Business Perturbation Realism:** The multiplicative Gaussian noise model assumes symmetric, magnitude-proportional errors. Real business data may have asymmetric, categorical, or systematic noise patterns; validity for non-Gaussian noise is untested.
- **Rank-Weighting Justification:** The harmonic weighting's alignment with business semantics is asserted rather than validated with domain experts; no evidence confirms that decision-makers perceive instability in top-ranked features as disproportionately material.
- **Reproducibility Gaps:** Exact dataset feature lists, categorical handling, and LIME hyperparameters are not disclosed, blocking exact replication. Preprocessing pitfalls (e.g., SMOTE before scaling) risk leakage.

## Confidence
- **CIES Discriminative Power:** High (statistically significant superiority over baseline across 24 configurations).
- **Rank-Weighting Mechanism:** Medium (robustness to weighting function shape is shown, but assumption of meaningful feature hierarchy is unverified).
- **Interpretability & Bounded Score:** Low (mathematically sound, but user comprehension by non-technical stakeholders is not evaluated).

## Next Checks
1. **Preprocessing Audit:** Replicate all pipeline steps on a held-out dataset, confirming that imputation/scaling/SMOTE are fit only on training data. Flag any test-set leakage in CIES calculation.
2. **Weighting Robustness Expansion:** Test additional weighting schemes (e.g., top-3 binary weights, exponential decay with varying base) on all 24 configurations. Re-evaluate Wilcoxon significance; if any non-harmonic scheme consistently invalidates the discriminative claim, the mechanism is less general.
3. **Non-Gaussian Noise Validation:** For 2–3 key model-dataset pairs, replace Gaussian noise with (a) asymmetric Laplace noise and (b) categorical feature perturbation. Compute CIES and rank correlation with original ε=0.03 results. A Spearman ρ < 0.7 indicates fragility to noise model choice.
