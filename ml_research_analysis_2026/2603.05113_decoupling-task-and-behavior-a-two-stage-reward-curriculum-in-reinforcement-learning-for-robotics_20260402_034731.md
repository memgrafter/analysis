# Decoupling Task and Behavior: A Two-Stage Reward Curriculum in Reinforcement Learning for Robotics

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2603.05113
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.05113
- Reference count: 24
- Primary result: A two-stage reward curriculum that decouples task and behavioral objectives significantly improves performance, robustness, and reduces sensitivity to reward weighting in robotic reinforcement learning across multiple benchmarks.

## Executive Summary
Deep reinforcement learning for robotics struggles with reward functions that combine task objectives (e.g., goal reaching) and behavioral terms (e.g., energy efficiency), often leading to reward hacking and high sensitivity to weight tuning. This paper introduces a two-stage reward curriculum: first train on a task-only reward to ensure effective exploration, then gradually introduce auxiliary behavioral rewards via linear annealing over 200 k steps while reusing all past experiences from a flexible replay buffer. Evaluated on DM Control Suite, ManiSkill3, and a MobileRobot navigation task, the method consistently outperforms baselines trained on the full reward from the start, showing substantial gains in average reward and success rate and demonstrating robust performance across a wide range of auxiliary weights.

## Method Summary
The approach (RC‑SAC / RC‑TD3) operates in two phases. Phase 1 trains exclusively on the base task reward `r_base` (weight `w=0`). After a convergence criterion is met—based on a Huber regressor fit to recent base‑reward trends—the agent switches to Phase 2. In Phase 2 the curriculum weight `w` is linearly annealed from 0 to a target `w_target` over a fixed duration (200 k steps). The replay buffer stores tuples `(s, a, r_base, r_aux, s')`; during each update the current combined reward `r_w = (1−w)·r_base + w·r_aux` is recomputed on‑the‑fly using the stored components. This reuse of Phase 1 experience stabilizes the transition. After annealing, training continues with `w = w_target`. The method is off‑policy, enabling the flexible buffer; it uses standard SAC/TD3 networks and hyperparameters (e.g., learning rate 3e‑4, γ=0.99).

## Key Results
- DM Control Suite: average combined reward increased from 0.637 to 0.690; base reward rose from 0.419 to 0.594.
- MobileRobot navigation: success rate improved from 52.4% to 65.8%.
- ManiSkill3 (`PullCubeTool‑v1`): with `w_target=0.25` success rate jumped from 62.1% to 97.6%.
The curriculum showed robust performance across a wide range of auxiliary weights, reducing the need for precise reward tuning.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- **Claim:** Training initially on a task‑only reward (`w=0`) prevents behavioral auxiliary terms from hindering exploration and causing premature convergence to local optima (reward hacking).
- **Mechanism:** The agent first learns a successful policy for the primary task without penalties for efficiency or smoothness, establishing a robust behavioral foundation. Only after the task is learned is the full reward, including behavioral terms, introduced via a curriculum weight `w`.
- **Core assumption:** Auxiliary behavioral objectives create conflicting gradients that can trap the agent in local optima (e.g., standing still) if presented simultaneously with the sparse task reward from the start.
- **Evidence anchors:** [abstract] "first train the agent on a simplified task‑only reward function to ensure effective exploration before introducing the full reward... substantially outperforming baselines trained directly on the full reward." [section 3] Phase description. [section 4.1] Gains in `finger‑spin` where baseline fails entirely.

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim:** A gradual annealing of the curriculum weight `w` (over ~200 k steps) stabilizes training by preventing sudden, disruptive shifts in the Q‑function targets when the reward structure changes.
- **Mechanism:** Upon phase transition, `w` in `r_w = (1−w)·r_base + w·r_aux` increases slowly. This makes the optimization objective change progressively, allowing the critic network to adapt its value estimates incrementally rather than receiving a shock from a sudden reward composition change.
- **Core assumption:** An instantaneous switch to the full reward would cause a large, abrupt change in TD‑error targets, destabilizing the policy and critic updates.
- **Evidence anchors:** [section 3.2] Discussion of abrupt changes. [section 4.3, Figure 5] Ablation shows longer annealing leads to better results; all annealing schedules outperform or match instantaneous switching. [section 3.2] Note on partial gradient alignment.

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim:** Reusing experiences from the first phase (stored as `(s, a, r_base, r_aux, s')`) in the second phase is critical for training stability and sample efficiency.
- **Mechanism:** The replay buffer stores both `r_base` and `r_aux` values. When `w` changes in Phase 2, the combined reward `r_w` is recomputed on‑the‑fly for old samples. This maintains a large, diverse buffer from the initial, stable exploration phase, preventing the buffer from being populated only with the new, potentially more constrained policy.
- **Core assumption:** Resetting the replay buffer at the phase switch would lead to a period where the buffer contains only samples from the new policy with non‑zero `w`, causing sample‑distribution shift and instability.
- **Evidence anchors:** [section 3.3] Explanation of buffer reuse. [section 4.3, Figure 6] Ablation: resetting buffer increases variance and instability. [section 4.3] Conclusion that buffer reuse is vital.

## Foundational Learning
- **Concept: Markov Decision Process (MDP)**
  - *Why needed:* The problem is framed as an MDP; understanding states, actions, transitions, and the scalar reward function `r_w` is essential to grasp how adjusting `w` changes the optimization objective.
  - *Quick check:* Changing the reward function composition by adjusting `w` does **not** alter the underlying MDP's state transition dynamics `P(s'|s,a)`.
- **Concept: Off‑Policy Reinforcement Learning (e.g., SAC, TD3)**
  - *Why needed:* The curriculum relies critically on off‑policy learning to reuse experiences from Phase 1 in Phase 2 (stored under `w=0` but used later with `w>0`).
  - *Quick check:* An off‑policy algorithm like SAC or TD3 is necessary because it can store and learn from transitions where the reward `r_w` was computed with a different `w` than the current one.
- **Concept: Reward Shaping & Multi‑Objective Trade‑offs**
  - *Why needed:* The paper addresses combining a primary task reward (`r_base`) with auxiliary behavioral rewards (`r_aux`) into a single scalar; understanding "reward hacking" (exploiting auxiliary terms at the expense of the task) is central to the motivation.
  - *Quick check:* If `w_target` is set too high from the start, the agent may learn a policy that minimizes `r_aux` (e.g., stays still to save energy) but fails the primary task.

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component map:**  
Curriculum Controller → (monitors base reward, determines switch time) → Phase State (`k`) → Reward Composer (computes current `w_t` and `r_w`) → RL Agent (SAC/TD3 networks)  
Replay Buffer (stores `s, a, r_base, r_aux, s'`) interacts with both the agent (sampling) and the reward composer (supplying stored components).  
Phase State controls whether `w_t = 0` (Phase 0) or `w_t = α(Δt)·w_target` (Phase 1 annealing).

**Critical path:**
- Phase 0: Collect experience with `w=0` (`r_w = r_base`). Store `(s, a, r_base, r_aux, s')`. The controller computes average base reward over the last 1000 steps, takes a 20‑point moving average, fits a Huber regressor to the last 75 points to obtain slope `β_t`.
- Switch: When `β_t < 0.001` for 10 consecutive steps **and** average `r_base` exceeds `1.5×` the random baseline, set `k=1` and record `t_switch`.
- Phase 1 (Annealing): For each gradient step, compute `w_t` via linear (or cosine) schedule: `w_t = α(Δt)·w_target`, where `Δt` is steps since switch. Sample a batch from the full replay buffer. Recompute `r_w` for each sample using current `w_t` and stored `r_base, r_aux`. Update policy and critic networks.
- Post‑annealing: After `T_ann` steps, `w_t` remains equal to `w_target`. Continue training until total steps.

**Design tradeoffs:**
- Annealing duration: longer (e.g., 200 k) increases stability but slows adaptation; shorter risks instability (ablation shows 200 k slightly best).
- Switch criterion: The Convergence method uses fixed `ε_slope=0.001` and plateau length `m=10` for all experiments, claiming generality; however, environments with highly stochastic rewards might require tuning.
- Auxiliary weight (`w_target`): The method is robust to a wide range (e.g., `{0.25,0.5,0.75}`), but extremely high values can still degrade performance.

**Failure signatures:**
1. No phase switch: `β_t` never drops below threshold; the agent never starts learning auxiliary behavior (possible if the base task is too hard or the criterion is too strict).
2. Catastrophic forgetting after switch: Resetting the replay buffer leads to sharp performance drop and increased variance (buffer reuse prevents this).
3. Reward hacking persists: In Phase 2, `r_base` remains low while combined reward `r_w` is moderate, indicating the policy exploits `r_aux` at the expense of the task (common with very high `w_target`).

**Three first experiments (for validation):**
1. DM Control `finger‑spin`: Baseline fails (low base reward), curriculum succeeds, highlighting that behavioral terms hinder exploration without the curriculum.
2. ManiSkill3 `PullCubeTool‑v1` with `w_target=0.25`: Baseline success ~62%, curriculum ~97.6%, demonstrating robustness to a moderate auxiliary weight.
3. MobileRobot navigation across `w_target ∈ {0.25,0.5,0.75}`: Curriculum consistently outperforms baseline, confirming generalizability in a complex multi‑term reward setting.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- **1. Universality of the switch criterion:** Is the Convergence‑based phase switch robust across diverse robotics environments without manual tuning of its hyperparameters (ε_slope, m)? The authors fix these values for all experiments but do not test sensitivity across a wider range of tasks.
- **2. Applicability to on‑policy methods:** The curriculum explicitly relies on an off‑policy replay buffer to reuse experiences with recomputed rewards. It remains unclear whether the idea can be adapted to on‑policy algorithms (e.g., PPO) without losing stability or sample efficiency.
- **3. Extreme auxiliary‑task conflict:** The method assumes partial alignment between base and auxiliary rewards (`r_base ⊂ r_w_target`). How does it perform when auxiliary terms directly oppose task completion (e.g., energy minimization discourages all movement)? This extreme conflict is not investigated.
- **4. Adaptive annealing schedule:** Annealing duration is fixed (200 k steps). Could a dynamic schedule that adapts to real‑time learning metrics (e.g., base‑reward improvement rate) improve training efficiency, especially for tasks with varying learning speeds?
- **5. Automatic decomposition:** The framework requires manual categorization of each reward term into "base" or "auxiliary." Can this decomposition be automated from the reward function’s structure (e.g., via gradient analysis or correlation)?

## Limitations
- Implementation details of the switch criterion (HuberRegressor parameters, moving‑average window) are underspecified, making exact replication challenging.
- The MobileRobot environment’s exact state vector composition (178‑dimensional) and some reward scalings (e.g., `r_a`) are ambiguous, preventing faithful reconstruction.
- The method is inherently off‑policy due to the flexible replay buffer; its extension to on‑policy methods is unknown and likely non‑trivial.
- All evaluations are conducted in simulation; no real‑world robotic experiments are presented, leaving transferability an open question.

## Confidence
- Curriculum effectiveness: **High** (consistent gains across three benchmark suites with clear baselines)
- Mechanistic explanations (annealing, buffer reuse): **Medium** (supported by ablations but not fully isolated; e.g., interaction effects not fully disentangled)
- Weight‑robustness: **High** (systematic `w_target` sweeps demonstrate strong robustness)
- Generalization to unseen tasks: **Medium** (tested on multiple simulation domains but lacking real‑robot evidence and limited diversity in reward structures)

## Next Checks
1. **Core result replication:** Implement RC‑SAC/RC‑TD3 and independently verify the reported improvements on DM Control (`finger‑spin`) and ManiSkill3 (`PullCubeTool‑v1`) with multiple random seeds.
2. **Buffer reuse ablation:** Reset the replay buffer at the phase switch and confirm the resulting instability/performance drop compared to the flexible buffer approach.
3. **Switch criterion sensitivity:** Vary `ε_slope` and plateau length `m` around the proposed defaults (0.001 and 10) to test robustness; expect stable performance within a reasonable hyperparameter range but possible degradation far from these values.
