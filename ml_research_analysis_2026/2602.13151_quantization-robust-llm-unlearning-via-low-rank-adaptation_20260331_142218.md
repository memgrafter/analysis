# Quantization-Robust LLM Unlearning via Low-Rank Adaptation

## Quick Facts
- **ArXiv ID:** 2602.13151
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.13151
- **Reference count:** 11
- **Primary result:** LoRA-based unlearning preserves effectiveness under aggressive 4-bit PTQ, improving utility by up to 7.93 points and reducing privacy leakage (PrivLeak from -25.68 to -5.86) while maintaining strong forgetting.

## Executive Summary
Standard LLM unlearning updates are often too small to survive aggressive post‑training quantization (PTQ), particularly at 4‑bit, leading the model to revert to its pre‑unlearning state. This paper proposes using Low‑Rank Adaptation (LoRA) to concentrate the unlearning into trainable low‑rank adapters, which are merged into the base model before quantization. By freezing the base model and optimizing only the adapters, the update magnitude becomes large enough to cross quantization bin boundaries, ensuring robustness. Evaluated on Llama‑2‑7B with the MUSE benchmark, LoRA improves 4‑bit utility by up to 7.93 points and drastically lowers privacy leakage without sacrificing forgetting performance.

## Method Summary
The method freezes the pre‑trained base model and injects LoRA adapters into selected linear layers. Unlearning is performed by optimizing the adapter parameters using a combination of forgetting objectives (either Gradient Ascent (GA) or Negate‑Positive‑Overlap (NPO)) and utility regularization (either Gradient‑Difference (GDR) or KL‑Regularization (KLR)). After training, the adapters are explicitly merged into the base model in full precision, which aggregates the low‑rank updates into a single weight tensor. Only then is 4‑bit post‑training quantization (PTQ) applied to the merged model. This sequence ensures the unlearning signal is large enough and properly positioned to survive quantization. The quantized model is then evaluated on forgetting (Verbatim/Knowledge memorization), privacy leakage (membership inference attack), and utility preservation.

## Key Results
- LoRA improves 4‑bit unlearning utility by up to **7.93 points** (NPO+GDR on BOOKS: 50.17 → 58.10) compared to full‑parameter fine‑tuning.
- Privacy leakage is dramatically reduced: **GA+KLR on BOOKS** moves PrivLeak from **‑25.68** to **‑5.86** (closer to ideal 0) under 4‑bit PTQ.
- Forgetting remains strong (VerMem, KnowMem near 0) while preserving utility, demonstrating an effective trade‑off even after quantization.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1: Optimization Dynamics and Step Size
- **Claim:** Freezing the base model and optimizing only low‑rank adapters allows the use of significantly higher learning rates without catastrophic utility degradation, producing larger numerical weight updates that can cross quantization bin boundaries.
- **Mechanism:** In full‑parameter fine‑tuning (Full‑FT), unlearning objectives (e.g., GA, NPO) must be balanced with utility preservation via small learning rates (η≈10⁻⁵–10⁻⁷) to avoid destroying general capabilities. This yields minute, diffuse updates (ΔW) that fall within the same quantization interval as W₀ after 4‑bit PTQ. LoRA constrains updates to a low‑rank subspace (Φ = {A, B}), acting as an implicit regularizer. This structural stability permits larger learning rates (e.g., η≈10⁻⁴), causing the accumulated values in A and B to grow rapidly and push the effective update ΔW = (α/r)BA beyond the coarse 4‑bit step size.
- **Core assumption:** The low‑rank constraint sufficiently regularizes the optimization to prevent utility collapse even with larger steps, a property supported by prior LoRA stability findings.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract]: "freeze the base model and concentrate unlearning into trainable low‑rank adapters... enables significantly higher learning rates without destroying general utility."
  - [section IV]: "Because the optimization is restricted to a subspace of rank r, the risk of distorting the model’s general features is significantly reduced... allows us to employ significantly larger learning rates (e.g., η≈10⁻⁴)... the accumulated values in matrices A and B rapidly grow large enough to push the effective weight update ΔW across the quantization boundary."
  - [corpus]: Weak direct evidence; neighbor papers (e.g., L4Q, Low‑Rank QAT) explore LoRA for quantization robustness but not specifically for unlearning. Assumption: LoRA's general stability in high‑update regimes transfers to the unlearning context.
- **Break condition:** If the learning rate is not increased sufficiently relative to Full‑FT, or if the rank r is too high (approaching full rank), the update magnitude may remain small and still be masked by 4‑bit quantization.

### Mechanism 2: Magnitude Control via Scaling and Architecture
- **Claim:** The LoRA scaling factor α and explicit layer selection linearly amplify the update magnitude and concentrate it into specific modules (e.g., MLPs, attention projections), ensuring ΔW is large enough in critical layers to survive PTQ without globally biasing the model toward the retain set.
- **Mechanism:** The effective weight update is ΔW = (α/r)BA. α acts as a direct amplitude knob, independent of learning rate. Furthermore, by applying LoRA only to selected layers (determined via grid search), the unlearning "budget" is concentrated. This forces the updates in those targeted layers to achieve a larger magnitude to minimize the unlearning loss, making them more likely to cross quantization thresholds. This contrasts with Full‑FT, where distributing tiny updates across all layers protects utility but leaves each individual update too small for 4‑bit.
- **Core assumption:** Knowledge to be unlearned is localized enough that targeting a subset of layers (e.g., all linear layers, MLP‑only) can capture it, and that concentrating updates there does not harm utility more than Full‑FT's distributed approach.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract]: "concentrate unlearning into trainable low‑rank adapters so that the effective update is preserved after quantization."
  - [section IV]: "The scaling factor α acts as a direct amplifier of this signal... Rather than distributing the unlearning budget across all layers, we target specific modules... this concentration of the unlearning objective... forces the update magnitude in those specific layers to be significantly higher... the magnitude of the LoRA unlearning matrix updates is enough to persist after quantization."
  - [corpus]: Neighbors like RILQ and SERQ use LoRA for quantization error compensation, implying low‑rank updates can be tuned for magnitude. However, they do not address unlearning. Corpus evidence for the *layer selection* aspect in unlearning is absent.
- **Break condition:** If α is set too low, or if the wrong layers are selected (e.g., layers not involved in storing the targeted knowledge), the concentrated update may still be insufficient in magnitude or irrelevant, failing to cross quantization boundaries.

### Mechanism 3: Pre‑Quantization Adapter Merging
- **Claim:** Explicitly merging the trained LoRA adapters (Wᵤ = W₀ + ΔW) **before** applying PTQ is essential; it subjects the final, amplified unlearning update to quantization, rather than quantizing the base and adapters separately and then merging, which would lose the update in the coarse 4‑bit space.
- **Mechanism:** PTQ is applied to a single weight tensor. If adapters are kept separate and quantized individually, the low‑precision adapters (ΔW) may have their values rounded to near‑zero after quantization, nullifying the unlearning signal when later added to the quantized base. By merging in FP16/BF16 first (Wᵤ = W₀ + (α/r)BA), the total displacement ΔW is full‑precision and of sufficient magnitude. Then, applying PTQ to Wᵤ means the quantization step size is applied to the *combined* value. If ΔW is large enough, it shifts Wᵤ into a different quantization bin than W₀, preserving the unlearning effect.
- **Core assumption:** The merge step is computationally feasible (memory for full ΔW) and that the quantization of the merged tensor (Wᵤ) will reflect the update's magnitude better than quantizing components separately.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract]: "concentrate unlearning into trainable low‑rank adapters, then **merge them before PTQ**... ensures that unlearning effects persist even in aggressive 4‑bit formats."
  - [section V]: "Crucially, for all LoRA‑based experiments, we **explicitly merge the trained low‑rank adapters into the base model parameters before quantization**. This ensures that the quantization step is applied to the final unlearned weights (Wunlearn = W0 + ∆W), thereby subjecting the unlearning updates to the potential masking effects..."
  - [corpus]: No direct mention in neighbors; this is a specific procedural insight for unlearning+quantization. Assumption: standard PTQ pipelines apply quantization to a single model instance, so merging is necessary for the update to be considered.
- **Break condition:** If merging is skipped or done after quantization (i.e., quantizing base and adapters separately), the small, quantized adapter values will not shift the base model's quantized bins, leading to update masking and reversion.

## Foundational Learning
- **Concept: Post‑Training Quantization (PTQ)**
  - **Why needed here:** The paper's core problem is that 4‑bit PTQ masks unlearning updates. Understanding PTQ's discretization (quantization bins, step size s) is essential to grasp why small ΔW fails.
  - **Quick check question:** If a weight W₀ = 10.2 and a 4‑bit PTQ step size s = 0.5, what range of ΔW is needed to change its quantized index? (Ans: ΔW must cross a bin boundary, e.g., if W₀ is in [10.0, 10.5), ΔW > 0.3 to push it to ≥10.5.)
- **Concept: Low‑Rank Adaptation (LoRA)**
  - **Why needed here:** LoRA is the proposed architectural solution. Understanding its parameterization (ΔW = BA, rank r, scaling α) is key to seeing how it concentrates updates.
  - **Quick check question:** How does increasing the LoRA scaling factor α affect the magnitude of the effective weight update ΔW, and what risk does this pose if α is too high? (Ans: ΔW scales linearly with α; too high may destabilize training or harm utility.)
- **Concept: Unlearning Objectives (GA vs. NPO) with Regularization**
  - **Why needed here:** The paper evaluates GA and NPO with GDR/KLR. Knowing their behavior (GA is unstable, NPO uses a reference model) explains why some methods fail under PTQ and why regularization is mandatory for LoRA experiments.
  - **Quick check question:** Why does the paper apply LoRA only to GA/GDR, GA/KLR, NPO/GDR, NPO/KLR and not to bare GA or NPO? (Ans: Unconstrained GA/NPO can completely collapse utility; LoRA is evaluated for *utility‑regularized* unlearning where the update magnitude is intentionally small, making PTQ robustness the main challenge.)

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map:**
  1. **Frozen Base Model:** Pre‑trained LLM (e.g., Llama‑2‑7B). Parameters θ₀ never updated.
  2. **LoRA Adapters:** Trainable matrices A ∈ ℝʳ×ᵏ, B ∈ ℝᵈ×ʳ injected into selected linear layers. Effective weight: W = W₀ + (α/r)BA.
  3. **Unlearning Loss Module:** Computes L_total = L_forget(Φ) + λ·L_retain(Φ), where Φ = {A, B}. L_forget uses GA or NPO on D_forget; L_retain uses GDR or KLR on D_retain.
  4. **PTQ Module:** Applies Round‑to‑Nearest (RTN) 4‑bit quantization to the **merged** model weights (W₀ + ΔW).
  5. **Evaluation Suite:** MUSE metrics (VerMem, KnowMem, PrivLeak, UtilityPres) on BOOKS/NEWS.
- **Critical path:**
  1. Initialize LoRA adapters (A, B) randomly; freeze W₀.
  2. Train (A, B) using unlearning loss (e.g., NPO+GDR) with elevated learning rate.
  3. **Merge adapters:** Compute final weights Wᵤ = W₀ + (α/r)BA in full precision.
  4. Apply 4‑bit PTQ to Wᵤ → Wᵤ⁽⁴ᵇⁱᵗ⁾.
  5. Evaluate Wᵤ⁽⁴ᵇ⁶ⁱᵗ⁾ on MUSE.
- **Design tradeoffs:**
  - **LoRA rank (r):** Lower rank (e.g., 16) increases per‑parameter update magnitude for a given loss but may limit expressivity. Higher rank (e.g., 128) allows more nuanced updates but dilutes magnitude. Grid search needed.
  - **Scaling (α):** Larger α directly amplifies ΔW, improving PTQ robustness but risking over‑unlearning (utility drop). Must be tuned with λ.
  - **Layer selection:** Targeting all linear layers captures more knowledge but may harm utility more than targeting only MLPs or attention. Requires empirical validation per dataset.
  - **Learning rate (η):** Higher η boosts update speed but can destabilize if too high, even with LoRA's regularization. Must be elevated relative to Full‑FT (10⁻⁴ vs. 10⁻⁵–10⁻⁷).
- **Failure signatures:**
  - **Post‑PTQ PrivLeak near ‑100 (or worse than pre‑unlearning):** Indicates update was masked; model retains D_forget membership information.
  - **UtilityPres plummets after PTQ (e.g., >20‑point drop):** Suggests update magnitude was either too large (over‑unlearning) or poorly concentrated, harming retain knowledge when quantized.
  - **No change between Full‑PTQ and 4‑bit PTQ metrics:** Classic update masking; ΔW < quantization step size.
  - **Training instability (loss NaN, exploding gradients):** η too high for chosen r/α, or conflicting unlearning/retain losses.
- **First 3 experiments:**
  1. **Baseline replication:** Run Full‑FT unlearning (e.g., NPO+GDR) on BOOKS in BF16, Int8, Int4. Confirm catastrophic forgetting (low VerMem/KnowMem) in BF16 but recovery (high scores) in Int4, reproducing Table I failure.
  2. **LoRA vs. Full‑FT head‑to‑head:** Apply NPO+GDR with LoRA (r=64, α=r, η=7e‑4) and Full‑FT (η=1e‑5). Merge LoRA adapters before PTQ. Compare 4‑bit VerMem/UtilityPres. Expect LoRA to show lower VerMem and higher UtilityPres at Int4.
  3. **Merging ablation:** For LoRA (NPO+GDR), train adapters, then create two 4‑bit models: (a) merge adapters then PTQ; (b) PTQ base model and adapters separately, then add. Evaluate PrivLeak. Expect (b) to show high PrivLeak (reversion), proving merge‑before‑PTQ necessity.
