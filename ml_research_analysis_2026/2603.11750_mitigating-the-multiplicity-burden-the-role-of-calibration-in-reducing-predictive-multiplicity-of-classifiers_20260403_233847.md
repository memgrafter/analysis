# Mitigating the Multiplicity Burden: The Role of Calibration in Reducing Predictive Multiplicity of Classifiers
## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2603.11750
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.11750
- **Reference count:** 28
- **Primary result:** Post-hoc calibration (Platt Scaling, Isotonic Regression, Temperature Scaling) significantly reduces predictive multiplicity (obscurity) within a Rashomon set of near-optimal classifiers, but its efficacy is notably weaker for minority-class observations in imbalanced credit scoring datasets.

## Executive Summary
Predictive multiplicity occurs when many near-optimal models produce conflicting predictions for the same individual, posing a burden of arbitrariness. This study investigates whether post-hoc probability calibration can harmonize the outputs of models within a Rashomon set—a set of models with nearly identical performance—to reduce this multiplicity. Using nine credit scoring datasets with varying class imbalance, the authors apply three calibration methods to models in the Rashomon set and measure changes in obscurity (individual-level disagreement) and confidence. Results show all calibration methods lower overall obscurity, with Platt Scaling and Isotonic Regression being most effective. However, the improvement is significantly smaller for minority-class instances, which retain higher obscurity and lower confidence even after calibration, indicating a persistent, unequal multiplicity burden linked to data sparsity.

## Method Summary
The study constructs a Rashomon set for each dataset by training ~20 diverse classifiers (via H2O AutoML) and selecting all models within 5% of the best AUC. A separate 20% calibration set is used to fit three post-hoc calibrators—Platt Scaling (logistic), Isotonic Regression (non-parametric monotonic), and Temperature Scaling—applied independently to every model in the Rashomon set. On a held-out test set, two primary metrics are computed per observation: **obscurity** (proportion of models disagreeing with the best model's hard prediction) and **confidence** (mean predicted probability for the predicted class). These metrics are aggregated by class (majority/minority) and compared between raw and calibrated model sets using stratified non-parametric statistical tests (Wilcoxon rank-sum, post-hoc Dunn with Bonferroni correction) across all datasets.

## Key Results
- All three calibration methods significantly reduce average obscurity across the Rashomon set, with Platt Scaling and Isotonic Regression showing the strongest effects.
- Minority-class observations exhibit significantly higher obscurity and lower confidence than majority-class observations, both before and after calibration.
- Calibration's ability to increase minority-class confidence is method-dependent: Platt Scaling yields a significant improvement, while Isotonic Regression and Temperature Scaling do not produce statistically significant changes.
- The disparity in calibration efficacy suggests that the benefits of reduced multiplicity are unevenly distributed, potentially exacerbating procedural fairness concerns for already disadvantaged groups.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1: Consensus via Shared Transformation
- **Claim:** Post-hoc calibration imposes a form of consensus among the diverse models within the Rashomon set, thereby reducing predictive multiplicity (measured as obscurity).
- **Mechanism:** Calibration methods learn a monotonic transformation of raw model scores on a held-out dataset. When the same transformation is applied to every model in the Rashomon set, it aligns their probability outputs toward a common empirical target. This reduces variance in predicted probabilities for the same observation across models, directly lowering obscurity.
- **Core assumption:** Miscalibration patterns (gap between raw scores and true probabilities) are learnable and sufficiently similar across the diverse models in the Rashomon set, allowing a single calibration function to realign multiple models effectively.
- **Evidence anchors:** 
  - [abstract] "...applying post-hoc calibration methods—specifically Platt Scaling, Isotonic Regression, and Temperature Scaling—is associated with lower obscurity across the Rashomon set."
  - [section 4.3(ii)] "All three calibration methods demonstrate an ability to mitigate predictive multiplicity... Platt Scaling and Isotonic Regression appear particularly effective..."
- **Break condition:** The consensus effect fails if the Rashomon set contains models with fundamentally different decision boundaries or inconsistent miscalibration patterns (e.g., one model overconfident on the minority class while another is underconfident). This risk is higher with highly heterogeneous model families (e.g., rule-based vs. neural networks) not explored here.

### Mechanism 2: Confidence Correction Reduces Disagreement
- **Claim:** Calibration reduces multiplicity by aligning prediction confidence with empirical reality, moving high-confidence predictions into regions of lower inherent model disagreement.
- **Mechanism:** Uncalibrated models often produce overconfident extreme probabilities. In regions of miscalibrated high confidence (where the model is wrong but certain), the Rashomon set may disagree wildly. Calibration tempers overconfidence and adjusts underconfidence, moving probability estimates away from the 0/1 boundaries. Since the study finds obscurity is lower in high-confidence regions, improving confidence accuracy via calibration reduces disagreement.
- **Core assumption:** A significant portion of predictive multiplicity arises from models being confidently wrong in the same regions. Correcting these confidence estimates removes a key driver of disagreements.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract] "...predictive multiplicity concentrates in regions of low predictive confidence..."
  - [section 4.3(i)] "A consistent trend across all datasets indicates that as the average confidence... increases, the obscurity decreases... in low-to-medium confidence regions, obscurity levels spike."
- **Break condition:** This mechanism is ineffective if the primary source of multiplicity is fundamental data ambiguity (e.g., label noise, insufficient features) that persists even with well-calibrated probabilities.

### Mechanism 3: Class-Dependent Regularization Weakening
- **Claim:** Calibration's efficacy in reducing multiplicity is class-dependent, with a weaker regularizing effect on minority-class observations due to data sparsity.
- **Mechanism:** Calibration methods require sufficient samples to estimate the true conditional probability function. For the minority class, scarcity leads to higher variance in the estimated calibration function, especially for non-parametric methods like Isotonic Regression. This results in a less reliable and less consistent adjustment for minority-class predictions, weakening the consensus effect and leaving a residual "multiplicity burden."
- **Core assumption:** Minority-class underrepresentation causes high variance in the calibration fit, preventing a stable, consensus-driving transformation from being learned.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract] "However, calibration’s impact is weaker for minority-class observations, which retain higher obscurity."
  - [section 4.3(ii) & Table 2] "For the minority class... the reduction remained significant, the statistical strength was notably lower... Isotonic Regression and Temperature Scaling failed to produce statistically significant changes in confidence."
  - [section 4.2] Imbalance ratios range from 2.3 to 20.2, with focus on minority class results.
- **Break condition:** This class-dependent effect diminishes on perfectly balanced datasets or if only robust calibrators (e.g., parametric Platt Scaling with regularization) are used. The paper shows Platt Scaling is more effective for minority confidence, supporting this condition.

## Foundational Learning
- **Concept: Rashomon Effect / Predictive Multiplicity**
  - *Why needed:* Defines the core problem—existence of many near-optimal models (Rashomon set) producing conflicting individual predictions (multiplicity). Framing calibration as a stability/consensus tool rather than an accuracy tool requires this foundation.
  - *Quick check:* "If two models have identical AUC on a test set, can they still disagree on the prediction for a specific applicant? What does that disagreement imply for fairness?"
- **Concept: Post-hoc Probability Calibration**
  - *Why needed:* It is the primary intervention mechanism. The study tests if adjusting predicted probabilities (via Platt, Isotonic, Temperature Scaling) can harmonize the Rashomon set. Distinguishing parametric (Platt) from non-parametric (Isotonic) methods is key to interpreting results.
  - *Quick check:* "What is the key functional difference between Platt Scaling (logistic) and Isotonic Regression (non-parametric monotonic) that might affect their performance on imbalanced data?"
- **Concept: Class Imbalance Dynamics in Model Evaluation**
  - *Why needed:* The central finding is that the multiplicity burden is disproportionate. Understanding how minority class sparsity affects model uncertainty, confidence estimates, and the reliability of learned transformations (like calibration) is essential.
  - *Quick check:* "Why might a model's predicted probability for a minority-class instance be less reliable (higher variance) than for a majority-class instance, even if overall accuracy is similar?"

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map:**
  Data Ingestion -> Model Generation Engine (H2O AutoML) -> Rashomon Set Filter (ε=5% AUC) -> Calibration Module (per-model Platt/Isotonic/Temp) -> Metrics Engine (Obscurity, Confidence) -> Statistical Validation Layer (Wilcoxon, Dunn) -> Visualization & Aggregation
- **Critical path:** Model Generation → Rashomon Set Filter → (for each model) Calibration → Metrics Computation → Statistical Testing. The analysis examines an ensemble of models, not a single pipeline.
- **Design tradeoffs:**
  - *Calibration Method:* Platt Scaling (parametric, robust to small samples) vs. Isotonic Regression (flexible, high variance on sparse classes). Trade-off between robustness and adaptability.
  - *Metric Choice:* Obscurity provides granular individual-level arbitrariness vs. global discrepancy. Using only global metrics masks class-specific effects.
  - *Rashomon Set Definition:* ε=0.05. Tighter ε yields a smaller, more similar model set; looser ε increases diversity, making calibration's consensus effect more impressive but may include worse models.
- **Failure signatures:**
  - Persistent minority obscurity post-calibration (as with Isotonic/Temp Scaling in Table 2).
  - Calibration decreases majority-class confidence but fails to increase minority-class confidence (or vice versa), indicating asymmetric fit.
  - On datasets with extreme imbalance (e.g., `poland_year2`, ratio=20.2), all methods show minimal minority-class obscurity reduction, suggesting data sparsity is a hard limit.
  - A method reduces obscurity but degrades calibration quality (e.g., increases ECE) on the minority class, indicating a trade-off between consensus and probability accuracy.
- **First 3 experiments:**
  1. **Baseline Multiplicity Profiling:** For each dataset, without calibration, compute per-observation obscurity and confidence for the Rashomon set. Generate the Confidence vs. Obscurity scatter plot and perform Wilcoxon rank-sum tests comparing minority vs. majority class metrics. Goal: Establish existence and class-disparity of the "multiplicity burden."
  2. **Calibration Efficacy Comparison:** Apply each calibration method (Platt, Isotonic, Temp) to all models in the Rashomon set. Re-compute metrics. Create the grand mean bar/stripchart (Figure 3) comparing raw vs. calibrated for each class. Goal: Visually identify which methods most robustly lower obscurity.
  3. **Class-Stratified Statistical Testing:** For each class and each calibration method, perform stratified post-hoc Dunn tests comparing calibrated obscurity/confidence against raw baseline. Apply Bonferroni correction. Goal: Formally test if observed reductions/increases are significant and whether effects are equal across classes (as in Table 2).

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- Why is the efficacy of post-hoc calibration in reducing predictive multiplicity systematically weaker for minority-class observations, and what underlying data or model properties drive this disparity?
- Do the findings on calibration's role as a "consensus-enforcing layer" generalize beyond binary credit scoring to multiclass classification tasks, and does the class-dependent disparity persist?
- What specific characteristics of the miscalibration in minority-class predictions cause flexible non-parametric methods (Isotonic Regression) to fail to significantly adjust confidence levels, while parametric methods (Platt Scaling) succeed?
- Is there an inherent trade-off between reducing predictive multiplicity (via calibration) and maintaining or improving the discriminatory power (e.g., AUC) of models within the Rashomon set?

## Limitations
- **Reproduction fidelity:** Critical details are unspecified, including exact H2O AutoML hyperparameters (e.g., max models, grid search) and whether the best model `f_best` for obscurity calculation is selected before or after calibration, risking inconsistent Rashomon set construction.
- **Scope constraints:** Results derive from nine credit scoring datasets with imbalance ratios 2.3–20.2; generalizability to other domains or extreme imbalance (e.g., ratio >50) is unknown.
- **Mechanism robustness:** The consensus assumption—that miscalibration patterns are similar across models—may fail with highly heterogeneous model families (e.g., mixing tree-based and neural networks), a scenario not explored.

## Confidence
- **Calibration reduces multiplicity (obscurity):** High. Consistently observed across all three methods and nine datasets, backed by statistical tests (section 4.3).
- **Class-dependent efficacy disparity:** Medium. Supported by stratified Dunn tests (Table 2), but effect strength varies and may not hold for balanced data or with robust calibrators like Platt Scaling.
- **Confidence-multiplicity link:** Medium. Correlation analysis shows lower obscurity in high-confidence regions (section 4.3.i), but causality is inferred, not experimentally isolated.
- **Generalizability beyond credit scoring:** Low. Study confined to binary credit risk tasks; multiclass extension is an open question.

## Next Checks
1. **Implement with full specifications:** Replicate using documented H2O AutoML settings (e.g., fixed random seed, explicit algorithm list) and resolve obscurity's reference model timing (pre-calibration) to ensure reproducible Rashomon sets.
2. **Stress-test model heterogeneity:** Construct Rashomon sets with intentionally diverse architectures (e.g., include SVMs, logistic regressions) to identify thresholds where calibration fails to harmonize outputs, probing break conditions.
3. **Assess calibration-error trade-offs:** Compute class-stratified Expected Calibration Error (ECE) and Brier score post-calibration to detect if multiplicity reduction compromises probability accuracy, especially for minority classes.
