# Forest canopy height estimation from satellite RGB imagery using large-scale airborne LiDAR-derived training data and monocular depth estimation

## Quick Facts
*   **arXiv ID:** 2602.06503
*   **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.06503
*   **Reference Count:** 10
*   **Primary Result:** Fine-tuned a monocular depth model on ~16,000 km² of airborne LiDAR and PlanetScope RGB to produce 3m canopy height maps, achieving ~2.5m RMSE in China and ~5.8m in the US, and outperforming a state-of-the-art global product (MetaCHM) by ~2m RMSE.

## Executive Summary
Accurate, large-scale mapping of forest canopy height is hindered by the sparse coverage of spaceborne LiDAR and uncertainties in existing deep learning products trained on GEDI data. This study introduces Depth2CHM, which adapts the monocular depth estimation model Depth Anything V2 using a large-scale, high-resolution training dataset of co-registered PlanetScope RGB imagery and airborne LiDAR-derived canopy height models (CHMs). Independent validation on sites in China (1 km²) and the United States (116 km²) confirms high accuracy and demonstrates a significant improvement in error metrics compared to a leading global canopy height product.

## Method Summary
The method transforms the canopy height estimation task into a monocular depth estimation problem. Training data consists of paired 3m resolution PlanetScope RGB tiles (summer, cloud/angle filtered) and corresponding airborne/UAV LiDAR-derived CHMs, with non-vegetation structures removed. The continuous CHM values are converted to pseudo-depth using the formula `pseudo_depth = 50 - CHM`, imposing an assumed 50m maximum height. A pretrained Depth Anything V2 model (ViT-L backbone) is fine-tuned to predict this pseudo-depth from RGB input. The final canopy height is retrieved by inverting the transformation. The model is trained for 25 epochs with a low learning rate (5e-6) on multiple GPUs.

## Key Results
*   Independent validation showed low bias (0.59 m in China, 0.41 m in the US) and RMSEs of 2.54 m and 5.75 m, respectively.
*   Compared to the MetaCHM product, Depth2CHM reduced mean absolute error by approximately 1.5 m and root mean square error by approximately 2 m.
*   The approach enables spatially continuous (3m resolution) canopy height mapping using only single-date, freely available satellite RGB imagery.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The paper does not provide an explicit mechanistic analysis. The core premise is that a powerful monocular depth estimation model, pre-trained on diverse terrestrial scenes, can be effectively re-purposed for the geometric task of predicting canopy height from RGB by treating height as an inverse depth proxy. The large, diverse airborne LiDAR training set provides the necessary ground truth to learn the complex visual-textural mappings between satellite RGB patterns and 3D forest structure.

## Foundational Learning
*   **Monocular Depth Estimation:** Predicts scene depth from a single image. Needed because the task reframes height prediction as a depth-from-image problem, leveraging models already understanding geometric relationships in pixels. *Quick Check:* Confirm the base model (Depth Anything V2) was trained on diverse scenes, not just forests.
*   **Airborne LiDAR-derived Canopy Height Models (CHMs):** The 3D ground truth. Needed to provide accurate, high-resolution training labels. *Quick Check:* Verify CHMs were generated consistently (DSM minus DEM) and non-vegetation features were masked.
*   **Pseudo-depth Transformation:** Linear conversion of height to depth. Needed to adapt the depth estimation model's output space (positive depth from camera) to the target variable (height above ground). *Quick Check:* The `50 - CHM` formula is applied; check if the 50m ceiling matches the training data's height distribution.
*   **PlanetScope Satellite Imagery:** The operational input. Needed for large-scale, frequent, and free coverage. *Quick Check:* Confirm imagery is summer, cloud/angle filtered, and co-registered to the CHM UTM grid.
*   **Transfer Learning:** Fine-tuning a pretrained model on a new domain. Needed to avoid training a complex vision model from scratch on a potentially limited (though large) dataset. *Quick Check:* Ensure only the output head or the full model was fine-tuned as described.

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component Map:** PlanetScope RGB Tile (3m) -> Depth Anything V2 (ViT-L, fine-tuned) -> Pseudo-Depth Prediction (0-500m scale) -> Inverse Transformation (50 - prediction) -> Canopy Height Model Tile (3m)

**Critical Path:** The path from input RGB through the fine-tuned visual transformer backbone to the regression head is critical. The pseudo-depth transformation is a key pre- and post-processing step that bridges the model's native task and the application.

**Design Tradeoffs:**
1.  **Pseudo-Depth Ceiling:** Using a fixed `max_depth=50m` in transformation simplifies the problem but risks saturating the model's representation for taller forests, potentially losing nuance.
2.  **Model Scale:** Using a large model (ViT-L) leverages strong pretrained features but requires significant GPU memory and may be prone to overfitting on smaller dataset subsets if not regularized.
3.  **Resolution Mismatch:** PlanetScope (3m) is coarser than the native resolution some depth models might expect, potentially limiting the finest-scale canopy detail recovery.

**Failure Signatures:**
*   Systematic under-prediction in tall forest stands (>45m) indicates the 50m pseudo-depth ceiling is too low.
*   Low SSIM between input RGB and predicted pseudo-depth over validation tiles suggests poor spatial correspondence or coregistration errors.
*   High errors over urban/barren areas in validation indicate incomplete masking of non-vegetation structures in the training CHMs.

**First Three Experiments:**
1.  **Ablation on Pseudo-Depth Max:** Retrain with different max height assumptions (e.g., 80m, 100m) and evaluate impact on RMSE in tall-forest validation patches.
2.  **Coregistration Stress Test:** Artificially offset RGB and CHM training tiles by small amounts (e.g., 1-3 pixels) and measure degradation in validation SSIM and RMSE to quantify sensitivity.
3.  **Data Augmentation Probe:** Introduce standard RGB augmentations (flip, rotation, color jitter) during training and compare validation performance to the no-augmentation baseline to assess overfitting.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
The paper identifies several open questions and directions for future research:
- How does the fixed pseudo-depth ceiling (50m) impact model performance in forests with taller canopies, and would a higher ceiling improve accuracy in such regions?
- What is the effect of coregistration quality between PlanetScope RGB and LiDAR-derived CHMs on model performance, and what are the tolerable error bounds?
- To what extent does the model generalize to other forest types and geographic regions, particularly in tropical or heavily disturbed ecosystems?

## Limitations
*   **Training details under-specified:** The exact loss function composition, data augmentation pipeline, and learning rate scheduler are not described, hindering exact replication.
*   **Fixed pseudo-depth ceiling:** The `pseudo_depth = 50 - CHM` transformation assumes a universal 50m maximum height, which may not hold for all forests and could bias results in taller ecosystems.
*   **Preprocessing opacity:** The uniformity and precision of the non-vegetation masking and CHM generation/resampling across the ~16,000 km² training area are not fully detailed, leaving potential for hidden biases.

## Confidence
*   **Claim:** Model achieves high accuracy (bias ~0.5m, RMSE 2.5-5.8m) on independent validation sites.
    *   **Confidence:** Medium. Validation sites are independent and substantial (116 km²), but the training/validation split strategy and full preprocessing pipeline are not transparent, leaving a risk of spatial leakage.
*   **Claim:** Depth2CHM significantly outperforms the MetaCHM global product (MAE ↓ ~1.5m, RMSE ↓ ~2m).
    *   **Confidence:** Medium-Low. The comparison is to a product with different native resolution (1m vs. 3m); resampling and evaluation protocol specifics are sparse, making direct attribution of superiority less certain.
*   **Claim:** The approach generalizes to new geographic regions (China/US).
    *   **Confidence:** Medium. Two distinct regions are tested, but broader global generalization remains unproven without more widespread external validation.

## Next Checks
1.  **Verify Training Height Distribution:** Compute the histogram of all training CHM values. If more than ~5% of pixels exceed 45m, the 50m pseudo-depth ceiling likely caused saturation loss; retrain with a higher ceiling (e.g., 80m).
2.  **Quantify Coregistration Quality:** For a random sample of training tile pairs, compute the Structural Similarity Index (SSIM) between the RGB image and the pseudo-depth label. SSIM values below 0.9 would indicate significant misalignment that needs correction.
3.  **External Generalization Test:** Apply the published model (if weights are released) to a novel, high-quality airborne LiDAR site (e.g., from the NEON network) not used in training or the cited validation. Report bias and RMSE to robustly test geographic transferability.
