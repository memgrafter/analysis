# FixationFormer: Direct Utilization of Expert Gaze Trajectories for Chest X-Ray Classification

## Quick Facts
*   **arXiv ID:** 2603.22939
*   **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.22939
*   **Reference Count:** 12
*   **Primary Result:** Achieves state-of-the-art chest X-ray classification accuracy on CXR-Gaze (84.11%) and Reflacx (70.06%) by directly fusing raw expert gaze trajectories with image features.

## Executive Summary
FixationFormer addresses the challenge of integrating noisy, sequential expert gaze data into medical image classification. Instead of reducing gaze to static heatmaps, it represents each fixation as a token encoding spatial location, duration, and temporal position. These tokens are fused with visual features from a pretrained Vision Transformer using cross-attention. On three chest X-ray datasets, the method improves upon prior gaze integration approaches and image-only baselines, demonstrating the value of preserving temporal dynamics and spatial structure in gaze trajectories.

## Method Summary
The core innovation is a token-based representation for gaze trajectories. Each fixation (a sequence of x,y coordinates and timestamp) is converted into a fixed set of tokens that capture its spatial location, dwell time, and relative temporal position within the viewing session. These gaze tokens are processed by a small transformer encoder and then fused with the patch tokens from a frozen, ImageNet-pretrained Vision Transformer (ViT) backbone through a series of cross-attention layers. This allows the model to learn contextual relationships between visual regions and the radiologist's scanning pattern over time. A classification head attached to the fused token sequence produces the final prediction.

## Key Results
*   Sets new SOTA on CXR-Gaze (84.11% vs. prior 83.18%) and Reflacx (70.06% vs. prior 64.51%).
*   Matches SOTA on the larger SIIM-ACR dataset (86.40%).
*   Ablation studies show consistent improvement over image-only ViT baselines; a gaze-only model performs above random chance, and relative gains are larger when using a weaker backbone, indicating the gaze signal provides complementary information.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
Direct tokenization of fixation trajectories preserves the sequential, temporal dynamics and precise spatial annotations that are lost when converting gaze to a summed heatmap. This allows the fusion mechanism to model *when* and *where* a radiologist looked, not just *where* they looked in aggregate. The cross-attention framework enables adaptive, context-aware integration, letting the model learn to attend to relevant image regions based on the evolving gaze sequence.

*   **Cross-Attention:** Mechanism to fuse two distinct token sequences (gaze and image). Needed for integration without forcing one modality to dominate. **Quick Check:** Verify attention weights between gaze and image tokens are learnable and not constrained.
*   **Gaze Fixation Sequences:** Raw, time-stamped lookpoint data from eye-tracking. Needed to capture diagnostic scanning behavior. **Quick Check:** Ensure input format correctly encodes (x, y, duration, timestamp) per fixation.
*   **Tokenization of Sequential Data:** Process of converting variable-length fixation sequences into a fixed set of representative tokens. Needed to handle variable viewing times across samples. **Quick Check:** Analyze if tokenization preserves temporal order and relative timing information.
*   **Medical Image Classification:** Downstream task requiring integration of visual and high-level expert knowledge. **Quick Check:** Evaluate if performance gains hold across different pathology subsets.
*   **Expert Knowledge Distillation:** Using radiologist gaze as a proxy for expert attention. Needed to embed procedural knowledge. **Quick Check:** Compare against synthetic or non-expert gaze to isolate the "expert" signal.

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component Map:** Raw Gaze Sequence -> Gaze Tokenizer & Positional Encoder -> Gaze Transformer Encoder -> Cross-Attention (with Frozen ViT Image Tokens) -> Fused Token Sequence -> MLP Classification Head

**Critical Path:** Input image is processed by a frozen ViT to extract a sequence of patch tokens. Concurrently, the raw gaze trajectory is encoded into a fixed-length sequence of gaze tokens with spatial and temporal embeddings. These two token streams interact through multiple cross-attention layers, where gaze tokens attend to image tokens (and potentially vice-versa), creating a fused multimodal representation. The [CLS] token from this fused sequence is used for classification.

**Design Tradeoffs:**
1.  **Token Count vs. Compute:** Representing each fixation with multiple tokens increases sequence length, raising computational cost for cross-attention.
2.  **Information Density vs. Noise:** A fine-grained token representation preserves detail but may entrench noise in raw gaze data; a coarser representation denoises but loses temporal precision.
3.  **Modality Balance:** A frozen, powerful ViT backbone may dominate learning, potentially limiting the gaze pathway's contribution.

**Failure Signatures:**
1.  **Gaze-Image Misalignment:** Poor performance on cases where the radiologist's gaze was distracted or followed an atypical pattern.
2.  **Failure to Propagate Temporal Cues:** Gaze tokens that are too aggregated fail to help with pathologies requiring a specific viewing sequence (e.g., comparing left/right lungs).
3.  **Backbone Dominance:** Negligible gain from gaze when using a very strong, task-specific image backbone, suggesting the image features already capture the relevant information.

**First Three Experiments:**
1.  **Gaze-Only Baseline:** Train the model with image tokens zeroed out or replaced with placeholders to establish a pure gaze-prediction upper bound and validate the token encoder's capacity.
2.  **Fusion Ablation:** Disable the cross-attention layers and instead fuse via simple concatenation or addition to isolate the contribution of the learned cross-modal interaction.
3.  **Efficiency Profiling:** Measure inference FLOPs, memory footprint, and latency for the full model against the frozen ViT-only baseline across varying gaze sequence lengths.

## Limitations
*   **Dataset and Evaluation Inconsistency:** Reported SOTA comparisons may not be fully controlled due to varying train/test splits and preprocessing across prior works.
*   **Gaze Data Generalizability:** Findings are based on expert gaze from a limited, specific set of radiologists and collection protocols; performance may degrade with different experts or viewing tasks.
*   **Computational and Deployment Cost:** The token-based fusion increases sequence length and complexity, with no reported analysis of inference speed/memory overhead for clinical deployment.

## Confidence
*   **SOTA accuracy on CXR-Gaze & Reflacx:** Medium. Gains are reported but lack controlled, re-implemented baseline comparisons.
*   **Utility of gaze token representation and fusion design:** High. Consistent improvements in ablations, especially larger gains with a weaker backbone, strongly support the core hypothesis within the tested setting.
*   **Generalization beyond chest X-rays:** Low. No evidence or experiments provided for transfer to other modalities (e.g., CT, mammography) or domains.

## Next Checks
1.  **Independent, Controlled Replication:** Re-run all experiments, including prior SOTA methods, on the official, fixed test splits of CXR-Gaze and Reflacx using a standardized codebase to confirm the magnitude of improvement.
2.  **Direct Comparison with Recent Gaze Methods:** Benchmark FixationFormer against other contemporary gaze-integration techniques (e.g., from the "Eyes on the Image" work) using their released models to isolate the benefit of the specific token-based fusion strategy.
3.  **Efficiency and Error Analysis:** Quantify the exact inference time and memory cost increase versus an image-only ViT. Perform a detailed error analysis on misclassified cases to determine if gaze fusion corrects specific failure modes (e.g., subtle consolidations) or introduces new error types.
