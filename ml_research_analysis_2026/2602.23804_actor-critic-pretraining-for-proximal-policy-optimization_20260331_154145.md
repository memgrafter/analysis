# Actor-Critic Pretraining for Proximal Policy Optimization
## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2602.23804  
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.23804  
- **Reference count:** 16  
- **Primary result:** Actor-critic pretraining reduces required environment interactions by 86.1% on average compared to no pretraining and by 30.9% compared to actor-only pretraining across 15 simulated robotic tasks.

## Executive Summary
Reinforcement learning for robotics suffers from high sample complexity. This work proposes pretraining both the actor and critic networks in Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO) using expert demonstrations. The actor is initialized via behavioral cloning, while the critic is pretrained on returns from rollouts of the pretrained policy. Evaluated on 15 robotic tasks, this joint actor-critic pretraining significantly reduces the number of environment steps needed to reach target performance, mitigates catastrophic forgetting, and improves convergence stability compared to actor-only pretraining or learning from scratch.

## Method Summary
The method comprises three stages. First, the actor network is pretrained using behavioral cloning (BC) on an offline dataset of state-action pairs from an expert policy, minimizing mean-squared error between predicted and expert actions. Second, rollouts are generated with this pretrained actor to collect a dataset of state-reward trajectories. Third, the critic network is pretrained to estimate state values by minimizing the mean-squared error between its predictions and the discounted returns observed in these rollouts. During PPO fine-tuning, the actor’s backbone is frozen while its decision head is updated, and both networks are further trained with the standard PPO objective. The approach also incorporates an extended rollout horizon to reduce value-function bias from truncation.

## Key Results
- Average sample reduction: 86.1% vs. no pretraining, 30.9% vs. actor-only pretraining.
- Mitigates catastrophic forgetting that plagues actor-only pretraining in several environments.
- Enables achieving target return within 1M steps in 9/15 environments where PPO from scratch fails.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1: Actor pretraining via behavioral cloning
BC initializes the actor close to expert behavior on the demonstrated state distribution, reducing the initial exploration burden and distribution shift during subsequent PPO fine-tuning. This starts PPO optimization from a high-return region rather than random parameters. The core assumption is that expert demonstrations sufficiently cover relevant states. Evidence includes the abstract and Section IV-A describing BC training on the expert dataset.

### Mechanism 2: Critic pretraining with rollout returns
The critic is trained to match Monte Carlo returns from rollouts of the pretrained policy. This yields an accurate value function estimate at the start of PPO, leading to better advantage estimates and more stable early policy updates. The core assumption is that these rollout returns are consistent targets for the critic because they are generated by the same policy the critic will evaluate initially. Evidence appears in the abstract and Section IV-B, where critic pretraining uses the rollout return.

### Mechanism 3: Joint actor-critic pretraining mitigates catastrophic forgetting
A well-initialized critic provides reliable advantage signals from the first PPO steps, preventing destructive policy oscillations that cause performance to drop below the BC-initialized level. This stabilizes early training dynamics. The core assumption is that actor-only pretraining’s forgetting is partly due to a poorly initialized critic. Correlative evidence is given in the abstract and Section V-A, showing ACP softens the forgetting dip seen in AP.

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component map:**  
Expert Dataset (D_exp) -> Actor (BC pretraining) -> Rollout Dataset (D_rol) -> Critic (pretraining) -> PPO Fine-tuning (Actor & Critic)

**Critical path:**  
1. Actor BC Pretraining: Minimize MSE between actor output and expert actions on D_exp.  
2. Rollout Generation: Execute pretrained actor to collect D_rol.  
3. Critic Pretraining: Minimize MSE between critic prediction and discounted returns from D_rol.  
4. PPO Fine-tuning: Standard PPO updates, with actor backbone frozen and decision head trainable, until target return is reached.

**Design tradeoffs:**  
- Frozen actor backbone vs. full fine‑training: Freezing preserves BC‑learned features but limits adaptability.  
- Rollout data quantity (n_rol): More rollouts improve critic pretraining but cost environment steps; benefits saturate beyond a task‑specific optimum.  
- Extended step limit (T_ext): Longer rollouts reduce truncation bias but increase rollout cost; a theoretical bound (Eq. 4) is provided.

**Failure signatures:**  
- Catastrophic forgetting in AP: Sharp drop of episodic return below the expert level during early PPO steps.  
- Non‑convergence with NP: PPO from scratch fails to reach target within 1 M steps in many environments.  
- No benefit from critic pretraining: ACP does not outperform AP in certain high‑dimensional tasks (e.g., Humanoid variants).  
- Overfitting in BC: Too few expert steps yields a low‑performing initial policy, increasing required fine‑tuning.

**First 3 experiments:**  
1. **Replicate core sample efficiency claim:** Run NP, AP, and ACP on Walker2D‑v3 with fixed n_exp; measure total steps to target return. Expect ACP ≪ AP < NP.  
2. **Investigate rollout sensitivity:** For ACP with fixed n_exp, vary n_rol (e.g., 0, 100k, 500k, 1M); plot total steps vs. n_rol. Expect a saturating curve with a clear minimum at intermediate n_rol.  
3. **Validate extended step limit:** Compare ACP with and without using T_ext for rollouts. The T_ext version should require fewer total steps (≈10.4% average reduction).

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
1. **How to determine required amounts of expert and rollout data?**  
   The paper acknowledges no clear method exists; both quantities are environment‑specific and non‑linear hyperparameters. A systematic heuristic or model based on environment properties would resolve this.

2. **Why does critic pretraining fail in some tasks?**  
   Critic pretraining did not improve over actor‑only in 3/15 tasks, all high‑dimensional Humanoids. The paper hypothesizes observation dimensionality as a factor, but causality is unproven. Controlled experiments varying dimensionality and other factors are needed.

3. **Can the approach extend to other algorithms and discrete actions?**  
   The study focuses on PPO with continuous actions. Extensions to off‑policy methods (e.g., Soft Actor‑Critic) and discrete action spaces require adjustments and empirical validation.

## Limitations
- PPO fine‑tuning hyperparameters (Optuna‑optimized) are not reported, preventing exact replication.  
- Optimal values of expert steps (n_exp) and rollout steps (n_rol) for each environment are unspecified.  
- Critic pretraining does not yield gains in 20% of tasks (high‑dimensional humanoids), and the underlying reasons remain unclear.

## Confidence
- Sample efficiency gains (86.1% vs NP, 30.9% vs AP): **High**  
- Mitigation of catastrophic forgetting: **Medium** (observed in some but not all environments)  
- Mechanism that critic pretraining stabilizes early updates: **Low** (correlative evidence only)
