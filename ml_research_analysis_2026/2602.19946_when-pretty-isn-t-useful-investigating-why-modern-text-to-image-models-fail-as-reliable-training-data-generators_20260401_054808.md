# When Pretty Isn't Useful: Investigating Why Modern Text-to-Image Models Fail as Reliable Training Data Generators

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2602.19946  
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.19946  
- **Reference count:** 40  
- **Primary result:** Despite gains in photorealism and prompt following, synthetic datasets from modern text-to-image models yield *worse* classifier accuracy on real ImageNet data, with a steady decline from ~0.6 (SD1.5) to below 0.2 (FLUX.1-dev, Qwen-Image).

## Executive Summary
This paper reveals a counterintuitive trend: as text-to-image (T2I) models improve in visual quality and prompt adherence, their utility as sources of synthetic training data for downstream vision tasks has deteriorated. Training ResNet-50 classifiers on images generated by thirteen models (2022–2025) and evaluating on real ImageNet-1k shows a consistent drop in transfer accuracy over time. The failure stems from a collapse toward narrow, "aesthetic-centric" modes: newer models sacrifice fine-scale texture and high-frequency details while preserving coarse structure, and they produce datasets with high feature-space density but low coverage of real data variation. Detailed captions partially rescue structure but do not fix texture deficits. The findings expose a fundamental trade-off: progress in perceptual appeal undermines the distributional diversity essential for learnability.

## Method Summary
The authors train image classifiers solely on synthetic datasets generated by thirteen open-source T2I models (e.g., Stable Diffusion 1.5–3.5, FLUX, Qwen-Image) for a 200-class subset of ImageNet-1k (500 images per class). Two prompt types are used: (1) class names and (2) detailed captions derived by recaptioning real images with GPT-4.1-nano. Generated images are downsampled to 256×256. A ResNet-50 is trained from scratch on each synthetic dataset (80 epochs, Adam, lr=1e−3, batch 1024) and evaluated on the real ImageNet-1k validation set (same 200 classes). The primary metric is top-1 accuracy (Synth→Real). Additional analyses probe texture/structure (using depth maps and patch classifiers), frequency response (high/low-pass filtering), and distributional coverage/density in CLIP feature space.

## Key Results
- **Steady accuracy decline:** Synth→Real top-1 accuracy drops from ~0.60 with SD1.5 to below 0.20 with FLUX.1-dev and Qwen-Image, despite these models being more photorealistic.
- **Texture degradation is systematic:** Newer models preserve global structure/low frequencies but degrade high-frequency details; detailed captions improve structure but not texture.
- **Distributional collapse:** Synthetic datasets from newer models exhibit high density and low coverage in CLIP feature space, indicating a narrow, non-diverse mode that fails to span real-world variation.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1: Texture and High-Frequency Deficiency
**Claim:** Modern T2I models increasingly degrade fine-scale texture and high-frequency details while preserving global structure.  
**Mechanism:** Optimization for aesthetic quality favors coherent, low-frequency layouts at the expense of textural variation.  
**Core assumption:** CNNs rely on high-frequency texture cues for classification; degrading them harms transfer even if images look realistic to humans.  
**Evidence:** Texture-based (BagNet) and high-pass filtered classifiers show larger performance gaps than structure-based (depth) and low-pass filtered ones.  
**Break condition:** If T2I architectures explicitly regularize for multi-scale fidelity during training.

### Mechanism 2: High-Density, Low-Coverage Feature Distributions
**Claim:** Newer models produce synthetic data with high feature-space density but low coverage of real data.  
**Mechanism:** Perceptual quality optimization (e.g., via human feedback) concentrates samples in a compact, visually pleasing region, increasing density but decreasing coverage of diverse real instances.  
**Core assumption:** Effective training data must cover the full diversity and extremes of the real distribution, not just occupy a dense mode.  
**Evidence:** CLIP-feature density/coverage metrics show an inverse trend with release date and correlate with Synth→Real accuracy.  
**Break condition:** If T2I training objectives are modified to maximize coverage (e.g., via diversity-promoting losses).

### Mechanism 3: Prompt-Following vs. Diversity Trade-off
**Claim:** Over-optimization for precise prompt adherence reduces diversity when using simple (class-label) prompts.  
**Mechanism:** Models tuned for detailed textual instructions default to a limited set of "canonical" interpretations for underspecified prompts, failing to capture intra-class variation.  
**Core assumption:** Simple prompts should elicit diverse plausible instances; excessive alignment hurts this.  
**Evidence:** GenEval/CLIPScore trends inversely with Synth→Real accuracy for class-name prompts; detailed captions narrow the gap for structure but not texture.  
**Break condition:** If models are explicitly trained to maintain diversity under underspecified prompts.

## Foundational Learning
- **Texture Bias in CNNs**  
  *Why needed:* Mechanism 1 hinges on CNNs’ reliance on high-frequency texture for discrimination. Understanding this bias explains why degraded textures hurt transfer.  
  *Quick check:* If an ImageNet model relied primarily on shape, would the Synth→Real gap be smaller? (The paper’s depth-based classifier experiment suggests yes.)

- **Data Distillation & Distributional Metrics (Density/Coverage)**  
  *Why needed:* Mechanism 2 quantifies "distributional realism" via density and coverage in feature space—key to understanding dataset utility beyond sample realism.  
  *Quick check:* What does high-density/low-coverage imply about learned decision boundaries? (They may be overfitted to a narrow synthetic mode.)

- **Conditional Diffusion and Mode Collapse**  
  *Why needed:* The "aesthetic-centric collapse" is a form of mode collapse in conditional generation, explaining diversity loss.  
  *Quick check:* How might CLIP-guided training encourage collapse into high-aesthetic-density modes? (By reinforcing a small set of high-scoring outputs.)

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component map:**  
Synthetic Dataset Generator (T2I models) -> Data Transformer (preprocessing, filtering, patch extraction) -> Feature Extractor (CLIP-ViT-L for density/coverage) -> Downstream Model Trainer (ResNet-50/ViT/ConvNeXt/Swin) -> Evaluator (real ImageNet test)

**Critical path:**  
T2I Generation → (Optional) Prompt Engineering → Dataset Creation → Feature Space Analysis (density/coverage) → Downstream Training → Real-world Evaluation.

**Design tradeoffs:**  
- *Scale vs. Depth:* Using a 200-class, 100k-image subset enables exhaustive multi-model testing but may miss class-specific failures.  
- *Automated vs. Human Evaluation:* Classifier accuracy provides an objective "utility" metric but may not capture all aspects of realism.  
- *Prompt Realism:* Detailed captions (from real images) offer an upper bound but are impractical for purely synthetic generation.

**Failure signatures:**  
- **Primary:** Downward Synth→Real accuracy trend over time despite upward GenEval/CLIPScore trends.  
- **Secondary:** Large texture/structure gap (BagNet vs. depth classifier); large high-frequency/low-frequency gap; high Real→Synth vs. low Synth→Real accuracy asymmetry.  
- **Secondary:** High CLIP-feature density and low coverage for newer models.

**First 3 experiments:**  
1. **Benchmark Trend Analysis:** Train ResNet-50 on datasets from 13 T2I models (2022–2025) with class-name and detailed captions; evaluate on real ImageNet to establish the negative correlation between model quality and transfer utility.  
2. **Probe Texture vs. Structure:** Evaluate trained classifiers on depth maps (structure) and 9×9 patches (texture) to isolate the texture degradation.  
3. **Probe Frequency Response:** Evaluate on high-pass and low-pass filtered images to quantify high-frequency fidelity loss.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
1. **How can T2I objectives be modified to optimize distributional diversity (coverage/density) without sacrificing prompt following and fidelity?**  
   *Basis:* Authors call for rethinking T2I design to prioritize diversity over surface realism and suggest incorporating learnability checks.  
   *Why unresolved:* Current objectives maximize perceptual quality, inadvertently causing collapsed distributions; no existing paradigm jointly optimizes fidelity and diversity.  
   *Resolution evidence:* New loss functions/regularizers that directly maximize coverage while maintaining prompt alignment, validated across architectures.

2. **Why do detailed prompts improve global structure but fail to remediate texture/high-frequency degradation?**  
   *Basis:* Captions narrow structure/low-frequency gaps but leave texture/high-frequency gaps largely unchanged.  
   *Why unresolved:* Architectural causes (e.g., VAE bottlenecks, frequency biases in U-Nets) are not analyzed; VAE ablations suggest contribution but mechanisms remain unclear.  
   *Resolution evidence:* Controlled ablations isolating model components (VAE, diffusion steps, conditioning) and measuring spectral properties of training data and latents.

3. **Do negative trends generalize to other vision tasks (detection, segmentation) and how do they interact with synthetic dataset scale?**  
   *Basis:* Main benchmark uses classification; preliminary detection/segmentation results for few models show similar trends; scaling behavior underexplored.  
   *Why unresolved:* Different tasks rely on different features; experiments limited to ResNet-50 and a 100k-scale subset; full-dataset and multi-task evaluation lacking.  
   *Resolution evidence:* Large-scale experiments across classification, detection, segmentation with consistent synthetic datasets; varying images-per-class to map scaling interactions.

4. **Is there a quantifiable trade-off between prompt following and distributional diversity, and can it be broken?**  
   *Basis:* Inverse correlation observed: newer models have higher GenEval/CLIPScore but lower coverage (and higher density).  
   *Why unresolved:* Correlation is observational; causality not established—it’s unclear if prompt following inherently reduces diversity or if both are side-effects of other factors (e.g., scaling, data curation).  
   *Resolution evidence:* Systematic evaluation of models fine-tuned with varying guidance scales or diversity losses to map the Pareto frontier; formal analysis of training dynamics.

## Limitations
- Exact 200-class ImageNet subset and detailed caption generation parameters (LLM, prompts) are unreported, introducing replication variance.  
- Generalizability limited to ResNet-50 and a 100k-scale subset; trends may shift with other architectures or full-dataset scaling.  
- Causality behind mechanisms (e.g., why texture is sacrificed) is inferred, not directly manipulated; correlation between density/coverage and utility is shown but not proven causal.

## Confidence
- **Core Trend Decline (SD1.5→FLUX):** High. Consistent across 13 models, multiple prompt types, and three architectures (ResNet/ViT/ConvNeXt).  
- **Mechanism 1 (Texture/High-Freq Degradation):** Medium. Strong experimental evidence (depth/BagNet, frequency filtering), but causality (why models sacrifice texture) is inferred.  
- **Mechanism 2 (High-Density/Low-Coverage):** Medium. CLIP-based metrics show correlation, but these are proxies; direct causal link to aesthetic optimization is plausible but unproven.  
- **Mechanism 3 (Prompt-Following vs. Diversity Trade-off):** Medium. Inverse correlation in data; however, prompt-following metrics (GenEval) are synthetic benchmarks themselves and may not capture real-world diversity needs.

## Next Checks
1. **Architecture Scope:** Train five additional architectures (e.g., EfficientNet, RegNet) on the same synthetic datasets to test if the accuracy decline is dataset-distributional rather than model-specific.  
2. **Distribution Metrics:** Compute FID and Precision-Recall between synthetic and real ImageNet samples to validate that newer models have lower FID (sample realism) but worse Precision-Recall (coverage/diversity), mirroring CLIP-space findings.  
3. **Texture Causality:** Adversarially train a ResNet-50 on synthetic data from a newer model (e.g., FLUX) while regularizing for texture invariance (e.g., via style augmentation); improved accuracy would confirm texture deficiency as a bottleneck.
