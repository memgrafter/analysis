# Digital Twin Driven Textile Classification and Foreign Object Recognition in Automated Sorting Systems

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2603.05230
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.05230
- **Reference count:** 29
- **Primary result:** The Qwen VLM family achieved up to 87.9% classification accuracy on a 223-scenario textile benchmark, with strong foreign object detection; Gemma3 provided a faster, competitive alternative for edge deployment.

## Executive Summary
This paper presents a robotic system for automating textile recycling, addressing the challenge of sorting deformable garments and detecting foreign objects in cluttered bins. The solution integrates a digital twin for collision-aware motion planning with dual-arm manipulators, using RGB-D sensing and capacitive tactile feedback. Garment classification is performed by Visual Language Models (VLMs) in a dedicated inspection zone. Benchmarking nine VLMs on real-world scenarios demonstrated that the Qwen models achieved the highest accuracy, while Gemma3 offered a favorable speed-accuracy trade-off for resource-constrained setups. The work shows how semantic VLM reasoning can be combined with traditional grasp detection and digital twin technology to create a scalable sorting pipeline.

## Method Summary
The system employs two UR7e robots: "Bob" stabilizes the basket, and "Alice" retrieves and sorts garments. Grasp prediction uses a modified CNN on RGB-D data from the basket (Cam1). After a grasp, capacitive fingertip sensors (CapTac) verify pickup success, and a wrist-shaking motion dislodges debris. The garment is placed in an inspection zone (zone B), where an RGB image (Cam2) is fed to a locally running VLM via Ollama. The VLM is prompted to output one of six tokens (`shirt`, `sock`, `trousers`, `underwear`, `other`, `empty`) to classify the item or flag a foreign object/empty scene. The segmentation of the garment in zone B generates a 3D point cloud, which is integrated into a digital twin (RViz) as a dynamic obstacle. MoveIt then plans collision-free paths for Alice to place the item into the correct bin, using the updated virtual scene.

## Key Results
- The Qwen model family achieved the highest overall classification accuracy (up to 87.9%) and strong foreign object detection on a benchmark of 223 real-world inspection scenarios.
- The lighter Gemma3 model (3.2B parameters) offered a competitive speed-accuracy trade-off (~0.65s inference), making it suitable for edge deployment despite lower accuracy on some classes.
- The integrated system demonstrated feasible end-to-end automation, combining semantic VLM classification with reliable grasp verification (CapTac) and collision-aware planning via a digital twin.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1: Digital Twin for Collision-Aware Planning
- **Claim:** Integrating a digital twin with MoveIt improves manipulation reliability by incorporating segmented 3D point clouds of deformable garments into the virtual environment as planning obstacles.
- **Mechanism:** The physical workspace and robots are mirrored in RViz. During operation, RGB-D data from the inspection zone is segmented to isolate the garment. This point cloud is converted and added to the digital twin as a 3D obstacle. MoveIt plans paths for the dual arms using this updated scene, avoiding collisions with both static elements and the garment model.
- **Core assumption:** A single-view 3D reconstruction sufficiently captures the garment's occupied volume for safe motion planning, despite deformability and potential occlusions.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract]: "A digital twin combined with MoveIt enables collision-aware path planning and integrates segmented 3D point clouds of inspected garments into the virtual environment for improved manipulation reliability."
  - [section 3]: Describes streaming Cam1's RGB-D into the digital twin and segmenting objects in zone B as pointclouds displayed in RViz.
- **Break condition:** Failure if segmentation is incorrect (e.g., includes background) or fails entirely, leading to an inaccurate or missing virtual obstacle and a potential collision.

### Mechanism 2: VLM-Based Garment Classification
- **Claim:** Off-the-shelf Visual Language Models, prompted with a constrained token set, can reliably classify garments and detect foreign objects in cluttered, real-world scenes without task-specific fine-tuning.
- **Mechanism:** An RGB image from the inspection zone (zone B) and a system prompt enforcing output into one of six tokens are sent to a local VLM (via Ollama). The returned token determines the garment class or flags a foreign object/empty scene.
- **Core assumption:** The VLM's pre-trained knowledge and zero-shot capabilities generalize to the specific, cluttered, and deformable textile scenarios presented.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract]: Reports VLM classification and benchmark results showing Qwen's high accuracy and strong foreign object detection.
  - [section 5]: Presents the benchmark (Table 1, Figure 5) quantifying accuracy per class and model on 223 scenarios.
- **Break condition:** The mechanism breaks if the VLM hallucinates (e.g., outputs a sentence instead of a token) or consistently misclassifies specific garment types or foreign objects under clutter.

### Mechanism 3: Tactile Grasp Verification with CapTac
- **Claim:** Capacitive tactile feedback provides immediate, reliable grasp success verification, enabling recovery from initial pickup failures and execution of spreading/shaking maneuvers.
- **Mechanism:** After the gripper closes, CapTac sensor arrays measure normal and shear forces. A baseline is recorded during initialization. Significant deviation from baseline during/after closure indicates a successful grasp; absence or anomalous signals trigger abort. After a successful pickup, a wrist-shaking motion dislodges by-catch before placement.
- **Core assumption:** The capacitive sensor's signal profile correlates reliably with a stable grasp of a textile item versus a failed grasp or grasp of unintended debris.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [section 4]: Details the grasp success determination via CapTac feedback and the subsequent shaking motion.
  - [section 2]: References prior work [27] establishing the CapTac sensor's design and basic validation.
- **Break condition:** Failure if sensor signal is noisy, drifts from calibration, or cannot distinguish a garment grasp from a foreign object grasp (e.g., a stiff bottle), causing false positives or negatives.

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map:** 
  - Perception Layer: Cam1 (RGB-D grasp), Cam2 (RGB inspection), CapTac (fingertip force) ->
  - Reasoning Layer: Grasp Prediction CNN (fast), VLM (Gemma3/Qwen) ->
  - Planning & Control Layer: ROS 2 Jazzy -> MoveIt (with RViz digital twin) -> UR7e controllers ->
  - Virtual Layer: RViz (static environment + dynamic garment point clouds) ->
  - Safety/Integration Layer: Arrowhead framework (secure sensor data)
- **Critical path:** Basket inspection (Cam1) -> Grasp prediction -> Pick & Shake (CapTac verified) -> Place in zone B -> VLM classification (Cam2) -> Segmentation -> Update digital twin -> Grasp prediction for sorted placement -> Place in zone C/Bob’s bin.
- **Design tradeoffs:**
  - **Accuracy vs. Latency:** Qwen models (high accuracy, ~12–20s) vs. Gemma3 (lower accuracy, ~0.65s).
  - **Perception vs. Planning:** Single-view 3D reconstruction for obstacle representation vs. computational cost of multi-view scanning.
  - **Generality vs. Robustness:** Zero-shot VLM capability vs. susceptibility to clutter, lighting, and unusual garment presentations.
- **Failure signatures:**
  - **VLM:** Outputs full sentences instead of tokens; returns `other` for all inputs; shows class-specific confusion (e.g., `llama3.2-vision` misclassifying shirts).
  - **Grasp/Segmentation:** Repeated "no candidate" from grasp algorithm; point cloud fails to appear in RViz.
  - **Tactile:** Grasp success reported despite empty gripper; failure detected immediately after a seemingly successful grasp.
- **First 3 experiments:**
  1. **Validate VLM pipeline isolation:** Run the Python script (Listing 1) with 10 curated test images (clear garment, foreign object, empty) to confirm each model returns only the expected token within time bounds.
  2. **Validate digital twin synchronization:** With a known object in zone B, trigger segmentation and verify the point cloud appears correctly aligned in RViz. Manually move the robot’s end effector near the virtual cloud to test MoveIt’s collision avoidance.
  3. **Validate tactile grasp loop:** Place a known garment in the basket. Run one pick cycle and monitor CapTac force data (via ROS 2 topic) to confirm: (a) baseline recorded, (b) force spike on closure, (c) "shake" executed only after signal.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- **Robot-to-robot handover:** How can reliable handover of deformable garments be achieved? The current system uses a single manipulator; handover introduces alignment, grasp stability, and collision challenges.
- **Multi-arm spreading for inspection:** How can multiple arms coordinate to spread garments for multi‑perspective inspection, and how does this affect 3D reconstruction quality? Single‑arm placement often leaves garments crumpled.
- **VLM ensembles:** How can ensembles combine Qwen’s accuracy with Gemma3’s speed to improve classification, especially for empty‑scene detection? No ensemble method was evaluated.

## Limitations
- The single-view point cloud may not fully capture dynamic deformations, risking planning failures.
- VLM zero‑shot generalization beyond the 223‑scenario benchmark is unproven; prompt details are sparse.
- Tactile grasp verification lacks quantitative validation of sensor discrimination across diverse garments and foreign objects.
- The modified grasp prediction algorithm and point cloud conversion steps are unspecified, hindering full replication.

## Confidence
- **Digital twin collision planning:** Medium (relies on single-view approximation of deformable objects)
- **VLM classification:** High (benchmark results provided on 223 scenarios)
- **Tactile grasp verification:** Medium (sensor design validated in prior work, but integration not quantitatively stress‑tested)
