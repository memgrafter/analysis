# EstLLM: Enhancing Estonian Capabilities in Multilingual LLMs via Continued Pretraining and Post-Training

## Quick Facts
*   **arXiv ID:** 2603.02041
*   **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.02041
*   **Reference count:** 40
*   **Primary result:** Continued pretraining with a balanced multilingual mixture and a final chat vector merge raised Estonian discriminative accuracy from 54.0% to 69.5% and Estonian instruction-following from 38.0% to 61.4%, while preserving competitive English performance.

## Executive Summary
Multilingual large language models are typically English-centric, leading to poor performance on low-resource languages like Estonian. This paper presents EstLLM, an 8B-parameter model derived from Llama 3.1 8B. The core method applies continued pretraining (CPT) on a carefully balanced 35.7B-token mixture that significantly increases Estonian exposure (8.6B tokens) while replaying English, code, and mathematics data to prevent catastrophic forgetting. This is followed by supervised fine-tuning (SFT), Direct Preference Optimization (DPO), and a novel "chat vector merging" step—additively composing the weight difference between the original Llama instruct and base models. The pipeline yields state-of-the-art Estonian capabilities for its size (e.g., 69.5% average accuracy on Estonian discriminative benchmarks) and effectively restores English instruction-following that was degraded by the Estonian-focused post-training.

## Method Summary
The authors began with the pretrained Llama 3.1 8B model and performed continued pretraining (CPT) for 3 epochs on a 35.7B token mixture. This mix consisted of a high-quality curated Estonian corpus (ENC, 8.6B tokens) alongside English "replay" data, code, mathematics, and instruction-augmented data to approximate the original pretraining distribution and maintain general skills. The resulting base model was then aligned via supervised fine-tuning (SFT) on a mixed Estonian/English instruction dataset, followed by DPO refinement on the English HelpSteer3 dataset. The final step, chat vector merging, added the delta between the original `Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct` and `Llama-3.1-8B` checkpoints (with scale factor α=0.5 and embeddings excluded) to the SFT+DPO model, transferring robust instruction-following behaviors.

## Key Results
*   Estonian discriminative benchmark average accuracy improved from 54.0% (base Llama) to 69.5%, exceeding other 8B-class models likeambo and Apertus-8B.
*   Estonian instruction-following (IFEval-et) surged from 38.0% (base instruct) to 61.4% after chat vector merging, outperforming the SFT-only variant (51.7%).
*   English performance remained strong (e.g., Winogrande 76.6% vs. base 78.5%), and chat vector merging specifically recovered English instruction-following (IFEval-en 81.7% vs. SFT-only 75.3%).

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1
*   **Claim:** Continued pretraining (CPT) with a balanced multilingual mixture enhances Estonian capabilities while preserving general knowledge.
*   **Mechanism:** The model is exposed to a diverse data mix during CPT that increases Estonian token exposure (8.6B tokens) while "replaying" English, code, and math data. This approximates the original pretraining distribution, mitigating catastrophic forgetting of non-Estonian skills. The inclusion of instruction-augmented data during CPT also preconditions the model for subsequent alignment stages.
*   **Evidence anchors:** [abstract] describes the mixture design; [section 3.1.2] details the 35.7B token composition; [section 6.1] shows monolingual Estonian CPT underperforms the balanced mix.
*   **Break condition:** If the Estonian proportion significantly outweighs replay components, the model forgets non-Estonian knowledge.

### Mechanism 2
*   **Claim:** Post-training alignment (SFT + DPO) establishes instruction-following, but chat vector merging uniquely restores English capabilities lost during Estonian-specific fine-tuning while compounding Estonian gains.
*   **Mechanism:** SFT teaches format adherence on a mixed instruction set; DPO refines responses on English preference data. Chat vector merging—adding `θ_instruct - θ_base` to the adapted model—transfers the instruction-following "dialect" from the original instruct model. This vector likely encodes language-agnostic conversational patterns and safety behaviors.
*   **Evidence anchors:** [abstract] notes recovered English IFEval (81.7%); [section 4.2.3] defines merging and shows merged model outperforms SFT-only on both languages; [section 6.4] states merging resolves English regression.
*   **Break condition:** If the chat vector scale factor α is too high (α > 0.5) or includes embedding layers, it may overwrite Estonian-specific CPT gains.

### Mechanism 3
*   **Claim:** Data quality and curated sources (like the Estonian National Corpus) outperform larger, noisier web corpora in CPT for a low-resource language.
*   **Mechanism:** High-quality, diverse in-domain data (literature, academic, Wikipedia) provides a cleaner signal for learning Estonian linguistic structures than large-scale filtered web crawls (FineWeb-2), which contain machine-translated noise. The model learns more robust representations from fewer, high-quality tokens.
*   **Evidence anchors:** [section 6.1] directly compares curated ENC vs. FineWeb-2, finding the smaller curated corpus stronger; [section 3.1.1] describes extensive ENC preprocessing; [corpus] cites neighbor paper supporting data composition's importance.
*   **Break condition:** If the curated corpus is too small or lacks domain diversity (e.g., missing conversational/technical text), gains may plateau. The paper found no benefit from repeating data beyond one epoch.

## Foundational Learning
*   **Concept: Catastrophic Forgetting in CPT**
    *   **Why needed:** CPT shifts the data distribution toward Estonian, risking overwriting weights encoding English/general knowledge. Understanding forgetting is key to designing the replay mixture.
    *   **Quick check:** Why might continuing pretraining on only Estonian data cause performance drops on English benchmarks?
*   **Concept: Instruction Tuning & Preference Alignment**
    *   **Why needed:** The base model after CPT lacks conversational ability and constraint-following. SFT and DPO are needed to make it useful as an assistant, but they risk degrading Estonian gains if not balanced.
    *   **Quick check:** What is the difference between supervised fine-tuning (SFT) and Direct Preference Optimization (DPO) in this pipeline?
*   **Concept: Model Merging (Delta Merging)**
    *   **Why needed:** Chat vector merging is the critical step that recovers English instruction-following without retraining. It exploits the linearity of fine-tuning for additive composition of capabilities.
    *   **Quick check:** How does computing `θ_instruct - θ_base` and adding it to `θ_CPT` transfer instruction-following behavior?

## Architecture Onboarding
*   **Component map:** Data Preparation -> CPT Warm-up -> Main CPT -> (Base Model Eval) -> SFT -> DPO -> Chat Vector Merge -> (Final Model Eval)
*   **Critical path:** The pipeline is sequential. Skipping CPT or the merging step leads to poor Estonian or English performance, respectively.
*   **Design tradeoffs:**
    *   *Data mixture:* More Estonian tokens boost Estonian tasks but risk forgetting; replay data preserves general skills but dilutes the target signal. Balance was key (~24% Estonian).
    *   *Merging:* Adding the chat vector improves both languages but requires tuning α and excluding embeddings to avoid overwriting CPT gains.
    *   *Tokenizer:* Keeping the original Llama tokenizer simplifies merging but may tokenize Estonian less efficiently.
*   **Failure signatures:**
    *   **Pattern A:** After CPT, Estonian improves but English/MMLU drop → insufficient replay or too aggressive Estonian-only training.
    *   **Pattern B:** After SFT, instruction-following improves in Estonian but English regresses → missing chat vector merging step.
    *   **Pattern C:** Merged model performs worse than base on some tasks → α too high or embedding layer included in merge.
*   **First 3 experiments:**
    1.  Monolingual CPT ablation: Train CPT with only Estonian data (ENC) vs. balanced mix. Verify balanced mix yields higher average Estonian discriminative score while retaining English (Table 4).
    2.  Chat vector parameter sweep: Vary α in `θ_CPT + α·∆θ` and test on IFEval-et and IFEval-en. Identify α=0.5 as optimal (Section 6.4).
    3.  Base model comparison: Apply identical CPT+SFT pipeline to Llama-3.1-8B vs. Apertus-8B. Confirm Llama-based model gains more from CPT, showing base model initial distribution affects adaptation (Section 6.2.2).

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
*   **Question 1:** What is the optimal continued pretraining data mixture and replay proportion for Estonian adaptation while preserving cross-lingual capabilities?
    *   **Basis:** The authors state the interaction between data composition, replay proportions, and cross-domain transfer remains only partially understood (Limitations).
    *   **Why unresolved:** They used a single fixed mixture without systematic variation due to computational cost.
    *   **What evidence:** Factorial experiments varying domain shares and replay ratios, measuring Estonian and English performance and transfer effects.
*   **Question 2:** Do the benefits of their CPT and chat vector merging pipeline hold for models larger than 8B parameters and across different base model pretraining distributions?
    *   **Basis:** All experiments used 8B models; authors note scalability is untested (Limitations). They also observed different responses between Llama and Apertus bases (Discussion).
    *   **Why unresolved:** Larger models were not evaluated; base model sensitivity suggests adaptation may not be universal.
    *   **What evidence:** Apply the same pipeline to 70B+ models and diverse bases, compare performance scaling.
*   **Question 3:** Why does adding a chat vector from a weak-in-Estonian instruction model improve instruction-following in both Estonian and English for the adapted model?
    *   **Basis:** The authors call this finding surprising and say the mechanism remains unclear (Section 7).
    *   **Why unresolved:** No probing of the vector's content; unclear what drives the dual-language gain.
    *   **What evidence:** Analyze the vector's representation space, test layer-wise merges, compare vectors from models with different language strengths.

## Limitations
*   **Data composition uncertainty:** The optimal mixture ratio for CPT remains empirical and may vary with base model distribution and target language similarity.
*   **Evaluation scope limitations:** Gains are measured on Estonian-specific and general English tasks; performance on other low-resource languages or cross-lingual scenarios is untested. The Estonian IFEval (IFEval-et) is a new benchmark, limiting comparability.
*   **Scaling and resource constraints:** Effectiveness at larger scales (70B+ parameters) is unknown, and training costs are substantial.
*   **Chat vector assumptions:** The linearity assumption that instruction-following signals are orthogonal to language-specific knowledge may not generalize to all architectures or instruction types.

## Confidence
*   **High Confidence:** The core claim that continued pretraining with a balanced mixture improves Estonian performance while preserving English capabilities. Supported by multiple ablations (monolingual vs. mixed CPT, base model comparisons).
*   **Medium Confidence:** The effectiveness of chat vector merging in recovering English instruction-following while enhancing Estonian performance. Positive results rely on assumptions about weight space linearity that may not universalize.
*   **Medium Confidence:** The assertion that curated high-quality data outperforms larger filtered web corpora for low-resource language adaptation. The comparison is limited to Estonian and one alternative corpus but aligns with related work.

## Next Checks
1.  **Cross-lingual Transfer Evaluation:** Test EstLLM's performance on other Uralic languages (Finnish, Hungarian) to assess whether Estonian-specific gains transfer, validating potential language family benefits.
2.  **Long-form Instruction Following:** Evaluate the model on extended conversational tasks and complex multi-turn instructions in both Estonian and English to verify robustness beyond simple queries.
3.  **Scaling Analysis:** Apply the same CPT + post-training + merging pipeline to a 70B parameter model to determine if the relative performance improvements scale with model size, or if diminishing returns emerge.
