# PARSA-Bench: A Comprehensive Persian Audio-Language Model Benchmark

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID**: 2603.14456
- **Source URL**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.14456
- **Reference count**: 0
- **Primary result**: Introduces the first Persian audio-language benchmark with 16 tasks and 8,000+ samples, revealing a consistent audio-text performance gap and near-random poetry meter (vazn) detection across all evaluated models.

## Executive Summary
PARSA-Bench establishes the first systematic evaluation suite for Persian audio-language models (LALMs), comprising 16 diverse tasks spanning over 8,000 annotated samples. Benchmarking eight existing LALMs uncovers a persistent gap between audio and text-only performance, suggesting audio processing remains a core bottleneck. Critically, all models achieve near-chance results (F1 ~0.10) on poetry meter detection, indicating a profound failure in prosodic perception regardless of model scale. Conversely, poetry style classification shows measurable benefit from audio input. The findings imply that scaling alone does not address culturally nuanced tasks, highlighting urgent needs for targeted architectural and data interventions.

## Method Summary
The paper introduces PARSA-Bench, a manually curated benchmark covering 16 Persian-specific tasks—including poetry meter (vazn) detection, style classification, and general audio-text understanding—with over 8,000 high-quality samples. Evaluation is conducted on eight off-the-shelf LALMs (e.g., Qwen-Audio, InternLM) in zero-shot or few-shot settings, comparing audio-input against text-only baselines. No model training or dataset construction details are provided beyond this high-level description.

## Key Results
- All evaluated LALMs exhibit a consistent audio-text performance gap; audio processing is the primary bottleneck.
- Poetry meter (vazn) detection yields near-random F1 scores (~0.10) universally, regardless of model scale.
- Poetry style classification benefits from audio, with Qwen3-Omni-30B achieving 0.640 accuracy versus 0.554 text-only.
- Model scale does not reliably improve performance on culturally specific tasks.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The paper does not propose a mechanistic explanation for the observed gaps. It identifies empirical patterns—particularly the failure on prosodic tasks—but does not investigate whether these stem from audio encoder limitations, fusion design, or training data deficiencies.

- **Critical path**: Raw audio → acoustic features → alignment with text embeddings → token generation.
- **Design tradeoffs**: Early vs. late fusion impacts efficiency and granularity of audio-text interaction; larger audio encoders increase cost but may capture subtle prosody.
- **Failure signatures**: Collapse on poetry meter (vazn) detection; small audio-text score delta on non-prosodic tasks.

## Limitations
- Dataset representativeness may be limited in dialect, poetic form, and audio condition diversity.
- Baseline models are not Persian- or prosody-specialized, possibly reflecting architectural biases.
- Poetry meter task design could be ambiguous or lack sufficient acoustic cues.
- Missing ablation studies prevent diagnosing whether failures lie in audio encoders, fusion, or training data.
