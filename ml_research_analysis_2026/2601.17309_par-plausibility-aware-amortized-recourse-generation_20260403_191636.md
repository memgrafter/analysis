# PAR: Plausibility-aware Amortized Recourse Generation

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2601.17309
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.17309
- **Reference count:** 33
- **Primary result:** Generates valid (>93% on Adult/Credit) and feasible recourse in <0.2s median time by amortizing constrained MAP inference with probabilistic circuits.

## Executive Summary
PAR tackles algorithmic recourse—generating actionable changes to reverse an unfavorable model decision—by producing counterfactuals that are both plausible (high likelihood under the accepted-class distribution) and feasible (respecting immutable, monotonic, and causal constraints). It formulates recourse as a constrained maximum a posteriori inference problem, using tractable probabilistic circuits (PCs) to evaluate exact likelihoods and enable gradient-based optimization. An amortized neural generator, conditioned on immutable features and a neighborhood summary of local accepted instances, outputs distributions over mutable features. Feasibility is enforced via hard masking for logical constraints and soft losses for validity, proximity, sparsity, and entropy. This approach achieves high validity and actionability with significantly lower runtime than per-instance optimization baselines.

## Method Summary
PAR first preprocesses data by discretizing continuous features and training a binary classifier and class-conditional probabilistic circuits (p+ for accepted class, p− for denied). During training, for each denied factual instance, it retrieves K nearest neighbors from a precomputed pool of accepted samples (using Hamming distance). A neighborhood encoder aggregates neighbor descriptors (distance and log p+ values) into an embedding. A recourse generator network takes immutable features, the factual’s log p+, and this embedding to produce logits for mutable features. Hard masking enforces monotonicity and causality constraints on these logits. The generator is trained end-to-end with a composite loss: validity (binary cross-entropy on classifier output), proximity (squared hinge on expected Hamming distance), sparsity (cross-entropy “stay-close” term), entropy minimization, and plausibility terms (attraction to p+ and repulsion from p−). gradients for the plausibility terms are computed exactly via the PC’s multilinear evaluation. At inference, the generator produces a candidate counterfactual, which is optionally refined with a local search procedure to improve sparsity and ensure feasibility.

## Key Results
- Achieves >93% validity on Adult and Credit, >85% on GMSC, with perfect actionability (immutable feature preservation).
- Outperforms baselines in plausibility (lower negative log-likelihood under p+) while being orders of magnitude faster (<0.2s median generation time).
- Local search refinement further improves sparsity and similarity to the factual instance.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1: Probabilistic Circuits for Exact Plausibility Optimization
PAR uses smooth, decomposable probabilistic circuits (SPNs) to compute exact likelihoods under the accepted-class distribution. The generator produces soft categorical distributions for mutable features; these are fed to the PC, which performs multilinear evaluation to yield log p+ and log p−. This enables exact gradient computation (via one-hot replacements in a bottom-up pass), allowing end-to-end training to maximize acceptance likelihood. The core assumption is that the PC accurately captures the data distribution after discretization. Break condition: if the PC is poorly fit (e.g., due to discretization artifacts), gradients become misleading, leading to implausible counterfactuals.

### Mechanism 2: Neighborhood Conditioning for Local Feasibility
For a denied factual x⁻, PAR retrieves K nearest neighbors from the accepted pool A using Hamming distance. Each neighbor is represented by (distance, log p+). An MLP ψ processes each neighbor vector; mean pooling followed by linear layer ρ yields a neighborhood embedding h. This h, along with immutable features and x⁻'s own log p+, conditions the generator G. The embedding personalizes recourse to the local context of x⁻. Core assumption: the local geometry is adequately captured by a small set of nearest accepted neighbors. Break condition: in sparse regions where x⁻ has few close neighbors, the embedding becomes uninformative or biased, leading to invalid or globally implausible recourses.

### Mechanism 3: Hybrid Constraint Enforcement via Masking and Soft Losses
PAR enforces hard feasibility constraints (immutability, monotonicity, causality) via deterministic logit masking: illegal changes (e.g., decreasing a monotonic feature) have their logits set to −∞ before decoding. The remaining objectives—validity, proximity, sparsity, entropy—are optimized through differentiable soft losses. Validity uses binary cross-entropy on the soft counterfactual’s classifier output. Proximity is a squared hinge loss on expected Hamming distance above a budget B. Sparsity is a cross-entropy “stay-close” term encouraging unchanged features. Entropy minimization sharpens output distributions. Core assumption: hard masking preserves differentiability and does not over-constrain; soft losses approximate discrete objectives well in expectation. Break condition: aggressive masking can leave no valid output, causing training collapse; soft losses may not guarantee discrete feasibility post-decoding.

## Foundational Learning

  - *Quick check question:* How do the smooth and decomposable properties of PCs enable exact gradient computation for soft categorical inputs?
- **Concept: Amortized Inference**
  - *Why needed here:* PAR learns a generator that maps factual instances directly to recourses, avoiding costly per-instance optimization. This amortization is key to its speed.
  - *Quick check question:* What is the trade-off between amortized inference and per-instance optimization in terms of solution quality and computational cost?
- **Concept: Algorithmic Recourse Constraints**
  - *Why needed here:* Recourse must satisfy immutability, monotonicity, causality, proximity, and sparsity. PAR encodes these as hard masks and soft losses.
  - *Quick check question:* How do hard constraints differ from soft losses in PAR’s framework, and why are both used?

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map:** 
  Fixed components: binary classifier f, class-conditional PCs p+ and p−. Trainable components: neighborhood encoder E (MLP on neighbor descriptors) and recourse generator G (MLP conditioned on immutable features, factual likelihood, and neighborhood embedding). Preprocessing: discretization module. Post-processing: local search refinement (optional).
  Discretized input x⁻ → f & p+ for validity & plausibility signals → E (from neighbor pool A) → G → masked logits → decoded x⁺ → local search (optional).
- **Critical path (inference):** 
  1. Input factual x⁻ → discretize → extract immutable features.
  2. Compute log p+(x⁻) using p+.
  3. Find K nearest neighbors in precomputed accepted pool A → compute neighbor descriptors (distance, log p+) → encode via E to get h.
  4. Concatenate [x⁻_I, log p+(x⁻), h] → G produces logits for each mutable feature.
  5. Apply hard masking for monotonicity/causality → softmax per feature → argmax to get discrete counterfactual x⁺.
  6. (Optional) Local search: if invalid, explore single-feature changes within Hamming budget; if valid, greedily revert changes while preserving validity and feasibility.
- **Design tradeoffs:** 
  Discretization enables exact PC evaluation but loses granularity. Amortization sacrifices per-instance optimality for speed. Hard masking ensures feasibility but can overly restrict the output space. Neighborhood conditioning adds personalization at the cost of nearest-neighbor search overhead.
- **Failure signatures:** 
  Low validity (<90%): generator failing to cross decision boundary. High NLL: counterfactuals in low-density regions (PC or generator issue). Constraint violations after decoding: masking bugs or soft loss mis-weighting. High runtime: nearest-neighbor search dominates if K large or pool A huge.
- **First 3 experiments:**
  1. **Sanity check:** Run PAR on a small fold of Adult with default hyperparameters; verify validity >90%, NLL comparable to train set accepted samples, and zero constraint violations.
  2. **Loss ablation:** Train PAR with individual loss terms (plausibility, validity, proximity, sparsity, entropy) disabled to observe isolated effects on validity, NLL, and sparsity (use Table 2 as reference).
  3. **Neighborhood encoder ablation:** Compare generator logits with/without E on denied instances sharing identical immutables. Expect significant logit changes, confirming the encoder’s role in personalization.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- **Open Question 1:** How can PAR accommodate dynamic changes in the set of immutable features at inference time without retraining the recourse generator? The generator’s input dimension and conditioning depend on a fixed set of immutables, requiring retraining for different specifications. A universal conditioning design (e.g., binary mask over features) could resolve this.
- **Open Question 2:** Can the amortized generator be enhanced to output recourses that already satisfy sparsity and constraint objectives, eliminating inference-time local search? Current loss components encourage but do not guarantee comparable quality; a differentiable surrogate for discrete constraints (e.g., Gumbel-Softmax, RL) would be needed.
- **Open Question 3:** Does discretization limit recourse precision and minimality, and can PAR be extended to continuous feature spaces without sacrificing tractable likelihood evaluation? Discretization may force coarse jumps. Developing PAR with continuous-leaf PCs (e.g., Gaussian mixtures) and comparing raw-unit changes would resolve this.
- **Open Question 4:** How does PC complexity (nodes, depth) impact training/inference efficiency, and can the approach scale to high-dimensional tabular data (hundreds of features)? Experiments with increasing dimensions and circuit sizes are needed to identify practical scaling limits.

## Limitations
- **Missing hyperparameters:** Exact architectures for neighborhood encoder and generator (layer sizes, activations), number of neighbors K, and precise loss weight values are not specified, hindering replication.
- **Discretization ambiguity:** Specific binning strategies for continuous features and handling of ordinal features are unclear, potentially affecting PC quality and recourse fidelity.
- **Threshold and pool details:** Classifier threshold for GMSC (Youden’s J on validation split) and size/distance metric for the neighbor pool A are not fully described.

## Confidence
- **PC-based plausibility:** Medium – relies on exact PC fit; discretization fidelity not proven.
- **Neighborhood conditioning:** Medium – may falter in sparse regions with few neighbors.
- **Hybrid constraints:** Medium–High – standard approach; break conditions manageable.
- **Overall results:** Medium – strong numbers but reproducibility gaps due to missing details.
