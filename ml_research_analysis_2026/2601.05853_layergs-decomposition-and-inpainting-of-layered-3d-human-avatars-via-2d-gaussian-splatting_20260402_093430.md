# LayerGS: Decomposition and Inpainting of Layered 3D Human Avatars via 2D Gaussian Splatting
## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2601.05853
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.05853
- Reference count: 40
- Primary result: LayerGS achieves state-of-the-art recomposition (SSIM 0.9873, PSNR 35.40) and inpainting metrics (CLIP, ImageReward) on 4D-Dress and Thuman2.0, outperforming GALA and VTON360.

## Executive Summary
LayerGS addresses the challenge of decomposing a clothed 3D human into animatable body and garment layers from multi-view images. The method represents each layer with 2D Gaussian Splatting (2DGS), which provides accurate surface geometry and sharp edges while avoiding the computational cost of volumetric rendering. To fill in regions occluded in the input views, a pretrained diffusion model is employed via Score Distillation Sampling (SDS) to inpaint missing details. The system is trained through a three-stage pipeline: whole-avatar reconstruction, inner body optimization, and outer garment refinement. Extensive evaluation on 4D-Dress and Thuman2.0 shows significant improvements over prior methods in both recomposition quality (SSIM 0.9873 vs 0.9801, PSNR 35.40 vs 34.60) and inpainting quality. The framework enables realistic virtual try‑on and garment swapping without requiring a 3D scan, opening new possibilities for AR/VR and e‑commerce applications.

## Method Summary
The pipeline takes multi-view RGB images, 2D segmentation masks, and SMPL(-X) shape and pose parameters. In Stage 1, a single-layer canonical avatar is built with 2DGS, trained with photometric (L1 + D-SSIM), depth distortion, normal consistency, and SDS losses. A coarse garment mesh is extracted via TSDF fusion of depth maps and 2D segmentation voting. Stage 2 freezes the outer layer and optimizes an inner body layer, using masked RGB loss, segmentation loss, split depth loss (distinguishing seen vs. occluded regions via a dummy SMPL(-X) layer), and SDS with a body‑specific prompt. Stage 3 freezes the inner layer and refines the outer layer using masked RGB, segmentation, depth (both composed and dummy‑guided), normal, and SDS losses, alongside a scaling constraint on Gaussian sizes during densification to prevent artifacts. After training, Gaussians are attached to the extracted canonical meshes, and linear blend skinning deforms them for novel poses, with optional Laplacian smoothing to resolve penetration.

## Key Results
- Recomposition quality: SSIM 0.9873 (vs GALA 0.9801), PSNR 35.40 dB (vs GALA 34.60).
- Inpainting: Higher CLIP and ImageReward scores than prior diffusion‑based approaches.
- Virtual try‑on: DINO similarity 0.506, surpassing VTON360’s 0.455.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1
- **Claim:** Encoding each layer (body, garment) as a set of *2D* Gaussian splats enables accurate surface geometry and efficient photorealistic rendering, avoiding volume rendering costs and better aligning with observed image-space evidence.
- **Mechanism:** 2D Gaussians are defined by a center position, 2D covariance (plane-oriented), opacity, and color. For a given camera view, splats are projected, sorted by depth, and alpha-blended. The splat's covariance is designed to be tangent to an underlying surface, which is enforced via normal consistency loss and depth distortion loss during training. This explicit surface-aligned representation yields sharper edges and more accurate silhouette reconstruction compared to volumetric NeRF-style representations.
- **Core assumption:** The human subject's clothing and body can be effectively represented as a set of opaque, surface-aligned planar elements under the static training pose. Splat densification and pruning adapt to the observed image gradients.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract] "encoding each layer as a set of 2D Gaussians for accurate geometry and photorealistic rendering"
  - [section 3.1] Details initialization from SMPL mesh surface and application of depth distortion loss & normal consistency loss to enforce surface geometry.
  - [corpus] Neighbor papers (GGAvatar, HFGaussian) also investigate Gaussian splatting for humans, but focus on single-layer or implicit deformations; LayerGS specifically adapts 2DGS for *explicit layer decomposition*.
- **Break condition:** If the garment exhibits extreme non-surface behavior (e.g., heavy folds, loose fabric that billows away from the body), the 2D planar assumption may fail, causing splats to overlap incorrectly or fail to capture volume.

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim:** A pretrained 2D diffusion model, via Score Distillation Sampling (SDS), provides a powerful, generalizable prior to inpaint *occluded* regions of each canonical layer, compensating for missing multi-view data without requiring 3D ground truth for those regions.
- **Mechanism:** For a given training view, the rendered image (or composition of layers) is perturbed with noise and passed to the diffusion model's denoising U-Net, conditioned on a text prompt (e.g., "a photo of a man wearing a t-shirt"). The model's prediction of the noise residual is used to compute a gradient ∇L_SDS that updates the Gaussian parameters (position, color, etc.) of the layer(s) responsible for the occluded region. This pushes the renderer's output towards the distribution modeled by the diffusion prior.
- **Core assumption:** The frozen 2D diffusion model has learned a sufficiently accurate and 3D-consistent prior of clothed human appearance from its 2D training data. The SDS gradient can be backpropagated through the differentiable 2DGS renderer to meaningfully alter the 3D scene parameters.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract] "inpainting hidden regions with a pretrained 2D diffusion model via score-distillation sampling (SDS)"
  - [section 3.1-3.3] The SDS loss is applied in all three stages, with prompts tailored per stage (e.g., whole subject vs. inner layers). The gradient formula (Eq. 4, 8) is provided.
  - [corpus] GALA and VTON360 also use diffusion inpainting, but GALA relies on a 3D scan for initialization and uses DMTet, while VTON360 performs inpainting *after* 3D reconstruction. LayerGS integrates SDS *during* 2DGS optimization.
- **Break condition:** If the text prompt is poorly matched to the subject/garment, or if the diffusion model lacks diversity for a specific garment style (e.g., intricate patterns), SDS can produce blurry, inconsistent, or semantically incorrect inpainting that degrades layer quality.

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim:** The three-stage training pipeline (whole → inner → outer) stabilizes layer decomposition by using the outer garment as a "mask" to constrain the optimization of the inner body, preventing inter-layer collisions and ensuring the inner layer respects garment boundaries.
- **Mechanism:** Stage 1 trains a coarse single-layer avatar and extracts a garment mesh. Stage 2 freezes the outer layer's Gaussians and optimizes the inner layer, using a *dummy* SMPL mesh rendered alongside to provide depth guidance for occluded body parts. A segmentation loss explicitly enforces that the inner layer's rendering does not appear in the garment mask region. Stage 3 freezes the inner layer and refines the outer layer, using a dummy outer mesh and a scaling constraint on Gaussian sizes to prevent large splats that cause artifacts under deformation.
- **Core assumption:** The outer garment mesh extracted in Stage 1 is a sufficiently accurate and watertight proxy for the garment's volume. The SMPL model is a reasonable prior for the inner body shape. The segmentation masks are accurate.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract] "trained in three stages: single-layer reconstruction, inner layer optimization, and outer layer refinement."
  - [section 3.2] Details Stage 2: freezing outer layer, dummy SMPL guidance for occluded depth, segmentation loss M⊙(S-S_gt).
  - [section 3.3] Details Stage 3: freezing inner layer, dummy outer mesh guidance, scaling constraint (threshold 0.01) to control Gaussian size.
  - [corpus] Ablation study (Fig. 6, Table 4) shows Stage 3 refinement improves CLIP/IR scores and removing dummy guidance increases mesh error. This staged, masked approach is unique compared to GALA's joint optimization.
- **Break condition:** If Stage 1's garment extraction fails (e.g., mesh has holes, incorrect segmentation), Stage 2's constraints become incorrect, leading to inner body "leaking" into garment regions or vice versa. Also, severe self-occlusion in garments (e.g., crossed arms with long sleeves) may confuse the segmentation-based depth guidance.

## Foundational Learning
- **Concept: 2D Gaussian Splatting (2DGS)**
  - **Why needed here:** It is the core 3D representation. Unlike 3D Gaussians, 2DGS explicitly orients Gaussians to be tangent to a surface, enforced via normal consistency, which is critical for sharp, mesh-like rendering of humans. It must be understood how rendering, densification, and surface regularization work.
  - **Quick check question:** How does the normal consistency loss in Eq. (3) ensure that neighboring splats lie on the same surface plane, and why is this more suitable for human avatars than volumetric density?
- **Concept: Score Distillation Sampling (SDS)**
  - **Why needed here:** It is the generative inpainting engine. The engineer must grasp how a 2D diffusion model's gradients can be used to optimize a 3D parameter set (Gaussian attributes) without 3D training data. Key is the reparameterization trick and the noise schedule.
  - **Quick check question:** In the SDS gradient Eq. (4), what roles do the text embedding *y_whole*, the noise schedule *w_t*, and the predicted noise *ϵ_ϕ* play in guiding the Gaussian parameters toward a text-conditioned appearance?
- **Concept: Linear Blend Skinning (LBS) & Mesh Attachment**
  - **Why needed here:** For animation/virtual try-on. After training canonical 2DGS layers, each Gaussian is attached to the nearest triangle on the extracted canonical mesh. LBS then deforms the mesh (and attached Gaussians) using SMPL pose parameters. Understanding this pipeline is essential for applying the trained model to novel poses.
  - **Quick check question:** Why are 2D Gaussians attached to a mesh *after* training, rather than optimizing their positions directly in posed space? What artifact does this prevent during novel pose rendering?

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map:**
  1. **Input Processor:** COLMAP for camera calibration, segmentation model for masks, SMPL(-X) fitting for pose/shape parameters.
  2. **2DGS Renderer:** Projects, sorts, and alpha-composites 2D Gaussians per camera.
  3. **Stage 1 Optimizer:** Single-layer 2DGS (G_whole) with L_rgb, L_d, L_n, L_SDS.
  4. **Mesh Extractor:** TSDF fusion from depth maps → canonical mesh → segmentation-based garment mesh extraction (distance threshold α=0.015).
  5. **Stage 2 Optimizer:** Inner layer G_body + frozen G_garment. Uses L_seg, L_d (split into seen/occluded via dummy SMPL), L_SDS (body prompt).
  6. **Stage 3 Optimizer:** Outer layer G_garment_refined + frozen G_body. Uses L_rgb(masked), L_seg, L_d (composition & dummy-guided), L_SDS (garment prompt), scaling constraint on densification.
  7. **Try-on Pipeline:** Attaches Gaussians to extracted meshes → LBS deformation → optional Laplacian smoothing for penetration resolution.
- **Critical path:** Preprocessing (cameras, masks, SMPL) → Stage 1 Training (whole avatar) → Mesh Extraction (garment segmentation) → Stage 2 Training (inner layer) → Stage 3 Training (outer refinement) → Mesh Attachment & LBS Setup.
- **Design tradeoffs:**
  - *2DGS vs. 3DGS:* 2DGS offers better surface alignment and silhouette quality for static/human surfaces but assumes thin, planar elements. 3DGS might better handle volumetric effects like hair but is less precise for sharp edges.
  - *SDS Guidance Timing:* Applying SDS in all stages improves inpainting but risks overfitting to the diffusion prior's style and introduces computational cost. The paper weights it with image losses.
  - *Staged vs. Joint Training:* Staged training (freezing layers) prevents optimization interference and simplifies constraints but may lock in suboptimal Stage 1 artifacts that Stage 3 must refine.
- **Failure signatures:**
  - **Mesh extraction failure:** Sparse or noisy Gaussians yield a non-manifold or fragmented TSDF mesh. Symptom: holes in canonical mesh.
  - **Segmentation loss failure:** inaccurate input masks cause the inner layer to "bleed" into garment region or vice versa. Symptom: floaters, wrong body parts in garment layer.
  - **SDS inconsistency:** Prompts not descriptive enough or diffusion model bias leads to blurry, non-3D-consistent textures in occluded areas. Symptom: smudged inpainted regions that change with viewpoint.
  - **Virtual try-on penetration:** Garment mesh significantly smaller than body mesh, or scaling constraint not applied. Symptom: body mesh visibly protrudes through garment.
- **First 3 experiments:**
  1. **Stage 1 Reconstruction-only Run:** Train only with L_rgb, L_d, L_n (no SDS). Compare qualitative output to full Stage 1. Expected: Occluded regions (under arms) remain empty/artifacts, confirming SDS's role in inpainting.
  2. **Ablation: Dummy Guidance in Stage 2:** Train Stage 2 with and without the dummy SMPL mesh providing occluded depth. Render inner layer mesh and compute point-to-mesh distance to ground-truth SMPL. Expected: Without dummy, inner layer mesh inflates/errs in occluded regions (e.g., torso under arms), per paper's heatmap.
  3. **Scaling Constraint Test:** After Stage 3, simulate an extreme pose (arm raised high) and render. Compare with/without the splitting/clipping scaling threshold (0.01). Expected: Without constraint, large Gaussians on the garment cause stretching artifacts ("tearing") at high-curvature regions.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- How can LayerGS be adapted for single-image input rather than multi-view? The current pipeline requires multi-view images for geometric reconstruction; single image introduces ambiguity in occluded regions.
- How to integrate physics‑based simulation with 2D Gaussian Splatting? The current deformation (LBS/Laplacian) ignores cloth stiffness, gravity, and collisions.
- How can LayerGS handle loose or self‑intersecting garments? Decomposition may fail due to dependence on SMPL(-X) linear blend skinning which assumes garment follows body joints.
- How to eliminate penetration artifacts during virtual try‑on for large body‑garment size mismatches? Existing Laplacian deformation is insufficient for large penetrations.

## Limitations
- Exact loss weight coefficients are not provided, hindering precise replication of the training balance.
- SDS implementation specifics (timestep schedule, guidance scale, ControlNet conditioning) are underspecified, affecting reproducibility and performance.
- Mesh extraction parameters (TSDF resolution, segmentation threshold) lack sensitivity analysis, potentially reducing robustness across diverse subjects.

## Confidence
- 2DGS representation quality: Medium
- SDS inpainting effectiveness: Medium
- Staged training stability: High

## Next Checks
1. **SDS impact isolation:** Train Stage 1 without SDS (only L_rgb, L_d, L_n). Verify that occluded regions (e.g., underarms) remain incomplete or contain artifacts, highlighting SDS’s inpainting contribution.
2. **Dummy guidance ablation:** In Stage 2, disable the dummy SMPL(-X) depth conditioning. After training, extract the inner layer mesh and compute point‑to‑mesh distance to ground‑truth SMPL. Expect significantly higher error in typical occlusion zones (torso under arms).
3. **Scaling constraint test:** After Stage 3, render the model under an extreme pose (e.g., arm raised vertically). Compare results with and without the Gaussian scaling threshold (0.01). Without the constraint, large Gaussians on the outer layer should cause stretching or tearing artifacts, especially around high‑curvature seams.
