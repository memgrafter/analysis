# Preserving Continuous Symmetry in Discrete Spaces: Geometric-Aware Quantization for SO(3)-Equivariant GNNs

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2603.05343  
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.05343  
- **Reference count:** 32  
- **Primary result:** W4A8 quantization achieves energy MAE 9.31 meV, reduces Local Equivariance Error by over 30× to 0.15 meV/Å, enables stable nanosecond-scale NVE simulations, and yields 2.39× speedup with 4× memory reduction on consumer hardware.

## Executive Summary
Quantizing SO(3)-equivariant graph neural networks (GNNs) for molecular simulations with naive low-bit methods breaks rotational symmetry, leading to conservation law violations and inaccurate forces. This paper introduces Geometric-Aware Quantization (GAQ) to preserve equivariance under aggressive 4-bit weight / 8-bit activation quantization. GAQ comprises three innovations: (1) Magnitude-Direction Decoupled Quantization (MDDQ), which separately quantizes a vector’s invariant length and its equivariant unit direction on the sphere; (2) symmetry-aware training with distinct quantization schedules for invariant scalar and equivariant vector branches, including a warm‑up period that stabilizes manifold optimization; and (3) robust attention normalization via L2‑normalizing queries and keys, bounding logits and reducing sensitivity to low‑bit noise. Evaluated on the rMD17 Azobenzene benchmark, GAQ W4A8 models surpass FP32 baselines (energy MAE 9.31 vs 23.20 meV), reduce the Local Equivariance Error by 30×, and sustain stable nanosecond‑scale NVE molecular dynamics with negligible energy drift. The framework also delivers a 2.39× inference speedup and 4× memory reduction on consumer hardware.

## Method Summary
GAQ integrates quantization directly into SO(3)-equivariant GNNs while maintaining rotational symmetry. Its core is MDDQ: decompose a vector **v** into magnitude *m* = ‖**v**‖₂ (invariant) and unit direction *u* = **v**/‖**v**‖₂ (equivariant point on S²). A standard linear quantizer handles *m*; a spherical codebook quantizer handles *u*. The overall quantizer is *Q*(**v**) = *Q*ₘ(*m*)·*Q*ᵣ(*u*). To train the non‑differentiable direction quantizer, the authors use a geometric straight‑through estimator (STE) that projects Euclidean gradients onto the tangent space of S², ensuring updates stay on the manifold. GAQ also adopts branch‑separated quantization‑aware training: invariant (ℓ=0) and equivariant (ℓ=1) branches employ different quantizers and schedules, with the equivariant branch’s quantization frozen during an initial warm‑up to allow the scalar branch to establish a coarse representation before the vector branch enters the difficult non‑convex optimization. Finally, the attention mechanism is stabilized by L2‑normalizing queries and keys (derived from invariant features) before dot‑product, scaling by a temperature τ to bound logits and mitigate outlier effects in low precision. A regularization term on the Local Equivariance Error (LEE) further encourages symmetry preservation.

## Key Results
- Energy MAE: **9.31 meV** (W4A8) vs **23.20 meV** (FP32) on rMD17.
- **Local Equivariance Error reduced by >30×** to **0.15 meV/Å**.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1: Magnitude-Direction Decoupled Quantization (MDDQ)
- **Claim:** Separating the quantization of a vector's invariant magnitude from its equivariant unit direction on S² preserves SO(3)-equivariant structure under low‑bit discretization.
- **Mechanism:** Decompose **v** → *m* = ‖**v**‖₂ (scalar) and *u* = **v**/‖**v**‖₂ (point on S²). Apply a linear quantizer *Q*ₘ to *m* and a spherical codebook quantizer *Q*ᵣ to *u*. Overall: *Q*(**v**) = *Q*ₘ(*m*)·*Q*ᵣ(*u*). The directional quantizer minimizes rotation‑induced discretization error by operating on a discrete manifold.
- **Core assumption:** Rotations mainly affect *u*; bounding quantization error on S² via a codebook with small covering radius keeps cumulative equivariance error bounded.
- **Evidence anchors:**  
  [abstract] "Magnitude–Direction Decoupled Quantization (MDDQ) scheme that separates invariant lengths from equivariant orientations to maintain geometric fidelity."  
  [section III.C] Formal definition (Eq. 2), analysis of approximate equivariance via commutation error ε_d(R,u) (Eq. 4) and angular bound (Prop. 3.4).  
  [corpus] Evidence weak: neighboring work on quantized SO(3)‑equivariant GNNs likely uses different approaches; direct comparison missing.
- **Break condition:** If the codebook covering radius δ_d is too large relative to model sensitivity, rotational error ε_d exceeds tolerance, causing high LEE and instability.

### Mechanism 2: Symmetry‑Aware Branch‑Separated Quantization‑Aware Training (QAT)
- **Claim:** Treating invariant scalar channels and equivariant vector channels with distinct quantization parameters and schedules prevents disproportionate accuracy loss in the equivariant branch.
- **Mechanism:** Split features into invariant (ℓ=0) and equivariant (ℓ=1). Invariant branch uses standard linear quantizer; equivariant branch uses MDDQ with geometric STE. A staged warm‑up freezes the equivariant branch’s quantization initially, letting the scalar branch learn a coarse geometric representation before the vector branch undergoes manifold optimization.
- **Core assumption:** Loss landscapes and gradient statistics differ fundamentally between scalars and Chi‑distributed magnitudes; uniform quantization drowns the equivariant learning signal. Branch separation tailors optimization to each geometry.
- **Evidence anchors:**  
  [abstract] "a symmetry‑aware training strategy that treats scalar and vector features with distinct quantization schedules."  
  [section III.D] Description of dual quantizers, staged warm‑up ("freeze the quantization of the equivariant branch for the first N_warm epochs"), and rationale based on heterogeneous optimization landscapes.  
  [corpus] Evidence missing: no prior work explicitly addresses branch‑separated QAT for equivariant/non‑equivariant streams.
- **Break condition:** Warm‑up too short → poor gradients for directional features, divergence or high LEE; warm‑up too long → invariant branch overfits before equivariant updates, harming final accuracy.

### Mechanism 3: Robust Attention Normalization via L2‑Normalized Queries/Keys
- **Claim:** L2‑normalizing attention queries and keys before dot‑product stabilizes low‑precision attention by bounding sensitivity to quantization noise and preventing outlier dominance.
- **Mechanism:** In the invariant attention module, normalize **q**ᵢ ← **q**ᵢ/‖**q**ᵢ‖₂ and **k**ⱼ ← **k**ⱼ/‖**k**ⱼ‖₂. Attention logits become cosine similarity **˜q**ᵢ^⊤**˜k**ⱼ scaled by temperature τ>1, confining values to [−τ, τ] regardless of original magnitudes, thus reducing softmax instability.
- **Core assumption:** The raw dot‑product is highly sensitive to low‑bit rounding when norms or outliers are large; normalization makes attention depend only on relative orientation, less affected by precision loss.
- **Evidence anchors:**  
  [abstract] "robust attention normalization mechanism to stabilize gradients in low‑bit regimes."  
  [section III.E] Detailed procedure, rationale ("prevents any single large magnitude from dominating the softmax"), and relation to prior cosine‑normalized attention [14,23].  
  [corpus] Partial: [23] supports cosine normalization generally, but its application to equivariant transformers and quantization stabilization is novel.
- **Break condition:** Poor τ choice (too low → overly smooth, too high → sharp/unstable) or asymmetric normalization breaks the effect. Method assumes queries/keys derive from invariant features; applying to equivariant messages directly would break equivariance.

## Foundational Learning

**SO(3) Group Representations & Irreducible Representations (irreps)**  
*Why needed:* MDDQ operates on ℓ=1 irreps (vectors). Understanding that vectors transform as **v** → R**v** under rotation explains why Cartesian quantization is destructive and spherical manifold quantization is appropriate.  
*Quick check:* If a vector **v** is rotated by R, how does its unit direction *u* transform, and why is preserving this transformation critical for force prediction equivariance?

**Quantization‑Aware Training (QAT) and Straight‑Through Estimator (STE)**  
*Why needed:* GAQ is a form of QAT. The geometric STE is a direct modification of the standard STE; without understanding STE’s role in approximating gradients through non‑differentiable quantizers, the tangent‑space projection is obscure.  
*Quick check:* Why does the standard STE ∂L/∂*u* ≈ ∂L/∂*q* cause gradient noise for features constrained to S²?

**Riemannian Manifold Optimization on S²**  
*Why needed:* The equivariant direction *u* lies on S². The geometric STE implements a Riemannian gradient by projecting Euclidean gradients onto the tangent space, ensuring updates remain on the manifold (required because *Q*ᵣ only operates on S² points).  
*Quick check:* What is the geometric meaning of the projection matrix I − **u** **u**^⊤ in Eq. 8, and why is it necessary for training MDDQ?

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component map:** Input Encoder: Atom types/charges → invariant scalar features *h*ᵢ⁽⁰⁾; Coordinates → relative vectors **r**ᵢⱼ → spherical harmonics *Y*₁ₘ(ˆ**r**ᵢⱼ). Dual Branch Network: Each layer has parallel paths – (a) invariant branch: scalar features via linear layers & attention; (b) equivariant branch: vector features updated via equivariant message passing (e.g., Clebsch‑Gordan tensor products) modulated by attention weights from invariant branch. Quantization Modules: Inserted after key linear projections/activations. Invariant branch: standard linear quantizer (W4/A8). Equivariant branch: MDDQ (Algorithm 1) with Geometric STE. Attention Module: invariant‑only (queries/keys from invariant branch), uses L2‑normalization + temperature τ, outputs weights to mix equivariant value vectors. Regularization & Loss: main energy/force MSE + LEE regularization (Eq. 1) on force outputs.

**Critical path:** Input → scalar/vector branching → invariant attention (with normalized Q/K) → compute attention weights → apply weights to equivariant messages → MDDQ on vector outputs → repeat layers → final heads (energy scalar, force vectors). *Key dependency:* attention weights must be computed from invariant features to maintain overall equivariance; the equivariant branch receives those weights but does not feed back into the attention scores.

**Design tradeoffs**
- **MDDQ codebook size vs. equivariance:** Smaller codebook saves memory but increases covering radius δ_d → higher LEE; larger codebook improves fidelity but erodes memory savings.
- **Warm‑up length vs. convergence:** Longer freeze of equivariant quantization stabilizes manifold initialization but slows overall QAT convergence.
- **Attention normalization τ:** Higher τ sharpens attention (good for performance) but sensitizes to low‑bit noise; lower τ stabilizes but may underfit. Must be tuned per bit‑width.

**Failure signatures**
1. **Energy drift in NVE simulation:** >0.1 meV/atom/ps drift indicates non‑conservative forces, often from high LEE (>1 meV/Å) due to broken equivariance (check LEE metric).
2. **Diverging training loss:** Typically caused by applying hard vector quantization (e.g., SVQ‑KMeans) without STE, or by too aggressive quantization on an un‑warmed equivariant branch.
3. **Catastrophic accuracy drop vs. FP32:** >2× MAE increase suggests naive quantization (cf. Naive INT8). If only force MAE degrades while energy MAE remains stable, suspect the equivariant branch.

**First 3 experiments**
1. **Equivariance stress test:** Pass a rotated validation batch through the quantized model. Compute LEE (Eq. 1). Target: LEE < 0.5 meV/Å. If higher, inspect MDDQ codebook coverage and warm‑up schedule.
2. **Short NVE stability:** Run 10 ps NVE simulation with 0.5 fs timestep. Monitor total energy. Target: drift < 0.05 meV/atom/ps. If drift occurs, check attention normalization (ablate τ) and LEE regularization strength.
3. **Branch sensitivity ablation:** Train variants: (a) quantize both branches with same scheme; (b) no warm‑up; (c) no LEE regularization. Compare against full GAQ to isolate each component’s contribution.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
1. **Extending MDDQ to higher‑order irreps (ℓ ≥ 2):** The conclusion mentions this as future work but provides no methodology. Extending spherical codebooks and geometric constraints beyond vectors is non‑trivial. *Resolution evidence:* a working quantization scheme for ℓ ≥ 2 with demonstrated equivariance on rMD17/NequIP‑style models.
2. **Theoretical link between LEE and long‑term energy conservation:** The paper links naive quantization to drift and uses LEE as a symmetry metric, but lacks a formal bound connecting per‑layer LEE to drift over MD timescales. *Resolution evidence:* an upper bound on total energy drift as a function of per‑layer LEE, validated by varying quantization errors.
3. **Generalization across molecular datasets:** Evaluation focuses on Azobenzene (rMD17); QM9 results are absent. Success on one molecule does not guarantee robustness across chemical space. *Resolution evidence:* comprehensive benchmarks on QM9, other MD17 molecules, and periodic systems (e.g., OC20) showing a single GAQ configuration maintains low LEE and accuracy.
4. **Automating branch‑separated QAT schedules:** The warm‑up period is hand‑tuned (freeze equivariant branch for N_warm epochs). Optimal timing likely varies with architecture and data. *Resolution evidence:* a schedule‑agnostic or learned strategy (e.g., triggered by validation LEE) matching hand‑tuned performance.
5. **Interaction of L2‑normalized attention with equivariance:** Normalizing queries/keys discards magnitude information, which could alter the equivariance of the overall attention‑augmented message passing. The paper does not prove that the normalized attention retains an equivariance property. *Resolution evidence:* a formal proof that L2‑normalized attention combined with the equivariant message function satisfies approximate equivariance, supported by empirical LEE measurements across attention layers.

## Limitations
- **Single‑molecule evaluation:** Results are reported only on Azobenzene from rMD17; generalizability to other molecules or materials is unproven.
- **Hyperparameter nondisclosure:** Key training details (learning rates, quantization ranges, LEE weight, temperature τ) are not provided, hindering exact replication.
- **Missing codebook specifications:** Spherical codebook size, initialization method, and covering radius are unspecified, leaving the trade‑off between equivariance and memory unclear.

## Confidence
- **Accuracy & stability:** Medium – improvements are striking but limited to one dataset; potential overfitting or implementation bias exists.
- **Efficiency gains:** Low – speedup numbers depend on unspecified hardware/software conditions; typical quantization speedups are more modest.
- **Mechanism validity:** Medium – theoretical analysis and proof‑of‑concept experiments are present, but ablations and broader validation are lacking.

## Next Checks
