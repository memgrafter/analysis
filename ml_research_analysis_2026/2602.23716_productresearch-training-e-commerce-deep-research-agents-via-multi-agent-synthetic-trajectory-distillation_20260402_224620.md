# ProductResearch: Training E-Commerce Deep Research Agents via Multi-Agent Synthetic Trajectory Distillation

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2602.23716
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.23716
- **Reference count:** 40
- **Primary result:** 30B MoE model fine-tuned on synthetic multi-agent trajectories improves RACE score from 31.78 to 45.40 and triples effective product coverage from 3.58 to 12.45.

## Executive Summary
E-commerce agents often fail at deep, multi-step product research due to limited training data and simplistic paradigms. This paper introduces a multi-agent framework (User, Research, Supervisor) that generates synthetic long-horizon trajectories with iterative supervision, then distills them via reflective internalization into single-role training examples. Fine-tuning a 30B MoE model on this data significantly improves performance on the RACE benchmark and product coverage metrics, surpassing ReAct baselines and approaching Gemini-DeepResearch.

## Method Summary
The authors build a three-agent system: a User agent formulates queries, a Research agent uses tools (search, comparison, etc.) to gather information, and a Supervisor agent iteratively critiques and refines the Research agent's trajectory. This produces diverse, long-horizon tool-use trajectories. These are processed through "reflective internalization," where multi-agent interactions are distilled into coherent single-role examples for supervised fine-tuning. The resulting dataset trains a 30B Mixture-of-Experts model specifically for e-commerce deep research.

## Key Results
- RACE score improved from 31.78 to 45.40 after fine-tuning.
- Effective product coverage tripled from 3.58 to 12.45.
- Outperforms all ReAct baselines in evaluation.
- Performance rivals Gemini-DeepResearch despite smaller model size.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The multi-agent framework mimics real-world research processes by separating query formulation, execution, and oversight, leading to more thorough and realistic trajectories. Reflective internalization ensures distilled training examples retain the depth and coherence of multi-agent collaboration while being suitable for single-agent training. This combination addresses the lack of long-horizon, tool-rich data in e-commerce, enabling models to learn complex research strategies.

## Foundational Learning
- **Synthetic Trajectory Generation**: Creates diverse training data where real data is scarce. *Why needed*: E-commerce research trajectories are expensive to collect. *Quick check*: Do synthetic trajectories cover a wider range of product categories than human-collected ones?
- **Multi-Agent Collaboration**: Simulates different roles (user, researcher, supervisor) to produce nuanced interactions. *Why needed*: Single-agent systems lack checks and balances, leading to shallow research. *Quick check*: Does adding a supervisor agent reduce hallucination in tool use?
- **Reflective Internalization**: Distills multi-agent dialogues into single-role examples without losing depth. *Why needed*: Directly training on multi-agent logs is ineffective for single-agent deployment. *Quick check*: Does internalization preserve the iterative refinement present in original trajectories?
- **Tool-Use Planning**: Emphasizes long-horizon sequences of tool calls. *Why needed*: E-commerce research requires multiple steps (search, compare, verify). *Quick check*: Are the generated trajectories longer and more tool-diverse than ReAct baselines?
- **Domain-Specific Fine-Tuning**: Adapts general models to e-commerce nuances. *Why needed*: Off-the-shelf models lack product-specific reasoning. *Quick check*: Does fine-tuning on synthetic data improve performance on e-commerce benchmarks but not on general QA tasks?

## Architecture Onboarding
Component map: User Agent -> Research Agent -> Supervisor Agent -> Synthetic Trajectories -> Reflective Internalization -> Training Examples -> Fine-tuned 30B MoE Model  
Critical path: Query from User Agent triggers Research Agent's tool-use; Supervisor iteratively critiques; multiple trajectories are distilled into training data; model fine-tuned on examples.  
Design tradeoffs: Synthetic data scale vs. fidelity (more trajectories may introduce noise); multi-agent complexity (three agents increase computational cost but improve depth) vs. deployment simplicity (single-agent final model); distillation aggressiveness (simplification may lose nuance) vs. training efficiency.  
Failure signatures: Research agent making irrelevant tool calls; Supervisor failing to correct errors; distilled examples lacking coherence; fine-tuned model not generalizing beyond seen product categories.  
First three experiments: 1) Ablate the Supervisor agent to measure its impact on trajectory quality and final model performance. 2) Compare different distillation strategies (e.g., summarization vs. direct transformation) on downstream RACE scores. 3) Evaluate the fine-tuned model on a held-out set of product domains to test generalization.

## Limitations
- Synthetic data may not capture real user intent complexity or rare edge cases.
- Benchmark metrics (RACE, product coverage) may not fully measure research depth (e.g., reasoning quality, source reliability).
- Performance gains might be domain-specific to the product categories and tools used in synthesis, with limited generalization.

## Confidence
- RACE/product coverage improvement (31.78→45.40; 3.58→12.45): High  
- Outperforming ReAct baselines: Medium  
- Rivaling Gemini-DeepResearch: Low  
- Synthetic distillation efficacy: Medium  

## Next Checks
1. Human-in-the-loop validation: Rate 100 synthetic vs. human expert trajectories for coherence, tool-use appropriateness, and thoroughness.
2. Cross-benchmark testing: Evaluate on ShoppingBench and DeepShop benchmarks to test domain generalization.
3. Direct replication: Re-implement pipeline with open-source models (e.g., Llama-3.1-70B) to verify gains without proprietary advantages.
