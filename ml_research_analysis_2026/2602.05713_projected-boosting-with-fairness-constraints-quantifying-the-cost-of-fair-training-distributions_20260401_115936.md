# Projected Boosting with Fairness Constraints: Quantifying the Cost of Fair Training Distributions

## Quick Facts

- **arXiv ID:** 2602.05713  
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.05713  
- **Reference count:** 12  
- **Primary result:** FAIRPROJ integrates fairness constraints into boosting via distribution projection, achieving reduced group unfairness with a theoretically bounded convergence cost.

## Executive Summary

This paper addresses the challenge of incorporating group fairness (e.g., equal opportunity, demographic parity) into boosting algorithms while preserving convergence guarantees. The proposed method, FAIRPROJ, modifies AdaBoost by projecting the exponentially-weighted distribution induced by the current ensemble onto a convex set of fair distributions defined by linear constraints (e.g., mass-balanced equal opportunity). Weak learners are trained on this projected fair distribution, but the boosting coefficients are computed using the original (unprojected) distribution to maintain the exponential loss recursion.

Experiments on UCI Adult (sex) and German Credit datasets show FAIRPROJ drastically reduces fairness gaps (e.g., equal opportunity gap on Adult drops from 0.185 to 0.029 with ε=0.10) at a moderate accuracy cost (∼6%). The theoretical bound—exponential loss ≤ n exp(−2 Σ_t (γ(w_t) − δ_t)^2)—validates a tradeoff: tighter fairness constraints increase the KL-based penalty δ_t, causing earlier stopping and lower final accuracy. Empirical results confirm the edge-transfer bound and monotonic loss decrease, demonstrating that fairness can be bought at a quantifiable convergence cost.

## Method Summary

FAIRPROJ is an AdaBoost-style algorithm that enforces fairness as a reweighting surrogate. Each iteration t proceeds as: (1) Compute the standard AdaBoost distribution \(q_t \propto \exp(-y_i f_{t-1}(x_i))\). (2) Project \(q_t\) onto the convex set \(C_\epsilon\) of distributions satisfying group fairness constraints (e.g., \(|\langle w, g_{\text{EOpp}} \rangle| \le \epsilon\)) by solving a KL minimization problem in the dual: minimize over \(\lambda\) \(\log(\sum q_i \exp(-\lambda^\top g(i))) + \epsilon\|\lambda\|_1\). The projected distribution is \(w_t(i) = q_i \exp(-\lambda^\top g(i))/Z(\lambda)\). (3) Train a weak learner \(h_t\) (decision stump) on the weighted dataset according to \(w_t\). (4) Evaluate the weak learner’s weighted error \(\epsilon_t^{(q)} = \sum q_i \mathbf{1}(y_i \ne h_t(x_i))\). If \(\epsilon_t^{(q)} \ge 0.5\), stop. (5) Compute the boosting coefficient \(\alpha_t = \frac{1}{2} \ln\left(\frac{1-\epsilon_t^{(q)}}{\epsilon_t^{(q)}}\right)\). (6) Update the ensemble \(f_t = f_{t-1} + \alpha_t h_t\). The algorithm optionally warm-starts \(\lambda\) for efficiency.

The theoretical analysis shows that projecting the distribution reduces the effective edge of the weak learner under \(q_t\) by at most \(\delta_t = \sqrt{\text{KL}(w_t\|q_t)/2}\), leading to an exponential loss bound \(L_{\text{exp}}(f_T) \le n \exp\left(-2 \sum_{t=1}^T (\gamma(w_t) - \delta_t)^2\right)\) provided \(\gamma(w_t) > \delta_t\) each round. This bound makes explicit the tradeoff between fairness (larger \(\epsilon\) → smaller projection → smaller \(\delta_t\)) and convergence (effective edge drives loss reduction). Empirically, the bound holds and the exponential loss decreases monotonically.

## Key Results

- FAIRPROJ (ε=0.10) reduces equal opportunity gap by 84% on Adult (0.185 → 0.029) at a cost of ~6% accuracy (0.854 → 0.804) compared to AdaBoost; similar improvements are observed on German Credit.
- Tighter fairness constraints increase the per‑round KL‑penalty δ_t, leading to earlier stopping and lower final accuracy, confirming the theoretical trade‑off between fairness and convergence.
- Empirical validation shows the edge‑transfer bound γ(q_t) ≥ γ(w_t) − δ_t holds across iterations and the exponential loss decreases monotonically.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1: Edge Transfer via Distribution Projection
- **Claim:** Projecting the ensemble-induced distribution \(q_t\) onto a convex set of fair distributions each round, trains weak learners on this fair distribution, but computes boosting coefficients using the original distribution. The effective weak learner edge is reduced by a quantity \(\delta_t = \sqrt{\text{KL}(w_t \| q_t)/2}\).
- **Mechanism:** The KL divergence between \(w_t\) (fair training distribution) and \(q_t\) (natural AdaBoost distribution) bounds the total variation distance, which in turn bounds the difference in edge (expected margin) under the two distributions. This formalizes the cost of fairness as a divergence penalty.
- **Core assumption:** The weak learner's performance (edge) is measured on the distribution it was trained on (\(w_t\)) but the boosting coefficient uses error under \(q_t\). The bound holds for any binary weak learner.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract] "The key theoretical insight is that projecting the training distribution reduces the effective edge of weak learners by a quantity controlled by the KL-divergence of the projection."
  - [section 5.1] Lemma 5.2: \(|\gamma(p)-\gamma(q)| \leq \text{TV}(p,q) \leq \sqrt{\frac{1}{2}\text{KL}(p\|q)}\), leading to \(\gamma(q_t) \geq \gamma(w_t) - \delta_t\).
  - [corpus] Neighbor papers (e.g., FairGBM, Exponentiated Gradient) do not derive such a per-round edge transfer bound linking distributional projection to convergence rate.
- **Break condition:** If \(\gamma(w_t) \leq \delta_t\), the effective edge becomes non-positive, violating a core AdaBoost assumption and causing the bound to fail; in practice, this triggers early stopping (\(\epsilon_t^{(q)} \geq 0.5\)).

### Mechanism 2: Fairness-Aware Exponential Loss Convergence
- **Claim:** FAIRPROJ achieves a geometric decrease in exponential loss at a rate determined by the *effective edge* \(\gamma(w_t) - \delta_t\), yielding \(L_{\text{exp}}(f_T) \leq n \exp\left(-2 \sum_{t=1}^T (\gamma(w_t) - \delta_t)^2\right)\) provided \(\gamma(w_t) > \delta_t\).
- **Mechanism:** The algorithm preserves the standard AdaBoost recursion for exponential loss because \(\alpha_t\) is computed using error under \(q_t\). The projection only affects which weak learner is selected (via \(w_t\)). The edge-transfer bound then substitutes into the standard per-round loss reduction factor.
- **Core assumption:** The weak learner must achieve positive *effective* edge on the fair distribution (\(\gamma(w_t) > \delta_t\)) every round; the bound requires this for all \(t\).
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract] "We prove an exponential-loss bound where the convergence rate depends on weak learner edge minus a 'fairness cost' term \(\delta_t\)."
  - [section 5.2] Theorem 5.4 provides the formal bound and proof via telescoping product.
  - [section 6.3] Figure 3 (top-left) empirically validates monotonic exponential loss decrease on Adult.
- **Break condition:** If for any round \(t\), \(\gamma(w_t) \leq \delta_t\), the per-round factor may not be \(< 1\), halting guaranteed geometric convergence. The algorithm implements a practical stopping rule (\(\epsilon_t^{(q)} \geq 0.5\)) which correlates with this condition.

### Mechanism 3: Reweighting Surrogate for Fairness
- **Claim:** Enforcing fairness constraints on the training distribution \(w_t\) (e.g., mass-balanced equal opportunity) acts as a *reweighting surrogate* that empirically reduces unfairness in the final ensemble classifier, without direct guarantees.
- **Mechanism:** The projection step forces \(w_t\) to satisfy linear moment constraints (e.g., \(|\langle w_t, g_{\text{EOpp}} \rangle| \leq \epsilon\)). This shifts the weight mass in training data toward under-represented group–label combinations (e.g., more weight on \(A=1, Y=1\) for equal opportunity). Weak learners trained on this balanced distribution may inherently learn less discriminatory decision boundaries, which compounds in the ensemble.
- **Core assumption:** There exists a positive correlation between satisfying the training distribution constraint and achieving better group fairness metrics on the final classifier. This is not a theoretical guarantee but an empirical hypothesis.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract] "We clarify that this enforces fairness as a reweighting surrogate—constraints on training distributions, not direct guarantees on classifier fairness."
  - [section 3.3] Definition 3.2 and Remark 3.3 explicitly label the constraints as a "reweighting surrogate" and note that classifier fairness is an empirical question.
  - [section 6.2] Tables 1 & 2 and Figures 1–2 show that tighter \(\epsilon\) (stronger reweighting) correlates with reduced EOpp/DP gaps on Adult/German, though not on COMPAS.
  - [corpus] Neighbor "Fair MP-BOOST" also uses reweighting but does not provide an explicit KL-based cost analysis linking it to boosting convergence.
- **Break condition:** If the weak learner class \(H\) is too simple (e.g., stumps) or the data's predictive features are highly entangled with the protected attribute, the reweighting may not translate to fairer predictions (as seen on COMPAS). The surrogate may also conflict with the goal of minimizing error, forcing a tradeoff.

## Foundational Learning

- **Concept: Exponential Loss & AdaBoost Recursion**
  - *Why needed here:* The entire theoretical analysis hinges on the recursion \(L_{\text{exp}}(f_t) = L_{\text{exp}}(f_{t-1}) \cdot \mathbb{E}_{i \sim q_t}[\exp(-\alpha_t y_i h_t(x_i))]\). This links per-round weak learner error (under \(q_t\)) to global loss reduction.
  - *Quick check question:* If you change the distribution used to compute \(\alpha_t\) from \(q_t\) to \(w_t\), why does the standard AdaBoost recursion (Eq. 2) no longer hold?
- **Concept: Edge (Margin)**
  - *Why needed here:* Edge \(\gamma(p) = \frac{1}{2} - \epsilon(p)\) is the fundamental measure of weak learner quality. The bound's convergence rate depends on the *effective* edge after distribution shift.
  - *Quick check question:* What is the relationship between a weak learner's edge under distribution \(p\) and its weighted error \(\epsilon(p)\)? How does a positive edge guarantee imply a reduction in exponential loss per round?
- **Concept: KL Divergence & Pinsker's Inequality**
  - *Why needed here:* KL divergence \(\text{KL}(w_t \| q_t)\) quantifies the "fairness cost" of projection. Pinsker's inequality links it to total variation, which directly bounds the edge shift.
  - *Quick check question:* Why is KL divergence the natural choice for the projection objective in the entropic mirror descent view of boosting? How does Pinsker's inequality convert KL to a bound on difference in expectations (edge)?

## Architecture Onboarding

- **Component map:**
  - `DistributionUpdater`: Computes \(q_t\) from current ensemble \(f_{t-1}\) via Eq. (1) (normalized negative log-loss weights).
  - `FairnessProjector`: Solves the dual problem (7) to get \(\lambda_t\), then forms \(w_t \propto q_t \exp(-\lambda_t^\top g(i))\) and computes \(\delta_t = \sqrt{\text{KL}(w_t \| q_t)/2}\) using Eq. (9).
  - `WeakLearnerTrainer`: Trains \(h_t\) on dataset weighted by \(w_t\).
  - `CoefficientComputer`: Computes \(\epsilon_t^{(q)}\) and \(\alpha_t = \frac{1}{2} \ln \frac{1-\epsilon_t^{(q)}}{\epsilon_t^{(q)}}\) using predictions of \(h_t\) on the *original* \(q_t\)-weighted data.
  - `StoppingMonitor`: Halts if \(\epsilon_t^{(q)} \geq 0.5\) (no useful weak learner under \(q_t\)) or after \(T\) rounds.
- **Critical path:** \(q_t\) → `FairnessProjector` → \(w_t\) → `WeakLearnerTrainer` → \(h_t\) → `CoefficientComputer` → \(\alpha_t\) → update \(f_t\). The projection step is the critical differentiator; it modifies the training distribution *before* weak learner selection but *not* the coefficient calculation.
- **Design tradeoffs:**
  - **Fairness vs. Convergence:** Tighter \(\epsilon\) increases projection divergence (\(\delta_t\)), reducing effective edge and causing earlier stopping (lower accuracy).
  - **Surrogate vs. True Fairness:** The reweighting surrogate (e.g., mass-balanced EOpp) may not translate to actual classifier fairness metrics if the weak learners cannot exploit the reweighting.
  - **Computational vs. Theoretical:** Warm-starting \(\lambda_t\) with \(\lambda_{t-1}\) is a heuristic for efficiency; the theoretical bound does not depend on it.
- **Failure signatures:**
  - High average \(\delta_t\) (>> average \(\gamma(w_t)\)) leading to very few rounds (e.g., <5) and large accuracy drop.
  - Constraint violation \(|\langle w_t, g_k \rangle| > \epsilon\) indicates projection solver failure.
  - No reduction in fairness gap despite active projection suggests misalignment between surrogate and true metric (as on COMPAS).
- **First 3 experiments:**
  1. **Validate Lemma 5.2 (Edge Transfer):** For a given run, plot \(\gamma(w_t)\), \(\gamma(q_t)\), and \(\gamma(w_t) - \delta_t\) per round. All points \(\gamma(q_t)\) should lie above the lower bound line.
  2. **Validate Theorem 5.4 (Monotonic Loss):** Plot \(L_{\text{exp}}(f_t)\) per round; it must decrease monotonically until stopping.
  3. **Tradeoff Exploration:** Run FAIRPROJ with a range of \(\epsilon\) values (e.g., \([0.05, 0.1, 0.15, 0.25, 0.4]\)) on Adult with EOpp surrogate. Plot accuracy vs. EOpp gap; tightening \(\epsilon\) should move the point along the Pareto frontier toward lower gap but lower accuracy, with earlier stopping rounds.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

1. **Does the fairness of the projected training distribution guarantee fairness of the final boosted classifier?**  
   - *Basis in paper:* [explicit] “Whether this translates to classifier fairness is an empirical question we investigate experimentally.”  
   - *Why unresolved:* The paper provides no theoretical link between the surrogate constraints and the classifier’s fairness metrics.  
   - *What evidence would resolve it:* A theorem that, given the projection constraints with slack \(\epsilon\), bounds the classifier’s fairness violation (e.g., equal opportunity gap) by a function of \(\epsilon\).

2. **What are necessary conditions on \(\epsilon\) and weak learner edge to maintain \(\gamma(w_t) > \delta_t\) and ensure convergence?**  
   - *Basis in paper:* [inferred] Theorem 5.4 assumes this; the authors note violation causes early stopping and give only a sufficient condition.  
   - *Why unresolved:* The feasible tradeoff region (\(\epsilon\) vs. convergence) is not characterized.  
   - *What evidence would resolve it:* A precise threshold \(\epsilon^*(\cdot)\) such that convergence is guaranteed iff \(\epsilon \ge \epsilon^*\), or a tighter bound on \(\gamma(w_t) - \delta_t\).

3. **Can the KL projection be performed efficiently for many constraints (e.g., intersectional groups) while preserving the loss bound?**  
   - *Basis in paper:* [inferred] The Discussion notes \(O(nK)\) cost may be prohibitive for large \(K\); only \(K \le 2\) is considered.  
   - *Why unresolved:* No scalable algorithm or theoretical analysis for approximate projection.  
   - *What evidence would resolve it:* An implementable approximate projection scheme with a proven edge‑transfer bound accommodating approximation error.

## Limitations

- **Implementation uncertainties (U1–U6):** Exact dataset preprocessing (Adult/German splits, encoding), dual solver hyperparameters, reweighting formula, Exponentiated Gradient’s ϵ scaling, constraint interpretation (mass-balanced), and weak learner specifics are unspecified, hindering exact replication.
- **Surrogate fairness guarantees:** The reweighting surrogate (constraints on \(w_t\)) lacks theoretical connection to final classifier fairness; empirical failures (e.g., COMPAS) indicate limited generalizability.
- **Convergence condition:** The bound requires \(\gamma(w_t) > \delta_t\) every round; for tight constraints this may be violated, causing early stopping and large accuracy loss. The feasible \(\epsilon\) vs. edge tradeoff region remains uncharacterized.

## Confidence

- **Theoretical bound (Lemma 5.2, Theorem 5.4):** High  
- **Empirical trade‑off (accuracy vs. fairness gap):** Medium  
- **Reweighting as fairness mechanism:** Low
