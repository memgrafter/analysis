# Pashto Common Voice: Building the First Open Speech Corpus for a 60-Million-Speaker Low-Resource Language
## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2603.27021
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.27021
- **Reference Count:** 14
- **Primary Result:** Built the first open Pashto speech corpus (147 hours, 1,483 speakers) and achieved a 13.4% WER by fine-tuning Whisper, versus 99.0% zero-shot.

## Executive Summary
This paper documents the creation of the first open speech corpus for Pashto, a language with over 60 million speakers but no prior publicly available dataset. The authors leveraged the Mozilla Common Voice framework, implementing interface localization and targeted data collection strategies, including a major outreach campaign with Voice of America. The resulting corpus, released through Common Voice, enables measurable progress in Pashto speech recognition, demonstrating a dramatic drop in Word Error Rate (WER) from a near-zero baseline via fine-tuning.

## Method Summary
The data collection pipeline combined platform localization of the Common Voice interface, extraction and filtering of sentences from Pashto Wikipedia to generate initial prompts, and the creation of phonemically targeted prompts for critical consonants to ensure phonetic coverage. A multi-channel outreach strategy, highlighted by a dedicated campaign with Voice of America Pashto broadcast, was central to accelerating speaker participation and diversity.

## Key Results
- Corpus scale reached 107,781 validated clips (82.33 hours) from 1,483 unique speakers across 13 domains by MCV23, from an initial 147 total hours collected.
- A VOA Pashto broadcast campaign drove a 108-fold increase in contributing speakers between CV17 and CV18.
- Fine-tuning Whisper Base on the MCV20 dataset achieved a 13.4% WER on the test split, a substantial improvement over a 99.0% zero-shot WER.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The project succeeded by addressing the core cold-start problem for a low-resource language: the absence of any open audio corpus. The mechanism involved (1) reducing the barrier to contribution via full UI localization, (2) ensuring linguistic diversity and coverage through both Wikipedia-derived and phonetically-targeted prompts, and (3) executing a high-impact, trusted media partnership (VOA) to rapidly onboard a critical mass of native speakers. This combination of technical preparation and mass outreach jump-started the network effects of a crowdsourced corpus.

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component Map:** Pashto Wikipedia Text -> Filtering -> Prompt Set A + Phonetic Prompt Set B -> Common Voice UI (Localized) -> Volunteer Recordings -> Validation (Multi-listener) -> Clips TSV -> Train/Val/Test Split -> Whisper Base -> Fine-tuned Whisper -> Test Split -> WER Calculation.

**Critical Path:** The most resource-intensive and sequential step is the *Volunteer Recordings* phase. All upstream work (text sourcing, prompt curation, UI work) feeds into maximizing the rate and diversity of recordings. Validation must follow recording. The fine-tuning experiment depends entirely on the released validated dataset.

**Design Tradeoffs:**
1.  **Prompt Quantity vs. Phonetic Coverage:** Using all filtered Wikipedia sentences maximizes quantity but risks gaps in phoneme coverage; adding phonetically targeted prompts improves coverage but reduces total available time for naturalistic sentences.
2.  **Outreach Scale vs. Quality Control:** A mass campaign (VOA) rapidly increases speaker count but may introduce recordings with higher noise or less careful enunciation, increasing validation burden.
3.  **Model Size vs. Low-Resource Training:** Whisper Base (~74M parameters) is small enough to fine-tune effectively on ~80 hours but may have less inherent capacity than larger models; a tradeoff between training feasibility and potential ceiling.

**Failure Signatures:**
- **Low speaker engagement despite localization:** Indicates UI/UX issues or lack of perceived community value.
-  **High validation rejection rate:** Suggests prompts are too difficult, unnatural, or recordings are consistently low quality.
-  **No WER improvement after fine-tuning:** Points to data quality issues (e.g., mislabeled text, extreme noise), incorrect train/val split, or inappropriate fine-tuning hyperparameters.
-  **Model performs well on test but fails on new real-world audio:** Suggests domain mismatch between the 13 corpus domains and target application.

**First Three Experiments:**
1.  **Reproduce Baseline:** Fine-tune Whisper Base on the *exact* MCV20 train split cited, using the same hyperparameters (if available) or standard settings (e.g., 3 epochs, batch size 16, learning rate 1e-4). Evaluate on the provided test split to confirm the 13.4% WER.
2.  **Zero-Shot Control:** Run the *identical* Whisper Base model (pre-fine-tuning) on the *same* MCV20 test split. Report the WER to establish a controlled baseline. If the paper's 99.0% was on a different set, this provides the true comparable figure.
3.  **Domain Ablation:** Fine-tune on subsets of the corpus (e.g., only Wikipedia-derived prompts, only phonetic prompts, only 5 of 13 domains) and evaluate on the full test set. This probes which data sources/domains contribute most to accuracy.

## Limitations
- Corpus domain composition (13 domains) is unspecified; critical for assessing dialect/register diversity.
- Whisper fine-tuning details (train/validation splits from MCV20, hyperparameters) are unreported, hindering reproducibility of the 13.4% WER.
- Zero-shot WER comparison (99.0%) likely uses a different test set or model variant; direct, controlled comparison on the same test split is absent.

## Confidence
- **High:** Corpus scale metrics (hours, speakers, clips) as these are direct outputs of the data collection platform.
- **Medium:** Claim that this is the *first* open Pashto speech corpus (possible unreleased academic/diacritic corpora not indexed).
- **High:** Dramatic WER improvement via fine-tuning (expected low-resource pattern) but confidence in *absolute* 13.4% value requires audit of test set conditions.
- **Low:** Attribution of speaker growth solely to VOA campaign lacks controlled analysis of other concurrent outreach efforts.
