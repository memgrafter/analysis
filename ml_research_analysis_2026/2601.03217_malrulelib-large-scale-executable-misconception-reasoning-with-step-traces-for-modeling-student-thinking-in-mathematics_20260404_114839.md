# MalruleLib: Large-Scale Executable Misconception Reasoning with Step Traces for Modeling Student Thinking in Mathematics
## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2601.03217
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.03217
- **Reference count:** 40
- **Primary result:** Models’ Malrule Reasoning Accuracy (MRA) drops from 66% on direct problem solving to 40% on cross-template; step traces improve MRA by 3–15%.

## Executive Summary
MalruleLib introduces a framework for modeling student misconceptions in mathematics by encoding 101 documented malrules as executable procedures and pairing them with 498 parameterized problem templates. This enables large-scale generation of dual-path step-by-step traces (correct and malrule-consistent) for supervised learning. Evaluations across nine language models (4B–120B) reveal a significant gap between solving mathematics correctly (66% CRA) and reasoning about student misconceptions (40% cross-template MRA). Step-by-step traces improve MRA by 3–15%, but cross-template generalization remains challenging with degradations of 10–21%.

## Method Summary
To reproduce MalruleLib, clone the public repository (https://github.com/luffycodes/malrulelib) and install dependencies. The framework comprises components that generate parameterized problem instances, produce correct step-by-step solutions, and apply one of 101 malrule algorithms to create flawed reasoning traces. Evaluation constructs (source, target) problem pairs either from the same template (same-template) or different templates (cross-template). For each pair, a language model is prompted with either just the source problem and wrong answer (answer-only) or also the malrule step trace (with-steps). The model must infer the underlying misconception and predict the student's answer on the target problem; accuracy is measured as Malrule Reasoning Accuracy (MRA). The paper reports that step traces improve MRA by 3–15% and that cross-template performance degrades by 10–21% relative to same-template.

## Key Results
- MRA drops from 66% (CRA) to 40% on cross-template, indicating a gap in modeling student thinking.
- Providing step-by-step reasoning traces improves MRA by 3–15%.
- Cross-template generalization remains difficult, with degradations of 10–21% across models.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1
- **Claim:** Encoding malrules as executable procedures enables consistent, large-scale generation of misconception-consistent student solutions.
- **Mechanism:** Each malrule is implemented as a deterministic algorithm that transforms problem inputs following a flawed rule (e.g., `√(a²+b²)=a+b`). When paired with parameterized templates, this generates correct and incorrect traces programmatically.
- **Core assumption:** Student misconceptions are stable, algorithmic procedures that can be codified.
- **Evidence anchors:** [abstract] "encodes 101 documented malrules as executable procedures"; [section 3.1] "each malrule is implemented as an executable procedure"; [corpus] Weak: neighboring papers discuss misconception modeling but not necessarily executable encoding.
- **Break condition:** If real student errors are non-algorithmic or context-dependent beyond template variation.

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim:** Dual-path (correct + malrule) step-by-step traces provide supervision for models to learn procedural differences between correct and incorrect reasoning.
- **Mechanism:** Models are exposed to aligned traces showing where and how a malrule diverges from correct steps, enabling them to simulate the misconception process rather than just recognize wrong answers.
- **Core assumption:** Step-level traces reveal the cognitive procedure behind errors, not just the final answer.
- **Evidence anchors:** [abstract] "produces paired dual-path traces for both correct reasoning and malrule-consistent student reasoning"; [section 5.2] "Providing student work yields consistent gains...step evidence produces consistent gains of 3–15 points"; [corpus] Partial: related work on reasoning traces for debugging, but not specifically for misconception simulation.
- **Break condition:** If traces do not capture authentic student thinking or if models ignore intermediate steps.

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim:** Template diversity (contexts, word problems, variants) forces models to abstract the malrule as a transferable procedure, not a surface pattern.
- **Mechanism:** By instantiating the same malrule across varied problem formats (e.g., algebraic expression → word problem), models must generalize the underlying flawed rule across contextual shifts.
- **Core assumption:** True understanding of a misconception requires applying it cross-context.
- **Evidence anchors:** [abstract] "cross-template generalization remains difficult, with degradations of 10–21%"; [section 3.2] "templates span 10 context domains" and "scaffolded complexity"; [corpus] Weak: no direct evidence on template diversity effects from neighbors.
- **Break condition:** If templates are not sufficiently distinct or if models rely on shallow heuristics that still generalize.

## Foundational Learning
- **Concept:** Malrules as documented, instructionally meaningful misconceptions.
  - *Why needed here:* Ensures the framework targets real student errors, not speculative ones, grounding it in learning science (67 sources).
  - *Quick check question:* "What is a common malrule for fraction addition, and what learning science source documents it?"

- **Concept:** Executable procedures for misconception generation.
  - *Why needed here:* Allows systematic, scalable creation of consistent wrong answers and traces, turning qualitative descriptions into computational objects.
  - *Quick check question:* "How would you encode the malrule 'distribute square root over addition' as a function?"

- **Concept:** Parameterized templates and cross-template generalization.
  - *Why needed here:* Tests whether models have abstracted the malrule or are memorizing template-specific patterns; essential for tutoring real students who face varied problems.
  - *Quick check question:* "Give an example of the same malrule instantiated in two different template types (e.g., symbolic vs. word problem)."

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map:** problem_generator.py (parameterized templates), correct_algorithm.py (correct step generator), malrule_algorithm.py (flawed step generator), test_malrule.py (unit tests)
- **Critical path:** Define malrule → select template → instantiate parameters → generate dual traces → evaluate MRA (infer malrule from one trace, predict answer on new template)
- **Design tradeoffs:**
  - Scalability vs. coverage: More templates increase generalization tests but require more malrule implementations.
  - Executable fidelity vs. realism: Algorithms must be crisp but should mirror actual student procedures, not just produce wrong answers.
  - Trace detail vs. noise: Steps should be cognitively plausible but not so fine-grained they become burdensome or artificial.
- **Failure signatures:**
  - High same-template MRA but sharp cross-template drop → model memorizes template patterns.
  - Steps improve MRA only slightly → traces may not capture key decision points or models ignore them.
  - Consistent category-specific failures (e.g., Coordinate Geometry) → malrule implementation or template design may need refinement.
- **First 3 experiments:**
  1. Run same-template vs. cross-template MRA (answer-only) to establish generalization gap.
  2. Ablate step traces (answer-only vs. with-steps) to measure trace utility.
  3. Per-category MRA analysis to identify weak malrule-template pairs or systematic model biases.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
**Open Question 1:** What specific cognitive or representational barriers cause large language models to fail at cross-template misconception generalization, despite sometimes exceeding correct problem-solving accuracy in same-template settings?
- *Basis in paper:* The authors identify cross-template degradation (10–21% drop) as the core bottleneck and note that same-template MRA can exceed CRA, indicating template-specific pattern matching rather than abstract malrule understanding.
- *Why unresolved:* The paper demonstrates the phenomenon but does not isolate whether failures stem from insufficient malrule abstraction, inability to map surface forms to underlying structure, or interference from pretraining priors toward correctness.
- *What evidence would resolve it:* Controlled experiments varying template semantic distance, probing model representations of malrule vs. template features, or training interventions that explicitly encourage abstraction.

**Open Question 2:** Which components of step-by-step reasoning traces are most critical for improving misconception prediction, and is there a minimal effective trace length or structure?
- *Basis in paper:* The authors show that providing student work steps improves cross-template MRA by 3–15%, but they do not analyze which steps contribute most.
- *Why unresolved:* Traces are treated as monolithic inputs; it is unknown whether all steps are equally informative or whether certain malrule-specific patterns are more diagnostic.
- *What evidence would resolve it:* Ablation studies removing or masking specific steps, perturbation experiments that alter trace coherence, or measuring performance against progressively simplified trace versions.

**Open Question 3:** Do the 101 encoded malrules cover the full spectrum of stable, instructionally relevant misconceptions in K–12 mathematics, or are there systematic gaps in domains or error types?
- *Basis in paper:* The framework is grounded in 67 learning-science sources and spans 22 categories, but the selection is a subset; the paper does not evaluate coverage completeness.
- *Why unresolved:* Unmodeled misconceptions could limit the benchmark’s generality and the applicability of trained models. Coverage is assumed based on literature synthesis, not empirical validation.
- *What evidence would resolve it:* Comparative analysis with other misconception inventories, expert evaluations of missing error patterns, or testing models on real student work beyond the distribution.

**Open Question 4:** Can supervised fine-tuning on MalruleLib’s large-scale dual-path traces eliminate or significantly reduce the gap between correct reasoning accuracy (CRA) and malrule reasoning accuracy (MRA), and what fine-tuning objectives work best?
- *Basis in paper:* The paper evaluates pretrained and instruction-tuned models in zero/few-shot settings only and generates >1M trace instances but does not test direct supervision.
- *Why unresolved:* The gap may reflect a fundamental supervision imbalance: models are trained to be correct, not to simulate incorrect stable procedures. It is unknown whether fine-tuning can overcome this bias.
- *What evidence would resolve it:* Systematic fine-tuning experiments using MalruleLib traces with various objectives (trace replication, malrule classification, answer prediction) and measuring resulting CRA-MRA gaps.

**Open Question 5:** What explains the large performance variability across mathematical domains (e.g., Functions at 82.1% vs. Coordinate Geometry at 28.8% MRA)? Is it due to malrule complexity, data sparsity, or inherent difficulty of modeling student thinking in those areas?
- *Basis in paper:* Table 6 and Figure 2 show a 53-point spread in MRA across NCTM categories, consistent across model families; authors note this suggests domain-sensitive tutoring systems but do not investigate causes.
- *Why unresolved:* The paper does not correlate performance with malrule properties, template features, or prevalence in literature.
- *What evidence would resolve it:* Error analysis per category, measuring malrule consistency in student traces, or controlling for template difficulty and malrule type in a balanced subset.

## Limitations
- Sampling & evaluation protocol is incompletely specified, risking irreproducible scores.
- Traces may not fully capture the noisy, context-sensitive nature of real student thinking.
- Coverage of 101 malrules may not represent the full spectrum of K–12 misconceptions.

## Confidence
- **Executable malrules enable scalable data generation:** High
- **Step traces improve MRA:** Medium
- **Cross-template generalization is hard:** High
