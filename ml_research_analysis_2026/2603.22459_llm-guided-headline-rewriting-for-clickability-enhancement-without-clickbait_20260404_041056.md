# LLM-guided headline rewriting for clickability enhancement without clickbait

## Quick Facts
- **ArXiv ID:** 2603.22459  
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.22459  
- **Reference count:** 18  
- **Primary result:** Introduced a FUDGE-based controllable rewriting system that navigates the engagement–clickbait trade‑off, with a clickbait scorer achieving AUROC > 0.99 on synthetic test data.

## Executive Summary
The paper reframes clickbait as disproportionate amplification of legitimate engagement cues and presents a controllable headline rewriting system. A base LLM is guided during decoding by two BERT-based models: a clickbait scorer (negative guidance) and an engagement attribute model (positive guidance). Both guides are trained on a synthetic dataset of neutral headlines and tactic‑annotated clickbait variants. By adjusting guidance weights, the system produces headlines along a continuum: unconstrained rewriting increases attention‑grabbing cues but also exaggeration, while dual guidance yields semantically faithful headlines that retain targeted engagement attributes. The clickbait scorer achieves near‑perfect discrimination (AUROC > 0.99) on the synthetic test set.

## Method Summary
The approach comprises three stages:  
1. **Synthetic data creation:** Neutral headlines (ISOT True.csv) are rewritten with GPT‑4o‑mini using prompts that activate specific “clickbait‑oriented” engagement attributes (e.g., curiosity gap, exaggeration). Each generated sample includes the headline, a binary clickbait label, and a sparse multi‑label attribute vector.  
2. **Guide model training:** Two BERT‑based models are fine‑tuned on all prefix decompositions of the synthetic data. The clickbait scorer is a binary classifier; the engagement attribute model is multi‑label. Training uses a length‑aware loss to down‑weight short prefixes.  
3. **Controlled generation:** A base LLM (e.g., GPT‑2) generates tokens one by one. At each step, the FUDGE controller combines the LLM’s log‑probabilities with guide scores:  
   `log p_adj = log p_LLM + λ_pos * Σ(ω_k * S_attr[k]) - λ_neg * S_cb`.  
   Hyperparameters λ_pos and λ_neg trade‑off engagement enhancement against clickbait suppression. The system is evaluated by sweeping these weights and analyzing the resulting headlines.

## Key Results
- Clickbait scorer reaches AUROC > 0.99 on synthetic test prefixes.  
- Varying λ_pos and λ_neg along a grid produces a continuum of outputs: λ_pos strengthens engagement attributes; λ_neg reduces clickbait scores; dual guidance yields balanced headlines that preserve semantics.  
- Ablative qualitative analysis confirms that positive guidance alone introduces exaggeration, whereas dual guidance yields proportional engagement cues.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- **Claim:** Clickbait is not a distinct genre but the result of disproportionate amplification of otherwise legitimate engagement cues.  
- **Mechanism:** Engagement is operationalized as a multidimensional rubric of linguistic attributes (e.g., curiosity gap, emphasis intensity). Each attribute has a proportional “clickability” interpretation and an exaggerated “clickbait” interpretation, transforming binary classification into continuous control.  
- **Core assumption:** Engagement mechanisms are separable and can be selectively strengthened or weakened without destroying semantic fidelity; clickbait emerges when a proportionality threshold is exceeded.  
- **Evidence anchors:**  
  - Abstract: “We frame clickbait not as a separate stylistic category, but as an extreme outcome of disproportionate amplification of otherwise legitimate engagement cues.”  
  - Section 1: “We define an engagement‑attribute rubric that separates clickbait‑oriented from clickability‑oriented realizations of common headline mechanisms.”  
- **Break condition:** If the attribute rubric fails to correlate with human perceived clickability, or if proportional vs. disproportionate use cannot be reliably distinguished in authentic headlines, the continuum assumption collapses.

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim:** Inference‑time dual‑guidance (positive for engagement attributes, negative for clickbait suppression) lets a base LLM navigate the engagement continuum while avoiding exaggeration.  
- **Mechanism:** FUDGE reweights the LLM’s next‑token probabilities at each step using two BERT‑based guides. The clickbait scorer (negative, weight λ_neg) penalizes clickbait‑likely continuations; the engagement attribute model (positive, weight λ_pos) encourages desired tactics. The combined score is:  
  `log p(y|x) ∝ log p_LLM + λ_pos·S_pos − λ_neg·S_cb`.  
- **Core assumption:** The two guides produce independent, prefix‑consistent signals that can be arithmetically combined, and the base LLM will respond to these soft constraints without losing fluency.  
- **Evidence anchors:**  
  - Section 3.5 describes the token selection score combination.  
  - Section 4.4 (Table 3) shows qualitative outputs where varying λ weights changes emphasis while preserving facts.  
- **Break condition:** If positive and negative signals are not orthogonal (e.g., promoting “curiosity gap” inherently raises clickbait score), weight adjustments cannot disentangle engagement from manipulation. Degenerate outputs (nonsensical, repetitive) under guidance also signal failure.

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim:** A synthetic dataset built by prompting an LLM to exaggerate specific rubric attributes from neutral headlines provides clean supervision for training both guide models.  
- **Mechanism:** Starting from real neutral headlines, GPT‑4o‑mini rewrites them using up to three specified “clickbait‑oriented” tactics (e.g., “Curiosity Gap”). This yields labeled pairs (headline, clickbait=1, active attributes). Neutral originals serve as negatives. The data trains a prefix‑level clickbait scorer and a multi‑label attribute model with explicit, non‑overlapping supervision per tactic.  
- **Core assumption:** LLM‑generated clickbait variants are realistic enough to generalize to naturally occurring clickbait, and tactic‑specific prompts produce exaggerated but coherent transformations.  
- **Evidence anchors:**  
  - Section 3.2 details the synthetic generation pipeline.  
  - Table 2 shows examples where core facts are preserved while tactics are amplified.  
  - Section 4.2 reports clickbait scorer AUROC > 0.99 on the synthetic test set.  
- **Break condition:** If the synthetic generation introduces artifacts absent in real clickbait, guides will learn spurious correlations and fail on authentic data. High synthetic AUROC is necessary but insufficient; real‑world validation is required.

## Foundational Learning
- **Concept: Controllable text generation via inference‑time guidance (e.g., FUDGE).**  
  - *Why needed here:* Steers a frozen LLM without fine‑tuning, using lightweight discriminators to reweight decoding. Understanding the Bayesian factorization P(text|attr) ∝ P(text)·P(attr|text) is key.  
  - *Quick check:* How does FUDGE differ from reinforcement learning or prompt‑tuning? Answer: It modifies next‑token probabilities using a discriminator’s forward pass on partial outputs, avoiding policy gradients or gradient‑based prompt updates.  

- **Concept: Multi‑label classification for rhetorical tactics.**  
  - *Why needed here:* The engagement model must detect co‑occurring tactics (e.g., Curiosity + Emotional Trigger) to provide granular positive signals.  
  - *Quick check:* Why not train separate binary classifiers per tactic? Answer: A shared BERT backbone is efficient; multi‑label also captures tactic co‑occurrence patterns, though the paper evaluates single‑tactic cases for clarity.  

- **Concept: Synthetic data generation for attribute control.**  
  - *Why needed here:* No existing dataset provides clean, tactic‑annotated pairs of proportional clickability vs. clickbait. Synthetic generation with explicit prompts creates the necessary supervision.  
  - *Quick check:* What is the primary risk of using LLM‑generated data to train models that will evaluate real headlines? Answer: Domain gap—the model may learn to detect “LLM exaggeration style” rather than human clickbait.

## Architecture Onboarding

### Component Map
Base LLM → (prefix) → Clickbait Scorer (S_cb) & Engagement Attribute Model (S_attr) → FUDGE Controller (adjusts log‑probs) → Sample next token → updated prefix.  
Synthetic Data Generator (offline) → training data → guides.

### Critical Path
1. Collect neutral headlines (ISOT True.csv).  
2. Generate synthetic clickbait variants with tactic‑specific prompts via GPT‑4o‑mini.  
3. Train clickbait scorer and engagement attribute model on all prefix decompositions using a length‑aware loss.  
4. Select base LLM (e.g., GPT‑2) and set λ_pos, λ_neg.  
5. For each neutral headline, run guided generation: initialize prefix, iterate decoding steps, at each step call base LLM and both guides, combine scores with FUDGE, sample token, update prefix until EOS.  
6. Sweep λ values to map the engagement‑clickbait trade‑off surface.

### Design Tradeoffs
- **Synthetic vs. real data:** Synthetic data gives perfect labels and tactic control but risks style bias; real clickbait datasets are noisy and lack attribute granularity.  
- **Prefix training:** Decomposing into all prefixes increases data size and exposes model to incomplete contexts, but introduces many short low‑information prefixes (mitigated by length‑aware loss).  
- **Guidance combination:** Linear sum assumes signal independence; more complex gating or product‑of‑experts may be needed if signals conflict.  
- **Base LLM choice:** Larger models may be more fluent but slower; smaller may be under‑expressive (paper does not specify).

### Failure Signatures
- High clickbait scorer AUROC on synthetic test but poor correlation with human judgments on real headlines.  
- Engagement model confuses adjacent tactics (e.g., “Exaggeration” vs. “Sensationalism”), leading to noisy positive guidance.  
- Guided outputs degenerate (nonsensical, repetitive) when λ_neg is too high, or remain clickbaity when λ_neg is too low despite high λ_pos.  
- Semantic drift: headlines change facts despite λ_neg (suggests attribute model over‑pushes or LLM lacks world knowledge).

### First Three Experiments
1. **Prefix Scorer Sanity Check:** Evaluate clickbait scorer on synthetic prefixes of varying lengths. Expected: low confidence on 1–3 tokens, rising and stabilizing after ~5–7 tokens. Early stability may indicate memorization of end‑token markers.  
2. **Attribute Model Disambiguation:** On single‑tactic synthetic headlines, compute per‑tactic precision/recall and confusion matrix. High mutual confusion (e.g., “Emotional Trigger” ↔ “Sensationalism”) signals rubric overlap or limited capacity.  
3. **Guidance Weight Sweep:** For fixed neutral headlines, generate outputs across a grid of (λ_pos, λ_neg). Measure: (a) engagement attribute presence (via attribute model), (b) clickbait score, (c) semantic similarity to source (e.g., BERTScore). Expectations: λ_pos ↑ → attribute scores ↑; λ_neg ↑ → clickbait score ↓; similarity may drop if constraints are too tight. Precipitous similarity drop indicates over‑constraint.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
1. **Tactic‑specific penalties:** The current scalar coefficients could be expanded to per‑tactic penalties, allowing fine‑grained suppression of individual clickbait strategies.  
2. **Human‑centric evaluation metrics:** To gauge editorial acceptability and real‑world engagement, large‑scale user studies measuring attractiveness, credibility, and click likelihood are needed.  
3. **Iterative/reinforcement‑based generation:** Multi‑step or reinforcement‑learning loops might improve robustness when multiple engagement tactics overlap, compared to the single‑pass FUDGE approach.  
4. **Generalization to longer‑form content:** Extending the framework beyond headlines to summaries or social media posts requires adapting the engagement rubric and possibly the guidance mechanism.

## Limitations
- **Synthetic data gap:** Guide models achieve near‑perfect AUROC on synthetic data, but real‑world generalization is unproven; LLM‑generated clickbait may not match authentic patterns.  
- **Specification gaps:** The base LLM identity and exact BERT variant (base/large) are undisclosed, hindering exact replication; length‑aware loss details are vague.  
- **Evaluation incompleteness:** No human studies measuring actual engagement or click‑through rates; no comparison to prior clickbait‑mitigation methods.

## Confidence
- Clickbait as continuous amplification: **Medium**  
- Dual‑guidance navigation: **Medium**  
- Synthetic supervision sufficiency: **Low**

## Next Checks
- **Real‑world guide evaluation:** Test clickbait scorer and attribute model on a held‑out authentic clickbait dataset; a significant AUROC drop would indicate overfitting to synthetic artifacts.  
- **Human‑centric rewrite assessment:** Conduct a user study comparing guided vs. unguided rewrites on perceived clickbait, trust, and likelihood to click, using the rubric’s attributes as rating dimensions.  
- **Ablation of guidance signals:** Systemically remove one guide, vary λ weights, and measure semantic fidelity (e.g., BERTScore) and attribute control to isolate each guide’s contribution and identify failure thresholds.
