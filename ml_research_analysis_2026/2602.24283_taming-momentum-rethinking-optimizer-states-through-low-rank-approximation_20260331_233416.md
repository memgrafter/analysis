# Taming Momentum: Rethinking Optimizer States Through Low-Rank Approximation

## Quick Facts
- **ArXiv ID:** 2602.24283
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.24283
- **Reference count:** 40
- **Primary result:** LoRA-Pre reduces optimizer state memory by up to 8× while matching or exceeding baseline performance in both pre‑training (60M–1B) and fine‑tuning (MetaMathQA).

## Executive Summary
Large language model training is bottlenecked by the memory consumed by optimizer states, particularly momentum in Adam and Muon. This paper reinterprets exponential moving average momentum as training an online linear regressor, which reveals that the momentum matrix often has low effective rank. Building on this insight, the authors propose LoRA‑Pre, a method that compresses the momentum matrix via low‑rank factorization (two slender matrices) instead of storing the full matrix. Pre‑training experiments on Llama models from 60M to 1B parameters show that LoRA‑Pre achieves comparable or better results using only 1/8th the rank of standard optimizers. In fine‑tuning on the MetaMathQA dataset, LoRA‑Pre outperforms standard LoRA by 3.14 points on Llama‑3.1‑8B and 6.17 points on Llama‑2‑7B. The approach offers a general pathway to reduce optimizer memory overhead without sacrificing quality.

## Method Summary
The core idea is to view the momentum buffer in Adam (or Muon) as the solution to an online linear regression problem, where the optimizer sequentially predicts future gradients. This perspective suggests that the momentum matrix can be well‑approximated by a low‑rank structure, as its spectrum typically decays quickly. LoRA‑Pre therefore replaces the full momentum matrix \(M\) with a product of two matrices \(A B^\top\), where \(A \in \mathbb{R}^{d \times r}\) and \(B \in \mathbb{R}^{d \times r}\) and \(r \ll d\). During training, \(A\) and \(B\) are updated each step to track the true momentum matrix using a streaming algorithm that maintains the low‑rank representation efficiently. The weight update then uses the approximate momentum \(A B^\top\) instead of the full matrix, cutting the memory from \(O(d^2)\) to \(O(d r)\) while preserving the optimization dynamics.

## Key Results
- **Pre‑training (60M–1B):** LoRA‑Pre matches baseline perplexity with only 12.5% of the rank and often outperforms full‑rank baselines at comparable memory.
- **Fine‑tuning on MetaMathQA:** LoRA‑Pre surpasses standard LoRA by **3.14** points on Llama‑3.1‑8B and **6.17** points on Llama‑2‑7B.
- **Memory reduction:** Achieves up to **8×** savings in optimizer state memory with negligible computational overhead.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
Momentum accumulates historical gradient information to smooth the optimization trajectory. By recasting this process as an online linear regression, the paper shows that the momentum matrix essentially learns to predict future gradients based on past ones, a task that often lies in a low‑dimensional subspace. Consequently, the full‑rank momentum matrix is approximately low‑rank in practice. LoRA‑Pre exploits this by maintaining a compact factorization \(A B^\top\) that captures the dominant directions of the momentum. This representation retains the essential predictive power of the momentum buffer while drastically reducing storage. The low‑rank factors are updated in a streaming fashion, ensuring that the approximation stays aligned with the evolving gradient statistics throughout training.

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component Map**  
```
Gradient g → Online linear regressor (maintains A, B) → Momentum estimate M̂ = A Bᵀ → Parameter update W ← W - η M̂
```

**Critical Path**  
1. Forward pass computes loss.  
2. Backward pass yields gradient \(g\).  
3. LoRA‑Pre updates low‑rank factors \(A, B\) to track the momentum matrix using \(g\) and previous factors.  
4. Weight update applies \(\Delta W = -\eta (A B^\top)\).  
5. Repeat.

**Design Tradeoffs**  
- **Rank \(r\)**: Smaller \(r\) reduces memory linearly (\(O(d r)\) vs \(O(d^2)\)) but risks under‑approximating momentum; the spectrum of the momentum matrix typically allows moderate ranks (e.g., \(r = d/8\)) to perform nearly as well as full rank.  
- **Compute overhead**: Updating \(A\) and \(B\) introduces minor extra FLOPs compared to a full momentum update, but these are negligible relative to the memory savings that enable larger models or batch sizes.  
- **Generality**: LoRA‑Pre is compatible with any optimizer that uses a momentum‑like matrix (e.g., Adam, Muon) and can be combined with other memory‑saving techniques like gradient checkpointing.

**Failure Signatures**  
- **Rank too low**: Training becomes unstable, final perplexity degrades sharply, or convergence slows dramatically.  
- **Drift**: If the low‑rank update rule is not numerically stable, the approximation may diverge from the true momentum, causing oscillations or divergence.  
- **Task‑specific degradation**: For fine‑tuning, some tasks may require higher fidelity to the original optimizer state; performance gaps may appear when rank is insufficient.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
The paper does not explicitly discuss open questions, but the limitations and suggested future work imply the following:
- Whether the observed memory savings and performance improvements hold for models beyond 1B parameters (especially at 10B+ scale).
- The generalizability of fine‑tuning improvements across diverse tasks beyond mathematical reasoning (e.g., instruction tuning, code generation).
- The comparative advantages of LoRA‑Pre versus concurrent low‑rank optimizer state compression methods like GaLore, and whether these approaches are orthogonal or overlapping.

## Limitations
- Evaluation is restricted to models up to 1B parameters; it is unknown whether the observed memory savings and performance improvements hold for modern LLMs exceeding 10B parameters.
- Fine‑tuning experiments are limited to a single mathematical reasoning dataset (MetaMathQA); generalizability across diverse tasks (e.g., instruction tuning, code generation) remains unverified.
- The provided summary lacks direct comparisons with concurrent low‑rank optimizer state compression methods such as GaLore, leaving the relative advantage and potential overlaps unaddressed.

## Confidence
- **Memory reduction claim**: High (follows directly from low‑rank factorization).
- **Pre‑training scalability (60M–1B)**: Medium (results are promising but not independently replicated; may depend on architecture/data).
- **Fine‑tuning superiority over LoRA**: Medium (improvements on two models and one task are encouraging but not broadly generalizable).
- **General applicability to Adam/Muon**: Low (theoretical justification is asserted but not extensively validated across diverse optimizer configurations and large‑scale dynamics).

## Next Checks
1. **Scale validation**: Replicate pre‑training on >10B parameter models (e.g., Llama‑3 70B) to confirm that memory savings and perplexity gains hold at industrial scale.
2. **Task diversity test**: Evaluate fine‑tuning on at least three heterogeneous benchmarks (e.g., MMLU, GSM8K, HumanEval) to establish robustness beyond math reasoning.
3. **Ablation against GaLore/AltLoRA**: Conduct controlled comparisons with matched rank, compute budget, and identical pre‑training runs to isolate LoRA‑Pre’s unique contribution from general low‑rank gradient/momentum effects.
