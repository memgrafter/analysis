# Gender Bias in MT for a Genderless Language: New Benchmarks for Basque

## Quick Facts
*   **arXiv ID:** 2603.08153
*   **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.08153
*   **Reference count:** 0
*   **Primary result:** Introduces Basque-focused gender bias datasets; finds MT systems systematically overgenerate masculine forms when translating from gender-neutral Basque into Spanish/French, with moderate correlation to real-world occupational statistics.

## Executive Summary
This paper tackles the underrepresentation of non-English languages in gender bias evaluation for machine translation. It introduces two novel benchmarks for Basque, a language without grammatical gender: **WinoMTeus**, which adapts the WinoMT challenge to test how neutral Basque occupations are rendered into gendered Spanish and French, and **FLORES+Gender**, which evaluates whether translation quality into Basque varies based on the gender of the source referent in Spanish or English. Results show a consistent, systematic preference for masculine translations in the WinoMTeus task across all tested systems. For FLORES+Gender, quality differences between masculine and feminine source sentences were generally small and model-specific, with some systems showing a significant advantage for masculine Spanish sources.

## Method Summary
The study establishes two contrastive evaluation frameworks. **WinoMTeus** consists of 1,827 Basque sentences containing gender-neutral occupational terms. These are translated into Spanish or French by various MT systems (including LLMs, open NMT models, and proprietary services). An extraction pipeline labels the gender of the translated occupation, which is then compared to real-world Basque labor statistics (Lanbide) using correlation (r) and the GRAPE metric. **FLORES+Gender** extends the FLORES+ corpus with manually created masculine/feminine variant pairs in Spanish (363) and English (155). Systems translate both variants into Basque, and automatic metrics (chrF++, TER) compare translation quality, with statistical tests measuring significance. Factor annotations (ME, PN, UM) probe linguistic drivers of any observed bias.

## Key Results
*   All evaluated MT systems exhibit a **systematic preference for masculine forms** when translating gender-neutral Basque occupations into Spanish/French (WinoMTeus).
*   The gender distribution of model outputs shows a **moderate positive correlation (r > 0.4)** with actual Basque labor statistics for some models, yet masculine forms remain overrepresented.
*   For translation *into* Basque (FLORES+Gender), quality differences between masculine and feminine source sentences are **generally small and model-specific**, though some systems show a significant advantage for masculine Spanish sources.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1
*   **Claim:** The absence of grammatical gender in Basque forces MT systems to make explicit, categorical gender choices when translating into gendered targets, exposing underlying bias.
*   **Mechanism:** Basque source sentences contain neutral occupational terms. Translating into Spanish/French requires selecting a masculine or feminine form. Aggregating these selections across a controlled dataset (WinoMTeus) reveals a systematic bias in the model's decision policy.
*   **Core assumption:** The gender choice is not random but reflects priors from training data where masculine forms are the statistical default.
*   **Evidence anchors:**
    *   [abstract] "WinoMTeus adapts the WinoMT benchmark... results reveal a systematic preference for masculine forms."
    *   [section 3] "This task cannot be applied to Basque... Hence, we translated the original dataset and created a Basque version, called WinoMTeus..."
*   **Break condition:** If models consistently output neutral or epicene target forms, the mechanism fails. The paper finds no such neutralization.

### Mechanism 2
*   **Claim:** Alignment between model output gender distribution and real-world occupational statistics quantifies how much models reflect societal patterns.
*   **Mechanism:** For each occupation in WinoMTeus, the proportion of feminine vs. masculine translations is compared to actual Basque labor statistics. Correlation (r) and GRAPE measure this alignment.
*   **Core assumption:** Official labor statistics are a valid reference distribution for evaluating social realism.
*   **Evidence anchors:**
    *   [section 5.1.1] "We follow prior work... comparing our results with actual labour distribution statistics... Pearson correlation coefficient..."
    *   [section 6] "Moderate alignment with labour distributions... GPT-5, NLLB-200-3.3B and Latxa 3.1 70B Instruct show the highest correlation (r > 0.4)."
*   **Break condition:** Near-zero or negative correlations across all models would indicate models are not capturing real-world distributions.

### Mechanism 3
*   **Claim:** The grammatical gender marking of the source text can causally influence translation quality into a genderless language.
*   **Mechanism:** Using FLORES+Gender, minimally different sentence pairs (masc. vs. fem.) are translated into Basque. Differences in automatic metrics (chrF++, TER) are attributed to the source gender variant.
*   **Core assumption:** Any systematic quality difference is caused by the gender variant itself, controlling for semantics via contrastive design.
*   **Evidence anchors:**
    *   [abstract] "FLORES+Gender... assesses whether translation quality varies... some models showed significant advantages for masculine Spanish sources."
    *   [section 4] "We construct contrastive versions... one containing all the sentences in masculine form and another with the same sentences in feminine form."
    *   [section 6] "For Spanish sources, most models produced slightly but rarely significant higher scores for masculine variants... Batua exhibits significant differences..."
*   **Break condition:** No significant quality differences (or consistent feminine advantage) across all models would unsupport the hypothesis. Results are mixed.

## Foundational Learning
*   **Concept:** Grammatical Gender vs. Lexical Gender
    *   **Why needed:** To understand the unique evaluation case of Basque. The paper hinges on the mismatch between Basque's lack of grammatical gender and the gender systems of Spanish/French.
    *   **Quick check:** If Basque has no grammatical gender, how is social gender still communicated? (Answer: Via lexical items like gendered nouns for professions, kinship terms, and proper names.)
*   **Concept:** Counterfactual/Contrastive Data Creation for Bias Evaluation
    *   **Why needed:** Both benchmarks are built by creating controlled pairs to probe bias where the source language lacks gender markings.
    *   **Quick check:** What is the key difference in how contrast is achieved in WinoMTeus vs. FLORES+Gender? (Answer: WinoMTeus contrasts *across systems* (neutral source -> different outputs). FLORES+Gender contrasts *within a system* (translating two source variants that differ only in gender.)
*   **Concept:** Evaluation Metrics for Bias (GRAPE, Correlation)
    *   **Why needed:** The study uses specialized metrics to quantify the *direction* and *societal alignment* of bias, not just accuracy.
    *   **Quick check:** What does GRAPE-M = 0.0 vs. 1.0 indicate for a female-dominated profession? (Answer: 0.0 = masculine forms appear exactly as often as in the real-world reference. 1.0 = masculine forms appear *twice as often* as expected.)

## Architecture Onboarding
*   **Component map:**
    Datasets (WinoMTeus, FLORES+Gender, Lanbide stats) -> Models Under Test (LLMs, NMT, Proprietary MT) -> Processing Pipeline (Inference -> Output Parsing -> Metric Computation -> Statistical Testing) -> Results (Tables/Figures)
*   **Critical path:**
    1.  Dataset preparation (ensure WinoMTeus glossed, FLORES+Gender pairs equivalent).
    2.  Model inference with consistent settings (temperature=0).
    3.  Output analysis (occupation extraction for WinoMTeus; metric calc for FLORES+Gender).
    4.  Aggregation & comparison (GRAPE/correlation, factor-wise deltas).
    5.  Validation (manual spot-checks, e.g., 100-sentence extraction accuracy).
*   **Design tradeoffs:**
    *   **Automatic vs. Manual Annotation:** Uses Claude for scalable extraction vs. manual validation. Tradeoff: speed vs. error propagation risk.
    *   **Binary Gender Simplification:** Treats gender as binary (M/F) to match target grammar. Tradeoff: clarity vs. exclusion of non-binary identities.
    *   **Automatic Metrics (chrF++, TER):** Used for scalability. Tradeoff: correlates with human judgment but may miss nuanced, gender-related semantic errors.
*   **Failure signatures:**
    *   **WinoMTeus extraction failures:** High [MISSING] rate or inconsistent article-gender matching indicates extraction prompt failure.
    *   **FLORES+Gender pair quality:** Low inter-annotator agreement during pair creation suggests semantic drift, confounding gender effects.
    *   **Statistical noise in FLORES+Gender:** Small dataset sizes (363 ES, 155 EN) yield non-significant results despite trends; wide confidence intervals.
*   **First 3 experiments:**
    1.  **Replicate WinoMTeus core analysis:** Use provided dataset, run a model (e.g., NLLB-200-3.3B) for Basque->Spanish, apply extraction, map to Lanbide, compute Pearson r. Target: confirm reported ~0.44.
    2.  **Replicate FLORES+Gender quality difference test:** For Batua (ES->EU), compute chrF++ for masc/fem subsets, perform paired randomization test. Target: confirm significant positive Δ for masculine.
    3.  **Factor interaction probe:** For Batua (ES->EU), filter FLORES+Gender by "UM" annotation, compute Δ for UM=yes vs. UM=no. Target: validate that UM has a strong effect on bias metrics.

## Limitations
*   **Dataset & Methodological Gaps:** The exact Claude extraction prompt and the manual occupation-to-Lanbide mapping table are not public, blocking full replication of the WinoMTeus pipeline. Model version specifics (e.g., "Claude 4 Sonnet", "GPT-5") and some HiTZ model identifiers are unspecified.
*   **Evaluation Constraints:** FLORES+Gender's reliance on automatic metrics on a small dataset may miss nuanced gender-related errors and limits statistical power. The binary gender framing excludes non-binary identities.
*   **Causal Inference:** While FLORES+Gender's design supports attribution, uncontrolled nuances in manually created pairs could confound results. The moderate correlation (r > 0.4) indicates association, not that models "learn" societal distributions.

## Confidence
*   **WinoMTeus Systematic Masculine Preference:** Medium Confidence. Core finding supported by paper's analysis, but reduced by non-public extraction/mapping steps needed for verification.
*   **FLORES+Gender Mixed Quality Differences:** Low Confidence. Small, model-specific advantages are fragile, dependent on metrics and sample size; replication could alter significance.
*   **Moderate Alignment with Real-World Data:** Medium Confidence. Credible empirical result, but operationalization of bias as deviation from labor stats is a methodological choice.
