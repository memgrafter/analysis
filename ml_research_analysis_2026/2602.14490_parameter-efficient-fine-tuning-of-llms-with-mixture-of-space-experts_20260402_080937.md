# Parameter-Efficient Fine-Tuning of LLMs with Mixture of Space Experts

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID**: 2602.14490
- **Source URL**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.14490
- **Reference count**: 29
- **Primary result**: MoSLoRA outperforms PEFT baselines, achieving up to +5.6% accuracy on MATH500 and +15.9% on MAWPS by routing tokens to specialized geometric spaces.

## Executive Summary
The paper introduces Mixture of Space (MoS), a novel parameter-efficient fine-tuning (PEFT) framework that extends LoRA by incorporating heterogeneous geometric experts in hyperbolic, spherical, and Euclidean spaces. Unlike conventional methods confined to Euclidean representations, MoSLoRA dynamically assigns tokens to the most suitable manifold via a lightweight routing mechanism, enabling the model to capture hierarchical, circular, and flat structural patterns inherent in language. The authors propose a unified stereographic projection for efficient, differentiable transformations between manifolds and decouple curvature optimization from LoRA weights. Extensive evaluations on mathematical reasoning (MATH500, MAWPS) and NLP benchmarks show consistent improvements, with gains of up to 5.6% and 15.9% respectively, highlighting the benefits of leveraging diverse geometries in PEFT.

## Method Summary
MoSLoRA augments a frozen LLM by injecting multiple low-rank adaptation experts, each operating in a distinct constant-curvature space (hyperbolic, spherical, Euclidean). A token router, based on the space-like component of the token embedding, assigns each token to the top-K (e.g., 4) of 8 experts. Each expert applies a LoRA update within its native geometry: the token is mapped via inverse stereographic projection, transformed by its low-rank matrices, and mapped back with stereographic projection. An auxiliary loss balances the load across experts and within each geometric group. Crucially, curvature parameters κ are optimized with a separate AdamW step, allowing independent adaptation. The setup yields a parameter overhead of roughly 1.31% while unlocking heterogeneous geometric inductive biases.

## Key Results
- **MATH500**: MoSLoRA surpasses baselines by up to **5.6%** accuracy.
- **MAWPS**: Achieves an improvement of **15.9%** over competing PEFT methods.
- **Broad benchmarks**: Consistent gains across GSM8K, SVAMP, AQuA, and NLP tasks (MRPC, CoLA, RTE) as well as commonsense QA (OBQA, CSQA).

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- **Claim**: Different geometric spaces capture distinct structural patterns in language data (hierarchical → hyperbolic, circular → spherical, flat → Euclidean), and tokens should dynamically select the appropriate space.
- **Mechanism**: MoSLoRA employs multiple LoRA experts, each confined to a specific manifold (hyperbolic, spherical, or Euclidean). A lightweight router examines token embeddings and assigns each token to the top-K geometric experts whose curvature aligns with the token's semantic structure. The outputs are merged in Euclidean space via stereographic projection.
- **Core assumption**: Semantic structures in language are heterogeneous—a single sentence may contain hierarchical parent-child relationships, circular synonym sets, and flat relational patterns that benefit from different geometric inductive biases.
- **Evidence anchors**:
  - [abstract] "forcing representations into a single manifold type ultimately limits expressiveness, even when curvature parameters are learnable"
  - [section] "different tokens to reside in the geometric space most suited to their structural properties: hierarchical concepts in hyperbolic space, circular patterns in spherical space, and flat relational structures in Euclidean space"
  - [corpus] Neighboring papers focus on "Mixture-of-Experts" and "heterogeneous" module designs, supporting the premise that heterogeneity improves PEFT performance.
- **Break condition**: If linguistic structures are predominantly Euclidean or if routing fails to distinguish between geometric needs (e.g., router collapses to always selecting Euclidean experts due to optimization difficulties or if training data lacks diverse geometric patterns).

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim**: Unified stereographic projection replaces expensive exponential/logarithmic maps, enabling efficient, differentiable transitions between manifolds without sacrificing expressiveness.
- **Mechanism**: Each token's space-like component is scaled and mapped via stereographic inverse projection into a unified Riemannian manifold representation. After expert transformation (learnable low-rank matrices), the result is projected back with a second stereographic projection. The scheme is scale-equivariant, allowing consistent transformations.
- **Core assumption**: Stereographic projection can preserve essential geometric relationships (angles, distances) while being computationally cheaper and fully differentiable than exp/log maps.
- **Evidence anchors**:
  - [section] "To address this challenge, we propose a unified stereographic projection, which projects inputs from the input space into three constant-curvature spaces and subsequently maps them back to the output space"
  - [section] Figure 1 illustrates the unified mapping integrated into the FFN layer and highlights efficiency gains.
  - [corpus] Neighbor papers like "Fully hyperbolic neural networks" rely on exp/log maps, confirming the computational bottleneck MoS addresses.
- **Break condition**: If stereographic projection introduces significant distortion for certain curvature ranges or if numerical instability emerges at extreme curvatures despite scaling.

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim**: Separating curvature parameters from capacity-related weights (via independent optimizers) enables stable, flexible exploration of geometric spaces without interference from other adaptation parameters.
- **Mechanism**: Curvature κ for each expert receives its own optimizer with dedicated learning rate, while LoRA matrices (A, B) use another optimizer. This prevents curvature from being "dragged" along with rank-limited updates and allows curvature to converge to optimal values more freely.
- **Core assumption**: Geometric properties (curvature) optimize on a different scale and with different dynamics than linear transformation weights; joint optimization leads to suboptimal or unstable curvature selection.
- **Evidence anchors**:
  - [section] "Considering that curvature, as a geometric parameter, differs fundamentally from other capacity-related model weights, we assign it an independent optimizer"
  - [section] "We observed that when using a unified optimizer, the curvature κ fail to adjust alongside other parameters, resulting in nearly static values and relatively poor task performance"
  - [section] Figure 2 shows curvature stabilizing around 6k–8k steps, suggesting distinct optimization trajectory.
- **Break condition**: If separate optimizers introduce new hyperparameter tuning complexity without clear benefits, or if curvature optimization becomes unstable (diverges) when decoupled from main weight updates.

## Architecture Onboarding

### Component map
- **Base LLM**: Frozen pretrained weights (e.g., Qwen2-1.5B).
- **LoRA experts**: Multiple (N=8) low-rank matrices (B_i∈R^{d_out×r}, A_i∈R^{r×d_in}) grouped by geometry type (3 hyperbolic, 3 spherical, 2 Euclidean).
- **Curvature parameters**: Learnable κ_i per expert (negative for hyperbolic, positive for spherical, zero fixed for Euclidean).
- **Unified stereographic projection**: Functions ρ_κ (forward) and ρ_κ^{-1} (inverse) with scaling factor γ.
- **Token router**: Computes routing weights R(x)_i based on token’s space-like component s_i; selects top-K experts per token.
- **Grouped auxiliary loss**: Balances load within each geometric group (H, S, E) rather than globally.
- **Separate optimizers**: One group for {κ_i}, one for {A_i, B_i}.

### Critical path
1. Token embedding enters FFN layer.
2. Space-like component extracted (s_i from token embedding).
3. Router computes scores for all experts → top-K selection.
4. For each selected expert:
   a. Inverse stereographic projection ρ_κ^{-1}(x) → (ξ, s).
   b. Linear transform: s' = W s_i (shared W across experts? In MoSLoRA, W is base weight; expert-specific updates? Paper says "G(W,x_i)", meaning W is base weight; the expert adaptation is geometric transformation on s_i? Need careful reading: Section 4.3 says "space-like component s_i as the routing unit" and expert definition G(W,x_i) = [√(∥W s_i∥²)·sgn(−κ)+φ(κ); W s_i]. So each expert applies the same base weight W to s_i but represents result in different geometry.)
   c. Compute ξ' = √(∥s'∥²·sgn(−κ)+φ(κ)).
5. Project back: ρ_κ(ξ', s') → Euclidean output.
6. Sum outputs from top-K experts, add to base residual.

### Design tradeoffs
- **Heterogeneous vs. homogeneous experts**: Mixing spaces increases representational capacity but requires careful routing to avoid expert collapse.
- **Top-K value**: Larger K improves performance but increases compute; paper uses K=4 with N=8.
- **Curvature initialization**: κ initialized to –1 (hyperbolic) and 1 (spherical); paper notes these are not tuned but work reasonably.
- **Grouped load balancing**: Balances within geometry groups; prevents one geometry from dominating routing but allows free inter-space transitions.
- **Separate optimizers**: Adds hyperparameters (learning rates for κ vs. LoRA weights) but found necessary for curvature adaptation.

### Failure signatures
- **Curvature stagnation**: All κ_i converge to same value or to Euclidean (κ≈0) → router may not be learning geometric specialization.
- **Router collapse**: >90% of tokens routed to Euclidean group only → geometric diversity not being utilized.
- **Training instability**: Loss spikes or NaNs during early steps, likely from numerical issues in stereographic projection at extreme curvatures or unscaled inputs.
- **No performance gain over LoRA**: Mixed-geometry experts fail to outperform single-space or vanilla LoRA → may indicate routing is ineffective or tasks don’t benefit from non-Euclidean spaces.

### First 3 experiments
1. **Curvature dynamics sanity check**: Plot κ_i per expert vs. training steps (like Figure 2). Expect: κ_i should move away from initialization and stabilize by ~6k steps. If all κ_i stay near initial values, routing/curvature optimization may be broken.
2. **Router distribution analysis**: Log fraction of tokens assigned to each expert and to each geometric group. Expect: non-uniform distribution within groups (some experts specialize) and inter-group variety (H, S, E all used). If one expert or group gets >80%, load balancing or top-K may be ineffective.
3. **Single-space ablation**: Run MoSLoRA with all experts hyperbolic-only, spherical-only, Euclidean-only, and full mixture. Expect: mixture should outperform single-space on average; but single-space may excel on specific tasks (hyperbolic on MATH). If mixture underperforms all singles, routing likely introduces harmful interference.

## Limitations
- Router design (scoring function) and auxiliary loss weight are unspecified, hindering exact replication.
- No empirical efficiency comparison of stereographic projection versus traditional exponential/logarithmic maps.
- Missing reported analysis on curvature stagnation or router collapse, leaving risks unquantified.
