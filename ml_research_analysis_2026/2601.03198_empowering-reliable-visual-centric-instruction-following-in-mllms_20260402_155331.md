# Empowering Reliable Visual-Centric Instruction Following in MLLMs

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2601.03198
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.03198
- Reference count: 38
- Primary result: VC-IFEval reveals that even state-of-the-art MLLMs (e.g., Qwen2.5-VL-32B) struggle with vision-dependent instruction following (67.3%), and fine-tuning on the proposed VC-IFInstruct and VC-IFDPO datasets yields substantial improvements (+4.45% average on SFT, +9.45% on DPO) while preserving performance on standard VQA benchmarks.

## Executive Summary
Existing multimodal instruction-following benchmarks often fail to isolate visual reasoning, allowing models to rely on textual priors. This paper introduces VC-IFEval, a benchmark with vision-dependent constraints (spatial, attribute, comparative), and constructs two datasets—VC-IFInstruct for supervised fine-tuning and VC-IFDPO for preference learning—using the VC-IFEngine pipeline. Models fine-tuned on these data show significant gains on visual instruction following without sacrificing performance on traditional VQA tasks. The work highlights the importance of visual grounding and provides a robust evaluation framework for future research.

## Method Summary
The authors build a data construction engine, VC-IFEngine, which filters images for quality and saliency, generates instruction tasks from a pool of templates, and creates visual constraints tailored to each image. This yields two datasets: VC-IFInstruct (10k SFT pairs) and VC-IFDPO (10k preference pairs with rejected responses from constraint ablation or image edits). Models are fine-tuned via standard SFT or DPO. Evaluation on VC-IFEval (300 instances) uses a hybrid protocol: a direct judge (GPT-4o) scores constraint satisfaction, and a comparative judge measures whether outputs change when the image is removed. The primary metric combines Constraint-following Accuracy (CFA) and Image Influence Score (IIS).

## Key Results
- VC-IFEval exposes a critical gap: top models like Qwen2.5-VL-32B achieve only 67.3%.
- SFT on VC-IFInstruct yields an average improvement of +4.45% across visual instruction-following benchmarks.
- DPO on VC-IFDPO yields a larger average gain of +9.45%, demonstrating the effectiveness of contrastive visual supervision.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
**Causal Mechanism 1: Benchmark Bias Correction via Vision-Dependent Constraints**  
Existing benchmarks neglect visual constraints, permitting models to exploit textual priors. VC-IFEval introduces vision-dependent constraints (spatial, attribute, comparative) and uses a hybrid evaluation that penalizes responses unchanged when the image is removed. This forces models to ground answers in visual evidence. Break condition: if constraints can be satisfied without the image, the benchmark fails.

**Causal Mechanism 2: Data-Driven Grounding via VC-IFEngine**  
The VC-IFEngine pipeline (image filtering → task generation → constraint generation) systematically creates (image, task, constraint) triples where constraints are directly tied to salient visual content. This trains models to bind textual constraint types to concrete visual referents, improving grounding. Break condition: inconsistency between constraints and image content degrades performance.

**Causal Mechanism 3: Preference Learning with Contrastive Visual Signals**  
VC-IFDPO constructs preference pairs by ablating constraints or editing images (e.g., removing key entities). The DPO loss then penalizes responses that ignore constraints or rely on perturbed visuals, sharpening the model's reward for faithful multimodal reasoning. Break condition: noisy pairs or trivial ablations undermine the contrastive signal.

## Foundational Learning
- **Instruction Following (IF) in LLMs/MLLMs**: The ability to correctly interpret and execute user instructions; in multimodal contexts, this requires understanding both text and visual intent, not just syntactic pattern matching. Quick check: What distinguishes satisfying an instruction's textual structure from its multimodal intent? (e.g., "Describe the leftmost object" requires spatial grounding, not just naming objects.)
- **Vision-Language Grounding**: Linking linguistic expressions to specific image regions or attributes. Required here because constraints like "spatial arrangement" demand referential grounding. Quick check: In the ski resort example, what visual evidence supports the "spatial arrangement" constraint? (e.g., actual positions of skiers, lifts, slopes.)
- **Benchmark Construction & Evaluation Validity**: Designing benchmarks that isolate the skill of interest and control for confounders. Here, VC-IFEval's comparative evaluation removes the image to detect textual prior reliance. Quick check: Why does comparing outputs with vs. without an image reveal dependence on visual input? A "Not influenced" judgment means the model ignored the image.

## Architecture Onboarding
Component map: Raw images (filtered by IC9600 and RAM) → VC-IFEngine pipeline → VC-IFInstruct (SFT) and VC-IFDPO (DPO) → Model fine-tuning (SFT/DPO) → Evaluation on VC-IFEval (and others).

Critical path: constrained data generation → contrastive preference learning → robust evaluation isolating vision-centric IF.

Design tradeoffs:
- Constraint diversity vs. coherence: More types improve coverage but risk contradictions; mitigated by quality judgment.
- Automatic vs. human validation: Fully automated pipeline is scalable but requires human checks (84% SFT, 80% DPO validity) to ensure quality.
- Evaluation cost vs. reliability: GPT-4o as judge is expensive but necessary; rule-based methods infeasible for open-ended constraints.

Failure signatures:
- Model-level: High MM-IFEval but low VC-IFEval → textual prior reliance.
- Data-level: Low human validation rate (<80%) → constraint-task-image incoherence.
- Evaluation-level: Low inter-annotator agreement → unstable evaluation.
- Training-level: DPO with full image removal underperforms image edit → model collapses to textual mode.

First 3 experiments:
1. Baseline assessment: Evaluate base Qwen2.5-VL-7B and LLaVA-NeXT on VC-IFEval, MM-IFEval, and VQA benchmarks.
2. SFT validation: Fine-tune on VC-IFInstruct-10k and measure VC-IFEval gains and VQA retention.
3. DPO ablation: Compare DPO variants (constraint ablation percentages, image-edit ratio) to identify most effective contrastive signal.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- How can visual instruction following evaluation be made more efficient and objective without relying on expensive models like GPT-4o?
- How should the number and types of visual constraints be adaptively determined based on image content to optimize instruction following without over-restriction?
- What are the practical applications and deployment challenges of vision-centric instruction following in real-world domains like education or healthcare?
- Does the predefined constraint pool in VC-IFEngine sufficiently cover the spectrum of visual reasoning needed for diverse instruction-following tasks?

## Limitations
- Evaluation judge opacity: Unreported GPT-4o prompt details and configuration risk bias.
- Data generation fidelity: VC-IFEngine prompts, thresholds, and quality judgment specifics are unspecified, hindering replication.
- Contrastive signal integrity: Image editing parameters (Stable Diffusion settings) and ablation strategies may produce inconsistent rejected responses, weakening DPO's effectiveness.

## Confidence
- Benchmark Bias Correction (VC-IFEval isolates vision-dependence): Medium
- Data-Driven Grounding (VC-IFEngine yields grounded instructions): Medium
- Preference Learning (DPO with visual edits improves alignment): High
