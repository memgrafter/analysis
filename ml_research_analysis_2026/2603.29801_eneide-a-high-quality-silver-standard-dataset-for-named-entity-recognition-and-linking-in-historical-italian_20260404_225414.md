# ENEIDE: A High Quality Silver Standard Dataset for Named Entity Recognition and Linking in Historical Italian

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2603.29801
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.29801
- **Reference count:** 10
- **Primary result:** First multi-domain silver standard dataset for historical Italian NERL (2,111 documents, 8,000+ annotations); fine-tuned GLiNER achieves NER F1 of 0.782 (Leopardi) and 0.876 (Moro), far exceeding zero-shot LLM performance.

## Executive Summary
This paper introduces ENEIDE, the first large-scale dataset for Named Entity Recognition and Linking (NERL) in historical Italian, addressing a severe resource gap. The dataset is derived semi-automatically from two scholarly digital editions (Leopardi's *Zibaldone* and Aldo Moro's works), leveraging their existing manual markup of named entities. After quality control and a targeted enhancement pipeline, ENEIDE provides standardized train/dev/test splits across two distinct domains (literary-philosophical and political-legal). Baseline experiments show that a fine-tuned domain-specific model (GLiNER) dramatically outperforms zero-shot large language models, demonstrating both the challenge of the historical domain and the value of specialized resources.

## Method Summary
ENEIDE is constructed by parsing HTML/RDFa annotations from two scholarly digital editions (DZ and AMD). For DZ, entities are extracted from HTML `<a>` tags; for AMD, from RDFa `span` elements with `class` attributes indicating type and `owl:sameAs` for linking. The raw extraction is followed by length filtering and a stratified temporal split. Quality is assessed via expert review of random samples (100 documents per partition). The AMD partition, initially lower in recall, undergoes a semi-automatic enhancement: frequently validated entity strings are propagated via exact matching, and a general NER model (Stanza) proposes additional candidates for expert validation. The final dataset contains 2,111 documents and over 8,000 annotations across four types (PER, LOC, ORG, WORK), with NIL entities for linking.

## Key Results
- ENEIDE provides the first multi-domain NERL dataset for historical Italian, with rigorous train/dev/test splits.
- Fine-tuned GLiNER substantially outperforms zero-shot LLMs: F1 scores of 0.782 (DZ) and 0.876 (AMD).
- Quality control yields high precision: assessed F1 of 95.6 for DZ and 79.8 for AMD, reflecting the silver standard nature and enhancement impact.
- The dataset highlights domain-specific challenges: DZ (literary) has higher NIL rate (7%) and lower entity type overlap with training, while AMD (political-legal) shows more contemporary entities.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
**Claim:** Structured markup in Scholarly Digital Editions (SDEs) enables semi-automatic extraction of high-quality named entity annotations.  
**How:** Expert editors in SDEs manually encode named entities as hyperlinks (HTML `<a>` in DZ) or RDFa `span` elements (in AMD) with attributes for entity type and linkage (e.g., `owl:sameAs`). A parser traverses these elements to extract `(mention, type, identifier)` triples directly, converting human-curated annotations into a structured dataset without re-annotating from raw text.  
**Evidence:** Extraction produced 2,111 documents with 8,000+ annotations. Parser details are in Section 3.1; DZ uses HTML links, AMD uses RDFa spans.  
**Break condition:** Inconsistent or non-standard markup in the SDE will cause missed annotations or misclassifications, requiring manual correction.

### Mechanism 2
**Claim:** A hybrid quality control and enhancement pipeline improves recall while preserving precision in the extracted data.  
**How:** First, experts validate a random sample to establish a baseline quality metric (F1). For the AMD partition with lower recall (70.6%), a semi-automatic enhancement is applied: frequently validated entity strings are matched exactly across the corpus; a general NER model (Stanza) proposes additional candidates; experts then validate these additions. This combines efficient pattern matching with expert oversight.  
**Evidence:** Section 3.2 reports the enhancement increased annotation completeness. Final F1 on sample: 79.8 for AMD vs. 95.6 for DZ.  
**Break condition:** If entities are mostly singletons or exhibit high orthographic variation, exact matching recovers few missing mentions. If Stanza is poorly adapted to historical Italian, it may propose many false positives, diluting precision despite validation.

### Mechanism 3
**Claim:** The diachronic and domain diversity of ENEIDE creates a challenging benchmark that exposes the domain adaptation gap between zero-shot general-purpose models and fine-tuned specialized models.  
**How:** The dataset combines two centuries (19th/20th) and two genres (philosophical-literary vs. political-legal), with entities from classical antiquity to contemporary figures. Zero-shot LLMs, trained on contemporary web text, lack specific knowledge of historical contexts, abbreviations, and literary metonymy. Fine-tuning on ENEIDE’s silver standard allows a smaller model (GLiNER) to learn domain-specific patterns and entity priors, closing the performance gap.  
**Evidence:** Section 4.1 shows GLiNER (fine-tuned) achieving F1 0.782 (DZ) / 0.876 (AMD) versus zero-shot LLMs maxing at 0.340/0.656. Table 3 shows unique Wikidata ID overlap: 93.19% for DZ (high repetition of core classical works/authors) vs. 75.38% for AMD (more diverse contemporary entities).  
**Break condition:** Leaky temporal splits (same historical figures in train/test) may overestimate generalization. Silver standard noise in specific entity types could lead to learning incorrect patterns.

## Foundational Learning
- **Silver Standard vs. Gold Standard Annotations**  
  *Why needed:* ENEIDE is explicitly silver standard, created by extracting and enhancing existing SDE annotations rather than de novo expert annotation. Residual noise from extraction/enhancement is a confounding factor for interpreting model performance.  
  *Quick check:* What are the principal sources of noise in a silver standard dataset derived from SDE markup, and how might that noise differ from gold standard human annotation error?

- **Scholarly Digital Editions (SDEs) as Annotation Sources**  
  *Why needed:* The entire dataset construction depends on the structure and editorial practices of the two source SDEs (Digital Zibaldone, Aldo Moro Digitale). Understanding their encoding (HTML links vs. RDFa) is crucial for reproduction or adaptation to other SDEs.  
  *Quick check:* How does the use of RDFa in AMD versus plain HTML links in DZ impact the robustness and complexity of the extraction script?

- **Entity Linking with NIL Entities in Historical Contexts**  
  *Why needed:* Historical texts contain entities absent from modern knowledge graphs (Wikidata). The inclusion of NIL entities (7% in DZ, 2% in AMD) is critical for realistic historical NERL, requiring models to predict when disambiguation is impossible.  
  *Quick check:* Why might the NIL rate be higher in DZ (literary/philosophical) than AMD (political/legal), and what does that imply for EL system design?

## Architecture Onboarding
1. Ingestion & Parsing -> 2. Sampling & Splitting -> 3. Quality Control Loop -> (4. Enhancement Pipeline if needed) -> 5. Dataset Export

**Critical path:** Parsing → Filtering → Splitting → Quality Control → (Enhancement if needed) → Export. Enhancement cannot precede initial extraction and quality assessment.

**Design tradeoffs:**
- *Automation vs. Expert Effort:* Extraction is highly automated but SDE-dependent; enhancement uses cheap automation (string match, Stanza) but requires expert validation to avoid drift.
- *Domain Specificity vs. Generalization:* Using two SDEs provides diachronic breadth but limits genre diversity; future extensions require adapting parsers to new SDE schemas.
- *Silver Standard Noise:* Accepting some noise for scalability; quality control samples only ~9.5% of documents, so full-dataset noise is estimated, not measured exhaustively.

**Failure signatures:**
- Low annotation count per document (<1) indicates over-filtering or extraction failure.
- Disproportionate NIL rate in one split may indicate Wikidata coverage bias or parsing bug.
- Extreme entity type imbalance (e.g., WORK only in DZ) may cause models to ignore minority classes without class-weighted loss.
- Low train/test ID overlap (<70%) signals severe domain shift; fine-tuned model may not generalize.

**First 3 experiments:**
1. Reproduce extraction: Run the parsing script on one HTML file from each SDE; verify extracted triples match visible hyperlinks.
2. Validate quality sample: Re-annotate 20 random documents from each partition using the guidelines; compute agreement (F1) with provided ENEIDE annotations to sanity-check 95.6/79.8 figures.
3. Baseline replication: Fine-tune GLiNER on the combined ENEIDE train split (DZ+AMD) and evaluate on corresponding test splits; target DZ F1 ~0.78 and AMD F1 ~0.88.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
1. **Systematic extraction from diverse SDEs:** How can we systematically extract and transform annotations from diverse Scholarly Digital Editions into reusable, standardized NERL datasets? (Basis: Introduction notes the challenge of transforming SDE annotations into reusable datasets.)
2. **Robustness to SDE evolution:** How can extraction pipelines be made robust to structural changes in evolving digital editions over time? (Basis: Section 3.1 notes that annotation structures are subject to change, necessitating adaptable strategies.)
3. **Enhancement consistency:** To what extent does the semi-automatic annotation enhancement pipeline applied to AMD affect consistency compared to the manually curated DZ partition? (Basis: Section 7 suggests subtle quality differences may exist due to semi-automatic additions.)
4. **Extension to broader varieties:** How can ENEIDE be extended to broader historical Italian varieties and domains to improve generalizability of NERL systems? (Basis: Section 7 states the dataset covers only two authors and two specific domains, calling for expansion.)

## Limitations
- **Silver standard noise:** Residual errors from semi-automatic extraction and enhancement (especially AMD’s lower F1=79.8) may propagate to models; full-dataset noise is estimated from a small (200-doc) quality sample.
- **Training and evaluation ambiguity:** Unclear whether DZ/AMD were concatenated or trained separately; exact GLiNER hyperparameters and handling of mismatched entity types (e.g., WORK) are unspecified.
- **Generalization scope:** Dataset spans only two SDEs (literary vs. political-legal); findings may not transfer to other historical genres or languages.

2. **Replicate GLiNER fine-tuning with ablations:** Train with reported hyperparameters (or a grid) on the concatenated train split; if classifier dimension mismatches occur, test both reinitialization and type mapping strategies.
