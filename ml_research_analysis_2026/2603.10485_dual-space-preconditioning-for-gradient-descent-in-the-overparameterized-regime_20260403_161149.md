# Dual Space Preconditioning for Gradient Descent in the Overparameterized Regime

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2603.10485
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.10485
- **Reference count:** 40
- **Primary result:** Dual-space preconditioned GD converges to an interpolating solution; for isotropic preconditioners it yields the minimum Euclidean norm solution, while for general preconditioners it is within a constant factor of GD's solution but may depend on the learning rate.

## Executive Summary
This paper investigates gradient descent with preconditioning in the dual space (i.e., preconditioning the gradient directly) for overparameterized linear models with convex separable losses. The authors introduce a novel adjusted Bregman divergence that enables proving convergence of the iterates to a solution $W_\infty$ satisfying $XW_\infty = Y$, even when the loss lacks a unique minimizer. For isotropic preconditioners of the form $K(G)=h(\|G\|_F)$ (e.g., Normalized GD, Gradient Clipping), they show that $W_\infty$ is exactly the minimum Euclidean norm interpolator, and convergence is linear. For more general preconditioners (e.g., Adam), they prove that the distance between $W_\infty$ and the solution found by standard GD is bounded by a constant factor, under additional convexity assumptions. However, empirical results reveal that for non-isotropic preconditioners the limit point can depend on the learning rate, complicating a clean implicit bias characterization.

## Method Summary
The paper's empirical evaluation focuses on synthetic overparameterized linear regression. Data matrices $X \in \mathbb{R}^{n \times d}$ with $n < d$ and labels $Y$ are generated (details unspecified). The loss is the squared loss $L(W) = \frac{1}{2}\|XW - Y\|_F^2$. Optimizers include standard GD and dual-space preconditioned variants such as Normalized GD, Gradient Clipping, and Adam (with entrywise preconditioning). Each optimizer is run from a random initialization $W_0$ until the gradient norm falls below a tolerance or a maximum number of iterations is reached. The converged weight matrix $W_\infty$ is then compared to: (i) the solution of standard GD ($W_{\text{GD},\infty}$), and (ii) norm-minimizing interpolants $W_p = \arg\min\{\|W - W_0\|_p : XW=Y\}$ for $p=1,2,\infty$. Key metrics are the Euclidean distances $\|W_\infty - W_p\|_2$ and $\|W_\infty - W_{\text{GD},\infty}\|_2$. Experiments systematically vary learning rates and preconditioner hyperparameters (e.g., $\epsilon$ for Adam) to probe the dependence of $W_\infty$ on these settings, thereby testing the theoretical predictions about implicit bias.

## Key Results
- **Convergence to interpolation:** Under suitable assumptions (convexity of $L^* - \eta K$, etc.), dual-space preconditioned GD always converges to a solution $W_\infty$ with $XW_\infty = Y$.
- **Isotropic bias:** When the preconditioner is isotropic ($K(G)=h(\|G\|_F)$ with $h$ strictly convex and $h'(0)=0$), $W_\infty$ is the unique minimizer of $\|W - W_0\|_F^2$ subject to $XW=Y$; it coincides with the limit of standard GD and converges linearly.
- **General preconditioners:** For arbitrary convex $K$ satisfying $K - \alpha L^*$ convex on the relevant set, the distance $\|W_0 - W_\infty\|_F$ is at most a constant factor $c$ times $\|W_0 - W_{\text{GD},\infty}\|_F$. However, empirical results show that for non-isotropic $K$ (e.g., Adam) the limit $W_\infty$ can depend on the learning rate $\eta$, indicating the bound may be loose or the bias not fully captured.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1: Adjusted Bregman Divergence for Convergence
The core of the convergence proof is the introduction of an *adjusted Bregman divergence* $\tilde{D}_L(A,B)$ that combines the Fenchel dual $L^*$ with a gradient alignment term. This divergence is designed so that for the update $W_i = W_{i-1} - \eta \nabla K(\nabla L(W_{i-1}))$, one obtains an exact decomposition (Proposition 1):
\[
\tilde{D}_L(W_{i+1}, W_i) - \eta D_K(\nabla L(W_i), \nabla L(W_{i+1})) \ge 0,
\]
which telescopes across iterations. Summing yields $\sum_i K(\nabla L(W_i)) < \infty$. Under the assumption that $K$ is convex, differentiable, and satisfies $K(A)=0 \Rightarrow \nabla K(A)=0$, this forces $\nabla L(W_i) \to 0$, and thus $XW_\infty = Y$. The key assumptions include convexity of $L^* - \eta K$ (Assumption 1.3) and that $L$ is convex with a point satisfying $\nabla L(W^*)=0$. If these fail—e.g., if $L^* - \eta K$ is non-convex or $K$ does not have the zero-gradient property—the non-negativity term may not hold, breaking the argument.

### Mechanism 2: Isotropic Preconditioners Yield Min-Norm Bias
For isotropic preconditioners $K(G)=h(\|G\|_F)$, the gradient $\nabla K(\nabla L)$ is proportional to $\nabla L$. This proportionality, together with the adjusted Bregman divergence, implies that the iterates follow a path that, in the limit, satisfies the optimality conditions of the Euclidean projection of $W_0$ onto the interpolation manifold $\{W : XW=Y\}$. More formally, under strict convexity of $h$, $h'(0)=0$, and per-coordinate $\mu$-strong convexity of the loss, one can show via KKT conditions that $W_\infty$ minimizes $\|W - W_0\|_F^2$ subject to $XW=Y$. This explains why Normalized GD and Gradient Clipping, which are isotropic, exhibit a clean min-norm bias. The assumptions are natural: $h$ strictly convex ensures uniqueness, $h'(0)=0$ prevents bias when the gradient is zero, and strong convexity of each sample’s loss controls curvature. Violating these (e.g., non-strict $h$ or $h'(0)\neq0$) would alter the bias.

### Mechanism 3: General Preconditioners Are Close to GD
When $K$ is not isotropic, the implicit bias is more complex. The paper shows that under the additional condition that $K - \alpha L^*$ is convex on the set $S = \{X^T L\}$ for some $\alpha>0$, and with other smoothness/strong convexity assumptions, the distance between the preconditioned iterate $W_i$ and a corresponding GD iterate $\hat{W}_i$ remains bounded. By further relating $\hat{W}_i$ to the true GD limit $W_{\text{GD},\infty}$, they prove $\|W_0 - W_\infty\|_F \le c \|W_0 - W_{\text{GD},\infty}\|_F$ for a constant $c$ independent of dimension. The proof uses a Lipschitz bound on $\nabla K$ and an exponential decay of the loss for the auxiliary sequence. The convexity of $K - \alpha L^*$ on $S$ is crucial; without it, the exponential decay fails and the bound may not hold. Moreover, the constant $c$ depends on $\eta$ and the condition number, and empirically $W_\infty$ can vary with $\eta$, suggesting the bound may be loose or that the true bias is more nuanced.

## Foundational Learning
- **Adjusted Bregman Divergence $\tilde{D}_f$**  
  *Why needed:* Standard Bregman divergences require $f$ to be convex and often strongly convex; here the loss $L$ is not strongly convex in the overparameterized regime. $\tilde{D}_L$ uses the Fenchel dual $L^*$ to measure discrepancy in gradient space, enabling an exact telescoping sum even when $L$ has a flat direction.  
  *Quick check:* How does $\tilde{D}_L(A,B)$ differ from the usual $D_f(A,B)=f(A)-f(B)-\langle\nabla f(B),A-B\rangle$, and why does the adjusted version handle non-strongly-convex $L$?

- **Fenchel Conjugation and Duality**  
  *Why needed:* The adjusted divergence and the convexity condition $L^* - \eta K$ rely on $L^*$, the convex conjugate of $L$. This links function values and gradients, allowing the identity in Proposition 1 that is key to the convergence proof.  
  *Quick check:* What does the relation $B \in \partial L^*(\nabla L(B))$ (Lemma 2) tell us about the connection between $L$ and $L^*$ at points where $\nabla L$ is evaluated?

- **Implicit Bias in Overparameterization**  
  *Why needed:* When $XW=Y$ has infinitely many solutions, the optimization algorithm implicitly selects one. This selection (bias) influences generalization; for linear models, min-norm solutions are known to generalize well. Understanding the bias of preconditioned GD is therefore important.  
  *Quick check:* Why does minimizing $\|W - W_0\|_F$ among interpolating solutions often lead to better generalization in overparameterized linear regression?

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map:**  
  Input: Data $(X, Y)$, initialization $W_0$. Compute gradient: $G = \nabla L(W) = \ell'(XW - Y) X^T$ (for separable losses). Apply preconditioner: $P = \nabla K(G)$. Update: $W \gets W - \eta P$. Repeat until $\|G\|_F$ small. Output: $W_\infty$ with $XW_\infty = Y$.

- **Critical path:**  
  1. Verify assumptions: Check strong convexity of per-sample losses $\ell_{\cdot,j}$, smoothness of $L$, convexity of $K$ and $L^* - \eta K$.  
  2. Choose preconditioner type: Isotropic (e.g., $K(G)=h(\|G\|_F)$) for min-norm guarantee; non-isotropic (e.g., Adam) for adaptive steps.  
  3. Set learning rate $\eta$: Must satisfy $\eta < n / L_K$ generally; for isotropic, optimal $\eta^*$ can improve rate.  
  4. Monitor gradient norm $\|\nabla L(W_i)\|_F$: Should decay exponentially if $K - \alpha L^*$ convex.

- **Design tradeoffs:**  
  - *Isotropic vs. non-isotropic:* Isotropic gives clean implicit bias (min-norm) but may be less adaptive; non-isotropic adapts per-coordinate, potentially faster early training but with opaque bias and possible $\eta$-dependence.  
  - *Learning rate:* Smaller $\eta$ ensures convexity conditions but slows convergence; larger $\eta$ may violate assumptions and cause $\eta$-dependent bias.  
  - *Preconditioner shape:* The form of $K$ directly influences the limit point; understanding $K$'s geometry is crucial.

- **Failure signatures:**  
  - Gradient norm does not decay: Possibly $L^* - \eta K$ non-convex or $\eta$ too large; check assumptions.  
  - $W_\infty$ not interpolating: Compute $\|XW_\infty - Y\|$; if large, increase iterations or reduce $\eta$.  
  - $\eta$-dependent bias for isotropic $K$: Should not happen; indicates violation of $h'(0)=0$ or strong convexity.  
  - Instability/divergence: For Adam-like, ensure $\eta < n\epsilon$ roughly; adjust $\epsilon$.

- **First 3 experiments:**  
  1. *Isotropic validation:* Use Normalized GD on synthetic linear regression; verify $W_\infty$ equals the min Euclidean norm interpolator (computed via pseudoinverse) and is closer to it than to GD's solution.  
  2. *Learning rate sensitivity:* For Adam ($K(G)=\sum_j (\epsilon+|G_j|)\log(\epsilon+|G_j|)$), vary $\eta$ and measure $\|W_\infty(\eta) - W_\infty(\eta_{\text{ref}})\|_F$; expect dependence per Fig. 2.  
  3. *General preconditioner bound:* Use a diagonal non-isotropic $K$ (e.g., $K(G)=\sum_i \kappa_i |G_i|$ with varying $\kappa_i$); compute ratio $c = \|W_0 - W_\infty\|_F / \|W_0 - W_{\text{GD},\infty}\|_F$; should be bounded by the constant from Theorem 2 and not grow with dimension.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
### Open Question 1  
- **Question:** For non‑isotropic preconditioners, under which precise conditions on the preconditioner function $K$ and the loss is the convergence point $W_\infty$ independent of the learning rate $\eta$?  
- **Basis in paper:** Theorem 1 includes the remark: “Whether $W_\infty$ is independent of the learning rate $\eta$ requires further investigation.”  
- **Why unresolved:** The paper proves independence for isotropic $K$ and standard GD, but empirically observes $\eta$‑dependence for general $K$ (e.g., Adam without momentum). No theoretical criterion exists to predict or guarantee independence.  
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Derivation of necessary and sufficient conditions (e.g., relating $K$ to the Fenchel dual $L^*$) that force $W_\infty$ to solve a fixed optimization problem regardless of $\eta$. Alternatively, a proof that $\eta$‑dependence is generic for non‑isotropic $K$.

### Open Question 2  
- **Question:** What exact optimization problem does $W_\infty$ solve for an arbitrary convex preconditioner $K$ (i.e., what is the implicit bias of dual‑space preconditioned GD in the overparameterized regime)?  
- **Basis in paper:** Abstract: “Empirically, the limit depends on the chosen learning rate, hindering a precise characterization of the implicit bias.” Conclusion: “providing the exact characterization of the point of convergence for non‑isotropic preconditioners.”  
- **Why unresolved:** For isotropic $K$, $W_\infty$ minimizes $\|W - W_0\|_F^2$ subject to $XW = Y$. For general $K$, only a distance bound $\|W_0 - W_\infty\|_F \le c \|W_0 - W_{\text{GD},\infty}\|_F$ is given, which does not uniquely identify $W_\infty$.  
- **What evidence would resolve it:** A variational principle (e.g., minimization of a Bregman‑type divergence or a potential involving $K$ and $L^*$) that holds for any $K$ meeting the assumptions. Or a counterexample showing no such simple characterization exists.

### Open Question 3  
- **Question:** Do the convergence and implicit bias results extend to non‑smooth preconditioners (e.g., those used in Muon or Shampoo), and how do they differ?  
- **Basis in paper:** Conclusion: “Future directions include extending the current analysis to non‑smooth preconditioners which would incorporate algorithms such as Muon [15].”  
- **Why unresolved:** The theory requires $K$ to be differentiable with a Lipschitz gradient (Assumptions 1.1, 1.6). Non‑smooth preconditioners violate these, so the proof technique based on the Adjusted Bregman Divergence may not apply.  
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Development of a new analytic framework (e.g., using subgradients, proximal operators, or Moreau envelopes) to prove convergence for non‑smooth $K$, and identification of the corresponding implicit bias (likely a different solution on the interpolation manifold).

### Open Question 4  
- **Question:** What are the convergence guarantees and implicit bias of stochastic dual‑space preconditioned gradient descent (i.e., with mini‑batch or single‑sample gradients)?  
- **Basis in paper:** Conclusion: “adapting our proof of convergence to the stochastic preconditioned gradient descent.”  
- **Why unresolved:** The current analysis assumes exact gradients and relies on deterministic properties of $L$ and $K$. Stochastic noise introduces variance that may affect both the rate and the limit point, potentially altering the implicit bias.  
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Stochastic convergence analysis showing almost‑sure convergence or convergence to a neighborhood of $W_\infty$ under suitable step‑size schedules. Characterization of the stochastic implicit bias (e.g., in expectation or high probability) and comparison with the deterministic case.

## Limitations
- The convexity condition $L^* - \eta K$ is non‑trivial to verify and may be violated for common choices (e.g., Adam with large $\eta$), potentially breaking the convergence argument.
- The theoretical analysis assumes per‑coordinate $\mu$‑strong convexity of the losses, which fails for non‑smooth losses (e.g., hinge) and is unrealistic in many deep learning settings.
- For non‑isotropic preconditioners, the limit $W_\infty$ exhibits empirical $\eta$‑dependence, and the constant‑factor bound $\|W_0 - W_\infty\|_F \le c \|W_0 - W_{\text{GD},\infty}\|_F$ may be loose; the true implicit bias is not fully characterized.

## Confidence
- **Adjusted Bregman divergence convergence (Proposition 1):** Medium  
- **Isotropic preconditioner min‑norm bias:** High  
- **General preconditioner constant‑factor bound (Theorem 2):** Medium

Test whether $c$ remains bounded as predicted or grows, indicating looser bounds.
