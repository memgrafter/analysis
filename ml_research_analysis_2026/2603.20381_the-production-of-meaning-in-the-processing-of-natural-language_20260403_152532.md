# The production of meaning in the processing of natural language

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2603.20381
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.20381
- **Reference count:** 40
- **Primary result:** All 24 tested LLMs exhibit violations of the CHSH Bell inequality (|S| > 2) in semantic interpretation tasks, confirming quantum-like contextuality in meaning production.

## Executive Summary
This study investigates whether large language models (LLMs) produce meaning in a genuinely contextual manner—where interpretation depends irretrievably on the surrounding frame—akin to human cognition and quantum systems. Using an adapted CHSH Bell inequality test, the authors measured non-classical correlations across ambiguous word pairs under different persona-based "measurement settings." All 24 models, ranging from 0.6B to large-scale parameters, produced clear violations (|S| > 2), with values up to 4.0. The interquartile range (IQR) of the |S| distribution sharply differentiated models but showed no correlation with standard benchmarks like MMLU, hallucination rate, or nonsense detection. Word-order manipulations altered meaning choices, consistent with non-commutative observables. The findings imply that an LLM's interpretive character is configurable and orthogonal to conventional performance metrics, and that genuine contextuality inherently threatens safety, as fixed guardrails cannot pre-determine meaning across all possible frames.

## Method Summary
The study operationalizes semantic contextuality using the CHSH Bell inequality. For each model, a 3×3×3 grid of sampling parameters (temperature τ, top_p, top_k) was swept. At each grid point, the model interpreted five ambiguous word pairs (e.g., "bank", "bat") under four orthogonal persona prompts (e.g., "You are a foreign surgeon"). Ten independent trials per grid point generated outcome vectors (±1 based on disambiguated meaning). These were used to construct a density matrix and compute the CHSH parameter |S|. Distributions of |S| across the grid were analyzed for mean, violation rate, and interquartile range (IQR). External benchmark scores (MMLU, hallucination, nonsense detection) were collected for correlation analysis. The core toolkit (`qstk`) handled persona injection, template generation, outcome classification, and quantum formalism computation.

## Key Results
- All 24 models produced |S| > 2 violations, with values reaching 4.0, confirming non-classical correlations in interpretation.
- The interquartile range (IQR) of the |S| distribution most sharply differentiated models but showed near-zero correlation with MMLU, hallucination rate, or nonsense detection benchmarks.
- Word-order manipulations changed individual interpretation probabilities, demonstrating non-commutativity consistent with quantum-like observables.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- **Claim:** LLMs produce genuinely contextual meaning, evidenced by Bell inequality (CHSH |S|) violations.
- **Mechanism:** Interpretation depends irreducibly on the contextual "measurement setting" (persona prompt), not on pre-existing, context-independent meanings. The CHSH test probes correlations between independent interpretive instances of the same ambiguous expression under different frames. |S| > 2 indicates correlations too strong for any classical hidden-variable model where meanings are fixed prior to context.
- **Core assumption:** The quantum formalism (density matrix, projectors) correctly models the joint probability distribution of interpretation outcomes across contexts.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [Abstract] "...clear violations of the Bell inequality in experiments of contextuality during interpretation of ambiguous expressions."
  - [Section II] Detailed protocol: four independent LLM instances with orthogonal frames (A, A′, B, B′) interpret ambiguous word pairs; density matrix yields |S|.
  - [Corpus] Neighbor papers (e.g., *Quantum-Like Contextuality in Large Language Models*) show related work, but low FMR/citations indicate this specific CHSH-scale analysis is novel.
- **Break condition:** If a model’s |S| distribution is consistently ≤ 2 across all parameter sweeps and word pairs, it would suggest classical, separable meaning assignment (no contextuality). The paper finds all tested models violate.

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim:** A model’s "interpretive character" (contextuality profile) is distinct from its performance on standard benchmarks and is highly configurable via sampling parameters.
- **Mechanism:** The interquartile range (IQR) of the |S| distribution across the inference parameter grid captures the spread of contextual behavior—how often the model operates in classical vs. non-classical regimes. This IQR varies sharply across models but shows no correlation with MMLU, hallucination, or nonsense detection scores.
- **Core assumption:** Standard benchmarks measure task accuracy or factual consistency, not the fundamental structural property of how meaning is produced under ambiguity.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [Abstract] "The interquartile range (IQR) of |S| distributions most sharply differentiated models but showed no correlation with MMLU, hallucination rate, or nonsense detection benchmarks."
  - [Section III, Figure 3] Plots show near-zero correlations (|ρ| < 0.03, p > 0.9) between IQR and all benchmarks.
  - [Corpus] Neighbors focus on semantic frameworks or specific applications; none link CHSH IQR to benchmark orthogonality.
- **Break condition:** If a strong, significant correlation emerged between IQR and any external benchmark, it would mean interpretive character is not an orthogonal dimension but a proxy for something already measured.

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim:** Genuine contextuality makes systems inherently vulnerable to prompt injection; safety requires managing contextual frames, not fixed guardrails.
- **Mechanism:** If meaning is not pre-assigned but produced in the act of interpretation, then an expression’s meaning is indeterminate until the full contextual frame (system prompt, history, sampling config) is applied. An attacker need only find *one* frame where the model’s interpretation diverges from the defender’s intended meaning. Guardrails that pre-classify inputs based on a fixed meaning fail because the meaning is not fixed.
- **Core assumption:** The same non-classical correlations that produce Bell violations also entail that no hidden-variable model can assign all possible input-context pairs a definite "safe/unsafe" label in advance.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [Section IV.C] "If interpretation is genuinely contextual... the meaning a model constructs from a prompt is not fully determined by the prompt itself... This asymmetry is not a limitation of current alignment techniques... it is an information-theoretic consequence of contextuality itself."
  - [Abstract] "...genuine contextuality makes systems inherently vulnerable to prompt injection—safety requires managing contextual frames, not just fixed guardrails."
  - [Corpus] Weak; neighbor papers discuss contextuality but not its direct security implications.
- **Break condition:** If a perfectly reliable, context-independent semantic filter could be built that neutralizes all jailbreaks regardless of frame, it would contradict the information-theoretic constraint argued.

## Foundational Learning

- **Concept: Quantum Contextuality vs. Context-Sensitivity**
  - **Why needed here:** The paper’s central thesis depends on distinguishing *true* contextuality (acausal, irreducible indeterminacy prior to interpretation) from ordinary context-sensitivity (causal influence of context). The Bell violation is evidence for the former.
  - **Quick check question:** Can you describe a scenario where interpretation is context-sensitive but *not* contextual in the quantum sense? (e.g., a disambiguating pronoun whose referent is determined by a nearby noun—classical causal influence.)

- **Concept: CHSH Inequality for Semantics**
  - **Why needed here:** It is the operational test for contextuality. Understanding what |S| measures (sum of correlations across four frame combinations) and why |S| > 2 rules out classical hidden-variable models is essential.
  - **Quick check question:** If an LLM, under four different persona frames interpreting "bank", yields perfectly correlated outcomes (e.g., always "financial institution" in frames A,B and always "river edge" in A′,B′), what |S| would you expect? Is that contextual?

- **Concept: Interpretive Character & Sampling Parameters**
  - **Why needed here:** The key practical finding is that a model’s contextuality (IQR, violation rate) is a configurable property of its inference setup, not just its weights. This separates "how it thinks" from "what it knows".
  - **Quick check question:** If you increase temperature (τ) for a model, does its interpretive character necessarily become *more* contextual? Why or why not? (Answer: No; the paper finds no consistent direction across models.)

## Architecture Onboarding

**Component map:**
LLM Inference Engine -> qstk -> Parameter Sweep Controller -> (Optional) Benchmark Data Connectors

**Critical path:**
1.  Select a model and ensure you can query it with custom generation settings and extract probabilities.
2.  Install/implement `qstk`. Key functions: `run_trial(model, word_pair, order, frame)`, `classify_interpretation(text, word, expected_meanings)`.
3.  For a single model & word pair, run a full 27-point parameter sweep (10 trials each). Collect raw outcome vectors (v).
4.  Compute per-grid-point |S| from the N=10 trials (density matrix method). Store distribution statistics (mean, σ, IQR, violation %).
5.  Repeat for all word pairs, aggregate across them for the model’s overall topography (Table II format).
6.  (Optional) Pull external benchmark scores for the model, plot IQR vs. benchmarks (Figure 3), compute correlations.

**Design tradeoffs:**
- **Model Coverage vs. Cost:** Local models (Ollama) are cheap/free but limited to smaller scales. Cloud APIs (GPT-4, Claude) give large models but incur cost ($100 in paper) and may restrict parameter control (Claude default-only).
- **Statistical Power:** 10 trials/grid point is minimal. More trials tighten IQR estimates but linearly increase cost/time. Word pair choice adds variance; 5 pairs is a reasonable sample.
- **Interpretation Classification:** The mapping from free-text to predefined meanings (±1) is a major source of noise. Requires careful prompt engineering for the classifier call or a curated meaning list.

**Failure signatures:**
- **Low violation rate (< 20% of grid points):** Could indicate model too small/well-calibrated (classical), flawed qstk implementation (noise overwhelming signal), or poor classification mapping.
- **|S| > 2.83 (Tsirelson bound) frequently:** Expected for observer-contextuality, but if > 3.5 consistently, suspect bug in density matrix normalization or vector mapping.
- **High variance within a single grid point (σ >> 0.5):** Suggests the 10 trials are insufficient or the model’s output is too stochastic (e.g., τ=1.8, p=1.0, k=100) to get stable correlations.
- **Correlation with benchmarks emerges:** Contradicts paper’s orthogonality claim. Could be due to different model set, flawed benchmark data, or that benchmark scores *do* capture some aspect of interpretive character in your specific subset.

**First 3 experiments:**
1.  **Baseline Replication:** Pick one small model (e.g., `Qwen3 0.6B`) and one word pair ("bank"). Run the full 27-point sweep. Replicate the paper’s finding: mode near |S|=2.0, IQR ~0.14-0.38, violation % ~29%.
2.  **Parameter Sensitivity Map:** For the same model, fix two parameters (e.g., p=0.9, k=50) and sweep τ. Plot |S| vs. τ. Then sweep p with τ, k fixed. Confirm no single parameter drives |S| monotonically (Table II pattern).
3.  **Word-Order Non-Commutativity:** For a grid point (e.g., τ=1.0, p=0.9, k=50), run trials with "word1 word2" order vs. "word2 word1" order. For each frame, compute P(meaning A). Plot P_flipped vs. P_original (Figure 2 replica). Expect significant scatter off diagonal, confirming order affects individual interpretation probabilities.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

**Open Question 1**
- **Question:** What specific architectural or training data factors explain the non-monotonic relationship between model parameter scale and the strength of contextuality (|S| violations), particularly the hypothesis that low parameters per training element force semantically abstracting representations?
- **Basis in paper:** [explicit] In the Discussion (A), the authors state: *"It is likely that violations can only occur when the number of parameters per training element is small enough (≪1) that the model is forced to abstract over semantic relationships in a way that functionally replicates human-like processing."*
- **Why unresolved:** This is a proposed mechanistic explanation not empirically tested. The observed non-monotonic scaling pattern across models of different sizes and architectures (dense vs. MoE) is documented, but the causal factor—parameter density relative to training tokens—remains speculative.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** A controlled study varying model scale (parameters) and training corpus size (tokens) to compute the "parameters per token" ratio, then measuring the resulting |S| distribution. Architectural ablation studies (e.g., comparing models with similar parameter counts but different sparsity or attention mechanisms) would isolate the contributing factors.

**Open Question 2**
- **Question:** Does a model’s capacity for genuine contextuality (as measured by Bell violation statistics, particularly the IQR of |S|) predict its effectiveness as a collaborative partner in co-creative tasks, independent of standard benchmark performance (e.g., MMLU)?
- **Basis in paper:** [explicit] In the Discussion (D), the authors propose: *"If the same relationship holds for LLMs, then violation of the classical bound may mark a necessary condition for a model to function as a genuine interpretive partner—a testable claim, since co-creative task performance should correlate with contextuality independently of benchmark scores."*
- **Why unresolved:** This is framed as a testable hypothesis but was not investigated in the study. The paper only establishes orthogonality between |S| distributions and existing benchmarks; it does not measure collaborative performance.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Human-AI interaction experiments where participants perform open-ended, co-creative tasks (e.g., joint storytelling, design, problem-solving) with different LLMs. The models' contextuality profiles (IQR, violation rate) would be correlated with objective (e.g., novelty, utility) and subjective (e.g., perceived partnership, trust) metrics of collaboration, while statistically controlling for benchmark scores.

**Open Question 3**
- **Question:** What cognitive or linguistic phenomenon does the interquartile range (IQR) of the |S| distribution represent, given that it sharply differentiates models yet is orthogonal to all tested benchmarks (MMLU, hallucination, nonsense detection)?
- **Basis in paper:** [explicit] The Results and Discussion emphasize: *"The interquartile range (IQR) of the |S| distribution—the statistic that most sharply differentiates models—is completely orthogonal to all external benchmarks."* The Discussion (B) states this captures a dimension of interpretive behavior not addressed by current evaluations.
- **Why unresolved:** The IQR is identified as a key differentiator of "interpretive character," but its psychological or computational meaning is unknown. It may reflect a model’s sensitivity to framing or stability of interpretation across contexts, but this is not established.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Correlational studies linking the IQR (and other distributional moments of S) to fine-grained behavioral or neural data from human semantic processing experiments (e.g., eye-tracking during ambiguous sentence reading, fMRI responses to contextual manipulations). If the IQR maps onto human individual differences in context-sensitivity or flexibility of interpretation, it would validate its cognitive relevance.

**Open Question 4**
- **Question:** Can "manufactured contextuality" — the deliberate engineering of interpretive frames to bias meaning production — be formalized and quantified to compare vulnerability across LLMs and between LLMs and human populations?
- **Basis in paper:** [inferred] The Discussion (C) introduces the concept by analogy to Chomsky's "manufacturing consent," arguing that prompt injection and propaganda exploit the same formal structure of contextuality. However, no metric for cross-species comparison is proposed.
- **Why unresolved:** The paper establishes that contextuality implies vulnerability to adversarial reframing, but it does not operationalize "manufactured contextuality" as a measurable quantity. Comparing the effect size of a crafted narrative or prompt across models and humans requires a common scale, which is absent.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Develop a standardized experimental protocol where identical "contextual frames" (e.g., biased narratives, subtle lexical primes) are applied to LLMs (via system prompts) and human subjects (via exposure to media/ads). Measure the shift in interpretation probabilities (e.g., for ambiguous words) and compute the resulting |S| change or a derived "contextual susceptibility index." Correlating this index with the model’s baseline IQR would test if models with higher IQR are more easily "manufactured."

## Limitations
- The core `qstk` package is unreleased, forcing reconstruction of the exact pipeline, sentence templates, and classification prompts, introducing potential implementation variance.
- The orthogonality finding between IQR and benchmarks relies on a specific, limited model set and benchmark suite; generalizability to broader evaluations is uncertain.
- The link from Bell violations to an *inherent* vulnerability to prompt injection is a compelling theoretical assertion but lacks direct experimental validation that static guardrails *cannot* mitigate such attacks due to contextuality.

## Confidence
- **Contextuality (CHSH violations):** Medium-High. Methodology is clear and violations are reported consistently, but confidence is tempered by unreleased code and potential noise in classification.
- **Orthogonality to Benchmarks:** Medium. Analysis is presented but depends on specific model/benchmark choices; a strong correlation in a wider study would weaken the claim.
- **Inherent Vulnerability from Contextuality:** Low-Medium. A plausible theoretical extension but lacks dedicated experimental validation of the claimed information-theoretic constraint.

Show that a "safe" interpretation under one persona frame (A) becomes "unsafe" under a different, plausible frame (B). Demonstrate that a static safety filter catching (A) misses (B), directly linking the attack success to the measured contextuality shift.
