# The Free-Market Algorithm: Self-Organizing Optimization for Open-Ended Complex Systems

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2603.24559
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.24559
- **Reference Count:** 17
- **Primary Result:** A single universal market engine discovered all 12 amino acids and key nucleobases from 900 atoms in under 5 minutes and achieved professional-forecaster-level GDP predictions without calibrated parameters, demonstrating cross-domain universality.

## Executive Summary
The Free-Market Algorithm (FMA) is a metaheuristic for open-ended optimization problems where the fitness function is emergent and the search space grows as new combinations become valid. It replaces a central optimizer with a universal market engine where autonomous agents trade, compete, and discover. Selection arises from market dynamics (creative destruction), and exploration is sustained by a persistent, ever-growing "recipe book" of valid transformations. The algorithm achieved its primary validations in two vastly different domains: prebiotic chemistry (discovering molecular synthesis pathways) and macroeconomics (predicting GDP from input-output tables) using identical core mechanisms, suggesting a new paradigm for self-organizing optimization in complex systems.

## Method Summary
FMA is structured in three layers: a universal, domain-agnostic market engine (Layer 1); a pluggable domain-specific rule set (Layer 2) providing `DomainCombine()` (how goods combine) and `DomainValidate()` (feasibility check); and a domain-specific observation layer (Layer 3) for metrics. Agents execute a five-phase cycle each simulation step: **Discover** (propose new recipes via domain rules), **Demand** (express preferences for goods, favoring higher assembly depth), **Trade & Produce** (buy inputs from neighbors and produce outputs), **Compete** (unprofitable firms close; idle agents may open new firms), and **Decay** (stochastic decomposition of goods). There is no external fitness function; a good's "fitness" is its sustained production and circulation. The persistent `RecipeBook` accumulates all valid recipes, enabling hierarchical, open-ended discovery via Kauffman's "adjacent possible."

## Key Results
- **Prebiotic Chemistry:** From 900 atoms (C, H, O, N), FMA discovered all 12 proteinogenic amino acids, 5 canonical nucleobases, formose sugars, and Krebs cycle intermediates within 5 minutes, generating up to 260 distinct synthesis routes per product.
- **Macroeconomic Forecasting:** Achieved a Mean Absolute Error (MAE) of 0.42 percentage points for non-crisis GDP prediction across 33 countries, matching professional forecasters, with **zero** calibrated parameters.
- **Cross-Domain Universality:** The identical, unmodified algorithm succeeded in both domains, demonstrating that the market-based mechanism generalizes across fundamentally different search landscapes.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1: Emergent Fitness via Market Dynamics
- **Claim:** FMA optimizes without a prescribed fitness function by letting fitness emerge from supply-and-demand interactions among autonomous agents.
- **Mechanism:** Agents express demand for goods (via GFCF preference for higher-depth items), creating a cascade of derived demand. Producers compete to sell outputs; unprofitable firms close; new firms enter to fill unmet demand. The sustained production and circulation of a good indicate its "fitness."
- **Core Assumption:** Agents act in self-interest using only local information (neighborhood trades, recent sales); aggregate market prices coordinate actions without central control.
- **Evidence Anchors:** Abstract: "Fitness is emergent... solutions take the form of complete hierarchical pathway networks." Section 3.3 (Trade, Compete phases) and 3.5 (Property 1). Corpus support is weak; related finance papers explore market dynamics but not FMA's specific mechanism.
- **Break Condition:** If all agents hold identical inventories or trade ceases, the demand signal disappears; the system collapses into a static state.

### Mechanism 2: Open-Ended Search via Persistent Memory
- **Claim:** FMA explores an ever-expanding search space because it maintains a monotonic recipe book that retains every validated combination, enabling hierarchical discovery of arbitrary depth.
- **Mechanism:** The `RecipeBook` grows monotonically; higher-depth products become accessible only after their prerequisite intermediate recipes have been discovered and remain available—realizing Kauffman’s "adjacent possible."
- **Core Assumption:** `DomainCombine` and `DomainValidate` are computable and allow novel combinations from existing goods; the recipe book is shared and accessible to all agents.
- **Evidence Anchors:** Abstract: "persistent memory (recipe book) for hierarchical discovery." Section 3.2 (Layer 2), 3.4 (pseudocode: `RecipeBook ∪=`), and 3.5 (Property 2).
- **Break Condition:** If the recipe book ceases to grow (no new valid products discovered), the system reaches a fixed point and cannot generate further novelty.

### Mechanism 3: Distributed Selection via Creative Destruction
- **Claim:** Selection occurs without a central arbiter through the entry and exit of firms (creative destruction), continuously reallocating resources toward productive activities.
- **Mechanism:** In the Compete phase, unprofitable producers close after a patience threshold; idle agents may launch new firms producing goods with unmet local demand. This turnover balances exploration (novel recipes) and exploitation (scaling successful firms).
- **Core Assumption:** Agents have limited memory but respond to recent profitability and local shortages; the neighborhood structure ensures spatial heterogeneity.
- **Evidence Anchors:** Abstract: "autonomous agents discover rules, trade goods, open and close firms... no centralized controller." Section 3.3 (Compete phase), 3.1 (Schumpeter’s Creative Destruction), 3.5 (Property 3).
- **Break Condition:** If (a) all agents become permanently idle or (b) no firms ever close, the adaptive filtering mechanism is lost, risking lock-in to suboptimal states.

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map:**  
  Layer 1 (Market Engine) -> Layer 2 (Domain Rules) -> Layer 3 (Observation)  
  Layer 1 is universal and immutable; Layer 2 is pluggable; Layer 3 is domain-specific and read-only.
- **Critical path:**  
  Main loop: for each step, all agents execute sequentially: (1) Discover (propose/validate new combos, update RecipeBook), (2) Demand (express GFCF preferences), (3) Trade & Produce (buy inputs, produce outputs), (4) Compete (close firms, open new ones), (5) Decay (stochastic decomposition). `RecipeBook` persists across all steps.
- **Design tradeoffs:**
  - **Discovery budget (τd) vs. production capacity (τp):** τd ≈ τp maximizes diversity and sustained production; τd << τp causes stagnation; τd >> τp causes combinatorial explosion ("Tar regime").
  - **Neighborhood size (k):** Small k increases spatial heterogeneity but limits trade efficiency; large k approaches global optimization but reduces emergent structure.
  - **Persistent memory vs. scalability:** Monotonic recipe book enables deep hierarchies but may require pruning for extremely long runs, risking loss of critical intermediates.
- **Failure signatures:**
  - Recipe book growth stalls early → discovery budget too low or `DomainValidate` too strict.
  - All producers close repeatedly → demand mechanism broken (e.g., α=1 in GFCF) or neighborhood connectivity insufficient.
  - Single good dominates inventories → insufficient exploration (low discovery rate/high decay) or `DomainCombine` overly restrictive.
  - Systematic domain metric bias (e.g., GDP over/under-forecast) → limitation in Layer 2 rules, not Layer 1.
- **First 3 experiments:**
  1. **Trivial chemistry validation:** Use atoms H and O with ΔE<0 validation. Expect water (H₂O) to dominate inventories quickly. Confirms basic Discover/Trade/Produce cycle.
  2. **Two-sector economy test:** Construct a 2×2 I–O table (e.g., Agriculture→Food, Manufacturing→Tools). Observe stable production network emergence. Checks Layer 2 economic rules.
  3. **Regime exploration via discoveryBudget:** Run with Budget=1,10,50. Plot species count, max depth, copy distribution. Should reproduce stagnation, sweet spot, and Tar regimes. Validates τd/τp balance.

## Limitations
- Exact parameter values (discoveryBudget, decay rates, patience threshold, neighborhood size `k`) for reported results are undisclosed, creating replication risk.
- `DomainValidate` implementation details (e.g., chemistry energy thresholds, economic I–O feasibility rules) are underspecified.
- Economic validation lacks statistical benchmarking (e.g., comparison to forecaster variance or significance tests).
- Corpus evidence for the core mechanisms (emergent fitness, open-ended search via recipe book, creative destruction) is weak or tangential.
- Cross-domain universality is tested on only two domains; no stress tests on additional open-ended problems.
