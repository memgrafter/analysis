# JUSTICE: Judicial Unified Synthesis Through Intermediate Conclusion Emulation for Automated Judgment Document Generation

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2602.08305
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.08305
- **Reference Count:** 18
- **Primary Result:** Proposed framework significantly outperforms state-of-the-art baselines, achieving a 4.6% improvement in prison term prediction and superior textual coherence via explicit modeling of a human-like "Pre-Judge" intermediate reasoning phase.

## Executive Summary
Automated judgment document generation faces the challenge of producing legally coherent and accurate outputs. Existing methods typically skip the critical "Pre-Judge" phase that human judges undergo, where they synthesize retrieved legal knowledge into a preliminary ruling. The JUSTICE framework addresses this by emulating the judicial workflow of "Search→Pre-Judge→Write" through three sequential components: a Referential Judicial Element Retriever (RJER) to acquire relevant laws and precedents, an Intermediate Conclusion Emulator (ICE) to produce a verifiable preliminary ruling tuple, and a Judicial Unified Synthesizer (JUS) to compile the final document. Evaluated on Chinese legal benchmarks, JUSTICE demonstrates substantial gains in legal accuracy and textual coherence, establishing the importance of an explicit intermediate reasoning stage.

## Method Summary
JUSTICE processes a case fact description (`F`) through a three-stage pipeline. First, the **RJER** performs specialized, parallel retrieval: a two-stage dense retrieval pipeline (bi-encoder recall, cross-encoder reranking) fetches the top-k2 relevant statutory law articles (`A_ret`), while a structure-aware model (SAILER) retrieves the most similar precedent case document (`C_doc`). Key elements (`E_case`) are then extracted from `C_doc` via a rule-based function. Second, the **ICE** module, a fine-tuned LLM, consumes `F`, `E_case`, and a combined set of supplementary articles (`A_ext = A_ret \ A_case`) within a structured prompt to generate a deterministic intermediate conclusion `J_pre`—a tuple containing the predicted charge, applicable articles, prison term, and fine. Third, the **JUS** synthesizer, another fine-tuned LLM, conditions on `F`, `J_pre`, the full precedent `C_doc`, and a document template (`T_format`) to generate the final, coherent judgment document (`D`). The system is designed for offline, confidential deployment using locally-hosted 7B-parameter LLMs.

## Key Results
- **Legal Accuracy:** JUSTICE achieves a 4.6% absolute improvement in prison term prediction accuracy over the strongest baseline (MRAG) on the Chinese JuDGE benchmark. It also sets new state-of-the-art in convicting and referencing accuracy (F1).
- **Textual Coherence:** The generated Reasoning and Judgment sections achieve significantly higher BERTScore and METEOR scores compared to prior work, demonstrating superior stylistic and structural alignment with human-written documents.
- **Ablation Evidence:** Removing the ICE (Pre-Judge) module causes a major drop in penalty accuracy. Removing RJER severely harms referencing accuracy, confirming the distinct, necessary roles of specialized retrieval and intermediate prediction.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- **Claim:** Explicitly modeling a human-like "Pre-Judge" intermediate reasoning phase significantly improves the legal accuracy and coherence of generated judgment documents.
- **Mechanism:** The ICE component takes retrieved legal articles and precedent elements, performs analogical reasoning to produce a structured, verifiable intermediate conclusion (`J_pre`), which then anchors the final synthesis. This decouples complex legal prediction from text generation.
- **Core assumption:** A high-quality, structured intermediate judicial decision (charge, articles, penalty) is a more reliable and controllable input for a writer model than raw retrieved context or direct end-to-end generation.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract] States that omitting the Pre-Judge phase leads to ineffective element acquisition and inadequate modeling, which JUSTICE addresses.
  - [section 3.2] Details ICE's design: it operationalizes the Pre-Judge phase by generating a verifiable `J_pre` tuple from `F`, `E_case`, and `A_ext`.
  - [corpus] Corpus shows related work on LJP and RAG but not explicit intermediate conclusion emulation; JUSTICE's novelty is inferred from the described gap.
- **Break condition:** If the ICE module's intermediate conclusion is replaced with ground truth (JUSTICE+GT), performance ceiling rises substantially, indicating ICE's output quality is the current primary limiter.

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim:** A specialized, multi-faceted retrieval component (RJER) is necessary to establish a robust referential foundation, as generic or low-quality retrieval directly harms the Pre-Judge process.
- **Mechanism:** RJER employs a two-stage retrieval pipeline (bi-encoder for recall, cross-encoder reranking) for legal articles and a structure-aware model for precedent cases, then extracts key elements (`E_case`) from the top precedent. It provides distinct, complementary inputs to ICE.
- **Core assumption:** Different types of judicial knowledge (statutory articles vs. precedent case elements) require tailored retrieval strategies, and their combined, high-quality provision is prerequisite for accurate analogical reasoning.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract] Notes that omitting Pre-Judge leads to ineffective acquisition of foundational judicial elements.
  - [section 3.1] Defines RJER's three streams: law retrieval, case retrieval, and element set combination (`A_ext` vs. `A_case`).
  - [section 4.5] Ablation shows removing RJER causes a large drop in Referencing Accuracy, confirming its role in providing legal grounding.
- **Break condition:** If the retrieved precedent case is highly irrelevant or the article set is noisy/insufficient, ICE's analogical reasoning (`Psi_analogize`) will be based on flawed premises, leading to cascade errors.

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim:** A dedicated synthesizer (JUS) that conditions on the structured intermediate conclusion, full precedent document, and a template produces a final document that is both legally coherent and stylistically appropriate.
- **Mechanism:** JUS uses a separate LLM fine-tuned to generate text given a composite prompt: `F` + `J_pre` + `C_doc` + `T_format`. The intermediate conclusion provides the logical skeleton, the precedent offers style/structural exemplars, and the template enforces format.
- **Core assumption:** Treating final document generation as a constrained "writing" task, guided by a prior "decision" (`J_pre`) and exemplars, yields superior textual and legal coherence compared to unguided or purely retrieval-augmented generation.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [section 3.3] Formalizes JUS's input prompt `P_JUS` and generation objective.
  - [section 4.3] Ablation (w/o ICE) shows feeding retrieved elements directly to JUS (bypassing Pre-Judge) harms penalty accuracy; w/o RJER harms referencing.
  - [section 4.6] Case study compares MRAG (direct generation) to JUSTICE, showing JUS leverages ICE's accurate conclusion for a sound final text.
- **Break condition:** If the intermediate conclusion (`J_pre`) is legally incorrect or the precedent document (`C_doc`) is structurally mismatched, JUS will synthesize a coherent but legally flawed document, demonstrating a "garbage-in, garbage-out" dependency.

## Foundational Learning
- **Concept:** **Legal Judgment Prediction (LJP) sub-task decomposition.**
  - *Why needed here:* JUSTICE extends LJP principles (predicting charge, articles, penalty) by treating these as an intermediate, verifiable output (`J_pre`) before full document generation. Understanding LJP's goal of structured outcome prediction is key to grasping ICE's purpose.
  - *Quick check question:* What are the typical output components of an LJP model, and why might generating them as a structured tuple be a useful intermediate step for full judgment writing?

- **Concept:** **Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG) with domain-specific retrieval.**
  - *Why needed here:* RJER is a domain-tailored RAG system. It requires understanding that retrieval quality (recall via bi-encoder, precision via reranker) and extraction (from case text) are critical bottlenecks. The framework assumes retrieved content is correct and sufficient for reasoning.
  - *Quick check question:* In a legal RAG pipeline, why might a two-stage retrieval (dense retrieval then reranking) be preferred over a single-stage approach, and what specific legal "elements" need extraction from a retrieved case?

- **Concept:** **Structured prediction for constrained generation.**
  - *Why needed here:* ICE produces a constrained output structure (`p_articles, p_charge, p_term, p_fine`). This requires learning to map unstructured facts and references to a fixed schema. JUS then consumes this structure. This is a shift from free-form text generation to structured intermediate representation.
  - *Quick check question:* How does forcing an LLM to output a fixed-format tuple (instead of natural language) change the training objective and what potential error modes does it introduce?

- **Concept:** **Analogies in legal reasoning.**
  - *Why needed here:* ICE's core function is analogical reasoning: mapping facts from the current case (`F`) to legal principles derived from the retrieved precedent's elements (`E_case`) and statutes (`A_ext`). This mirrors how judges use precedents.
  - *Quick check question:* What are the key components of analogical reasoning in law (e.g., identifying relevant similarities, distinguishing differences), and how might they be operationalized in a prompt to an LLM?

- **Concept:** **Document structure templates for constrained generation.**
  - *Why needed here:* The JUS module uses a fixed template (`T_format`) to enforce the standard sections of a Chinese judgment document (e.g., Court, Prosecution, Defense, Facts, Reasoning, Judgment). This ensures stylistic and legal formality.
  - *Quick check question:* Why is using a rigid template more effective for legal document generation than prompting the model to "write a judgment," and what are the risks of over-constraining the output?

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map:**
  F -> RJER -> (A_ext, C_doc, E_case) -> ICE -> J_pre -> JUS -> D
  (Parallel retrieval within RJER: Law stream -> A_ret -> A_ext; Case stream -> C_doc -> E_case)

- **Critical path:** The end-to-end generation latency is dominated by the sequential chain: Retrieval (RJER) -> Prediction (ICE) -> Synthesis (JUS). Parallelization is only possible within RJER (law and case retrieval run in parallel). Any failure in an upstream component propagates downstream.

- **Design tradeoffs:**
  1. **Single vs. Multiple Precedents:** JUSTICE uses one precedent case (`Top-1`). This simplifies ICE's analogical reasoning but risks bias/irrelevance if retrieval fails. More precedents would increase complexity and noise.
  2. **LLM Sizes:** Uses smaller (7B) locally-deployable LLMs due to data confidentiality constraints. Tradeoff: lower quality/robustness vs. privacy/control.
  3. **Structured vs. Free-form Intermediate:** `J_pre` is a rigid tuple. This aids verification and JUS synthesis but may discard nuanced reasoning a judge would express. The paper assumes the tuple captures essential decision points.

- **Failure signatures:**
  - **RJER failure:** Low Referencing Accuracy (F1) in ablation (w/o RJER). Indicates poor statute/retrieval. Look for: irrelevant `A_ret`, missing key articles in `A_ext`, or extracted `E_case` missing critical fields (e.g., `c_charge`).
  - **ICE failure:** Sharp drop in Penalty Accuracy (prison/fine) when ICE is removed (w/o ICE). Indicates flawed analogical reasoning. Symptoms: `J_pre` contains wrong charge, impossible penalty values, or omitted articles.
  - **JUS failure:** Degraded textual quality (METEOR/BERTScore) when `C_doc` or `T_format` are ablated (Appendix D). Look for: incorrect document structure, missing standard sections, or stylistic inconsistency despite correct `J_pre`.

- **First 3 experiments:**
  1. Replicate RQ1 (Overall Performance): Run JUSTICE and all baselines (especially MRAG) on JuDGE test set. Confirm ~4.6% prison term gain and higher BERTScore. Validates core claim.
  2. Run RQ2 Ablation: Remove RJER and/or ICE. Measure degradation in Referencing/Convicting/Penalty Accuracy. Confirms component necessity and their distinct roles (RJER for articles, ICE for prediction).
  3. Run RQ4 (Element Impact): Vary `k2` (number of retrieved articles) and ablate specific element types from `E_ref`. Re-run ICE and measure impact on `J_pre` accuracy. Validates RJER's design choices (reranking need, multi-element importance).

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- **Open Question 1:** Does the JUSTICE framework generalize effectively to legal systems beyond the Chinese context it was designed for?
  - *Basis in paper:* Limitations section (E) states applicability to other jurisdictions is unvalidated. Components are tailored to Chinese legal documents and statutes.
  - *Why unresolved:* Different jurisdictions have varying terminologies, document structures, and reasoning patterns, potentially requiring redesign.
  - *What evidence would resolve it:* Evaluation on judgment datasets from other jurisdictions (e.g., US, UK), comparing key metrics and analyzing jurisdiction-specific failure modes.

- **Open Question 2:** How can JUSTICE be adapted to leverage more powerful LLMs while respecting the data confidentiality constraints of real-world judicial deployment?
  - *Basis in paper:* Limitations section (E) notes practical deployment requires strict offline processing, restricting use to smaller, locally deployed LLMs due to API privacy risks.
  - *Why unresolved:* Current implementation uses 7B models for confidentiality. A technical solution for secure, offline-friendly integration of advanced models is missing.
  - *What evidence would resolve it:* A hybrid architecture (sensitive components on-premise, non-sensitive generation via APIs with anonymization) with empirical benchmarks of performance vs. privacy trade-offs.

- **Open Question 3:** To what extent does JUSTICE’s performance depend on the quality and relevance of the single precedent case retrieved by RJER, and would incorporating multiple precedents or handling retrieval errors improve robustness?
  - *Basis in paper:* Limitations section (E) highlights dependency on single precedent case quality. RQ4 studies element types but not precedent-case variability.
  - *Why unresolved:* Framework assumes top-1 precedent is optimal. Impact of retrieval imperfections (irrelevant, noisy cases) and strategies like multi-case fusion are unexplored.
  - *What evidence would resolve it:* Controlled experiments perturbing the precedent case (replace with irrelevant cases, truncate) to measure degradation. Testing architectures that ingest multiple precedents and evaluating robustness to retrieval failures.

- **Open Question 4:** Can human-in-the-loop correction of ICE’s intermediate conclusions significantly enhance the reliability and trustworthiness of JUSTICE’s outputs in high-stakes legal settings?
  - *Basis in paper:* Appendix C's "JUSTICE+GT" variant shows large gains, and authors conclude the intermediate conclusion is a "critical checkpoint" for enhancing reliability, implying correction potential.
  - *Why unresolved:* Practical correction mechanisms, interface design, error types fixable by experts, and impact on efficiency are uninvestigated.
  - *What evidence would resolve it:* User studies with legal professionals editing ICE outputs, measuring changes in final accuracy and time savings. Developing uncertainty quantification for ICE to flag low-confidence predictions.

- **Open Question 5:** What is the optimal retrieval configuration (number and types of judicial elements) for balancing accuracy and noise in the ICE module?
  - *Basis in paper:* RQ4 analyzes impact of retrieved article quantity (`k2`) and element types (laws vs. precedent elements). Results show performance peaks at `k2≈10` and each element type is crucial, but optimal trade-off for different case complexities is not fully mapped.
  - *Why unresolved:* Interaction between element quantity, case difficulty (e.g., multi-charge cases), and ICE accuracy is not deeply modeled. Systematic exploration across diverse case distributions is lacking.
  - *What evidence would resolve it:* Large-scale ablation studies varying `k2` and element combination strategies across case subgroups (by charge type, fact length). Correlating retrieval success metrics (recall of ground-truth articles) with ICE prediction accuracy to derive adaptive policies.

## Limitations
- **Underspecified retrieval training:** Critical details for law retriever (`Φ_ret`, `Φ_rerank`)—including model architecture, negative sampling for LCE loss, and data splits—are missing, preventing faithful replication of RJER’s core functionality.
- **Opaque precedent extraction:** The rule-based `Ψ_Extract` function for pulling `E_case` from raw judgment text is completely undescribed. Its accuracy directly impacts ICE’s analogical reasoning, but its design and error rate are unknown.
- **Small dataset risk:** Training on ~2k JuDGE cases may cause overfitting or instability for LLM fine-tuning, yet no validation on out-of-distribution legal domains beyond LeCaRDv2-Doc is reported.

## Confidence
- **Claim 1 (Pre-Judge phase necessity):** *Medium.* Supported by strong ablations (w/o ICE hurts penalty accuracy), but reliant on correctly implemented RJER/ICE. Retrieval/extraction unknowns limit certainty.
- **Claim 2 (RJER’s tailored retrieval):** *Medium.* Ablation shows referencing drops without RJER, but without replicable retrieval specs, the claimed "specialized" design cannot be independently verified.
- **Claim 3 (JUS synthesis quality):** *High.* Document generation metrics (BERTScore) and template use are clearly specified; ablation of `C_doc`/`T_format` in Appendix D directly supports this mechanism.

## Next Checks
1. **Standalone RJER evaluation:** Compute law article Recall@k on a held-out JuDGE set and manually inspect top-1 case retrieval relevance for 50 random queries. This isolates retrieval quality before ICE.
2. **ICE accuracy isolation:** Quantify ICE’s tuple prediction (`J_pre`) against ground truth (charge, articles, term, fine) on validation data *without* JUS. This measures the Pre-Judge step’s standalone legal accuracy.
3. **k2 sensitivity sweep:** Re-run ICE/JUS with varying `k2` (e.g., 5, 10, 20) using the *same* trained reranker. Plot Penalty Accuracy vs. k2 to identify the optimal trade-off and confirm reported `k2=10` choice is robust.
