# OmniNeuro: A Multimodal HCI Framework for Explainable BCI Feedback via Generative AI and Sonification

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2601.00843
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.00843
- Reference count: 6
- Primary result: Achieved 58.52% mean accuracy (62.91% for responsive subjects) on PhysioNet (N=109); 14% reduction in inter-trial feature variance; qualitative pilot (N=3) reported reduced frustration and improved mental effort regulation.

## Executive Summary
Brain-Computer Interfaces often operate as "black boxes," providing users with minimal feedback during skill acquisition, leading to frustration and slow learning. OmniNeuro addresses this by introducing a decoder-agnostic Human-Computer Interaction layer that transforms neural features into transparent, multimodal feedback. It leverages three interpretability engines—Physics (Energy), Chaos (Fractal Complexity), and Quantum-Inspired uncertainty—to drive real-time neuro-sonification and generative AI clinical reports. Evaluated on the PhysioNet dataset (N=109), the system achieved a mean accuracy of 58.52% (62.91% for responsive subjects). A simulated user analysis indicated a 14% reduction in inter-trial feature variance, suggesting faster convergence of mental strategies. Qualitative pilot feedback (N=3) confirmed that the explainable audio and AI reports reduced frustration and helped users regulate mental effort during failures.

## Method Summary
OmniNeuro is a framework that operates in parallel to any standard BCI decoder (e.g., CSP+LDA, EEGNet). It accepts preprocessed EEG signals (64 channels, bandpass 8–30 Hz) and computes three real-time metrics via dedicated engines: (1) the logarithmic energy ratio `Lidx` between motor cortex channels (C3/C4) as a measure of effort intensity; (2) the Higuchi Fractal Dimension difference `ΔHFD` between C3 and C4 to differentiate focused cognitive states from rest or over-efforting; and (3) a quantum-inspired probability `Pmove` derived from mapping neural features onto a Bloch Sphere, providing a geometrically stable confidence measure. These metrics are mapped to auditory parameters (volume, timbre, pitch) for immediate neuro-sonification and to an asynchronous generative AI (Gemini) clinical report via a constrained prompt. A safety protocol uses a `Pmove` threshold (0.4–0.6) and an artifact veto to enter a "Neutral/Unknown" state, preventing misleading reinforcement. The framework was evaluated using 5-fold cross-validation on the PhysioNet motor imagery dataset.

## Key Results
- Mean decoding accuracy: 58.52% (62.91% for responsive subjects) on PhysioNet (N=109).
- Simulated analysis showed a 14% reduction in inter-trial feature variance, indicating faster stabilization of user mental strategies.
- Qualitative pilot feedback (N=3) reported that the explainable audio and AI reports reduced frustration and helped users self-regulate mental effort during performance drops.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Physics Engine (Energy) for Effort Intensity Feedback
**Claim:** Mapping logarithmic energy ratio (Lidx) between motor cortex channels (C3/C4) to auditory volume enables users to perceive and regulate their mental effort intensity in real-time.  
**Mechanism:** The system computes `Lidx = ln((Var(XC4)+ϵ)/(Var(XC3)+ϵ))`. This scalar metric of hemispheric power imbalance is directly mapped to audio amplitude (loudness). A higher ratio indicates greater relative effort for the target hand, providing an intuitive, continuous analog signal.  
**Core assumption:** Users can perceptually correlate loudness with effort and use it to self-modulate cognitive investment, closing the neurofeedback loop.  
**Evidence anchors:** Paper describes the transformation in abstract and Section 2.2.1; the formula and mapping are explicit.  
**Break condition:** If artifact contamination (e.g., muscle EMG) inflates variance measurements, the `Lidx` becomes misleading, potentially teaching the user to increase noise rather than true neural effort.

### Chaos Engine (Fractal Complexity) for Cognitive State Differentiation
**Claim:** The Higuchi Fractal Dimension (HFD) difference (`ΔHFD = HFD(C3) - HFD(C4)`) distinguishes between "focused cognitive computation" and "resting/over-efforting," allowing the sonification's timbre to signal mental strategy quality.  
**Mechanism:** HFD quantifies the roughness or complexity of the EEG signal. A significant ΔHFD suggests active, task-related processing in the target hemisphere versus the contralateral side. This metric controls audio timbre (pure tone vs. distorted noise), giving the user a non-verbal clue about whether their mental state is "productive" or "chaotic."  
**Core assumption:** The fractal dimensionality of motor imagery EEG correlates with the quality of neural computation, and users can interpret timbral changes as directives to focus or relax.  
**Evidence anchors:** Paper mentions Chaos engine in abstract and defines ΔHFD in Section 2.2.2.  
**Break condition:** The HFD is sensitive to non-stationarities and noise. During transitions between mental states or with high ocular/muscular artifacts, `ΔHFD` may fluctuate erratically, causing confusing or contradictory auditory feedback.

### Quantum-Inspired Uncertainty Engine for Stable Decision Signaling
**Claim:** Representing neural state as a vector on a Bloch Sphere and deriving probability `Pmove = sin²(θ/2)` provides a continuous, geometrically stable measure of classification confidence that reduces "feedback flicker" compared to softmax probabilities.  
**Mechanism:** The system maps features to a quantum-like state vector |ψ⟩. The angle θ on the Bloch Sphere encodes the system's state between "left" and "right." The derived probability `Pmove` smoothly transitions between 0 and 1 as θ changes. This formalism inherently models ambiguity ("superposition") and provides a dampened confidence signal, avoiding the near-binary jumps of a standard classifier's output at decision boundaries.  
**Core assumption:** The geometric probability model on the Bloch Sphere is a valid and superior mathematical proxy for modeling the ambiguity of a BCI decoder's state during mental imagery, and its smoothness is perceptually beneficial for user learning.  
**Evidence anchors:** Paper describes the quantum engine in abstract and Section 2.2.3, and compares it to softmax in Section 6.3.  
**Break condition:** The mapping from neural feature space to the Bloch Sphere is an arbitrary dimensional reduction. If the chosen features do not linearly separate well in this reduced geometric space, the `Pmove` confidence may not correlate with actual decoder accuracy, misleading the user.

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component map:** Preprocessed EEG (64 ch, 8-30Hz) -> [Physics Engine, Chaos Engine, Quantum Engine] -> Safety Gate (`Pmove` threshold & artifact veto) -> (if CONFIRMED: parallel Neuro-Sonification & Gemini Report trigger; if REJECT: mute audio, show "unclear")

**Critical path:** Raw EEG → Feature Engines → Safety Threshold (Pmove/artifact) → If CONFIRMED: **Parallel** sonification (immediate) + trigger LLM report (delayed). If REJECT: mute audio, show "unclear." The safety gate on `Pmove` is the critical control point for preventing misleading reinforcement.

**Design tradeoffs:**
- Transparency vs. Latency: Deepest interpretability comes from the AI report (LLM), but its latency is high (async). Real-time feedback uses simple metric→sound mappings, sacrificing semantic detail for immediacy.
- Generality vs. Personalization: Engine formulas are fixed (general), but sonification mappings (e.g., pitch range) and LLM prompts likely need user-specific calibration (not addressed in paper).
- Local vs. Cloud: Core feedback loop (engines + sonification) is local/lightweight (<50ms). LLM requires cloud/API, introducing dependency and privacy risk. Offline fallback is rule-based, less intelligent.

**Failure signatures:**
- High variance in `Lidx`/`ΔHFD` with stable `Pmove`: May indicate noisy EEG metrics but a confident (possibly overconfident) classifier. Sonification becomes erratic despite "certain" classification.
- Frequent REJECT state (low `Pmove`): Could mean user cannot generate discriminable signals, poor electrode contact, or decoder is unsuitable. User sees "Signal Unclear" repeatedly, risking learned helplessness if not coached.
- LLM Report Contradiction: If the generated text advice contradicts the immediate sonification's signal, user trust erodes. This suggests poor prompt engineering or metric misalignment.

**First 3 experiments:**
1. **Single-Engine Validation:** Isolate each engine (Physics, Chaos, Quantum). Run closed-loop neurofeedback with *only* its corresponding auditory stream (e.g., only volume from `Lidx`). Compare learning curve (feature variance reduction) vs. binary feedback baseline. Tests if each modality is individually instructive.
2. **Multimodal Integration & Safety Threshold Tuning:** Test the full OmniNeuro pipeline with varied `Pmove` thresholds (e.g., 0.3-0.7). Measure trade-off between feedback *availability* (percent of trials with sound) and feedback *reliability* (how often sound correlates with correct decoder output). Identifies optimal safety margin.
3. **Decoder-Agnosticism Stress Test:** Plug three radically different decoders (CSP+LDA, EEGNet, Riemannian MDM) into the *same* OmniNeuro HCI layer. For each, measure subjective user frustration (post-session questionnaire) and objective strategy convergence (feature variance). Tests the claim that HCI utility is independent of decoder choice.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
1. **Long-term neuroplasticity and functional recovery:** Does OmniNeuro's explainable feedback lead to measurable improvements in stroke patients compared to black-box BCI systems? The paper lacks longitudinal clinical trial data.
2. **Personalization of sonification mappings:** What is the optimal method for personalizing auditory feedback (e.g., pitch for confidence, timbre for cognitive load) to individual user sensory preferences and cognitive profiles? Current mappings are fixed.
3. **Autonomous operation of AI reports:** Can the Generative AI Clinical Reports effectively operate with reduced clinician oversight in remote/tele-rehabilitation without increasing risk of reinforcing maladaptive strategies or causing harm? The paper mandates clinician-in-the-loop but does not test autonomous use.
4. **Comparison to post-hoc XAI:** How does OmniNeuro's intrinsic, real-time multimodal feedback compare to post-hoc, model-agnostic XAI techniques (e.g., SHAP, LIME) in improving user learning stability and reducing frustration? This comparative hypothesis is untested.

## Limitations
- **Implementation gaps:** Critical parameters are missing, blocking faithful reproduction: the artifact veto autoencoder (architecture, training, threshold), exact Higuchi Fractal Dimension parameters (`k_max`, step), and the full generative AI prompt/sonification scaling curves.
- **Causal inference weakness:** The link between the specific multimodal mappings and accelerated learning is inferred from a short-term simulated variance reduction (N=109) and qualitative feedback (N=3). This is insufficient to establish mechanism causality.
- **Generalization scope:** The decoder-agnostic claim and engine validity are tested only on one dataset (PhysioNet motor imagery) with a limited pipeline. Performance on other BCI paradigms (e.g., P300, SSVEP) or with noisy real-world data is unknown.

## Confidence
- **High:** Framework exists as a decoder-agnostic HCI layer integrating the three described engines.
- **Medium:** The three engines (Energy, Chaos, Quantum) are mathematically defined and produce the claimed output metrics.
- **Low:** That this specific multimodal design *causally* reduces user frustration and improves learning stability compared to other explainable feedback methods.

## Next Checks
1. **Engine Benchmarking:** Re-implement all three metric engines (`Lidx`, `ΔHFD`, `Pmove`) using only the paper's formulas. Validate their output ranges, sensitivity, and noise robustness on a standardized motor imagery dataset (e.g., BCI Competition IV-2a) and compare against reported values.
2. **Perceptual Mapping Pilot:** Conduct a within-subject study where participants control synthetic metrics (e.g., fixed `Lidx` values) via a slider. Test if they can reliably identify and differentiate the intended auditory correlates (volume, pitch, timbre) after minimal training, confirming the mappings' perceptual discriminability.
3. **Decoder Stress Test:** Integrate OmniNeuro's HCI layer with at least three architecturally distinct open-source BCI decoders (e.g., Riemannian MDM, EEGNet, ShallowConvNet). Run a user study (N≥10) to measure if the reported reduction in frustration and feature variance holds across decoders, or if effects are decoder-specific.
