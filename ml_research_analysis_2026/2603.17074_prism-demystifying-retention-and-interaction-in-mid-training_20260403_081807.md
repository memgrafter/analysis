# PRISM: Demystifying Retention and Interaction in Mid-Training

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID**: 2603.17074  
- **Source URL**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.17074  
- **Reference count**: 40  
- **Primary result**: Mid‑training on ~27B domain‑focused tokens yields large reasoning gains (+15–40 pts math, +5–12 pts code, +6–13 pts science) while preserving general ability; subsequent RL (GRPO) further amplifies performance 3–4×, whereas RL on base models fails on hard tasks.

## Executive Summary
Mid‑training—a stage between pretraining and supervised fine‑tuning—has been used empirically to improve reasoning, but its interaction with later reinforcement learning (RL) was poorly understood. This paper conducts controlled experiments across seven models (3B–24B) and two architectures (Transformer and Transformer‑Mamba hybrid). The authors show that a retention‑aware mid‑training phase using ~27 B high‑quality, domain‑focused tokens produces substantial gains on math, code, and science benchmarks while maintaining general knowledge. When this mid‑training is followed by RL via GRPO, the combined pipeline (“PRISM”) dramatically amplifies reasoning performance; the six‑benchmark macro‑average jumps from under 12 to 29–42, a 3–4× improvement. In stark contrast, applying RL directly to base models yields negligible success on hard tasks (e.g., AIME scores near zero). Mechanistic analysis reveals that mid‑training densely restructures >90% of model weights, while RL sparsely refines ~5% and preserves the mid‑training representational geometry (CKA > 0.998). Crucially, the composition of mid‑training data—particularly the inclusion of science reasoning data—strongly influences the RL gains, with up to a +28‑point improvement on GPQA. These findings offer concrete guidance for designing effective mid‑training pipelines that produce reasoning‑capable base models amenable to RL.

## Method Summary
A practical reproduction uses the open‑source **Granite‑3.3‑8B** model. Mid‑training data mixtures combine *general web documents* (DCLM‑EDU, filtered by quality ≥ 3) with *domain‑specific reasoning data*: for the Math+Code mixture, Open‑R1‑MoT (math, code), additional math sources (Megamath‑Web‑Pro) and code sources (OpenCodeReasoning‑2, StarCoder2) after quality filtering. A feasible token budget of **10 B tokens** (mid‑training alone) already captures most of the reported benefit (Table 9). Training follows AdamW (learning rate 5×10⁻⁵, cosine decay with 500‑step warm‑up, final LR 0.1×, weight decay 0.1, β (0.9, 0.95)), context length 8 k, and FSDP. Compute steps as `total_tokens/(global_batch_size×context_length)`; with global batch 64, 10 B tokens requires ~19 500 steps. After mid‑training, reinforcement learning with **GRPO** is applied for 200 steps (full runs use 1 000). The prompt pool uses the *balanced* configuration (19 k prompts per domain): math from DeepScaleR‑Preview/INTELLECT‑2‑RL, code from DeepCoder‑Preview/OpenCodeInstruct, and science from Nemotron‑PT‑v1‑stem with instruction templates. Core RL hyperparameters include KL‑beta 0.05, a format reward for , stop‑token/repetition penalties, truncated importance‑sampling constant 2.0, and linear learning‑rate decay from 5×10⁻⁷. Evaluation covers reasoning (MATH500, AIME24/25, LiveCodeBench, Codeforces, GPQA‑Diamond) and general (LB‑V1/V2) suites.

## Key Results
- Mid‑training alone yields **+15 – 40 points** on math benchmarks, **+5 – 12 points** on code, and **+6 – 13 points** on science while preserving general abilities.
- The full **PRISM → RL** pipeline achieves a **3 – 4×** improvement on the six‑benchmark macro‑average (from <12 to 29–42). In contrast, applying RL directly to base models fails (AIME scores near 0).
- **Data composition** is the dominant factor for RL gains; including science during mid‑training can increase GPQA by up to **+28 points**. Changes to the RL mix have minimal impact (<2 points).

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
Mid‑training serves as a **dense restructuring** phase that modifies over 90% of model weights, embedding robust reasoning patterns across the network. The retention of DCLM‑EDU general documents protects broad knowledge, limiting catastrophic forgetting. Subsequent RL performs a **sparse refinement**, adjusting only about 5% of parameters, primarily the top layers, while preserving the representational geometry established by mid‑training (CKA > 0.998). This separation explains why RL after mid‑training is highly effective, whereas RL on a base model (without the mid‑training scaffold) fails: the dense reasoning scaffold is missing, making it difficult for RL to discover coherent reasoning chains from scratch. The inclusion of science data in mid‑training further expands the reasoning foundation, enabling RL to transfer learning across domains, especially to scientific benchmarks. Together, these mechanisms form a two‑stage system: a broadly trained, dense reasoning backbone refined by goal‑directed sparsity.

- **Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO)**: On‑policy RL with sampled outputs and group‑based advantages.  
  *Why needed*: Provides stable gradients and prevents mode collapse in reasoning tasks.  
  *Quick check*: KL divergence between policy and reference stays below 0.2; success rate on a held‑out set increases monotonically.

- **Centered Kernel Alignment (CKA)**: Representational similarity metric between neural layers or checkpoints.  
  *Why needed*: Quantifies the preservation of mid‑training representations after RL; high CKA indicates minor, beneficial changes.  
  *Quick check*: Compute CKA on 50 prompt embeddings; expect >0.998 between mid‑trained and RL‑fine‑tuned models.

- **Quality‑focused data filtering**: Selection of high‑quality tokens (e.g., DCLM‑EDU quality ≥ 3, code samples with QwQ‑judged correctness).  
  *Why needed*: Ensures the model learns from clean, informative examples, avoiding noise that could hinder reasoning.  
  *Quick check*: Manually inspect a random sample of 10 examples per source; look for coherent reasoning and accurate answers.

- **Long‑context restoration** (optional): Linear merge of base (15%) and mid‑trained (85%) checkpoints followed by a short long‑context extension (LC).  
  *Why needed*: Mid‑training at 8 k context degrades long‑context retrieval (RULER).  
  *Quick check*: Evaluate RULER@128 k; after restoration, score should exceed 0.8.

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component map** (plain text):  
Pretraining → Base Model → Mid‑training (DCLM‑EDU + Domain data) → Mid‑trained Checkpoint → RL (GRPO) → Final Policy

**Critical path**:  
1. Assemble and filter mid‑training data.  
2. Train mid‑training to hit the token budget (e.g., 10 B tokens) with the specified hyperparameters.  
3. (If long‑context needed) Merge 15% base + 85% mid‑trained and run LC extension.  
4. Build the balanced RL prompt pool (19k per domain, science templated).  
5. Run GRPO for target steps (200 for a quick check, 1 000 for full).  
6. Evaluate on reasoning and general suites.

**Design tradeoffs**:  
- **Token budget vs. cost**: Larger budgets improve gains but linearly increase compute; 10 B tokens already captures most of the benefit.  
- **Data distribution**: More domain data faster reasoning gains but risks forgetting; DCLM‑EDU proportion mitigates this.  
- **RL on mid‑trained vs. base**: Only the former yields large improvements; the mid‑training scaffold is non‑negotiable.  
- **Long‑context restoration**: LC extension costs a few thousand steps but necessary for RULER tasks; can be skipped for pure reasoning.

**Failure signatures**:  
- Mid‑training: >5‑point drop on LB‑V1 general tasks → increase DCLM‑EDU ratio.  
- RL: Stagnant or decreasing correctness → check prompt pool has at least one correct sample per prompt; reduce KL‑beta or learning rate.  
- CKA <0.99: RL altered representations too much; lower LR or clip bounds.  
- Weight‑delta L2 >0.01 (instead of ~0.0003): learning rate or steps too high.  
- AIME24 unchanged after PRISM→RL: insufficient mid‑training tokens or missing science data.

**First three experiments**:  
1. Mid‑train with DCLM‑EDU + OpenR1‑MoT‑Math only (2 B tokens); evaluate MATH500 – expect a clear jump over base.  
2. Extend to Math+Code mixture (add OpenCodeReasoning‑2) for another 2 B tokens; evaluate LiveCodeBench – further improvement.  
3. Apply GRPO (200 steps) to the mid‑trained checkpoint from (2) using a small balanced prompt set (1 k each domain); test AIME24 – base ~0%, mid‑trained RL >5%.
