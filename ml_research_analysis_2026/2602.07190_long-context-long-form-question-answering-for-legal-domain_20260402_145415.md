# Long-Context Long-Form Question Answering for Legal Domain

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2602.07190
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.07190
- **Reference Count:** 24
- **Primary Result:** LCLF-QA improves recall by up to 4.8% (0.6798 vs 0.6486) and coverage by up to 4.3% (0.6722 vs 0.6446) over LongRAG on a legal QA benchmark.

## Executive Summary
Long-form question answering in the legal domain faces unique challenges due to complex document layouts, dense cross-references, and specialized terminology. This paper introduces LCLF-QA, an extension of LongRAG tailored for legal texts. It combines a domain-specific query re-writer, layout-aware smart chunking that respects document hierarchy, and a generator fine-tuned on legal vocabulary. Hybrid retrieval with reciprocal rank fusion selects relevant chunks. Evaluated on 546 expert-curated legal QA pairs, the system achieves statistically significant gains in recall and coverage, demonstrating the value of domain-aware processing for long-context legal QA.

## Method Summary
LCLF-QA builds on the LongRAG framework with three key legal-domain adaptations. First, a query re-writer expands acronyms and clarifies user intent. Second, documents are parsed into parent-child linked chunks based on sections, footnotes, and other structural elements, preserving metadata. Third, the generator is trained or fine-tuned on a legal vocabulary corpus. Relevant chunks are retrieved via a hybrid of BM25 and dense embeddings, fused with reciprocal rank fusion. The retrieved context and rewritten query are then used to generate a long-form answer.

## Key Results
- Recall improves by up to 4.8% (0.6798 vs 0.6486) compared to LongRAG.
- Coverage score improves by up to 4.3% (0.6722 vs 0.6446).
- Gains are reported as statistically significant on the in-house benchmark of 546 QA pairs.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
Legal documents are highly structured and terminology-dense, causing generic long-context QA systems to fail. LCLF-QA addresses this by adapting each pipeline stage to legal specifics: the query re-writer resolves domain acronyms and ambiguous phrasing; layout-aware chunking maintains logical boundaries (e.g., keeping a footnote attached to its section) to prevent context fragmentation; and the legal-vocabulary generator produces accurate, domain-appropriate language. Hybrid retrieval leverages both lexical and semantic matching to cover varied legal query styles.

## Foundational Learning
- **LongRAG:** The baseline retrieval-augmented generation system for long contexts. *Why needed:* Provides the foundational retrieval-generation loop that LCLF-QA extends. *Quick check:* Explain how LongRAG handles documents longer than the model context window via chunking and retrieval.
- **Layout-Aware Document Chunking:** Parsing documents by structural elements (sections, subsections, footnotes) instead of fixed-size splits. *Why needed:* Legal meaning is often contained within hierarchical structures; breaking these harms coherence. *Quick check:* Given a sample contract, identify natural chunk boundaries that preserve clause integrity.
- **Domain-Specific Query Rewriting:** Expanding acronyms and disambiguating intent based on legal context. *Why needed:* Legal queries use shorthand (e.g., "FRCP" for Federal Rules of Civil Procedure) and assume domain knowledge; expansion improves retrieval. *Quick check:* Rewrite a query containing a common legal acronym into its full form and clearer phrasing.
- **Reciprocal Rank Fusion (RRF):** A method to combine results from multiple retrieval sources (BM25 and dense embeddings). *Why needed:* Legal queries may benefit from both keyword matching (for precise terms) and semantic search (for conceptual questions). *Quick check:* Given two ranked lists from different retrievers, compute RRF scores for top documents.
- **Legal Vocabulary Fine-Tuning:** Adapting the generator's language model to legal terminology and style. *Why needed:* Generic models may avoid jargon or produce informal language, reducing accuracy in legal answers. *Quick check:* Evaluate a generic vs. legal-fine-tuned model on generating a definition for a term like "force majeure".

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component Map:**  
Query Rewriter -> Layout-Aware Chunker -> Hybrid Retriever (BM25 + Dense Embeddings with RRF) -> Domain-Specific Generator

**Critical Path:**  
User query enters the Query Rewriter, which produces an expanded, clarified query. This query is used by the Layout-Aware Chunker to segment the legal document corpus into linked chunks with metadata. The Hybrid Retriever fetches the most relevant chunks using RRF. Finally, the Domain-Specific Generator synthesizes a long-form answer from the retrieved context.

**Design Tradeoffs:**  
- Domain specificity vs. generalizability: Customizations improve legal performance but may hinder transfer to other domains.
- Chunk granularity: Finer chunks preserve detail but may lose cross-reference context; hierarchical linking mitigates this but adds complexity.
- Retrieval diversity: BM25 captures exact terms, dense embeddings capture semantics; RRF balances but requires tuning weighting.

**Failure Signatures:**  
- Query re-writer fails to expand a critical acronym, leading to missed retrieval.
- Chunker misparses document structure, splitting a coherent legal argument across chunks.
- Retriever over-relies on one modality (e.g., BM25) for a semantically complex query.
- Generator includes non-legal terminology or omits necessary qualifiers (e.g., "may" vs. "shall").

**First Three Experiments:**  
1. Isolate the Query Rewriter: Measure retrieval accuracy (e.g., recall@k) using original vs. rewritten queries on a held-out set of legal questions.
2. Ablate Chunking Strategies: Compare layout-aware chunking against fixed-size chunking on retrieval metrics and answer completeness, using the same retriever.
3. Generator Vocabulary Probe: Evaluate answer quality with the domain-specific generator vs. the base model on a subset requiring precise legal terminology, using expert judgment or a legal accuracy metric.

## Limitations
- Evaluation relies on a single in-house corpus of 546 QA pairs; generalizability is uncertain.
- Dataset details (size, diversity, public availability) are not provided, hindering replication.
- Statistical significance is claimed but without error bars, variance estimates, or details of tests used.
- Ablation results are derived from the same test set, risking overly optimistic performance estimates.
- The isolated impact of the query re-writer and legal-vocabulary generator is not measured, making their specific contributions unclear.

## Confidence
- Overall performance improvement over LongRAG: **Medium** (statistical significance noted, but limited transparency and single benchmark).
- Ablation results indicating necessity of each component: **Medium** (same limitations as above).
- Efficacy of domain-specific adaptations (query rewriting, legal vocabulary training): **Low** (no direct component-level validation; benefits inferred from full system).
- Reproducibility of results: **Low** (lack of dataset/code/hyperparameter details).
