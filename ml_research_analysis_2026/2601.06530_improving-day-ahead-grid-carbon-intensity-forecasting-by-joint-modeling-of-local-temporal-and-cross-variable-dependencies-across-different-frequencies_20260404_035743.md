# Improving Day-Ahead Grid Carbon Intensity Forecasting by Joint Modeling of Local-Temporal and Cross-Variable Dependencies Across Different Frequencies

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2601.06530
- Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.06530
- Reference count: 16
- Primary result: The proposed model reduces MAE by up to 26.9% in high-volatility South Australia compared to state-of-the-art models.

## Executive Summary
Accurate day-ahead forecasting of grid carbon intensity (CIF) is challenged by fine-grained local temporal patterns, dynamic cross-variable dependencies, and multi-frequency interactions. This paper introduces a deep learning model with two parallel modules: LT-MWKC for localized temporal pattern extraction using wavelet convolutional kernels on overlapping variable-length patches, and CV-DWCC for capturing dynamic cross-variable dependencies via wavelet-based local multiple regression across time-frequency domains. Evaluated on four Australian electricity markets, the model consistently outperforms existing methods, with notable gains in high-volatility regions. Ablation studies confirm the complementary roles of both modules, and the model offers interpretability through adaptive attention mechanisms during grid disruptions.

## Method Summary
The architecture processes hourly multivariate inputs—CIF, grid load demand, renewable/non-renewable generation, and temperature—to forecast next-day CIF. The LT-MWKC branch segments the time series into overlapping patches of varying lengths and applies parallel 1D convolutions with multiple wavelet kernels (e.g., Morlet, Mexican Hat) to capture multi-frequency local temporal patterns. The CV-DWCC branch computes continuous wavelet transforms for each variable, then for every variable combination performs local multiple regression in wavelet coefficient space to derive correlation strength and dominant drivers at each time-scale point; the resulting 2D correlation maps are processed by 2D convolutions. Both branches’ outputs are concatenated, passed through fully connected layers, and fused via a softmax-weighted mechanism that adaptively balances temporal versus cross-variable contributions. Training uses sliding windows with 5-fold cross-validation and grid search for hyperparameter tuning; performance is assessed via RMSE, MAE, and SMAPE.

## Key Results
- Achieves state-of-the-art performance across four Australian electricity markets (NSW, SA, QLD, VIC).
- Reduces MAE by up to 26.9% in high-volatility South Australia compared to the best baseline.
- Ablation studies show removal of CV-DWCC increases MAE by >46% and removal of LT-MWKC by 22–42%, confirming complementary roles.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1: LT-MWKC for Local-Temporal Dependencies
The LT-MWKC module improves extraction of fine-grained local-temporal dependencies (LTD) by processing overlapping patches of variable lengths with multiple wavelet convolutional kernels. Overlapping patches preserve continuity across segment boundaries, while wavelet kernels (e.g., Morlet, Mexican Hat) act as band-pass filters sensitive to specific frequency bands and transient shapes. The multi-kernel approach captures complementary multi-frequency patterns within short windows, and learnable fusion weights (`α_m`) combine them. This addresses non-stationary CIF patterns that span multiple overlapping time scales, which fixed-length non-overlapping segments would miss.

### Mechanism 2: CV-DWCC for Dynamic Cross-Variable Dependencies
The CV-DWCC module captures dynamic, higher-order cross-variable dependencies (CVD) across time-frequency domains. For each variable combination, it applies continuous wavelet transform (CWT) to each variable and performs wavelet-based local multiple regression (WLMR). At each time point `s` and scale `j`, local regression (kernel-weighted around `s`) estimates the relationship; the variable maximizing the local coefficient of determination (R²) is marked as the dominant driver. This yields a correlation tensor (WLMC coefficient map) and a dominant-variable tensor. 2D convolutions then learn spatial patterns of dynamic CVD, accommodating non-linear, time-varying, frequency-dependent inter-variable relationships that static correlations miss.

### Mechanism 3: Adaptive Fusion
The adaptive fusion mechanism (softmax-weighted combination) allows the model to dynamically leverage the complementary strengths of LT-MWKC and CV-DWCC based on input context. The model can rely more on LT-MWKC for sharp local fluctuations (e.g., renewable ramping) and more on CV-DWCC for complex variable interplay (e.g., demand-supply mismatches). Grad-CAM visualizations confirm shifting attention during disruptive events, indicating context-aware weighting.

## Foundational Learning
- **Continuous Wavelet Transform (CWT) for Multi-Frequency Analysis**: CWT provides a time-frequency representation (scalogram) with continuous time and scale, essential for capturing non-stationary transients (like solar ramps) that discrete transforms or fixed Fourier bins miss. The scale parameter controls time-frequency trade-off: large scales give good frequency resolution but poor time resolution, while small scales give good time resolution but poor frequency resolution. This trade-off is crucial for modeling both rapid CIF changes and slower trends.
  - *Quick check:* How does the scale parameter in CWT control the trade-off between time resolution and frequency resolution, and why is this important for modeling both rapid CIF changes and slower trends?

- **Local Multiple Regression (LMR) / Wavelet Local Multiple Correlation (WLMC)**: LMR is a kernel-weighted regression that estimates local relationships. WLMC extends LMR to the wavelet domain, applying local regression to wavelet coefficients and extracting the dominant variable's R² as a correlation measure at each (time, scale) point. This enables quantifying dynamic, multi-frequency correlations among multiple variables, which is necessary because inter-variable relationships in CIF forecasting are non-linear, time-varying, and frequency-dependent.
  - *Quick check:* In the LMR loss function (Eq. 4), what is the role of the temporal weighting function `θ(t−s)`, and how does its choice affect the locality of the estimated dependency?

- **Gradient-weighted Class Activation Mapping (Grad-CAM) for Multivariate Time Series**: Grad-CAM is used to interpret the model's decisions by highlighting which input time steps and variables were most influential for a forecast. When applied to models with 2D convolutional layers processing time-frequency maps (like CV-DWCC), a high Grad-CAM score at a specific (time, scale) location indicates that the model considered the pattern at that time-frequency point as important for the prediction. This interpretability is crucial for validating model behavior during rare disruptive events and understanding the adaptive fusion.

## Architecture Onboarding
### Component Map
Input time series X (T×N) -> LT-MWKC branch: transpose to N×T -> patch extraction (varying lengths k) -> parallel 1D convs with M wavelet kernels per k -> fusion via learnable weights α_m -> additional 1D conv blocks -> output F'_LT. CV-DWCC branch: transpose -> CWT per variable -> for each variable combination P: compute WLMC tensor C_P and dominant variable tensor D_P -> concatenate [C_P∥D_P] -> 2D convolution -> output F_CV. Fusion: concatenate F'_LT and F_CV -> FC layers -> softmax weighting -> final S-step CIF forecast.

### Critical Path
The two parallel branches (LT-MWKC & CV-DWCC) are independent until concatenation. Implementation must ensure their output feature dimensions are compatible for concatenation. The paper does not specify exact channel numbers; this requires tuning. Also, the WLMC computation is complex and must be correctly implemented for each variable combination.

### Design Tradeoffs
- Computational cost vs. resolution: More wavelet types (M), patch lengths (d), and variable combinations (∼N²) increase parameters and FLOPS. Choices for M, d, and combination pruning are critical.
- Interpretability vs. complexity: WLMC and dominant-variable selection add steps but enable Grad-CAM on structured correlation maps. Simpler attention might be less interpretable.
- Data hungry vs. generalization: Many parameters (wavelet kernels, LMR weights, conv layers) require sufficient data; performance on four Australian markets is shown, but generalizability to other markets is uncertain.

### Failure Signatures
- Ablation pattern: Removal of CV-DWCC causes larger MAE increases (>46%) than removal of LT-MWKC (~22-42%). If a new market shows the opposite, relative importance may differ, indicating misalignment with local dynamics.
- Grad-CAM anomaly: During normal operation, Grad-CAM should show variable importance aligning with known physics (e.g., solar output affecting CIF midday). Persistent misalignment (e.g., ignoring renewables) suggests the model is not learning meaningful CVD.
- High volatility penalty: The model claims advantage in high-volatility SA. If on a new volatile market it underperforms SOTA, check if wavelet scales and patch lengths cover the dominant fluctuation frequencies.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- **Generalizability**: How does the model perform on electricity markets outside Australia with different grid structures, fuel mixes, and regulatory frameworks? The study only evaluates four Australian states.
- **Practical Deployment**: Can real-time CIF forecasts from this model be effectively utilized in demand-side management systems to optimize load scheduling and reduce emissions? The paper does not test optimization strategies.
- **Interpretability Robustness**: Is the model's interpretability (Grad-CAM attention shifts during disruptive events) robust and consistent across a wide range of grid conditions and event types? Only one atypical event was analyzed.
- **Input Variable Expansion**: To what extent does expanding the input variable set (beyond CIF, GLD, REG, NEG, temperature) improve forecasting accuracy and robustness? The paper uses a limited set.

## Limitations
- Exact hyperparameters and WLMC implementation details (temporal weighting function, bandwidth, wavelet families, scale levels) are not disclosed, hindering exact replication.
- Evidence for core mechanisms relies on self-reported experiments; external literature on wavelet-patch processing and WLMC for carbon intensity is sparse, limiting confidence in broader generalizability.
- Computational cost scales quadratically with number of variables and linearly with wavelet kernels, raising scalability concerns for larger grids.

## Confidence
- **LT-MWKC mechanism**: Medium. Ablation shows meaningful impact (MAE increases 22–42%), but external validation is absent.
- **CV-DWCC mechanism**: Medium. Ablation reveals larger sensitivity (MAE +46%), yet WLMC details are underspecified and risk of overfitting remains.
- **Adaptive fusion**: Medium-High. Grad-CAM visualizations suggest dynamic attention shifts, but these could be post-hoc rationalizations.
- **Overall performance claim**: Medium. Four-market evaluation is solid, but single-country focus may not transfer to other regions.
