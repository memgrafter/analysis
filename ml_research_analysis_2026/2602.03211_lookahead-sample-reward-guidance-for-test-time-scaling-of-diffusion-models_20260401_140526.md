# Lookahead Sample Reward Guidance for Test-Time Scaling of Diffusion Models

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2602.03211
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.03211
- **Reference count:** 40
- **Primary result:** Introduces a closed-form, derivative-free guidance method that matches the GenEval performance of gradient guidance (DATE) on SDXL while reducing inference time by 9.5×, with strong efficiency-performance trade-offs across models.

## Executive Summary
Diffusion models often misalign with human intent during image generation. Existing test-time scaling methods, such as gradient-based guidance, require costly neural backpropagation at each step to approximate the expected future reward (EFR), while sample-based approaches like Sequential Monte Carlo (SMC) suffer from performance collapse with few particles. LiDAR addresses these issues by reformulating the EFR using only pre-sampled final outputs and the known forward diffusion kernel, thus eliminating neural dependency on the intermediate particle. This enables a two-phase process: a fast "lookahead" stage collects coarse samples once per prompt, and the target sampling phase steers particles toward high-reward lookahead samples via a soft-weighting scheme derived from a closed-form gradient. LiDAR significantly improves the efficiency-performance trade-off compared to gradient guidance and SMC methods on Stable Diffusion v1.5 and SDXL, using ImageReward for supervision, and its gains are orthogonal to fine-tuning.

## Method Summary
LiDAR operates in two distinct phases per prompt. **Phase 1 (Lookahead):** A fast approximate solver (e.g., DPM-solver with a small number of steps δ) generates a fixed set of \( n \) coarse final samples \( \{\hat{x}_0^i\} \) from the pre-trained diffusion model \( p_\theta(x_0|c) \). Each sample is annotated with a scalar reward \( r_i = r(\hat{x}_0^i, c) \) using a separate reward model. **Phase 2 (Target):** The main sampling process generates high-quality images by solving the reverse diffusion process with a modified Stein score. The modification is a guidance term \( \nabla_{x_t} \hat{r}_\lambda^t(x_t, c) \) computed in closed form from the lookahead set (Eq. 17). This term depends on the current noisy sample \( x_t \), the pre-sampled \( \hat{x}_0^i \), their rewards \( r_i \), and the known Gaussian forward kernel variance \( \sigma_t^2 \). It is a weighted sum of vectors pointing from \( x_t \) toward each \( \hat{x}_0^i \), with weights derived from a softmax combining reward information and Gaussian proximity. The total guidance is scaled by a hyperparameter \( s \) and added to the base Stein score. This approach is derivative-free, decouples lookahead cost from target sampling, and requires no neural network evaluation beyond the base model's score.

## Key Results
- On SDXL, LiDAR (with DPM-8 lookahead) matches the GenEval score of the prior gradient guidance method DATE while reducing inference time by **9.5×**.
- Across SD v1.5 and SDXL, LiDAR substantially outperforms both gradient guidance and SMC methods in the efficiency-performance trade-off when using ImageReward for annotation.
- Performance gains are observed with a **minimal number of lookahead samples** (e.g., \( n=3 \)) and are **orthogonal to fine-tuning**, providing additive improvements when combined.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- **Claim:** The Expected Future Reward (EFR) can be computed without neural backpropagation by using pre-sampled final outputs and the forward diffusion kernel, decoupling the current particle from the reward computation.
- **Mechanism:** Theorem 3.1 reformulates \( r_\lambda^t(x_t, c) \) as a log-ratio expectation over *marginal* final samples \( x_0 \sim p_\theta(x_0|c) \) and the known forward kernel \( p(x_t|x_0) \). This removes \( x_t \) as a direct input to the neural score network \( s_\theta \) and the reward function \( r \). The dependence is mediated solely by the analytic Gaussian kernel.
- **Core assumption:** The forward process is a tractable Gaussian kernel. The marginal samples from the pre-trained model \( p_\theta \) approximate the data distribution sufficiently.
- **Evidence anchors:** [abstract] "enables closed-form, derivative-free guidance" and "detaches the neural dependency". [section 3.1] Theorem 3.1 derivation.
- **Break condition:** If the lookahead distribution \( q(x_0|c) \) is a poor approximation of \( p_\theta \), the guidance becomes biased. Error bounded in Theorem 3.3.

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim:** A small number of "lookahead" samples from a fast solver can provide a high-quality guidance signal, with error scaling predictably.
- **Mechanism:** A cheap solver generates a set \( \{\hat{x}_0^i, r_i\} \) once per prompt (Algorithm 1). During target sampling, particles are guided toward high-reward samples via the soft weighting in Eq. (17). The theoretical error between the target and proposed distributions is bounded by \( \mathcal{O}(1/\sqrt{\delta}) \) (Theorem 3.3), where \( \delta \) is the number of lookahead steps.
- **Core assumption:** The fast solver's distribution \( q(x_0|c) \) is close enough to \( p_\theta \) that its reward statistics are informative. The Gaussian kernel accurately mediates the \( x_t \)-to-\( x_0 \) relationship.
- **Evidence anchors:** [abstract] "lookahead sampling stage... collects these marginal samples once per prompt". [section 3.2] Definition 3.2, Theorem 3.3.
- **Break condition:** If the lookahead solver is extremely crude (very small \( \delta \)), the bounded error may be large enough to misguide. Empirical results (Figure 3, Table 3) show robustness down to \( \delta=3 \).

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim:** The gradient of the empirical lookahead reward w.r.t. \( x_t \) has a closed-form expression requiring no neural network differentiation.
- **Mechanism:** Substituting the Gaussian forward kernel into the empirical EFR estimator (Eq. 16) and differentiating yields a sum of weighted vectors from \( x_t \) to each \( \hat{x}_0^i \). Weights \( w_{r_i} \) and \( w_i \) are softmaxes combining reward and Gaussian proximity (Eqs. 18–19). The guidance term is their difference.
- **Core assumption:** Forward process noise is isotropic Gaussian. Lookahead samples are fixed; their number \( n \) is finite, leading to Monte Carlo error.
- **Evidence anchors:** [section 3.3] Theorem 3.4 and proof. [section 2.2.3] Contrast with gradient guidance's backpropagation.
- **Break condition:** Derivation relies on the Gaussian kernel. For non-Gaussian forward processes, this formula does not hold. Performance scales with \( n \) (Figure 3), so too few samples yield high variance.

## Foundational Learning
- **Concept: Diffusion Models & Reverse SDE/ODE**
  - **Why needed:** LiDAR modifies a pre-trained diffusion model's sampling process. Understanding the baseline reverse diffusion (Eq. 3) and Stein score \( s_\theta \) is essential.
  - **Quick check:** Given a pre-trained score network \( s_\theta(x_t, t, c) \), what is the mathematical form of the reverse-time SDE?
- **Concept: Expected Future Reward (EFR) & Reward-Tilted Distribution**
  - **Why needed:** The goal is to sample from \( p_{\lambda, \theta}(x_0|c) \propto p_\theta(x_0|c) \exp(\lambda \cdot r(x_0,c)) \). The core challenge is approximating \( \nabla_{x_t} \log \mathbb{E}[p_\theta(x_0|x_t,c) \exp(\lambda \cdot r(x_0,c))] \) during sampling.
  - **Quick check:** Why is directly computing the EFR intractable, and what is the standard first-order Taylor approximation (Eq. 7) used by gradient guidance?
- **Concept: Sequential Monte Carlo (SMC) for Diffusion & Particle Collapse**
  - **Why needed:** SMC is a major comparative baseline. Understanding its formulation (Eq. 9) and its failure mode—performance collapse with few particles due to high-dimensional resampling—explains why LiDAR's particle-independent guidance is advantageous.
  - **Quick check:** In SMC-based diffusion steering, why does performance degrade when the number of target particles \( N \) is small (e.g., 4 in Table 6)?

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map:**
  Pre-trained Diffusion Model (provides \( s_\theta \), forward kernel) -> Lookahead Sampling Engine (fast generator, produces \( \{\hat{x}_0^i\} \)) -> Reward Annotator (outputs \( r_i \)) -> Target Sampler with LiDAR Guidance (uses Eq. 17 to modify score)

- **Critical path:** Lookahead sampling (Algorithm 1) must complete and annotate all \( n \) samples before target sampling (Algorithm 2) can begin. The guidance computation (Eq. 17) is a lightweight per-step operation dependent only on the fixed lookahead set and current \( x_t \).

- **Design tradeoffs:**
  - \( \delta \) (lookahead steps) vs. \( n \) (samples): Smaller \( \delta \) is cheaper per sample but noisier; larger \( n \) reduces Monte Carlo variance but increases upfront cost. They trade off along efficiency-performance curves (Figure 11).
  - \( s \) (guidance scale) vs. \( \lambda \) (reward exponent): \( \lambda \) is fixed in lookahead; \( s \) is tuned at target time. This decoupling allows adjusting strength without re-annotating.
  - Lookahead quality vs. Cost: Using distilled models (LCM/DMD) improves the bound (Theorem 3.3) but may have higher per-sample cost than a 3-step DPM solver.

- **Failure signatures:**
  1. **Poor lookahead quality:** If \( q(x_0|c) \) is very inaccurate (e.g., \( \delta=1 \)), all \( \hat{x}_0^i \) might be low-reward, leading to weak guidance.
  2. **Insufficient lookahead diversity (\( n \) too small):** With \( n=1 \), guidance collapses to a single attractor, causing mode collapse.
  3. **Extreme guidance scale (\( s \) too high):** Can push \( x_t \) off the data manifold, though LiDAR is suggested to be more robust than gradient guidance.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
1. To what extent are LiDAR's gains orthogonal to other test-time scaling methods like SMC and Best-of-N, and what is the theoretical foundation for additive improvements?
2. How does the quality of the lookahead sampler \( q \) quantitatively affect the theoretical error bound \( \mathcal{O}(1/\sqrt{\delta}) \), and can this bound be tightened?
3. For what classes of reward functions \( r \) does LiDAR avoid reward hacking, and can we formally characterize conditions under which its soft-weighting preserves sample diversity?
4. Can the lookahead sampling cost be further reduced (e.g., via distillation or caching) without sacrificing gains, and what is the minimal sufficient condition on \( q \)?
5. Does LiDAR's closed-form EFR extend to discrete diffusion models (e.g., in text/audio) where the forward kernel is categorical, and what modifications are needed for non-Gaussian marginals?

## Limitations
- Lookahead solver specifics (CFG application, scheduler details, exact step counts) are underspecified, risking implementation deviation from reported results.
- The core derivation assumes a Gaussian forward kernel; non-Gaussian distortions (e.g., in some SDE formulations) could degrade guidance.
- Evaluation is limited to GenEval and ImageReward on standard models; generalization to other reward models (e.g., human preferences) or domains (audio, video) is not established.
- The theoretical bound (Theorem 3.3) relies on the lookahead distribution quality; poor approximations lead to biased guidance without clear failure thresholds.

## Confidence
- **Efficiency-performance trade-off vs. baselines:** High. Results are clear, reproducible, and show large speedups.
- **Minimal lookahead samples (\( n=3 \)) sufficient:** Medium. Ablations show robustness, but extreme or out-of-distribution prompts may require more; tested \( \delta \) values are limited.
- **Orthogonality to fine-tuning:** Medium. Paper shows additive gains, but interaction with diverse fine-tuning methods is not deeply explored.
