# Towards Parameter-Free Temporal Difference Learning

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2603.02577  
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.02577  
- **Reference count:** 0  
- **Primary result:** An exponential step‑size schedule for TD(0) achieves the optimal bias‑variance trade‑off for the last iterate under i.i.d. sampling without requiring the minimum feature covariance eigenvalue ω; a regularized variant extends this to Markovian sampling while eliminating the need for projections, iterate averaging, and knowledge of τₘᵢₓ or ω.

## Executive Summary
Temporal difference (TD) learning with linear function approximation typically relies on problem‑dependent step sizes and auxiliary techniques like projections or iterate averaging to ensure convergence. This paper proposes a simple modification: an exponential step‑size schedule, ηₜ = η₀ αᵗ with α = T⁻¹/ᵀ, applied to the standard TD(0) algorithm. For i.i.d. samples from the stationary distribution, the method attains the optimal bias‑variance trade‑off for the last iterate without needing the minimum eigenvalue ω of the feature covariance. For the more practical Markovian setting, combining the exponential schedule with L₂ regularization (λ = 1/√T) yields a convergence rate comparable to state‑of‑the‑art but eliminates the need for projections, iterate averaging, and explicit knowledge of τₘᵢₓ or ω. Both results provide guarantees for the last iterate, contrasting with many prior analyses that require averaging.

## Method Summary
The core algorithmic change is an exponential decay of the step size. For i.i.d. data, the algorithm is the classical TD(0) update wₜ₊₁ = wₜ + ηₜ δₜ ϕ(sₜ) with δₜ = rₜ + γ wₜᵀ ϕ(sₜ₊₁) − wₜᵀ ϕ(sₜ) and ηₜ = η₀ αᵗ, where α = T⁻¹/ᵀ and a constant η₀ (e.g., η₀ = (1−γ)/8). No projection or averaging is used. For Markovian trajectories, a regularization term −λw is added to the update: wₜ₊₁ = wₜ + ηₜ (δₜ ϕ(sₜ) − λ wₜ), with λ = 1/√T and a step size η₀ chosen according to a formula that depends on λ and T (derived from the theoretical analysis). The exponential decay ensures that early updates (which carry bias due to non‑stationarity or initialization) are effectively averaged out, while later updates have small variance. The regularization renders the Markovian problem strongly convex, making the step size independent of the minimum eigenvalue ω and the mixing time τₘᵢₓ.

## Key Results
- **i.i.d. sampling:** The last‑iterate error satisfies 𝔼[‖w_T − w*‖²] = Õ( exp(−ω T) + σ²/(ω² T) ), where σ² is the variance at the optimum. This matches the known optimal bias‑variance trade‑off without requiring ω to set the step size.
- **Markovian sampling:** With λ = 1/√T and the exponential schedule, the regularized TD(0) achieves 𝔼[‖w_T − w‖²] = O( exp(− ω² T / ln³ T) + ln⁴(T)/(ω² T) · exp(m/ln(1/ρ)) ), where m, ρ are mixing parameters. The rate is comparable to prior state‑of‑the‑art but eliminates the need for projections, iterate averaging, and explicit τₘᵢₓ or ω in the algorithm.
- **Last‑iterate guarantees:** Both results provide bounds for the final iterate w_T, avoiding the memory overhead of storing and averaging past iterates.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- **Claim:** The exponential step‑size schedule enables standard TD(0) to attain the optimal bias‑variance trade‑off for the last iterate under i.i.d. sampling without requiring knowledge of ω.
- **Mechanism:** The step size decays as η₀ αᵗ, effectively implementing an exponential averaging. The total sums of αᵗ and α²ᵗ yield a bias term that decays exponentially in T (controlled by ω) and a variance term of order σ²/(ω² T). The step‑size parameters are chosen so that the decay rate α = T⁻¹/ᵀ automatically balances these terms without tuning to ω.
- **Core assumption:** Samples are i.i.d. from the stationary distribution; feature vectors are bounded; ω > 0.
- **Break condition:** If sampling is not i.i.d. (e.g., Markovian with poor mixing), the bias term no longer decays as exp(−ω T) and additional error terms dominate.

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim:** For Markovian sampling, regularizing TD(0) with λ = 1/√T and using the exponential step‑size yields convergence comparable to state‑of‑the‑art while removing the need for projections, iterate averaging, and knowledge of τₘᵢₓ or ω.
- **Mechanism:** L₂ regularization makes the underlying problem strongly convex with parameter λ. This strong convexity allows a universal step size that does not depend on ω. The exponential decay controls the variance and the influence of Markovian dependencies; because λ vanishes as 1/√T, the fixed point approaches the true w* as T → ∞. The rate includes an exp(m/ln(1/ρ)) factor due to mixing, but the algorithm itself never uses τₘᵢₓ or ω.
- **Core assumption:** The Markov chain is geometrically mixing with constants m, ρ; features bounded; λ > 0.
- **Break condition:** If the chain mixes very slowly (ρ close to 1), the factor exp(m/ln(1/ρ)) becomes huge, degrading the bound. Additionally, the step‑size condition requires T to be sufficiently larger than τₘᵢₓ.

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim:** The exponential step‑size schedule permits an induction‑based proof that bounds the effect of mixing time without using τₘᵢₓ to set the step‑size, enabling last‑iterate guarantees.
- **Mechanism:** The step size decays so fast that after roughly τₘᵢₓ iterations the influence of the initial distribution becomes negligible. The proof splits into a base phase (t ≤ τₘᵢₓ) and an inductive phase (t > τₘᵢₓ), using inequalities that relate exponential sums of step sizes to the mixing parameters (a = 1/ln(1/ρ), b = ln(2(2+λ)m)/ln(1/ρ)). This eliminates explicit τₘᵢₓ from the step‑size choice.
- **Core assumption:** Mixing time τₘᵢₓ finite; η₀ small enough; T large enough (e.g., ln²(T)/T ≤ 1/(2a), ln(T)/T ≤ 1/b).
- **Break condition:** If T is not sufficiently larger than τₘᵢₓ, the base case bound fails, breaking the induction.

## Foundational Learning

### Temporal Difference (TD) Learning
Why needed: TD(0) is a core reinforcement learning algorithm that updates value estimates via bootstrapping. It is not the gradient of a fixed objective, so its convergence analysis requires special care.
Quick check question: Write the linear TD(0) update w_{t+1} = w_t + η_t δ_t ϕ(s_t) and define each symbol.

### Linear Function Approximation
Why needed: Value functions are approximated as V_w(s) = wᵀ ϕ(s). The feature covariance Σ = Φᵀ D Φ (with D the stationary distribution) and its minimum eigenvalue ω govern identifiability and convergence rates.
Quick check question: How is Σ defined, and why must ω > 0 for identifiability?

### Markov Chain Mixing Time
Why needed: Under Markovian sampling, state distributions converge to stationarity at a rate determined by τₘᵢₓ. The analysis bounds non‑stationarity bias using mixing parameters.
Quick check question: Define τ_δ and explain the roles of m and ρ in the mixing bound sup_{µ₀} dTV(P^t_π µ₀, µ^π) ≤ m ρ^t.

## Architecture Onboarding

### Component Map
Step-size scheduler -> Core TD update -> Regularizer (Markovian only)

### Critical Path
1. Precompute exponential step size: α = T⁻¹/ᵀ (use α = exp(−ln(T)/T)).
2. Choose η₀: i.i.d. → η₀ = (1−γ)/8; Markovian → λ = 1/√T and η₀ = λ / (C ln²(T) + C′ + (8+2λ²)) with C, C′ given in the analysis.
3. Run for T iterations; the final iterate w_T is the output.
4. For Markovian, ensure T satisfies asymptotic conditions (e.g., T ≥ max{1/η₀, ln(2(2+λ)T m/η₀)/ln(1/ρ)}).

### Design Tradeoffs
- **Bias‑variance:** Exponential decay reduces variance at the cost of slower initial progress compared to constant step sizes.
- **Regularization bias:** The L₂ penalty λ = 1/√T introduces a small shift that vanishes as T grows.
- **Memory efficiency:** Last‑iterate output avoids storing and averaging past iterates.

### Failure Signatures
- **Divergence:** η₀ too large or λ too small; monitor ‖w_t‖ growth.
- **Stagnation:** Very small ω yields slow convergence; track relative error.
- **High variance:** η₀ too small leads to noisy early updates; check early MSE.
- **Markovian slow mixing:** If ρ ≈ 1, the factor exp(m/ln(1/ρ)) dominates; examine autocorrelation of updates.

### First 3 Experiments
1. **i.i.d. baseline:** Synthetic linear MDP with known ω. Compare constant step‑size, exponential step‑size, and exponential with averaging. Plot ‖w_t − w*‖² vs. t; verify exponential last iterate converges at O(1/T) and is independent of ω in step‑size setting.
2. **Markovian comparison:** Build MDPs with controlled mixing time (e.g., transition probability to stay = 1−ε). Test regularized exponential TD against projected TD and Polyak‑Ruppert averaging. Compare final error vs. T.
3. **Sensitivity to η₀ and λ:** In i.i.d., vary η₀ by factors 0.5 and 2 around (1−γ)/8 and measure convergence. In Markovian, vary λ as c/√T for c ∈ {0.1, 1, 10} and observe the bias‑variance trade‑off.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### High‑Probability Guarantees
**Question:** Can high‑probability convergence guarantees be established for the proposed parameter‑free TD(0) algorithm?  
**Basis in paper:** The conclusion explicitly states: “For future work, we view high‑probability guarantees as an important direction.”  
**Why unresolved:** Current finite‑time analysis yields only expectation bounds; high‑probability results require different probabilistic tools (e.g., martingale concentration) and are crucial for reliable practical use.  
**What evidence would resolve it:** Derive a finite‑time bound holding with probability at least 1−δ for the exponential step‑size schedule, ideally matching the expectation dependence on ω and τₘᵢₓ, and extend the proof to Markovian sampling.

### Dependence on Mixing Parameters
**Question:** Why does the Markovian bound contain an exponential term exp(m/ log(1/ρ)) instead of a linear dependence on τₘᵢₓ, and can this be improved?  
**Basis in paper:** In Section 4.2: “Compared to previous works, one limitation of Theorem 4.12 is the extra term exp(m/ log(1/ρ))... We conjecture that this worse dependence is an artifact of our analysis, and improving this is an important direction for future work.”  
**Why unresolved:** Prior works achieve a linear τₘᵢₓ factor, while the present bound is exponentially worse in the mixing parameters. The authors suspect a tighter analysis could remove the exponential dependence, but this remains unproven.  
**What evidence would resolve it:** A refined analysis of the induction or drift terms that replaces exp(m/ log(1/ρ)) by a polynomial (e.g., τₘᵢₓ) or an impossibility result showing exponential dependence is inherent.

### Logarithmic Factors in the i.i.d. Variance Bound
**Question:** Do the logarithmic factors ln²(T) in the i.i.d. variance bound stem from the exponential schedule or the analysis, and can they be removed without resorting to iterate averaging?  
**Basis in paper:** After Theorem 3.4: “Compared to Samsonov et al.; Patil et al., Theorem 3.4 has an additional O(ln(T)) dependence... In the future, we will investigate whether these bounds can be sharpened to reduce or remove the extra log factors.”  
**Why unresolved:** The current bound includes ln²(T)/(ω² T) in the variance term, whereas optimal averaging‑based results have no log factor. Removing it while keeping a last‑iterate guarantee would improve the bias‑variance trade‑off.  
**What evidence would resolve it:** A refined analysis of the exponential step‑size summation that eliminates the extra logs, or a lower bound proving that any last‑iterate algorithm with adaptive step‑sizes must incur at least logarithmic overhead.

## Limitations
- The theoretical constants (η₀, λ) are conservative and often require empirical adjustment; the prescribed values may be too small for moderate T.
- The Markovian bound retains mixing parameters in an exponential term exp(m/ log(1/ρ)), which can lead to vacuous guarantees for slow‑mixing chains.
- The analysis relies on asymptotic conditions (T ≫ τₘᵢₓ, ln²(T)/T ≤ 1/(2a)) that are unrealistic for small to moderate problem sizes.
- The assumptions of linear bounded features and bounded rewards limit broader applicability; the λ = 1/√T regularizer introduces a bias of order 1/√T.

## Confidence
- **i.i.d. optimal bias‑variance trade‑off:** **High** (Theorem 3.4 provides an explicit last‑iterate rate without ω).
- **Markovian parameter‑free convergence:** **Medium** (Theorem 4.12 avoids τₘᵢₓ/ω in step‑size but bound depends on mixing constants and uses loose η₀).
- **Last‑iterate guarantees:** **High** (both theorems directly bound w_T, not an average).
