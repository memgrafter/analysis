# Tailoring AI-Driven Reading Scaffolds to the Distinct Needs of Neurodiverse Learners

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2603.28370
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.28370
- **Reference count:** 0
- **Primary result:** No single scaffold is universally optimal; effectiveness is diagnosis- and complexity-dependent.

## Executive Summary
This within-subject pilot study compared four AI-driven reading scaffolds (plain text, sentence segmentation, segmentation plus pictograms, segmentation plus pictograms with keyword labels) for 14 primary-school learners with Special Educational Needs and Disabilities (SEND). Comprehension and experience were measured across standardized reading passages. Results revealed highly heterogeneous responses: learners with Language Disorders improved with segmentation and pictograms but were hindered by keyword labels, while those with Emotional Disorders performed worse with pictograms. Some diagnoses reached peer-level comprehension with pictogram-only supports. The findings underscore that scaffold effectiveness is not uniform but depends on specific neurodiverse profiles and task complexity, advocating for personalized, adjustable supports in supervised inclusive settings.

## Method Summary
The study employed a within-subject design where each participant (N=14, clinically diagnosed with SEND including DCD, ADHD, LD, SLD, MMD, ED) completed one reading passage per modality from the Italian 4th/5th grade MT test bank. Modality assignment to passages was counterbalanced to mitigate order and text effects. Comprehension was assessed immediately via 10–14 multiple-choice questions per passage. Post-session, learners and therapists rated each modality on Likert scales, and open-ended feedback was collected. Primary outcomes were absolute and relative modality gains; secondary outcomes included rating differences and qualitative responses. Mixed-design ANOVAs analyzed Modality × diagnosis/comorbidity interactions.

## Key Results
- Comprehension gains were heterogeneous by diagnosis: learners with Language Disorders (LD) improved with segmentation+pictograms but adding keyword labels reduced benefit; learners with Emotional Disorders (ED) performed worse when pictograms were introduced.
- Learners with Developmental Coordination Disorder (DCD) and LD achieved peer-level comprehension scores in the pictogram-only condition.
- Relative gain patterns varied with comorbidity level; children with higher comorbidity rated baseline text as significantly harder to understand.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The paper does not explicitly theorize a cognitive or pedagogical mechanism. However, the observed diagnosis-dependent effects imply that scaffolds interact with neurodiverse learners' specific cognitive profiles. Pictograms may reduce extraneous cognitive load for some (e.g., by offloading verbal processing) but introduce irrelevant visual noise or semantic mismatch for others (e.g., ED). Keyword labels likely increase generative processing demands that benefit some learners but overwhelm those with language or attention difficulties. The supervised context (therapist presence) may also moderate effectiveness by enabling dynamic adjustment, though this is not isolated in the design.

## Foundational Learning
- **Scaffolding Theory:** Temporary supports to bridge current ability to task demand; must fade as competence grows. *Why needed:* Explains why over-scaffolding (e.g., redundant keywords) can hinder autonomy. *Quick check:* Does each scaffold layer independently improve comprehension for the target group?
- **Neurodiversity (LD, ED, DCD):** Distinct cognitive and behavioral profiles affecting reading (e.g., language processing, emotional regulation, motor coordination). *Why needed:* Predicts divergent scaffold responses. *Quick check:* Are diagnostic subgroups sufficiently powered to detect interactions?
- **Within-Subject Design:** Each participant experiences all conditions, controlling for individual baseline differences. *Why needed:* Isolates scaffold effect from learner variability. *Quick check:* Was counterbalancing fully orthogonal for text and order?
- **Absolute & Relative Gain Metrics:** Absolute gain = score(modality) – score(baseline); relative gain = (gain)/(max possible gain). *Why needed:* Captures improvement magnitude and efficiency. *Quick check:* Are gains computed per participant or aggregated?
- **ARASAAC Pictogram Repository:** Standardized pictogram set for augmentative communication. *Why needed:* Provides consistent visual symbols. *Quick check:* Are pictogram definitions semantically aligned with target keywords?
- **Multilingual Sentence Transformers:** Embedding models for cross-lingual semantic similarity. *Why needed:* Disambiguates pictogram selection in Italian context. *Quick check:* Is cosine similarity threshold reported?

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component Map:**  
Input Text -> Sentence Segmentation -> Keyword Extraction (e.g., YAKE) -> Pictogram Retrieval (ARASAAC) -> Contextual Disambiguation (multilingual transformer cosine similarity) -> Output Modality (segmentation only / +pictograms / +labels)

**Critical Path:** Pictogram disambiguation step—depends on embedding model choice and similarity threshold; high risk of semantic mismatch.

**Design Tradeoffs:** 
- Scaffold richness vs. cognitive overload: Adding layers (pictograms, labels) may help some but harm others.
- Automation vs. supervision: Fully automated pictogram selection may misalign; therapist oversight can correct but limits scalability.
- Generalizability vs. personalization: Fixed pipeline simplifies deployment but ignores individual diagnostic needs.

**Failure Signatures:** 
- Pictograms irrelevant to sentence context (e.g., homonym mismatch).
- Keyword labels redundant or confusing for LD/ED learners.
- Small subgroup sizes (e.g., ED n=3) yielding unstable interaction effects.

**First Three Experiments:**
1.  **Pipeline Validation:** Run the full pictogram generation on 50 Italian sentences; have two raters judge contextual accuracy (binary: appropriate/inappropriate). Target >80% agreement.
2.  **Subgroup Pilot:** Test the four modalities on 5 new participants per diagnosis (LD, ED, DCD) to verify direction of reported effects (e.g., LD benefit from pictograms, ED harm). Use within-subject design, analyze by subgroup.
3.  **Counterbalancing Robustness:** Re-analyze the original data under all feasible Latin square permutations (if original scheme unknown) to check if Modality×Diagnosis interactions persist.

## Limitations
- Exact reading passages from the Italian MT test bank are unspecified, preventing stimulus replication.
- Pictogram disambiguation details (model, threshold) are vague, risking implementation variance.
- Small, heterogeneous sample (N=14) with uneven diagnostic distribution limits statistical power and generalizability.
- Findings are confined to therapist-supervised sessions; unsupervised efficacy is unknown.

## Confidence
- **Heterogeneous scaffold effectiveness (no universal optimal):** Medium (within-subject design shows participant variation, but small N and unreported passage equivalence weaken certainty).
- **Diagnosis-specific patterns (e.g., LD benefits from pictograms, ED harmed):** Low (post-hoc subgroup analyses with minimal cell sizes prone to overfitting).
- **Personalization necessity:** Medium-High (divergent gains robustly support adjustable scaffolds, though specific diagnostic rules are low-confidence).
