# Goal Force: Teaching Video Models To Accomplish Physics-Conditioned Goals
## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2601.05848  
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.05848  
- **Reference count:** 40  
- **Primary result:** Introduces Goal Force, a framework that conditions video generation on explicit goal force vectors, enabling zero-shot planning for complex physical tasks.

## Executive Summary
Video-based world models for robotics and planning require precise goal specification, but text is too abstract and target images often infeasible for dynamic physical tasks. This paper introduces Goal Force, a framework that conditions video generation on explicit goal force vectors—spatial-temporal signals representing the desired effect on a target object. The method uses a multi-channel control tensor encoding direct force (the cause), goal force (the effect), and optional mass information. A video diffusion model (Wan2.2) is fine-tuned via ControlNet on a synthetic dataset of causal primitives (domino chains, ball collisions, plant dynamics). Crucially, during training one of the force channels is randomly masked, forcing the model to learn bidirectional reasoning: from goal to plan and from action to outcome. Despite training only on simple synthetic physics, the model demonstrates strong zero-shot generalization to complex real-world scenarios, including tool manipulation and multi-object causal chains. Human evaluations show a consistent preference for Goal Force over text-only baselines (74.5% for tool-object, 72.0% for collisions). The model also achieves >85% visual planning accuracy in scenes with natural blockers and correctly leverages mass information to adjust impact speeds. These results indicate that video models can serve as implicit neural physics simulators, enabling physics-aware planning without external engines.

## Method Summary
Goal Force builds upon the Wan2.2 video diffusion model by incorporating a ControlNet that conditions generation on a three-channel control tensor. Each channel represents a physical quantity: the direct force applied to an initiator object, the desired goal force on a target object, and an optional mass map for relevant objects. Force channels are encoded as moving 2D Gaussian blobs; trajectory encodes direction, and blob size/duration encodes magnitude. The model is trained on synthetically generated videos of causal physics primitives (e.g., domino topples, elastic collisions) produced with a physics engine. Each training example includes paired direct and goal force annotations. To teach bidirectional reasoning, for every video one of the force channels is randomly zeroed out during training, creating two objectives: (1) given a goal force, infer the necessary direct force (planning), and (2) given a direct force, predict the resulting goal force (simulation). The ControlNet's layers are fine-tuned while the base Wan2.2 model remains frozen. At inference, the full control tensor, an initial frame, and a text prompt are provided, and the model generates a video of the antecedent action that achieves the specified goal force.

## Key Results
- **Zero-shot generalization** to real-world tool manipulation and multi-object causal chains.
- **Human evaluations:** 74.5% preference for Goal Force over text-only in tool-object interaction; 72.0% in two-object collisions.
- **Visual planning accuracy** >85% across diverse test scenes with natural blockers.
- **Proper use of mass information** to adjust projectile speeds appropriately.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1
- **Claim:** Randomly masking either the direct force or goal force channel during training forces the model to learn bidirectional physical reasoning (inferring causes from effects and vice versa), enabling it to generate an antecedent action when given a goal force.
- **Mechanism:** The multi-channel control signal provides paired (direct force, resulting goal force) data from synthetic causal primitives. During training, for each video, one of these channels is zeroed out. This creates two distinct learning objectives: (1) *Goal→Plan*: Given a desired goal force on a target object, infer the necessary direct-force event on an initiator object; (2) *Action→Outcome*: Given a direct-force event, simulate the physical propagation to predict the resulting goal force. The model must learn to propagate forces through spacetime to satisfy whichever objective is presented.
- **Core assumption:** Learning these paired mappings on simple, synthetic physics data (e.g., domino chains, elastic collisions) is sufficient to bootstrap the ability to perform implicit causal planning for novel, complex scenes.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract] "...training a video diffusion model on a curated dataset of synthetic causal primitives...the model exhibits remarkable zero-shot generalization to complex, real-world scenarios..."
  - [section 3.2] "Our key training strategy is to *randomly mask the causal information*. For each training video, we provide *either* the direct force... *or* the goal force... This forces the model to learn the physical reasoning: *Goal→Plan* and *Action→Outcome*."
  - [corpus] Weak/absent support. Neighboring papers (e.g., Phy-Tac, PhysGen) focus on different conditioning schemes or explicit simulators, not this specific bidirectional masking strategy.
- **Break condition:** If removing the random masking (e.g., always providing both channels) does not degrade performance on the *Goal→Plan* task, the mechanism is likely not the primary driver of the planning capability.

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim:** The video generation model itself, fine-tuned on physics-conditioned data, acts as an *implicit neural physics simulator* at inference, eliminating the need for an external dynamics engine.
- **Mechanism:** The pre-trained video model (Wan2.2) provides a rich prior over natural video appearance and plausible motion. The ControlNet module, fine-tuned on physics-conditioned synthetic data, learns to modulate this prior using the multi-channel control tensor. Because the training data explicitly encodes cause-effect relationships (forces → motion), the fine-tuned model learns an internal representation of physical dynamics. It then samples plausible future frames by "running" this internal simulator forward from the initial state, guided by the specified goal force.
- **Core assumption:** A sufficiently powerful video diffusion model, when trained with explicit physical control signals, can internalize approximate Newtonian dynamics (e.g., momentum conservation, force propagation) without being given explicit equations or a differentiable physics engine.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract] "...models can emerge as *implicit neural physics simulators*, enabling precise, physics-aware planning *without reliance on external engines*."
  - [section 1] "...our approach differs fundamentally: we do not use any physics simulator at inference time. Instead, we train the generative model itself to act as an approximate 'neural simulator'..."
  - [section 3.3] "We build our model on Wan2.2... We use a ControlNet module to condition on our physics signal."
- **Break condition:** If ablating the ControlNet (relying solely on the base model's text-conditioned prior) yields comparable performance on goal force adherence in zero-shot real-world tasks, the claim that the *fine-tuned model* acts as an implicit simulator is weakened.

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim:** Training on a *curriculum of simple, synthetic causal primitives* (rigid collisions, chain reactions, simple non-rigid swaying) enables the model to *compose* these primitives to plan for complex, out-of-distribution scenarios (e.g., tool use, multi-object chains with blockers).
- **Mechanism:** The synthetic dataset systematically varies object counts, force magnitudes, collision types, and scene geometries. This exposes the model to a distribution of fundamental physical interactions. The model's large-scale pre-training (on diverse internet video) provides a rich visual and semantic prior (e.g., what a "golf club" looks like, how a "hand" moves). The fine-tuning bridges these: it forces the pre-trained visual prior to conform to the causal constraints of the synthetic physics data. At inference, this allows the model to *compose* learned primitive interactions (strike → transfer momentum → displace) using novel object pairs from its visual prior, as long as the physical relationship is isomorphic.
- **Core assumption:** The space of physical interactions *relevant for planning* is low-dimensional and can be covered by a finite set of causal primitives. Generalization to novel object-appearance combinations is enabled by the base model's visual prior.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract] "Despite training only on simple synthetic physics data, our model achieves strong *zero-shot generalization* to complex real-world scenarios including *tool manipulation* and *multi-object causal chains*."
  - [section 1] "We train the model on simple, synthetic examples of causal primitives... Crucially, we find that this grounded training enables *non-trivial generalization* to highly diverse scenarios."
  - [section 4.1] Human study results across categories (tool-object, human-object) showing consistent preference over text-only baselines.
- **Break condition:** If performance on complex, out-of-distribution tasks (e.g., tool use) drops to near-zero when the base video model is replaced with a less-capable prior (e.g., a model trained only on synthetic data), the composition claim fails—generalization would then be attributed solely to the base model's existing knowledge, not the physics fine-tuning.

## Foundational Learning
- **Concept: Causal Chain Reasoning**  
  - *Why needed here:* The core task is to generate an *antecedent action* that *causes* a specified future outcome. This requires reasoning backwards from effect to cause along a multi-step physical chain (e.g., to make ball B move, ball A must strike it; to make ball A strike, a hand must swing a club).  
  - *Quick check:* Given a goal force on a target object and a scene with a blocked direct path and a potential tool, can the learner identify the necessary intermediate objects and their required interactions?

- **Concept: Force Vector Representation & Propagation**  
  - *Why needed here:* The control signal encodes forces as spatial-temporal Gaussian blobs. The model must interpret these as *directional quantities* with magnitude, and understand how they transfer between objects upon collision (conservation of momentum, impulse).  
  - *Quick check:* Does the learner understand that a goal force on a heavy object requires a faster/longer-duration input force from the initiator compared to a light object, all else being equal?

- **Concept: Leveraging Visual Priors for Physical Inference**  
  - *Why needed here:* The model must generalize from synthetic balls/dominos to real-world objects (golf clubs, roses, rubber ducks). It must use learned visual semantics (e.g., "this is a rigid club," "this is a flexible stem") to inform its physics plans without being explicitly taught each object's properties.  
  - *Quick check:* Can the learner use the visual appearance of an object (e.g., a long, thin tool vs. a short, heavy block) to hypothesize its role as an initiator or its mass properties when mass is not explicitly provided?

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map:**
  1. **Input Encoder:** Processes initial frame (image), text prompt (semantic context), and a 3-channel control tensor `tilde{π}` (frames × 3 × H × W). The control tensor is the key novel input.
  2. **ControlNet Module:** A cloned subset (first 10 DiT layers) of the base video model (Wan2.2), fine-tuned. It ingests the patched control tensor and text embedding, outputting features that are injected into the frozen base model via zero-convolutions.
  3. **Base Video Model (Frozen Wan2.2):** A Mixture-of-Experts latent diffusion model. It receives the ControlNet's modulated features and generates the latent video sequence.
  4. **Decoder:** Transforms final latent video into pixel frames.

- **Critical path:** `Control Tensor (Goal Force Ch) → ControlNet (Fine-tuned DiT layers) → Feature modulation via zero-convolutions → Frozen Base Wan2.2 → Latent video → Frames`. The text prompt and initial frame are also inputs to the base model, setting semantic context and initial state.

- **Design tradeoffs:**
  - **Synthetic Data Purity vs. Real-World Gap:** Training only on clean, perfectly labeled synthetic physics ensures the model learns clean causal structure but risks failing on real visual noise and complex textures. Tradeoff managed by the base model's strong real-world prior.
  - **Control Signal Fidelity vs. User Flexibility:** The Gaussian blob encoding is simple and learnable but coarse. It sacrifices precise force profile specification for intuitive, scalable user input. Mass channel is optional, trading off user convenience for potential planning accuracy.
  - **Fine-tuning Scope:** Only fine-tuning the high-noise expert's ControlNet (first 10 DiT layers) balances stability (frozen base preserves quality) with learning capacity (high-noise expert controls global structure/dynamics).

- **Failure signatures:**
  - **Planning Failure:** Generated video shows the target object moving *without* a plausible initiator (spontaneous motion) or with an initiator that is physically blocked (ignoring "natural blockers").
  - **Mode Collapse:** For multi-modal tasks (e.g., 5 possible initiator dominos), all outputs select the same initiator (low diversity score δ(p)).
  - **Mass Insensitivity:** Varying object mass in the control signal does not result in perceptibly different projectile speeds/impacts in the output video.
  - **Visual Degradation:** Artifacts like object hallucination, morphing, or incoherent motion, indicating the ControlNet modulation conflicted with the base model's prior.

- **First 3 experiments:**
  1. **In-Distribution Validation:** Test on a held-out set of simple ball collisions or domino chains from the *same* synthetic distribution. Check for >95% force adherence and no visual artifacts. Validates that the basic bidirectional mapping was learned.
  2. **Zero-Shot Generalization Probe:** Test on a *simple* real-world scene not in training (e.g., a video of two real balls on a table, with a goal force specified). Measures if the model can apply the learned principle ("strike to cause") to novel object appearances. Expected: plausible causal chain.
  3. **Privileged Information Utilization:** For a fixed goal force, vary the mass channel for a projectile ball. Generate multiple videos and measure (via tracking) the projectile's speed at collision. Test if higher mass (in channel) correlates with lower speed (conserving momentum for fixed target mass). This isolates the model's use of the provided mass signal.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
### Open Question 1
- **Question:** What is the maximum complexity (e.g., chain length, branching factor) of causal physical interactions that Goal Force can reliably plan, and where does its planning capability break down?
- **Basis in paper:** [inferred] The paper tests domino chains up to length 10 in training and uses small-scale planning benchmarks (e.g., selecting one initiator among a few distractors). It does not systematically evaluate the limits of chain depth or graph complexity.
- **Why unresolved:** The paper claims the model can "propagate forces through time and space" and handle "chains of events" but provides no stress test for scalability. Understanding failure modes is critical for applications requiring long-horizon planning.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** A systematic evaluation varying the number of objects in a causal chain (e.g., domino lines of length 5, 10, 20, 50) and measuring success rate degradation. For branching, test multiple targets from a single initiator or multiple initiators converging.

### Open Question 2
- **Question:** How quantitatively accurate is the model's physical simulation compared to ground-truth physics engines (e.g., in terms of momentum conservation, energy dissipation, collision response) on held-out interactions?
- **Basis in paper:** [inferred] The evaluation relies on human pairwise preference for "goal force adherence" and visual quality, not on numerical physical accuracy. The paper mentions leveraging mass information but only qualitatively discusses speed adjustments.
- **Why unresolved:** The paper positions Goal Force as an "implicit neural physics simulator," but without physical accuracy metrics, it's unclear if the model learns correct physics or merely visually plausible heuristics. This is crucial for safety-critical robotics applications.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Benchmark the generated videos against simulations from a physics engine (e.g., PyBullet) for the same initial conditions and forces. Compute metrics like trajectory error, momentum error, or energy drift on a set of collision scenarios.

### Open Question 3
- **Question:** To what extent does the zero-shot generalization to novel tools and interactions depend on (a) the base video model's pretrained visual priors versus (b) the physics-focused fine-tuning with the multi-channel control signal?
- **Basis in paper:** [inferred] The paper attributes generalization to "grounding video generation in fundamental physical interactions" but does not ablate the contribution of the base model versus the physics training. The use of a strong pretrained model (Wan2.2) confounds this.
- **Why unresolved:** Understanding the source of generalization is key to scaling the approach. If it's mostly from the base model, then the physics training may be less critical. If the physics training is essential, then future work should focus on richer synthetic data.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Conduct ablation studies: (i) use a different base video model architecture; (ii) train the same ControlNet from scratch without base model; (iii) fine-tune only the ControlNet vs. full model. Compare generalization performance.

### Open Question 4
- **Question:** Under what conditions does the model fail to correctly utilize provided mass information for planning, particularly in out-of-distribution scenarios involving atypical object appearances or force magnitudes?
- **Basis in paper:** [explicit] The paper reports that in an out-of-distribution mass reasoning test, "our results satisfy three of them, while the fourth is very close" (p8), indicating imperfect usage of mass information.
- **Why unresolved:** The mass channel is presented as an optional but useful input. Knowing its reliability boundaries is important for users who might rely on it for precise planning. The paper doesn't analyze why one relationship failed or how to improve robustness.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Design a comprehensive benchmark varying object appearance (size, texture, shape) and force magnitude, measuring whether the model adjusts projectile speed correctly according to mass principles. Perform error analysis on failure cases.

### Open Question 5
- **Question:** How does the model's performance in the "visual planning" task (selecting a valid initiator among distractors) vary with the number and physical constraints of distractor objects, and what are the common failure modes?
- **Basis in paper:** [explicit] The paper introduces a "visual planning" benchmark with "natural blockers" and reports accuracy per scene (Table 2). They note "most failure cases involve the target object moving spontaneously" (p7).
- **Why unresolved:** The planning benchmark uses only a few scenes with few distractors. The model's ability to reason about multiple physical constraints (blockers, tool use) is central to the claim of emergent planning, but the evaluation is narrow. Understanding failure modes (spontaneous motion) is critical for improvement.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Create a more diverse planning benchmark with varying numbers of distractor objects, different types of constraints (static blockers, movable obstacles, tool requirements), and analyze failure cases via human inspection or automatic detection of spontaneous motion.

## Limitations
- The necessity of random channel masking for bidirectional reasoning is unvalidated (no ablation).
- The claim that the fine-tuned model acts as an implicit physics simulator is confounded by the base model’s prior; without a ControlNet-only ablation, the simulator role cannot be isolated.
- Zero-shot generalization may be primarily driven by the strong pretrained visual prior of Wan2.2 rather than the physics-focused fine-tuning; replacing the base model with a weaker prior could reveal this.

## Next Checks
1. **Ablate Random Masking:** Train a variant without channel masking (always providing both direct and goal forces). Evaluate zero-shot planning accuracy; a significant drop would confirm masking’s necessity.
2. **ControlNet Ablation:** At inference, zero out ControlNet’s modulated features, feeding only text + initial frame to the frozen Wan2.2 base model. Measure goal force adherence; performance collapse would prove the fine-tuned ControlNet is essential.
3. **Base Model Replacement:** Replace Wan2.2 with a video model lacking strong real-world priors (e.g., trained only on synthetic data). Test tool-use generalization; failure would indicate that the base model’s visual knowledge is the primary driver of generalization, not the physics fine-tuning.
