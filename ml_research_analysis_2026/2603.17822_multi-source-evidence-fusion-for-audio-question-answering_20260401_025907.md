# Multi-Source Evidence Fusion for Audio Question Answering

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2603.17822
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.17822
- Reference count: 0
- Primary result: System ranked 1st in reasoning quality (MMAR-Rubrics: 69.8) and 2nd in accuracy (76.9%) on Interspeech 2026 Audio Reasoning Challenge; dual-source fusion provided a 3.2–4.3 pp accuracy gain.

## Executive Summary
This paper introduces a system for audio question answering that produces dense, verifiable reasoning chains. It fuses observations from two independent large audio language models (LALMs) with a tiered reliability framework of 25 acoustic tools. A contradiction-driven verification loop actively resolves conflicts before an answer is selected. The architecture addresses the opacity and hallucination risks of standalone LALMs by ensuring every inference step is reliability-tagged. On the Interspeech 2026 Audio Reasoning Challenge (MMAR), it achieved top rankings in reasoning quality and competitive accuracy, with ablations confirming the statistical significance of its dual-source fusion mechanism.

## Method Summary
The system processes an audio clip and question through a multi-stage pipeline. First, two open-weight LALMs (StepAudioR1, Qwen3-Omni) independently analyze the full audio and three segments, generating descriptive observations (not final answers). Next, 25 acoustic tools (e.g., ASR, diarization, beat detection) are invoked; each tool belongs to one of four reliability tiers (Analytic, Probabilistic, Heuristic, LALMs) with assigned base confidence caps. A Unified Evidence Analyzer merges LALM observations and tool outputs, computing per-item confidence via a formula: `tier_base_cap × corroboration_multiplier (1.5×) × direct_answer_bonus (1.3×) × domain_appropriateness_factor`, while enforcing tier-specific caps. A three-stage Contradiction Detector identifies conflicts and hallucination risks (via heuristic reclassification, risk assessment, and an LLM classifier), generating hypotheses for targeted verification. A final reasoning LLM (Kimi-K2-Thinking) selects an answer and generates a seven-section prose justification from the reliability-tagged evidence set.

## Key Results
- Achieved MMAR-Rubrics reasoning score of 69.8 (1st place) and answer accuracy of 76.9% (2nd place) on Interspeech 2026 Audio Reasoning Challenge.
- Dual independent LALM fusion provided a statistically significant accuracy gain of 3.2–4.3 percentage points over single-LALM ablations.
- The contradiction-driven verification loop and tiered confidence scheme produced dense, auditable reasoning chains with explicit reliability tags for every claim.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
Independent dual-source LALM observations reduce hallucination and anchor bias. Two LALMs analyze the audio independently across full and segmented views; their raw answer predictions are hidden from the reasoning agent, which performs semantic corroboration (corroborated/source-specific/disagreement). This creates an early reliability signal. The core assumption is that independent model errors are not perfectly correlated—disagreement flags uncertainty while agreement boosts confidence in high-level perceptual claims. Evidence is from the paper's own ablation (Table 6) showing the fusion gain.

### Mechanism 2
A tiered reliability framework with confidence caps and domain appropriateness adjustments prevents over-trust in deterministic but brittle tools and under-trust in probabilistic but useful ones. Tools are partitioned into four tiers with base caps (Analytic: 0.90, Probabilistic: 0.75, Heuristic: 0.60, LALMs: 0.70). Confidence is computed as `base_cap × (1.5 if corroborated) × (1.3 if direct answer)`, capped per tier. Tools applied outside their primary domain receive a reduced domain factor. The assumption is that output determinism and reproducibility predict reliability in noisy real-world audio.

### Mechanism 3
A contradiction-driven verification loop actively resolves conflicts by generating targeted verification hypotheses, rather than passively aggregating evidence. The three-stage detector: (1) adjusts LALM confidence via heuristic reclassification (keyword overlap), (2) assesses hallucination risk based on tier, corroboration, and domain guards (e.g., speaker count clustering vs. diarization), and (3) uses an LLM to classify inter-tool conflicts and logical pitfalls. Unresolved contradictions trigger segment-specific tool invocation. The assumption is that many errors manifest as detectable contradictions between heterogeneous sources.

## Foundational Learning
- **Concept: Acoustic Tool Taxonomy and Output Determinism**  
  *Why needed:* To implement the tiered reliability framework, understand why certain tools (e.g., beat detection) are more brittle than others (e.g., energy measurement) in real-world audio, and how to assess reproducibility.  
  *Quick check:* For Whisper ASR, what properties make its outputs more or less deterministic than CREPE pitch estimation, and how would you map it to a reliability tier?

- **Concept: Confidence Calibration and Multiplicative Combination**  
  *Why needed:* The system combines confidences from different sources using multipliers and caps; an engineer must reason about the global corroboration bonus (1.5×) and the effect of capping LALM evidence at 0.70.  
  *Quick check:* If an Analytic-tier tool (cap 0.90) and a corroborated LALM observation (effective cap 0.70) both support the same claim, how does the weighting scheme prevent the LALM claim from dominating despite its lower base reliability?

- **Concept: LLM Anchoring and Sycophancy Bias**  
  *Why needed:* The architecture hides the LALMs' final answer predictions from the reasoning agent to prevent anchoring on a possibly incorrect prediction and fabrication of supporting reasoning.  
  *Quick check:* Why is it insufficient to simply provide the reasoning model with both the observations *and* the LALMs' selected answers? What failure mode does the "evidence-only" design specifically mitigate?

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map:** Dual LALM Analyzers (StepAudioR1 & Qwen3-Omni) -> Unified Evidence Analyzer -> Tool Registry (25 tools with tiers) -> Evidence Scorer -> Contradiction Detector -> [if conflict] Verification Executor -> Answer Selector (Kimi-K2) -> Reasoning Generator (Kimi-K2-Thinking)
- **Critical path:** Audio -> Dual LALM observations -> Unified corroboration -> [if conflict/low confidence] -> Verification loop -> Final evidence set -> Answer selection -> Reasoning generation.
- **Design tradeoffs:** Latency (~8-10 min) vs. verification depth; generality vs. tuned confidence caps (empirically set for MMAR distribution); completeness vs. conciseness of seven-section reasoning template.
- **Failure signatures:** High LALM disagreement rate (>40%); low tool invocation for certain question types (e.g., rhythm tools <50% for music questions); non-monotonic accuracy vs. confidence buckets; verification loop exhaustion (>3 rounds without convergence).
- **First 3 experiments:**  
  1. Replicate dual-source ablation: run system with only StepAudioR1 or only Qwen3-Omni observations; confirm 3-4 pp accuracy drop via McNemar's test.  
  2. Tier cap sensitivity: remove all confidence caps or equalize base caps; measure impact on accuracy and reasoning score.  
  3. Contradiction detector ablation: disable the three-stage detection; compare accuracy and rate of "speculative" vs. "hallucinated" flags.

## Limitations
- Fixed confidence caps and multipliers were empirically tuned for the MMAR challenge distribution; their generalization to new domains or tool suites is unknown.
- The contradiction detector's independent performance is not evaluated; its contribution is entangled with other components in system-level results.
- Results are tied to the proprietary Interspeech 2026 MMAR dataset; public validation is blocked, and exact prompts/model weights for key components (e.g., Kimi-K2-Thinking) are undisclosed.

## Confidence
- **Dual-source fusion accuracy gain (3.2-4.3 pp):** High (supported by specific ablation with statistical testing).
- **Tiered reliability framework design:** Medium (principled but fixed parameters lack published sensitivity analysis).
- **Contradiction-driven loop robustness:** Low (core claim not isolated in standalone ablation; effect entangled with other components).

## Next Checks
1. Replicate the dual-source ablation on a held-out subset of MMAR or a comparable public audio QA benchmark (e.g., AudioCaps QA), confirming the accuracy delta via McNemar's test.
2. Stress-test the tier confidence caps by removing caps or equalizing base caps; measure accuracy degradation and calibration error using synthetic audio with known ground truth.
3. Benchmark the contradiction detector independently: construct a gold-standard dataset of audio-evidence pairs with labeled contradictions; evaluate its precision/recall against a baseline that skips verification.
