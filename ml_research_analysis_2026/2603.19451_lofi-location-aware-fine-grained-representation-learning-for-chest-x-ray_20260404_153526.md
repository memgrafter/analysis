# LoFi: Location-Aware Fine-Grained Representation Learning for Chest X-ray

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2603.19451
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.19451
- **Reference count:** 29
- **Primary result:** Achieves state-of-the-art chest X-ray retrieval and phrase grounding by jointly optimizing contrastive, captioning, and location-aware captioning losses to enable fine-grained representation learning.

## Executive Summary
LoFi introduces a method to learn fine-grained representations for chest X-rays by addressing the lack of region-level supervision. It jointly optimizes a sigmoid contrastive loss for retrieval, an autoregressive captioning loss for report understanding, and two novel location-aware captioning losses—grounding and dense captioning—to provide supervision at the sentence-box level. This multi-task framework enables spatially resolved feature learning. For inference on external datasets with different annotation styles, LoFi integrates its encoder into a retrieval-based in-context learning (ICL) pipeline, feeding demonstrations to an instruction-tuned LVLM (MedGemma) to predict boxes without dataset-specific fine-tuning. Trained on MIMIC-CXR reports and MIMIC-Ext text-box pairs, LoFi sets new records on MIMIC-CXR retrieval and PadChest-GR phrase grounding, significantly outperforming prior work.

## Method Summary
LoFi uses a SigLIP2-400M model as a shared image (`E_I`) and text (`E_T`) encoder, each adapted with LoRA, and a lightweight Gemma-3-270M LLM. The total training loss combines four components: a pairwise sigmoid contrastive loss (`L_s`) for global image-text alignment; an autoregressive captioning loss (`L_c`) for full report generation; a grounding loss (`L_g`) that predicts a bounding box `b` from an image and a sentence `s`; and a dense captioning loss (`L_d`) that predicts a sentence `s` from an image and a box `b`. The losses are summed as `L_t = L_s + λ·(L_c + L_g + L_d)/2`, with `λ=5`. During training, sentences and associated boxes are randomly sampled from annotated pairs. For zero-shot phrase grounding on an external dataset (PadChest-GR), LoFi’s fine-grained encoder retrieves `k` similar image-sentence-box triplets from a candidate pool. These demonstrations, formatted with fixed prompts, are provided to an instruction-tuned MedGemma-1.5-4B to predict boxes for a new query.

## Key Results
- On MIMIC-CXR retrieval, LoFi improves image-to-text R@1 to 13.90 (from CARZero's 11.64) and text-to-image R@1 to 11.91 (from 9.67).
- On PadChest-GR external validation (ICL, k=40), it increases F1@0.5 to 25.34 (from MedGemma's 2.03) and Robustness (Ro/L) to 63.55 (from K2Sight's 49.81).
- On internal fine-tuning validation, LoFi attains F1@0.5 of 33.44, surpassing MAIRA-2 (32.93) with far fewer parameters (270M vs. 7B).

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1
- **Claim:** Location-aware captioning losses (`L_g`, `L_d`) provide region-level supervision, enabling fine-grained representation learning beyond whole-image contrast.
- **Mechanism:** `L_g` forces the model to predict a box `b` from an image conditioned on a sentence `s`. `L_d` forces the model to generate a sentence `s` from an image and a box `b`. This dual objective compels the visual encoder to develop spatially resolved features that align with local text descriptions.
- **Core assumption:** Supervising at the region-text level is necessary to learn representations that capture spatially confined findings, which whole-report supervision alone cannot provide.
- **Evidence anchors:** [abstract] mentions region-level supervision via grounding and dense captioning; [section] formally defines `L_g` and `L_d` (Equations 3, 4).
- **Break condition:** Noisy or anatomically coarse text-box pairs (e.g., from MIMIC-Ext) could corrupt the region-level signal, harming fine-grained feature learning.

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim:** Joint optimization of sigmoid, captioning, and location-aware captioning losses balances global and local alignment, preventing overfitting to any single objective.
- **Mechanism:** The total loss `L_t = L_s + λ·(L_c + L_g + L_d)/2` (with `λ=5`) combines a pairwise sigmoid contrastive loss (`L_s`) for retrieval, an autoregressive captioning loss (`L_c`) for long-text understanding, and the region-level losses (`L_g`, `L_d`). This ensures the visual encoder retains global semantic coherence while learning localizable patterns.
- **Core assumption:** No single objective (contrastive, captioning, or grounding) is sufficient for robust retrieval and grounding representations.
- **Evidence anchors:** [abstract] states joint optimization of the three losses; [section] details loss composition and weighting (Equation 5).
- **Break condition:** Poor `λ` tuning (too high) may neglect `L_s`, harming retrieval; too low may underdevelop region-level features.

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim:** Retrieval-based ICL with fine-grained representations allows adaptation to external phrase grounding tasks without task-specific fine-tuning, mitigating annotation variability.
- **Mechanism:** At inference, a query image-sentence pair retrieves `k` similar image-sentence-box triplets from a candidate pool using cosine similarity of fine-grained embeddings. These demonstrations are provided to an instruction-tuned LVLM (MedGemma), which then predicts boxes for the query. The quality of retrieved demos depends on the fine-grained representation space.
- **Core assumption:** A representation space that clusters images by fine-grained visual-semantic details will retrieve semantically and spatially relevant demonstrations, enabling the LVLM to generalize to new grounding queries.
- **Evidence anchors:** [abstract] mentions integrating a fine-grained encoder into retrieval-based ICL; [section] describes the ICL pipeline and similarity-based retrieval (Fig 1b).
- **Break condition:** If the encoder fails to distinguish subtle visual differences, retrieved demos will be spatially irrelevant, causing inaccurate box predictions.

## Foundational Learning
- **Concept:** Contrastive Learning (Sigmoid Loss)
  - **Why needed here:** Provides initial global alignment between image and report embeddings, creating a semantic scaffold to organize fine-grained features. Without it, the model lacks a coherent global representation.
  - **Quick check:** What would happen to `R@1` retrieval scores if `L_s` were removed?

- **Concept:** Autoregressive Captioning
  - **Why needed here:** Enables learning from long-form radiology reports, capturing nuanced findings and narrative structure that short sentence sampling misses. This complements contrastive learning by forcing coherent text generation.
  - **Quick check:** How might `L_c` influence the model's ability to handle reports with multiple, distinct findings?

- **Concept:** Retrieval-Based In-Context Learning
  - **Why needed here:** Allows application to external datasets (like PadChest-GR) with different annotation styles without fine-tuning, by leveraging retrieved in-domain examples to adapt the LVLM.
  - **Quick check:** Why does ICL performance improve when demonstrations (`k`) increase, up to a point?

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map:**
  Visual encoder (SigLIP2-400M + LoRA rank-16 + 64-attention pooling) -> Projection layer -> LLM (Gemma-3-270M + LoRA rank-4 on Q,V)
  Text encoder (SigLIP2-400M + LoRA rank-16) -> Projection layer -> LLM
  For ICL: Fine-grained encoder (`E_I`) -> Candidate pool embeddings -> Cosine similarity retrieval -> Instruction-tuned MedGemma-1.5-4B with demonstrations

- **Critical path:**
  1. Preprocess MIMIC-CXR reports: split into sentences/short sub-reports; extract text-box pairs from MIMIC-Ext, normalize boxes to [0,1000] and convert to strings.
  2. Forward pass: `E_I(I)` and `E_T(T)` compute embeddings; for samples with boxes, randomly sample a sentence `s` and its box `b`.
  3. Loss computation: `L_s` on image-text pairs; `L_c` on image + full report; `L_g` on image + `s` -> box string; `L_d` on image + `b` -> `s`. Total loss `L_t` weights `L_s` with λ=5 scaling the others.
  4. Backward pass: Update LoRA parameters for SigLIP2 encoders and Gemma.
  5. ICL inference: Encode all candidate images with `E_I`; for a query, retrieve top-`k` via cosine similarity; construct prompt with demos; query MedGemma to generate box string.

- **Design tradeoffs:**
  - **LLM context length vs. box detail:** Stringifying boxes (e.g., "[10,20,30,40]") consumes tokens; longer reports or more demos (`k`) risk exceeding context limits.
  - **Batch size vs. diversity:** Small batch (16) limits in-batch negatives for `L_s` but fits memory with LLM forward passes and multiple loss computations.
  - **LoRA rank vs. adaptation:** Higher LoRA rank (16 on `E_I`) improves fine-tuning capacity but increases parameters; rank 4 on LLM is minimal to preserve instruction tuning.

- **Failure signatures:**
  - **Degraded Ro/L score:** Indicates poor localization; likely due to insufficient `L_g,d` supervision or weak fine-grained features in `E_I`.
  - **Low F@0.5 despite high Ro/L:** Suggests predicted boxes are large (high recall) but imprecise (low precision); may need to adjust `λ` or prompt design for `P_g`.
  - **ICL fails to improve over zero-shot:** Retrievals are semantically similar but spatially irrelevant; indicates `E_I` embeddings lack fine-grained discrimination for grounding.

- **First 3 experiments:**
  1. **Ablate `L_g,d`:** Train without location-aware losses. Expect significant drop in grounding F@0.5 (internal/external) and possibly retrieval R@40.
  2. **Vary `λ`:** Test `λ ∈ {1, 5, 10}`. Expect `λ=5` optimal per paper; higher `λ` may hurt retrieval, lower `λ` may hurt grounding.
  3. **Test ICL `k` sweep:** On PadChest-GR external validation, vary `k` (5, 20, 40, 80). Expect improvement up to `k=40` then plateau/decline due to noise or context limits.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- **Open Question 1:** How can the computational overhead of retrieval-based ICL in LoFi be reduced to enable scalable real-world deployment?
  - Basis: Paper explicitly states ICL overhead may limit scalability.
- **Open Question 2:** Through what specific mechanism does retrieval-based ICL mitigate inter-observer variability in phrase grounding annotations, and how robust is this mitigation across varying levels of annotation noise?
  - Basis: Paper claims ICL mitigates annotation inconsistency without analysis.
- **Open Question 3:** Do fine-grained representations learned from anatomical-level annotations (MIMIC-Ext) transfer effectively to finding-level grounding tasks (PadChest-GR), and is the location-aware captioning loss critical for this transfer?
  - Basis: MIMIC-Ext uses anatomical-level boxes; PadChest-GR uses finer finding-level boxes; transferability not formally tested.
- **Open Question 4:** Is the fixed loss weighting λ=5 optimal across retrieval and grounding tasks, or does task-specific tuning yield significant gains?
  - Basis: λ is set to 5 without per-task sensitivity analysis.
- **Open Question 5:** Does representing bounding boxes as strings of coordinates normalized to [0,1000] impact the efficiency and accuracy of autoregressive location-aware captioning compared to alternative representations?
  - Basis: Coordinate formatting choice is described but not justified or compared.

## Limitations
- Region-level supervision relies on noisy silver-standard annotations (MIMIC-Ext), which may be anatomically coarse, limiting fine-grained learning.
- The optimal loss weight `λ=5` is dataset-dependent and may not generalize; no dynamic weighting or per-task tuning is explored.
- ICL inference latency scales with candidate pool size and demonstration count `k`, posing a bottleneck for deployment.

## Confidence
- **Region-level supervision via `L_g,d`:** Medium confidence. Ablations support contribution, but direct evidence of improved fine-grained feature learning (e.g., probing) is lacking.
- **Joint multi-task loss (`λ=5`):** Medium confidence. Theoretically sound but dataset-specific; no comparison to alternative weightings or dynamic schemes.
- **Retrieval-based ICL for external grounding:** Medium confidence. Large gains on PadChest-GR are reported, but it's unclear if improvements stem from fine-grained representations or simply more in-domain examples; internal fine-tuning outperforms ICL.
- **Architecture details (fusion, formatting):** Low confidence. Key implementation specifics (e.g., how SigLIP features fuse with Gemma inputs, exact box string formatting) are underspecified, requiring assumptions for reproduction.

## Next Checks
1. **Ablate `L_g` and `L_d`:** Train without location-aware losses and evaluate on MIMIC-CXR retrieval and PadChest-GR (both ICL and fine-tuned) to quantify isolated impact.
2. **λ sensitivity sweep:** Train with `λ ∈ {0.1, 1, 5, 10, 20}` and plot trade-offs between retrieval R@1 and grounding F@0.5 to validate the chosen weighting.
3. **Demonstration relevance analysis:** For a sample of PadChest-GR ICL queries, compute IoU between query phrase boxes and boxes in retrieved demos for the same phrase to measure fine-grained discrimination.
