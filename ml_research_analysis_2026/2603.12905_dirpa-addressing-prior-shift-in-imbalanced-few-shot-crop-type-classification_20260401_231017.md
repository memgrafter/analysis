# DirPA: Addressing Prior Shift in Imbalanced Few-shot Crop-type Classification

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2603.12905  
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.12905  
- **Reference count:** 16  
- **Primary result:** DirPA (Dirichlet Prior Augmentation) improves Cohen’s kappa and overall accuracy in few-shot crop-type classification by regularizing the model to be prior-agnostic, achieving up to 33% relative gain at 5-shot in highly imbalanced regimes.

## Executive Summary
DirPA addresses a critical mismatch in few-shot learning for agricultural land-cover classification: balanced training sets (e.g., k-shot per class) differ from imbalanced real-world test distributions, harming model reliability. The method dynamically samples pseudo-class priors from a symmetric Dirichlet distribution during training and adds a scaled log-prior adjustment to the classifier logits. This forces the model to learn representations robust to varying prior distributions. Evaluated on EuroCropsML 2.0 across eight EU countries, DirPA consistently enhances Cohen’s kappa and overall accuracy, with the largest gains in low-shot (≤20 shots) settings and on datasets with high natural imbalance. Improvements extend to hierarchical robustness, where errors are more often confined to sub-classes sharing the same parent category.

## Method Summary
DirPA is a training-time augmentation for few-shot classification under prior shift. Given a mini-batch, a pseudo-prior vector \(\tilde{\pi}\) is sampled from \(\text{Dir}(\alpha \cdot \mathbf{1})\) (symmetric Dirichlet). The model’s raw logits \(z\) are adjusted to \(z' = z + \tau \cdot \log(\tilde{\pi})\) before the softmax, effectively reweighting class probabilities. This logit perturbation exposes the model to a wide spectrum of plausible prior distributions, encouraging features that are invariant to the specific prior encountered at test time. The approach is model-agnostic and adds negligible computational cost. Training follows a standard fine-tuning pipeline: a Transformer encoder backbone is pre-trained on Austria+Germany, then the linear classification head is reinitialized for a target country and fine-tuned with DirPA using cross-entropy or focal loss. Hyperparameters (\(\alpha, \tau\)) are tuned per dataset via Bayesian optimization, with model selection based on validation Cohen’s kappa.

## Key Results
- DirPA significantly improves Cohen’s kappa and overall accuracy across eight EU countries, with relative gains up to 33% at 5-shot in Belgium (high imbalance).
- Performance gains positively correlate with dataset imbalance (Gini coefficient) and prior shift magnitude (Bhattacharyya distance), confirming the method’s sensitivity to the underlying problem.
- Hierarchical robustness is enhanced: DirPA yields larger and more consistent improvements at the parent level (HCAT level 3) than at the fine-grained level (HCAT level 6), indicating that errors are more frequently restricted to related sub-classes.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- **Claim:** DirPA mitigates prior shift by simulating a diverse range of class-prior distributions during training, forcing the model to learn representations that are robust to the specific prior of the imbalanced test set.
- **Mechanism:** At each iteration, a pseudo-prior \(\tilde{\pi} \sim \text{Dir}(\alpha \cdot \mathbf{1})\) is sampled. Adding \(\tau \cdot \log(\tilde{\pi})\) to logits reweights the softmax probabilities, making predictions less dependent on any single prior.
- **Core assumption:** The feature extractor can be regularized to become prior-agnostic if trained with logits perturbed by a wide spectrum of plausible priors, assuming the test prior lies within the Dirichlet support.
- **Evidence anchors:** Abstract; Section 4.1 (defines sampling and adjustment); Section 7 (maps features to density on simplex).
- **Break condition:** If the test prior is extremely skewed and not covered by the chosen \(\alpha\) (e.g., \(\alpha\) too high), the model may lack regularization for that shift.

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim:** The Dirichlet-based logit adjustment acts as a structured regularizer that prevents overconfidence on the few available majority-class samples in a balanced few-shot training set, reducing catastrophic overfitting.
- **Mechanism:** In a balanced k-shot episode, standard softmax drives logits toward one-hot targets. DirPA’s addition of \(\tau \cdot \log(\tilde{\pi})\) softens the effective target distribution, encouraging raw logits \(z\) to be less extreme and more discriminative across all classes.
- **Core assumption:** The optimal model for an imbalanced test set does not correspond to maximum-likelihood on balanced training; regularizing logit space toward a density yields features better suited for skewed deployment.
- **Evidence anchors:** Section 7 (prevents feature hallucination, captures uncertainty); Section 6 (largest κ gains in 1-shot and 5-shot regimes).
- **Break condition:** If the base model is already heavily regularized (e.g., strong augmentation, weight decay), DirPA may over-smooth, occasionally trading off fine-grained F1-scores.

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim:** By encouraging prior-agnostic features, DirPA improves hierarchical classification consistency, so that even when fine-grained distinctions fail, the parent land-cover category is more often correct.
- **Mechanism:** The Dirichlet prior over all \(K\) fine-grained classes exposes the model to diverse artificial imbalances, forcing representations where semantically related sub-classes (e.g., wheat types) remain closer than unrelated classes (e.g., wheat vs. vineyards), even when some are down-weighted.
- **Core assumption:** Errors within a parent category are less severe than errors crossing parent boundaries; DirPA’s regularization implicitly promotes this error structure.
- **Evidence anchors:** Abstract (enhances parent-level accuracy/F1); Section 6, Tables 5b–12b (consistent level-3 gains); Section 7 (errors restricted to closely related sub-classes).
- **Break condition:** If the hierarchical taxonomy is poorly defined (sub-classes from different parents are more similar than within-parent), DirPA could disrupt desired structure.

## Foundational Learning
- **Concept: Few-Shot Learning (FSL) Paradigm**  
  Why needed here: DirPA is designed for the n-way k-shot episode construction where the support set is artificially balanced, creating the prior shift problem.  
  Quick check: In a standard 5-way 5-shot task, what is the class prior in the support set? How does it differ from a real-world agricultural field distribution?

- **Concept: Label (Prior) Distribution Shift**  
  Why needed here: The core problem is the mismatch between balanced training prior \(p_{\text{train}}(y)\) and imbalanced test prior \(p_{\text{test}}(y)\). DirPA is a training-time solution.  
  Quick check: What does it mean for a model to be "prior-agnostic"? How would a non prior-agnostic model fail on an imbalanced test set?

- **Concept: Dirichlet Distribution as a Prior**  
  Why needed here: DirPA’s name and mechanism derive from using the Dirichlet to sample pseudo-priors; its properties (concentration \(\alpha\), support over simplex) are central.  
  Quick check: What is the effect of \(\alpha\) on sampled priors? What does symmetric \(\text{Dir}(\alpha \cdot \mathbf{1})\) with \(\alpha < 1\) vs \(\alpha > 1\) produce?

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map:** Backbone Transformer Encoder -> Linear Classification Head -> DirPA Augmentation Module -> Loss Function  
- **Critical path:**  
  1. Pre-train full model (backbone + head) on downsampled Austria+Germany with standard cross-entropy and label smoothing.  
  2. For target country, reinitialize linear head for \(K\) classes; load pre-trained backbone weights.  
  3. Fine-tune with DirPA: each mini-batch sample \(\tilde{\pi}\), compute logits \(z\), adjust to \(z'\), compute loss on \(\text{Softmax}(z')\), backpropagate.  
  4. Select checkpoint with best validation Cohen’s \(\kappa\) (not accuracy) for test evaluation.  
- **Design tradeoffs:**  
  - Hyperparameter sensitivity: Performance highly depends on \(\alpha\) and \(\tau\); no universal defaults, requiring per-dataset tuning via TPE.  
  - Computational overhead: Negligible (sampling \(K\)-dim Dirichlet and log-scaling is \(O(K)\)).  
  - Metric vs. utility tradeoff: Improves Cohen’s \(\kappa\) (global reliability) but may slightly reduce fine-grained macro-F1 in some regimes—a deliberate trade-off for system-wide stability.  
- **Failure signatures:**  
  - No gain or drop when fine-tuning dataset has low inherent imbalance (e.g., Spain) or high shot count (100-shot), where training prior already resembles test prior.  
  - Inconsistent hyperparameter importance across countries and shot settings; a setting for Estonia 1-shot may fail for Portugal 20-shot.  
  - Asymmetric Dirichlet attempts (focusing on a "hard" class) did not outperform symmetric version.  
- **First 3 experiments:**  
  1. **Baseline validation on Belgium:** Run CE vs. CE+DirPA for 5-way 5-shot; expect ~33% relative gain in \(\kappa\) (0.278 -> 0.371).  
  2. **Gain-imbalance correlation:** For all countries, compute gain (\(\kappa_{\text{DirPA}} - \kappa_{\text{baseline}}\)) and plot against Gini coefficient; expect strong positive correlation.  
  3. **Hierarchical robustness check:** For Lithuania, compare HCAT level 6 vs. level 3 F1-scores for CE vs. CE+DirPA across shots; expect larger and more consistent gains at level 3.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
1. **Automatic hyperparameter setting:** How can \(\alpha\) and \(\tau\) be automatically set or learned for a given dataset, avoiding manual tuning? The paper states performance is highly dependent on these values and preliminary dynamic learning attempts failed.
2. **Zero-shot conditions:** Can DirPA maintain benefits when the training support set does not contain examples from all test classes? The paper’s design assumes full class coverage; behavior under open-set conditions is unknown.
3. **Loss function interaction:** What is the mechanism by which DirPA interacts with different losses (e.g., CE vs. focal loss)? Gains are less consistent with focal loss, suggesting possible over-regularization or gradient conflict.
4. **Generalization beyond crop-type classification:** To what extent does DirPA generalize to other remote sensing tasks or domains with similar prior shift? Evaluation is limited to EuroCropsML 2.0; other domains may have different feature distributions or imbalance patterns.

## Limitations
- Unknown implementation details block exact replication: (1) precise HCAT level 6 → 3 taxonomy mapping rules, and (2) pre-training downsampling strategy (random vs. temporally stratified) for classes >10k samples.
- Gains diminish on low-imbalance datasets or at high shot counts; interaction with focal loss is inconsistent; break condition (test prior outside Dirichlet support) is theoretical but not thoroughly stress-tested.
- Hyperparameter fragility requires costly per-dataset tuning; no universal defaults provided.

## Confidence
- DirPA improves Cohen’s κ and aOA in low-shot (≤20) imbalanced regimes: **High** (strong empirical evidence across 8 countries, correlation with imbalance metrics).  
- DirPA enhances hierarchical robustness (HCAT level 3): **High** (consistent level-3 gains across countries and shots).  
- Mechanism: Dirichlet-sampled priors regularize the model to be prior-agnostic: **Medium** (theoretically plausible, supported by ablation logic, but lacks direct feature-space ablation or comparison to alternative prior-sampling strategies).

## Next Checks
1. **Reproduce hierarchical mapping fidelity:** Obtain or reconstruct the official HCAT taxonomy mapping; re-evaluate all reported level-3 results to ensure aggregation consistency.
2. **Validate pre-training downsampling protocol:** Implement both random and stratified temporal downsampling to ≤10k samples/class on Austria+Germany; measure impact on downstream performance and DirPA sensitivity.
3. **Stress-test Dirichlet support boundaries:** For a highly imbalanced target (e.g., Belgium), evaluate DirPA with extreme \(\alpha\) values (e.g., 0.001, 100) to confirm gains collapse when simulated priors exclude the true test prior.
