# CLaRE-ty Amid Chaos: Quantifying Representational Entanglement to Predict Ripple Effects in LLM Editing

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2603.19297
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.19297
- **Reference count:** 40
- **Primary result:** CLARE predicts model editing ripple effects using single-layer forward activations, achieving 62.2% average Spearman correlation improvement over GradSim while being 2.74× faster and using 2.85× less memory.

## Executive Summary
Model editing in large language models (LLMs) introduces unintended "ripple effects"—changes to unrelated facts—due to representational entanglement. Existing predictors like GradSim are computationally expensive. This paper introduces CLARE, a lightweight method that estimates entanglement using cosine similarity of forward activations from a single "last critical layer." Validated on three models and five editing techniques across 11,427 facts, CLARE dramatically outperforms GradSim in correlation with observed ripples while being vastly more efficient. It enables scalable pre-edit risk assessment and robust preservation set construction for safer model editing.

## Method Summary
CLARE requires no training. For each factual prompt, the model is run forward only up to a designated "last critical layer" *L*, and the residual stream activation at the last token of the subject is extracted and stored as a *d*-dimensional vector. Entanglement between two facts is defined as the cosine similarity between their corresponding vectors: CLARE(*i*, *j*) = cos(**h**<sub>*L*</sub><sup>*i*</sup>, **h**<sub>*L*</sub><sup>*j*</sup>). The layer *L* is identified via prior causal tracing or approximated as one-third of the model's depth. These similarity scores predict the magnitude of ripple effects when one fact is edited. The method avoids backward passes and gradient storage, drastically reducing compute and memory compared to gradient-based approaches like GradSim.

## Key Results
- CLARE achieves up to 0.92 Spearman correlation with observed ripple magnitudes, a 62.2% average improvement over GradSim's 0.45–0.68 range.
- It is 2.74× faster and uses 2.85× less peak GPU memory than GradSim, while requiring ~1.64-million-fold less storage for per-fact representations.
- The one-third depth heuristic for layer selection yields correlations within 1.5 percentage points of the exact critically traced layer.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1: Representational Entanglement Predicts Ripple Effects
- **Claim:** Facts with similar activation patterns at the last critical layer are more likely to interfere when one is edited.
- **Mechanism:** Compute cosine similarity between the hidden state vectors (at layer *L*) for two facts; high similarity indicates shared representational subspace, so editing one shifts the subspace and affects the other.
- **Core assumption:** Ripple effects arise from geometric overlap in the model's internal representations prior to editing.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract] "CLARE ... quantifies entanglement between facts using forward activations from a single intermediate layer"
  - [section 4] Definition of CLARE(*i*, *j*) = cos(**h**<sub>*L*</sub><sup>*i*</sup>, **h**<sub>*L*</sub><sup>*j*</sup>).
  - [corpus] The corpus of 11,427 facts provides diverse pairs to measure correlation.
  - [section 5.1] CLARE achieves ρ<sub>s</sub> up to 0.92 vs GradSim's 0.45–0.68.
- **Break condition:** If the model stores facts in highly distributed or nonlinear ways not captured by a single layer's linear activations, the cosine similarity may not correlate with ripple effects.

### Mechanism 2: Last Critical Layer as Optimal Probe
- **Claim:** The deepest layer of factual mediation (*L*) yields the most informative representation for entanglement.
- **Mechanism:** Identify *L* via causal tracing (or approximate by one-third depth) and take activations at that layer; this layer is where the fact is still clearly represented before mixing by later layers.
- **Core assumption:** Interference potential is maximized when representations are most pure for the facts, i.e., before extensive composition.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [section 3] Describes causal tracing to locate critical MLP layers storing facts.
  - [section 5.2] Layerwise correlation peaks at *L* (Figure 8, Table 3), with difference from peak <1 percentage point.
  - [appendix E] Approximation by one-third depth yields near-optimal correlations (differences <1.5 p.p.).
- **Break condition:** If the model architecture deviates from standard transformers (e.g., non-residual, different normalization), the critical layer might not follow the one-third pattern and may require tailored tracing.

### Mechanism 3: Forward-Only Efficiency Enables Scalable Analysis
- **Claim:** By avoiding backward passes and gradient storage, CLARE enables corpus-wide entanglement mapping with drastically reduced compute and memory.
- **Mechanism:** Only perform forward pass up to *L* for each fact; store *d*-dimensional vectors; compute pairwise cosine similarity in O(*n*²*d*) for all *n* pairs.
- **Core assumption:** Forward activations carry sufficient predictive signal; gradients are unnecessary for estimating ripple susceptibility.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract] "CLARE ... avoids costly backward passes", reports 2.74× speedup, 2.85× lower memory.
  - [section 5.2] Table 2 gives detailed breakdown; CLARE uses only kilobytes per fact vs gigabytes for GradSim.
  - [corpus] The corpus of 11,427 facts was processed entirely with CLARE, releasing large-scale graphs.
  - [appendix A] Complexity analysis shows CLARE O(*n*d*) storage vs GradSim O(*n*L*d*²).
- **Break condition:** If the critical layer is very deep, forward pass may still be costly; also, if the model's factual representations require gradients to capture directional derivatives, forward-only might lose information.

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component map:**
Fact Encoder -> Vector Bank -> Similarity Engine -> Graph Builder -> Preservation Set Generator

**Critical path:**
1. Determine *L*: either from prior causal tracing or by using the one-third-depth heuristic (Appendix E).
2. Encode all facts: run forward pass for each fact's prompt, capture **h**<sub>*L*</sub>[*S*] (last subject token), normalize.
3. Build graph: compute cosine(*i*,*j*) for all *i*,*j*; filter by threshold to produce adjacency list.
4. For a new edit query, compute its **h**<sub>*L*</sub> vector and find top-*k* entangled facts from the graph.

**Design tradeoffs:**
- Exact *L* vs approximation: Exact *L* from causal tracing is optimal but requires initial tracing; heuristic is fast but may be off by a few layers.
- Threshold selection: Lower threshold increases recall of ripple-prone facts but may include false positives; 0.7 is empirically effective (Appendix D).
- Memory vs coverage: Storing all vectors is cheap, but pairwise similarity for 11k facts is O(*n*²); can optimize with approximate nearest neighbor search for large corpora.

**Failure signatures:**
- Poor correlation: If CLARE's entanglement scores show low Spearman with ripple magnitudes (<0.5), suspect mis-identification of *L* or that the model's knowledge is not localized.
- Outlier high scores: Some fact pairs may have high CLARE but no ripple; this is expected as CLARE is probabilistic (Section I). Use thresholding cautiously.
- Memory blowup: For huge corpora (>1M facts), full pairwise matrix is infeasible; use sampling or indexing.

**First 3 experiments:**
1. Validate *L* selection: For a given model, run causal tracing on a small sample of facts to find *L*, then compute CLARE correlations; compare with using one-third depth.
2. Preservation set efficacy: Edit a fact and include top-5 entangled facts from CLARE graph in preservation set; measure success vs baseline (no preservation) and vs random preservation.
3. Cross-model generalization: Compute CLARE graphs on GPT2-XL and test if they predict ripple effects on edits performed on GPT-J (i.e., transferability of entanglement).

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- **Open Question 1:** What causal mechanisms link representational entanglement to ripple effects during model editing?
  - **Basis:** Limitations state: "Investigating the causal pathways by which knowledge is stored, shared, and disrupted during pretraining and editing remains an open and important direction."
  - **Why unresolved:** CLARE establishes a strong correlation but provides no causal explanation.
  - **What evidence would resolve it:** Causal interventions that directly manipulate entangled representations while measuring ripple magnitudes.
- **Open Question 2:** How can CLARE's pre‑edit entanglement scores be integrated into editing algorithms to actively minimize unintended ripple effects?
  - **Basis:** Limitations propose: "Future research could build on CLARE's preservation sets to create entanglement‑aware editing techniques."
  - **Why unresolved:** CLARE functions only as a diagnostic; existing methods do not use entanglement constraints during weight updates.
  - **What evidence would resolve it:** An editing framework incorporating CLARE's graph as a regularizer with empirical validation showing fewer ripples.
- **Open Question 3:** Is the observed ~0.7 cosine similarity threshold for high‑risk entanglement universal across model architectures, scales, and languages?
  - **Basis:** Appendix D notes a sharp increase above 0.7, but without theoretical justification.
  - **Why unresolved:** The threshold may be dataset‑ or model‑specific; its stability for different architectures, scales, or multilingual facts is unexplored.
  - **What evidence would resolve it:** Systematic evaluation across diverse model families, scales, and languages.
- **Open Question 4:** Why does the last critical layer consistently yield near‑optimal entanglement predictions?
  - **Basis:** Section 5.2 shows the last critical layer's correlation lies within 0–1 p.p. of the global maximum. The hypothesis is it is "the deepest point where the fact is still clearly represented before downstream mixing."
  - **Why unresolved:** The hypothesis is intuitive but unproven; the theoretical relationship between causal tracing scores and representational overlap is not formalized.
  - **What evidence would resolve it:** Layer‑wise analysis of information flow to formally define the trade‑off between representation specificity and downstream mixing.

## Limitations
- Subject token mapping across 212 prompt templates is not detailed; tokenization variations may cause mis‑alignment.
- Exact integer *L* per model is unreported; reliance on the one‑third heuristic may misrepresent the truly optimal layer.
- The full 11,427‑fact corpus and the sampling method for the 1,000 edit–control pairs are not described, preventing exact replication.
- Ambiguities in ripple measurement (which logits are used, handling multi‑token answers) could affect reproducibility.

## Confidence
- **Entanglement prediction:** High – Empirically strong correlations (ρ<sub>s</sub> up to 0.92) across diverse models and editing techniques.
- **Critical layer optimality:** Medium – Heuristic yields near‑peak results, but model‑specific calibration might be needed for non‑standard architectures.
- **Efficiency and scalability:** Medium – Speed and memory gains are proven; scalability beyond 11k facts requires approximate similarity search to avoid O(*n*²) costs.
