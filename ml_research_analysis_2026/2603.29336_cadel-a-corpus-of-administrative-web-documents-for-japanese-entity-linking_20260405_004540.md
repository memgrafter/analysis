# CADEL: A Corpus of Administrative Web Documents for Japanese Entity Linking

## Quick Facts
- **ArXiv ID:** 2603.29336  
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.29336  
- **Reference count:** 40  
- **Primary result:** Built CADEL, a Japanese administrative web document corpus with mention, coreference, and Wikidata linking annotations (distinguishing exact and related links), achieving high inter-annotator agreement (mention F1≥0.79, coreference LEA≥0.91, exact-link F1≥0.83).

## Executive Summary
Japanese entity linking has been hindered by the scarcity of annotated resources, especially for domain-specific texts. CADEL addresses this gap by providing a manually curated corpus of 85 administrative web documents (160 articles, 8,082 mentions) from Japanese government sites. The annotation schema distinguishes "exact" links (direct concept matches) from "related" links (conceptual overlaps such as part-whole or diachronic correspondence), and includes mention detection, coreference clustering, and Wikidata linking. Inter-annotator agreement is high for key subtasks: mention detection (F1≥0.79), coreference (LEA≥0.91), and exact-link assignment (F1≥0.83).

A baseline string-matching disambiguation experiment reveals a stark performance gap: Recall@1 reaches 0.755 for exact-link mentions but only ~0.10 for related-link cases, with approximately 1,240 non-trivial instances identified. The official test split is curated to prioritize these difficult cases, enabling more robust evaluation of future systems. CADEL thus provides a valuable benchmark for advancing Japanese entity linking in a high-stakes domain.

## Method Summary
CADEL construction follows a rigorous manual pipeline. First, documents are selected from Japanese government websites and preprocessed into a hierarchical XML structure (doc > subdoc > section). Mention annotation starts with automatic tagging using GiNZA with ENE entity types, followed by thorough manual correction. Coreference annotation clusters mentions within each article, and link annotation assigns either a precise Wikidata Q-identifier (for exact links) or a related-link type (e.g., PART_OF, DIACHRONIC_CORRESPONDENCE) for conceptual overlaps. A subset of 10 articles is double-annotated to measure inter-annotator agreement.

The paper also presents a preliminary disambiguation baseline for named mentions. After normalizing mention strings (NFKC, parenthesis removal), candidate Wikidata entities are retrieved via a SPARQL query that matches the normalized string against Japanese labels and alt labels, excluding disambiguation pages. Three ranking heuristics are evaluated: Uniform (random), SmallerID (ascending Q-ID), and MoreWikiBLs (descending by Japanese Wikipedia backlinks, obtained via MediaWiki API). Expected Recall@k is computed separately for exact-link and related-link mentions, providing a lower bound for system performance.

## Key Results
- High inter-annotator agreement: mention F1≥0.79, coreference LEA≥0.91, and exact-link F1≥0.83 on the double-annotated subset.
- Preliminary disambiguation shows Recall@1 of 0.755 for exact links but only ~0.10 for related links, highlighting the challenge of conceptual overlap cases.
- The test split is designed to emphasize difficult instances (low string-matching recall), with about 1,240 non-trivial related-link cases identified.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The success of CADEL stems from its domain-specific focus and careful annotation policy. Administrative documents contain many formal, Japan-specific entities, providing a rich yet consistent testbed. By explicitly separating exact and related links, the corpus avoids forcing ambiguous decisions and captures nuanced relationships that are common in government content. The two-stage annotation (automatic pre-tagging plus manual refinement) leverages existing NLP tools while ensuring accuracy. High inter-annotator agreement confirms that the guidelines are clear and executable, making the corpus a reliable gold standard.

## Foundational Learning
- **Japanese Entity Linking**
  - *Why needed:* The core task addressed by CADEL; requires recognizing mentions and linking them to a knowledge base (Wikidata) in Japanese, which has unique linguistic challenges.
  - *Quick check:* Given a Japanese sentence, identify the entity IDs from Wikidata for all named mentions (e.g., "東京" → Q65).
- **Coreference Resolution**
  - *Why needed:* CADEL annotates clusters of mentions referring to the same entity across a document, essential for full document understanding.
  - *Quick check:* Given two mentions like "首相" and "菅義偉" in the same article, determine if they corefer.
- **Inter-Annotator Agreement (IAA)**
  - *Why needed:* To quantify annotation quality and reliability; CADEL achieves high IAA, validating its usefulness as a gold standard.
  - *Quick check:* Compute F1 between two annotators' mention spans; a value >0.8 indicates good agreement.
- **Wikidata**
  - *Why needed:* The target knowledge base for linking; provides a structured, multilingual resource with unique identifiers.
  - *Quick check:* Look up the Japanese label for a Q-ID (e.g., Q62 yields "安倍晋三") to confirm the entity.

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component map (plain text):**
Raw Government Web Pages → XML Preprocessing → Mention Annotation (GiNZA + manual) → Coreference & Link Annotation (manual) → CADEL Corpus
Baseline System: Mentions → SPARQL Candidate Retrieval → Ranking (Uniform/SmallerID/MoreWikiBLs) → Predictions

**Critical path:** Document selection → XML conversion → mention detection → coreference & linking → IAA measurement → corpus release.

**Design tradeoffs:**
- Manual vs automatic: Semi-automatic (GiNZA) speeds up mention tagging but manual correction ensures high quality.
- Distinguishing exact vs related links: This design yields more informative annotations but complicates evaluation; requires annotators to understand nuanced relationships.
- Domain restriction to administrative texts: Improves consistency and relevance for government applications but limits generalizability.

**Failure signatures:**
- Low IAA on double-annotated subset indicates ambiguous guidelines.
- Missing or malformed XML breaks downstream parsing.
- SPARQL candidate retrieval failures (e.g., no results) often stem from insufficient normalization.

**Three first experiments:**
1. Recompute inter-annotator agreement on the 10 double-annotated articles using the same metrics (mention F1, coreference LEA, link F1) to verify reported numbers.
2. Reproduce the baseline disambiguation Recall@1 for exact and related links, comparing against the reported 0.755 and ~0.10 values.
3. Analyze the test split to confirm that it contains a high proportion of difficult related-link cases (e.g., by measuring string-matching recall distribution).

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
### Open Question 1
- **Question:** How do state-of-the-art end-to-end entity linking systems perform on CADEL, and how does disambiguation accuracy vary between exact-link and related-link cases when evaluated in an integrated framework?
- **Basis in paper:** [explicit] The authors explicitly state in Section 6.3 that "further analysis using CADEL would enable more detailed system evaluation, including end-to-end evaluation that incorporates mention recognition and separate evaluation of disambiguation accuracy for exact-link and related-link cases." They also note the large performance gap between exact and related links from their preliminary experiment.
- **Why unresolved:** The paper only reports a preliminary string-matching experiment on named mentions, not full system performance. While Sawada et al. (2026) evaluated disambiguation on CADEL, their work did not include end-to-end mention recognition and disentangled exact vs. related link analysis as advocated.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Running standard neural EL pipelines (with separate mention detection and disambiguation modules) on the official CADEL test split, reporting standard metrics (e.g., micro F1) for each subtask, and providing breakdowns for exact-link and related-link cases to identify bottlenecks.

### Open Question 2
- **Question:** What specific linguistic or discourse features distinguish successfully disambiguated related-link cases from failed ones in Japanese administrative texts, and can these features be leveraged to improve disambiguation models?
- **Basis in paper:** [inferred] The string-matching experiment yielded only ~10% Recall@1 for related-link cases, indicating substantial difficulty. The authors defined eight related-link types (e.g., PART_OF, DIACHRONIC_CORRESPONDENCE) but did not analyze which types or contextual characteristics are most challenging.
- **Why unresolved:** The paper presents no error analysis or feature inspection of related-link failures, leaving the sources of difficulty—such as mention ambiguity, KB coverage gaps, or contextual insufficiency—unclear.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Conducting a fine-grained error analysis on the related-link mentions, categorizing failures by related-link type, mention properties (length, formality), context size, and KB candidate characteristics; then using these insights to design features or training objectives for improved disambiguation.

### Open Question 3
- **Question:** To what extent does the domain-specific nature of CADEL (administrative government documents) limit the transferability of EL models trained on it to other Japanese text domains (e.g., news, social media)?
- **Basis in paper:** [inferred] CADEL is constructed exclusively from administrative web documents, which likely feature formal language, standardized terminology, and a high concentration of Japan-specific entities. The authors highlight this as a strength for Japan-specific EL but do not address cross-domain generalization.
- **Why unresolved:** The paper provides no cross-domain evaluation; all data originates from government sites, so it is unknown whether models trained on CADEL will perform well on texts with different styles, entity distributions, or mention variability.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Training representative EL systems on CADEL's training set and evaluating them on existing Japanese EL datasets from other domains (e.g., Jawikify from news, JWC from blogs/Twitter), measuring performance drops and identifying domain-shift factors through targeted analysis.

## Limitations
- Annotation format ambiguity: The mapping between mention spans, coreference clusters, and link targets in the public release may differ from the original Access schema, requiring inference.
- Normalization rules: The exact string preprocessing beyond NFKC and parenthesis removal is unspecified, affecting baseline candidate retrieval and reproducibility.
- Related-link taxonomy: Without full annotation guidelines, consistency of conceptual overlap annotations (e.g., PART_OF vs. DIACHRONIC_CORRESPONDENCE) is uncertain.
