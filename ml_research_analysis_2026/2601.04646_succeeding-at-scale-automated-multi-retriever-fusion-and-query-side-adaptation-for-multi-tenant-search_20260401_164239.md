# Succeeding at Scale: Automated Multi-Retriever Fusion and Query-Side Adaptation for Multi-Tenant Search

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2601.04646
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.04646
- **Reference count:** 40
- **Primary result:** Introduces DevRev-Search for automated benchmark generation from dark query logs and Index-Preserving Adaptation via query-only PEFT, achieving >95% of full bi-encoder performance while eliminating re-indexing.

## Executive Summary
The paper addresses two critical challenges in multi-tenant retrieval systems: the lack of labeled data for new tenants (the "dark data" problem) and the prohibitive cost of re-indexing when symmetrically fine-tuning dual-encoder models. It proposes two contributions: (1) DevRev-Search, an automated pipeline that synthesizes high-quality training data by fusing results from multiple retrievers and filtering with an LLM-as-Judge, and (2) Index-Preserving Adaptation, which fine-tunes only the query encoder using Parameter-Efficient Fine-Tuning (PEFT) to avoid re-indexing. Experiments on DevRev-Search and public datasets show that query-only tuning recovers over 95% of full bi-encoder performance, and that LoRA with rank 32–64 matches or exceeds full fine-tuning while training less than 2% of parameters. This work enables scalable, cost-effective tenant-specific adaptation.

## Method Summary
DevRev-Search construction starts from raw production query logs and a corpus of tenant documents. Queries are cleaned and documents are chunked. Candidate generation uses an ensemble of seven diverse retrievers (six dense, one sparse); the union of their top-60 results forms a candidate pool per query. An LLM-as-Judge then filters this pool using a prompt that asks for consistency with the query's answer, producing binary relevance labels. This yields a fully automated, high-quality training set without human annotation.

For adaptation, the document encoder and its precomputed index are frozen. Only the query encoder is fine-tuned with a contrastive InfoNCE loss, using asynchronously updated hard negatives (ANCE). The authors explore several Parameter-Efficient Fine-Tuning (PEFT) strategies, finding LoRA with rank 32–64 optimal: it matches or surpasses full fine-tuning while updating fewer than 2% of parameters. This asymmetric approach eliminates re-indexing costs and supports rapid multi-tenant scaling.

## Key Results
- DevRev-Search pipeline effectively creates training data from unlabeled query logs, addressing label scarcity.
- Query-only adaptation recovers >95% of full bi-encoder performance on DevRev-Search and SciFact.
- Optimal PEFT (LoRA, rank 32–64) matches or exceeds full fine-tuning while training <2% of parameters, providing a quality-efficiency trade-off that enables scalable tenant-specific adaptation.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1: DevRev-Search Data Synthesis
- **Claim:** DevRev-Search synthesizes high-quality training data from unlabeled "dark" query logs, addressing label scarcity for multi-tenant retrieval.
- **Mechanism:** Combines fusion-based candidate generation (pooling results from seven diverse retrievers) to maximize recall, then uses LLM-as-Judge to filter candidates based on substantive answer content rather than superficial similarity.
- **Core assumption:** The union of heterogeneous retrievers covers a sufficiently broad and complementary set of candidates such that, after aggressive LLM filtering, the remaining positives are both precise and comprehensive.
- **Evidence anchors:** Abstract and Section 3.3 describe the pipeline; neighbor papers discuss fusion and LLM-as-judge but do not directly validate this specific automation's noise robustness.
- **Break condition:** If fusion introduces excessive noise that the LLM filter cannot reliably prune, or if the LLM's judgments systematically diverge from true relevance (e.g., prompt bias), the resulting dataset would be noisy and degrade model training.

### Mechanism 2: Query-Only Adaptation
- **Claim:** Query-Only Adaptation eliminates the prohibitive re-indexing cost of symmetric bi-encoder fine-tuning while recovering >95% of full performance.
- **Mechanism:** Freezes the document encoder and its corpus index; fine-tunes only the query encoder, assuming the query manifold can be adapted to align with the frozen document space.
- **Core assumption:** The document encoder's representations are already sufficiently general that adapting the query side yields most of the possible gain; the frozen index remains a good approximation of the optimal document embedding space.
- **Evidence anchors:** Abstract states >95% recovery; Table 1 shows minimal (<5% relative drop) performance loss for Q vs. QD across datasets and models; related work on query encoder distillation supports viability.
- **Break condition:** If a tenant's domain shift is severe enough that the frozen document embeddings become fundamentally misaligned, query-only tuning would hit a performance ceiling significantly below full tuning.

### Mechanism 3: Parameter-Efficient Fine-Tuning (PEFT)
- **Claim:** PEFT (specifically LoRA with rank 32–64) matches or exceeds full query-encoder fine-tuning while training <2% of parameters, offering an optimal quality-efficiency trade-off.
- **Mechanism:** LoRA approximates weight updates with a low-rank decomposition (A·B), introducing an implicit regularizing bias and reducing overfitting risk. The adaptation needed for tenant-specific queries lies in a low-dimensional subspace.
- **Core assumption:** The optimal adaptation directions are low-rank, and LoRA's residual learning pattern is superior to directly fine-tuning a small subset of layers or adding a head.
- **Evidence anchors:** Abstract reports the result; Table 2 shows LoRA often equals/surpasses full fine-tuning; Fig 1a shows performance peaks at rank 32/64 for qwen3-4b; theoretical support from intrinsic dimensionality literature.
- **Break condition:** If required adaptation has high intrinsic dimensionality (rank >128) or if LoRA's residual pattern is suboptimal for contrastive loss, full fine-tuning would consistently outperform PEFT and rank ablations would show monotonic improvement without saturation.

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component map:** Raw Query -> Cleaned Query -> Candidate pool (via fusion) -> Filtered positives -> Training data -> (PEFT) Tuned query encoder -> (Serving) Query embedding -> Search against frozen index.

**Critical path:** The process begins with query logs and a document corpus. Queries are cleaned and documents are segmented. For each query, seven retrievers (6 dense, 1 sparse) produce top-60 candidates; the union forms a candidate pool. An LLM-as-Judge filters this pool, outputting binary relevance labels to create training pairs. The query encoder is then fine-tuned with PEFT (e.g., LoRA) using contrastive loss with hard negatives. At serving time, the adapted query encoder generates embeddings that search against the precomputed, frozen document index.

**Design tradeoffs:**
- **Fusion vs. Filtering:** More retrievers increase recall but increase noise load on the LLM filter; too few retrievers risk missing candidates.
- **PEFT Method:** LoRA offers best quality-efficiency; unfrozen layers are simpler but use more parameters; linear/FFN heads are parameter-cheap but may lack capacity. Rank choice: higher rank improves capacity but risks overfitting and inefficiency.
- **LLM Judge Cost:** Using a powerful LLM for filtering every candidate is computationally expensive for large datasets.

**Failure signatures:**
- **Poor dataset quality:** Validation recall@420 from fusion is low (<90%) or human validation of LLM labels shows high error rates.
- **Query-only tuning fails:** Q variant's NDCG@10 lags behind QD by >10% relative on key datasets, indicating need for document-side adaptation.
- **PEFT instability:** Training metrics oscillate or collapse with ANCE or certain LoRA ranks; performance degrades sharply compared to full tuning.
- **Rank mis-specification:** Performance drops significantly when LoRA rank is too low (e.g., r=8) or degrades/plateaus at high ranks (r=128), indicating inefficient parameter use.

## Limitations
- **LLM judge specification:** The exact LLM model used for filtering in DevRev-Search is unreported, risking variability in label quality and reproducibility.
- **PEFT configuration details:** Precise LoRA settings (target modules, alpha) for Table 2 entries are not itemized; replication relies on inferred optimal ranks, possibly causing minor performance deviations.
- **Domain shift generalization:** The >95% recovery claim is validated on three datasets; extreme tenant-specific shifts (e.g., niche jargon, corrupted queries) may break the assumption that frozen document embeddings remain aligned.

## Confidence
- **DevRev-Search synthesis:** Medium – The fusion+LLM pipeline is well-described but lacks independent validation of noise robustness across diverse dark data.
- **Query-only adaptation:** High – Consistent <5% performance drop vs. full tuning across multiple datasets and backbones strongly supports the claim.
- **PEFT optimality:** High – Clear rank-performance peaks and matches/exceeds full tuning with theoretical grounding provide robust evidence.
