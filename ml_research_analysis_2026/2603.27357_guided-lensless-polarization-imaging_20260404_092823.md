# Guided Lensless Polarization Imaging

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2603.27357  
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.27357  
- **Reference count:** 40  
- **Primary result:** Achieves PSNR 35.13 vs. 13.87 (FISTA) and 28.85 (FISTA+Transformer) on three-angle grayscale PIP dataset, with strong generalization to other datasets and real-world prototype.

## Executive Summary
Lensless polarization imaging compresses measurements via spatial multiplexing and a low-pass diffuser, making reconstruction highly ill-posed. This paper introduces an RGB-guided two-stage pipeline: a physics-based solver (FISTA/ADMM) first produces a coarse but measurement-consistent polarization estimate, which is then refined by a Swin Transformer fusion network that incorporates high-frequency structural details from a co-aligned conventional RGB camera. Trained on simulated data, the method significantly outperforms lensless-only baselines across multiple datasets and yields high-quality results on real hardware without fine-tuning.

## Method Summary
The pipeline first solves an optimization problem with data fidelity (y = Ax) and a 3D TV prior via FISTA or ADMM, yielding an initial reconstruction that respects the forward model but lacks fine details. This initial estimate and a registered RGB image are fed into a Swin Transformer-based fusion network. The network processes each modality separately, then uses alternating self- and cross-attention blocks (ACF) to fuse features, allowing the polarization branch to query structural cues from the RGB branch. A skip connection from the initial estimate preserves measurement consistency. The network is trained with an ℓ1 + LPIPS loss on simulated data from the PIP, UPLight, and ZJU-RGB-P datasets, with translation augmentation to tolerate minor misalignment.

## Key Results
- On PIP three-angle grayscale, PSNR improves from 13.87 (FISTA) and 28.85 (FISTA+Transformer) to **35.13** with the proposed two-stage method.
- Generalizes across multiple simulated datasets (PIP, UPLight, ZJU-RGB-P) and remains robust to PSF mismatches.
- Delivers compelling real-world reconstructions on a physical prototype without dataset-specific fine-tuning.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1
- **Claim:** Decoupling physics-based initialization and RGB-guided refinement balances measurement consistency with high-frequency recovery.
- **Mechanism:** FISTA/ADMM enforces fidelity to the compressed measurements and a TV prior, producing a low-frequency consistent estimate. The Swin Transformer fusion network then refines this estimate by adaptively incorporating structural details from the aligned RGB image via cross-attention, while a skip connection maintains consistency with the forward model.
- **Core assumption:** The initial estimate is a reliable low-frequency approximation; the RGB image shares structural information (edges, textures) with the polarization image.
- **Evidence anchors:** [abstract] describes the two-stage pipeline; [section 3.3] details the optimization; [section 3.4] details the refinement network and skip connection.
- **Break condition:** Severely degraded initial reconstruction (extreme under-sampling/noise) or poor RGB alignment can cause artifacts or failure.

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim:** Swin Transformer with attention-guided cross-domain fusion effectively integrates polarization and RGB features to recover details.
- **Mechanism:** Separate branches extract features; fusion uses ACF blocks that alternate self-attention (within each modality) and cross-attention (between modalities). Cross-attention lets one modality query the other, enabling adaptive transfer of RGB structure into polarization features. Ablations show this outperforms simple concatenation.
- **Core assumption:** Polarization and RGB images of the same scene have correlated high-frequency structures (e.g., object boundaries).
- **Evidence anchors:** [section 3.4] describes ACF; [section 4.3 ablation] shows visual artifacts with simple fusion; corpus cites SwinFuSR for thermal-RGB fusion supporting the architecture.
- **Break condition:** Misalignment beyond the trained translation range (e.g., rotations, scaling) misguides cross-attention; if initial estimate lacks structure, correspondences fail.

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim:** The auxiliary RGB camera compensates for the low-pass effect of the diffuser and spatial multiplexing in the lensless system.
- **Mechanism:** The diffuser low-pass filters the scene, and the polarization mask spatially multiplexes states, compressing information and causing high-frequency loss. The RGB camera captures high-resolution intensity structure. Given correlation between intensity and polarization geometry, the RGB image serves as a structural prior for the polarization reconstruction.
- **Core assumption:** Scene intensity structure (RGB) correlates with polarization structure (e.g., surface orientation variations).
- **Evidence anchors:** [abstract] states RGB provides complementary geometric/edge information; [section 3.1] describes the ill-posed forward model; [section 4.1] shows quantitative gains over lensless-only baselines.
- **Break condition:** Decorrelation between polarization and intensity (e.g., uniform polarization over textured areas, specular highlights) misleads guidance; spectral mismatches reduce correlation.

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map:**  
  Lensless measurement y + aligned RGB f_RGB -> FISTA/ADMM -> Initial reconstruction x0 -> Swin Transformer fusion network (with ACF blocks) -> Refined polarization output

- **Critical path:**  
  1. Capture synchronized lensless measurement and RGB image.  
  2. Preprocess: convert raw data, white balance, resize to 250×250.  
  3. Run FISTA (10k iterations synthetic, 500 real) with TV prior to obtain x0.  
  4. Align RGB to x0 (homography if needed).  
  5. Feed x0 and aligned RGB into fusion network.  
  6. Evaluate (PSNR, SSIM, LPIPS) if ground truth available.

- **Design tradeoffs:**  
  - FISTA iterations: More iterations improve x0 quality but increase compute; network tolerates moderately poor x0.  
  - Network architecture: Swin Transformer with cross-attention is crucial; concatenation fusion causes intensity artifacts.  
  - Alignment: Trained with ±4 pixel translation; larger misalignments (rotations, scaling) degrade performance.  
  - Generalization: Training on PIP generalizes, but fine-tuning on a few target samples improves results.

- **Failure signatures:**  
  - Blurry/oversmooth output: insufficient FISTA iterations, overly strong TV, or ineffective fusion.  
  - Intensity shifts/color bleeding: misalignment causing wrong cross-attention focus.  
  - Checkerboard/ringing artifacts: from TV prior or Swin Transformer patch processing.  
  - Inconsistent details across angles: fusion may not preserve multi-angle consistency.  
  - PSF mismatch: coherent artifacts persist if FISTA uses incorrect PSF.

- **First 3 experiments:**  
  1. **Verify forward model and FISTA:** Simulate a high-contrast pattern using the measured PSF and mask, run FISTA, and compare x0 to ground truth.  
  2. **Train fusion network on subset:** Use 100 PIP images, train briefly, check PSNR improvement over FISTA on validation.  
  3. **Test on real data:** Capture static scene, align RGB via homography, run full pipeline, compare visually to FISTA-only output.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- **Q1: Real-time operation?** Future work aims to improve computational efficiency. Current pipeline uses 10k FISTA iterations and a Swin Transformer; no runtime analysis or optimization is provided. Evidence to resolve: benchmark runtimes, test reduced iterations, explore learned unrolled solvers or fully deep alternatives, present speed–accuracy trade-offs.
- **Q2: Dynamic scenes?** Future work includes extension to dynamic scenes. All evaluations are static; temporal consistency, motion artifacts, and video reconstruction are unaddressed. Evidence to resolve: capture synchronized video, compute temporal metrics, design temporal fusion modules (recurrent/3D attention), report motion robustness.
- **Q3: Generalization to other lensless designs?** PSF-mismatch ablation tests only two additional PSFs with a fixed mask; robustness to broader design changes (diffusers, mask patterns) is unknown. Evidence to resolve: systematically simulate/test diverse PSFs and mask patterns, build real prototypes with alternative masks, measure reconstruction quality across designs.
- **Q4: Misalignment tolerance?** Uses homography alignment and ±4px translation augmentation, implying mild misalignment is expected; larger errors unexplored. Real deployments may involve parallax, lens distortion, or large displacements. Evidence to resolve: conduct experiments with synthetic/real misalignments (rotations ≤15°, scaling ≤10%), quantify degradation, develop alignment-robust training or joint refinement.

## Limitations
- Sensitive to precise RGB–polarization alignment and accurate PSF measurement; misalignment or PSF mismatch causes artifacts.
- Assumes structural correlation between intensity (RGB) and polarization; fails when polarization is uniform over textured areas or dominated by specular highlights.
- Critical architecture details (Swin Transformer patch size, head counts) are not fully specified, hindering exact replication.

## Confidence
- **Overall performance claims (multi-dataset gains):** High
- **Two-stage decoupling mechanism:** Medium (benefits shown but sensitive to initial estimate quality)
- **Cross-attention fusion:** Medium (ablations confirm superiority over concatenation, fails under misalignment)
- **RGB guidance efficacy:** High (clear improvement over lensless-only baselines)
- **Generalization to unseen PSFs:** Medium (limited ablation scope)

## Next Checks
1. **Initial reconstruction robustness:** Systematically vary FISTA iterations (1k–20k) and TV regularization strength to measure network tolerance to poor initial estimates.
2. **Misalignment stress test:** Apply controlled rotations (≤15°) and scaling (≤10%) to RGB guidance to quantify degradation thresholds in cross-attention fusion.
3. **Correlation assumption test:** Generate synthetic scenes where polarization maps are decorrelated from intensity (e.g., random polarization over textured RGB) to evaluate if RGB guidance still improves reconstruction.
