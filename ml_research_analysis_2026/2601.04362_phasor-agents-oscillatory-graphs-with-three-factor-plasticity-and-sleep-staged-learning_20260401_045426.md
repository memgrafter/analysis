# Phasor Agents: Oscillatory Graphs with Three-Factor Plasticity and Sleep-Staged Learning

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2601.04362
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.04362
- **Reference count:** 40

## Executive Summary
Phasor Agents address the problem of dynamic instability in oscillatory neural networks, where continuous online learning destroys phase‑coded representational diversity through synchrony collapse. The method uses a graph of coupled Stuart‑Landau oscillators as a computational substrate. Learning follows a three‑factor plasticity rule: local eligibility traces based on phase coincidence are gated by sparse global modulators (rewards or intrinsic progress pulses) and by oscillation‑timed windows. Crucially, the algorithm separates wake (interaction, tagging) from sleep (offline consolidation and replay), inspired by synaptic tagging‑and‑capture and biological sleep stages. Experiments show that wake/sleep separation expands the stable learning regime by 67% under a fixed weight‑norm budget. REM‑like replay improves maze success by +45.5 percentage points, and phase‑coherent retrieval outperforms diffusive baselines by a factor of 4. Moreover, agents exhibit a Tolman‑style latent‑learning signature after unrewarded exploration, and intrinsic motivation signals based on compression progress survive timestamp‑shuffle controls, confirming their timing‑specificity. The work demonstrates that biologically inspired staging can stabilize phase‑coded learning without backpropagation.

## Method Summary
Phasor Agents encode information in the relative phases of coupled Stuart‑Landau oscillators. Each node state is \(z_i = r_i e^{i\phi_i}\). Interactions are defined by coupling operators (e.g., diffusive: \(W_{ij}(e^{i\theta}z_j - z_i)\)) with normalization constraints. Learning uses three‑factor plasticity: an eligibility trace \(e_{ij}(t)\) is updated based on local phase coincidence \(h_{ij}(t) = \cos(\Delta\phi_{ij})\). A sparse global modulator \(M(t)\) (extrinsic reward or intrinsic progress pulse) gates weight updates via \(\Delta W_{ij} = \eta\, g(t)\, M(t)\, e_{ij}(t)\). Dual timescales separate fast trace accumulation during wake from slow trace consolidation during NREM. The system operates under a stability budget (monitoring Frobenius norm \(\|W\|_F\) and order parameter \(R\)) and employs guardrails (weight decay, phase kicks, jitter). A scheduler orchestrates three phases: **Wake** – agent acts, fast traces are tagged; **NREM** – no external input, spindle‑gated windows open to commit slow‑trace updates under homeostasis; **REM** – no input, the network runs autonomously, generating trajectories (replay) that improve value estimates. Intrinsic motivation pulses \(M(t)\) are triggered by compression‑progress detectors that compare prediction error across windows.

- Phase‑coherent retrieval achieves 4× the performance of diffusive (phase‑scrambled) baselines.

- After unrewarded exploration, agents show immediate competence and a detour advantage, a Tolman‑style latent‑learning signature emerging from replay.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1: Three‑Factor Plasticity with Dual‑Timescale Eligibility
Local credit assignment without backpropagation is achieved via eligibility traces that gate weight updates. Coincidence detection (phase alignment \(\cos(\Delta\phi_{ij})\)) creates an eligibility trace \(e_{ij}\). A sparse, phasic global modulator \(M(t)\) (e.g., reward) gates updates only when the trace is active. Two timescales (\(e_{\text{fast}}\) during wake, \(e_{\text{slow}}\) during NREM) separate immediate credit tagging from slower consolidation, extending the credit horizon. Evidence: formal rule \(\Delta W_{ij} = \eta g(t) M(t) u_{ij}(t) e_{ij}(t)\); break condition shows credit consistency drops when reward delays exceed \(\tau_f\).

### Mechanism 2: Wake/Sleep Separation for Dynamical Stability
Continuous online weight updates can drive the oscillator network into pathological regimes like global synchrony collapse, which erases phase‑coded representations. By deferring potent weight changes to NREM, where updates are gated by spindle‑like windows and stability guardrails (e.g., phase homeostasis), the system prevents destabilization. Evidence: wake‑only learning fails weight‑norm budgets quickly; adding NREM yields higher performance within the same budget (67% gain). Guardrails removal increases synchrony collapse dramatically (92.8% → 4.4%). The primary role of NREM is thus stability control rather than mere memory retention.

### Mechanism 3: REM‑Like Replay with Phase‑Coherent Reconstruction
During REM, the network runs without external input, internally reconstructing state trajectories using its learned phasor couplings. This mental simulation generates structured experiences that improve planning and generalization. Phase coherence of the weight matrix is critical: phase‑scrambled controls destroy the benefit (unseen‑region success drops to 8.8% vs. wake‑only’s 54.1%). REM replay also yields a Tolman latent learning signature: immediate competence after unrewarded exploration. Evidence: REM improves maze success by +45.5 pp; phase‑scramble falsifier proves necessity of coherent phase.

## Foundational Learning

**Concept: Stuart‑Landau Oscillators**  
- **Why needed:** Provide a stable limit‑cycle dynamical system where phase encodes relative timing and amplitude tracks gain; essential for phase‑coded representations and interference‑based computation.  
- **Quick check:** If two oscillators are perfectly phase‑locked (\(\Delta\phi = 0\)), they belong to the same coherent assembly; their relative timing is fixed, indicating a binding relationship.

**Concept: Three‑Factor Plasticity & Eligibility Traces**  
- **Why needed:** Enables biologically plausible, local learning from sparse, delayed modulatory signals without backpropagation.  
- **Quick check:** In \(\Delta W_{ij} \propto M(t) \cdot e_{ij}(t)\), if \(M(t)\) arrives after \(e_{ij}\) decayed to zero, no update occurs; credit is lost, stressing the need for matched timescales.

**Concept: Phase‑Coded Representation & Interference**  
- **Why needed:** Information is stored in relative phase patterns; retrieval and replay rely on coherent phase evolution. Destructive interference from superposed patterns must be managed.  
- **Quick check:** Global synchrony (\(R \rightarrow 1\)) collapses representational diversity; all phase differences vanish, making stored patterns indistinguishable.

## Architecture Onboarding

**Component map**  
Phasor Graph -> Coupling Operators -> Plasticity Engine -> Intrinsic Motivation Module -> Stability Budget & Diagnostics -> Scheduler

**Critical path**  
*Wake → Tag:* External input drives dynamics. Coincidence \(h_{ij}\) updates \(e_{\text{fast}}\). Sparse \(M(t)\) may trigger small immediate \(\Delta W\). *NREM → Consolidate:* No input. Dynamics run without external drive. Spindle‑gated windows open. \(e_{\text{slow}}\) (integrated from \(e_{\text{fast}}\)) updates \(W\) under PRP and homeostasis. Goal: commit salient traces safely, prevent \(R\) collapse. *REM → Replay:* No input. Network autonomously evolves under \(W\), generating trajectories. Rollout states are used to update values/policy. Goal: generate structured variance for planning.

**Design tradeoffs**  
- *Stability vs. Plasticity:* Aggressive wake learning risks \(R\) collapse and budget overflow. Deferring to NREM with gated capture stabilizes but slows immediate adaptation.  
- *Phase Coherence vs. Capacity:* Global synchrony (\(R\approx1\)) destroys diversity; some desynchronization (\(R\sim0.7\!-\!0.8\)) is optimal. Too much incoherence (\(R\approx0\)) hinders coherent retrieval.  
- *Replay Fidelity vs. Compression:* Holographic storage is compressed but noisy; REM benefits depend on sufficient model fidelity; poor NREM consolidation can hurt.  
- *Sleep Schedule:* REM‑only benefits flexibility tasks; NREM+REM can degrade by consolidating interference; optimal phase duration/order is task‑dependent.

**Failure signatures**  
1. **Synchrony collapse:** \(R \ge 0.95\) sustained during NREM/REM. Indicates guardrails failed or coupling too strong.  
2. **Weight norm exceedance:** \(\|W\|_F > B\) (budget). Wake‑only typically violates quickly.  
3. **Phase‑scramble vulnerability:** Random phase permutation during NREM or REM causes large performance drops.  
4. **Timestamp‑shuffle vulnerability:** Intrinsic motivation pulses shuffled in time breaks learning.  
5. **Reversal learning interference:** Inability to recover original mapping after reversal suggests over‑consolidation.

**First 3 experiments**  
1. **s1‑02: Topology & Normalization.** Sweep graph topologies (chain, ring, modular) under raw, row‑normalized, and symmetric coupling. Onboarding goal: Understand that apparent “topology effects” are confounded by coupling distribution; normalization is a critical control.  
2. **s2‑03: Eligibility Trace Horizon.** Measure credit consistency (alignment of \(\Delta W\) with causal edges) vs. reward delay. Onboarding goal: Validate dual‑timescale traces extend credit assignment; tune \(\tau_f\) relative to expected delays.  
3. **s3‑07: Wake/NREM Stability Budget.** Impose \(\|W\|_F \le 2.0\). Compare wake‑only vs. wake+NREM across learning rates. Onboarding goal: Demonstrate that two‑phase learning expands the achievable stable‑and‑performant regime; observe the “wedge” where wake‑only fails but wake+NREM succeeds.

## Limitations
- **Scalability:** Core demonstrations are on small, discrete mazes; it is unknown if the design scales to high‑dimensional continuous control or complex visual RL.  
- **Biological plausibility:** The implementation uses engineered Stuart‑Landau oscillators and explicit global modulators; correspondence to biological neural populations and sleep architecture remains speculative.  
- **Theoretical guarantees:** The analysis is empirical; formal understanding of why the staged update schedule expands the stable learning regime for phase‑coded memories is lacking.

## Confidence
- **High confidence:** Core empirical claim that wake/sleep separation expands stable learning under a weight‑norm budget; necessity of phase coherence for REM replay benefits.  
- **Medium confidence:** Dual‑timescale three‑factor plasticity enabling local credit assignment; emergence of Tolman‑style latent learning from replay.  
- **Low confidence:** Generalizations to biological sleep function; assertion that NREM’s primary role is stability guardrailing rather than consolidation.

## Next Checks
1. **Benchmark Transfer:** Implement Phasor Agents on a continuous control benchmark (e.g., `dm_control`) using the same staged protocol; check if stability and performance advantages transfer without extensive task‑specific tuning.  
2. **Hyperparameter Robustness Sweep:** Systematically vary \(\tau_{\text{fast}}\), \(\tau_{\text{slow}}\), and sleep‑stage durations across multiple maze tasks; a robust system should show a broad performance plateau.  
3. **Ablation of Staging Order & Necessity:** Test critical permutations: REM before NREM, concurrent wake+consolidation (no clear separation), and REM‑only (no NREM). The primary claim predicts these degrade stability and/or replay fidelity compared to the proposed wake→NREM→REM order.
