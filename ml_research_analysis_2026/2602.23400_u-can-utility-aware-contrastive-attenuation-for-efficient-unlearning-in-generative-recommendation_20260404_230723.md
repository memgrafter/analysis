# U-CAN: Utility-Aware Contrastive Attenuation for Efficient Unlearning in Generative Recommendation

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2602.23400
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.23400
- **Reference count:** 40
- **Primary result:** U-CAN achieves superior forgetting-utility trade-offs with one-shot unlearning, far outperforming retraining and gradient-based baselines on standard metrics.

## Executive Summary
U-CAN is a one-shot machine unlearning method for generative recommendation (GenRec) systems built on large language models. It addresses the Polysemy Dilemma—where neurons encoding private user data also support general reasoning—by precisely localizing and attenuating high-risk parameters within LoRA adapters. Using contrastive activation analysis, utility-aware calibration, and adaptive soft attenuation, U-CAN selectively down-scales neurons responsible for memorizing sensitive interactions while preserving overall recommendation quality. Experiments on ML-100k and Pantry show strong forgetting (high KL divergence, perplexity increases) with minimal utility loss (Recall@10 close to full retain), and superior efficiency compared to retraining or gradient ascent.

## Method Summary
U-CAN operates on a frozen LLM backbone and its task-specific LoRA adapter. Given a forget set D_f and retain set D_r, it first computes mean activations for each layer via forward passes on both sets. A contrastive gap score r_gap highlights neurons that fire more on D_f than D_r, indicating memorization. A utility score r_imp combines the adapter weight magnitude and retain-set activation norms to identify dimensions critical for performance. These scores are fused (with weight λ) and thresholded to select high-risk neurons. Selected neurons are then attenuated by scaling their adapter columns with a smooth decay factor α, which is smaller for higher risk. The process requires only forward passes (one-shot) and avoids full retraining.

## Key Results
- Trade-off@10 of 29.45 on ML-100k, far exceeding retraining (13.94) and other unlearning baselines (some negative).
- Strong forgetting evidenced by large KL divergence increases (0.13 ML-100k, 0.41 Pantry) and perplexity surges (23.83, 69.67).
- Retain-side Recall@10 remains ~0.11, comparable to retraining’s 0.1131.
- One-shot execution with lower time and higher throughput than gradient ascent or pruning.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
U-CAN’s effectiveness stems from three synergistic mechanisms:
1. **Contrastive activation isolation**: It identifies neurons that are highly sensitive to the forgetting set but suppressed on the retention set by computing a gap score `r_gap = ReLU(v_f - γ·v_r)`. This asymmetry indicates neurons that preferentially encode memorized private information.
2. **Utility-aware calibration**: To protect essential reasoning, it scores each neuron’s contribution via `r_imp = (1/d_out)·||W_col||_1·√(∑(x⊙x))`, fusing static weight magnitude and dynamic retain-set activation norms. High scores mark dimensions critical for utility.
3. **Adaptive soft attenuation**: For neurons with fused risk `R_dim > τ_risk`, it applies a continuous decay factor `α = α_max·(1 - (R_dim-τ)/(1-τ))^β` to scale the adapter weights. This smooth downscaling disrupts memorized pathways while preserving connectivity.

These steps collectively mitigate the Polysemy Dilemma: contrastive isolation finds risky neurons, utility scoring safeguards indispensable ones, and soft attenuation selectively weakens the former without hard pruning, which would damage the network’s functional structure.

## Foundational Learning
- **Polysemy Dilemma**: In LLM-based generative recommendation, neurons often encode both private user interactions and general reasoning patterns, making simple removal (e.g., gradient ascent or pruning) catastrophic for utility. U-CAN is designed specifically to address this entanglement.
  - *Quick check*: If a neuron fires for both a user's sensitive interaction and a general genre pattern, why would removing it likely break general reasoning?
- **LoRA (Low-Rank Adaptation)**: A parameter-efficient fine-tuning method that injects low-rank matrices into the model. U-CAN confines unlearning to these adapters, confining changes to a small, modular subset and enabling one-shot intervention without full retraining.
  - *Quick check*: How does restricting unlearning to the adapter (rather than the entire LLM) affect the risk-utility trade-off?
- **Activation-based Localization**: Instead of gradient-based saliency, U-CAN uses forward passes to compute average activations on forget and retain sets. This non-invasive approach efficiently locates neurons with asymmetric responses, a signal for memorization.
  - *Quick check*: Why might comparing activations on forget vs. retain sets be a more reliable indicator of memorized neurons than gradient magnitudes on the forget loss?

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component map**:
Data Partition → Activation Extractor → (Risk Scorer, Utility Scorer) → Fusion & Thresholding → Attenuator → Fused Model

**Critical path**: For each layer, compute v_f and v_r via forward passes → compute contrastive r_gap → compute utility r_imp → fuse with λ to get R_dim → threshold with τ_risk → apply soft attenuation α to selected adapter columns.

**Design tradeoffs**:
- Contrastive activation requires two forward passes (forget/retain) but avoids backpropagation, trading compute for memory.
- The balance between forgetting and utility is controlled by λ (fusion weight) and τ_risk (threshold). Lower λ / higher τ_risk weakens forgetting but improves utility.
- Soft attenuation preserves network topology but may leave residual signal; hard pruning is more decisive but risks catastrophic damage.
- Adapter-only changes keep modifications local but assume the backbone does not memorize sensitive patterns.

**Failure signatures**:
1. Forgetting ineffective: KL divergence unchanged, recall on D_f remains high → τ_risk too high or λ too low.
2. Utility collapse: retain metrics drop sharply → λ too high, τ_risk too low, or α_max too small.
3. High execution time → bottleneck in activation norm streaming over large D_r; check batch sizes.
4. Many layers with no selected units → weak contrastive signal or memorization not localized to adapters.

**First 3 experiments**:
1. Compute mean activations v_f and v_r on 100 samples each; visualize histogram of v_f,j - v_r,j. Expect a positive tail indicating asymmetry.
2. For a layer, plot r_gap vs. r_imp across neurons. Low correlation is favorable; high correlation suggests severe polysemy.
3. Run U-CAN with extreme attenuation (α_max=0.01, β=1, τ=0) and confirm retain Recall@10 drops significantly, validating the attenuation potency.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
1. Can U-CAN be certified under formal privacy frameworks like differential privacy? The paper only uses empirical forgetting metrics and does not provide theoretical privacy guarantees. Integration with DP-SGD or analysis of noise propagation would be needed.
2. Does U-CAN fully disentangle polysemous neurons? Residual private signals might remain under adversarial probing; neuron-level analysis (e.g., activation patching) is required to verify complete disentanglement.
3. How robust are hyperparameters across datasets and architectures? Settings (γ, λ, τ_risk, α_max, β) are tuned on two datasets; sensitivity on other data or larger models (e.g., 13B/70B) and automated tuning methods are unexplored.
4. Does U-CAN scale efficiently to larger models and adapters? Efficiency claims are on Llama-2-7B; scaling to larger backbones or more complex adapters (DoRA, AdaLoRA) and high-frequency deletion scenarios need evaluation.
5. Can U-CAN extend beyond sequential recommendation? Applications to conversational recommendation, multi-behavior sequences, or non-LLM backbones (e.g., diffusion models) have not been tested; adaptation of the pipeline to different modalities and tasks is open.

## Limitations
- Undisclosed critical implementation details (LoRA hyperparameters, prompt template, token masking, evaluation protocol).
- Core reliance on the assumption that contrastive activation asymmetrically identifies memorized neurons, which may not hold if forget/retain activations are symmetric; utility scoring fusion (r_imp) is heuristic and its calibration not directly validated.
- Hyperparameter settings (γ, λ, τ_risk, α_max, β) are hand-tuned and may not generalize; sensitivity analysis is limited.

## Confidence
- Contrastive activation isolates sensitive neurons: Medium
- Utility-aware calibration preserves essential parameters: Medium
- Adaptive soft attenuation maintains connectivity while forgetting: Medium
- Overall superior forgetting-utility trade-off (empirical): High
