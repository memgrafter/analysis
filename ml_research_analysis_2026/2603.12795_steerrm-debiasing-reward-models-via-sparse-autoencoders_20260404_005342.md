# SteerRM: Debiasing Reward Models via Sparse Autoencoders

## Quick Facts
*   **arXiv ID:** 2603.12795
*   **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.12795
*   **Reference count:** 40
*   **Primary result:** SteerRM, a training-free method using sparse autoencoders, reduces format bias in reward models, improving Hard-split accuracy on RM-Bench by 7.3 points on average while preserving performance on non-biased evaluations.

## Executive Summary
Reward models (RMs) often suffer from superficial biases, such as preferring well-formatted responses (e.g., Markdown) over semantically superior plain-text ones. Existing debiasing methods typically require costly retraining or lead to catastrophic performance collapse. SteerRM introduces a novel, training-free intervention at inference time. It uses sparse autoencoders (SAEs) to isolate and suppress format-related features in the model's internal representations. The method synthesizes pairs of responses that are identical in content but differ only in formatting, identifies bias-related SAE features via a strength-stability criterion, and then reconstructs the model's forward pass after ablating those features. Evaluated on six Llama-based RMs using the RM-Bench benchmark, SteerRM significantly improves accuracy on the format-biased "Hard" split while maintaining performance on the "Normal" and overall splits. Its effectiveness generalizes to a different model architecture (Gemma) and a different bias type (politeness), demonstrating the broad potential of SAE-based steering.

## Method Summary
SteerRM operates in three stages without modifying the reward model's weights. First, it constructs a probing dataset of response pairs that share identical semantic content but differ in surface formatting (e.g., Markdown vs. plain text). Second, for a selected range of early layers (0-9) where SAE reconstruction quality is high, it extracts the model's hidden states for both formats. These states are decomposed into sparse features using a pretrained SAE dictionary. Format-related features are identified by scoring each feature based on the consistency (low variance) and magnitude (high mean) of its activation difference between the two formats. The top-K features globally across layers are selected as bias indicators. Third, at inference, the RM's forward pass is intercepted at the identified layers. The hidden states are encoded via the SAE, the selected bias feature activations are zeroed out, and the modified state is reconstructed via the SAE decoder before being passed to subsequent layers. This targeted intervention suppresses format signals while preserving the model's semantic evaluation capability.

## Key Results
*   On average across six Llama-based RMs, SteerRM improves Hard-split accuracy on RM-Bench by **7.3 points** compared to the unsteered baseline.
*   The method achieves this improvement **without degradation** on the Normal split or overall average accuracy, where direct activation suppression causes severe performance collapse.
*   SteerRM demonstrates **cross-architecture generalization** (to a Gemma model) and **cross-bias generalization** (mitigating politeness bias), suggesting its mechanism is not tied to a specific model or bias type.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
*   **Claim:** Format bias can be isolated by comparing reward model responses to content-matched pairs differing only in surface formatting.
*   **Mechanism:** Synthesize paired responses (Markdown vs. plain text) with identical semantic content. The reward score difference between these pairs (`∆(x)`) is attributed solely to formatting, as content is controlled.
*   **Core assumption:** The plain-text transformation preserves all semantic content while removing only formatting syntax, and no other confounding variables vary between pairs.
*   **Evidence anchors:**
    *   [abstract] "SteerRM isolates stylistic effects using contrastive paired responses."
    *   [section 3.2] Details the data synthesis pipeline, including regex validation and manual audit to ensure content preservation.
    *   [corpus] Not directly supported; corpus focuses on other bias mitigation methods.
*   **Break condition:** If the plain-text version inadvertently alters semantics, the measured score difference conflates format and content effects.

### Mechanism 2
*   **Claim:** Format-related SAE features are identifiable through a strength-stability criterion applied to paired activation differences.
*   **Mechanism:** For each SAE feature, compute the mean (`µj`) and variance (`σ²j`) of its activation difference between format-contrasting pairs. Normalize both globally, then score as `scorej = ¯µj · (¯σj + ϵ)⁻¹`. Features with high positive mean and low variance (stable, strong response) are selected.
*   **Core assumption:** Format bias manifests as a subset of SAE features that react consistently (low variance) and preferentially (high mean) to formatting cues across diverse prompts.
*   **Evidence anchors:**
    *   [section 3.3] Formal definition of the strength-stability criterion and global top-K selection.
    *   [section 4.1] Figure 3 shows concentration of selected features in early layers, supporting the criterion's ability to localize format signals.
    *   [corpus] Not directly supported; corpus explores other SAE applications.
*   **Break condition:** If format cues are encoded in a distributed, high-variance manner, the top-K selection may miss bias signals or select noise.

### Mechanism 3
*   **Claim:** SAE-based feature ablation disentangles format bias from semantic evaluation by operating in a sparse, interpretable feature space, avoiding the catastrophic failure of direct activation suppression.
*   **Mechanism:** At inference, intercept hidden states at identified layers, encode via SAE, zero out selected bias-related feature coordinates, and reconstruct modified hidden states via SAE decoder. This removes format signals while staying within the SAE's learned manifold.
*   **Core assumption:** SAE decomposition separates format-related features from content-semantic features, enabling targeted intervention without disrupting the latter.
*   **Evidence anchors:**
    *   [abstract] "direct activation suppression degrades performance due to representation entanglement" and "SAE-based interventions can mitigate reward-model biases without retraining."
    *   [section 4.3] Table 1 shows direct activation suppression causes severe degradation, while SAE-based steering preserves performance.
    *   [section 4.1] Reconstruction quality metrics confirm SAEs preserve reward-relevant signals in early layers.
*   **Break condition:** If format and content signals are not sparsely separable, ablation may also suppress content-relevant features.

## Foundational Learning
*   **Concept: Sparse Autoencoders (SAEs)**
    *   Why needed here: SAEs decompose dense neural activations into sparse, interpretable features, enabling targeted intervention without retraining. Understanding their encoder-decoder structure and sparsity objective is essential.
    *   Quick check question: What does the L1 sparsity term in the SAE training objective encourage, and why is this property useful for debiasing?
*   **Concept: Reward Model Format Bias**
    *   Why needed here: The entire method targets the phenomenon where RMs prefer well-formatted responses over semantically identical plain-text ones. Recognizing this bias as a superficial shortcut is key.
    *   Quick check question: In RM-Bench's "Hard" split, what specific design element creates a format-biased comparison, and why does baseline performance drop there?
*   **Concept: Representation Entanglement**
    *   Why needed here: Directly suppressing activation differences fails because format and content signals are mixed. SAEs are proposed as a solution to disentangle them. This explains the necessity of the SAE step.
    *   Quick check question: Why would subtracting the mean activation difference vector `dℓ = E[h_md − h_pl]` from hidden states harm performance on format-fair evaluations?

## Architecture Onboarding
*   **Component map:**
    Reward Model (RM) -> SAE Dictionary (Encoder `Eℓ`, Decoder `Dℓ` for layers 0-9) -> Intervention Hook (Intercepts `hℓ`, applies encode/zero-reconstruct/decode) -> Modified Forward Pass.
    Feature Identifier (Offline: computes strength-stability scores from paired probing data) -> Suppressed Feature Set `Sℓ`.
*   **Critical path:**
    1.  **Layer Selection:** Evaluate SAE reconstruction quality (MSE, reward delta, L0) on target RM. Select layers (0-9) with stable, low-error reconstruction.
    2.  **Feature Identification:** Generate 500 format-contrasting pairs. For selected layers, compute per-feature paired activation differences, apply strength-stability scoring, and globally top-K select bias features.
    3.  **Inference Steering:** For each input, run RM forward pass. At identified layers, apply hook: encode `hℓ → zℓ`, zero `Sℓ` coordinates, decode `→ ˜hℓ`, substitute `h′ℓ = ˜hℓ`. Final score from steered pass.
*   **Design tradeoffs:**
    *   **K (number of suppressed features):** Larger K reduces bias more but risks removing content-relevant signals, harming Easy split and average performance.
    *   **Probe data source:** Synthetic pairs give clean isolation but may not capture all natural patterns; RM-Bench pairs are natural but noisier.
    *   **Layer range:** Early layers (0-3) concentrate format features. Including deeper layers risks entanglement and reconstruction failure.
*   **Failure signatures:**
    *   **Catastrophic performance collapse:** Intervention on deep layers (>9) with poor SAE reconstruction, or suppressing too many features (K ≫ 30).
    *   **No debiasing effect:** Excluding early layers from intervention, or feature selection failing to identify format-related features due to noisy probe data.
    *   **Content degradation:** K too high or feature selection includes content-related features, causing large drop in Normal/Easy accuracy.
*   **First 3 experiments:**
    1.  **Reconstruction validation:** For the target RM, run a batch of real queries through SAEs in candidate layers (0-9). Compute per-layer MSE, L0, and reward delta between original and reconstructed scores. Confirm stable, low-error reconstruction.
    2.  **Feature set sanity check:** After identifying top-K features, inspect their top-activating examples to verify they respond to formatting tokens (Markdown markers, code fences). Ensure few activate on semantic content tokens.
    3.  **Baseline comparison test:** Run the RM on RM-Bench with three conditions: (a) no intervention, (b) direct activation suppression (subtract mean `dℓ`), (c) SAE-based steering. Verify (b) collapses Normal/Easy performance while (c) improves Hard with minimal drop elsewhere.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
*   **Question:** Can SteerRM generalize to mitigating other common reward model biases (e.g., length preference, verbosity) beyond formatting and politeness?
*   **Basis in paper:** The authors claim generalization "across bias types" but only evaluate one non-format bias (politeness) and one architecture (Gemma), leaving other biases untested.
*   **Why unresolved:** Reward models exhibit diverse biases; without systematic evaluation, the method's breadth remains speculative.
*   **What evidence would resolve it:** Apply SteerRM to benchmarks with controlled biases (e.g., length-controlled splits of RM-Bench) and report consistent Hard-split improvement without overall degradation.

### Open Question 2
*   **Question:** To what extent are the identified "format-related" SAE features purely sensitive to surface formatting, versus entangled with semantic content?
*   **Basis in paper:** Appendix D.7 shows features activating on both format and content (e.g., code comment separators), suggesting the strength-stability criterion may not fully isolate pure format signals.
*   **Why unresolved:** If features capture content cues, suppressing them risks harming semantic evaluation, contradicting the claim of preserved Normal accuracy.
*   **What evidence would resolve it:** Quantify feature purity via independent format/content manipulations: compute activation correlations on datasets where format and content are orthogonal, or perform feature ablation with synthetic pairs that vary only one factor.

### Open Question 3
*   **Question:** What mechanistic properties of SAE decomposition prevent the catastrophic performance collapse observed with direct activation suppression?
*   **Basis in paper:** Direct suppression degrades Normal/Easy accuracy while SAE intervention preserves it; authors attribute this to "representation entanglement" but provide no geometric analysis.
*   **Why unresolved:** Without understanding why SAE latents disentangle format and content better (e.g., sparsity, orthogonality), the core advantage remains anecdotal.
*   **What evidence would resolve it:** Compare activation difference vectors in raw space vs. SAE latent space: measure sparsity (L0), norm distribution, and alignment with format/content probes; or visualize with dimensionality reduction.

## Limitations
*   Probe data fidelity depends on synthetic pairs fully capturing natural format bias without semantic leakage; the paper includes manual audits but lacks full human evaluation.
*   The strength-stability criterion's sensitivity to hyperparameters (`ϵ`, normalization scope) is unspecified; no sensitivity analysis is reported.
*   Claims about SAE's necessity rely on comparisons to direct suppression; the exact decomposition quality needed for safe intervention is not quantified.
