# Benchmarking Large Language Models for Quebec Insurance: From Closed-Book to Retrieval-Augmented Generation
## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2603.07825
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.07825
- **Reference count:** 13
- **Primary result:** Chain-of-thought models with RAG reach ~79% accuracy, but RAG can cause catastrophic failure (e.g., Gemini-2.5-pro: 74.65% → 14.06%).

## Executive Summary
This study introduces AEPC-QA, a gold-standard multiple-choice benchmark (807 questions) derived from Quebec insurance regulatory handbooks, to evaluate 51 large language models in closed-book and retrieval-augmented generation (RAG) settings. The results reveal a "specialization paradox": massive generalist models consistently outperform smaller, domain-specific French fine-tuned models. While RAG significantly boosts weak models (>35 percentage points), it induces "context distraction" in others, causing severe accuracy drops. The top RAG accuracy (~79%) approaches expert proficiency, yet a persistent ~20-point gap between proprietary and open models, combined with retrieval instability, cautions against autonomous deployment without robust calibration.

## Method Summary
The study evaluates models on the private AEPC-QA benchmark (4-choice single-answer questions) using two settings: closed-book zero-shot prompting and RAG. For RAG, a pipeline retrieves the top 5 semantically similar chunks from the Quebec Automobile Insurance Expertise Reference Corpus (QAIERC, 168,600 sentences) using `text-embedding-ada-002`, applies an undisclosed context compressor, and prepends the context to the QA prompt. Model accuracy is the primary metric, computed via 10-fold stratified cross-validation over 10 seeds, with Z-tests for significance between settings.

## Key Results
- Top models (o3-2025-04-16, o1-2024-12-17) achieve RAG accuracies of 78.68% and 75.18%, nearing expert human levels.
- RAG provides dramatic gains for weaker models (>35 percentage points) but causes catastrophic failure in others (Gemini-2.5-pro: 74.65% → 14.06%).
- A "specialization paradox" emerges: large generalist LLMs consistently beat smaller, French fine-tuned, domain-specific models.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The performance hierarchy stems primarily from **reasoning capacity**. Chain-of-thought (CoT) models (e.g., o-series) internally simulate step-by-step inference, allowing them to better integrate and reason over retrieved context. Their superior general capabilities enable them to parse complex regulatory text and apply it accurately, even when the retrieval is noisy. In contrast, smaller fine-tuned models lack foundational reasoning depth, limiting their ability to leverage external knowledge. RAG's benefit for weak models comes from providing direct textual evidence, bypassing the need for internal knowledge retrieval. However, RAG's failure mode ("context distraction") occurs when retrieved context triggers safety refusals (e.g., "I cannot give legal advice") or format non-compliance (e.g., outputting explanations instead of a single letter), likely due to prompt sensitivity and misaligned safety tuning.

## Foundational Learning
*   **Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG):** Augments LLM input with retrieved documents to ground responses in external knowledge. **Why needed:** Overcomes LLM knowledge cutoffs and hallucinations for domain-specific, up-to-date tasks like regulatory QA. **Quick check:** Does adding relevant context improve accuracy more than adding irrelevant context?
*   **Chain-of-Thought (CoT) Prompting:** Encourages the model to generate intermediate reasoning steps before a final answer. **Why needed:** Unlocks more complex reasoning in large models, critical for applying nuanced rules. **Quick check:** Do CoT models show a larger RAG improvement over non-CoT models on the same base?
*   **Embedding-based Dense Retrieval:** Uses vector embeddings (e.g., `text-embedding-ada-002`) to find semantically similar text chunks. **Why needed:** Provides precise, semantic matching for legal/regulatory corpora where keyword search fails. **Quick check:** Is retrieval effectiveness (e.g., hit rate) correlated with final QA accuracy?
*   **Context Compression/Filtering:** Prunes or summarizes retrieved chunks before LLM input. **Why needed:** Manages LLM context window limits and reduces noise. **Quick check:** Does an explicit compressor improve results vs. naively concatenating top-5 chunks?
*   **Specialization Paradox:** The counterintuitive finding that larger, generalist models outperform smaller, domain-fine-tuned ones. **Why needed:** Challenges assumptions that domain adaptation is always optimal. **Quick check:** Is the gap correlated with model size/reasoning capability, or specific to French fine-tuning data?
*   **Format Non-Compliance & Safety Refusals:** Model outputs that violate the expected response format (single letter) or trigger ethical/legal safeguards. **Why needed:** Key failure signatures in RAG for high-stakes domains like law/insurance. **Quick check:** What percentage of failed RAG outputs are refusals vs. gibberish vs. wrong letter?

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component Map:** Raw Document Corpus (QAIERC) -> Chunking -> Embedding Model (`text-embedding-ada-002`) -> Vector Index -> Query -> Top-5 Chunk Retrieval -> Context Compressor (undisclosed) -> Compressed Context + QA Prompt -> LLM (e.g., o3, Gemini) -> Single Letter Output.

**Critical Path:** The end-to-end accuracy depends on the weakest link: retrieval relevance (finds useful info) -> compression quality (keeps signal, removes noise) -> LLM's ability to follow format and reason over context without refusal.

**Design Tradeoffs:** *Retrieval vs. Distraction:* More chunks provide more evidence but increase noise and refusal risk. *Compression:* Aggressive compression loses detail; minimal compression risks context window overflow. *Prompt Engineering:* Strict "letter only" prompts reduce format errors but may increase safety refusals by making the task seem drier.

**Failure Signatures:**
1.  **Catastrophic RAG Regression:** Accuracy drops >20 pp (e.g., Gemini-2.5-pro). Typical cause: safety refusal or format violation (outputs paragraph, not letter).
2.  **No RAG Gain:** Model's closed-book accuracy is near random (<30%). Indicates insufficient reasoning foundation to use retrieved context.
3.  **Inconsistent RAG Gains:** Large variance across cross-validation folds. Suggests unstable retrieval or model sensitivity to slight context variations.

**First Experiments:**
1.  **Replicate Gemini-2.5-pro Collapse:** Run the exact described RAG pipeline with `Gemini-2.5-pro`. Log 100 failure cases: Are they refusals ("I cannot advise...") or non-letter outputs? Then, systematically vary prompt strictness (e.g., "Answer only with A, B, C, or D." vs. "Provide a one-letter answer.").
2.  **Isolate Compressor Impact:** Using a fixed retriever (top-5) and model (e.g., `Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct`), run with: (a) no compressor, (b) an identity "compressor" (truncate), (c) a simple heuristic compressor (e.g., keep first and last sentence of each chunk). Measure accuracy change to quantify compressor's contribution.
3.  **Test Specialization Paradox Generalization:** Take a public French legal QA dataset (e.g., from `LegalBench` or `Jurix`). Evaluate a large generalist model (e.g., `GPT-4o`) vs. a smaller, publicly available French fine-tuned legal model (if one exists). Does the generalist still dominate, confirming the paradox is not dataset-specific?

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
*   **How can RAG architectures be made robust to context distraction?** The paper identifies the problem but no systematic mitigation exists. Resolution requires controlled experiments isolating prompt, safety, and chunking factors.
*   **What methods can distill reasoning power of large CoT models into smaller, cost-effective ones?** The proprietary-open gap persists. Evidence would come from benchmarking distillation techniques (fine-tuning on CoT traces, self-consistency) on AEPC-QA.
*   **How should evaluation protocols evolve beyond multiple-choice?** Current benchmarks miss generative justification and multi-turn conversation with citations. Resolution needs a new corpus of open-ended queries with required citations and metrics for fidelity.
*   **Why the "specialization paradox"?** Why do massive generalists beat domain-specific French models? Evidence requires ablation studies on model size, fine-tuning data, and error pattern analysis.

## Limitations
*   **Private Benchmark:** Exact replication of results (e.g., 78.68%) is impossible without access to the AEPC-QA test set and QAIERC corpus.
*   **Undisclosed Pipeline Details:** The "context compressor" algorithm, chunk size/overlap, and exact prompt formats are unspecified, blocking faithful recreation of the context distraction effect.
*   **Unquantified Expert Baseline:** The claim that top model accuracy "approaches expert proficiency" lacks a measured human expert score on the same benchmark.

## Confidence
*   **High:** Reasoning models (o3/o1) achieve top RAG accuracies (~79%); Massive generalist models consistently outperform smaller French fine-tuned models (specialization paradox).
*   **Medium:** RAG boosts weak models by >35 percentage points; Catastrophic drop for `Gemini-2.5-pro` (14.06%) attributable to format non-compliance or safety refusals.
*   **Low:** Top result (~79%) "approaches expert proficiency" without a defined human expert benchmark.
