# Creative Convergence or Imitation? Genre-Specific Homogeneity in LLM-Generated Chinese Literature
## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2603.14430
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.14430
- **Reference count:** 40
- **Primary result:** LLM-generated Chinese web literature exhibits severe structural homogenization due to rigid adherence to high-frequency plot templates and near-zero comprehension of rare narrative functions.

## Executive Summary
This paper investigates whether the consistency of large language model (LLM)-generated Chinese web literature reflects creative convergence or mere imitation. The authors develop a Proppian-inspired framework defining 34 genre-specific narrative functions and construct a human-annotated corpus. Testing major LLMs (e.g., Qwen3, Doubao) reveals they poorly understand narrative functions, especially rare ones, and produce highly repetitive, templated sequences (e.g., a fixed A→Q→S pattern for battles). The study concludes that homogenization stems from LLMs statistically overfitting to common function sequences in their training data, failing to grasp the abstract, relational semantics of narrative roles.

## Method Summary
The core method involves a two-phase approach. First, the authors design a 34-label taxonomy of narrative functions tailored to Chinese web genres (e.g., "Golden Finger," "Face-Slapping"). Using this schema, human annotators create a gold-standard corpus of 1,000 text segments. Second, they design a probing pipeline that prompts an LLM to annotate text passages with sequence symbols. This pipeline is used to: (1) evaluate LLM function recognition accuracy on a fixed test set; (2) extract dominant narrative function sequences ("paradigms") from both human-written and LLM-generated continuations across six plot types; and (3) analyze the logical coherence and diversity of these sequences.

## Key Results
- LLMs achieve recognition accuracy up to 60% for common narrative functions but near zero for rare functions, indicating a failure to learn the full function spectrum.
- Across all tested LLMs and plot types (battle, emotional, etc.), generated texts adhere to highly consistent, repetitive function sequences (e.g., battles follow A→Q→{O/S}), with paradigm frequency ≥60%.
- Logical connections between functions in LLM outputs are weak, and sequences are shallow, Markovian chains rather than flexible, causally coherent graphs.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1
- **Claim:** Structural homogenization arises because LLMs **statistically overfit** to high-frequency narrative function sequences present in their training data, failing to generalize to less common patterns.
- **Mechanism:** During pre-training, models learn token prediction based on surface co-occurrence. Frequent functions form robust pathways; rare ones with sparse cues do not, leading to default templates (e.g., A→Q→S for battles).
- **Core assumption:** Training data has a long-tail function distribution; next-token prediction does not incentivize abstract, relational semantics.
- **Evidence anchors:** Abstract states LLMs "unable to correctly comprehend the meanings of narrative functions"; section 4.2 shows Qwen3's accuracy gap between common and uncommon functions; corpus notes function frequencies but does not directly analyze model training data.
- **Break condition:** If models trained on a balanced function-annotated corpus showed a diminished common/rare accuracy gap, the mechanism would be supported. The paper provides no such evidence.

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim:** Homogenization is driven by the model's **inability to model causal and logical dependencies** between narrative functions, reducing them to linear, shallow chains.
- **Mechanism:** Narrative functions are nodes in a constraint network (e.g., Villainy necessitates Counteraction). LLMs, optimized for local prediction, learn frequent adjacency patterns (e.g., A often precedes Q) producing locally plausible but globally stereotyped sequences.
- **Core assumption:** Proppian functions are constraint-based; humans manipulate the network, LLMs approximate it with Markovian transitions.
- **Evidence anchors:** Section 4.3 notes weak logical connections and insufficiently smooth transitions; section 4.2 presents identified paradigms as default sequences; annotation guidelines emphasize relational criteria but evaluation measures only label accuracy.
- **Break condition:** If LLMs generated sequences violating observed paradigms (e.g., a battle without initial situation A), the mechanism would be falsified. No such violations are shown.

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim:** LLMs treat narrative functions as **surface stylistic patterns** (keywords, tropes) rather than abstract, domain-invariant plot units.
- **Mechanism:** Model representations conflate a function's semantic definition with its typical textual instantiation (e.g., "Lack (I)" with phrases about a missing artifact). Learning genre-specific trope-language mappings (e.g., "golden finger") prevents mapping diverse texts to abstract categories, causing misclassification of rare functions.
- **Core assumption:** Narrative functions are compositional semantics; true understanding requires recognition across varied realizations and genres.
- **Evidence anchors:** Abstract states LLMs fail to grasp "structured, role-driven, and intent-oriented abstraction"; section 3.1's taxonomy includes web-fiction-specific terms; section 4.1's low cross-genre recognition is suggestive but taxonomy may conflate surface/form.
- **Break condition:** If an LLM correctly annotated passages using only abstract function definitions (no examples), it would demonstrate semantic understanding. The paper does not test this.

## Foundational Learning
- **Concept: Proppian Narrative Functions**
  - **Why needed here:** The entire analysis maps text to a 34-label taxonomy derived from Propp. Understanding that a function is a plot unit tied to character roles and narrative goals is essential for framework design and interpretation.
  - **Quick check question:** Can you explain the difference between "Lack (I)" and "Villainy (H)" in terms of their narrative role and typical position in a sequence?

- **Concept: Chinese Web Literature Genre Conventions**
  - **Why needed here:** The taxonomy is tailored to tropes like "Golden Finger" and "Face-Slapping." Misunderstanding these conventions invalidates annotations and output interpretation.
  - **Quick check question:** Why is "Transfiguration (Fa)" a separate function in this taxonomy, and how might it manifest differently in Xianxia vs. Urban romance?

- **Concept: LLM Evaluation as Probing**
  - **Why needed here:** The study uses LLMs as tools to analyze their own outputs via function annotation. This meta-annotation approach requires understanding that classification performance reflects internal representations, not just generation quality.
  - **Quick check question:** If an LLM accurately identifies a rare narrative function in a passage, what does that imply about its training? What if it fails?

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map:** Taxonomy Engine -> Gold-Standard Corpus -> Probing Pipeline -> Pattern Miner -> Continuation Generator
- **Critical path:** Taxonomy design -> Corpus construction -> Probe design -> Run probes on human/LLM texts -> Extract paradigms -> Continuation experiments -> Compare sequence diversity.
- **Design tradeoffs:** 
  - *Granularity:* 34 functions capture genre-specific tropes but increase annotation complexity; a coarser set would simplify but might miss homogenization nuances.
  - *Corpus size:* 1k segments balances cost and coverage; larger scale could reveal rarer patterns.
  - *LLM-as-annotator:* Using a strong LLM (Qwen3) for initial labeling speeds mining but introduces bias; human review is essential.
- **Failure signatures:**
  - Low inter-annotator agreement (κ << 0.83) on the gold corpus indicates ambiguous definitions.
  - Model recognition accuracy near random (1/34 ≈ 2.9%) suggests the probing task is misunderstood.
  - Generated continuations show no repeated sequence patterns (paradigm frequency <60%), contradicting homogenization.
- **First 3 experiments:**
  1. **Replicate Function Recognition:** Use the exact same 10-text segment test set and evaluation protocol (5 rounds, ACC/Recall/F1) on your LLM. Expect ≈30-40% ACC for common functions, near 0% for rare ones.
  2. **Sequence Extraction:** For a plot type (e.g., battle), sample 30+ passages from human web novels and from your LLM. Annotate both. Compute entropy of function sequences; LLM sequences should have significantly lower entropy.
  3. **Paradigm Stress Test:** Provide the LLM with a "dominant paradigm" in a system prompt (e.g., "All battles must follow A→Q→S") and generate continuations. Measure if compliance increases homogeneity versus zero-shot.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
The paper implicitly raises several unanswered questions about the nature and origins of LLM homogenization:
- How does the **actual distribution of narrative functions** in pretraining corpora (e.g., Common Crawl Chinese web text) compare to the observed long-tail in the gold corpus? Could dataset curation explain the bias?
- Is the observed **template rigidity a cause or symptom** of poor representation learning? Would models that better capture abstract function semantics still produce homogenized outputs if training data itself is templated?
- To what extent does the 34-function **taxonomy itself impose artificial granularity** that misses human author variability? Could a different, perhaps coarser, functional model change the conclusions about diversity?

## Limitations
- The link between observed function frequency gaps and **training data distribution** is inferred, not measured (models' pretraining corpora are opaque).
- **Causal necessity** is unclear: does rigid template adherence *cause* homogenization, or is it a *symptom* of deeper representational issues?
- The 34-function taxonomy may impose artificial granularity; human author variability might fall outside this schema.

## Confidence
- **Structural homogenization exists:** High. Direct sequence analysis shows low entropy, high-paradigm adherence.
- **LLMs misunderstand rare functions:** High. Recognition accuracy near zero is robust.
- **Mechanism 1 (statistical overfitting):** Medium. Plausible and consistent with accuracy gaps, but untested against controlled training distributions.
- **Mechanism 2 (lack of causal modeling):** Medium. Supported by weak logical transitions, but relational understanding not directly probed.
- **Mechanism 3 (surface pattern matching):** Medium. Low cross-genre generalization is suggestive but taxonomy may conflate surface/form.

## Next Checks
1. **Controlled Distribution Shift:** Fine-tune a base LLM on a **balanced, function-annotated subset** of Chinese web novels (upsampling rare functions). If the common/rare accuracy gap diminishes, it supports Mechanism 1.
2. **Abstract Definition Probing:** Prompt LLMs to annotate passages **using only the 34 function definitions** (no examples). Success on rare functions would indicate semantic grasp, isolating surface-pattern failure (Mechanism 3).
3. **Causal Intervention Test:** Generate continuations under **perturbed narrative constraints** (e.g., mandate a battle scene *without* the A→Q sequence). Measure if models can produce logically valid but non-paradigmatic sequences, testing causal flexibility (Mechanism 2).
