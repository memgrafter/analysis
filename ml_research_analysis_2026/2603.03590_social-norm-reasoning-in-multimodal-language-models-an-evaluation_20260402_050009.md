# Social Norm Reasoning in Multimodal Language Models: An Evaluation
## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID**: 2603.03590
- **Source URL**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.03590
- **Reference count**: 5
- **Primary result**: Multimodal LLMs achieve significantly higher accuracy on textual social norm reasoning (95.33%) than on image-based tasks (83.58%), with GPT-4o performing best overall.

## Executive Summary
This study evaluates five multimodal large language models on their ability to reason about social norms using a dataset of 60 short stories—30 text narratives and 30 corresponding four-panel comic strip images. The stories span five common social scenarios (e.g., littering, seat-offering) and six normative variants (adherence, violation, praise, sanction, meta-norm, control). Each model answers eight structured questions per story, with performance measured against human-annotated ground truth. Results show a robust performance gap favoring text over images, with all models struggling most on meta-norm reasoning and on visually depicting the “Seat-offering” scenario. GPT-4o demonstrates the strongest and most consistent performance across both modalities.

## Method Summary
The authors construct a balanced dataset of 60 stories (5 norms × 6 variants × 2 modalities). Text stories (~100 words) and matched comic strip images are generated using GPT-4o, with minimal manual editing for image coherence. Human evaluators establish ground truth for eight standardized questions per story, achieving high inter-rater agreement (Cohen’s κ = 0.90–0.93). Five MLLMs (GPT-4o, Qwen-2.5VL, Gemini 2.0 Flash, Intern-VL3, LLaMA-4 Maverick) are evaluated in a zero-shot setting: for text, the model receives the story and questions; for images, the model first describes the scene before answering. Accuracy is computed per question, and statistical comparisons (paired t-tests, Friedman, Nemenyi, Wilcoxon) assess differences across models, modalities, and variants.

## Key Results
- MLLMs average 95.33% accuracy on text vs. 83.58% on images; paired t-test confirms a significant gap (p < 0.001, d = 0.82).
- GPT-4o achieves the highest scores: 98.75% (text) and 92.5% (image), significantly outperforming three competing models (p < 0.01, r > 0.67).
- Meta-norm reasoning (Variant V5) is consistently the most difficult, with only 75% median accuracy across models and modalities.
- “Seat-offering” scenarios show lower and more variable performance in image-based evaluation compared to other norms.
- Models are better at detecting norm violations than recognizing adherence or meta-norms (p < 0.01, r > 0.61).

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1
- **Claim**: MLLMs achieve higher accuracy on textual norm reasoning than on image-based tasks primarily because their pre‑training data distribution and architectural inductive biases favor textual processing over visual social scene understanding.
- **Mechanism**: MLLMs are trained on massive text corpora with dense semantic supervision, while visual inputs are often paired with relatively sparse captions. This imbalance leads to stronger textual inference skills; visual reasoning, especially for nuanced social interactions depicted in static comic panels, requires additional grounding that current models only partially acquire.
- **Core assumption**: The performance gap reflects inherent capability differences rather than experiment‑specific artifacts.
- **Evidence anchors**:
  - [Abstract]: "Our results show that MLLMs demonstrate superior performance in norm reasoning in text than in images."
  - [Section 4.3]: Paired‑samples t‑test confirmed a significant difference (text M = 0.95, image M = 0.84, t(149)=8.91, p < 0.001, Cohen’s dz=0.82).
  - [Section 5]: Authors interpret the gap as "existing LLMs possess more advanced capabilities in textual inference and reasoning than in visual understanding of social contexts."
- **Break condition**: If future MLLMs are trained on balanced multimodal datasets with explicit objectives for social visual reasoning, the text‑image performance gap may narrow or disappear.

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim**: GPT‑4o’s consistent superiority across both modalities stems from its more advanced multimodal integration and larger scale of training, which yields better grounding of social norms in both textual and visual contexts.
- **Mechanism**: GPT‑4o likely benefits from a larger parameter count, more diverse training data, and a fusion architecture that aligns textual and visual representations more effectively, enabling it to capture subtle normative cues that other models miss.
- **Core assumption**: Model scale and architectural design directly improve normative reasoning ability up to a point of diminishing returns.
- **Evidence anchors**:
  - [Abstract]: "GPT‑4o performed the best (98.75% text, 92.5% image), followed by free model Qwen‑2.5VL."
  - [Section 4.3]: Post‑hoc tests show GPT‑4o significantly outperforms LLaMA‑4 (p < 0.001, r=0.81), Intern‑VL3 (p < 0.01, r=0.68), and Gemini 2.0 Flash (p < 0.01, r=0.67).
- **Break condition**: If emerging architectures decouple scale from reasoning efficiency or if domain‑specific fine‑tuning becomes widespread, the advantage of large proprietary models may shrink.

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim**: Meta‑norm reasoning (variant V5) is disproportionately difficult because it requires recursive mentalizing: identifying a norm violation, detecting a sanction, and then evaluating whether bystanders who did not sanction are themselves sanctioned—a three‑level nested reasoning chain that exceeds typical MLLM inference depth.
- **Mechanism**: The elaboration of meta‑norms adds logical depth that likely overwhelms the models’ ability to maintain a consistent world state across multiple hops, leading to higher error rates and larger variance in answers.
- **Core assumption**: MLLMs lack explicit mechanisms for maintaining and updating nested belief states during sequential reasoning tasks.
- **Evidence anchors**:
  - [Section 4.1] (Text): "Variant V5 consistently underperforms across scenarios, questions, and models, with lower medians and higher variability."
  - [Section 4.2] (Image): "Variant V5 shows lower median and wider spread" and "V5 performed the worst among all variants."
  - [Section 5]: Authors speculate the difficulty "due to the reasoning about metanorm including multiple deeper levels of analysis (level 1‑to 3)."
- **Break condition**: If models incorporate structured reasoning modules (e.g., belief tracking) or are trained on hierarchical social reasoning data, meta‑norm performance may improve.

## Foundational Learning
- **Concept: Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs)**
  - **Why needed**: The paper evaluates five MLLMs; understanding that these models process both text and images is crucial to grasp the experimental design and the modality comparison.
  - **Quick check**: How does an MLLM differ from a standard LLM? (It can accept and reason over visual inputs in addition to text.)
- **Concept: Social Norm Types and Variants**
  - **Why needed**: The study manipulates five norms and six variants (e.g., adherence, violation, praise, sanctions, meta‑punishment). Knowing these categories is essential to interpret the results, especially the difficulty with meta‑norms.
  - **Quick check**: What is a meta‑norm? (A norm that governs how one should respond to norm violations, e.g., punishing those who fail to sanction.)
- **Concept: Zero‑Shot Evaluation**
  - **Why needed**: The paper uses zero‑shot prompting to assess innate model capabilities without task‑specific fine‑tuning, which affects interpretation of performance ceilings.
  - **Quick check**: What is the trade‑off of zero‑shot vs. fine‑tuned evaluation? (Zero‑shot probes general ability but may understate potential with adaptation.)

## Architecture Onboarding
Story Generation Module -> Comic Strip Generator -> Model Inference Engine -> Answer Evaluator -> Statistical Analyzer

### Critical Path
1. Define five norms (scenarios) and six variants (V1–V5) per norm.
2. Generate 30 text stories (5 × 6) with GPT‑4o.
3. For each text story, generate a four‑panel comic strip via GPT‑4o.
4. Establish ground truth: two authors (plus external evaluators) answer the eight questions for all stories; resolve discrepancies.
5. Prompt each MLLM with every text story (or image) and collect answers.
6. Score each answer against ground truth.
7. Aggregate accuracies and perform statistical comparisons across models, modalities, and variants.
8. (Optional bias check) Repeat steps 3–7 with an alternative image generator (Seedream 4.0).

### Design Tradeoffs
- Zero‑shot vs. fine‑tuned: Zero‑shot probing assesses innate capabilities but may understate potential with task‑specific tuning.
- Single image generator: Using GPT‑4o for both text and image creation simplifies reproducibility but risks bias; mitigated by a Seedream replication.
- Human ground truth: Consensus among authors increases reliability; external evaluators achieved >95% agreement.
- Fixed question set: Provides structured evaluation but may omit other normative reasoning aspects.
- Static comic panels: Simplify visual storytelling but may lack dynamic cues present in video or real interactions.

### Failure Signatures
- Low inter‑rater agreement (Cohen’s κ < 0.8) among human evaluators → ambiguous stories or unclear norms.
- High variance across model predictions for a given variant (e.g., outlier scores) → brittle model understanding or confusing story formulation.
- Image generation artifacts (inconsistent characters, missing objects) → degrade visual reasoning; manual editing was needed for a few cases.
- Model refusals or irrelevant outputs → prompt mis‑specification or safety filters interfering.
- Statistical non‑significance in pairwise comparisons → insufficient power or too similar model capabilities.

### First 3 Experiments
1. Text‑only norm reasoning: Evaluate all five MLLMs on the 30 text stories, compute per‑question accuracy, and compare across models.
2. Image‑based norm reasoning: Evaluate the same models on the 30 comic‑strip images using the identical eight questions.
3. Bias mitigation test: Regenerate all images with the Seedream 4.0 model and rerun the image‑based evaluation to ensure the observed modality gap is not an artifact of GPT‑4o’s generation style.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
### Open Question 1
- **Question**: Why do MLLMs perform significantly worse on meta-norm reasoning (variant V5) compared to other norm variants?
- **Basis in paper**: Results show V5 has the lowest median accuracy (75%) across scenarios, questions, and models; heatmaps reveal particularly poor performance on question Q8 for V5.
- **Why unresolved**: The paper identifies V5 as difficult but does not investigate whether this stems from model architecture limitations, insufficient training on hierarchical social reasoning, or shortcomings in the evaluation design itself.
- **What evidence would resolve it**: Detailed error analysis of model responses on V5, probing model attention or intermediate reasoning steps when processing V5 stories, and controlled experiments varying the complexity of meta-norm descriptions.

### Open Question 2
- **Question**: What specific factors contribute to the substantial performance gap between text-based and image-based norm reasoning in MLLMs?
- **Basis in paper**: Paired t-test confirms significantly higher accuracy on text (95.33%) vs. images (83.58%), p<0.001; discussion attributes this to "text-dominant reasoning strengths" and potential visual understanding limitations.
- **Why unresolved**: While the gap is quantified, the paper does not isolate whether this arises from poor visual grounding of social concepts, suboptimal image generation (comic strips), or inadequate multimodal fusion in the models.
- **What evidence would resolve it**: Ablation studies replacing comic strips with alternative visual formats (e.g., photorealistic images, annotated diagrams), evaluating model-generated textual descriptions of images for completeness, and analyzing cross-modal attention patterns.

### Open Question 3
- **Question**: How can the evaluation framework be refined to better capture MLLMs' understanding of visually nuanced norms like "Seat-offering," which showed lower performance in image-based scenarios?
- **Basis in paper**: Results indicate "Seat-offering" had wider spreads and lower medians in image analysis; discussion hypothesizes that seat-offering may not have been well depicted in the generated comic strips.
- **Why unresolved**: The paper speculates on depiction issues but does not validate this hypothesis or explore whether the norm itself is inherently harder to represent visually versus other norms like "Littering."
- **What evidence would resolve it**: Human evaluations rating the clarity of each image variant, correlating image quality scores with model accuracy, and designing follow-up studies using improved or alternative visual stimuli for seat-offering.

### Open Question 4
- **Question**: Would fine-tuning MLLMs on multimodal norm reasoning data improve performance on complex variants (e.g., meta-norms) and reduce the text-image gap?
- **Basis in paper**: Discussion suggests future work should explore fine-tuning and RAG, implying current zero-shot evaluation may not reflect upper-bound capabilities; the observed struggles on V5 and images indicate potential for improvement via training.
- **Why unresolved**: The study only evaluates off-the-shelf models without adaptation, leaving unknown whether targeted training could close performance gaps and enhance reasoning depth.
- **What evidence would resolve it**: Experiments fine-tuning models on a subset of the dataset, measuring changes in accuracy on V5 and image tasks, and comparing fine-tuned vs. zero-shot performance across multimodal inputs.

## Limitations
- **API & model specifics**: Unknown inference parameters (temperature, max_tokens) and exact model versions may affect reproducibility and performance comparisons.
- **Scoring rubric**: Open-ended answers (Q1–2) lack a detailed, operationalized scoring guide, risking inconsistent human–model evaluation alignment.
- **Image generation artifacts**: Unclear which comic panels were manually edited post‑generation, potentially confounding visual reasoning difficulty.

## Confidence
- **Text–image gap claim**: High (p < 0.001, d = 0.82; consistent across models and statistical tests).
- **GPT‑4o superiority**: Medium (significant post‑hoc results but tied to specific proprietary version; scale/architecture contribution not isolated).
- **Meta‑norm difficulty**: Medium (replicated across modalities but mechanistic explanation remains inferential; no direct probe of reasoning depth).
