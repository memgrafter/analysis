# ADAPT: Hybrid Prompt Optimization for LLM Feature Visualization

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2602.17867
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.17867
- **Reference count:** 30
- **Primary result:** ADAPT significantly outperforms prior methods (GCG, BEAST, EPO) in maximizing activation of Sparse Autoencoder latents across all model layers, with gains on 70.4% of features (p < 10⁻²⁸), and produces more interpretable prompts.

## Executive Summary
Feature visualization for large language models (LLMs) seeks text inputs that maximally activate specific latent directions in a model’s representation, but is challenged by discrete text spaces and local minima. ADAPT introduces a hybrid optimization framework combining beam-search initialization, adaptive mixing of gradient-guided and gradient-free mutations, and a diversity-preserving slot management system. Evaluated on Sparse Autoencoder latents from Gemma 2 2B, ADAPT consistently achieves higher activation ratios and better activation rank than prior methods. A strong correlation between activation ratio and human-rated interpretability (r=0.645) validates the approach, establishing feature visualization as a tractable task for LLMs with domain-specific design.

## Method Summary
ADAPT optimizes discrete prompts to maximize activation of a target Sparse Autoencoder (SAE) latent. It begins with parallel beam searches from single-token seeds, using right-appends and middle-inserts to generate diverse starting prompts. In the main loop, each prompt generates mutation candidates via two operations: a GCG-style gradient-guided token swap (using a one-hot gradient estimate) and a gradient-free logit-swap (sampling from the model’s predicted distribution). A slot management system maintains diversity by preserving the best prompt from each initialization group in “guaranteed” slots while filling remaining slots globally by improvement score, with per-group caps to prevent monopolization. A sigmoid-scheduled fluency penalty regularizes output quality, ramping up over iterations.

## Key Results
- ADAPT achieves higher activation than GCG on 70.4% of tested features (Wilcoxon signed-rank test p < 10⁻²⁸), with consistent performance gains across all model layers.
- Human evaluation shows a strong positive correlation (r=0.645) between activation ratio and prompt interpretability; ADAPT prompts are rated significantly more interpretable than those from GCG, BEAST, and EPO.
- ADAPT exhibits a stable performance profile across layers, unlike baselines whose success varies dramatically by layer, indicating robust handling of diverse latent activation patterns.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Beam-Search Initialization Mitigates Local Minima
ADAPT runs multiple independent beam searches in parallel from single-token seeds. Candidates are generated by right-appending tokens sampled from the model’s distribution and performing random middle-inserts. The best prompts across all beams and lengths are retained as starting points for the main optimization loop. This explores multiple promising regions of the prompt space upfront, reducing the chance that all optimization trajectories start in poor basins and get stuck.

### Adaptive Mutation Mix Balances Exploitation and Exploration
Each iteration, for each prompt, ADAPT generates candidates via either GCG-swap (using a gradient estimate to find low-gradient tokens) or logit-swap (sampling from model logits), chosen with equal probability. The GCG estimate, while noisy, can indicate beneficial swaps; logit-swap introduces variety to escape narrow minima. This hybrid mix allows the method to exploit when the gradient signal is useful and explore when it is deceptive, smoothing the discrete optimization landscape.

### Diversity-Preserving Slots Maintain Multiple Trajectories
Prompts are grouped by their initialization beam. Each group has “guaranteed” slots that always retain the group’s historically best prompt. “Free” slots are filled globally by candidates with the highest improvement score (current activation minus the group’s previous best), but each group is capped at twice its proportional share. This ensures that diverse, independently evolved group trajectories are preserved throughout optimization, preventing premature convergence to a single localized optimum and yielding a set of varied activating examples.

## Foundational Learning
- **Sparse Autoencoder (SAE) latents:** These are learned directions in an LLM’s activation space intended to be monosemantic. Feature visualization targets a specific latent’s activation at a designated sequence position, not a behavioral output.
  - *Quick check:* What does an SAE latent represent, and why is its activation measured at a specific sequence position?
- **Gradient-based discrete optimization (GCG):** GCG is a key baseline and one of ADAPT’s mutation types. It estimates the impact of swapping a token by computing the gradient of the target activation with respect to the input’s one-hot embeddings, then selects tokens with low estimated gradients.
  - *Quick check:* How does GCG compute a gradient estimate for discrete token swaps, and what is the key assumption behind it?
- **Activation ratio and rank metrics:** Activation ratio normalizes a found prompt’s activation by the activation of the top dataset example (maximally activating example, MAE). Rank indicates the prompt’s position if sorted among the top 15 deduplicated MAEs.
  - *Quick check:* Why compare against dataset MAEs instead of using raw activation values?

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map:** Initializer → Mutation Engine → Evaluator → Selector → Loop Controller → (back to Mutation Engine for next iteration)
- **Critical path:** Beam-search initialization produces grouped starting prompts. For T iterations: each prompt generates GCG-swap and logit-swap candidates; evaluator computes latent activation and fluency; selector updates slots using improvement scores and group caps; loop controller advances iteration and adjusts fluency penalty schedule.
- **Design tradeoffs:** Beam search initialization cost vs. better starting distribution; GCG-swap computational overhead (backward pass) vs. targeted refinement; logit-swap cheap but less directed; diversity slots (more groups/slots) vs. slower convergence; fluency penalty schedule (early unconstrained optimization) vs. risk of gibberish basins.
- **Failure signatures:** All groups stagnate at similar low activation (init failed to find promising regions); GCG-swap candidates rarely improve (gradient estimate too noisy); free slots monopolized by one group (diversity caps too weak or correlated init); high activation but gibberish prompts (fluency penalty too lenient/started too late).
- **First 3 experiments:** 1) Benchmark comparison: Run ADAPT, GCG, BEAST, EPO on 486 latents across all layers; compute mean activation ratio per layer and perform Wilcoxon tests. 2) GCG estimate validation: For GCG runs, compare improvements from candidates generated via GCG estimate vs. logit-swap across gradient-rank bins. 3) Fluency ablation: Run GCG with varying constant fluency penalty weights; compare activation ratio distributions to assess regularization impact.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- Does ADAPT’s performance advantage hold for larger models (>7B parameters) and SAEs trained on non-residual components (e.g., MLP layers, attention heads)?
- Can ADAPT’s prompts be reliably evaluated using automated interpretability metrics instead of human judgment, enabling scalable assessment?
- How can ADAPT be extended to optimize for multiple latents with conflicting activation patterns without sacrificing performance?
- Should ADAPT’s fixed mutation mix (50% gradient-guided, 50% logit-swap) be adaptively tuned per latent based on characteristics like locality or density?

## Limitations
- Exact latent indices and dataset MAEs used for the 486-latent evaluation are not released, preventing faithful replication of the core benchmark.
- Fluency penalty schedule parameters (steepness `s`, midpoint `t_mid`, threshold `τ`) are unspecified, making optimization dynamics partially opaque.
- Missing codebase requires reimplementation; hyperparameter details (e.g., mergepoint timing, slot cap enforcement) are inferential, and human evaluation stimuli are unavailable.
- Evaluation focused solely on Gemma 2 2B residual stream SAEs; generalizability to other models and components is untested.

## Confidence
- **Performance superiority:** High (strong statistical evidence, consistent across layers).
- **Beam-search initialization:** Medium (ablations support role, but isolated contribution unclear).
- **Adaptive mutation mix:** Medium (gradient noise documented, but fixed 0.5 probability may not be optimal).
- **Diversity-preserving slots:** Medium (indirectly supported by stable layer performance, lacking sensitivity analysis).
- **Interpretability correlation:** Medium-Low (strong correlation but relies on non-blinded annotator exposure to author explanations).
