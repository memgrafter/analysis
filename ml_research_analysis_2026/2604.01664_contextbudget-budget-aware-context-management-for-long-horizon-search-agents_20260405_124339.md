# ContextBudget: Budget-Aware Context Management for Long-Horizon Search Agents

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2604.01664
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.01664
- Reference count: 37
- Primary result: BACM-RL achieves over 1.6× F1 improvement on 32-objective QA and matches a 235B model with only an 8k token budget on BrowseComp-Plus.

## Executive Summary
Long-horizon LLM agents must balance retaining interaction history against strict context window limits. Existing context compression methods are budget-agnostic, leading to either over-compression under loose budgets or overflow under tight ones. This paper introduces Budget-Aware Context Management (BACM), which reformulates compression as a sequential decision problem conditioned on the remaining context budget. BACM is operationalized as BACM-RL, extending Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO) with a progressively tightened curriculum and overflow penalties. Empirical evaluations on multi-objective QA and web browsing benchmarks show consistent gains across model scales (7B and 30B) and task complexities. Notably, BACM-RL yields a >1.6× F1 improvement on the hardest QA setting and maintains performance as budgets shrink from 16k to 4k tokens, even enabling a 30B model with an 8k budget to outperform a 235B model with a 128k window.

## Method Summary
BACM-RL frames context management as an MDP where the state includes the current context, remaining token budget (computed before appending a pending observation), and the pending observation’s size. Before each new observation is added, the policy selects a compression action from three mutually exclusive categories: `NONE` (no compression), `PARTIAL` (merge a selected subset of semantically contiguous segments), or `FULL` (collapse all segments). This deferred loading ensures the compressed context plus the new observation always fits the budget. The policy is trained with GRPO using a progressive curriculum: the maximum allowed context length (`max_model_len`) is decreased linearly over stages; only rollouts that satisfy the stage‑specific budget throughout the trajectory receive the final task reward (e.g., summed F1). This curriculum forces the agent to learn increasingly aggressive compression while generalizing across budget levels.

## Key Results
- BACM-RL improves summed F1 by more than 1.6× on 32‑objective compositional QA compared to prior methods like MEM1 and Summary Agent.
- It degrades minimally when the context budget is reduced from 16k to 4k tokens, whereas baselines exhibit significant performance drops.
- On BrowseComp-Plus, a 30B model with an 8k token budget surpasses a 235B model equipped with a 128k window.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1: Budget‑Conditioned State with Deferred Loading
**Claim:** The agent evaluates remaining context capacity before appending new observations, preventing overflow or premature over‑compression.  
**Mechanism:** The MDP state is augmented with `r_t = B - |C_t|` and the pending observation size `|o_t|`. The policy samples an action `u_t` to produce a compressed context `C'_t` satisfying `|C'_t| ≤ B - |o_t|`. Only after this step is the observation appended.  
**Core assumption:** Token counts can be accurately measured prior to loading; the environment reliably reports `|o_t|`.  
**Evidence:** Section 3.1 defines the budget‑conditioned state; unlike MEM1 or Summary Agent, BACM defers loading.  
**Break condition:** Inaccurate token counting or summarization overshoot could cause overflow despite the check.

### Mechanism 2: Commit‑Block Aggregation
**Claim:** The agent adaptively controls compression intensity via a unified action space, balancing retention and reduction under varying budgets.  
**Mechanism:** The context buffer is divided into `K` coherent segments. The policy chooses among `NONE`, `PARTIAL` (merge a chosen subset of segments), or `FULL` (collapse all segments).  
**Core assumption:** Segments are meaningful units; discarding entire segments does not lose critical dispersed facts.  
**Evidence:** Section 3.2 describes the three mutually exclusive actions; prior methods lack such dynamic regimes.  
**Break condition:** Poor segmentation that splits a coherent fact across blocks could harm performance under `PARTIAL`/`FULL`.

### Mechanism 3: Budget‑Aware GRPO with Progressive Curriculum
**Claim:** Reinforcement learning with a gradually tightening budget curriculum yields robust compression strategies that generalize across horizons and budgets.  
**Mechanism:** BACM‑RL uses GRPO. Training spans `J` stages with monotonically decreasing `max_model_len` (e.g., 8192 → 4096). Within a stage, only rollouts where every turn respects the current budget receive a non‑zero reward equal to the final task performance. Group‑relative advantages are broadcast token‑wise.  
**Core assumption:** Final F1 adequately reflects context‑management quality, and the curriculum enables smooth adaptation.  
**Evidence:** The curriculum is detailed in Section 3.3; ablations (random budget) degrade performance.  
**Break condition:** Sparse rewards may fail to credit individual actions; overly aggressive curriculum steps could cause training collapse.

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component map:**  
Environment (task simulator) -> Budget Manager (computes remaining budget before loading) -> Policy Network (samples compression action) -> Commit‑Block Aggregator (folds selected segments) -> Updated context buffer -> Environment.  
Training adds: Curriculum Scheduler (adjusts max_model_len) and GRPO optimizer (updates Policy Network).

**Critical path (inference):**  
1. Observe current context length `|C_t|` and pending observation size `|o_t|`.  
2. Construct budget‑conditioned state `b_t = (s_t, r_t, |o_t|)` with `r_t = B - |C_t|`.  
3. Policy selects structured action `u_t` (NONE/PARTIAL/FULL).  
4. Aggregator applies folding to produce compressed context `C'_t`.  
5. Append `o_t` to obtain `C_{t+1}` and repeat.

**Design tradeoffs:**  
- Segmentation granularity: Coarser blocks simplify policy but risk mixing unrelated info; finer blocks preserve semantics but increase token overhead.  
- Curriculum pacing: Too slow yields little compression learning; too fast may overwhelm the policy.  
- Reward shaping: Using only final F1 provides a sparse signal; intermediate bonuses could improve credit assignment.

**Failure signatures:**  
- Context overflow after appending: indicates misestimated `|o_t|` or aggregator not respecting budget.  
- Performance drop when budget decreases: may signal over‑ or under‑compression; inspect action distribution (e.g., `NONE` dominating).  
- Zero compression calls during training: suggests curriculum pressure insufficient; verify budget schedule.

**First 3 experiments (validation):**  
1. Unit test budget calculation: Feed synthetic contexts and observations; verify `|C'_t| + |o_t| ≤ B`.  
2. Action distribution vs. budget: Clamp `r_t` to different values; expect shift from NONE → PARTIAL → FULL as budget shrinks.  
3. Ablation of budget signal: Remove `r_t` from state; expect significant performance drop per Table 2.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
1. **Credit assignment:** How can sparse and delayed rewards be improved to effectively train compression policies?  
2. **Finer‑grained compression:** Would token‑ or span‑level importance modeling preserve more information than commit‑block aggregation, especially for fact‑intensive tasks?  
3. **Generalization to realistic environments:** How does BACM perform in open‑ended tool use, multimodal reasoning, or human‑agent interaction settings?  
4. **Optimal RL algorithm:** Is GRPO the most effective choice, or could alternatives (PPO, SAC) yield better performance under identical constraints?  
5. **Segmentation strategy:** How does the granularity and method of segmenting interaction history (fixed‑length vs. semantic blocks) affect the compression‑efficiency trade‑off?

## Limitations
- The definition and update mechanism for context segments remain underspecified, leaving ambiguity in aggregation granularity.  
- The implementation of merged commit generation (which model, decoding parameters, caching) is not detailed, affecting reproducibility and token counts.  
- Reliance on sparse outcome‑based rewards (final F1) may hinder learning efficiency and could be noisy when using LLM‑as‑judge metrics.

## Confidence
- Task performance under tight budgets: **High**  
- Necessity of budget‑conditioned state: **High**  
- Progressive curriculum for robust adaptation: **Medium**
