# PartisanLens: A Multilingual Dataset of Hyperpartisan and Conspiratorial Immigration Narratives in European Media

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2601.03860
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.03860
- **Reference count:** 27
- **Primary result:** Introduced PARTISANLENS, a multilingual dataset of 1,617 Spanish, Italian, and Portuguese headlines annotated for hyperpartisanship, PRCT, stance, and rhetorical bias; LLM benchmarks achieved macro-F1 scores up to 0.82 (PRCT), with persona conditioning improving LLM-human agreement for specific tasks and languages.

## Executive Summary
This paper introduces PARTISANLENS, the first multilingual dataset focused on hyperpartisan and Population Replacement Conspiracy Theory (PRCT) narratives around immigration in European media. The dataset comprises 1,617 headlines in Spanish, Italian, and Portuguese, each annotated with multiple labels and rhetorical span markers. The authors establish baseline performance for various LLMs and encoder models, finding that PRCT detection is the easiest task (macro-F1 up to 0.82) while stance classification is the hardest (up to 0.61). A key innovation is testing whether LLMs can be conditioned on socio-economic personas to mimic human annotators; this improved agreement for PRCT across all languages and for stance in Spanish and Italian with `gpt5-mini`, but yielded mixed results for hyperpartisan detection.

## Method Summary
The dataset was built via a hybrid curation pipeline: a broad scrape of ~400k immigration-related headlines using Media Cloud and language-specific keywords, followed by filtering with a local Llama 3.1 8B model to surface rare hyperpartisan/PRCT instances. A balanced final sample was manually annotated across six dimensions (three content-level, three rhetorical span-level). For evaluation, the authors tested multiple models (mBERT, Llama variants, Mistral Nemo) under zero-shot, few-shot, and supervised fine-tuning regimes, with some variants further augmented by human-written rationales. The persona experiments involved prompting LLMs with profiles derived from real annotators' Political Compass and PRISM test results, then measuring Cohen's κ against the corresponding human's labels.

## Key Results
- Macro-F1 scores on test set: hyperpartisan up to 0.71 (Llama 70B fine-tuned), PRCT up to 0.82 (same), stance up to 0.61 (few-shot mBERT).
- Persona conditioning with `gpt5-mini` improved LLM-human Cohen's κ for PRCT in all languages (e.g., Spanish: 0.483 → 0.635) and for stance in Spanish/Italian.
- Rationale augmentation during fine-tuning benefited larger models (Llama 70B) for hyperpartisan detection but showed mixed or negative effects for other tasks/models.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1
- **Claim:** A hybrid data collection strategy (keyword filtering + LLM-assisted selection) effectively curates a focused, multilingual dataset of hyperpartisan and conspiratorial narratives from a large, noisy initial corpus.
- **Mechanism:** An initial broad scrape via Media Cloud using language-specific immigration/PRCT keywords yields a high-recall but low-precision corpus (~400k headlines). A lightweight, local LLM (Llama 3.1 8B) then performs binary classification on this corpus to filter for hyperpartisan and PRCT content, surfacing rare positive instances (e.g., PRCT <1% initially). Researchers manually review and balance the final sample from this filtered set.
- **Core assumption:** A relatively small, efficient LLM can serve as a credible first-pass filter to identify latent semantic categories (hyperpartisan, PRCT) within a large, unlabeled corpus, reducing human curation effort while maintaining category coverage.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [section 3.1] Explicitly describes using "Llama 3.1 8B to perform an initial classification" on ~400k headlines to guide the selection of the final 1,617-headline balanced sample.
  - [abstract] States the dataset addresses the "scarcity" by being the "first multilingual dataset" for these specific narratives, implying the hybrid method was necessary to build it.
  - [corpus] The final dataset statistics (Table 2) show successful capture of the target classes, including the low-frequency PRCT (e.g., 9 in PT), which the initial LLM filter was designed to surface.
- **Break condition:** If the initial LLM filter has a high false negative rate for subtle or linguistically nuanced instances of the target categories, the final dataset will lack representation of those phenomena, limiting its utility for training or evaluating models on the full spectrum of the tasks.

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim:** Fine-tuning with label-specific rationales (short natural-language explanations derived from annotated rhetorical spans) can improve LLM classification performance and inter-annotator agreement for certain nuanced tasks by aligning model reasoning with human evidence-spotting.
- **Mechanism:** During supervised fine-tuning, the model's input is augmented not only with the headline and task label but also with a human-written rationale (e.g., "The headline appeals to fear..."). The model is trained to produce the correct label while being exposed to the linguistic cues and persuasive strategies (loaded language, fear appeal) that humans used to justify their judgment.
- **Core assumption:** Providing models with human-generated rationales teaches them to recognize and weight the same rhetorical features that underlie human annotation decisions for hyperpartisan and PRCT content, leading to predictions that better mimic human patterns.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [section 4.2] Defines the "rationales" variant: models "fine-tuned not only on classification labels but also on rationales... generated from annotated templates."
  - [section 4.4] Reports that rationale augmentation "benefits Llama 70B" for hyperpartisan detection and shows mixed/negative results for other models/tasks, indicating the mechanism's effect is model and task-dependent.
  - [section 6.3] Shows that persona-conditioned models (which implicitly use profile-based reasoning) see larger gains for PRCT and Stance, suggesting that aligning model "reasoning" (via persona or rationale) helps for content-focused tasks more than pure style detection.
- **Break condition:** If the rationales are poorly aligned with the actual decision criteria for a given task (e.g., stance may rely more on broader context than headline-local rhetoric) or if the model's capacity is insufficient to learn from the rationale signal, this method will not improve performance and may introduce noise.

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim:** Conditioning LLMs on socio-economic and ideological profiles (persona prompting) can reduce the gap between LLM and human annotation agreement for tasks where human judgment is known to vary by background, by simulating the diversity of human interpretive perspectives.
- **Mechanism:** A structured prompt injects a simulated annotator's demographic and political profile (derived from Political Compass and PRISM test results of real human annotators) before the classification task. The hypothesis is that an LLM, prompted to "be" a specific persona, will adopt that persona's latent biases and interpretive frameworks, producing annotations that align more closely with the corresponding human annotator's judgments.
- **Core assumption:** A significant portion of human disagreement on subjective tasks like hyperpartisan or stance detection stems from stable, profile-linked differences in values and perception. An LLM's output distribution can be shifted to mimic these value-based differences via prompt-based persona conditioning.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [section 6] Entire section dedicated to the "User-Persona LLMs" experiment, explicitly testing whether "LLMs can emulate patterns of human annotation behaviour when conditioned on realistic user profiles."
  - [section 6.3] Table 5 shows that for **PRCT**, persona conditioning (with both Llama 70B and gpt5-mini) consistently improves agreement with the matched human compared to the generic (non-persona) LLM baseline across all languages (e.g., SPA PRCT: 0.483 -> 0.635 with gpt5-m).
  - [section 6.3] For **Stance**, gains with gpt5-mini are noted in SPA/ITA (0.364 -> 0.767), but effects are weaker/mixed for Hyperpartisan and absent in PT, indicating the mechanism's success is task- and model-specific.
- **Break condition:** If the LLM's underlying training data and alignment process have strongly encoded a default "average" or "safe" perspective that is resistant to persona prompting, or if the persona descriptions are too coarse to capture the nuanced determinants of human disagreement, the conditioning will have negligible or inconsistent effects on agreement.

## Foundational Learning
- **Concept: Multilingual Corpus Curation for Low-Resource Phenomena**
  - **Why needed here:** The paper's core contribution is a dataset in three European Romance languages for niche tasks (hyperpartisan, PRCT). Replicating this requires understanding how to combine broad scraping (Media Cloud) with targeted LLM filtering to overcome data scarcity and language imbalance.
  - **Quick check question:** What are the risks of using a single, small LLM (e.g., Llama 3.1 8B) as a filter for a complex, culturally embedded phenomenon like PRCT, and how might they be mitigated?
- **Concept: Annotation Theory & Measuring Inter-Annotator Agreement**
  - **Why needed here:** The dataset's validity hinges on reliable human labels (Fleiss' κ in Table 1) and understanding why agreement varies by language/task. The LLM-as-annotator evaluation directly depends on using human-human agreement as the gold-standard benchmark (Cohen's κ in Table 4).
  - **Quick check question:** Why might Fleiss' κ be "almost perfect" for Hyperpartisan in Spanish (0.876) but only "substantial" for Stance in Italian (0.598), and what does this imply for training/evaluating models?
- **Concept: LLM Prompting for Subjective Tasks & Persona Simulation**
  - **Why needed here:** The paper's third experiment tests a cutting-edge method (persona prompting) to align LLM outputs with diverse human judgments. Implementing this requires grasping prompt engineering for role-playing, the use of psychological/political profiling instruments (Compass, PRISM), and metrics for agreement.
  - **Quick check question:** According to the results, for which specific task and language did persona conditioning with *gpt5-mini* most successfully narrow the gap with human-human agreement, and what might that suggest about the task's nature?

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map:** Data Pipeline -> Annotation Schema -> Evaluation Engine -> Persona Simulation Module
- **Critical path:** 1. Finalize keyword lists for Media Cloud per language. 2. Run initial corpus scrape and LLM (Llama 3.1 8B) filtering. 3. Conduct multi-round human annotation with web tool, calculating Fleiss' κ iteratively. 4. For benchmarking: select model architectures (mBERT, Llama variants, Nemo), define prompting/fine-tuning strategies (zero/few-shot, labels, labels+rationales), run experiments, compute macro-F1. 5. For persona experiments: collect annotator socio-political profiles, generate persona prompts, run conditioned inference with target LLMs (Llama 70B, gpt5-mini), compute pairwise Cohen's κ against each human.
- **Design tradeoffs:**
  - **Dataset size vs. annotation depth:** Small final dataset (1.6k) enables fine-grained multi-task annotation but may limit model generalizability. The choice to use headlines (not full articles) avoids copyright but sacrifices contextual discourse.
  - **Model choice:** Using a small, local LLM (8B) for filtering prioritizes cost/control over potential accuracy. For benchmarking/evaluation, comparing encoder (mBERT) vs. decoder (Llama) models and different scales (8B vs. 70B) reveals tradeoffs between parameter efficiency and task performance.
  - **Rationale vs. Persona:** Rationale augmentation adds interpretability but showed inconsistent performance gains. Persona conditioning aims to model annotator variability but improved agreement only selectively (best for PRCT).
- **Failure signatures:**
  - **Data-level:** Extremely low class counts (e.g., PRCT in PT, n=9) make any model evaluation or agreement statistic unstable and non-generalizable.
  - **Model-level:** Persistent negative bias across all models (Appendix F) where systems fail to recall positive instances, leading to deceptively high accuracy but useless detection for rare classes.
  - **Persona-level:** Degraded performance for Hyperpartisan and Stance under persona conditioning (Table 5), suggesting the persona cues are misaligned with the decision criteria for those tasks or that the model's base behavior overpowers the prompt.
- **First 3 experiments:**
  1. **Replicate Data Collection & Filtering:** Scrape a small sample using Media Cloud with the paper's keyword lists (Appendix repository), run Llama 3.1 8B binary classification for hyperpartisan/PRCT, and verify the distribution of positives matches the reported scarcity.
  2. **Run Benchmarking Baseline:** Fine-tune mBERT and run zero/few-shot prompts with Llama 8B on the provided `train/dev/test` splits for all three tasks, reproducing the macro-F1 scores in Table 3 to establish a working baseline.
  3. **Implement Persona Conditioning:** Using the provided annotator persona templates (Table 10), generate prompts for a held-out annotator profile, run inference with an LLM (e.g., gpt5-mini), and compute Cohen's κ between this persona-LLM and the *actual* annotations from that matched human, comparing it to the generic LLM performance from Experiment 2.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- **Open Question 1:** How do state-of-the-art models perform on hyperpartisan and PRCT detection in European languages beyond Spanish, Italian, and Portuguese?
  - **Basis in paper:** [explicit] "Future work will (i) enlarge language coverage to other European languages"
  - **Why unresolved:** The current dataset is limited to three Romance languages; performance on other European languages (e.g., Germanic, Slavic) with different linguistic and political landscapes is unknown.
  - **What evidence would resolve it:** Extend PARTISANLENS to additional European languages (e.g., German, French, Polish) and benchmark LLMs and fine-tuned models to compare cross-lingual generalization and language-specific challenges.
- **Open Question 2:** What is the impact of incorporating full article context (beyond headlines) on the detection of hyperpartisan, PRCT, and stance in immigration narratives?
  - **Basis in paper:** [explicit] "Future work will (ii) add document-level context to enable discourse-aware models"
  - **Why unresolved:** The dataset consists only of headlines due to copyright constraints; headlines lack broader argumentative structure and contextual cues which may be crucial for stance and conspiratorial framing.
  - **What evidence would resolve it:** Collect full articles corresponding to the headlines, extend annotations to document level, and evaluate models with and without contextual snippets to measure impact on each task.
- **Open Question 3:** Why does persona conditioning in LLMs consistently improve PRCT detection across languages but yields mixed or negative results for hyperpartisan and stance classification?
  - **Basis in paper:** [inferred] Section 6.3 shows persona conditioning (especially with gpt5-mini) increases Cohen's κ for PRCT in all languages but does not similarly benefit hyperpartisan (mixed) or stance (largely unchanged).
  - **Why unresolved:** The paper does not explain the underlying reasons; it may relate to PRCT's reliance on ideologically charged content that aligns with persona profiles, whereas hyperpartisan is more stylistic and stance requires nuanced pragmatic cues less influenced by socio-economic profiles.
  - **What evidence would resolve it:** Controlled experiments decomposing persona components (e.g., isolating ideological vs demographic traits) and analyzing model attention patterns to understand which aspects of the persona prompt drive improvements for PRCT versus other tasks.
- **Open Question 4:** How do cultural and linguistic nuances (e.g., sarcasm, metaphor, language-specific connotations) affect the reliability of LLM-based annotation for hyperpartisan and PRCT detection?
  - **Basis in paper:** [inferred] Error analysis (Section 6.4) highlights failures in sarcasm, metaphor, and cross-language connotation (e.g., Portuguese "Imigrantes" ambiguity, "invading" connotations).
  - **Why unresolved:** LLMs showed rigid interpretations and missed contextual meanings, suggesting limited cultural sensitivity; the paper cautions that local norms may lead to mischaracterization.
  - **What evidence would resolve it:** Develop culturally informed benchmark tests (e.g., annotate culture-specific examples with local experts) and evaluate LLM agreement; integrate external cultural knowledge sources or fine-tune on region-specific data.

## Limitations
- Few-shot DPP selection details are unreported, risking irreproducibility.
- The `gpt5-mini` model is unspecified, blocking exact replication of persona experiments.
- Rationale generation prompts are missing, affecting rationale-based fine-tuning.
- Extreme class imbalance (e.g., PRCT in Portuguese, *n*=9) threatens evaluation reliability.
- Persona conditioning benefits are inconsistent across tasks and languages.

## Confidence
- **High:** PARTISANLENS is a novel multilingual dataset for hyperpartisan and conspiratorial immigration narratives.
- **Medium:** Reported macro-F1 scores are replicable under the described setup but are sensitive to few-shot set composition and model choice.
- **Medium:** Persona conditioning can improve LLM-human agreement for PRCT (all languages) and stance (Spanish/Italian) but fails for hyperpartisan.
- **Low:** Exact magnitude of persona gains due to reliance on the opaque `gpt5-mini`.
