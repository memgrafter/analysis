# Text Has Curvature

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2602.13418
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.13418
- **Reference count:** 40
- **Primary result:** Proves intrinsic curvature exists in natural text beyond embedding choices, introduces Texture—a word-level curvature primitive—and demonstrates significant gains in long-context pruning and retrieval without geometric training.

## Executive Summary
The paper tackles a fundamental question: does text possess intrinsic curvature independent of geometric embedding decisions? It introduces Texture, a novel, text-native curvature measure defined at the word level by reconciling left and right context beliefs via a Schr\\"odinger bridge midpoint. Through theoretical proofs and empirical validation, the authors establish that natural language exhibits non-flat two-sided inference, with Texture yielding signed curvature (positive for focus, negative for fan-out). Applied to long-context systems, curvature signals enable efficient context management: CURVPRUNE selects high-magnitude spans for compression, and CURVFLAG uses negative curvature mass to trigger retrieval. Evaluations on LongBench benchmarks show substantial improvements over strong baselines, positioning curvature as both a measurable property of text and a practical primitive for inference-time control.

## Method Summary
Texture operates on a frozen language model (e.g., distilroberta-base) to extract left and right context beliefs for each token, each represented as a distribution over candidate slot states. From these beliefs and token embeddings, a symmetric semantic cost and reversible Markov kernel are constructed, inducing a stationary distribution. A Schr\\"odinger bridge—an entropic optimal transport problem—is then solved to find the unique midpoint distribution that minimally distorts a neutral reference path while matching the endpoint beliefs. Curvature κ arises from the free-energy defect of this midpoint relative to the chord connecting the endpoints, normalized by the bridge energy. For downstream tasks, κ magnitudes guide token selection: CURVPRUNE greedily retains spans with high |κ| under a token budget, while CURVFLAG aggregates negative κ mass over a query to set a retrieval budget and augment the query with a high-curvature anchor. The entire pipeline requires no geometric training and is evaluated using Meta-Llama-3-8B-Instruct on LongBench QA and summarization tasks.

## Key Results
- **Existence:** Proves non-flat two-sided inference in natural text via holonomy and contextual evidence interaction (CEI) certificates; controls (suffix-swap, local-shuffle) fail to replicate the effect.
- **Definition:** Texture yields signed curvature κ from the Schr\\"odinger-bridge midpoint’s free-energy defect; positive κ indicates focus, negative indicates fan-out.
- **Utility:** CURVPRUNE boosts pruning (2WikiMQA F1: 13.1 vs. 3.4 BM25); CURVFLAG improves retrieval routing (HotpotQA F1: 29.1 vs. 21.4 FLARE) on LongBench.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
Natural text exhibits non-flat two-sided inference, detectable via holonomy and CEI without assuming a curvature definition. Holonomy captures order-sensitivity when expanding context around a token; CEI measures deviation from additive evidence combination (Product-of-Experts). Both derive from the two-sided posterior field µ(L,R). Evidence: theoretical equivalence to additive separability (Theorems C.1/C.2); empirical separation of real text from coherence-destroying controls. Break condition: if holonomy and CEI distributions do not separate real from control, the existence claim fails—likely due to an insensitive belief extractor or insufficient context radii.

### Mechanism 2
Texture defines signed curvature from the Schr\\"odinger-bridge midpoint’s free-energy defect. Given left/right beliefs (µL, µR) and a reversible neutral kernel (π, K), the bridge midpoint µmid is the KL-closest reconciliation. Curvature sign is read from µmid’s position relative to the chord in free-energy space Φ=KL(·∥π): positive if µmid lies below (focus), negative if above (fan-out). Normalization uses bridge energy D². Evidence: detailed construction in Section 5 (Eqs. 10–13, Theorem D.2); scaling properties. Break condition: if µmid collapses to an endpoint or the free-energy gap sign reverses systematically, the sign interpretation falters.

### Mechanism 3
Curvature magnitude correlates with task-relevant context importance, enabling geometry-free inference-time control. High |κ| spans indicate strong two-sided evidence reconciliation (focus) or branching (fan-out). CURVPRUNE allocates token budget to high-|κ| spans; CURVFLAG triggers retrieval when negative κ mass exceeds a threshold. Evidence: substantial gains on LongBench (Tables 1–2) vs. heuristics like BM25 and FLARE. Break condition: if curvature-guided selection underperforms simple heuristics across tasks, or gains vanish with stronger baselines, the utility assumption fails.

## Foundational Learning
- **Kullback–Leibler divergence:** Quantifies deviation from a reference distribution; used to define free-energy Φ and bridge energy D².  
  Quick check: *If KL(P∥Q)=0 for distributions P and Q on the same support, what must be true?* → P and Q must be equal almost everywhere.
- **Schr\\"odinger bridge (entropic optimal transport):** Provides a symmetric midpoint reconciliation of boundary distributions by minimizing KL to a reference path under marginal constraints, ensuring reversibility and a unique solution.  
  Quick check: *What role does the reference path measure R play in minₚ KL(P∥R)?* → It regularizes the bridge toward a neutral dynamics, preventing collapse to the endpoints.
- **Discrete holonomy / path independence:** In a flat geometry, incremental context updates commute (holonomy Ω=0); non-zero Ω certifies intrinsic left-right interaction beyond additive models.  
  Quick check: *Why does a non-zero unit-square holonomy Ω(L,R) rule out u(L,R)=α(L)+β(R)?* → Additive separable forms yield zero curvature and zero holonomy; non-zero Ω implies curvature and non-additivity.

## Architecture Onboarding
### Component Map
Belief extractor (frozen LM) -> Kernel builder (symmetric cost, Gibbs affinity, reversible kernel K, stationary π) -> Bridge solver (Sinkhorn/IPFP to find midpoint µmid via scaling factors a,b) -> Curvature readout (compute ∆Φ, D², κ) -> Controllers (CURVPRUNE, CURVFLAG).

### Critical Path
Belief extraction provides left/right beliefs µL,µR; kernel builder constructs cost matrix and K; bridge solver computes µmid; curvature readout derives κ from free-energy gap and bridge energy; controllers apply κ to select context or trigger retrieval. The bridge step depends critically on the kernel.

### Design Tradeoffs
- **Support size k:** Larger k improves belief accuracy but increases Sinkhorn cost; typical k∈[10,50].
- **Kernel temperature ε:** Smaller ε sharpens affinities but risks numerical instability; ε≈0.1–0.5 used.
- **Guard ϵ₀:** Prevents division by near-zero D² (set ~10⁻⁶); may mask near-flat cases.
- **One-sided belief sources:** Using the same model for µL and µR introduces correlation; orthogonal encoders could improve independence.

### Failure Signatures
- **Numerical:** Sinkhorn fails to converge (check matrix condition number); κ becomes NaN or ±∞ (inspect D², ϵ₀).
- **Interpretation:** High holonomy but near-zero κ—bridge midpoint insensitive; reconsider kernel cost.
- **Utility:** CURVPRUNE selects irrelevant spans (low F1 gain); verify |κ| correlates with answer spans via oracle.
- **Controls:** Swap/shuffle conditions fail to reduce CEI/holonomy—belief extractor may be one-sided insensitive.

### First 3 Experiments
1. **Existence replication:** Compute holonomy magnitude h and CEI on WikiText-2/OpenWebText using distilroberta-base; verify real text > swap/shuffle controls (Figure 2).
2. **Stability sweep:** Vary support k={10,20,50,100}, temperature ε∈{0.05,0.1,0.5}, context radii; report rank correlation of κ across configurations.
3. **Utility validation:** Run CURVPRUNE (budget B=2048) and CURVFLAG (cost T=2048) on HotpotQA and 2WikiMQA; compare to BM25/FLARE baselines (Table 1–2).

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
1. **Connection to continuous Ricci curvature:** How does discrete Texture curvature relate to continuous Ricci-type measures? Unresolved; no formal link via heat-kernel limits yet. Evidence needed: derive limit, validate on synthetic curved manifolds.
2. **Learning task-aware neutral kernels:** Can the hand-crafted symmetric kernel be adapted to tasks? Unresolved; task-specific learning could improve discriminativity. Evidence: parameterize kernel, train on labeled data where curvature utility is known.
3. **Curvature for controllable generation and uncertainty:** Can κ steer generation or calibrate confidence? Unresolved; only pruning and retrieval explored. Evidence: integrate κ into decoding, measure controllability and calibration metrics.
4. **Dependency on belief extractor:** Is curvature stable across different LMs? Unresolved; only distilroberta-base used. Evidence: compute κ with diverse LLMs (GPT-4, Llama 3) and compare distributions/utility.
5. **Impact of asymmetric kernel costs:** Does enforcing symmetry distort curvature in some domains? Unresolved; symmetry assumed neutral. Evidence: introduce asymmetric costs (directed attention, predicate-argument), measure κ changes and performance.

## Limitations
- **Reliability on extractor/kernel:** Curvature measurement depends on the frozen belief extractor and hand-crafted symmetric kernel; limited validation across models or cost functions.
- **Generalizability of utility:** Demonstrated only on specific long-context QA/summarization tasks with Llama-3-8B; transfer to other domains, tasks, or stronger baselines is unproven.
- **Scalability and stability:** Schr\\"odinger bridge via Sinkhorn may become costly or unstable for large contexts; guard ϵ₀ can obscure near-flat cases where κ becomes noisy.
