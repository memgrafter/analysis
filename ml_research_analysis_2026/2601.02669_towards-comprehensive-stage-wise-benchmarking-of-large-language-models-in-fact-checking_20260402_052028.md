# Towards Comprehensive Stage-wise Benchmarking of Large Language Models in Fact-Checking

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2601.02669
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.02669
- **Reference count:** 40
- **Primary result:** FactArena enables end-to-end benchmarking of LLMs across claim extraction, evidence retrieval, and verification, revealing significant gaps between static verification accuracy and holistic competence.

## Executive Summary
Fact-checking is a multi-stage process, yet current LLM evaluations focus almost exclusively on final verdict accuracy. This paper introduces FactArena, an automated arena framework that benchmarks LLMs across the complete pipeline: claim extraction, evidence retrieval, and verification. It employs stage-wise pairwise battles judged by a diverse panel following consolidated guidelines, and an adaptive claim‑evolution module that generates semantically harder claims from ones all models answer correctly. Evaluating 16 LLMs yields stable Elo‑based rankings with GPT‑o3 and DeepSeek‑R1 at the top. Results show major discrepancies between static verification performance and full pipeline competence; evolved claims reduce average model accuracy from 100 % to ~68 %, exposing robustness limitations. The guideline‑driven judging achieves high inter‑judge agreement (up to 92.6 %) and strong alignment with human evaluators (0.75), demonstrating a more reliable and nuanced assessment paradigm.

## Method Summary
FactArena begins with a seed set of complex claims (400 from HOVER and FEVEROUS). Each target LLM processes a claim through three stages: (1) decompose into sub‑claims, (2) retrieve evidence via a web‑search tool, (3) produce a justification and verdict. Model outputs per stage are paired in “battles” and evaluated by a panel of four judge LLMs using stage‑specific prompts. For claim extraction, guidelines are iteratively consolidated from all sub‑claims; for retrieval, judges reference a curated Wikipedia evidence set; for verification, they assess helpfulness, informativeness, soundness, and readability. Pairwise outcomes update Elo and Bradley‑Terry rankings. If all models correctly verify a claim, an evolver agent (GPT‑o4 mini) first reverses the claim’s semantics; if still too easy, it iteratively refines the claim into a harder variant by analyzing judge critiques. New claims re‑enter the pipeline, creating an adaptive, self‑hardening benchmark.

## Key Results
- **Stable Elo rankings:** GPT‑o3 and DeepSeek‑R1 achieve top positions; DeepSeek‑R1 gains relative advantage on harder evolved claims.
- **Claim evolution increases difficulty:** Average model accuracy drops from 100 % on initial claims to ~68 % on evolved claims, successfully probing robustness boundaries.
- **Guideline‑driven judgment reliability:** Inter‑judge agreement reaches 92.6 % and human alignment hits 0.75, significantly improving over unguided setups.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

**Mechanism 1: Stage‑wise evaluation uncovers hidden weaknesses**  
By evaluating claim extraction, evidence retrieval, and verification separately, FactArena provides a comprehensive assessment. The core assumption is that fact‑checking ability is not monolithic; early‑stage failures (e.g., poor claim decomposition) can invalidate even correct verification. Evidence: Models like Gemini 2.5 Pro show rank shifts across stages, confirming stage‑specific strengths/weaknesses that end‑to‑end metrics miss.

**Mechanism 2: Guideline‑driven arena judgment ensures consistency**  
A diverse panel of LLM judges compares pairwise outputs using consolidated stage‑specific guidelines, with identities and order blinded. Aggregation via Elo/Bradley‑Terry yields robust rankings. The core assumption is that collective judgment from heterogeneous models, anchored by clear criteria, mitigates individual biases and closely mimics human experts. Evidence: Inter‑judge agreement rises to 92.6 % with guidelines, and human agreement reaches 0.75.

**Mechanism 3: Adaptive claim evolution probes factual robustness**  
For claims all models verify correctly, the system first reverses semantics and then iteratively generates harder variants by analyzing judge critiques of model weaknesses. This closed‑loop evolution targets unknown failure modes and avoids static benchmark saturation. Evidence: Average accuracy falls from 100 % to ~68 % after evolution, and Elo rankings shift notably (e.g., DeepSeek‑R1 improves), demonstrating increased discrimination.

## Foundational Learning
- **Arena‑style pairwise evaluation:** Avoids absolute ground‑truth labels by comparing outputs relatively. *Why needed:* Fact‑checking stages often lack a single correct answer; pairwise comparisons capture subtle quality differences. *Quick check:* What are the advantages of pairwise comparisons over direct scoring for generative model outputs?
- **Stage‑wise task decomposition:** Isolates claim extraction, evidence retrieval, and verification to diagnose failure points. *Why needed:* End‑to‑end metrics hide which stage limits overall performance. *Quick check:* Why might a model excel at verification but struggles with evidence retrieval? What does this imply for system design?
- **Adaptive data synthesis via model feedback:** Generates harder claims based on model performance to counter static benchmark leakage. *Why needed:* Fixed test sets become saturated; adaptive evolution targets current weaknesses. *Quick check:* How does the evolver agent use judge analyses to decide how to make a claim more challenging?

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component map**  
Seed claims → Pipeline Executor (per target LLM) → Stage outputs (sub‑claims, evidence, verdicts) → Judge Panel (pairwise stage comparisons) → Elo Ranking Engine → Evolution Engine (reversal + iterative hardening) → new claims. Supporting components: Guideline Consolidation Module (for extraction), Tool Interface (Google Search), Data Store.

**Critical path**  
1. Load 400 seed claims.  
2. For each claim and each target LLM: run claim extraction, evidence retrieval, verification; store outputs.  
3. For each stage, randomly pair model outputs; assign a judge (different from models) to compare using stage‑specific prompts; record win/loss/tie.  
4. Aggregate all pairwise judgments to update Elo ratings.  
5. Identify “too easy” claims (all models correct). If found, trigger evolution: reverse semantics → re‑evaluate; if still too easy, iterate up to 3 rounds using evolver agent to produce harder claims.  
6. Add evolved claims to pool and repeat steps 2‑4.  
7. After sufficient battles (~104 per model pair), output final rankings.

**Design tradeoffs**  
- *Judge number vs. cost:* More judges improve reliability but increase API calls; paper uses 4 as a practical trade‑off.  
- *Automation vs. correctness:* Fully automated judging risks LLM biases; guidelines mitigate but do not eliminate errors.  
- *Evolution rounds vs. noise:* Additional rounds yield harder claims but may introduce unnatural complexity; diminishing returns after 2–3 rounds.  
- *Tool reliance:* Dependency on Google Search; API changes could affect reproducibility.

**Failure signatures**  
- Low inter‑judge agreement (< 85%) despite guidelines → judges ignoring criteria or ambiguous guidelines.  
- No accuracy drop after evolution → evolution not increasing difficulty or models overly robust.  
- High Elo variance across random seeds → insufficient battle counts.  
- Judge bias toward specific model families → mitigate with diverse panel and blind assignment.

**First 3 experiments**  
1. **Baseline replication:** Run FactArena on the 16 models with 400 original claims; verify top‑3 rankings (GPT‑o3, DeepSeek‑R1, GPT‑o4 mini) and Elo stability.  
2. **Guideline ablation:** Disable guideline consolidation (use raw judge prompts); compare inter‑judge consistency and human alignment to the full system.  
3. **Evolution impact:** Compare accuracy on original, reversed, and evolved claims; track Elo changes, expecting DeepSeek‑R1 to gain on harder claims while others decline.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- How can FactArena be extended to evaluate LLMs on multimodal fact‑checking tasks involving images or videos?  
- What is the optimal number and composition of judge agents to balance evaluation quality, cost, and bias?  
- Can uncertainty‑aware evaluation be incorporated into the arena‑style judgment to improve reliability and diagnostics?  
- How effective is the arena‑driven claim evolution at uncovering robustness limitations that static benchmarks miss?

## Limitations
- The framework currently focuses only on textual fact‑checking; multimodal extensions remain unexplored.  
- Judge panel selection criteria and diversity parameters are underspecified, limiting reproducibility and generalizability.  
- The semantic validity and real‑world applicability of automatically evolved claims are not externally validated, raising concerns about overfitting to model weaknesses.

## Next Checks
1. **Reproduce tool‑augmented retrieval:** Implement the exact Google Search interface (top‑K snippets) and test whether iterative querying improves retrieval scores versus a single‑call baseline.
2. **Ablate judge panel:** Replace the default 4‑judge panel with random subsets of other models and measure Elo stability and human alignment variance to validate the “diverse panel” assumption.
3. **Cross‑dataset evolution:** Run claim evolution on a held‑out fact‑checking dataset (e.g., fresh FEVER samples) and verify that the accuracy drop persists, confirming evolution generalizes beyond seed claims.
