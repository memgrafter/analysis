# Structural-Ambiguity-Aware Translation from Natural Language to Signal Temporal Logic

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2603.28426  
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.28426  
- **Reference count:** 25  
- **Primary result:** Proposes an ambiguity-preserving pipeline for NL-to-STL translation that retains multiple syntactically valid interpretations, evaluated on a set of navigation task descriptions.

## Executive Summary
This paper tackles structural ambiguity in natural-language to Signal Temporal Logic (STL) translation, where modifier attachment and scope yield multiple valid STL formulas. Existing one-best methods cannot represent such ambiguities. The proposed symbolic pipeline uses three stages: n-best CCG parsing to retain multiple syntactic analyses; STL-oriented template-based semantic composition to build intermediate meaning representations; and canonicalization with score aggregation to produce a deduplicated set of candidate STL formulas with plausibility scores. Evaluation on 12 representative navigation task descriptions shows the method generates exactly the reference interpretations for genuinely ambiguous inputs (2–5 candidates) and collapses unambiguous inputs to a single formula. A ChatGPT baseline on the five ambiguous sentences recovered 11 of 14 reference formulas, failing due to temporal anchoring errors (2 cases) and structurally unsupported readings (1 case). The symbolic pipeline systematically preserves all linguistically valid interpretations while merging equivalent derivations, providing a compact, interpretable candidate set for downstream selection.

## Method Summary
The translation pipeline comprises three stages. First, a probabilistic CCG parser (depccg) generates up to 40 highest-scoring parse trees per sentence, retaining structural alternatives such as modifier attachment and logical scope. Second, each parse tree is fed to a semantic composer (based on ccg2lambda) that applies a fixed library of lexical-semantic templates (e.g., "reach X" maps to λI.F(I,X), "while" maps to λQ.λP.(P ∧ Q)) to produce intermediate meaning representations. Third, these representations are converted to well-formed STL formulas, canonicalized to a normal form (handling associativity, commutativity, interval standardization), and scores from all derivations leading to the same formula are summed to yield a final plausibility score. The output is a deduplicated set of STL candidates. The method assumes explicit temporal expressions in the input and relies on a limited template set tailored to navigation tasks.

## Key Results
- For genuinely ambiguous inputs (sentences S8–S12), the pipeline generates exactly the reference set of interpretations (2–5 candidates), preserving all linguistically valid readings.
- Unambiguous inputs (S1–S7) collapse to a single formula matching the reference, demonstrating no spurious branching.
- Compared to a ChatGPT baseline on the five ambiguous sentences, the pipeline recovers all 14 reference formulas, whereas ChatGPT misses 3 due to temporal anchoring errors and structurally unsupported extra readings.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1
- **Claim:** Retaining multiple high-scoring CCG parse trees preserves linguistically valid structural ambiguities (modifier attachment, scope) that would otherwise be collapsed by a one-best parser.
- **Mechanism:** A probabilistic CCG parser (depccg) generates an n-best list of syntactic analyses. Each parse tree encodes a different attachment or grouping of phrases (e.g., where a "while avoiding A" clause attaches). These distinct trees are passed forward, allowing different scope interpretations to survive to later stages.
- **Core assumption:** Structural ambiguity in the input sentence corresponds to multiple plausible syntactic derivations under the CCG formalism.
- **Evidence anchors:** [abstract] "retain multiple plausible syntactic analyses instead of forcing a single interpretation at the parsing stage."; [section IV.B] "retains multiple high-scoring parse trees... both interpretations can be preserved and passed to the semantic composition stage."; [corpus] Corpus shows related work on NL-to-STL translation (e.g., TLINet, LLM-based approaches) but none explicitly preserve n-best parses for ambiguity; evidence for this specific mechanism is from the paper's own design and evaluation, not external validation.
- **Break condition:** The parser fails to generate multiple syntactically distinct trees for a genuinely ambiguous input (e.g., due to model limitations or lack of training coverage for the ambiguity pattern).

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim:** Task-specific lexical-semantic templates composed along each parse tree convert syntactic structure into intermediate STL-oriented meaning representations, mapping linguistic constituents to temporal logic operators while preserving scope differences.
- **Mechanism:** Each lexical item/phrase is assigned a fixed template (e.g., "reach X" → λI.F(I, X); "while" → λQ.λP.(P ∧ Q)). These are combined via λ-calculus reduction following the CCG derivation. Different parse trees yield different composition orders, producing intermediate representations with distinct logical groupings (e.g., whether a modifier combines with a disjunct or the entire disjunction).
- **Core assumption:** A small, fixed set of templates can compositionally generate all required STL constructs for the target domain (navigation with reach/avoid, and/or/while, time bounds).
- **Evidence anchors:** [abstract] "STL-oriented template-based semantic composition to build intermediate meaning representations."; [section IV.C] "semantic composition assigns a lexical-semantic template... different combinatory structures lead to different elements of M(d)."; [corpus] Corpus includes LLM-based NL2STL systems (e.g., DeepSTL, NL2STL) that use different mechanisms (end-to-end neural or prompt-based); no direct evidence for template-based composition's effectiveness relative to alternatives in preserving ambiguity.
- **Break condition:** The input uses linguistic constructions not covered by the template set (e.g., implicit temporal references, novel task predicates), causing composition to fail or produce invalid intermediate forms.

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim:** Canonicalization of intermediate representations into a normal form, followed by score aggregation, deduplicates semantically equivalent STL formulas while preserving a plausibility score that reflects support across multiple derivations.
- **Mechanism:** Each intermediate representation is converted to a canonical STL formula (e.g., reordering commutative operands, standardizing interval notation). Formulas derived from different parse trees but logically equivalent under STL semantics are merged. Their parser scores are summed to produce a final plausibility score for the unique formula.
- **Core assumption:** The canonicalization rules correctly identify all semantically equivalent STL formulas arising from different derivations (i.e., no false negatives in deduplication).
- **Evidence anchors:** [abstract] "canonicalization with score aggregation to produce a deduplicated set of candidate STL formulas with plausibility scores."; [section IV.D] "prevents the final candidate set from being artificially inflated by duplicate formulas... scores are aggregated at the formula level."; [corpus] Corpus contains work on STL inference and conformance (e.g., Conformalized STL Inference) but not on canonicalization for ambiguity preservation; evidence is internal to the paper's implementation.
- **Break condition:** Canonicalization either fails to merge equivalent formulas (leading to redundant candidates) or incorrectly merges distinct formulas (collapsing genuine ambiguity).

## Foundational Learning
### Combinatory Categorial Grammar (CCG)
**Why needed here:** CCG provides a fine-grained, lexicalized syntax formalism where small changes in parse structure (e.g., argument attachment) yield distinct derivation trees. This is the mechanism for capturing structural ambiguity at the syntactic level.  
**Quick check question:** How would the CCG parse for "avoid A while reaching B" differ from "reach B while avoiding A" in terms of operator scope, and why would that lead to different STL formulas?

### Signal Temporal Logic (STL) semantics
**Why needed here:** To understand what constitutes a valid STL formula, how temporal operators (F_I, G_I, U_I) and logical connectives (∧, ∨) compose, and crucially how scope differences (e.g., modifier attachment) change the formula's truth conditions over trajectories.  
**Quick check question:** For the ambiguous sentence "Reach B or reach C while avoiding A", why does attaching "while avoiding A" to "reach C" versus the whole disjunction produce formulas with different robustness intervals for the avoidance condition?

### Lambda calculus for semantic composition
**Why needed here:** The templates are expressed as λ-expressions (e.g., λQ.λP.(P ∧ Q)). Composition follows the CCG derivation via β-reduction. This formalizes how meaning builds from parts, preserving scope differences from syntax.  
**Quick check question:** If the template for "while" is λQ.λP.(P ∧ Q) and for "or" is λQ.λP.(P ∨ Q), how does the order of application in a derivation determine whether the avoidance condition scopes over one disjunct or both?

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component map:** Input -> CCG Parser (depccg) -> Composer (ccg2lambda with Template Library) -> Converter & Canonicalizer -> Score Aggregator -> Output.  
**Critical path:** Parsing → Composition → Canonicalization/Aggregation.  
**Design tradeoffs:**  
- **n-best size (N):** Larger N increases recall of valid parses but raises computational cost and may include spurious parses with low scores. Paper uses N=40.  
- **Template coverage vs. overgeneration:** A richer template set handles more constructions but risks generating invalid or nonsensical intermediate forms for out-of-domain inputs.  
- **Canonicalization precision/recall:** Aggressive deduplication might collapse genuinely distinct formulas (false positives); permissive deduplication leaves redundant candidates.  
**Failure signatures:**  
- **No ambiguity preserved:** Output set has |C(d)|=1 for clearly ambiguous sentences (e.g., S8–S12). Likely cause: n-best list lacks syntactically distinct trees or canonicalization over-merges.  
- **Duplicate candidates:** Multiple formulas in C(d) that are logically equivalent but not canonicalized. Likely cause: incomplete canonicalization rules (e.g., missing commutativity for ∧/∨ on same-level operands).  
- **Invalid STL formulas:** Output contains formulas with malformed intervals, unmatched parentheses, or unsupported operators. Likely cause: template miscomposition or converter failure on intermediate forms.  
- **Temporal anchoring errors:** Avoidance or timing condition applies to wrong subformula (as seen in ChatGPT baseline). Likely cause: parse tree incorrectly scopes the modifier, and no downstream correction.  
**First 3 experiments:**  
1. **Ambiguity preservation test:** Run the pipeline on S8 ("Within 10 seconds, reach B or reach C while avoiding A"). Verify |C(d)|=2 and formulas match φ₁ and φ₂ from Eq. (5)-(6). Check that one has G[0,10]¬ϕA scoped only under F[0,10]ϕC, the other under the whole disjunction.  
2. **Unambiguous collapse test:** Run on S1 ("Reach B within 10 seconds"). Verify |C(d)|=1 and formula is F[0,10]ϕB. Ensures no spurious branching from n-best parsing.  
3. **Canonicalization test:** Create two different parse trees that should yield the same STL (e.g., due to conjunction associativity). Verify they merge into one candidate with summed score.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- **Question 1:** How can the pipeline be extended to generate STL formulas when temporal constraints are implicit or omitted in the input sentence?  
  *Basis:* Section VI identifies that the current formulation assumes explicit temporal information and is not yet handled in a principled manner.  
  *Why unresolved:* Missing temporal bounds lead to incomplete intermediate representations and conversion failures.  
  *Evidence needed:* A formal mechanism (e.g., default intervals, context-aware inference) that reliably infers missing temporal bounds, evaluated on implicit-timing instructions.

- **Question 2:** To what extent can the template library be expanded to capture richer linguistic phenomena (e.g., implicit temporal operators, context-dependent expressions) while preserving the ambiguity-preserving property?  
  *Basis:* Section VI notes the current template set covers only a limited class of task expressions and ambiguity patterns.  
  *Why unresolved:* Unknown constructions may lead to incomplete derivations or missed ambiguities; no systematic methodology for extension.  
  *Evidence needed:* A method for adding new templates, tested on diverse natural-language task descriptions including comparatives, conditionals, anaphora, with coverage and correctness measurements.

- **Question 3:** How does the quality of the underlying CCG parser (coverage, accuracy) affect the completeness and correctness of the generated STL candidate set?  
  *Basis:* Section VI states that parsing errors or coverage limitations may directly affect candidate quality.  
  *Why unresolved:* The pipeline inherits the parser's biases and blind spots; unknown or misparsed structures may omit valid interpretations or introduce spurious ones.  
  *Evidence needed:* An error analysis correlating parser confidence/parse tree diversity with STL candidate correctness, possibly using multiple parsers or a manually annotated ambiguity gold standard.

- **Question 4:** Why do neural language models (e.g., ChatGPT) fail to enumerate all structurally licensed STL readings for ambiguous sentences, and what does this imply for the necessity of symbolic ambiguity-preserving pipelines?  
  *Basis:* Section V-D shows ChatGPT recovered only 11/14 reference interpretations, failing due to temporal anchoring errors and structurally unsupported extra readings.  
  *Why unresolved:* The exact reasons for LLM failures (e.g., training data biases, lack of explicit structural representation) are not analyzed; nor is the boundary of cases where symbolic methods are strictly superior.  
  *Evidence needed:* A controlled study varying prompt strategies, model sizes, and ambiguity patterns, combined with analysis of whether LLM outputs align with parse-tree-derived candidate sets.

- **Question 5:** How well does the ambiguity-preserving approach generalize beyond the navigation domain to other STL-specified tasks (e.g., robotic assembly, multi-agent coordination)?  
  *Basis:* The paper restricts evaluation to navigation/path-planning but notes the formulation itself is not limited.  
  *Why unresolved:* Templates are tailored to reach/avoid predicates and simple temporal modifiers; other domains may require new atomic propositions and operators, potentially introducing new ambiguity patterns.  
  *Evidence needed:* A portability study applying the pipeline (with adapted templates) to task descriptions from a different domain, measuring coverage of valid STL interpretations and need for template extension.

## Limitations
- Tiny evaluation set (12 sentences) risks overfitting and may miss ambiguity patterns.
- Fixed templates may not cover complex or out-of-domain linguistic constructions.
- Canonicalization rules are underspecified; could over-/under-merge equivalent formulas.

## Confidence
- Ambiguity preservation: Medium
- Deduplication & scoring: Medium
- LLM superiority: Low

## Next Checks
1. Scale evaluation to 50-100 diverse ambiguous sentences; measure agreement on plausible interpretations and collapse rate for unambiguous inputs.

3. Systematically test canonicalization on equivalence classes (e.g., associativity, commutativity) to verify merging correctness and non-merging of distinct formulas.
