# Towards a Diagnostic and Predictive Evaluation Methodology for Sequence Labeling Tasks
## Quick Facts
* **arXiv ID:** 2602.12759
* **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.12759
* **Reference count:** 0
* **Primary result:** A handcrafted benchmark (BLAS) for sequence labeling provides diagnostic insights, model selection guidance, and strong predictive correlation (median Pearson r=0.85) with external performance.

## Executive Summary
The paper introduces a novel evaluation methodology for sequence labeling tasks that replaces large, scraped test sets with a small, carefully crafted benchmark covering a comprehensive set of span attributes (e.g., length, casing, position). Demonstrated on the task of anglicism identification in Spanish using the BLAS benchmark, the approach uncovers systematic model weaknesses such as sensitivity to quotation marks or capitalization. It also yields actionable model selection guidance and exhibits strong predictive power: performance scores on BLAS attributes correlate highly with results on external datasets (median Pearson r=0.85). The study reveals that models excelling on standard in-distribution benchmarks (e.g., COALAS) may fail dramatically on BLAS, while few-shot large language models (Llama3) show greater robustness.

## Method Summary
The core of the methodology is the construction of a handcrafted benchmark, BLAS, which exhaustively covers a predetermined set of span attributes. The authors first identify relevant attributes that could influence model behavior (e.g., token length, capitalization, position within sentence, surrounding punctuation). They then manually select or generate examples that span the full range of attribute values, ensuring balanced representation. To evaluate a model, predictions are run on BLAS and performance metrics (e.g., F1) are computed both overall and per attribute value. This fine-grained breakdown diagnoses where the model struggles. Finally, the per-attribute scores are correlated with the model’s performance on multiple external datasets to assess whether BLAS can predict generalization. The entire process replaces a single aggregate score on a large, noisy test set with a suite of controlled, interpretable evaluations.

## Key Results
- **Diagnostic insights:** BLAS identifies systematic failures such as poor recall on spans surrounded by quotation marks or on lowercased tokens.
- **Predictive power:** BLAS attribute-level scores correlate highly with true performance on external datasets (median Pearson r=0.85).
- **Robustness contrast:** Supervised models that achieve high recall on in-distribution benchmarks (COALAS, ~78%) collapse on BLAS (6-36% recall), whereas few-shot LLMs (Llama3) maintain more stable performance across both.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The handcrafted, attribute-centric design forces the benchmark to isolate specific linguistic phenomena that often cause errors. Because each attribute is systematically varied, any performance drop on a particular attribute value directly points to a model weakness, enabling precise diagnosis. Moreover, the attribute-level performance serves as a multi-dimensional fingerprint of a model’s capabilities; if these fingerprints are similar between BLAS and external data, performance will correlate. The high reported correlation suggests that the chosen attributes capture fundamental generalization factors across datasets, making BLAS a reliable predictor despite its small size.

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component map**: Attribute Definition -> Handcrafted Benchmark Construction -> Model Inference -> Attribute-wise Scoring -> Diagnostic Analysis -> Correlation with External Data

**Critical path**: 1) Define span attributes relevant to the task. 2) Curate a small set of examples exhaustively covering attribute combinations (BLAS). 3) Run candidate models on BLAS to obtain predictions. 4) Compute overall and per-attribute performance metrics. 5) Diagnose weaknesses by inspecting attribute-level scores. 6) Gather external datasets for the same task, evaluate models on them, and compute Pearson correlation between BLAS attribute scores and external performance.

**Design tradeoffs**:
- Handcrafted vs. automatic curation: Manual construction provides control and balance but requires expert effort and may not scale to many tasks.
- Attribute exhaustiveness vs. benchmark size: Covering all combinations of many attributes can explode the number of examples; the authors limited attributes to keep BLAS small (~few hundred examples).
- Task specificity vs. generalizability: BLAS is tailored to anglicism detection; while the methodology is generic, the attribute set may need adaptation for other tasks.
- Diagnostic depth vs. predictive simplicity: More attributes yield finer diagnosis but risk overfitting the correlation; the study selected a moderate set.

**Failure signatures**:
- Sharp performance drops on specific attribute values (e.g., short spans, lowercased tokens, quotes) indicate model brittleness.
- Inconsistency between BLAS and standard benchmark rankings suggests overfitting to in-distribution data.
- Low correlation between BLAS attribute scores and external performance signals that the chosen attributes do not capture the true generalization factors for that task.

**First experiments**:
1. **Baseline evaluation**: Run several supervised models (e.g., BERT-based) and LLMs (Llama3) on BLAS; compute overall F1 and per-attribute scores to observe immediate patterns.
2. **Predictive validation**: Collect at least three external Spanish anglicism datasets, evaluate the same models, and calculate Pearson correlation between each BLAS attribute score and external F1 to replicate the reported median r=0.85.
3. **Attribute ablation**: Remove one attribute at a time from BLAS (or collapse its values) and re-compute correlation with external performance; identify which attribute(s) most strongly drive the predictive signal.

## Limitations
- **Generalizability**: The study is confined to anglicism identification in Spanish; it remains unclear whether the findings extend to other sequence labeling tasks and languages.
- **Benchmark bias**: BLAS, being handcrafted, may contain inadvertent biases specific to the task or the annotators’ intuitions.
- **Predictive robustness**: The reported high correlation lacks transparency on the number and diversity of external datasets used; the statistical robustness of the median Pearson r=0.85 is therefore uncertain.

## Confidence
- Diagnostic identification of systematic failures: **Medium-High**
- Predictive correlation with external performance: **Medium**
- Robustness differences (LLMs vs. supervised models): **Medium**

## Next Checks
1. **Cross-task generalization**: Apply the methodology to at least three other sequence labeling tasks (e.g., NER, chunking) across multiple languages, measuring both diagnostic and predictive performance.
2. **Attribute ablation**: Systematically vary the set of span attributes in BLAS to isolate which ones drive the external performance correlation and test the stability of the predictive signal.
3. **Expanded model evaluation**: Test a broader spectrum of models, including recent large LLMs (e.g., GPT-4, Claude) and diverse supervised architectures, to verify the reported robustness hierarchy.
