# Self-Supervised JEPA-based World Models for LiDAR Occupancy Completion and Forecasting

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2602.12540
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.12540
- Reference count: 40
- Primary result: AD-LiST-JEPA self-supervised pretraining improves LiDAR occupancy completion and forecasting with ~0.85% absolute IoU gain on Waymo.

## Executive Summary
The paper introduces AD-LiST-JEPA, a self-supervised framework that learns world models from multi-frame LiDAR data without labels using a joint-embedding predictive architecture (JEPA). It employs a group BEV-guided masking strategy to separate ego motion from scene dynamics. Pretrained encoders are evaluated on downstream occupancy completion and forecasting (OCF) tasks, showing consistent improvements over training from scratch on the Waymo dataset, with the best variant (SIGReg regularization) achieving IoU_full of 39.41% and IoU_close of 43.86%.

## Method Summary
AD-LiST-JEPA utilizes a JEPA-based approach to learn latent representations by predicting future embeddings from past ones. The framework processes multi-frame LiDAR sequences, applying a group BEV-guided masking that masks regions of interest in bird's-eye view to isolate dynamic scene elements from ego motion. The pretrained encoder is then transferred to an OCF model which completes and forecasts occupancy grids. The entire pretraining is self-supervised, requiring no manual labels.

## Key Results
- JEPA-pretrained encoders improve OCF performance compared to training from scratch.
- Best variant (SIGReg) yields IoU_full=39.41% and IoU_close=43.86% on Waymo.
- Absolute gains approximately 0.85% (full) and 0.99% (close) over baseline.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The JEPA objective forces the model to learn representations that are predictive of future scene states. By masking the BEV in groups, the model focuses on motion-relevant regions while disentangling ego vehicle motion from environmental dynamics. This yields a latent world model that captures spatiotemporal patterns, which transfers effectively to OCF tasks requiring both perception and prediction.

## Foundational Learning
1. **Joint-Embedding Predictive Architecture (JEPA)**  
   *Why needed*: Provides a self-supervised framework to learn representations by predicting future embeddings without reconstructing inputs.  
   *Quick check*: Verify that the model uses an encoder to produce embeddings and a predictor to map past to future embeddings; loss is based on similarity in embedding space.

2. **Bird's-Eye View (BEV) Representation**  
   *Why needed*: Unifies multi-sensor and multi-frame data into a top-down grid suitable for motion reasoning.  
   *Quick check*: Confirm that LiDAR points are projected into a fixed-size BEV grid with height or occupancy channels.

3. **Group BEV-guided Masking**  
   *Why needed*: Separates ego motion from scene dynamics by masking groups of BEV cells, forcing the model to infer dynamics from the unmasked context.  
   *Quick check*: Ensure masking pattern is applied across time and space, possibly based on flow or motion cues.

4. **Occupancy Completion and Forecasting (OCF)**  
   *Why needed*: Combines perception (filling incomplete LiDAR) and prediction (forecasting future occupancy) for autonomous driving.  
   *Quick check*: Downstream task takes current and past occupancy and outputs both completed present and future occupancy grids.

5. **Self-Supervised Pretraining**  
   *Why needed*: Avoids costly manual labeling of large-scale LiDAR sequences.  
   *Quick check*: Pretraining uses raw multi-frame LiDAR without ground-truth labels; loss is derived from contrastive or predictive objectives.

6. **SIGRegularization (SIGReg)**  
   *Why needed*: Stabilizes training and improves representation quality by regularizing the embedding space.  
   *Quick check*: Identify whether SIGReg is applied as an auxiliary loss or normalization in JEPA.

## Architecture Onboarding
Component map (plain text):  
Input LiDAR sequences -> BEV Encoder -> JEPA predictor -> Latent embeddings -> OCF decoder

Critical path:
1. LiDAR point clouds from consecutive frames are aggregated into a BEV grid.
2. The BEV encoder produces feature maps.
3. Group BEV-guided masking is applied to the feature maps.
4. The JEPA predictor takes masked past features and predicts future features.
5. The model is trained with a contrastive loss aligning predicted and target future embeddings.
6. The pretrained encoder is frozen or fine-tuned for OCF, where a decoder predicts completed and future occupancy.

Design tradeoffs:
- Masking strategy: aggressiveness vs. information loss; too much masking hinders learning, too little fails to isolate dynamics.
- JEPA vs. reconstruction: JEPA avoids pixel-level reconstruction, focusing on semantics but may lose detail needed for completion.
- Self-supervised vs. supervised: No labels required but may not reach performance of supervised pretraining with sufficient data.
- Computation: Pretraining adds overhead but can be done offline; OCF training benefits from transfer.

Failure signatures:
- Poor OCF performance when masking does not account for ego motion, leading to entangled representations.
- Training collapse if predictor is too powerful relative to encoder (common in JEPA).
- Overfitting to Waymo data; lack of generalization to other datasets or conditions.

First three experiments to onboard:
1. Verify reconstruction vs. prediction: Compare JEPA loss curve against a baseline that uses pixel-wise reconstruction to ensure predictive objective is active.
2. Masking ablation: Train with no masking, random masking, and BEV-guided masking to observe OCF performance differences.
3. Transfer test: Pretrain on a small subset, freeze encoder, evaluate OCF on a held-out sequence to check feature quality.

## Limitations
- Limited external validation: experiments only on Waymo dataset; no tests on other sensors, weather conditions, or geographic regions.
- Performance gains are marginal (<1% absolute IoU) and lack statistical rigor (no variance, confidence intervals, or significance tests).
- Incomplete comparisons: baseline only from-scratch training; no evaluation against alternative self-supervised methods or SOTA occupancy forecasting.

## Confidence
- Self-supervised JEPA framework works as described: Medium
- BEV-guided masking contributes to performance: Medium
- Pretrained encoder improves OCF: Medium
- Reported IoU gains (~0.85%): Low

## Next Checks
1. **Dataset generalization:** Replicate experiments on at least two additional datasets (e.g., nuScenes, Argoverse) and report mean IoU with standard deviation across multiple random seeds.
2. **Statistical significance:** Run the training multiple times (e.g., 5 seeds) and perform paired t-tests comparing JEPA-pretrained encoders vs. scratch training to establish if gains are statistically significant.
3. **Alternative SSL baselines:** Compare AD-LiST-JEPA with other self-supervised learning methods for LiDAR (e.g., point cloud contrastive learning, masked autoencoders) under identical pretraining and downstream protocols to isolate JEPA's contribution.
