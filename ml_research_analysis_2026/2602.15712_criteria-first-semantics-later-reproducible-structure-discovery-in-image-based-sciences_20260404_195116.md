# Criteria-first, semantics-later: reproducible structure discovery in image-based sciences

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2602.15712
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.15712
- **Reference count:** 40
- **Primary result:** A unified framework separates criterion-driven structure extraction from semantic mapping, yielding reproducible, transferable structural products for FAIR, AI-ready digital objects.

## Executive Summary
The dominant semantics-first paradigm in image-based science—directly mapping measurements to fixed domain ontologies—fails under open-ended discovery, cross-sensor comparability, and long-term monitoring where ontologies drift. This paper proposes a deductive inversion: first extract a stable, semantics-free structural product (partitions, graphs, fields) using explicit optimality criteria (e.g., robustness, scale coherence), then map that product downstream to evolving domain ontologies. This separation makes structural products reproducible by design, transferable across contexts, and amenable to plural interpretations, shifting validation from class accuracy to intrinsic stability criteria.

## Method Summary
The framework defines a criterion-parameterised structure-extraction operator \( S_C \) that transforms a measurement field \( X \) (e.g., an image or spatio-temporal grid) into a structural product \( S \) by optimising an explicitly declared criterion \( C \) (e.g., robustness, scale coherence). The extraction is deterministic for given \( (X, C) \), decoupled from any semantic labels. Validation targets the stability and optimality of \( S \) itself under perturbations, not agreement with a particular ontology. Downstream, multiple semantic mappings \( M_i \) can be applied to the same \( S \) without re-running extraction, enabling FAIR packaging of \( S \) as a digital object with full provenance and stability metadata.

## Key Results
- The semantics-first approach is inadequate for open-ended discovery, cross-sensor comparability, and longitudinal studies due to ontology drift.
- A criteria-first workflow extracts stable, semantics-free structural products first via explicit optimality criteria, then maps them downstream to domain ontologies.
- Validation shifts from label accuracy to criterion-driven stability metrics: robustness, scale coherence, compressibility, global consistency, and pluralism.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1
Structural products are reproducible and transferable because they are defined by explicit, community-agnostic optimality criteria rather than domain-specific label sets. A criterion-parameterised operator \( S_C \) transforms a measurement field \( X \) into a structural product \( S \) by minimising an energy or satisfying constraints defined by \( C \). This makes \( S \) deterministic for given \( (X, C) \), enabling reproducibility. Ontology drift only affects the downstream mapping, not \( S \). The core assumption is that stability properties (e.g., invariance to sensor shift) can be operationalised as criteria more fundamental than semantic labels. A break condition occurs if \( C \) is insufficiently expressive, yielding stable but scientifically inadequate \( S \).

### Mechanism 2
Separating structure extraction from semantic mapping enables pluralism—multiple, evolving ontologies can be mapped onto the same stable \( S \) without re-running extraction. The semantic mapping \( M_i \) is a deliberate, documented function applied *after* \( S \) is fixed. Different communities or time periods can define different \( M_i \) for the same \( S \). The core assumption is that semantic labels are purpose-bound and historically contingent, whereas criterion-defined structure can be invariant. A break condition arises if \( S \) is too coarse or misaligned with all relevant ontologies, making downstream mapping lossy or impossible.

### Mechanism 3
Validation shifts from agreement with shifting ground-truth labels to testing the stability and adequacy of \( S \) itself under perturbations, using criteria like robustness, scale coherence, and compressibility. Instead of measuring pixel-level label accuracy, validation targets criteria intrinsic to \( C \); e.g., test if boundaries persist under noise, resolution changes, or covariate shift; test if \( S \) compresses the measurement stream efficiently; test global optimality. The core assumption is that scientific validity is tied to stability under declared perturbations and optimality under an explicit criterion, not match to a particular consensus snapshot. A break condition is if declared criteria \( C \) are poorly correlated with domain-important phenomena, yielding stable but scientifically useless \( S \).

## Foundational Learning
- **Concept: Cybernetic observation-as-distinction**  
  Why needed: Provides the epistemic foundation that measurement is about drawing reproducible distinctions, not capturing pre-existing semantics. This justifies why a criterion-defined structural layer can be stable while semantics drift.  
  Quick check: How does "observation is an operation of drawing distinctions" logically imply that structure should precede semantics in a reproducible workflow?

- **Concept: Information-theoretic separation of information from meaning**  
  Why needed: Grounds the approach in Shannon’s framework, where the goal is uncertainty reduction under constraints (criteria), not interpretation. This makes optimality criteria transportable across domains.  
  Quick check: Why is "Shannon information" (constraint-based reduction of uncertainty) a better foundation for reproducible structure extraction than "semantic agreement"?

- **Concept: Variational principle as explicit criterion**  
  Why needed: Many classical methods (Mumford-Shah, normalized cuts) are formally variational—they define a structural product as the optimum of a well-posed functional. Recognising this pattern allows unification across domains.  
  Quick check: Given an energy functional \( E_C(X, S) = \text{data fidelity} + \lambda \cdot \text{boundary length} \), what does the optimum \( \hat{S} \) represent, and why is it "semantics-free"?

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map:**  
  Input (measurement field \( X \)) -> Criterion Module \( C \) -> Extraction Operator \( S_C \) -> Structural Product \( S \) (partition, graph, hierarchy) -> Validation Suite -> Stable \( S \) -> Semantic Mapping \( M_i \) -> Ontology-aligned output -> Digital Object (FAIR packaging).  
  Alternative paths: \( S_C \) may output hierarchies or fields; Validation tests robustness, scale coherence, etc.; Multiple \( M_i \) may apply to same \( S \).

- **Critical path:** \( X \xrightarrow{\text{with } C} S_C \xrightarrow{\text{validate}} \text{stable } S \xrightarrow{M_i} \text{ontology-aligned output} \). Criterion definition and validation must precede any semantic mapping.

- **Design tradeoffs:**  
  - Criterion expressivity vs. tractability: more complex criteria (e.g., higher-order topological constraints) may be NP-hard; simpler criteria may fail to capture relevant structure.  
  - Determinism vs. robustness: strict global optimisation yields reproducibility but may be brittle; stochastic methods may produce varying \( S \) even for fixed \( C \).  
  - Resolution/scale of \( S \): choosing the level of a multiscale hierarchy for downstream mapping is a semantic decision; too coarse loses detail, too fine may be noisy.

- **Failure signatures:**  
  - Upstream: \( S \) changes drastically under minor perturbations (noise, affine transforms) despite \( C \) claiming robustness → criterion implementation or parameterisation inadequate.  
  - Midstream: different solvers/initialisations for same \( (X, C) \) produce non-isomorphic \( S \) → lack of determinacy; need convex relaxation or global optimisation.  
  - Downstream: single \( S \) cannot support multiple plausible \( M_i \) (e.g., one mapping requires merging cells that another requires splitting) → \( S \) not a flexible substrate; criterion may be too specific.

- **First 3 experiments:**  
  1. **Stability benchmark:** Apply \( S_C \) to \( X \) under perturbations (noise, illumination shift, downsampling). Quantify boundary stability (e.g., Variation of Information) across perturbations. Pass if stability exceeds threshold for declared perturbation family.  
  2. **Mapping pluralism test:** For a fixed \( S \), define two distinct semantic mappings \( M_1, M_2 \) (e.g., different land-cover legends). Assess whether both can be applied without modifying \( S \) (via merging/splitting of partition cells). Pass if both feasible with <X% forced modification to \( S \).  
  3. **FAIR digital object conformance:** Package \( S \) as a DO with metadata (\( C \), software hash, stability envelope). Test interoperability by loading into a different domain’s pipeline (e.g., Earth observation \( S \) used in microscopy). Pass if downstream user can load and apply their own \( M_i \) without upstream re-running.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- What are the general families of optimality and stability criteria that can guide criteria-first structure extraction across diverse imaging domains?
- How should structural benchmarks be designed to evaluate reproducibility and transferability via robustness, scale coherence, compressibility, and global consistency, moving beyond semantic accuracy?
- What schemas, versioning, and metadata standards are needed to treat semantics-free structural products as FAIR digital objects for long-term monitoring and digital twins?

## Limitations
- **Implementation agnosticism:** The framework prescribes *what* to optimise (criterion \( C \)) but not *how*; selecting an expressive, tractable, domain-appropriate \( S_C \) is an unsolved, domain-specific challenge.
- **Criterion-product gap:** The optimal \( S \) under a mathematically convenient \( C \) may be scientifically meaningless; the theory assumes a correlation between criterion-optimality and domain-relevant structure, which is unproven.
- **Validation conceptual gap:** Proposed validation criteria (robustness, pluralism) are abstract; without domain-grounded definitions, tests risk becoming circular or insufficiently connected to scientific utility.

## Next Checks
1. **Perturbation-Stability Benchmark with Human-Abstraction:** For a chosen domain (e.g., histopathology), define \( S_C \) (e.g., texture-based superpixel with contrast-homogeneity criterion). Apply to a standard dataset under a curated perturbation set (stain shifts, noise, affine warps). Check: Measure Variation of Information between \( S \) on original vs. perturbed images. Validatable claim requires a pre-registered minimum VI tolerance for declared perturbation magnitude.
2. **Pluralism Stress Test on Ambiguous Landscapes:** Use a geospatial dataset with ambiguous transitions (e.g., agro-forestry mosaics). Extract a single \( S \) (hierarchical segmentation). Define two incommensurate semantic mappings \( M_1 \) (e.g., ESA land-cover) and \( M_2 \) (e.g., local farmer plots). Check: Quantify percentage of \( S \)'s atomic regions requiring different merging/splitting for \( M_1 \) vs. \( M_2 \). Utility requires this to be below a domain-relevant threshold (e.g., <20%).
3. **FAIR Interoperability Transfer Trial:** Package \( S \) from a satellite image time series as a FAIR Digital Object (with full \( C \), solver version, stability envelope). Provide to a third-party team in a different domain (e.g., microscopy) with no prior exposure. Check: Without re-running extraction, can the receiving team apply their own \( M_i \) to \( S \) for a downstream analysis (e.g., texture quantification)? Success is binary: task completion without requesting modifications to upstream \( S \).
