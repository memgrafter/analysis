# Variable-Length Audio Fingerprinting

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2603.23947
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.23947
- **Reference count:** 40
- **Primary result:** VLAFP attains state-of-the-art robustness to time-stretching and other distortions, achieving F1 scores of 75.19% on FMA and 46.93% on LibriSpeech, and top‑1 hit rate 59.55% for 1‑s DTR queries.

## Executive Summary
Existing deep audio fingerprinting methods rely on fixed-length segmentation, which can cut across semantic boundaries, include redundant context, and misalign under distortions like time-stretching. This paper introduces Variable-Length Audio FingerPrinting (VLAFP), the first deep model that fingerprints audio of arbitrary length for both training and testing. VLAFP employs a transformer with inter-frame self-attention and frame-to-segment cross-attention, coupled with a novel spectral entropy-based variable-length segmentation. On Commercial-Broadcast Retrieval (CBR) across three datasets, VLAFP achieves the best or second-best F1 scores: 75.19% on FMA, 46.93% on LibriSpeech, and 43.75% on AudioSet. On Dummy-Target Retrieval (DTR) with FMA, VLAFP attains a 59.55% top-1 hit rate for 1-second queries. Ablation studies confirm the importance of both attention mechanisms, with F1 dropping 10.72% without self-attention. VLAFP also reduces storage by generating fewer segments and uses fewer parameters than NAFP while training faster, though inference is slower due to variable-length handling.

## Method Summary
VLAFP processes raw waveform at 8 kHz, converting it to a mel-spectrogram (256 bands, 300–4000 Hz). Instead of fixed-length windows, it segments audio using a spectral entropy criterion: starting from a minimum length (0.5 s), it accumulates frames until the next frame’s spectral entropy z‑score exceeds a threshold θ, with a maximum of 5 s. Each segment is projected to frame embeddings and passed through L=4 transformer blocks. Each block contains inter‑frame multi‑head self‑attention (with RMSNorm and residual) followed by a feed‑forward network, and then frame‑to‑segment multi‑head cross‑attention where learnable segment embeddings (H=8) are updated by attending to the frame embeddings. After the final block, the segment embeddings are mean‑pooled and L2‑normalized to produce a 256‑dimensional fingerprint. Training uses supervised contrastive loss with a batch of 60 samples (15 anchor groups, each with 3 positive augmented copies). Augmentations include time‑stretching (0.8–1.2), background noise mixing, and impulse‑response convolution. The model is optimized with Adam (lr=1e‑5) for 100 epochs. For Commercial‑Broadcast Retrieval, test audios are segmented, fingerprinted, and stored; queries are distorted versions of a commercial mixed into a broadcast, and inner‑product search retrieves candidates.

## Key Results
- On CBR across FMA, LibriSpeech, AudioSet: VLAFP achieves best or second‑best F1 (75.19%, 46.93%, 43.75%) and outperforms NAFP by up to 7.64% on FMA.
- On DTR (FMA) for 1‑s queries: VLAFP attains 59.55% top‑1 hit rate vs. NAFP’s 53.85%, and maintains higher rates for longer queries.
- Ablation: Removing self‑attention reduces F1 by 10.72%; removing cross‑attention reduces by ~5%, confirming both are critical.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- **Claim:** Variable-length segmentation based on spectral entropy creates segments that align with acoustically coherent boundaries, avoiding cuts through semantic units.
- **Mechanism:** The algorithm accumulates audio frames until the spectral entropy of new frames deviates beyond a z-score threshold (θ). Low entropy indicates tonal/steady content; stable entropy within a window signals a coherent segment. This produces segments that respect natural acoustic changes.
- **Core assumption:** Acoustic coherence (low spectral entropy variance) correlates with meaningful audio units (e.g., a note, phoneme, or sustained sound).
- **Evidence anchors:**  
  [abstract]: "variable-length segmentation... cutting across semantic boundaries."  
  [section 1]: "Variable-length segmentation... aligned with semantic boundaries."  
  [section 4]: Algorithm 2 details entropy-based window expansion.  
  [corpus]: Corpus shows papers on segment length impact, but direct spectral entropy use in fingerprinting is novel; evidence for entropy stability as coherence proxy is implicit.
- **Break condition:** If spectral entropy varies wildly within a true semantic unit (e.g., a rapid arpeggio), the method may oversegment.

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim:** The dual-attention transformer (inter-frame self-attention + frame-to-segment cross-attention) robustly aggregates variable-length frame representations into a fixed-dimensional fingerprint.
- **Mechanism:** Self-attention layers model temporal dependencies across all frames within a segment. Cross-attention layers use learnable segment embeddings as queries to aggregate information from all frames, producing a set of segment-level embeddings that are pooled into the final fingerprint.
- **Core assumption:** (1) Frame relationships are crucial for robustness (self-attention), and (2) a hierarchical aggregation from frames to segment is necessary for variable-length inputs (cross-attention).
- **Evidence anchors:**  
  [section 3.1]: Explicit architecture with "Inter-frame Self-Attention" and "Frame-to-segment Cross-Attention."  
  [section 5.4]: Ablation shows F1 drops 10.72% without self-attention and ~5% without cross-attention.  
  [corpus]: Transformer-based audio work exists, but the specific cross-attention design for variable-length segmentation is novel.
- **Break condition:** If self-attention fails to capture long-range dependencies or cross-attention queries are poorly initialized, performance collapses.

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim:** Supervised contrastive learning with diverse distortions (time-stretch, noise, reverb) forces fingerprints to be invariant to real-world degradations while remaining discriminative.
- **Mechanism:** For each anchor segment, multiple distorted positives are created. The contrastive loss (Eq. 1) minimizes embedding distance between anchor and positives while maximizing distance to negatives (other audios in batch).
- **Core assumption:** The distortion set (TS, BG, IR) sufficiently covers deployment distortions (e.g., broadcast speed changes, background noise, room acoustics).
- **Evidence anchors:**  
  [section 3.2]: "We adopt supervised contrastive learning... with a chain of time-stretching, background noise mixing, impulse response convolution."  
  [section 5.5]: Ablation in Table 9 shows all three augmentations together yield best CBR F1 (72.60% vs. 63.31% with TS+BG only).  
  [corpus]: Robustness-focused papers exist, but the specific combination and quantified impact are paper-specific.
- **Break condition:** If deployment distortions differ significantly from training augmentations (e.g., heavy codec artifacts), robustness degrades.

## Architecture Onboarding

**Component map:**  
Input (audio segment) → STFT (1024/256) → Mel-spectrogram (T×256) → Linear projection (256→256) → Frame embeddings (T×256) → Stacked L=4 blocks (each: RMSNorm → Multi-head Self-Attention → FFN → RMSNorm → Frame-to-segment Cross-Attention) → Segment embeddings (H=8) initialized at block 1 from mean‑pool(˜h₁)→project→replicate → After final block, mean‑pool segment embeddings → L2‑normalization → Fingerprint (256-D)

**Critical path:** The variable-length spectrogram yields an initial segment embedding s⁰ at block 1. Self-attention layers enhance frame representations (˜hₗ) before each cross-attention step aggregates them into updated segment embeddings. The final fingerprint is the L2‑normalized mean of the last segment embeddings.

**Design tradeoffs:**  
- Variable-length segmentation vs. inference speed: shorter segments increase database size and query time; longer segments risk semantic cuts.  
- Cross-attention head count (H): more heads increase parameters/computation but allow diverse aggregation strategies; H=8 used in paper.  
- Fingerprint dimension (d): larger d improves discriminability but increases storage; 256-D vs. NAFP’s 128-D.

**Failure signatures:**  
- Segmentation failure: If θ is too low (θ=0) all segments become minimum length; if too high (θ=+∞) all become maximum length. Monitor segment length distribution (Fig. 4).  
- Attention collapse: Without self‑attention, CBR F1 drops ~11% (Table 5). Check ˜hₗ diversity; uniform outputs indicate failure.  
- Augmentation mismatch: Removing time‑stretch reduces performance on time‑warped queries (CBR F1 from 72.60% to 70.86% in Table 9). Ensure distortion pool covers deployment conditions.

**First 3 experiments:**  
1. **Baseline replication**: Implement fixed‑length segmentation (θ=0) with the same transformer. Compare CBR F1 on FMA; expect lower score due to loss of semantic alignment.  
2. **Ablation of attention layers**: Remove self‑attention blocks or cross‑attention blocks separately. Evaluate on CBR and DTR; expect significant F1/hit rate drops (Tables 5, 6).  
3. **Threshold sweep**: Train VLAFP with θ∈{0,0.5,1,2,+∞} on FMA. Plot CBR F1 and DTR hit rate vs. average segment length; expect optimum near θ=0.75 (75.80% F1 in Table 14).

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

- **Automatic θ selection:** How can θ be determined automatically across diverse audio content? The paper shows performance varies with θ and requires manual tuning. A data‑driven or differentiable segmentation method would be valuable.
- **Self‑supervised alternatives:** Can VLAFP be trained without hand‑crafted distortions using self‑supervised losses (e.g., masked modeling) while maintaining robustness? This remains to be explored.
- **Cross‑domain robustness:** How does VLAFP perform on completely unseen audio domains (e.g., environmental, industrial) without fine‑tuning? Limited experiments suggest domain sensitivity; systematic evaluation is needed.

## Limitations

- Key details (segment embedding initialization, FFN size, exact entropy computation, augmentation pools, distortion parameters, training schedule) are unspecified, hindering exact replication.
- The entropy threshold θ must be manually tuned for each dataset, limiting out‑of‑the‑box usability.
- Cross‑domain robustness is uncertain; only three datasets were evaluated, and BAF transfer showed lower recall.
