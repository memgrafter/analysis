# Why AI systems don't learn and what to do about it: Lessons on autonomous learning from cognitive science

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID**: 2603.15381
- **Source URL**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.15381
- **Reference count**: 40
- **Primary result**: Proposes a cognitive-inspired A-B-M architecture and evolutionary-developmental bilevel optimization to overcome integration roadblocks and enable AI agents to autonomously learn from raw, interactive experience.

## Executive Summary
Current AI systems lack autonomous learning capability, failing to adapt post-deployment without human-curated data and algorithmic pipelines. This paper argues that the root cause lies in fragmented paradigms (separating perception, control, and meta-learning) and externalized learning processes.Drawing inspiration from cognitive science and developmental psychology—particularly how human infants learn through observation, action, and self-directed exploration—the authors propose a tripartite architecture: System A (self-supervised observational learning), System B (action-based reinforcement learning), and System M (meta-control). These components are jointly initialized and co-adapted via an evolutionary-developmental bilevel optimization conducted in simulation. The framework aims to bootstrap agents that continuously learn from raw sensory streams and interactive experience, reducing dependence on curated data and improving robustness through lifelong adaptation.

## Method Summary
The proposed method combines a modular architecture with a two-level optimization regime. System A processes raw sensory inputs using self-supervised objectives to learn compact latent representations and predictive world models. System B consumes these representations (or raw sensors) to select actions via reinforcement learning, optimizing for extrinsic or intrinsic rewards. System M functions as a meta-controller, monitoring low-dimensional internal states (e.g., prediction errors, uncertainty) and dynamically routing data flows and learning modes among A, B, and episodic memory. These components are not trained separately; instead, an outer evolutionary loop optimizes a meta-parameter vector ϕ that initializes the entire agent (architecture and weights). An inner developmental loop then runs the agent’s lifetime in a simulated environment with System M actively orchestrating the interaction of A and B. The outer loop’s fitness function evaluates the agent’s overall learning performance and adaptation across a distribution of environments, thereby evolving inductive biases and developmental programs that enable robust autonomous learning.

## Key Results
- Identifies three core roadblocks hindering autonomous learning in AI: fragmented paradigms (decoupled perception/control/meta-learning), externalized learning (reliance on human-curated pipelines), and scalability issues (difficulty co-adapting components).
- Introduces the A-B-M conceptual framework, where meta-control (System M) dynamically switches between observational learning (System A) and action-based learning (System B) using internal signals like uncertainty or somatic states.
- Outlines an evolutionary-developmental bilevel optimization approach to jointly initialize and co-adapt all three systems in simulation, aiming to produce agents that learn like human infants from raw, interactive experience.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- **Claim**: Integrating System A (observational learning) and System B (action-based reinforcement learning) improves sample efficiency and robustness by using self-supervised representations to constrain the RL search space.
- **Mechanism**: System A learns compact latent representations and predictive world models from raw sensory streams without human labels. These representations serve as state descriptors for System B, reducing policy search dimensionality. Prediction errors from System A can also provide intrinsic rewards that guide System B’s exploration toward informative environmental regions.
- **Core assumption**: The features captured by System A’s self-supervised objectives align with the control-relevant structure of the task environment.
- **Evidence anchors**: 
  - Abstract: "System A provides System B with predictions of future states conditioned on past states and actions, with hierarchical abstractions over possible actions, and a SSL loss that can be used for curiosity/exploration."
  - Section 2.3: "By observing the world as experienced by the agent, System A can learn abstract representations of observations through SSL methods... This can be used as a proxy for the representation of world states that are more abstract and more compact than raw sensory data."
  - Corpus: Weak—neighbors discuss red teaming, explanations, and learning from failure, not direct integration of observational and RL mechanisms.
- **Break condition**: If System A’s representations fail to capture causally relevant features (e.g., focusing on texture over shape), or if intrinsic rewards derived from System A lead System B to explore irrelevant states, the coupling degrades learning efficiency.

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim**: System M (meta-control) automates the MLOps pipeline by dynamically routing data streams and switching learning modes based on internal signals, enabling continuous adaptation without human intervention.
- **Mechanism**: System M monitors low-dimensional meta-states (e.g., prediction errors, uncertainty, somatic signals) and emits meta-actions that open/close connections between System A, System B, and episodic memory. This dynamic routing assembles and disassembles learning and inference pipelines on the fly—for example, pausing action execution to replay experiences or shifting from exploitation to exploration based on environmental volatility.
- **Core assumption**: A relatively simple, hardwired meta-policy (inspired by biological control) can robustly orchestrate learning across diverse tasks and non-stationary environments.
- **Evidence anchors**:
  - Abstract: "System M for meta-control to dynamically switch modes using internal signals like uncertainty or somatic states."
  - Section 3.1: "Biological agents exhibit meta-control through multiple mechanisms... These mechanisms can be grouped into three broad categories: Input Selection, Loss/Reward Modulation, and Mode of Operation Control."
  - Corpus: Weak—corpus neighbors focus on red teaming, user actions from explanations, and learning from system failures, not meta-control architectures.
- **Break condition**: If meta-signals are too noisy or ambiguous, or if the environment changes faster than System M can adapt, inappropriate routing may occur—e.g., switching to exploration during critical exploitation, or failing to engage learning modes, leading to instability or stagnation.

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim**: The evolutionary-developmental bilevel optimization resolves the chicken-and-egg problem of co-adapting Systems A, B, and M by bootstrapping their joint initialization and learning curricula in simulation.
- **Mechanism**: In the outer (evolutionary) loop, a meta-parameter ϕ is optimized to maximize a fitness function evaluated over the agent’s complete life cycle. ϕ initializes the architecture (A₀, B₀, M₀). In the inner (developmental) loop, a fixed M orchestrates the interaction of A and B with the environment, updating their parameters. The outer loop thus searches for ϕ that yields robust behavior across environments, effectively evolving an inductive bias and developmental program for the inner loop.
- **Core assumption**: The fitness function and simulated environments are sufficiently rich and aligned with real-world demands to select for general autonomous learning capabilities, and that bilevel optimization scales to high-dimensional neural architectures.
- **Evidence anchors**:
  - Abstract: "This bootstraps joint learning of components in simulated environments, aiming to enable AI agents that autonomously learn from raw, interactive experience like human infants."
  - Section 4.2: "Each agent is defined by a set of parameters ϕ that correspond to the information in its genetic code... In the developmental scale (inner loop), the agent learns in interaction with its environment... On the evolutionary timescale (outer loop), ϕ is optimized through an externally handcrafted fitness function L."
  - Corpus: Weak—corpus topics (red teaming, knowledge tracing, etc.) are unrelated; no evidence found on bilevel optimization for autonomous learning architectures.
- **Break condition**: If the simulation-to-real gap is too large, or if the fitness function does not capture autonomous learning essence (e.g., overemphasizing final performance over learning efficiency), the evolved ϕ may produce agents that fail to adapt in real-world deployment, causing inner-loop learning collapse.

## Foundational Learning
- **Self-Supervised Learning (SSL)**  
  *Why needed here*: System A is fundamentally an SSL system; understanding its objectives (e.g., contrastive, predictive coding) is essential to grasp how observational representations are learned without human labels.  
  *Quick check*: What is the typical formulation of a self-supervised objective (e.g., contrastive learning), and how does it avoid the need for explicit supervision?
- **Reinforcement Learning (RL)**  
  *Why needed here*: System B operates as an RL agent; core concepts like policies, rewards, and the exploration-exploitation trade-off are necessary to understand action-based learning.  
  *Quick check*: How does the discount factor γ in RL influence an agent’s prioritization of immediate versus future rewards, and what challenges does this introduce?
- **Bilevel Optimization**  
  *Why needed here*: The proposed training regime uses bilevel optimization to co-evolve meta-parameters (ϕ) and learning systems (A, B) across timescales; grasping the hierarchy of optimization problems is key.  
  *Quick check*: In bilevel optimization, what distinguishes the upper-level problem from the lower-level problem, and what are common approaches to solving such problems (e.g., gradient-based vs. gradient-free)?

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map**:  
  System A (SSL perception/world model) -> System B (RL control) -> System M (meta-control)  
  Episodic memory connected via System M  
  Data pathways: High-bandwidth streams (observations, actions) between A and B; low-bandwidth control streams (meta-states, meta-actions) from M to all components.
- **Critical path**:
  1. **Initialization**: Outer loop provides ϕ, defining initial parameters and architecture of A, B, M.
  2. **Interaction**: Agent interacts with environment; observations → A; A computes representations → B; B selects actions → environment.
  3. **Meta-control**: M continuously monitors meta-states (e.g., uncertainty from A, reward volatility from B) and may trigger mode switches (e.g., pause actions, engage replay, adjust learning rates).
  4. **Lifecycle end**: After fixed steps or goal, compute fitness L(ϕ) from performance metrics (e.g., task success, adaptation speed).
  5. **Evolutionary update**: Update ϕ via bilevel optimization to improve L across many parallel simulated life cycles; repeat.
- **Design tradeoffs**:
  - Hardwired vs. learned meta-control: Hardwired M ensures stability and interpretability but may lack flexibility; a learned M could adapt to new domains but risks uninterpretable or degenerate policies.
  - Fidelity vs. speed in simulation: Evolutionary search requires vast life cycles; high-fidelity simulators are slow, while abstract simulators risk missing crucial real-world dynamics.
  - Representation grounding: System A trained on passive observation may learn features not useful for control; incorporating active data from B improves grounding but requires a curriculum to prevent catastrophic forgetting in A.
- **Failure signatures**:
  - System A: Representations do not cluster according to task-relevant semantics (e.g., low linear probe accuracy); prediction errors remain high on held-out sequences.
  - System B: Learning curves flat or sample efficiency does not improve with A’s representations; policies fail to generalize to minor environmental perturbations.
  - System M: Meta-actions show random or periodic switching unrelated to task demands; performance degrades in non-stationary environments.
  - Evolutionary: Fitness plateaus across generations; high variance in agent performance across lifecycles indicates brittle ϕ.
- **First 3 experiments**:
  1. **Unit test System A**: Train A on a large corpus of natural videos (e.g., Kinetics) using a self-supervised objective (e.g., V-JEPA). Evaluate: (a) future frame prediction error (PSNR), (b) linear probe accuracy on an action classification task from frozen representations.
  2. **Unit test System B**: Freeze a pre-trained System A representation. Train System B on a suite of MuJoCo continuous control tasks. Compare sample efficiency (steps to threshold) against model-based RL (Dreamer) and model-free RL (SAC) baselines.
  3. **Integration test**: Deploy full A-B-M in a lightweight embodied simulator (e.g., MiniWorld with visual observations and locomotion). Measure: (a) adaptation speed when a physical parameter (gravity, friction) changes mid-life, (b) frequency and appropriateness of mode switches (e.g., engagement of replay during rest vs. action), (c) overall task completion rate across a distribution of procedurally generated mazes.

## Limitations
- Framework is conceptual; no empirical validation of A-B-M integration or bilevel optimization.
- Corpus analysis reveals minimal directly related work, indicating speculative integration.
- Key risks: simulation-to-real gap, ambiguous meta-control signals, misaligned representations.

## Confidence
- **Architecture viability (A-B-M integration)**: Low – coupling mechanisms untested.
- **Meta-control effectiveness**: Low – no evidence for robust mode switching.
- **Bilevel optimization feasibility**: Low – scalability and generalization uncertain.

## Next Checks
1. Validate System A representations: train a self-supervised model (e.g., V-JEPA) on video data; evaluate via linear probing on action recognition and prediction error.
2. Measure sample efficiency of System B using frozen A representations vs. Dreamer/SAC on MuJoCo tasks.
3. Test full A-B-M in MiniWorld with dynamic perturbations (e.g., gravity shifts); compare adaptation speed and meta-switch appropriateness to ablations without meta-control.
