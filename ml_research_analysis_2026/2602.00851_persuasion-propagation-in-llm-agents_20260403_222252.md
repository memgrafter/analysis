# Persuasion Propagation in LLM Agents

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2602.00851  
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.00851  
- **Reference count:** 31  
- **Primary result:** Belief-prefilled agents conducted 26.9% fewer searches and visited 16.9% fewer unique sources in web research tasks, while disbelief-prefilled agents did not deviate from neutral behavior.

## Executive Summary
This paper studies how task-irrelevant persuasion influences the downstream behavior of LLM agents in long-horizon tasks such as coding and web research—a phenomenon termed *persuasion propagation*. By comparing two experimental settings—on-the-fly persuasion (exposure during task execution) and prefilled belief conditioning (explicit belief specification at task start)—the authors find that on-the-fly persuasion induces weak and inconsistent behavioral changes. In contrast, belief-prefilled agents (relative to neutral-prefilled) exhibit systematic reductions in exploratory actions, conducting 26.9% fewer searches and visiting 16.9% fewer unique sources. These results highlight that persistent, integrated belief states—not mere surface-level agreement—alter agent execution patterns, underscoring the need for process-level evaluation over outcome-only metrics.

## Method Summary
The study employs a three-stage experimental pipeline: (1) persuasion stage, where agents are exposed to task-irrelevant claims either via on-the-fly injection (multi-turn dialogue with probing, injection, and commitment reinforcement) or prefilled belief conditioning (explicitly starting the task with a belief/disbelief/neutral statement); (2) downstream task execution using AutoGen to orchestrate multi-step interactions (web research or coding loops); (3) behavioral trace analysis, capturing intermediate actions (searches, edits, tool calls) and computing process metrics. The design isolates belief representation from transient persuasion effects by comparing prefill conditions (belief vs. neutral) and on-the-fly conditions (with/without neutral injection). Experiments run across three LLM backbones (gpt-4.1-nano, mistral-nemo-12b, llama-3.1-8b) and seven persona prompts (Neutral, GPT, Claude, LLaMA, Mistral, Qwen, Gemini), with metrics aggregated via baseline-relative deltas and PCA-based constructs.

## Key Results
- Prefilled belief conditioning reduces web exploration: belief-prefilled agents perform 26.9% fewer searches and visit 16.9% fewer unique sources than neutral-prefilled agents (statistically significant, p<0.02).
- On-the-fly persuasion yields weak and inconsistent behavioral shifts; pooled effects are small and persona-level dispersion often exceeds pooled means.
- Disbelief-prefilled agents do not deviate from neutral behavior, indicating an asymmetry in how positive vs. negative beliefs integrate.
- Persona definitions strongly modulate persuasion propagation, with some personas showing opposing behavioral deltas under the same persuasion tactic.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1: Persistent Belief Conditioning
When a belief is explicitly specified at task initialization, it becomes part of the agent’s latent context that persists across all decision steps. This conditioned belief acts as a prior that shapes the agent’s planning and information-gathering heuristics, leading to systematic reductions in exploratory actions (e.g., fewer web searches) even when the belief is task-irrelevant. The effect manifests in the process trajectory, not necessarily the final output. Evidence: Prefilled belief agents show significant reductions in search/URL counts versus neutral (p<0.02), while on-the-fly persuasion does not. Break condition: If the effect vanishes when controlling for prompt length/lexical priming, or if identical effects appear for disbelief, the mechanism of belief integration is weakened.

### Mechanism 2: Transient On-the-Fly Persuasion
On-the-fly persuasion is injected mid-execution when the agent’s attention is dominated by task-specific goals and tool feedback. The persuasive signal is transient and suffers from recency bias, failing to integrate into a persistent belief state. Any induced agreement is superficial (compliance without internalization), leading to high variance in behavioral outcomes across personas and tactics. Evidence: Pooled on-the-fly effects are small; persona-level dispersion (IQR up to 0.464) exceeds means. Break condition: If longer, multi-turn persuasive dialogues during execution produce large, consistent shifts comparable to prefill, the assumed superiority of pre-task integration is challenged.

### Mechanism 3: Persona Modulation
System-level persona prompts (e.g., “cooperative and pragmatic”) establish stable interaction styles that interact with persuasion tactics and belief conditioning, altering how an agent internalizes stances and translates them to execution. Persona acts as a higher-order conditioning variable on the persuasion-behavior link. Evidence: Large persona-specific deltas with opposing signs within the same backbone (e.g., gpt “Neutral” persona: ∆Dpt = -0.320; “Mistral” persona: ∆Dpt = +0.298). Break condition: If persona effects disappear with instruction-tuned models or fine-tuning on task data, the role of system-level persona is undermined.

## Foundational Learning

- **Concept:** Belief State as Latent Context  
  **Why needed:** Distinguishes between surface-level agreement and integrated belief that shapes multi-step behavior. Understanding that an agent’s context window contains a persistent “belief state” is key to why pre-task conditioning matters.  
  **Quick check:** If an agent says “I believe X” at the start but never references X again, how could X still influence its search queries or code revisions?

- **Concept:** Behavioral Trace Analysis vs. Outcome Metrics  
  **Why needed:** The core finding is that persuasion propagation is invisible in final outputs but evident in process metrics (searches, revisions, domain entropy). This challenges standard evaluation paradigms.  
  **Quick check:** What would you log from a web research agent to detect reduced exploration, even if the final summary looks correct?

- **Concept:** Experimental Disentanglement of Persuasion Timing  
  **Why needed:** Separating *when* belief is introduced (prefill vs. on-the-fly) from *whether* it changes stated belief isolates the causal effect of persistent belief conditioning.  
  **Quick check:** Why is a “neutral prefill” condition necessary, and how does it differ from a “no-injection” baseline?

## Architecture Onboarding

Component map:  
Persona Configurator -> Persuasion Injector -> Task Executor -> Trace Logger -> Metric Engine -> Statistical Aggregator

Critical path:  
Initialize Agent with Persona -> Apply Persuasion/Belief Regime -> Execute Downstream Task -> Log Full Trace -> Calculate Behavioral Metrics -> Compare to Baseline & Pool

Design tradeoffs:  
- Prefill vs. On-the-fly: Prefill offers clean belief isolation but lacks ecological validity; on-the-fly is more realistic but noisy due to context competition.  
- Persona Control: Fixed stylistic prompts increase experimental control but may not capture model-specific biases; removing personas might increase variance.  
- Metric Aggregation: PCA-based construct scores reduce noise but risk obscuring interpretability of individual metrics.

Failure signatures:  
1. High within-persona variance: Same persona+tactic shows conflicting behavioral deltas across runs → execution too stochastic.  
2. Baseline sensitivity: Large differences between “no-injection” and “neutral prefill” baselines → initialization artifacts contaminate belief effect estimates.  
3. No belief persistence: Stance probe shows no change from initial to final post-exposure → persuasion failed; downstream behavior likely unaffected.  
4. Corpus mismatch: Neighboring papers focus on prompt injection or agent benchmarks, not belief propagation; weak external validation.

First 3 experiments:  
1. **Replication Core:** Run prefill belief conditioning on web research with one backbone (e.g., gpt-4.1-nano) and “Neutral” persona. Verify belief-prefilled agents show a statistically significant reduction in #searches and #unique URLs vs. neutral prefill (p<0.05).  
2. **Tactic Sensitivity:** For same backbone/persona, run on-the-fly persuasion with all five tactics (authority, evidence, etc.). Confirm no single tactic produces a significant pooled behavioral shift (check p-values and IQR dispersion).  
3. **Trace Inspection:** Manually inspect execution traces from one belief-prefilled vs. neutral-prefilled web research trial. Look for patterns: Does the belief agent issue fewer, more focused queries? Terminate earlier? Visit fewer distinct domains? Confirm qualitative shift behind quantitative metrics.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

**Open Question 1:** Do code-specialized LLMs differ from generalist models in persuasion propagation during coding?  
*Basis:* Appendix H used gpt-4.1-nano and suggests code-specialized models may differ. *Why unresolved:* No code-specific backbone tested. *Evidence needed:* Test code-specialized backbones, measure TRS/EVS, compare to generalists.

**Open Question 2:** How do persuasion strength, repetition, and timing affect belief integration and behavior?  
*Basis:* Authors note minimal persuasion (single claim) and that stronger/repeated/temporal interventions may cause deeper integration. *Why unresolved:* Only minimal persuasion examined. *Evidence needed:* Vary persuasion parameters (length, repetitions, spacing) and measure belief and behavioral outcomes.

**Open Question 3:** Why does belief prefill cause behavioral changes while disbelief prefill does not?  
*Basis:* Results show belief-prefilled agents reduced searches/URLs; disbelief-prefilled matched neutral. *Why unresolved:* Mechanism unknown; could reflect belief integration differences. *Evidence needed:* Compare agent traces, tool use, internal representations; test across tasks/models.

**Open Question 4:** What are the main factors (backbone, persona, task, technique) influencing persuasion propagation?  
*Basis:* Authors state behavior depends on factor space and urge exploration. *Why unresolved:* Factors not quantified; heterogeneity unexplained. *Evidence needed:* Conduct factorial experiment across factor levels; use statistical modeling to estimate contributions.

## Limitations
- Implementation details (AutoGen tools, claim generator) and baseline computation methods are not fully disclosed, hindering faithful replication.
- PCA aggregation specifics (centering, scaling, loadings) for web constructs are missing; slight variations could alter composite scores.
- Lack of determinism settings (temperature, seeds) may lead to high variance, making subtle effects hard to measure consistently.

## Confidence
- **Prefill belief effect:** Medium (significant reductions reported, but baseline and PCA details undisclosed; break condition partially supported by disbelief ≈ neutral)
- **On-the-fly persuasion:** Medium (weak pooled effects and high dispersion support inconsistency, but single-turn design may understate potential)
- **Persona modulation:** Low (large persona deltas observed, but underlying cause unanalyzed; effect could be prompt artifacts)

## Next Checks
1. **Prompt confound control:** Match belief/neutral prefill prompts for length and lexical content. A persistent effect supports belief integration rather than lexical priming.
2. **Extended on-the-fly persuasion:** Use multi-turn persuasive dialogues during execution. A significant shift would challenge the prefill superiority claim.
3. **Persona independence:** Test on instruction-tuned models and without persona prompts. Robust belief effects with reduced persona dispersion would clarify the base model’s role.
