# IOSVLM: A 3D Vision-Language Model for Unified Dental Diagnosis from Intraoral Scans

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2603.16781
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.16781
- **Reference count:** 26
- **Primary result:** Achieves macro accuracy of 77.23% and macro F1 of 50.39%, outperforming strong 2D/3D baselines by at least +9.58% accuracy and +1.46% F1, and surpasses larger proprietary models like Gemini 3 Pro and GPT-5.

## Executive Summary
Intraoral scans (IOS) are increasingly used in dentistry but existing AI methods fragment the multi-disease diagnosis task by treating each disease separately or rely on 2D renderings that lose native 3D geometry. IOSVLM addresses this gap with an end-to-end 3D vision-language model that directly processes IOS as point clouds for unified diagnostic visual question answering. It introduces a geometry-to-chromatic proxy (GCP) to bridge the distribution gap between color-free IOS data and color-pretrained 3D encoders, and employs a two-stage curriculum training on a large-scale IOSVQA dataset. The model sets new performance benchmarks and achieves reliable structured output parsing.

## Method Summary
IOSVLM takes an intraoral scan represented as a point cloud (10,000 points) and a textual query about dental diseases. The 3D encoder (ReCon++) processes the point cloud, augmented by GCP—a mapping of surface normals to pseudo-color channels that mimics the local separability cues of RGB without requiring actual color data. The encoder outputs three feature streams (absolute position, local, global) which are projected into the language model’s token space via separate MLPs. The LLM (Qwen3VL-8B-Instruct) then generates a response, either a diagnostic label or label with rationale. Training proceeds in two stages: Stage 1 trains the 3D encoder and projectors on a large corpus of mixed-quality data with the LLM frozen; Stage 2 fine-tunes the projectors and LLM (via LoRA) on a smaller, higher-quality subset with optional rationales. The IOSVQA dataset provides 19,002 cases and 249,055 VQA pairs covering 23 dental diseases.

## Key Results
- Macro accuracy of 77.23% and macro F1 of 50.39% on the test set.
- Outperforms strong 2D and 3D baselines by at least +9.58% in accuracy and +1.46% in F1.
- Surpasses larger proprietary models (Gemini 3 Pro, GPT-5) despite significantly fewer parameters.
- Achieves a 100% parsing rate for reliably structured outputs, indicating robust generation in the expected label space.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1: GCP stabilizes fine-grained geometric perception
- **Claim:** The Geometry-to-Chromatic Proxy (GCP) enables color-pretrained 3D encoders to adapt to color-free IOS while preserving local discriminative cues.
- **Mechanism:** GCP maps surface normals to pseudo-color via `GCP(n_i) = |n_i|/||n_i||_2`, providing spatially coherent boundaries analogous to RGB’s role as a local separability cue rather than a semantic feature.
- **Core assumption:** Pretrained 3D encoders rely on color primarily for stable local feature extraction, not semantic meaning; thus a geometry-derived proxy suffices.
- **Evidence anchors:** (Abstract) explicitly states GCP bridges the distribution gap; (Section 2.3) details the rationale that RGB is beneficial as a local separability cue, not for semantic color.
- **Break condition:** If surface normals become spatially incoherent (e.g., on extremely smooth or noisy surfaces), GCP fails to provide discriminative signals, degrading encoder performance.

### Mechanism 2: Two-stage curriculum builds robust perception before generative tuning
- **Claim:** Staged training first establishes robust 3D geometry-language alignment on large noisy data, then refines generation on high-quality data without overwriting visual features.
- **Mechanism:** Stage 1 trains the 3D encoder and projectors with frozen LLM on mixed-quality data to learn general geometric representations. Stage 2 freezes the encoder and fine-tunes projectors and LLM (via LoRA) on cleaner, rationale-augmented data to adapt language generation.
- **Core assumption:** Early exposure to diverse, noisy data builds resilience to label noise; subsequent focused tuning adapts the LLM without destabilizing learned visual features.
- **Evidence anchors:** (Abstract) mentions the curriculum enhances robustness; (Section 2.4) defines Stage 1 for alignment and Stage 2 for generation under higher-quality supervision.
- **Break condition:** If Stage 1 introduces entrenched biases (e.g., systematic misclassification of minority diseases) that Stage 2 cannot correct, the curriculum fails to improve final diagnostic performance.

### Mechanism 3: End-to-end native 3D modeling preserves fine-grained morphology
- **Claim:** Processing raw point clouds retains subtle 3D surface deformations lost in 2D multi-view renderings.
- **Mechanism:** The point-cloud pipeline (with ReCon++) extracts absolute positional, local, and global features directly from continuous 3D structure, avoiding distortions from view selection and discrete projections that weaken spatial relationships.
- **Core assumption:** Key dental abnormalities (e.g., spacing, crossbite) manifest as fine-grained 3D shape changes better captured by native geometry than any set of 2D views.
- **Evidence anchors:** (Abstract) emphasizes direct leverage of native 3D geometry versus rendered 2D images; (Section 2.2–2.3) argues rendering relies on view selection and weakens spatial relationships.
- **Break condition:** If downsampling to 10,000 points discards critical small-scale features (e.g., early caries), native 3D modeling may not outperform carefully selected multi-view 2D inputs.

## Foundational Learning
- **Point Cloud Representation Learning**  
  *Why needed:* IOS data is inherently a 3D surface; the model must handle unordered point sets with permutation invariance and multi-scale geometric patterns.  
  *Quick check:* How does ReCon++ achieve permutation invariance and capture local-to-global geometric context?

- **Vision-Language Alignment in Multimodal LLMs**  
  *Why needed:* Projectors must map continuous 3D features into the LLM’s discrete token space; misalignment causes the LLM to ignore visual cues.  
  *Quick check:* What architectural choices (e.g., MLP depth, normalization) help align high-dimensional 3D features with language embeddings?

- **Curriculum Learning for Noisy Multi-Label Data**  
  *Why needed:* IOSVQA has mixed-quality labels and co-occurring diseases; staged training aims to build robust perception before adapting to diagnostic generation.  
  *Quick check:* Why freeze the 3D encoder in Stage 2? What risk does joint tuning pose on limited high-quality data?

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map:**  
  Input preprocessor -> 3D encoder (ReCon++) -> Projectors (ϕ_ape, ϕ_local, ϕ_global) -> LLM (Qwen3VL-8B-Instruct)
- **Critical path:** Point cloud (with GCP) -> ReCon++ -> absolute/local/global feature streams -> MLP projectors -> visual tokens (concatenated with learnable prompts) -> LLM -> generated text (label or label+rationale).
- **Design tradeoffs:**
  - **Point count (N):** Higher N preserves detail but increases compute; N=10,000 balances fidelity and efficiency.
  - **GCP vs. no proxy:** GCP improves performance by providing geometry-driven separability but requires normal computation; constant white color removes this signal.
  - **Stage-1 LLM frozen:** Prevents early semantic biases but delays language-tuning; joint end-to-end training from start may cause instability.
  - **LoRA in Stage-2:** Efficient LLM adaptation; full fine-tuning could yield gains but risks overfitting on smaller Stage-2 data.
- **Failure signatures:**
  - Parsing rate < 100%: LLM generates free-form text outside expected label space, often due to insufficient instruction tuning.
  - Macro F1 << Macro Accuracy: Severe class imbalance; model collapses to majority-class predictions.
  - GCP ablation drop >5% accuracy: Encoder relies on color-like proxy; without it, pretrained features fail to transfer.
  - Stage-2 full tuning (V&P&L) underperformance: Catastrophic forgetting of Stage-1 geometric representations.
- **First 3 experiments:**
  1. **Ablate GCP:** Train with/without GCP (use constant white pseudo-color); expect >5% accuracy drop if GCP is effective.
  2. **Compare training schedules:** Train without Stage 1 (i.e., only Stage 2 on high-quality data) vs. proposed curriculum; expect curriculum to yield higher macro F1 and precision/recall balance.
  3. **Test LLM scalability:** Replace Qwen3VL-8B with a smaller (1.5B) or larger (14B) LLM; measure trade-offs in accuracy, F1, and majority-class bias.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- **Alternative geometry proxy:** Which descriptor (e.g., curvature) best mimics color separability for pretrained 3D encoders? (Basis: Paper notes other descriptors may yield gains but only normals were tested.)
- **Curriculum bias prevention:** How to avoid LLM bias from low-quality data during early training? (Basis: Paper acknowledges early LLM updates under noisy supervision may bias generation and limit high-quality adaptation.)
- **Generalization to unseen diseases/scanners:** Will IOSVLM work on novel diseases or different IOS hardware? (Basis: Dataset covers only 23 diseases and two scan forms; no out-of-distribution tests reported.)
- **Mesh vs. point cloud:** Does mesh-based representation improve diagnosis by preserving topology? (Basis: Paper cites complex IOS topology as a challenge but uses point clouds without mesh comparison.)

## Limitations
- Private datasets (MaloccIOS, DiseaseIOS) restrict full external validation and reproducibility.
- Missing training details (LoRA rank, MLP dimensions, data splits) hinder exact replication.
- Macro F1 (50.39%) is substantially lower than macro accuracy (77.23%), indicating severe class imbalance and potential collapse on rare diseases.
- GCP’s reliance on surface normals may fail on extremely smooth or noisy surfaces where normals are ambiguous.
- Downsampling to 10,000 points risks discarding subtle pathological features (e.g., early caries lesions).

## Confidence
- GCP stabilizes fine-grained geometric perception: **Medium** (plausible mechanism but limited independent evidence; break condition not tested)
- Two-stage curriculum builds robust alignment before generation: **Medium** (common in multimodal training, but specific ordering lacks rigorous ablation)
- End-to-end native 3D modeling preserves morphology: **Medium** (empirical gains shown, yet resolution trade-offs not fully explored)

## Next Checks
1. **GCP ablation:** Train with constant white pseudo-color instead of normal-based GCP; expect >5% accuracy drop if proxy is effective.
2. **Curriculum ablation:** Train without Stage 1 (only Stage 2 on high-quality data) to assess whether large noisy pre-training truly improves robustness, especially on minority classes.
3. **Resolution sensitivity:** Vary point cloud size (5k, 10k, 20k) and measure macro F1 on rare diseases; identify if N=10k discards critical fine-grained cues.
