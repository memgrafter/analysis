# Learning Can Converge Stably to the Wrong Belief under Latent Reliability

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2603.21491
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.21491
- **Reference count:** 17
- **Primary result:** Standard learning algorithms can converge with stable, decreasing loss to systematically incorrect solutions when feedback reliability is unobservable and persistently biased; a trajectory-level trust modulation framework (MTR) can limit bias accumulation and enable recovery.

## Executive Summary
The paper identifies a fundamental failure mode: when feedback reliability (e.g., correct vs. corrupted labels or rewards) is latent and persistent bias exists, standard learners can converge to wrong solutions while optimization appears stable—bias is locally indistinguishable from reliable signal. To address this, the authors propose the Monitor-Trust-Regulator (MTR) framework. MTR infers reliability from aggregated trajectory-level statistics (like parameter instability) over a slow trust variable, which then modulates the effective learning rate. This allows the system to downweight updates during unreliable phases and recover when reliability returns, as demonstrated in reinforcement learning (PPO on MuJoCo) and supervised learning (ResNet on CIFAR-10) with staged corruption.

## Method Summary
The MTR framework augments a standard base learner (e.g., SGD, PPO) with three components: (1) a **Monitor** that computes a windowed instability statistic \(S_t\) from recent parameter updates (e.g., average squared norm of parameter changes), (2) a **Trust Estimator** that maintains a slow-varying reliability estimate \(\tau_t \in [0,1]\) via a low-rate integration of normalized \(S_t\), and (3) a **Regulator** that scales the base learning rate by \(\tau_t\) to produce the effective update \(\theta_{t+1} = \theta_t - \eta \tau_t g_t\). The core idea is that persistent bias induces distinct trajectory-level statistics compared to reliable learning, even when single-step gradients are locally indistinguishable. The trust variable's slow evolution filters transient noise, allowing sustained instability to reduce trust and attenuate biased updates.

## Key Results
- Standard learners maintain stable loss curves but fail to recover performance after a persistent corruption phase, converging to an incorrect solution.
- MTR-limited updates during corruption, reducing bias accumulation, and enables recovery to near-clean performance once reliability returns.
- Trajectory-level instability measure \(S_t\) achieves high separability (AUC ≈ 0.85) between reliable and biased regimes, whereas single-step signals (e.g., gradient magnitude) are at chance (AUC ≈ 0.5).

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1: Trajectory-level statistics distinguish regimes under persistent bias
- **Claim:** Windowed statistics like \(S_t\) can differentiate reliable from biased feedback regimes even when individual gradients cannot.
- **Mechanism:** Under a persistent regime \(i\) (reliable or biased), the expected gradient \(F_i(\theta)\) differs. While a single gradient is locally ambiguous, the parameter path \(\theta_t\) over a window \(W\) yields \(S_t = \frac{1}{W}\sum_{k=0}^{W-1} \|\theta_{t-k} - \theta_{t-k-1}\|^2\) that converges to \(\mathbb{E}[\|\eta F_i(\theta)\|^2]\), a regime-specific quantity.
- **Core assumption:** Reliability regimes persist for intervals ≥ \(W\) (bias is not ultra-short-term noise).
- **Evidence anchors:** Theorem 2 formalizes convergence of \(S_t\); Fig. 4 shows separable \(S_t\) distributions in clean vs. corrupt phases.
- **Break condition:** If reliability switches faster than \(W\), \(S_t\) cannot stabilize to regime-specific distributions, destroying separability.

### Mechanism 2: Slow-timescale trust integrates noisy trajectory signals
- **Claim:** A slow-updating trust variable \(\tau_t\) can reliably estimate feedback reliability from trajectory statistics.
- **Mechanism:** Trust evolves much slower than parameters (e.g., exponential moving average of normalized \(S_t\) with decay \(\beta_\tau \ll 1\)). This temporal smoothing filters step-wise noise and requires sustained instability to shift trust, preventing overreaction to transient fluctuations.
- **Core assumption:** Reliability changes are slow relative to trust update rate (regime duration >> trust timescale).
- **Evidence anchors:** Fig. 6 shows \(\tau_t\) drifting down during corruption and recovering after, demonstrating slow integration.
- **Break condition:** If trust updates too quickly, it tracks noise; if too slow, it lags regime changes, causing delayed or incorrect modulation.

### Mechanism 3: Trust-modulated learning rate bounds bias accumulation
- **Claim:** Scaling updates by \(\tau_t\) limits bias during unreliable phases and permits recovery afterward.
- **Mechanism:** When \(\tau_t\) decreases under persistent instability, effective learning rate \(\alpha_{\text{eff}} = \tau_t \alpha\) drops, restraining the pull of biased gradients. When \(\tau_t\) recovers, normal learning resumes, allowing movement back toward the true optimum since the bias is no longer reinforced.
- **Core assumption:** The correct solution remains reachable after corruption (corruption is persistent but not destructive; clean data distribution unchanged).
- **Evidence anchors:** Fig. 3 shows PPO+Trust recovering while standard PPO remains degraded; Section 5.4 links trust dynamics to reduced bias accumulation.
- **Break condition:** If \(\tau_t\) collapses to zero and never recovers, learning halts; if modulation is too weak, bias accumulates as in standard learning.

## Foundational Learning
- **Concept: Stochastic Approximation & ODE Method**  
  Why needed: Theorem 2 uses stochastic approximation to argue that under small step size \(\eta\), the parameter recursion approximates an ODE \(\dot{\theta} = -F_i(\theta)\) within a persistent regime, justifying \(S_t\) as a proxy for \(\|\eta F_i(\theta)\|^2\).  
  Quick check: How does the law of large numbers for martingale differences enable the convergence of the windowed statistic \(S_t\) to its expectation within a regime?

- **Concept: Timescale Separation in Dynamical Systems**  
  Why needed: MTR couples fast parameter dynamics with slow trust dynamics. Analysis treats them as separate timescales to ensure trust integrates persistent signals without tracking noise.  
  Quick check: Why would running trust updates at the same timescale as parameters cause system failure under latent reliability?

- **Concept: Parametric Identifiability**  
  Why needed: The problem is framed as epistemic identifiability under unobservable reliability—can the learner distinguish which data-generating process it is in? Single-step observables are not identifiable, but trajectory-level statistics are (scale-dependent identifiability).  
  Quick check: What structural property (persistence) allows trajectory-level data to be identifiable when single-step data is not?

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map:** Monitor → Trust Estimator → Regulator → Base Learner
- **Critical path:** Parameter update generates new \(\theta_t\); Monitor extracts instability \(S_t\) from recent history; Trust Estimator updates \(\tau_t\) from \(S_t\); Regulator sets effective learning rate \(\alpha_{\text{eff},t} = \tau_t \cdot \alpha_{\text{base}}\) for the next parameter update. Trust update must lag parameter updates sufficiently.
- **Design tradeoffs:**  
  - **Window size \(W\):** Larger \(W\) smooths \(S_t\) and improves noise immunity but slows detection of regime changes; smaller \(W\) is noisier and risks false positives.  
  - **Trust decay rate \(\beta_\tau\):** Smaller \(\beta_\tau\) (slower trust) robust to transients but sluggish recovery; larger \(\beta_\tau\) more responsive but may overreact.  
  - **Trust initialization:** High \(\tau_0\) starts optimistic; low \(\tau_0\) starts cautious—choice may depend on prior reliability expectations.
- **Failure signatures:**  
  - **Trust collapse:** \(\tau_t \to 0\) early and stays near zero → learning stalls.  
  - **Trust oscillation:** \(\tau_t\) fluctuates rapidly → likely \(W\) too small or \(\beta_\tau\) too high.  
  - **No separability in \(S_t\):** Histograms of \(S_t\) overlap between known reliable/biased regimes → monitor signal not informative; revisit feature (e.g., use gradient variance).  
  - **Bias accumulation despite trust:** Trust decreases but not enough to prevent convergence to wrong solution → modulation strength insufficient or trust floor too high.
- **First 3 experiments:**  
  1. **Replicate trajectory-level separability:** In a minimal setting (e.g., biased quadratic), compute \(S_t\) during known clean vs. biased phases. Verify AUC for \(S_t\) ≫ 0.5 while single-step gradient magnitude is near chance (AUC ≈ 0.5).  
  2. **Trust dynamics sanity check:** Run a staged corruption (clean → corrupt → clean). Plot \(\tau_t\) and verify: (a) \(\tau_t\) stays high in clean, (b) decreases during corruption, (c) recovers in final clean. Vary corruption strength and confirm \(\min(\tau)\) correlates with bias magnitude.  
  3. **Recovery comparison:** Train standard vs. MTR through clean-corrupt-recovery. Compare performance drop during corruption and final performance after recovery (MTR should reach near-clean baseline, standard degraded). Also track final parameter distance to clean optimum if available.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- **Open Question 1:** How can MTR handle rapidly switching or highly non-stationary reliability regimes where persistent patterns are short-lived?  
  Basis: Section 6 states that rapidly switching reliability is harder and largely open.  
  Why unresolved: Analysis assumes persistent regimes (constant ρ over intervals ≥ \(W\)); rapid switching violates this, potentially making trajectory-level statistics unreliable or slow to detect.  
  Evidence to resolve: Empirical benchmarks with controlled switching frequencies; theoretical analysis of minimum regime duration for separability; modifications to trust dynamics for faster adaptation.

- **Open Question 2:** What trajectory-level statistics beyond \(S_t\) maximally separate reliable and biased regimes in high-dimensional or non-convex settings?  
  Basis: Future work suggests "richer trajectory descriptors" could improve MTR; current monitor uses only simple squared increment statistic.  
  Why unresolved: Paper shows \(S_t\) works in tested settings but does not explore whether other dynamics (e.g., update autocorrelation, curvature, higher-order moments) provide stronger or more robust signals across diverse bias types.  
  Evidence to resolve: Systematic comparison of multiple trajectory features in RL/supervised tasks with varied corruption; statistical evaluation of separability (AUC) and sensitivity to bias magnitude.

- **Open Question 3:** What precise theoretical conditions (e.g., minimum regime length \(W\), drift strength, step size \(\eta\)) guarantee trajectory-level identifiability under latent reliability?  
  Basis: Theorem 2 requires "sufficiently large \(W\)" and small \(\eta\); Figure 1C shows detectability increases with drift strength, but exact quantitative relationships are not provided.  
  Why unresolved: Paper gives asymptotic convergence in probability but no finite-sample guarantees or phase transitions, limiting practical guidance for choosing \(W\) or understanding failure modes.  
  Evidence to resolve: Analytical derivation of bounds linking \(W\), \(\eta\), bias magnitude, and separability metrics; simulations mapping identifiability region in parameter space.

- **Open Question 4:** Does MTR scale effectively to large-scale models (e.g., deep neural networks with millions of parameters) and real-world datasets with latent feedback issues?  
  Basis: Section 6 notes "large-scale and production pipelines need further study."  
  Why unresolved: Experiments use ResNet-18 on CIFAR-10 and small MuJoCo tasks; computational overhead, interaction with complex optimization landscapes, and performance under realistic biases (e.g., in LLM training) remain unknown.  
  Evidence to resolve: Application to state-of-the-art architectures (transformers, diffusion models) on large datasets with synthetic/realistic biases; measurement of training stability, overhead, and recovery compared to standard methods.

## Limitations
- **Robustness to regime switching:** Current analysis assumes persistent regimes; performance under rapidly switching or highly non-stationary reliability is uncertain and likely degraded.
- **Hyperparameter sensitivity:** MTR introduces new hyperparameters (window size \(W\), trust decay \(\beta_\tau\), normalization) whose optimal values depend on task timescales and bias strength; systematic guidance is lacking.
- **Scalability and overhead:** Applicability to very large models and real-world, complex bias structures (e.g., in large language models) remains unvalidated; computational overhead of monitoring and trust updates is not characterized for massive-scale training.

## Confidence
- **Standard learner failure under latent persistent bias:** High confidence. Theoretical argument (wrong ODE fixed point) and consistent empirical failures across tasks are solid.
- **Trajectory-level identifiability via instability measure \(S_t\):** Medium confidence. Theorem and separability experiments support it, but the result depends on specific measure, window size, and bias type; other dynamics may not exhibit the property.
- **MTR effectiveness in limiting bias and enabling recovery:** Medium confidence. Experiments show improvement, but hyperparameter choices are not thoroughly analyzed, and robustness to varying corruption strengths or faster regime switches is unclear.
