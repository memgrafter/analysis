# Fibration Policy Optimization

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2603.08239
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.08239
- **Reference count:** 9
- **Primary result:** FiberPO introduces a Fiber Bundle Gating framework that decomposes the importance-sampling ratio into trajectory-level (global trust-region) and token-level (local deviation) components, yielding a surrogate objective with a block-diagonal Jacobian, on-policy identity, and a restorative gradient, while extending hierarchically without new primitives to improve token efficiency and multi-scale stability.

## Executive Summary
Policy optimization for large language models (LLMs) suffers from instability that spans token, trajectory, and hierarchical levels, which traditional methods like PPO or TRPO address only partially. Fibration Policy Optimization (FiberPO) resolves this by reformulating trust-region constraints via the Aggregational Policy Censoring Objective (APC‑Obj), which provides an exact unconstrained equivalent of total‑variation TRPO. It then introduces Fiber Bundle Gating (FBG), a mathematical structure that cleanly separates global trajectory drift from local token deviations. This separation produces a surrogate objective whose gradient Jacobian is block‑diagonal over trajectories, reduces to the identity near the on‑policy point, and includes a restorative mechanism to correct drift. Because FBG is built from composable fibrations, the approach naturally extends to arbitrary hierarchies (e.g., domain, prompt group, trajectory, token), enabling independent trust‑region budgets at each level. The result is improved sample efficiency and principled multi‑scale stability control, unifying trust‑region theory with algebraic compositionality.

## Method Summary
FiberPO operates on importance ratios \(r_{s,a} = \pi_\theta(a|s)/\pi_{\theta_{\text{old}}}(a|s)\). It first replaces a constrained TV‑TRPO problem with the unconstrained APC‑Obj, which couples per‑token clipping bounds via an aggregational constraint. Then, via FBG, it decomposes the ratio gating into two orthogonal parts: a trajectory‑level base weight \(w_{\text{base},\tau}\) computed from aggregated positive/negative log‑ratios using a smooth clipping function \(g_{\text{agg}}\), and a token‑level residual weight \(\tilde{r}_{\text{fiber},i}\) computed from a sign‑aware \(\logclip\) that uses the same‑sign and opposite‑sign aggregates. The final gated ratio is \(r_i^{\text{gated}} = w_{\text{base},\tau} \cdot \tilde{r}_{\text{fiber},i}\). For hierarchies, FGH stacks multiple such decompositions along a tree of fibrations, each with its own trust‑region budget, summing the reflected gated contributions from all levels while preserving orthogonality. Training proceeds by maximizing the resulting surrogate objective \(\hat{J}_{\text{FiberPO}}\) with gradient ascent.

## Key Results
- The surrogate objective’s gradient Jacobian is block‑diagonal over trajectories, preventing interference between independent trajectories.
- At the on‑policy point (\(r\equiv 1\)), the Jacobian reduces to the identity, ensuring first‑order agreement with the true policy gradient.
- A restorative gradient component actively counteracts trajectory drift, improving stability.
- The framework extends to arbitrary hierarchies (e.g., domain → prompt‑group → trajectory → token) without new primitives, enabling independent multi‑scale trust‑region control.
- Empirically, FiberPO improves token efficiency over PPO, GRPO, and GSPO and maintains stable learning across diverse domains.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1: APC‑Obj as Unconstrained TV‑TRPO
- **Claim:** Clipping‑based objectives (PPO, GRPO) and trust‑region TRPO are dual formulations via the Aggregational Policy Censoring Objective (APC‑Obj), which exactly reformulates sample‑based TV‑TRPO.
- **Mechanism:** APC‑Obj converts the per‑state TV constraint into coupled per‑token clipping bounds \(B_{s,a}=T_s\delta - \sum_{(a',\tau',t')\neq (a,\tau,t)} |r_{s,a'}-1|\). Maximizing this surrogate yields the same update as TV‑TRPO.
- **Core assumption:** The batch covers all state‑action pairs where \(\pi_\theta\) differs from \(\pi_{\theta_{\text{old}}}\) and satisfies \(\sum_{a\in X_s} n_{s,a} r_{s,a} = T_s\).
- **Evidence anchors:** Abstract states APC‑Obj is an exact unconstrained reformulation; Section 3.2 defines APC‑Obj and Theorem D.10 proves equivalence.
- **Break condition:** Incomplete state‑action coverage leads to misestimated TV and misallocated trust‑region budgets, causing divergence from the intended constraint.

### Mechanism 2: Fiber Bundle Gating (FBG)
- **Claim:** FBG decouples trajectory‑level global drift from token‑level local deviations, producing a ratio gating Jacobian that is block‑diagonal over trajectories, reduces to identity on‑policy, and features a restorative gradient.
- **Mechanism:** Model tokens plus a sign channel as a fiber bundle \(E\to B\) (base = trajectory‑sign pairs). Push token densities to the base via \(\pi_{E*}\), apply a base‑level gate \(g_{\text{Base}}\) with budget \(\delta\), reflect back with a Markov kernel \(K\) satisfying \(\pi_{E*}K=\text{id}_B\), subtract this global component to get a residual, gate the residual with a fiber‑level \(g_{\text{Fiber}}\) using budget \(\varepsilon\), and combine both contributions. The overall gating \(G = R\circ\mathcal{G}\circ F\) yields the desired Jacobian properties.
- **Core assumption:** Atomic gates satisfy \(g_{\text{Base}}(p)=p\), \(g_{\text{Fiber}}(q)=q\) and have derivative 1 at the on‑policy density; the reflecting condition \(\pi_{E*}K=\text{id}_B\) holds exactly.
- **Evidence anchors:** Abstract describes FBG properties; Section 4.2 defines FBG and Theorem 4.5 proves first‑order agreement; Section 5.4 derives the Jacobian (Prop 5.2).
- **Break condition:** If \(\pi_{E*}K\neq\text{id}_B\), the residual retains base‑level information and the fiber gate double‑counts global drift; if gates are not identity at reference density, the Jacobian deviates from \(\mathbf{I}\).

### Mechanism 3: Fibration Gating Hierarchy (FGH)
- **Claim:** Because fibrations compose algebraically, FBG extends naturally to a Fibration Gating Hierarchy (FGH) that supports independent trust‑region budgets at arbitrary hierarchical levels without new primitives.
- **Mechanism:** Build a chain of strata \(B^0\leftarrow B^1\leftarrow\dots\leftarrow B^n\equiv E\) with fibrations \(\pi_k:B^k\to B^{k-1}\). For each stratum \(k\), define a base gate \(g_{\text{Base},k}\) acting on the residual after lower strata, using a kernel \(K_{k-1}\) with \(\pi_{k*}K_{k-2}=\text{id}_{B_{k-1}}\). The overall gating sums reflected gated contributions from all levels: \(\mathcal{G}(\sigma)=\sum_{k=0}^n K^{n\leftarrow k}\bigl(g_{\text{Base},k}(\pi^{k\leftarrow n}_*\sigma)\bigr)\).
- **Core assumption:** Hierarchical categories form a well‑defined tree; reflecting conditions hold at every stratum; atomic gates obey identity/derivative conditions on‑policy.
- **Evidence anchors:** Abstract mentions FGH and FiberPO‑Domain; Section 6.1 formalizes the Uniform Fibration Hierarchy; Section 6.2 derives FiberPO‑Domain (n=3).
- **Break condition:** Overlapping hierarchical classifications (e.g., a trajectory spanning two domains) break the chain structure and cause double‑counting; poorly tuned per‑level budgets can over‑gate and stall learning.

## Foundational Learning

### Importance Sampling Ratio and Policy Gradient Basics
- **Why needed:** FiberPO operates entirely on ratios \(r_{s,a}\); APC‑Obj’s clipping bound and FBG’s pushforward rely on the per‑state identity \(\sum_a \pi_{\theta_{\text{old}}}(a|s) r_{s,a}=1\).
- **Quick check:** "Explain why \(\sum_{a} \pi_{\theta_{\text{old}}}(a|s) r(s,a) = 1\) holds for any \(\pi_\theta\). How does this justify \(\hat D_{\text{TV}}(s) = \frac{1}{T_s}\sum_a n_{s,a}|r_{s,a}-1|\)?"  
  *Answer:* By definition, \(r(s,a)=\pi_\theta(a|s)/\pi_{\theta_{\text{old}}}(a|s)\), so \(\sum_a \pi_{\theta_{\text{old}}}(a|s)r(s,a)=\sum_a \pi_\theta(a|s)=1\). In samples, \(n_{s,a}/T_s\) approximates \(\pi_{\theta_{\text{old}}}(a|s)\), making the weighted absolute sum a valid TV proxy.

### Trust Region Policy Optimization (TRPO) and Total Variation Distance
- **Why needed:** The paper’s motivation stems from the vanishing trust‑region radius at \(\gamma=1\); TV distance is the stability criterion. Understanding the surrogate‑gap bound explains why APC‑Obj relaxes \(\delta\) and why TV remains meaningful at \(\gamma=1\).
- **Quick check:** "Derive the per‑state TV distance \(D_{\text{TV}}(s)\) in terms of \(r_{s,a}\). How does \(\hat D_{\text{TV}}(s)\) approximate it?"  
  *Answer:* \(D_{\text{TV}}(s) = \frac{1}{2}\mathbb{E}_{a\sim\pi_{\theta_{\text{old}}}}[|r_{s,a}-1|]\). With empirical frequencies \(n_{s,a}/T_s \approx \pi_{\theta_{\text{old}}}(a|s)\), \(\hat D_{\text{TV}}(s) = \frac{1}{T_s}\sum_a n_{s,a}|r_{s,a}-1|\) estimates (twice) \(D_{\text{TV}}(s)\) (often using the un‑halved version).

### Algebraic Compositionality and Fibrations
- **Why needed:** FBG and FGH rely on fiber bundle theory to formalize token‑to‑context aggregation and hierarchical decomposition. Pushforward maps, Markov kernels, and reflecting conditions ensure orthogonal decomposition, enabling clean scaling.
- **Quick check:** "Given a uniform fibration hierarchy \(B^0\leftarrow B^1\leftarrow B^2\), write the pushforward \(\pi_*\) from token space \(E=B^2\) to base \(B^0\). Explain how \(\pi_{1*} \circ K_0 = \text{id}_{B^0}\) prevents double‑counting."  
  *Answer:* \(\pi_{0\leftarrow2*} = \pi_{1*} \circ \pi_{2*}\). The kernel \(K_0:B^0\to B^1\) embeds the base density; \(\pi_{1*}K_0=\text{id}\) ensures the reflected density’s projection onto \(B^0\) equals the original, so the residual \(\sigma - K_0(\pi_{0\leftarrow2*}\sigma)\) has zero \(B^0\) component, guaranteeing orthogonal contributions.

## Architecture Onboarding

**Component map**  
apc_obj.py -> rgf.py -> fbg.py -> fiber_po.py, fgh.py -> fiber_po_domain.py, with baselines ppo.py, grpo.py, gspo.py.

**Critical path**  
1. Verify APC‑Obj ↔ TV‑TRPO equivalence on a tiny MDP (analytical and numerical).
2. Implement FBG’s decomposition: compute \(\sigma=F(r)\), base gate \(g_{\text{Base}}(\pi_{E*}\sigma)\), residual \(\sigma-K(\pi_{E*}\sigma)\), fiber gate, and recovery \(R\).
3. Derive FiberPO by applying APC‑Obj’s four relaxation steps and confirm Eq 7.
4. Extend to FGH: define 4‑level fibrations and kernels; ensure per‑level gates use independent \(\delta_k\) and reflecting conditions hold.
5. Integrate into an LLM fine‑tuning loop (e.g., Llama‑3‑8B + reward model) and reproduce token‑efficiency and multi‑domain stability.

**Design tradeoffs**  
- Hyperparameters: total TV budget \(\delta\), split \(C^+ + C^- = \delta\) (recommend \(C^- < C^+\)); token‑level clip \(\varepsilon < C^\pm\). Larger values increase permissiveness but risk instability; smaller values are conservative.
- Rollback slope: \(-T_\tau\) in \(g_{\text{agg}}\) makes the rollback regime narrow for long trajectories, effectively hard‑clipping; desirable for context‑heavy tasks but reduces smoothing.
- Hierarchical budgets: order typically \(\delta_{\text{domain}} \le \delta_{\text{group}} \le \delta_{\text{trajectory}}\); equal budgets may over‑gate some levels.
- Computational cost: \(O(T_\tau)\) overhead per trajectory for aggregates and residuals; negligible vs. forward passes but must be fused with data loading.

**Failure signatures**  
1. Probability constraint violation: Monitor \(\big|\sum_{a\in X_s} n_{s,a} r_{s,a} - T_s\big|\). Persistent deviation indicates coverage issues or renormalization errors.
2. On‑policy identity test: At \(\theta=\theta_{\text{old}}\), compute Jacobian \(\partial G/\partial \log r\) via finite differences; non‑diagonal 1 or off‑diagonal non‑0 implies gate/reflect errors.
3. Double‑counting in hierarchy: Compare total gated weight in FiberPO‑Domain vs. FiberPO‑Trajectory; systematic drift (e.g., smaller weights) suggests broken reflecting conditions at intermediate levels.
4. Rollback skewness: If \(\text{logs}^-_\tau\) consistently dominates and base weight collapses to ~1, \(C^-\) may be too large; reduce relative to \(C^+\).

**First 3 experiments**  
1. *Jacobian block‑diagonality and on‑policy identity.* Create synthetic batch of 3 trajectories (lengths 5,6,7) with random logits. Compute \(\mathcal{G}(r)\) and Jacobian w.r.t. \(\log r\) via finite differences. Verify: zero cross‑trajectory gradients, unit diagonal on‑policy, and rollback slope \(-T_\tau\) when \(\text{logs}^\pm\) exceed budgets.
2. *Token‑efficiency over PPO/GRPO/GSPO on a bandit.* Multi‑armed bandit (10 arms, arm 1 optimal). Train small policy network with each algorithm for 100 updates. Measure probability of optimal action and updates to reach 0.8 probability. FiberPO should reach threshold faster by preserving gradients for sub‑optimal tokens.
3. *Multi‑domain stability with FiberPO‑Domain.* Simulate two independent bandits (A high variance, B low variance), both with a single optimal arm. Use domain‑conditioned policy and train with FiberPO‑Domain, assigning separate \(\delta_A,\delta_B\). Verify improvement in both domains and balanced gradient norms per domain; compare against FiberPO (no domain gating), which may let high‑variance domain dominate.

## Limitations
- Theoretical assumptions (full state‑action coverage, exact pushforward/reflecting conditions) may not hold in large‑scale LLM training.
- Experimental description is sparse; missing ablations against strong baselines on standard RLHF benchmarks.
- Hierarchical budget allocation is manual; no guidance for automatic tuning.

## Confidence
- **Mechanism 1 (APC‑Obj duality):** High – rigorous proof under clear assumptions.
- **Mechanism 2 (FBG structure):** High – derivations verify Jacobian and on‑policy identity.
- **Mechanism 3 (FGH composability):** Medium – algebraically sound but not empirically validated; risk with overlapping categories.

## Next Checks
1. *APC‑Obj ↔ TV‑TRPO equivalence:* Implement both on a tiny MDP (e.g., 2 states, 3 actions) and compare updates under varying coverage conditions.
2. *FBG Jacobian integrity:* With synthetic trajectories, compute \(\partial\mathcal{G}/\partial\log r\) via finite differences; confirm zero cross‑trajectory gradients, unit diagonal on‑policy, and rollback slope \(-T_\tau\) when \(\text{logs}^\pm\) exceed budgets.
3. *FGH orthogonal contributions:* Construct a 3‑level hierarchy (domain → group → token) with independent \(\delta_k\); verify per‑level gated weights sum to total weight and that violating \(\pi_{k*}K_{k-1}=\text{id}\) causes double‑counting.
