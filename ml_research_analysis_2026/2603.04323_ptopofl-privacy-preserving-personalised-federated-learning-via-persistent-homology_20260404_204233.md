# PTOPOFL: Privacy-Preserving Personalised Federated Learning via Persistent Homology

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2603.04323  
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.04323  
- Reference count: 40  
- Primary result: Achieves state-of-the-art AUCs (0.841 healthcare, 0.910 benchmark) and reduces reconstruction risk by 4.5× compared to gradient sharing.

## Executive Summary
PTOPOFL introduces a novel federated learning framework that replaces gradient communication with persistent homology (PH) descriptors to simultaneously address privacy leakage and non-IID data degradation. Each client maps its local dataset to a 48-dimensional topological summary that encodes multi-scale connectivity; this many-to-one abstraction makes inverting the descriptor to recover raw data provably ill-posed. Clients are then clustered by the Wasserstein distance between their PH descriptors, and intra-cluster model aggregation uses topology‑weighted averaging to mitigate gradient divergence. An anomaly detector identifies adversarial clients based on descriptor outliers. The theoretical analysis supports information contraction and linear convergence with a smaller error floor than FedAvg. Empirically, PTOPOFL attains superior AUCs on healthcare and benchmark tasks, remains robust to poisoning attacks, and extends to deep neural networks.

## Method Summary
Clients compute a 48‑dimensional PH descriptor (ϕ_k) from a subsample of their local data via Vietoris‑Rips filtration. The descriptors are sent to the server, which performs hierarchical agglomerative clustering once (Round 0) on the ℓ2‑normalized ϕ_k to assign clients to M clusters. In every training round, the server computes a trust weight t_k for each client by measuring how outlier its descriptor is relative to the population. For each cluster, local models are aggregated with weights proportional to n_k·exp(−‖ϕ_k−ϕ_C‖₂)·t_k, then blended with a global consensus model (blending factor β_blend). The resulting personalized cluster models are broadcast back. Clients track descriptor drift over rounds; if any client’s topological shift Δ_k exceeds a threshold, clustering is recomputed. For deep models, PH is computed on penultimate‑layer activations instead of raw data.

## Key Results
- State‑of‑the‑art AUCs: 0.841 (healthcare scenario) and 0.910 (benchmark scenario) under severe non‑IID conditions.
- Reconstruction risk reduced by a factor of 4.5× compared to conventional gradient sharing.
- Robustness: graceful degradation under up to 30% adversarial clients; linear convergence with a strictly smaller error floor than FedAvg.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

1. **Persistent homology descriptors reduce information leakage**  
   The mapping Φ : dataset → 48‑D persistence diagram is many‑to‑one, so reconstructing individual samples from Φ is an ill‑posed inverse problem. Theorem 3.7 quantifies information contraction: I(x_i; ϕ_k) ≤ (m/p)·(c²/L²)·I(x_i; ∇F_k(w)) when m ≪ p. This structural privacy is not formal (ε,δ)-DP but significantly raises the bar for inversion attacks.

2. **Topology‑weighted clustering mitigates non‑IID degradation**  
   Clients with similar data geometry (small Wasserstein distance between PH descriptors) tend to have aligned gradients. By grouping such clients and weighting aggregations by exp(−λ·W_p(PD_k, ¯PD)), the effective gradient divergence term B²_α is reduced. Theorem 3.9 proves linear convergence with a smaller error floor than FedAvg when the number of clusters M > 1. The global consensus blend (β_blend) prevents over‑specialization.

3. **Anomaly detection via descriptor outliers**  
   Adversarial data poisoning typically distorts the local distribution’s topology, making its PH descriptor stand out. The server computes each client’s mean Euclidean distance δ_k to all others; a high z‑score triggers an exponential trust decay t_k = exp(−max(z_k−1,0)). Theorem 3.5 guarantees that if adversarial clients have mean distance at least Δ larger than honest ones, their total aggregation weight becomes ≤ ε² when λΔ ≥ log((1−ε)/ε).

## Foundational Learning
- **Persistent Homology (PH)**: A topological data analysis tool that captures multi‑scale features (connected components, loops, voids) of a point cloud. The many‑to‑one nature of PH is key to privacy; it provides a compact, stable geometric summary. *Quick check*: Why does the many‑to‑one mapping of PH improve privacy over transmitting gradients?
- **Wasserstein Distance between Persistence Diagrams**: A metric on the space of PH diagrams used to compare topological summaries. It enables principled clustering and weighting, and its stability (Eq. 4) ties it to the underlying data’s Hausdorff distance. *Quick check*: How does p‑Wasserstein differ from Euclidean distance on the descriptor vector?

## Architecture Onboarding
Client-side processing: Local data → Subsample → Vietoris‑Rips filtration → Persistence diagram PD_k → 48‑D descriptor ϕ_k → Augment local features (e.g., distance to centroid, Betti entropy) → Local SGD → Transmit ϕ_k and model update.

Server-side operations: Receive ϕ_k → (Round 0) Hierarchical clustering on normalized ϕ_k → Each round: compute trust weights t_k from δ_k → Intra‑cluster aggregation: θ_Cj = Σ_{k∈C_j} w_k θ_k with w_k ∝ n_k·exp(−‖ϕ_k−ϕ_Cj‖)·t_k → Inter‑cluster blending: θ*_Cj = (1−β_blend)θ_Cj + β_blend ¯θ → Broadcast θ*_Cj to cluster members → Track ϕ_k^{(r)} for drift Δ_k.

Critical path: Clustering (O(K²)) is done once; clusters persist unless any client’s topological drift Δ_k exceeds a threshold, triggering re‑clustering. The abort condition is Δ_k > threshold for any client.

Design tradeoffs:
- **Communication**: PH descriptors (48‑D) drastically reduce payload versus full gradients (10⁴–10⁶ dims), but descriptor computation is O(n_k²).
- **Computation**: Vietoris‑Rips PH is O(n_k³) worst‑case; mitigated by subsampling (n_sub = 80–200). For deep models, PH runs on penultimate‑layer activations.
- **Personalization vs. generalization**: β_blend controls the blend of cluster and global models; ablation shows β_blend = 0.3 optimal.
- **Cluster count M**: Manually set (e.g., 2 or 3) based on expected heterogeneity; automatic selection is an open issue.

Failure signatures:
- **High topological drift Δ_k**: Indicates client distribution shift; requires re‑clustering. If ignored, assignments become stale.
- **Adversarial mimicry**: A poisoning attack that preserves the client’s PH descriptor (topology‑preserving) avoids detection; trust weight remains high and attack succeeds. Threshold τ must be finely tuned.
- **Poor cluster separation**: If inter‑cluster Wasserstein gaps are small, descriptor noise can change assignments. Monitor gaps; increase n_sub or Betti curve resolution L for more discriminative descriptors.

First 3 experiments:
1. **Healthcare Scenario**: 8 hospitals (2 adversarial). Result: PTOPOFL AUC 0.841 vs FedAvg 0.790; adversarial influence is gracefully suppressed.
2. **Benchmark Scenario**: 10 clients with pathological class‑imbalance skew. Result: AUC 0.910; SCAFFOLD fails (0.846).
3. **Deep Model Transfer**: CIFAR‑10 (ResNet‑18) and FEMNIST (ConvNet‑2). Result: +3‑6% top‑1 accuracy over FedAvg under Dirichlet α=0.1, confirming scalability to non‑convex losses.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
1. **Formal differential privacy for PH descriptors** – Section 6 tasks deriving (ε,δ)-DP bounds for the PH abstraction operator and studying composition with secure aggregation. Current analysis is limited to information contraction, not worst‑case indistinguishability.
2. **Convergence for non‑convex losses** – Remark 3.10 notes that extending the convergence theory to deep models requires bounding the Lipschitz constant in non‑convex settings, which remains open.
3. **Automatic cluster count M selection** – Section 6 suggests automating M via Wasserstein gap or silhouette scores to avoid manual specification.
4. **Evaluation on real federated healthcare datasets** – Experiments used synthetic splits; evaluation on datasets like MIMIC or ChestX‑ray 14 is needed to confirm practical relevance.
5. **Precise compression factor α_c for PH** – The privacy bound (Eq. 11) uses an empirical α_c≈0.1; a rigorous characterization for specific data families would strengthen the privacy claims.

## Limitations
- Privacy guarantee is not differential privacy; it relies on the heuristic hardness of inverting a many‑to‑one topological map.
- Clustering stability depends on descriptor discriminativeness; small inter‑cluster Wasserstein gaps can cause reassignment under noise or subsampling variation.
- The anomaly detector assumes honest clients’ descriptors form a tight cluster; natural topological outliers in real federations could be mis‑flagged.

## Confidence
- Privacy improvement via PH descriptors: Medium  
- Mitigation of non‑IID degradation via topology‑weighted clustering: High  
- Robustness to adversarial clients via topology‑based anomaly detection: Medium  
- Overall performance and linear convergence: High (with noted scalability concerns)

## Next Checks
1. **Systematic inversion study**: Implement gradient‑descent reconstruction from PH descriptors and measure recovery fidelity across varying sample sizes and dimensions to quantify the ill‑posedness.
2. **Clustering stability audit**: Run hierarchical clustering with multiple random subsamples; compute adjusted Rand index to assess consistency; correlate inter‑cluster Wasserstein distances with gradient alignment to validate the core assumption.
3. **Topology‑preserving poisoning test**: Design a label‑flip attack that keeps the client’s PD within the honest cluster distribution; evaluate the anomaly detector’s detection rate and the resulting impact on global model AUC.
