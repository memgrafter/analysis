# Positional Segmentor-Guided Counterfactual Fine-Tuning for Spatially Localized Image Synthesis

## Quick Facts
- **ArXiv ID**: 2603.21213  
- **Source URL**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.21213  
- **Reference count**: 0  
- **Primary result**: Pos-Seg-CFT enables spatially localized counterfactual image synthesis by subdividing anatomical structures into regional segments and using segmentation-derived measurements per region for fine-tuning, achieving lower intervention error in targeted regions and reduced off-target changes compared to baselines.

## Executive Summary
Counterfactual image generation aims to synthesize images under hypothetical interventions, but existing methods often produce global changes that lack spatial precision. This limitation hinders applications like modeling disease progression in specific anatomical subregions. The proposed Positional Segmentor-guided Counterfactual Fine-Tuning (Pos-Seg-CFT) addresses this by introducing regional segmentation guidance. It partitions each structure of interest (e.g., coronary artery) into predefined segments (proximal, mid, distal) and computes independent measurements (e.g., plaque area) within each segment using a frozen segmentation model. These regional values serve as targets during fine-tuning of a pretrained Deep Structural Causal Model (DSCM), steering the generator to alter structure only in the intervened region.

On a dataset of coronary CT angiography, Pos-Seg-CFT quantitatively reduces intervention error in the targeted region relative to both non-fine-tuned (No-CFT) and standard region-agnostic CFT (Reg-CFT) baselines. Qualitatively, difference maps exhibit anatomically coherent modifications confined to the intended segment, with minimal changes elsewhere. This capability enables finer-grained simulation of spatially localized pathologies, offering a powerful tool for medical image analysis and planning.

## Method Summary
The approach starts with a pretrained Deep Structural Causal Model (DSCM) comprising a hierarchical variational autoencoder (HVAE) for image generation conditioned on causal parent variables representing anatomical structures. A separate segmentation model (segmentor) is trained beforehand and kept frozen. For counterfactual generation, the DSCM first performs abduction: it infers latent noise variables from a given source image. Then, with an intervention that sets desired target values for specific regional variables (e.g., plaque area in the proximal segment), the DSCM predicts a counterfactual image.

During Counterfactual Fine-Tuning (CFT), only the HVAE parameters are updated. The loss for a generated counterfactual image is computed as follows: the frozen segmentor produces a probability map of the structure; this map is multiplied by binary masks corresponding to each predefined region (e.g., proximal, mid, distal) and summed to obtain scalar measurements. The total loss is the sum of L1 (or L2) distances between these predicted measurements and the intervention’s target values. By applying loss terms only within the masked regions, the generator learns to modify structure precisely in the intervened region while preserving others. The design requires no pixel-level target masks and reuses a single segmentor across all regions.

## Key Results
- Pos-Seg-CFT achieves significantly lower intervention error (effectiveness metric) in the targeted region compared to No-CFT and Reg-CFT baselines.
- Off-target changes in non-intervened regions are reduced relative to Reg-CFT, demonstrating improved spatial specificity.
- Difference maps show anatomically coherent, region-specific modifications without global artifacts.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1: Spatial supervision via positional segmentation
- **Claim**: Positional segmentation-derived measurements provide spatially-aware supervision signals that guide the generator to produce localized structural changes.
- **Mechanism**: A pretrained, frozen segmentor predicts a segmentation map for each generated counterfactual image. This map is masked by predefined anatomical region masks (e.g., proximal, mid, distal) and summed within each region to yield scalar measurements (e.g., plaque area). These region-specific scalars are used as target values in the fine-tuning loss, encouraging modifications only within the intervened region.
- **Core assumption**: The segmentor's predictions accurately capture the spatial extent and composition of the structure, and its representations align with intervention goals.
- **Evidence**: The abstract and Section 3 describe the regional measurement computation and its role. Related work supports the trend of segmentation-guided generation, though positional subdivision is novel.
- **Break condition**: Segmentor failure to correctly segment specific regions (e.g., due to anatomical variants) leads to incorrect measurements and misguided fine-tuning, causing spatially incoherent outputs.

### Mechanism 2: Loss-based disentanglement of regional representations
- **Claim**: The Pos-Seg-CFT loss function explicitly penalizes discrepancies between generated and target regional measurements, causally disentangling representations of adjacent anatomical segments.
- **Mechanism**: The total fine-tuning loss is the sum of individual losses (e.g., L1) over all regions. Minimizing this multi-term loss forces the generator’s latent space to independently modulate structural attributes for each region, reducing the entanglement that causes global changes in Reg-CFT.
- **Core assumption**: The generator has sufficient capacity to satisfy multiple, spatially disjoint constraint objectives without interference.
- **Evidence**: Results show consistently lower error in intervened regions and often reduced error in non-intervened regions compared to Reg-CFT, indicating better spatial specificity. The corpus provides limited direct evidence.
- **Break condition**: Overlapping or anatomically implausible region definitions cause conflicting loss terms, preventing the model from satisfying all constraints and potentially degrading image quality or causing leakage at boundaries.

### Mechanism 3: Practical reuse of a frozen segmentor
- **Claim**: Reusing a single frozen segmentor with positional masks avoids the need for user-defined counterfactual masks or training new models, making spatially localized interventions practical and scalable.
- **Mechanism**: During fine-tuning, the same pretrained segmentor processes all generated images; only the fixed binary masks change per region. No pixel-level target masks are required—only scalar regional target values. The segmentor remains unchanged.
- **Core assumption**: The segmentor’s internal representations remain valid when its output is cropped; predicting structure probability in a sub-region is a stable task.
- **Evidence**: The abstract notes the flexibility and applicability to arbitrary region definitions, and Section 3 details the masking procedure. Related work often requires explicit masks, highlighting the novelty.
- **Break condition**: If the segmentor relies heavily on global context, masking may yield semantically invalid or uncertain regional measurements, destabilizing the fine-tuning signal.

## Architecture Onboarding

**Component map**:  
Pretrained DSCM (HVAE + Normalizing Flows) generates images → frozen segmentor (ψ) processes them → positional masks (R_k) crop segmentor output → regional measurement calculator sums probabilities → loss module computes per-region errors and updates DSCM parameters.

**Critical path**:
1. For a given source image, DSCM performs abduction to obtain latent noise z.
2. Set intervened parent values (target regional measurements) and generate counterfactual image ex = g_θ(z, fpax).
3. Feed ex to frozen segmentor ψ to get segmentation map bm.
4. For each region k, apply mask R_k to bm and sum to get predicted measurement cpa_x^(k).
5. Compute loss L(cpa_x^(k), fpa_x^(k)) (sum over k).
6. Backpropagate to update DSCM parameters θ, ϕ; keep ψ and masks fixed.

**Design tradeoffs**:
- **Segmentor accuracy vs. generalization**: An accurate segmentor on training data yields clean signals but may fail on out-of-distribution counterfactuals; a more robust segmentor might provide noisier but more consistent gradients.
- **Region definition granularity**: More regions (higher K) offer finer control but increase loss terms and risk of optimization conflicts; requires more precise anatomical landmarks.
- **Frozen segmentor vs. joint training**: Freezing ψ is cheap and avoids forgetting but limits the signal to fixed knowledge; joint training could adapt ψ to counterfactual domains but risks destabilizing spatial semantics and is computationally heavier.

**Failure signatures**:
- **Quantitative**: High intervention error in non-targeted regions comparable to Reg-CFT, indicating failed spatial disentanglement.
- **Qualitative**: Difference maps show changes leaking into adjacent regions or appearing globally, not confined to the intervened R_k.
- **Training**: Loss for a targeted region stagnates at a high value while others optimize, suggesting conflicting constraints or segmentor inability to distinguish the region.
- **Visual**: Generated images exhibit unrealistic artifacts (e.g., broken vessel continuity) at region boundaries, implying the model "paints" to satisfy scalar loss without spatial coherence.

**First 3 experiments**:
1. **Measurement extraction validation**: Apply the segmentor and mask pipeline to a held-out set of real images; manually verify that summed regional plaque values correlate with human estimates of area in those regions.
2. **Single-region intervention stress test**: Perform a large-magnitude intervention (e.g., do(CPA-proximal) with a big Δ). Compare Pos-Seg-CFT vs. Reg-CFT on error in non-intervened regions (mid, distal). Success: Pos-Seg-CFT shows significantly lower error in those regions.
3. **Boundary coherence visualization**: For interventions on adjacent regions (e.g., do(CPA-mid)), generate difference maps. Inspect whether changes are sharply confined within R_mid with a clean transition at mask edges (success) versus a gradient crossing the boundary (failure).

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
1. **Generalization to irregular anatomies**: The method was evaluated only on linearly partitioned coronary arteries; its applicability to branching or curved structures remains unknown. Validation on diverse 3D datasets with alternative region definitions would be needed.
2. **Segmentor robustness on counterfactuals**: The frozen segmentor may produce unreliable predictions on extreme counterfactuals, potentially degrading the fine-tuning signal. Requires analysis of segmentor accuracy on generated images across intervention magnitudes.
3. **Handling interdependencies between regions**: The method assumes regional independence, but real plaque dynamics may involve cross-region influences. Introducing synthetic or observed dependencies and comparing to a version that models them would test this assumption.
4. **Extrapolation beyond observed data**: Interventions are limited to the empirical range; performance on out-of-distribution values (e.g., extreme plaque burden) is untested. Systematic evaluation on extrapolated targets is required.
5. **Optimal region granularity**: The number of positional regions was fixed to three; trade-offs between spatial precision and learning stability are unexplored. An empirical study varying K (e.g., 2, 3, 5, 10) is needed to identify a practical upper bound.

## Limitations
- Replication details missing: DSCM architecture specifics (network sizes, conditioning, flows, abduction), fine-tuning hyperparameters (learning rates, epochs, loss weighting), exact region mask definitions, and segmentor training protocol.
- The frozen segmentor assumption may be brittle if counterfactuals significantly alter anatomy, leading to erroneous measurement signals.
- The evaluation was restricted to a single dataset (coronary CCTA sCPR), limiting generalizability claims.

## Confidence
- **Mechanism 1** (spatially-aware supervision): **High** – strongly supported by equations and logical soundness.
- **Mechanism 2** (loss disentanglement): **Medium** – quantitative results support but causal link to loss design is inferential.
- **Mechanism 3** (frozen segmentor practicality): **Low** – core assumption unvalidated; segmentor failure on counterfactuals could break the pipeline.
