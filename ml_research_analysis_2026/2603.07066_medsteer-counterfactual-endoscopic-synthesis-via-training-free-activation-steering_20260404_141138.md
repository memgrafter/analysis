# MedSteer: Counterfactual Endoscopic Synthesis via Training-Free Activation Steering

## Quick Facts
- **ArXiv ID:** 2603.07066  
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.07066  
- **Reference count:** 35  
- **Primary result:** MedSteer achieves counterfactual flip rates up to 0.950 and superior structural preservation (Bg‑LPIPS 0.1449) on endoscopic datasets, outperforming inversion‑based baselines and improving downstream polyp detection AUC to 0.9755.

## Executive Summary
Diffusion models struggle to generate anatomy‑matched counterfactual medical image pairs because small semantic edits often alter non‑target structures. MedSteer introduces a training‑free activation steering method that operates within a frozen diffusion transformer. By estimating “pathology vectors” from contrastive prompt pairs in the cross‑attention layers, it learns a linear direction that separates a target clinical concept (e.g., polyp) from its counterfactual (e.g., normal mucosa). During inference, with a shared noise seed, it generates a pair of images: one unsteered and one steered via Spatially Selective Pathology Steering (SSPS), which subtracts only the concept‑aligned component of activations while preserving all other features. Evaluations on Kvasir v3 and HyperKvasir show high flip rates (0.800–0.950), excellent background fidelity (Bg‑LPIPS 0.1449), and significant gains in downstream polyp detection (AUC 0.9755 vs. 0.9083 for re‑prompting).

## Method Summary
MedSteer uses a frozen PixArt‑α diffusion transformer (DiT) fine‑tuned on endoscopic images. The method has two stages:

1. **Offline pathology vector estimation.** For a contrastive concept pair (e.g., “polyp” vs “normal cecum”), prompts are passed through the DiT over multiple random seeds. Cross‑attention outputs at selected layers and timesteps are collected, averaged across seeds and spatial tokens for each prompt, and the L2‑normalized difference (`v_{l,t}`) becomes the pathology vector. This vector encodes a linear direction in activation space that isolates the target concept.

2. **Inference with SSPS.** For a fixed noise seed `z`, two generation branches run in parallel: (a) unsteered standard generation with prompt `T_pos`; (b) steered generation where, at layers `l ∈ [L_s, L_e]` and all timesteps, each activation `h_{l,t}` is updated as `h'_{l,t} = h_{l,t} - α ⋅ σ_{l,t} ⋅ v_{l,t}`. The gate `σ_{l,t} = max(⟨h_{l,t}, v_{l,t}⟩, 0)` is a cosine‑similarity weighting that ensures only tokens aligned with the pathology vector are affected. Because both branches share the same initial noise and all layers up to the intervention are identical, any difference between outputs arises solely from SSPS, guaranteeing anatomy‑matched counterfactuals by construction.

## Key Results
- Counterfactual flip rates of **0.800, 0.925, and 0.950** across three clinical concept pairs, surpassing inversion‑based baselines.
- Background preservation measured by **Bg‑LPIPS = 0.1449**, indicating minimal structural change.
- Downstream polyp detection augmentation yields a **ViT AUC of 0.9755**, significantly higher than the 0.9083 achieved with quantity‑matched re‑prompting.
- Dye disentanglement achieves **75% dye removal** compared to 20% (PnP) and 10% (h‑Edit).

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
**Claim:** Pathology vectors estimated from contrastive prompt pairs encode a linear direction that separates a target clinical concept from its counterfactual in the model's cross‑attention space.  
**Mechanism:** For concept `P`, prompts `T_pos` (with `P`) and `T_neg` (without `P`) are processed through a frozen DiT. Their cross‑attention outputs `h_{l,t}` (per layer `l`, timestep `t`) are averaged over multiple seeds and spatial tokens. The mean‑difference (`¯h_pos - ¯h_neg`) is L2‑normalized into a unit vector `v_{l,t}`, capturing the shared semantic difference attributable to `P`. The core assumption is that the difference in text‑conditioned activations for a concept is stable across seeds and corresponds to a linear direction (linear representation hypothesis). Evidence comes from the paper’s derivation in Section 3.2 and experiments showing consistent steering.

### Mechanism 2
**Claim:** Spatially Selective Pathology Steering (SSPS) suppresses only the concept‑aligned component of activations using a cosine‑similarity gate, preserving non‑target structure by construction.  
**Mechanism:** During generation with a shared noise seed, SSPS computes per‑token alignment `σ_{l,t} = max(⟨h_{l,t}, v_{l,t}⟩, 0)` at selected layers and timesteps. The activation is updated as `h'_{l,t} = h_{l,t} - α σ_{l,t} v_{l,t}`, subtracting only the component parallel to `v_{l,t}` scaled by strength `α`. Tokens expressing the concept more strongly receive proportionally stronger steering. The orthogonal components encoding anatomy, texture, and viewpoint remain untouched. Evidence includes visualizations (Section 4.6) showing spatially selective activation footprints and comparisons with baselines.

### Mechanism 3
**Claim:** Using an identical noise seed for both unsteered and steered inferences guarantees that all differences between the generated pair arise solely from the SSPS intervention, making counterfactuals anatomy‑matched by construction.  
**Mechanism:** Both generation branches start from the same noise `z`. The unsteered branch runs the frozen DiT normally. The steered branch inserts SSPS at the same layers/timesteps. Because diffusion trajectories are deterministic given a fixed seed and model, the non‑target structure (layout, texture, viewpoint) is fully determined by early diffusion steps and the shared seed; any later divergence is solely due to activation subtraction. This design guarantee is stated in the abstract and Section 3.3.

## Foundational Learning
- **Cross‑Attention in Diffusion Transformers**  
  *Why needed:* MedSteer intervenes exclusively on cross‑attention outputs because they are the sole conduit for text semantics to influence image tokens. Knowing *where* to steer is essential.  
  *Quick check:* In a DiT, which attention module’s output directly incorporates text prompt information into the visual token processing stream?

- **Contrastive Prompt Design for Concept Isolation**  
  *Why needed:* The pathology vector relies on prompts that differ only in the target concept. Poor prompt design (e.g., changing context words) causes `v_{l,t}` to capture confounders instead of the pathology.  
  *Quick check:* For the concept “dyed lifted polyp,” what must be identical between `T_pos` (“An endoscopic image of a dyed lifted polyp”) and `T_neg` (“An endoscopic image of a polyp”) to ensure `v_{l,t}` captures only the dye attribute?

- **Linear Representation Hypothesis in Latent Spaces**  
  *Why needed:* MedSteer assumes a concept is a linear direction (`v_{l,t}`) in activation space. This underlies the subtraction operation; if concepts are represented non‑linearly, the steering vector will be an imperfect proxy.  
  *Quick check:* If concept `C` is linearly represented, what geometric operation on activations (e.g., addition, subtraction) should ideally toggle `C` on/off while preserving other attributes?

## Architecture Onboarding

### Component map
Shared noise `z` + prompt → Frozen DiT Backbone → SSPS Module (uses Pathology Vector Store) → Generated counterfactual pair (with Noise Seed Manager ensuring seed consistency across branches).

### Critical path
1. Offline: For each concept pair `(T_pos, T_neg)` and a set of random seeds `Z`, run forward passes through the frozen DiT to capture cross‑attention outputs `h_{l,t,n}`. Compute `¯h_pos`, `¯h_neg` by averaging over seeds and tokens; then compute `v_{l,t} = normalize(¯h_pos - ¯h_neg)` and store in the Pathology Vector Store.
2. Online: For a chosen seed `z`, produce an unsteered image using `T_pos` via standard DiT generation. Produce a steered image by injecting SSPS at layers `l ∈ [L_s, L_e]` for all `t`, using the precomputed `v_{l,t}` and steering strength `α`. The two outputs form an anatomy‑matched counterfactual pair.

### Design tradeoffs
- **Layer range `[L_s, L_e]`** – Early layers capture global layout; late layers capture fine details. Ablations show layers 8–16 optimal; narrower windows reduce flip rate.
- **Steering strength `α`** – Too low yields insufficient concept flip; too high causes over‑suppression. Paper identifies `α=2.5` best for Polyp→Normal Cecum.
- **Number of seeds `Z` for vector estimation** – More seeds stabilize `v_{l,t}`. Convergence appears around `Z=50`.

### Failure signatures
- **Non‑target drift:** High Bg‑LPIPS indicates poor seed sharing, mis‑calibrated SSPS gating, or low‑quality `v_{l,t}`.
- **Low concept flip:** Flip rate below expectation suggests inappropriate `α`, wrong layer window, or prompt design that fails to isolate the concept.
- **Over‑suppression (e.g., high DDR in dye experiment):** `v_{l,t}` is entangled with morphology; requires a cleaner negative prompt.

### First experiments
1. **Downstream polyp detection augmentation** – Train detectors on real data augmented with MedSteer pairs; measure AUC on HyperKvasir. Expected: MedSteer yields AUC 0.9755, significantly above quantity‑matched re‑prompting (0.9083).
2. **Counterfactual generation across clinical pairs** – For Polyp→Normal Cecum, evaluate flip rate and Bg‑LPIPS. Expected: flip ≈ 0.8, Bg‑LPIPS ≈ 0.145, better than PnP/h‑Edit.
3. **Dye disentanglement** – Steer “dyed lifted polyp”→“polyp”. Measure dye detection rate (DDR). Expected: DDR ≈ 0.25 (successful dye removal) vs. ~0.8–0.9 for baselines.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
1. **Generalization to other modalities and 3D data**  
   *Basis:* The conclusion explicitly states future work will extend to 3D volumetric data, video endoscopy, and cross‑institutional deployment.  
   *Why unresolved:* Experiments were limited to 2D endoscopic images (Kvasir v3, HyperKvasir). Cross‑attention representations in a DiT trained on gastrointestinal scenes may not transfer to CT, MRI, or volumetric data without adaptation.  
   *Resolution evidence:* Apply MedSteer to a diffusion model trained on a 3D medical dataset (e.g., chest CT) and generate counterfactual volumes; assess concept flip (via oracular classifier) and structural preservation (e.g., volumetric LPIPS, Dice on unaffected tissues).

2. **Temporal consistency in counterfactual videos**  
   *Basis:* The conclusion mentions “video endoscopy sequences” as future work, indicating video is not currently supported.  
   *Why unresolved:* Current implementation treats each frame independently with a shared seed per frame. Without temporal coordination, flickering and anatomical drift would occur across frames.  
   *Resolution evidence:* Adapt MedSteer to a video diffusion model (e.g., by incorporating temporal attention or propagating steering signals across frames). Generate interventional video pairs and measure smoothness with FVD and temporal LPIPS; compare against a frame‑wise baseline.

3. **Automatic hyperparameter selection**  
   *Basis:* Ablations (Table 4) show performance depends heavily on layer window and `α`. The authors manually identified optimal settings (layers 8–16, `α=2.5`) for one concept pair; no general automated procedure is proposed.  
   *Why unresolved:* Clinical concepts vary in representation depth and granularity; manual tuning for each new concept limits scalability and may introduce bias.  
   *Resolution evidence:* Design an automatic hyperparameter optimization (e.g., using a small validation set to maximize flip rate while minimizing background change). Evaluate on all three concept pairs; success if automated parameters match or exceed manual performance.

4. **Robustness to domain shifts across hospitals/endoscopes**  
   *Basis:* “Cross‑institutional deployment” is listed as future work, meaning multi‑center robustness is untested.  
   *Why unresolved:* Datasets (Kvasir v3, HyperKvasir) likely come from limited sources. Differences in hardware, lighting, resolution, and preprocessing could alter activation distributions, making a pathology vector learned in one domain less effective in another.  
   *Resolution evidence:* Test MedSteer on endoscopic images from multiple hospitals with diverse equipment (or simulate style shifts). Measure degradation in flip rate and Bg‑LPIPS compared to original domain. Consistent performance would establish robustness.

## Limitations
- **Undisclosed fine‑tuning details:** The method depends on a specific PixArt‑α checkpoint and LoRA hyperparameters (rank, modules, training steps), which are not fully described, hindering exact replication.
- **Prompt design sensitivity:** Contrastive prompts must isolate the target concept perfectly; small wording changes may introduce confounders, compromising pathology vector purity.
- **Reliance on oracular classifiers:** Evaluation uses classifiers with reported AUC 0.98; their robustness is unverified, potentially inflating performance metrics.
- **Limited generalization:** Validated only on 2D endoscopy and a single diffusion architecture; multi‑concept steering remains unexplored.

## Confidence
- **Counterfactual generation quality (flip & preservation):** Medium – Strong ablations on two datasets but confined to one model and domain.
- **Linear pathology vector assumption:** Low‑Medium – Inferred from performance; no direct validation of linearity or disentanglement in cross‑attention space.
- **Downstream task improvement:** Medium – Substantial AUC gain on one detection task; needs broader validation.
