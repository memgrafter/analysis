# DiReCT: Disentangled Regularization of Contrastive Trajectories for Physics-Refined Video Generation

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2603.25931
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.25931
- **Reference count:** 40
- **Primary result:** Applied to Wan 2.1-1.3B, DiReCT improves the physical commonsense score on VideoPhy by 16.7% relative to the baseline and 11.3% over SFT, while achieving the highest WorldModelBench total score (5.68) among compared models, without increasing training time.

## Executive Summary
Flow-matching video generators often produce physically implausible content because their training objectives do not explicitly distinguish between plausible and implausible dynamics. Contrastive flow matching, which could address this, fails under text-conditioned generation due to semantic-physics entanglement: positive and negative video prompts that are semantically similar share overlapping velocity-field structures, causing the contrastive gradient to oppose the flow-matching objective. DiReCT resolves this with entanglement-aware multi-scale contrastive learning. A macro-scale term uses partition-exclusive semantically distant negatives to separate global trajectories without interference. A micro-scale term uses LLM-generated physics-perturbed hard negatives to target fine-grained physical distinctions. A velocity-space distributional anchor prevents catastrophic forgetting of pretrained knowledge. When applied to the Wan 2.1-1.3B model, DiReCT raises the Physical Commonsense score on VideoPhy by 16.7% relative to the baseline and 11.3% over supervised fine-tuning (SFT), and achieves the highest WorldModelBench total score (5.68) among compared models, with no increase in training time.

## Method Summary
DiReCT augments conditional flow matching training with a multi-scale contrastive regularization that disentangles semantic and physical factors. In the **macro** phase, training prompts are clustered via k-means on frozen text-encoder embeddings. For a positive prompt from cluster *Cᵢ*, random negatives are sampled exclusively from other clusters *Cⱼ* (*j* ≠ *i*), ensuring large velocity-field gaps. In the **micro** phase, an LLM (Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct) generates a hard-negative prompt by minimally perturbing the positive along one of five physics dimensions while preserving scene semantics. This hard-negative is rendered by the base model offline, and its latent is cached. During online training, the loss combines: (1) standard flow matching (*L*<sub>FM</sub>) on positives; (2) a contrastive term (*L*<sub>rand</sub>) pushing apart positives and random negatives; (3) a contrastive term (*L*<sub>hard</sub>) focusing on physics-relevant distinctions using hard negatives; and (4) a velocity-space anchor loss (*L*<sub>anc</sub>) that regularizes the current velocity field toward a frozen reference copy. The weights (λ<sub>rand</sub>, λ<sub>hard</sub>, λ<sub>anc</sub>) are tuned sequentially. This design ensures contrastive gradients align with, rather than oppose, the flow-matching objective, enabling effective physics learning without harming reconstruction or semantic adherence.

## Key Results
- Physical Commonsense score on VideoPhy improved by 16.7% relative to the baseline and 11.3% over SFT.
- Highest WorldModelBench total score (5.68) among all compared models.
- No increase in training time relative to the baseline flow-matching model.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1: Gradient Conflict in Naive Contrastive Flow Matching
**Claim:** Naive contrastive flow matching fails under text-conditioned video generation because semantic-physics entanglement causes the contrastive gradient to oppose the flow-matching gradient when positive and negative samples share velocity-field structure.
**Mechanism:** The combined gradient direction depends on the alignment between the flow-matching residual (*u*⁺ − *v*) and the velocity gap δ = *u*⁺ − *u*⁻. When prompts are semantically similar, δ → 0, making the self-interference term −∥*u*⁺ − *v*∥² dominate, yielding ⟨*g*<sub>FM</sub>, *g*<sub>c</sub>⟩ < 0 (opposition).
**Core assumption:** Text prompts couple scene content (semantics) with physical behavior (dynamics), so nearby embeddings in text space have overlapping velocity fields.
**Evidence anchors:** Abstract and Sec. 4: "...semantic-physics entanglement... naive negative sampling draws conditions whose velocity fields largely overlap... causing the contrastive gradient to directly oppose the flow-matching objective." No direct citation from neighbor corpus; the mechanism is novel.
**Break condition:** Conflict avoided when ⟨*u*⁺ − *v*, δ⟩ > ∥*u*⁺ − *v*∥². Requires either large δ (semantically distant negatives) or a δ aligned with the physics-relevant subspace of (*u*⁺ − *v*).

### Mechanism 2: Macro-Contrastive Sampling (MaNS) for Global Trajectory Separation
**Claim:** Partition-exclusive macro-contrastive sampling provides interference-free global separation by ensuring negatives have large velocity gaps with positives.
**Mechanism:** Prompts are clustered (k-means) on frozen text-encoder embeddings. For a positive from cluster *Cᵢ*, negatives are sampled only from other clusters *Cⱼ* (*j* ≠ *i*). By construction, cross-cluster prompts share minimal velocity-field structure, ensuring ∥δ∥ is large and satisfying the alignment condition.
**Core assumption:** Semantic clusters correspond to neighborhoods with similar velocity-field structures; cluster boundaries mark the scale where semantic overlap causes conflict.
**Evidence anchors:** Sec. 4.1: "We partition the resulting embedding manifold into K semantic regions... For an anchor condition y∈Cᵢ, the macro-contrastive sampling rule restricts negatives to a different cluster... By construction, such negatives share minimal velocity-field structure with the positive." Sec. 5.4 and Table S5: Performance drops with too small *K* (coarse clusters), supporting the boundary assumption.
**Break condition:** Fails if cluster granularity is too coarse (*K* too small), allowing semantically similar (velocity-overlapping) prompts within the same partition, reintroducing gradient conflict.

### Mechanism 3: Micro-Contrastive Sampling (MiNS) for Fine-Grained Physics Discrimination
**Claim:** LLM-generated physics-perturbed hard negatives enable fine-grained physics discrimination by concentrating the velocity gap δ on a single targeted physics dimension, even under high semantic similarity.
**Mechanism:** For an anchor prompt, an LLM (Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct) minimally perturbs the text along one of five physics dimensions (kinematics, forces, material, interaction, magnitude), keeping scene semantics intact. The perturbed prompt is rendered by the base model to produce a negative video. The resulting δ, though small in norm, is concentrated on the physics-relevant subspace. Because a well-pretrained model's flow-matching residual (*u*⁺ − *v*) is already concentrated there, the alignment condition ⟨*u*⁺ − *v*, δ⟩ ≳ ∥*u*⁺ − *v*∥² can hold despite small ∥δ∥.
**Core assumptions:** (1) LLM perturbations isolate a single physics dimension without altering other semantics; (2) after large-scale pretraining, the model's primary failure is physics-violating dynamics, so (*u*⁺ − *v*) is predominantly in the physics-relevant subspace.
**Evidence anchors:** Sec. 4.2: "MiNS constructs hard negatives sharing full scene semantics... but differing along a single, LLM-perturbed axis of physical behavior... δ for this pair is concentrated in the physics-relevant subspace." Remark 1 formalizes that for a physics-concentrated residual, the condition relaxes to ∥δ∥ ≳ ∥*P*<sub>phys</sub>(*u*⁺ − *v*)∥. Sec. 5.4: MiNS yields largest gains in Temporal Common Sense.
**Break condition:** Fails if LLM perturbations alter scene semantics (measured by low text-encoder cosine similarity), if the base model's residual is not physics-concentrated (e.g., early training), or if the physics perturbation is too weak to create meaningful δ in the latent velocity field.

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component map:**
Offline: Training prompts → frozen T5 text encoder → k-means clustering (K=32) defines MaNS partitions; for each prompt, LLM (Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct) generates a physics-perturbed variant → base model (Wan 2.1-T2V-1.3B) renders hard-negative video → latent cached. Online: For each batch, positive prompt *y* → text encoder; random negative prompt sampled from different MaNS cluster; hard-negative latent retrieved from cache; current model *v*<sub>θ</sub> and frozen reference copy *v*<sub>θ<sub>ref</sub></sub> provide velocity fields. Losses: *L*<sub>FM</sub> (positive), *L*<sub>rand</sub> (random negative), *L*<sub>hard</sub> (hard negative), *L*<sub>anc</sub> (anchor). Sum weighted losses → backpropagate to update *v*<sub>θ</sub>.

**Critical path:**
1. Precompute: cluster prompts; generate and cache hard-negative latents.
2. Training loop: sample positives and corresponding negatives; compute *L*<sub>FM</sub>, *L*<sub>rand</sub>, *L*<sub>hard</sub>, *L*<sub>anc</sub>; sum; backward pass.

**Design tradeoffs:**
- *MaNS K*: Low *K* → intra-cluster semantic diversity → gradient conflict; high *K* → noisy, fragmented clusters → reduced negative diversity. Optimum at *K*=32 (Table S5).
- *MiNS similarity threshold*: Too low → alters semantics; too high → insufficient physics change. 0.87 cosine threshold balances (App. B).
- *λ weights*: λ<sub>rand</sub> sensitive (Table S2); λ<sub>hard</sub> more tolerant (Table S3); λ<sub>anc</sub> prevents forgetting but over-constrains if too high (Table S1).
- *Hard-negative generation cost*: One-time ~13 GPU-hours vs. negligible per-iteration cost (Table S4). Tradeoff vs. DPO's doubled forward passes.

**Failure signatures:**
- **Degraded semantic adherence (SA drop):** Overly large λ<sub>rand</sub> or λ<sub>hard</sub>, or too coarse MaNS clusters (*K*=8). Contrastive term harms reconstruction.
- **No physics gain:** λ<sub>hard</sub>=0, or MiNS prompts fail to preserve semantics (low cosine similarity), or *K* too high (*K*=128) fragmenting clusters.
- **Training instability/loss spikes:** Excessive λ<sub>hard</sub> (0.1) or missing λ<sub>anc</sub>. Loss masking (>50) helps (App. D).
- **Catastrophic forgetting:** λ<sub>anc</sub> too low (0.1) → visual quality drifts; λ<sub>anc</sub> too high (0.3) → PC gains vanish.

**First 3 experiments:**
1. **Ablation on negative sampling:** Run Table 1 experiment. Verify random negatives (ΔFM) hurt performance vs. baseline, MaNS improves Physical Commonsense, MiNS improves Temporal Common Sense, and combination is additive. Confirms gradient conflict and scale complementarity.
2. **Gradient alignment measurement:** Compute cosine similarity between *g*<sub>FM</sub> and *g*<sub>c</sub> (Eq. 13) for ΔFM (random neg.) and DiReCT (MaNS). Expect ΔFM ~+0.12 (opposition), DiReCT ~−0.05 (cooperation) per Table S6.
3. **Cluster count sensitivity:** Sweep *K* in {8,16,32,64,128} (Table S5). Expect inverted-U in Physical Commonsense, peak at *K*=32. Validates partition granularity assumption.

## Limitations
- Generalizability of MaNS to other models/text encoders is unknown; validation only on Wan 2.1 training set.
- LLM-generated MiNS may still introduce subtle semantic drift despite the cosine similarity threshold (0.87).
- Velocity-space anchor loss (λ<sub>anc</sub>) optimality is model-specific; too high risks over-constraining physics learning.
- Hard-negative cache relies on the base model's initial (flawed) physics, potentially propagating its systematic biases.

## Confidence
- **Gradient Conflict Mechanism (Proposition 1):** Medium (formal derivation sound but relies on theoretical alignment condition not directly measured; evidence indirect via gradient cosines).
- **MaNS & MiNS Complementarity:** High (strong ablation evidence, additive gains, sensitivity studies validate design choices).
- **Overall Benchmark Results (VideoPhy, WorldModelBench):** Medium-High (significant improvements reported, but benchmarks have limitations; comparisons primarily against SFT and one baseline).
