# Taiwan Safety Benchmark and Breeze Guard: Toward Trustworthy AI for Taiwanese Mandarin

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2603.07286  
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.07286  
- **Reference count:** 11  
- **Primary result:** Breeze Guard, an 8B safety model fine-tuned on Taiwanese Mandarin data, achieves a 0.17 absolute F1 improvement over Granite Guardian 3.3 on the new TS-Bench, with especially large gains in scam (+0.66 F1) and financial malpractice (+0.43 F1) detection.

## Executive Summary
Global AI safety models lack cultural and linguistic nuance for Taiwanese Mandarin, missing region-specific harms like local scams and culturally embedded hate speech. This work introduces TS-Bench, a 400-prompt benchmark covering financial fraud, medical misinformation, discrimination, and political manipulation, and Breeze Guard, an 8B safety model fine-tuned from Breeze 2—a base model pretrained on Taiwanese Mandarin data—using a large-scale human-verified synthesized dataset of Taiwan-specific harms. The core hypothesis is that safety detection needs the base model's cultural grounding, not just fine-tuning. Breeze Guard outperforms Granite Guardian 3.3 on TS-Bench, confirming the importance of pre-training cultural knowledge. However, performance on English benchmarks is slightly lower, reflecting the trade-off of regional specialization.

## Method Summary
The authors propose a two-part solution: (1) TS-Bench, a benchmark of 400 prompts (200 harmful, 200 benign hard negatives) targeting six risk categories: SCAM, FIN_MALPRACTICE, HEALTH_MISINFO, GENDER_BIAS, GROUP_HATE, and POL_MANIPULATION. The benign prompts are carefully constructed to closely resemble the harmful ones, testing precision on nuanced discrimination. (2) Breeze Guard, a safety model built by supervised fine-tuning (SFT) of the Taiwanese Mandarin pretrained model Breeze 2 (8B parameters). The SFT uses a large-scale synthetic dataset (~12k source prompts, augmented to ~24k with think/no-think variants) generated via a four-stage pipeline: seed curation by experts, few-shot generation with Gemini 3 Pro, automated diversification, and human-in-the-loop verification. The training objective minimizes cross-entropy over a restricted output format (`<score>yes/no</score>` or category tags) with an optional chain-of-thought (`<think>`). The model is evaluated on TS-Bench, ToxicChat (English), and AegisSafetyTest, showing regional superiority but a modest drop on English benchmarks.

## Key Results
- Breeze Guard attains a 0.17 absolute F1 improvement over Granite Guardian 3.3 on TS-Bench.
- Largest gains are in SCAM (+0.66 F1) and FIN_MALPRACTICE (+0.43 F1).
- Performance on English benchmarks (ToxicChat, AegisSafetyTest) is slightly lower, as expected for a regionally specialized model.
- Case studies (Section 5.1) demonstrate Breeze Guard's ability to correctly identify culturally specific slurs and stereotypes that global models miss.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1
- **Claim:** Cultural grounding for Taiwanese Mandarin safety detection must be acquired during pre-training, not introduced later via safety fine-tuning.
- **Mechanism:** A base model pre-trained on a corpus rich in Taiwanese Mandarin linguistic patterns, cultural references, and local discourse (e.g., Breeze 2) encodes essential priors. Safety fine-tuning then primarily adjusts decision boundaries and output format, leveraging these priors to recognize nuanced, region-specific harm (e.g., "ATM解除分期" scams, "台女" slurs). Without this grounding, a model lacks the intrinsic knowledge to identify culturally embedded cues, leading to systematic blind spots.
- **Core assumption:** Socio-linguistic knowledge (slang, stereotypes, local institutions) is best learned from broad, diverse pre-training data rather than being effectively injected into a model that lacks that foundational context.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract] States the core hypothesis: "safety detection needs the base model's cultural grounding, not just fine-tuning."
  - [section 5.1] Provides comparative case studies where Breeze 2 (Instruct) correctly identifies a Hakka stereotype and a local ableist slur that Granite Guardian 3.3 (Instruct) misses, demonstrating intrinsic cultural knowledge in the base model.
  - [corpus] Corpus evidence is weak; neighboring papers (e.g., TMLU, Breeze Taigi) focus on general Taiwanese language benchmarks, not safety-specific cultural grounding. This mechanism's support is primarily from the paper's own model comparisons.
- **Break condition:** If a globally pre-trained base model (without targeted Taiwanese data) were fine-tuned on the same Breeze Guard dataset and achieved comparable TS-Bench performance, the core hypothesis would be falsified. Current results show Granite Guardian 3.3 significantly underperforms, supporting the mechanism.

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim:** A high-quality, culturally tailored, human-verified synthetic data pipeline can efficiently generate the large-scale, diverse training data required for regional safety models where public, annotated datasets are nonexistent.
- **Mechanism:** Starting from human-curated seed exemplars encoding Taiwan-specific risk patterns (e.g., Shopee phishing, "投顧老師" schemes), few-shot prompting with a capable model (Gemini 3 Pro) generates large volumes of variants. These are diversified via paraphrasing, style transfer, and adversarial perturbations (entities, amounts, code-switching). A Human-in-the-Loop (HITL) review stage validates labels, realism, and category boundaries, ensuring quality and mitigating generation artifacts or overfitting to template patterns.
- **Core assumption:** Human experts can define accurate, comprehensive seed templates that capture the essence of Taiwan-specific harms, and HITL review can scale sufficiently to validate a dataset large enough for effective SFT.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [section 6.2] Details the four-stage pipeline (Seed Curation, Large-Scale Generation, Automated Filtering, HITL Review), including concrete methods for diversification and quality control.
  - [section 6.4] Notes data governance: all training data is synthetic or sanitized, with no real user data, and HITL verifies category correctness.
  - [corpus] No direct corpus anchor; neighboring papers do not describe similar synthetic data pipelines for safety.
- **Break condition:** If post-deployment monitoring reveals high false negative rates on novel, real-world Taiwanese scam scripts or misinformation that diverg significantly from the seeded patterns, it would indicate the synthetic distribution failed to capture the true data manifold. Performance decay over time without dataset refresh would be a signature.

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim:** A benchmark with culturally grounded hard negatives (benign prompts closely resembling harmful ones in topic and phrasing) is essential for measuring a safety model's precision and avoiding over-refusal on legitimate Taiwanese Mandarin discourse.
- **Mechanism:** TS-Bench pairs each harmful prompt with a benign counterpart sharing vocabulary, structure, and context (e.g., a shrimp/lemon myth *spreader* vs. *debunker*; a derogatory vs. affirming description of a person). This forces the model to rely on pragmatic reasoning (intent, urgency, framing) rather than keyword spotting, accurately testing its ability to distinguish genuine risk from benign discussion—a key failure mode for regionally specialized models.
- **Core assumption:** Over-refusal (flagging safe content as harmful) is a major risk for narrowly tuned safety models, and standard benchmarks with obviously benign/harmful pairs fail to measure this adequately.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [section 4.2] Explicitly states harmless prompts are constructed as "hard negatives—benign queries that resemble harmful topics."
  - [Table 2] Provides concrete minimal-pair examples (e.g., GENDER_BIAS: "so indecent!" vs. "so cool!").
  - [section 8] Notes a current limitation is "over-sensitivity in ambiguous contexts" and that future work aims to "reduce false positives," directly linking benchmark design to this failure mode.
- **Break condition:** If a model achieves high F1 on TS-Bench but deployment user testing shows frequent complaints about legitimate content being blocked (e.g., government advice flagged as scam), the benchmark's hard negatives may not be sufficiently representative of real-world benign edge cases, or the model's precision is still inadequate despite benchmark scores.

## Foundational Learning
- **Concept: Cultural Grounding in Pre-training**
  - **Why needed here:** The paper's central thesis is that recognizing Taiwan-specific harms (like local scams or ethnic slurs) requires innate knowledge of Taiwanese society, language, and common fraud patterns. This knowledge is baked into a model's parameters during pre-training on a representative corpus and cannot be fully taught via fine-tuning on classification data alone.
  - **Quick check question:** Can your base model, without any safety fine-tuning, understand why calling someone "很客家" in a specific context might be derogatory? If not, fine-tuning alone will likely fail to teach this effectively.

- **Concept: Hard-Negative Evaluation Design**
  - **Why needed here:** To build a trustworthy safety system, you must measure not just recall (catching harms) but also precision (avoiding blocking safe content). TS-Bench's paired design tests the model's ability to use subtle pragmatic cues (satire, education, debate) rather than superficial keywords, which is critical for a regional model where benign discussions might share lexicon with harmful ones.
  - **Quick check question:** For a given risk category (e.g., financial scams), can you construct a benign query that uses the same key terms and structure as a scam prompt but is clearly safe? If you cannot, your benchmark may overestimate model precision.

- **Concept: Deterministic Safety Instructions & Output Formatting**
  - **Why needed here:** Breeze Guard uses a constrained output (e.g., `<score>yes/no</score>`) and a structured "think" mode. This enforces consistent, parseable predictions for evaluation and deployment. The instruction template explicitly includes culturally grounded examples to anchor the model's judgment.
  - **Quick check question:** Is your safety model's output reliably machine-readable (e.g., a single tag) under all conditions, or does it generate free-form text that requires fragile post-processing?

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map:**
  1.  **Base Model (Breeze 2 8B Instruct):** Frozen for safety training. Provides Taiwanese Mandarin cultural/linguistic backbone. Contains a frozen ViT+MLP (multimodal path unused).
  2.  **Safety Instruction Template:** System prompt defining the 6 risk categories, judgment principles (description vs. advocacy), and output format (`<score>` tag).
  3.  **SFT Dataset:** ~12k source prompts (plus 24k think/no-think variants) covering 6 risk categories + `NON_RISK`. Built via seed curation -> LLM generation -> HITL review.
  4.  **Training Loop:** Standard supervised fine-tuning (cross-entropy loss) on formatted conversations (system + user + assistant with <score>).
  5.  **Inference Modes:** `no_think` (direct `<score>`), `think` (produces `<think>` rationale then `<score>`).
  6.  **Evaluation Harness:** Runs model on TS-Bench/ToxicChat/Aegis with the safety template, parses `<score>`, computes F1/AUC.

- **Critical path:**
  1.  Acquire/verify Breeze 2 8B Instruct checkpoint.
  2.  Prepare SFT data in conversation format with correct `<score>` labels and optional `<think>` blocks.
  3.  Run SFT (typically 1-3 epochs, low LR to preserve base knowledge).
  4.  Evaluate on TS-Bench *first* to confirm regional efficacy before checking global benchmarks.
  5.  Test both `think` and `no_think` modes; choose based on need for interpretability (`think`) vs. speed/aggregate in-domain F1 (`no_think`).

- **Design tradeoffs:**
  - **Regional Specialization vs. General Safety Performance:** Optimizing for TS-Bench (Taiwanese scams, local slurs) inherently sacrifices some performance on English-centric benchmarks (ToxicChat) where the model lacks exposure to those cultural patterns. This is an expected, acceptable tradeoff.
  - **Think vs. No-Think Mode:** `think` mode offers better performance on out-of-distribution (English) data and interpretability but is slower and can sometimes hurt in-domain aggregate F1 (per Table 4). `no_think` is faster and slightly better on average TS-Bench F1 but provides no rationale.
  - **Data Synthesis Scale vs. Quality:** Relying on LLM-generated data risks template leakage or missing novel, emerging scam scripts. HITL review mitigates this but limits scale. There's a tradeoff between dataset size/diversity and review cost/consistency.

- **Failure signatures:**
  1.  **High false positive rate on benign Taiwanese government/health communications:** Model flags legitimate official notices (e.g., National Pension reminders) as scams due to surface similarity (urgency, official impersonation cues).
  2.  **Poor generalization to novel phrasing:** Performance drops sharply on real-world scam scripts collected after the SFT data was generated, indicating the synthetic data distribution is stale.
  3.  **Inconsistent output format:** Model generates free-text explanations instead of strict `<score>` tags, breaking the evaluation/deployment harness. This suggests insufficient emphasis on the output constraint during SFT.
  4.  **Collapse on code-switched or heavily slang-laden input:** Performance degrades on prompts mixing Taiwanese Mandarin with other languages (Hakka, English) or extreme informal orthography, as these are underrepresented in the SFT corpus.

- **First 3 experiments:**
  1.  **Sanity Check - Replicate TS-Bench Results:** Run the provided evaluation script on Breeze Guard and Granite Guardian 3.3. Confirm the reported +0.17 overall F1 gap, especially the large gains in `SCAM` and `FIN_MALPRACTICE`. This validates your setup and the core claim of cultural grounding advantage.
  2.  **Ablation - Base Model Knowledge:** Evaluate the *instruction-tuned* Breeze 2 (pre-SFT) on a subset of TS-Bench or the case studies from Section 5.1. It should show poor performance, confirming that raw cultural knowledge is insufficient without safety-specific fine-tuning.
  3.  **Failure Mode Probe - Hard Negative Stress Test:** Create a new mini-set of 20-30 "very hard" benign Taiwanese Mandarin prompts that are adjacent to the risky categories (e.g., a news article *reporting on* a scam, a satirical political meme). Run `no_think` mode. If the model flags >50% as harmful, it indicates over-sensitivity and the need for more boundary-case data or template refinement.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
### Open Question 1
- **Question:** How can Breeze Guard reduce false positives on ambiguous content (e.g., satire, educational material) without sacrificing recall?
- **Basis in paper:** [explicit] Section 8 identifies over-sensitivity in contexts like legitimate government advice as a limitation.
- **Why unresolved:** Current SFT data lacks sufficient nuanced benign examples; CoT reasoning not fully leveraged for boundary cases.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Lower false positive rate on a new ambiguous subset of TS-Bench while maintaining ≥0.86 F1 on original.

### Open Question 2
- **Question:** How can Breeze Guard be extended to detect visual risks (e.g., fraudulent screenshots) relevant to Taiwanese Mandarin safety?
- **Basis in paper:** [explicit] Section 8 notes the vision-enabled Breeze 2 backbone and proposes multimodal safety as future work.
- **Why unresolved:** Model is currently text-only; no multimodal SFT data or evaluation.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Creation of a multimodal TS-Bench with image+text scams, and SFT on such data achieving >0.8 F1 on visual scam detection.

### Open Question 3
- **Question:** How can TS-Bench be expanded to incorporate code-switching (e.g., Mandarin–Hakka) and multi-turn dialogues to better reflect Taiwan's linguistic reality?
- **Basis in paper:** [explicit] Section 8 lists benchmark expansion to code-switched items and multi-turn scenarios.
- **Why unresolved:** TS-Bench v1 only covers single-turn Mandarin; lacks representation of Taiwan's multilingual interactions.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** TS-Bench v2 with code-switched prompts and multi-turn sessions, showing stronger correlation with real-world deployment outcomes.

### Open Question 4
- **Question:** Does the proposed localization methodology (culturally grounded taxonomy, seed-driven synthesis, HITL verification) generalize to other non-English regions with unique threat patterns?
- **Basis in paper:** [explicit] Section 8 claims the methodology generalizes naturally; needs empirical validation.
- **Why unresolved:** No studies replicating the approach in other linguistic/cultural contexts; potential regional differences may affect effectiveness.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Successful replication in at least two distinct regions (e.g., Korean, Arabic) with region-specific taxonomies, yielding similar F1 improvements over global baselines.

## Limitations
- **Reproducibility gaps:** Training hyperparameters, full SFT dataset, and annotation guidelines not released.
- **Synthetic data scope:** Pipeline may not generalize to novel/emerging scam tactics; HITL scalability unknown.
- **Known issue:** Over-sensitivity in ambiguous contexts (e.g., legitimate government/health communications flagged as scams).

## Confidence
- **Cultural grounding must be pre‑trained:** Medium
- **Synthetic pipeline effectiveness:** Medium
- **Hard‑negative benchmark necessity:** High
- **Breeze Guard performance advantage on TS‑Bench:** High (but generalizability to other benchmarks low)

## Next Checks
