# TwinPurify: Purifying gene expression data to reveal tumor-intrinsic transcriptional programs via self-supervised learning

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2601.18640  
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.18640  
- **Reference count:** 40  
- **Primary result:** TwinPurify learns tumor-specific embeddings from bulk RNA-seq using adjacent-normal tissue as self-supervised guidance, achieving robust molecular subtyping under high normal contamination (e.g., PAM50 Macro-F1 >0.75 at 60% dilution) and outperforming baselines in grade prediction, pathway enrichment, and survival analysis.

## Executive Summary
TwinPurify is a self-supervised method for purifying tumor gene expression profiles from normal contamination without external references. It adapts the Barlow Twins objective by generating two distorted views of each tumor sample through mixing with different adjacent-normal profiles. This forces the encoder to be invariant to normal signals while decorrelating latent dimensions, yielding disentangled tumor-intrinsic programs. Evaluated on breast cancer cohorts, TwinPurify maintains high accuracy in predicting molecular subtypes and grades even under severe synthetic dilution, enriches more biologically interpretable pathways, preserves immune signatures, and improves survival prediction compared to autoencoders, VAEs, and PCA. The approach demonstrates that structured perturbations from matched normal tissue can effectively guide unsupervised purification.

## Method Summary
TwinPurify aims to learn tumor-intrinsic representations from bulk RNA-seq without requiring ground-truth purity estimates or external references. Input is a gene expression matrix of tumor and adjacent-normal samples from the same cohort, preprocessed with log₂(x+1) transformation and restricted to a fixed set of protein-coding genes. For each tumor sample, the method creates two synthetic “contaminated” views by convex mixing (tumor proportion α=0.27) with two different synthetic normal references. Each synthetic normal is a weighted average (random weights) of five distinct adjacent-normal profiles. Both views pass through a shared encoder (MLP) and a projector; the Barlow Twins loss is applied to the projections. This loss minimizes off-diagonal cross-correlations (decorrelation) and pushes diagonal terms to 1 (invariance), expressed as L_TP = Σ(1−Cᵢᵢ)² + λ Σ_{i≠j} Cᵢⱼ² with λ=54.9. Training optimizes the encoder and projector on tumor samples only, using adjacent-normals from the training set as perturbation guides. After training, the projector is discarded and the frozen encoder maps any bulk tumor sample to a fixed-dimensional purified embedding. Downstream tasks (e.g., PAM50 classification, grade prediction, survival analysis) use lightweight models (logistic regression, Cox PH) on these embeddings. Baselines include autoencoders, VAEs, and PCA trained on the same data without self-supervised purification.

## Key Results
- Under increasing synthetic normal contamination on breast cancer cohorts, TwinPurify's PAM50 subtype classification Macro-F1 remains above 0.75 at 60% dilution, while AE, VAE, and PCA drop below 0.5.  
- For histological grade prediction, TwinPurify achieves 3–7% higher F1 scores than baselines and sustains F1 >0.50 at 50% dilution.  
- The learned embeddings yield significantly more enriched GO biological pathways (uniqueness scores p<0.001) and preserve immune cell signatures better than competitors.  
- Gene signatures extracted from TwinPurify dimensions produce superior survival prediction (higher C-index, lower p-values) across multiple breast cancer cohorts.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- **Claim:** The Barlow Twins loss functions as a tumor-purifying objective by forcing invariance to normal-tissue perturbations while decorrelating latent dimensions.  
- **Mechanism:** For each tumor sample, two distorted views are created by mixing with synthetic normal references. The shared encoder maps both to projections. The cross-correlation loss drives diagonal terms to 1 (invariance to normal perturbation) and off-diagonals to 0 (decorrelation), encouraging the model to discard normal-tissue signals and learn orthogonal axes representing distinct tumor programs.  
- **Core assumption:** Adjacent-normal profiles approximate the contaminating signal in bulk tumors.  
- **Evidence anchors:** Abstract and method section describe the distortion process and loss formulation; corpus lacks direct evidence for this specific design, so the mechanism is primarily paper‑authored.  
- **Break condition:** If adjacent-normals are not representative (e.g., due to cohort‑specific batch effects or disease‑state differences), the structured perturbation fails to model true confounding, leading to discarded tumor signals or convergence failure.

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim:** Using biologically grounded perturbations (adjacent‑normal mixing) is superior to generic noise (e.g., Gaussian) for disentangling tumor‑specific signals.  
- **Mechanism:** The distortion uses real adjacent‑normal expression, creating a “negative” supervision signal that is the specific transcriptional signature of the contaminating cells. The model learns invariances directly to that signal, purging it from the embedding. Random noise lacks this biological specificity.  
- **Core assumption:** Contamination is linearly additive and locally similar to adjacent‑normal expression.  
- **Evidence anchors:** Abstract and method section describe the mixing process; ablation shows BT with Gaussian noise identifies fewer pathways than TP, supporting superiority.  
- **Break condition:** If linear additivity is violated or adjacent‑normals are infiltrated by tumor/immune cells, the perturbation may misguide learning.

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim:** The decorrelation constraint of the Barlow Twins objective produces latent dimensions that are statistically independent and map to distinct, interpretable biological pathways.  
- **Mechanism:** The off‑diagonal penalty minimizes covariance between latent dimensions. Combined with invariance, this allocates different biological signals to separate dimensions. Empirically, TP shows low gene overlap across dimensions and dimension‑specific GO term enrichment.  
- **Core assumption:** Major non-tumor variations can be represented as orthogonal factors separable via this objective.  
- **Evidence anchors:** Section reports significantly higher uniqueness scores (p<0.001) vs. AE/VAE; heatmap (Fig. 5a) shows dimension‑specific themes (immune, cartilage, DNA replication).  
- **Break condition:** If confounding signals are highly correlated, perfect disentanglement is impossible; decorrelation may force artificial separation or fail to untangle co‑regulated programs.

## Foundational Learning
- **Concept: Self‑Supervised Learning via Redundancy Reduction (Barlow Twins)**  
  *Why needed:* The challenge is unsupervised purification (no ground‑truth tumor‑only data). SSL provides a pretext task (invariance to known perturbations) without labels. Barlow Twins’ redundancy reduction explicitly seeks invariance (similar representations for perturbed views) and decorrelation (independent latent dimensions), aligning with removing a specific confounder (normal tissue) and separating remaining programs.  
  *Quick check:* If two distorted views of a sample are made more similar (low cross‑correlation off‑diagonals), what property about the information common to both views is enforced? (Answer: Invariance to the perturbation; the model should ignore what is common to both views, i.e., the normal background.)

- **Concept: Biological Perturbation Design vs. Synthetic Noise**  
  *Why needed:* The method’s efficacy hinges on the quality of the “negative” signal. Generic noise does not correspond to real biological variation, so learned invariances may be irrelevant to purification. Using adjacent‑normal profiles injects the actual transcriptional signature of the contaminating cell population, making invariance directly useful.  
  *Quick check:* Why might mixing in random Gaussian noise not help a model learn to remove normal tissue signals from a tumor sample? (Answer: Gaussian noise does not mimic the structured, gene‑coordinated expression program of a specific cell type like normal epithelium or stroma.)

- **Concept: Deconvolution vs. Purification (Paradigm Shift)**  
  *Why needed:* Traditional deconvolution estimates cell‑type proportions or signatures, requiring a reference and often failing due to mismatches. TwinPurify bypasses explicit cell‑type estimation; instead, it learns a latent space where the normal signal is implicitly projected out via the SSL objective. The output is a purified embedding, not deconvolved profiles.  
  *Quick check:* What is a key practical difference between CIBERSORT (deconvolution) and TwinPurify (purification)? (Answer: CIBERSORT outputs estimated cell type abundances; TwinPurify outputs a lower‑dimensional vector per sample—a purified tumor embedding—with no direct interpretation as cell fractions.)

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map:**  
  Raw expression → Distortion Engine (generates two mixed views per tumor) → Shared Encoder (MLP) → Projector (MLP) → Cross‑correlation computation → TP Loss (L_TP) → Backpropagation (update encoder & projector). After training: Encoder (frozen) → Purified embedding → Downstream classifier (e.g., logistic regression) or survival model.

- **Critical path:**  
  1. **Data preparation:** Split cohort into train/test; identify tumor and adjacent‑normal samples; apply log₂(x+1) transformation; keep consistent gene set.  
  2. **Model training:** For each batch of tumor samples, generate two independent views per tumor using the Distortion Engine (synthetic normals from five randomly sampled adjacent‑normals with random weights). Forward through encoder and projector; compute TP loss; backpropagate.  
  3. **Hyperparameter tuning:** Use Optuna to search optimal mixing coefficient α and loss weight λ (paper finds α=0.27, λ=54.9).  
  4. **Model selection:** Save encoder weights of best model based on a proxy (e.g., validation set classification or held‑out purity task).  
  5. **Embedding extraction:** Load best encoder; pass all tumor samples through it to obtain fixed‑dimensional embeddings.  
  6. **Downstream evaluation:** Train separate lightweight models (e.g., logistic regression, Cox PH) on the frozen embeddings and evaluate on test set.

- **Design tradeoffs:**  
  - *Hyperparameter sensitivity (α, λ) vs. biological interpretability:* Optimal α depends on data; too high fails to teach removal of normal signals, too low swamps tumor signal. λ balances invariance vs. decorrelation; improper setting can harm disentanglement.  
  - *Encoder complexity vs. overfitting:* A deep encoder can capture nonlinear invariances but may overfit to cohort‑specific noise in adjacent‑normals. Simpler encoders regularize better but have less capacity.  
  - *Purification generality vs. task‑specificity:* Embeddings are optimized for general tumor signal recovery; if a downstream task relies on a normal‑related signal (e.g., fibroblast‑related prognosis), TP may have removed it, harming performance.

- **Failure signatures:**  
  1. **Dilution collapse:** Macro‑F1 for PAM50 or grade drops rapidly and non‑linearly with dilution, similar to AE/VAE/PCA baselines, indicating failure to learn invariances.  
  2. **Pathway entanglement:** GSEA on latent dimensions yields highly overlapping gene sets and similar enriched pathways across all dimensions; uniqueness score non‑significant (p > 0.05) vs. chance.  
  3. **Immune signal loss:** Enrichment of immunologic signatures (e.g., C7) in embeddings is significantly lower than in raw bulk or VAE baseline, suggesting over‑subtraction of tumor microenvironment signals.  
  4. **Cohort transfer failure:** A model trained on SCAN‑B (RNA‑seq) produces poor embeddings on METABRIC (microarray) even after standard preprocessing, hinting that learned invariances are overly specific to platform or cohort‑specific batch effects.

- **First 3 experiments:**  
  1. **Replicate Dilution Robustness:** Implement dilution protocol (mix tumor with adjacent‑normal at 0–100%). Train TP and baselines (AE, VAE, PCA) on undiluted SCAN‑B training set. For each dilution level on held‑out test, generate embeddings, train logistic regression on undiluted training embeddings (5‑fold CV), and predict test dilutions. Plot Macro‑F1 vs. dilution; expect TP >0.75 at 60% dilution while others fall below 0.5.  
  2. **Validate Pathway Uniqueness:** For trained TP encoder, compute Pearson correlation of each embedding dimension with all genes. For each dimension, take top/bottom 1000 genes. Compute pairwise Jaccard overlap between gene lists of different dimensions. Perform 10,000 permutation tests (shuffle dimension labels) to obtain p‑value for observed overlap fraction. Expect very low overlaps (high uniqueness) with p < 0.001.  
  3. **Ablate Perturbation Source:** Train three Barlow Twins models on same data: (a) TP (adjacent‑normal mixing), (b) BT‑Gaussian (add Gaussian noise with same variance as normal profiles), (c) BT‑Shuffled (shuffle gene values within a sample). For each model, count dimensions with ≥5 significantly enriched GO‑BP terms (FWER < 0.05/ndim). Expect TP > BT‑Gaussian > BT‑Shuffled, confirming necessity of biologically structured perturbation.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
1. **General validity of adjacent‑normal assumption:** How universally valid is the assumption that adjacent‑normal tissue profiles represent a consistent “background” reference for disentangling tumor signals across diverse cancer types and stages? The method is only evaluated in breast cancer; extension to other cancers is untested.  
2. **Absolute validation without dilution:** Can we develop a more absolute, reference‑free validation framework for tumor purification that does not rely on synthetic dilution or external labels? Synthetic dilution may not capture real biological complexity.  
3. **Biological independence of latent dimensions:** To what extent do the decorrelated embedding dimensions correspond to truly independent biological programs versus statistically orthogonal but functionally overlapping pathways? Orthogonality does not guarantee biological independence.  
4. **Immune signal suppression risk:** Does TwinPurify’s use of adjacent‑normal tissue as a perturbation inadvertently suppress tumor‑associated immune signals in cancers where the immune microenvironment closely resembles normal tissue (e.g., immune‑cold tumors)? The method treats all adjacent‑normal signal as “background,” which may remove beneficial immune signatures if they are transcriptionally similar to normal immune cells.

## Limitations
- **Missing implementation details:** Encoder/projector architectures (layer sizes, activations), training hyperparameters (optimizer settings, LR schedule, batch size, early stopping), and precise dilution protocol (which normal references to use) are not fully reported, hindering exact replication.  
- **Assumption generalizability:** The method relies on adjacent‑normal tissue to model contamination; this assumption may not hold across all cancer types or stages with different microenvironment compositions (e.g., desmoplastic stroma), potentially limiting generalizability.  
- **Decorrelation limitations:** The decorrelation constraint cannot guarantee separation of highly co‑regulated biological pathways; it may force artificial splits or fail to disentangle truly interdependent programs.

## Confidence
- **Robustness to dilution:** High  
- **Biological interpretability (pathway enrichment, uniqueness, immune preservation):** Medium  
- **Mechanistic necessity of adjacent‑normal perturbation:** Medium
