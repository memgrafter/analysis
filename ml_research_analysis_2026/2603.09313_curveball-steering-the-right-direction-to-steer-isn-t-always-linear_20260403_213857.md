# Curveball Steering: The Right Direction To Steer Isn't Always Linear

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID**: 2603.09313
- **Source URL**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.09313
- **Reference count**: 40
- **Primary result**: Curveball steering yields substantially larger behavior shifts than linear PCA steering on binary tasks (e.g., +47% vs +16% for power-seeking in Llama, +93% vs +2% for corrigibility in Phi).

## Executive Summary
The paper reveals that activation steering methods based on linear directions (e.g., PCA) often fail because LLM activation manifolds are geometrically distorted: geodesic distances between points can be much larger than Euclidean distances, meaning straight-line interpolation pushes representations off the manifold, causing unnatural or ineffective interventions. This non-Euclidean geometry varies across behavioral concepts. To address this, the authors propose Curveball steering, which uses polynomial kernel PCA to map activations into a high-dimensional feature space where the manifold is approximately linear. Steering directions are computed as the normalized difference of class means in that space. At inference, the direction is applied and then projected back via a pre-image reconstruction (kernel ridge regression) while preserving the residual orthogonal to the KPCA subspace to stay on-manifold. Evaluations on Llama-3.2-1B and Phi-3.5-mini across eight behavioral and linguistic traits show that Curveball consistently outperforms linear PCA, achieving much larger shifts in target behavior probabilities on binary-choice tasks and higher judge-rated trait changes on open-ended generation. Synthetic experiments with artificially high-curvature manifolds further confirm that Curveball excels exactly where linear steering falters.

## Method Summary
Curveball's pipeline begins by collecting contrastive activation data (neutral vs. positive behavior) for a target concept using a dataset builder. A VAE ensemble trained on the model's activations provides a decoder whose Jacobians enable computing the pullback Riemannian metric, which approximates geodesic distances along the activation manifold. The geometric analyzer uses this metric to estimate geodesic-to-Euclidean distance ratios, confirming substantial distortion for many concepts. If nonlinearity is present, polynomial kernel PCA (degree 2–3) is fitted on the centered activations, selecting enough components (≈20) to retain 95% variance. The steering direction is obtained by taking the normalized difference of the mean KPCA projections of the positive and neutral classes. For each generation step, the current activation is centered, projected to KPCA space, perturbed by α·ˆz, and then inverted to the original space using kernel ridge regression to approximate the pre-image. Crucially, the residual between the original activation and its KPCA reconstruction is added back after perturbation to avoid off-manifold drift. This residual-preserving step ensures that the intervention respects the underlying manifold geometry.

## Key Results
- On binary-choice tasks, Curveball yields significantly larger probability shifts than linear PCA steering: e.g., power-seeking +47% vs +16% (Llama) and corrigibility +93% vs +2% (Phi).
- Across eight behavioral/linguistic traits, Curveball consistently improves both binary and open-ended evaluations, demonstrating robustness.
- Synthetic high-curvature manifold experiments show that when geodesic distances greatly exceed Euclidean ones, Curveball outperforms linear steering, validating its geometry-awareness.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

#### Mechanism 1: Nonlinear kernel PCA captures curved manifold structure
- **Claim**: Nonlinear kernel PCA captures the curved manifold structure in activation spaces where geometric distortion (geodesic >> Euclidean) invalidates global linear steering.
- **Mechanism**: The polynomial kernel implicitly maps activations to a feature space where nonlinear manifolds become approximately linear; the steering direction is computed in this space via class mean differences; then the mapping is inverted with residual preservation to stay on-manifold.
- **Core assumption**: Activation manifolds have concept-dependent curvature; straight-line interpolation leaves the manifold, so linear steering pushes points off it.
- **Evidence anchors**: 
  - Abstract: "analyzing geodesic-to-Euclidean distance ratios... reveal substantial concept-dependent geometric distortion... propose Curveball steering, which uses polynomial kernel PCA...".
  - Section 2.1: Activation spaces contain regions with R≫1, indicating non-Euclidean geometry and concept-dependent structure.
  - Corroborating work on steering vector fields notes unreliability of linear vectors, motivating nonlinear approaches.

#### Mechanism 2: Adaptive steering magnitude via nonlinear inverse mapping
- **Claim**: Curveball automatically adapts steering magnitude per input position via the nonlinear inverse mapping, unlike fixed-magnitude linear steering.
- **Mechanism**: The pre-image reconstruction a_final = ϕ⁻¹(ϕ(a) + α·ˆz) yields position-dependent effective displacement ||a_final – a|| in ambient space because the KPCA-to-original-space transformation distorts vectors anisotropically based on local curvature.
- **Core assumption**: The optimal push strength varies across the manifold; a uniform α is suboptimal.
- **Evidence anchors**: 
  - Section 6.2: "the KPCA vector magnitude adjusts automatically... conceptually related to token-level gating... Curveball induces a similar form of adaptive intervention implicitly."
  - Table 2: Spearman correlations between effective magnitude and paired-class distance are significant (|ρ| > 0.37, p << 0.01) for most concepts, confirming position-dependent magnitude; corrigible-more is an exception (negative correlation).

#### Mechanism 3: Integration of multiple local steering directions
- **Claim**: Curveball integrates multiple local steering directions through the global nonlinear kernel, avoiding the geometric compromise of a single global direction.
- **Mechanism**: The polynomial kernel implicitly computes inner products in a high-degree feature space where local linear directions from different clusters become aligned via nonlinear mixing; the single KPCA steering vector approximates a weighted combination of region-specific optimal vectors.
- **Core assumption**: Activation space contains clusters with divergent optimal local steering directions; a single global mean-difference direction misaligns with many regions.
- **Evidence anchors**: 
  - Section 6.1: "sub-cluster steering vectors are away from the global one, and form clusters themselves... linear steering could be improved if enacted on different parts individually."
  - Section 6.3: Cosine similarities of local directions show clear multimodal structure; KPCA vectors cluster.
  - External: "The Geometry of Harmfulness in LLMs through Subconcept Probing" finds harm dimensions vary across subconcepts, supporting premise.

## Foundational Learning
- **Concept: Pullback Riemannian Metric**
  - **Why needed here**: Measures true manifold geometry by pulling back Euclidean metric through the VAE decoder; enables computing geodesic distances that respect curved structure.
  - **Quick check**: Given a VAE decoder f(z) with mean µ(z) and variance σ²(z) outputs, write the pullback metric g(z) as g(z)=J_µ(z)^T J_µ(z) + J_σ(z)^T J_σ(z) and explain how it captures curvature from the decoder's Jacobians.

- **Concept: Kernel PCA Pre-image Problem**
  - **Why needed here**: Steering operates in KPCA feature space but must return to original activation space; solving the pre-image approximates the exact inverse of the implicit nonlinear mapping.
  - **Quick check**: Why is exact pre-image intractable for polynomial kernel PCA, and how does kernel ridge regression provide an approximate solution via ŷ = ∑ w_i(z)·a_i?

- **Concept: Geodesic-to-Euclidean Distance Ratio**
  - **Why needed here**: The distortion ratio R = d_geo / d_euc quantifies nonlinearity; R > 1 indicates straight-line interpolation leaves the manifold, explaining why linear steering degrades.
  - **Quick check**: If R ≫ 1 on a dataset, what does that imply about interpolating between two activation vectors with a linear steering step, and how would that affect model outputs?

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map (plain text)**: Dataset builder → VAE ensemble trainer → Geometric analyzer → Kernel PCA module → Steering vector calculator → Inference-time steers.

- **Critical path**: 
  - *Training*: Collect contrastive activations → (optionally compute distortion ratio R) → fit polynomial kernel PCA (kernel matrix, eigendecomposition) → compute class means in KPCA space.
  - *Inference*: For each token, extract activation A_curr → center using training mean → project via ϕ(A_curr) → add α·ˆz → reconstruct via pre-image (kernel ridge) → compute residual r = A_curr − ϕ⁻¹(ϕ(A_curr)) → final steered activation = recon + r → replace in forward pass.
  - Important: Pre-image reconstruction must be consistent between training (when computing class means) and inference; training mean centering must be stored.

- **Design tradeoffs**:
  - **Polynomial degree p**: Higher p captures more nonlinearity but risks overfitting; p=2–3 is a good balance. RBF kernel avoided because it emphasizes local over global structure.
  - **KPCA dimension m**: Smaller m = faster but loses variance; larger m ≈ original dim reduces nonlinear benefit. An elbow at m≈20 (≈95% variance) is used.
  - **Pre-image method**: Nadaraya-Watson (kernel-weighted average) simpler but bandwidth σ sensitive; kernel ridge regression more stable but needs λ regularization.
  - **Residual preservation**: Essential to avoid off-manifold drift; omitting it degrades output quality. However, large residuals may overwhelm the steering effect if they dominate.

- **Failure signatures**:
  1. **High reconstruction error** (train/test): Pre-image quality insufficient → steering points far from training manifold → unnatural outputs. Diagnostic: plot reconstruction MSE vs. m; increase m or σ.
  2. **No improvement over linear** when R ≫ 1: Check kernel hyperparameters (degree, γ); maybe polynomial insufficient; consider RBF or deeper kernels.
  3. **Steering magnitude collapse**: If effective ||a_steered – a|| nearly constant across α, inverse mapping may be nearly linear; verify by magnitude-vs-α plots.
  4. **Concept-specific failure** (e.g., corrigible-more negative Spearman correlation): Indicates nonlinear mapping maladaptive for that concept; try lower p, different kernel, or fallback to linear.
  5. **Inference slowdown**: KPCA per-token O(nd) dominates; pre-image may be bottleneck. Profile; reduce m, subsample KPCA training set, or use Nyström approximation.

- **First 3 experiments**:
  1. Compute distortion ratios for each concept using VAE ensemble pullback metrics; confirm R > 1.5 for at least 3/8 to validate nonlinearity premise.
  2. Synthetic manifold validation: Generate hypersphere patches with high curvature (κ > 10); compare Curveball vs. linear on target distance and tangent deviation across α; expect Curveball better in high-κ regions.
  3. Full steering evaluation: For each (model, concept), sweep α (log-scale) and plot response curves (binary: p(target behavior); trait: judge score). Extract maximum Δ and compare to linear baseline per Table 1; expect improvement on power-seeking/self-awareness (Llama) and corrigible (Phi).

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- **Open Question 1**: How to reduce computational overhead of kernel PCA for real-time inference? The authors acknowledge increased latency; optimizing approximate kernel methods or architectural adaptations could maintain effectiveness while improving speed.
- **Open Question 2**: Do nonlinear steering advantages scale to larger language models (>10B parameters) and deeper layers? Current evaluation limited to ≤4B models due to geometric analysis costs; activation geometry of larger models may differ.
- **Open Question 3**: What are general principles for automatically selecting kernel PCA hyperparameters (polynomial degree, component count, kernel parameters) across diverse concepts and models? Manual tuning hinders adoption; data-driven selection heuristics are needed.
- **Open Question 4**: How can Curveball steering be adapted for continuous or multi-class behavioral traits? The method uses binary class means; extension to fine-grained scores or multiple classes remains unexplored.

## Limitations
- Exact kernel ridge regression hyperparameters (λ, bandwidth) and polynomial degree are not fully specified, hindering faithful reproduction.
- Steering strength α per concept and the protocol for determining the reported maximum Δ (peak vs. fixed) are not provided, creating ambiguity.
- Evaluation of open-ended traits relies on an LLM-as-judge, which may be biased and not correlate with human judgments.
