# MentalSeek-Dx: Towards Progressive Hypothetico-Deductive Reasoning for Real-world Psychiatric Diagnosis

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2602.03340
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.03340
- **Reference count:** 40
- **Primary result:** MentalSeek-Dx (14B) achieves state-of-the-art on MentalDx Bench with 83.99% Category Accuracy, 70.08% Disorder Accuracy, and 69.38% Joint Accuracy.

## Executive Summary
This paper tackles the gap between computational psychiatry and clinical practice by introducing MentalDx Bench, the first benchmark for disorder-level diagnosis using 712 real-world electronic health records (EHRs) annotated under ICD-11. Evaluation of 18 LLMs reveals that models excel at coarse categorization but systematically fail at fine-grained disorder diagnosis, relying on associative shortcuts instead of hypothetico-deductive reasoning. To bridge this gap, the authors propose MentalSeek-Dx, a 14B-parameter medical LLM trained via supervised trajectory construction and curriculum-based reinforcement learning to internalize clinical reasoning. On MentalDx Bench, MentalSeek-Dx achieves state-of-the-art performance: 83.99% Category Accuracy, 70.08% Disorder Accuracy, and 69.38% Joint Accuracy, significantly surpassing larger general-purpose and medical LLMs. This demonstrates that cognitive alignment with clinical logic, not parameter scale, is critical for reliable psychiatric diagnosis.

## Method Summary
MentalSeek-Dx adopts a Hypothetico-Deductive Reasoning (HDR) framework that decomposes diagnosis into three sequential stages: symptom categorization, hypothesis generation (a ranked list of candidate disorders), and differential diagnosis. Each stage is grounded in a structured knowledge base K, which contains ICD-11-aligned clinical definitions, symptom clusters, and diagnostic criteria. Training proceeds in two phases: first, supervised learning constructs correct reasoning trajectories; second, reinforcement learning with curriculum-based reward scheduling progressively emphasizes fine-grained accuracy. Rewards for each stage (R_cat, R_hypo, R_diff) are combined with dynamically adjusted weights across epochs, and the policy is optimized via Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO). At inference, the model processes an EHR, retrieves relevant knowledge, and generates the three-step reasoning chain to produce a final diagnosis.

## Key Results
- MentalSeek-Dx attains SOTA on MentalDx Bench: 83.99% Category Accuracy, 70.08% Disorder Accuracy, 69.38% Joint Accuracy.
- It outperforms significantly larger LLMs (e.g., >70B parameters), demonstrating the advantage of reasoning alignment over scale.
- The model closes the performance gap on fine-grained disorder diagnosis, previously a systematic failure point for LLMs.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
**Mechanism 1: Trajectory Construction**  
Decomposing diagnosis into supervised hypothetico-deductive reasoning replaces associative matching with stepwise clinical logic. The model is trained to produce three intermediate outputs (category, hypothesis list, differential) using a structured knowledge base for grounding. This mirrors clinicians' iterative hypothesis testing and yields large improvements in disorder accuracy over direct SFT. The approach fails if the knowledge base lacks coverage, leading to invalid hypotheses.

**Mechanism 2: Curriculum Reinforcement Learning**  
Stage-aware RL with progressive reward scheduling sharpens focus from coarse categories to fine-grained differentials. Rewards for each stage are dynamically reweighted across epochs, stabilizing early training on categories while later emphasizing disorders. This curriculum yields more stable convergence and higher final accuracy compared to fixed or disorder-only rewards. Improper weighting could cause reward hacking or instability.

**Mechanism 3: Knowledge Base Grounding**  
A clinically curated, ICD-11-aligned knowledge base grounds all reasoning steps in standardized definitions, reducing variability. Automatic retrieval from the KB during trajectory construction ensures each step references authoritative criteria. This grounding is essential for reliable differential diagnosis; omission of exclusion criteria would impair rule-out capability.

## Foundational Learning
### Diagnostic Hierarchy (ICD-11)
- **Why needed:** The task requires mapping from symptoms → category → specific disorder within a fixed taxonomy; confusion at the category level dooms fine-grained accuracy.
- **Quick check:** Can the learner distinguish between "Schizophrenia" and "Bipolar Disorder" based on core symptom dimensions (psychotic features vs. mood episodes) rather than just keyword matches?

### Hypothetico-Deductive Reasoning
- **Why needed:** The paradigm misalignment stems from models lacking this structured process; they must learn to generate and eliminate hypotheses, not just recall associations.
- **Quick check:** Given a case with overlapping symptoms (e.g., fatigue, concentration issues), can the model reason about which disorders are more probable and why, referencing absence/presence of key criteria?

### Reward Shaping for Complex Tasks
- **Why needed:** Disorder-level accuracy is sparse; shaping rewards for intermediate steps (category, hypothesis list quality) provides denser feedback and guides curriculum.
- **Quick check:** Does increasing the weight of differential reward in later epochs actually improve final disorder accuracy, or does it cause early-stage instability?

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component map**  
EHR → Input Encoder → Knowledge Retriever → Trajectory Generator (Category Reasoner → Hypothesis Lister → Differential Discriminator) → Output Parser  
During training, the Reward Computer computes R_cat, R_hypo, and R_diff after each respective stage, and the RL Policy Head updates the model using GRPO with curriculum-adjusted total reward.

**Critical path**  
1. Input EHR is processed; Knowledge Retriever fetches candidate categories from KB.  
2. Category Reasoner predicts a category c; Reward Computer computes R_cat based on correctness.  
3. Using c, Knowledge Retriever retrieves disorders within that category.  
4. Hypothesis Lister generates a ranked list h of possible disorders.  
5. Reward Computer computes R_hypo by checking inclusion/ranking of the ground-truth disorder d in h.  
6. Differential Discriminator selects the final disorder d from h using diagnostic criteria; Reward Computer computes R_diff.  
7. Total reward = α₁R_cat + α₂R_hypo + α₃R_diff, where α weights are scheduled across five training epochs.  
8. RL Policy Head updates model parameters via Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO) using group-normalized advantages derived from the total reward.

**Design tradeoffs**  
- **Trajectory depth vs. error propagation:** More reasoning steps improve clinical alignment but risk compounding early mistakes.  
- **Reward sparsity vs. complexity:** Dense intermediate rewards stabilize training but require careful weighting to avoid reward hacking.  
- **Knowledge base size vs. retrieval noise:** Larger coverage increases relevance but may introduce irrelevant entries that confuse the reasoner.  
- **Model scale:** The 14B parameter model outperforms 7B and even 70B+ general LLMs, indicating that reasoning design matters more than raw size.

**Failure signatures**  
- **Category collapse:** Model predicts the most frequent category regardless of evidence.  
- **Hypothesis list overgeneration:** Including nearly all disorders in the category to guarantee a high R_hypo, undermining the differential step.  
- **Reward hacking:** Exploiting early curriculum by achieving high R_cat while ignoring disorder accuracy if R_diff is underweighted.  
- **Knowledge base mismatch:** Colloquial symptom terms in EHRs not matching KB definitions cause retrieval failures.

**First 3 experiments**  
- **Ablate HDR:** Train a 14B model (e.g., Qwen2.5-14B) with direct supervised fine-tuning (no trajectory) on the same data; expect significant drop in Disorder Accuracy and Joint Accuracy, confirming the necessity of structured reasoning.  
- **Curriculum ablation:** Compare progressive reward scheduling against a fixed ratio (α₁:α₂:α₃ = 1:1:1) across all epochs; measure convergence stability and final Joint Accuracy to validate the curriculum approach.  
- **KB coverage test:** Remove disorder-specific differential criteria from the knowledge base for 20% of disorders; measure the resulting performance drop to quantify the KB's contribution to rule-out reasoning.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
**Open Question 1: Multimodal Integration**  
- **Question:** How to integrate multimodal signals (e.g., speech, video) with textual EHRs within the HDR framework?  
- **Basis:** [explicit] Dataset is text-only; may miss non-verbal cues (Limitations).  
- **Why unresolved:** Model uses only text; multimodal integration method unknown.  
- **What evidence would resolve:** Build a multimodal benchmark; extend HDR trajectory; show accuracy gains.

**Open Question 2: Comorbid Disorders**  
- **Question:** How to extend the HDR framework to handle comorbid psychiatric disorders?  
- **Basis:** [explicit] "Cases involving comorbidity are excluded" (Section 3).  
- **Why unresolved:** Single-label ignores real-world comorbidity; cannot diagnose multiple conditions.  
- **What evidence would resolve:** Multi-label benchmark; modify HDR for multiple hypotheses; evaluate with multi-label metrics.

**Open Question 3: Interpretability and Safety**  
- **Question:** How to improve interpretability and safety (uncertainty, bias) for LLM-based psychiatric diagnosis?  
- **Basis:** [explicit] Authors note interpretability and safety challenges, including hallucinations and biases in rare disorders.  
- **Why unresolved:** Model lacks uncertainty estimates and explanations; may fail on rare cases.  
- **What evidence would resolve:** Calibrated confidence scores; clinician-validated explanations; bias audits; rare-disorder stress tests; human studies.

## Limitations
- Knowledge base incompleteness: The ICD-11-aligned knowledge base may lack nuanced exclusion criteria or rare symptom presentations, risking invalid hypothesis generation.
- Reward scheduling fragility: Curriculum-based α-weights require meticulous tuning; inappropriate values could cause reward hacking or training instability.
- EHR format dependence: Training on structured EHRs may not generalize to messy, free-text clinical notes.

## Confidence
- HDR mechanism efficacy: High
- Curriculum RL stability: Medium
- KB grounding necessity: Medium-Low

## Next Checks
1. **KB coverage stress test:** Remove differential/exclusion criteria for 20% of disorders in K; measure drop in Disorder Accuracy to quantify reliance on fine-grained rules.
2. **Reward coefficient sweep:** Vary α₁:α₂:α₃ across epochs (e.g., disorder-focused vs. balanced); track Joint Accuracy stability to identify hacking thresholds.
3. **Cross-dataset robustness:** Evaluate MentalSeek-Dx on an external EHR corpus with unstructured clinical notes (e.g., raw progress notes) to test generalization beyond curated inputs.
