# VERSE: Visual Embedding Reduction and Space Exploration. Clustering-Guided Insights for Training Data Enhancement in Visually-Rich Document Understanding

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2601.05125  
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.05125  
- Reference count: 40  
- Primary result: VERSE improves F1 on MERIT Secret by up to +0.09 (Donut) and matches/exceeds SaaS solutions (GPT-4, Pixtral).  

## Executive Summary
VERSE addresses the misalignment between human-perceived synthetic data quality and a model's internal visual embedding distribution in visually-rich document understanding (VrDU). It reduces and visualizes model embeddings (PCA) to identify error-prone clusters, then guides targeted synthetic data augmentation for those regions. On MERIT Secret, Donut improves from 0.6712 to 0.7607 (+0.09) and Idefics2 from 0.7556 to 0.8101 (+0.06). These optimized on‑premise models match or exceed GPT‑4 (0.7821) and Pixtral (0.7267), with improvements concentrated in problematic clusters (e.g., low zoom, double tables) without harming overall generalization.

## Method Summary
VERSE first extracts embeddings from a frozen visual encoder for the validation set, reduces them to 2D/3D via PCA, and overlays human‑observed document features (Table A.8) and per‑sample F1 scores to locate low‑performing clusters. Next, for each problematic cluster, it isolates dominant features (e.g., double tables, low zoom) to construct a "booster" subset of synthetic training data containing those characteristics. Finally, it fine‑tunes the model's textual decoder or LoRA adapters on a mixture of a base synthetic dataset (e.g., MERIT Digital or Render) and the booster subset. Evaluation shows per‑cluster F1 gains without degrading performance on other clusters.

## Key Results
- Donut F1 on MERIT Secret: 0.6712 → 0.7607 (+0.0895).  
- Idefics2 F1: 0.7556 → 0.8101 (+0.0545).  
- Optimized models achieve or surpass GPT‑4 (0.7821) and Pixtral (0.7267) on the same benchmark.  

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
VERSE corrects the distribution mismatch between human‑labeled synthetic data quality and the model's internal representation space. The PCA‑based RES directly visualizes where the model makes errors (low‑F1 clusters). By augmenting training with synthetic samples that specifically populate these under‑performing regions, the model learns to handle previously problematic cases. This targeted approach is efficient because only the decoder (or adapters) is updated while the visual encoder remains frozen, preserving general knowledge. The focus on embedding space rather than raw image space ensures that augmentation aligns with the model's actual decision boundaries.

## Foundational Learning
- **Visual embeddings**: Derived from a frozen encoder; capture task‑agnostic visual features. *Why needed: Form the basis for clustering.* Quick check: Extract and inspect a few embedding vectors.  
- **PCA**: Reduces high‑dimensional embeddings to 2D/3D while retaining variance. *Why needed: Enables visual identification of clusters.* Quick check: Compute explained variance ratio; aim for >80% in 2 components.  
- **Clustering via visual overlay**: Not algorithmic but based on coloring RES by F1/features to spot groupings. *Why needed: Direct human insight into error patterns.* Quick check: Color points by F1; observe concentration.  
- **Targeted synthetic augmentation**: Using existing synthetic corpora to add samples with specific features. *Why needed: Provides labeled data for weak regions.* Quick check: Filter a dataset for a feature (e.g., low zoom) and count samples.  
- **Fine‑tuning with LoRA/decoder**: Updates only part of the model, keeping encoder fixed. *Why needed: Efficient adaptation without forgetting.* Quick check: Train a LoRA adapter and monitor validation loss.  

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component map**: Validation images → Visual Encoder (frozen) → Embeddings → PCA → Reduced Embedding Space (RES) → Feature/F1 overlay → Cluster identification → Booster subset → Fine‑tune (decoder/LoRA) → Model evaluation.  

**Critical path**: The workflow flows from data extraction to model fine‑tuning, with RES analysis and booster creation as intermediate decision points.  

**Design tradeoffs**:  
- **Frozen encoder** reduces compute but may limit domain adaptation; full fine‑tuning could yield larger gains at higher cost.  
- **PCA** is fast and interpretable but linear; non‑linear methods (t‑SNE, UMAP) might reveal clearer clusters but are less stable.  
- **Human‑curated features** provide transparency but may miss latent factors; automatic feature discovery on embeddings could be more comprehensive albeit less explainable.  

**Failure signatures**:  
- **Low Silhouette/trustworthiness**: PCA fails to preserve neighborhood structure → clusters are artifacts.  
- **Drop in overall F1 after booster training**: Booster data causes overfitting or distribution shift.  
- **No correlation between chosen features and low‑F1 clusters**: Feature set is inadequate; need to explore other metrics.  

**Three first experiments**:  
1. Extract embeddings and compute PCA; evaluate structure via trustworthiness/proximity metrics.  
2. Correlate projected features with per‑sample F1 scores using statistical tests (e.g., ANOVA) to validate feature relevance.  
3. Perform a small‑scale booster training (+10% of base data) targeting one feature and measure per‑cluster F1 change.  

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- Can the VERSE pipeline be inverted to generate synthetic training data directly from problematic embedding regions, and would such data effectively improve model performance while preserving visual plausibility?  
- To what extent do the principal components in the Reduced Embedding Space (RES) represent disentangled, human‑interpretable features versus polysemantic combinations, and how does this affect the reliability of feature‑based explanations?  
- What is the primary driver of richer, more task‑aligned visual embeddings: the visual encoder's pre‑training data alignment with documents, or its internal projection architecture (e.g., MLP+Perceiver vs. linear), given that models like Idefics2 and PaliGemma share the SigLIP base but differ in both aspects?  
- How generalizable is the VERSE methodology and its identified error‑inducing features to other VrDU tasks or document domains beyond Spanish academic transcripts?  

## Limitations
- NDA‑restricted MERIT Secret validation set is inaccessible; public split may not replicate distribution.  
- The paper does not release code for computing the human‑observed features (Table A.8), hindering exact reproduction.  
- Confidence in quantitative gains is low due to reliance on a private benchmark.
