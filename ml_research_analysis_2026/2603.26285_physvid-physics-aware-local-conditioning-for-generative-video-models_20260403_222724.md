# PhysVid: Physics Aware Local Conditioning for Generative Video Models

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID**: 2603.26285
- **Source URL**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.26285
- **Reference Count**: 40
- **Primary Result**: Improves Physical Commonsense score by ~33% relative on VideoPhy and up to ~8% on VideoPhy2 compared to larger baselines.

## Executive Summary
Generative video models often produce outputs that violate basic physical principles. PhysVid addresses this by introducing physics-aware conditioning at the level of short temporal video chunks. During training, each chunk is annotated with a structured physics description using a Vision-Language Model, and these local prompts are fused with the global text prompt via a novel chunk-aware cross-attention mechanism. At inference, counterfactual physics prompts are generated from the caption to guide the model away from implausible dynamics via classifier-free guidance. On the VideoPhy and VideoPhy2 benchmarks, PhysVid (1.7B parameters) significantly outperforms the much larger Wan-14B (14B) baseline in physical plausibility metrics, demonstrating the effectiveness of localized physical conditioning.

## Method Summary
PhysVid fine-tunes a pretrained flow-matching video generator (Wan-1.3B) using a two-stage process. First, training videos are split into 7 contiguous temporal chunks (~0.7s each). A VLM (VideoLLama3-7B) processes each chunk with the global caption to generate a structured physics prompt. The model architecture is extended with parallel chunk-aware cross-attention layers in every transformer block; these layers use Rotary Positional Embeddings on a 2D grid (chunk index, intra-chunk position) to tightly bind local text tokens to their corresponding video spatiotemporal region. During training, the model optimizes the flow-matching loss using both global and local prompts. For inference, an LLM generates both positive local prompts and deliberately physics-violating counterfactual prompts from the global caption alone. The sampling loop applies classifier-free guidance that combines predictions from both prompt sets to steer generation toward physically plausible outcomes.

## Key Results
- On VideoPhy, PhysVid (1.7B) achieves a ~33% relative improvement in Physical Commonsense (PC) score over the Wan-14B baseline.
- On VideoPhy2, it achieves up to ~8% relative PC improvement.
- Ablations confirm that both the chunk-aware local conditioning and counterfactual guidance contribute significantly to the gains, outperforming standard fine-tuning approaches.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1
*Claim*: Injecting physics knowledge at short temporal chunks captures local phenomena more precisely than global text alone, improving physical commonsense scores.

*Mechanism*: Videos are divided into contiguous chunks (~0.7s). A VLM processes each chunk with the global caption to produce a natural-language description focused on dynamics, shape, and optics. These chunk-level prompts are used alongside the global prompt during training.

*Core Assumption*: Physical laws manifest over short intervals and can be reliably extracted by an off-the-shelf VLM; the temporal granularity matches the model’s conditioning capacity.

*Evidence Anchors*: Abstract; Section 3.1 details VLM annotation procedure and output structure (JSON with `visible_elements`, `thinking`, `physics`).

*Break Condition*: Inconsistent, contradictory, or factually incorrect VLM descriptions across chunks would degrade rather than improve plausibility.

### Mechanism 2
*Claim*: Chunk-aware cross-attention aligns each physics prompt with its corresponding video segment, preventing interference between local and global conditioning.

*Mechanism*: Additional cross-attention layers are inserted into every transformer block. Video query tokens use 3D RoPE (frame, height, width). Concatenated chunk-level text keys use 2D RoPE (chunk index, intra-chunk position). This position-aware design makes attention weights depend on the relative spatiotemporal grid, so a token from chunk *i* primarily attends to text from the same chunk.

*Core Assumption*: Explicit positional encoding across both modalities creates a tight chunk-level coupling without disrupting the global pathway.

*Evidence Anchors*: Abstract; Section 3.2 presents Algorithm 1 and explains text grid construction, RoPE application, and attention logit design.

*Break Condition*: Mis-specified chunk boundaries, incorrect RoPE parameters, or variable token counts per chunk would cause physics information to leak into wrong segments, producing incoherent outputs.

### Mechanism 3
*Claim*: Counterfactual prompting during inference acts as a negative guidance signal, pushing generation away from physically implausible trajectories and improving PC score.

*Mechanism*: Given the global caption, an LLM first generates local positive prompts (as in training). Then, for each positive prompt, it produces a counterfactual prompt that deliberately violates the same physics principles (e.g., wheels rotating too fast). Classifier-free guidance uses both: `(1+w)*pred(positive) - w*pred(counterfactual)` during denoising.

*Core Assumption*: Explicitly modeling what *should not* happen helps the model learn a sharper boundary between feasible and infeasible physical states, functioning as a regularizer.

*Evidence Anchors*: Abstract; Section 3.3 describes counterfactual generation, the guidance equation, and CFG application at both global and chunk levels.

*Break Condition*: Poorly crafted counterfactuals (not truly contradictory or too extreme) could destabilize sampling or reduce visual fidelity.

## Foundational Learning
- **Concept**: Diffusion / Flow Matching models  
  *Why needed*: PhysVid builds on Wan2.1, a flow-matching video generator. Understanding iterative denoising and conditioning injection is fundamental.  
  *Quick check*: What is the role of the neural network in flow matching, and how does it differ from standard diffusion?

- **Concept**: Cross-Attention in Transformers  
  *Why needed*: Local conditioning relies on cross-attention between video tokens and text prompts. Knowing the original mechanism and positional embedding incorporation is essential.  
  *Quick check*: How does Rotary Positional Embedding (RoPE) encode position within the attention score calculation?

- **Concept**: Vision-Language Models (VLMs)  
  *Why needed*: The entire data annotation pipeline depends on a VLM to extract physics descriptions. Appreciating VLM limitations (hallucination) is crucial for interpreting training set noise.  
  *Quick check*: What are common failure modes when using VLMs to generate structured outputs from video, and how can they be mitigated?

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map**: Pretrained Wan2.1 generator -> Added chunk-aware cross-attention modules (inserted per block) -> VLM (VideoLLama3-7B) for training annotation -> LLM for inference prompt generation -> Data pipeline (video loader -> frame sampling -> temporal chunking -> VLM annotation) -> Training script (two-stage optimization) -> Inference script (LLM prompt generation, sampling with CFG) -> Evaluation tools (VideoPhy, VideoPhy2).

- **Critical path (training)**:  
  1. Load video, extract frames, split into *N* chunks.  
  2. For each chunk, run VLM with global caption to obtain physics prompt.  
  3. Concatenate all chunk prompts as local condition.  
  4. For every transformer block, compute global cross-attention (to global caption) and chunk-aware cross-attention (to local prompts).  
  5. Compute flow-matching loss against target clean latent.  
  6. Stage 1: update only added modules (1000 steps). Stage 2: update all (2000 steps).  
  (Inference: generate local & counterfactual prompts from global caption; run denoising with Eq. 2.)

- **Design tradeoffs**:
  - *Chunk size*: Smaller chunks increase granularity and VLM calls but improve physics detail; larger chunks reduce overhead but may miss short phenomena.
  - *Parameter budget*: Adding ~400M parameters boosts realism but slows training/inference (~1.7× slower than Wan-1.3B) and increases GPU memory use.
  - *Two-stage training*: Stabilizes new module incorporation but adds hyperparameter complexity (different LRs, step counts).
  - *VLM/LLM reliance*: Automated annotations are cheap but may contain hallucinations; manual verification improves quality at higher cost.
  - *Fidelity vs. physics*: Gains in PC often come with a slight drop in semantic alignment and similarity metrics (LPIPS, FVD).

- **Failure signatures**:
  - No PC improvement (or regression) on VideoPhy/VideoPhy2 → conditioning ineffective, possibly due to prompt quality.
  - Visible flickering or abrupt changes at chunk boundaries → misaligned RoPE or incorrect chunk partitioning.
  - Large increase in FVD/LPIPS vs. baseline → over-specialization on physics at expense of visual quality.
  - Training loss unstable after unfreezing base → learning rate too high; need gradient clipping or warm-up.
  - VLM annotation crashes (non-JSON output) → insufficient parsing robustness; add format checks.

- **First 3 experiments**:
  1. **Main quantitative evaluation**: Run PhysVid on VideoPhy and VideoPhy2; compare PC and SA against Wan-1.3B and Wan-14B to confirm the reported ~33% relative PC gain.
  2. **Ablation – no local conditioning**: Fine-tune Wan-1.3B on the same dataset *without* adding chunk-aware cross-attention blocks; measure the drop in PC to isolate the contribution of local conditioning.
  3. **Ablation – no counterfactual guidance**: Run PhysVid inference with blank local prompts (i.e., no negative guidance); evaluate PC decrease to quantify the benefit of counterfactual prompting.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
### Open Question 1
- **Question**: How robust is the VLM-based annotation process to hallucination and misalignment when processing longer or more physically complex videos?
- **Basis in paper**: Appendix 6.4 states VLM hallucination and misalignment limit reliable physics extraction from longer or complex videos.
- **Why unresolved**: The method depends on VLM accuracy for chunk-level prompts during annotation and inference. The authors acknowledge limitations but do not quantify how these failures affect final video quality for complex scenes.
- **What evidence would resolve it**: Systematic evaluation on videos with diverse complexity (multi-object interactions, occlusions, long-duration phenomena) where ground-truth physics annotations exist. Measure correlation between VLM output errors and degradation in PC score. Controlled studies with manually corrected prompts could isolate annotation error impact.

### Open Question 2
- **Question**: To what extent does the train-test distribution mismatch in VLM prompt generation (with visual input during training annotation vs. text-only during inference) limit local physics conditioning effectiveness?
- **Basis in paper**: Appendix 6.4 notes the mismatch: inference prompt generation relies solely on global text without visual input, potentially leading to less coherent local prompts.
- **Why unresolved**: Despite reported PC improvements, the paper does not measure performance loss due to this mismatch. The lack of visual context during inference could reduce prompt coherence and affect realism.
- **What evidence would resolve it**: Ablate inference-stage prompt generation: (1) text-only (current method) vs. (2) with access to ground-truth frames (simulating training). Compare PC scores and human evaluations of physical plausibility to quantify mismatch impact.

### Open Question 3
- **Question**: How can semantic misalignment between chunk-level physics prompts be minimized without prohibitive computational cost, to ensure temporally coherent physical realism?
- **Basis in paper**: Appendix 6.4 states that while the global caption in the VLM instruction helps alignment, it does not explicitly prevent semantic misalignment among chunks. Future work could reduce computational cost of local pathways and improve inter-chunk alignment.
- **Why unresolved**: The current method lacks explicit consistency enforcement between consecutive chunks (e.g., continuous motion, persistent object states), potentially causing jarring transitions or contradictory physics descriptions.
- **What evidence would resolve it**: Develop and test lightweight consistency constraints during VLM annotation (e.g., penalizing state changes between adjacent chunks) or during training (e.g., inter-chunk contrastive losses). Evaluate using metrics measuring temporal smoothness of physical properties (velocity continuity, object persistence) alongside PC scores.

## Limitations
- **VLM annotation quality**: Training relies on VideoLLama3-7B to generate chunk-level physics prompts; hallucinations or noise could propagate and undermine the local conditioning signal.
- **Architecture details unspecified**: Exact implementation of chunk-aware cross-attention (head count, projection dimensions, RoPE frequencies) is unclear, risking misalignment between local text and video tokens.
- **Guidance formulation ambiguity**: The combination of global and local classifier-free guidance (Eq. 2) and LLM sampling parameters is ambiguous; poorly tuned negative prompts could destabilize sampling.

## Confidence
- **Reported PC improvements on VideoPhy/VideoPhy2**: High (benchmark numbers are provided and statistically significant).
- **Mechanistic explanations (local conditioning, chunk-aware attention, counterfactual guidance)**: Medium (plausible but reproducibility gaps in implementation details).
- **Robustness to VLM errors and train-test mismatch**: Low (the paper acknowledges these as open questions without quantification).
