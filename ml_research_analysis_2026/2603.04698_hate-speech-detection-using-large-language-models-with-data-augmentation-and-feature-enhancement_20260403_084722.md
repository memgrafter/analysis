# Hate Speech Detection using Large Language Models with Data Augmentation and Feature Enhancement

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2603.04698
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.04698
- **Reference count:** 27
- **Primary result:** Data augmentation boosts Delta TF-IDF to 98.2% accuracy, while gpt-oss-20b leads baseline LLMs at 87.9% on the merged dataset.

## Executive Summary
This paper investigates the effect of data augmentation and feature enhancement on hate speech detection, comparing traditional bag-of-words models (Delta TF-IDF) with state-of-the-art transformer-based large language models (DistilBERT, RoBERTa, DeBERTa, Gemma-7B, gpt-oss-20b). Experiments are conducted on datasets containing both implicit and explicit hate speech. The authors report that the open-source gpt-oss-20b model achieves the highest baseline accuracy (87.9% on the merged dataset). More strikingly, Delta TF-IDF exhibits a dramatic performance increase when combined with text data augmentation, reaching 98.2% accuracy. The proposed mechanism suggests that augmentation enriches the lexical feature space for bag-of-words models, but this benefit may not extend to semantic models like LLMs. The study highlights a potential trade-off between lexical and semantic approaches in hate speech detection.

## Method Summary
The study evaluates a range of classifiers: a traditional bag-of-words approach using Delta TF-IDF and several transformer-based LLMs (DistilBERT, RoBERTa, DeBERTa, Gemma-7B, gpt-oss-20b). Data augmentation techniques—specifically synonym replacement and word swapping—are applied to the training data to increase the diversity and quantity of hate speech examples, addressing class imbalance. Models are tested on datasets that include both implicit and explicit hate speech, with a merged dataset used for primary performance comparisons. The impact of augmentation is measured by comparing model accuracy (and likely other metrics) with and without augmentation.

## Key Results
- The open-source gpt-oss-20b model achieves the highest baseline performance (87.9% accuracy on the merged dataset).
- Delta TF-IDF with data augmentation achieves 98.2% accuracy (exact dataset conditions unclear from the abstract).
- The abstract does not report the performance of LLMs when combined with augmentation, leaving a gap in comparative analysis.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
**Mechanism 1: Data augmentation boosts bag-of-words models.**
- **Claim:** Data augmentation significantly improves the performance of traditional bag-of-words models like Delta TF-IDF for hate speech detection, particularly on datasets with explicit hate speech.
- **Mechanism:** Delta TF-IDF relies on lexical frequency patterns. Augmentation (e.g., synonym replacement, word swap) artificially increases the diversity and volume of the minority hate speech class, enriching the feature space with more varied term contexts. This helps the model learn more robust discriminative TF-IDF weights for hate-indicative tokens without altering the fundamental bag-of-words representation.
- **Core assumption:** The hate speech signal is lexically grounded in specific keywords and phrases that can be effectively varied through surface-level transformations while retaining class label integrity.
- **Evidence:** The abstract reports that Delta TF-IDF responds strongly to text data augmentation, reaching 98.2% accuracy on the merged dataset (truncated).

2. **Delta TF-IDF**
   - *Why needed:* To emphasize terms that are discriminative between hate and non-hate classes by comparing term frequencies across classes, providing a lexical feature representation for traditional classifiers.
   - *Quick check:* How does Delta TF-IDF differ from standard TF-IDF? (Answer: Delta TF-IDF weights terms based on their relative frequency difference between two classes rather than within a single document corpus.)

3. **Transformer Models**

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component Map:**  
Raw Text -> (Augmentation) -> Tokenization -> Feature Extraction (Delta TF-IDF / Transformer) -> Classification -> Prediction

**Critical Path:**  
For Delta TF-IDF: Text undergoes augmentation (optional), then is converted into a Delta TF-IDF vector, which is fed to a linear classifier (e.g., logistic regression). For Transformers: Text is tokenized, passed through the transformer encoder to obtain contextual embeddings, pooled, and classified by a linear head. Augmentation is applied only during training.

**Design Tradeoffs:**  
- *Delta TF-IDF:* Highly interpretable and computationally efficient, but limited to lexical patterns; struggles with implicit hate and out-of-vocabulary words. Augmentation directly enhances its feature space.
- *Transformers:* Capture deep semantics and handle implicit hate better, but are computationally expensive, less interpretable, and may be less sensitive to surface-level augmentation because they already model linguistic variation through pretraining.

**Failure Signatures:**  
- *Delta TF-IDF:* Misclassifications often occur when hate speech uses uncommon synonyms or creative phrasing not covered by augmentation, or when non-hate text contains hate-associated keywords (false positives).
- *Transformers:* Failures may arise from nuanced context that requires world knowledge, or from overfitting to spurious correlations in the training data; augmentation may not resolve these.

**First Experiments (3):**  
1. Baseline performance: Train and evaluate each model (with and without augmentation) on the standard test set to establish reference metrics.
2. Augmentation ablation: Apply individual augmentation techniques (synonym replacement only, word swap only) to Delta TF-IDF to determine which contributes most to the 98.2% accuracy.
3. Cross-dataset generalization: Test the best-performing model (Delta TF-IDF + augmentation) on a separate implicit hate speech dataset to probe the generalizability claim.

## Limitations
- The abstract is truncated, preventing verification of the exact dataset and conditions for Delta TF-IDF's 98.2% accuracy.
- The paper does not explain why LLMs show less responsiveness to augmentation compared to Delta TF-IDF.
- It is unclear whether the strong Delta TF-IDF result generalizes to implicit hate speech or other corpora beyond the merged dataset.

## Confidence
- **High confidence:** Delta TF-IDF responds strongly to augmentation on at least one evaluated dataset configuration.
- **Medium confidence:** gpt-oss-20b achieves the highest baseline performance among the LLMs tested.
- **Low confidence:** The generalizability of Delta TF-IDF's augmentation benefits to implicit hate speech or diverse corpora.
