# Developing and evaluating a chatbot to support maternal health care

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2603.13168
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.13168
- Reference count: 40
- Primary result: A stage-aware safety triage system combined with hybrid retrieval and evidence-conditioned LLM generation achieves high recall in emergency detection (86.7%) and improves end-to-end safety metrics when deployed for maternal health queries in low-resource, multilingual settings.

## Executive Summary
The paper presents a multi-component chatbot system for maternal health support in low-resource, multilingual settings with typically short, code-mixed, and underspecified user queries. The core contributions include a stage-aware safety triage layer that routes high-risk queries to pre-approved templates before retrieval/generation, a hybrid retrieval approach (BM25 plus dense embeddings with Reciprocal Rank Fusion and clinical reranking) to accurately surface evidence-bearing guideline chunks, and a constrained LLM generation stage with explicit safety labeling and post-generation escalation checks. Evaluation is performed using a synthetic triage benchmark (N=150) and a multi-evidence retrieval benchmark, complemented by LLM-as-judge on 781 real queries and expert validation. Results show that the triage achieves 86.7% recall and 89.7% precision on emergencies, hybrid retrieval outperforms single-modality retrievers on early ranking metrics, and the full pipeline improves correctness, reduces spillage, and enhances emergency flagging compared to a simple RAG baseline. The work underscores the necessity of defense-in-depth design and multi-method evaluation for high-stakes deployments.

## Method Summary
The authors designed and evaluated a maternal health chatbot for deployment in low-resource, multilingual environments. The system integrates three main safety mechanisms: a pre-generation stage-aware triage that classifies queries as `EMERGENCY NOW`, `SAME DAY`, or `PASS` using rule-based pattern matching and semantic similarity against a curated symptom bank; hybrid retrieval (BM25 + multilingual dense embeddings) combined via Reciprocal Rank Fusion and reranked with a clinical cross-encoder (MedCPT) to prioritize guideline chunks containing direct answers; and evidence-conditioned generation with GPT-4-Turbo, constrained by a label-first output format and strict grounding in retrieved context, along with a post-generation parser to catch any missed escalations. Evaluation leveraged synthetic benchmarks for component testing (150-profile triage set, 100-query multi-evidence retrieval set), LLM-as-judge comparisons across system variants on 781 historical queries, and expert validation on safety-critical cases. The results demonstrate that combining these layers improves safety metrics such as emergency recall, spillage reduction, and response grounding compared to a baseline RAG system without triage.

## Key Results
- Stage-aware triage on a labeled benchmark (N=150) achieved 86.7% recall and 89.7% precision for `EMERGENCY NOW` classification, with 100% precision on real-world emergency template escalations validated by experts.
- Hybrid retrieval (RRF + MedCPT) outperformed single-modality retrievers on early ranking metrics for a multi-evidence synthetic set, achieving a Mean Reciprocal Rank (MRR) of 0.618 and Recall@5 of 0.340 for `DIRECT` evidence chunks.
- End-to-end LLM-as-judge evaluation (N=781) showed that adding safety triage to RAG improved correctness (1.57→1.39 on a 1–3 scale, lower better), reduced spillage (1.18→1.03), and improved emergency flagging compared to a simple RAG baseline.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1: Stage-Aware Safety Triage
- **Claim:** A pre-generation stage-aware safety triage layer reduces the probability of the LLM producing unsafe or inappropriate responses for high-risk queries by routing them to expert templates before retrieval/generation.
- **Mechanism:** The system uses deterministic pattern matching (high precision) and semantic similarity (improved recall) to classify queries into `EMERGENCY NOW`, `SAME DAY`, or `PASS` based on life stage and symptom patterns. High-risk classifications bypass the LLM entirely, returning fixed, vetted escalation templates.
- **Core assumption:** In low-resource, multilingual settings with underspecified queries, a simple, low-latency rule-based+semantic layer can reliably identify the most critical safety cases (missed emergencies are costlier than over-escalation) and prevent an unconstrained LLM from handling them.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - Section 5.1 details the stage-aware taxonomy and routing policy, e.g., "fever ≥102°F" is `SAME DAY` for pregnancy but `EMERGENCY NOW` for neonates.
  - Section 7.3 reports the labeled triage benchmark results: 86.7% emergency recall, 89.7% precision. 95.6% recall for `EMERGENCY NOW` vs. 77.8% for `SAME DAY`.
  - Section 7.3 expert validation on real queries (N=17) showed 100% precision on emergency escalation templates (0 false negatives).
- **Break condition:** If the false negative rate for `EMERGENCY NOW` queries rises significantly above ~13.3% (from the benchmark) in real deployment, or if expert review finds missed emergencies in passed queries, the triage thresholds or symptom bank require recalibration.

### Mechanism 2: Hybrid Retrieval
- **Claim:** Hybrid retrieval (sparse + dense fusion) improves the early ranking of multi-fragment, evidence-bearing guideline chunks compared to either retriever alone, increasing the likelihood that the LLM receives sufficient context for safe, complete answers.
- **Mechanism:** Reciprocal Rank Fusion (RRF) combines BM25 (lexical precision for clinical terms) and multilingual dense embeddings (semantic robustness to code-mixing/paraphrasing). This surfaces answer-bearing (`DIRECT`) evidence higher in the candidate list, which is crucial because missing a single critical chunk (e.g., an escalation criterion) can lead to unsafe guidance.
- **Core assumption:** For maternal health queries, topical similarity is insufficient; retrieval must prioritize chunks that *directly* contain answer-critical information (danger signs, thresholds), and these chunks are more likely to be ranked early by a hybrid approach.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - Section 6 explains the design: "Escalation is not confined to this pre-RAG layer; the generation stage retains the ability to escalate... but retrieval quality... is better characterized by evidence sufficiency."
  - Section 7.2 synthetic benchmark results (Table 2): Hybrid RRF + reranker achieves higher MRR (0.618) and R@5 (0.340) than dense-only (MRR 0.492, R@5 0.237) or BM25-only (MRR 0.341, R@5 0.182). Hit@50 is ~0.93 for all hybrids, but early precision differs.
  - Section 7.2 gold-set audit: Only 5.0% of non-`DIRECT` top-10 chunks were newly identified as answer-bearing, confirming ranking errors, not annotation gaps, drive performance.
- **Break condition:** If, on the synthetic multi-evidence benchmark, hybrid retrieval fails to significantly outperform the best single retriever on early metrics (R@5, MRR), the fusion parameters (e.g., RRF constant) or candidate pool size need optimization.

### Mechanism 3: Evidence-Conditioned Generation
- **Claim:** Evidence-conditioned LLM generation, combined with post-generation safety checks, reduces "spillage" (unsolicited medical information) and improves grounding in retrieved guidelines, making responses more conservative and safe under uncertainty.
- **Mechanism:** The generator (GPT-4-Turbo) receives a prompt with explicit constraints: answer *only* using provided context, emit a safety label first (e.g., `NOW-MED`, `PASS`), avoid prescriptions/gender speculation, and state uncertainty if evidence is insufficient. A post-generation parser checks the first line for escalation signals, providing a second safety layer.
- **Core assumption:** Constraining the LLM's output format and grounding it in retrieved, curated documents reduces hallucination and overconfidence, especially for ambiguous queries where a free-form LLM might speculate.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - Section 4: "Guardrails are enforced via (i) routing to templates... and (ii) constrained generation... prompting to ground claims in retrieved evidence, avoid unsupported assertions..."
  - Section 8.1 LLM-as-judge results (Table 6): `RAG + Safety Triage` vs. `Simple RAG` shows lower spillage (1.03→1.18), better "don't-know" usage (1.23→1.42), and improved RAG grounding (1.89→2.48, lower is better).
  - Section 8.1: "Adding triage also improves faithfulness under retrieval: RAG Grounding improves substantially (2.48→1.89), suggesting that routing and constrained templates reduce the tendency to ignore retrieved evidence."
- **Break condition:** If spillage scores do not decrease or grounding scores do not improve when adding the evidence-conditioned prompt + label-first constraint (vs. a less constrained baseline), the prompt engineering or the LLM's instruction-following capability for this task is insufficient.

## Foundational Learning

### Concept: Stage-Aware Triage
- **Why needed here:** User queries are short, code-mixed, and lack clinical context. Identical symptoms (e.g., headache) have different urgency across pregnancy, postpartum, and newborn stages. A one-size-fits-all triage would misroute many cases.
- **Quick check question:** How would you design a rule to differentiate a "same-day" vs. "emergency" fever for a 2-week-old newborn versus a 30-week pregnant woman, based on the paper's policy?

### Concept: Evidence Sufficiency vs. Topical Similarity in Retrieval
- **Why needed here:** In maternal health, a passage can be on-topic (e.g., "preeclampsia") but omit the critical escalation threshold (e.g., "BP >160/110"). Retrieval must find *all* necessary fragments, not just vaguely related ones.
- **Quick check question:** Why is Recall@5 (early ranking) more important than Recall@50 for this system's RAG pipeline?

### Concept: Defense-in-Depth for High-Stakes Deployment
- **Why needed here:** No single component (triage, retrieval, or generation) is perfect. Failures in one layer must be caught by another (e.g., generation-time escalation check after retrieval). Relying solely on LLM judgment is unsafe.
- **Quick check question:** List the three independent safety checks in the pipeline (Figure 1) and what failure mode each primarily addresses.

## Architecture Onboarding

### Component map
Input Normalization -> Stage Extraction -> Pre-Generation Triage -> Hybrid Retrieval -> Evidence-Conditioned Generation -> Post-Generation Escalation Check

### Critical path
Query -> Stage/Triage (if emergency → template) -> Retrieval/Rerank -> Generate (label-first) -> Post-check (if emergency → template) -> Response.

### Design tradeoffs
- **Triage:** High precision (few false alarms) vs. high recall (catch all emergencies). Paper prioritizes recall for `EMERGENCY NOW` (86.7%) but accepts lower `SAME DAY` recall (77.8%).
- **Retrieval:** Early ranking sharpness (MRR) vs. candidate recall (Hit@50). Hybrid improves early ranking; reranker choice (MiniLM vs. MedCPT) trades topical relevance for clinical salience (danger signs).
- **Generation:** Completeness vs. safety. `NoRAG` is more complete but less safe; `RAG + Triage` is safer but may refuse valid questions or mismatch language (templates in English).

### Failure signatures
- **Triage failure:** False negative (emergency query passed to LLM) → potential unsafe generation. False positive (non-urgent query escalated) → user anxiety, system load.
- **Retrieval failure:** Retrieved chunks lack a key `DIRECT` evidence piece (e.g., "go to hospital if bleeding >1 pad/hour") → LLM generates incomplete/unsafe answer.
- **Generation failure:** LLM ignores retrieved evidence (low grounding), spills prohibited content (drug names, gender prediction), or fails to emit a safety label (`NOW-*`) when evidence warrants it.

### First 3 experiments
1. **Triage benchmark replication:** Run the 150-profile labeled triage benchmark. Confirm emergency recall >85% and precision >85%. If not, adjust semantic similarity thresholds (`τ_now=0.50`, `τ_sd=0.30`) or symptom bank.
2. **Retrieval ablation on synthetic multi-evidence set:** Compare `Hybrid RRF + MedCPT` vs. `Hybrid RRF + MiniLM` vs. `Dense-only` on DIRECT evidence MRR and R@5. Expect MedCPT to improve safety-oriented generation even if synthetic ranking is slightly worse (Table 2 vs. Table 3).
3. **LLM-as-judge evaluation with/without triage:** Use the 781 real queries, comparing `Simple RAG` vs. `RAG + Safety Triage`. Expect spillage (1.18→1.03) and grounding (2.48→1.89) to improve. If language match degrades severely for non-English queries, prioritize template localization.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- **Question:** What is the real-world effectiveness and safety of the maternal health chatbot when deployed to actual users, and how does it compare to offline evaluation benchmarks?
- **Basis in paper:** [explicit] "Future work will evaluate the performance of the chatbot with real users; one limitation of the current study is that users who interact with the system may ask different questions than in the historical question set."
- **Why unresolved:** The current evaluation relied on historical data and synthetic benchmarks, but live user queries and interaction patterns may differ, potentially affecting triage accuracy, response quality, and safety outcomes.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Conducting a prospective pilot deployment with real users, systematically recording interactions, and measuring key metrics (e.g., emergency recall/precision, user satisfaction, harm incidents) against the offline benchmark results.

### Open Question 2
- **Question:** How can the triage system be enhanced to improve recall for same-day urgent cases (currently 77.8%) without significantly increasing over-escalation and false alarms?
- **Basis in paper:** [inferred] Table 4 shows overall emergency recall of 86.7%, but the breakdown reveals 95.6% recall for EMERGENCY NOW vs. 77.8% for SAME DAY. The authors note that "83.3% of missed emergencies are SAME DAY queries," indicating a recall gap for urgent-but-not-immediate scenarios.
- **Why unresolved:** The rule-based and semantic matching components prioritize precision, which may sacrifice recall for same-day urgency. The optimal balance between safety and over-escalation has not been systematically optimized.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** An ablation study adjusting semantic matching thresholds or training a learned classifier that uses additional features (e.g., symptom clusters, contextual cues) to better identify same-day urgency, while closely monitoring false positives on a labeled validation set.

### Open Question 3
- **Question:** What localization strategies are most effective for delivering safety-critical response templates in multiple languages, given that language mismatch degraded Language Match scores for non-English queries?
- **Basis in paper:** [inferred] LLM-as-judge results (Table 6) show Language Match degradation for triage-enabled variants because emergency/urgent templates are only available in English. The authors state: "This motivated localizing templates (or generating template variants per language) for deployment."
- **Why unresolved:** The paper identifies the problem but does not implement or compare any localization approaches. The impact of translation (machine vs. human) or generative methods on safety, accuracy, and user comprehension remains unknown.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** A controlled experiment in target languages comparing several template localization methods (e.g., professional translation, machine translation, context-aware generation) on dimensions such as language match, clinical correctness, cultural appropriateness, and user understanding.

### Open Question 4
- **Question:** How reliable are coarse-grained evaluation dimensions (e.g., Communication Quality) when assessed by human experts, and how can evaluation protocols be refined to improve consistency?
- **Basis in paper:** [inferred] Expert inter-rater agreement for Communication Quality was low (QWK=0.14, range -0.04–0.19). The authors attribute this to inherent subjectivity but do not propose a remedy.
- **Why unresolved:** Inconsistent scoring undermines the credibility of expert evaluations, particularly for subjective but critical aspects like tone and clarity. No systematic effort to improve rater agreement or operationalize rubrics was presented.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Develop a detailed rubric with concrete exemplars and conduct rater training; measure agreement before and after training. Additionally, explore complementary methods (e.g., user studies, comprehension tests) to validate communication quality assessments.

## Limitations

- **Corpus & benchmark secrecy:** The exact maternal health guideline corpus, its chunking, and the full 22-symptom bank/rule patterns are not released, blocking exact replication of retrieval and triage.
- **LLM-as-judge reliability:** End-to-end safety assessment relies on LLM-as-judge (Gemini Pro) without sufficient human validation for non-emergency responses, risking misalignment with expert safety judgment.
- **Real-world query distribution:** The 781-query sample may not capture the full diversity of underspecified, code-mixed inputs in low-resource settings; deployment performance could differ.

## Confidence

- **Mechanism 1 (Stage-aware triage):** High confidence. Strong evidence from synthetic benchmark (86.7% recall) and expert validation on real emergents (100% precision).
- **Mechanism 2 (Hybrid retrieval):** Medium confidence. Synthetic multi-evidence benchmark shows hybrid gains, but real-world retrieval for ambiguous, fragmented queries remains unproven.
- **Mechanism 3 (Evidence-conditioned generation):** Low confidence. LLM-judge metrics improve, but without human evaluation of non-escalated outputs, true safety/grounding gains are uncertain.
