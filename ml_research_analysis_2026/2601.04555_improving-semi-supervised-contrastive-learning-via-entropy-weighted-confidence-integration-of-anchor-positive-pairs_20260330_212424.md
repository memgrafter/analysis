# Improving Semi-Supervised Contrastive Learning via Entropy-Weighted Confidence Integration of Anchor-Positive Pairs

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2601.04555
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.04555
- Reference count: 0
- Key outcome: Entropy-weighted confidence integration improves semi-supervised contrastive learning accuracy by up to 1.3% on CIFAR-100

## Executive Summary
This paper introduces a semi-supervised contrastive learning method that addresses the limitation of conventional approaches where samples with low predicted probabilities are discarded. The proposed method incorporates entropy-based confidence estimation and adaptive weighting for both anchor and positive samples, enabling more effective utilization of unlabeled data. By modifying the loss function to account for sample confidence, the approach assigns pseudo-labels to a broader range of samples. Experiments on CIFAR-10 and CIFAR-100 datasets demonstrate improved classification accuracy, particularly under low-label conditions, with gains of up to 1.3% on CIFAR-100 compared to baseline methods.

## Method Summary
The method extends semi-supervised contrastive learning by introducing entropy-based confidence estimation for unlabeled samples. It computes the entropy of predicted probability distributions to assess sample confidence, then applies adaptive weighting to both anchor and positive pairs based on their confidence levels. The proposed loss function uses the geometric mean of anchor and positive weights, allowing the model to down-weight contrastive pairs where either sample has low confidence. Samples with entropy below a threshold are assigned pseudo-labels and weighted based on their confidence level, while those above the threshold are treated as in conventional methods. The approach is evaluated on CIFAR-10 and CIFAR-100 using WideResNet-28-2 architecture with varying numbers of labeled samples per class.

## Key Results
- Proposed method achieves up to 1.3% higher classification accuracy on CIFAR-100 compared to baseline contrastive learning
- Performance improvements are most pronounced under low-label conditions (4 labels per class)
- Entropy-based weighting enables broader pseudo-label assignment while maintaining quality through confidence-based weighting
