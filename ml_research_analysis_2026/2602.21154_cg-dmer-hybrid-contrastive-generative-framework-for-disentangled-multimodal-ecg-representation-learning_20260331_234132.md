# CG-DMER: Hybrid Contrastive-Generative Framework for Disentangled Multimodal ECG Representation Learning

## Quick Facts
* **arXiv ID:** 2602.21154
* **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.21154
* **Reference count:** 0
* **Primary result:** Achieves state-of-the-art 90.31% AUC on PTBXL-Super linear probing (100% data), exceeding prior best (88.67%), and 76.00% average AUC in zero-shot classification.

## Executive Summary
CG-DMER tackles two core challenges in multimodal ECG learning: (1) intra-modality neglect of spatial-temporal lead dependencies, and (2) inter-modality noise from free-text reports. It introduces a hybrid framework that combines spatial-temporal masked ECG reconstruction with a disentanglement strategy. The model separates modality-specific and modality-shared features, then applies contrastive (SigLIP) and generative losses to align shared representations while reconstructing full inputs. This design captures fine-grained ECG dynamics and mitigates report noise, yielding superior performance in linear probing and zero-shot classification across three datasets.

## Method Summary
The method processes 12-lead ECG and clinical reports in three stages. First, ECG signals are split into temporal patches per lead; each patch is tokenized via a CNN and enriched with lead-specific and temporal position embeddings. Text is tokenized using Med-CPT with 15% random masking. Second, separate Transformer encoders process each modality. Their `[CLS]` outputs are fed into disentanglement heads (MLPs) that split features into shared (`h_sh`) and specific (`h_sp`) vectors. Third, a hybrid loss combines: (a) orthogonality loss (`L_orth`) penalizing similarity between `h_sh` and `h_sp`; (b) SigLIP and InfoNCE contrastive losses (`L_SigLIP`, `L_Cons`) applied only to `h_sh`; (c) reconstruction losses—MSE for masked ECG patches (`L_e_rec`) and masked language modeling for text (`L_t_rec`). Training uses AdamW with a combined loss weighting these components.

## Key Results
* **Linear probing:** On PTBXL-Super with 100% labeled data, CG-DMER achieves 90.31% macro AUC, surpassing MERL (88.67%).
* **Zero-shot classification:** Averages 76.00% macro AUC across datasets, outperforming MERL (75.25%).
* **Ablation:** Removing any key component (orthogonality, SigLIP, or reconstruction losses) degrades performance, confirming each contribution.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
**Claim:** Spatial-temporal masked ECG modeling captures fine-grained temporal dynamics and inter-lead spatial dependencies that lead-agnostic methods miss.  
**Mechanism:** The ECG is partitioned into lead-specific temporal patches. Tokens combine patch features with lead embeddings (spatial) and shared temporal embeddings. A random subset of patches across both leads and time is masked. A Transformer encoder processes unmasked tokens; a decoder reconstructs masked patch values via MSE.  
**Core assumption:** Diagnostic patterns depend on precise lead context and waveform timing; reconstructing missing information in both dimensions forces the model to learn these dependencies.  
**Evidence anchors:** Abstract and Section 2.1 describe the masking and tokenization; ablation (Variant 4 vs. full model) shows removing spatial masking harms performance.  
**Break condition:** Removing spatial masking or the orthogonality constraint (`L_orth`) reduces AUC, indicating both are necessary for the gain.

### Mechanism 2
**Claim:** Disentangling modality-specific and modality-shared representations reduces noise and bias from free-text reports, leading to cleaner cross-modal alignment.  
**Mechanism:** After encoding, MLPs split each modality's `[CLS]` into `h_sp` (specific) and `h_sh` (shared). An orthogonality loss (`L_orth`) penalizes cosine similarity between a modality's own `h_sp` and `h_sh`. Contrastive losses (SigLIP, InfoNCE) are applied only to the shared vectors.  
**Core assumption:** Free-text reports contain noise (e.g., phrasing variability) absent in ECG signals; aligning all features would embed this noise. Separating shared forces invariant features to drive alignment.  
**Evidence anchors:** Abstract states the goal to "mitigate unnecessary noise and modality-specific biases"; Section 2.2 defines the disentanglement equations and `L_orth`.  
**Break condition:** Ablation removing `L_orth` (Variant 3) lowers performance, suggesting collapse or interference without explicit separation.

### Mechanism 3
**Claim:** The hybrid contrastive-generative objective (SigLIP + reconstruction) synergistically improves representation quality by combining instance discrimination and detailed structural learning, while avoiding objective conflict.  
**Mechanism:** Total loss `L_Full` sums `L_Cons` (bidirectional contrastive on `h_sh`), `L_SigLIP` (sigmoid-based contrastive on `h_sh`), and reconstruction losses (`L_e_rec`, `L_t_rec`) on full representations. Contrastive losses operate only on shared features.  
**Core assumption:** Contrastive alone may ignore fine-grained structure; generative alone may lack discriminative power. Their combination is complementary, but applying contrastive to disentangled shared features prevents the objectives from competing in the same space.  
**Evidence anchors:** Abstract mentions "hybrid contrastive-generative framework"; Section 2.2 describes loss composition; neighbor paper on time series hybrid framework provides indirect support.  
**Break condition:** Ablations removing either reconstruction or SigLIP, or applying contrastive to non-disentangled features, degrade results, confirming the specific pairing and separation.

## Foundational Learning
* **Concept: Self-Supervised Learning (SSL) Pretext Tasks**  
  *Why needed:* Pretraining uses unpaired ECG-text data. Understanding how masked reconstruction (generative) and contrastive learning (discriminative) act as pretext tasks explains learning without labels.  
  *Quick check:* What key difference in learned representations does a contrastive pretext task enforce versus a generative (masked modeling) pretext task?
* **Concept: Multimodal Representation Disentanglement**  
  *Why needed:* The core innovation is separating shared vs. specific features. Grasping why naive alignment fails and how orthogonal constraints enforce separation is essential.  
  *Quick check:* If `h_ecg_sh` and `h_ecg_sp` are highly correlated (high cosine similarity), what downstream zero-shot classification problem arises?
* **Concept: Spatial-Temporal Modeling in Multivariate Time Series**  
  *Why needed:* ECG is a multivariate time series; spatial (leads) and temporal dimensions are both critical. Understanding why treating leads as independent channels is insufficient is key.  
  *Quick check:* How does adding lead-specific embeddings (`spa_l`) alongside temporal position embeddings (`temp_n`) change the model's inductive bias compared to a single channel embedding?
* **Concept: Orthogonality Constraints in Representation Learning**  
  *Why needed:* The orthogonality loss (`L_orth`) enforces separation between shared and specific features. Understanding its role prevents feature collapse.  
  *Quick check:* What would happen to the mutual information between `h_sh` and `h_sp` if `L_orth` were removed or set too strong?

## Architecture Onboarding
Raw ECG (12×T) + Clinical Report → [ECG Tokenizer (CNN + lead/temp emb)] → [Text Tokenizer (Med-CPT, 15% mask)] → [ECG Encoder (8-layer Transformer)] + [Text Encoder (Med-CPT)] → [Disentanglement Heads (MLPs → h_sh, h_sp)] → [ECG Decoder (Transformer)] + [Text Decoder (MLM head)] → Losses (`L_orth`, `L_SigLIP`, `L_Cons` on `h_sh`; `L_e_rec`, `L_t_rec` on full).  
**Critical path:** Tokenization → Modality-specific encoding → Disentanglement → Alignment losses (SigLIP, Cons on shared) ↔ Reconstruction losses (on full). All losses update entire network.  
**Design tradeoffs:**  
* Patch number (N): Small N loses temporal resolution; large N increases sequence length and may dilute lead signals. Optimal N=100.  
* Masking ratio: High ratio (75%) forces harder reconstruction but risks signal loss; lower ratio eases training but may not yield robust features.  
* Loss weights (λ): Balance λ₁ (reconstruction) vs λ₃ (SigLIP); tuning needed per dataset.  
* Shared encoder capacity: Simpler MLP heads may fail to separate; deeper heads risk collapse without strong `L_orth`.  
**Failure signatures:**  
* Low linear probing AUC: Check if `L_e_rec` weight too low or spatial masking inactive.  
* Zero-shot near random: Inspect alignment losses (`L_Cons`, `L_SigLIP`); increase λ₃ or ensure text encoder is fine-tuned.  
* Ablation sensitivity: If removing `L_orth` hurts most, disentanglement failing—monitor `h_sp`/`h_sh` similarity.  
* Blurry ECG reconstruction: Decoder capacity insufficient or masking ratio too high.  
**First 3 experiments:**  
1. Ablation (Table 3): Systematically remove `L_e_rec`, `L_t_rec`, `L_orth`, `L_SigLIP` to confirm each component's contribution to linear and zero-shot AUC.  
2. Patch Number & Masking Ratio Sweep (Fig 2): Vary N (25, 50, 100, 200) and masking ratio (50%, 65%, 75%, 90%) to find optimal trade-off.  
3. t-SNE Visualization (Fig 3): Compare `h_ecg_sh` clustering against SimCLR, ST-MEM, MERL; verify CG-DMER yields more compact, separated diagnosis clusters.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
* **Question 1:** How much does modeling inter-lead spatial dependencies contribute to performance gains over purely temporal modeling?  
  *Basis:* Spatial-temporal masking is proposed but not isolated from temporal-only masking in ablations.  
  *Why unresolved:* No direct comparison between spatial-temporal and temporal-only masking.  
  *Needed evidence:* Downstream AUC with temporal-only vs. spatial-temporal masking across datasets.
* **Question 2:** What specific biases in free-text ECG reports are most harmful for cross-modal alignment, and how effectively does disentanglement remove them?  
  *Basis:* Paper identifies "modality-specific biases" but does not characterize them.  
  *Why unresolved:* No analysis of report content or correlation with ECG-label noise.  
  *Needed evidence:* Analysis of report linguistic variability correlated with noise, probing shared vs. specific representations for bias signals.
* **Question 3:** Does the orthogonality constraint ensure meaningful disentanglement, or do shared features still contain modality-specific information?  
  *Basis:* `L_orth` minimizes cosine similarity but no direct disentanglement metric is reported.  
  *Why unresolved:* Performance gains could stem from other factors; invariance/specificity not verified.  
  *Needed evidence:* Evaluate downstream tasks using only shared or specific features; compute mutual information or similar metrics.
* **Question 4:* How robust is zero-shot classification to variations in prompt design, and does CG-DMER learn representations less dependent on prompt phrasing?  
  *Basis:* Zero-shot uses GPT-4o generated prompts but does not test alternative prompts.  
  *Why unresolved:* Single prompt set used; sensitivity to phrasing unknown.  
  *Needed evidence:* Systematic evaluation with multiple prompt templates per disease, measuring AUC variance and possible prompt-augmentation training.

## Limitations
* Loss weights (λ₁, λ₂, λ₃) and ECG decoder architecture (layers, dimensions, weight sharing) are unspecified, hindering exact reproduction.
* Text encoder specifics (Med-CPT model size, fine-tuning scope) and exact GPT-4 prompt templates for zero-shot are missing.
* Linear probing training regimen (epochs, LR schedule, regularization) is only briefly described.

## Confidence
* **Mechanism 1 (spatial‑temporal masked modeling):** High. Ablation shows masking across both dimensions is critical; design directly addresses lead dependencies.
* **Mechanism 2 (disentanglement via orthogonality):** Medium. Ablation indicates benefit, but no direct metric confirms feature separation; confidence depends on λ tuning.
* **Mechanism 3 (hybrid contrastive‑generative):** Medium. Combined loss improves results, but potential objective conflict under varying data regimes is unexplored.

## Next Checks
