# Understanding the Interplay between LLMs' Utilisation of Parametric and Contextual Knowledge: A keynote at ECIR 2025

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2603.09654
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.09654
- **Reference count:** 19
- **Primary result:** LLMs often ignore provided contextual knowledge when it conflicts with parametric memory; fact dynamicity reduces persuasion, and synthetic datasets overestimate conflict impact compared to real-world retrieval.

## Executive Summary
The paper addresses a critical gap in understanding when and how LLMs integrate contextual knowledge that conflicts with their parametric memory. It introduces a unified framework to compare instance and neuron attribution methods, alongside two new datasets—DynamicQA for probing conflict dynamics and DRUID for realistic RAG evaluation. Key findings reveal that instance and neuron attribution offer complementary explanations, fact dynamicity (temporal or disputable nature) strongly predicts resistance to contextual override, and synthetic conflict datasets exaggerate real-world context repulsion effects.

## Method Summary
The core methodological contributions are a unified attribution framework that aligns influential training instances (via instance attribution) with key neurons (via neuron attribution) and evaluates their faithfulness; the DynamicQA dataset with metrics (semantic entropy for intra-memory conflict, coherent persuasion score for context-memory conflict); and the DRUID dataset with the ACU (Aggregate Context Utilization) metric to measure context usage in real-world RAG scenarios. Context manipulation techniques (CMTs) are also developed to systematically vary context properties.

## Key Results
- Instance attribution and neuron attribution yield different, complementary explanations for model predictions.
- Fact dynamicity (temporal/disputable facts) is a strong negative indicator of model persuasion from conflicting context.
- Synthetic conflict datasets (e.g., CounterFact) exaggerate the prevalence and impact of knowledge conflict and context repulsion compared to real-world retrieved contexts (DRUID).

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1
The claim is that instance attribution (IA) and neuron attribution (NA) provide complementary explanations by identifying different influential knowledge sources. The mechanism uses a unified framework to align top IA instances with key neurons in MLP layers, then tests faithfulness via ablation and fine-tuning. Evidence shows IA and NA do not consistently overlap; fine-tuning on IA-selected instances performs similarly to random instances, suggesting NA captures distinct mechanisms. A break condition would be if neuron ablation minimally affects predictions, challenging the assumption that knowledge is localized in MLP neurons.

### Mechanism 2
Fact dynamicity (temporal/disputable nature) negatively predicts model persuasibility from context. The mechanism employs DynamicQA to measure intra-memory conflict via semantic entropy (parametric output variation) and context-memory conflict via coherent persuasion score (output shift with conflicting context). Evidence shows dynamic facts exhibit lower persuasion scores. A break condition would be if, on a new dataset with clear dynamicity labels, the correlation reverses or disappears.

### Mechanism 3
Synthetic conflict datasets overestimate conflict and repulsion effects versus real retrieved contexts. The mechanism compares DRUID (real claim-verification contexts) against synthetic ones using ACU and correlates usage with context features (assertiveness, recency). Evidence shows real contexts have lower conflict impact; features like assertiveness and recency increase usage. A break condition would be if large-scale real-world RAG logs show similar high conflict impact as synthetic data.

## Foundational Learning
- **Instance Attribution (e.g., influence functions, TRAK):** Identifies which training examples most influence a specific prediction. *Why needed:* Forms one half of the comparative framework to explain model behavior. *Quick check:* How might the diversity of top-attributed training instances affect fine-tuning effectiveness on them?
- **Intra-memory vs. Context-memory Conflict:** Distinguishes instability from contradictory training data (intra-memory) versus conflict between provided context and training (context-memory). *Why needed:* Central to diagnosing why models ignore context. *Quick check:* If a model is inconsistent on a fact without any provided context, what conflict type is likely present?
- **Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG) Evaluation Pitfalls:** Synthetic evaluation data provides clean conflicts but lacks the noise and irrelevance of real retrieval. *Why needed:* Highlights the gap between controlled experiments and deployed RAG systems. *Quick check:* Why might a context with high lexical similarity be less useful than one from an authoritative post-claim source?

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component map:** Datasets (DynamicQA, DRUID) -> Model (LLaMA/GPT) -> Attribution Suite (IA, NA) / Conflict Probes (Metrics) / RAG Evaluator (ACU) -> Analysis.  
**Critical path:** 1) Select model/task. 2) For conflict analysis: run model with/without conflicting context on DynamicQA, compute metrics. 3) For context usage: run RAG on DRUID, compute ACU, correlate with context features. 4) Compare attribution explanations if full model access available.  
**Design tradeoffs:**  
- Synthetic (DynamicQA) vs. Real (DRUID) data: Synthetic offers control but lacks noise; real has ecological validity but messier signals.  
- Full model access (for NA) vs. API-only: NA requires internal weights, limiting model choice; IA and DRUID work with black-box APIs.  
- Metric simplicity vs. fidelity: Semantic entropy is cheap but may miss nuanced conflict; coherent persuasion score requires controlled context pairs.  
**Failure signatures:**  
- Model ignores all provided context (ACU ≈ 0) regardless of quality.  
- High context usage only on high query-context lexical similarity, failing on semantically relevant but lexically different sources.  
- Neuron ablation identifies "important" neurons whose suppression does not change predictions (low faithfulness).  
**First 3 experiments:**  
1. Replicate DynamicQA persuasion analysis: Compute coherent persuasion scores on LLaMA-7B for static vs. dynamic facts; expect significantly lower persuasion for dynamic facts.  
2. Validate DRUID ACU: Correlate ACU with assertiveness and recency annotations; expect positive correlations.  
3. Test synthetic vs. real gap: For matched claims, compare ACU for real retrieved contexts vs. synthetic opposing contexts; expect synthetic shows higher persuasion (lower ACU).

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- **When should LLMs override parametric knowledge with contextual information, and what mechanisms decide this?** The interplay and overwrite conditions are underexplored. Evidence needed: Probe internal states (attention, neurons) across diverse conflict types and context features, possibly with interventions.  
- **How to measure intra-memory conflict and context utilization reliably, especially in low-certainty domains?** Current metrics (semantic entropy, coherent persuasion score) are unvalidated and lack standardization. Evidence needed: Build benchmarks with human annotations; design metrics correlating with them and generalizing across models/domains.  
- **Why do synthetic contexts overestimate conflict effects vs. real retrieval, and how to create realistic evaluations?** Synthetic data provides clean conflicts; real retrieval adds noise and complexity. Evidence needed: Compare models on matched synthetic/real pairs, test noise impact, and design synthetic pipelines mimicking real retrieval statistics.

## Limitations
- Dataset access and preprocessing details for DynamicQA and DRUID are unclear, hindering exact replication.
- Exact model checkpoints, prompt templates, and decoding parameters used in experiments are not disclosed.
- DRUID’s curated claim-verification pairs may not fully represent noisy, open-domain RAG logs, limiting generalizability.

## Confidence
- **Instance vs. neuron attribution complementarity:** Medium (framework and tests provide evidence, but neuron ablation’s minimal effect in the paper questions localization assumptions).
- **Fact dynamicity as negative persuasion indicator:** Medium (DynamicQA supports this, but lacks external validation; corpus anchors weak).
- **Synthetic datasets exaggerate conflict impact:** High (DRUID comparison and neighbor benchmarks strongly indicate overestimation; break condition plausible but untested).
