# Left Behind: Cross-Lingual Transfer as a Bridge for Low-Resource Languages in Large Language Models

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2603.21036
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.21036
- **Reference count:** 4
- **Primary result:** LLMs show a consistent 13.8–16.7 percentage point accuracy drop on low-resource languages (Kazakh, Mongolian) versus English; cross-lingual transfer (CLT) yields gains only for bilingual architectures (+2.2pp to +4.3pp), not English-first models; the explicitly multilingual Aya Expanse collapses catastrophically on Kazakh (15.7%) and Mongolian (4.7%), frequently outputting Kyrgyz instead of Kazakh.

## Executive Summary
This paper investigates why large language models (LLMs) underperform on low-resource languages (LRLs) like Kazakh and Mongolian, despite maintaining surface fluency. The authors benchmark eight models using 50 hand-crafted questions, evaluating accuracy, fluency, and completeness under direct prompting and a cross-lingual transfer (CLT) pipeline. Results reveal a severe accuracy-fluency dissociation: models generate seemingly fluent LRL text but with substantially incorrect content. CLT provides selective benefits only for models with pre-existing bilingual competence, while English-dominant models see no gain or even harm. Notably, the broad-coverage multilingual Aya Expanse model fails catastrophically, confusing Kazakh with its close relative Kyrgyz. The study underscores that model language support claims are unreliable without targeted LRL validation and that CLT is not a universal remedy.

## Method Summary
The study builds a benchmark of 50 original questions (covering factual, reasoning, technical, and culture-specific topics for Kazakh and Mongolian), each with professional human translations into English, Kazakh, and Mongolian, plus a reference answer sheet. Eight LLMs are grouped by training profile: English-first (5), Bilingual (2), Multilingual (1). Each model answers all 50 questions under five conditions: English baseline, direct Kazakh, CLT Kazakh (translate→reason→translate), direct Mongolian, CLT Mongolian. This yields 2,000 model–question pairs. Responses are evaluated by Claude Sonnet 4.5 using a 0–2 scale for Accuracy, Fluency (strict language identity), and Completeness against the reference sheet. The analysis compares English-LRL gaps and CLT deltas (CLT score minus direct score) across model categories.

## Key Results
- LLMs suffer a consistent 13.8–16.7 percentage point accuracy drop on Kazakh and Mongolian compared to English, while fluency degrades far less, creating a deceptive "fluency illusion."
- Cross-lingual transfer (CLT) improves accuracy for bilingual models by +2.2pp (Kazakh) and +4.3pp (Mongolian), but yields negligible or negative effects for English-first models.
- The explicitly multilingual Aya Expanse model collapses on LRLs, scoring only 15.7% on Kazakh and 4.7% on Mongolian, frequently producing output in Kyrgyz—a closely related but distinct language—instead of Kazakh.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- **Claim:** LLMs maintain surface-level fluency in low-resource languages (LRLs) while suffering severe accuracy degradation, creating a deceptive "fluency illusion" for users.
- **Mechanism:** The model's internal representations, heavily optimized for high-resource languages, produce syntactically correct LRL text by leveraging learned morphological and sequential patterns, but lack deeper semantic and factual knowledge grounding in the LRL's cultural and factual context. Outputs are locally coherent but globally incorrect or hallucinated.
- **Core assumption:** Fluency transfers via learned syntax and phrasing patterns, whereas accuracy requires specific factual/cultural knowledge disproportionately missing from LRL training data.
- **Evidence anchors:** [abstract] "...with models maintaining surface-level fluency while producing significantly less accurate content." [section 4] "Fluency degrades far less than Accuracy... Models generate text that reads naturally... while containing factual errors."

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim:** Cross-lingual transfer (CLT) yields performance gains only for models with pre-existing bilingual competence, not for English-dominant models.
- **Mechanism:** For bilingual models trained on substantial non-English corpora, the CLT pipeline (translate→reason→translate) leverages their likely stronger reasoning in a high-resource language while offloading translation to existing multilingual capabilities. For English-first models, which likely reason in English-dominated representations, explicit CLT steps add noise and error propagation without benefit.
- **Core assumption:** CLT effectiveness is gated by the model's underlying multilingual representational capacity; bilingual models have a balanced latent space enabling reliable translation steps.
- **Evidence anchors:** [section 3] "Bilingual models show the clearest CLT benefit: +2.2pp for Kazakh and +4.3pp for Mongolian." [section 4] "CLT not as a universal strategy but as targeted scaffolding for models with partial multilingual competence."

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim:** Explicitly multilingual models can catastrophically fail on specific LRLs due to catastrophic interference or language confusion within closely related language families.
- **Mechanism:** During training on 100+ languages, the model's parameters for typologically similar languages (e.g., Kazakh and Kyrgyz) may fail to form distinct representations. When prompted in a low-resource language, activation patterns collapse toward the more frequent or higher-resource neighbor (Kyrgyz), especially if the LRL's unique features are underrepresented.
- **Core assumption:** Broad-coverage multilingual training can sacrifice depth and discriminability for any single LRL, particularly those with close relatives in the training corpus.
- **Evidence anchors:** [section 4] "Aya Expanse... collapses to 15.7% for Kazakh and 4.7% for Mongolian... frequently producing Kyrgyz—a closely related but distinct language—instead of Kazakh."

## Foundational Learning
- **Concept:** Low-Resource Language (LRL) Degradation Profile
  - **Why needed here:** Understanding that performance loss is not uniform across metrics (accuracy vs. fluency) is critical to diagnosing model failures and user risk. A user might be fooled by fluent but incorrect output.
  - **Quick check question:** If a model outputs grammatically perfect Kazakh but states an incorrect historical date for a Kazakh event, which evaluation metric (Accuracy, Fluency, Completeness) is most violated, and what does this imply about the model's knowledge gap?
- **Concept:** Architecture-Dependent Cross-Lingual Transfer (CLT)
  - **Why needed here:** CLT is not a silver bullet. Its success hinges on the model's pre-training composition. Applying it blindly to all models will waste compute and potentially hurt English-first models.
  - **Quick check question:** You have two models: Model A (trained mostly on English) and Model B (trained on balanced Chinese and English data). You need the best performance for Mongolian. Based on the paper's findings, which model should you apply the CLT pipeline to, and why?
- **Concept:** Language Confusion in Broad-Coverage Multilingual Models
  - **Why needed here:** Selecting a model because it *claims* to support a language (e.g., "100+ languages") is insufficient. For specific LRLs, especially within a language family, targeted performance validation is mandatory.
  - **Quick check question:** The paper finds Aya Expanse outputs Kyrgyz when prompted in Kazakh. What does this suggest about the semantic granularity of the model's language representations, and what would be a minimal test to detect this before deployment?

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map:** Benchmark Suite → Model Zoo (grouped by training profile: English-first, Bilingual, Multilingual) → Condition Engine (Direct vs. CLT Pipeline) → Evaluation Harness (LLM-as-judge with strict language ID) → Analysis Pipeline (Aggregation, gap calculation, architecture comparison).
- **Critical path:**
  1. Benchmark Preparation: Finalize LRL question bank with native-speaker validation of translations and reference answers.
  2. Model Inference: Run all models through all conditions with independent conversations; log raw responses, timestamps, token counts.
  3. Automated Grading: Feed each response + reference sheet to judge model; implement strict language check for Fluency.
  4. Statistical Aggregation: Compute mean scores per (Model, Condition); calculate English-LRL gaps and CLT deltas.
  5. Architecture Comparison: Group models, plot score vs. latency, identify outliers (like Aya Expanse).
- **Design tradeoffs:**
  - Automated vs. Human Eval: Used LLM-as-judge for scale, but risks missing nuanced cultural errors; mitigated by exhaustive reference sheet and fluency enforcement.
  - Benchmark Size vs. Depth: 50 questions is small but hand-crafted and culturally grounded; tradeoff is statistical power vs. quality/cultural authenticity.
  - Internal vs. External Translation: CLT uses the model's own translation; using a separate MT system would change the mechanism and results.
- **Failure signatures:**
  - Fluency-Accuracy Decoupling: High Fluency (≥85%) with low Accuracy (≤70%) in LRL conditions.
  - CLT Backfire: Negative delta (CLT score < Direct score) for English-first models.
  - Language Collapse: Consistently wrong language output (e.g., Kyrgyz for Kazakh) scoring Fluency=0, especially in a "multilingual" model.
  - Latency Explosion: CLT conditions taking 3–5x longer than direct without accuracy gain.
- **First 3 experiments:**
  1. Replicate Core Benchmark: Run the exact 8-model, 5-condition, 50-Q protocol on a new pair of LRLs (e.g., Uzbek and Tibetan) to test generalizability of the gap and CLT selectivity.
  2. CLT Source Language Ablation: For a bilingual model, run CLT but change the "reason in English" step to "reason in Chinese" (or another high-resource language in its training set). Does the benefit persist if reasoning happens in a non-English HRL?
  3. Fine-tuning vs. CLT Intervention: Take an English-first model. Fine-tune it on a small (e.g., 1k example) parallel corpus of Kazakh Q&A. Compare its direct Kazakh performance against its CLT performance *before* fine-tuning. Does fine-tuning close the gap more effectively than CLT?

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- Does the observed 13.8–16.7 percentage point performance gap between English and low-resource languages generalize beyond the 50-question benchmark?
- Can targeted fine-tuning on small low-resource language corpora close the performance gap more effectively than cross-lingual transfer prompting?
- Why does cross-lingual transfer yield measurable gains for bilingual architectures but negligible or negative effects for English-first models?
- What factors cause a purpose-built multilingual model (Aya Expanse) to systematically produce a related but incorrect language (Kyrgyz) instead of the target (Kazakh)?
- Do the performance gaps and cross-lingual transfer effects observed for Kazakh and Mongolian hold for other Turkic and Mongolic languages?

## Limitations
- The exact benchmark questions and reference answer sheet are inaccessible (GitHub link unverified); reproduction requires reconstruction, risking validity drift.
- Cited model versions (e.g., "Claude Opus 4.5") are ambiguous—likely API-only—with unspecified inference parameters (temperature, prompts).
- Grading rubric details (partial credit handling, cultural nuance judgment) are unspecified; LLM-as-judge reliability for LRLs is unvalidated.
- The small benchmark (50 questions) prioritizes cultural authenticity over statistical power; findings may not generalize to broader LRL distributions.

## Confidence
- Fluency-Accuracy Dissociation: High
- CLT Selectivity (Bilingual Gain): High
- Multilingual Model Collapse: Medium

## Next Checks
1. Generalize the LRL Gap: Replicate the benchmark protocol on a new language pair (e.g., Uzbek & Tibetan) to test if the 13.8–16.7pp accuracy-drop pattern holds for other LRL families.
