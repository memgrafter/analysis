# Information-Theoretic Storage Cost in Sentence Comprehension

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2602.18217
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.18217
- **Reference count:** 28
- **Primary result:** An information-theoretic storage cost measure, estimated using BERT, predicts naturalistic reading times beyond traditional surprisal and grammar-based Dependency Locality Theory storage cost, and recovers classic processing asymmetries from distributional statistics alone.

## Executive Summary
This paper introduces an information-theoretic operationalization of storage cost in sentence comprehension, defined as the sum of contextualized predictive potentials (half-pointwise mutual information) contributed by all prior context words. It addresses limitations of grammar-based metrics like Dependency Locality Theory (DLT), which rely on discrete, uniform counts of predicted syntactic elements. The proposed measure is estimated using a pre-trained masked language model (BERT) under a conditional independence assumption. analyses show that it successfully recovers well-known processing difficulties (e.g., center embeddings, object relative clauses) from distributional statistics. In a treebank analysis, it exhibits a moderate positive correlation with DLT storage cost. Critically, in two large naturalistic reading-time datasets (Natural Stories and OneStop), adding the information-theoretic measure to baseline models containing traditional predictors (unigram and GPT-2 surprisal) yields significant improvements in held-out log-likelihood. Furthermore, it explains independent variance beyond DLT storage cost, suggesting complementary mechanisms in human sentence comprehension.

## Method Summary
The core method computes a per-word "InfoStor" score. For each target position *k* in a sentence, it sums the predictive potential *P*<sub>pred</sub>(*wᵢ*; *W*<sub>[k:N]</sub>) for every prior word *wᵢ* (*i* < *k*). Predictive potential is approximated as the Kullback-Leibler (KL) divergence between BERT's token-level probability distribution for the future context (positions *k* to end) when *wᵢ* is present in the context versus when it is replaced by `[MASK]`. This requires a strong conditional independence assumption that the joint distribution over future tokens factorizes. InfoStor values are aggregated from BERT's subword tokens to match Universal Dependencies word boundaries. The measure is evaluated in three ways: (1) visualization on minimal pairs of easy vs. difficult constructions, (2) correlation with a grammar-based DLT storage count on the UD_English-GUM treebank, and (3) as a predictor in linear mixed-effects models of per-word reading times, with 10-fold cross-validated held-out log-likelihood (∆LL) as the primary metric. Baseline predictors include word position, length, unigram surprisal, GPT-2 surprisal, and spillover terms; DLT storage is added in separate conditions.

## Key Results
- The information-theoretic storage cost measure cleanly separates classic processing asymmetries: center-embedded sentences show higher storage than right-branching ones, and object relative clauses show higher storage than subject relative clauses, despite no explicit structural modeling.
- In the UD_English-GUM treebank, InfoStor correlates moderately with DLT storage cost (Pearson *r* = 0.338, *p* < .001), indicating some alignment with structural memory load but also substantial divergence.
- Across five reading-time measures in two naturalistic datasets (Natural Stories SPR/A-Maze, OneStop eye-tracking), adding InfoStor to a baseline model with traditional surprisal predictors yields significant ∆LL improvement in four out of five cases. Adding InfoStor to a model already containing DLT storage still provides significant improvement in most cases, demonstrating largely independent explanatory power.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1
- **Claim:** Storage cost in sentence comprehension is the sum of the *predictive potential* (contextualized half-pointwise mutual information) that each prior word carries about all future words.
- **Mechanism:** For a target word *wᵢ* at position *i* and a future context *W*<sub>[k:N]</sub> at position *k* (where *i* < *k*), the predictive potential *P*<sub>pred</sub>(*wᵢ*; *W*<sub>[k:N]</sub>) is defined as the Kullback-Leibler (KL) divergence between the predictive distribution of the future given the full context *w*<sub>[1:k]</sub> and the distribution given the context with *wᵢ* removed. The total storage cost at position *k* is the sum of *P*<sub>pred</sub> for all *i* < *k*.
- **Core assumption:** Maintaining a syntactic/lexical prediction in working memory corresponds to retaining the information that reduces uncertainty about future input.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract] The measure recovers classic processing asymmetries (center embeddings, object relatives) from distributional statistics alone.
  - [section 3.2] Formal definition of predictive potential as KL divergence (Equation 6).
  - [section 4.1] Visualization shows higher storage cost for more difficult constructions (CE > RB, ORC > SRC).
- **Break condition:** If the monotonic decay property (Predictive potential decreases on average with distance) fails empirically for a model or language, the core memory decay assumption is violated.

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim:** Predictive potential can be practically estimated using a pre-trained masked language model (BERT) under a conditional independence assumption.
- **Mechanism:** For each pair (*i*, *k*), BERT is used to generate token-level probability distributions for the future tokens *W*<sub>[k:N]</sub> in two conditions: (1) with *wᵢ* visible in the context, and (2) with *wᵢ* masked. The sum of the KL divergences across all masked future positions approximates *P*<sub>pred</sub>(*wᵢ*; *W*<sub>[k:N]</sub>).
- **Core assumption:** The joint distribution over future tokens *p*(*w*<sub>[k:N]</sub> | context) factorizes into a product of independent token distributions conditional on the context.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [section 3.3] Explicit description of the BERT estimation procedure and the conditional independence assumption.
  - [section 4.3] The estimated measure is used as a predictor in regression models of reading times.
- **Break condition:** The conditional independence assumption is strong; if future tokens exhibit strong local dependencies that BERT captures but the sum-of-KLs estimator misses, the estimated storage cost will be biased. Failures would show as poor correlation with grammar-based storage or human reading data that other estimators capture.

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim:** Information-theoretic storage cost explains independent variance in naturalistic reading times beyond both traditional surprisal and grammar-based storage cost (DLT).
- **Mechanism:** Linear mixed-effects regression models predict per-word reading times. Adding the proposed measure (INFO) to a baseline with word position, length, unigram surprisal, and GPT-2 surprisal yields a significant improvement in held-out log-likelihood. Furthermore, adding INFO to a model already containing DLT storage (INFO-ON-DLT) still provides a significant improvement, indicating non-redundant predictive power.
- **Core assumption:** Reading time is monotonically increasing in the cognitive load imposed by the cumulative information that must be maintained from prior context.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract] The measure predicts reading-time variance over and above traditional predictors.
  - [section 4.3.3] Figure 5 shows significant ∆LL improvements for INFO in 4/5 datasets, and for INFO-ON-DLT in most cases.
  - [section 5] Discussion notes the largely orthogonal contributions of information-theoretic and grammar-based storage, linked to the HDMI hypothesis.
- **Break condition:** If the INFO coefficient in the regression is consistently negative or non-significant across multiple large-scale naturalistic datasets after controlling for baseline predictors and DLT, the core linking hypothesis (higher storage cost → slower reading) would be falsified for this operationalization.

## Foundational Learning
- **Concept: Pointwise Mutual Information (PMI) & Surprisal**
  - *Why needed here:* The storage cost is defined as an expected PMI (half-PMI) between a context word and the future. Surprisal (*-log p*) is the foundational information-theoretic measure for processing difficulty. Understanding PMI as a measure of association is key to understanding predictive potential.
  - *Quick check question:* What does a high positive PMI value between word *A* and word *B* indicate about their distributional relationship?
- **Concept: Dependency Locality Theory (DLT) Storage Cost**
  - *Why needed here:* The paper's proposal is a direct alternative to DLT storage cost. Understanding DLT—counting predicted syntactic heads—is necessary to grasp the limitations being addressed (discrete, grammar-dependent, uniform costs) and to interpret the correlation analysis.
  - *Quick check question:* In the center-embedded sentence "The reporter who the senator who Mary met attacked ignored the president," what is the DLT storage cost at the word "Mary" and why?
- **Concept: Masked Language Models (e.g., BERT) for Psycholinguistics**
  - *Why needed here:* The proposed measure is estimated using BERT. One must understand that MLMs provide contextualized probability distributions over vocabulary given a bidirectional context, and that masking is used to simulate the "removal" of a word's information.
  - *Quick check question:* How does BERT's prediction for a masked token differ from a left-to-right language model like GPT-2, and why is this difference relevant for estimating the proposed storage cost?

## Architecture Onboarding
The processing pipeline flows: Input Parser (tokenization, UD parse alignment) -> Storage Cost Estimator (INFO) -> DLT Storage Calculator -> Feature Engineering (z-scoring, spillover terms) -> Model Fitter (linear mixed-effects regression). The critical path is the INFO estimator, as it is the novel component whose output drives the key analyses. Design tradeoffs include: granularity (word vs. subword vs. chunk), estimation method (sum-of-KLs under independence vs. joint distribution), and scope (all prior words vs. filtered set). Failure signatures: internal (non-monotonic predictive potential decay), construct (failure to show asymmetry in hand-crafted stimuli), criterion (zero/negative correlation with DLT or no reading-time improvement), implementation (high variance across similar sentences). The first three experiments are: (1) Illustrative validation on minimal pairs, (2) Corpus correlation on UD_English-GUM, (3) Reading-time prediction on Natural Stories/OneStop.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- **Question 1:** Does the independence between information-theoretic storage cost and DLT storage cost reflect genuinely distinct cognitive mechanisms, or is it an artifact of noise or model misspecification in the reading-time data?
- **Question 2:** What is the optimal granularity for representing "pending information" in an information-theoretic storage cost? Are word-level tokens the correct unit, or should the model use compressed, conceptual chunks?
- **Question 3:** To what extent does the current BERT-based estimation method, with its strong conditional independence assumption for future tokens, bias the measure and limit its validity?
- **Question 4:** Does the information-theoretic storage cost generalize cross-linguistically, particularly to head-final (SOV) languages where pre-verbal memory demands are predicted to be structurally higher?
- **Question 5:** Is the theoretically predicted monotonic decay of a word's predictive potential with increasing distance to the future a robust empirical property, and does its slope correlate with working memory capacity?

## Limitations
- The exact operationalization of DLT storage cost (count of unseen tokens whose co-dependents have been seen) is ambiguously defined, risking inconsistent implementations.
- The BERT-based estimation relies on a strong conditional independence assumption among future tokens, which likely induces bias in InfoStor values.
- Aggregating BERT subword predictions to UD word boundaries introduces potential alignment errors that could distort storage cost metrics.

## Confidence
- **Storage cost definition (half-PMI sum):** Medium (theoretically grounded but linking hypothesis not directly validated)
- **BERT estimation procedure:** Low (depends on unverified independence assumption and model-specific biases)
- **Empirical reading-time validity:** High (robust, cross-validated improvements across multiple large datasets)
