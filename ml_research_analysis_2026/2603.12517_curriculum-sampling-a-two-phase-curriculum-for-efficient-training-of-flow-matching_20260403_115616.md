# Curriculum Sampling: A Two-Phase Curriculum for Efficient Training of Flow Matching

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID**: 2603.12517
- **Source URL**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.12517
- **Reference count**: 4
- **Primary result**: On CIFAR-10, Curriculum Sampling achieves a best FID of 3.22 vs. 3.85 for the Uniform baseline (16.4% relative improvement) and reaches peak performance at 100k training steps instead of 150k (33% faster convergence).

## Executive Summary
Flow Matching (FM) training involves learning a velocity field to transform noise into data. The choice of timestep sampling distribution \(p(t)\) critically impacts training speed and final sample quality. Static middle-biased distributions (e.g., Logit-Normal) accelerate early learning but under-sample trajectory boundaries (\(t \to 0,1\)), leading to poorer asymptotic fidelity. This paper identifies a persistent U-shaped loss profile across timesteps, with largest errors at the boundaries. To reconcile speed and quality, they propose Curriculum Sampling: a two-phase schedule that begins with Logit-Normal sampling for rapid structure acquisition and switches to Uniform sampling to refine boundary details. On CIFAR-10, this curriculum yields a 16.4% FID improvement (3.22 vs. 3.85) and peaks 33% earlier (100k vs. 150k steps) compared to a Uniform baseline.

## Method Summary
The method trains a conditional Flow Matching model with a linear probability path \(z_t = (1-t)x + t\epsilon\) and velocity target \(v_t = \epsilon - x\). The key innovation is a curriculum over the timestep sampling distribution. Phase 1 uses a Logit-Normal distribution \(t = \text{sigmoid}(u), u \sim \mathcal{N}(\mu, \sigma^2)\) with \(\mu=0.8\) (shifting mass toward the noise side) to emphasize mid-to-late timesteps where the transport is smoother. After a switch at step \(T_s\) (optimally ~60k), Phase 2 switches to Uniform sampling to evenly cover all timesteps, thereby addressing the under-trained boundaries. Training uses a U-Net (~55M parameters), Adam optimizer (lr=6e-4), EMA, and an adaptive loss weighting \(w(t) = (t(1-t))^{0.75}\). Models are evaluated every 10k steps by generating 50k samples with a 50-step Euler solver and computing FID.

## Key Results
- Best FID: 3.22 (curriculum) vs. 3.85 (Uniform baseline), a 16.4% relative improvement.
- Faster convergence: peak FID at 100k steps (curriculum) vs. 150k steps (Uniform), a 33% reduction.
- Optimal switch time: around 60k steps (40% of total training) with Logit-Normal (\(\mu=0.8, \sigma=1.0\)) → Uniform.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1: Middle-biased sampling speeds early learning but harms final quality.
Static middle-biased distributions concentrate training updates on mid-timesteps, where the velocity field is smoother and learning is faster. This down-weights the boundary timesteps (\(t\to0,1\)), where prediction difficulty is highest and fine details are resolved. The under-trained boundaries accumulate residual errors that cap asymptotic generation fidelity. Evidence: Abstract states the trade-off; Section 5.1.2 shows Logit-Normal yields lower FID early (40k–90k steps) but plateaus worse than Uniform.

### Mechanism 2: The loss profile is U-shaped, with boundary errors persisting.
The time-dependent training loss exhibits a persistent U-shape: highest near \(t=0\) and \(t=1\), lowest in the middle. This reflects that predicting at data and noise endpoints requires precise estimation of sharp details and initial noise departure, tasks inherently higher in variance/sensitivity. The middle regime involves smoother transitions, yielding lower loss. Evidence: Abstract references the U-shaped difficulty; Section 5.1.3 (Fig. 3) visualizes the U-shaped loss across samplers, with slower reduction at boundaries across all static p(t).

### Mechanism 3: Two-phase curriculum exploits phase-specific strengths.
The curriculum first uses Logit-Normal to rapidly acquire global structure via mid-t emphasis, then switches to Uniform to reintroduce boundary sampling, allowing the model to reduce the residual errors that were chronically under-trained. This leverages the non-stationary optimal sampling distribution: early training benefits from focusing on easier mid-t learning, while later training requires balanced coverage to refine boundaries. Evidence: Abstract reports FID improvement and earlier peak; Section 5.2 (Table 1) shows curriculum consistently beats static baselines; optimal switch ~60k steps.

## Architecture Onboarding

### Component map
Sampler -> CurriculumController -> TrainingLoop -> LossTracker -> Checkpointer

### Critical path
1. Initialize model, optimizer, sampler (with curriculum if active).
2. For step = 1 to total_steps:
   a. Sample batch (x, ε) from data and standard normal.
   b. Sample t from current p(t) (via CurriculumController if active).
   c. Compute interpolant \(z_t = (1-t)x + t\epsilon\).
   d. Compute target velocity \(v_{\text{target}} = \epsilon - x\) (for linear path).
   e. Predict \(v_\theta(z_t, t)\); compute loss \(\|v_\theta - v_{\text{target}}\|^2\) (optionally weighted by \(w(t) = (t(1-t))^{0.75}\)).
   f. Backward pass, optimizer step.
   g. If step % eval_interval == 0: generate 50k samples via Euler solver (50 NFEs), compute FID; Checkpointer saves model if FID improves.
3. After training, select checkpoint with lowest FID.

### Design tradeoffs
- **Switch time \(T_s\)**: Too early under-utilizes Phase 1; too late leaves insufficient steps for boundary refinement. Optimal around 60k (40% of training) on CIFAR-10.
- **Phase 1 distribution**: Logit-Normal parameters (\(\mu, \sigma\)) control mid-t emphasis. Best \(\mu=0.8\) (shifts toward noise side) suggests noise‑side boundaries may need extra attention; \(\sigma=1.0\) provides sufficient spread.
- **Phase 2 distribution**: Uniform yields balanced boundary coverage; a U‑shaped “Mode” (Beta) may over‑sample boundaries and degrade final quality.

### Failure signatures
- Curriculum FID never exceeds Uniform baseline → switch too early/late, or Phase 1 distribution too extreme (e.g., σ too narrow).
- FID drops sharply after switch then rises → possible overfitting to boundary regimes; consider reducing Phase 2 duration or using less aggressive p2.
- Loss tracker shows persistent high loss at mid‑t after switch → Phase 1 likely insufficient; increase \(T_s\) or use broader Phase 1 distribution.
- No U‑shape in per‑timestep loss profile → check loss calculation and binning; may indicate implementation error or atypical probability path.

### First 3 experiments
1. **Baseline replication**: Train Uniform and static Logit‑Normal (\(\mu=0.8, \sigma=1.0\)) for 150k steps. Confirm trade‑off: Logit‑Normal lower FID early but worse final FID vs. Uniform.
2. **Loss profiling**: During Uniform training, bin t values and compute average loss per bin every 10k steps. Visualize to verify U‑shaped loss evolution and slower boundary convergence.
3. **Curriculum sweep**: Test curriculum Logit‑Normal (\(\mu=0.8\)) → Uniform across \(T_s \in \{40k, 50k, 60k, 70k, 80k\}\). Identify peak FID and step; confirm optimal \(T_s \approx 60k\) and that curriculum beats both static Uniform and static Logit‑Normal.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

- **What causes the U-shaped per-timestep loss in Flow Matching training?**  
  *Basis*: Figure 3 shows U‑shaped loss across all static samplers, with boundary peaks.  
  *Why unresolved*: No explanation whether due to objective, data, or architecture; hinders theory.  
  *Resolving evidence*: Theoretical loss decomposition; synthetic experiments; correlation with velocity field properties.

- **Does adaptive timestep sampling improve over fixed two-phase curriculum?**  
  *Basis*: Paper uses fixed switch \(T_s\); hints at dynamic protocols.  
  *Why unresolved*: Fixed schedules need manual tuning; adaptivity could auto-align with learning progress.  
  *Resolving evidence*: Real‑time loss‑monitoring controller; test on CIFAR‑10/ImageNet; compare FID and steps.

- **Does Curriculum Sampling generalize to higher resolutions and transformer architectures?**  
  *Basis*: Experiments only on CIFAR‑10 with U‑Net; claim broader importance but untested.  
  *Why unresolved*: Scaling and architectural changes may alter loss landscape and optimal curriculum.  
  *Resolving evidence*: Repeat on ImageNet 256×256 with DiT; measure FID, switch sensitivity, and loss profiles.

## Limitations
- **Implementation gaps**: Exact adaptive weighting function (p=0.75), precise U‑Net architecture, data augmentations, and FID evaluation specifics are missing, hindering exact replication.
- **Generalizability**: Results solely on CIFAR‑10; unclear if benefits extend to higher resolutions, other datasets, or transformer‑based models.
- **Hyperparameter sensitivity**: Optimal switch time and Logit‑Normal parameters are dataset‑dependent; no procedure for automatic tuning, and robustness across hyperparameter ranges is not fully explored.
