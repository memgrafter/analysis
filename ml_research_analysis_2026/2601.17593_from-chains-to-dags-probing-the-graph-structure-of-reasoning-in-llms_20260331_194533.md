# From Chains to DAGs: Probing the Graph Structure of Reasoning in LLMs

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2601.17593  
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.17593  
- **Reference count:** 40  
- **Primary result:** LLM hidden states linearly encode the geometry (depth, pairwise distance) of multi-step reasoning DAGs, with peak recoverability in intermediate layers; stronger encoding correlates with correct final answers.

## Executive Summary
The paper investigates whether large language models (LLMs) internally structure multi-step reasoning as directed acyclic graphs (DAGs) rather than linear chains. Using ProofWriter proofs, authors construct gold DAGs of reasoning steps and train lightweight linear probes on frozen LLM hidden states to recover graph properties like node depth and pairwise distance. Results show DAG geometry is linearly accessible, peaking in intermediate layers (e.g., depth Spearman ~0.8 for Qwen3-14B). Deeper nodes become more discriminable in later layers, suggesting progressive construction. Ablations (node-only, bag-of-words, label-shuffle) fail confirmingly, and higher probe scores correlate with correct generation, linking internal DAG encoding to reasoning success.

## Method Summary
The method builds reasoning DAGs from ProofWriter examples, where nodes are statements and edges represent rule dependencies. Gold labels include longest-path depth and directed pairwise distance. For a frozen LLM, mean-pooled hidden states for each node’s text span are extracted across layers. Separate rank-1 linear probes are trained per layer: a ranking loss for depth (predicting relative order of adjacent-depth nodes) and MSE for distance (predicting |z_u – z_v|). Evaluation metrics are depth/distance Spearman correlation and sink accuracy (identifying the final conclusion). DAG reconstruction uses thresholds on predicted depth differences and distances. Ablations remove contextual theory, use static bag-of-words features, or shuffle labels to test specificity.

## Key Results
- DAG structure is linearly recoverable: depth Spearman ~0.8, distance Spearman ~0.5, sink accuracy ~0.9 (Qwen3-14B).  
- Recoverability peaks in intermediate layers; deeper nodes show minimal prediction error in later layers, indicating progressive integration.  
- Probe scores are significantly higher for examples with correct final generations, especially for sink identification.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1: Linear accessibility of DAG geometry
- **Claim:** Reasoning DAG structure is encoded in frozen LLM hidden states and can be recovered by simple linear probes, indicating linearly separable signals of graph properties.
- **Mechanism:** Low-rank linear probes predict depth (via pairwise ranking) and distance (via regression) from node representations. High accuracy shows these properties are linearly accessible.
- **Core assumption:** Linear predictability from frozen activations implies meaningful encoding, not probe artifact.
- **Evidence anchors:** [abstract] depth Spearman ~0.8, sink accuracy ~0.9; [section 2] probe design; [ablations] node-only/bag-of-words/label-shuffle fail.

### Mechanism 2: Progressive layerwise construction
- **Claim:** DAG structure emerges across layers, with intermediate layers showing peak recoverability and deeper nodes becoming discriminable later.
- **Mechanism:** Layerwise probe metrics improve in early layers, peak in mid-layers, then decline slightly. Mean absolute error for depth shows a diagonal pattern: shallow nodes best recovered earlier, deep nodes later.
- **Core assumption:** The layer where a property is most accessible reflects when it is explicitly encoded.
- **Evidence anchors:** [section 3] Figure 2 layerwise curves; Figure 5 MAE heatmap by node depth.

### Mechanism 3: Functional relevance to generation
- **Claim:** Stronger internal DAG recoverability correlates with correct final answers, suggesting the encoded structure supports successful reasoning.
- **Mechanism:** Examples are grouped by generation correctness; probe metrics (depth Spearman, sink accuracy) are compared. Correct cases show higher, more concentrated scores.
- **Core assumption:** If structure is causally involved, its accessibility should be higher when reasoning succeeds.
- **Evidence anchors:** [section 3] Figure 8 distributions; [abstract] correlation with correct generation.

## Architecture Onboarding
Input: ProofWriter examples (theory, query, gold proof) -> DAG construction (nodes=statements, edges=rule applications; compute gold depth and pairwise distance) -> Representation extraction (mean-pool hidden states for node spans per layer) -> Probe training (per-layer rank-1 linear probes: ranking loss for depth, MSE for distance) -> Evaluation (metrics: depth/distance Spearman, sink accuracy; DAG reconstruction via thresholding).  
Critical path: DAG construction must align precisely with extracted node representations; any misalignment breaks the analysis.  
Design tradeoffs: Probe capacity (low-rank linear vs. nonlinear), objective choice (ranking for ordinal depth), reconstruction thresholds (τ_dist, τ_gap) tuned on dev.  
Failure signatures: Baselines (node-only, bag-of-words, label-shuffle) collapsing to chance; flat layerwise pattern; scaling anomalies (larger models not improving).

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
1. Is the linearly recoverable DAG structure causally involved in reasoning, or merely epiphenomenal? (Probes measure accessibility, not necessity.)  
2. Does DAG encoding generalize beyond proof-style datasets like ProofWriter to naturalistic, open-ended reasoning? (Limited to synthetic proofs.)  
3. What causes the mild decline in recoverability in later layers? (May indicate representational shift toward generation objectives.)  
4. Why do reasoning-oriented training variants yield only modest gains compared to scaling model size? (DAG encoding may mainly emerge in pretraining.)

## Limitations
- Probe linearity may miss nonlinear encoding; high linear scores do not rule out richer structure.  
- Evidence restricted to ProofWriter, a small synthetic dataset; generalizability to natural reasoning is unproven.  
- Critical hyperparameters (probe LR, threshold grids) and data splits are unspecified, hindering exact replication.

## Confidence
- **DAG geometry recoverability:** High. Robust correlations (depth ~0.8) and ablations collapse.  
- **Progressive layerwise construction:** Medium. Consistent patterns but causal inference indirect.  
- **Functional relevance to correct reasoning:** Medium. Correlation observed but causation not established.

## Next Checks
1. **Cross-dataset generalization:** Apply the probing pipeline to diverse benchmarks (e.g., ARC, math word problems) to test robustness beyond ProofWriter.  
2. **Nonlinear probing:** Train two-layer MLP probes on frozen states; a substantial boost would reveal that linear probes underfit.  
3. **Causal intervention:** Perturb predicted node depths (e.g., add constant to pivotal nodes) and measure impact on answer correctness to test functional role.
