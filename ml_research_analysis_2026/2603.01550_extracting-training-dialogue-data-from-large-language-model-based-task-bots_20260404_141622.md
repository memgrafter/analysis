# Extracting Training Dialogue Data from Large Language Model based Task Bots
## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2603.01550
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.01550
- **Reference count:** 29
- **Primary result:** Proposed a targeted extraction attack achieving over 70% precision for event-level dialogue states and 100% precision for individual slot values, recovering thousands of training examples from an LLM-based task bot.

## Executive Summary
This paper investigates memorization vulnerabilities in LLM-based task-oriented dialogue systems (TODS), where fine-tuned models leak sensitive user information like travel plans from their training data. The authors develop a two-stage extraction attack: first, a schema-guided sampling method uses a model-against-model loop to extract the target bot's service scope and constrain generation to valid dialogue states; second, a debiased conditional perplexity (DC-PPL) metric ranks candidate states for accurate membership inference. In untargeted settings, they extract values with up to 67% precision. In targeted settings with partial state prefixes, they achieve over 70% precision for full states and 100% for slot values. The study also analyzes factors influencing memorization, finding that substring repetition increases risk while the one-to-many nature of dialogue reduces it.

## Method Summary
The attack pipeline operates in two phases. First, a "model-against-model" loop uses an LLM (e.g., ChatGPT) to probe the target task bot, extracting its dialogue schema (domains and slots) via iterative question generation and response parsing, refined by inter-temperature consistency. Second, for targeted extraction, the attacker seeds generation with partial dialogue state prefixes from the training data. During suffix decoding, token sampling is constrained to the extracted schema to ensure validity and diversity. Each candidate (prefix, suffix) pair is scored using DC-PPL, which normalizes conditional perplexity by the suffix's standalone perplexity to reduce bias toward generic text. Candidates are deduplicated, ranked by score, and evaluated against the training set for precision and recall. The untargeted variant omits prefixes and uses standard sampling with perplexity-based ranking.

## Key Results
- Untargeted extraction recovered values with up to 67% precision and full states at 26% precision.
- Targeted extraction with partial prefixes achieved >70% precision for event-level states and 100% precision for individual slot values, recovering thousands of training examples.
- Schema-guided sampling produced up to 3,000+ valid states, and DC-PPL significantly outperformed PPL and PPL-zlib in ranking memorized states.
- Analysis showed substring repetition increases memorization, while the one-to-many mapping from prefixes to valid suffixes dilutes it.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Schema-guided sampling
Constrains generation to the task bot's actual service scope by pruning the vocabulary for domain/slot tokens to an extracted schema. This ensures generated states are valid and diverse, avoiding the generic or malformed outputs of unconstrained sampling. The method assumes a stable service scope that can be approximated via probing with a reliable oracle like ChatGPT.
### Debiased Conditional Perplexity (DC-PPL)
Improves membership inference by normalizing conditional perplexity (C-PPL) with the standalone perplexity of the suffix. This downweights suffixes that are inherently predictable (e.g., common phrases) even if present in training, focusing on those that are *conditionally* likely given the specific prefix. It accounts for the one-to-many nature of dialogue where many values are contextually plausible.
### Repetition vs. one-to-many dynamics
In turn-level training, early dialogue states are repeated in every subsequent turn's instance, increasing memorization likelihood. Conversely, for a given partial prefix, multiple valid suffix values exist due to user variability, making the conditional distribution flatter and exact recovery harder. This creates a trade-off where repetition boosts extraction but one-to-many dilutes it.

- **Membership Inference Attacks (MIA):** Metrics like perplexity are used to score whether a candidate was in the training set. Needed because extraction requires ranking millions of suffixes to find those likely memorized.

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map:**
  Target Task Bot (LLM fine-tuned on MultiWOZ) -> Schema Extractor (ChatGPT probing loop) -> Candidate Generator (queries task bot with prefixes) -> Vocabulary Constraint Engine (applies extracted schema) -> Membership Scorer (computes DC-PPL/C-PPL) -> Deduplication & Ranking Pipeline -> Evaluator (compares to training data)
- **Critical path:**
  1. Finetune target LLM on MultiWOZ using turn-level modeling.
  2. Extract dialogue schema via model-against-model probing.
  3. For each prefix type, generate suffix candidates with schema-guided sampling.
  4. Score candidates with membership inference metrics.
  5. Deduplicate, rank, and evaluate against training set.
- **Design tradeoffs:**
  - Schema extraction: High-temperature sampling vs. ChatGPT probing. High-temp offers high recall but is costly and imprecise; ChatGPT is efficient and precise but depends on its reliability.
  - Suffix decoding: Greedy vs. diverse sampling. Greedy hits training data more (high precision) but covers less; diverse sampling increases coverage but produces more invalid states.
  - Membership inference: DC-PPL vs. C-PPL. DC-PPL reduces bias but requires extra computation for suffix standalone perplexity.
- **Failure signatures:**
  - Schema extraction returns <90% domain recall → many out-of-scope states in generation.
  - Greedy decoding yields <500 unique states → under-sampling; increase `top-k` or temperature.
  - PPL/zlib ranking shows no precision improvement over random → severe bias; switch to C-PPL/DC-PPL.
  - Extracted values have high precision but states have very low precision → strong one-to-many effect; use shorter prefixes or value-level evaluation.
- **First 3 experiments:**
  1. Reproduce strawman baseline: untargeted extraction with vanilla/beam sampling and PPL ranking; expect state precision <15%.
  2. Schema extraction validation: implement model-against-model loop; compare extracted schema precision/recall to ground truth; expect >80% domain precision.
  3. Targeted extraction with schema-guided sampling: use gold partial prefixes (length=3) and constrained decoding; expect ~3000 valid states and state precision >30%.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- How effective are the proposed dialogue-level modeling and value copy mechanism in reducing memorization while maintaining task performance? The paper suggests these as defenses but provides no empirical evaluation.
- To what extent do the findings on memorization factors (substring repetition, one-to-many nature) hold for other TOD datasets and LLM architectures beyond MultiWOZ and Llama2? The study is limited to one dataset and model family.
- Can membership inference metrics be further refined to more accurately distinguish memorized dialogue states from non-memorized ones, given the structured and context-dependent nature of TODS? The proposed DC-PPL shows overlap in distributions (Fig. 11), indicating room for improvement.
- How do practical deployment constraints (API rate limiting, output filtering, lack of probability access in strict black-box settings) affect the feasibility and effectiveness of these extraction attacks? The threat model assumes a middle-ground with score-based access.

## Limitations
- Exact decoding hyperparameters for the best-case results are undisclosed, preventing exact replication of claimed precision numbers.
- Proposed defenses (value copy, counterfactual augmentation) lack implementation details, so their effectiveness cannot be assessed.
- Evaluation is restricted to MultiWOZ and Llama2-7B; generalizability to other datasets and models is uncertain.
- Observed links between repetition and memorization, and one-to-many and dilution, are correlational, not causally proven.

## Confidence
- Schema-guided sampling effectiveness: Medium (depends on accurate schema extraction; plausible break conditions exist if service scope is dynamic).
- DC-PPL for membership inference: High (strong empirical improvement over baselines, well-defined metric).
- Repetition increases memorization; one-to-many reduces it: Medium (evidence is observational, causal mechanism not established).
