# AQuA: Toward Strategic Response Generation for Ambiguous Visual Questions

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2603.07394
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.07394
- **Reference count:** 40
- **Primary result:** Fine-tuned Qwen2.5-VL-3B achieves 86.28% strategic accuracy on ambiguous VQA, substantially outperforming baselines like GPT-5 (22.86%).

## Executive Summary
This paper addresses the limitation of visual question answering (VQA) models in handling ambiguous queries, which typically respond with overconfident single answers. It introduces AQUA, a dataset that categorizes ambiguous VQA into four levels with defined response strategies (e.g., direct answer, listing options, clarification). Models are trained using supervised fine-tuning followed by Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO) to learn strategic response selection. The tuned model shows significant improvements in strategic accuracy and robustness, demonstrating that explicit strategy-aware training enables vision-language models to recognize and adapt to varying ambiguity.

## Method Summary
The method uses a two-stage training pipeline on the AQUA dataset. First, supervised fine-tuning (SFT) teaches models the response repertoire for each ambiguity level. Second, GRPO reinforcement learning optimizes for correct strategy selection using a reward model (GPT-5-mini) that judges strategic alignment and factual consistency. The AQUA dataset is constructed from COCO images with bounding box annotations, ensuring balanced coverage of four ambiguity levels through programmatic generation and LLM-based filtering.

## Key Results
- The tuned Qwen2.5-VL-3B model achieves 86.28% strategic accuracy across all ambiguity levels.
- Performance surpasses open-source and closed-source baselines by large margins (e.g., GPT-5 at 22.86%).
- Training reduces the inherent bias toward single-answer responses and improves handling of ambiguous boundaries, with gains validated in follow-up dialogue where clarification yields accurate final answers.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
1. **Taxonomy-driven strategy selection:** The four-level ambiguity taxonomy provides a structured decision space. Models learn to map visual-question patterns to strategies via SFT, with output formats (e.g., lists vs. questions) signaling the chosen strategy. Core assumption: Ambiguity can be recognized from visual and linguistic features. Break condition: Boundary cases or strong salience priors may override learned strategies.
2. **GRPO for strategic optimization:** After SFT establishes response repertoires, GRPO refines the policy using group-relative rewards from an LLM judge, encouraging correct strategy choice without catastrophic forgetting. Core assumption: The reward model provides a reliable signal for strategic correctness. Break condition: Misaligned judge or poor KL tuning leads to strategy collapse or factual drift.
3. **Overcoming single-answer bias:** Base VLMs have a strong prior for definitive answers from standard VQA training. Strategy-aware training disrupts this by teaching models to detect ambiguity cues (e.g., multiple objects, referential terms) and override the default answer generation pathway. Core assumption: Ambiguity cues are detectable from low-level features. Break condition: Subtle ambiguity or insufficient training coverage may persist.

## Foundational Learning
- **Standard VQA Paradigm:** Traditional VQA expects one correct answer per image-question pair. Why needed: Contrasts with AQUA's multi-strategy approach. Quick check: For an image with three cars and "What color is this car?", standard VQA forces a single answer, while AQUA might require clarification.
- **Referential Ambiguity & Saliency:** Ambiguity from pronouns like "this" depends on object saliency in the image. Why needed: Levels 1-3 hinge on resolvability of referents. Quick check: Determine if "this" is resolvable by assessing the number of salient objects and query specificity.
- **Reinforcement Learning from Feedback (RLHF/RLAIF):** Using reward models to fine-tune policies. Why needed: GRPO is key for strategy optimization beyond SFT. Quick check: In GRPO, why use group-relative advantages? To improve robustness by comparing samples within a group, reducing variance.

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map:** Input Encoder (vision + text) -> Fusion/Adapter -> Autoregressive Decoder (with implicit strategy classifier) -> [Training: Reward Module -> GRPO Optimizer]
- **Critical path:** 1. Encode image and question into fused representation. 2. Decoder generates response; for training, sample multiple responses. 3. Reward model judges each response's strategy and factual consistency. 4. GRPO computes group advantages and updates decoder policy with KL regularization. 5. Inference uses single forward pass for autoregressive generation.
- **Design tradeoffs:** Synthetic data (COCO with bounding boxes) ensures coverage but may miss organic ambiguity; discrete levels simplify learning but force arbitrary boundaries; LLM-as-judge reward is scalable but introduces dependency; small model training shows gains but limits domain generalization.
- **Failure signatures:** 1. Persistence of "Answer-All" bias, outputting definitive answers for clear ambiguous cases. 2. Boundary confusion between adjacent levels (e.g., Level 2 vs. 3). 3. Salience-driven misclassification, latching onto prominent objects despite high ambiguity. 4. Factual drift during GRPO, where strategy correctness improves but groundedness decreases.
- **First 3 experiments:** 1. Replicate zero-shot evaluation of Qwen2.5-VL-3B on AQUA test set to observe baseline bias. 2. Perform SFT-only training and evaluate, expecting residual overfitting to certain levels. 3. Conduct full SFT+GRPO pipeline and verify improved strategic accuracy with more diagonal confusion matrices.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
1. How can reward design in GRPO be improved beyond the current binary framework to handle nuanced trade-offs between strategy selection and factual consistency?
2. Can the four-level ambiguity categorization be reliably extended to more continuous or hierarchical degrees of ambiguity in real-world visual queries?
3. To what extent do the strategic response capabilities learned on AQUA generalize to ambiguous queries in completely different visual domains (e.g., medical images, satellite imagery)?
4. How does the model's strategic choice performance correlate with actual user satisfaction and task success in interactive, multi-turn dialogue settings beyond a single clarification turn?
5. Can models be made more sample-efficient in learning strategic responses, reducing dependence on large-scale synthetic data like AQUA?

## Limitations
- Dataset construction relies on unreported GPT-5 prompts for filtering, risking variability in synthetic ambiguity generation.
- GRPO depends on GPT-5-mini as a judge; its alignment, cost, and potential biases are uncharacterized and may affect reproducibility.
- The four-level taxonomy forces arbitrary boundaries, leading to inconsistent human-model agreement on edge cases despite high reported inter-annotator agreement.

## Confidence
- **Mechanism 1 (Taxonomy-induced strategic behavior):** Medium – Grounded in defined levels and SFT results, but taxonomy validity hinges on unreported prompt details.
- **Mechanism 2 (GRPO shifts strategy selection):** Medium – Supported by SFT vs. GRPO gains, yet dependent on LLM judge reliability and small sample stability.
- **Mechanism 3 (Overcoming single-answer bias):** Medium-High – Strong evidence from zero-shot confusion matrices and GRPO's bias reduction, but long-tail boundary errors may remain.
