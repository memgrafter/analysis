# Categorical Perception in Large Language Model Hidden States: Structural Warping at Digit-Count Boundaries

## Quick Facts
*   **arXiv ID:** 2603.28258
*   **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.28258
*   **Reference Count:** 32
*   **Primary Result:** Hidden states in decoder-only LLMs show a specific geometric warping (categorical perception) precisely at digit-count boundaries (e.g., 9→10), driven by structural tokenization discontinuities rather than semantic category knowledge.

## Executive Summary
This study investigates whether large language models (LLMs) exhibit categorical perception (CP)—a warping of representational space at category boundaries—in their internal hidden states. Using representational similarity analysis (RSA) across six decoder-only models, the authors find robust evidence for a CP-like geometric effect at digit-count boundaries (e.g., between "9" and "10", "99" and "100"). This effect is best fit by a model combining continuous log-distance with a boundary boost, and it is specific to these structurally defined boundaries, not appearing at control positions or in the continuous temperature domain. A key dissociation emerges: some models ("classic CP") show both geometric warping and explicit neural category identification, while others ("structural CP") show geometric warping alone. Causal interventions suggest early layers contain category-critical directions that influence discrimination behavior.

## Method Summary
The authors employ representational similarity analysis (RSA) on hidden state activations from six decoder-only LLMs (Gemma, Qwen, Llama, Mistral, Phi). They generate sequences of numbers around critical boundaries (e.g., 8, 9, 10, 11) and extract token-level hidden states. The core analysis compares the observed pairwise dissimilarity matrix (based on Euclidean distance) between number tokens against predictions from two models: a pure continuous model (log-transformed magnitude) and a CP-additive model (log-distance plus a boundary boost term). The CP-additive model provided a superior fit at boundary layers across all models. Control analyses tested non-boundary positions (e.g., 18→19) and a continuous temperature domain. To probe functionality, they identified "category directions" in early and peak-geometry layers via linear probes and performed causal interventions (subtracting these directions) to measure impacts on a discrimination task.

## Key Results
*   **Universal CP Geometry:** At digit-count boundaries (9→10, 99→100), a CP-additive model fits the hidden state geometry better than a purely continuous model at 100% of primary layers in all six tested models.
*   **Specificity to Structural Boundaries:** This geometric warping is absent at control numerical positions (e.g., 18→19) and in the continuous temperature domain, indicating dependence on a structural input discontinuity.
*   **Model Dissociation:** Two sub-patterns emerge: "classic CP" models (Gemma, Qwen) show both geometric warping and explicit category identification by linear probes; "structural CP" models (Llama, Mistral, Phi) show geometric warping without explicit identification, suggesting CP geometry can be decoupled from category-specific signals.
*   **Causal Link & Functional Disconnect:** Early-layer category directions causally influence discrimination behavior in a boundary task, yet intervening on the peak-geometry layers (where warping is strongest) minimally impacts output, suggesting a complex relationship between representational geometry and function.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The effect stems from the **structural discontinuity** in the token sequence at digit-count boundaries. Because sub-word tokenization (e.g., BPE) treats "10" as a single token distinct from the token "1" followed by "0", the input embedding space experiences a sharp break in the linear token index. The model's internal processing, particularly in mid-layers, appears to warp the continuous magnitude signal (log-distance) to accommodate this categorical input boundary, creating a geometric "boost" in dissimilarity across the boundary. This warping is a direct consequence of the model's need to reconcile a continuous conceptual domain (numerical magnitude) with its discrete, structure-bound token inventory.

## Foundational Learning
*   **Categorical Perception (CP):** The phenomenon where stimuli from different categories are perceived as more dissimilar than equally distant stimuli from the same category. *Why needed:* The core theoretical lens for interpreting the hidden state geometry. *Quick check:* Does the representational space show a greater-than-expected jump in dissimilarity at a known category boundary?
*   **Representational Similarity Analysis (RSA):** A method that compares two dissimilarity matrices (e.g., from neural data vs. a computational model). *Why needed:* The primary tool for quantifying geometric warping against continuous vs. CP models. *Quick check:* Is the model's RDMs better fit by a CP-additive model than a pure continuous one at target boundaries?
*   **Tokenization Discontinuity:** The break in the sequential token stream caused by merging digits into multi-digit tokens (e.g., "10" is one token, not "1"+"0"). *Why needed:* The hypothesized source of the structural boundary that triggers CP geometry. *Quick check:* Are boundaries only present where tokenization creates a single-token vs. multi-token transition?
*   **Decoder-Only Transformer Architecture:** The autoregressive architecture (like GPT) processing tokens sequentially. *Why needed:* The substrate where hidden state geometry is measured. *Quick check:* Does the effect manifest in layers responsible for integrating contextual and sequential information?
*   **Linear Probing:** Training a simple classifier (e.g., linear readout) on frozen representations to extract a signal (e.g., "category direction"). *Why needed:* To identify and manipulate putative category-specific neural directions. *Quick check:* Can a linear classifier reliably distinguish tokens from "before" vs. "after" the boundary?
*   **Causal Intervention (Representation Steeping):** A method (e.g., difference-in-means) to remove a targeted direction from a layer's activations and measure downstream effects. *Why needed:* To test the functional necessity of the identified category directions. *Quick check:* Does ablating the early-layer category direction impair a discrimination task more than ablating the peak-geometry layer direction?

## Architecture Onboarding
*   **Component Map:**
    Input Tokens -> Token Embedding -> Transformer Blocks (Multi-Head Attention + Feed-Forward Network, with LayerNorm) -> Final Layer Norm -> Output Logits
    *Critical Path for CP Effect:* Mid-level Transformer Blocks (e.g., layers 10-20 in a 32-layer model) show peak geometry warping. Early blocks (e.g., layers 1-5) contain category direction signals that causally affect discrimination.
*   **Design Tradeoffs:**
    *   **Metric Choice (RSA):** Uses linear Euclidean distance, which may miss non-linear geometry. Tradeoff: interpretability vs. completeness.
    *   **Control Conditions:** Comparing to non-boundary numbers and temperature domain isolates structural vs. semantic effects. Tradeoff: choosing perfectly matched controls is challenging (e.g., frequency, semantic salience).
    *   **Model Scope:** Focus on decoder-only LLMs. Tradeoff: depth of analysis vs. architectural generality (encoder-only, multimodal untested).
*   **Failure Signatures:**
    1.  No differential model fit (CP-additive vs. continuous) at any boundary layer.
    2.  CP geometry appearing at non-boundary control positions or in the temperature domain.
    3.  Category direction probe performing at chance for all layers.
    4.  Causal interventions showing no effect on the discrimination task.
*   **First Three Experiments:**
    1.  **Replication & Extension:** Run the identical RSA pipeline on at least two other decoder-only LLMs (e.g., GPT-family, Claude) to confirm the 100% layer-fit result.
    2.  **Category Generalization Test:** Replace numbers with another categorical domain where tokenization creates boundaries (e.g., common words vs. their morphological variants, or specific color names if tokenized uniquely). Apply the same RSA analysis.
    3.  **Tokenization Manipulation:** Fine-tune or prompt a model with an alternative tokenizer that eliminates the digit-count boundary (e.g., by always splitting digits, adding explicit boundary tokens). Test if CP geometry disappears while semantic magnitude processing remains.

## Limitations
*   Analysis restricted to six decoder-only LLMs; findings may not generalize to other architectures (e.g., encoder-decoder, small models, masked LM).
*   Focus exclusively on digit-count boundaries; the effect's presence for other semantic categories (e.g., color terms, animal species) remains unknown.
*   RSA's reliance on linear distance metrics may not fully capture the potentially non-linear nature of representational warping.
*   The "classic" vs. "structural" CP model split is post-hoc and may not reflect fundamental mechanistic differences.
*   Causal interventions are correlational and do not quantify the impact of perturbations on overall model performance or generation quality.
*   Control conditions may not fully rule out alternative confounds like general magnitude processing or token frequency effects.

## Next Checks
1.  Extend RSA to a diverse set of ≥10 LLMs (including smaller, encoder-only, and multimodal models) and test non-numeric categorical boundaries (e.g., color terms, animal categories) to evaluate the generality of the phenomenon.
2.  Conduct targeted layer-specific ablation and stimulation of identified category directions, measuring both task-specific discrimination accuracy and broader generation quality (e.g., coherence, factuality) to quantify functional roles.
3.  Manipulate the tokenization system itself (e.g., via alternative BPE merges, added boundary tokens, or character-level modeling) to causally confirm that the input-format discontinuity is sufficient to induce the observed CP geometry.
