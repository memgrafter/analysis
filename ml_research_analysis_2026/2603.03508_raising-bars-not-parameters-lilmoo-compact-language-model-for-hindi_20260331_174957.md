# Raising Bars, Not Parameters: LilMoo Compact Language Model for Hindi

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2603.03508
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.03508
- **Reference count:** 39
- **Primary result:** LilMoo, a 0.6B-parameter Hindi model, outperforms comparably sized multilingual baselines on Hindi benchmarks with ~100× less compute.

## Executive Summary
LilMoo is a compact Hindi language model trained from scratch on a high-quality curated corpus (GigaLekh, ~90B tokens) using a multi-stage filtering pipeline. Two training recipes are explored: a monolingual single-stage (v0.1) and a bilingual multi-stage with curated English augmentation (v0.2). Despite using only ~1.3e21 FLOPs—about 100 times less than baselines—LilMoo achieves significantly higher normalized preferred metric (NPM) scores on a comprehensive Hindi evaluation suite, demonstrating that targeted, language-specific pretraining can rival large multilingual models at sub-billion scale and address underrepresentation of low-resource languages.

## Method Summary
The method centers on constructing the GigaLekh corpus via a rigorous pipeline: sourcing diverse Hindi text, applying language identification (FT176/GlotLID), heuristic filtering, MinHash deduplication, and LLM-as-judge annotation (Qwen2.5-32B) followed by training Hindi-RoBERTa classifiers for educational quality and toxicity. Two training recipes differ in data mixture: v0.1 is monolingual Hindi-only with cosine LR decay, while v0.2 uses a bilingual curriculum (≈50:50 Hindi:English) with a trapezoidal LR schedule. The architecture is Llama-style (28 layers, 16 heads, GQA with 8 KV heads, hidden dim 1536, SwiGLU, RMSNorm, RoPE) with a 49,152-token SentencePiece vocabulary trained on a Hindi/English/code mix. Training employs AdamW (lr=7e-4, wd=0.1 selective), gradient clipping, and optimizations (FlashAttention-2, Liger kernels) on 8x A100 GPUs, targeting ~356–376B tokens with high Model FLOP Utilization (MFU ~70%).

## Key Results
- LilMoo v0.1 achieves NPM 8.70 vs. Qwen2.5-0.5B at 1.92; v0.2 achieves NPM 9.94 vs. Qwen3-0.6B at 4.08.
- LilMoo surpasses baselines on 5 of 6 Hindi benchmarks (ARC, HellaSwag, MMLU, CSQA, MILU).
- Uses ~1.3e21 FLOPs, approximately 100× less compute than multilingual baselines.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1
- **Claim:** High-quality, language-specific pretraining data can yield superior performance for a low-resource language compared to large, multilingual models.
- **Mechanism:** The GigaLekh corpus (~90B tokens) uses a multi-stage filtering pipeline (heuristics + LLM-as-a-judge) to maximize linguistic relevance and minimize noise/toxicity for Hindi. This curated signal density allows a smaller model to learn more efficiently from its limited parameter budget.
- **Core assumption:** A smaller, monolingual or carefully bilingual model can achieve deeper linguistic competence on its target language(s) than a large multilingual model that dilutes capacity across many languages with uneven data quality.
- **Evidence anchors:** Abstract and Section 3.2 detail LLM annotation and classifier training; Sections 3 and Appendix B show deliberate source selection for Hindi-dominance and educational value.
- **Break condition:** If the curated corpus lacks linguistic diversity (e.g., modern slang, technical jargon, regional variants), performance may not transfer broadly.

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim:** A multi-stage training curriculum with curated bilingual augmentation (Hindi + high-quality English) can enhance model performance beyond monolingual training at similar compute budgets.
- **Mechanism:** The multi-stage recipe (v0.2) progressively shifts the data mixture, starting with a balanced mix and up-sampling higher-quality subsets, while maintaining an approximately 50:50 Hindi:English ratio. This introduces cross-lingual transfer from high-resource English educational/reasoning data, boosting robustness without catastrophic forgetting of Hindi.
- **Core assumption:** Strategic exposure to high-quality, conceptually aligned English data improves general knowledge and reasoning in a low-resource language model through shared semantic structures.
- **Evidence anchors:** Abstract notes v0.2's higher NPM; Section 6.5 describes the three-stage curriculum; Section 7.3 shows v0.2 outperforms v0.1.
- **Break condition:** If English data is not carefully curated, it may introduce bias, reduce culture-specific task performance (as with Global PIQA), or cause representation interference.

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim:** Transparency and optimized, compute-efficient training can produce models that achieve better performance-per-FLOP than opaque, large-scale multilingual baselines.
- **Mechanism:** Training from scratch with an open, reproducible pipeline on a focused corpus avoids inefficiencies of continued pretraining on black-box models. Architectural choices (GQA, FlashAttention, Liger kernels) and hardware-aware design maximize MFU (~70%), squeezing more learning from the same FLOP budget.
- **Core assumption:** A significant portion of compute for massive multilingual models is spent on cross-lingual representations and noisy data, less efficient for a specific low-resource language than targeted training.
- **Evidence anchors:** Abstract cites ~100× less compute; Section 5.2 details optimizations and MFU; Figure 5 quantifies FLOP reduction.
- **Break condition:** Efficiency advantage may not hold for scaling beyond 0.6B parameters or for languages with extremely sparse digital presence.

## Foundational Learning
- **Concept: Data-Centric Pretraining Pipelines**
  - **Why needed here:** The value of LilMoo rests on GigaLekh's quality; understanding the multi-stage filtering (source screening, language ID, heuristics, deduplication, LLM-based annotation) is critical for replication.
  - **Quick check question:** What are potential failure modes at each filtering stage, and how does the distilled classifier scale annotation?

- **Concept: Compute-Optimal Scaling Laws for Small Models**
  - **Why needed here:** DeepSeek scaling laws derive common hyperparameters (batch size, LR) across recipes, anchoring experimental design to a compute budget for fair comparison.
  - **Quick check question:** How would adjusting the `C` estimate (total compute) change the derived optimal batch size and learning rate?

- **Concept: Multi-Stage / Curriculum Learning**
  - **Why needed here:** The v0.2 innovation is the multi-stage recipe with changing data mixtures and non-standard LR cooldown; understanding the rationale for up-sampling and `sqrt` decay is key.
  - **Quick check question:** What cognitive or modeling benefits are hypothesized from the "sqrt" cooldown versus linear decay, and how might this interact with changing data distribution?

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map:**
  Data Sources → Trafilatura (text extraction) → FT176/GlotLID (language ID) → Heuristic filters → MinHash deduplication → LLM-as-judge (Qwen2.5-32B) → Hindi-RoBERTa classifiers → GigaLekh Corpus → SentencePiece Tokenizer (49,152 vocab) → Llama-style Model (28 layers, GQA, SwiGLU, etc.) → PyTorch DDP with FlashAttention-2/Liger → AdamW + Trapezoidal/Cosine LR → Evaluation Harness (Hindi suite) → NPM aggregation.

- **Critical path:** Corpus Curation → Tokenizer Training → Stage 1 Training (Monolingual/Baseline) → Stage 2/3 Training (Bilingual/Multi-stage) → Intermittent Validation → Final Evaluation.

- **Design tradeoffs:**
  - Monolingual vs. Bilingual: Depth (v0.1) vs. Cross-lingual transfer (v0.2); v0.1 risks limited knowledge, v0.2 risks cultural dilution (Global PIQA drop).
  - Depth vs. Width: Chose "deep and slim" (28 layers) based on MobileLLM/SmolLM insights for small models vs. wider alternatives.
  - Tokenizer Domain: Training on Hindi/English/code mix vs. Hindi-only; mix improves code-mixed text efficiency but increases token count for pure Hindi.

- **Failure signatures:**
  - Training instability: Loss spikes or rollbacks indicate poor LR schedule or data batch issues.
  - Poor benchmark correlation: High volatility/low SNR on key metrics suggests noisy evaluation for this model/task.
  - Cultural performance drop: Significant decrease on culturally grounded tasks (e.g., Global PIQA) when English data is introduced.
  - Tokenizer inefficiency: High fertility/PCW or OOVs from poor language fit in tokenizer training mix.

- **First 3 experiments:**
  1. **Data Quality Ablation:** Train a 100M-parameter model for 1B tokens on (a) raw crawled Hindi, (b) heuristic-filtered Hindi, (c) LLM-filtered Hindi. Compare loss curves and zero-shot on a small Hindi holdout to isolate filter impact.
  2. **Recipe Sanity Check:** Implement single-stage (v0.1) for 10B tokens. Validate loss decrease, monotonic NPM improvement on retained benchmarks (HellaSwag, CSQA, ARC), and >random baseline on at least one benchmark.
  3. **Tokenizer Efficiency Test:** Tokenize a fixed 10k-word Hindi test set with custom tokenizer vs. Qwen's baseline. Measure Subword Fertility and Proportion of Continued Words; success requires lower values than baseline.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- **Open Question 1:** How do alternative data filtering pipelines (e.g., JQL Ali et al. (2025)) compare to FineWeb2 in effectiveness for low-resource language pretraining? Basis: Future work could compare different data pipelines. Unresolved due to no comparative analysis. Resolution: Train identical models on same raw corpus using different pipelines, evaluate Hindi task performance.
- **Open Question 2:** What is the optimal multi-stage curriculum (data mixtures, language ratios, compute allocation) for sub-billion parameter models in low-resource settings? Basis: Training curriculum ablations not fully explored. Unresolved due to lack of systematic ablations. Resolution: Controlled experiments varying curriculum components with Hindi benchmark scores.
- **Open Question 3:** Does bilingual augmentation with English benefit other low-resource languages beyond Hindi, and what linguistic or resource factors modulate this effect? Basis: Cross-lingual transfer analysis not tested extensively. Unresolved as findings limited to Hindi. Resolution: Replicate pipeline for diverse low-resource languages (e.g., Tamil, Swahili) and compare monolingual vs. bilingual performance.
- **Open Question 4:** Do larger model sizes (1B–3B parameters) trained with similar recipes retain the performance advantage over multilingual baselines observed at 0.6B scale? Basis: Focused on 0.5B–0.6B models; scaling laws may change efficiency. Unresolved as study only covers sub-billion scales. Resolution: Train 1B, 2B, 3B models with identical recipes, compare against comparable multilingual baselines.

## Limitations
- Exact source file lists and download procedures are unspecified, hindering full replication.
- Bilingual training (v0.2) causes cultural performance drop on tasks like Global PIQA, indicating trade-offs with English data mixing.
- Performance advantages may not scale beyond sub-billion parameter regime or to languages with sparser digital footprints.

## Confidence
- **Data-quality gains:** High confidence (LLM-as-judge filtering well-documented with strong ablation evidence).
- **Bilingual curriculum benefits:** Medium confidence (v0.2 outperforms v0.1 overall but shows brittleness on cultural tasks).
- **Compute efficiency vs. baselines:** Medium confidence (transparent metrics but advantage may diminish for larger models or sparser languages).
