# Robotic Ultrasound Makes CBCT Alive

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2603.10220
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.10220
- **Reference count:** 21
- **Primary result:** Proposed method reduces forward-backward residual by 53% and folding ratio by nearly half while matching RAFT accuracy in updating CBCT slices, but runs 5× faster and avoids geometric distortions.

## Executive Summary
Static cone-beam CT (CBCT) cannot track soft-tissue deformations during interventions, limiting its utility. This paper introduces a framework that uses robotic ultrasound (US) streams to infer dense tissue motion and update CBCT slices in real time. The core is USCorUNet, a lightweight bidirectional network trained with optical flow-guided supervision to estimate deformation from US. It achieves comparable accuracy to the state-of-the-art RAFT model for CBCT updating while being five times faster and avoiding the geometric distortions of prior methods like LC2-FFD. The system integrates calibrated robot-US-CBCT geometry, a specialized deformation transfer with spatial weighting, and a training objective that enforces bidirectional consistency and physical plausibility.

## Method Summary
The pipeline takes as input a calibrated spatial transform between the robotic US probe and CBCT coordinate system, plus sequential US frame pairs. A rigid LC2 registration first aligns the initial CBCT slice to the US field-of-view. USCorUNet, a ResUNet-style encoder-decoder with a shared correlation module, then predicts bidirectional deformation fields (forward and backward) from a 5-channel input (the two frames, their difference, and gradient magnitudes). It is trained using pseudo-labels distilled from RAFT flows via direct and bisected composition, combined with confidence-weighted photometric consistency and a regularization loss that penalizes folding. The estimated deformation is corrected for probe-induced convex compression and spatially attenuated using an Euclidean Distance Transform from the US boundary before warping the CBCT slice. This localized, smoothly decaying application prevents artifacts in the larger CBCT volume.

## Key Results
- Reduces forward–backward residual by 53% (1.81 → 0.85) and folding ratio from 0.24% to 0.13% versus RAFT.
- Matches RAFT’s CBCT updating accuracy (MAE 0.33 vs 0.34, SSIM 0.22 vs 0.21) while running 5× faster (11.25 ms vs 56.24 ms).
- Avoids LC2-FFD’s geometric distortions and is 512× faster than that method.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- **Claim:** Distilling optical flow from a pre-trained model (RAFT) provides a robust initialization for USCorUNet’s deformation estimation, improving bidirectional consistency over training from scratch on noisy ultrasound.
- **Mechanism:** Pseudo-labels for bidirectional deformation fields are generated by composing direct and bisected RAFT flows between adjacent ultrasound frames. The network learns to mimic these flows while being fine-tuned with additional photometric and regularization losses.
- **Core assumption:** RAFT’s flow, despite potential inaccuracies in ultrasound due to speckle and artifacts, provides a more reliable structural prior than random initialization for the lightweight USCorUNet to refine.
- **Evidence anchors:** The method section describes RAFT pseudo-label generation via direct and bisect candidates selecting lower post-warp misalignment. Ablations in Table 1 show that removing the correlation branch (which encodes this prior) degrades photometric alignment.
- **Break condition:** If the domain gap between RAFT’s training data (natural images) and the ultrasound domain is too large, the pseudo-labels could mislead the student network, worsening FB consistency and folding metrics.

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim:** A bidirectional training objective combining flow distillation, confidence-weighted photometric consistency, and regularization reduces forward-backward residuals and folding while maintaining alignment.
- **Mechanism:** The loss function explicitly enforces that the composed forward and backward fields sum to near-zero identity (via L_flow) and penalizes folding (det(I+∇F)<0) via L_reg, while L_photo ensures intensity consistency after warping using a confidence map to downweight unreliable regions.
- **Core assumption:** The combination of these three loss terms balances data fidelity (photometric), cycle consistency (bidirectional flow), and physical plausibility (smoothness/folding) better than any single term alone.
- **Evidence anchors:** Section 2.2 defines the total loss L = λ_flow L_flow + λ_photo L_photo + λ_reg L_reg. Table 1 ablations show removing L_reg increases folding, removing L_photo reduces FB consistency/Dice, and removing the correlation branch (part of L_flow structure) hurts photometric alignment.
- **Break condition:** If the weighting hyperparameters (λ_flow=1, λ_photo=0.2, λ_reg=0.05) are poorly tuned for a new dataset, the network may overfit to photometric noise (increasing folding) or become overly smooth (losing fine deformation details).

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim:** Spatially regularizing and smoothly decaying the ultrasound-inferred deformation field before applying it to the CBCT slice prevents geometric distortions and enables plausible updates without repeated radiation.
- **Mechanism:** The pointwise deformation from probe contact is first corrected for convex compression using a Gaussian profile. Then, an Euclidean Distance Transform (EDT) from the ultrasound boundary generates a smooth spatial weight that attenuates the deformation field toward the CBCT slice periphery.
- **Core assumption:** Tissue motion induced by the ultrasound probe is localized and should smoothly dissipate beyond the visible acoustic window; abrupt transfer would cause unnatural warping in the larger CBCT field-of-view.
- **Evidence anchors:** Section 2.3 details the Gaussian correction and EDT-based weighting (D_final = D_pad ⊙ W). Table 4 shows LC2-FFD introduces distortions while the proposed method avoids them, matching RAFT accuracy.
- **Break condition:** If the probe-induced deformation is not primarily localized (e.g., large global respiratory motion), the Gaussian correction and EDT decay may under-apply needed deformation in distant regions, leading to residual misalignment.

## Foundational Learning
- **Concept: Optical Flow Distillation**
  - **Why needed:** Provides a structured prior from a powerful but slow model (RAFT) to train a real-time student (USCorUNet), addressing the efficiency-accuracy trade-off.
  - **Quick check:** Why use bidirectional flow composition (direct + bisected) for pseudo-labels instead of just direct flow?
- **Concept: Bidirectional Consistency & Folding Penalty**
  - **Why needed:** Ultrasound’s speckle and low texture make pure photometric alignment ambiguous; explicit cycle consistency and invertibility constraints ensure deformation fields are physically plausible for tissue.
  - **Quick check:** How does a folding penalty (det(Jacobian)<0) relate to tissue plausibility, and why isn’t photometric loss alone sufficient?
- **Concept: Spatial Weighting via EDT**
  - **Why needed:** Ultrasound provides only a partial, local view of deformation; naive application to the entire CBCT slice causes artifacts at boundaries. EDT-based smooth decay propagates deformation plausibly beyond the visible region.
  - **Quick check:** What would happen if the spatial weight W(x,y) were replaced with a uniform weight (all ones)?

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map:** Calibration module -> LC2 registration -> USCorUNet -> Deformation correction (Gaussian + EDT weighting) -> CBCT slice warping.
- **Critical path:** Calibrated frame alignment (CTU) → LC2 rigid refinement → USCorUNet deformation estimation → Correction & spatial weighting → Warp CBCT slice. A failure in LC2 alignment propagates downstream.
- **Design tradeoffs:**
  - Lightweight network (USCorUNet) vs. detail: Fewer parameters and correlation at 1/8 resolution sacrifice fine detail for speed (5× faster than RAFT) but may struggle with very large displacements if not well-initialized.
  - Photometric vs. physical loss: Heavy reliance on λ_photo (0.2) risks fitting speckle; λ_reg (0.05) is mild to avoid over-smoothing.
  - Probe-specific correction: Gaussian profile assumes known probe displacement direction/magnitude (d_robot); inaccurate robot pose estimates degrade correction.
- **Failure signatures:**
  - High forward-backward residual (>1.0) and folding ratio (>0.2%) indicate non-invertible or inconsistent deformation.
  - Low photometric alignment (MAE > 0.06) after warping suggests the network failed to capture true motion, possibly due to poor initialization or severe artifacts.
  - Visual tearing or folding in updated CBCT slices, especially near slice borders, indicates failed spatial weighting or incorrect d_robot.
- **First 3 experiments:**
  1. Calibration & LC2 validation on a phantom with known landmarks: Measure residual landmark error after LC2 refinement to ensure <1-2 mm before proceeding.
  2. USCorUNet synthetic test on a controlled deformation phantom (e.g., gel with embedded grid): Apply known displacement, capture US before/after, estimate deformation, compute endpoint error against ground truth.
  3. CBCT update qualitative check on a phantom with a distinct structure (e.g., metal bead): Apply probe press, update CBCT slice, verify the bead moves consistently with the observed US deformation without edge artifacts.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- **Open Question 1:** How can semantic segmentation be effectively integrated into the USCorUNet pipeline to improve deformation estimation in anatomically complex regions?
  - **Basis:** Explicitly stated in Discussion as future work.
  - **Why unresolved:** Current method relies solely on intensity/structural features, which may struggle with ambiguous textures; semantic context could disambiguate motions.
- **Open Question 2:** To what extent do the estimated deformation fields adhere to biomechanical constraints beyond folding penalties, and how can physical plausibility be further improved?
  - **Basis:** Inferred from discussion of data-driven approaches lacking physical constraints; only Jacobian folding penalty is used.
  - **Why unresolved:** No volume preservation or elasticity limits enforced, critical for clinical trust.
- **Open Question 3:** How well does USCorUNet generalize to new patients, anatomical sites, or deformation types without regime-specific fine-tuning?
  - **Basis:** Inferred from results showing fine-tuning needed for probe-induced vs. externally induced motions (Table 3).
  - **Why unresolved:** Base model trained on in-vivo arm data; adaptation required for other scenarios raises zero-shot generalization concerns.
- **Open Question 4:** How robust is the deformation estimation to ultrasound image artifacts (e.g., speckle, shadowing, depth-dependent distortion) that violate optical flow assumptions?
  - **Basis:** Inferred from introduction noting artifacts violate standard optical flow assumptions; method uses optical flow-guided supervision.
  - **Why unresolved:** Impact of artifacts not explicitly analyzed; robustness not quantified.

## Limitations
- LC2 calibration and refinement details are underspecified, risking upstream alignment errors.
- RAFT pseudo-label generation lacks explicit parameters (e.g., intermediate frames, selection threshold), affecting distillation fidelity.
- Assumes localized probe deformation; global motions could invalidate EDT decay approach.
- Validation restricted to forearm/upper‑arm and gel phantoms; generalisation to other anatomies and imaging conditions remains unproven.

## Confidence
- **Speed‑accuracy trade-off:** High (Table 1 confirms 5× speedup with minimal accuracy loss).
- **Flow distillation benefit:** Medium (ablations show improvement, but domain gap may limit ultrasound performance).
- **Bidirectional loss efficacy:** Medium (improves metrics, but λ‑weight sensitive).
- **EDT spatial weighting:** High (eliminates LC2‑FFD distortions, Table 4).
- **Real‑time feasibility:** Medium (validated on limited datasets; requires broader testing).
