# EvoESAP: Non-Uniform Expert Pruning for Sparse MoE

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID**: 2603.06003
- **Source URL**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.06003
- **Reference count**: 40
- **Primary result**: EvoESAP finds better non-uniform pruning allocations than uniform pruning, improving open-ended generation metrics by up to +19.6% on MATH-500 at 50% sparsity.

## Executive Summary
Sparse Mixture-of-Experts (SMoE) models reduce per-token compute but retain all experts in memory, leading to high memory and throughput costs. Post-training expert pruning reduces these costs, but uniform sparsity across layers can degrade open-ended generation quality. EvoESAP introduces a two-stage approach: first, ranking experts within each layer using importance criteria (Frequency, EAN, SEER, REAP); second, using evolutionary search under a global budget to find optimal non-uniform layer-wise sparsity allocations. Fitness is guided by the Expected Speculative Acceptance Proxy (ESAP), a cheap teacher-forced metric that measures distribution overlap between full and pruned models. Experiments on OLMoE-7B, ERNIE-21B, and Qwen3-30B at 25% and 50% sparsity show consistent gains over uniform pruning, with improvements of up to +19.6% on math tasks while preserving multiple-choice accuracy. ESAP also speeds up search by ~18× compared to using true speculative decoding acceptance.

## Method Summary
EvoESAP decouples pruning into two steps: (1) compute a fixed per-layer expert pruning order by sorting experts according to an importance score (e.g., REAP) on a calibration dataset; (2) search over integer allocation vectors that specify how many experts to prune per layer under a fixed global budget, using an evolutionary algorithm with budget-preserving level-switch mutations. Each candidate allocation is evaluated by ESAP, which averages the token-wise min(p, q) overlap between full and pruned model distributions on a small search set under teacher forcing. This avoids costly autoregressive decoding. After search, the best allocation is applied to remove the least important experts per layer according to the precomputed orders.

## Key Results
- At 50% sparsity, EvoESAP with Frequency on ERNIE-21B yields +15.2% on math and +19.6% on MATH-500 compared to uniform pruning.
- On Qwen3-30B, it achieves +6.6% code, +7.2% creative writing, and +9.5% math.
- Multiple-choice accuracy remains comparable, and ESAP reduces search time by ~18× versus true speculative decoding acceptance.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1
- **Claim:** Decoupling within-layer expert ranking from across-layer sparsity allocation enables the discovery of non-uniform schedules that better preserve open-ended generation quality compared to uniform pruning.
- **Mechanism:** First, compute a fixed per-layer pruning order by sorting experts with an importance criterion (e.g., REAP). Second, under a fixed global budget, use evolutionary search to optimize an integer vector specifying how many experts to prune per layer, evaluating each candidate with ESAP.
- **Core assumption:** The optimal layer-wise sparsity pattern is non-uniform and varies with the model and pruning metric; uniform allocation is suboptimal.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract]: "We decouple pruning into: (1) ranking experts within each layer using criteria... (2) searching for optimal non-uniform layer-wise sparsity under a fixed global budget."
  - [section 3.2]: Explicitly formulates the two-step decoupling and defines the search problem.
  - [corpus]: Weak direct evidence; related works like DiEP and Dropping Experts explore non-uniform allocation, but EvoESAP's specific decoupling is novel and evaluated in this paper.
- **Break condition:** If uniform allocation consistently matches or exceeds non-uniform across models and criteria, or if within-layer rankings are so unreliable that allocation has negligible impact.

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim:** Expected Speculative Acceptance Proxy (ESAP) serves as a bounded, stable, and computationally cheap fitness function that correlates with speculative decoding acceptance, enabling efficient inner-loop search.
- **Mechanism:** ESAP computes the expected acceptance probability under teacher-forced contexts by summing min(p(v), q(v)) over the vocabulary for each next-token position, avoiding Monte Carlo variance and autoregressive decoding costs.
- **Core assumption:** The teacher-forced min-overlap between full and pruned model distributions reliably predicts their compatibility during open-ended generation.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract]: "ESAP is cheap and stable, avoiding expensive autoregressive decoding."
  - [section 3.4]: Derives ESAP as 1 - total variation and shows in Table 4 that it reduces search time by ~18× versus true speculative decoding while achieving comparable performance.
  - [corpus]: No direct mentions in neighbor abstracts; corpus evidence is absent for ESAP specifically.
- **Break condition:** If ESAP scores do not correlate with actual generation quality across diverse models or if teacher-forced contexts fail to capture distribution shifts in autoregressive decoding.

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim:** Evolutionary search with budget-preserving level-switch mutations effectively explores the discrete space of non-uniform allocations under global constraints, guided by ESAP.
- **Mechanism:** Initialize population with uniform, patterned, and random feasible allocations. Mutate offspring by transferring a budget Δ between two layers (level-switch) while keeping total budget constant, composing multiple steps for exploration. Select top candidates per generation via ESAP fitness.
- **Core assumption:** Local mutations can traverse the feasible allocation space and ESAP provides a gradient-like signal for optimization.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [section 3.3]: Details the algorithm, initialization, mutation operator, and constraint handling.
  - [section 4.2]: Empirical gains over uniform pruning across models and criteria demonstrate search effectiveness.
  - [corpus]: Neighbors like "Efficient Expert Pruning" use evolutionary search for expert selection, supporting the paradigm, but EvoESAP's mutation is tailored for allocation.
- **Break condition:** If search converges prematurely to suboptimal allocations or if mutation steps are too small/large to escape local optima in the fitness landscape.

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map:** Calibration module -> Search engine -> Fitness evaluator -> Model instantiator
  - Calibration module: Computes per-layer expert importance scores (e.g., REAP) from a dataset.
  - Search engine: Manages evolutionary loop (population, mutation, selection).
  - Fitness evaluator: Calculates ESAP scores via teacher-forced forward passes, caching full model logits.
  - Model instantiator: Applies the best allocation to prune experts according to fixed orders.
- **Critical path:** 
  1. Calibrate importance scores on a dataset to get per-layer pruning orders.
  2. Initialize search population with feasible allocations (uniform, patterned, random).
  3. For each generation, evaluate ESAP fitness for all candidates (using cached full logits).
  4. Select elite candidates, apply level-switch mutations to generate offspring while preserving global budget.
  5. Repeat for T generations, track best allocation by ESAP.
  6. Prune model using best allocation and within-layer orders.
- **Design tradeoffs:** 
  - ESAP vs. SPEC-DEC: ESAP trades minor accuracy for ~18× speedup and stability; may miss dynamic acceptance nuances.
  - Evolutionary search vs. grid search: Evolutionary handles large discrete spaces efficiently but introduces stochasticity; grid search is exhaustive but infeasible for many layers.
  - Non-uniform allocation: Complexity increases but gains are model-dependent; sensitive to calibration data choice.
- **Failure signatures:** 
  - High ESAP but low generation quality: fitness proxy misalignment.
  - Search converges to uniform allocation quickly: mutation ineffective or fitness landscape flat.
  - Large drops on specific tasks (e.g., code): allocation overly aggressive in critical layers or poor calibration.
- **First 3 experiments:** 
  1. **Baseline replication:** Implement uniform pruning with the same within-layer order to verify setup and establish performance reference.
  2. **Calibration test:** Vary calibration datasets (e.g., evol-codealpaca vs. C4) to assess sensitivity of pruning orders and ESAP fitness.
  3. **Search validation:** Compare EvoESAP's non-uniform allocation against random non-uniform patterns and heuristic allocations (e.g., frequency-based layer-wise counts) to confirm evolutionary search benefits.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
### Open Question 1
- **Question:** Does non-uniform layer-wise sparsity allocation universally improve open-ended generation quality for SMoE pruning across diverse models and expert-importance criteria, or are its benefits contingent on specific model architectures and pruning metrics?
- **Basis in paper:** [explicit] The authors state that the benefit of non-uniform allocation "remains an open question" and their results show inconsistent patterns (e.g., REAP helps OLMoE but hurts Qwen3 at 25% sparsity). They also note mixed prior evidence (SEER-MoE vs. MoE-I2).
- **Why unresolved:** The paper presents EvoESAP as a framework to find beneficial allocations but does not provide a universal principle predicting when non-uniform schedules help vs. harm. The observed benefits are empirical and vary across models/criteria.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** A large-scale study across many SMoE architectures (e.g., varying depth, expert count, routing mechanisms) and pruning orders, identifying systematic factors (e.g., layer depth, task sensitivity) that predict allocation effectiveness.

### Open Question 2
- **Question:** Can jointly optimizing within-layer expert selection and across-layer allocation (instead of the decoupled approach in EvoESAP) yield further improvements in generation quality under a fixed global sparsity budget?
- **Basis in paper:** [explicit] The authors explicitly call this an "open problem" in their limitations: "we currently assume a fixed within-layer pruning order and optimize only the across-layer allocation, leaving joint expert selection and allocation as an open problem."
- **Why unresolved:** EvoESAP assumes a fixed per-layer ranking (e.g., from REAP) and only searches over how many experts to prune per layer. The interaction between *which* experts are kept and *how many* are kept per layer may be non-separable.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** An extension of EvoESAP (or a new method) that jointly searches over both the set of retained experts per layer and the layer-wise counts under a global constraint, with fitness still guided by ESAP or a similar proxy.

### Open Question 3
- **Question:** Is ESAP an optimal or theoretically-grounded proxy for maximizing speculative decoding acceptance rate, or is it merely one effective heuristic among many possible teacher-forced distribution overlap metrics?
- **Basis in paper:** [inferred] ESAP is introduced as a "speculative-decoding-inspired, teacher-forced metric" that correlates with generation quality. The paper shows it outperforms KL, NLL, and SAP in ablations but does not establish a theoretical link to the true expected acceptance rate under autoregressive decoding.
- **Why unresolved:** ESAP is defined as the expected min(p, q) over a fixed teacher-forced dataset. While this matches the per-token speculative acceptance expectation, the true decoding acceptance depends on sequential dependencies (prefix drift, block size). The paper shows ESAP reduces search cost versus true SPEC-DEC but does not prove it as a lower bound or consistent estimator of the true objective.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Theoretical analysis relating ESAP to the expected trajectory-level acceptance rate under speculative decoding (e.g., bounds under independence assumptions). Alternatively, extensive empirical correlation studies across diverse models and decoding settings to see if ESAP maximization always predicts higher true acceptance.

### Open Question 4
- **Question:** How sensitive is the effectiveness of any expert pruning method (including EvoESAP) to the choice of calibration dataset used to compute within-layer importance scores (e.g., REAP, Frequency)?
- **Basis in paper:** [explicit] The paper reports a substantial performance drop when calibrating on C4 instead of evol-codealpaca-v1 (e.g., ~40% drop on code benchmarks). They note "pruning can be highly sensitive to the choice of calibration data" and recommend evol-codealpaca-v1 for balance.
- **Why unresolved:** The paper demonstrates the sensitivity but does not investigate why certain calibration sets (like evol-codealpaca-v1) yield better pruning orders. It is unclear if there is an optimal calibration distribution or if sensitivity is inherent to the criterion.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** A controlled study comparing many calibration datasets (domains, sizes, qualities) for a fixed pruning criterion (e.g., REAP), measuring correlation between calibration data distribution and final pruned model performance across tasks. Possibly developing a calibration-invariant importance score.

### Open Question 5
- **Question:** Can the EvoESAP search process (evolutionary with level-switch mutation) be significantly accelerated or replaced by a differentiable optimization to make non-uniform allocation search nearly cost-free?
- **Basis in paper:** [inferred] EvoESAP requires running evolutionary search (e.g., 20–50 generations) with repeated ESAP evaluations, which adds overhead (hours of search time). The paper presents this as acceptable but notes "evolutionary search introduces additional compute overhead during compression."
- **Why unresolved:** The search is a black-box optimization over integer allocations. There may be structure in the ESAP landscape (e.g., convexity, smoothness) that allows gradient-based or dynamic programming solutions. Level-switch mutation is heuristic; a more efficient method could make non-uniform allocation standard.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Reformulating the allocation search as a continuous optimization (e.g., relaxing integer constraints) with a smooth surrogate for ESAP, then benchmarking convergence speed and solution quality against evolutionary search across models and sparsity levels.
