# GDPO: Group reward-Decoupled Normalization Policy Optimization for Multi-reward RL Optimization

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID**: 2601.05242
- **Source URL**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.05242
- **Reference count**: 40
- **Primary result**: Decoupled group-wise normalization preserves distinct reward dimensions that GRPO collapses, improving stability and accuracy across multi-reward RL tasks.

## Executive Summary
The paper addresses a critical flaw in applying Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO) to multi-reward reinforcement learning: normalizing summed rewards causes distinct reward combinations to collapse into identical advantages, degrading training signals and causing suboptimal convergence or failure. GDPO introduces decoupled group-wise normalization: each reward is normalized separately within a group, then summed and batch-normalized to preserve relative differences while maintaining stable scale. Experiments across tool calling, math reasoning, and code generation show GDPO stabilizes training and avoids GRPO's collapse, consistently delivering improvements. For tool-calling with Qwen2.5-1.5B, GDPO reaches 55.4% accuracy vs 50.6% for GRPO and 80.7% format correctness vs 76.3%. On math (DeepSeek-R1-1.5B), AIME accuracy rises from 23.1% to 29.4% while length-exceeding drops from 91.5% to 6.5%. On coding with three rewards, GDPO matches pass rates but reduces bug rates and length violations. GDPO thus offers a more stable, precise, and generalizable multi-reward RL optimization.

## Method Summary
GDPO modifies GRPO by normalizing each reward dimension separately within a group of rollouts before summation, then applying batch-wise normalization to the summed advantages. For G rollouts per question, each raw reward rₖ is normalized as Aₖ = (rₖ - meanₖ)/stdₖ across the G rollouts, then summed: A_sum = Σₖ Aₖ. Finally, batch normalization ensures Â = (A_sum - batch_mean)/batch_std + ε. This preserves distinct reward combinations while stabilizing the training scale independent of reward count. The method also includes conditioned reward shaping to enforce priority alignment between objectives of disparate difficulty.

## Key Results
- Tool calling (Qwen2.5-1.5B): GDPO reaches 55.4% accuracy vs 50.6% for GRPO; format correctness improves to 80.7% vs 76.3%
- Math reasoning (DeepSeek-R1-1.5B): AIME accuracy increases from 23.1% to 29.4%; length-exceeding ratio drops from 91.5% to 6.5%

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: Decoupled group-wise normalization preserves distinctions across reward dimensions that GRPO collapses.
- Mechanism: GRPO aggregates rewards per rollout then normalizes across the group, causing different reward vectors to map to identical advantages. GDPO normalizes each reward separately within the group before summation, retaining fine-grained differences.
- Core assumption: Rewards are not perfectly correlated and distinct reward patterns carry meaningful learning signal.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "decoupling the normalization of individual rewards, more faithfully preserving their relative differences"
  - [section 3.1] Equations (4) show per-reward group normalization: Aᵏ = (rᵏ − meanₖ)/stdₖ, then summed.
  - [section 2] Figure 2 demonstrates that with two binary rewards and two rollouts, GRPO yields only 2 distinct advantage groups while GDPO yields 3.
  - [corpus] Provided neighbors discuss other GRPO variants but do not directly validate decoupling; evidence is internal to the paper.

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: Batch-wise normalization of summed advantages stabilizes the training scale independent of reward count.
- Mechanism: After summing the per-reward normalized advantages per rollout, GDPO applies batch-level mean-std normalization. This prevents the variance of the final advantage from growing linearly with the number of rewards, avoiding gradient instability.
- Core assumption: Batch statistics computed from a diverse sample reliably represent the current distribution of summed advantages.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "batch-wise advantage normalization … maintain a stable numerical range independent of reward count and improve update stability"
  - [section 3.1] "ensures that the numerical scale of the final advantage ^A_sum remains stable and does not grow as additional rewards are included"
  - [appendix A] Figure 8 shows that removing batch normalization leads to occasional convergence failures.

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: Conditioned reward shaping more effectively enforces priority alignment between objectives of disparate difficulty than weight adjustment alone.
- Mechanism: Instead of only scaling reward weights, transform easier reward rₖ to rₖ′ = rₖ · 𝟙(rₗ ≥ threshold). The model only receives rₖ credit after satisfying the prioritized rₗ, forcing attention to harder objectives first.
- Core assumption: There exists a natural difficulty hierarchy; the easier reward can be maximized trivially unless constrained by the harder one.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [section 3.2] Equation (8) defines conditioned reward; text notes it "force[s] the model to always maximize the human-prioritized reward first."
  - [section 4.2.1] Figure 6 and Table 4 compare weight tuning vs. conditioning; conditioned length reward yields more predictable relaxation of length constraint while preserving accuracy gains.
  - [section 4.2.1] Reducing weight alone required extreme values (0.25) to affect behavior, whereas conditioning works reliably across weights.

## Architecture Onboarding

### Component map
Reward Computation Module -> Per-Reward Group Normalizer -> Summation Engine -> Batch Normalizer -> Policy Update

### Critical path
1. For each question in the batch, generate G rollouts using the current policy.
2. For each rollout, compute n raw rewards.
3. For each reward dimension k (1…n):
   - Gather all G values rₖ for the current question.
   - Compute meanₖ and stdₖ.
   - Normalize: Aₖ = (rₖ − meanₖ)/stdₖ for each rollout.
4. For each rollout, compute A_sum = Σₖ Aₖ.
5. Across all rollouts in the batch, compute batch-wide mean and std of A_sum.
6. Final advantage: Â = (A_sum − batch_mean)/batch_std + ε.
7. Compute policy loss with Â.

### Design tradeoffs
- Decoupling overhead: Requires n separate normalizations per group; negligible compared to forward pass cost.
- Batch norm sensitivity: Depends on batch diversity; small batches may need alternative schemes (e.g., running statistics) but may reduce stability.
- Weight placement: Weights wₖ can be applied before per-reward normalization (affects group stats) or after (applied to Aₖ). Paper uses after (Eq. 7) to preserve normalized scale.

### Failure signatures
- Training collapse: Correctness reward degrades after initial improvement (e.g., GRPO in Fig 5).
- Zero learning on a dimension: Format reward never rises (GRPO w/o std in Fig 4) leading to 0% format compliance (Table 2).
- Advantage magnitude explosion: Without batch norm, advantages scale with reward count, causing gradient spikes (Appendix A).

## Limitations
- Decoupling benefit depends on rewards not being perfectly correlated; strong correlations may still collapse distinctions
- Conditioned reward shaping could stall learning if prioritized reward is too difficult early in training
- Absence of ablation on conditioned reward shaping mechanism and reliance on tuned hyperparameters for each task

## Confidence
- **High confidence**: Mathematical formulation of decoupled normalization is explicit and verifiable; synthetic experiments clearly demonstrate advantage-space collapse issue
- **Medium confidence**: Empirical results consistent across tasks and show improvements over GRPO baselines, though lack of ablation and tuned hyperparameters slightly weaken universal applicability
- **Low confidence**: Paper doesn't provide extensive analysis of failure modes under extreme reward correlation or when prioritized reward is too difficult
