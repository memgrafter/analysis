# Explaining the Explainer: Understanding the Inner Workings of Transformer-based Symbolic Regression Models

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2602.03506
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.03506
- **Reference Count:** 40

## Executive Summary
The paper addresses the interpretability gap in transformer-based symbolic regression (SR) models, which produce human-readable equations but lack transparency in how they generate operators. The authors introduce PATCHES, a circuit discovery framework that optimizes sparse masks over model components using CMA-ES to isolate subgraphs responsible for specific symbolic behaviors (e.g., sin, add). Circuits are evaluated using three formal criteria—faithfulness, completeness, and minimality—via functional accuracy and logit-based metrics. Applied to the NeSymReS model, PATCHES isolates 28 circuits, with 13 satisfying all criteria under mean patching and functional evaluation. Mean patching is shown to produce smaller, more faithful circuits compared to resample patching. In contrast, direct logit attribution and probing classifiers only capture correlational features and are unsuitable for causal circuit discovery. This work establishes symbolic regression as a promising domain for mechanistic interpretability and provides a principled methodology for circuit identification.

## Method Summary
PATCHES is a circuit discovery framework that uses CMA-ES to optimize binary masks over model components (layers, heads, neurons). The masks define a subgraph (circuit) that is intended to implement a specific behavior. The optimization preserves the model’s output on a task-specific dataset while encouraging sparsity. Isolated circuits are evaluated on three criteria: faithfulness (does the circuit reproduce the full model’s behavior on all relevant inputs?), completeness (does the circuit contain all necessary components?), and minimality (is the circuit as small as possible?). Two patching strategies are compared: mean patching (averaging activations across many inputs) and resample patching (sampling single inputs). The framework is applied to NeSymReS, a transformer-based symbolic regression model, to isolate circuits for basic operators.

## Key Results
- PATCHES isolates 28 circuits in NeSymReS; 13 meet all three correctness criteria (faithfulness, completeness, minimality) under mean patching and functional evaluation.
- Mean patching yields circuits that are significantly smaller and more faithful than those obtained with resample patching.
- Direct logit attribution and probing classifiers fail to identify causal circuits, as they only capture correlational features.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
PATCHES succeeds because CMA-ES efficiently searches the combinatorial space of sparse masks, balancing optimization of behavior preservation with mask simplicity. Mean patching improves circuit quality by averaging out noise from individual input activations, resulting in more stable and minimal subgraphs. The three correctness criteria ensure that identified circuits are not only faithful and complete but also parsimonious, making them suitable for mechanistic analysis. In contrast, attribution methods that look at single-input effects are confounded by correlated features and do not establish causality.

- **Transformer Models:** Neural architecture based on self-attention; needed as the model class under study. *Quick check:* Does the model use multi-head attention to process sequences?
- **Circuit Discovery:** The process of identifying subgraphs that implement specific functions; core methodology. *Quick check:* Does isolating the candidate circuit preserve the targeted behavior?
- **Faithfulness:** Criterion that the circuit reproduces the full model’s behavior on all relevant inputs; needed to ensure the circuit is behaviorally equivalent. *Quick check:* Is the circuit’s output identical to the full model’s across a held-out set?
- **Completeness:** Criterion that the circuit contains all necessary components; needed to avoid missing critical parts. *Quick check:* Does removing any component from the circuit degrade performance?
- **Minimality:** Criterion that no smaller subgraph suffices; needed for interpretability. *Quick check:* Can a proper sub-circuit still perform the behavior?

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component Map:**  
Input Data (points) -> Embedding Layer -> Transformer Encoder (stack of identical layers) -> Transformer Decoder (stack of identical layers) -> Output Head (linear projection + softmax) -> Equation Token Sequence.  

**Critical Path:**  
The information flow from raw data to generated equation: data points are encoded into contextual representations; the decoder autoregressively generates tokens, attending to the encoder output and previously generated tokens.

**Design Tradeoffs:**  
NeSymReS uses an encoder-decoder transformer, which balances flexibility in handling variable-length data with model complexity. Deeper/wider models increase expressive power but may obscure circuits through distributed representations. The choice of transformer size directly impacts the difficulty of circuit discovery.

**Failure Signatures:**  
- A circuit fails faithfulness if its outputs diverge from the full model on unseen inputs.
- Incompleteness is indicated if any masked component can be removed without performance drop.
- Non-minimality is present if a proper sub-circuit also satisfies faithfulness and completeness.
- Instability across patching methods (mean vs resample) suggests unreliable circuit identification.

**First Three Experiments:**

2. Compare circuit sizes (number of masked components) between mean patching and resample patching across multiple operators.
3. Ablate components outside the identified circuit and measure the change in output to confirm causal necessity.

## Limitations
- Generalizability: Findings are based solely on NeSymReS; it is unclear if PATCHES works for other SR architectures or standard LLMs.
- Sufficiency of criteria: Faithfulness, completeness, and minimality are necessary but may not guarantee full human-interpretable mechanistic understanding.
- Domain specificity: Symbolic regression’s discrete, compositional outputs may facilitate circuit discovery compared to continuous or less-structured domains.

## Confidence
- PATCHES isolates causal circuits in NeSymReS: High
- SR is a promising domain for mechanistic interpretability: Medium
- Mean patching yields superior circuits vs. resample patching: High
- Direct logit attribution/probing are unsuitable for causal discovery: Medium
