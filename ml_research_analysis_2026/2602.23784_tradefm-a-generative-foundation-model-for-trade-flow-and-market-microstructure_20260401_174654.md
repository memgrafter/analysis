# TradeFM: A Generative Foundation Model for Trade-flow and Market Microstructure

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2602.23784  
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.23784  
- **Reference count:** 40  
- **Primary result:** A 524M-parameter generative Transformer for market microstructure that achieves 2–3× lower distributional error than Compound Hawkes baselines and generalizes zero-shot to out-of-distribution APAC markets.

## Executive Summary
TradeFM introduces a generative foundation model for market microstructure, trained on over 10 billion trade events from 9,000+ US equities. It addresses cross-asset generalization through scale-invariant features and a universal tokenization scheme that converts heterogeneous order flow into a unified discrete sequence without asset-specific calibration. Integrated with a deterministic simulator, the model generates order rollouts that reproduce key stylized facts of financial returns—heavy tails, volatility clustering, and lack of return autocorrelation. Quantitatively, TradeFM significantly outperforms Compound Hawkes baselines in distributional fidelity (2–3× lower Kolmogorov-Smirnov and Wasserstein errors) and demonstrates robust zero-shot generalization to APAC markets with only moderate perplexity degradation.

## Method Summary
The method transforms raw trade messages into scale-invariant features (relative price depth, log volume, normalized interarrival time) and discretizes them via a mixed-base encoding into a single token per event, supplemented by contextual indicators (liquidity bin, price change bin, market/participant flag). This yields a univariate sequence for autoregressive training. The model is a decoder-only Transformer (524M parameters, Llama-style, grouped-query attention, rotary embeddings) trained with next-token prediction on 10.7B tokens. A deterministic limit order book simulator enforces price-time priority; seeded with real events, the model generates subsequent tokens recursively, producing simulated mid-price series. Evaluation compares stylized facts and distributional distances (Kolmogorov-Smirnov, Wasserstein) against real data and baselines.

## Key Results
- Simulated returns exhibit heavy tails, volatility clustering, and negligible autocorrelation, matching canonical stylized facts.
- Distributional error (Kolmogorov-Smirnov, Wasserstein) on order flow features is 2–3× lower than Compound Hawkes baselines.
- Zero-shot generalization to APAC markets shows only moderate perplexity increase, demonstrating cross-asset transferability.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1: Scale-Invariant Features Enable Universal Representations
Scale-invariant features (e.g., relative price depth, log volume) yield stationary distributions across assets and liquidity regimes, allowing a single model to learn transferable dynamics. Raw features vary widely due to price level, liquidity, and tick size; normalization removes asset-specific noise while preserving universal principles of price formation. Evidence: Abstract and Section 5.3 describe feature construction; Figure 7 shows stable distributions across liquidity tiers. Break condition: Non-scale-invariant effects (e.g., tick-size constraints, regulatory limits) may degrade zero-shot performance.

### Mechanism 2: Universal Tokenization via Mixed-Base Encoding
A composite tokenization maps multi-feature events into a single integer token, enabling standard autoregressive training while preserving multimodal information. Continuous features are binned (equal-frequency for price, equal-width for log features); bin indices become digits in a mixed-base number system, producing a vocabulary of 16,384. Contextual features are separate inputs. Core assumption: Binning captures essential variations without excessive loss; interdependencies are learnable via attention. Evidence: Section 6.2 and Figure 2 detail token composition and bin calibration; Figure 6 shows controllability via context tokens. Break condition: Too coarse binning may merge rare but important tail events, harming tail modeling.

### Mechanism 3: Closed-Loop Simulation Reveals Emergent Stylized Facts
Integrating TradeFM with a deterministic simulator creates a closed-loop system where generated order flow reproduces stylized facts, validating capture of endogenous dynamics. The simulator maintains a limit order book with price-time priority; at each step, TradeFM predicts the next token, the simulator executes and updates the mid-price, and new context is fed back. Recursive interaction allows second-order effects (e.g., price impact) to emerge. Evidence: Section 8 describes architecture; Section 9.1 and Table 2 show stylized facts and distributional superiority; Appendix D.2 validates simulator fill statistics. Break condition: Simulator oversimplification (no hidden orders, latency) or model generating locally plausible but inconsistent long-term dynamics may cause divergence.

## Architecture Onboarding

### Component Map
Raw trade messages → Scale-invariant feature extraction → Binning → Composite token (itrade) + context tokens (liquidity, price change, market/participant) → Decoder-only Transformer → Next token prediction → Deterministic limit order book simulator (price-time priority) → Mid-price update → New context → (loop). Evaluation: Simulated returns → Stylized facts & distributional metrics.

### Critical Path
1. Preprocess data: Compute EW-VWAP, derive relative features, calibrate bin edges on first 30 days, bin all features, create sequences with context.
2. Train model: Next-token prediction on 10.7B tokens, 4 epochs, AdamW, fp16 on 3×A100.
3. Validate simulator: Replay real orders through simulator, compare fill volume and lot count CDFs to real data (target correlation >0.9).
4. Closed-loop generation: For held-out asset/month, use 1,024 real events as seed, generate 1,024 events recursively, record mid-price.
5. Evaluate: Aggregate rollouts; compute return autocorrelation, absolute return autocorrelation, kurtosis decay, and distributional distances (KS, Wasserstein) for order flow features.

### Design Tradeoffs
- Partial observability vs. Simplicity: Uses trade messages (Level 3) instead of full LOB snapshots, reducing dimensionality and matching participant view but missing latent liquidity.
- Fixed tokenization vs. Adaptive encoding: Universal bin edges enable cross-asset training but may not optimally capture extreme values in new regimes; outlier bins partially mitigate.
- Deterministic simulator vs. Stochastic realism: Fixed rules (price-time priority) ensure transparency and speed, but ignore exogenous noise, latency, and stochastic order flow—acceptable for relative evaluation.
- Model size vs. Compute: 524M parameters follows Chinchilla scaling for 10B tokens; larger models need more compute/data, smaller may lose capacity.

### Failure Signatures
- Perplexity: High (>200?) on held-out US assets, especially low-liquidity, indicates poor learning.
- Stylized facts: Simulated returns show significant autocorrelation or no volatility clustering.
- Distributional fidelity: Wasserstein/K-S distances for volumes, interarrival times, or spreads exceed Compound Hawkes baseline.
- Simulator crashes: Orders with invalid price/volume cause LOB errors—indicates decoding or action mapping bugs.
- Controllability: Varying liquidity or market/participant tokens does not change variance of generated volumes/interarrival times.

### First 3 Experiments
1. **Tokenization sanity**: Take 1,000 real events, apply binning, verify token indices in [0,16383] and reconstructed bins match original; check bin occupancy distribution for uniformity.
2. **Held-out perplexity**: Compute average perplexity on a held-out month of US equities across liquidity tiers; should be significantly below uniform baseline and stable across tiers.
3. **Basic stylized fact**: For a high-liquidity stock (e.g., AAPL), generate 100 rollouts of 1,024 events from a real seed. Compute autocorrelation of returns up to lag 50 and kurtosis over Δt = 10, 30, 60 sec. Compare to real data: expect near-zero autocorrelation and kurtosis decay toward normality.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
1. To what extent does the deterministic simulator's design influence the reproduction of stylized facts, versus TradeFM's intrinsic generative quality? The paper notes full disentanglement remains future work.
2. What specific market microstructure differences (e.g., Itayose auctions, price limits) cause the moderate perplexity degradation in zero-shot APAC generalization, and can scale-invariant features be adapted?
3. Can TradeFM's implicit spread dynamics be enhanced to match the explicit Compound Hawkes process performance on spread metrics, without degrading other fidelity measures?
4. How robust is the fixed binning tokenization to extreme, unprecedented market regimes (e.g., flash crashes, COVID volatility), where feature distributions may shift beyond calibration?

## Limitations
- Proprietary data specifics (asset selection, corporate action handling, message filtering) are undisclosed, impairing reproducibility.
- Simulator oversimplification: deterministic price-time priority without latency or stochastic order flow may miss key market dynamics.
- Fixed binning strategy: equal-frequency/width discretization risks losing tail events; outlier bins are a partial fix but may not handle regime shifts.

## Confidence
