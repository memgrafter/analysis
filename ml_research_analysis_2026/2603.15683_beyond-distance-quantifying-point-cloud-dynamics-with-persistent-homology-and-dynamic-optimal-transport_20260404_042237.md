# Beyond Distance: Quantifying Point Cloud Dynamics with Persistent Homology and Dynamic Optimal Transport
## Quick Facts
*   **arXiv ID:** 2603.15683
*   **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.15683
*   **Reference count:** 40
*   **Primary result:** Proposes a reconstruction-based framework combining TpOT, persistent homology, and hypergraph entropy to capture transient, localized topological reorganizations in time-varying point clouds, validated on synthetic bifurcations and stroke fMRI.

## Executive Summary
The scalar Topological Optimal Transport (TpOT) distance, while useful for comparing time-varying point clouds, can mask transient and localized topological reorganizations. This paper introduces a framework that constructs a geodesic interpolation between frames by reconstructing physically plausible intermediate geometries from the TpOT coupling (via MDS) and then recomputing persistent homology on each. This yields a continuous trajectory of hypergraphs representing the evolution of topological networks. Multi-scale entropy indicators—macroscopic persistence entropy, mesoscopic dual-perspective hypergraph entropy (vertex/edge), and a propagated point-level entropy field—are introduced to quantify dynamic distortion and local rewiring. Validation on synthetic bifurcations (Rayleigh-Van der Pol, double-well), a biological aggregation model (D’Orsogna), and longitudinal stroke fMRI demonstrates the approach's ability to recover tipping points from sparse observations and provide a hierarchical view of topological dynamics, including region-specific functional reorganization in the brain.

## Method Summary
The pipeline processes a time series of point clouds. For each consecutive pair, it first solves an entropic TpOT problem to obtain optimal spatial ($\Pi_v^*$) and topological ($\Pi_e^*$) couplings. Using $\Pi_v^*$, it constructs a kernel $k_t = (1-t)k + t k'$ over matched point pairs and applies Multidimensional Scaling (MDS) to recover intermediate coordinates $X_t$. Persistent homology (1D) is then recomputed on each $X_t$ to extract topological cycles, which are encoded as hyperedges in a measure topological network via an incidence matrix. To evaluate dynamics, TpOT is solved between the reference network at $t=0$ and each interpolated network at $t$, yielding geometric, topological, and hypergraph distortion curves. Entropy metrics are derived: Persistence Entropy (PE) from barcode lengths; vertex-perspective ($HE_V$) and edge-perspective ($HE_E$) hypergraph entropy from incidence degree/size distributions; and a symmetric combination $HE_{sym}$. Finally, cycle-level entropy changes are aligned via $\Pi_e^*$ and propagated through the incidence matrix to generate a point-level entropy field highlighting epicenters of change.

## Key Results
*   Distortion curves ($L_{\text{geom}}$, $L_{\text{topo}}$, $L_{\text{hyper}}$) reveal a hierarchical relaxation process across macro-, meso-, and microscopic scales.
*   Entropy spikes or drops precisely locate phase transitions in synthetic bifurcations and anticipate vortex formation in the D’Orsogna model.
*   The point-level entropy field identifies critical vertices; in stroke fMRI, decoupled vertex- and edge-entropies expose region-specific functional reorganization not captured by global metrics.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1: Geometry-Guided Interpolation
*   **Claim:** Interpolating the underlying spatial geometry via MDS from the TpOT spatial coupling $\Pi_v^*$ and recomputing persistent homology ensures intermediate point clouds represent physically plausible states, avoiding artifacts from directly interpolating abstract topological parameters.
*   **Mechanism:** $\Pi_v^*$ defines a kernel over matched pairs; MDS embeds this kernel to recover $X_t$. PH on $X_t$ guarantees extracted cycles correspond to actual loops in the interpolated geometry.
*   **Core assumption:** Valid intermediate topological structures are determined by the geometry of the point cloud; algebraic blending of barcodes can yield unrealizable structures.
*   **Evidence anchors:** [section 2.3]: "Instead of directly interpolating abstract network parameters, our method interpolates the underlying spatial geometry and rigorously re-compute the valid topological structures."; [corpus]: Unique focus on coupling transport with dynamic reconstruction.
*   **Break condition:** Fails for highly nonlinear manifolds or discrete topological changes (e.g., merging clusters) where MDS from blended kernels cannot generate $X_t$ with target PH.

### Mechanism 2: Dual-Perspective Hypergraph Entropy
*   **Claim:** Vertex-perspective ($HE_V$) and edge-perspective ($HE_E$) hypergraph entropy detect asynchronous local topological rewiring that global TpOT distance or persistence entropy averages out.
*   **Mechanism:** Entropy computed from normalized vertex degrees and hyperedge sizes in the cycle hypergraph. Abrupt addition/removal of a cycle changes the incidence distribution, forcing a non-zero shift in the probability simplex (Theorem 1).
*   **Core assumption:** Hypergraph incidence structure encodes higher-order connectivity; uniformity/sharp changes reflect system-level rewiring.
*   **Evidence anchors:** [section 2.4]: Definitions 1–2, Theorem 1 proves sensitivity except for measure-zero cases; [section 3.3]: In D’Orsogna, $HE_{sym}$ drops earlier than PE, anticipating vortex formation.
*   **Break condition:** Requires sufficient active vertices/hyperedges; extremely sparse topologies yield degenerate distributions, reducing discriminative power.

### Mechanism 3: Point-Level Entropy Propagation
*   **Claim:** Propagating cycle-level entropy differences onto vertices via the incidence matrix produces a field that localizes where topological changes occur.
*   **Mechanism:** Align reference and target hypergraphs using optimal TpOT correspondence. For matched cycles, compute entropy difference $\Delta H_j$. Multiply by normalized vertex memberships in target cycles and sum: $s_i = \sum_j \hat{P}[i,j] |\Delta H_j|$.
*   **Core assumption:** Vertices with high membership in cycles undergoing large entropy shifts are epicenters of reorganization.
*   **Evidence anchors:** [section 2.5]: Equations (18)–(20) define propagation; applied to stroke fMRI in Figure 14; [abstract]: "The point-level entropy field identifies critical vertices."
*   **Break condition:** Relies on accurate cycle matching via $\Pi_e^*$; diffuse or noisy couplings smear entropy changes across unrelated vertices.

## Foundational Learning
*   **Optimal Transport & TpOT**
    *   **Why needed:** Provides the optimal coupling $\Pi_v^*$ that guides geometric interpolation and defines the geodesic. Understanding entropic regularization and Sinkhorn updates is essential.
    *   **Quick check:** How does the trade-off parameter $\beta$ in TpOT balance geometry vs. topology in the coupling?
*   **Persistent Homology & Barcodes**
    *   **Why needed:** Generates topological features (cycles) that form hypergraph hyperedges. Must understand birth/death pairs, dimension-wise computation, and stability.
    *   **Quick check:** Why might 1D homology be chosen for analyzing loops in point cloud dynamics?
*   **Hypergraph Incidence Representation**
    *   **Why needed:** Encodes which points belong to which topological cycles. Entropy metrics operate on vertex degrees and hyperedge sizes derived from this matrix.
    *   **Quick check:** How does regularizing a hypergraph (constant vertex degree) affect $HE_V$?

## Architecture Onboarding
*   **Component map:** Input point clouds $\{X^{(i)}\}$ -> TpOT solver (computes $\Pi_v^*, \Pi_e^*$) -> Geometric interpolation (kernel $k_t$ from $\Pi_v^*$, MDS $\rightarrow X_t$) -> Topological reconstruction (PH on $X_t$ $\rightarrow$ cycles $Y_t$, incidence $\Omega^{(t)}$) -> Hypergraph construction ($P_t$) -> Dynamic evaluation (TpOT($P_0, P_t$) $\rightarrow$ distortion curves) -> Entropy computation (PE, $HE_V$, $HE_E$) -> Point-level field (propagation via $\hat{P}$).
*   **Critical path:** $\Pi_v^*$ -> MDS -> PH -> $\Omega$ -> entropy.
*   **Design tradeoffs:** Reconstruction vs. linear interpolation (validity vs. cost); entropy perspective choice ($HE_V$ vs. $HE_E$ vs. $HE_{sym}$); PH dimension selection (requires problem-specific choice, e.g., 1D for loops).
*   **Failure signatures:** Hypergraph instability (few/no cycles $\rightarrow$ entropy undefined); TpOT over-diffusion (excessive $\epsilon$ masks events in distortion curves); MDS non-PSD kernels (blended $k_t$ not PSD $\rightarrow$ invalid $X_t$).
*   **First 3 experiments:** Rayleigh-Van der Pol (validates bifurcation detection), Double-Well (negative control for 1D specificity), D’Orsogna (tests high-dimensional self-organization and robustness to sparsity).

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
1.  **Scalability:** How can TpOT and PH be scaled to whole-brain voxel-level fMRI without parcellation? Current approach uses Yeo7 (~600 points); whole-brain involves ~10⁵–10⁶ points, making computation intractable.
2.  **Biomarker potential:** Can hypergraph entropy trajectories ($HE_V$, $HE_E$) serve as robust predictive biomarkers for stroke recovery outcomes? Current evidence is single-subject; generalization across patients and correlation with clinical scales unknown.
3.  **Reconstruction failure modes:** Under what geometric/topological conditions does the MDS+PH interpolation fail to recover the true TpOT geodesic? No theoretical guarantees or a posteriori diagnostics are provided.
4.  **Comparative sensitivity:** Do $HE_V$, $HE_E$ offer superior sensitivity to transient reorganizations compared to standard persistence entropy or diagram Wasserstein distance? Lacks systematic benchmarking against other dynamic TDA metrics.

## Limitations
*   **PH hyperparameters unspecified:** Max bar count and persistence cutoff (critical for hypergraph size and entropy sensitivity) are not detailed for synthetic experiments, hindering reproduction.
*   **MDS alignment ambiguity:** The rigid transformation step (Procrustes? Kabsch? PCA?) is not specified; choice may affect intermediate geometry and downstream metrics.
*   **MDS interpolation failure risk:** For highly nonlinear topology changes or discrete events (e.g., cluster merging), blended kernels may yield $X_t$ with incorrect PH, causing fidelity loss.

## Confidence
*   Geodesic interpolation validity: **Medium** (sound theory, but only smooth synthetic trajectories tested; abrupt changes untested).
*   Dual-perspective hypergraph entropy sensitivity: **High** with sufficient active cycles (supported by Theorem 1 and D’Orsogna results); **Medium** in sparse regimes.
*   Point-level entropy field: **Medium** (fMRI results plausible but lack quantitative ground truth).
*   Overall multiscale tipping detection: **Medium‑High** (consistent across validations but needs broader testing).

## Next Checks
1.  **PH parameter sensitivity:** Vary persistence cutoff and max bar count on synthetic models; assess stability of entropy spikes and distortion curves to define robust parameter ranges.
2.  **Alignment ablation:** Substitute Procresutes, Kabsch, and PCA alignment in the MDS step; compare downstream metrics to ensure invariance to alignment choice.
3.  **Cross-dataset generalization:** Apply the framework to additional public time-series point cloud datasets (e.g., molecular dynamics, crowd movement) and benchmark against standard change-point detectors to assess broad applicability.
