# AGMark: Attention-Guided Dynamic Watermarking for Large Vision-Language Models
## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2602.09611
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.09611
- Reference count: 40
- Primary result: AGMark achieves at least 99.36% detection AUC and 88.61% robust attack AUC, while improving visual semantic fidelity (+1.7% CHAIR) and text quality (+4.96 BLEU on Llava-Next) compared to baselines, with minimal inference overhead. It particularly maintains performance over longer generation sequences.

## Executive Summary
AGMark addresses key limitations in watermarking large vision-language models (LVLMs). Prior methods either ignore visual semantics, leading to irrelevant watermarked tokens, or rely on static one-time visual dependence estimation, which fails to adapt during generation and degrades visual fidelity. AGMark introduces a two-stage framework: it dynamically identifies semantic-critical tokens at each decoding step by fusing attention-guided vision-critical weights with context-critical weights, and adaptively determines the protected token proportion using token entropy (uncertainty awareness) and weight density (evidence calibration). This alignment preserves both watermarked detection and generation quality.

Extensive evaluation shows AGMark attains ≥99.36% detection AUC and ≥88.61% robust attack AUC, improves visual semantic fidelity (+1.7% CHAIR) and text quality (+4.96 BLEU on Llava-Next), incurs minimal overhead, and remains robust over long sequences.

## Method Summary
AGMark operates during the autoregressive generation of an LVLM. At each decoding step *t*, it first extracts semantic-critical weights for all vocabulary tokens (Stage A). Vision-critical weights are derived from the model's attention distribution over visual tokens, while context-critical weights come from the current hidden state. These are standardized, fused via a tunable convex weight, and min-max normalized to produce a unified score `ψ_t(k)`. The vocabulary is then sorted by this score to obtain `V*_t`.

In Stage B, the method adaptively determines the proportion of tokens to protect. It computes the normalized entropy of the next-token distribution as an uncertainty measure, and the weight density `ρ_t` as the minimal fraction of top-ranked tokens needed to exceed a cumulative threshold `τ`. The protected ratio `η_t = α * ρ_t * (1 - H_norm_t)` yields the size of the critical token set `C_t`. The initial green list (from a secret hash) and red list are then adjusted: tokens in `C_t ∩ R_t` are swapped into the green list, replacing the least-critical tokens from the original green list. Finally, a fixed bias `δ` is added to the logits of tokens in the updated green list before sampling. This per-step adaptation ensures that watermark injection aligns with the model's evolving visual grounding and contextual coherence.

## Key Results
- Detection: ≥99.36% AUC (unattacked) and ≥88.61% AUC under robust attacks.
- Visual fidelity: +1.7% improvement in CHAIR (lower hallucination).
- Text quality: +4.96 BLEU on Llava-Next; lower perplexity and higher BertScore compared to baselines; minimal inference overhead; robust performance over long generations.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1
- **Claim:** Dynamically extracting semantic-critical token weights at each decoding step via fused attention-guided vision relevance and contextual coherence prevents watermark injection from disrupting visual grounding and narrative flow.
- **Mechanism:** At step *t*, compute vision-critical weights using attention over visual tokens (Equation 3) and context-critical weights using the current hidden state (Equation 4). Standardize and convex-combine them (Equation 6) into a unified, min-max normalized score (Equation 7) that ranks tokens by their estimated importance to the current multimodal context.
- **Core assumption:** The model’s focus on visual evidence (via attention) and discourse coherence (via hidden state) provides a real-time signal for which tokens are semantically critical to preserve, and this signal changes dynamically across generation steps.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract] "dynamically identifies semantic-critical tokens at each decoding step by fusing attention-guided vision-critical weights with context-critical weights"
  - [section 3.1] Details the computation of `Av_t`, `ψ^v_t(k)`, `ψ^c_t(k)`, and their fusion into `ψ_t(k)`
  - [corpus] VLA-Mark [21] is cited as using "static, one-time estimation," establishing the contrast with AGMark's dynamic approach.
- **Break condition:** If attention weights over visual regions become uniformly diffuse (low signal) or the contextual hidden state loses coherence (e.g., in very long, degenerate generations), the fused weight `ψ_t(k)` fails to reliably indicate truly critical tokens, leading to misprioritization.

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim:** Adaptively determining the proportion of tokens to protect (green-list size) using token entropy (uncertainty awareness) and weight density (evidence calibration) prevents low-quality or irrelevant tokens from being forced into the protected set, balancing detectability and fidelity.
- **Mechanism:** Calculate normalized entropy `H_norm_t` (Equation 10) from the next-token distribution. Characterize weight distribution density `ρ_t` as the minimal fraction of top-weighted tokens needed to exceed cumulative threshold `τ` (Equations 11-12). Compute protected token ratio `η_t = α * ρ_t * (1 - H_norm_t)` (Equation 13), then swap high-weight tokens from the red list into the green list up to size `⌈η_t |V|⌉` (Equation 14).
- **Core assumption:** Low entropy (high model confidence) and dispersed high-weight evidence (high `ρ_t`) indicate a generation step where protecting a larger, well-supported subset of semantic-critical tokens is both safe (quality-preserving) and effective (detectable). High entropy or overly concentrated weights signal caution.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract] "adaptively determines the protected token proportion using token entropy (uncertainty awareness) and evidence calibration (weight density)"
  - [section 3.2] Defines `H_norm_t`, `ρ_t`, and `η_t`, and the swapping operation (Equation 15).
  - [corpus] MorphMark [29] is noted for using "probability of green tokens" without density calibration; AGMark's ablation table shows removing entropy or density degrades performance.
- **Break condition:** If the entropy calculation is noisy (e.g., due to model softmax temperature quirks) or if the weight density threshold `τ` is poorly matched to the typical distribution shape, `η_t` will be miscalibrated—either too large (injecting watermark into low-evidence tokens, harming quality) or too small (reducing detectable signal).

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim:** The end-to-end dynamic, adaptive framework aligns watermark injection with the model’s evolving visual-semantic needs, particularly in later generation stages, thereby improving visual fidelity (lower hallucination) and text quality without sacrificing detectability.
- **Mechanism:** By recomputing critical weights and the protected proportion *at every step*, the watermark strength is concentrated on tokens that are both visually grounded *and* contextually appropriate *at that moment*, especially when the model is confident and evidence is well-distributed. This avoids the "long-tail" problem of static methods that protect irrelevant tokens once and for all.
- **Core assumption:** Visual dependence and semantic-critical evidence are not static properties of a prompt but evolve during autoregressive generation; watermarking must adapt to this dynamics to avoid corrupting later-stage coherence and visual alignment.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract] "fails to account for dynamic changes in visual dependence during generation... AGMark... particularly maintains performance over longer generation sequences."
  - [section 5.3] Line charts show AGMark's metrics (Perplexity, BertScore, CHAIR) degrade much more gracefully with increasing token length compared to baselines.
  - [corpus] VLA-Mark [21] is the primary recent LVLM watermarking baseline criticized for "static, one-time estimation."
- **Break condition:** If the generation context drifts significantly (e.g., the model starts hallucinating objects), the attention weights may lock onto spurious visual features, causing the dynamic mechanism to protect hallucinated content. The system assumes the base LVLM's generation remains reasonably grounded.

## Foundational Learning
### Concept: Attention Mechanism in LVLMs
- **Why needed here:** AGMark’s vision-critical weight (`ψ^v_t`) is directly derived from the model’s attention distribution over visual tokens (`A^v_t`). Understanding that attention reflects the model’s *current* focus on image regions is key to trusting its use as a real-time semantic guide.
- **Quick check question:** If a token like "ocean" has high attention weight at step *t*, what does that imply about the model’s visual grounding at that moment?

### Concept: Token Entropy as Uncertainty Metric
- **Why needed here:** Entropy (`H_t`) quantifies the spread of the next-token probability distribution. AGMark uses normalized entropy to decide *how many* tokens to protect—low entropy (peaked distribution) means the model is confident, so protecting more semantic-critical tokens is less likely to harm quality.
- **Quick check question:** Why would you reduce watermark injection strength (via `η_t`) when token entropy is high?

### Concept: Logits-based Watermarking (Green-Red List)
- **Why needed here:** AGMark operates within the logits-based paradigm (like KGW). It modifies the standard static green/red list partition by *adaptively swapping* tokens based on its dynamic weights. The core idea—biasing logits of a secret subset—must be understood before seeing how AGMark makes that subset smarter.
- **Quick check question:** What is the fundamental trade-off that all logits-based watermarking methods (including AGMark) must manage?

## Architecture Onboarding
Component map:
1. **Base LVLM** (e.g., Llava-Next): Vision Encoder → Adapter → LLM decoder. Standard forward pass produces `l_t`, `p_t`, `A_t`, and last-layer hidden state `E_h`.
2. **AGMark Stage A (Weight Extraction):** Takes `A_t` (vision slice), `E_h`, and vocabulary embeddings `E_t(k)` to compute `ψ_t(k)` for all *k* in *V*. Outputs sorted vocabulary `V*_t`.
3. **AGMark Stage B (Adaptive Partition):** Takes `p_t` (for entropy) and `V*_t` with `ψ_t(k)` values. Computes `ρ_t` (density), `H_norm_t` (entropy), then `η_t`. Swaps tokens between initial green/red lists (from hash key) to form final `G_t`, `R_t`.
4. **Watermarked Sampling:** Modified logits `l_t(k) + δ` if *k* ∈ `G_t`, then sample from `p̂_t`.

Critical path: Input image + text prompt → [Base LVLM forward to step *t*] → **Stage A** (uses `A_t`, `E_h`) → **Stage B** (uses `p_t`, `V*_t`) → modified `G_t/R_t` → bias logits → sample token *y_t* → repeat.

Design tradeoffs:
- **Detection vs. Quality:** Increasing `α` or `τ` improves quality (fewer low-evidence tokens protected) but may reduce detectable signal (fewer green tokens biased). Table 2 shows this trade-off.
- **Vision vs. Context:** Fusion weight `ω` balances attention-guided vision salience vs. context coherence. Ablation (Table 3) shows removing either hurts; extreme `ω` values degrade both quality and detection.
- **Adaptivity vs. Stability:** The per-step swap cap (mentioned after Equation 15) prevents oscillating green/red lists but limits agility. Too aggressive swapping harms detection statistics.

Failure signatures:
- **High CHAIR / low BertScore:** Visual hallucinations or semantic misalignment. Likely causes: `ω` too low (context overpowers vision), or `η_t` too small (insufficient protection of vision-critical tokens).
- **Low AUC / Accuracy:** Watermark undetectable. Likely causes: `α` or `τ` too high (too few tokens effectively biased), or `ω` extreme (green list misaligned with model's natural preferences).
- **High Perplexity:** Fluency degradation. Often accompanies high CHAIR; points to overly aggressive swapping (`η_t` too large) introducing unnatural tokens.

First 3 experiments:
1. **Ablation Study (Table 3):** Run full AGMark vs. `w/o Vision` (`ω=0`), `w/o Context` (`ω=1`), `w/o Entropy`, `w/o Density`, `FixedScale`. Expect: Full model best; removing any component degrades quality/detection; `w/o Vision` hurts visual fidelity most (↑CHAIR); `w/o Entropy` hurts text quality (↑PPL).
2. **Hyperparameter Sensitivity (Table 2):** Systematically vary `ω` (0.3-0.6), `α` (0.23-0.29), `τ` (0.90-0.99). Expect: `ω` non-monotonic; `α`/`τ` show clear quality/detection trade-off; optimal balance around `ω=0.5`, `α=0.27`, `τ=0.98`.
3. **Length-wise Analysis (Figure 4):** Generate outputs of varying lengths (60-200 tokens) and plot Perplexity, BertScore, CHAIR. Expect: All baselines degrade with length; AGMark's curves are flattest, showing robustness in later stages due to dynamic adaptation.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
### Open Question 1
- **Question:** How reliable are attention weights over visual tokens as a proxy for visual semantic relevance in LVLMs, and do they consistently align with human-perceived visual grounding across diverse inputs?
- **Basis in paper:** [inferred] The paper uses attention weights to compute vision critical weights (Equation 3), but acknowledges that relying solely on visual similarity can misidentify tokens (Section 5.4), indicating potential unreliability.
- **Why unresolved:** The paper does not validate the correlation between attention weights and true visual relevance, leaving open the risk of selecting incorrect tokens for watermarking.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** A large-scale study measuring the agreement between attention-based weights and human annotations or alternative grounding methods (e.g., object detectors) across multiple LVLMs and datasets.

### Open Question 2
- **Question:** Can the hyperparameters controlling AGMark's adaptive vocabulary partitioning (𝛼, 𝜏, 𝜔) be automatically determined per generation context to eliminate manual tuning while maintaining the detectability-quality trade-off?
- **Basis in paper:** [explicit] The paper shows that these hyperparameters significantly affect performance (Table 2, Section 5.4) and require careful tuning to balance detection and generation quality.
- **Why unresolved:** The current approach uses fixed hyperparameters, which may not generalize to different models, tasks, or input variability, limiting practical deployment.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Development of a learning-based or heuristic controller that predicts optimal parameters on-the-fly, validated by cross-model and cross-dataset experiments showing consistent performance without manual adjustment.

### Open Question 3
- **Question:** What is the computational cost of the detection phase for AGMark compared to static watermarking methods, and how can detection be made efficient without requiring full model inference for each verification?
- **Basis in paper:** [inferred] The paper measures generation latency (Table 4) but does not discuss detection complexity. Since AGMark's green list is dynamically computed using model attention and logits, detection may need to replicate these computations, potentially incurring high overhead.
- **Why unresolved:** Without an efficient detection mechanism, the watermark's practicality for large-scale verification is questionable.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Analysis of detection requirements and design of an optimized detection algorithm (e.g., using cached features or approximate weights), with empirical benchmarks of detection speed and accuracy.

### Open Question 4
- **Question:** Does AGMark's design generalize to other LVLM tasks beyond image captioning, such as visual question answering or multimodal reasoning, where the generation pattern and visual dependence differ?
- **Basis in paper:** [inferred] The experimental evaluation is restricted to captioning datasets (AMBER and MS-COCO). The dynamic weighting strategy might behave differently in tasks with interactive or shorter responses.
- **Why unresolved:** The paper does not evaluate on diverse tasks, so it is unclear whether the observed improvements in visual fidelity and detection hold universally.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Comprehensive experiments on a suite of LVLM benchmarks (e.g., VQA, OK-VQA, MME) comparing AGMark with baselines, analyzing performance across task types.

## Limitations
- Key implementation details are unspecified: attention layer/head selection, swap logic, and embedding type, hindering exact replication.
- Hyperparameter transferability across models and datasets remains unknown; optimal values may require per-model tuning.
- The reliability of attention weights as a proxy for visual relevance is not validated; they may misidentify semantic-critical tokens.

## Confidence
- **Mechanism 1 (Dynamic Weight Fusion):** Medium – Conceptual foundation and ablations support it, but confidence is reduced by uncertainty over which attention signals to use.
- **Mechanism 2 (Adaptive Partitioning):** High – Precise formulation and strong ablation evidence (removing entropy/density degrades performance).
- **Mechanism 3 (End-to-End Alignment):** High – Supported by length-generalization results and clear contrast to static baselines.
