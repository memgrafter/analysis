# The Viscosity of Logic: Phase Transitions and Hysteresis in DPO Alignment

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2601.17260  
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.17260  
- **Reference count:** 5  
- **Primary result:** DPO alignment capability exhibits sharp, architecture-specific phase transitions and persistent hysteresis, decoupling preference margins from true reasoning performance.

## Executive Summary
This paper challenges the assumption that Direct Preference Optimization (DPO) improves model alignment monotonically with increasing alignment pressure (β). Through systematic β sweeps on three 7B model families, it reveals that alignment capability—measured by logic-probe margins—is non-monotonic and marked by abrupt, architecture-dependent phase transitions. Notably, in Mistral-7B, positive margins appear only within a narrow β band near 0.01, while in LLaMA-2-7B, preference margins anticorrelate strongly with reasoning skill. Furthermore, exposure to high β causes irreversible capability degradation (hysteresis), surviving even after β reduction. These findings imply that common proxy metrics (e.g., preference margins) can diverge from true capability, necessitating capability-resolved evaluation across β.

## Method Summary
The authors performed a controlled sweep of the DPO β parameter across three 7B model families (Mistral-7B, LLaMA-2-7B, and one other unspecified) using a fixed training recipe and dataset. Models were evaluated at multiple β values using logic probes (binary classification margins) and reasoning benchmarks. The study examined alignment trajectories, training roughness, and capability retention after annealing (reducing β from high to low values). No code or seed details were released.

## Key Results
- Alignment capability (logic-probe margins) is non-monotonic with β, exhibiting sharp phase transitions; in Mistral-7B, margins become positive only within a narrow band (β≈0.01), with boundaries sensitive to random seed and marked by a 37% collapse in training roughness.
- In LLaMA-2-7B, DPO preference margin anticorrelates with reasoning capability (Pearson r = -0.91), suggesting margin-based selection may harm true skill.
- High-β exposure induces persistent hysteresis: models annealed from high β show significant logic loss compared to those trained directly at low β (p=0.032, dz=1.45), indicating irreversible degradation.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The paper does not propose or test a mechanistic explanation for the observed phase transitions or hysteresis; it documents empirical patterns without causal analysis.

- **Preference margin:** Difference in log-likelihood between chosen and rejected responses. *Why needed:* Proxy metric studied. *Quick check:* Is a higher margin always indicative of better alignment?

## Architecture Onboarding
Component map: Preference dataset → DPO loss (with β) → Model parameter update → Evaluation (logic probes, reasoning benchmarks)

Critical path: β sweep → training → evaluation at each β → comparison of direct vs. annealed paths.

Design tradeoffs: Fixed training recipe controls for confounding but limits generalizability; logic probes isolate reasoning but may not capture broader skills.

Failure signatures: 1) Sharp drop in training roughness at transition boundaries, 2) Negative correlation between margin and reasoning in LLaMA-2, 3) Significant capability loss after high-β annealing.

First three experiments: 1) Replicate β sweep on a single architecture with 10+ seeds to map transition stability. 2) Compare linear vs. step annealing schedules to characterize hysteresis recovery. 3) Run logic probes alongside coding and factual benchmarks to test decoupling breadth.

## Limitations
- Study restricted to three 7B architectures; findings may not generalize to other models or scales.
- Fixed training recipe and data; interactions with optimization hyperparameters or data distribution remain unknown.
- Statistical evidence for hysteresis is marginal (p=0.032) with no multiple-comparison correction; seed variability undisclosed.

## Confidence
- **Non-monotonic phase transitions in alignment capability:** Medium (observed across three models but limited diversity).
- **Architecture-specific transition patterns:** High (clear dichotomy between Mistral-7B and LLaMA-2-7B).
- **Persistent hysteresis after high-β exposure:** Medium (statistically significant but effect size moderate; needs replication).
- **Decoupling of preference margins from true capability:** Medium (strong negative correlation in LLaMA-2, but sample clarity unclear).

## Next Checks
1. Replicate β sweeps across additional model families (e.g., Falcon, Gemma) and larger scales (13B, 34B) to test robustness of phase transitions.
2. Conduct systematic hysteresis experiments on all architectures with varied annealing schedules and track capability recovery trajectories.
3. Perform a high-seed-count study (≥10 seeds per β) to quantify stability of phase boundaries and validate margin-capability correlations.
