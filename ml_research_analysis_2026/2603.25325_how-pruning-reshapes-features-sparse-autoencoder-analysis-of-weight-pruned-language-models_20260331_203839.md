# How Pruning Reshapes Features: Sparse Autoencoder Analysis of Weight-Pruned Language Models
## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2603.25325
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.25325
- **Reference count:** 16
- **Primary result:** Rare SAE features survive weight pruning dramatically better than frequent ones (near-perfect negative correlation, ρ = −1.0), with Wanda pruning preserving up to 3.7× more features than magnitude pruning, and surviving features showing no greater causal effect than fragile ones upon ablation.

## Executive Summary
This study investigates how unstructured weight pruning affects the internal feature representations of language models, using Sparse Autoencoders (SAEs) as a probe. By systematically pruning pre-trained models (Gemma 3 1B, Gemma 2 2B, Llama 3.2 1B) at various sparsity levels via magnitude and Wanda methods, the authors compare feature geometry between dense and pruned models. They find that features with low activation frequency (rare features) are preserved disproportionately well compared to high-frequency (generic) features. Wanda pruning, which incorporates activation magnitudes into its pruning scores, significantly outperforms pure magnitude pruning in maintaining feature geometry. Crucially, features that survive pruning geometrically (similar decoder directions) do not exhibit greater causal importance when ablated, suggesting a dissociation between a feature's resilience to weight destruction and its functional role.

## Method Summary
The paper conducts a controlled empirical study comparing SAE dictionaries trained on the same layer's activations from dense versus pruned models. For each model, activations from a mid-layer residual stream are collected from ~1.5M tokens of FineWeb-Edu. Models are pruned globally to sparsities ranging from 20% to 60% using either unstructured magnitude pruning or Wanda (which weights pruning scores by activation norms). Separate SAEs (dictionary size 8× model dimension, TopK non-linearity) are trained on activations from each pruned model and from the original dense model. Feature survival is defined by matching decoder vectors between dense and pruned SAEs via Mutual Nearest Neighbor (MNN) search with a cosine similarity threshold (τ=0.7). Causal importance is measured by zeroing a single feature's decoded contribution in the model's residual stream and evaluating the KL divergence of the final output distribution over 64 hold-out prompts.

## Key Results
- Feature survival rate is strongly and negatively correlated with firing rate in the dense model, with a near-perfect Spearman correlation (ρ = −1.0) in 11 of 17 experimental conditions.
- At 50% sparsity, Wanda pruning preserves up to 3.7× more features (by MNN match rate) than magnitude pruning.
- Pre-trained SAEs transfer reasonably to Wanda-pruned models up to 50% sparsity (low FVU), whereas transferability degrades much faster for magnitude pruning.
- There is no meaningful difference in causal effect (measured by output KL divergence upon single-feature ablation) between features that survive pruning geometrically and those that are destroyed; the mean difference is less than 1.2%.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1
- **Claim:** Weight pruning acts as an implicit feature selector, preferentially destroying high-frequency (generic) SAE features while preserving low-frequency (specialized) ones.
- **Mechanism:** Pruning removes weights that contribute less to output or activation magnitude. Generic features, which activate frequently across many inputs, rely on weight configurations that are more widely distributed and thus more likely to contain low-magnitude components pruned. Specialized rare features rely on more specific weight configurations that, by chance, avoid being the lowest-magnitude elements.
- **Core assumption:** Feature firing rate correlates with the distributional spread and redundancy of its underlying weight representation.
- **Evidence anchors:** The within-condition Spearman correlation between mean firing rate and survival rate is ρ = −1.0 in most conditions; this implies rare features are structurally less vulnerable.
- **Break condition:** The negative correlation disappears at larger model scales (7B+) or with different pruning schedules (e.g., iterative pruning with fine-tuning that could recover features).

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim:** Wanda pruning preserves internal feature geometry substantially better than magnitude pruning at matched sparsity levels.
- **Mechanism:** Wanda's pruning score multiplies weight magnitude by the norm of the input activations that use that weight. This activation-awareness biases preservation toward weights that are both large *and* frequently used during forward passes, thus better respecting the directions (features) the model has learned to rely on.
- **Core assumption:** Cosine similarity between decoder vectors from dense and pruned SAEs is a valid proxy for whether the same computational feature persists.
- **Evidence anchors:** Wanda preserves up to 3.7× more features by MNN; SAE transferability (FVU) remains lower for Wanda across sparsities, indicating better preservation of the activation manifold.
- **Break condition:** Wanda's advantage vanishes in architectures with different activation distributions (e.g., Mixture-of-Experts) or when pruning non-matrix parameters.

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim:** There is a dissociation between a feature's geometric survival (cosine similarity of its decoder vector) and its causal importance (effect on output logits upon ablation).
- **Mechanism:** Pruning may preserve the *directional representation* of a feature (high cosine similarity) without preserving the *magnitude* of its contribution to the final output. The surviving feature's decoder weight might point in a similar direction but with a much smaller norm, or downstream computation may have re-routed reliance. Conversely, a fragile feature might have a very different decoder direction but still occupy a critical bottleneck.
- **Core assumption:** Single-feature ablation KL divergence is a sufficient measure of a feature's causal relevance to model outputs.
- **Evidence anchors:** Geomatically surviving features show no greater causal effect than fragile ones upon ablation (<1.2% KL difference). This holds across models and pruning methods.
- **Break condition:** Multi-feature ablation or circuit-level analysis reveals that robust features collectively have a larger causal aggregate effect, or if KL divergence proves insensitive to specific causal roles.

## Foundational Learning
- **Concept: Sparse Autoencoders (SAEs)**
  - *Why needed here:* SAEs are the core interpretability probe used to extract monosemantic features from model activations and compare feature dictionaries across dense and pruned models.
  - *Quick check question:* What does a high MNN rate between two SAE dictionaries indicate about their corresponding models?
- **Concept: Unstructured Weight Pruning**
  - *Why needed here:* The independent variable; the study examines how removing a fraction of weights (unstructured sparsity) impacts learned features. Distinguishing between magnitude-only (weight magnitude) and activation-aware (Wanda) pruning is central.
  - *Quick check question:* Why might Wanda's use of calibration activations lead to better feature preservation than pure magnitude pruning?

## Architecture Onboarding
Base LLM -> Pruner -> Activation Extractor -> SAE Trainer -> Feature Matcher -> Ablation Engine
Critical path: For each condition (model, pruning method, sparsity): 1) Prune model weights using calibration data if required; 2) Extract residual stream activations from a fixed layer using a large corpus; 3) Train multiple SAEs with different seeds on those activations; 4) Match decoder vectors between SAEs from pruned and dense models (using a reference dense SAE seed); 5) Bin features by their firing rate in the dense model; 6) Compute survival rate per bin; 7) For causal analysis, select top/bottom matched features and run single-feature ablations on hold-out prompts, measuring output KL divergence.
Design tradeoffs:
- *SAE size (d_sae = 8×d_model):* Larger dictionaries capture more features but increase matching ambiguity (lower seed MNN baseline).
- *Matching threshold (τ):* Higher τ (0.8) is stricter, more conservative; lower τ (0.6) captures more potential matches but includes noise. Paper uses τ=0.7.
- *Ablation scope:* Single-feature ablation on the final token may miss mid-layer or distributed effects; KL divergence may not capture all types of causal relevance.
Failure signatures:
- Float16 overflow → NaN activations in deep layers (observed in Gemma 3 L18); switch to bfloat16/float32.
- Very low absolute MNN rates (~2–4%) making signal hard to distinguish; must show >10× gap over seed-stability baseline.
- SAE training instability: alive feature count drops, FVU remains high; adjust LR or resampling.
- Catastrophic perplexity (>10^6) indicates model destruction; feature analysis moot.
First 3 experiments:
1. Reproduce baseline WikiText-2 perplexity for each dense model to establish reference.
2. Train one SAE on a dense model; verify alive feature count ≈ d_sae and FVU matches paper (~0.367 for Gemma 3).
3. Train two SAEs on the *same* dense model with different seeds; compute MNN at τ=0.7. Expect ~2–4% match rate (Figure 1 baseline) to validate matching pipeline.

## Limitations
- Study uses only 1B–2B parameter models; findings may not generalize to larger scales or architectures like Mixture-of-Experts.
- Feature matching relies on a low baseline MNN rate (~2–4%), raising signal-to-noise concerns.
- Single-feature ablation on final token KL divergence may miss circuit-level or distributed causal roles.
- Exact dataset splits and prompt lists for ablation evaluation are undisclosed, hindering exact replication.

## Confidence
- Rare features survive pruning better (ρ ≈ −1 in many conditions): **Medium** – strong evidence on small models but untested at scale or with iterative fine-tuning.
- Wanda preserves feature geometry more than magnitude pruning: **High** – consistent advantage across survival rates, transferability (FVU), and mechanistic alignment.
- Dissociation between geometric survival and causal importance: **Medium** – KL differences tiny (<1.2%) and single-feature ablation may be insensitive to broader roles.

## Next Checks
1. Replicate the firing-rate vs. survival correlation on models with ≥7B parameters and under iterative pruning with fine-tuning.
2. Perform cumulative ablations on feature groups (e.g., top/bottom deciles) to test if robust features have greater combined causal impact.
3. Systematically vary the matching threshold τ (0.6–0.9) to assess robustness of the negative correlation to matching stringency.
