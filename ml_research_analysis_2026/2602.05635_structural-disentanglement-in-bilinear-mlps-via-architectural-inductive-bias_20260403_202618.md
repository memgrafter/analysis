# Structural Disentanglement in Bilinear MLPs via Architectural Inductive Bias

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID**: 2602.05635  
- **Source URL**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.05635  
- **Reference count**: 40  
- **Primary result**: Bilinear MLPs with explicit multiplicative interactions induce structural disentanglement, enabling surgical unlearning with >600% selectivity and stable long‑horizon extrapolation (80+ steps) via a non‑mixing property under gradient flow.

## Executive Summary
Neural networks often suffer from fragile selective unlearning and poor long‑horizon generalization due to entangled internal representations. This paper investigates bilinear multilayer perceptrons (MLPs) as an architectural inductive bias that enforces explicit multiplicative interactions. Theoretically, under gradient flow, bilinear parameterizations exhibit a non‑mixing property: orthogonal functional modes evolve independently, preventing interference. Empirically, across modular arithmetic, cyclic reasoning, and Lie group dynamics, bilinear models recover ground‑truth algebraic operators—e.g., Fourier entropy near the theoretical ideal for modular addition, sharply decaying singular values for multiplication, and near‑permutation transition matrices that preserve accuracy over dozens of compositions. In surgical unlearning benchmarks, bilinears achieve high selectivity ratios (>600% on rank‑4 tasks) and near‑zero distortion of retained components, whereas ReLU baselines incur significant interference. The results underscore that architectural bias governing representational structure is central to reliable unlearning and systematic generalization.

## Method Summary
The study compares bilinear MLPs against several baselines (ReLU, SwiGLU, GeGLU, Tanh, Sigmoid) on synthetic tasks with known algebraic structure. A bilinear layer computes \(h = (Wx) \odot (Vx)\); for two‑input tasks, each operand is embedded separately before the Hadamard product. Datasets cover modular addition \((a+b)\bmod p\) and multiplication \((a\cdot b)\bmod p\) (all pairs in \([0,p-1]\)), cyclic successor over a cycle of length 400, and Lie group dynamics (SO(3) rotations, SL(2) volume preservation). Training uses AdamW with early stopping at >99.9% validation accuracy. Key metrics include Fourier entropy of extracted interaction matrices, singular value decay, column entropy of transition operators, multi‑step accuracy, norm/determinant drift, and unlearning selectivity ratios. Interaction operators are obtained by full probing or via weight decomposition.

## Key Results
- Bilinear architectures display a theoretical non‑mixing property: under gradient flow, orthogonal modes decouple, enabling independent learning.
- On modular addition, Fourier entropy approaches the ideal \(\log p\); on multiplication, singular values decay sharply.
- For cyclic tasks, learned transition matrices remain near‑permutation, preserving accuracy over 80+ composition steps, whereas pointwise models collapse.
- In surgical unlearning, bilinear models achieve selectivity ratios >600% (rank‑4) and near‑zero distortion for orthogonal components, while ReLU baselines suffer substantial interference.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1: Bilinear decoupling under gradient flow
- **Claim**: Bilinear parameterizations inherently decouple orthogonal functional modes during gradient‑based training.
- **Mechanism**: The composite operator \(Q = \sum_k \alpha_k w_k v_k^\top\) evolves under gradient flow as \(\dot{Q} = -(Q-Q^\star) VV^\top - UU^\top (Q-Q^\star)\). Expanding in the singular basis of the target \(Q^\star = \sum_i s_i u_i v_i^\top\) yields independent scalar ODEs for each mode coefficient \(c_i(t)\) because singular vectors are orthonormal, thus preventing mixing.
- **Core assumptions**: Small initialization, continuous gradient flow, squared Frobenius loss, and a target operator \(Q^\star\) with orthogonal singular modes.
- **Evidence anchors**: 
  - Abstract: "analytically, bilinear parameterizations exhibit a ‘non‑mixing’ property under gradient flow conditions."
  - Section 4, Eq. (5) shows \(\dot{c}_i(t) = -(a_i^2(t)+b_i^2(t))(c_i(t)-s_i)\), independent across modes.
  - Related work on bilinear layers for interpretability (arXiv:2410.08417) shows weight structures consistent with disentanglement.
- **Break condition**: Large initialization, non‑orthogonal target, different loss, or high‑variance stochastic optimization can induce coupling; stochastic noise with high learning rates may also disrupt independence.

### Mechanism 2: Structural disentanglement enables surgical unlearning
- **Claim**: Because learned operators are sums of orthogonal rank‑1 terms, zeroing the coefficient of an undesired mode affects only its subspace, preserving other functionalities.
- **Mechanism**: The orthogonal decomposition \(Q = \sum_i c_i u_i v_i^\top\) implies that removing a specific mode (e.g., via fine‑tuning or pruning) does not alter the span of the remaining modes, provided they remain orthogonal.
- **Core assumptions**: Tasks correspond to orthogonal interaction operators and the architecture preserves these subspaces during training.
- **Evidence anchors**:
  - Abstract: "in surgical unlearning benchmarks, bilinear architectures achieve high selectivity ratios (e.g., >600% in rank‑4 tasks) and near‑zero task preservation distortion."
  - Section 6.2 (Fig. 2) shows neuron specialization and an ideal Pareto frontier for pruning.
  - Related disentanglement work (e.g., arXiv:2409.14851) supports the principle that orthogonal decomposition aids selective editing.
- **Break condition**: If tasks share non‑orthogonal components, unlearning one degrades others. Failure to learn orthogonal modes (as in pointwise baselines) collapses selectivity.

### Mechanism 3: Orthogonal mode preservation yields stable long‑horizon extrapolation
- **Claim**: For compositional tasks, preserving the true singular basis prevents error accumulation during iteration.
- **Mechanism**: When the learned operator \(Q\) shares singular vectors with the target \(Q^\star\), powers are computed as \(Q^k = V \Lambda^k V^\top\); eigenvalues scale independently, maintaining structure over many compositions.
- **Core assumptions**: Underlying dynamics are generated by an operator diagonalizable in an orthogonal basis, and the model learns the correct eigenbasis.
- **Evidence anchors**:
  - Abstract: "in cyclic tasks, learned transition matrices maintain near‑permutation sparsity, preserving accuracy over 80+ composition steps versus collapse in pointwise models."
  - Sections 6.4 and 6.5 demonstrate stable extrapolation in cyclic reasoning and Lie group dynamics.
  - Related work on grokking algebraic operations (arXiv:2602.19533) aligns with the idea that structural alignment aids extrapolation.
- **Break condition**: Subspace drift due to overfitting or noise, or if dynamics are non‑linear/non‑autonomous, can degrade extrapolation.

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component map**: A bilinear MLP layer consists of two weight matrices \(W, V \in \mathbb{R}^{d_{\text{out}} \times d_{\text{in}}}\). Given input \(x\), it computes \(u = Wx\), \(v = Vx\), and output \(h = u \odot v\). For two‑input tasks, embed each operand as \(e_a, e_b\) and compute \(h = (e_a W_1) \odot (e_b W_2)\). An optional linear output layer maps \(h\) to logits.

**Critical path**: The core multiplicative path: input → separate linear projections (\(Wx\), \(Vx\)) → Hadamard product → hidden representation → linear output.

**Design tradeoffs**:
- *Capacity*: Bilinear layers represent low‑rank interactions (rank ≤ hidden dimension). Deeper stacks may be needed for complex functions.
- *Optimization*: Convergence can be faster on multiplicative tasks, but careful small initialization is crucial to preserve the non‑mixing property.
- *Interpretability*: Explicit factorization \(w_k v_k^\top\) facilitates weight‑based analysis, unlike ReLU.
- *Expressivity*: Lacks pointwise nonlinearities; may require wider layers to approximate non‑multiplicative functions.

**Failure signatures**:
- Training accuracy low on algebraic tasks.
- Fourier entropy (addition) far from \(\log p\); singular values (multiplication) decay slowly.
- Neuron specialization analysis shows predominantly mixed or dead neurons.
- Selectivity ratio ≤ 1 (interference during unlearning).
- Extrapolation accuracy drops sharply after few composition steps.

**First 3 experiments** (to validate implementation):
1. **Modular addition**: Train on \((a+b) \bmod 97\). Compute mean Fourier entropy of interaction matrices \(M_k\); expect ≈ 4.57 (theoretical ideal) or at least > 3.5.
2. **Entangled superposition**: Train on \(y = x_1^\top A_{12} x_2 + x_2^\top A_{23} x_3\) with shared \(x_2\). Prune neurons by cross‑norm and plot retention of \(f_{23}\) vs. forgetting of \(f_{12}\); expect a Pareto curve near the top‑left corner.
3. **Cyclic reasoning**: Train on successor function over cycle length 400. Compute transition operator \(T\) and accuracy after 80 composition steps; expect > 80% accuracy.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
**Open Question 1**: What is the minimal architectural inductive bias sufficient to guarantee structural disentanglement and reliable selective unlearning?
- *Basis*: The authors state that characterizing the bare‑minimum bias remains an open problem.
- *Why unresolved*: The study focuses on bilinear MLPs and close relatives (SwiGLU, GeGLU). It is unclear whether the essential property is multiplicativity, rank restriction, or something else.
- *What evidence would resolve*: Systematic ablation of architectural features (varying rank, replacing multiplication with other non‑linearities) combined with theoretical analysis linking features to mode‑decoupling guarantees.

**Open Question 2**: Do the benefits of multiplicative inductive biases extend to unstructured real‑world domains such as vision or language?
- *Basis*: The authors note their analysis focuses on settings with identifiable compositional or algebraic structure; results may not transfer to perceptual or open‑ended domains.
- *Why unresolved*: All experiments are on synthetic structured tasks; it remains unknown whether similar disentanglement occurs in complex domains.
- *What evidence would resolve*: Apply bilinear or gated architectures to standard benchmarks (e.g., ImageNet, language modeling) and measure modularity of representations and support for precise unlearning or robust long‑horizon predictions.

**Open Question 3**: Does the theoretical non‑mixing property hold under practical training conditions (SGD, finite learning rates, cross‑entropy loss)?
- *Basis*: The authors admit their analysis relies on idealized assumptions and provide no formal guarantees beyond that regime.
- *Why unresolved*: The proof assumes gradient flow, squared Frobenius loss, and small initialization. Deviations (e.g., larger steps, different losses) could break mode independence; the extent is unknown.
- *What evidence would resolve*: Rigorous mathematical analysis relaxing assumptions or extensive empirical stress‑testing across optimizers, learning rates, and loss functions to quantify degradation.

## Limitations
- The architectures and training details for Tanh/Sigmoid baselines are unspecified, potentially affecting capacity‑matched comparisons.
- The method for extracting interaction matrices in large‑p tasks (e.g., cyclic reasoning with p=400) is not fully clarified (full vs. sampled), which may impact reproducibility.
- Missing reproducibility information (random seeds, exact initialization variances, early‑stopping criteria) hinders exact replication.
- Experiments are limited to synthetic algebraic domains; generalization to real‑world data is unverified.
- The non‑mixing guarantee may break under non‑orthogonal targets or high learning rates, which are not thoroughly explored.

## Confidence
- **Non‑mixing property**: *High*. Theoretical derivation (Eq. 5) clearly shows decoupled mode evolution; empirical evidence (Fourier entropy ≈ log p, sharp SVD decay) strongly supports the claim.
- **Surgical unlearning**: *Medium*. Orthogonal task experiments show >600% selectivity and near‑zero distortion, but only rank‑4 superpositions were tested; higher ranks or non‑orthogonal overlaps may degrade performance.
- **Stable extrapolation**: *Medium*. Bilinear models maintain accuracy over many composition steps in cyclic and Lie group tasks, but this relies on accurate eigenbasis learning, which could drift under noisy optimization or more complex dynamics.

## Next Checks
1. **Stochastic optimization robustness**: Train bilinear models with AdamW (non‑zero learning rate, mini‑batches) and track per‑mode coefficients over time to verify that non‑mixing persists under practical SGD conditions.
2. **Non‑orthogonal interference**: Construct entanglements with varying cosine similarity between true operators; measure unlearning selectivity and retention to map the boundary where disentanglement breaks down.
3. **Scaling to higher rank**: Test orthogonal superpositions of rank 8 and 16; report selectivity ratio and preservation distortion; compare against ReLU baselines to see if bilinear advantage scales.
