# Adam Converges Without Any Modification On Update Rules

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2603.02092
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.02092
- **Reference count:** 40
- **Primary result:** Adam converges without modification for a wide range of (β₁, β₂) when β₂ is large enough and β₁ < √β₂; divergence occurs only in a problem-dependent region that shrinks with batch size.

## Executive Summary
The paper resolves the long-standing conflict between Adam's empirical success and theoretical divergence by showing that the order of selecting hyperparameters versus the problem matters. When the problem (including mini-batch count *n*) is fixed first, Adam converges for a wide range of (β₁, β₂) provided β₂ is sufficiently large (β₂ ≥ 1 - O((1-β₁)/n⁵)) and β₁ < √β₂. The authors prove convergence theorems with an O(log T / √T) rate and an error term that vanishes as β₂ → 1. They also construct a counterexample demonstrating divergence when β₂ is small, revealing a phase transition in the (β₁, β₂) plane whose boundary depends on *n*. The findings offer practical guidance: increase β₂ inversely with batch size and then choose β₁ < √β₂. No modifications to Adam's update rule are needed.

## Method Summary
The theoretical analysis relies on smoothness (L) and a generalized variance condition (D₀, D₁) defining the problem class. Key tools include a potential function that cancels historical momentum effects and a concentration result for 1/√*v*ₖ around its expectation, which holds when β₂ is large. This yields convergence guarantees for both with-replacement and random-shuffling Adam. Empirical validation includes: (1) a synthetic divergence counterexample confirming the phase transition; (2) grid searches on MNIST (one-hidden-layer net) and CIFAR-10 (ResNet-18) showing low error for large β₂; (3) LLM pre-training experiments where increasing β₂ with smaller batches improves validation loss.

## Key Results
- Convergence theorems for Adam (with-replacement and random shuffling) under smoothness and variance assumptions, achieving O(log T / √T) expected squared gradient norm with an additional error O(δ(β₂)√D₀) that vanishes as β₂ → 1.
- Divergence construction: For small β₂, there exists a region in the (β₁, β₂) plane where Adam diverges; the boundary expands with the number of mini-batches *n*, revealing a phase transition.
- Practical guidance: To ensure convergence, set β₂ ≥ 1 - O((1-β₁)/n⁵) and β₁ < √β₂; increasing β₂ inversely with batch size is recommended.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The paper identifies three core mechanisms:

1. **Problem-first hyperparameter selection**: Theoretical divergence proofs fix hyperparameters first then construct a problem; in practice, the problem is fixed first and hyperparameters are tuned accordingly. This changes the divergence region: when *n* is fixed, the region shrinks, yielding a phase transition.
2. **Large β₂ stabilizes preconditioner**: A large β₂ (close to 1) makes the second-order momentum *v*ₖ change slowly. Under the variance condition, 1/√*v*ₖ concentrates around 1/√E[*v*ₖ] with high probability, making the effective update direction close to the negative gradient.
3. **Potential function with delayed momentum**: The analysis introduces *z*ₖ = *x*ₖ - β₁ⁿ *x*ₖ₋ₙ/(1-β₁ⁿ) to cancel historical momentum up to step *k-n*. This aligns the difference *z*ₖ - *z*ₖ₊₁ with a scaled gradient when β₂ is large, enabling a descent lemma.

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map:**  
  first-order momentum *m*ₜ -> second-order momentum *v*ₜ -> adaptive update *m*ₜ/√*v*ₜ -> parameter update *x*_{t+1} = *x*ₜ - ηₜ *m*ₜ/√*v*ₜ; auxiliary variable *z*ₖ used in analysis.
- **Critical path:**  
  1. Fix problem (dataset, model, mini-batch count *n*).  
  2. Choose β₂ large enough to satisfy β₂ ≥ γ₁(*n*) (threshold increases with *n*).  
  3. Choose β₁ < √β₂ (commonly β₁ ∈ [0.9, 0.99] when β₂ ≈ 0.999).  
  4. Empirically: if loss decreases slowly, increase β₂ relative to batch size.
- **Design tradeoffs:**  
  Larger β₂ stabilizes √*v*ₜ and aids convergence proofs but increases memory and slows adaptation. Smaller β₁ reduces momentum influence but may lose noise reduction. Batch size and β₂ are inversely related: smaller batches require larger β₂.
- **Failure signatures:**  
  Divergence region (Theorem 3.5): For small β₂, iterates, gradients, and objective diverge; boundary expands with *n*. Empirically: loss increases or oscillates without bound, especially with small β₂ (<0.7) and large *n*. Misconfiguration: β₂ < γ₁(*n*) or β₁ ≥ √β₂.
- **First 3 experiments:**  
  1. Reproduce synthetic divergence: Minimize the constructed function (Eq 3.1) with cyclic Adam over a grid of (β₁, β₂); observe divergence in the danger region.  
  2. Reproduce phase transition on real data: Train one-hidden-layer net on MNIST (batch size 1) and ResNet-18 on CIFAR-10 (batch size 16), sweep β₁,β₂; verify low error in large-β₂ region.  
  3. Verify batch-size scaling: Train a small transformer on a language task with varying batch sizes; increase β₂ for smaller batches; check validation loss improvement.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- **Exact boundary characterization:** The precise shape and problem-dependent formula for the divergence-convergence boundary (β₁*, β₂*) remains unknown.
- **Intermediate variance regimes:** Does Adam diverge for D₁ ∈ (*n*, 2*n*²)? Existing examples cover D₁ = *n* and D₁ = 2*n*².
- **Adam vs. SGD speedup:** Under which problem structures does Adam achieve a faster convergence rate than SGD with momentum? The paper proves the same O(log T/√T) rate as SGD.
- **Tightness of β₂ threshold:** Can the required β₂ threshold (β₂ ≥ 1 - O((1-β₁)/*n*⁵)) be reduced? The current polynomial dependence on *n* may be loose.
- **Optimal (β₁, β₂) within safe region:** Which specific combination yields the optimal convergence rate or final accuracy? The paper only guarantees convergence for β₁ < √β₂ with large β₂.

## Limitations
- The exact convergence threshold depends on unknown problem constants (L, D₀, D₁), preventing precise safe β₂ for real datasets.
- Assumption 2.2 (generalized variance) may not hold for deep neural nets with heavy-tailed gradients.
- The required β₂ > 1 - O((1-β₁)/*n*⁵) is extremely close to 1 for typical *n*, yet common Adam uses β₂ = 0.999 which might be outside the proven region.
- Convergence rate O(log T/√T) is slower than the optimal O(1/√T) for SGD; it's unclear if empirical rates match.
- Theory assumes convex/smooth objectives; non-convex deep learning is only partially addressed.
