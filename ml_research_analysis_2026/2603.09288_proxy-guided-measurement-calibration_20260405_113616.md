# Proxy-Guided Measurement Calibration

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2603.09288
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.09288
- **Reference count:** 40
- **Primary result:** A proxy-guided calibration framework using a two-stage variational autoencoder and matching estimator accurately recovers and corrects for systematic measurement bias in survey and administrative data.

## Executive Summary
Systematic measurement error in observational data hinders credible causal inference. This paper introduces a principled framework to quantify and correct such miscalibration by leveraging proxy variables that are causally independent of the bias mechanism. The approach uses a causal graph to separate latent content factors (driving true outcomes) from latent bias factors (causing distortion). A two-stage variational autoencoder disentangles these latents: the first stage learns content from proxies alone, while the second isolates bias from observed outcomes conditional on the learned content. A matching estimator then compares outcomes between units with high and low bias scores to estimate the additive bias magnitude. The method shows strong performance in synthetic and semi-synthetic evaluations and reveals geographic heterogeneity in reporting bias for U.S. disaster loss data.

## Method Summary
The core method combines causal identification with deep learning. First, a causal graph posits that proxy variables depend only on latent content \(Z\) (and environment \(E\)), while the mismeasured outcome \(Y_{\text{obs}}\) depends on both \(Z\) and latent bias \(A\). Under the “proxy exclusion” assumption, conditioning on \((E, Z)\) blocks all backdoor paths from \(A\) to \(Y_{\text{obs}}\), identifying the bias effect. To estimate \(Z\) and \(A\) from data, a two-stage variational autoencoder is trained: Stage 1 (ZVAE) learns \(q_\phi(z \mid Y_{\text{proxy}}, E)\) using only proxies and \(E\); Stage 2 (AVAE) then learns \(q_\psi(a \mid Y_{\text{obs}}, \hat{z}, E)\) with \(\hat{z}\) frozen from Stage 1. Finally, a nearest-neighbor matching estimator contrasts \(Y_{\text{obs}}\) between units with high vs. low \(\hat{a}\) within similar \(\hat{Z}\) strata to estimate the additive bias parameter \(\alpha\).

## Key Results
- Synthetic experiments: The method accurately recovers the true bias parameter \(\alpha\) across diverse data-generating processes.
- Semi-synthetic evaluations (OHIE, JOBS): It substantially outperforms baselines that ignore proxy structure when injecting known bias into real trial data.
- Application to SHELDUS disaster losses: Reveals county-level geographic heterogeneity in reporting bias, with floods showing the largest systematic distortion, followed by tornadoes.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1
- **Claim:** Proxy variables that depend only on latent content (not on bias) enable identification of the measurement bias effect on the outcome.
- **Mechanism:** The causal graph separates latent content \(Z\) from latent bias \(A\). Proxies \(Y_{\text{proxy}}\) are children of \(Z\) only, while \(Y_{\text{obs}}\) depends on both \(Z\) and \(A\). Conditioning on \((E, Z)\) satisfies the backdoor criterion for the effect of \(A\) on \(Y_{\text{obs}}\), identifying the bias-adjusted mean \(\mu(e, z) = \mathbb{E}[Y_{\text{obs}} \mid A=0, E=e, Z=z]\).
- **Core assumption:** **Proxy exclusion** (\(Y_{\text{proxy}} \perp\!\!\!\perp A \mid Z, E\)) and that \((E, Z)\) contains all confounders of \(A \rightarrow Y_{\text{obs}}\) with no descendants of \(A\).
- **Evidence anchors:** Abstract and Sections 4.1–4.2 define the generative model and prove identification (Proposition 1). The approach contrasts with prior work on reporting bias that does not leverage this causal structure.
- **Break condition:** Violation of proxy exclusion or unobserved confounders of \(A \rightarrow Y_{\text{obs}}\) invalidates adjustment.

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim:** A two-stage variational autoencoder disentangles latent content \(Z\) and bias \(A\) by first learning content from proxies (unaffected by bias) and then learning bias from the outcome conditioned on the learned content.
- **Mechanism:** Stage 1 trains a VAE on proxies and environment: encoder \(q_\phi(z \mid Y_{\text{proxy}}, E)\), prior \(p(z \mid E)\), decoder \(p(Y_{\text{proxy}} \mid z)\). This isolates \(Z\) without exposure to \(A\). Stage 2 freezes \(\hat{z}\) and trains a second VAE on \(Y_{\text{obs}}\): encoder \(q_\psi(a \mid Y_{\text{obs}}, \hat{z}, E)\), prior \(p(a \mid E)\), decoder \(p(Y_{\text{obs}} \mid \hat{z}, a)\). Residual variation in \(Y_{\text{obs}}\) not explained by \(\hat{z}\) is attributed to \(A\).
- **Core assumption:** Proxy exclusion holds; VAE families are sufficiently expressive; \(Z\) is identifiable up to affine transformations.
- **Evidence anchors:** Abstract and Section 4.3 describe the two ELBOs (\(\mathcal{L}_Z\) and \(\mathcal{L}_A\)). The two-stage design specifically prevents information leakage from \(A\) to \(Z\), unlike end-to-end disentanglement.
- **Break condition:** Weak proxy signals, underfitting, posterior collapse, or optimization failure degrade \(\hat{z}\) quality, contaminating \(A\).

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim:** After disentanglement, a matching estimator compares outcomes between units with high vs. low bias scores (matched on content) to estimate the additive bias magnitude \(\alpha\).
- **Mechanism:** Under \(Y_{\text{obs}} = Y_{\text{true}} + \alpha A\) with \(Y_{\text{true}} = g(Z, E) + \varepsilon\), units are split by thresholded \(\hat{a}\). For each treated unit, its \(K\) nearest neighbors in \(\hat{Z}\) among controls are found. The estimator \(\hat{\alpha} = \frac{1}{|I_1|} \sum_{i \in I_1} \left( Y_{\text{obs},i} - \frac{1}{K} \sum_{j \in N_K(i)} Y_{\text{obs},j} \right)\) contrasts outcomes while holding \(g(Z, E)\) constant via matching.
- **Core assumption:** Additive bias model; matching on \(\hat{Z}\) (and \(E\)) balances \(g(Z, E)\); sufficient overlap in propensity scores of \(A\) given \(Z\).
- **Evidence anchors:** Abstract and Section 4.4 define the matching estimator and argue that \(\hat{z}\) serves as a sufficient adjustment set. Matching is common in causal inference but novel for measurement bias correction with disentangled latents.
- **Break condition:** Poor overlap in \(\hat{Z}\) between bias groups inflates variance; incorrect additive form (e.g., \(A \times Z\) interaction) or non‑monotonic effects invalidate the constant \(\alpha\) interpretation.

## Foundational Learning
- **Causal identification via backdoor criterion**
  - **Why needed:** Explains why \((E, Z)\) is a sufficient adjustment set for the effect of \(A\) on \(Y_{\text{obs}}\), underpinning Proposition 1 and the validity of the matching adjustment.
  - **Quick check:** Given the DAG \(E \rightarrow Z \rightarrow Y_{\text{true}}\), \(E \rightarrow A\), \(Z \rightarrow Y_{\text{obs}}\), \(A \rightarrow Y_{\text{obs}}\), and \(Z \rightarrow Y_{\text{proxy}}\), why is \((E, Z)\) sufficient? What if an unobserved variable affects both \(Z\) and \(A\)?
- **Variational autoencoder (VAE)**
  - **Why needed:** To learn latent representations \(Z\) and \(A\) from high-dimensional proxies and outcomes without direct supervision, using the ELBO and amortized inference.
  - **Quick check:** What is the role of the KL divergence term in the VAE objective? How does freezing \(\hat{z}\) in Stage 2 prevent information leakage from \(A\) to \(Z\)?
- **Nearest-neighbor matching**
  - **Why needed:** To non‑parametrically estimate the bias parameter \(\alpha\) by contrasting outcomes between similar units, avoiding misspecification of the outcome function \(g(Z, E)\).
  - **Quick check:** Why match on \(\hat{Z}\) rather than directly on observed covariates \(E\)? What assumption about \(g\) is implicitly made when matching balances it?

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map:** Data loader -> Stage‑1 VAE (ZVAE) -> Stage‑2 VAE (AVAE) -> Latent extraction -> Bias estimation (matching) -> Evaluation
- **Critical path:** Load data -> Train ZVAE (minimize \(\mathcal{L}_Z\)) -> Freeze ZVAE encoder, generate \(\hat{z}\) -> Train AVAE (minimize \(\mathcal{L}_A\) using \(\hat{z}\)) -> Extract \(\hat{a}\), threshold -> Perform matching -> Compute \(\hat{\alpha}\).
- **Design tradeoffs:**
  - **Two‑stage vs. end‑to‑end:** Two‑stage enforces \(Z\) learned only from proxies, preventing \(A\) influence; but Stage‑1 errors propagate.
  - **Latent dimension \(d_z\):** Too small loses content; too large risks capturing bias‑related variance. Stability found for \(d_z \in \{5,10,15,20\}\).
  - **Matching \(K\):** Small \(K\) reduces bias but increases variance; large \(K\) may include less comparable controls.
  - **Thresholding \(\hat{a}\):** Arbitrary; paper uses validation split. A continuous effect model (regression on \(\hat{a}\)) could be more efficient.
- **Failure signatures:**
  - High proxy reconstruction RMSE: ZVAE failed; \(\hat{Z}\) uninformative.
  - Low reduction in \(Y_{\text{obs}}\) reconstruction error when adding \(A\): AVAE did not attribute residual variation to \(A\).
  - Poor overlap between \(\hat{Z}\) distributions of high‑/low‑\(\hat{a}\) groups: matching yields few comparable pairs, high variance.
  - Latent collapse: KL weight too high causes \(\hat{z}\) to collapse to prior.
- **First 3 experiments:**
  1. **Synthetic validation:** Sample from known DGP (linear, Gaussian/Poisson noise). Verify recovery of true \(Z\) (permuted \(R^2 > 0.9\)) and \(A\); check \(\hat{\alpha} \to \alpha\) as \(n\) increases.
  2. **Semi‑synthetic (OHIE & JOBS):** Inject known \(\alpha\) (1, 5, 10) into real trial outcomes. Compare against baselines (proxy‑only, env‑only, TEDVAE); expect lower \(\alpha\) error, especially on OHIE.
  3. **Real SHELDUS case study:** Apply to disaster loss data with remote‑sensing proxies and socio‑economic \(E\). Produce county‑level bias maps by hazard; expect floods to show largest distortion (per literature).

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
### Open Question 1
- **Question:** What are the identifying conditions for the bias magnitude \(\alpha\) when monotonicity (\(\alpha \ge 0\)) is not imposed?
- **Basis in paper:** The authors state that relaxing the monotonicity assumption is an important future direction.
- **Why unresolved:** Current identification proof uses the sign restriction; without it, the causal estimand may be non‑identified or confounded.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Derive identification conditions for unrestricted \(\alpha\) or prove impossibility under given assumptions.

### Open Question 2
- **Question:** Under what additional assumptions or data can unit‑specific bias magnitudes (ITE) be identified instead of only CATEs?
- **Basis in paper:** The paper notes it identifies CATEs at the unit level, not ITEs, due to fundamental limits.
- **Why unresolved:** The matching approach yields average effects within strata; individual bias may be fundamentally non‑identified without further variation.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Theoretical analysis of ITE identifiability in this latent‑variable model, or empirical validation with data containing known individual bias.

### Open Question 3
- **Question:** How sensitive is the method to violations of the proxy exclusion restriction (A1), and can we develop diagnostics or robustifications?
- **Basis in paper:** The framework critically assumes proxies depend only on \(Z\); the paper only evaluates settings where this holds.
- **Why unresolved:** If proxies are influenced by \(A\), disentanglement may fail and \(\alpha\) estimates become biased; sensitivity is unknown.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Simulations with controlled proxy‑bias dependence, and methods to test for exclusion violations or adjust estimates.

### Open Question 4
- **Question:** How can the framework be generalized to other domains where proxy variables might be high‑dimensional, non‑linear, or scarce?
- **Basis in paper:** The discussion calls for extension to public health, administrative records, environmental monitoring.
- **Why unresolved:** Current implementation uses simple tabular proxies and a specific architecture; domain‑specific data structures may require different modeling choices.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Applied case studies in new domains, with adaptations to the VAE architecture, and systematic assessment of proxy selection.

## Limitations
- The **proxy exclusion assumption** cannot be tested empirically; any hidden influence of \(A\) on proxies breaks identification.
- The **additive bias model** may be overly restrictive; interactions \(A \times Z\) or nonlinear effects are not accommodated.
- **Thresholding** continuous bias scores into “high/low” groups is arbitrary and may affect matching quality; a continuous effect model could be more efficient.

## Confidence
- **Identification:** High (formal proof under causal assumptions; synthetic validation confirms backdoor blocking).
- **Disentanglement:** Medium (VAEs recover latents in controlled experiments; real‑world expressiveness/identifiability may degrade).
- **Bias magnitude estimation:** Medium (matching assumes additivity and overlap; semi‑synthetic results promising but real‑data lack gold standard).

## Next Checks
1. **Proxy exclusion sensitivity test:** Generate synthetic data where proxies are also influenced by \(A\); measure degradation of \(\hat{\alpha}\) and develop diagnostic checks (e.g., conditional independence tests).
2. **Model misspecification ablation:** Systematically vary VAE depth, latent dimensions, KL‑weight, decoder likelihood; evaluate stability of \(\hat{\alpha}\) across diverse synthetic DGPs, including non‑Gaussian and nonlinear mechanisms.
3. **Cross‑dataset replication:** Apply to other administrative domains with known validation subsets (e.g., self‑reported health vs. electronic medical records) to quantify calibration improvement and compare against alternative bias‑adjustment methods.
