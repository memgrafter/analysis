# EventCast: Hybrid Demand Forecasting in E-Commerce with LLM-Based Event Knowledge

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2602.07695
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.07695
- **Reference count:** 40
- **Primary result:** Deployed hybrid system reduces MAE by up to 57.0% and MSE by 83.3% versus best industrial baseline during event-driven periods.

## Executive Summary
EventCast improves e-commerce demand forecasting during high-impact periods (e.g., flash sales, holidays) by integrating future event knowledge. It uses a frozen large language model (LLM) to reason over unstructured operational business data (campaigns, holidays, incentives), producing interpretable textual summaries that capture semantic nuance and cultural context. These summaries are fused with historical time-series features in a dual-tower neural architecture, keeping event signals explicit and avoiding the numerical precision issues of direct LLM forecasting. The system has been in production across four countries and 160 regions since March 2025, demonstrating substantial accuracy gains during volatile event periods.

## Method Summary
The system processes two data sources: historical multivariate time series and an operational database of future events (often unstructured). For each forecast date, a frozen LLM executes a prompt template over the event database to generate a structured textual summary (e.g., `Holiday:Eid;CampaignLevel:9;LogisticsThreshold:High`). Historical features are encoded by a Transformer. Text summaries are tokenized into learnable embeddings. A dual-tower design aligns the historical embedding (`h_hist`) and textual embedding (`h_sem`) via simple addition (`h_aligned = h_hist + h_sem`). A residual feed-forward network with two heads predicts a historical trend (`y_trend`) and an event adjustment (`y_event`). The final forecast `y = λ*y_trend + (1-λ)*y_event` combines both. Only the historical encoder, alignment layer, and heads are trained; the LLM and textual token embeddings are frozen. Training uses a 14-day lookback to predict T+1 to T+4 with MSE loss, retrained weekly on a rolling 13-month window.

## Key Results
- Reduces MAE by up to **57.0%** and MSE by **83.3%** versus the best industrial baseline during event-driven periods.
- Shows up to **86.9% MAE** and **97.7% MSE** improvement over an ablated variant without event knowledge.
- Outperforms direct LLM forecasting approaches (e.g., Time-LLM) by a large margin, confirming the decoupled design's advantage.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1: LLM Semantic Extraction from Unstructured Business Data
The LLM processes messy operational text (with typos, abbreviations, and cultural specifics) via chain-of-thought prompting to output structured summaries. This captures semantic nuance and novel event combinations that engineered structured features would miss. **Evidence:** Abstract and Section 3.2 highlight world knowledge for cultural context (e.g., Ramadan vs. Eid) and robustness to data inconsistencies. Ablation in Section 4.2 shows adding these summaries significantly boosts performance. **Break condition:** If LLM reasoning becomes inconsistent or hallucinatory on new data formats, summary quality degrades.

### Mechanism 2: Dual-Tower Architecture Prevents Signal Dilution
Separate encoders for historical time series and textual summaries ensure the future event signal remains explicit and is not diluted within the high-dimensional latent space of historical patterns. A linear fusion (`h_hist + h_sem`) allows the model to learn when to overweight event signals. **Evidence:** Section 3.3 states the design "preserves rather than dilutes" event info. Table 3 ablation shows consistent gains from adding event features, unlike concatenation baselines that degrade at short horizons. **Break condition:** If the interaction between history and events is highly nonlinear or multiplicative, simple additive fusion may be insufficient.

### Mechanism 3: Decoupling LLM Reasoning from Numerical Prediction
Using the LLM only for semantic parsing avoids fundamental mismatches between language modeling and precise temporal dynamics (e.g., tokenization quantization, autoregressive error accumulation). A lightweight neural network handles all numeric modeling. **Evidence:** Abstract states "avoids direct LLM numerical prediction." Section 4.2 shows EventCast (MAE 0.885) vastly outperforms Time-LLM (1.370) and OneFitsAll (1.464). Authors note LLMs are "ill-suited for directly modeling temporal dynamics." **Break condition:** If events have a direct, quantifiable numerical impact (e.g., a 15% discount), the qualitative summary may lack precise magnitude.

## Foundational Learning
- **Concept: Time-series non-stationarity & regime shifts**
  - *Why needed:* Events cause abrupt, non-repeating shifts that break models trained on regular patterns.
  - *Quick check:* Why would a model with strong weekly seasonality fail on Black Friday?
- **Concept: LLM in-context learning & prompt design**
  - *Why needed:* The entire pipeline hinges on prompts that reliably extract structure from noisy, domain-specific text.
  - *Quick check:* How would you design a prompt to correctly interpret "tg" as "target" vs. "thanksgiving" in a campaign note?
- **Concept: Multimodal feature fusion (early vs. late)**
  - *Why needed:* The dual-tower uses late fusion (adding embeddings) to keep signals separate; understanding why this beats early fusion (concatenation) is key.
  - *Quick check:* What are the risks of concatenating embeddings from two different pretrained encoders?
- **Concept: Transformer encoders for time series**
  - *Why needed:* The historical encoder transposes data and applies self-attention; this is a non-standard application needing justification.
  - *Quick check:* Why transpose the input `(T x d)` to `(d x T)` before applying multi-head attention?
- **Concept: Forecast decomposition (trend + residual)**
  - *Why needed:* The two-head output (`y_trend`, `y_event`) explicitly separates stable historical patterns from event adjustments.
  - *Quick check:* How does a two-head design help with interpretability versus a single-head model?

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map:**
  Event Database -> LLM Reasoning Module (frozen) -> Text Summary Parser -> Text Tokenizer -> Text Embeddings (learnable) -> h_sem
  Historical Data -> Historical Transformer Encoder -> h_hist
  h_hist -> Linear Projection (to d_align) -> + (add) -> h_aligned -> Residual FFN (L layers) -> 2 Heads (y_trend, y_event) -> λ-weighted sum -> Forecast
- **Critical path:** Data flows from operational databases through the frozen LLM to generate a summary. The summary is tokenized and embedded. Historical time-series features are encoded. The two embeddings are added and passed through a trained FFN to produce the final prediction. Training updates only the historical encoder, projection layer, and FFN.
- **Design tradeoffs:**
  - *LLM choice:* Larger models improve reasoning but increase latency/cost; frozen avoids training costs but limits domain adaptation.
  - *Prompt engineering:* Requires expert input to cover all business logic; more questions increase token cost and failure points.
  - *Fusion simplicity:* Additive fusion is computationally light but may underfit complex interactions between history and events.
  - *λ static weight:* Set to 0.4 after validation; may not be optimal for all regions or event types, but avoids complex dynamic gating.
  - *Tokenizer mismatch:* Using a general LLM tokenizer (GLM) on business jargon risks unknown tokens and information loss.
- **Failure signatures:**
  - *Parsing failure:* LLM output missing `<result>` tags or inconsistent field counts crashes the pipeline.
  - *Event omission:* LLM fails to capture overlapping events or misrates intensity (e.g., "major" vs. "mega" sale).
  - *Historical overfitting:* λ too low (e.g., <0.2) causes model to ignore event signals, reverting to naive extrapolation during spikes.
  - *Training instability:* Gradients from the dual paths may conflict if historical and event features are poorly correlated; mitigated by residual connections and batch norm.
- **First 3 experiments:**
  1. **Prompt validation audit:** Manually inspect 100 LLM outputs across normal, single-event, multi-event, and typo-filled dates. Verify semantic accuracy of key fields (holiday day, campaign level). Iterate prompt until error rate <5%.
  2. **Fusion mechanism ablation:** On an event-heavy validation set, compare additive fusion (as in paper) against concatenation and a single-head model that concatenates `h_hist` and `h_sem` before the FFN. Measure MAE/MSE; additive should match or beat others, especially at T+1.
  3. **λ sweep on event dates:** Sweep λ from 0 to 1 in 0.1 increments on dates flagged as "event-driven" only. Expect optimal λ ~0.4. If optimal is near 0 or 1, the dual-path design is not effectively balancing signals.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
### Open Question 1
How can the prompt template for LLM-based event reasoning be automatically optimized or adapted for new event types, regions, or business contexts without manual expert intervention? The paper's prompt design is manual and relies on business specialists; no automation method is proposed.

### Open Question 2
What is the theoretical or empirical reason that the dual-tower architecture (separate encoding followed by addition) outperforms alternative fusion methods (e.g., concatenation, attention-based fusion) in integrating historical and event features, especially at short forecasting horizons? The paper attributes success to avoiding "dilution" but lacks formal analysis.

### Open Question 3
To what extent does EventCast generalize to e-commerce markets with different cultural event calendars, sparse business records, or novel promotion structures beyond the four countries studied? No cross-country zero-shot/few-shot tests are conducted; the per-country expert database may limit transfer.

### Open Question 4
Can the fusion weight λ be learned dynamically per instance or time period based on contextual signals (e.g., event density, historical volatility, forecast uncertainty) to optimize accuracy beyond a static value? The paper fixes λ=0.4; an adaptive mechanism could handle heterogeneity.

### Open Question 5
What is the long-term operational cost and effort required to maintain the expert database as business events evolve, and how does this compare to the feature engineering costs of traditional or baseline systems? The paper claims reduced overhead but provides no quantitative maintenance analysis.

## Limitations
- **LLM specification & prompt engineering are opaque:** Exact model version, API parameters, and final prompt template are undisclosed; minor variations could alter summary quality.
- **Event database schema & labeling are proprietary:** Logic for campaign levels (1–12) and "event-driven" flags is unknown, preventing exact recreation of training labels.
- **Hyperparameter details are missing:** Key training settings (attention heads, FFN width, dropout, learning schedule, λ search range, train/val split) are omitted, limiting precise replication.

## Confidence
- **Mechanism 1 (LLM semantic extraction):** Medium. Strong paper evidence and ablation support, but reliance on frozen LLM without fine-tuning is risky; corpus shows weak direct support for robustness to noisy operational text.
- **Mechanism 2 (Dual-tower fusion):** Medium. Ablation validates additive fusion, but the assumption that linear fusion suffices for nonlinear interactions is not thoroughly stress-tested.
- **Mechanism 3 (Decoupled prediction):** High. Clear empirical gap vs. direct LLM forecasters and strong theoretical rationale; consistent with broader LLM limitations in numeric tasks.

## Next Checks
1. **LLM summary accuracy audit:** Manually verify 100 random dates (normal/event/edge-case) for correct field extraction and cultural nuance. Measure consistency (same input → same output). Failure indicates prompt refinement needed.
2. **Fusion modality ablation:** On an event-heavy validation set, compare additive fusion against concatenation and a simple attention-based fusion. If additive underperforms, the core architectural claim is weakened.
3. **λ sensitivity sweep on event dates:** Sweep λ ∈ [0, 1] only on dates labeled as event-driven. Optimal λ should be ~0.4. If optimal is near 0 or 1, the dual-path design isn't effectively balancing signals.
