# X-REFINE: XAI-based RElevance input-Filtering and archItecture fiNe-tuning for channel Estimation

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2602.22277
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.22277
- **Reference Count:** 13
- **Primary Result:** Proposes a joint input-filtering and architecture fine-tuning framework using LRP-based relevance scores, achieving up to 62.41% FLOPs reduction while maintaining robust BER performance for 6G channel estimation.

## Executive Summary
X-REFINE tackles the black-box nature and computational inefficiency of deep learning models for 6G OFDM channel estimation. Unlike prior perturbation-based XAI methods that only filter inputs, it introduces a double optimization strategy. By employing a decomposition-based, sign-stabilized Layer-wise Relevance Propagation (LRP-ε) rule, the framework derives high-resolution relevance scores for both input subcarriers and hidden neurons. These scores guide a structured grid search to prune irrelevant inputs and redundant neurons simultaneously, followed by brief retraining. Extensive simulations demonstrate that X-REFINE significantly reduces model complexity (e.g., up to 62.41% FLOPs) across varying channel conditions and modulation orders, all while preserving bit error rate (BER) performance, establishing a superior interpretability-performance-complexity trade-off.

## Method Summary
The method begins by training a baseline fully-connected neural network (FNN) on channel estimation data. For the trained model, X-REFINE computes global relevance scores using a sign-stabilized LRP-ε rule, attributing the output estimate back to individual input subcarriers and each neuron in the hidden layers. It then formulates a double optimization problem to find binary masks for inputs (`m_in`) and architecture (`m_arch`) that minimize computational cost (FLOPs) subject to a BER constraint. This is solved via a structured grid search over a relevance threshold `τ` (for inputs) and a neuron pruning percentile `P` (for architecture). For each `(τ, P)` candidate, masks are applied, the pruned model is briefly retrained, and validation loss and BER are evaluated. The mask pair yielding the lowest complexity that meets the BER target is selected to produce the final optimized model `F*`.

## Key Results
- Achieves up to **62.41% FLOPs reduction** for LF-QPSK, outperforming prior XAI-CHEST (39.51%).
- Maintains robust **BER performance** across different modulation schemes (QPSK/64QAM) and channel frequency selectivity (LF/HF).
- Establishes a **superior trade-off** between interpretability (via sparse relevance), performance (BER), and complexity (FLOPs).

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1
- **Claim:** Joint input filtering and architecture pruning reduces computational complexity more than optimizing inputs alone while maintaining BER.
- **Mechanism:** LRP-derived scores apply two binary masks: a value-based threshold `τ` on input subcarrier scores and a percentile-based threshold `P` on hidden neuron scores. The masks are optimized via grid search, and the pruned model is retrained.
- **Core Assumption:** Relevance scores accurately reflect each subcarrier's and neuron's contribution; pruning low-scoring elements does not harm core functionality.
- **Evidence Anchors:** [section III.A] defines the double optimization; [corpus] simulation results show superior FLOPs reduction vs. input-only methods.
- **Break Condition:** If relevance is not monotonic with performance impact, pruning could degrade BER abruptly. The grid search mitigates but cannot guarantee avoidance.

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim:** The decomposition-based, sign-stabilized LRP-ε rule yields more stable and interpretable relevance distributions for complex-valued OFDM estimation than perturbation-based XAI.
- **Mechanism:** LRP backpropagates the prediction through the network’s weights using a conservation law and an ε-stabilizer to prevent numerical collapse while preserving sign polarity. Relevance is initialized with `|ĥ|` to handle complex regression.
- **Core Assumption:** Backpropagation through trained weights provides more faithful attribution than external input perturbation.
- **Evidence Anchors:** [section III.B] details LRP-ε; [section IV.B] shows sparser distributions vs. XAI-CHEST in Fig. 2.
- **Break Condition:** If the FNN has highly non-linear/activation patterns, LRP’s linear decomposition may produce scores that do not correlate with actual influence, leading to mispruning.

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim:** The structured grid search over discrete `τ` and `P` effectively navigates the non-monotonic trade-off space to find `(m*in, m*arch)` minimizing FLOPs subject to a BER constraint.
- **Mechanism:** For each `(τ, P)`, masks are applied, the pruned model is briefly retrained, and validation loss/BER are checked. The pair with lowest loss satisfying `BER ≤ BER_target` is selected. The search ranges and step sizes (e.g., `τ` from 0 to max relevance, `P` from 0% to 90%) are heuristically bounded to cover Pareto-optimal regions without exhaustive cost.
- **Core Assumption:** The optimal `(τ, P)` lies within a discrete, bounded search space and retraining after pruning recovers minor performance loss.
- **Evidence Anchors:** [section III.A] describes the grid search; [Algorithm 1] outlines the loop; [section IV.D] gives specific `(τ, P)` values used and search steps.
- **Break Condition:** If the BER constraint is too strict, the search may find no valid pair, defaulting to no pruning. Retraining cost also scales with search space size; insufficient granularity may miss optimal points.

## Foundational Learning
- **Concept: Layer-wise Relevance Propagation (LRP)**
  - Why needed: X-REFINE’s core innovation over perturbation-XAI is using LRP to attribute the channel estimate back to subcarriers and neurons, enabling targeted pruning.
  - Quick check: How does the sign-stabilized ε term in Eq. (6) prevent numerical instability while preserving the polarity of relevance contributions during backpropagation through complex-valued weights?
  - Unknown: The exact impact of the ε parameter on relevance sparsity across different channel conditions is not analyzed.

- **Concept: Feed-forward Neural Network (FNN) for Channel Estimation**
  - Why needed: Pruning decisions depend on the specific FNN architecture (e.g., 3 hidden layers) and neuron roles.
  - Quick check: Why might the first hidden layer consistently be preserved (minimal pruning) across scenarios according to the results? (Hypothesis: Early layers learn robust, low-level features less sensitive to channel variations.)
  - Unknown: Whether findings generalize to deeper or convolutional architectures is untested.

- **Concept: Mean Squared Error (MSE) vs. Bit Error Rate (BER) as Objectives**
  - Why needed: The double optimization minimizes MSE but constrains BER, reflecting that channel estimation quality is ultimately measured by communication BER.
  - Quick check: Explain why minimizing training MSE on `(h, ĥ)` does not guarantee minimal final BER after the channel estimate is used in demodulation. (Answer: BER depends on post-equalization decision regions, not just estimation error magnitude; non-linear effects and modulation-specific noise can decouple MSE from symbol errors.)
  - Assumption: A validation BER constraint during grid search effectively bounds final system BER; however, the paper does not prove this surrogate relationship holds universally.

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map:** Pre-trained FNN -> LRP Engine -> Mask Generator -> Prune & Retrain Module -> Selector
- **Critical path:** LRP scoring (Phase I) -> grid search loop (mask -> retrain -> evaluate) -> best mask selection -> final optimized model `F*`.
- **Design tradeoffs:**
  - *Value-based (τ) vs. percentile-based (P):* Inputs use absolute threshold to exploit known sparsity (e.g., pilot subcarriers); architecture uses percentile to enforce structural sparsity and prevent layer collapse.
  - *Retraining necessity:* Essential to recover performance after abrupt weight removal, but multiplies grid search cost.
  - *Search space granularity:* Finer steps yield better trade-offs but increase trials; balance precision vs. time. The paper uses step sizes like 0.01 for τ and 5% for P.
- **Failure signatures:**
  - **BER spike after pruning:** Indicates critical neuron/subcarrier removal; check if first layer was pruned or τ too stringent.
  - **Zero neurons pruned in middle layer:** Relevance scores may be uniformly high, suggesting overcapacity or failed LRP discrimination.
  - **Retraining fails to recover BER:** Aggressive pruning (`P` too high or `τ` too low); reduce sparsity thresholds.
  - **High FLOPs reduction but poor BER:** Search missed valid `(τ, P)` due to insufficient grid coverage or too strict `BER_target`.
- **First 3 experiments:**
  1. **LRP sanity check:** Run LRP on full FNN with small validation set; visualize `R_in`. Expect pilots to score highest; if many data subcarriers score positive, model uses more than just pilots.
  2. **Single-dimension pruning baseline:** Fix architecture, vary only `τ` for input-only optimization; then fix inputs, vary only `P` for architecture-only. Compare joint results to quantify synergy.
  3. **Threshold sensitivity test:** For best `(τ*, P*)`, perturb `τ` and `P` by half-steps and re-evaluate BER. High sensitivity suggests a brittle point; consider a less aggressive choice.

## Limitations
- **Architecture details underspecified:** Exact FNN layer sizes, activations, and initialization beyond the example (15-15-15) are not provided, limiting reproducibility and analysis of how architecture affects pruning patterns.
- **Physical channel parameters missing:** LF/HF channel models (e.g., delay spread, Doppler spectrum) are not specified, hindering accurate data generation replication and assessment of robustness to specific channel conditions.
- **Implementation ambiguity:** Pseudocode for LRP-ε on complex-valued regression is absent; details like handling complex weights and initialization with `|ĥ|` could vary, altering relevance scores.
- **Hyperparameter gaps:** Grid search ranges (τ min/max, P steps) and retraining protocol (epochs, learning-rate schedule, early stopping) are omitted, making it unclear how optimal `(τ, P)` were found and whether results are sensitive to these choices.
- **Monotonicity assumption unverified:** The core assumption that relevance scores monotonically correlate with performance impact is not validated. Non-monotonicity could cause catastrophic BER degradation if low-score elements are unexpectedly critical.
- **Generalizability uncertain:** Experiments are limited to specific channel models (EPA/EVA) and modulation orders; it is unknown if X-REFINE transfers to other 6G scenarios (e.g., mmWave, massive MIMO) or different NN architectures (e.g., CNNs, transformers).
- **Computational cost of search:** The grid search’s overhead (multiple retraining runs) is not quantified; for large models or tight latency constraints, this cost may offset FLOPs gains.

## Confidence
- **Joint input-filtering + architecture pruning superiority while maintaining BER:** **Medium** (strong empirical results across multiple scenarios, but relies on unverified LRP monotonicity and undisclosed retraining details that could affect reproducibility.)
- **Decomposition-based sign-stabilized LRP-ε yields more stable relevance than perturbation-based XAI:** **Medium** (visual evidence in Fig. 2 shows sparser distributions, but no quantitative stability metrics or comparison on same validation set; perturbation methods might be tuned differently.)
- **Structured grid search reliably finds optimal (τ, P) under BER constraint:** **Low** (heuristic search with undisclosed ranges/step size may miss global optima; no convergence analysis or comparison to Bayesian optimization; success heavily dependent on retraining recovery.)

## Next Checks
1.  **Monotonicity of relevance scores:** Systematically ablate subcarriers/neurons ranked by LRP score in the full model (without retraining) and measure BER increase. Compute correlation between score and performance drop; low correlation would violate the core assumption.
2.  **Ablation of joint vs. separate pruning:** Run identical grid searches for input-only (fixed architecture) and architecture-only (fixed inputs) baselines. Compare Pareto fronts of FLOPs reduction vs. BER to quantify claimed synergy and identify if joint optimization consistently dominates.
3.  **Robustness to retraining hyperparameters:** Vary retraining epochs (e.g., 100 vs. 500 vs. 1000) and learning-rate schedules after masking. Confirm that reported BER recovery and FLOPs reductions hold under less favorable (shorter or less aggressive) retraining, which would better reflect real-world fine-tuning constraints.
4.  **Sensitivity to search grid resolution:** Repeat grid search with finer (e.g., τ step 0.005, P step 1%) and coarser steps. Assess stability of selected `(τ*, P*)` and resulting FLOPs/BER to validate that reported results are not artifacts of a particular granularity.
5.  **Generalization test:** Apply X-REFINE to a different channel model (e.g., with higher mobility or different delay profile) and/or a different FNN architecture (e.g., deeper or with ReLU). Measure whether FLOPs reduction and BER preservation hold, testing the framework’s robustness.
