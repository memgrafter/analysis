# From Baselines to Preferences: A Comparative Study of LoRA/QLoRA and Preference Optimization for Mental Health Text Classification

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2604.00773
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.00773
- **Reference count:** 27
- **Primary result:** QLoRA with generative objectives peaks at macro-F1 0.3399; ORPO with class rebalancing achieves best overall macro-F1 0.3798.

## Executive Summary
This study tackles the lack of practical guidance for selecting optimization strategies in mental health text classification. Using the DAIC-WOZ dataset, the authors conduct a controlled comparison across three stages: classical/encoder baselines (XGBoost, BERT), supervised fine-tuning with LoRA/QLoRA (discriminative vs. generative objectives), and preference optimization (DPO, ORPO, KTO). The evaluation focuses on macro-F1 scores for a 4-class joint classification derived from PHQ-4 scores.

Key findings reveal that generative objectives in supervised fine-tuning consistently outperform discriminative ones, with QLoRA reaching a peak macro-F1 of 0.3399. Among preference methods, ORPO proves superior, and when combined with class rebalancing it yields the best overall result (macro-F1 0.3798). Notably, an optimized BERT baseline remains a strong, stable performer (macro-F1 0.3518). The work concludes that the choice and configuration of methods are more critical than simply adding advanced stages, offering a clear, reproducible optimization narrative for practitioners.

## Method Summary
The study employs a three-stage experimental pipeline on the DAIC-WOZ conversational dataset. First, discriminative baselines are established: XGBoost using TF-IDF features and BERT-family encoders (bert-base, distilbert, roberta) fine-tuned across context window sizes (94, 192, 384, 512 tokens) with optional temperature scaling. Second, parameter-efficient fine-tuning (PEFT) via LoRA and QLoRA is applied to an LLaMA-family model, comparing discriminative (cross-entropy) and generative (causal language modeling) objectives. Generative outputs follow three schemas: label only, label with confidence, or label with rationale. Optimizers (AdamW, Adafactor, Adam8bit) are varied. Third, preference optimization methods DPO, ORPO, and KTO are layered on top of the best SFT configurations, testing both vanilla and class-rebalanced training profiles. All experiments report macro-F1 as the primary metric, alongside weighted-F1, precision, and recall.

## Key Results
- Generative SFT consistently outperforms discriminative SFT; QLoRA with a "label only" generative schema achieves a peak macro-F1 of 0.3399.
- ORPO is the strongest preference optimization method; its rebalanced variant attains the overall best macro-F1 of 0.3798, significantly surpassing DPO and KTO.
- An optimized BERT baseline (temperature-scaled) yields a stable macro-F1 of 0.3518, serving as a robust reference point.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1
- **Claim:** Generative objectives in supervised fine-tuning (SFT) yield higher and more stable performance than discriminative objectives for this mental health joint classification task.
- **Mechanism:** Treating classification as constrained text generation (e.g., generating "label only" or "label confidence" strings) may encourage the model to learn more robust, contextualized representations by mapping input text to a structured output space, rather than a direct class probability distribution. This could reduce overfitting on sparse discriminative signals in the limited dataset.
- **Core assumption:** The generative formulation provides a richer optimization landscape or better inductive bias for the multimodal, conversational DAIC-WOZ texts.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract]: "generative objectives in SFT consistently outperform discriminative ones"
  - [section 5.2]: "generative setups achieve higher peak and average F1 than discriminative setups across both profiles and adapter modes"
- **Break condition:** If the dataset were significantly larger or the labels more straightforward, the discriminative gap might close. If the generative schema (e.g., "label rationale") introduces noise, performance could drop below discriminative levels.

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim:** ORPO is the most effective and robust preference optimization method for this task, showing significant gains from class rebalancing where DPO shows moderate gains and KTO shows none.
- **Mechanism:** ORPO's monolithic objective (combining preference loss and a reference-model-free likelihood term) may interact more favorably with the reweighted loss from class rebalancing, leading to more stable and targeted updates. Its odds-ratio-based contrast may also be less sensitive to the specific reward model initialization issues that can plague DPO.
- **Core assumption:** The preference learning signal in this mental health context is best captured by ORPO's specific loss formulation, and class imbalance disproportionately harms pairwise methods that rely on relative comparisons.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract]: "ORPO is superior, particularly when combined with class rebalancing"
  - [section 5.3]: "ORPO is the strongest method... rebalancing yields major gains for ORPO and DPO. In contrast, KTO remains unchanged"
  - [section 5.4]: "ORPO rebalanced... exceeds all other rows in macro-F1"
- **Break condition:** If the preference data quality or pairing strategy changes, ORPO's advantage might diminish. On a task with different class distributions, KTO's static behavior might become beneficial.

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim:** Class rebalancing during preference training is a high-impact intervention for ORPO and DPO, but not for KTO, indicating that the sensitivity to data distribution is method-specific.
- **Mechanism:** Rebalancing (likely via loss weighting or sampling) corrects the skewed optimization signal. For ORPO and DPO, which optimize based on pairwise or relative preferences, a balanced view of minority-class preferences prevents the model from ignoring them. KTO's use of binary ("good"/"bad") labels per instance, independent of pairwise comparison, may make it inherently less sensitive to the overall class distribution in the training set.
- **Core assumption:** The core limitation for ORPO/DPO is the under-representation of minority-class preferences in the pairwise or reference comparisons, which rebalancing directly addresses.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract]: "class rebalancing being decisive for some objectives but not others"
  - [section 5.3]: "rebalanced... ORPO improves markedly... DPO also benefits from rebalancing... KTO remains unchanged"
  - [section 3.2/4.8]: The paper explicitly designs a "Rebalanced" profile for preference methods to test this exact question.
- **Break condition:** If preference pairs were constructed to be inherently balanced (e.g., forced pairing across classes), the rebalancing intervention might show no effect for any method.

## Foundational Learning
### Discriminative vs. Generative Objectives
- **Why needed:** The core SFT comparison hinges on this distinction. Understanding that "generative" means predicting a text string (like "anxiety") vs. "discriminative" means predicting a class index is key to interpreting why one might work better.
- **Quick check:** If a model is trained to generate the string "depression" for a depressed input, what underlying representation might it learn that differs from a model trained to output class 2?

### Parameter-Efficient Fine-Tuning (PEFT: LoRA/QLoRA)
- **Why needed:** All advanced stages (SFT and preference) use LoRA/QLoRA. A practitioner must understand that these are adapter methods that freeze the base model and train low-rank matrices, affecting both performance and memory footprint.
- **Quick check:** What is the fundamental trade-off between LoRA and QLoRA as described in the paper?

### Preference Optimization Objectives (DPO/ORPO/KTO)
- **Why needed:** The paper's climax is comparing these three. Knowing that DPO uses a reference model, ORPO is reference-model-free, and KTO uses binary "good/bad" labels (not pairs) is essential to grasp their different behaviors.
- **Quick check:** Why might KTO be less sensitive to class rebalancing than DPO or ORPO based on their objective definitions?

## Architecture Onboarding
#### Component Map
Input Processor → Backbone Models (XGBoost, BERT, LLaMA) → Adaptation Modules (LoRA/QLoRA) → Training Heads (Discriminative, Generative, Preference) → Optimizers (AdamW, Adafactor, Adam8bit) → Post-Processing (Temperature scaling, Class rebalancing)

#### Critical Path
Raw Text → Windowing → Encoder/LLM Backbone → [Adapter] → Task Head → Loss (CE/Seq2Seq/Preference) → Optimizer → Evaluation (Macro/Weighted F1)

#### Design Tradeoffs
- **Stability vs. Peak Performance:** QLoRA + Adam8bit can yield high peaks (e.g., 0.3399) but lower mean scores, indicating instability. LoRA + Adafactor provides more stable means.
- **Objective Complexity:** "Label only" generative schema is strongest/most stable; adding "confidence" or "rationale" introduces variance.
- **Cost of Preference Training:** ORPO Rebalanced gives the best score (0.3798) but requires constructing preference pairs and rebalancing, adding pipeline complexity over standard SFT.

#### Failure Signatures
- KTO consistently fails to improve over baselines (Best F1=0.120), indicating it's unsuitable for this multi-class setting.
- DistilBERT/RoBERTa show high variance with larger windows (e.g., collapse at 512), suggesting context length tuning is critical.
- "Label rationale" generative schema is consistently weakest, indicating the supervision signal may be too noisy or difficult for the model capacity.

#### First 3 Experiments
1. **Establish the Encoder Baseline:** Train `bert-base-uncased` with vanilla and temperature-optimized profiles across windows (94, 192, 384, 512). Confirm peak at window 384 (Optimized F1=0.3518). This sets the strong, stable reference.
2. **Probe SFT Objective Impact:** For a single adapter mode (e.g., QLoRA) and optimizer (e.g., Adam8bit), compare `Vanilla Generative (label only)` vs. `Vanilla Discriminative` at the best window from Exp 1 (384/512). Expect generative to show clear superior F1 (~0.34 vs ~0.30). This validates the generative shift.
3. **Test Preference Method Sensitivity:** Using the best SFT configuration as a backbone (e.g., QLoRA Generative label only), run `Vanilla ORPO`, `Vanilla DPO`, and `Vanilla KTO`. Then repeat with `Rebalanced` versions. Expect ORPO to be strongest, DPO to gain from rebalancing, KTO to show no change. This isolates the preference method effect and rebalancing impact.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
### Q1: Dataset characteristics for ORPO+rebalancing superiority
- **Basis:** The abstract and introduction state the paper addresses "which optimization strategy to use, when, and why," and results show ORPO rebalanced achieves the best overall macro-F1 (0.3798). The methodology explicitly tests rebalancing as a profile for preference methods.
- **Why unresolved:** The study is limited to the DAIC-WOZ dataset; conditions for ORPO's superiority (e.g., imbalance severity) are not isolated or varied systematically.
- **Resolving evidence:** Multi-dataset experiments across mental health corpora with controlled variations in class distribution, data size, and text properties, complemented by statistical tests comparing effect sizes of rebalancing across datasets.

### Q2: Why "label only" generative schema outperforms "confidence" and "rationale"
- **Basis:** Table 6 shows "label only" consistently achieves higher mean and best macro-F1 in generative settings, while confidence and rationale show higher variance and lower averages. The text notes these schemas "introduce performance volatility."
- **Why unresolved:** The paper reports the pattern but does not investigate underlying causes, such as whether auxiliary outputs (confidence/rationale) introduce noise, optimization difficulties, or misalignment with the primary task.
- **Resolving evidence:** Ablation studies comparing loss curves, error analyses of model outputs for each schema, and perhaps probing whether confidence/rationale generation distracts from the core classification objective.

### Q3: Interaction between context window size and optimization strategy
- **Basis:** The methodology evaluates multiple context windows and notes model-specific window sensitivity (e.g., BERT peaks at 256–384, XGBoost benefits up to 512). Results tables show varying best windows per family.
- **Why unresolved:** While window effects are reported, interaction effects between window size and optimization method (SFT objective, preference type) are not formally analyzed; window choices may be confounded with model architecture.
- **Resolving evidence:** Factorial experimental designs crossing window sizes with method families, using mixed-effects models to quantify interaction significance, and identifying window ranges that maximize gains for each optimization strategy.

### Q4: Generalizability to other mental health tasks and datasets
- **Basis:** The study focuses solely on the DAIC-WOZ dataset for a joint PHQ-4-based classification task. The conclusion proposes an "optimization narrative" but acknowledges the work does not introduce a new benchmark, implying limited external validation.
- **Why unresolved:** Mental health tasks vary in linguistic markers (e.g., suicidal ideation vs. depressive symptoms) and data sources (e.g., social media vs. clinical interviews); robustness of the narrative to such variations is unknown.
- **Resolving evidence:** Replication studies on diverse mental health datasets (e.g., Reddit, clinical notes) using the same optimization comparison protocol, reporting whether the same method rankings hold across tasks and domains.

## Limitations
- Base LLM identity and exact version (e.g., Llama-2-7B vs. Llama-3-8B) are not specified.
- Preference pair construction methodology is not detailed, hindering reproducibility of DPO/ORPO/KTO.
- Hyperparameters (LoRA rank, learning rates, epochs) and generative label schema format are missing, blocking exact replication.
- Generalizability is restricted to the DAIC-WOZ dataset and LLaMA-family models.

## Confidence
- Generative SFT superiority: High
- ORPO as best preference method: High
- Rebalancing sensitivity differs by method: High
- Broader applicability to other mental health tasks: Low
