# Certified Per-Instance Unlearning Using Individual Sensitivity Bounds

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2602.15602
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.15602
- Reference count: 40
- Primary result: Certified per-instance unlearning with adaptive noise injection, reducing required noise by up to 5× for ridge regression and showing empirical per-instance heterogeneity in deep models.

## Executive Summary
The paper tackles the inefficiency of using uniform worst-case sensitivity bounds in certified machine unlearning. It introduces per-instance noise calibration that tailors the amount of injected noise to each data point's actual influence on the learned model. By combining per-instance differential privacy with the shifted interpolation framework, the authors derive high-probability sensitivity bounds for ridge regression trained via Langevin dynamics. This yields (ε, δ)-unlearning guarantees with substantially less noise compared to uniform calibration. Experiments on MNIST with linear ridge regression confirm that per-instance calibration improves the accuracy-privacy trade-off, with noise requirements varying by up to a factor of five across points. Additionally, empirical audits on CIFAR-10 using a non-linear model reveal heterogeneous effective ε under fixed noise, suggesting that per-instance unlearning difficulty persists beyond convex settings.

## Method Summary
The method operates in two phases: learning and unlearning. During learning, a ridge regression model is trained on the full dataset using Langevin dynamics, which adds Gaussian noise at each gradient step to achieve differential privacy. The training is analyzed via the shifted interpolation privacy accounting technique, which tracks the privacy loss along the combined learn‑then‑unlearn trajectory and exploits contraction of the gradient updates under strong convexity. The key innovation is per‑instance sensitivity calibration: for a deletion request of point i, the authors compute high‑probability, point‑specific bounds s_i,k on the per‑step gradient contribution of that point. These bounds are derived analytically by exploiting the Gaussian residual isotropy property of the Langevin iterates in the linear ridge setting. The noise scale for the unlearning phase is then chosen as the smallest σ that satisfies the target (ε, δ) budget given the accumulated privacy loss from both learning and unlearning. Unlearning proceeds by running additional Langevin steps on the dataset without point i, initialized from the trained model and using the calibrated σ. The entire process provides a certified (ε, δ)-unlearning guarantee tailored to each data point.

## Key Results
- Per‑instance noise calibration reduces required unlearning noise by up to 5× compared to uniform sensitivity for ridge regression on MNIST, leading to better accuracy‑privacy trade‑offs.
- The framework yields theoretical (ε, δ)-per‑instance unlearning guarantees for strongly convex models trained with Langevin dynamics.
- Empirical privacy audits on CIFAR‑10 show widely varying effective ε across points under identical unlearning noise, indicating that per‑instance heterogeneity exists even in non‑convex deep models.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1: Per‑Instance Sensitivity Calibration
The method tailors the unlearning noise to each point’s actual influence by computing high‑probability bounds s_i,k on the norm of its gradient contribution at every training step. Because these bounds reflect the point’s true contribution, the required noise can be reduced for low‑influence points while still meeting the overall (ε, δ) guarantee. The core assumption is that sharp per‑step sensitivity bounds can be derived analytically for the chosen model and training algorithm, as is done for ridge regression under Langevin dynamics. Evidence: the abstract states the approach “yields substantially less noise injection”; Section 4.3, Theorem 4.3 provides the theoretical bound; related work on per‑instance DP supports the principle.

### Mechanism 2: Shifted Interpolation Privacy Accounting
This technique compares two trajectories: training on the full dataset then unlearning the target point, versus training and retraining on the dataset without that point. By constructing an interpolated process and using contractivity of the gradient updates (c < 1), the total Gaussian DP parameter µ_i can be expressed as a sum of per‑step contributions that incorporate the per‑instance sensitivities. Optimizing the interpolation shifts yields a closed‑form expression for µ_i that amplifies privacy through iteration, achieving tighter (ε, δ) guarantees than generic composition. The method relies on strong convexity and a step size η = 1/L to guarantee contraction. Evidence: the abstract references the adaptation of the shifted interpolation framework; Proposition 4.2 gives the µ‑GDP derivation; Section 2 discusses related work.

### Mechanism 3: High‑Probability Bounds via Gaussian Residual Isotropy
For ridge regression with Langevin dynamics, the per‑sample residuals are Gaussian with isotropic covariance due to the linear model and independent output‑wise noise. Consequently, the squared residual normalized by its variance follows a non‑central χ² distribution. This property enables deriving sharp high‑probability bounds on the per‑step sensitivity via a χ² quantile. A union bound over all training steps ensures the bounds hold uniformly with high confidence. The resulting deterministic bound s_i,k = η∥x_i∥ √v_i,k · q_i,k(1−δ_s/T) is used in the privacy accounting. This mechanism is specific to the linear‑Gaussian setting; extending it to non‑linear models is non‑trivial. Evidence: Proposition 4.4 and Lemmas C.2–C.3 in the appendix provide the exact formulas and recursions.

## Foundational Learning
#### Differential Privacy & Gaussian Differential Privacy (GDP)
- **Why needed:** Unlearning guarantees are expressed via (ε, δ)-DP; GDP offers tighter composition for iterative noisy algorithms and is used for privacy accounting throughout the paper.
- **Quick check:** How does per-instance DP differ from standard DP? (Answer: Both satisfy the DP inequality, but per-instance DP allows the privacy loss ε to vary with the data point, tailoring protection to individual contributions.)

#### Sensitivity (Global vs. Per‑Instance)
- **Why needed:** The noise scale in DP is proportional to sensitivity. Uniform (global) sensitivity often overestimates the influence of typical points, causing unnecessary utility loss. Per‑instance sensitivity instead quantifies the actual effect of a specific point, enabling adaptive noise injection.
- **Quick check:** How is the per‑instance sensitivity Δ_i,k defined in this work? (Answer: Δ_i,k = η∥∇θℓ(θ_k; x_i, y_i)∥, the norm of that point’s gradient contribution at step k.)

#### Langevin Dynamics as Noisy Optimization
- **Why needed:** Langevin dynamics injects Gaussian noise at each update, which can be used to satisfy DP. Its discrete version with appropriate step size also satisfies the contraction property required for shifted interpolation, enabling privacy amplification by iteration.
- **Quick check:** Why Langevin instead of standard gradient descent? (Answer: Langevin inherently provides randomness that can be accounted for to achieve DP, and under strong convexity with step size η = 1/L, the updates contract, which is essential for the shifted‑interpolation accounting.)

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component map (plain text):**  
Preprocessing (X, Y, A, B) → Training (T Langevin steps, θ_T) → Sensitivity‑bound computation (s_i,k) → Noise calibration (σ_unlearn) → Unlearning (K Langevin steps, θ_{T+K}) → Output model.

**Critical path:**  
1. Complete the learning phase (T steps) to obtain the initial model θ_T.  
2. For each deletion request i:  
   a. Compute the per‑step sensitivity bounds s_i,k for k = 0…T using closed‑form recursions.  
   b. Calibrate the unlearning noise scale σ_unlearn by finding the minimal σ such that the target (ε, δ) is satisfied given the accumulated privacy loss.  
   c. Run K unlearning steps on D_{-i} with σ_unlearn starting from θ_T to produce the updated model θ_{T+K}.  
The sensitivity computation does not require simulating random trajectories; it depends only on the data and hyperparameters and can be pre‑computed.

**Design tradeoffs:**  
- *Precomputation vs. latency:* Computing s_i,k for all n training points avoids per‑request delay but costs O(nT) time and O(n) storage; for large n one may compute on demand or subsample representative points.  
- *Memory usage:* Storing the full design matrix X (n×p) can be heavy; recomputing inner products trades compute for memory.  
- *δ‑splitting:* The total δ is split into δ_s (sensitivity tail) and δ_m (composition). Smaller δ_s yields tighter sensitivity bounds but consumes more of the δ‑budget, affecting final ε; this hyperparameter needs careful tuning.  
- *Unlearning horizon K:* Larger K reduces required σ_unlearn for a fixed ε due to more contraction and noise spreading, but increases computation; diminishing returns typically appear, so K should be chosen based on utility‑speed trade‑offs.

**Failure signatures:**  
- *Sensitivity bound violation:* If the observed gradient norm exceeds s_i,k with probability > δ_s, the (ε, δ) guarantee is broken (δ budget exceeded). This may indicate inaccurate model assumptions or numerical instability.  
- *Contraction failure:* Using η ≠ 1/L or encountering a non‑strongly convex objective makes the contraction factor c ≥ 1, causing the privacy amplification to be over‑estimated and likely leading to under‑injection of noise.  
- *GDP mis‑conversion:* Incorrect conversion between the GDP parameter µ and (ε, δ) yields overly optimistic privacy estimates; the implementation must follow the exact formula (e.g., Dong et al. 2022).  
- *Non‑linear extension pitfalls:* Applying the same calibrated noise to deep models without certified guarantees risks providing meaningless (ε, δ); empirical auditing is needed instead.

**First experiments:**  
1. **Reproduce Figure 5:** Train multi‑output ridge regression on MNIST (ResNet‑50 features) with fixed hyperparameters. For several deletion points (covering low to high sensitivity), compute per‑instance calibrated unlearning and compare test accuracy to the uniform baseline across ε. Verify that per‑instance curves dominate the uniform baseline.  
2. **Reproduce Figure 8:** For a fixed target ε (e.g., ε=1), compute the required σ_unlearn for many points (or the 7 representative quantiles). Confirm that noise levels vary by up to 5×, illustrating the heterogeneity in per‑instance requirements.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
1. **Extend certified per‑instance unlearning to non‑convex models.**  
   *Basis:* The conclusion identifies the contraction assumption as essential but not holding for deep networks, calling for per‑instance guarantees for more general dynamics.  
   *Why unresolved:* Without contraction, the shifted interpolation privacy accounting fails; no current method provides certified per‑instance unlearning for non‑convex settings.

2. **Explain and predict heterogeneity in effective ε across data points in deep unlearning.**  
   *Basis:* CIFAR‑10 experiments show that fixing σ_unlearn leads to ε_i varying widely; this heterogeneity is not theoretically understood.  
   *Why unresolved:* No prior work explains why some points are harder to unlearn in non‑convex models; lacking predictive metrics.

3. **Reduce computational overhead of per‑instance sensitivity computation for large‑scale settings.**  
   *Basis:* The method stores per‑point quantities and recomputes bounds per deletion; experiments are on small datasets only, leaving scalability unclear.  
   *Why unresolved:* Memory and time costs may scale poorly with n and p, hindering adoption; amortized estimation techniques are needed.

## Limitations
- Certified guarantees are restricted to strongly convex models (ridge regression) with Langevin dynamics; extension to non‑convex/deep models remains empirical.
- The practical benefit hinges on accurate per‑instance sensitivity bounds; any mis‑specification (e.g., violated contraction, inaccurate δ_s split) can compromise the (ε, δ) certificate.
- Non‑linear CIFAR‑10 results rely on empirical privacy auditing without theoretical backing, leaving open whether observed heterogeneity generalizes or reflects auditing flaws.

## Confidence
- **Theoretical framework (shifted interpolation + per‑instance sensitivity):** High – derivations are explicit, assumptions stated, and proof sketch provided.
- **Per‑instance noise reduction (up to 5×) on MNIST ridge regression:** Medium – experimental setup is reproducible but exact hyperparameters (δ_s split, point selection thresholds) are unspecified; reproduction required for confirmation.
- **Empirical per‑instance heterogeneity in non‑linear settings:** Low – auditing methodology details (probe set size, distinguisher architecture) are vague; results may not be robust to implementation choices.
