# Prompting Underestimates LLM Capability for Time Series Classification

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2601.03464
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.03464
- **Reference count:** 40
- **Primary result:** Linear probing of frozen LLM representations yields large improvements in time series classification F1 scores over zero-shot prompting (e.g., average F1 from 0.15–0.26 to 0.61–0.67), revealing that prompting underestimates LLM capabilities.

## Executive Summary
This paper investigates whether the poor performance of large language models (LLMs) on time series classification tasks stems from inherent limitations of the models or from shortcomings in the evaluation methodology—specifically, the reliance on prompt-based zero-shot generation. The authors compare two paradigms: standard zero-shot prompting, where the model generates a textual answer given a time series formatted as text or image, and linear probing, where a simple logistic regression classifier is trained on frozen hidden representations extracted using the same prompts. Across three strong LLMs/vLLMs (Llama, Mistral, Qwen) and six time series datasets, probing improves Macro F1 scores dramatically, from the 0.15–0.26 range under prompting to 0.61–0.67—levels that match or surpass specialized time-series models. Further analysis shows that class-discriminative signals appear early in transformer layers and are enhanced by multimodal (visual + text) inputs. The results expose a systematic mismatch between what LLMs internally represent and what prompt-based evaluations reveal, suggesting that current prompting approaches are a flawed interface for accessing the models' representational capacity on time series tasks.

## Method Summary
The study evaluates LLMs on six time series classification datasets using three input modalities: digit-based text (`d`), visual plots/spectrograms (`v`), and a combination (`d+v`). For each model and modality, the same prompt is used in two evaluation modes. In **prompting**, the model generates a textual answer, and Macro F1 is computed from parsed responses. In **probing**, the final-token hidden states from all transformer layers are extracted, and separate linear (logistic regression) probes are trained on each layer’s training-set representations via 5-fold cross-validation; the resulting classifiers are evaluated on test-set hidden states to obtain layer-wise F1 scores. Baselines include random-weight probes (to establish a practical floor), majority/prior/uniform heuristics, and several state-of-the-art time-series models. Additional controlled experiments assess prompt stability (via multiple prompt variants), model sampling variability, and layer-wise emergence of discriminative information.

## Key Results
- Probing increases average Macro F1 by ~0.4–0.5 over prompting across all models, reaching 0.61–0.67 and matching/exceeding specialized time-series classifiers.
- Visual input modality (`v`) yields the highest linear separability in probe scores; multimodal (`d+v`) also improves over text-only (`d`) and reduces prompting instability.
- Class-discriminative temporal structure emerges in early transformer layers (often within the first 5–20 layers), and visual inputs amplify this early separation.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1
Prompt-based evaluation systematically underestimates LLMs' representational capacity for time series classification because the decoding interface (autoregressive generation) fails to reliably extract discriminative signals that are linearly separable in the model's internal hidden states. While zero-shot prompting performs near chance, linear probes on frozen representations yield large gains (ΔF1 ≈ 0.4–0.5), indicating that class-relevant temporal information is present but inaccessible via the next-token prediction objective. The core assumption is that linear probes diagnose the presence of linearly separable features without requiring the model to actively reason during generation. Evidence includes the consistent gap across models and datasets in Tables 1–2, and the fact that random-weight probes remain at chance, confirming the signal is learned. If prompting were equally effective, the gap would be minimal; the observed gap contradicts that.

### Mechanism 2
Multimodal (visual + text) input representations enhance the linear separability of time series classes compared to text-only, as shown by higher probe scores for `v` and `d+v` over `d`. Visualizations (line plots, spectrograms) provide spatial patterns that are encoded more robustly than digit strings, leading to richer features. This improvement is observed across all models (e.g., Llama’s best probe with `v`: 0.678) and also benefits prompting stability. The underlying assumption is that the gain reflects a genuinely higher quality representation, not an artifact of the probe. The effect is particularly strong on the EMG dataset, and neighbor work on patch-based prompting supports the promise of visual representations. If text-only were equally separable, probe scores would not differ; the consistent superiority of visual modalities rejects that.

### Mechanism 3
Class-discriminative temporal information emerges early in transformer layers (often within the first 5–20), rather than requiring deep hierarchical processing. Layer-wise probing reveals a sharp rise in F1 in early layers followed by a plateau or decline. For instance, Qwen encodes useful information in layers 1–2, Llama peaks around layer 5–10, and Mistral shows a more gradual improvement around layer 12. Visual inputs accelerate and clarify this emergence, producing a clean separation from the random baseline earlier than text-only. This indicates that the initial layers are sufficient to encode features needed for linear separation. If discriminative signals only appeared in final layers, probe scores would increase monotonically with depth; the observed early peak contradicts that.

## Foundational Learning
### Linear Probing as a Diagnostic Tool
**Why needed here:** Probing measures the *latent representational capacity* of frozen LLMs, separate from the *decoding interface* (prompting). It tests whether task-relevant information is linearly separable in hidden states, not whether the model can be prompted to produce the correct answer.
**Quick check question:** If a linear probe achieves high accuracy on frozen LLM representations for a task, what does this *not* necessarily imply about the LLM's behavior during free-form text generation?

### Time Series Tokenization and Visualization Strategies
**Why needed here:** The paper manipulates input modality (`d`, `v`, `d+v`), and understanding how raw time series are transformed into tokens or images is key to interpreting why some modalities work better for probing vs. prompting.
**Quick check question:** Why might digit-based tokenization (`d`) introduce challenges for an LLM's next-token prediction objective compared to its internal processing of the same numerical sequence?

### Baseline-Controlled Comparative Evaluation
**Why needed here:** The paper emphasizes comparing probing results to a random-weight model baseline and specialized time-series models to contextualize whether gains are meaningful beyond trivial feature structure or supervised learning.
**Quick check question:** What is the purpose of the "Random-Probe" baseline (probing a randomly initialized LLM), and what does it control for?

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component map:** Input Transformer -> LLM/vLLM Encoder -> Probe Trainer -> Evaluation Comparator -> Layer Analyzer

**Critical path:** 1. Preprocess each dataset into the three modalities (`d`, `v`, `d+v`). 2. For each model and prompt variant: (a) run the LLM to collect generated outputs for prompting evaluation; (b) extract per-layer hidden states of the final token. 3. For each layer, train an independent logistic regression probe (5-fold CV, C-grid search) on the training-set hidden states and evaluate on the test set. 4. Compare prompting F1, probe F1 (across layers), and baseline scores (random-weight probes, specialized TS models). 5. Analyze layer-wise curves to identify emergence points.

**Design tradeoffs:**
- *Probe complexity*: Linear probes are interpretable and avoid overfitting, but may miss nonlinear separability.
- *Representation fidelity*: Using the same prompt for both paradigms ensures fairness, but the prompt format (e.g., system instruction) may bias the hidden-state content.
- *Modality choice*: `v` yields stronger probe separability but requires a vision-capable LLM; `d` is universal but noisier; `d+v` combines cues but may introduce interference.

**Failure signatures:**
- High variance in prompting F1 across prompt variants (∆F1 > 0.05) indicates instability.
- Probe scores below the random-weight baseline imply no discriminative signal in the representations.
- No early improvement in layer-wise probe curves (i.e., random-like until very deep layers) contradicts the emergence claim.

**First 3 experiments:**
1. Replicate the main gap: For Llama on EMG, confirm probing (`d+v`) F1 >> prompting F1.
2. Modality comparison: For the same model/dataset, run both paradigms on `d`, `v`, `d+v`; verify `v`/`d+v` give highest probe F1 and that `v` is the most prompt-stable.
3. Layer-wise validation: Plot probe F1 vs. layer for `d` and `d+v`; confirm early emergence (peak within first 20 layers) and earlier/clearer separation from random baseline for `d+v`.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
**Open Question 1:** Do LLMs' internal representations contain sufficient discriminative temporal structure for classification, and is prompting a flawed interface for extracting it?
- *Basis:* Explicitly posed in the introduction as the core research question.
- *Why unresolved:* The paper shows a gap but does not explain *why* prompting fails.
- *Resolving evidence:* Analysis of decoding dynamics, output token distributions, or alternative generation constraints.

**Open Question 2:** How much linearly inseparable temporal information do LLMs encode beyond what linear probes can extract?
- *Basis:* The study relies solely on linear probes.
- *Why unresolved:* Non-linear patterns might require more complex probes.
- *Resolving evidence:* Evaluating non-linear probes (e.g., kernel SVMs, shallow neural networks) on the same representations.

**Open Question 3:** Are the findings robust to variations in time series characteristics (e.g., length, dimensionality) and model scales?
- *Basis:* Experiments limited to six datasets and five models.
- *Why unresolved:* Generalizability to other temporal patterns or larger models is unknown.
- *Resolving evidence:* Extending to a more diverse benchmark suite and scaling model parameters.

**Open Question 4:** Why does visual input reduce prompt sensitivity compared to text-based input for time series classification?
- *Basis:* Prompt stability analyses show visual modality is more consistent.
- *Why unresolved:* The cause of this effect is not investigated.
- *Resolving evidence:* Comparative analysis of tokenization artifacts, attention maps, or feature space geometry across modalities.

## Limitations
- Uncertainty in prompt reconstruction (only CTU template provided) and incomplete random-weight baseline across models limit reproducibility.
- The reliance on linear probes may overlook nonlinear discriminative patterns, and findings are confined to six datasets and five LLMs/vLLMs, restricting generalizability.

## Confidence
- Claim 1 (Prompting underestimates capability): High
- Claim 2 (Multimodal enhances separability): Medium
- Claim 3 (Early-layer emergence): Medium
