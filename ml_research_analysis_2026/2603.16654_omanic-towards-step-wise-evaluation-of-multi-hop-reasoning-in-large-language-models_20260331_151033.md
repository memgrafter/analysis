# Omanic: Towards Step-wise Evaluation of Multi-hop Reasoning in Large Language Models

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2603.16654
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.16654
- Reference count: 40
- Primary result: State-of-the-art LLMs achieve 73.11% accuracy on OmanicBench; CoT exhibits a knowledge floor effect; SFT on OmanicSynth improves external benchmarks by 7.41 points on average.

## Executive Summary
The paper introduces Omanic, a benchmark for multi-hop question answering that provides explicit decomposed sub-questions and intermediate answers. This structure enables fine-grained analysis of where reasoning fails in large language models (LLMs). Evaluations show that even top LLMs struggle, achieving only 73.11% accuracy on a carefully curated test set. Step-wise examination reveals a “knowledge floor” effect: Chain-of-Thought (CoT) improvements shrink as missing factual steps accumulate. Moreover, errors amplify in later hops. Training on the synthetically generated OmanicSynth dataset significantly boosts performance on a suite of reasoning and math benchmarks, demonstrating the dataset's quality and transferability.

## Method Summary
Omanic consists of two parts: a machine-generated training set (OmanicSynth, 10,296 instances) and an expert-reviewed test set (OmanicBench, 967 instances). Each instance is a 4-hop question with four sub-questions, intermediate answers, three distractors per sub-question, and a final multiple-choice answer. The questions span eight domains and must include at least one mathematically grounded hop. The data construction pipeline begins with triplet retrieval from Wikidata5M using MuSiQue 2-hop answers as anchors. Constrained synthesis (using Claude-Sonnet-4.5) merges original MuSiQue components with new single-hop questions, enforcing domain constraints, mathematical hops, and one of three reasoning graph topologies (Bridge, Chain, Converging). Automated filtering discards questions correctly answered by an ensemble of four smaller models to maintain difficulty. Finally, expert annotators verify factual accuracy, distractor plausibility, logical integrity, and mathematical correctness. Models are trained via supervised fine-tuning (SFT), either full-parameter or LoRA, and evaluated with Direct and CoT prompting. Transfer effectiveness is measured by average improvement on six external reasoning and math benchmarks.

## Key Results
- State-of-the-art LLMs achieve 73.11% accuracy on OmanicBench.
- CoT performance degrades monotonically as the number of erroneous single-hop steps increases, demonstrating a knowledge floor effect.
- Supervised fine-tuning on OmanicSynth yields a 7.41 average point improvement across GSM8K, MATH, and other reasoning benchmarks.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1: Step-wise annotations enable precise failure diagnosis
**Claim:** Structural step annotations allow diagnosing where and why LLMs fail in multi-hop reasoning.  
**Mechanism:** By decomposing questions into sub-questions with intermediate answers, each hop can be evaluated independently, isolating knowledge retrieval errors from reasoning composition errors.  
**Core assumption:** Intermediate steps are correctly annotated and represent necessary reasoning paths.  
**Evidence anchors:** [abstract] "provides decomposed sub-questions and intermediate answers as structural annotations for analyzing reasoning processes"; [section 3.3] "step-level annotations allow us to quantitatively examine... knowledge floor effect and error propagation".  
**Break condition:** If annotator agreement is low or sub-questions contain systematic errors, diagnosis becomes unreliable and may misattribute failure modes.

### Mechanism 2: Knowledge floor effect reveals CoT's dependency on factual completeness
**Claim:** CoT's effectiveness diminishes when underlying atomic facts are missing, establishing a factual completeness threshold.  
**Mechanism:** By counting erroneous single-hop steps within a chain, the paper shows CoT gains drop monotonically, vanishing when three steps are wrong. This quantifies that CoT cannot compensate for missing knowledge.  
**Core assumption:** Single-hop sub-questions accurately reflect the atomic facts required for the multi-hop answer.  
**Evidence anchors:** [abstract] "CoT’s performance hinges on factual completeness, with its gains diminishing under knowledge gaps"; [section 3.3 RQ1] "CoT gain decreases monotonically as the number of erroneous single-hop steps increases... knowledge floor".  
**Break condition:** If the mapping from multi-hop to single-hop is many-to-many or context-dependent, the "number of errors" metric may be ambiguous and the floor effect could be an artifact.

### Mechanism 3: Synthetic training data transfers to broader reasoning capabilities
**Claim:** Supervised fine-tuning on machine-generated OmanicSynth improves performance on external reasoning and math benchmarks.  
**Mechanism:** The dataset's emphasis on mathematical hops and cross-domain composition forces models to learn generalizable reasoning patterns rather than memorizing facts.  
**Core assumption:** OmanicSynth's quality (validated by expert review) ensures training signal is sound and not merely superficial.  
**Evidence anchors:** [abstract] "supervised fine-tuning on the machine-generated training set... yields a 7.41 average point improvement across six reasoning and math benchmarks"; [section 3.2] "fine-tuned models consistently outperform their vanilla counterparts... validating the dataset’s quality".  
**Break condition:** If transfer gains are due to benchmark overlap, data leakage, or overfitting to Omanic's specific topology, the improvement isn't truly generalizable.

## Foundational Learning
- **Concept: Multi-hop reasoning decomposition**  
  *Why needed here:* Understanding how to break complex queries into atomic steps is essential for both designing benchmarks (like Omanic's decomposition) and interpreting model performance (e.g., identifying which hop fails).  
  *Quick check:* For the question "What is the capital of the country where the author of *Candida* was born?", what are the intermediate sub-questions and their answers?

- **Concept: Chain-of-Thought (CoT) prompting**  
  *Why needed here:* CoT is the primary method for eliciting intermediate reasoning; Omanic compares direct vs. CoT to analyze reasoning quality and introduces the "knowledge floor" effect relative to CoT performance.  
  *Quick check:* How does CoT prompting differ from direct prompting in terms of output structure, and why does it matter for diagnosing reasoning failures?

- **Concept: Knowledge graph-based data synthesis**  
  *Why needed here:* Omanic builds 4-hop questions from Wikidata triplets; this pipeline (retrieval → constrained synthesis → filtering) is core to creating scalable training data with structural annotations.  
  *Quick check:* What are the risks of using a knowledge graph (like Wikidata) to generate reasoning chains, and how does Omanic's "constrained synthesis" mitigate them?

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component map:** Triplet retrieval -> Constrained synthesis -> Automated filtering -> Expert review

**Critical path:** Constrained synthesis is most critical—it determines reasoning graph topology and integrates math hops. If synthesis produces invalid chains or non-mathematical hops, downstream components cannot salvage dataset quality.

**Design tradeoffs:**
- Scale vs. quality: Training set is machine-generated (10,296) for SFT scale; test set is expert-reviewed (967) for diagnostic reliability.
- Math integration: Mathematical hops are embedded within the chain (not appended) to enforce dependency, increasing complexity but preventing bypass.
- Topology constraints: Using three predefined graphs ensures structured dependencies but may limit natural question diversity compared to free-form chains.

**Failure signatures:**
- Low annotator agreement on sub-question correctness or distractor quality indicates ambiguous decomposition or poorly constructed options.
- High Step 1 error rates across models suggest question clarity issues or fundamental knowledge gaps in LLMs.
- Poor transfer gains from OmanicSynth fine-tuning may indicate dataset diversity failure (e.g., overrepresentation of certain domains or topologies).

**First 3 experiments:**
1. Run multiple LLMs on OmanicBench with CoT, extract sub-answers, compare to gold sub-answers. Compute per-hop error rates and correlate with the knowledge floor pattern.
2. Take correctly answered questions, corrupt one sub-answer (e.g., change "Ireland" to "England"), feed wrong answer into subsequent steps, measure final accuracy drop. Compare "chain evaluation" vs. "independent evaluation" to observe error amplification.
3. Fine-tune a base model (e.g., Qwen3-8B) on OmanicSynth, evaluate on GSM8K or MATH. Replicate the ~7.41 average point gain; if gains are absent, check training data mix, hyperparameters, or benchmark compatibility.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
### Open Question 1: How can Omanic be extended to support multilingual multi-hop reasoning evaluation?
**Basis in paper:** The limitations section explicitly states that Omanic is restricted to English only, limiting its applicability to multilingual reasoning.  
**Why unresolved:** The benchmark's current monolingual design prevents assessing whether models' multi-hop reasoning capabilities generalize across languages or are language-specific.  
**What evidence would resolve it:** Developing parallel non-English versions (e.g., in Spanish, Chinese) with native-speaker annotations and evaluating cross-lingual transfer performance of models fine-tuned on OmanicSynth.

### Open Question 2: What are the fundamental causes of the "knowledge floor" effect, where Chain-of-Thought gains diminish when atomic facts are missing?
**Basis in paper:** The paper observes this effect in Section 3.3 (RQ1) but does not investigate whether it stems from retrieval failures, reasoning capacity limits, or the interaction between knowledge and reasoning modules.  
**Why unresolved:** Understanding the root cause is critical for designing interventions; the paper only quantifies the phenomenon without causal analysis.  
**What evidence would resolve it:** Controlled experiments decoupling knowledge retrieval (e.g., providing perfect factual inputs) from reasoning steps, or probing model internals to see if missing facts trigger specific failure modes in intermediate layers.

### Open Question 3: Can architectural or training modifications mitigate error amplification in later hops of multi-hop chains?
**Basis in paper:** Section 3.3 (RQ2) documents growing error rates in later hops under both Direct and CoT prompting, but the paper does not explore solutions beyond dataset-based training.  
**Why unresolved:** Error amplification threatens the reliability of long-chain reasoning; the paper identifies the pattern but leaves mitigation strategies unexplored.  
**What evidence would resolve it:** Comparing different model architectures (e.g., graph-based vs. sequential transformers) or training objectives (e.g., hop-wise verifiers, loss functions penalizing error propagation) on OmanicBench, measuring whether later-hop error rates decrease relative to baselines.

### Open Question 4: To what extent do the graph topology constraints (Bridge, Chain, Converging) in Omanic influence the observed error propagation and knowledge floor effects?
**Basis in paper:** The paper uses three predefined reasoning topologies (Section 2) but aggregates results across them in analysis (Section 3.3), without examining topology-specific failure patterns.  
**Why unresolved:** Different topologies may impose distinct dependency structures; aggregating them could mask topology-sensitive weaknesses in models.  
**What evidence would resolve it:** Disaggregating performance metrics by topology type, identifying whether error amplification is worse in highly interdependent graphs (e.g., Converging) versus linear chains, and analyzing if CoT’s benefits vary by topology.

### Open Question 5: What specific characteristics of OmanicSynth (e.g., mathematical hops, domain diversity, distractor design) drive its transfer effectiveness to external reasoning benchmarks?
**Basis in paper:** The paper shows strong transfer gains from fine-tuning on OmanicSynth (Section 3.2) but does not perform ablation studies to isolate which dataset features contribute most to generalization.  
**Why unresolved:** Knowing the key factors would guide the creation of future synthetic datasets and clarify whether transfer stems from mathematical reasoning practice, factual diversity, or other properties.  
**What evidence would resolve it:** Creating controlled variants of OmanicSynth (e.g., removing math hops, reducing domain diversity, simplifying distractors) and measuring transfer differences to benchmarks like MATH or LogiQA.

## Limitations
- **Dataset construction opacity:** Exact models used for the "ensemble of four" in automated filtering and full GRPO hyperparameters are unspecified, hindering full replication of the data curation and training pipelines.
- **Annotation quality dependency:** Diagnostic claims rely on correctness and consistency of expert sub-question decompositions; inter-annotator agreement statistics are not provided.
- **Transfer generalizability:** The observed improvements on external benchmarks could partially reflect benchmark overlap, data leakage, or overfitting to Omanic’s specific mathematical hop structure rather than truly generalizable reasoning.

## Confidence
- Mechanism 1 (Step-wise Diagnosis): Medium-High.
- Mechanism 2 (Knowledge Floor): Medium.
- Mechanism 3 (Synthetic Data Transfer): Medium.

## Next Checks
1. **Verify Annotation Consistency:** Randomly re-annotate a stratified sample (e.g., 100 instances) of the test set with a new set of experts. Measure Cohen's Kappa for sub-question validity and distractor plausibility. Low agreement (<0.6) would undermine diagnostic validity.
2. **Control for Benchmark Contamination:** Audit the Wikidata-based synthesis pipeline for potential synthesis paths that directly mirror questions in GSM8K/MATH. If >1% of training chain patterns are found in test benchmarks (via fuzzy matching of reasoning graphs), re-run transfer experiments with a cleaned dataset.
3. **Isolate Mathematical Hop Contribution:** Ablate the "mathematical hop embedding" constraint during synthesis to create a variant `Omanic-Synth-NoMath`. Fine-tune identical models on this variant and compare transfer gains to the original. A significant drop would confirm the mathematical integration as a key driver.
