# CAMEL-CLIP: Channel-aware Multimodal Electroencephalography-text Alignment for Generalizable Brain Foundation Models

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID**: 2603.13272  
- **Source URL**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.13272  
- **Reference count**: 9  
- **Primary result**: Achieves state-of-the-art linear-probing balanced accuracy on multiple EEG tasks (e.g., 0.8158 on TUAB pathology, 0.7988 on CHB-MIT seizure detection, 0.9439 on MUMTAZ depression) and improves EEG-text retrieval Precision@1 by 10.4% on gender classification.

## Executive Summary
CAMEL-CLIP presents a multimodal EEG-text foundation model designed to overcome channel heterogeneity–a key barrier to generalizing brain decoding across datasets. The framework introduces three core components: Channel Attribute-based Positional Encoding (CAPE) to inject semantic channel knowledge, Dynamic Channel Projection (DCP) to process arbitrary channel subsets without compression, and Dual-level Contrastive Learning (DCL) that jointly optimizes channel-specific and global alignment. Pretrained on TUAB EEG-clinical report pairs, the model demonstrates strong transferability, achieving superior linear-probing performance on downstream tasks (TUAB, CHB-MIT, MUMTAZ) and robust cross-modal retrieval—all without full fine-tuning.

## Method Summary
The architecture combines a frozen ClinicalBERT text encoder with a finetuned CBraMoD EEG encoder. CAPE encodes anatomical attributes (region, hemisphere, number, reference) of each channel into learnable embeddings, which are concatenated and passed through an MLP to yield channel-specific positional codes. DCP applies independent linear projections to EEG channel features and fuses the clinical report embedding (from ClinicalBERT) with a channel knowledge embedding derived from textual descriptions of the channel’s anatomy. During training, random channel removal (p_remove=0.5, up to 80% removal) simulates variable channel configurations. DCL merges Channel-level Contrastive Loss (using N=3 channels per sample) and Sample-level Contrastive Loss (with attention-pooled global EEG) with equal weighting (λ=0.5). For text-based classification, synthetic reports are created by sampling sections from label-specific report pools; their embeddings are averaged to form class prototypes. Pretraining spans 100 epochs with batch size 512; checkpoints are selected by highest mean balanced accuracy on TUAB validation tasks.

## Key Results
- Linear-probing pathology classification on TUAB: **balanced accuracy 0.8158**, surpassing EEG-CLIP (0.7905).  
- Seizure detection on CHB-MIT: **balanced accuracy 0.7988±0.0046**, beating LaBraM (0.7075±0.0358).  
- Depression classification on MUMTAZ-2016: **balanced accuracy 0.9439±0.0019**.  
- EEG-text retrieval **Precision@1 improves by 10.4%** on gender classification.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
CAMEL-CLIP tackles channel heterogeneity through three synergistic mechanisms. CAPE provides a semantic prior by encoding anatomical channel attributes, enabling the model to generalize across naming conventions and montages. DCP processes each channel independently, preserving information when channels are missing or added, avoiding lossy compression. The dual contrastive objective (CCL + SCL) aligns EEG and text at both fine-grained channel and holistic sample levels, encouraging representations that are both diagnostically precise and globally coherent. This combination yields embeddings that transfer robustly to unseen datasets with different channel sets and support zero-shot retrieval.

## Foundational Learning
- **Multimodal alignment**: CAMEL-CLIP achieves this through Dual-level Contrastive Learning (DCL), which aligns EEG and text at both channel and global levels. *Why needed*: Without explicit per-sample labels, contrastive learning learns a shared embedding space for cross-modal retrieval and zero-shot classification. *Quick check*: In a trained model, cosine similarity of positive EEG-text pairs should be higher than that of negative pairs.
- **Channel heterogeneity**: The paper addresses channel variability across EEG systems via Channel Attribute-based Positional Encoding (CAPE) and Dynamic Channel Projection (DCP). *Why needed*: Real-world EEG datasets differ in channel count, names, and references; robustness to such variation is crucial for a foundation model. *Quick check*: Model performance should remain stable when channels are randomly removed or new channels (with defined attributes) are introduced.
- **Foundation model**: CAMEL-CLIP is pretrained on a large EEG-clinical report dataset (TUAB) and can be adapted to downstream tasks with minimal linear probing. *Why needed*: Reduces the need for task-specific data collection and training, enabling broad applicability. *Quick check*: Freezing the pretrained encoder and adding a linear classifier should yield high accuracy on diverse tasks.
- **Contrastive learning**: The training objective uses a combination of Channel-level Contrastive Loss (CCL) and Sample-level Contrastive Loss (SCL). *Why needed*: Aligns EEG and text without requiring aligned samples at the channel level; instead, it treats channel-text pairs as positives within a batch. *Quick check*: Loss should decrease as training progresses, and the model should achieve high precision@1 in retrieval.
- **Dynamic channel projection (DCP)**: Each channel’s EEG feature vector is projected by an independent linear layer, allowing arbitrary channel subsets without compression. *Why needed*: Avoids information loss from padding or pooling, preserving channel-specific information. *Quick check*: The model can process inputs with any number of channels (up to the maximum seen during training) without shape errors.
- **Channel attribute-based encoding (CAPE)**: Encodes anatomical attributes (region, hemisphere, number, reference) into learnable embeddings that serve as channel-specific positional codes. *Why needed*: Provides a semantic prior that generalizes beyond observed channel names, allowing the model to infer function of similar channels even if not seen during training. *Quick check*: Cosine similarity between CAPE embeddings of channels sharing the same region/hemisphere should be higher than between randomly paired channels.

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map**: Raw EEG -> CBraMoD (EEG encoder) -> Channel features -> CAPE (attribute embedding) -> [Channel features + CAPE] -> DCP (per-channel projection) -> Channel-specific EEG embeddings; Clinical Report -> ClinicalBERT (frozen) -> Report embedding; Channel knowledge text -> ClinicalBERT -> Knowledge embedding -> Fusion (FC) -> Channel-specific text embeddings; DCL: {CCL: channel EEG vs. channel text | SCL: global EEG (attention pooling) vs. report text}.  
- **Critical path**: Core learning loop computes DCL loss from channel-aligned and globally-aligned pairs, updates CBraMoD and fusion layers (ClinicalBERT frozen).  
- **Design tradeoffs**: (1) CAPE uses handcrafted attributes – interpretable but may omit subtle relationships; (2) Random channel removal in DCP improves robustness but risks dropping diagnostically critical channels; (3) Balanced λ=0.5 for CCL/SCL works for pretraining but might require tuning in other domains.  
- **Failure signatures**: (a) Performance collapse on datasets with different channel counts indicates channel-handling flaw; (b) High linear-probing accuracy but low retrieval precision suggests misalignment between channel-specific and global representations; (c) CAPE embeddings not reflecting spatial proximity (e.g., F3 and F4 distant) signals attribute mapping errors.  
- **Three first experiments**: 1) Validate CAPE by measuring pairwise cosine similarities; expect channels with same region/hemisphere to be significantly closer than random pairs. 2) Train a small TUAB subset with DCL; verify channel removal mask is correctly applied and loss decreases monotonically. 3) Run linear probing on TUAB pathology using the released checkpoint; reproduced balanced accuracy should be ≈0.815.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- How does the model’s performance scale with an increased number of channels beyond those seen in pretraining (e.g., >21 channels)? The current channel attribute set is limited to the TUAB montage; extending to arbitrary channel sets requires defining attributes for new electrodes.  
- Could the synthetic report generation process for zero-shot classification be replaced with a more principled approach that does not rely on sampling from label-specific pools? The paper’s current method may not fully capture the diversity of real clinical reports.  
- Is dual-level contrastive learning optimal for multimodal alignment, or would other objectives (e.g., triplet loss, masked modeling) yield better generalizability, especially to tasks with limited data?  
- The paper evaluates only linear probing of a model finetuned on TUAB; true zero-shot evaluation on entirely unseen channel configurations and datasets remains an open challenge.

## Limitations
- The **channel knowledge corpus** (textual descriptions for each EEG channel) is underspecified, hindering faithful recreation of DCP’s channel-specific text branch.  
- **Synthetic prompt generation** for TUAB reports lacks details on section parsing and sampling strategy, affecting text-based classification results.  
- Evaluation relies on **linear probing of a model finetuned on TUAB**; true zero-shot generalizability to entirely unseen channel configurations remains unproven.
