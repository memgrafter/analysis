# No evaluation without fair representation : Impact of label and selection bias on the evaluation, performance and mitigation of classification models

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2603.09662
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.09662
- Reference count: 40
- Primary result: Biased test sets distort fairness metric evaluations, and fair evaluation reveals no inherent fairness-accuracy trade-off.

## Executive Summary
The paper investigates how biased test sets lead to erroneous assessments of fairness metrics and mitigation methods, challenging the presumed fairness-accuracy trade-off. Using real-world datasets (Student Performance, OULAD) assumed to represent fair worlds, the authors develop a framework to inject controlled label and selection bias into training data. Models trained on biased data are evaluated on the original fair test set. Results demonstrate that label bias selectively skews accuracy-dependent metrics (GEI, Equalized Odds), while selection bias distorts all common fairness measures. When evaluated fairly, no trade-off between fairness and accuracy emerges, and the effectiveness of mitigation techniques varies dramatically with bias type—reweighing excels for selection bias, while relabeling methods can backfire under mismatched bias conditions. The study underscores that reliable fairness evaluation requires unbiased, representative test sets.

## Method Summary
The authors begin with two educational datasets considered to have minimal discrimination (low SPD). They artificially introduce two types of bias into the training data: (1) label bias, which lowers scores for the unprivileged group by a tunable parameter plus noise, and (2) three subtypes of selection bias (random, self-selection, malicious) that undersample the unprivileged group according to different rules. A suite of fairness-agnostic classifiers (Random Forest, decision tree, MLP) is trained on these biased datasets, both with and without eight popular fairness mitigation methods (pre- and post-processing). All models are evaluated on the original, unbiased test set, and metrics (Accuracy, SPD, Equalized Odds, GEI, BCC) are compared between fair and biased test conditions. This controlled setup reveals how bias propagation affects both model performance and the perceived efficacy of fairness interventions.

## Key Results
- Label bias corrupts metrics that depend on ground-truth labels (Accuracy, GEI, Equalized Odds) but leaves label-independent metrics (SPD, BCC) unaffected.
- Selection bias distorts every fairness metric because it alters group base rates and the relationship between predictions and sensitive attributes.
- Under fair evaluation, no inherent fairness-accuracy trade-off exists; many mitigation methods can improve both simultaneously, but their success hinges on matching the method to the bias type.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1: Label bias distorts accuracy‑dependent metrics
When labels are biased (e.g., unprivileged group receives lower scores), metrics that rely on the ground‑truth label (accuracy, GEI, EqOd) become misleadingly optimistic because they are computed on the same corrupted labels. The bias propagates from the biased training labels into the model's predictions, and since the evaluation uses the same distorted labels, these metrics shift together with the bias. If label bias is extreme, the model cannot learn any signal beyond the bias, and all metrics collapse to the biased distribution.

### Mechanism 2: Selection bias skews all fairness metrics
Selection bias (any undersampling that changes the joint distribution of sensitive attribute, features, and labels) distorts every standard fairness metric because the test set no longer reflects the base rates and conditional probabilities of the fair world. By removing individuals based on group membership or score, selection bias alters group proportions and the prediction-sensitive attribute relationship. SPD uses predicted outcomes only; EqOd and GEI rely on label distributions; BCC compares neighbors drawn from a different subpopulation—all become inconsistent with their values on the fair distribution.

### Mechanism 3: Fair evaluation reveals no fairness‑accuracy trade‑off and bias‑type‑dependent mitigation success
Evaluating models on an unbiased test set shows that fairness and accuracy can be improved simultaneously. Some mitigation methods (e.g., reweighing for selection bias, ROC‑SPD for label bias) align predictions with the fair distribution without sacrificing accuracy. Others (e.g., massaging under selection bias) can worsen fairness because they optimize a biased metric. The baseline "fair world" dataset is crucial; if it is not sufficiently fair, the observed absence of trade‑off may not hold.

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component map**:  
Data loader -> Bias injector -> Data splitter -> Model trainer -> Evaluator -> Analyzer

**Critical path**:  
Load fair data -> choose bias type & intensity -> inject bias -> split (train/validation/test) -> train model (with/without mitigation) -> evaluate on fair test -> repeat across intensities & folds -> aggregate & visualize.

**Design tradeoffs**:  
- *Real vs synthetic data*: Real datasets provide authentic distributions but may have hidden biases; synthetic injection offers control but may not capture complex interactions.  
- *Pre- vs post-processing*: Pre‑processing modifies training data, integrating with any model but possibly distorting true labels; post‑processing changes predictions, needing a validation set and risking edge cases.  
- *Bias intensity range*: Too low hides effects; too extreme may break learning.  
- *Fairness‑agnostic algorithm*: Robustness vs sensitivity trade‑off; RF is chosen for stability.

**Failure signatures**:  
- Mitigation backfires (e.g., massaging under self‑selection increases SPD).  
- Post‑processing corner cases (EOP/ROC fail when no positive predictions for a group).  
- High variance on small datasets (Student) requiring careful cross‑validation.  
- Mis‑specified bias type leads to harmful reverse discrimination.

## Limitations
- OULAD feature engineering is incompletely specified, risking dataset divergence.
- Model hyperparameters beyond listed ones (e.g., RF n_estimators, MLP architecture) rely on unspecified defaults.
- Self‑selection bias removal probability function is only qualitatively described.
- Label bias scaling ("half the difference between max and min S") is ambiguous regarding which split computes extrema.
- Post‑processing mitigations may fail due to edge cases (e.g., no positive predictions for a group).
- The "fair world" assumption for original datasets might be violated by hidden biases.

## Confidence
- Label bias distorts accuracy‑dependent metrics: **High**
- Selection bias skews all fairness metrics: **High**
- Fair evaluation shows no fairness‑accuracy trade‑off and mitigation effectiveness depends on bias type: **Medium**
