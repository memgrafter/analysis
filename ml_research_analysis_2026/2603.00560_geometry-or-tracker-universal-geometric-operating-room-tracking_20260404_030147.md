# Geometry OR Tracker: Universal Geometric Operating Room Tracking

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2603.00560  
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.00560  
- **Reference count:** 27  
- **Primary result:** Rectification reduces cross-view depth disagreement by over 30× (mean error: 1.4115m → 0.0459m) and achieves state-of-the-art tracking metrics (Average Jaccard 89.73, Occlusion Accuracy 96.28, Median Trajectory Error 3.46) on the MM-OR benchmark.

## Executive Summary
Operating room (OR) multi-view 3D tracking suffers from unstable calibration and RGB-D registration, causing geometric inconsistency and "ghosting" artifacts. This paper introduces Geometry OR Tracker, a two-stage pipeline that first rectifies metric geometry across views and then performs occlusion-robust tracking in a unified world frame. The rectification module recovers a global metric scale and corrects camera parameters, drastically reducing cross-view depth disagreement. The tracking module fuses multi-view observations into a single 3D feature cloud, enabling robust correspondence even under occlusions. Evaluated on the MM-OR benchmark, the system outperforms baselines across key tracking metrics and ablations confirm that improved geometric consistency directly enhances tracking accuracy.

## Method Summary
The pipeline decouples geometry correction from tracking. Stage 1 (Multi-view Metric Geometry Rectification) uses a geometry foundation model (MapAnything [11]) on the first synchronized multi-view frame, optionally taking noisy calibration hints (intrinsics, extrinsics, depth) to predict a global metric scale $m$, rectified camera parameters $\{\tilde{K}_v, \tilde{P}_v\}$, and per-frame rectified depth maps $\{\tilde{D}_v^t\}$. This single rectification is applied to the entire sequence under the assumption of a predominantly static OR scene. Stage 2 (Occlusion-Robust 3D Point Tracking) lifts each frame’s RGB observations into a fused metric 3D feature point cloud $\mathcal{Y}_t$ using the rectified geometry. A transformer-based tracker (MVTracker [18]) then iteratively refines 3D query positions by retrieving local neighborhoods in $\mathcal{Y}_t$, outputting trajectories and visibility. The design isolates calibration noise as the primary bottleneck, allowing a standard point tracker to operate in a stable metric space.

## Key Results
- Rectification reduces mean cross-view depth reprojection error from 1.4115m to 0.0459m (over 30× improvement).
- Full system achieves best Average Jaccard (89.73), threshold-averaged accuracy (93.65), and Occlusion Accuracy (96.28).
- Lowest Median Trajectory Error (3.46) among compared methods.
- Ablations show strong correlation between rectification quality (depth error) and tracking accuracy (AJ, OA).

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1: Global Metric Rectification Eliminates Cross-View Inconsistency
The Multi-view Metric Geometry Rectification module jointly optimizes for a single global scale $m$, per-camera rectified intrinsics $\tilde{K}_v$, and extrinsics $\tilde{P}_v$, producing per-frame metric depth $\tilde{D}_v^t$. This ensures that back-projected 3D points from all views align in a shared room frame, resolving ghosting from calibration noise. The core assumption is a predominantly static scene over the sequence, allowing a single calibration from the first frame to apply throughout. Evidence includes the 30× reduction in cross-view depth error (Table 1) and the abstract claim. Break condition: extreme initial calibration noise may cause non-convergence or amplified errors.

### Mechanism 2: Unified 3D Tracking Robust to Occlusions
By operating directly in the rectified world frame, the tracker fuses multi-view data into a single 3D feature cloud $\mathcal{Y}_t$. Correspondence via local 3D k-NN retrieval remains valid across viewpoint changes because all points share a common metric coordinate system. The core assumption is that dynamic targets (instruments, staff) are sparsely observable—at least one view sees the target at any time. Evidence includes high Occlusion Accuracy (96.28) and low MTE (3.46), plus ablations showing tracking quality correlates with rectification (Table 3). Break condition: prolonged full-body occlusions halt updates; rectification errors corrupt neighborhood retrieval.

### Mechanism 3: Decoupling isolates Calibration Noise
The two-stage design ensures the tracker receives a stable, ghosting-free geometric interface. The rectifier handles scale recovery and misalignment correction, freeing the tracker to focus on robust correspondence. The core assumption is that calibration noise is the primary bottleneck for metric tracking in ORs, not the tracker’s inherent capabilities. Evidence: the variant without rectification (“w/o. MMCR”) performs significantly worse (Table 2), and tracking metrics improve as rectification depth error decreases (Table 3). Break condition: if the tracker backend (MVTracker) has fundamental limitations (e.g., poor temporal modeling), even perfect rectification cannot yield optimal performance.

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map:** Input (multi-view RGB-D + noisy calibration) -> Multi-view Metric Geometry Rectification (MapAnything backbone) -> Metric Lifting (back-project depth) -> Feature Extraction (2D backbone) -> Fused 3D Feature Cloud $\mathcal{Y}_t$ -> Occlusion-Robust 3D Point Tracking (MVTracker) -> Output (trajectories, visibility).
- **Critical path:** Rectify first frame to obtain $(m, \{\tilde{K}_v, \tilde{P}_v\})$; apply to all frames; build $\mathcal{Y}_t$ per frame; run iterative transformer tracking over time. Rectification is a one-time (or infrequent) cost; tracking runs online.
- **Design tradeoffs:** Rectification uses feed-forward inference (fast) vs. potentially less accurate than per-frame bundle adjustment. Fusing all views improves occlusion robustness but increases compute and may include ghosts if rectification is imperfect. Global single-scale $m$ simplifies but ignores per-camera/time drift.
- **Failure signatures:** High cross-view depth reprojection error (>0.1m) indicates rectification failure; sudden trajectory jumps or high MTE indicate tracking failure; low visibility $v^n_t$ when target is observed suggests missed updates.
- **First 3 experiments:**
  1. **Rectification sanity check:** Run MMCR on MM-OR’s first frame with raw vs. GT calibration. Visualize aligned point clouds $X_1$; raw should show ghosts, rectified should align. Quantify reprojection error reduction (target: ~30×).
  2. **Input ablation replication:** Vary rectification inputs (no hints, RGB-only, RGB+K, etc.). Correlate output depth error (AbsRel/RMSE) with downstream tracking AJ on held-out sequences to verify strong coupling.
  3. **Tracking backend isolation:** Using identical rectified geometry, replace MVTracker with a simpler tracker (e.g., CoTracker3 lifted to 3D). Compare AJ, OA, MTE to full system to isolate MVTracker’s contribution.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- **Temporal calibration drift:** The system assumes static cameras and applies a single rectification from the first frame, but real ORs may experience shifts over long surgeries. How can online rectification be incorporated?
- **Foundation model dependence:** Rectification relies on MapAnything; its generalization to diverse OR conditions (lighting, instruments) is unclear. How sensitive is performance to the choice of geometric model?
- **Minimal geometric priors:** Which combination of calibration hints (intrinsics, extrinsics, depth) is sufficient for acceptable rectification, and how does performance degrade as hints become missing or noisy?
- **Deformable structures:** The method targets rigid objects (tools, staff). Can it be extended to track deformable anatomy (organs) without assuming consistent local geometry?

## Limitations
- **Dependency on external models:** Rectification and tracking rely on black-box pretrained models (MapAnything, MVTracker); unavailable or changed implementations block reproduction.
- **Static scene assumption for rectification:** Estimating a single global calibration from the first frame fails if the scene is highly dynamic initially or if cameras drift over time.
- **Benchmark-limited evaluation:** Results are only on MM-OR with five Kinect cameras; generalization to other sensor setups or non-OR environments is unproven.
- **Global scale simplification:** Using one scale factor $m$ ignores potential per-camera or per-frame scale drift, which could accumulate in long sequences.

## Confidence
- **Rectification effectiveness (cross-view consistency):** High for observed reduction on MM-OR; Medium for generalizability due to reliance on MapAnything and static-scene assumption.
- **Occlusion-robust tracking:** Medium—strong benchmark metrics but not isolated from MVTracker design; generalization unknown.
- **Decoupling bottleneck claim:** Medium—ablations support that rectification improves tracking, but a more advanced tracker without rectification might close the gap; not tested.
