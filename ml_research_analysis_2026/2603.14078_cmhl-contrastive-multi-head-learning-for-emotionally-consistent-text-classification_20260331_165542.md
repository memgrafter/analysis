# CMHL: Contrastive Multi-Head Learning for Emotionally Consistent Text Classification

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2603.14078
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.14078
- **Reference count:** 40
- **Primary result:** CMHL (125M parameters) achieves SOTA F1 93.75% on dair-ai Emotion and 72.50% F1 with 73.30% recall on SWMH mental health dataset, outperforming LLMs up to 56× larger and domain-specific models.

## Executive Summary
Textual Emotion Classification (TEC) traditionally relies on large, computationally expensive models. This paper introduces CMHL, a parameter‑efficient single‑model architecture that employs multi‑task learning to jointly predict primary emotions, valence, and intensity. The design incorporates psychologically‑grounded auxiliary supervision derived from Russell’s circumplex model and a contrastive contradiction loss that penalizes mutually incompatible emotion predictions. With only 125 M parameters, CMHL sets a new state‑of‑the‑art F1 score of 93.75% on the dair‑ai Emotion dataset and achieves 72.50% F1 and 73.30% recall on the mental‑health SWMH dataset, surpassing specialized baselines.

## Method Summary
CMHL uses a shared RoBERTa‑base encoder to produce a 768‑dimensional [CLS] representation. For emotion classification, three lightweight linear heads predict primary emotion (6‑way), valence (3‑way), and intensity (2‑way). The loss combines cross‑entropy for each task with weights α₁=0.3 (valence) and α₂=0.2 (intensity). A contrastive contradiction loss L_excl penalizes high‑confidence co‑predictions of emotions with opposite valence, using an adaptive threshold τᵢⱼ = τ₀ + α·d(eᵢ,eⱼ) where d is normalized affective distance. The total loss is L_task + λ·L_excl with λ=0.4. For mental‑health classification, the same encoder feeds two heads (diagnosis and intensity) whose outputs are fused via an attention‑based gating mechanism before final prediction. Training uses AdamW with modest learning rates (2e‑5 for emotion, 1.5e‑5 for mental health), small batch sizes, and early stopping. Model selection on the mental‑health task maximizes a weighted score of validation F1 and average prediction confidence.

## Key Results
- **SOTA performance:** 93.75% F1 on dair‑ai Emotion, beating LLMs up to 56× larger.
- **Mental health advantage:** 72.50% F1 and 73.30% recall on SWMH, exceeding MentalBERT/RoBERTa.
- **Efficiency:** Achieves these results with only 125 M parameters via single‑model multi‑head design.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### 1. Joint Multi‑Task Learning
The shared encoder must produce features simultaneously discriminative for primary emotions, valence, and intensity. Gradients from all three cross‑entropy losses force the model to capture cross‑task emotional invariances, yielding a richer affective representation. The auxiliary tasks provide additional supervision that regularizes the main emotion classifier.

### 2. Psychologically‑Grounded Auxiliary Supervision
Discrete emotion labels are automatically mapped to valence and intensity using fixed rules from Russell’s circumplex model. This injects a coherent, theory‑driven structure into the multi‑task objectives, reducing label noise and aligning task semantics without extra annotation.

### 3. Contrastive Contradiction Loss
L_excl explicitly enforces logical consistency by penalizing high‑confidence predictions of psychologically incompatible emotions (e.g., joy and anger). The penalty uses an adaptive threshold based on affective distance, which allows limited blends while suppressing clear contradictions, thus improving prediction reliability.

- **Psychological Grounding of NLP Tasks**  
  *Why needed:* The entire auxiliary supervision design rests on applying Russell’s circumplex model; this is a deliberate prior, not a data‑discovered property.  
  *Quick check:* What risk arises if the dataset’s emotion labels do not obey the assumed circumplex structure?

- **Contrastive Loss for Consistency (beyond similarity)**  
  *Why needed:* L_excl is a specialized contrastive loss that pushes *incompatible* predictions apart in probability space, differing from standard contrastive learning that pulls similar examples together.  
  *Quick check:* How does the adaptive threshold τᵢⱼ differ from a fixed margin in Triplet Loss?

## Architecture Onboarding

### Component Map
- **Input & Encoder:** Text → token IDs → RoBERTa‑base → [CLS] vector (768‑d)
- **Task‑Specific Heads (Emotion):** Three linear layers (emotion 6‑way, valence 3‑way, intensity 2‑way) projecting [CLS]
- **Loss Calculator:** L_task = CE_emotion + 0.3·CE_valence + 0.2·CE_intensity; L_excl from emotion probabilities; L_total = L_task + 0.4·L_excl
- **Mental Health Extension:** Two heads (diagnosis M‑way, intensity 3‑way) plus attention‑based gating (d_g=128) fusing predictions
- **Checkpoint Selector:** Validation score = 0.7·F1 + 0.3·mean_confidence; best checkpoint saved

### Critical Path
The RoBERTa encoder is the central bottleneck; all task gradients and the L_excl gradient must flow through it successfully for multi‑task benefits. The L_excl gradient path is the novel constraint injector.

### Design Tradeoffs
- **Efficiency vs. Specialization:** Single encoder vs. ensemble; shared features must suffice.
- **Psychological Prior Strength:** Fixed mapping rules are simple but brittle; a learnable mapping would be more flexible but loses guaranteed consistency.
- **Loss Weight Sensitivity:** Performance hinges on α₁, α₂, λ; these likely need re‑tuning for new datasets.
- **Gating Complexity (Mental Health):** Attention gating adds parameters and compute; benefit must outweigh cost vs. simple averaging.

### Failure Signatures
- **Multi‑task interference:** Primary emotion F1 drops vs. single‑task baseline. Check per‑task loss ratios and α weights.
- **Contradiction loss over‑penalizing:** Model fails on valid blends (e.g., bittersweet). Inspect probability distributions; adjust τᵢⱼ or λ.
- **Derived label mismatch:** Valence/intensity head performance poor; check if dataset aligns with circumplex mapping.
- **Gating mechanism instability:** Attention weights collapse (e.g., always constant). Try regularization or different initialization.

### First 3 Experiments
1. **Ablate auxiliary tasks:** Remove valence/intensity heads; measure primary emotion F1 change.
2. **Ablate contrastive loss:** Set λ=0; compare F1 and examine emotion probability vectors for high‑confidence incompatible pairs.
3. **Validate derived labels:** Manually annotate a subset for valence/intensity; compute accuracy of automatic Russell mapping.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
1. **Generalization to low‑resource languages:** Performance equity across diverse linguistic contexts is untested.
2. **Interpretability of gating mechanism:** What linguistic cues drive the attention weights in mental health classification, and how do they enhance recall?
3. **Calibration of τᵢⱼ:** Is the adaptive threshold psychologically grounded or purely performance‑tuned? How does it relate to empirical affective distances?
4. **Isolation of contributions:** The relative gains from auxiliary supervision vs. contrastive loss vs. their combination are not quantified.
5. **Portability of gating:** Can the attention‑based gating generalize to other clinical NLP tasks (e.g., symptom severity, treatment outcome)?

## Limitations
- **Hyperparameter opacity:** τ₀, α, and distance scaling for L_excl are not fully described, hindering exact replication.
- **Theoretical assumption risk:** Russell’s circumplex mapping is applied blindly; misalignment with the dataset could mislead the auxiliary supervision.
- **Incomplete mental‑health details:** Number of diagnosis classes (M), confidence metric definition, and warm‑up schedule are unspecified, blocking faithful reproduction of that extension.

## Confidence
- **Joint MTL improves representation quality and generalization:** Medium (benefit shown vs. single‑task baseline but ablation missing).
- **Psychologically‑grounded auxiliary supervision provides coherent structure:** Medium (depends on circumplex fit; no validation of derived labels).
- **Contrastive contradiction loss enforces consistency and boosts F1:** Medium (gain reported but hyperparameter sensitivity and break conditions unclear).
