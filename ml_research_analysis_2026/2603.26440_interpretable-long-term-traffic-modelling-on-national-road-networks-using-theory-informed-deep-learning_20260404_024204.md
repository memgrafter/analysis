# Interpretable long-term traffic modelling on national road networks using theory-informed deep learning

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2603.26440
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.26440
- **Reference count:** 40
- **Primary result:** DeepDemand integrates travel demand theory into a deep learning framework, achieving R²=0.718 (MAE=7,406 vehicles) under random CV and R²=0.665 under spatial CV on the UK strategic road network, with interpretable deterrence patterns and polycentric demand structures.

## Executive Summary
DeepDemand addresses the trade-off between interpretability, transferability, and accuracy in long-term traffic modelling on national road networks. By embedding travel demand theory into a differentiable deep learning architecture, it models origin-destination (OD) interactions and travel-time deterrence while maintaining geographic transferability. Evaluated on the UK strategic road network (5,088 segments, 2017–2024), it outperforms linear, ridge, random forest, and gravity baselines. The model’s learned parameters reveal stable nonlinear deterrence and polycentric demand aligned with employment centres, providing actionable insights for planning.

## Method Summary
The method combines a competitive two-source Dijkstra procedure with a factorized deep neural network. The Dijkstra partitions the network around each target edge into exclusive origin and destination basins (within a 1-hour travel-time cutoff) and screens OD pairs whose shortest paths necessarily include the edge. The neural network encodes LSOA-level socioeconomic features into separate origin/destination embeddings, scores OD interactions, and applies a learned travel-time deterrence function. The sum over edge-valid OD pairs predicts traffic flow. Inputs include an OSM-derived road network with travel-time weights, PCA-reduced LSOA features (64 dimensions), and matched TRIS AADT counts. Training uses stochastic edge sampling and AdamW, with evaluation via random and spatial (by English region) cross-validation.

## Key Results
- Random 5-fold CV: R² = 0.718, MAE = 7,406 vehicles.
- Spatial CV (by English region): R² = 0.665, demonstrating geographic transferability.
- Learned deterrence function shows a stable nonlinear decay; origin/destination potentials reveal polycentric structures aligned with major employment centres.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1: Competitive Two-Source Dijkstra for OD Region Extraction
**Claim:** The model isolates spatially relevant OD interactions for each target edge using a competitive two-source Dijkstra, preventing ambiguous territory assignments.
**Mechanism:** Simultaneous Dijkstra searches from the upstream (origin-side) and downstream (destination-side) nodes share a priority queue. The first search to reach a node claims it permanently for its territory (origin or destination). This partitions the local network into mutually exclusive origin and destination sets within a travel-time cutoff (e.g., 1 hour).
**Core assumption:** Shortest-path travel time is a reasonable proxy for functional spatial interaction, and nodes must belong unambiguously to either origin or destination basins to avoid double-counting.
**Break condition:** If the travel-time cutoff is too low, valid OD pairs may be excluded; if too high, computation grows superlinearly and the competitive claim becomes less meaningful.

### Mechanism 2: Differentiable Architecture Embedding Demand Modeling Steps
**Claim:** The model embeds trip generation, attraction, distribution, and assignment within a single differentiable architecture, allowing end-to-end learning while preserving interpretability.
**Mechanism:** For each valid OD pair (screened to necessarily traverse the target edge), separate MLPs encode LSOA features into origin and destination embeddings. These are concatenated and scored via an interaction network, and a learned deterrence function modulates the score by travel time. Summing over edge-valid OD pairs implicitly performs assignment.
**Core assumption:** The factorized structure is expressive enough to capture observed link flows, and the shortest-path-based screening accurately approximates contributing trips.
**Break condition:** If the shortest-path assumption fails (e.g., due to congestion rerouting), the screening will exclude trips that actually use the edge, leading to under-prediction.

### Mechanism 3: Grounding in External Drivers and Transferability
**Claim:** By tying OD pair contributions directly to LSOA-level features and enforcing a monotonic deterrence prior, the model ensures predictions are grounded in external drivers and transferable across space.
**Mechanism:** Node features are z-score normalized and PCA-reduced; deterrence input is standardized travel time passed through a sigmoid. Training on edges across the nation forces the model to learn relationships that hold across regions.
**Core assumption:** The relationship between LSOA demographics/land use and trip generation/attraction is stable across the network, and travel-time sensitivity can be captured by a single shared function.
**Break condition:** If the network includes regions with fundamentally different travel cultures, the single deterrence function and node encoders may not transfer.

## Foundational Learning
- **Competitive multi-source shortest-path search:** Solves partitioning the network around an edge into exclusive origin/destination basins without overlap. *Quick check:* What problem does the "competitive" aspect solve that independent forward/backward Dijkstra does not? (Answer: It prevents nodes from being reachable from both sides, eliminating ambiguity in which trips' paths include the target edge.)
- **Gravity model analog and trip distribution:** The factorized OD score mimics a gravity model where origin/destination masses are learned embeddings and the deterrence function replaces a fixed impedance. *Quick check:* How does the learned deterrence function differ from a classic gravity model's impedance term? (Answer: It is a flexible MLP, not a fixed power/exponential function; its shape is data-derived.)
- **SHAP for post-hoc feature attribution:** Used to interpret which LSOA features influence OD pair scores, making the embeddings explainable. *Quick check:* Why are separate random forest models fit per feature group instead of one pooled model? (Answer: To avoid confounding from correlated features and allow meaningful comparison across domains.)

## Architecture Onboarding
### Component Map
Data ingestion -> Network simplification & travel-time weighting -> LSOA feature normalization & PCA -> Per-edge competitive Dijkstra (O/D extraction & OD screening) -> For each valid OD pair: origin/destination encoders -> OD interaction scorer -> learned deterrence -> aggregation -> edge prediction -> training (stochastic edge sampling, AdamW) -> interpretation (deterrence curve, SHAP, potentials)

### Critical Path
The OD extraction and screening (competitive Dijkstra and shortest-path checks) is the computational bottleneck, done offline once and parallelized. Training then proceeds by sampling edges, assembling valid OD pairs, forward pass, and backpropagation. Memory usage scales with the number of valid OD pairs per edge.

### Design Tradeoffs
- **Accuracy vs. interpretability:** Separate MLPs for components retain factorized interpretability versus a monolithic GNN that might be more accurate but opaque.
- **Transferability vs. local fit:** The single national model sacrifices regional parameter specialization for spatial transferability, evidenced by the drop from R²=0.718 (random CV) to 0.665 (spatial CV).
- **Computation vs. coverage:** The 1-hour cutoff makes the model tractable but may exclude long-distance trips on national corridors; sensitivity to τ is untested.

### Failure Signatures
- **Poor spatial CV performance** (R² significantly below 0.665, high regional variance) indicates failure to learn transferable relationships.
- **Unstable deterrence curves** across CV folds (high variance) suggests the model is not converging to a consistent impedance pattern.
- **Systematic residual clustering** (e.g., high errors in urban cores or rural peripheries) indicates structural misspecification, such as unmodeled congestion or multimodal trips.

### First 3 Experiments
1. **Sanity-check OD extraction** on a small subgraph: visualize O/D territories and verify that for a known major corridor, the majority of OD pairs whose shortest path uses a target edge are retained.
2. **Ablate the competitive Dijkstra:** Replace with independent forward/backward expansion (allow overlap) and measure change in predictive performance and training stability. Expect performance drop due to ambiguous attribution.
3. **Test deterrence function forms:** Replace the MLP `f_t` with a parametric form (e.g., exponential decay) and compare MGEH/R² on validation set; this confirms the claim that a flexible form is essential.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
1. **Modal split integration:** How can modal split be explicitly integrated into the DeepDemand framework to enable direct evaluation of mode choice policies, particularly in dense urban areas? (Current model lacks explicit mode representation.)
2. **Equilibrium-based assignment:** Can equilibrium or dynamic traffic assignment replace the static shortest-path aggregation to improve accuracy under congestion and capture route choice heterogeneity?
3. **Cross-country generalization:** Does the model generalize to national road networks outside the UK, given differences in spatial planning, car dependency, and data availability?
4. **Uncertainty quantification:** How can uncertainty in predictions be quantified to support risk-aware planning decisions, especially under change scenarios?

## Limitations
- **Data harmonization and matching:** Procedures for harmonizing LSOA features (121 raw to 64 PCA) and matching TRIS sensors to OSM edges (5 km buffer, 10 m tolerance) are not fully specified; small changes could affect results.
- **Architectural hyperparameters:** Specific MLP layer sizes, activation details, and validation split ratio are not provided; these affect capacity and convergence.
- **Theoretical break conditions:** Sensitivity to travel-time cutoff (τ=1h) is untested; too low excludes long-distance trips, too high increases computation and may blur O/D exclusivity.

## Confidence
- **High confidence:** Competitive two-source Dijkstra mechanism and factorized architecture are clearly described and visualized.
- **Medium confidence:** Stability and interpretability of the learned deterrence function are supported by CV results, but exact shape and regional variation are from a single model instance; geographic transferability within England is demonstrated but cross-country generalizability is unknown.
- **Low confidence:** Exact specification and performance of the gravity baseline are ambiguous; optimality of chosen hyperparameters (MLP sizes, learning rate, τ) is not systematically validated.

## Next Checks
1. **OD Extraction Sanity Check:** On a small, well-understood subgraph, manually verify that for a target edge with known high traffic, the competitive Dijkstra retrieves disjoint O/D sets and that screening retains OD pairs whose shortest paths include the edge.
2. **Ablation of Competitive Mechanism:** Replace competitive Dijkstra with independent forward/backward searches (allowing node overlap). Retrain and evaluate on the same spatial CV folds. Expect significant drop in R² or increase in error variance.
3. **Deterrence Function Form Test:** Replace the learned MLP deterrence with a standard parametric form (e.g., exponential decay). Re-tune parameters on validation and compare MGEH/R² to the published MLP result to test the claim that a flexible form is essential.
