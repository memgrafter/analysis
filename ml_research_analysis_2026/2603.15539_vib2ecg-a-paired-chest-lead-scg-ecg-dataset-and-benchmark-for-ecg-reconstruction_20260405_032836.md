# Vib2ECG: A Paired Chest-Lead SCG-ECG Dataset and Benchmark for ECG Reconstruction

## Quick Facts
*   **arXiv ID:** 2603.15539
*   **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.15539
*   **Reference Count:** 40
*   **Primary Result:** A U-Net reconstructs chest-lead ECG from chest vibrations with an average L1 distance of 0.1524, but exhibits "hallucination" of spurious waveforms, which is mitigated by decomposing vibration into low-frequency SCG (2-20 Hz) and high-frequency PCGL (>20 Hz) components.

## Executive Summary
This study introduces Vib2ECG, a novel dataset comprising synchronized twelve-lead electrocardiogram (ECG) and seismocardiography (SCG) signals collected from six chest positions on 17 human subjects. The core objective is to reconstruct clinical-grade, chest-lead ECG waveforms from low-cost, IMU-based vibration measurements. Using a compact U-Net model (364K parameters), the authors achieve a mean L1 distance of 0.1524 between reconstructed and ground-truth ECGs. A critical challenge identified is a "hallucination" phenomenon where the model generates spurious ECG morphologies. An ablation study demonstrates that preprocessing the vibration signal by decomposing it into a primary SCG band (2-20 Hz) and a residual high-frequency component (PCGL, >20 Hz) significantly reduces these hallucinations and improves reconstruction fidelity. However, temporal generalization tests over eight days show performance oscillation, with noise cited as a primary contributing factor.

## Method Summary
The Vib2ECG dataset was collected using synchronized twelve-lead ECG and a single inertial measurement unit (IMU) placed sequentially at six distinct chest locations on each of 17 subjects. The vibration signal from each position serves as the input for ECG reconstruction. The primary model is a U-Net architecture with 364,000 parameters. A key preprocessing step involves a frequency-domain decomposition of the raw chest vibration: the SCG component is isolated via a bandpass filter (2-20 Hz), while the remaining high-frequency content is retained as the PCGL (Pressure-Cardiogram-Like) component. Both decomposed components are provided as input channels to the U-Net, which is trained to map the multi-channel vibration data to the corresponding twelve-lead ECG waveform. Model performance is evaluated using the L1 distance (mean absolute error) between predicted and actual ECG signals.

## Key Results
*   Introduced Vib2ECG, the first paired dataset of synchronized twelve-lead ECGs and multi-position chest SCG from IMUs.
*   A 364K-parameter U-Net reconstructs chest-lead ECGs with a low average L1 distance of 0.1524.
*   Frequency decomposition of input vibration (into 2-20Hz SCG and >20Hz PCGL) is an effective ablation that reduces the occurrence of spurious "hallucinated" waveforms and improves reconstruction accuracy.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The study posits, based on ablation results, that the decomposition strategy is central to mitigating hallucinations. Separating the low-frequency SCG band (2-20 Hz), which contains the dominant mechanical cardiac signal, from the higher-frequency PCGL component (>20 Hz), which likely captures noise, respiration artifacts, and smaller-amplitude vibrations, provides a cleaner and more disentangled input representation. This preprocessing acts as a form of regularization, making the mapping from vibration to ECG less ambiguous for the model and reducing its tendency to generate plausible-but-incorrect waveforms from ambiguous or noisy signal patterns. The exact physiological or physical reason why this specific frequency split is optimal is not mechanistically explained.

## Architecture Onboarding
*   **Component Map:**
    Raw Chest Vibration Signal -> Frequency Decomposition (Bandpass 2-20Hz & Highpass >20Hz) -> Concatenated Multi-Channel Input (SCG + PCGL) -> U-Net Encoder (Downsampling) -> U-Net Bottleneck -> U-Net Decoder (Upsampling with Skip Connections) -> Reconstructed 12-Lead ECG Waveforms

*   **Critical Path:** The most direct route from vibration to ECG involves: 1) acquiring a clean, synchronized vibration signal at a relevant chest location, 2) applying the 2-20Hz / >20Hz decomposition to create the two input channels, 3) processing these through the U-Net's contracting and expanding paths, where spatial/feature resolution is traded for context in the bottleneck and then restored using skip connections, and 4) producing a time-series output for each of the twelve leads.

*   **Design Tradeoffs:**
    *   **Simplicity vs. Expressivity:** The 364K-parameter U-Net is relatively small, favoring efficiency and reduced overfitting on a small dataset at the potential cost of capturing complex, subject-specific mappings.
    *   **Input Representation:** Using decomposed frequency bands adds a heuristic, domain-specific preprocessing step. This improves performance (reduces hallucinations) but may discard subtle information in the transition bands and assumes the 2-20Hz split is optimal.
    *   **Lead Independence:** The model outputs all twelve leads simultaneously. A tradeoff exists between modeling inter-lead correlations (beneficial for consistency) and allowing lead-specific error patterns (potentially harmful for clinical diagnosis).

*   **Failure Signatures:**
    *   **Hallucination:** The appearance of clear, periodic P-QRS-T complexes in the reconstruction that do not align with the ground truth ECG, especially in noisy or ambiguous input segments.
    *   **Amplitude/Scaling Error:** Systematic under- or over-estimation of ECG waveform amplitudes.
    *   **Morphological Distortion:** Blurring or compression of key ECG features (e.g., fused T-waves, flattened P-waves).
    *   **Temporal Drift/Jitter:** Phase shifts between predicted and actual waveforms that vary over time or across days.

*   **Exactly 3 First Experiments:**
    1.  **Ablation on Decomposition Frequencies:** Re-run the key ablation experiment by systematically varying the cutoff frequency between the SCG and PCGL bands (e.g., 15Hz, 25Hz) to determine the optimal split and test the robustness of the 2-20Hz finding.
    2.  **Controlled Noise Injection:** Artificially add calibrated levels of Gaussian noise and motion artifact simulations to the SCG/PCGL inputs of a trained model to quantify the relationship between signal-to-noise ratio (SNR) and the frequency of hallucination events.
    3.  **Architecture Comparison:** Re-implement the same task with a fundamentally different architecture (e.g., a transformer-based sequence-to-sequence model or a conditional GAN) using the identical Vib2ECG dataset and decomposition preprocessing. Compare the absolute L1 error and, crucially, the rate and nature of hallucinations against the reported U-Net baseline.

## Limitations
*   **Small, Homogeneous Cohort:** The dataset includes only 17 subjects, limiting statistical power and raising serious questions about generalizability across diverse demographics, body types, and cardiac pathologies.
*   **Unexplained Hallucination Mechanism:** While the decomposition ablation shows correlation, the fundamental reason why the model generates spurious waveforms—whether due to architectural bias, data scarcity, or inherent ambiguity in the SCG→ECG mapping—is not mechanistically investigated.
*   **Temporal Generalization Instability:** Performance degrades and oscillates over an 8-day period. The exclusive attribution to noise is preliminary; unmodeled physiological drifts (e.g., hydration, electrolyte balance) or sensor placement variance could also be significant factors.

## Next Checks
1.  **External Validation on a Diverse Cohort:** Replicate the study on a larger, multi-center dataset encompassing a wide range of ages, sexes, body mass indices, and cardiac conditions (e.g., arrhythmias, conduction disorders, hypertrophy) to quantify the drop in performance and determine if hallucinations correlate with specific pathologies.
2.  **Mechanistic Failure Mode Analysis of Hallucinations:** Conduct a detailed error analysis. Segment the reconstructions by ECG phase (P-wave, QRS, ST, T-wave). Correlate hallucination frequency with local signal quality metrics (SNR of SCG/PCGL), respiratory phase, and known arrhythmic events. This requires expert cardiologist review to classify the clinical plausibility of generated waveforms.
3.  **Generalizability of the Decomposition Principle:** Test whether the 2-20Hz / >20Hz decomposition strategy is model-agnostic. Apply the identical preprocessing pipeline to at least two other reconstruction architectures (e.g., a Temporal Convolutional Network and a Transformer). Determine if the reduction in hallucinations and improvement in L1 error persist across architectures or are specific to the U-Net design.
