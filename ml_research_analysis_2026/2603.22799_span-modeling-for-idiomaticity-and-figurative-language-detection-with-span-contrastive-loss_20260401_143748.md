# Span Modeling for Idiomaticity and Figurative Language Detection with Span Contrastive Loss

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2603.22799  
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.22799  
- **Reference count:** 31  
- **Primary result:** Achieved state-of-the-art sequence accuracy on MAGPIE (random) and demonstrated strong cross-dataset generalization, particularly when trained on PIFL-OSCAR.

## Executive Summary
Non-compositional idioms and figurative language pose challenges for language models due to tokenization artifacts and contextual ambiguity. This paper addresses the problem by finetuning BERT and RoBERTa models with a dual-objective approach: a token-level slot loss for precise span边界 detection and a novel span contrastive loss (SCL) with hard negative reweighting. Spans are represented as the mean of their constituent token embeddings, enabling a fixed-size vector that captures multi-word expression semantics. The method achieves state-of-the-art sequence accuracy on the MAGPIE dataset and shows robust cross-dataset performance, especially when pretrained on PIFL-OSCAR. The authors also propose the geometric mean of F1 and sequence accuracy as a span-aware evaluation metric and validate SCL’s effectiveness through ablations.

## Method Summary
The task is span labeling for idiomatic/figurative multi-word expressions using a BIO tagging scheme. The model uses a pretrained BERT or RoBERTa encoder to produce contextual token embeddings. Token-level predictions are trained with a standard cross-entropy slot loss. For span-level learning, gold (or predicted) contiguous spans are pooled via mean of their token embeddings to form fixed-size vectors. A supervised contrastive loss is applied across span vectors in a minibatch: positive pairs are spans sharing the same label, negatives are all others. A hard negative reweighting variant amplifies the loss contribution of the top‑k most similar negatives per anchor. The total loss combines the slot loss and the span contrastive loss weighted by λ_span, which is tuned per dataset on the development set. Training uses 80/10/10 splits, with evaluation based on sequence accuracy (exact span match), token-level F1, and their geometric mean.

## Key Results
- Achieved new state-of-the-art sequence accuracy on the MAGPIE (random) dataset.
- Demonstrated strong cross-dataset generalization, with models trained on PIFL-OSCAR transferring well to other benchmarks.
- Ablation studies confirmed the effectiveness of span contrastive loss with hard negative reweighting across multiple datasets; introduced the geometric mean of F1 and sequence accuracy as a robust span-aware metric.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
Mean pooling of contextual token embeddings yields a fixed-size span vector that preserves the overall semantics of a multi‑word expression while smoothing out noise from individual tokens. This simple, differentiable operation creates representations suitable for contrastive learning. The approach assumes that the average captures essential features, but may dilute rare important tokens in very long spans or blur contradictory semantics (e.g., in irony).

### Mechanism 2
Hard negative reweighting in the span contrastive loss focuses learning on the most confusable negative spans within a batch. For each anchor span, the top‑k hardest negatives (highest cosine similarity) are given extra weight in the loss denominator, amplifying gradient pressure to separate them from the anchor. This targets the decision boundary and accelerates convergence to a more separable embedding space. However, if hard negative mining accidentally groups semantically valid similar spans, it may harm generalization.

### Mechanism 3
Joint optimization of token‑level slot loss and span‑level contrastive loss complements boundary precision with discriminative span representations. The slot loss ensures accurate BIO tagging, while the contrastive loss explicitly shapes the embedding space to cluster spans by label (idiomatic vs. literal). This addresses the challenge that non‑compositional expressions often look similar token‑wise but require global span discrimination. The balance λ_span must be tuned; inappropriate values can cause one loss to dominate.

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map:**  
  Encoder (BERT/RoBERTa) → Token Classifier (linear layer) → Span Extractor (gold/predicted BIO) → Span Pooling (mean) → Contrastive Loss Module (in‑batch positives/negatives with hard reweighting) → Loss Combiner (L_total = L_slot + λ_span × L_SCL) → Optimizer (AdamW)  
- **Critical path:**  
  Input sentence → tokenization → encoder → token embeddings.  
  Parallel path A: token embeddings → token classifier → BIO predictions → slot loss.  
  Parallel path B: gold/predicted spans → span pooling → span vectors → contrastive loss over batch.  
  Total loss = slot loss + λ_span × SCL loss → backpropagation.  
- **Design tradeoffs:**  
  - *Pooling choice:* Mean pooling is simple but may blur salient tokens; max or attention pooling could preserve key features at added complexity.  
  - *In‑batch negatives:* Larger batches increase negative diversity but introduce many easy negatives that contribute little.  
  - *λ_span tuning:* Requires validation search; optimal value appears dataset‑dependent.  
  - *Hard negative selection:* *k* controls how many top‑similar negatives are reweighted; too small may miss confusing cases, too large may include false positives.  
  - *Gold vs. predicted spans:* Using gold spans provides a clean signal but creates a train‑test mismatch; predicted spans with confidence weighting could mitigate this.  
- **Failure signatures:**  
  - Sequence accuracy much lower than token F1 → poor span boundary detection.  
  - Span embeddings show no class separation in visualizations → SCL ineffective.  
  - Large cross‑dataset drops → overfitting to training distribution.  
  - SCL loss dominates early training → λ_span too high.  
  - Loss spikes/NaNs with hard negatives → unstable similarity scaling; adjust temperature or clamp.  
- **First 3 experiments:**  
  1. **λ_span ablation:** Train with λ_span ∈ {0.0, 0.1, …, 1.0} per dataset; select best λ on dev sequence accuracy.  
  2. **Cross‑dataset generalization:** Train on each dataset (optimal λ) and evaluate on all others; compute mean and worst‑case geometric mean to assess robustness.  
  3. **Embedding visualization:** Extract span embeddings from models with λ=0 and best λ; apply PCA/t‑SNE to inspect cluster separation.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- **Cross‑lingual span similarity:** The authors observed untrained cross‑lingual clusters in t‑SNE for SemEval and suggest exploring whether explicitly modeling this can improve low‑resource language performance.  
- **BERT vs. RoBERTa discrepancy:** BERT often outperforms RoBERTa despite RoBERTa’s enhanced pretraining; the cause (e.g., tokenization, overfitting) is unexplained.  
- **Geometric mean metric validity:** The proposed √(F1·SA) metric lacks validation against human judgments or comparison with alternatives; its robustness is unknown.  
- **FLUTE/SemEval adaptation:** These datasets were not originally labeled for slot‑filling, leading to poor performance; specific mismatches and redesign strategies are unanalyzed.

## Limitations
- **Reproducibility gaps:** Dataset splits, tokenization details (e.g., subword span handling), and critical hyperparameters (temperature τ, hard negative *k*, learning rate, epochs) are not fully specified.  
- **Architecture ambiguities:** Unclear whether a CRF was used for token classification and how training with gold spans transitions to inference with predicted spans. Batch construction for contrastive learning (number of spans, sampling) is unspecified, risking insufficient positive pairs in small batches.  
- **Cross‑dataset confounders:** Performance drops on FLUTE/SemEval may stem from language differences (Portuguese) or annotation inconsistencies, not purely model limitations; the paper does not control for these factors.

## Confidence
- **Mean pooling yields representative span vectors:** Medium. Sound theoretically and externally validated, but no within‑study ablation against alternatives.  
- **Hard negative reweighting improves discriminative power:** Medium‑High. Supported by ablation results, yet details on *k* and batch interactions remain unclear.  
- **Combined slot loss and SCL yields SOTA performance:** High. Empirical gains across datasets are strong, but reproducibility uncertainties temper full confidence.
