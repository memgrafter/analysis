# Curia-2: Scaling Self-Supervised Learning for Radiology Foundation Models

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2604.01987
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.01987
- **Reference count:** 26
- **Primary result:** Curia-2 achieves state-of-the-art on CuriaBench (88.5% 2D avg, 88.6% 3D avg), scaling ViTs to 1.3B parameters for radiology.

## Executive Summary
Curia-2 adapts the DINOv2 self-supervised framework for large-scale radiological CT/MRI pre-training. It introduces three key modifications: content-aware cropping to stabilize training by discarding uninformative background-heavy images, anatomically-guided masking to focus iBOT's reconstruction on meaningful anatomy, and SigReg regularization to preserve clinically similar structures in the embedding space. The system scales Vision Transformers up to 1.3B parameters—a first for multi-modal radiology foundation models—and evaluates on a restructured CuriaBench with separate 2D and 3D tracks. Curia-2's large variants (L, g) set new benchmarks, outperforming all competing radiology FMs and rivaling vision-language models on findings detection.

## Method Summary
Curia-2 modifies DINOv2's student-teacher distillation with medical-specific adaptations. Pre-training uses a two-stage pipeline: first at 256×256 resolution, then fine-tuning at 512×512 via positional embedding interpolation. The data pipeline applies content-aware cropping (discard images >50% background; enforce crops ≤70% background) and anatomically-guided masking (Gaussian-weighted prior centered on estimated anatomy). The loss combines DINO (global crop alignment), iBOT (masked patch reconstruction), and SigReg (embedding isotropy), trained with an increased learning rate of 1e-3. Model scaling targets ViT-B (86M), ViT-L (303M), and ViT-g (1.3B). Downstream evaluation uses frozen encoders with linear probes averaged over 5 seeds on 19 CuriaBench tasks.

## Key Results
- Curia-2 (L, g variants) achieves 88.5% average on the 2D track.
- Curia-2 L leads the 3D track at 88.6% average.
- Outperforms all competing radiology foundation models and competes with vision-language models on findings detection.
- Demonstrates first successful scaling of ViTs to 1.3B parameters in multi-modal radiology.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Content-aware cropping stabilizes training
- **Claim:** Cropping based on foreground content prevents contrastive learning from focusing on irrelevant background.
- **Mechanism:** Images with >50% uniform background are discarded. Remaining images generate global and local crops containing at most 70% background, ensuring meaningful regions for DINO's feature alignment.
- **Break condition:** Overly strict thresholds cause excessive data loss; poor background estimation (e.g., due to pathology) yields uninformative crops.

### Anatomically-guided masking improves iBOT signal
- **Claim:** Biasing mask selection toward anatomical structures makes patch reconstruction a more meaningful task.
- **Mechanism:** A Gaussian-weighted prior, centered on estimated anatomical regions and adjusted for crop offsets, guides the blockwise masking away from background.
- **Break condition:** Misestimated anatomical center (from low-resolution crops or atypical anatomy) directs masks to non-informative patches.

### SigReg preserves clinically meaningful clusters
- **Claim:** Pulling embeddings toward an isotropic Gaussian maintains density without artificially separating clinically similar anatomy (e.g., normal variants).
- **Mechanism:** SigReg regularizes the embedding distribution's covariance toward identity, unlike KoLeo's neighbor-pushing which disrupts natural anatomical topology.
- **Break condition:** Excessive SigReg weight overly smooths embeddings, reducing discriminative power between distinct pathologies.

## Architecture Onboarding
Data loader -> Content-aware cropping -> Augmentation (crops + masking) -> Student/Teacher ViTs -> Losses (DINO + iBOT + SigReg) -> Backprop/Update -> High-res fine-tuning -> Linear probe evaluation.

- **Component map:**  
  3D volume loading -> Slice extraction (2D) / averaging (3D) -> Content-aware filter -> Global/local crop generation -> Anatomical mask prior application -> Student/Teacher forward -> Loss computation -> Student update; Teacher momentum update -> Positional embedding interpolation -> 512px fine-tuning -> Frozen feature extraction -> Linear head training.

- **Critical path:**  
  1. Batch loading with content-aware filtering.  
  2. Apply anatomically-guided masks to student views.  
  3. Compute total loss = α·DINO + β·iBOT + γ·SigReg.  
  4. Backpropagate, update student; update teacher by momentum.  
  5. After pre-training, interpolate positional embeddings, fine-tune at 512×512 for 20k steps.  
  6. Downstream: average-pool features (3D), train linear probe, average 5 seeds.

- **Design tradeoffs:**  
  - Cropping thresholds: Stricter (e.g., 50% discard) reduces noise but discards data; looser risks uninformative crops.  
  - Masking prior: Gaussian needs reliable anatomical center estimation; uniform is simpler but less targeted.  
  - SigReg weight: Balances isotropy vs. clinical discriminativeness; too high erases meaningful clusters.  
  - Resolution: 256px pre-training is efficient but may lose details; 512 fine-tuning adds compute.  
  - Model size: Larger models (g) have higher capacity but show diminishing returns; cost increases superlinearly.

- **Failure signatures:**  
  - High discard rate (>20%): thresholds too strict or data quality issues → small batches, unstable gradients.  
  - Stagnant iBOT loss: masking not challenging (poor anatomical prior) or capacity insufficient.  
  - Embedding collapse: all features similar → excessive SigReg or insufficient crop diversity.  
  - No gain from high-res fine-tuning: low-res features already sufficient or schedule inadequate.  
  - Scaling plateau: minimal gain from L to g (<0.5% avg) → data/optimization limits.

- **First 3 experiments:**  
  1. Baseline collapse: Train DINOv2 (ViT-B, 256px, random crops, KoLeo). Expect instability and ~46.9% average (Table 2).  
  2. Cropping impact: Add content-aware cropping. Measure stability and jump to ~81.5% average.  
  3. Regularization & masking swap: Replace KoLeo with SigReg and use anatomical masking vs uniform. Evaluate incremental gain on anatomy tasks (A1–A3) and overall.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- **Ultra-large-scale training effects:** How do drastic increases in data, batch size, and training duration affect scaling laws in medical imaging?  
  *Basis:* Future work will explore ultra-large-scale strategies from natural images.  
  *Why unresolved:* Curia-2 used fixed dataset and compute; benefits/saturation in medical domain unknown.  
  *Evidence needed:* Incremental scaling experiments measuring downstream accuracy to derive domain-specific curves.

- **Gram anchoring from DINOv3:** Does post-training Gram anchoring improve representation quality for radiology?  
  *Basis:* Future work will explore post-training refinements like Gram anchoring.  
  *Why unresolved:* Gram anchoring's transfer to medical anatomy priors and clinical tasks unevaluated.  
  *Evidence needed:* Apply Gram anchoring to Curia-2 and compare linear probing on CuriaBench and clinical tasks.

- **Billion-parameter scaling bottlenecks:** What prevents proportional gains when scaling ViTs beyond 1.3B in medical imaging?  
  *Basis:* Curia-2 g performance close to L, indicating unresolved scaling challenges.  
  *Why unresolved:* Paper observes diminishing returns without investigating causes (data, optimization, architecture).  
  *Evidence needed:* Scaling experiments beyond 1.3B with analyses of training stability, gradients, and representation similarity.

## Limitations
- **Data access:** Pre-training uses 130 TB private CT/MRI; no public release or equivalent dataset specified.
- **Hyperparameter gaps:** Gaussian σ for masking, SigReg weight/covariance, and high-res fine-tuning schedule are not detailed.
- **Benchmark leakage risk:** CuriaBench tasks originate from same private hospital as pre-training data.
- **Compute barrier:** ViT-g training requires 128×A100 GPUs for ~9.7k GPU-hours, limiting reproducibility.
- **Scaling plateau:** Minimal gain from ViT-L to ViT-g suggests unresolved optimization/data constraints at billion-parameter scale.
