# Fusion Complexity Inversion: Why Simpler Cross View Modules Outperform SSMs and Cross View Attention Transformers for Pasture Biomass Regression

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2603.07819
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.07819
- **Reference count:** 40
- **Primary result:** A two-layer gated depthwise convolution attains R²=0.903 on the CSIRO pasture biomass benchmark, significantly outperforming more complex cross-view attention (0.833), bidirectional SSMs (0.819), and full Mamba (0.793) architectures.

## Executive Summary
This study investigates cross-view fusion modules for estimating pasture biomass from dual-view imagery, using the small (n=357) CSIRO benchmark. It discovers a "fusion complexity inversion": simpler local fusion (a two-layer gated depthwise convolution) dramatically outperforms more sophisticated global mechanisms like cross-view attention and state-space models. Crucially, the quality of the vision backbone's pretraining—specifically scaling from DINOv2 to DINOv3—yields a far larger performance gain (+5.0 R²) than any fusion design choice. Furthermore, incorporating metadata available only during training (species, state, NDVI) collapses all performance differences and harms the best model by 7.4 R² points, creating a ceiling near R²=0.829. The actionable insight for sparse agricultural benchmarks is to prioritize large-scale backbone pretraining over complex fusion and to exclude inference-unavailable features.

## Method Summary
The task is multi-output regression of five pasture biomass components from dual-view images. Each 2000×1000px image is split into two 512×512 left/right crops, normalized with ImageNet statistics. Targets are log(1+y) transformed. A weight-tied shared backbone (e.g., DINOv3-ViT-L) processes each view to a 1024-token grid (dim 1024). The two token sequences are concatenated (2048×1024) and passed through a configurable fusion module. The fused sequence is globally average-pooled to a 1024-d vector, which feeds three independent two-layer MLP heads (hidden dim 512, GELU, Softplus output) predicting Green, Dead, and Clover components; their sum yields the composite targets. Training uses AdamW with differential learning rates (backbone: 1e-5, heads: 5e-4), weight decay 1e-2, Huber loss (β=5.0), cosine annealing with warmup, gradient clipping, and augmentation. Evaluation employs 5-fold stratified group cross-validation (stratify on Dry Total quintiles, group by image ID) with early stopping. The primary metric is a weighted R² with weights [0.1, 0.1, 0.1, 0.2, 0.5].

## Key Results
- **Fusion complexity inversion:** A two-layer GatedDWConv (kernel size 5) achieves R²=0.903, outperforming global fusion modules: Cross-View Global Attention (0.833), Bidirectional SSM (0.819), and full Mamba (0.793, below the no-fusion Identity baseline at 0.819).
- **Backbone pretraining scale dominates:** Upgrading from DINOv2 to DINOv3 on the same ViT-L architecture yields a +5.0 R² gain. A strict monotonic improvement is observed from EfficientNet-B3 (0.555) → VMamba-B (0.717) → DINOv2-ViT-L (0.853) → DINOv3-ViT-L (0.903).
- **Metadata shortcut collapses performance:** Adding training-only metadata (species, state, NDVI, height) causes all fusion modules to converge to a similar performance ceiling of ~R²=0.829. The top GatedDWConv+DINOv3 model suffers a -7.4 point drop, while simpler models are less affected.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1: Local Fusion Suffices Due to Strong Intra-View Backbone Representations
- **Claim:** On this sparse agricultural dataset, the large pretrained backbone already provides sufficient global context within each view; the fusion module only needs to perform local cross-view alignment, which a lightweight depthwise convolution can do efficiently without overfitting.
- **Mechanism:** The DINOv3-ViT-L backbone (24 self-attention layers) processes each 512×512 view into rich, spatially coherent features that already capture long-range patterns. Therefore, inter-view communication need only correlate corresponding local regions (e.g., matching grass blades across the stereo pair) rather than model global dependencies across the entire concatenated 2048-token sequence. A two-layer gated depthwise convolution with a small kernel (k=5) provides this localized interaction with minimal parameters, avoiding overfitting on ~286 training images per fold.
- **Evidence:** [abstract] reports the GatedDWConv outperforms all global fusion modules. [section IV-E] explicitly states: "Since global self attention is already performed across 24 transformer layers within each view by the DINOv3-ViT-L backbone, the fusion module need only enable local cross view communication." The failure of Full Mamba below Identity further suggests global fusion is counterproductive.
- **Break condition:** If the dataset were orders of magnitude larger (e.g., tens of thousands of images), global fusion mechanisms might no longer overfit and could capture richer cross-view patterns, potentially reversing the inversion.

### Mechanism 2: Backbone Pretraining Scale Monopolizes Performance Gains
- **Claim:** The quality of generic visual features learned during massive pretraining is the primary bottleneck for downstream performance on this small dataset; architectural innovations in fusion cannot compensate for poorly initialized features.
- **Mechanism:** Self-supervised pretraining on increasingly large corpora (ImageNet-1K → LVD-142M → LVD-1.7B) teaches the backbone to produce more linearly separable and robust representations. For the CSIRO target task with only 357 images, the fine-tuned backbone's feature quality is the limiting factor. The DINOv2→DINOv3 upgrade (+5.0 R²) with identical architecture proves representation learning scale dominates fusion design.
- **Evidence:** [section IV-G] Table VI shows a strict monotonic increase in R² as backbone pretraining scale grows: EfficientNet-B3 (0.555) → VMamba-B (0.717) → DINOv2-ViT-L (0.853) → DINOv3-ViT-L (0.903). The gain from pretraining (+0.348 from ImageNet-1K to LVD-1.7B) dwarfs the maximum effect of fusion choice (+0.084 from Identity to GatedDWConv). [section IV-H] Figure 11 shows DINOv3 produces more coherent vegetation segmentations than VMamba, paralleling the R² ranking.
- **Break condition:** If the backbone were already saturated on a vastly larger pretraining corpus (e.g., >10B images), fusion complexity might become the dominant variable. Alternatively, if the task required reasoning absent from standard image pretraining (e.g., explicit physical growth models), more complex fusion might be necessary.

### Mechanism 3: Training-Only Metadata Acts as a Harmful Shortcut
- **Claim:** Metadata available only during training (species, state, NDVI) serves as a strong predictive shortcut that high-capacity models latch onto, causing a severe performance drop at inference when metadata is absent.
- **Mechanism:** Species and state are highly correlated with biomass (e.g., "lucerne in Victoria" yields more). When concatenated with visual features, the metadata MLP pathway exploits these shortcuts, reducing pressure on the visual stream to learn fine-grained biomass cues. At test time, the missing metadata causes a distribution shift. High-capacity visual models (like GatedDWConv+DINOv3) suffer the largest drop because their learned visual representations were under-utilized during training; simpler models (Identity, BidirMamba) rely less on visual quality and are less affected.
- **Evidence:** [section IV-F] Table V shows metadata inclusion collapses the fusion performance spread from 8.4 points to 0.1 points, clustering all models around R²≈0.829. The top model (GatedDWConv) drops -0.074, while the Identity baseline gains slightly (+0.009). [section IV-H] Figure 10(d) shows NDVI and height jointly stratify Dry Total, confirming their predictive signal.
- **Break condition:** If metadata dropout during training were very aggressive or the metadata pathway were heavily regularized (e.g., nonlinear bottleneck), the shortcut might be broken. If the metadata were actually available at inference (e.g., real-time NDVI sensors), the harm would not occur.

## Foundational Learning
- **Concept: Self-Supervised Vision Transformers (DINO family)**  
  *Why needed:* The performance hierarchy rests on understanding that DINO models differ mainly in pretraining data scale, not architecture. DINO's self-distillation without labels learns transferable features from up to 1.7B images.  
  *Quick check:* How does a self-distillation framework like DINO encourage features useful for downstream regression with no semantic labels?

- **Concept: Cross-View Fusion in Dual-Branch Architectures**  
  *Why needed:* The core manipulation is the fusion module between two image halves. Grasping the spectrum from no fusion (Identity) to local (convolution) to global (attention, SSM) is key to understanding the inversion finding.  
  *Quick check:* What are the computational (O(Nk) vs. O(N²)) and statistical (sample efficiency) tradeoffs between depthwise convolution and full cross-view attention when N=1024 tokens?

- **Concept: Sparse Data Generalization and Shortcut Learning**  
  *Why needed:* The dataset is small (n=357), zero-inflated, and imbalanced. The metadata paradox is a classic shortcut-learning case where models exploit spurious training correlations that vanish at test time.  
  *Quick check:* Why might adding a predictive training-only feature (like pasture species) hurt final performance more on a high-capacity model than on a simpler one?

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map:**  
  Input Splitter -> Weight-Tied Backbone (DINOv3-ViT-L) -> Fusion Module (e.g., GatedDWConv) -> Adaptive Pooling -> Compositional Heads (3× MLP) -> [Sum for composites]. Optional: Metadata Encoder (MLP) -> concat before pooling.
- **Critical path:** Dual-view image -> split into left/right halves -> each half processed by shared backbone -> token sequences concatenated -> fusion module performs cross-view interaction -> global average pooling -> three independent heads predict component biomasses -> sum yields composites. The backbone and fusion are the sole cross-view interaction points; metadata bypasses fusion.
- **Design tradeoffs:**  
  - *Fusion complexity:* Local (DWConv) vs. Global (Attention/SSM). Global adds parameters and O(N²) cost but risks overfitting on 286 samples/fold; local is parameter-efficient and stable.  
  - *Metadata inclusion:* Training-only signals create a shortcut. Short-term training gain vs. long-term inference harm; modality dropout (p=0.2) proved insufficient.  
  - *Backbone scale:* Larger pretraining (DINOv3) gives biggest gain but increases VRAM; gradient checkpointing and differential LR mitigate this.
- **Failure signatures:**  
  - *Metadata collapse:* All fusion types converge to ~R²=0.829 when metadata is included; best no-metadata model drops by 7.4 points.  
  - *Fold instability:* Fold 4 (hardest) shows 5-12 point drops across models; proposed model has highest CV (7.0%), indicating sensitivity to edge-case pastures.  
  - *Mamba underperformance:* Full Mamba (0.793) falls below Identity (0.819), suggesting SSM inductive biases mismatch task/data scale.
- **First 3 experiments:**  
  1. **Baseline establishment:** Train Identity (no fusion) with DINOv3-ViT-L to confirm backbone-only performance (expected R²≈0.819). Establishes fusion benefit baseline.  
  2. **Fusion depth sweep:** Test GatedDWConv with 1, 2, 4 blocks to locate the inverted-U peak at 2 blocks (R²=0.903). Identifies optimal local receptive field (kernel 5×2=9 tokens).  
  3. **Metadata harm validation:** Add species/state/NDVI to the 2-block GatedDWConv model. Expect drop to ~R²=0.829, confirming the shortcut effect and validating "exclude inference-unavailable features."

## Limitations
- Dataset is small (357 images) and region-specific, limiting generalizability and increasing overfitting risk despite cross-validation.
- Model hyperparameters (e.g., dropout rates, bias terms) are incompletely specified, risking implementation variance.
- DINOv3 pretrained weights may not be publicly available, forcing proxies that could alter the performance hierarchy.
- The "metadata shortcut" claim is inferential; causal mechanisms (e.g., exact reliance on species/state) are not directly proven.
- Evaluation relies on a custom weighted R²; robustness to alternative metrics is unexplored.

## Confidence
- **Fusion complexity inversion:** Medium – strong internal evidence but restricted to one dataset; external replication needed.
- **Backbone pretraining scale dominance:** High – clear monotonic gains across backbones with large effect sizes.
- **Metadata inclusion harms high-capacity models:** High – consistent ablative results across fusion types.

## Next Checks
1. **External dataset test:** Deploy the best GatedDWConv+DINOv3 model on an independent dual-view pasture biomass dataset (e.g., from a different continent) and benchmark against cross-view attention and SSM modules.
2. **Pretraining scale isolation:** If intermediate DINOv3 checkpoints are available, evaluate them on CSIRO to verify monotonic R² improvement with pretraining data volume; alternatively, compare ViT models pretrained on ImageNet-1K, LVD-142M, and LVD-1.7B.
3. **Shortcut intervention:** Train a model with a gradient reversal layer between the metadata encoder and fusion to force the network to ignore metadata, then evaluate test performance without metadata; expect the inversion effect to recover and the performance gap to narrow.
