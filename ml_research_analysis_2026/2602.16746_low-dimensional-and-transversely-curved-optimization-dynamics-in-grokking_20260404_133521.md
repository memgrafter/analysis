# Low-Dimensional and Transversely Curved Optimization Dynamics in Grokking

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2602.16746  
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.16746  
- **Reference count:** 18  
- **Primary result:** Optimization trajectories during grokking are confined to a low-dimensional execution manifold; transverse curvature, measured via commutator defects, grows sharply before generalisation and is triggered by a spectral near-degeneracy in attention matrices.

## Executive Summary
This paper investigates the delayed generalisation phenomenon known as grokking in transformers trained on modular arithmetic tasks. Through geometric analysis of weight-space trajectories—using principal component analysis (PCA) and commutator defects—the authors reveal that training evolves predominantly within a low-dimensional execution manifold, with a single principal component capturing 68–83% of the variance. Curvature of the loss landscape accumulates almost entirely in directions orthogonal to this manifold (residual fraction ρ≈1), and its onset consistently precedes generalisation by a lead time that follows a power law across a wide learning-rate sweep. A spectral mechanism is identified: a transient near-degeneracy in the leading singular values of attention matrices drives the curvature spike, and its subsequent breaking coincides with grokking. Interventions confirm that motion along the manifold is necessary for grokking, while merely increasing curvature is insufficient. The findings replicate across six operations, multiple seeds, and different regimes.

## Method Summary
The study trains 2‑layer Transformers (~290k parameters) on six binary operations modulo 97 (e.g., addition, multiplication) with a 50/50 train/test split. Models are optimised with AdamW (lr=1e⁻³, weight decay=1.0, batch=512) until test accuracy exceeds 98% three times; the grokking step is the first test accuracy ≥90%. Weight trajectories of attention matrices (WQ, WK, WV, WO) are logged every 100 steps. PCA on centred weight changes identifies a low‑dimensional execution subspace; the top 2–3 principal components are embedded into the full parameter space to form an orthonormal basis 𝔅. The commutator defect *D*—a Hessian‑free curvature probe—is computed by measuring the non‑commutativity of successive gradient steps on two mini‑batches. The defect vector δ is projected onto 𝔅 to obtain the residual fraction ρ=‖δ−𝔅𝔅ᵀδ‖/‖δ‖, quantifying transience. Defect onset is defined as the first step where *D* exceeds 10× its early baseline and 20. Interventions after step 500 either suppress gradient components along 𝔅 (varying strength *s*) or artificially boost curvature by adding kicks along δ.

## Key Results
- A single principal component captures 68–83% of trajectory variance, revealing a rank‑1 execution manifold.
- Commutator vectors are almost entirely orthogonal to this manifold (ρ≈1.000), indicating curvature accumulates transversely without deflecting the trajectory.
- Curvature growth precedes generalisation with a power‑law lead time (exponent α=1.27) across a 300× learning‑rate sweep.
- Suppressing gradients along the execution manifold prevents grokking (necessary), but artificially boosting curvature does not accelerate it (insufficient).
- Spectral analysis links curvature to a transient near‑degeneracy (σ₁≈σ₂) in the leading singular values of attention matrices; grokking occurs when this degeneracy breaks and one singular mode dominates (σ₁≫σ₂).

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1: Low‑Dimensional Confinement
- **Claim:** During grokking, the weight‑space trajectory is confined to a rank‑1 execution manifold.
- **Mechanism:** PCA on attention weight changes reveals a single principal component captures 68–83% of trajectory variance, indicating strong compression into one dominant direction.
- **Core assumption:** Optimization biases (e.g., weight decay) favour low‑norm solutions, restricting motion to a low‑dimensional subspace.
- **Evidence anchors:**  
  [abstract] “PCA of attention weight trajectories reveals that training evolves predominantly within a low‑dimensional execution subspace, with a single principal component capturing 68–83% of trajectory variance.”  
  [Section 4.1] “Weight Evolution is Rank‑1” presents spectra and z‑scores confirming genuine concentration.  
  [corpus] Consistent with Xu [2026] (corpus ID 261058), who first reported low‑dimensional execution manifolds in modular arithmetic.
- **Break condition:** Removing weight decay (λ=0) reduces PC1% and prevents grokking, showing the manifold formation is tied to regularisation.

### Mechanism 2: Orthogonal Curvature Accumulation
- **Claim:** Loss‑landscape curvature, measured via commutator defects, grows sharply in directions orthogonal to the execution manifold and this onset precedes generalisation.
- **Mechanism:** The commutator defect (non‑commutativity of successive gradient steps) projects almost entirely onto the normal bundle (ρ≈1), meaning curvature accumulates transversely without deflecting the trajectory. This builds geometric tension that eventually enables escape.
- **Core assumption:** The execution manifold is empirically invariant under SGD; curvature concentrates in directions not used by the optimiser.
- **Evidence anchors:**  
  [abstract] “commutator vectors are almost entirely orthogonal (residual fraction ρ≈1) indicating curvature accumulates transversely.”  
  [Section 4.2] “The Execution Manifold Exhibits Empirical Invariance” shows ρ≈1.000 across 36 conditions and random‑subspace controls (exec/random ratio 2–3×).  
  [Section 4.4] “Curvature Growth Precedes Generalisation” documents temporal ordering (sign test p=2⁻¹²).  
  [corpus] “The Geometry of Multi‑Task Grokking” (corpus ID 260801) also describes transverse instability; “Early‑Warning Signals of Grokking” (corpus ID 260482) links loss‑landscape geometry to grokking precursors.
- **Break condition:** Interventions suppressing orthogonal gradient flow prevent grokking (Section 4.7.1); artificially boosting curvature does not accelerate it (Section 4.7.2), so orthogonal growth is necessary but not sufficient.

### Mechanism 3: Spectral Symmetry‑Breaking Trigger
- **Claim:** The curvature explosion is driven by a transient near‑degeneracy in the leading singular values of attention matrices, creating representation instability; grokking occurs when this degeneracy breaks and one singular mode dominates.
- **Mechanism:** As training progresses, the spectral gap σ₁−σ₂ shrinks to near zero (σ₁≈σ₂), causing a spike in commutator defect. Then one mode becomes dominant (σ₁≫σ₂), aligning the operators and coinciding with generalisation.
- **Core assumption:** The singular value spectrum controls non‑commutativity of gradient steps; near‑degeneracy makes the representation basis unstable.
- **Evidence anchors:**  
  [Section 5] “Spectral Mechanism Underlying the Commutator Transition” presents the timeline (g₂₃↓ → σ₁≈σ₂ → D↑ → [WQ,WK]↑ → σ₁≫σ₂ → grok) and phase portraits (Figures 17–18).  
  [abstract] “Spectral analysis links curvature to a transient near‑degeneracy in the leading singular values of attention matrices.”  
  [corpus] “Grokking as a Variance‑Limited Phase Transition” (corpus ID 268829) also examines spectral dynamics, though the specific symmetry‑breaking loop appears to be a novel contribution of this work (corpus evidence limited).
- **Break condition:** Non‑grokking operations fail to exhibit the spectral loop; their trajectories are diffuse without a directed symmetry‑breaking event.

## Architecture Onboarding

- **Component map:** Token Embedding -> Positional Embedding -> Transformer Encoder (2 layers, d_model=128, 4 heads, d_ff=256, pre‑norm, GELU) -> Final LayerNorm -> Linear Head (~290k parameters). Logs WQ, WK, WV (from fused in‑proj), WO every 100 steps.

- **Critical path:** Train with AdamW (β₁=0.9, β₂=0.98), lr=1e⁻³, weight decay=1.0, batch=512, clip=1.0. Early stop if test acc>98% for 3 evals. Grok step = first test acc≥90%. Compute PCA on flattened weight changes from init; take top 2 PCs per matrix, embed into full param space, orthonormalise (QR) → basis 𝔅 (dim ~16). Compute commutator defect: sample two mini‑batches, compute θᴬᴮ, θᴮᴬ with η=1e⁻³, *D*=‖θᴬᴮ−θᴮᴬ‖/(‖ηgₐ‖‖ηgʙ‖). Median of 9 samples. Project defect δ onto 𝔅: ρ=‖δ−𝔅𝔅ᵀδ‖/‖δ‖. Interventions after step 500: gradient suppression (*g*→*g*∥+(1−*s*)*g*⊥, *s*∈[0,1]); directional forcing (add α·δ every 50 steps, α scaled).

- **Design tradeoffs:** Weight decay is essential for grokking; higher learning rates compress timeline and reduce predictive lead time. Logging every 100 steps balances resolution and storage. Top *K*=16–24 PCA directions capture manifold while avoiding noise.

- **Failure signatures:** Non‑grokking ops (a²+ab+b², a³+ab) show only moderate curvature growth (30–50×) and no spectral loop. No‑wd runs show curvature but no generalisation. Random projection only suppresses grokking at full strength (*s*=1), unlike PCA‑specific monotonic delay.

- **First 3 experiments:**  
  1. Compare PC1% and commutator defect across grokking vs. non‑grokking operations.  
  2. Sweep learning rate (3e⁻⁵ to 1e⁻²) to measure scaling of grok time and curvature lead time.  
  3. Intervention: suppress gradients along PCA subspace (vary *s*) to test necessity; apply kicks along commutator direction to test sufficiency.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
1. **Generalisability to larger models and real‑world data:** The experiments are limited to small Transformers and synthetic tasks; it remains unclear if the observed low‑dimensional manifolds and transverse curvature generalise to large‑scale language models and real datasets. Systematic replication on billion‑parameter models or theoretical arguments demonstrating invariance would resolve this.

2. **Efficient proxies for geometric diagnostics:** Commutator defect computation is expensive (multiple forward–backward passes per sample), limiting scalability. Developing cheap approximations (e.g., gradient covariance, spectral statistics) that correlate strongly with true defects and retain predictive power is an open direction.

3. **Theoretical framework for phase transitions:** A complete analytical model capturing defect accumulation, manifold formation, and damping‑controlled dynamics is lacking. Derivations in simplified setups (linear networks, two‑layer models) or stochastic differential equations that reproduce the empirical power‑law scaling and regime distinctions are needed.

4. **Why some tasks show curvature without grokking:** Non‑grokking operations exhibit 30–50× defect growth but never generalise, while grokking tasks reach 500–2000×. The precise condition distinguishing these outcomes—beyond local curvature—remains unknown, possibly involving interactions with global landscape structure or regularisation.

## Limitations
- Scope confined to 2‑layer Transformers (~290k parameters) and synthetic modular arithmetic; generalisation to deeper architectures or real‑world data is uncertain.
- Results rely on AdamW with weight decay = 1.0; other optimisers or regularisation schemes may yield different dynamics.
- Analysis restricted to attention matrices (WQ, WK, WV, WO); embeddings and feed‑forward layers might exhibit distinct behaviour.
- The spectral link to curvature is correlative and may be specific to attention’s linear structure; causality not established.
- Grokking step definition (test acc ≥ 90%) could affect measured lead times and scaling exponents.

## Confidence

- Transverse curvature (ρ≈1): **High**  
- Curvature onset precedes generalisation: **High**

## Next Checks
1. **Scale and architecture generality:** Replicate the full geometric pipeline (PCA, commutator defects, interventions) on deeper Transformers (3+ layers) and MLPs trained on algorithmic tasks to test robustness of low‑dimensional manifolds and transverse curvature.
2. **Full‑parameter analysis:** Include all parameters (embeddings, feed‑forward layers) in PCA and ρ computation to verify whether the execution manifold spans the entire weight space or is confined to attention.
3. **Spectral causality test:** Implement a direct intervention that penalises the second singular value relative to the first (e.g., via spectral regularisation) to establish whether forcing σ₁≫σ₂ advances the curvature onset and accelerates grokking.
