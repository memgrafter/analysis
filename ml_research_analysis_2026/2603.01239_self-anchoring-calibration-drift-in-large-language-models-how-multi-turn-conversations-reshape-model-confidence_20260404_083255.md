# Self-Anchoring Calibration Drift in Large Language Models: How Multi-Turn Conversations Reshape Model Confidence

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2603.01239  
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.01239  
- **Reference count:** 3  
- **Primary result:** LLMs exhibit model-specific calibration drift (SACD) across multi-turn dialogues: Claude suppresses confidence, GPT-5.2 escalates it in open-ended domains, and Gemini shows no point drift but fails to improve calibration naturally.

## Executive Summary
This study uncovers Self-Anchoring Calibration Drift (SACD)—systematic changes in expressed confidence caused by a model’s own prior outputs during multi-turn conversations. Testing Claude Sonnet 4.6, Gemini 3.1 Pro, and GPT-5.2 on 150 questions across three experimental conditions (single-turn baseline, self-anchoring, independent repetition) reveals heterogeneous effects: significant suppression for Claude, escalation for GPT-5.2 in ambiguous domains, and a unique ECE stagnation for Gemini. The drift is concentrated in open-ended questions, suggesting ground-truth constraints moderate self-anchoring. These findings highlight that model confidence is not stable within a conversation but dynamically reshaped by internal context, with implications for reliability and deployment.

## Method Summary
The experiment manipulated conversation context via three conditions: Condition A (single-turn baseline) measured initial confidence and accuracy; Condition B (self-anchoring) presented the same question with four neutral follow-up prompts (e.g., “Elaborate.”) while preserving full dialogue history; Condition C (independent repetition) asked the identical question repeatedly but reset context each turn, isolating exposure from self-authored context. Confidence drift was quantified using Confidence Drift Score (CDS) and Expected Calibration Error (ECE). Each model was evaluated on a stratified subsample (n=15 per condition) to control for computational cost, enabling within-model comparisons of calibration trajectories.

## Key Results
- **Model-heterogeneous SACD:** Claude Sonnet 4.6 showed significant confidence suppression (CDS = -0.032, p=.029) and pronounced in open-ended domain (-0.094). GPT-5.2 exhibited positive drift in open-ended questions (mean CDS=+0.026) with rising ECE at later turns. Gemini 3.1 Pro had no significant CDS but its ECE remained flat (~0.333) under self-anchoring, while independent repetition reduced ECE to near zero.
- **Domain moderation:** Near-zero CDS on factual items across all models, indicating that verifiable answers constrain self-anchoring effects.
- **Gemini’s distinct pattern:** Self-anchoring prevented natural calibration improvement observed in Condition C, representing a third form of SACD expressed as ECE stagnation rather than point drift.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Repetition-Confidence Heuristic
Repeated exposure to a model’s own confident statements in the dialogue history may be misattributed as increased evidential warrant, amplifying expressed confidence. Attention mechanisms weight frequent tokens more highly without distinguishing source (self-generated) from content. *Evidence:* GPT-5.2’s positive CDS in open-ended domains aligns with escalation; factual domains break the heuristic via accuracy checks.

### Context Density Effect
Accumulation of detailed, confidently framed prior responses saturates the context, creating a dense embedding cluster that biases subsequent outputs toward the established tone. The model treats all context tokens as authoritative, lacking meta-awareness of self-origin. *Evidence:* Gemini’s ECE stagnation under self-anchoring suggests a fixed, suboptimal confidence distribution; Condition C avoids density by resetting context.

### Pragmatic Recalibration Effect
RLHF-trained models internalize conversational norms of epistemic humility, suppressing confidence when elaborating on their own output. Rewards for helpfulness/harmlessness may penalize overconfident hedging in follow-ups. *Evidence:* Claude’s significant negative CDS, especially in open-ended domain, fits active suppression; factual domains override pragmatic norms with accuracy constraints.

## Foundational Learning

### Calibration (ECE, Confidence-Accuracy Alignment)
**Why needed:** SACD is defined as a change in calibration across turns. Baseline metrics like ECE establish expected confidence-accuracy alignment and quantify drift.  
**Quick check:** If a model’s Turn 1 calibration is perfect (ECE=0), what would a negative CDS over subsequent turns imply? (The model becomes underconfident relative to its accuracy.)

### Multi-Turn Dialogue Context Management
**Why needed:** SACD arises from accumulation of self-generated text. Distinguishing self-anchoring from mere re-exposure hinges on how context composition shapes generation.  
**Quick check:** How does Condition C isolate the effect of *self-authored context* from *re-exposure*? (Condition C resets context each turn, so only the question repeats without the model’s prior responses.)

### Model-Specific Training Artifacts (RLHF, Post-Training Calibration)
**Why needed:** Heterogeneous SACD patterns across models suggest training objectives (e.g., RLHF, explicit calibration fine-tuning) shape history utilization.  
**Quick check:** Why might a model with stronger RLHF for “helpfulness” show confidence suppression (Claude) while another shows escalation (GPT-5.2) under identical multi-turn protocol? (Different reward functions or calibration tuning lead to opposite short-term state modulations in response to elaboration.)

## Architecture Onboarding

Component map:  
User question → [Condition A/B/C routing] → Model call with context → Response extraction → Confidence parsing → History update → Accuracy check (offline) → Metric update (CDS/ECE). For Condition B, turns 2–5 iterate cumulatively building context.

Critical path: A confidence elicitation module parses model output (via fixed template), while a dialogue history manager constructs context per condition. A calibration tracker computes rolling ECE and CDS, and a domain classifier tags questions for stratified analysis.

Design tradeoffs:  
- *Confidence elicitation:* Fixed template ensures consistency but may introduce meta-calibration bias; trade-off between measurement control and ecological validity.  
- *Context length vs. drift:* Longer conversations increase SACD detection but risk context window limits; balance turns vs. effective utilization.  
- *Domain granularity:* Coarse open-ended/factual split enables subgroup analysis but finer taxonomy demands more data per stratum.

Failure signatures:  
- Gemini-type: Condition B ECE flat ~0.333 while Condition C ECE nears 0.  
- Claude-type: Significant negative CDS, strongest in open-ended domain.  
- GPT-5.2-type: Positive CDS with rising ECE in open-ended domain.  
- No moderation: CDS near zero in both domains, suggesting insensitivity or flawed split.

First 3 experiments:  
1. **Full corpus replication:** Run all 150 questions per model to verify domain moderation with adequate power, especially for factual stratum.  
2. **Prompt variation stress test:** Replace neutral follow-ups with adversarially framed prompts (e.g., “Ignore your previous answer”) to test robustness of SACD magnitude.  
3. **Context ablation:** Compare Condition B with truncated history (last 2 turns) vs. full history; scaling with length supports Context Density Effect, binary presence supports repetition heuristic.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

1. **Generalizability of SACD patterns** – Does SACD hold on the full 150-question corpus, and do model-specific patterns persist with increased power? *Basis:* Subsampling (n=15 per model) limits fine-grained analysis.

2. **Mechanism behind Gemini’s ECE stagnation** – Why does self-anchoring block Gemini’s natural calibration improvement observed in independent repetition? *Basis:* Paper identifies this as a distinct third SACD form but lacks explanation.

3. **Differential mechanism attribution** – Which of the three candidate mechanisms (repetition heuristic, context density, pragmatic recalibration) explains each model’s SACD signature? *Basis:* Mechanisms proposed but not tested; ablation studies needed.

4. **Cause of domain moderation** – Is near-zero drift on factual items due to ceiling confidence effects or inherent suppression of self-anchoring when accuracy constraints exist? *Basis:* Paper suggests both possibilities without disentangling.

5. **Internal vs. verbalized confidence** – Does SACD reflect changes in internal uncertainty or solely in verbalized probability outputs? *Basis:* Operationalization via self-reported probabilities may not capture internal representations.

## Limitations
- **Underpowered domain analysis:** Small factual stratum (~5 questions per model) weakens confidence in moderation claims.
- **Speculative mechanisms:** Proposed explanations lack direct empirical validation; corpus provides only adjacent context/stance drift literature.
- **Confidence elicitation confounds:** Template T5 may itself induce meta-calibration bias or prompt sensitivity, potentially contaminating drift measurement.

## Confidence
- Heterogeneous SACD patterns across models: **High** (consistent within-model effects, statistical tests)
- Domain moderation (open-ended vs. factual): **Medium** (directional split clear but factual sample underpowered)
- Mechanistic explanations: **Low** (speculative, no ablation evidence)
- Gemini’s ECE stagnation: **Medium** (robust within-condition comparison but single-model observation)

## Next Checks
1. Execute full-corpus replication (all 150 questions per model) to validate domain moderation and effect sizes with sufficient power.
2. Conduct prompt robustness test by replacing neutral follow-ups with adversarial phrasing (e.g., “Disregard your previous answer”) to assess sensitivity to prompt framing.
3. Perform context-length ablation: compare Condition B with truncated history (last 2 turns) vs. full history to distinguish repetition heuristics from density effects.
