# Generating from Discrete Distributions Using Diffusions: Insights from Random Constraint Satisfaction Problems

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2603.20589
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.20589
- Reference count: 40
- Primary result: Continuous diffusions consistently outperform masked discrete diffusions for generating uniform random solutions of random k-SAT/XORSAT, with smart ordering extending feasible clause density to the dynamical phase transition α_d.

## Executive Summary
This paper evaluates continuous and masked discrete diffusion models for generating uniformly random solutions to random k-SAT and k-XORSAT formulas. The denoisers include belief propagation (BP) and learned neural networks, and variable ordering strategies are investigated. Results show continuous diffusions consistently outperform masked discrete diffusions, aligning with theoretical thresholds. Neural denoisers match BP performance, demonstrating they can approximate the ideal local denoiser. Smart ordering (e.g., reversed-leaf for XORSAT) dramatically improves success rates, extending the feasible clause density up to the dynamical phase transition α_d. Experiments across problem sizes confirm these findings, and approximate uniformity tests indicate generated samples are close to uniform.

## Method Summary
The task is to generate uniformly random solutions for random instances of k-SAT and k-XORSAT with clause density α = M/N. Two diffusion paradigms are used: continuous denoising diffusion (additive Gaussian noise) and masked discrete diffusion (sequential variable revelation). Denoisers include BP variants (uniform and cavity initialization) and a neural network architecture `RadiusRNet` with specific MLP components. Training employs AdamW with EMA, cosine learning rate decay, and cross-entropy or MSE losses. Ordering strategies: random ordering, reversed-leaf for XORSAT, and reversed-degree for SAT. Evaluation metrics are success rate (validity) and approximate uniformity via estimated KL divergence. Datasets consist of instance-solution pairs, with up to N=900 and k up to 8.

## Key Results
- Continuous diffusions consistently outperform masked discrete diffusions across all tested k and N.
- Neural denoisers achieve performance comparable to BP, effectively approximating the ideal local denoiser.
- Smart variable ordering (reversed-leaf/reversed-degree) extends the feasible clause density up to the dynamical phase transition α_d, with success rates near 1 for α well below α_d and gradual decline approaching α_d.
- Approximate uniformity tests show generated samples are close to uniform.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The superior performance of continuous diffusions over masked discrete diffusions aligns with theoretical thresholds α_diff > α_mask, suggesting continuous processes better navigate the solution space. Neural denoisers succeed because they learn to approximate the ideal local marginals computed by BP. Smart ordering strategies exploit structure in random CSPs: reversed-leaf ordering for XORSAT leverages the leaf-removal core, while reversed-degree for SAT prioritizes constrained variables, both aligning with the dynamics of the underlying constraint graph and enabling sampling near critical densities.

## Architecture Onboarding
Random formula generator -> Ordering strategy (random)/(reversed-leaf)/(reversed-degree) -> Diffusion process (continuous/masked) with Denoiser (BP/Neural NN) -> Output solution.

Critical path: (1) generate formula, (2) compute ordering, (3) initialize variables according to ordering, (4) iterate diffusion steps using denoiser, (5) check validity of final assignment.

Design tradeoffs: Continuous diffusion uses Gaussian noise and may flow better in solution space; masked is discrete but sequential. BP denoiser is exact for trees but approximate on loopy graphs; neural denoiser is flexible but requires training. Smart orderings exploit structure but need instance-specific computation (e.g., BP for reversed-degree).

Failure signatures:
- BP overflow: messages blow up; clip atanh arguments.
- NN instability on XORSAT: success rate near random; remedy with curriculum training.
- Ordering failure: reversed-leaf does not remove all variables; indicates α ≥ α_d.
- Insufficient diffusion steps: L<500 reduces success near α_d.
- Distribution mismatch: training α range doesn’t cover test α; generalization fails.

First three experiments:
1. Validate BP denoiser on a tiny k-XORSAT instance (N=10, α=0.3) by comparing computed messages to known marginals.
2. Train neural denoiser (RadiusRNet) on a small dataset (100 instances) with N=50, α=0.4, observe loss curve and success rate on validation set.
3. Compare success rates for continuous vs. masked diffusion on N=100, k=4, α=0.5, using random ordering and BP denoiser.

## Limitations
- Dependence on unspecified cavity BP parameters (population size, iterations, t-grid) affecting the ideal denoiser baseline.
- Ambiguity in dynamic reversed-degree ordering implementation for SAT, including BP recomputation frequency and tie-breaking.
- Potential mismatch in diffusion hyperparameters (cosine β schedule values, DDPM variant) and training curriculum details for XORSAT neural networks.

## Confidence
- Continuous diffusions > masked discrete diffusions: **High**
- Neural denoisers ≈ BP performance: **High**
- Smart ordering extends feasible density to α_d: **Medium**
- Approximate uniformity of generated samples: **Medium**
