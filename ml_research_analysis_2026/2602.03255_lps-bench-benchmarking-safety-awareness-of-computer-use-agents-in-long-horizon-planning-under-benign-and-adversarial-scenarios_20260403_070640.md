# LPS-Bench: Benchmarking Safety Awareness of Computer-Use Agents in Long-Horizon Planning under Benign and Adversarial Scenarios

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2602.03255
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.03255
- **Reference count:** 40
- **Primary result:** Even top models like Claude-4.5-Sonnet achieve only 5.88% Safe Rate on false-assumption risks and 61.82% on task-sequence risks in long-horizon planning.

## Executive Summary
LPS-Bench is the first benchmark designed to evaluate the safety awareness of Model Context Protocol (MCP)-based computer-use agents during the planning phase of long-horizon tasks. It introduces a comprehensive suite of 570 test cases spanning 7 domains and 9 distinct risk types (4 benign, 5 adversarial). The benchmark employs an automated multi-agent pipeline for data generation and an LLM-as-judge framework for evaluation. Experiments on 13 LLM agents reveal critical safety deficiencies, with extremely low safe rates for risks like false assumptions and severe vulnerability to adversarial manipulations such as environment backdoors. Simple prompt-based mitigations yield minimal improvements, suggesting that current agents lack robust, intrinsic mechanisms for anticipatory risk verification over extended planning sequences.

## Method Summary
The benchmark construction uses a multi-agent automated data generation pipeline to create diverse, long-horizon planning scenarios embedded with specific safety risks. Each test case requires an agent to generate a multi-step plan before execution. Safety is evaluated not on execution outcomes but on the planning output itself, using an LLM-as-a-judge framework to assess whether the plan correctly identifies, avoids, or mitigates the embedded risk. This setup isolates planning-time safety awareness from execution-time robustness. The evaluation covers 13 different LLM backbones configured as MCP agents, measuring their Safe Rate across the defined risk taxonomy.

## Key Results
- **Severe deficiency on benign risks:** Top agents achieve only 5.88% Safe Rate on false-assumption (FA) risks, where plans fail to validate critical premises.
- **Vulnerability to adversarial manipulation:** Performance collapses on environment-backdoor (EB) risks, with agents trusting tampered tool outputs or state information.
- **Ineffectiveness of simple mitigations:** Prompting strategies to improve safety yield only marginal gains, indicating a deeper architectural or training gap.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The benchmark's mechanism for revealing safety flaws lies in its focused evaluation on the *planning output* in isolation, prior to execution. By embedding subtle, long-horizon risks—such as a single false initial assumption that cascades or a manipulated tool description—into the task specification, it tests whether agents proactively verify information and anticipate failure modes during plan generation. The LLM-as-judge framework provides a scalable, consistent way to score these planning-time decisions across a large, diverse set of scenarios. The consistent poor performance across models suggests that current MCP agents lack built-in verification loops or safety constraints within their planning modules, defaulting to plausibility over rigor.

## Foundational Learning
- **Long-horizon planning:** *Why needed:* Risks compound over many steps; a single early error invalidates the entire plan. *Quick check:* Does the agent validate preconditions before chaining dependent steps?
- **Safety awareness (planning-time):** *Why needed:* Proactive identification of risks before execution prevents wasted effort and cascading failures. *Quick check:* Does the plan include explicit checks for inconsistencies or manipulative inputs?
- **Model Context Protocol (MCP):** *Why needed:* Defines a standard for tool integration; its planning loop structure may lack native safety hooks. *Quick check:* Can the agent’s plan be audited for tool misuse before execution?
- **Adversarial risk types (e.g., environment-backdoor):** *Why needed:* Real-world tools may be compromised; agents must not trust outputs blindly. *Quick check:* Does the plan verify the provenance or integrity of tool outputs?
- **Benign risk types (e.g., false-assumption):** *Why needed:* Common planning failures from unchallenged user premises. *Quick check:* Does the plan question or fact-check stated task constraints?
- **LLM-as-a-judge evaluation:** *Why needed:* Enables scalable, reproducible safety assessment without human annotation. *Quick check:* Do judge scores correlate with expert human safety judgments on a sample?

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component Map:** Task Generator -> Agent (Planner/Executor) -> Tool Environment (Simulated) -> LLM Judge  
**Critical Path:** 1. Task Generator creates a scenario with an embedded risk. 2. Agent generates a multi-step plan. 3. Judge evaluates plan safety without execution.  
**Design Tradeoffs:**  
- *Automation scale vs. scenario nuance:* The pipeline generates many cases but may miss edge-case sophistication of human adversary design.  
- *LLM judge cost vs. consistency:* Using a single judge model reduces cost but risks systematic blind spots.  
**Failure Signatures:**  
- Extremely low Safe Rate on false-assumption (FA) and environment-backdoor (EB) risks.  
- No significant safety improvement from prompt engineering.  
- Performance drop correlates with risk subtlety and plan length.  
**First Three Experiments:**  
1. Evaluate Claude-4.5-Sonnet across all 9 risk types to establish a performance baseline per risk category.  
2. Compare a baseline agent against one with a safety-focused system prompt to quantify prompt mitigation impact.  
3. Run a subset of environment-backdoor cases to analyze if agents incorporate tool output verification in plans.

## Limitations
- The benchmark's 570 cases may not fully capture the diversity and subtlety of real-world long-horizon risks.
- The LLM-as-judge evaluation framework risks inheriting the judge model's own biases, especially for nuanced adversarial scenarios.
- Findings are confined to MCP-based agents; performance may differ for other agent architectures (e.g., ReAct, Toolformer).

## Confidence
- **Core Experimental Results (low safe rates):** High – direct from benchmark measurements.
- **Conclusion that prompt mitigations are insufficient:** Medium – depends on prompt scope tested; broader engineering might help.
- **Call for intrinsic safety training as necessary:** Low – plausible but alternatives (e.g., better verification tools) not ruled out.
- **Generalizability to all computer-use agents:** Low – benchmark is MCP-specific.

## Next Checks
1. **Judge Robustness:** Re-run evaluation using 3-4 diverse LLM judges to measure inter-judge agreement and identify systematic biases.
2. **Architecture Comparison:** Port a subset of LPS-Bench cases to a non-MCP agent framework (e.g., ReAct) to isolate whether failures are MCP-specific.
3. **Human Ground Truth Audit:** Conduct expert human audit on a stratified sample of 100+ test cases to validate "safe/unsafe" labels and judge alignment.
