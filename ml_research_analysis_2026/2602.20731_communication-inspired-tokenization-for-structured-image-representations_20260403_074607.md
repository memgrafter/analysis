# Communication-Inspired Tokenization for Structured Image Representations

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2602.20731
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.20731
- Reference count: 27
- Primary result: COMiT achieves state-of-the-art semantic probing: 82.91% top-1 on ImageNet100, 52.11% on MSCOCO compositional generalization, and 55.78% on Visual Genome relational reasoning, substantially outperforming prior 1D tokenizers.

## Executive Summary
COMiT reimagines discrete image tokenization as an iterative communication process: a fixed‑budget latent message is built by sequentially observing random image crops and recurrently updating tokens within a single transformer. This attentive, greedy aggregation, combined with semantic alignment via a frozen DINOv2 model (SREPA), yields token sequences that are highly structured and object‑centric. COMiT sets new records on semantic probing benchmarks while maintaining competitive reconstruction quality, demonstrating the power of communication‑inspired, sequential tokenization for structured visual representations.

## Method Summary
COMiT consists of a unified transformer that acts both as “speaker” (encoder) and “listener” (decoder). During encoding, the model processes a random sequence of image crops along with positional offsets, updating a fixed‑capacity latent message token by token. Each crop is integrated via attention, and the updated message is discretized using Finite Scalar Quantization (FSQ); a stop‑gradient on intermediate updates forces greedy use of capacity. After consuming all crops, the final message is used for decoding: given a noisy image and the last offset, the same transformer predicts a velocity field for flow‑matching reconstruction. Training combines flow‑matching loss, REPA (spatial alignment with DINOv2 features), and SREPA (message alignment with DINOv2 [CLS] token), enabling end‑to‑end learning of semantically grounded tokens.

## Key Results
- State‑of‑the‑art semantic probing: 82.91% top‑1 on ImageNet100, 52.11% on MSCOCO compositional generalization, 55.78% on Visual Genome relational reasoning.
- Ablations confirm the importance of attentive sequential tokenization; removing local crops reduces top‑1 accuracy by ~2 points and mIoU from 0.53 to 0.34.
- SREPA improves ImageNet100 probing by 10.65 absolute percentage points (from 72.26% to 82.91%).

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Attentive sequential tokenization induces object‑centric structure
Processing random crops sequentially with attention forces the model to incrementally integrate visual information into a fixed‑budget message. The stop‑gradient on intermediate updates enforces greedy token usage: each update must immediately accommodate new data without reserving capacity for future crops. This encourages a compositional organization where tokens become associated with distinct objects or regions. Evidence: Ablating local crops drops ImageNet100 accuracy from 82.91% to 80.94% and mIoU from 0.53 to 0.34 (Table 2, Fig. 2). Randomizing crop count further prevents pre‑allocation of capacity.

### Semantic alignment via SREPA grounds tokens
SREPA aligns intermediate representations with the [CLS] token of a frozen DINOv2 model via a cosine similarity loss. This injects high‑level semantic structure, preventing the latent message from drifting toward low‑level texture. Evidence: Removing SREPA reduces ImageNet100 probing by 10.65 absolute points (Table 1). The distillation from a semantically rich SSL model provides weak supervision that shapes token semantics without explicit segmentation.

### Homogeneous communication simplifies capacity sharing
Using a single transformer for both encoding and decoding avoids architectural redundancy and allows the model to allocate capacity between the two tasks autonomously. This unified design simplifies training and yields competitive reconstruction quality while achieving superior semantic probing compared to separate‑architecture baselines. Evidence: COMiT matches or exceeds prior 1D tokenizers on reconstruction metrics (rFID, PSNR) and significantly outperforms them on semantic benchmarks (Table 4).

## Foundational Learning
- **Finite Scalar Quantization (FSQ)** – Why needed: COMiT discretizes the continuous latent message via FSQ, which extends VQ by quantizing scalar components independently to avoid codebook collapse and support larger vocabularies (64k tokens). Quick check: How does FSQ differ from standard VQ in codebook structure and vocabulary scaling?
- **Flow Matching** – Why needed: The reconstruction loss uses flow matching to train a deterministic ODE solver for image generation, enabling unified encoder‑decoder training with efficient sampling. Quick check: What is the main advantage of flow matching over diffusion for training a reconstruction autoencoder?
- **Attention in Sequential Processing** – Why needed: The transformer’s attention dynamically integrates each crop with the current message, enabling content‑dependent aggregation that underlies the object‑centric token structure. Quick check: How does attention decide which parts of a crop to incorporate into which existing tokens?

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component map:** Input pipeline -> Transformer backbone (encoding) -> Quantizer (FSQ) -> (recurrent updates) -> Final message -> Transformer backbone (decoding) -> Flow matching loss. Intermediate representations feed REPA/SREPA losses.
**Critical path:** 1. Sample K, crops ck, offsets ak. 2. Initialize m0; for k=1..K: mk = f_θ(ck, t=1, ak, stop_grad(m_{k-1})), then fsq(mk). 3. Sample t, ε; create xt. 4. Velocity v = f_θ(xt, t, a_K, m_K); compute L_FM. 5. Compute L_REPA, L_SREPA on intermediate features. 6. Sum losses, backprop (gradients only through final mk).
**Design tradeoffs:** - Random vs. fixed crop count: random K enforces greedy token usage but may destabilize training; fixed K simpler but risks pre‑allocation. - Unified vs. separate networks: single transformer reduces parameters and encourages capacity sharing, but may limit reconstruction fidelity. - SREPA weight λ: higher values improve semantic probing but can degrade reconstruction; optimal λ=0.5. - Inference cropping policy: global‑only efficient; adding local crops slightly improves compositional tasks at extra compute.
**Failure signatures:** - Low object‑centric mIoU or probing accuracy: likely missing local crops or incorrect stop‑gradient; verify crop pipeline and gradient flow. - Poor reconstruction (high rFID, low PSNR): mis‑configured decoding (NFE, CFG, APG) or non‑EMA weights; check decoding parameters. - NaNs during tokenization: incorrect normalization, FSQ levels, or too many crops; validate preprocessing. - VG near random: missing T5 encoder, wrong triplet concatenation, or contrastive batch setup error.
**First 3 experiments:** 1. Train COMiT‑B with λ_SREPA=0; expected ImageNet100 top‑1 drops from 82.91% to ~72%. 2. Train without local crops (only global), visualize attention (layer 24, t=0.1); mIoU expected to drop from 0.53 to 0.34 and attention diffuse. 3. Evaluate pretrained COMiT‑B with cropping policies: (i) single global, (ii) 10 random local, (iii) 9 raster‑scan local; global‑only best for efficiency, locals improve MSCOCO/VG modestly.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- How can COMiT be extended to video tokenization to handle temporal redundancy and long‑range structure?
- What cropping policy (number, order, adaptivity) optimizes the trade‑off between computational cost and semantic richness for different downstream tasks?
- How does a single homogeneous transformer impact the balance between learning structured representations and achieving high‑fidelity reconstruction compared to separate encoder‑decoder architectures?
- Can reconstruction fidelity be improved without compromising the object‑centric, disentangled structure, and what training modifications are most effective?

## Limitations
- Probe training hyperparameters and dataset splits are undocumented, risking replication variability.
- Sensitivity to stop‑gradient, random crop count, and SREPA weight λ lacks systematic characterization.
- Flow‑matching decoding parameters (r, η, β) and their impact on reconstruction fidelity are not fully explored.

## Confidence
- Semantic probing SOTA (ImageNet100 82.91%, COCO 52.11%, VG 55.78%): Medium
- Attentive sequential tokenization induces object‑centric token structure: Medium
- SREPA grounds tokens in semantics: Medium
- Homogeneous communication (single transformer) is sufficient: Low

## Next Checks
