# Mind the Gap: A Framework for Assessing Pitfalls in Multimodal Active Learning

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2603.29677
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.29677
- **Reference count:** 40
- **Primary result:** Existing active learning query methods fail to mitigate modality imbalance and do not reliably outperform unimodal strategies in multimodal settings.

## Executive Summary
This paper identifies three critical pitfalls in multimodal active learning (AL): missing modalities, modality imbalance, and varying modality interaction structures. To systematically evaluate these, the authors introduce a benchmark framework based on synthetic datasets (QuintFeatures) that isolates each pitfall. They compare seven query methods—five unimodal (BADGE, BALD, Entropy, KCG, Random) and two multimodal (BMMAL, GRACE)—across multiple label regimes. Results show models consistently develop imbalanced representations, relying heavily on one modality, and no query method reliably corrects this imbalance or surpasses unimodal alternatives. These findings persist in real-world validation on Food101 and MIMIC datasets, highlighting fundamental limitations in current multimodal AL approaches.

## Method Summary
The authors construct a controlled benchmark using the QuintFeatures synthetic dataset generator to create four scenarios: Missing (one modality frequently absent), Share (modalities share information but differ in learnability), Unique (each modality contains complementary information), and Synergy (joint multimodal information is required). Models employ modality-specific encoders (e.g., EfficientNet for images, MiniLM for text) followed by a transformer fusion layer and a classifier. Active learning is conducted via an iterative loop: train on a small labeled set, compute acquisition scores per unlabeled sample using a query method, label the top-k samples, and repeat. Performance is evaluated by Area Under the Learning Curve (AULC) on test sets that separately assess single-modality and multimodal performance. Seven query strategies are tested, with ModDrop (random modality dropout) applied during training except in the Synergy setting. Experiments run across low, mid, and high label regimes, averaging over three seeds.

## Key Results
- Models exhibit persistent modality imbalance, with performance on the "strong" (easier) modality dominating the multimodal result, irrespective of the query method used.
- No active learning query strategy—including multimodal-specific BMMAL and GRACE—reliably improves performance on the weak modality or outperforms the best unimodal method.
- Modality dropout (ModDrop) harms performance in synergy settings where modalities provide complementary information, indicating training-time regularization must be interaction-structure aware.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1: Simplicity Bias Drives Imbalance
- **Claim:** Neural networks develop imbalanced multimodal representations by disproportionately relying on the "strong" (easier) modality while neglecting the "weak" one, degrading overall performance.
- **Mechanism:** Due to a simplicity bias, models exploit the modality with lower learning complexity first, causing gradients and learned features to focus on that modality. This creates a positive feedback loop where the strong modality dominates predictions, and the weak modality's representations remain under-trained even after convergence.
- **Evidence anchors:** [abstract]: "models consistently develop imbalanced representations..."; [section 3 P2]: "Neural networks tend to exploit the simplest learning pathways..."; [corpus]: Consistent with neighboring works observing modality imbalance.
- **Break condition:** Perfectly balanced modality learnability or explicit regularization (e.g., ModDrop in non-synergy settings) reduces but does not fully eliminate imbalance.

### Mechanism 2: Query Methods Ignore Modality-Specific Informativeness
- **Claim:** Existing active learning query methods are ineffective at mitigating modality imbalance because they are fundamentally modality-agnostic in their informativeness estimation.
- **Mechanism:** Query strategies compute scores based on model outputs, gradients, or representations without explicitly tracking per-modality contribution. When a model is already imbalanced, these scores reflect the dominant modality's uncertainty or diversity, failing to prioritize samples that would improve the weak modality.
- **Evidence anchors:** [section 6.2]: "No method reliably improves the weak modality performance... Random performs as well..."; [section 6.3]: Modality imbalance persists on real data; [corpus]: Neighboring frameworks acknowledge these limitations.
- **Break condition:** A query method that explicitly weights sample informativeness by the weak modality's current performance (e.g., using modality-specific uncertainty) could potentially break this.

### Mechanism 3: ModDrop Disrupts Synergistic Learning
- **Claim:** In synergy settings (where information is complementary), modality dropout during training harms joint multimodal performance because it disrupts necessary cross-modal feature interactions.
- **Mechanism:** When the task requires both modalities for correct prediction, randomly dropping one modality during training prevents the model from learning the synergistic feature combinations, impairing robust joint representations.
- **Evidence anchors:** [section 6.1 & Table 1]: For Synergy, ModDrop significantly lowers AULC; [section 4.2 P3.3]: Task definition requires complementary information.
- **Break condition:** If synergy is weak or ModDrop probability is very low, harm may be reduced; more sophisticated dropout preserving cross-modal attention might avoid this.

## Foundational Learning
- **Concept: Active Learning Query Strategies**  
  *Why needed:* Understanding uncertainty-based (BALD, Entropy), diversity-based (KCG), and hybrid (BADGE) methods is essential to interpret why they fail in multimodal settings.  
  *Quick check:* How does BADGE's gradient embedding differ from KCG's representation diversity, and why might both be vulnerable to modality imbalance?

- **Concept: Modality Fusion Architectures**  
  *Why needed:* The paper uses a late-fusion architecture; this design influences how imbalance manifests and how query methods interact with representations.  
  *Quick check:* In a late-fusion setup, where does the model "choose" to rely on one modality, and how might that affect gradient-based query methods?

- **Concept: Evaluation Metrics for Active Learning**  
  *Why needed:* Area Under the Learning Curve (AULC) aggregates performance across AL iterations, crucial for comparing query methods over time.  
  *Quick check:* Why is reporting per-modality AULC (e.g., on 'Only Modality A' test sets) necessary to expose imbalance, rather than only 'Both' modality performance?

## Architecture Onboarding
Unimodal encoders -> Fusion layer (transformer) -> Classifier -> Query method module -> AL loop controller

Critical path: Inputs processed by separate encoders; fused representations yield predictions; loss drives backpropagation; after training, query method scores unlabeled samples; top-k selected, labeled, and added to pool; repeat.

Design tradeoffs:
- Late-fusion simplicity vs. early-fusion potential: Late-fusion is modular but may limit cross-modal interactions; findings may not generalize to tighter integrations.
- Modality dropout: Helpful for mitigating imbalance in most settings but harmful in synergy settings; requires prior knowledge of interaction type.
- Query method scope: Self-contained methods (no training changes) are prioritized, excluding methods that modify training (e.g., [15,21,25]) that might better address pitfalls.

Failure signatures:
- Imbalance: Weak-modality-only test accuracy lags despite improving 'Both' performance, which tracks the strong modality.
- Query failure: No consistent AULC improvement over Random, especially for weak modality or synergy tasks.
- ModDrop harm: In Synergy experiments, applying ModDrop causes a significant drop in 'Both' AULC relative to no-ModDrop.

First 3 experiments:
1. **Baseline replication:** Run Random query on the 'Share' synthetic dataset (CIFAR-10/MNIST) with and without ModDrop. Verify that performance on MNIST (weak) lags behind CIFAR-10 (strong) and that 'Both' performance tracks MNIST.
2. **Unimodal query stress test:** Apply BADGE, BALD, Entropy, KCG on the 'Unique' dataset. Check if any method improves the 'Partial' modality AULC meaningfully above Random, especially in low/mid-label regimes.
3. **Multimodal query validation:** Test BMMAL and GRACE on the 'Missing' dataset (90%/10% missing rates). Confirm they do not select a more balanced set of modalities and that their AULC does not surpass the best unimodal method for the rare modality.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- **Question:** Can existing query strategies identify informative samples under modality imbalance?  
  *Basis:* [explicit] "This raises the question of whether existing query strategies can still identify informative samples when such imbalances are present."  
  *Why unresolved:* All tested methods failed consistently, but underlying reasons (e.g., optimization dynamics) remain unclear.  
  *Resolving evidence:* Diagnostic analyses (e.g., gradient magnitude per modality) and new metrics for balanced informativeness.

- **Question:** How can query methods be designed to explicitly monitor and correct modality imbalance?  
  *Basis:* [explicit] "We believe that effective multimodal AL will require query methods that explicitly monitor and correct modality imbalance during the acquisition process."  
  *Why unresolved:* No current method integrates such monitoring; multimodal strategies do not reliably improve weak-modality performance.  
  *Resolving evidence:* Novel query algorithm with a balance regularizer (e.g., modality-specific uncertainty) demonstrating consistent improvement on benchmarks.

- **Question:** How do the identified pitfalls generalize to settings with more than two modalities?  
  *Basis:* [inferred] Focus is on two modalities; pitfalls may grow more complex with additional ones.  
  *Why unresolved:* All experiments use two modalities; multi-modality could compound imbalances.  
  *Resolving evidence:* Extend QuintFeatures to three+ modalities; test current query methods for scalability and robustness.

- **Question:** How do different fusion architectures (e.g., early fusion, cross-attention) interact with pitfalls and affect query method performance?  
  *Basis:* [inferred] All experiments use late-fusion; alternative strategies may interact differently.  
  *Why unresolved:* Fusion choice may influence modality reliance and query effectiveness; late-fusion results may not generalize.  
  *Resolving evidence:* Re-run experiments using alternative fusion modules; measure imbalance and AL performance.

## Limitations
- Synthetic datasets (QuintFeatures) may not fully capture real-world complexity, limiting generalization.
- Real-world validation is restricted to two datasets (Food101, MIMIC); broader applicability is unknown.
- Critical implementation details for BMMAL/GRACE, BALD MC-dropout, and MIMIC preprocessing are unspecified, hindering exact replication.

## Confidence
- **Imbalance due to simplicity bias:** High – Strong empirical evidence across synthetic and real data.
- **Query methods fail to address imbalance:** High – Consistent results showing no reliable improvement in weak-modality performance.
- **ModDrop harms synergy:** High – Clear performance drop in Synergy experiments with/without ModDrop.
- **Real-world persistence of pitfalls:** Medium – Validated on two datasets; may not generalize.

## Next Checks
