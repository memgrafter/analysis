# Do LLMs Know What They Know? Measuring Metacognitive Efficiency with Signal Detection Theory

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2603.25112
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.25112
- **Reference count:** 10
- **Primary result:** Metacognitive efficiency (M-ratio) is separable from factual accuracy (d′), revealing models that "know what they don't know" versus those merely appearing well-calibrated.

## Executive Summary
This paper introduces a Type-2 Signal Detection Theory (SDT) framework to disentangle an LLM's factual knowledge (Type-1 sensitivity, d') from its metacognitive monitoring efficiency (Type-2 sensitivity, meta-d'). The key metric, the M-ratio (meta-d'/d'), measures how effectively a model's confidence (proxied by normalized log-probability) signals correctness relative to its raw knowledge. Evaluated on four 7–9B parameter models across 224,000 QA trials, the study finds that high factual knowledge does not imply high metacognitive efficiency. For instance, Mistral has the highest d' but the lowest M-ratio, while Gemma has near-optimal metacognitive efficiency despite lower factual accuracy. Furthermore, metacognitive efficiency varies by knowledge domain, and temperature manipulation can independently shift confidence policy (criterion) without altering metacognitive capacity (meta-d') in some models. This framework demonstrates that traditional calibration metrics (e.g., AUROC₂) and M-ratio produce inverted model rankings, confirming they measure fundamentally different properties.

## Method Summary
The authors evaluate four open-weight models (Llama-3-8B Instruct/Base, Mistral-7B-Instruct-v0.3, Gemma-2-9B-Instruct) on factual QA from TriviaQA and Natural Questions. For each trial, the model generates an answer; its confidence is operationalized as the Normalized Log-Probability (NLP)—the mean token log-probability of the generated answer. NLP is validated as a graded evidence variable by confirming accuracy increases monotonically across NLP quartiles. For SDT analysis, continuous NLP is binned into eight ordered rating categories (four per accuracy side) using quantile edges defined at temperature T=1.0. These fixed edges ensure temperature comparisons are not confounded by distribution shifts. 2×8 count matrices (incorrect/correct × rating) are constructed, corrected via the Hautus (+0.5) method, and meta-d' is estimated via maximum likelihood assuming equal variances using the `metadpy` toolkit. The M-ratio is meta-d' divided by d'. Bootstrap resampling (10k) generates 95% confidence intervals for all metrics. The pre-registered analysis tests four hypotheses: (H1) M-ratio < 1 on average; (H2) domain-specific M-ratio variation; (H3) temperature dissociates Type-2 criterion from meta-d'; (H4) AUROC₂ and M-ratio rankings are inverted.

## Key Results
- **M-ratio and d' are dissociated across models:** Mistral achieves the highest factual sensitivity (d' = 1.597) but the lowest metacognitive efficiency (M-ratio = 0.852), while Gemma has lower d' (0.946) but near-optimal M-ratio (1.048).
- **Metacognitive efficiency is domain-specific:** The weakest monitoring domain differs by model (e.g., Llama-3-Instruct and Gemma-2 are worst in Science & Technology; Mistral is worst in Arts & Literature).
- **Temperature manipulation dissociates criterion from capacity:** For Mistral and Gemma, increasing temperature shifts the Type-2 criterion (confidence threshold) while meta-d' remains stable, indicating confidence policy can be adjusted independently of underlying metacognitive capacity. This dissociation does not hold for Llama-3-Base.
- **AUROC₂ and M-ratio produce fully inverted model rankings:** This demonstrates AUROC₂ primarily reflects factual accuracy (d') rather than normalized monitoring efficiency, making M-ratio a distinct and necessary metric.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1: Separating Knowledge from Monitoring
**Claim:** Metacognitive efficiency (M-ratio) is separable from factual accuracy (Type-1 d′), allowing models with similar knowledge to differ in how well they monitor that knowledge.
**Mechanism:** The framework uses Type-2 SDT to compute meta-d′, which measures the sensitivity of the confidence signal (NLP) to correct vs. incorrect responses. The M-ratio (meta-d′/d′) normalizes this by the model's Type-1 discriminative capacity. A model with high d′ but low M-ratio (like Mistral) has a confidence signal that carries less information about correctness than its answer quality warrants.
**Core assumption:** Normalized log-probability (NLP) functions as a continuous, graded evidence variable for correctness, validated by monotonic accuracy increases across NLP quartiles.
**Evidence anchors:**
- [abstract] "Mistral achieves the highest d′ (1.597) but lowest M-ratio (0.852), while Gemma has lower d′ (0.946) but near-optimal M-ratio (1.048)"
- [section 4.2] "The critical finding is the variation across models. Mistral achieves the highest d′ (1.597) yet the lowest M-ratio (0.852)"
**Break condition:** If NLP monotonicity fails across conditions (section 4.1), the foundational evidence variable is invalid, collapsing the Type-2 SDT model.

### Mechanism 2: Domain-Specific Monitoring
**Claim:** Metacognitive efficiency is domain-specific; a model's weakest knowledge area for monitoring can differ from another model's, a pattern hidden by aggregate metrics.
**Mechanism:** The analysis computes M-ratio separately for each TriviaQA knowledge domain. Variation in M-ratio within a model across domains indicates that the quality of the confidence signal is not uniform but depends on the subject matter, likely reflecting differences in training data or representation.
**Core assumption:** Domain labels from TriviaQA provide meaningful, coherent sub-populations for evaluating metacognitive sensitivity.
**Evidence anchors:**
- [abstract] "metacognitive efficiency is domain-specific, with different models showing distinct weakest domains"
- [section 4.4] "The weakest domain differs by model: Llama-3-Instruct and Gemma-2 are worst in Science & Technology; Mistral is worst in Arts & Literature"
**Break condition:** If domain-specific M-ratio differences vanish with larger sample sizes per domain (as suggested by wide CIs in Table 6), the observed pattern may be noise rather than a stable property.

### Mechanism 3: Temperature Dissociation
**Claim:** Temperature scaling can dissociate policy (Type-2 criterion) from capacity (meta-d′), meaning confidence inflation/deflation does not necessarily improve or degrade the underlying metacognitive signal.
**Mechanism:** Increasing temperature increases output randomness, which primarily shifts the threshold (criterion) at which the model expresses high vs. low NLP. The meta-d′ estimate, which captures the *discriminability* of the confidence distribution between correct/incorrect trials, remains stable if the underlying evidence representation for monitoring is unchanged.
**Core assumption:** Temperature sampling affects the generative policy (choice distribution) but does not fundamentally alter the internal representation that gives rise to the NLP-confidence relationship used for monitoring.
**Evidence anchors:**
- [abstract] "temperature manipulation shifts Type-2 criterion while meta-d′ remains stable for two of four models"
- [section 4.5] "For Mistral and Gemma, d′ varies with temperature while meta-d′ remains stable... The pattern is that instruction-tuned models from different families both show the dissociation"
**Break condition:** If meta-d′ changes systematically with temperature across all models (as it does for Llama-3-Base), the dissociation is not universal, and temperature may alter the metacognitive representation itself in some architectures.

## Foundational Learning
- **Concept: Type-1 vs. Type-2 Signal Detection Theory**  
  *Why needed here:* The paper's core innovation is applying the Type-2 SDT framework to LLMs. Type-1 is about discriminating correct/incorrect answers (d'), while Type-2 is about discriminating one's own correct/incorrect answers via confidence (meta-d').  
  *Quick check:* Can a model have high accuracy (Type-1 d′) but poor M-ratio? What does that imply about its confidence reports?

- **Concept: Metacognitive Efficiency (M-ratio)**  
  *Why needed here:* M-ratio is the central metric. It answers: "Given what the model knows (d′), how much of that knowledge is accessible through its confidence?" An M-ratio <1 indicates inefficiency.  
  *Quick check:* If Model X has d′=2.0 and meta-d′=1.5, and Model Y has d′=1.0 and meta-d′=0.9, which is more metacognitively efficient? Which provides more useful confidence signals?

- **Concept: Normalized Log-Probability (NLP) as Evidence**  
  *Why needed here:* NLP is the operational confidence variable. Its validity rests on it being a monotonic proxy for correctness. The paper empirically checks this (section 4.1, Appendix A).  
  *Quick check:* Why is using the model's internal NLP (vs. prompted verbal confidence) advantageous for SDT analysis? What is the "sampling bottleneck" and why is it not a confound?

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component map:**
Data Collection Engine -> NLP Calculator -> Binner -> SDT Estimator -> Bootstrap Wrapper
Data Collection Engine (generates answers + token log-probs for QA pairs across temperatures) feeds into NLP Calculator (computes mean token log-prob per answer, normalizes). This feeds into Binner (discretizes continuous NLP into ordered rating categories using quantiles from a reference condition). The Binner output feeds into SDT Estimator (applies Hautus correction, fits equal-variance meta-d′ via MLE using `metadpy`). Finally, the Bootstrap Wrapper (resamples trials, recomputes full pipeline) generates confidence intervals.

**Critical path:** Raw logits → NLP calculation → binning (edges fixed across T) → count matrix construction → meta-d′ MLE → M-ratio calculation → bootstrap CI. The monotonicity check (step before binning) is a gatekeeper.

**Design tradeoffs:**
- *Binning granularity (K):* Finer bins capture more rating structure but risk sparse cells. Primary analysis uses K=4; robustness check tests K=3,6.
- *Equal-variance assumption:* Simplifies meta-d′ estimation but may mis-fit if confidence distributions for correct/incorrect have very different variances (addressed by R2 robustness check with unequal-variance meta-d′).
- *Fixed bin edges:* Ensures temperature comparisons are not confounded by shifting NLP distributions, but assumes ordinal relationship holds across temperatures (validated by monotonicity).

**Failure signatures:**
- Non-monotonic accuracy across NLP quartiles in any cell → invalid evidence variable, pipeline halt.
- M-ratio estimates >10 or extreme instability across bootstrap resamples → degenerate contingency table (insufficient trials per bin).
- Massive swing in model rankings when changing K → binning-driven artifact.

**First 3 experiments:**
1. **Replicate Core Finding:** On TriviaQA T=1.0, compute d′ and M-ratio for all four models. Expect Mistral: high d′ / low M; Gemma: low d′ / high M. Check CIs.
2. **Binning Sensitivity (R1):** Repeat experiment 1 with K=3 and K=6. Verify model ordering and M-ratio values are stable (|ΔM| < 0.1).
3. **Temperature Dissociation:** For Mistral and Gemma, plot d′ and meta-d′ across T∈{0.3,0.5,0.7,1.0}. Expect d′ to trend with T while meta-d′ remains flat (within TOST δ=0.3). For Llama-3-Base, expect both to vary.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- **Open Question 1:** Does metacognitive efficiency (M-ratio) scale with model size and architecture, or do the observed patterns (e.g., Mistral's high d' but low M-ratio) persist, diminish, or amplify in larger (70B+) or frontier models?
- **Open Question 2:** What is the mechanistic explanation for the temperature dissociation observed in Mistral and Gemma (stable meta-d') versus Llama-3-Base (unstable meta-d')? Does instruction tuning specifically enable independent adjustment of Type-2 criterion?
- **Open Question 3:** For models that do not expose internal log-probabilities (e.g., many API models), can prompted verbal confidence scales reliably recover true metacognitive efficiency (meta-d') after correcting for discretization artifacts, or do they measure a fundamentally different construct?
- **Open Question 4:** Are the observed domain-specific variations in M-ratio (e.g., Gemma weakest in Science & Technology, Mistral weakest in Arts & Literature) robust and predictable from model training data or architectural features, or are they noise from limited domain-level trial counts?

## Limitations
- Exact dataset splits and answer alias lists are missing, blocking exact replication.
- Model inference details (GGUF source, context window, stop tokens) are unspecified; variations may affect NLP calculations.
- Fixed binning assumes NLP monotonicity across temperatures; violation invalidates the SDT model.
- Domain-specific M-ratio differences have wide confidence intervals due to limited per-domain samples.
- The equal-variance assumption in meta-d' estimation may not always hold.

## Confidence
- **Separation of metacognitive efficiency from factual accuracy:** High
- **Domain-specific metacognitive efficiency:** Medium (pattern observed but CIs overlap, limited per-domain samples)
- **Temperature dissociation of criterion vs. capacity:** Medium (stable for two models but not universal, may depend on architecture)
- **AUROC₂ vs. M-ratio ranking inversion:** High (direct empirical observation consistent across models)
