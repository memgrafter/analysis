# Inner Speech as Behavior Guides: Steerable Imitation of Diverse Behaviors for Human-AI coordination

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID**: 2602.20517
- **Source URL**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.20517
- **Reference count**: 40
- **Primary result**: MIMIC significantly outperforms state-of-the-art behavior cloning on D3IL and Overcooked tasks, achieving higher success rates (e.g., +13.8% on Aligning), higher behavioral entropy, lower Wasserstein distances (up to 93% reduction), and enabling fine-grained steerable generation without additional training data.

## Executive Summary
Imitation learning struggles to capture the diversity and non-Markovian aspects of human behavior and lacks inference-time steering. MIMIC introduces an “inner speech” mediator—a latent linguistic representation generated from visual history via a CVAE bootstrapped by a VLM. This inner speech conditions a diffusion-based policy, enabling diverse action sampling and designer control through linguistic prompts. On D3IL manipulation tasks and Overcooked human-AI collaboration, MIMIC surpasses SOTA behavior cloning (DDPM-T) with improved success rates, entropy, fidelity (Wasserstein distance), and demonstrates steerable generation without extra data.

## Method Summary
MIMIC consists of three main stages. First, offline, a vision-language model (e.g., GPT-4o) generates natural language descriptions of demonstration trajectories, which are encoded into embeddings (via CLIP) serving as target inner speech. Second, a conditional variational autoencoder (CVAE) is trained to map a sliding window of past images to a distribution over inner speech embeddings; simultaneously, a diffusion policy is trained to output actions conditioned on the current state and the corresponding inner speech. During deployment, the CVAE periodically (every W steps) regenerates inner speech from the recent visual history, and the diffusion policy uses this context to select actions. This architecture decouples high-level behavioral intent (inner speech) from low-level motor commands, allowing both diversity and inference-time control via linguistic prompts.

## Key Results
- MIMIC improves success rates on D3IL tasks (e.g., +13.8% on Aligning) and achieves higher behavioral entropy compared to the DDPM-T baseline.
- It reduces the Wasserstein distance to the demonstration distribution by up to 93%, indicating higher fidelity.
- The framework enables fine-grained steerable behavior generation at inference time without additional training data, as shown via prompt-based control studies.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1
- **Claim**: Modeling inner speech as a latent variable between perception and action captures behavioral diversity in human demonstrations.
- **Mechanism**: The agent’s policy decomposes as \(p(a|s) = \int p(a|s,m)p(m|s)dm\), where inner speech \(m\) is generated from behavioral history \(H_t\). A CVAE produces \(m\) from a window of past images, encoding predicativity, semantic condensation, and temporal regulatory dynamics (Section 3.1.2–3.1.3). The diffusion policy then conditions on this \(m\) to produce diverse actions.
- **Core assumption**: Assumption: Human decision-making includes an intermediate linguistic representation that is not directly observable but can be inferred from visual context.
- **Evidence anchors**:
  - [abstract] "inner speech guides action selection before execution" and "MIMIC employs the novel use of vision-language models as linguistic scaffolding to train a conditional variational autoencoder capable of generating inner speech from observations."
  - [section] Equation (1) in Section 3.1.2 formalizes the mediation decomposition; Algorithm 2 in Section 3.2.2 describes periodic inner speech generation during simulation.
  - [corpus] Weak: neighboring papers do not address inner speech mediation; they focus on diffusion policies or behavioral diversity without a linguistic bottleneck.
- **Break condition**: If the CVAE fails to generate meaningful latent representations (e.g., due to KL collapse or poor training), the policy will receive uninformative inner speech, leading to a collapse in behavioral diversity (low entropy) and reduced success rates.

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim**: Pre-trained vision-language models can provide the initial linguistic descriptions needed to train the inner speech generator without human annotations.
- **Mechanism**: Demonstrations are converted to GIFs; a VLM (e.g., GPT-4o) generates descriptive external speech for each trajectory. CLIP encodes these captions into embeddings \(m^{(i)}\). The CVAE learns to reconstruct similar embeddings from image windows, effectively internalizing language (Algorithm 1). This scaffolding bootstraps inner speech generation from purely visual data.
- **Core assumption**: Assumption: External linguistic descriptions produced by VLMs correlate sufficiently with the latent cognitive processes that generated the behavior.
- **Evidence anchors**:
  - [abstract] "MIMIC employs the novel use of vision-language models as linguistic scaffolding."
  - [section] "By using pre-trained VLMs, we leverage their rich linguistic knowledge to generate descriptions that capture behaviorally-relevant attributes." (Section 3.2.2)
  - [corpus] Weak; corpus neighbors focus on imitation learning or language interfaces but not on VLM-based bootstrapping of latent mediators.
- **Break condition**: Noisy or semantically misaligned VLM captions will pollute the CVAE’s training signal, causing the learned inner speech distribution to diverge from the true behavioral intents, which degrades policy performance.

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim**: A diffusion-based behavior cloning policy conditioned on inner speech produces diverse, high-fidelity actions, and periodic regeneration of inner speech implements temporal regulatory dynamics.
- **Mechanism**: The policy \(\pi_\theta(a|s,m)\) is a DDPM-T that denoises actions given state \(s\) and inner speech \(m\) (Section 3.2.1). During simulation, the CVAE updates \(m\) every \(W\) steps based on recent history, providing a temporally extended context (Algorithm 2). Classifier‑free guidance allows operation even when \(m\) is dropped during training.
- **Core assumption**: Assumption: Action distributions conditioned on inner speech are easier to model than the full marginal, and periodic updates are sufficient to capture non‑Markovian aspects of behavior.
- **Evidence anchors**:
  - [abstract] "A diffusion-based behavior cloning policy then selects actions conditioned on current observations and the generated inner speech."
  - [section] Section 3.2.1 details the diffusion objective and transformer architecture; Table 1 shows improved fidelity and entropy over non‑inner‑speech baselines.
  - [corpus] Neighbors such as Diffusion BC [33] confirm diffusion’s strength in modeling multimodal action distributions; MIMIC extends this with linguistic conditioning.
- **Break condition**: Underfitting in the diffusion model or an poorly chosen update interval \(W\) will cause the policy to either ignore inner speech or produce inconsistent actions, lowering success metrics and potentially increasing Wasserstein distance to demonstrations.

## Foundational Learning
- **Behavior Cloning (BC)**
  - *Why needed here*: MIMIC builds on BC; its limitation (direct state‑to‑action mapping) motivates the inner‑speech extension (Section 2).
  - *Quick check question*: What is the primary covariate‑shift issue in standard BC that MIMIC’s inner speech helps mitigate?

- **Diffusion Models**
  - *Why needed here*: The action policy is a diffusion model (DDPM‑T); understanding forward/reverse processes (Appendix D) clarifies how actions are sampled iteratively.
  - *Quick check question*: How does the training loss of a diffusion model differ from that of a standard generative model like a VAE?

- **Conditional Variational Autoencoders (CVAEs)**
  - *Why needed here*: The CVAE generates inner speech embeddings from behavioral history, implementing semantic condensation (Section 3.2.2).
  - *Quick check question*: What role does the KL‑divergence term play in the CVAE objective, and how does it relate to information compression?

- **Vision‑Language Models (VLMs)**
  - *Why needed here*: VLMs (e.g., GPT‑4o, CLIP) provide the initial textual descriptions that bootstrap inner speech training without per‑step annotations (Section 3.2.2).
  - *Quick check question*: Why does MIMIC avoid the need for explicit human thought annotations, unlike Thought Cloning?

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map**:  
  Offline: Demonstrations -> VLM (GPT-4o) -> CLIP caption embeddings -> CVAE (Psi_enc, Psi_dec) training.  
  Online: Observations -> Visual CNN -> Sliding window H_t -> CVAE (Psi_enc) -> m (via Psi_dec, refreshed every W steps) -> Diffusion Policy (DDPM-T) -> Actions.
- **Critical path**:
  1. **Training**: For each demonstration, VLM caption → CLIP embedding m → augment (s,a) pairs with m. Train CVAE to reconstruct m from image windows; train diffusion policy on (s,a,m) triples.
  2. **Simulation**: Initialize m = 0 or user-provided description. At each step t, if t mod W = t0, generate new m from recent images; then use diffusion policy πθ(a|s,m) to select action, step environment.
- **Design tradeoffs**:
  - **Update window W**: Smaller W yields more frequent inner-speech refresh (adaptable but possibly jittery); larger W provides stability but may lag behind distribution shifts.
  - **VLM choice**: Larger models (GPT-4o) offer richer captions but higher cost; smaller models may trade fidelity for efficiency.
  - **Latent dimensionality**: Higher dimensionality can encode nuanced strategies but risks overfitting and slower training.
- **Failure signatures**:
  - **Low behavioral entropy** in rollouts indicates collapsed diversity (inner speech not providing variation).
  - **High Wasserstein distance** between generated and training state/time distributions (Section 4.2.1) signals fidelity loss.
  - **Drop in collective reward** in Overcooked compared to BC suggests the inner speech is misleading coordination (Section 4.2.2).
  - **Training instability** (e.g., CVAE KL divergence diverging) suggests improper β annealing or insufficient data.
- **First 3 experiments**:
  1. **Fidelity comparison on D3IL**: MIMIC vs BC (DDPM‑T) measuring success rate, distance, entropy, and Wasserstein distances (Section 4.2.1, Table 1).
  2. **Human‑AI coordination in Overcooked**: MIMIC agents paired with human proxy models achieve higher collective reward than BC agents (Section 4.2.2, Table 2).
  3. **Ablation studies**: Removing inner speech (random/clustered vectors), changing embedding (CLIP vs MPNET), swapping VLM (GPT‑4o, o4‑mini, Qwen), and evaluating steerable generation with specified prompts (Section 4.2.3–4.2.4, Figures 4–5).

## Limitations
- **Cognitive validity**: The assumption that VLM-generated captions reflect latent human inner speech lacks direct validation.
- **VLM dependency and bias**: Performance relies on off-the-shelf VLMs, which may introduce noise or cultural bias; this sensitivity is not thoroughly ablated.
- **Human-AI coordination proxy**: Overcooked experiments use simulated human proxies (BC agents) rather than real humans, limiting ecological validity.

## Confidence
- **High**: Empirical gains on D3IL tasks (success rates, entropy, Wasserstein distance) and the diffusion policy’s capacity for multimodal action modeling.
- **Medium**: The mechanistic decomposition \(p(a|s) = \int p(a|s,m)p(m|s)dm\) and periodic inner speech updates as a solution to non‑Markovian behavior; supported by ablations but the causal role of \(m\) remains inferential.
- **Low**: The core assumption that VLM-produced external speech correlates with true internal “inner speech” mediating human behavior; no direct cognitive or neuroscientific evidence is provided.
