# Causal Circuit Tracing Reveals Distinct Computational Architectures in Single-Cell Foundation Models: Inhibitory Dominance, Biological Coherence, and Cross-Model Convergence

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2603.01752
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.01752
- **Reference Count:** 14
- **Primary Result:** Causal circuit tracing via SAE feature ablation uncovers pervasive inhibitory dominance (~65–89%) and identifies 1,142 conserved domain pairs across Geneformer and scGPT, though CRISPRi validation shows only 56.4% directional accuracy.

## Executive Summary
This study introduces *causal circuit tracing*—ablating features from sparse autoencoders (SAEs) fitted to single-cell foundation models—to map computational graphs in Geneformer and scGPT. Across four biological conditions and 96,892 traced edges, both models exhibit strong inhibitory dominance and moderate biological coherence (~53% shared ontology). A cross-model consensus of 1,142 domain pairs shows significant enrichment for shared biological function and disease association. However, gene-level CRISPRi validation reveals low directional accuracy (56.4%), suggesting the traced circuits capture co-expression patterns more reliably than causal regulatory relationships.

## Method Summary
The authors applied SAEs to extract interpretable, sparse features from the latent representations of Geneformer and scGPT. They then performed causal circuit tracing by individually ablating (zeroing) each SAE feature and measuring the downstream change in model output for specific prediction tasks. This generated directed, weighted edges between source and target features. Edges were analyzed for inhibitory vs. excitatory effects, biological coherence via shared gene ontology (GO) terms, and conservation between the two models. Validation involved comparing predicted regulatory directions (source→target) against independent CRISPRi knockdown experiments.

## Key Results
- Both models show **inhibitory dominance** (65–89% of edges) and **invariant edge-weight distributions** across cell types and architectures.
- **Biological coherence** is moderate: ~53% of source–target feature pairs share GO terms.
- **Cross-model consensus** identifies 1,142 domain pairs with 10.6× GO-term enrichment (p<0.001); disease-associated domains are 3.59× more likely to be consensus.
- **CRISPRi validation** yields low directional accuracy (56.4%),indicating models encode co-expression more than causal regulation.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
Ablating SAE features isolates the causal contribution of monosemantic concepts (e.g., protein domains, cell-type markers) to model outputs. Because SAEs decompose dense activations into sparse, interpretable features, targeted ablation allows mapping of feature-to-feature influence pathways. The high inhibitory dominance suggests the models rely heavily on suppression mechanisms to refine predictions, a pattern consistent across different single-cell architectures. Cross-model convergence on specific domain pairs indicates these represent evolutionarily or functionally conserved computational motifs.

## Foundational Learning
- **Sparse Autoencoders (SAEs):** Learn sparse, linear decompositions of neural activations into human-interpretable features. *Why needed:* To extract disentangled biological features (e.g., domains, pathways) from dense model representations. *Quick check:* Verify feature sparsity (e.g., >90% zeros) and reconstruction fidelity.
- **Causal Circuit Tracing:** Systematically ablating model components to measure downstream effects and infer causal graphs. *Why needed:* To move beyond correlation and map functional influence pathways. *Quick check:* Ablate a known critical feature (e.g., a cell-type marker) and confirm expected output suppression.
- **Biological Coherence (GO-term overlap):** Using shared Gene Ontology annotations to assess functional relatedness of features. *Why needed:* Provides a standardized, automated metric for interpretability. *Quick check:* Enrichment analysis should yield significant (p<0.05) GO terms for feature gene sets.
- **Inhibitory vs. Excitatory Edges:** Classifying ablation effects as decreasing (inhibitory) or increasing (excitatory) target activation. *Why needed:* Reveals model reliance on suppression vs. activation. *Quick check:* Distribution of edge signs should be statistically distinguishable from random.
- **Cross-Model Consensus:** Identifying feature pairs with causal influence in both models. *Why needed:* Suggests robust, architecture-agnostic biological signals. *Quick check:* Consensus pairs should show higher GO enrichment than model-specific edges.
- **CRISPRi Validation:** Experimental perturbation to test predicted regulatory directions. *Why needed:* External benchmark for causality. *Quick check:* Accuracy should significantly exceed random guessing (50%); if not, co-expression bias is likely.

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component Map:**  
Gene Tokenization -> Embedding Layer -> Transformer Blocks (N layers) -> SAE Feature Extraction (per layer/block) -> Task-Specific Head (e.g., cell-type classification) -> Output

**Critical Path:**  
Input genes → Transformer layers (contextual integration) → SAE-decoded features (interpretable units) → Ablation analysis → Output change measurement.

**Design Tradeoffs:**  
- *SAE sparsity vs. coverage:* Higher sparsity improves feature interpretability but may miss subtle signals.  
- *Layer choice for SAEs:* Applying SAEs to intermediate vs. final layers affects abstraction level; final layers yield task-relevant features but may be less biologically transparent.  
- *Ablation scope:* Zeroing entire features vs. partial masking; full ablation is simpler but may cause nonlinear secondary effects.

**Failure Signatures:**  
- Low biological coherence (<40% GO overlap) suggests SAE features are noisy or non-monosemantic.  
- Random edge-weight distributions (no inhibitory dominance) indicate tracing is not capturing meaningful circuits.  
- CRISPRi accuracy near 50% implies traced edges reflect correlation, not causation.

**First Three Experiments:**  
1. **SAE Feature Quality Check:** Train SAEs on frozen model activations; evaluate reconstruction error, feature sparsity, and activate features on known gene sets (e.g., cell-type markers).  
2. **Layer-Wise Tracing Comparison:** Perform causal tracing on features from early, middle, and late transformer layers; compare inhibitory dominance and GO enrichment to identify where biologically coherent circuits emerge.  
3. **Ablation Sanity Test:** Ablate a feature corresponding to a well-studied biological process (e.g., “mitochondrial respiration”) and measure predicted impact on relevant cell-type outputs; expect strong, directionally consistent effects.

## Limitations
- SAE features may conflate multiple signals or capture spurious correlations, undermining causal interpretations.
- Biological coherence metric (GO-term overlap) does not confirm functional relevance or context-specificity.
- Low CRISPRi directional accuracy (56.4%) suggests traced edges co-expression rather than causal regulation.
- Findings are limited to two single-cell models; generalizability to other architectures or species is unknown.

## Confidence
- *Inhibitory dominance (65–89%) & invariance:* **High** (consistent across models/cell types).
- *Cross-model consensus (1,142 pairs, 10.6× enrichment):* **High** (statistically robust).
- *Biological coherence (~53% shared ontology):* **Medium** (measurable but not mechanistically validated).
- *CRISPRi directional accuracy (56.4%):* **Low** (near-random, weakens causal claims).

## Next Checks
1. **Perturbation-forward validation:** Use CRISPRi or RNAi to knock down top-predicted *source* domains from consensus edges, then measure *target* gene expression changes in relevant cell types. Success: >70% directional consistency in new validation sets.
2. **Motif enrichment in traced features:** For high-confidence SAE features, scan upstream promoter sequences (e.g., ±2kb) for transcription factor binding motifs; test if feature activations correlate with motif presence/affinity across contexts.
3. **Architectural ablation study:** Remove or scramble key layers (e.g., final transformer blocks) in both models and re-run causal tracing. If consensus edges disappear, they rely on specific stages; if they persist, they arise from deeper representations.
