# WST-X Series: Wavelet Scattering Transform for Interpretable Speech Deepfake Detection

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID**: 2602.02980
- **Source URL**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.02980
- **Reference count**: 33
- **Primary result**: WST-X1 (J=2, Q=10, M=2) on Deepfake-Eval-2024 achieves minDCF 0.3408 and EER 14.18%, significantly beating mel-spectrogram, linear filterbank, CQT, and PT-XLSR baseline.

## Executive Summary
Speech deepfake detection requires features that balance interpretability and semantic richness. Hand-crafted acoustic features are interpretable but lack high-level semantics, while self-supervised (SSL) features are robust but opaque and may overlook fine spectral artifacts. The WST-X series introduces the wavelet scattering transform (WST) as a front-end to combine these advantages. WST provides translation-invariant, deformation-stable multi-scale representations. Two fusion strategies are proposed: WST-X1 (parallel integration of 1D WST with SSL) and WST-X2 (cascaded 2D WST on SSL feature maps). Extensive evaluation on the Deepfake-Eval-2024 benchmark shows that WST-X1 with optimal parameters (J=2, Q=10, M=2) achieves a minDCF of 0.3408 and EER of 14.18%, substantially outperforming traditional acoustic features and the SSL-only PT-XLSR baseline. Visual analyses confirm that WST features better expose subtle synthesis artifacts, enhancing interpretability. This work demonstrates that physically meaningful acoustic representations can be effectively combined with deep semantic models to improve detection performance.

## Method Summary
The WST-X series combines wavelet scattering with a frozen XLSR-300M model. For each 4-second audio chunk at 16 kHz:
- **WST-X1**: A 1D WST (J=2, Q=10, M=2) processes the raw waveform. Scattering coefficients are globally averaged over time and paths, linearly projected to 144-D, and repeated across 205 timesteps. The SSL branch freezes XLSR-300M, prepends 6 learnable prompt tokens to its CNN feature map (≈199 timesteps), passes the extended sequence through the 24-layer transformer encoder to obtain E24 (205×1024), and linearly projects to 144-D. The two 205×144 branches are concatenated to 205×288 and fed into a classifier of 12 Mamba blocks.
- **WST-X2**: The same SSL branch produces E24 (205×1024). This tensor is treated as a 2D image and fed to a 2D WST (J=2, L=10, M=2), yielding a scattering tensor of shape (121, 51, 256). The last dimension is linearly projected to 144-D, and the first two dimensions are flattened to a sequence of length 6171, which is classified by 12 Mamba blocks (input dim 144). Training uses Adam (lr=5e-4) for 100 epochs with binary cross-entropy loss; the checkpoint with lowest development EER is selected.

## Key Results
- WST-X1 (J=2, Q=10, M=2) achieves **minDCF 0.3408** and **EER 14.18%** on Deepfake-Eval-2024.
- This outperforms **mel-spectrogram (minDCF 0.9158)**, **linear filterbank (0.7243)**, **constant-Q cepstral (0.6356)**, and the **PT‑XLSR SSL‑only baseline (0.4052)**.
- Visual inspection of scattering coefficients reveals clearer exposure of synthesis artifacts compared to traditional features, supporting the interpretability claim.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
Wavelet scattering transform (WST) computes multi-scale, translation-invariant representations by applying cascades of wavelet convolutions, modulus non-linearities, and low-pass averaging. This process yields features that are stable to small deformations and rich in fine spectral details. Self‑supervised models like XLSR‑300M capture high-level semantic content but may overlook such subtle cues due to their focus on broad temporal patterns. The WST‑X fusion strategies explicitly supply complementary acoustic information: the parallel design (X1) lets each branch retain its native representation, while the cascaded design (X2) directly injects spectral structure into SSL features. The optimal small averaging scale (J=2) preserves sensitivity to fine artifacts, and high frequency (Q=10) and directional (L=10) resolutions retain discriminative spectral and phase details. Together, these elements enable the classifier to detect deepfakes more accurately than either approach alone.

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component map (WST‑X1)**: Raw waveform -> 1D WST (J=2,Q=10,M=2) -> Global average pool -> Linear(144) -> Repeat(205) -> PT‑XLSR branch: XLSR‑300M (frozen) + 6 prompt tokens -> Transformer encoder (24 layers) -> Linear(144) -> Concatenation (205×288) -> Mamba classifier (12 blocks) -> Output probability.

**Component map (WST‑X2)**: Raw waveform -> PT‑XLSR branch (as above) -> E24 tensor (205×1024) -> 2D WST (J=2,L=10,M=2) -> Tensor (121,51,256) -> Linear(D_scat→144) -> Flatten (6171×144) -> Mamba classifier (12 blocks) -> Output probability.

**Critical path**: The end‑to‑end data flow from raw audio to final sigmoid must preserve temporal alignment in X1 and maintain correct tensor reshaping in X2; any mismatch halts learning.

**Design tradeoffs**:  
- *Parallel (X1) vs. cascaded (X2)*: X1 treats branches independently, possibly easier to debug; X2 directly injects spectral structure into SSL features but yields a much longer sequence (6171 vs 205), increasing memory/computation.  
- *WST parameters*: Smaller J captures finer artifacts but increases output size; higher Q/L improves resolution but adds computational burden.  
- *Freezing SSL*: Reduces trainable parameters and overfitting but limits adaptation; prompt tuning offers a lightweight alternative to full fine‑tuning.  
- *Mamba vs. alternative classifiers*: Mamba scales well to long sequences; however, its implementation details (hidden size, state dimension) are unspecified, affecting reproducibility.

**Failure signatures**:  
- Shape mismatches (e.g., expected 205 but get 199).  
- Zero gradients in prompt tokens or WST branch (indicating no learning).  
- Training loss not decreasing or dev EER far above reported values (>0.6 minDCF).  
- Memory overflow in X2 due to large flattened sequence length (6171).

**First experiments**:
1. **Baseline PT‑XLSR**: Train the PT‑XLSR branch alone with a simple linear classifier to verify it learns and reaches reasonable performance (EER ~0.4 from baseline).  
2. **WST‑X1 sanity check**: Use a random WST output (non‑learnable) and train only the Mamba classifier to ensure the pipeline runs and gradients flow through the concatenation.  
3. **Full WST‑X1 training**: With both branches trainable, train for a few epochs to confirm loss decreases and dev EER improves; tune learning rate if unstable.

## Limitations
- **Major unknowns**: Exact Mamba classifier architecture (hidden size, state dimension, layer norms) is unspecified, hindering faithful replication.
- **Ambiguities**: WST‑X1’s global average pooling scope and temporal expansion mechanism are not defined; prompt token placement (single prepend vs. per‑layer) is ambiguous; deterministic length of E0 for 4‑second chunks may vary, affecting input dimensions.
- **Confidence in results**: Reported benchmark numbers are of low confidence due to these unresolved implementation details; method description confidence is medium.
