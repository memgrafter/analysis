# Experience-Guided Self-Adaptive Cascaded Agents for Breast Cancer Screening and Diagnosis with Reduced Biopsy Referrals
## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2602.23899
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.23899
- **Reference count:** 25
- **Primary result:** Reduced diagnostic escalation from 84.95% to 58.72% and overall biopsy referrals from 59.50% to 37.08% across 10 datasets.

## Executive Summary
This paper tackles the critical issue of excessive false positives in breast ultrasound screening, which leads to unnecessary patient anxiety, diagnostic burden, and costly biopsy referrals. It introduces BUSD-Agent, a novel cascaded multi-agent framework that mimics clinical triage: a lightweight screening agent first filters low-risk cases, and only suspicious cases are escalated to a more comprehensive diagnostic agent for detailed analysis. The core innovation is an experience-guided in-context adaptation mechanism, where a memory bank of pathology-confirmed decision trajectories is used to dynamically calibrate the agents' escalation and biopsy policies for new cases without any parameter updates. Evaluated on 10 public breast ultrasound datasets, this approach substantially reduces unnecessary diagnostic escalation and biopsies while improving specificity.

## Method Summary
BUSD-Agent implements a two-stage cascade. The screening agent employs an ensemble of 14 pre-trained classification models and an LLM orchestrator to produce a binary decision (Clear or Escalate). The diagnostic agent, invoked only for escalated cases, integrates a richer "tool zoo"—including segmentation, detection, and fine-tuned radiological description models—with an LLM via a ReAct loop to generate a final biopsy recommendation. The key adaptive mechanism is a memory bank storing structured decision trajectories (image embeddings, tool outputs, actions, pathology labels). For each new case, the system retrieves the top-K most similar past trajectories using a combined similarity metric that jointly considers visual features and model confidence vectors. These retrieved exemplars are formatted as in-context demonstrations for the LLM, enabling it to condition its current decision policy on the collective experience of similar, pathology-validated historical cases.

## Key Results
- Diagnostic escalation rate reduced from 84.95% (baseline) to 58.72%.
- Overall biopsy referral rate reduced from 59.50% to 37.08%.
- Average screening specificity improved by 68.48% and diagnostic specificity by 6.33%, while maintaining sensitivity.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1: Cascaded Triage for Efficiency
**Claim:** A cascaded multi-agent structure reduces overall diagnostic burden by filtering low-risk cases, preventing them from entering the expensive diagnostic stage.
**Mechanism:** The system operates in two selective stages: (1) A screening agent with a lightweight classification ensemble rapidly evaluates all cases and only escalates those with high malignancy risk/uncertainty; (2) Only escalated cases undergo full diagnostic analysis with richer tools. This avoids subjecting the majority of benign/normal screens to costly deep analysis.
**Core assumption:** Most screening cases are benign/normal, and their features can be reliably identified and safely cleared by the efficient first-stage agent.
**Evidence anchors:** The abstract and Section 2.1 define the two-stage selective process and detail the divergent tool compositions for screening (ensemble) vs. diagnosis (localization, segmentation, description). The clinical triage paradigm is established domain knowledge.
**Break condition:** A significant rise in the screening agent's false negative rate (missed malignancies) would violate the core safety assumption of the cascade, necessitating a review of screening thresholds or model capability.

### Mechanism 2: Experience-Guided Policy Adaptation
**Claim:** A pathology-confirmed decision trajectory memory bank enables dynamic policy calibration without model parameter updates.
**Mechanism:** Past cases are stored as trajectories combining image embeddings, screening/diagnostic tool outputs, agent actions, and final pathology. For a new case, top-K similar trajectories are retrieved based on a joint similarity metric in the combined feature space. This retrieved set is provided as in-context exemplars to the LLM orchestrator, which then adapts its trust in tool outputs and final decisions based on patterns of how similar cases were correctly/incorrectly handled historically.
**Core assumption:** Visually and tool-output-similar cases share transferable optimal decisions, and the LLM can effectively induce the correct policy shifts from these exemplars.
**Evidence anchors:** The abstract and Section 2.2 formalize the memory bank, retrieval similarity metric (λ·cosine(embeddings) + (1-λ)·cosine(confidences)), and conditioning process. Figure 3 illustrates the trajectory structure.
**Break condition:** Retrieved neighbors for a query case are pathologically discordant (mixed benign/malignant labels) or the LLM fails to extract a consistent pattern from them, leading to maladaptive policy shifts.

### Mechanism 3: Tool-Augmented LLM Orchestration for Reliable Reasoning
**Claim:** Integrating specialized perceptual tools with an LLM via a ReAct loop enables robust multi-modal clinical reasoning, improving biopsy decision specificity.
**Mechanism:** The LLM acts as a central orchestrator, iteratively accessing domain-specific tools (e.g., segmentation, BIRADS classification). Tool outputs provide structured, verifiable facts (e.g., spatial masks, attribute scores) that anchor the LLM's reasoning, reducing hallucination and providing a clinically interpretable basis for the final biopsy decision.
**Core assumption:** Specialized models outperform the LLM on narrow perceptual tasks, and the LLM can effectively synthesize their structured outputs into a coherent clinical judgment.
**Evidence anchors:** The abstract and Section 2.1 describe the tool zoo for the diagnostic agent and the ReAct paradigm. Section 3/Table 1 shows BUSD-Agent outperforms raw VLMs in balanced accuracy and specificity trade-offs.
**Break condition:** Degradation in any key tool's performance (e.g., poor segmentation, inaccurate BIRADS prediction) provides low-quality inputs to the LLM, undermining the entire diagnostic reasoning chain.

## Foundational Learning
- **Cascaded Decision Systems:** Needed to manage the high volume of negative screening cases efficiently while preserving analytical depth for positives. **Quick check:** How do the error characteristics (false negative vs. false positive) of the first stage propagate and define the safety and efficiency constraints for the second stage?
- **In-Context Learning from Demonstrations:** The core adaptation mechanism relies on the LLM's ability to modify its behavior based on retrieved historical examples, not fine-tuning. **Quick check:** What minimal set of state, action, and outcome information must a demonstration trajectory contain to safely guide policy adjustment in a high-stakes medical context?
- **Clinical Triage Workflow Principles:** The agent design directly emulates the radiologist's "screen-diagnose-biopsy" workflow, aiming to optimize the trade-off between comprehensive assessment and overdiagnosis. **Quick check:** How does the concept of "overdiagnosis" specifically constrain the design of an AI screening triage system compared to a simple sensitivity-maximization goal?

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component map:** Breast Ultrasound Image -> Screening Agent (Ensemble of 14 Classifiers + LLM Orchestrator) -> [Decision: Clear | Escalate] -> if Escalate -> Diagnostic Agent (Tool Zoo: Segmentation, Detection, Multi-task Attribute Classifiers, MedGemma Descriptor + LLM Orchestrator) -> Biopsy Decision (No Biopsy | Biopsy). Memory Bank is queried by both agents using a combined similarity metric over image embeddings and tool confidence vectors.

**Critical path:** 1) New image input. 2) Screening agent runs ensemble, computes malignancy confidence vector, retrieves top-K similar trajectories, LLM produces Clear/Escalate decision. 3) If Escalate, image enters diagnostic agent. 4) Diagnostic agent runs full tool suite, generates feature vector/description, retrieves top-K trajectories from diagnostic memory, LLM integrates all outputs to produce biopsy decision. 5) Final diagnostic output.

**Design tradeoffs:** Specificity-Sensitivity: Screening threshold and retrieval-guided adaptation balance false positives/negatives. Tool Complexity vs. Latency: Full diagnostic toolset is computationally heavy; applying it only to the escalated subset controls overall system delay. Memory Size vs. Retrieval Relevance: Larger memory banks offer more examples but increase noise and retrieval time; K=10 was empirically optimal.

**Failure signatures:** Screening stage: Escalation rate fails to decrease (or increases) with retrieval, indicating ineffective exemplars or LLM ignoring them; concurrent drop in true positive rate. Diagnostic stage: Biopsy specificity does not improve or true positive rate falls, suggesting poor diagnostic tool quality or LLM's failed integration of retrieved experience. General: High performance variance across datasets indicates insufficient or non-representative memory bank content for certain distributions.

**First 3 experiments:** 1) **Baseline vs. Proposed:** Compare the framework with and without memory-retrieval conditioning, measuring primary outcomes (escalation rate, biopsy referrals). 2) **Retrieval Ablation & K-Sweep:** Ablate retrieval components (image-only vs. vector-only vs. joint similarity) and sweep K value to determine optimal configuration (Fig. 7). 3) **Cross-Dataset Generalization:** Test a memory bank built from a subset of datasets on held-out datasets to evaluate the transferability of learned experience.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
1. How does the optimal K and λ vary across different clinical settings or population subgroups, and can they be dynamically adjusted per case?
2. To what extent does the performance gain depend on the quality and diversity of the memory bank, and what is the minimal required memory size or pathology confirmation rate for stable operation?
3. Why do proprietary and open-source VLMs exhibit such unstable operating points compared to the tool-augmented BUSD-Agent, and what does this reveal about the fundamental limitations of monolithic VLMs for sequential clinical decision-making?
4. Can the experience-guided in-context adaptation mechanism generalise beyond breast ultrasound to other multi-stage diagnostic pathways with different tools and decision logic?
5. How does the cascaded design itself—with its hard escalation rule—compare to a single, more complex agent that processes all cases with the full diagnostic toolset, in terms of overall efficiency, cost, and clinical safety?

## Limitations
- **Reproducibility gaps:** Exact LLM prompt templates, ReAct loop specifications, and diagnostic tool training details (e.g., MedGemma LoRA hyperparameters, segmentation/detection datasets) are not fully disclosed.
- **Generalization risk:** Memory bank built from 20% reserved samples per dataset; efficacy on truly unseen populations, imaging protocols, or with noisy historical labels is uncertain.
- **Clinical deployment unknowns:** Real-world integration with radiologist workflows, latency impacts of multi-tool invocation plus LLM calls, and requirements for human-in-the-loop oversight are unaddressed.

## Next Checks
1. **Retrieval relevance audit:** For a balanced sample of benign/malignant cases, manually verify if top-K retrieved neighbors share similar pathology labels and radiological features. Measure average pathology concordance within retrieved sets.
2. **Prompt & parsing robustness test:** Systematically vary LLM prompt phrasing and output parsing logic; measure decision consistency (e.g., Cohen’s κ) and failure rate (non-binary responses) to quantify orchestration fragility.
3. **Negative-case cross-dataset stress test:** Evaluate the screening agent's false negative rate on datasets with higher malignancy prevalence or different annotation protocols to stress-test the cascade's safety boundary.
