# Artificial Agency Program: Curiosity, compression, and communication in agents

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID**: 2602.24100
- **Source URL**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.24100
- **Reference count**: 18
- **Key outcome**: A falsifiable research program unifying predictive compression, intrinsic motivation, empowerment, and communication as selective information bottlenecks under explicit observation, action, and compute budgets.

## Executive Summary
This paper proposes a unified framework for building embedded, resource-bounded agents that develop through curiosity-as-learning-progress under physical and computational constraints. The core idea is to treat observation, action, and deliberation as interchangeable budgeted tokens, with agents optimizing predictive compression while dynamically allocating resources. The framework explicitly addresses the reality gap in current AI by incorporating developmental constraints and human-AI coupling through improved sensing, understanding, and actuation.

## Method Summary
The method implements agents with a limited budget across observation (O), action (A), and deliberation (C) tokens per step, optimizing a unified objective that balances task performance against interface quality and resource costs. Agents use learning-progress curiosity as intrinsic reward, measured by improvement in horizon-H predictive loss. A meta-controller dynamically allocates the budget between gathering observations, executing actions, and internal deliberation (including optional self-communication tokens). The framework is staged: starting with synthetic POMDPs with tunable bottlenecks, progressing to ARC-AGI-style interactive inference, and finally scaling to multimodal VLAs with frozen backbones and lightweight meta-control.

## Key Results
- Unifies predictive compression, intrinsic motivation, empowerment, and communication as selective information bottlenecks
- Proposes five testable hypotheses about learning-progress alignment, boundary pressure, constraint-induced pressure, adaptive compute optimality, and self-communication benefits
- Introduces a task-relative unification score measuring interface quality across observation, action, and communication channels
- Outlines a concrete experimental agenda from synthetic environments to multimodal meta-control

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- **Claim**: Optimizing predictive learning progress under resource constraints aligns with gaining useful control over task-relevant environmental degrees of freedom.
- **Mechanism**: The intrinsic reward incentivizes improving prediction of future observations. Under fixed budgets, actions that improve long-horizon prediction also expand the agent's channel capacity from actions to future observations (empowerment), because both require accurate world models and actionable influence.
- **Core assumption**: The environment's predictive structure is sufficiently aligned with control-relevant structure.
- **Evidence anchors**: Abstract's unification claim; section 2.1 on empowerment; corpus on related work.
- **Break condition**: Environments where high predictive performance is achievable without corresponding empowerment (e.g., passive observation of chaotic but uncontrollable systems).

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim**: A meta-controller that dynamically allocates observation, action, and deliberation tokens outperforms fixed allocation schedules under the same total budget.
- **Mechanism**: Adaptive compute allows the agent to spend more tokens on observation in high-uncertainty states, more on deliberation for complex planning, or more on action when opportunities are clear, maximizing the ratio of learning progress or task reward per total cost.
- **Core assumption**: The marginal benefit of allocating an extra token changes over time and is learnable.
- **Evidence anchors**: Abstract's claim on adaptive meta-control; section 3.4 on H4; corpus on adaptive computation.
- **Break condition**: Overhead of meta-decision-making dominates the gains from better allocation.

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim**: An explicit private self-communication channel improves sample efficiency and performance on long-horizon tasks beyond latent recurrence alone.
- **Mechanism**: Self-communication forces the agent to encode intermediate reasoning into a compressed, re-readable format, creating a "communicator–interpreter loop" that induces a third-person stance on its own states, aiding planning and credit assignment.
- **Core assumption**: The cost of emitting and parsing self-communication tokens is lower than the equivalent latent state manipulation, or it provides a structuring benefit that latent recurrence cannot match.
- **Evidence anchors**: Abstract's claim on private self-communication; section 4 on language as bottleneck; corpus on reasoning traces.
- **Break condition**: The private channel collapses into verbose, redundant codes; or latent recurrence under equal compute dominates.

## Foundational Learning

- **Concept**: Resource-bounded agency
  - **Why needed here**: The entire framework treats observation, action, and deliberation as interchangeable budgeted tokens. Without grasping that every interaction has an explicit cost, the objective and hypotheses are meaningless.
  - **Quick check question**: If an agent has 100 tokens per step, what are three ways it could allocate them, and what cost would each incur under equation (12)?

- **Concept**: Learning-progress curiosity (Schmidhuber, 2010)
  - **Why needed here**: This is the intrinsic driver. It's not raw novelty but improvement in predictive compression that fuels development. Misunderstanding this leads to random exploration instead of capability-expanding play.
  - **Quick check question**: Given predictive losses L(θ_old) and L(θ_new) over a horizon H, how is the intrinsic reward computed? What horizon choice might bias the agent toward myopic vs. far-sighted exploration?

- **Concept**: Information bottlenecks and unification
  - **Why needed here**: Unification (Eq. 15) quantifies interface quality. It operationalizes how much sensing/acting/communication inefficiency remains. Without this, you cannot measure whether the agent is investing to improve its interface.
  - **Quick check question**: What does a high R_O (observation inefficiency) indicate, and how could an agent reduce it by spending budget?

## Architecture Onboarding

- **Component map**: Token budget manager -> Perception/backbone -> World model/predictor -> Meta-controller -> Self-communication module -> Environment

- **Critical path**: 
  1. Implement tokenized environment with explicit costs for O, A, C (Stage 1: synthetic POMDPs).
  2. Train a fixed backbone with a random or heuristic meta-controller to collect baseline data.
  3. Introduce learnable meta-controller optimizing Eq. (12) with intrinsic reward.
  4. Add interface-modifying actions and measure unification score.
  5. Scale to more complex environments (ARC-AGI, then VLA).

- **Design tradeoffs**:
  - Granularity of tokens: Coarser tokens reduce decision complexity but limit precision.
  - Fixed vs. adaptive backbone: Frozen backbone simplifies training but limits plasticity.
  - Cost calibration: Setting λ weights arbitrarily can distort behavior.

- **Failure signatures**:
  - Budget collapse: Agent spends all tokens on a single type early on, never acting or observing.
  - Unification saturates prematurely: Agent achieves high U by cheap tricks without real capability gain.
  - Self-communication degeneracy: Private tokens become repetitive or copy latent states verbatim.
  - Meta-control overhead: The meta-controller itself consumes so many C-tokens that net performance drops vs. fixed schedule.

- **First 3 experiments**:
  1. **Synthetic POMDP with switchable bottlenecks**: Validate H1 and H2. Agent can spend budget to reduce observation noise or increase action cardinality. Measure whether spending budget on interface improvements correlates with improved learning progress and control metrics.
  2. **ARC-AGI grid with variable difficulty**: Test H4. Compare a learned meta-controller against fixed periodic schedules. Primary metric: normalized score vs. total token budget.
  3. **Latent vs. private token deliberation**: Test H5. Two agents: one uses only latent recurrence for planning; the other can emit fixed-length private text tokens per deliberation step. Both have equal total C-token budgets. Compare sample efficiency and final performance on a task requiring long-horizon credit assignment.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- **Question**: Does optimizing learning progress also improve control/empowerment under resource bounds?
- **Basis in paper**: Explicit - H1 (Pragmatic alignment)
- **Why unresolved**: The regime where predictive compression aligns with control is not yet empirically mapped.
- **What evidence would resolve it**: Consistent positive correlation between learning progress and control metrics across tasks; counterexamples would refine the hypothesis boundaries.

### Open Question 2
- **Question**: When agents can modify interfaces, do they invest to improve unification when long-term gains outweigh costs?
- **Basis in paper**: Explicit - H2 (Boundary pressure)
- **Why unresolved**: Requires measuring resource allocation under varying bottleneck costs.
- **Evidence**: Agent spends to widen interfaces until U saturates; never investing or always over-investing would falsify.

### Open Question 3
- **Question**: Under viability constraints and nonzero processing costs, are agents driven to better prediction and selective control even without an explicit intrinsic reward?
- **Basis in paper**: Explicit - H3 (Constraint-induced pressure)
- **Why unresolved**: Need to show constraints alone induce predictive structuring.
- **Evidence**: Constrained policies exhibit improved predictive performance and reduced wasteful reactivity versus unconstrained baselines.

### Open Question 4
- **Question**: Does a dynamic meta-controller allocating compute outperform any fixed schedule at equal total budget?
- **Basis in paper**: Explicit - H4 (Adaptive compute optimality)
- **Why unresolved**: Adaptation overhead may offset benefits; depends on task variability.
- **Evidence**: Adaptive allocation achieves higher performance than the best-tuned fixed schedule across varying difficulties.

### Open Question 5
- **Question**: Does a private self-communication channel improve performance and sample efficiency on long-horizon tasks beyond latent-only recurrence under matched compute?
- **Basis in paper**: Explicit - H5 (Self-communication bottleneck)
- **Why unresolved**: Language-like traces might be redundant; latent recurrence may suffice.
- **Evidence**: Bandwidth-regularized private tokens yield faster learning or higher success compared to latent-only; no benefit or degeneracy would disprove.

## Limitations

- The tokenization and cost mapping of continuous modalities remain underspecified, leaving room for implementation variation that could affect outcomes.
- The meta-controller architecture and its integration with the world model is ambiguous, which could influence training stability and performance.
- Hyperparameter calibration, particularly the λ weights balancing different costs, lacks principled guidance.
- The learning-progress reward is sensitive to the predictive model's training procedure and horizon choice, potentially leading to optimization of superficial prediction improvement rather than meaningful understanding.

## Confidence

- **High Confidence**: The core mechanism linking learning-progress curiosity to empowerment (H1) is well-grounded in prior literature and conceptually sound.
- **Medium Confidence**: The claim that adaptive meta-control outperforms fixed schedules (H4) is plausible but depends critically on the overhead of meta-decision-making and task variability.
- **Low Confidence**: The benefit of private self-communication over latent recurrence (H5) is the most speculative, as it's unclear whether explicit tokens provide a structuring benefit that latent recurrence cannot match under the same cost.
