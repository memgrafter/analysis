# Distribution-Aware End-to-End Embedding for Streaming Numerical Features in Click-Through Rate Prediction

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2602.03223  
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.03223  
- **Reference Count:** 40  
- **Primary Result:** Sets new SOTA on three datasets across six backbones and achieves a 2.307% ARPU lift in a large-scale online A/B test, while simplifying the preprocessing pipeline.

## Executive Summary
Click-through rate (CTR) prediction in streaming environments faces challenges with numerical feature embedding: static binning suffers from semantic drift, neural embeddings discard distributional information, and end-to-end training is hindered by non-i.i.d. data and lack of field-awareness. The proposed DAES method addresses these via three core components: jump reservoir sampling for efficient online quantile estimation, quantile-space interpolation for density-aware smooth encoding, and field-aware modulation to dynamically adjust embeddings based on categorical context. DAES consistently outperforms prior art offline and delivers significant revenue lift in a production deployment on a major short-video platform.

## Method Summary
DAES processes each numerical feature in three stages. First, **distribution estimation** uses jump reservoir sampling to maintain a fixed-size uniform sample of historical values, from which equi-probable quantiles are extracted. Second, the **quantile encoder** maps an input value to a bin index and interpolation weight within the quantile bins, constructing a thermometer-style vector that encodes cumulative density. Third, a **field-aware modulator** (affine or gating) conditions this vector on a selected categorical field's embedding to produce context-sensitive weights. These weights linearly combine a set of learnable meta-embeddings to yield the final numerical embedding. The entire module integrates seamlessly with any CTR backbone and trains end-to-end via binary cross-entropy loss.

## Key Results
- **SOTA Performance:** Achieves the highest average rank across three datasets and six backbone models (DeepFM, DCN v2, etc.), with significant AUC and LogLoss improvements over strong baselines like DAE and PLE.
- **Production Impact:** A 7-day online A/B test on a platform with hundreds of millions of daily active users shows a 2.307% lift in Average Revenue Per User (ARPU) compared to the static binning baseline.
- **Operational Simplification:** Removes the need for offline preprocessing and manual binning, streamlining the pipeline while improving metrics.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1: Jump Reservoir Sampling for Streaming Quantile Estimation
**Claim:** Provides unbiased quantile estimates from non-stationary data streams with sub-linear computational overhead.  
**Mechanism:** Maintains a fixed-size reservoir where each incoming sample replaces a random element with probability *m/t*. Jump sampling uses the survival function *S(δ) = (t/(t+δ))^m* to skip large blocks of samples, avoiding per-element random number generation.  
**Core Assumption:** The reservoir uniformly samples the historical stream; the continuous Poisson approximation holds for large *t*.  
**Evidence Anchors:** Theorem 3.2 (consistency) and Lemma 3.4 (complexity *O(m log t)*) in the paper. Neighboring work highlights drift in streaming CTR, underscoring the need for robust online estimation.  
**Break Condition:** If concept drift is extremely rapid (distribution shifts faster than reservoir refresh), quantiles become stale and biased.

### Mechanism 2: Quantile Space Interpolation for Density-Aware Encoding
**Claim:** Generates smooth, continuous embeddings that reflect cumulative density, avoiding topological errors in long-tail or sparse regions.  
**Mechanism:** Maps input *x* to a quantile coordinate: finds bin index *j* (based on *M* quantiles) and interpolation weight *w = (x - q_j)/(q_{j+1} - q_j)*. Constructs a thermometer-style vector *v_raw*: first *j* entries = 1, (*j*+1)-th entry = *w*, rest = 0.  
**Core Assumption:** Values within each equi-probable quantile bin are uniformly distributed (local uniformity).  
**Evidence Anchors:** Equations (7)-(8) and discussion contrasting with value-space encoding. Prior work (e.g., PLE) uses offline quantile interpolation, but DAES's end-to-end quantile-space approach is novel.  
**Break Condition:** If the distribution is highly multimodal or has sharp density changes within bins, local uniformity fails, degrading density representation.

### Mechanism 3: Field-Aware Modulation for Contextual Adaptation
**Claim:** Dynamically adapts numerical embeddings to categorical context (field), capturing conditional distributions without combinatorial explosion of per-field models.  
**Mechanism:** Two differentiable strategies: (i) affine: *w = β·Sigmoid(Reshape(W_tran e_f)·v_raw) + (1-β)·v_raw*; (ii) gating: *w = β·(v_raw ⊙ Sigmoid(W_gate e_f)) + (1-β)·v_raw*. Field embedding *e_f* reweights quantile bins before aggregating meta-embeddings.  
**Core Assumption:** Categorical field embeddings *e_f* convey information about the conditional distribution of the numerical feature.  
**Evidence Anchors:** Equations (9)-(11) and modulation design. Neighbors like "Distribution-Aware Robust Learning" stress distribution awareness; "Always Strengthen Your Strengths" uses field-aware drift adaptation, supporting the principle.  
**Break Condition:** If chosen fields are statistically independent of the numerical feature's conditional distribution, modulation adds noise and may harm performance.

## Foundational Learning
- **Concept: Reservoir Sampling**  
  **Why needed:** Core to online quantile estimation; ensures each historical sample has equal inclusion probability *m/t*, yielding an unbiased empirical distribution.  
  **Quick check:** "What is the probability that a sample arriving at time *t* (*t* > *m*) enters a reservoir of size *m*?" Answer: *m/t*.

- **Concept: Quantile-Based Discretization**  
  **Why needed:** Transforms continuous values into representations that encode cumulative density, enabling smooth interpolation and preserving ordinality.  
  **Quick check:** "How does DAES construct the thermometer vector *v_raw* for a value *x*?" Answer: Determine bin index *j* where *x* falls; set first *j* entries to 1, (*j*+1)-th entry to fractional weight *(x - q_j)/(q_{j+1} - q_j)*, rest to 0.

- **Concept: Field-Aware Feature Interaction**  
  **Why needed:** Numerical semantics depend on context (e.g., price meaning differs by category). Modulation adapts embeddings per field without separate per-field distribution models, avoiding combinatorial blowup.  
  **Quick check:** "What are the two field-aware modulation mechanisms in DAES?" Answer: Affine transformation and gating mechanism.

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map:** Input (numerical value *x*, categorical field embedding *e_f*) -> Distribution Estimation (jump reservoir sampling updates reservoir *R*; sorted to extract quantiles *Q*) -> Quantile Encoder (computes bin index *j*, weight *w*, builds *v_raw*) -> Modulator (uses *e_f* to produce weight vector *w_tran* or *w_gate*) -> Aggregator (weighted sum of meta-embedding matrix *E* yields final embedding *e_final*) -> Output embedding *e_final* integrated into CTR backbone.

- **Critical path (forward pass per numerical feature):**  
  1. Reservoir *R* updated asynchronously as new data streams; quantiles *Q* recomputed only when reservoir changes.  
  2. For each *x*: map to quantile bin using *Q* → *v_raw*.  
  3. Compute *w* = modulator(*v_raw*, *e_f*).  
  4. *e_final* = *w^T E*.  
  5. Gradients flow from prediction loss through modulator and aggregator to *E* and modulation parameters; reservoir update is non-differentiable but occurs out-of-band.

- **Design tradeoffs:**  
  - Reservoir size *m*: Larger *m* improves quantile accuracy but increases memory (~400KB per feature) and sorting latency; paper uses *m*=10^5.  
  - Number of meta-embeddings *M*: More *M* yields finer representation but more parameters; DAES is less sensitive to *M* than methods like AutoDis.  
  - Modulation *β*: Balances raw vs. modulated weights; optimal around 0.5. Higher *β* risks overfitting to context.  
  - Field selection: Using statistically significant fields (via Kruskal-Wallis/Wasserstein) improves performance; arbitrary selection may add noise.

- **Failure signatures:**  
  - Degrading AUC/LogLoss over time suggests reservoir stale due to unmodeled rapid drift.  
  - Memory overflow if many numerical fields each have a reservoir; consider sharing reservoirs or reducing *m*.  
  - QPS drop (~1.8% in deployment) from sorting during reservoir updates; monitor latency spikes.  
  - Poorly initialized modulation hyperparameters (*β*, *W_tran/W_gate*) may cause training divergence or plateau.

- **First 3 experiments:**  
  1. **Offline validation:** DAES beats all baselines across three datasets and six backbones, ranking first on average with significant AUC/LogLoss gains.  
  2. **Online A/B test:** 7-day test on a short-video platform shows 2.307% ARPU lift over production static binning; also eliminates offline preprocessing.  
  3. **Ablation study:** (a) Distribution estimation: reservoir sampling (RS) reduces KL divergence vs. order statistics (OS); jump sampling (JRS) retains accuracy with fewer random calls. (b) Modulation impact: *β* > 0 yields improvements. (c) Field selection: using statistically significant fields outperforms random selection.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- **Expiration mechanisms:** How to optimally balance distribution stability and temporal adaptivity via periodic discarding of distant samples?
- **Cold-start population:** What is the best strategy for populating the reservoir during initial deployment to minimize early-stage bias?
- **Differentiable sketches:** Can specialized streaming quantile algorithms (e.g., KLL sketch, t-digest) be adapted to be differentiable for end-to-end training?
- **Dynamic reservoir sizing:** How can reservoir size be adjusted online based on quantile stability to optimize bias-variance trade-off?

## Limitations
- **Reproducibility gaps:** Exact quantile extraction method, precise field-selection criteria (statistical thresholds, cardinalities), reservoir update scheduling during streaming training, and meta-embedding initialization/β scheduling are unspecified.
- **Assumption sensitivity:** Local uniformity assumption in quantile encoding may fail for multimodal or sharply changing distributions; fixed reservoir may lag under rapid concept drift.
- **Unvalidated components:** Jump reservoir variant and field-aware modulation lack external validation; theoretical guarantees assume stationarity, which may not hold in real-world streams.

## Confidence
- **Empirical claims (SOTA gains, ARPU lift):** High. Supported by large-scale A/B test and consistent offline results.
- **Theoretical guarantees (Theorem 3.2, Lemma 3.4):** Medium. Proofs assume stationarity; drift impact on consistency is not bounded.
- **Mechanism efficacy (JRS accuracy, modulation benefit):** Low-Medium. Ablations show contribution but depend on paper-specific hyperparameters without broad sensitivity analysis.

## Next Checks
1. **Quantile accuracy under controlled drift:** Simulate streaming data with known quantiles (e.g., shifting Gaussian). Measure KL divergence between DAES-estimated quantiles and ground truth across drift rates; validate sub-linear cost without bias.
2. **Field-selection robustness:** Apply Kruskal-Wallis + Wasserstein tests with varying thresholds (p=0.01, 0.05, 0.1). Compare performance when using top-*k* fields vs. random/non-significant fields; quantify sensitivity.
3. **Modulation ablation:** Train DAES with *β*=0, affine only, gating only, and both. Measure AUC/LogLoss delta on validation sets across field cardinalities; test if modulation helps only for fields with high conditional variance.
