# D$^2$Quant: Accurate Low-bit Post-Training Weight Quantization for LLMs

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2602.02546  
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.02546  
- **Reference count:** 6  
- **Primary result:** D²Quant achieves state-of-the-art 2-bit weight quantization on Qwen-3-8B, improving average zero-shot accuracy from 54.05 to 57.22 and lowering WikiText2 perplexity from 20.24 to 14.10.

## Executive Summary
Weight-only post-training quantization (PTQ) for large language models (LLMs) below 4-bit precision suffers significant accuracy loss, primarily due to the sensitivity of down-projection matrices in the MLP and the activation distribution shift in the post-attention LayerNorm. D²Quant introduces two complementary components: the Dual-Scale Quantizer (DSQ), which optimizes an absorbable column-wise scaling factor to smooth the down-projection’s weight distribution, and the Deviation-Aware Correction (DAC), which estimates and corrects the mean shift in the post-attention LayerNorm activations caused by quantized attention. Applied in a block-wise sequential pipeline, DSQ and DAC yield substantial gains across multiple model families and bit-widths, establishing a new state-of-the-art for sub-4-bit weight-only quantization.

## Method Summary
D²Quant is a block-wise PTQ pipeline that processes each transformer layer sequentially. First, the attention modules (Q/K/V/O) are quantized. Then, DAC is applied: it collects full-precision and quantized activations at the post-attention LayerNorm, computes a per-channel mean deviation bias `µ` from the calibration set, and adds this bias during inference to correct the mean shift. Next, the MLP’s up-projection and gate projection are quantized, followed by DSQ on the down-projection. DSQ iteratively optimizes an auxiliary column-wise scale `sc` for the down-projection’s quantization objective, then merges `sc` into the up-projection’s existing per-channel scales, requiring no extra bit budget. A forward pass with the partially quantized block updates calibration data for subsequent layers. The process repeats until all blocks are quantized, producing a fully quantized model ready for standard weight-only inference.

## Key Results
- On Qwen-3-8B at 2-bit, D²Quant achieves **57.22 average zero-shot accuracy** over seven tasks (vs. 54.05 for prior SOTA) and reduces WikiText2 perplexity to **14.10** (from 20.24).
- On LLaMA-3.1-8B at 2-bit, D²Quant achieves **51.04 average accuracy** versus 44.04 with GPTQ, with consistent perplexity reductions on WikiText2 and C4.
- Ablation studies confirm additive gains: DSQ alone improves MMLU by +3.48, DAC alone improves average accuracy by +0.69.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1: Dual-Scale Quantizer (DSQ)
DSQ addresses the severe quantization error in down-projection matrices below 4 bits. It introduces an auxiliary column-wise scaling factor `sc` directly into the down-projection’s quantization objective: `min ||W_down - Q(W_down) ⊙ sc||²_F`. This scale is optimized iteratively and then perfectly absorbed into the per-channel scales of the preceding up-projection, effectively smoothing the down-projection’s distribution without extra bit cost. The core assumption is that the equivalent transformation between up- and down-projections allows the up-projection’s quantization difficulty to remain unchanged while the down-projection’s error is reduced. If absorrability fails (e.g., in architectures without a preceding linear layer), the benefit is lost.

### Mechanism 2: Deviation-Aware Correction (DAC)
Quantizing the attention module introduces a pronounced per-channel mean shift in the activations at the subsequent LayerNorm. DAC estimates a bias correction term `µ` by computing the mean deviation between full-precision and quantized post-attention LayerNorm outputs over the calibration set. Adding `µ` (`Y_aligned = Y_q + µ`) during inference eliminates this dominant error component. Theoretical analysis shows the error reduction is `SNR/(1+SNR)` per channel. The method assumes the deviation is primarily a mean shift (high SNR); if other distribution changes dominate (low SNR), bias correction is ineffective.

### Mechanism 3: Pipeline Integration
D²Quant applies DSQ and DAC sequentially within each block. DAC corrects activation drift immediately after attention quantization, before the MLP processes the signal. DSQ then optimizes the down-projection weights, with its scale absorbed upstream. This tight coupling ensures that weight errors (DSQ) and activation errors (DAC) are addressed in concert. Ablation results show additive improvements, confirming complementarity. The pipeline assumes per-block effects are approximately independent; non-local activation drift in very deep models could limit DAC’s local correction.

## Foundational Learning
- **Per-Channel Quantization in LLMs:** Each output channel has its own scale `s` and zero-point `z`, broadcasting along the input dimension to reconstruct `cW = (W - z) ⊙ s`. DSQ extends this by adding a column-scale that is later merged.
- **Transformer Block Structure:** Standard decoder layers follow `Attention → PostAttnLN → MLP (Up/Gate → Down) → PreLN`. DSQ targets the MLP down-projection; DAC targets the LayerNorm right after attention.
- **Calibration in Post-Training Quantization:** A small dataset (e.g., 128 WikiText2 samples) is used to compute quantization parameters (scales, biases, corrections). Both DSQ’s `sc` and DAC’s `µ` rely on calibration data statistics.

## Architecture Onboarding
Input -> Pre-trained FP16 model + calibration set X -> [Block-wise loop: Quantize Attn -> DAC on PostAttnLN -> Quantize Up/Gate -> DSQ on Down -> Merge scale -> Forward update] -> Quantized model.

Critical path:  
1. Iterate blocks sequentially; each block’s quantization affects the next block’s calibration data.  
2. For each block: must finish attention quantization and collect its activations before applying DAC.  
3. DSQ’s iterative optimization runs on the down-projection, then immediately merges `sc` into the up-projection’s per-channel scales.

Design tradeoffs:  
- **DSQ iterations vs. time:** More iterations improve `sc` convergence (saturates ~15) but increase quantization time linearly.  
- **Calibration set size for DAC:** Larger sets give more reliable `µ` (optimal ~128), but increase memory/time.  
- **Absorability constraint:** DSQ’s benefit depends on merging `sc` into up-projection; fails in architectures without an absorbable predecessor.

Failure signatures:  
- **DAC overfitting:** Unrepresentative calibration data yields noisy `µ`, degrading accuracy (seen with 16 samples).  
- **DSQ divergence:** Poorly converged `sc` amplifies down-projection error, increasing perplexity.  
- **Pipeline bleed:** Omitting the forward pass update after each block causes calibration misalignment accumulation.

First 3 experiments:  
1. **Baseline replication:** Quantize Qwen-3-8B with GPTQ at 2-bit; confirm WikiText2 perplexity >20 and Avg Acc ~54.  
2. **Component ablation:** Apply `+DSQ` only and `+DAC` only; verify individual gains (e.g., DSQ improves MMLU, DAC improves Avg Acc).  
3. **Calibration sensitivity:** Test DAC with [16, 32, 64, 128, 256] samples; validate 128 is optimal and detect overfitting/underfitting.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- **Extending DSQ to QKV and up/gate projections:** DSQ may extend to other projections, but all branches must share a single column scale, imposing design constraints; extending under these constraints is future work.
- **General activation correction beyond mean shift:** Other distribution shifts (e.g., variance, higher moments) may exist; identifying patterns and designing general corrections could improve robustness.
- **Calibration sensitivity across models and bit-widths:** The paper uses a fixed 128-sample WikiText2 set; the robustness of D²Quant to calibration set size, domain, and model architecture is unexplored.

## Limitations
- **DSQ absorbability:** Relies on preceding up-projection’s structure; may fail in MoE or non-standard architectures without an absorbable layer.
- **DAC mean-shift assumption:** Assumes dominant mean shift at post-attention LayerNorm (high SNR); low SNR reduces correction efficacy and may introduce distortions.
- **Pipeline complementarity:** Assumes per-block error independence; non-local activation drift in very deep models could limit DAC’s local fixing.
