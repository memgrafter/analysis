# Mechanistic Interpretability of Large-Scale Counting in LLMs through a System-2 Strategy

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID**: 2601.02989
- **Source URL**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.02989
- **Reference count**: 21
- **Primary result**: A test-time System-2 strategy combining structured partitioning (with "|" separators) and Chain-of-Thought prompting enables LLMs to count long lists accurately by decomposing the task into sub-problems, revealing mechanistic encoding at partition boundaries and transfer via dedicated attention heads.

## Executive Summary
Large language models (LLMs) struggle with counting long sequences due to inherent architectural limitations, specifically the degradation of implicit counting as sequence length increases. This paper demonstrates that a test-time intervention—structuring the input with separators and requiring intermediate reasoning steps—allows models to surpass their native counting capacity. The approach partitions a long list into smaller chunks, each counted implicitly, then aggregates the partial results. Across diverse models (Qwen2.5, Llama, Gemma, GPT-4o, Gemini), accuracy improves dramatically, with Gemma 3 27B going from 0% to 50% on 41–50 items. Mechanistic probing and causal mediation analyses show that counts are encoded at partition boundaries, transferred to generated reasoning tokens through specific middle-to-late attention heads, and finally summed. Activation patching confirms the causal role of these pathways, all without parameter modifications.

## Method Summary
The study evaluates counting performance on lists of repeated items (e.g., fruits, animals) of lengths 11–100, comparing four conditions: (1) unstructured input without Chain-of-Thought (CoT), (2) unstructured with CoT, (3) structured with separators but no CoT, and (4) structured with CoT. Structured inputs are partitioned into sub-lists of model-specific sizes (6–9 for open-source models, 15–25 for closed-source models). Models are prompted with fixed templates to generate either a direct answer or intermediate steps (e.g., "part1: X", "part2: Y", "Final answer: Z"). Behavioral metrics (exact-match, mean absolute error) are computed per length bucket. Mechanistic analyses include CountScope probing to locate count representations, attention visualization, layer/head ablations (zero masking), and cross-context activation patching to establish causality.

## Key Results
- LLMs systematically fail at counting long lists without decomposition, with accuracy dropping sharply as item count grows.
- The combination of structured partitioning and CoT yields substantial gains (e.g., Gemma 3 27B: 0% → 50% on 41–50 items; Llama 3.2 8B: 5% → 42% on 11–20 items), while either technique alone provides limited improvement.
- Mechanistically, partition counts are encoded in the residual stream at the final item and separator tokens, transferred to intermediate reasoning tokens via concentrated attention heads in middle-to-late layers (e.g., layer 22 head 13 in Qwen2.5 7B), and aggregated to produce the final answer; activation patching swaps final sums when intermediate representations are swapped, confirming causality.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1
**Claim**: LLMs encode the count of a partition at its final item and separator tokens.  
**Mechanism**: As the model processes a sequence of identical items, it incrementally accumulates a count in the residual stream. At the partition boundary (marked by a separator), the count becomes locally represented at the last item token and the separator token.  
**Core assumption**: The model's implicit counter has limited capacity; within a partition of manageable size, the count is accurately encoded.  
**Evidence anchors**: abstract (latent counts stored in final item representations); section 5.1 (CountScope probing shows high ground-truth probability at final items and separators).  
**Break condition**: If partition size exceeds the model's reliable counting range, the encoding becomes inaccurate or lost.

### Mechanism 2
**Claim**: Partition counts are transferred to intermediate reasoning tokens via dedicated attention heads in middle-to-late layers.  
**Mechanism**: When generating "part X: N", the model attends to the corresponding partition's boundary tokens. Specific heads (e.g., layer 22 head 13) mediate this transfer.  
**Core assumption**: Intermediate reasoning tokens serve as explicit containers for partial results, enabling later aggregation.  
**Evidence anchors**: abstract (transferred via dedicated attention heads concentrated in middle-to-late layers); section 4 (attention heatmaps show peaks from reasoning tokens to final items/separators in layers 19–23); section 5.3 (attention knockout of head 13 in layer 22 reduces accuracy for intermediate counts).  
**Break condition**: Disrupting these attention heads impairs transfer, causing incorrect intermediate numbers.

### Mechanism 3
**Claim**: The final count is aggregated from intermediate reasoning tokens via attention pathways.  
**Mechanism**: During final answer generation, the model attends to the numbers in the intermediate steps (e.g., "4", "2", "3") and sums them.  
**Core assumption**: The aggregation process itself is reliable because the numbers are small and within the model's arithmetic capability.  
**Evidence anchors**: abstract (aggregated to produce the final answer); section 4 (attention from final answer tokens to intermediate reasoning tokens); section 5.4 (cross-context activation patching: swapping intermediate representations swaps the final sum).  
**Break condition**: If intermediate reasoning tokens are absent or corrupted, aggregation fails.

## Architecture Onboarding
#### Component map
Input preprocessor -> Prompt template -> Transformer model -> Analysis toolkit

#### Critical path
1. Partitioned input tokens are processed; count encoded at partition boundaries.  
2. During intermediate step generation, attention heads transfer counts to reasoning tokens.  
3. Final answer generation aggregates from reasoning tokens.  
Key checkpoints: boundary token representations, intermediate reasoning tokens, final answer.

#### Design tradeoffs
- Partition size vs. number of partitions: Larger partitions reduce steps but risk exceeding counting capacity; smaller partitions increase steps but more reliable. Choose based on model's known counting limit.
- Model depth: Deeper models can handle larger partitions; adjust partition size accordingly.
- Computational cost: More partitions mean longer context and more generation steps; balance accuracy and latency.

#### Failure signatures
- Behavioral: Accuracy drops when partitions are too large or when CoT is disabled.
- Attention: Lack of focused attention on partition boundaries during intermediate generation.
- Causal: Zero ablation of boundary tokens or knockout of key heads has minimal effect (suggesting alternative pathways or failure).
- Activation patching: No transfer of counts between contexts when patching wrong tokens/layers.

#### First 3 experiments
1. **Behavioral validation**: Run counting tasks with varying partition sizes and CoT settings; confirm accuracy improvements from structured+CoT.
2. **Attention localization**: For a fixed configuration, compute average attention from reasoning tokens to input tokens; identify layers/heads with peak attention to partition boundaries.
3. **Causal verification**: Perform zero ablation on partition boundary tokens and attention knockout on candidate heads; measure drop in intermediate and final accuracy.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
### Open Question 1
**Question**: Does the System-2 test-time strategy generalize to other reasoning tasks beyond counting (e.g., arithmetic, logical deduction)?  
**Basis**: The conclusion states: "While our experiments focus on counting, the same analysis framework applies to other reasoning tasks where implicit representations saturate and explicit decomposition enables correct behavior." However, no other tasks were empirically tested.  
**Why unresolved**: The authors provided no experimental evidence for other domains; the claim remains hypothetical.  
**What evidence would resolve it**: Evaluate the partitioning and CoT approach on multi-step reasoning benchmarks (e.g., mathematical word problems, symbolic logic), then perform attention and causal mediation analyses to check for similar mechanistic patterns.

### Open Question 2
**Question**: Are the specific attention heads identified (e.g., layer 22 head 13 in Qwen2.5 7B) responsible for count transfer consistently across different model architectures and scales?  
**Basis**: Mechanistic analysis (attention maps, knockout, patching) was performed only on Qwen2.5 7B; for other models (Llama, Gemma, GPT-4o, Gemini) only behavioral results are reported.  
**Why unresolved**: It is unclear whether the same circuit architecture exists in other models or if different layers/heads mediate System-2 counting.  
**What evidence would resolve it**: Replicate the full suite of causal mediation experiments (layer masking, attention knockout, cross-context patching) on other models, then compare the critical layers and heads.

### Open Question 3
**Question**: Can the partition size in the System-2 strategy be automatically adapted to each model’s reliable counting range without manual specification?  
**Basis**: The limitations section notes that the approach "assumes prior knowledge of the model’s reliable counting range," and partition sizes were manually fixed (6–9 or 15–25 items) based on pilot tests.  
**Why unresolved**: Manual tuning limits applicability to new models or contexts; an adaptive mechanism would improve robustness.  
**What evidence would resolve it**: Develop a method to estimate on-the-fly the maximum sub-list size a model can count accurately (e.g., by monitoring confidence or consistency of partial counts), then dynamically adjust partition boundaries; validate across diverse models and context lengths.

## Limitations
- Partition size selection lacks systematic capacity probing; optimal sizes may vary with model depth and training.
- Tokenization of items into multiple tokens could weaken boundary representations, an effect not controlled.
- Identified attention heads might be model- or seed-specific; generalization unclear.

## Confidence
- Boundary encoding: High
- Transfer via heads: Medium
- Aggregation: High
