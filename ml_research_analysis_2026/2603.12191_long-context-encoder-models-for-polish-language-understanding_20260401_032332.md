# Long-Context Encoder Models for Polish Language Understanding

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID**: 2603.12191
- **Source URL**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.12191
- **Reference Count**: 39
- **Primary Result**: Introduced `polish-roberta-8k`, a Polish RoBERTa model extended to 8192 tokens, achieving state-of-the-art average (85.93%) across 25 tasks and significantly outperforming on long-text benchmarks.

## Executive Summary
Standard Polish encoder models like BERT and RoBERTa are constrained by a 512-token context window, which is insufficient for processing long documents common in legal, financial, and literary domains. This paper addresses this gap by presenting `polish-roberta-8k`, a model that extends the context to 8192 tokens through a carefully designed two-stage training procedure, architectural efficiency improvements (Flash Attention, contamination-free packing), and subsequent knowledge distillation to produce lighter variants. Evaluated on a comprehensive suite of 25 Polish NLP tasks, the 8k model sets a new state-of-the-art average score while demonstrating a pronounced advantage on long-context tasks without sacrificing performance on shorter ones.

## Method Summary
The authors start from `polish-roberta-large-v2` and extend its absolute positional embeddings from 512 to 8192 dimensions. Training proceeds in two stages on a 150B-token Polish corpus: first, only the new positional embeddings are trained for one epoch while all other weights are frozen, preventing catastrophic forgetting. Second, all parameters are trained for four epochs with a 1M-token batch size, leveraging Flash Attention for memory efficiency and a contamination-free packing strategy that blocks attention between different documents within a packed sequence to maintain data integrity. The final model is an average of checkpoints from epochs 2–4. Compressed variants (12-layer, 6-layer) are created via knowledge distillation, using mean squared error (MSE) loss on final-layer token representations, with a progressive distillation path (24L → 12L → 6L) yielding the best small model.

## Key Results
- `polish-roberta-8k` achieves a state-of-the-art average score of **85.93%** across 25 diverse Polish tasks.
- On long-text tasks (e.g., Banking-Long), it significantly outperforms all Polish and multilingual baselines, scoring **88.35%**.
- Distilled models (especially the progressive 6-layer variant) surpass other similarly-sized models and compete with full-sized baselines.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1: Two-Stage Training Prevents Forgetting
The claim is that freezing all weights while training only the new positional embeddings (Stage 1) allows the extended model to adapt to long sequences without destabilizing the pre-trained representations. Only after positional alignment is the full model unfrozen for continued pretraining (Stage 2). Evidence is found in the paper's description of gradient fluctuations when full training is attempted immediately and the successful maintenance of short-text performance. A break condition would be degraded short-text validation loss after Stage 1 or an inability to recover long-context capability in Stage 2.

### Mechanism 2: Efficiency Modifications Enable Feasible Training
Flash Attention and contamination-free packing are claimed necessary for computationally feasible and data-efficient pretraining on 8192-token sequences. Flash Attention reduces memory I/O, while contamination-free packing (blocking attention across packed document boundaries) prevents the model from learning spurious cross-document correlations during MLM. The core assumption is that naive packing corrupts the learning signal. Evidence includes the paper's implementation details and the reported ability to maintain short-task performance. A break condition would be no validation loss decrease with packing or a post-training drop on short tasks versus a non-packed baseline.

### Mechanism 3: Progressive Distillation Transfers Long-Context Knowledge
Knowledge distillation via MSE on final-layer token representations is claimed to effectively compress the 24-layer teacher into 12-layer and 6-layer students. Progressive distillation (first to 12L, then to 6L) is superior to direct distillation (24L → 6L), likely due to a warmer start and more compatible representation space. Evidence is Table 3, showing the 6L-progressive model outperforming the 6L-simple variant. A break condition would be a >5% absolute performance drop on key tasks for the 6L model compared to the 12L and base models.

## Foundational Learning
- **Concept: Positional Embedding Scaling & Adaptation**
  - **Why needed here**: The base model's absolute position embeddings are fixed to 512 positions. Extending to 8192 requires initializing and learning 7680 new embeddings so the model understands longer-range dependencies.
  - **Quick check**: When training only positional embeddings on long sequences, the gradient signal comes from the MLM loss, which depends on the model's attention patterns influenced by positions. This updates position vectors without altering token embeddings or layer weights, preserving short-text knowledge.

- **Concept: Contamination-Free Packing (Causal Masking per Document)**
  - **Why needed here**: Standard packing concatenates multiple documents into one sequence, allowing attention to leak between unrelated texts during pretraining. This teaches a pattern absent at inference time and harms model quality.
  - **Quick check**: For a packed sequence of two documents (lengths L1, L2), the attention mask would be a block-diagonal matrix: a block of ones for positions [0, L1-1], zeros elsewhere; and a block of ones for positions [L1, L1+L2-1]. Token at position L1-1 (end of doc1) cannot attend to position L1 (start of doc2).

- **Concept: Knowledge Distillation for Encoder Compression**
  - **Why needed here**: Removing encoder layers reduces compute but degrades representation quality. Distillation uses the teacher's final-layer token embeddings as a target to train the student, transferring its long-context representation ability.
  - **Quick check**: Distilling attention patterns is O(N²) in sequence length, infeasible for 8192 tokens (67M entries). MSE on final-layer embeddings is O(N*d), feasible (8192 * 768 ≈ 6.3M entries), and captures contextualized output quality.

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map**:
  Polish Corpus -> Tokenizer -> Document Packing (with boundary IDs) -> Contamination-Free Attention Mask -> Model (Extended Pos. Embeddings + Flash Attention Layers) -> MLM Head -> Loss. For distillation: Teacher Model -> Student Model (fewer layers) -> MSE Loss between final-layer token embeddings.
- **Critical path**:
  1. Modify `polish-roberta-large-v2` to support 8192 positional embeddings (random init). Implement Flash Attention and packing with per-document attention masks.
  2. Stage 1: Freeze all weights except new pos. embeddings. Train 1 epoch on 150B corpus (LR=2e-5, batch=128x8192).
  3. Stage 2: Unfreeze all. Train 4 more epochs with same hyperparams. Save checkpoints after epochs 2,3,4.
  4. Merge weights from epochs 2-4 checkpoints (method unspecified) to create final 8k model.
  5. Distillation: For 12L, remove every other layer from 24L teacher, train 1 epoch (MSE loss, LR=5e-5, batch=64). For progressive 6L: first distill 24L->12L, then 12L->6L.
- **Design tradeoffs**:
  - *Pos. embedding init*: Random (used) vs. interpolated from 512. Random is simpler but may require longer Stage 1; interpolated might speed convergence but risk poor scaling.
  - *Packing*: Larger batches (128x8192) improve stability but consume memory; packing is essential to utilize capacity without discarding short docs.
  - *Distillation objective*: MSE on final layer is feasible for long sequences; attention/ intermediate layer distillation is richer but O(N²) or memory-prohibitive.
  - *Checkpoint merging*: Simple averaging of epochs 2-4 is used. Could instead weight by validation scores on short vs. long tasks for better balance.
- **Failure signatures**:
  - Stage 1: Significant degradation in short-task validation loss during Stage 1, indicating positional updates destabilize base layers.
  - Post-training: Long-task scores (e.g., Banking-Long) remain at baseline 512-token levels, suggesting context extension failed—likely positional embeddings didn't learn.
  - Distillation: 6L model performance drops >5% absolute on both short and long tasks vs. 12L, and is worse than `polish-roberta-base-v2`, indicating insufficient distillation signal.
  - Packing bug: Incorrect mask (e.g., all-to-all attention) may cause artificial long-task improvement during pretraining but crash on real long docs.
- **First 3 experiments**:
  1. *Positional embedding sanity*: After Stage 1, extract position embeddings for 8192 slots. Compute pairwise cosine similarities. Expect smooth decay with distance; chaotic patterns indicate unstable adaptation.
  2. *Packing mask integrity*: Pack two synthetic docs (50, 70 tokens) into one 120-token sequence. Programmatically verify token 60 (last of doc1) has zero attention to token 61 (first of doc2), while intra-doc attention is fully enabled.
  3. *Distillation efficacy*: On 10k-sample subset, compute MSE between teacher (8k) and student (6L simple/progressive) final-layer embeddings. Expect progressive MSE < simple MSE. Also, linearly probe both students on Banking-Long: progressive should converge faster/higher.

## Limitations
- **Corpus specifics undisclosed**: The exact composition, cleaning, and tokenization of the 150B-token pre-training corpus are not provided, hindering exact replication and raising contamination concerns.
- **Checkpoint merging ambiguity**: The algorithm for merging epochs 2–4 checkpoints (e.g., averaging, weighting) is unspecified, creating uncertainty in the final model's short/long performance balance.
- **Ablation gaps absent**: No controlled experiments isolate the individual contribution of contamination-free packing versus Flash Attention, nor compare the two-stage training against alternative positional initialization schemes.

## Confidence
- **SOTA performance (85.93% avg)**: High – Direct evaluation on 25 tasks with reported numbers; observable result.
- **Two-stage training prevents forgetting**: Medium – Supported by gradient stability notes and analogous work (e.g., E2LLM), but lacks direct ablation vs. single-stage or different warm-up.
- **Architectural mods feasibility**: Medium – Flash Attention and packing are described; neighboring work confirms efficiency focus, but no benchmark comparing packed vs. naive packing on memory/throughput is provided.
- **Progressive distillation superiority**: High – Table 3 directly shows 6L-progressive > 6L-simple and competitiveness with base models.

## Next Checks
1. **Positional embedding sanity**: After Stage 1, extract position embeddings and compute a cosine similarity matrix. Expect a smooth decay with increasing distance between positions; absence of cliffs or noise indicates stable adaptation.
2. **Packing mask integrity**: Programmatically construct a packed batch with two known-length documents. Assert that the attention mask block-diagonal structure is correct, with no cross-document attention links.
3. **Distillation transfer efficacy**: On a small held-out corpus subset, calculate MSE between teacher and student final-layer embeddings. Confirm progressive distillation yields lower MSE. Additionally, run a linear probe on a long task (e.g., Banking-Long) to verify progressive student converges faster and to a higher accuracy than the simple student.
