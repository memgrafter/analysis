# AnCoder: Anchored Code Generation via Discrete Diffusion Models

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2602.17688  
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.17688  
- **Reference count:** 40  
- **Primary result:** AnchorTree improves syntax validity (HumanEval: 65.58%→74.72%, MBPP: 74.46%→80.12%) and Pass@1 (HumanEval: 3.29%→5.45%, MBPP: 6.34%→9.10%) in 809M-parameter diffusion models with 2048 denoising steps.

## Executive Summary
AnCoder tackles a key weakness of diffusion language models for code generation: their tendency to produce syntactically invalid programs by ignoring code’s rigid hierarchical structure. It introduces AnchorTree, a soft anchoring framework that leverages abstract syntax tree (AST) hierarchies to prioritize the denoising of syntactically and semantically salient tokens—keywords and identifiers—based on their depth in the AST, with tokens closer to the root receiving higher weights. This establishes a structural scaffold that guides the diffusion process. AnCoder uses a two-stage architecture where a large anchor network predicts anchor tokens, and a smaller denoiser network generates the full code conditioned on these anchors. Trained on code corpora via a modified diffusion objective (ANELBO), AnCoder significantly outperforms non-anchored diffusion baselines on HumanEval and MBPP, showing higher syntax validity and functional correctness. It also scales better with more denoising steps and beats hard anchoring strategies, demonstrating the efficacy of hierarchical soft anchoring for high-quality, parameter-efficient code generation.

## Method Summary
AnCoder is an anchored discrete diffusion model with a two-stage architecture. The anchor network (θᴬ, 24 layers, 494M parameters) predicts token distributions to identify anchor tokens (keywords and identifiers). Each token’s anchor weight µ(l) = ω(l)·η(l), where ω(l) indicates if the token is a keyword/identifier, and η(l) = γ·exp(−β·max(depth(l)−d₀,0)) decays exponentially with AST depth. The anchor network’s output probabilities are converted into soft embeddings (by multiplying with the embedding matrix) and added to the denoiser network’s (θᴰ, 12 layers, 315M parameters) token embeddings. Both networks are initialized from Qwen2.5-Coder-0.5B, with θᴬ taking the full model and θᴰ the first 12 layers. Training minimizes the ANELBO loss: a weighted sum of the standard diffusion denoising loss (over all tokens) and an auxiliary anchor loss (weighted by µ(l)) that supervises θᴬ to recover anchor tokens early. The model is trained in three stages on OpenCoder datasets with a cosine noise schedule. During inference, starting from a fully masked sequence (after the prompt), the model iteratively denoises for T steps (e.g., 2048), sampling from the denoiser’s logits for masked positions, and randomly remasking a fraction (0.1) of previously unmasked tokens each step to aid exploration.

## Key Results
- **Syntax validity** increases substantially: HumanEval from 65.58% to 74.72%, MBPP from 74.46% to 80.12% (vs. MDLM baseline).
- **Pass@1** improves: HumanEval from 3.29% to 5.45%, MBPP from 6.34% to 9.10% (809M model, 2048 denoising steps).
- AnchorTree **scales better** with more denoising steps and **outperforms hard anchoring** strategies.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

**Mechanism 1: Hierarchical soft anchoring via AST depth improves denoising order**  
AnchorTree assigns per-token weights µ(l)=ω(l)·η(l), where η(l) decays exponentially with AST depth. This biases the anchor network to unmask root-proximal tokens (keywords/identifiers) earlier, establishing a structural scaffold. The denoiser then conditions on these salient anchors, allowing subsequent tokens to be generated in a globally consistent context. The core assumption is that tokens higher in the AST hierarchy are more critical for establishing syntactico-semantic scaffolding. Evidence includes the abstract’s claim that AnchorTree prioritizes syntactically and semantically salient tokens, and Section 4.2’s experiment showing unmasking AST ancestors improves token prediction probability most when closer ancestors are revealed first (in-out order). If AST depth does not correlate with token importance (e.g., in obfuscated code), the weighting may mis-prioritize anchors.

**Mechanism 2: Two-stage anchor-denoiser architecture decouples hierarchical scaffold recovery from contextual detail generation**  
The model decomposes the denoising prediction: x_θ(zt) = x_θD(y_θA(zt)). The anchor network y_θA is trained with auxiliary supervision (via anchor term in ANELBO) to recover anchor tokens early, while the denoiser x_θD operates on the anchor-augmented context. Parameter partitioning (494M vs 315M) enables specialization. The core assumption is that separating the tasks of identifying structural skeleton and filling details yields better performance, especially under limited data/compute. Evidence includes the abstract’s parameter count details and Section 3.2’s formal ANELBO definition. If anchor tokens are poorly chosen or the separation creates an information bottleneck, a monolithic jointly trained model might perform better.

**Mechanism 3: AnchorTree enables more effective test-time scaling (more denoising steps T)**  
The anchored scaffold established early reduces uncertainty in the denoising trajectory. Each subsequent step has a stronger conditioned context, so iterative refinement converges more reliably. The remask rate (0.1) aids exploration around anchors. The hierarchy ensures coarse structure is locked before fine details, preventing early corruptions from propagating. The core assumption is that diffusion quality scales with T when the process is well-guided. Evidence includes the abstract’s claim about better scaling with T, and Tables 2 & 3 showing larger absolute gains at T=2048 than at T=256. If the anchor weight (γ) is too high, the scaffold becomes overly rigid, preventing necessary refinements and yielding diminishing returns with more steps.

## Foundational Learning

**Concept:** Abstract Syntax Tree (AST) Hierarchies  
Why needed: AnchorTree’s depth-based weight η(l) explicitly depends on AST depth. Understanding how code is parsed into a tree with nested nodes (e.g., function calls inside assignments inside loops) is essential to grasp why root-proximal tokens are prioritized.  
Quick check: For `def f(): print(x)`, the shallowest depth tokens are `def` (keyword at root) and possibly `f` (function name as identifier under the function definition node). The token `x` (inside `print`) would have greater depth.

**Concept:** Discrete Diffusion Language Models (Masked Diffusion)  
Why needed: The entire framework is built on masked diffusion: forward noising (gradual masking), reverse denoising (iterative unmasking), training with NELBO/ANELBO, and the role of the noise schedule.  
Quick check: In the reverse process, if a position `l` has `z_l^t ≠ m` (already unmasked), it is carried over unchanged; if `z_l^t = m` (masked), it is resampled from a categorical distribution predicted by the model.

**Concept:** Two-Stage Anchor-Denoiser Decomposition  
Why needed: This architectural choice is central to AnCoder. The anchor network generates an intermediate context that conditions the denoiser. Understanding the ANELBO loss, carry-over unmasking, and parameter partitioning is necessary to implement or modify the design.  
Quick check: In Equation (8), `x_θ(zt) = x_θD(y_θA(zt))`. If `y_θA` were perfect (exactly recovers all anchor tokens), `x_θD` would ideally only need to predict non-anchor tokens correctly, with anchor positions fixed to their true values.

## Architecture Onboarding

**Component map:**  
AST Parser & Depth Calculator → Anchor Label Generator → Two-Stage Diffusion Model (AnchorNet & DenoiserNet) → Training Loop (ANELBO) → Inference Engine (iterative denoising with remask)

**Critical path:**  
1. Data preparation: collect code snippets.  
2. AST parsing & anchor weight assignment: parse each program, map tokens to AST nodes, compute depth(l), set ω(l) for keywords/identifiers, compute µ(l).  
3. Initialize backbone & split: load Qwen2.5-Coder-0.5B; create 24-layer AnchorNet with full weights; create 12-layer DenoiserNet with first 12 layers plus own embedding/LM head.  
4. Train with ANELBO loss: for each batch, sample t, compute forward noising, run AnchorNet → soft embeddings → DenoiserNet, compute weighted NELBO + anchor term, backpropagate.  
5. Inference: start from fully masked sequence after prompt; for T steps, forward through both networks, sample new tokens for masked positions, remask 10% of previous unmasks (excluding prompt).  
6. Evaluation: generate multiple samples, compute Pass@1 and syntax validity.

**Design tradeoffs:**  
- Hard vs. Soft Anchoring: Hard (binary ω) is simpler but loses granularity; soft (depth-weighted η) captures nuance but introduces hyperparameters (β, d₀, γ).  
- Parameter Partitioning: Making θᴬ larger (494M) gives it capacity to learn anchor patterns but may leave θᴰ smaller and underpowered; alternative splits (equal size, residual connections) are untested.  
- Remask Strategy: AnCoder uses fixed 0.1 remask; adaptive schedules based on anchor confidence might further improve scaling.

**Failure signatures:**  
- Low syntax validity (<70% on HumanEval): Anchor weights too low (γ), poor keyword/identifier coverage (ω), or AST parser failures on complex code.  
- Low Pass@1 despite good syntax: Anchor scaffold too rigid (excessive γ) causing semantically incorrect but syntactically valid programs; or θᴰ underpowered relative to θᴬ.  
- No scaling with T: Remask rate not tuned, or anchor network failing to provide useful conditioning (e.g., θᴬ not properly trained).

**First 3 experiments:**  
1. Replicate AST Ancestry Benefit: On a pretrained DLM, test unmasking in-out vs. out-in on a held-out code set. Verify that closer AST ancestors improve token prediction more.  
2. Anchor Weight Ablation: Train minimal AnCoder variants (e.g., 200M total params) with γ ∈ {0.001, 0.01, 0.1} on a small Python subset. Measure syntax and Pass@1 to calibrate γ.  
3. Component Isolation: Train ablation models: (a) monolithic network (no split), (b) hard anchoring (β=0), (c) no anchor loss (γ=0). Compare against full AnchorTree to quantify contribution of soft hierarchy vs. architecture.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
1. Which anchor tokens and depth-weight functions maximize code generation quality? The paper tested only keywords+identifiers with exponential decay; alternatives (e.g., control-flow only, linear decay) may be more effective.
2. How do different anchor network architectures (e.g., residual connections, weight sharing) affect performance? The design space of anchored diffusion remains nascent.
3. Can AnchorTree incorporate dynamic execution information (e.g., control-flow graphs) to reason about runtime behavior? The current method relies solely on static AST hierarchies.

## Limitations
- **Anchor integration ambiguity:** The exact method of feeding anchor network output into the denoiser (soft embeddings assumed) is unspecified.
- **AST-to-token mapping underspecified:** Alignment algorithm for subword tokens and AST node spans is unclear, affecting depth assignment.
- **Training details incomplete:** Diffusion step count T, optimizer hyperparameters (β₁/β₂, weight decay, clipping), precise loss weighting (whether anchor term multiplied by λᵢ), and handling of padding tokens are not fully described.
- **Dataset sourcing uncertainty:** Exact sources and preprocessing of OpenCoder corpora are not provided, hindering exact replication.

## Confidence
- **Performance superiority (Pass@1, syntax validity):** Claim confidence High (results are clear), replication confidence Medium (missing training details).
- **Hierarchical soft anchoring improves denoising:** Claim confidence Medium (ablations support it), replication confidence Medium (AST mapping and weight function sensitivity unknown).
- **Two-stage architecture decouples tasks:** Claim confidence Medium (ablations show gains), replication confidence Medium (isolated effect of parameter split vs. anchoring not cleanly separated).
- **Better scaling with more denoising steps:** Claim confidence Medium (empirical improvement shown), replication confidence Medium (depends on remask implementation and scaffold rigidity).

## Next Checks
1. **AST ancestry replication:** On a held-out code set, train a simple masked LM and evaluate token prediction when unmasking AST ancestors in root-first, random, and leaf-first orders. Expect root-first to yield highest accuracy, confirming hierarchical importance.
2. **Anchor hyperparameter sweep:** Train small-scale AnCoder variants (∼100M params) on a Python subset, varying γ (anchor weight), β (depth decay), and d₀ (depth offset). Measure syntax validity and Pass@1 to verify the paper’s chosen values are near-optimal.
3. **Architecture ablation:** Compare full AnchorTree against (a) a monolithic model with same total parameters, (b) a two-stage model with hard anchoring (binary ω, no decay), and (c) a two-stage model with random anchor weights. Isolate the contribution of soft hierarchical anchoring from mere architectural separation.
