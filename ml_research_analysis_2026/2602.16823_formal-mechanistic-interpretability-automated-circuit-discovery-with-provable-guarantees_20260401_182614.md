# Formal Mechanistic Interpretability: Automated Circuit Discovery with Provable Guarantees

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2602.16823
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.16823
- **Reference count:** 40
- **Primary result:** A framework for automated circuit discovery using neural network verification to produce circuits with provable guarantees (100% certified robustness vs. 19–46% for sampling-based methods).

## Executive Summary
This paper presents a rigorous framework for automated circuit discovery in mechanistic interpretability, leveraging neural network verification to yield circuits with formal robustness guarantees. By encoding circuits within a siamese network architecture, the authors enable certification of both input-domain and patching-domain robustness. The work formalizes four notions of minimality (quasi, local, subset, cardinal) and uncovers theoretical connections: under a monotonicity condition, a greedy algorithm converges to a subset‑minimal circuit, while a duality with blocking sets enables approximation of cardinally minimal circuits. Empirically, using α‑β‑CROWN on vision models, the provable approach achieves perfect certified robustness compared to sampling baselines, albeit at higher computational cost. The results provide a principled foundation for future provable circuit discovery research.

## Method Summary
The method transforms circuit discovery into a series of neural network verification queries. Given a trained model and a set of candidate components (e.g., neurons), the framework constructs a siamese network that compares the full model against a candidate circuit under specified robustness conditions (input‑domain or patching‑domain). A faithfulness predicate Φ(C) is defined as a bound on the output norm difference between the two branches. The discovery algorithms (greedy elimination, binary search, minimum hitting set) iteratively test component removals or subsets via the verifier, guided by theoretical minimality guarantees. The approach is implementation‑agnostic but relies on a sound verification backend (e.g., α‑β‑CROWN) and assumes monotonicity of Φ for certain minimality results.

## Key Results
- **Certified robustness:** Provable discovery yields 100% certified robustness across benchmarks, while sampling‑based baselines achieve only 19–46%.
- **Circuit size:** Provably discovered circuits are comparable in size to those from sampling methods on CIFAR‑10, MNIST, GTSRB, and TaxiNet.
- **Computational cost:** Provable verification incurs hundreds to thousands of seconds per circuit, versus sub‑second for sampling, but eliminates the risk of infinitesimal perturbations breaking faithfulness.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- **Claim:** Siamese network encoding reduces circuit robustness certification over continuous domains to a standard neural network verification problem.
- **Mechanism:** The full model and a candidate circuit are stacked in a siamese architecture. For input robustness, the circuit branch shares the same input as the full model and patches non‑circuit nodes with a constant; for patching robustness, the circuit branch receives the base input while a separate patching branch supplies replaced activations. The robustness predicate becomes an output norm bound on the two branches.
- **Core assumption:** The faithfulness predicate can be expressed as a bound on an output norm; the verifier supports the resulting siamese network (typically piecewise‑linear after relaxation).
- **Evidence anchors:** [abstract] Encoding within siamese architecture; [section] Sections 3.1 and 3.2 detail encodings; [corpus] Neighboring papers do not describe siamese verification, suggesting novelty.
- **Break condition:** Verification times out or the siamese network exceeds the verifier’s capacity (memory/layer limits).

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim:** When the faithfulness predicate is monotonic, greedy backward elimination converges to a subset‑minimal circuit.
- **Mechanism:** The algorithm iterates over components, removing a component if the predicate still holds. Monotonicity guarantees that if a component cannot be removed at some stage, then every subset that excludes it would have been considered earlier and failed; thus no proper subgraph remains faithful.
- **Core assumption:** Φ is monotonic (adding components never breaks faithfulness). For robustness predicates, this holds when the patching domain contains the input domain and activation spaces are closed under concatenation.
- **Evidence anchors:** [abstract] Theoretical connections under monotonicity; [section] Section 4.3 defines monotonicity and proves Proposition 4 (greedy → subset‑minimal) and Proposition 5 (monotonicity conditions for robustness).
- **Break condition:** Φ fails monotonicity (e.g., discrete non‑continuous patching), greedy may reach a non‑minimal circuit.

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim:** Under monotonic Φ, the minimum hitting set (MHS) of circuit blocking sets yields (or tightly approximates) a cardinally minimal circuit.
- **Mechanism:** A blocking set is a set of components whose removal breaks every faithful circuit. The MHS of all blocking sets is the smallest set that intersects every blocker; this set itself is faithful and minimal by cardinality. Algorithm 2 enumerates small blocking sets (by checking ¬Φ(G\S)) and computes their MHS.
- **Core assumption:** Monotonicity, ability to enumerate enough blocking sets, and feasible MHS computation (e.g., via MaxSAT).
- **Evidence anchors:** [abstract] Duality with blocking sets; [section] Section 4.4 defines blocking sets and states Proposition 7 (MHS duality) and Proposition 8 (Algorithm 2).
- **Break condition:** Larger blocking sets are required but not enumerated, the MHS may only be a lower bound; problem becomes NP‑hard for large graphs.

## Architecture Onboarding
Model loader -> Siamese builder -> Verifier adapter -> Algorithm orchestrator -> Metrics & logger

**Critical path:**
1. Load model and determine component granularity (neurons/filters).
2. Choose robustness domain, radii (ε), and tolerance (δ).
3. Initialize circuit C (often G).
4. For a candidate removal (or subset), build siamese network representing Φ(proposal).
5. Query verifier; if predicate holds, update C; else keep component.
6. Repeat until termination; output final C and statistics.

**Design tradeoffs:**
- Provable vs. heuristic: Provable checks guarantee robustness but are orders of magnitude slower; sampling is fast but fragile.
- Minimality strength: Greedy (subset‑minimal) requires fewer verifier calls than MHS (cardinal‑minimal) but may yield larger circuits.
- Siamese size: The siamese network roughly doubles the original model; for large architectures verification may be impractical.

**Failure signatures:**
- Verifier timeouts (many “unknown” results) → incomplete robustness data.
- Non‑monotonic predicate (e.g., certain patching) → greedy does not reach subset‑minimality.
- Memory crash during siamese construction → model too deep for verifier.

**First 3 experiments:**
1. Validate siamese encoding on a XOR network: ensure a known minimal circuit passes provable checks.
2. Compare greedy sampling vs. provable discovery on MNIST: observe robustness 19% vs. 100% and runtime differences.
3. Run MHS enumeration (t_max=2) on a small CNN; compare obtained circuit size to greedy and verify cardinal minimality when MHS itself is robust.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
### Open Question 1
How can the provable circuit discovery framework be extended to support modern neural network architectures (e.g., Transformers) and non‑piecewise‑linear activations, given its heavy reliance on existing verification tools?  
*Basis:* [explicit] The authors state a key limitation: reliance on neural network verification queries, which remain limited for state‑of‑the‑art models. Experiments are confined to ReLU‑based vision models.

### Open Question 2
What efficient approximation algorithms exist for computing (near‑)cardinal‑minimal circuits under the monotonicity condition, and what theoretical guarantees (e.g., approximation ratios) can be provided?  
*Basis:* [inferred] Algorithm 2 relies on solving a Minimum Hitting Set (NP‑complete). The paper bounds search by small `t_max` and notes tractability for small circuits but lacks analysis of worst‑case behavior or approximation quality for larger graphs.

### Open Question 3
How do the formal minimality notions (quasi, local, subset, cardinal) relate to human‑interpretability of circuits? Does the strongest notion (cardinal‑minimal) produce circuits that are measurably more understandable to practitioners?  
*Basis:* [explicit] The authors acknowledge that smaller circuits are generally considered more interpretable, but they do not evaluate the impact of different minimality criteria on actual interpretability.

### Open Question 4
Can the framework be extended to provide probabilistic or statistical guarantees (e.g., robustness with high probability under input distribution) rather than deterministic guarantees over a continuous domain?  
*Basis:* [explicit] The conclusion mentions future work on probabilistic and statistical guarantees; current definitions are purely deterministic.

## Limitations
- Model weights are not publicly available; reproduction requires sourcing or retraining, which may yield different results.
- Siamese network implementation details (e.g., parameter sharing, branch concatenation) are underspecified, risking misimplementation.
- Verification configuration (timeouts, α‑β‑CROWN defaults) lacks full documentation, affecting robustness certification.
- Sampling baseline procedures (e.g., number of inputs, patching means) are vague, introducing variance.
- Hyperparameters (ϵ_in, ϵ_p, δ) per dataset are incomplete, hindering exact replication.
- MHS enumeration scalability and handling of timeouts are not detailed for larger graphs.

## Confidence
- **Siamese Encoding for Certification:** Medium (theory solid, but implementation gaps could compromise soundness)
- **Greedy Algorithm for Subset‑Minimal Circuits:** High (monotonicity proofs are rigorous, but real‑world predicates may not always satisfy monotonicity)
- **Blocking Set Duality for Cardinal Minimality:** Medium (duality established, but NP‑hardness and enumeration limits affect practicality)
- **Provable Robustness Gains (100% vs 19–46%):** Medium (empirical evidence strong but relies on irreproducible settings)
