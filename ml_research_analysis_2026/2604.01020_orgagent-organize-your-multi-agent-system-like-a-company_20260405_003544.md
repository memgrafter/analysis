# OrgAgent: Organize Your Multi-Agent System like a Company

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2604.01020  
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.01020  
- **Reference count:** 30  
- **Primary result:** A hierarchical multi-agent framework with governance, execution, and compliance layers consistently outperforms flat MAS and single-agent baselines, achieving up to a 102.73% F1-score improvement on SQuAD 2.0 while reducing token consumption by 74.52%.

## Executive Summary
OrgAgent introduces a company-style hierarchical organizational structure for multi-agent systems (MAS) to improve complex reasoning. It decomposes collaboration into three layers: governance (planning and resource allocation), execution (task solving and review), and compliance (final answer validation). Evaluated on reasoning benchmarks (MuSR, MuSiQue, SQuAD 2.0) with multiple LLMs, the hierarchical approach generally surpasses flat MAS and single-agent baselines. Token efficiency is notably improved, especially on SQuAD 2.0, while behaviors such as answer abstention and stable skill assignment are positively influenced.

## Method Summary
OrgAgent organizes agents into a hierarchy. Layer A (CEO, CTO, COO) performs upfront planning: it analyzes the task, selects skills from a shared worker pool, and configures execution parameters (mode, policy, risk). Layer B executes the core reasoning pipeline, consisting of a Drafter, an optional Reviewer, and an optionally invoked Specialist. Execution can be direct (Drafter only), light (Drafter + Reviewer), or full (adds Specialist). Layer C (CSO, CCO) enforces output formatting and structural compliance. The framework uses execution policies (STRICT, BALANCE, NOCAP, AUTO) to trade off accuracy versus token cost. Flat MAS, in contrast, uses peer-to-peer agent interactions without layered oversight.

## Key Results
- On SQuAD 2.0 with GPT-OSS-120B, hierarchical OrgAgent improves F1-score by 102.73% and reduces token consumption by 74.52% compared to flat MAS.
- Hierarchical organization yields stable skill assignment (e.g., Domain Specialist often acts as Drafter) and increases abstention on unanswerable questions in SQuAD 2.0.
- Gains vary by benchmark: dramatic improvements on MuSiQue and SQuAD 2.0, but limited or no improvement on MuSR with certain models.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1
- **Claim:** Hierarchical governance (Layer A) reduces redundant interactions and aligns resources to task needs.
- **Mechanism:** CEO/CTO/COO agents perform upfront planning, assign skilled workers from the pool, and set execution constraints (e.g., max rounds, risk mode). This top-down routing prevents unguided multi-agent debates common in flat MAS.
- **Core assumption:** Pre-execution planning sufficiently captures task structure to guide downstream work without excessive re-planning.
- **Evidence anchors:** Abstract notes token reduction; Section 3.5 describes Layer A determining execution configuration; neighboring hierarchical MAS work shows coordination benefits.
- **Break condition:** If task requirements are highly dynamic or poorly specified, fixed Layer A planning may misallocate skills, causing downstream rework that negates token savings.

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim:** Structured execution cycles (Layer B) improve answer quality through layered verification (draft → review → specialist).
- **Mechanism:** The Drafter produces an initial answer, the Reviewer critiques it, and a Specialist is invoked only for error-prone components. This verification pipeline catches inconsistencies before finalization.
- **Core assumption:** Errors can be reliably identified by a separate Reviewer role and corrected by a relevant Specialist without requiring open-ended debate.
- **Evidence anchors:** Section 3.6 defines execution modes; Section 5.3 shows stable skill assignment; neighboring works use hierarchical review but not identical roles.
- **Break condition:** If the Drafter's initial answer is fundamentally flawed or the Reviewer lacks expertise, the cycle may propagate rather than correct mistakes, especially under strict token limits.

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim:** Dedicated compliance (Layer C) increases answer reliability by separating format control from reasoning.
- **Mechanism:** CSO aligns output to benchmark format (e.g., span extraction for SQuAD), while CCO checks structural compliance (e.g., JSON schema). This prevents technically correct but improperly formatted answers from scoring poorly.
- **Core assumption:** Benchmark-specific output constraints can be mechanistically enforced without contaminating the reasoning process in earlier layers.
- **Evidence anchors:** Section 3.5 describes Layer C; Section 5.3 shows higher abstention on unanswerable SQuAD 2.0 questions under hierarchical policies; compliance officers are a novel addition from this paper.
- **Break condition:** For tasks with ambiguous or flexible output requirements, the compliance layer may introduce unnecessary overhead or incorrectly reject valid answers.

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component map:** Layer A (CEO, CTO, COO) -> Layer B (Drafter, Reviewer, Specialist) -> Layer C (CSO, CCO), all drawing from a shared skill-based worker pool.

**Critical path:**  
1) Layer A analyzes input, selects skills, and sets execution mode/policy.  
2) Layer B executes: Drafter produces answer, Reviewer critiques, optionally Specialist is invoked and then Drafter revises, repeating until pass or round limit.  
3) Layer C: CSO formats answer to benchmark standards, CCO validates structure.

**Design tradeoffs:**  
- STRICT policy minimizes tokens but may under-exploit specialist support, risking lower accuracy on complex tasks.  
- NOCAP policy maximizes collaboration flexibility and often higher accuracy on MuSiQue but at 5–10× token cost of STRICT.  
- FULL MAS adds specialist but increases max rounds (5 vs. 3) versus LIGHT MAS, raising cost; LIGHT may suffice for moderate complexity.

**Failure signatures:**  
- High token consumption without performance gain vs. flat MAS (e.g., GPT-OSS-120B on MuSR).  
- Low abstention rate (<10%) on SQuAD 2.0 unanswerables under hierarchical settings, indicating compliance layer ineffectiveness.  
- Highly diffuse skill assignment (e.g., LLaMA-3.1-8B) suggests the backbone model cannot reliably map skills to roles, weakening hierarchical benefits.

**First 3 experiments:**  
1. **Policy sweep on SQuAD 2.0:** Run hierarchical AUTO, STRICT, BALANCE, NOCAP. Expect: STRICT lowest tokens (~1.5k), NOCAP highest (~3.5k), all outperform flat baseline (~15k tokens, lower F1). Check abstention rates—NOCAP should be highest.  
2. **Token-performance tradeoff on MuSiQue:** Compare FULL MAS (high accuracy, high tokens) vs. LIGHT MAS (moderate both) vs. DIRECT (low both). Validate that STRICT policy within each mode yields the steepest token reduction.  
3. **Skill stability test:** On a fixed subset of SQuAD 2.0, log Drafter and Specialist skill assignments across 10 runs under AUTO. Expect stable concentration (e.g., >80% Domain for Drafter with GPT-5mini). High variance indicates unreliable role allocation.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- **Why does hierarchical organization yield smaller performance gains on constrained-answer benchmarks (e.g., MMLU) compared to open-ended reasoning tasks?**  
  Basis: explicit in Limitations. Why unresolved: the paper does not investigate whether constrained answer format reduces the need for multi-agent coordination or if hierarchical overhead outweighs benefits. What evidence: controlled experiments comparing hierarchical vs. flat MAS across tasks with varying answer constraints (multiple-choice, free-text), measuring coordination steps and performance.

- **How can hierarchical MAS dynamically determine sufficient coordination rounds to avoid premature termination of agent discussions?**  
  Basis: explicit in Limitations (fixed maximum rounds). Why unresolved: fixed rounds may cut off productive discussions or waste tokens; an adaptive mechanism could improve efficiency and answer quality. What evidence: develop and evaluate adaptive halting criteria (e.g., based on inter-agent agreement or confidence scores) compared to fixed rounds on benchmarks with known convergence behaviors.

- **How does organizational structure affect practical deployment metrics such as latency, stability across random seeds, and human-perceived output quality?**  
  Basis: explicit in Limitations (not examined). Why unresolved: these factors are critical for real-world applications but unexplored for hierarchical MAS, leaving operational trade-offs unclear. What evidence: measure wall-clock time, performance variance over multiple runs, and conduct user studies comparing hierarchical vs. flat outputs for correctness and usefulness.

- **What factors drive the model-specific skill specialization patterns in hierarchical MAS (e.g., GPT-5mini vs. LLaMA-3.1-8B assigning different drafter roles)?**  
  Basis: inferred from divergent skill distributions in Section 5.3. Why unresolved: understanding these patterns could inform better role assignment, but underlying determinants (e.g., model capabilities) are unknown. What evidence: correlate model performance on skill-specific probes with their skill selection in hierarchical MAS, possibly via ablation of skill pools.

- **What intrinsic task properties (e.g., context length, multi-hop depth, ambiguity) most strongly predict the advantage of hierarchical over flat coordination?**  
  Basis: inferred from abstract claim that hierarchy helps when tasks benefit from stable skill assignment, controlled information flow, and layered verification. Why unresolved: generalizability to diverse tasks remains unclear; identifying key properties would guide when to use hierarchical MAS. What evidence: create tasks with controlled variations in properties like evidence dispersion and answer ambiguity, then measure performance differences between organizational structures.

## Limitations
- **Implementation gaps:** Agent prompts, turn-taking protocols, execution policy parameters, and the exact skill-assignment algorithm are not fully disclosed, preventing exact replication.
- **Benchmark boundaries:** Large gains on SQuAD 2.0 and MuSiQue, marginal on MuSR; break conditions (e.g., dynamic tasks, ambiguous outputs) remain untested.
- **Model sensitivity:** Smaller models (e.g., LLaMA-3.1-8B) show unstable skill assignment, suggesting hierarchical benefits rely on powerful LLMs.

## Confidence
- Hierarchical superiority (empirical): High  
- Governance resource alignment: Medium  
- Execution verification pipeline: Medium  
- Compliance layer impact: Low-Medium
