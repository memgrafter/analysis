# Explaining, Verifying, and Aligning Semantic Hierarchies in Vision-Language Model Embeddings

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2603.26798  
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.26798  
- **Reference count:** 40  
- **Primary result:** Vision-language models exhibit a trade-off: image encoders yield higher zero-shot accuracy but lower hierarchical consistency with human ontologies, while text encoders are more plausible; post-hoc alignment can steer embeddings toward a target taxonomy with low tree distance (~0.08 nUTED) and moderate (5–15%) accuracy drop.

## Executive Summary
Vision-language models (VLMs) encode semantic hierarchies in their shared embedding spaces, but these structures are poorly understood and differ across modalities. The authors introduce a post‑hoc framework to (1) extract binary trees by agglomeratively clustering class centroids and naming internal nodes via a concept bank, (2) verify plausibility against reference ontologies using hierarchical consistency edge scores, and (3) align induced hierarchies by learning an embedding transformation via UMAP with ontology‑guided neighborhoods. Across 13 VLMs and 4 datasets, they find image encoders achieve +22±13% higher zero‑shot accuracy while text encoders yield +0.015±0.032 higher hierarchical consistency (p<0.002). Zero‑shot performance and hierarchical consistency are negatively correlated (r < –0.45, p<0.006). Uncertainty‑aware early stopping during tree traversal substantially reduces semantic error on ImageNet (LCA distance from 4.3 to 2.47). Post‑hoc alignment reduces normalized UTED to ~0.08 at a cost of 5–15% zero‑shot accuracy. Faithfulness degrades with dataset scale: from 94% on CIFAR‑10 to 26% on ImageNet. The work illuminates a fundamental modality trade‑off and offers tools to analyze and steer VLM representations.

## Method Summary
The framework operates in three stages. First, **Extraction**: For a given set of leaf classes, class centroids are computed from a pretrained VLM encoder (image or text branch). Ward’s agglomerative clustering (cosine affinity, average linkage) produces a binary tree; parent embeddings are the normalized mean of their children’s centroids. Internal nodes are labeled by one‑to‑one matching to embeddings from a concept bank (e.g., WordNet‑derived terms). Second, **Verification**: The tree is evaluated against reference ontologies (SUMO, OpenCyc, Yago). For each edge, the least common ancestor (LCA) of the leaves under its parent and child in the ontology is found; the edge score depends on the directed path lengths, averaged to yield **Hierarchical Consistency** (S_onto). Normalized Unordered Tree Edit Distance (nUTED) measures similarity to a target hierarchy. Third, **Alignment**: A transformation is learned to map original embeddings to a space where the extracted tree matches a target hierarchy (e.g., the text‑encoder tree or a valid ontology subtree). UMAP is run on training instances with a custom pairwise distance combining original cosine distance, tree distance in the target hierarchy (weight β), and a midpoint regularization (weight γ). The UMAP output is approximated by a two‑layer neural network, enabling efficient inference. Zero‑shot classification uses either transformed text embeddings or midpoint embeddings. Tree‑traversal inference selects the child with closest embedding at each node; **Uncertainty‑Aware Early Stopping (UAES)** halts if the best child similarity falls below a p‑quantile (e.g., 0.01) of training similarities.

## Key Results
- Image encoders achieve +22±13% higher zero‑shot accuracy; text encoders achieve +0.015±0.032 higher hierarchical consistency (p<0.002).
- Zero‑shot performance and hierarchical consistency are negatively correlated (r < –0.45, p<0.006).
- UAES reduces semantic error on ImageNet (LCA distance from 4.3 to 2.47).
- Post‑hoc alignment yields low nUTED (~0.08) and moderate zero‑shot drop (5–15%).
- Faithfulness declines with leaf count: CIFAR‑10 94% → ImageNet 26%.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
Class centroids in VLM embedding spaces naturally cluster according to semantic similarity; agglomerative clustering uncovers a hierarchy reflecting this geometry. Matching internal nodes to a concept bank anchors the geometric structure to human‑readable concepts. Hierarchical consistency quantifies alignment by checking whether edges respect directed paths in reference ontologies. Alignment succeeds because UMAP, guided by a metric that blends original semantics, target tree distances, and midpoint preservation, can reorient embeddings while maintaining neighbourhood relations. The neural approximator makes the transformation practical. UAES exploits the distribution of training similarities to detect unlikely child choices, preventing deep incorrect traversals.

## Foundational Learning
- **Agglomerative Clustering (Ward’s method)**
  - *Why needed*: Builds a binary hierarchical tree from class centroids, forming the basis for extraction.
  - *Quick check*: Dendrogram has exactly N‑1 internal nodes for N leaf classes; linkage distances are monotonic.
- **Concept Bank (WordNet synset embeddings)**
  - *Why needed*: Provides a semantic vocabulary to label internal nodes, linking geometry to human concepts.
  - *Quick check*: For an internal node, the nearest concept bank term has high cosine similarity (>0.7) and is semantically related to its descendant leaves.
- **Hierarchical Consistency (S_onto)**
  - *Why needed*: Quantifies tree alignment with reference ontologies; primary verification metric.
  - *Quick check*: For a known valid subtree, S_onto should be near 1.0.
- **Normalized Unordered Tree Edit Distance (nUTED)**
  - *Why needed*: Measures tree similarity for alignment evaluation.
  - *Quick check*: nUTED=0 for identical trees; ~1 for random binary trees of same size.
- **UMAP with Custom Metric**
  - *Why needed*: Learns a transformation that respects both original embedding geometry and target tree distances.
  - *Quick check*: 2D UMAP visualization shows clusters corresponding to target tree branches.
- **Uncertainty‑Aware Early Stopping (UAES)**
  - *Why needed*: Enhances faithfulness by stopping traversal when child similarity is unusually low.
  - *Quick check*: Compute p‑quantile (e.g., 0.01) of training best‑child similarities per node; test samples with errors near leaves should stop earlier.

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component Map**  
Data → Encoder → Class Centroids → Agglomerative Clustering → Binary Tree → Node Labeling (Concept Bank) → Verification Metrics (S_onto, nUTED) → Alignment (UMAP + NN) → Transformed Embeddings → Tree Traversal (with UAES) → Predictions

**Critical Path**  
Centroids → Clustering → Tree → Labeling → (optional) Alignment → Transformed Embeddings → Traversal. The most computationally intensive steps are agglomerative clustering (O(N²) for N classes) and UMAP (O(M²) for M instances). Neural approximation speeds inference after alignment.

**Design Tradeoffs**
- **Linkage choice**: Ward yields balanced trees but may over‑split; average linkage gives more natural groupings but less balance.
- **Alignment hyperparameters** (α_orig, β_onto, γ_midp): Higher β_onto improves tree alignment (lower nUTED) but harms zero‑shot accuracy; α_orig preserves original semantics; γ_midp prevents midpoint collapse.
- **UAES threshold (p‑quantile)**: Lower p stops earlier but increases leaf errors; higher p risks deep wrong paths.

**Failure Signatures**
- Hierarchical consistency <0.2 suggests ontology preprocessing error.
- Zero‑shot drop >20% after alignment indicates overfitting or bad UMAP parameters.
- Tree‑traversal accuracy near random (<<1/N) implies unstable clustering or poor concept bank matching.
- High variance in trees across seeds indicates centroid noise; mitigate with more training samples.

**First Three Experiments**
1. **Modality Trade‑off Replication**: Compute zero‑shot accuracy and hierarchical consistency for image vs. text encoders across CLIP, ALIGN, SigLIP. Expect significant image advantage in accuracy and text advantage in consistency.
2. **Alignment on CIFAR‑10**: Align using the text‑encoder tree as target. Verify nUTED ≈0.08 and zero‑shot drop 5–10%.
3. **UAES on ImageNet**: Compare LCA distance with and without UAES. Expect reduction from ~4.3 to ~2.47.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- **OQ1**: *What causes the persistent trade‑off between zero‑shot accuracy and hierarchical plausibility?* Basis: Negative correlation (r < –0.45, p<0.006) observed but mechanism unknown.
- **OQ2**: *Why do text encoders induce hierarchies more aligned with human ontologies, and can this modality gap be closed?* Basis: Text encoders achieve significantly higher hierarchical consistency (+0.015±0.032, p<0.002) across 13 models.
- **OQ3**: *How can the faithfulness of hierarchical tree‑traversal inference be improved for large‑scale datasets?* Basis: Faithfulness drops to 26% on ImageNet; current methods (e.g., UAES) offer limited gains.
- **OQ4**: *Is the 5–15% drop in zero‑shot accuracy during post‑hoc alignment inherent or reducible?* Basis: Alignment demonstrates moderate drop; unknown whether fundamental or due to method limitations.

## Limitations
- Ambiguous handling of leaf classes with multiple WordNet synsets (single‑synset selection may bias hierarchies).
- Hyperparameters for UMAP and neural approximator beyond α, β, γ are incompletely specified, hindering exact replication.
- Detailed mappings between WordNet and reference ontologies (SUMO, OpenCyc, Yago) are not provided, affecting hierarchical consistency computation.

## Confidence
- **Trade‑off between zero‑shot accuracy and hierarchical consistency:** Medium  
- **Negative correlation (r < –0.45):** Medium  
- **UAES reduces semantic error:** Medium  
- **Alignment achieves low nUTED (~0.08) with moderate drop:** Low  
- **Faithfulness degrades with leaf count (CIFAR‑10 → ImageNet):** High
