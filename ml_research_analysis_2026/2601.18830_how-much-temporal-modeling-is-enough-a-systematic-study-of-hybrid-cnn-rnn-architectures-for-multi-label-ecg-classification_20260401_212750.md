# How Much Temporal Modeling is Enough? A Systematic Study of Hybrid CNN-RNN Architectures for Multi-Label ECG Classification

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2601.18830
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.18830
- **Reference count:** 27
- **Primary result:** CNN integrated with a single BiLSTM layer achieved the lowest Hamming loss (0.0338) and highest subset accuracy (0.5723) on PTB-XL, demonstrating optimal trade-off between temporal modeling and architectural simplicity.

## Executive Summary
This study systematically evaluates hybrid CNN-RNN architectures for multi-label ECG classification on the PTB-XL dataset, addressing challenges of class imbalance and long-range temporal dependencies. By combining a CNN backbone with various recurrent configurations (LSTM, BiLSTM, GRU, stacked variants), the authors find that a single bidirectional LSTM layer yields the best performance across key metrics, including lowest Hamming loss and highest subset accuracy. Deeper or more complex recurrent stacks do not consistently improve results and often introduce overfitting, underscoring that architectural simplicity aligned with ECG’s temporal structure outperforms increased recurrent depth.

## Method Summary
The study uses the PTB-XL dataset (21,799 10-second 12-lead ECG recordings at 100 Hz) with patient-wise stratified 10-fold splits (folds 1–8 train, 9 validation, 10 test). Input signals undergo per-lead standardization using training-set statistics. The baseline CNN backbone comprises three 1D convolutional blocks (kernel sizes 15, 10, 5; filters 64, 128, 256) with batch normalization, ReLU, max-pooling, and spatial dropout (0.1). Recurrent modules (LSTM, BiLSTM, GRU, stacked combinations) are appended to capture temporal context. A global average pooling layer aggregates the sequence, followed by a feature-wise gating module (two fully connected layers with ReLU and sigmoid activations) to adaptively reweight channels. The classification head includes two fully connected layers (512, 256 units) with batch normalization and dropout (0.5), producing 23 sigmoid outputs. Training uses Adam (lr=0.001), binary cross-entropy with label smoothing (value unspecified), mixed precision, early stopping on validation AUROC, and learning rate reduction on plateau. Data augmentation includes additive Gaussian noise and random amplitude scaling. Evaluation metrics: Hamming loss, subset accuracy, macro-AUPRC, micro-F1, with a fixed 0.5 decision threshold.

## Key Results
- The optimal CNN-BiLSTM model achieved Hamming loss 0.0338, subset accuracy 0.5723, macro-AUPRC 0.4715, and micro-F1 0.6979.
- Adding deeper recurrent stacks (e.g., GRU+BiLSTM+LSTM) did not improve subset accuracy (0.5487 vs. 0.5723) and sometimes degraded performance, indicating diminishing returns.
- Bidirectional models consistently outperformed unidirectional LSTM, highlighting the benefit of full-sequence context for offline ECG analysis.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1
- **Claim:** The CNN backbone extracts local morphological features (e.g., QRS complexes, ST-segments) that serve as a robust foundation for classification, independent of temporal context.
- **Mechanism:** Stacked 1D convolutional layers with increasing filter depths learn hierarchical local patterns from raw multi-lead ECG waveforms. Max-pooling reduces temporal resolution while preserving morphological characteristics. Batch normalization and spatial dropout stabilize training and suppress noise.
- **Core assumption:** Local waveform morphology is sufficiently discriminative for many cardiac conditions and provides a strong prior that reduces the burden on subsequent temporal layers.
- **Evidence anchors:** 
  - [abstract] "The CNN component serves as a morphology-driven baseline..."
  - [section 2.8] "These layers progressively extract local morphological patterns..."
- **Break condition:** If the task requires modeling very long-range dependencies that span entire recordings (e.g., rare arrhythmias with intermittent patterns), CNN alone may fail without recurrent augmentation.

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim:** A single bidirectional LSTM (BiLSTM) layer optimally integrates temporal context by capturing dependencies in both forward and backward directions, which aligns with the need to interpret ECG segments without strict causality constraints.
- **Mechanism:** The BiLSTM processes sequences of CNN-extracted features, maintaining hidden states that aggregate information from past and future time steps. This allows the model to associate a waveform abnormality with preceding/following rhythms (e.g., ischemic changes relative to QRS).
- **Core assumption:** ECG interpretation benefits from full-sequence context within the fixed-length window (10s), as many diagnostic patterns are defined by relationships across multiple cardiac cycles.
- **Evidence anchors:** 
  - [abstract] "Bidirectional temporal context is beneficial, but architectural simplicity... outperforms increased recurrent depth."
  - [section 3] "The BiLSTM model attains the lowest Hamming Loss (0.0338) and the highest Subset Accuracy (0.5723)..."
- **Break condition:** If computational constraints are extreme (e.g., edge deployment with millisecond latency), unidirectional models may be necessary, though with some performance trade-off. If temporal dependencies are extremely short (isolated beats), BiLSTM may be superfluous.

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim:** Feature-wise gating (attention) after global pooling adaptively reweights latent feature dimensions, enhancing robustness by emphasizing diagnostically relevant channels and suppressing noisy or redundant ones.
- **Mechanism:** A two-layer MLP with sigmoid activation generates a channel-wise attention vector from the globally pooled sequence. This vector multiplicatively modulates the pooled features before classification, performing soft feature selection.
- **Core assumption:** Not all learned convolutional/RNN features are equally informative for every label; adaptive recalibration at the channel level improves multi-label discrimination, especially under class imbalance.
- **Evidence anchors:** 
  - [section 2.10] "This adaptive reweighting allows the model to emphasize diagnostically informative latent dimensions while suppressing less relevant components."
  - [section 4.1] "The proposed method integrates feature-wise gating to enable adaptive feature recalibration, a capability not explicitly addressed in either prior work [23,24]."
- **Break condition:** If the feature space is already highly discriminative and low-dimensional (e.g., after strong handcrafted feature extraction), the gating module may introduce unnecessary parameters and risk overfitting on rare classes.

## Foundational Learning
- **Concept: Multi-label classification**
  - Why needed here: ECG recordings frequently show multiple concurrent abnormalities (28.6% in PTB-XL). Using mutually exclusive softmax would force artificial single-label assignment, losing clinical realism. Independent sigmoid outputs model label co-occurrence.
  - Quick check question: Why is binary cross-entropy with sigmoid outputs appropriate for this ECG dataset instead of categorical cross-entropy?
- **Concept: Severe class imbalance**
  - Why needed here: Prevalence ratio of ~560:1 (NORM vs. PMI) biases threshold-dependent metrics. Models may appear accurate by ignoring rare classes. Requires careful metric selection (AUPRC, subset accuracy) and possibly loss adjustments.
  - Quick check question: How does the 560:1 imbalance ratio specifically threaten the validity of macro-F1 scores, and why might AUROC remain high for rare classes?
- **Concept: Sequence modeling with RNN variants**
  - Why needed here: ECG is a time-series where diagnostic patterns (e.g., ST depression evolution, arrhythmia sequences) span multiple beats and leads. CNNs have limited receptive fields; RNNs/LSTMs model long-range dependencies.
  - Quick check question: What is the key architectural difference between LSTM and GRU, and why might BiLSTM be preferred over unidirectional LSTM for offline ECG analysis?

## Architecture Onboarding
Input (1000 timesteps, 12 leads) -> Per-lead standardization -> CNN backbone (3 conv blocks) -> Recurrent module (LSTM/BiLSTM/GRU/stacked) -> GlobalAveragePooling1D -> Feature-wise gating (FC-ReLU -> FC-sigmoid) -> Classification head (FC-512 -> FC-256 -> FC-23) -> Output (23 sigmoids)

Critical path: The recurrent layer is the key variable; removing it yields the CNN baseline. Gating is applied after pooling to avoid temporal perturbation.

Design tradeoffs:
- Depth vs. overfitting: Stacking recurrent layers increases parameters but yields diminishing returns; deeper stacks sometimes lower subset accuracy (e.g., GRU+BiLSTM+LSTM: 0.5487 vs. BiLSTM: 0.5723).
- Bidirectionality vs. causality: BiLSTM uses full-sequence context (acceptable offline), while unidirectional LSTM is causal but underperformed (subset acc 0.5605).
- Model complexity vs. clinical deployment: Simpler models (CNN-BiLSTM) offer better trade-offs for resource-limited settings vs. stacked RNNs or Transformers.

Failure signatures:
- Rare class collapse: High AUROC but low F1/AUPRC for classes with <50 samples (e.g., PMI), indicating conservative thresholding.
- Precision-recall imbalance: CNN-heavy models high precision/lower recall; BiLSTM-heavy higher recall/lower precision; imbalance can cause one dominant label to drive loss.
- Overfitting in deep recurrent stacks: Validation loss plateaus/increases despite training loss decrease, suggesting poor generalization.

First 3 experiments:
1. Ablation of recurrent module: Train CNN-only vs. CNN+BiLSTM. Expect CNN-BiLSTM improves subset accuracy and Hamming loss by better capturing label co-occurrence across time.
2. Bidirectional vs. unidirectional: Compare CNN+LSTM vs. CNN+BiLSTM. Expect BiLSTM improves micro-F1 and subset accuracy by leveraging future context.
3. Recurrent depth sweep: Train CNN+BiLSTM, CNN+BiLSTM+LSTM, CNN+BiLSTM+BiLSTM. Expect performance peaks at single BiLSTM; deeper stacks degrade or give marginal gains not justifying complexity.

## Limitations
- Exact hyperparameters for data augmentation (noise std, scaling range) and label smoothing are unreported, hindering full reproducibility.
- The attention module’s architecture (hidden dimension, bias usage) is vaguely described, requiring assumptions.
- Claims rely solely on PTB‑XL; generalization to other ECG sources or noisy real‑world signals is unknown.
- Rare classes (e.g., PMI) remain poorly detected despite high AUROC, indicating thresholding or imbalance issues.

## Confidence
- CNN as robust morphology extractor: Medium (supported by baseline performance but not validated by feature visualization)
- Single BiLSTM optimal temporal integration: High (backed by systematic comparisons across multiple recurrent configurations)
- Feature-wise gating improves robustness: Low (module design ambiguous; contribution not isolated in ablations)
