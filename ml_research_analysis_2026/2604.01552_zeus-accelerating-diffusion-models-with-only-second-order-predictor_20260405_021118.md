# ZEUS: Accelerating Diffusion Models with Only Second-Order Predictor

## Quick Facts
- arXiv: 2604.01552
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.01552
- Reference count: 40
- Primary result: ZEUS accelerates diffusion sampling by reducing denoiser evaluations, achieving up to 3.2× speedup while matching or improving perceptual quality (LPIPS/FID) over baselines like TaylorSeer and SADA, with fewer than 20 lines of code to integrate.

## Executive Summary
ZEUS is a training-free method that speeds up diffusion model sampling by skipping denoiser evaluations. It employs a second-order predictor that leverages the current denoiser output and its backward difference, and stabilizes multi-step skipping through an interleaved reuse pattern. The approach requires no architectural modifications and is compatible with various diffusion models (e.g., SDXL, FLUX, Wan 2.1). Experiments show speedups of up to 3.2× while preserving or even improving perceptual fidelity compared to existing accelerators.

## Method Summary
The method introduces three simple components: an observed cache that stores the latest full denoiser output ψ_t and its backward difference Δψ_t; an interleaved predictor that, for r skipped steps after a full evaluation, alternates between using ψ_t and its extrapolation 2ψ_t - ψ̂_{t+1} as approximations; and a skipping scheduler that applies this scheme only to the middle 70% of the trajectory (first 20% and last 10% use full evaluations). The integration requires modifying the sampling loop to replace denoiser calls for skipped steps with the predictor's output, adding only O(1) state. No additional training is needed.

## Key Results
- **Speedup:** Up to 3.2× acceleration on image and video generation benchmarks (SDXL, FLUX, Wan 2.1) compared to full-evaluation baselines.
- **Fidelity:** Matches or improves perceptual metrics (LPIPS, FID) relative to state-of-the-art acceleration methods such as TaylorSeer and SADA.
- **Simplicity:** Requires fewer than 20 lines of code to integrate into existing HuggingFace Diffusers pipelines.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1
- **Claim:** Under aggressive (≥2×) uniform skipping, the *only* causally grounded information available for predicting skipped denoiser outputs is the *observed information set*—the current full evaluation output ψ_t and its backward difference Δ^(1)ψ_t = ψ_t − ψ̂_{t+1}.
- **Mechanism:** The scarcity constraint (at most one fresh evaluation per local window) means approximated states precede any fresh evaluation. The state x̂_t exists only because the numerical solver advanced from x̂_{t+1} using the *approximated* output ψ̂_{t+1}. Hence, the difference ψ_t − ψ̂_{t+1} encodes the true model response to the actual (approximated) trajectory, which is lost if only ψ_t is reused.
- **Core assumption:** The sampling trajectory is locally smooth (ψ ∈ C²), making a second-order (affine) local model appropriate. The forward process schedules (α_s, σ_s) are twice differentiable.
- **Evidence anchors:** 
  - [abstract] "...at most one fresh evaluation per local window—leaving the computed output and its backward difference as the only causally grounded information."
  - [section 4.1] "At step t, the denoiser has computed ψ_t = ψ_θ(x̂_t, s_t). But observe that the denoising state x̂_t itself exists only because the solver advanced from x̂_{t+1} using the signal ψ̂_{t+1}... This yields a key insight: the backward difference Δ^(1)ψ_t := ψ_t − ψ̂_{t+1} encodes information that ψ_t alone cannot provide..."
- **Break condition:** If the skipping schedule is non-uniform or adaptive in a way that allows two fresh evaluations within a three-step window, the scarcity principle and the unique sufficiency of {ψ_t, Δψ_t} may no longer hold.

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim:** Chaining second-order extrapolations for consecutive reduced steps amplifies error (variance growth Θ(j²)), but constant reuse (ignoring slope) introduces bias Θ(jΔ). The *interleaved approximation* scheme avoids both by reusing the same observed information set for all reduced steps in a block, alternating between the two values.
- **Mechanism:** Instead of recursively applying ψ̂_{t−j} = 2ψ̂_{t−j+1} − ψ̂_{t−j+2} (predictor-only, which amplifies any error in ψ̂_{t+1}), ZEUS sets {ψ̂_{t−j}} to cycle between ψ_t and 2ψ_t − ψ_t+1. This ensures: (i) at the first skipped step (j=1), it uses the BLUE (unbiased, minimum variance under C²); (ii) for j>1, it reuses the anchor ψ_t, capping variance growth while maintaining O(Δ²) bias only at j=1 where it matters most.
- **Core assumption:** The noise (estimation error in ψ̂_{t+1}) is zero-mean and homoscedastic. The local trend ϕ is C², so second-order bias is O(Δ²). The interleaving pattern prevents recursive propagation of approximation errors.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [section 4.2] "Unrolling this recurrence yields ψ̂_{t−j} = (j+1)·ψ_t − j·ψ̂_{t+1}. Because the coefficients grow linearly in j, any error in ψ̂_{t+1} is amplified... Reuse-only sets ψ̂_{t−j} = ψ_t for all j. This avoids error amplification but ignores slope information... The interleaved pattern applies the second-order predictor once (at j=1), then resets to ψ_t..."
  - [section 4.2, Figure 4] Visualizes overshoot in predictor-only and lag in reuse-only, with ZEUS alternating preserving detail.
  - [section 5.3, Figure 3c-d] Ablation on SDXL shows interleaved scheme matches or outperforms both baselines as number of consecutive reduced steps increases.
- **Break condition:** If the skipped-step run length r is very large (e.g., >4) and the underlying ODE trajectory has high curvature, even the j=1 second-order approximation may become inaccurate, and the alternating pattern may not fully compensate.

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim:** Higher-order (k≥3) numerical predictors are *detrimental* under aggressive skipping because: (i) they must incorporate stale or approximate earlier evaluations, amplifying variance exponentially (Θ(4ᵏ)); (ii) under C² smoothness (the weakest justified regularity), the minimax bias rate is Θ(Δ²), already achieved by second-order; (iii) equispaced polynomial interpolation (implied by higher-order Lagrange) suffers from Runge phenomenon and exponential sensitivity to perturbations.
- **Mechanism:** Higher-order predictors use k past evaluations to fit a polynomial of degree k−1. When these past evaluations themselves include approximated values (due to skipping), the polynomial fit becomes unstable. The theoretical analysis (Appendix A.3.4) shows the ℓ² norm of Lagrange weights grows like 4ᵏ, leading to noise amplification Ω(4ᵏσ²). Meanwhile, the bias cannot drop below Θ(Δ²) for C² functions (minimax lower bound).
- **Core assumption:** The true trajectory ψ ∈ C²([0,1]) is the weakest regularity justified by the diffusion forward process (twice differentiable schedules). Observations are corrupted by i.i.d. Gaussian noise.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [section 4.1, Table 1] Ablation on SDXL: third-order predictor (k=3) underperforms second-order predictor (q=2) on MSE and LPIPS, even when given the true previous full evaluation (not approximated).
  - [section 4.1] "Surprisingly, the third-order predictor (k=3) consistently underperforms the second-order predictor (k=2)... error grows rapidly as the number of consecutive reduced steps r increases, regardless of predictor order."
  - [appendix A.3.4] Theorem A.15: variance amplification Ω(4ᵏσ²). Corollary A.12: minimax rate Θ(Δ²) under C².
- **Break condition:** If the underlying ODE trajectory is smoother than C² (e.g., C⁴ or analytic) *and* the noise level is extremely low, higher-order methods could theoretically achieve lower bias without overwhelming variance. However, the paper argues such conditions are unrealistic for learned diffusion models.

## Foundational Learning
- **Concept:** Probability Flow ODE (PF-ODE)
  - **Why needed here:** ZEUS accelerates sampling along the deterministic PF-ODE trajectory. Understanding that diffusion sampling integrates an ODE (dx_s = v(x_s, s)ds) is essential to see why step skipping can be framed as approximating future states on a continuous trajectory, and why numerical stability matters.
  - **Quick check question:** If you skip a denoiser evaluation at step t, what is the mathematical object you must approximate to continue Euler integration?

- **Concept:** Denoiser Output Parameterizations (ϵ, x₀, v, score, flow)
  - **Why needed here:** ZEUS operates directly on the model output ψ_θ(x, s) regardless of training target. The paper unifies these via linear relationships (Theorem A.3). Knowing they are equivalent via deterministic affine readouts assures that the second-order predictor on ψ_t works universally without target-specific tuning.
  - **Quick check question:** Given an ϵ-prediction model and a v-prediction model with identical weights, how are their outputs at (x_s, s) related? Can you derive the reconstruction formula for x₀ from each?

- **Concept:** Numerical Predictor Design & Bias–Variance Trade-off
  - **Why needed here:** The core of ZEUS is choosing a predictor that balances bias (from smoothness assumptions) and variance (from noise propagation) under the constraint of scarce fresh evaluations. The paper’s analysis (BLUE, minimax rate) explains why second-order is optimal and why higher-order fails.
  - **Quick check question:** Using only ψ_t and ψ_{t+Δ}, derive the unique linear unbiased estimator for ψ_{t−Δ} assuming the trend is affine. What is its bias order if the true trend is C²?

## Architecture Onboarding
The ZEUS system consists of the following components:

- **Denoiser (unchanged):** ψ_θ(x, s) as in the original HuggingFace Diffusers pipeline.
- **Numerical Solver (unchanged):** Euler or DPM-Solver++ that steps through the PF-ODE.
- **Observed Cache (new, O(1) state):** Stores the pair (ψ_t, Δψ_t) after each full evaluation, where Δψ_t = ψ_t − ψ̂_{t+1} (ψ̂_{t+1} is the predictor used for the immediately following reduced step).
- **Interleaved Predictor (new, <20 LOC):** Given (ψ_t, Δψ_t), for the r reduced steps after t, it sets ψ̂_{t−j} = (2ψ_t − ψ̂_{t+1}) if j odd, else ψ_t.
- **Skipping Scheduler (new, config):** Uniform 1:r schedule (e.g., 1:2, 1:3, 1:4) with warm-up/cooldown steps using full evaluations.

The critical path:
1. At a full-evaluation step t, compute ψ_t = ψ_θ(x̂_t, s_t).
2. Compute Δψ_t = ψ_t − ψ̂_{t+1} (where ψ̂_{t+1} was the approximation used for the immediately following reduced step).
3. For the next r steps (t−1 down to t−r), apply the interleaved formula using cached (ψ_t, Δψ_t). No new denoiser calls.
4. After r reduced steps, the next step t−r−1 becomes a fresh evaluation. Goto 1.

Design tradeoffs:
- **Speed vs. Fidelity:** Larger r (more skipping) gives higher speedup but risks perceptual drift. The interleaved scheme mitigates this without extra cost.
- **Solver order vs. Predictor:** ZEUS works with any solver (Euler, DPM++). Higher-order solvers improve baseline fidelity; ZEUS still adds the same predictor overhead and benefits.
- **Warm-up/cooldown:** The paper uses full evaluations in the first 20% and last 10% of steps due to high curvature. Omitting this may cause instability at trajectory ends.
- **Window size:** The interleaved scheme reuses the same observed pair for all r reduced steps. If r is very large, the bias for large j may accumulate, but the alternating pattern caps variance.

Failure signatures:
- **Overshoot/artifacts:** If predictor-only (no interleaving) is used, consecutive extrapolations amplify error, causing overshoot and visual artifacts.
- **Lagging detail:** If reuse-only (no slope) is used, fine details erode as the trajectory lags behind.
- **End-of-trajectory decay:** Skipping too aggressively near s=0 (data) can degrade sample quality; hence the cooldown period.
- **Memory blowup:** ZEUS deliberately avoids layer-wise caches; if one mistakenly adds feature caching, memory scales with model depth, breaking the O(1) claim.

First 3 experiments:
1. **Quality sanity check (1:1 skipping):** Implement ZEUS with r=1 on a small model (SD-2) using DPM-Solver++ (50 steps). Compare LPIPS/PSNR against full evaluation. Expect near-identical metrics.
2. **Interleaving validation (1:2 vs. 1:3):** Test r=2 and r=3 with both "predictor-only" (chain) and "interleaved" schemes. Measure LPIPS and visual artifacts. Interleaved should remain stable while predictor-only degrades at r=3.
3. **Cross-backbone portability:** Apply the same <20-line code change to SDXL (U-Net), FLUX (Transformer), and a video model (Wan 2.1) without modification. Verify speedup and LPIPS/FID improve or match SOTA baselines. This confirms parameterization- and architecture-agnosticism.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
### Open Question 1
- **Question:** Under what conditions does the observed information set {ψ_t, Δψ_t} remain sufficient to guarantee near-optimal reconstruction for acceleration ratios beyond 2×?
- **Basis in paper:** [explicit] The authors claim that beyond 2× skipping, structural scarcity limits causal information to this pair (Sec. 4.1, Theorem A.5). They prove the second-order predictor is the BLUE under C² smoothness and homoscedastic noise.
- **Why unresolved:** Real diffusion trajectories may violate C² regularity or exhibit correlated approximation errors, potentially breaking the optimality guarantee.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Formal relaxations of smoothness/noise assumptions plus empirical validation across diverse model architectures and samplers to identify failure modes.

### Open Question 2
- **Question:** Is the interleaved schedule (cycling between ψ_t and its extrapolation) provably optimal among all deterministic reuse patterns for multi-step skipping?
- **Basis in paper:** [explicit] ZEUS introduces Eq. (2) to avoid error amplification empirically (Sec. 4.2). The authors show bias–variance trade-offs qualitatively but do not prove optimality.
- **Why unresolved:** Alternative patterns (e.g., longer cycles) could better trade bias and variance depending on acceleration ratio and noise level.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Derivation of an optimal deterministic policy minimizing a combined error metric (e.g., MSE over a window) and experiments comparing to other cyclic schemes.

### Open Question 3
- **Question:** How well does ZEUS generalize to non-visual generative models (e.g., audio, 3D) and to fine-tuned or domain-specific diffusion variants?
- **Basis in paper:** [inferred] Experiments cover only image and video models. The claimed modality-agnostic property lacks verification beyond these.
- **Why unresolved:** Different modalities may exhibit distinct PF-ODE curvature or noise characteristics, making second-order extrapolation less effective.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Systematic benchmarking on representative audio, 3D, and fine-tuned SD checkpoints, measuring speed–fidelity curves.

### Open Question 4
- **Question:** Can ZEUS be combined with architectural sparsification methods (e.g., token pruning, sparse attention) to yield multiplicative speedups without compounding quality loss?
- **Basis in paper:** [inferred] The authors state ZEUS is orthogonal to within-step sparsification but provide no hybrid evaluations.
- **Why unresolved:** Interactions between trajectory skipping and within-step approximations could cause error accumulation not captured by either method alone.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Integrate ZEUS with leading sparsification methods and compare combined speedups and perceptual metrics against individual baselines.

## Limitations
- The exact recurrence for Δψ_t when previous steps were approximated and the precise "middle 70%" schedule boundaries require author code to confirm.
- Theoretical analysis assumes C² smoothness; if the true trajectory is smoother (e.g., C⁴) and noise is extremely low, higher-order predictors could outperform second-order—a regime not explored.
- Corpus support for the backward-difference principle is weak; neighboring work focuses on caching, so the novelty claim needs stronger community validation.

## Confidence
- **Mechanism 1 (scarcity ⇒ observed info sufficiency):** Medium
- **Mechanism 2 (interleaved approximation stabilizes):** High
- **Mechanism 3 (second-order optimal, higher-order detrimental):** High
