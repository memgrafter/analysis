# RepSPD: Enhancing SPD Manifold Representation in EEGs via Dynamic Graphs

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2602.22981
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.22981
- **Reference count:** 40
- **Primary result:** RepSPD achieves 86.43% accuracy on TUSZ seizure detection (+3.3% absolute over prior SOTA) and 81.05% on BCIC-IV-2a motor imagery (+2.23% absolute), with superior robustness and lower variance.

## Executive Summary
RepSPD tackles the limitation of static SPD-based EEG analysis, which neglects frequency-specific synchronization and local topological structures, by integrating dynamic graph representations into Riemannian manifold learning. It proposes a dual-path architecture where an EEG encoder produces covariance-based SPD matrices and a graph encoder yields topology-aware SPD matrices from time-varying functional connectivity. These are fused via a manifold cross-attention mechanism that modulates SPD features with graph-derived priors. A geometry-aware alignment loss further stabilizes tangent-space embeddings by enforcing consistency with global graph features. Evaluated on seizure detection (TUSZ) and motor imagery (BCIC-IV-2a), RepSPD sets new state-of-the-art with significant absolute gains, and ablations confirm the necessity of both the cross-attention fusion and the alignment loss.

## Method Summary
RepSPD employs two parallel encoders. The EEG encoder processes raw signals through a CNN followed by a GRU to capture spectral-temporal dynamics and output covariance-based SPD matrices \(S_t\). The graph encoder constructs a dynamic connectivity graph from channel-wise spectral features (via STFT and similarity-based adjacency), then models node and edge attributes with GRUs and a GNN to produce graph-derived SPD matrices \(C_t\) and a global graph feature \(g_i\). A manifold cross-attention module projects \(S_t\) and \(C_t\) to a shared low-dimensional SPD space via BiMap layers; \(C_t\) serves as queries while \(S_t\) provides keys/values. Attention weights are computed using Log-Euclidean distance between query and key SPD matrices. The weighted combination of values is mapped back to the SPD manifold to yield an enhanced representation \(\tilde{S}_t\). This is projected to a tangent space via the logarithmic map, flattened, and fed to a linear classifier with cross-entropy loss. A geometry-aware alignment loss \(L_{\text{GeoTop}}\) additionally aligns the pooled tangent embedding with \(g_i\) using contrastive learning, stabilizing the representation. The total loss is \(L = L_{\text{CE}} + \beta L_{\text{GeoTop}}\) with \(\beta=0.3\).

## Key Results
- On the TUSZ seizure detection dataset, RepSPD achieves **86.43% accuracy**, a **3.3% absolute improvement** over the previous state-of-the-art.
- On the BCIC-IV-2a motor imagery dataset, it achieves **81.05% accuracy**, a **2.23% absolute gain**.
- Ablations demonstrate that removing either the manifold cross-attention mechanism or the geometry-aware alignment loss leads to substantial performance drops (e.g., F1 score decrease from ~43 to ~38 on BCIC), confirming both components are critical.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1: Dynamic Graph-Guided Manifold Cross-Attention
**Claim:** Dynamic graph-guided manifold cross-attention modulates SPD features with frequency-specific, topology-aware priors, enhancing representation expressiveness beyond static covariance aggregation.  
**Mechanism:** Graph-derived connectivity \(A_t\) is encoded into spectral SPD matrices \(C_t\), which serve as *queries* in a Riemannian cross-attention module, while EEG-derived SPD matrices \(S_t\) serve as *keys/values*. Attention scores are computed via Log-Euclidean distance \(d_{\text{LE}} = \|\log K - \log Q\|_F\) on the SPD manifold. The enhanced SPD \(\tilde{S}_t\) is formed by a Log-Euclidean weighted mean of the values, followed by an exponential map back to the manifold. This injects local, time-varying functional topology into the geometric SPD representation.  
**Core assumption:** Functional connectivity patterns, when encoded as graph-derived SPD queries, provide meaningful, discriminative priors that can guide the modulation of the base statistical SPD on a shared geometric space.  
**Evidence anchors:**  
- Abstract: "...integrate dynamic graph representations... via manifold cross-attention mechanism to modulate SPD features with graph-derived topological priors..."  
- Section 4.1: Details the attention operation transformed via Log-Euclidean geodesic distance and mapping back via exponential map.  
- Section 5.2: Shows that without the graph prior (SPDNet, MAtt), tangent-space clusters are less separated; RepSPD yields more compact, well-separated clusters.  
**Break condition:** If the dynamic graph connectivity \(A_t\) is poorly estimated (noisy or non-informative), the query priors misguide the modulation, degrading SPD quality. Ablations in Table 1 show node-based SPD queries work best, implying the quality and type of graph-derived query is critical.

### Mechanism 2: Geometry-Aware Alignment Loss
**Claim:** The geometry-aware alignment loss \(L_{\text{GeoTop}}\) mitigates curvature-induced distortions in tangent-space embeddings, stabilizing discriminative structure and improving robustness.  
**Mechanism:** After projecting \(\tilde{S}_t\) to tangent space \(M_t = \log(\tilde{S}_t)\), \(L_{\text{GeoTop}}\) performs a contrastive alignment between the pooled tangent embedding \(u_i\) and the global graph feature \(g_i\). It pulls together corresponding pairs while repelling non-corresponding ones via cosine similarity, regularizing the tangent-space geometry to be consistent with the global graph topology and counteracting "dimensional deformation" caused by manifold curvature during projection.  
**Core assumption:** The tangent-space embedding and the global graph representation are two complementary, semantically aligned views of the same underlying brain state; enforcing consistency yields a more geometrically stable and discriminative tangent space.  
**Evidence anchors:**  
- Abstract: "...coupled with a geometry-aware alignment loss to stabilize tangent-space embeddings."  
- Section 4.2: Defines \(L_{\text{GeoTop}}\) as encouraging SPD embeddings to be closer to corresponding graph structures while repelling irrelevant ones.  
- Section 5.2: States that without cross-view calibration, clusters overlap noticeably; RepSPD yields more compact clusters; contrastive loss explicitly enforces geometric consistency.  
- Table 2: Removing \(L_{\text{GeoTop}}\) (λ=0) drastically drops F1 (42.92 → 37.80), confirming its necessity.  
**Break condition:** If the two views (tangent embedding and global graph feature) are fundamentally misaligned (capture different dynamics), the contrastive objective could become noisy or adversarial, harming performance. Table 2 shows too high a λ (0.7) degrades performance, indicating over-regularization is harmful.

### Mechanism 3: Dual-Path Architecture for Complementary Dynamics
**Claim:** The dual-path architecture (SPD path + dynamic graph path) explicitly models both spectral temporal statistics and evolving functional topology, providing complementary information that a single static SPD path cannot capture.  
**Mechanism:** The EEG encoder produces \(S_t\) from raw signals via CNN+GRU, capturing spectral-temporal covariance. The graph encoder constructs a *dynamic* graph from time-frequency channel similarities and produces \(C_t\). The cross-attention fuses these. The graph path explicitly models channel relationships that the spatial covariance in \(S_t\) treats uniformly, adding non-linear, time-evolving relational priors.  
**Core assumption:** Covariance-based SPD (\(S_t\)) alone is insufficient because it (a) weights all channel pairs equally, (b) assumes temporal stationarity within an epoch, and (c) neglects explicit topological structure. A separate dynamic graph pathway provides complementary, non-linear, time-evolving relational priors.  
**Evidence anchors:**  
- Abstract: "...SPD-based methods focus predominantly on statistical aggregation of EEGs, with frequency-specific synchronization and local topological structures of brain regions neglected."  
- Introduction: "SPD treats all channels equally without any weighting or intra-channel geometry modeling... ignore local dynamics and time-course of brain connectivity."  
- Section 4.1: Explains development of reciprocal geometric-topology integration; graph connectivity provides explicit topological structure.  
- Section 5.2 and Figure 4: Show that without the graph prior (SPDNet, MAtt), clusters are less separated; the graph prior adds discriminative structure.  
**Break condition:** If the EEG data's discriminative features are purely second-order stationary statistics with no relevant dynamic connectivity patterns, the graph pathway adds noise. However, consistent gains on TUSZ (dynamic seizure) and BCIC (coordinated MI networks) argue against this.

## Foundational Learning

**Concept: Riemannian Geometry of SPD Manifolds**  
- **Why needed here:** The entire SPD pathway operates on the space of symmetric positive definite matrices, a curved Riemannian manifold. Operations like BiMap, ReEig, and the log/exp maps must preserve positive definiteness and respect the manifold's geometry; standard Euclidean layer logic fails.  
- **Quick check:** Why is the log operation used to project an SPD matrix to tangent space, and what manifold property does it preserve?  
  *Answer:* It maps SPD to symmetric matrices (tangent space) while preserving geodesic distances under the Log-Euclidean metric, enabling Euclidean vector-space operations without distorting geometry.

**Concept: Dynamic Graph Neural Networks for Time-Evolving Connectivity**  
- **Why needed here:** The graph pathway must capture non-stationary, time-varying functional connectivity between EEG channels. A static graph per trial is insufficient. The "time-then-graph" framework (GRUs on node/edge attributes, then GNN) generates time-aware graph representations.  
- **Quick check:** In the "time-then-graph" framework, what do the GRUs model, and what does the subsequent GNN integrate?  
  *Answer:* GRUs model the temporal evolution of node (channel spectral) and edge (connectivity strength) attributes independently. The GNN then integrates these to produce a final, time-aware node/edge embedding per segment, capturing both temporal dynamics and spatial structure.

**Concept: Tangent Space Distortion & Geometric Consistency**  
- **Why needed here:** Projecting curved manifold points (\(\tilde{S}_t\)) to a flat tangent space (\(M_t = \log(\tilde{S}_t)\)) introduces distortions—nearby points on the manifold can become far apart in tangent space due to positive curvature. \(L_{\text{GeoTop}}\) combats this by aligning the tangent embedding with a complementary, curvature-free graph view, promoting a more faithful, discriminative Euclidean representation.  
- **Quick check:** What is the core risk of using a single log mapping for all SPD points to tangent space, and how does \(L_{\text{GeoTop}}\) mitigate it?  
  *Answer:* The risk is that curvature causes nonlinear stretching/compression, distorting class geometry. \(L_{\text{GeoTop}}\) mitigates by enforcing that the tangent embedding's structure aligns with the graph's topology, regularizing the embedding to be more geometrically consistent and cluster-friendly.

## Architecture Onboarding

**Component map:**  
EEG Encoder (CNN+GRU) -> \(S_t\); Graph Encoder (STFT -> dynamic graph -> GRU on nodes/edges -> GNN) -> \(C_t\) and \(g_i\); Cross-Attention (Q from \(C_t\), K/V from \(S_t\)) -> \(\tilde{S}_t\); Tangent Projection (log) -> \(M_t\); Classifier (Linear) -> \(L_{\text{CE}}\). Alignment: Project \(u_i\) (pooled \(M_t\)) and \(g_i\) -> \(L_{\text{GeoTop}}\). Total loss: \(L = L_{\text{CE}} + \beta L_{\text{GeoTop}}\).

**Critical path:**  
Raw EEG -> EEG Encoder -> \(S_t\) -----\  
                              -> Cross-Attention (Q from Graph Encoder's \(C_t\), K/V from \(S_t\)) -> \(\tilde{S}_t\) -> Tangent -> \(M_t\) -> Classifier.  
Simultaneously, Graph Encoder concurrently produces \(C_t\) (for queries) and \(g_i\) (for alignment). The alignment loss links the pooled tangent embedding \(u_i\) and \(g_i\).

**Design tradeoffs:**  
- **Graph sparsity (τ):** Sparse graphs (low τ) reduce noise but may miss weak connections; dense graphs (high τ) capture more but introduce redundancy. τ=3 optimal on TUSZ.
- **Loss weighting (β):** High β over-regularizes, suppressing task-specific power; low β under-utilizes geometric prior. β=0.3 optimal.
- **Query design:** Node SPD (\(C_t\)) outperforms edge SPD; mixed queries degraded, suggesting domain-consistent, single-source queries are cleaner for manifold attention.
- **Geometry vs. complexity:** Log-Euclidean distance and BiMap/ReEig ensure SPD constraints but add computational overhead (eigendecompositions) versus Euclidean GNNs.

**Failure signatures:**  
- **Visual (Tangent Space):** PCA of \(M_t\) shows overlapping, poorly separated class clusters; reconstructed spatial patterns are blurry, spatially drifted, or fragmented.
- **Visual (Topography):** Reconstructed topographies [Figure 5] deviate from ground truth.
- **Quantitative:** Large gap between training and test accuracy without meta-optimization; high variance across cross-validation folds.
- **Loss behavior:** \(L_{\text{GeoTop}}\) converges too low (over-regularization) or fails to decrease (misaligned views).

**First 3 experiments:**  
1. **Ablate graph pathway:** Remove cross-attention; use only \(S_t\) (SPDNet baseline) or only \(C_t\) (graph SPD -> tangent). Expect: \(S_t\) alone loses dynamic topology; \(C_t\) alone may have unstable geometry.
2. **Validate manifold attention metric:** Replace Log-Euclidean distance in Eq. (1) with a Euclidean dot-product. Expect: Performance drop (86.4% → 84.0% per Appendix).
3. **Probe \(L_{\text{GeoTop}}\) sensitivity:** Train with varied β (0.0, 0.1, 0.3, 0.5, 0.7). Expect: U-shaped performance curve; visualize tangent clusters with/without \(L_{\text{GeoTop}}\).

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
1. **Adaptive graph learning:** The paper notes that dynamic graphs are fixed after initial construction. Future work will explore joint structure optimization to enable end-to-end adaptive graph learning.
2. **Alternative Riemannian metrics:** The paper uses Log-Euclidean metric; comparison with other metrics like affine-invariant is left for future work to assess impact on SPD representation for EEG.
3. **Generalization across EEG paradigms:** Evaluation is limited to seizure detection and motor imagery; generalizability to other tasks (e.g., emotion recognition, sleep staging) requires further validation.
4. **Dynamic hyperparameter β:** The fixed β=0.3 was set via validation; adaptive schemes (e.g., learned or scheduled) to adjust the geometry-task tradeoff during training are unexplored.

## Limitations
- Missing implementation details for dynamic graph construction (STFT parameters, similarity metric) and the meta-optimizer for alternating Riemannian/Euclidean gradients hinder exact replication.
- Hyperparameter sensitivity (graph sparsity τ, loss weight β) may limit transfer to other EEG tasks or channel configurations; optimal values are dataset-specific.
- Scalability to high-density EEG (e.g., >32 channels) is untested; eigendecompositions could become computationally prohibitive.

## Confidence
- **Accuracy gains on TUSZ/BCIC:** Medium (results clear but replication uncertain due to incomplete protocols).
- **Necessity of proposed mechanisms (cross-attention, alignment loss):** Medium (ablations show drops but interactions not fully explored).
- **Superior robustness and lower variance:** Low (claims based on limited experiments without systematic stress testing).
