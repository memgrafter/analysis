# CtrlAttack: A Unified Attack on World-Model Control in Diffusion Models

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2603.13435
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.13435
- **Reference count:** 40
- **Primary result:** CtrlAttack achieves >90% white-box and >80% black-box attack success rates with minimal perceptual impact (ΔFID≤6, ΔFVD≤130) by manipulating trajectory control signals in diffusion-based I2V models.

## Executive Summary
Diffusion-based image-to-video (I2V) models rely on trajectory control conditions to guide object motion. This paper uncovers a novel attack surface: by perturbing these control trajectories, an adversary can systematically disrupt the model's state transitions and produce videos with incorrect motion while maintaining visual quality. The proposed CtrlAttack formulates adversarial perturbations as a temporally structured velocity field, which is integrated into a smooth displacement field and applied either to internal feature sampling (white-box) or directly to the input trajectory (black-box). Experiments on two I2V models show high attack success rates (>90% white-box, >80% black-box) with negligible degradation in perceptual metrics (ΔFID≤6, ΔFVD≤130), demonstrating a stealthy and effective threat.

## Method Summary
CtrlAttack represents adversarial perturbations using a low-dimensional temporal code \(c_t\) that is transformed via a discrete cosine transform (DCT) basis into a velocity field \(v_t\). This velocity field is temporally integrated to produce a displacement field \(u_t = \sum_{\tau \leq t} v_\tau\), ensuring smoothness and integrability. In the white-box setting, the displacement directly deforms the internal region-of-interest (ROI) feature sampling grid, thereby affecting the model's state transitions during denoising. In the black-box setting, a mapping function \(\Delta(\cdot)\) projects \(u_t\) onto the bounding box control signal, and the perturbation is optimized via Natural Evolution Strategies (NES) without internal access. A unified objective encourages the attacked video to deviate from the intended motion trajectory while preserving overall visual coherence. This design allows the same perturbation structure to be effective under both attack paradigms, exploiting the sensitivity of I2V models to smooth trajectory deformations.

## Key Results
- Attack success rates exceed 90% in white-box and 80% in black-box settings across victim models.
- Perceptual quality remains high: ΔFID ≤ 6 and ΔFVD ≤ 130, indicating minimal visual degradation.
- Ablation studies confirm the necessity of temporal coupling and integration for effective, sustained trajectory deviation.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1
- **Claim:** Representing adversarial perturbations as a low-dimensional, temporally structured velocity field enables systematic manipulation of the I2V model's state transition process while maintaining smooth control signals.
- **Mechanism:** The attack defines a time-dependent velocity field \(v_t\) parameterized via DCT basis coefficients. This field is integrated into a displacement field \(u_t = \sum_{\tau \leq t} v_\tau\), which is added to the original trajectory condition \(\tau_t\) to produce a perturbed trajectory \(\tilde{\tau}_t = \tau_t + u_t\). This construction inherently enforces first-order temporal continuity on the perturbation, making it smooth and integrable.
- **Core assumption:** The victim I2V model's state evolution is sensitive to smooth, low-frequency deformations applied to its control trajectory. The model's internal consistency mechanisms assume trajectory smoothness, so structured perturbations can exploit this prior without causing immediate perceptual artifacts.
- **Evidence anchors:** Abstract states "we represent the perturbation as a low-dimensional velocity field and construct a continuous displacement field via temporal integration, thereby affecting the model’s state transitions while maintaining temporal consistency"; Section 4.1 formalizes the definitions and discusses integrability.
- **Break condition:** If the victim model's trajectory control mechanism is inherently robust to low-frequency offset patterns (e.g., via strong normalization or trajectory clipping), the integrated displacement may not induce significant state deviation.

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim:** Temporal integration of the velocity field into a displacement field (\(u_t = \sum v_\tau\)) is critical for inducing coherent, long-horizon trajectory deviations that accumulate over time, rather than causing isolated, frame-wise disruptions.
- **Mechanism:** By integrating velocity over time, perturbations at early timesteps contribute to displacements at all future timesteps. This allows a small, smooth perturbation to gradually accumulate into a significant trajectory shift, aligning with the model's state transition dynamics which operate over a temporal horizon.
- **Core assumption:** The I2V model's state at time \(t+1\) depends causally on the state and control at time \(t\). A persistent displacement in the control signal will therefore propagate forward through the generation process, leading to a systematic deviation from the intended trajectory.
- **Evidence anchors:** Section 4.1 notes that the velocity-based parameterization "yields more stable, predictable, and controllable state-transition manipulation"; Section 5.3 ablation shows that removing temporal coupling weakens attack effectiveness.
- **Break condition:** If the victim uses strong frame-by-frame correction mechanisms (e.g., explicit optical flow warping that resists drift), accumulated displacement may be counteracted at each step, limiting long-term deviation.

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim:** A unified parameterization (low-dimensional temporal code \(c_t\) → velocity field \(v_t\) → displacement \(u_t\)) allows the same attack structure to be instantiated effectively in both white-box (manipulating internal state representations) and black-box (manipulating control inputs) settings.
- **Mechanism:** In white-box, \(u_t\) deforms the internal ROI feature sampling grid (\(\tilde{g}_t = g_t + \hat{u}_t\)). In black-box, \(u_t\) is mapped via a frozen \(\Delta(\cdot)\) directly onto the bounding box control signal (\(\tilde{b}_t = b_t + \Delta(u_t)\)). The optimization objective changes (feature-level vs. output-level), but the perturbation structure (\(c_t\) basis coefficients) is shared.
- **Core assumption:** The vulnerability resides in the *control process* itself, not in a specific implementation detail. Therefore, corrupting the control signal at its source (the trajectory) or at its point of application (internal sampling) both disrupt the same underlying state transition mechanism.
- **Evidence anchors:** Abstract mentions "mapping the perturbation to the observation space, making the method applicable to both white-box and black-box attack settings"; Sections 4.2 and 4.3 detail the two instantiations.
- **Break condition:** If white-box and black-box victim models implement trajectory control via fundamentally different, non-sharable mechanisms (e.g., one uses bounding boxes, another uses density maps), a single parameterization may fail to transfer effectively between settings.

## Architecture Onboarding
### Component map
The attack system comprises: (1) Victim I2V Model (G), which takes an image I, a trajectory condition \(\tau^{1:T}\), and noise z to produce a video \(x^{1:T}\); (2) Trajectory Control Signal (\(\tau^{1:T}\)), e.g., bounding boxes specifying object motion; (3) Perturbation Module (P), which generates a velocity field \(v_t\) from a low-dimensional temporal code \(c_t\) (via DCT basis P) and integrates it to a displacement field \(u_t\); (4) Attack Application Point: in white-box, \(u_t\) perturbs the internal ROI feature sampling grid (\(\tilde{g}_t = g_t + \hat{u}_t\)); in black-box, \(\Delta(u_t)\) is added to the input trajectory (\(\tilde{b}_t = b_t + \Delta(u_t)\)); (5) Attack Objective (J), which in white-box may align features and in black-box uses an external motion estimator (ObjMC) to measure deviation from intended velocity.

### Critical path
The attack proceeds as: Initialize low-D temporal code \(c_t\) → Compute velocity field \(v_t = c_t P\) → Integrate to displacement \(u_t = \sum_{\tau \leq t} v_\tau\) → Apply perturbation (either to internal grid or input trajectory) → Run victim model G with perturbed inputs → Compute loss J on output/features → Update \(c_t\) via gradient descent (white-box) or NES (black-box). This loop iterates until convergence or query budget exhaustion.

### Design tradeoffs
- *Low-dimensional basis (k) vs. Attack Strength:* Lower k ensures smoothness and reduces query cost (black-box) but limits expressivity; higher k may increase ASR but risk visible artifacts or instability.
- *White-box precision vs. Black-box practicality:* White-box directly targets internal state alignment but requires model access; black-box is universally applicable but query-inefficient and relies on external trackers.
- *Temporal smoothness vs. Stealth:* Integrated displacement is smooth by design, helping evade frame-wise detectors but potentially limiting abrupt, high-impact attacks.

### Failure signatures
- High ΔFID/ΔFVD (>6, >130): Perturbations cause visible artifacts (blur, flicker), indicating over-corruption or poor control.
- Low ASR (<80%): Perturbation magnitude too low, basis k insufficient, or victim model inherently robust to this deformation type.
- Black-box query explosion: NES fails to converge, suggesting objective J is noisy or non-responsive to \(c_t\) changes.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
1. **Multi-object or multi-trajectory interactions:** How do interactions affect vulnerability and attack strategies? The paper focuses on a single trajectory; extension to multiple objects remains future work.
2. **Alternative temporal basis functions:** What is the impact of using wavelets, learned bases, or random projections instead of DCT on attack effectiveness, optimization stability, and perceptual stealth?
3. **Architectural correlates of vulnerability:** Which specific components or training paradigms of I2V models (e.g., free inference-time optimization, cross-attention mechanisms) correlate with increased susceptibility to trajectory-control attacks?
4. **More query-efficient black-box strategies:** Can gradient-free methods beyond NES (e.g., CMA-ES, Bayesian optimization) improve query efficiency for this structured problem?
5. **Perceptual limits of stealth:** Do automatic metrics (FID, FVD) fully capture detectable artifacts, or can humans reliably perceive motion inconsistencies even when these metrics show small changes?

## Limitations
- **Generalizability:** Evaluations only on two diffusion-based I2V models; effectiveness on other video generation paradigms (e.g., transformer-based, autoregressive) remains unknown.
- **Stealthiness assessment:** Relies solely on FID/FVD; no evaluation against temporal anomaly detectors or forensic analysis to determine detectability.
- **Black-box practicality:** Query cost of NES not studied; attack may degrade under strict query budgets or with inaccurate external motion estimators.
