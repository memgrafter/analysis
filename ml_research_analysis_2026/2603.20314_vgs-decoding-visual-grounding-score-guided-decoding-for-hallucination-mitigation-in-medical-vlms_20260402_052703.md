# VGS-Decoding: Visual Grounding Score Guided Decoding for Hallucination Mitigation in Medical VLMs

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2603.20314  
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.20314  
- **Reference count:** 28  
- **Primary result:** A training-free decoding method that amplifies visually grounded tokens and suppresses hallucinations, achieving up to +9.12% accuracy on VQA-RAD and +8.98% recall on MIMIC-Diff-VQA with only 2× inference overhead.

## Executive Summary
Medical vision-language models (VLMs) often generate hallucinated responses by over-relying on language priors rather than visual evidence. This paper introduces Visual Grounding Score Guided Decoding (VGS-Decoding), a training-free inference technique that mitigates hallucinations without additional training. VGS-Decoding computes a per-token visual grounding score by comparing token probability distributions from the original image and a noise-distorted version. Tokens whose probabilities decrease under distortion are considered visually grounded and are amplified, while tokens that maintain or increase probability (indicative of hallucinations) are suppressed. Evaluations on VQA-RAD and MIMIC-Diff-VQA across three medical VLMs (LLaVA-Med, CheXagent, MedGemma) show consistent improvements, with up to +9.12% overall accuracy gain and +8.98% open-ended recall improvement, at a modest 2× inference cost.

## Method Summary
VGS-Decoding operates during the autoregressive decoding phase. For each candidate token at a decoding step, the method computes two probability scores: one using the original image and another using a noise-distorted version of the same image. The Visual Grounding Score (VGS) is defined as the difference between these two probabilities (distorted minus original). A negative VGS indicates that the token's likelihood drops when visual evidence is degraded, suggesting strong grounding in the original image. The decoding probabilities are then adjusted: tokens with negative VGS are amplified (probability increased), while tokens with non-negative VGS are suppressed (probability decreased). This adjustment is applied at each decoding step, steering the generation toward tokens that are more sensitive to visual perturbations—and thus more likely to be visually grounded—without any model retraining or fine-tuning.

## Key Results
- Up to **+9.12%** improvement in overall accuracy on VQA-RAD across LLaVA-Med, CheXagent, and MedGemma.
- Up to **+8.98%** improvement in open-ended recall on MIMIC-Diff-VQA.
- Consistent gains across all three tested medical VLMs with a reported **2× inference overhead** and no additional training data or parameters.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The method assumes that visually grounded tokens are more sensitive to alterations in the input image; distorting the image with noise should reduce the model's confidence in such tokens because their generation depends on specific visual features. Conversely, hallucinations—tokens generated from language priors or dataset biases—should be less affected or even become more likely when visual evidence is degraded, as the model falls back on statistical patterns. By amplifying tokens that drop in probability (negative VGS) and suppressing those that do not, VGS-Decoding biases the decoding process toward outputs that are more consistent with the actual image content. The noise distortion acts as a lightweight, unsupervised way to probe the model's reliance on visual versus linguistic cues.

## Foundational Learning
1. **Visual Grounding**  
   *Why needed:* To ensure model outputs are tied to actual image content rather than textual priors.  
   *Quick check:* Does token probability decrease when the image is noise-distorted?

2. **Language Prior Bias**  
   *Why needed:* Medical VLMs trained on curated datasets may rely on frequent medical terms regardless of visual evidence, causing hallucinations.  
   *Quick check:* Compare token probabilities on original vs. distorted images; language-prior tokens show little change.

3. **Hallucination in VLMs**  
   *Why needed:* Generated text that is unsupported or contradictory to the image is a critical failure mode in medical contexts.  
   *Quick check:* Suppress tokens with non-negative VGS, which are more likely hallucinations.

4. **Noise Distortion as Probe**  
   *Why needed:* Simulates degradation of visual evidence to test token dependence on the image.  
   *Quick check:* Apply simple noise (e.g., Gaussian) and observe probability shifts.

5. **Token-Level Probability Adjustment**  
   *Why needed:* Decoding-time intervention allows fine-grained control without retraining.  
   *Quick check:* Modify next-token probabilities based on VGS during beam search or sampling.

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component map:**  
Image + Question Encoder → Token Decoding Loop → [For each candidate token: compute p_original and p_distorted] → VGS = p_distorted - p_original → Adjust probabilities (amplify if VGS<0, suppress if VGS>=0) → Sample/select next token → Repeat until end-of-sequence.

**Critical path:**  
1. Encode image and question.  
2. At each decoding step, for every token in the vocabulary, compute the probability given the current context using both the original image and a noise-distorted version.  
3. Calculate VGS for each candidate.  
4. Modify the probability distribution (e.g., additive or multiplicative adjustment based on VGS).  
5. Choose the next token from the adjusted distribution.  
6. Append token and repeat.

**Design tradeoffs:**  
- **Noise severity:** Too little noise may not sufficiently affect grounded tokens; too much may destroy all visual information, making VGS meaningless.  
- **Overhead:** Two forward passes per token (original and distorted) roughly double inference cost; caching could reduce but not eliminate this.  
- **Adjustment strength:** The scaling factor for amplification/suppression is a hyperparameter; improper tuning may overcorrect or undercorrect.

**Failure signatures:**  
- If the model is already robust to noise (e.g., from data augmentation), VGS may fail to distinguish grounded from hallucinated tokens.  
- Over-suppression could occur for tokens that are grounded but for which the model's probability increases under noise (false negatives).  
- Generated text may become less fluent or coherent if amplification/suppression is too aggressive.

**First three experiments:**  
1. Ablate noise type (Gaussian, blur, salt-and-pepper) and severity to measure impact on VGS-Decoding performance and robustness.  
2. Analyze VGS distributions for gold-standard correct tokens versus incorrect tokens to validate the core assumption.  
3. Test on a new medical VQA dataset (e.g., MIMIC-CXR) and a general-domain VLM (e.g., LLaVA) to assess cross-dataset and cross-domain generalization.

## Limitations
- Noise distortion parameters (type, severity) are not systematically studied; VGS robustness may vary.  
- Evaluation limited to VQA-RAD and MIMIC-Diff-VQA; generalizability to other medical datasets or modalities (e.g., pathology, dermatology) is unclear.  
- No statistical significance tests (e.g., confidence intervals) for reported gains; confidence in improvements is uncertain.  
- The "2× inference overhead" claim may vary with model architecture and hardware; real-world latency not measured.  
- The assumption that VGS indicates visual grounding is not validated against ground-truth grounding annotations.  
- No human evaluation of clinical relevance or safety of the generated outputs.
