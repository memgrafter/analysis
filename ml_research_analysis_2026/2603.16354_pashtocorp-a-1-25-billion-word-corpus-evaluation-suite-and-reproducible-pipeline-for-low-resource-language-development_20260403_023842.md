# PashtoCorp: A 1.25-Billion-Word Corpus, Evaluation Suite, and Reproducible Pipeline for Low-Resource Language Development

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2603.16354 | Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.16354 | Reference count: 19
- Primary result: Domain-adaptive MLM pretraining on a 100M-word PashtoCorp subset reduces held-out perplexity by 25.1% and improves WikiANN NER entity F1 by 10% relative, with ~7× lower training variance.

## Executive Summary
PashtoCorp addresses the severe underrepresentation of Pashto in NLP by constructing a 1.25-billion-word corpus from 39 web sources via a reproducible pipeline that includes Arabic-script tokenization, deduplication, and filtering. Continued pretraining of XLM-R-base on a 100M-word subset yields substantial intrinsic and extrinsic gains: a 25.1% reduction in held-out perplexity and a 10% relative improvement in WikiANN NER entity F1, along with dramatically reduced training variance. The corpus covers 97.9% of WikiANN entity vocabulary, and Wikipedia is identified as the most critical source for NER. This work also establishes the first published LLM baseline for Pashto on Belebele (64.6% accuracy). All resources—corpus, pretrained model, and code—are publicly released to support low-resource language development.

## Method Summary
The authors assembled PashtoCorp by scraping 39 diverse web sources and applying a reproducible preprocessing pipeline: (1) language identification via Pashto Unicode range (U+0600–U+06FF) filtering, retaining documents with ≥70% Pashto tokens; (2) deduplication using SHA-256 hashes; (3) minimum token filter (≥10 tokens). For domain-adaptive pretraining, a 100M-word subset of the corpus was used to continue MLM training on the `xlm-roberta-base` checkpoint for 750 gradient steps (batch 32, LR 1e-4, 200 warmup steps, MLM probability 0.15). Fine-tuning for NER on WikiANN Pashto used the standard 100/100/100 sentence split, 10 epochs, batch 16, LR 5e-5, and reported entity-level F1 over 5 random seeds to measure variance.

## Key Results
- Held-out perplexity reduced by 25.1% (8.08→6.06) after continued pretraining on the 100M-word subset.
- WikiANN NER entity F1 improved by 10% relative (19.0%→21.0%) with nearly 7× reduction in training variance; largest gain at 50 training sentences (+27%).
- Corpus covers 97.9% of WikiANN entity vocabulary; Wikipedia (0.7% of documents) is the most critical source—its removal in ablation reduces NER F1 by 47%.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
Domain-adaptive pretraining on in-domain Pashto text exposes the model to language-specific patterns, vocabulary, and syntactic structures that are underrepresented in the original multilingual XLM-R training. The large-scale corpus provides a sufficient signal to adjust the model's representations, leading to lower perplexity on held-out Pashto text. For NER, the adaptation improves entity recognition by learning contextual cues and entity mention patterns from diverse sources, with Wikipedia's high-quality, structured text being particularly valuable for entity spans. The dramatic reduction in training variance indicates more stable gradient updates and better initialization from the adapted model, especially crucial in low-data regimes.

## Foundational Learning
- **Masked Language Modeling (MLM)**: Randomly masks tokens for context prediction; foundational for self-supervised pretraining. *Why needed*: Enables learning bidirectional representations without labeled data. *Quick check*: Confirm MLM probability (0.15) and loss masking implementation.
- **Unicode-based Language Filtering**: Uses script ranges (U+0600–U+06FF) to identify Pashto documents; critical for low-resource languages lacking robust LID tools. *Why needed*: Ensures corpus linguistic purity from noisy web sources. *Quick check*: Verify ≥70% Pashto token threshold applied consistently.
- **Deduplication via SHA-256**: Hashes documents to remove exact duplicates; prevents overfitting to repeated content. *Why needed*: Maintains dataset diversity and realistic token distribution. *Quick check*: Compare document counts before/after deduplication to assess retention.
- **Vocabulary Coverage Analysis**: Measures overlap between corpus vocabulary and downstream task entities; indicates corpus suitability for specific applications. *Why needed*: Predicts potential performance on tasks like NER. *Quick check*: Compute percentage of task-specific entities present in corpus.
- **Sample Efficiency in Low-Data Regimes**: Performance gains at very small training sizes (e.g., 50 sentences) demonstrate effective knowledge transfer from pretraining. *Why needed*: Validates utility for truly low-resource scenarios. *Quick check*: Plot downstream F1 against training set size to identify inflection points.

## Architecture Onboarding
Component map: Web Scraping -> Script Filtering -> Deduplication -> Minimum Token Filter -> Subsampling -> MLM Pretraining -> NER Fine-tuning -> Evaluation

Critical path: Data collection (scraping) → preprocessing (filtering, deduplication) → model training (pretraining, fine-tuning) → evaluation. Delays in scraping or preprocessing directly impact training timeline.

Design tradeoffs: Using a 100M-word subset vs. full 1.25B-word corpus reduced compute but may underutilize data; Arabic-script filtering may inadvertently exclude code-switched or Roman-script Pashto text; fixed small NER train set (100 sentences) increases sensitivity to random seed variance.

Failure signatures:
1. Tokenization mismatch: Using a tokenizer without the Arabic-script filter changes document counts and vocabulary.
2. Data version drift: Using a different snapshot of web sources alters document/vocabulary statistics.
3. Evaluation split inconsistencies: Using different NER train/test splits or seeds invalidates variance comparisons.

First experiments:
1. Replicate perplexity reduction on the held-out 2,000-doc set.
2. Fine-tune on WikiANN NER with the exact 5 seeds to confirm mean F1 and variance.
3. Perform source ablation at the individual source level (not just domain groups) to identify top contributors.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- What performance gains in perplexity and WikiANN NER F1 are attainable by continuing MLM pretraining on the full 1.25B-word PashtoCorp corpus? (The 100M-word results are lower bounds.)
- Which individual sources within PashtoCorp are most vital for NER performance? (Group-level leave-one-out masks variation among sources within the same domain.)
- How can PashtoCorp be expanded to better represent under-documented Pashto dialects (e.g., Pakistani Pashto, Waziri), and does this improve model fairness and dialect-specific downstream performance?

## Limitations
- The exact 100M-word subset used for pretraining is unspecified (random? first documents? stratified?), introducing unreported variability in reproduction.
- WikiANN NER evaluation uses an unrevealed set of 5 random seeds and a tiny 100-sentence train set, making exact replication of the low-variance results difficult.
- The held-out perplexity set (2,000 documents) is not clearly released; corpus versioning is not pinned, risking drift in web-sourced data.

## Confidence
- **High**: Corpus construction pipeline (filtering, deduplication) and coverage metrics (97.9% of WikiANN entity vocabulary).
- **Medium**: Continued pretraining gains (perplexity −25.1%) and WikiANN NER improvement (+10% relative), due to unreleased subset sampling and evaluation seeds.
- **Low**: Belebele LLM baseline (64.6%), as it is a single-model, first-effort benchmark with no prior Pashto results for comparison.
