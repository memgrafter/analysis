# Enactor: From Traffic Simulators to Surrogate World Models

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2603.18266
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.18266
- **Reference count:** 2
- **Primary result:** A transformer-based world model using polar coordinates and closed-loop simulation reduces KL-divergence on speed and travel-time distributions by over 10x compared to IntTrajSim and cuts red-light violations from 199 to 5 with rear-bumper awareness.

## Executive Summary
Enactor addresses the challenge of long-horizon physical consistency and intersection-specific dynamics in traffic simulation. It introduces a transformer-based world model that represents vehicle states in polar coordinates relative to the intersection center and uses spatial and temporal attention to model multi-vehicle interactions. Trained and evaluated in a closed-loop simulation-in-the-loop framework over 40,000 timesteps (4000 seconds), Enactor significantly outperforms the prior state-of-the-art (IntTrajSim) in matching realistic speed and travel-time distributions and improves safety metrics. An enhanced variant, Enactor(N), incorporating rear-bumper awareness, dramatically reduces red-light violations.

## Method Summary
Enactor learns a differentiable world model for signalized intersections. It encodes each vehicle's state and context—including neighboring vehicles, map polylines, and traffic signals—using a polar coordinate system anchored at the intersection center. A transformer architecture applies spatial attention per timestep to capture interactions and temporal attention over a historical context window to capture motion patterns. The model predicts a Gaussian distribution over Cartesian displacements (Δx, Δy) conditioned on ground-truth speed and heading. During closed-loop evaluation, the model's predictions are recursively fed back as inputs, enabling long-horizon rollout without ground-truth data.

## Key Results
- Enactor reduces KL-divergence on speed and travel-time distributions by more than 10× compared to IntTrajSim over 4000-second closed-loop simulations.
- The base Enactor model still exhibits safety issues (e.g., high red-light violations, Time-to-Collision events).
- Enactor(N), by adding explicit rear-bumper awareness, reduces red-light violations from 199 to 5 at the first intersection.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1: Polar Coordinate Representation
- **Claim:** Polar coordinates anchored at the intersection center enable more physically consistent trajectory generation, especially for curved turning movements.
- **Mechanism:** Expressing states (radial distance, angle, speed, heading) relative to the center aligns with the natural geometry of intersection flows, simplifying the learning of curvilinear paths and enforcing geometric constraints.
- **Core assumption:** Intersection traffic can be effectively modeled using a radial coordinate system where the intersection center is a natural origin.
- **Evidence anchors:** [abstract] "transformer-based world model using polar coordinates"; [section 3] "to better capture the curved trajectories commonly observed in traffic intersections."
- **Break condition:** If intersection geometry is highly non-radial or movements are predominantly straight, polar coordinates may add unnecessary complexity.

### Mechanism 2: Spatial and Temporal Attention
- **Claim:** Transformer attention effectively models multi-vehicle interactions and temporal dependencies for realistic traffic dynamics.
- **Mechanism:** Spatial attention computes weighted interactions with neighbors, map polylines, and signals per timestep. Temporal attention aggregates historical spatial embeddings to capture motion evolution. This flexible, non-fixed graph approach handles dynamic interaction patterns.
- **Core assumption:** Traffic behavior emerges from contemporaneous interactions and past state history, modelable via attention over variable-sized sets and time.
- **Evidence anchors:** [abstract] "capture the actor-actor interaction"; [section 4] detailed attention equations.
- **Break condition:** If attention fails to identify relevant neighbors due to noisy inputs or distribution shift, it may produce unrealistic collisions or ignore critical signals.

### Mechanism 3: Closed-Loop Simulation
- **Claim:** Closed-loop simulation is crucial for long-horizon physical consistency by exposing the model to its own prediction errors.
- **Mechanism:** Recursively using model predictions as inputs mimics deployment, exposing the model to covariate shift and error propagation, encouraging learning of stable dynamics over many steps. The 40,000-step evaluation tests this property.
- **Core assumption:** A model trained only via teacher-forcing may not generalize to multi-step rollout due to exposure bias.
- **Evidence anchors:** [abstract] "trained in closed loop" and "simulation-in-the-loop"; [section 5] "run for 40000 steps (4000 seconds)".
- **Break condition:** If training is purely teacher-forced (no closed-loop exposure), closed-loop performance may diverge rapidly.

## Foundational Learning
- **Concept:** Polar coordinate representation for trajectory modeling
  - **Why needed here:** Intersection traffic involves curved paths around a central point; polar coordinates naturally encode radial and angular components, simplifying learning of turning dynamics and geometric constraints.
  - **Quick check:** How would you convert a vehicle's Cartesian position (x, y) to polar coordinates (r, θ) relative to an intersection center? What does each component convey about the vehicle's relation to the intersection?

  - **Why needed here:** Vehicles interact with numerous neighbors and infrastructure in a dynamic, non-fixed graph. Attention provides a flexible way to weight the influence of each entity based on the current context.

  - **Why needed here:** To simulate traffic flow over long horizons (e.g., 4000 seconds), the model must generate consistent trajectories without ground truth inputs. World models learn environment dynamics enabling such rollouts.
  - **Quick check:** What is the difference between an open-loop trajectory predictor and a closed-loop world model? Why is closed-loop evaluation critical for traffic simulation?

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map:** Input Encoder -> Spatial Attention -> Spatial Embedding -> Temporal Attention -> Output Distribution -> Sample State -> (loop back to Input Encoder)
- **Critical path:** For each actor: encode current state and context → spatial attention → spatial embedding. For each future step: combine historical spatial embeddings with learned motion query and positional encoding → temporal attention → latent → spatial attention → predict Δx, Δy, speed, heading → sample new state → update all actors → repeat.
- **Design tradeoffs:**
  - Polar vs. Cartesian: Polar simplifies curved trajectories but may complicate straight-line modeling; intersections naturally involve curves.
  - Spatial attention scope: Using nearest neighbors by distance is efficient but may miss globally relevant information (e.g., distant traffic signal); paper uses a local region but does not specify radius.
  - Training regime: Teacher-forcing stabilizes training but may cause exposure bias; closed-loop training would improve robustness but is more challenging. Paper mentions "closed-loop training" but implements teacher-forcing, indicating a tradeoff.
- **Failure signatures:**
  - High counts of red-light violations, mid-intersection stoppages, pre-stopbar stoppages.
  - Elevated time-to-collision (TTC) events or unsafe decelerations.
  - Speed or travel-time distributions with high KL divergence from ground truth.
  - Trajectories with physical impossibilities (e.g., teleportation, reverse motion).
- **First 3 experiments:**
  1. Baseline comparison: Train Enactor and IntTrajSim on identical intersection data; evaluate closed-loop for 4000 seconds; compare aggregate KL divergence (speed, travel time) and safety metrics.
  2. Coordinate system ablation: Replace polar representation with Cartesian (x, y, speed, heading); train and evaluate on same data; measure impact on safety metrics and KL divergence to test importance of polar coordinates.
  3. Cross-intersection generalization: Train on one intersection geometry (e.g., four-leg with all lanes) and test on a variant (e.g., with middle lanes removed) without retraining; assess metric degradation to evaluate transferability.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- **Open Question 1:** How can the model's performance on close-range interaction metrics like Time-to-Collision (TTC) be improved?
  - **Basis in paper:** [explicit] The conclusion states: "our results highlight opportunities for further improvement, particularly as evidenced by performance on the Time-to-Collision metric. This suggests that more accurate modeling of close-range interactions and deceleration behavior remains an open challenge."
  - **Why unresolved:** The model achieves good aggregate metrics but exhibits poor safety metric performance (e.g., high TTC events), indicating a gap in learning precise, safe braking behaviors during critical interactions.
  - **What evidence would resolve it:** A revised architecture or training objective that specifically reduces TTC events in closed-loop simulation, validated by statistically significant improvement on the TTC metric across multiple intersections.
- **Open Question 2:** What is a more robust and comprehensive set of evaluation metrics for assessing the safety and realism of generated intersection traffic?
  - **Basis in paper:** [explicit] The conclusion identifies: "the development of a more robust and comprehensive set of evaluation metrics that can faithfully assess the realism and safety of simulated traffic remains an important direction for future research."
  - **Why unresolved:** Current metrics (e.g., red-light violations, TTC) are necessary but may be insufficient to capture all failure modes of a generative world model; the paper notes that low trajectory error does not guarantee rule compliance.
  - **What evidence would resolve it:** A proposed suite of metrics correlated with expert human evaluation or real-world accident data, demonstrating higher predictive validity for safety-critical outcomes than existing metrics.
- **Open Question 3:** Can transfer learning enable a model trained on one intersection geometry to generalize to new, unseen intersection configurations?
  - **Basis in paper:** [explicit] The future work states: "In future work, we aim to investigate transfer learning strategies that enable driving behaviors learned at one intersection to be adapted to new and unseen intersection configurations."
  - **Why unresolved:** The model is currently trained and evaluated on only two specific intersection geometries; its ability to generalize to novel topological layouts (e.g., different lane counts, turning lanes) is unknown.
  - **What evidence would resolve it:** An experiment where a model pre-trained on a source intersection is fine-tuned with limited data on a target intersection, showing improved sample efficiency and performance on the target compared to training from scratch.
- **Open Question 4:** Does the polar coordinate representation fully capture necessary spatial information for safe car-following, specifically the precise longitudinal position of a leading vehicle's rear bumper?
  - **Basis in paper:** [inferred] The ablation study introducing Enactor(N) incorporates "rear-bumper awareness" and dramatically reduces red-light violations (199 to 5). This implies the base model's state representation lacked this critical spatial feature, leading to inaccurate stopping positions.
  - **Why unresolved:** The paper's core state representation (r, sin θ, cos θ, s, sin α, cos α) uses radial distance from the intersection center, which may not encode the gap to the rear of the preceding vehicle—a key variable for safe following.
  - **What evidence would resolve it:** An ablation study comparing the base polar representation against one augmented with explicit rear-bumper relative position, measuring its impact on a suite of safety metrics (e.g., pre-stopbar stoppage, hard braking events).
- **Open Question 5:** How does varying inbound traffic demand (arrival rate/density) affect the learned driving behavior and the model's closed-loop stability?
  - **Basis in paper:** [inferred] The problem definition notes: "In future work we would also like to see how inbound traffic rate or density which captures the flow characteristics entering the intersection effect driving behavior." The current evaluation uses fixed, simulator-generated demand.
  - **Why unresolved:** Traffic density fundamentally changes interaction density, spacing, and the occurrence of stop-and-go waves. The model's performance under high-demand, congested conditions—a critical scenario for traffic engineering—is not evaluated.
  - **What evidence would resolve it:** A systematic evaluation of the model across a range of controlled inbound traffic volumes (e.g., from free-flow to oversaturated), measuring changes in stability metrics (e.g., queue lengths, propagation of disturbances) and safety metrics.

## Limitations
- Hyperparameters (embedding size, attention heads, layers, history/prediction lengths) are not specified, blocking precise replication.
- Neighbor selection mechanism (e.g., radius or k‑nearest) is unclear, making spatial attention input reconstruction ambiguous.
- The second intersection’s geometry and signal timing are not provided, preventing cross‑intersection evaluation reproduction.
- An omitted post‑processing step that likely improves safety metrics is not described; its impact is unknown.
- Polar coordinates assume radial symmetry around the intersection center; for intersections with predominantly straight movements, this representation may not help and could hinder.
- Training regimen is ambiguous: the paper mentions “closed‑loop training” but may use teacher‑forcing, risking exposure bias in long rollouts.

## Confidence
- **Polar coordinate representation:** Medium. The approach is theoretically sound for curved trajectories but lacks an in‑paper ablation; effectiveness likely geometry‑dependent.
- **Spatial attention for interactions:** Medium. The transformer design is detailed, but neighbor set definition and attention visualizations are missing, leaving uncertainty about learned interactions.
- **Closed‑loop simulation:** High for evaluation (40k‑step rollout demonstrated), Low for training (unclear if closed‑loop training was actually used).
- **Rear‑bumper awareness (Enactor(N)):** Medium. The reported reduction in red‑light violations is large but based on a single intersection; generalizability unproven.
