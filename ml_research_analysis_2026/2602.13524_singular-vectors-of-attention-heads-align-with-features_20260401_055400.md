# Singular Vectors of Attention Heads Align with Features

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2602.13524
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.13524
- **Reference count:** 40
- **Primary result:** Singular vectors of attention heads robustly align with linearly represented features under isotropic conditions and bounded interference, detectable via the proposed sparse attention decomposition (SAD) test.

## Executive Summary
This paper investigates when and why the singular vectors of an attention head's QK matrix align with linearly represented features in a model's activations. This alignment is a key but previously unproven assumption in mechanistic interpretability. Using a toy model with directly observable features, theoretical analysis, and a novel diagnostic called sparse attention decomposition (SAD), the authors show that alignment emerges robustly when a head attends to specific feature pairs under isotropic feature distributions and limited interference. They validate SAD in GPT-2 and Pythia models on the IOI task, finding that relative attention decomposes sparsely in the SVD basis during training, correlates with feature presence, and vanishes after random rotations of the basis, confirming that SVF alignment drives the phenomenon.

## Method Summary
The study employs a controlled toy model where token representations are sparse sums of explicit feature vectors. An attention head is trained jointly with an autoencoder to reconstruct inputs and match target attention logits. The QK matrix’s singular vectors are then compared to the true feature directions. Theoretically, they prove alignment is optimal under isotropic feature arrangements. For real models, they analyze pre-trained GPT-2 and Pythia checkpoints on the indirect object identification (IOI) task. They compute the SVD of head QK matrices and decompose "relative attention" (logit differences) in the resulting basis, measuring sparsity as a function of training and after applying random, spectrum-preserving rotations to the SVD bases.

## Key Results
- SVF alignment emerges robustly in the toy model when a head is trained to attend to specific feature pairs, and is theoretically optimal under isotropic feature distributions and bounded interference.
- Sparse attention decomposition (SAD) — where relative attention concentrates on a few SVD basis terms — appears during training in both toy and real models when relevant features are present.
- SAD sparsity vanishes after random rotations of the SVD basis, confirming it is driven by SVF alignment rather than low-rank structure alone.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1: Training Drives Alignment to Target Feature Pairs
When an attention head is trained to produce high logits for specific source-target feature pairs, its QK matrix Ω learns a bilinear form that strongly activates when those features are present. Optimization drives the top singular vectors of Ω to align with those feature directions, as this configuration minimizes attention loss while suppressing responses to irrelevant features to avoid interference. This relies on the linear representation hypothesis (token activations as sums of feature vectors).
- **Evidence anchors:** Toy model experiments show u₀ aligns with w₀ and v₀ aligns with w₁ when only that pair is the training target. Alignment degrades if features are not distinct or interference exceeds head capacity.
### Mechanism 2: Optimality under Isotropy
Under idealized conditions where feature directions are orthogonal (tight frame) and interference is bounded, the optimal Ω for attending to a specific feature pair is a rank-1 matrix whose singular vectors exactly match those features (Theorem 2). With mild anisotropy, approximate alignment holds if feature interference remains low (Theorem 3). This explains why alignment is a natural outcome when features spread isotropically during joint training with the autoencoder.
- **Evidence anchors:** Theorem 2 & 3; observed orthogonalization of non-attended features in the toy model.
### Mechanism 3: SAD as a Diagnostic of Alignment
If SVF alignment holds, the logit ℓ(r,s) decomposes as Σₖ σₖ (rᵀuₖ)(vₖᵀs). When only a few relevant features are present in r and s, only the corresponding terms are large. Therefore, "relative attention" (ℓⱼ - mean(ℓᵢ)) isolates active feature pairs and yields a sparse vector in the σₖ-weighted SVD basis. SAD thus serves as a testable prediction of SVF alignment.
- **Evidence anchors:** SAD emerges during toy model training only for the attended pair; in Pythia, SAD sparsity increases during IOI training and collapses after random SVD basis rotation.

## Foundational Learning
- **Concept: Linear Representation Hypothesis (LRH)**
  - **Why needed:** The entire analysis depends on features being additively encoded in token representations (r = Σ fᵢ wᵢ). Without LRH, the notion of a "feature direction" is undefined.
  - **Quick check:** If a "gender" feature is linear, how does it appear in a residual stream? (Answer: as a direction w such that projecting a residual vector onto w yields a scalar proportional to gender strength.)
- **Concept: Singular Value Decomposition (SVD) of QK Matrix**
  - **Why needed:** SVD yields the basis (U, V) and scales (Σ) that define the attention head's intrinsic computational directions. Alignment is measured as cosine similarity between these singular vectors and feature vectors.
  - **Quick check:** What do left singular vectors (U) represent geometrically? (Answer: Query-space directions that, when projected onto keys, produce the highest activation scores.)
- **Concept: Interference Minimization**
  - **Why needed:** The toy model's autoencoder encourages features to become orthogonal to minimize reconstruction error when features are sparse. Low interference is necessary for multiple feature pairs to each claim distinct singular vectors without superposition.
  - **Quick check:** Why would two distinct concept features interfere? (Answer: If their directions are not orthogonal, activating one partially activates the other, causing reconstruction error when only one is present.)

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map:** Features wᵢ ∈ ℝᴰ → Token r = Σ fᵢ wᵢ → Attention logits ℓ(r,s) = rᵀΩs with Ω=W_QᵀW_K → SVD: Ω = UΣVᵀ.
- **Critical path:**
  1. Initialize feature matrix W and head weights W_Q, W_K randomly.
  2. Sample tokens with sparse feature activations (Bernoulli(p)).
  3. Compute L_recon (autoencoder reconstruction) and L_attn (target logit matching).
  4. Joint optimization (AdamW) causes features to spread isotropically while head singular vectors align with target feature pairs.
- **Design tradeoffs:**
  - Head dimension H vs. number of features of interest: If #features > H, superposition occurs; low-priority features may share smallest singular vectors.
  - Loss weight λ: Balances feature learning (L_recon) vs. attention behavior (L_attn). Too high λ distorts feature isotropy.
  - Feature sparsity p: Lower p requires more training steps for features to appear sufficiently for alignment.
- **Failure signatures:**
  - SAD sparsity metric S(v) does not increase during training or collapses after random SVD rotation.
  - Alignment cosine similarities for top singular-value features remain low (e.g., < 0.5).
  - Toy model feature inner products remain high (failure to orthogonalize).
- **First 3 experiments:**
  1. **Toy single-pair alignment:** Train with N=20, D=10, H=10, target pair (w₀,w₁). Verify |cos(u₀,w₀)|≈1, |cos(v₀,w₁)|≈1, and Σ has one large singular value.
  2. **Robustness sweep:** Vary λ, p, N, H, number of pairs m. Confirm alignment holds across ranges (e.g., λ∈[1,8], p∈[0.1,0.9]).
  3. **SAD validation:** For trained toy model, generate inputs with only target pair present. Compute relative attention decomposition in SVD basis, calculate sparsity S(v). Expect low S(v). For Pythia, load checkpoints, extract QK for IOI circuit heads, plot S(v) over training; verify increase and collapse after rotating U and V.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- **How are features deconflicted when the number of features of interest to an attention head exceeds the head’s representational capacity (i.e., more than H features)?** The paper presents preliminary evidence from Appendix D that the least “important” features share a common singular vector pair, but a general understanding is lacking.
- **Across multiple attention heads in a real model, how are singular vectors collectively allocated to features, and what role do “control signals” (nearly identical singular vectors across heads) play in deconflicting features?** The study is limited to single heads; cross-head allocation and coordination remain unexplored.
- **Is there a higher-level organizational principle governing the set of singular vectors across all heads?** Can we observe coordinated use of singular vectors across multiple heads, as suggested by toy models of superposition? The present work does not examine cross-head coordination.

## Limitations
- Theoretical proofs assume idealized conditions (orthogonal features, bounded interference) that may not fully hold in real models.
- SAD validation in real models is indirect and restricted to the IOI circuit; generalizability to other tasks and heads is unproven.
- The analysis presumes linear feature representations, which is a core but contestable assumption in mechanistic interpretability.

## Confidence
- **Toy model SVF alignment:** High (robust empirical emergence across hyperparameters, supported by theory under isotropic conditions).
- **Theoretical optimality:** Medium (proofs rely on idealized assumptions; real models may violate isotropy and bounded interference).
- **SAD validation in real models:** Medium (SAD correlates with feature presence in Pythia and GPT-2, and vanishes under rotations, but the test is indirect and narrow in scope).

## Next Checks
1. Test SAD across diverse tasks (e.g., factual recall, reasoning) and multiple heads in various model families (Llama, Claude) to assess generalizability.
2. Perform causal interventions by applying spectrum-preserving rotations to SVD bases of key heads and measure impacts on model behavior and task performance.
3. Train small models from scratch with controlled, non-orthogonal feature distributions to verify predicted failure of SVF alignment and SAD, testing the theory's boundary conditions.
