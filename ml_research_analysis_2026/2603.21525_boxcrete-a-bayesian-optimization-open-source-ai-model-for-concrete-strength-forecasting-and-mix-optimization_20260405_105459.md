# BOxCrete: A Bayesian Optimization Open-Source AI Model for Concrete Strength Forecasting and Mix Optimization
## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID**: 2603.21525
- **Source URL**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.21525
- **Reference count**: 40
- **Primary result**: Open-source framework achieving R²=0.94, RMSE=0.69 ksi in strength forecasting and enabling concrete mix designs with ≥6 ksi strength and GWP 120–150 kg CO₂e/yd³.

## Executive Summary
The paper introduces BOxCrete, an open-source AI framework that addresses the challenge of optimizing concrete mix designs for both high compressive strength and low embodied carbon. It combines Gaussian Process (GP) regression for probabilistic time-dependent strength forecasting with multi‑objective Bayesian Optimization to navigate the high‑dimensional mix design space. The system is trained on a newly released dataset of 533 strength measurements from 123 unique mortar and concrete mixes, with features including binder content, water-to-binder ratio, supplementary cementitious materials, and admixture dosages. The GP model achieves strong predictive performance (R² = 0.94, RMSE = 0.69 ksi) on a held-out test set, and the Bayesian optimization loop successfully identifies mix formulations meeting ≥6 ksi at 28 days while maintaining Global Warming Potential (GWP) between 120–150 kg CO₂e/yd³. By releasing both the dataset and code, the study aims to accelerate the development of sustainable, high‑performance concrete.

## Method Summary
BOxCrete tackles two linked tasks: (1) forecasting compressive strength over 1–28 days from mix composition and age, and (2) performing multi‑objective optimization to balance strength against embodied carbon (GWP). The input dataset comprises 533 measurements from 123 mixes, with features such as binder content, water-to-binder ratio, cement/fly ash/slag percentages, HRWR dosage, and aggregate fractions; ages cover 1, 3, 5, 14, and 28 days. GWP is computed per mix using the OpenConcrete LCA tool.

For strength prediction, a Gaussian Process regression model is employed with a composite kernel:
k((x,t),(x′,t′)) = α·k_time(log t, log t′) + β·k_joint((x,log t),(x′,log t′)),
where k_time is an exponentiated quadratic and k_joint is a Matérn‑5/2 with automatic relevance determination (ARD). Artificial observations at age 0 with strength 0 are added to anchor the model. Hyperparameters are optimized by maximizing marginal likelihood. Data are split 80% train / 20% test by mix identity to avoid leakage.

For optimization, BOxCrete uses Expected Hypervolume Improvement (EHVI) via the BoTorch library. The GP surrogate predicts mean strength and uncertainty; GWP is treated as a deterministic objective (or potentially a separate surrogate). The EHVI acquisition function guides the search toward mixes that improve the Pareto front of strength vs. GWP. The framework thus enables inverse design of mix recipes that satisfy user‑defined strength and carbon constraints.

## Key Results
- Predictive performance: R² = 0.94, RMSE = 0.69 ksi on held‑out test mixes.
- Optimization outcome: Concrete mixes achieving ≥6 ksi at 28 days with GWP as low as 120–150 kg CO₂e/yd³.
- Open‑science contribution: Public release of the dataset (533 measurements from 123 mixes) and the BOxCrete codebase.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The success of BOxCrete stems from two synergistic components. First, the GP with composite kernel captures both the temporal evolution of strength (via the time‑only term) and the complex interactions between mix ingredients and age (via the joint term). This structure respects the physical reality that strength gain follows a sigmoidal curve over time and that mix composition influences both the rate and asymptote. The model also provides well‑calibrated uncertainty estimates, which are crucial for guiding Bayesian optimization in regions of high uncertainty. Second, the multi‑objective Bayesian optimization using EHVI efficiently explores the high‑dimensional design space without requiring gradient information. By treating strength prediction probabilistically and GWP deterministically (or with a surrogate if needed), the algorithm balances exploration and exploitation to converge on a set of Pareto‑optimal mixes that meet the dual objectives. The use of mix‑level splits ensures realistic performance estimates, while the inclusion of artificial zero‑age data points helps the GP extrapolate to early ages.

## Foundational Learning
- **Gaussian Process Regression**: *Why needed*: Offers non‑linear regression with explicit uncertainty quantification, enabling safe decision‑making in optimization. *Quick check*: Confirm that predictive variance increases away from training data and that mean predictions capture known trends (e.g., strength increases with age).
- **Composite Kernel Design**: *Why needed*: Separates temporal dynamics from feature‑age interactions, allowing the model to capture the distinct phases of strength development. *Quick check*: Compare model log‑likelihood or predictive performance using the composite kernel versus a standard Matérn kernel on combined features.
- **Multi‑objective Bayesian Optimization (EHVI)**: *Why needed*: Approximates the Pareto front for conflicting objectives (strength vs. GWP) without exhaustive search. *Quick check*: Monitor hypervolume improvement across BO iterations; it should increase and eventually plateau.
- **Life Cycle Assessment (LCA) for GWP**: *Why needed*: Quantifies environmental impact to be minimized alongside mechanical performance. *Quick check*: Recompute GWP for a subset of mixes using the OpenConcrete methodology and verify they match reported values within rounding error.
- **Mix‑level Data Splitting**: *Why needed*: Prevents data leakage where measurements from the same mix appear in both train and test sets, which would artificially inflate performance. *Quick check*: Ensure that all rows for a given mix belong exclusively to either train or test; recompute metrics after enforcing this split to see if they drop substantially.

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component Map**: Data Collection → Preprocessing (add t=0, log‑transform age) → Mix‑level Train/Test Split → GP Model Training with Composite Kernel (BoTorch) → Surrogate Strength Predictor → Bayesian Optimization (EHVI) → Candidate Mix Proposals → GWP Computation (OpenConcrete) → Pareto Front Identification.

**Critical Path**: 1) Acquire and clean the dataset; 2) Perform preprocessing (add artificial zero‑age points, log age); 3) Split mixes into training and testing sets; 4) Train the GP model, optimizing hyperparameters via marginal likelihood; 5) Validate predictive accuracy on the test set; 6) Define objectives (strength at 28 days, minimize GWP) and run Bayesian optimization; 7) Evaluate GWP for candidate mixes and extract non‑dominated solutions.

**Design Tradeoffs**:
- Composite kernel vs. simpler kernel: Greater expressivity captures nuanced strength development but increases hyperparameter count, raising overfitting risk on limited data.
- GP vs. alternative surrogates: GP provides accurate uncertainty estimates and works well with small datasets, but computational cost grows with data size; here the dataset is modest so GP is suitable.
- Deterministic GWP vs. surrogate modeling: GWP is cheap to compute via OpenConcrete, so it is evaluated directly; if it were expensive, a surrogate would be needed, adding approximation error.
- Exploration–exploitation balance: EHVI naturally balances these, but the q‑batch size and number of restarts in BoTorch can affect convergence speed and diversity of candidates.
- Artificial zero‑age observations: They anchor the GP at a physical boundary condition but could bias predictions if the true strength‑age relationship deviates near zero.

**Failure Signatures**:
- Predicted strength curves lose the expected sigmoidal shape or show large early‑age errors → investigate kernel specification and hyperparameter optimization.
- Test metrics (R²/RMSE) are suspiciously high → check for data leakage by ensuring mix‑level split; re‑compute metrics after correcting split.
- Optimization fails to produce mixes meeting strength/GWP targets → verify GWP computation factors and confirm correct integration of BO loop.
- GP hyperparameters converge to extreme values or training fails → examine feature scaling and kernel initial values.

**First Experiments** (exactly 3):

## Limitations
- Exact mix‑level train/test split indices are not disclosed, preventing exact replication of the reported performance metrics.
- The role and precise calculation of GWP within the optimization loop are ambiguous; emission factors, transportation distances, and system boundaries for the OpenConcrete tool are not detailed.
- The dataset (123 mixes, 533 measurements) is relatively small, which may limit the model’s ability to generalize to the full diversity of concrete materials and processes.

## Confidence
- **Dataset release**: High
- **Forecasting metrics (R²=0.94, RMSE=0.69 ksi)**: Medium
- **Optimization results (≥6 ksi, GWP 120–150)**: Low
- **Generalizability**: Low
