# Simplex-to-Euclidean Bijection for Conjugate and Calibrated Multiclass Gaussian Process

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2603.16621
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.16621
- **Reference Count:** 23
- **Primary Result:** A mapping of class probabilities from the simplex to Euclidean space via the isometric log-ratio (ILR) bijection enables conjugate exact inference for multi-class Gaussian process classification, yielding competitive accuracy and significantly better calibration than standard approaches.

## Executive Summary
Multi-class Gaussian process (GP) classification typically suffers from non-conjugate inference and poor probability calibration. This paper introduces a model that uses the isometric log-ratio (ILR) bijection to transform simplex-constrained class probabilities into an unconstrained Euclidean space. Classification then reduces to Gaussian process regression in \(K-1\) latent dimensions, where inference is conjugate and exact. The method employs Gaussian pseudo-observations at class-specific targets and scales to larger datasets via sparse variational inducing points. Empirical evaluations on synthetic and UCI datasets demonstrate that the approach matches or slightly improves the accuracy of a conjugate Dirichlet-based GP baseline while achieving substantially lower expected calibration error (ECE) compared to softmax-based and auxiliary-variable GP classifiers.

## Method Summary
The core innovation is the application of the ILR transformation, an isometric bijection between the probability simplex and \(\mathbb{R}^{K-1}\). This transformation allows the multi-class likelihood to be expressed as a product of independent Gaussians in the ILR space, conditioned on latent GP functions. Exact Bayesian inference becomes conjugate by placing Gaussian priors on these latent functions and treating the transformed class indicators as Gaussian observations with known variance. For scalability, the authors incorporate a sparse variational GP framework with inducing points, which approximates the full GP while preserving the conjugate structure in the variational bounds. Predictions are made by mapping the latent GP posterior back to the simplex via the inverse ILR transform.

## Key Results
- The ILR-GP model achieves competitive classification accuracy on synthetic and UCI datasets, matching or slightly improving upon the conjugate Dirichlet GP baseline.
- Calibration, measured by expected calibration error (ECE), is significantly better than that of standard softmax GP and auxiliary-variable GP classifiers.
- Sparse variational inducing points provide a viable path to scalability, though empirical evidence for large numbers of classes (\(K > 100\)) is not presented.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The ILR bijection linearizes the simplex geometry, converting the multinomial log-likelihood into a product of independent Gaussian distributions in the transformed space. This structure is compatible with Gaussian process priors, enabling exact conjugate inference without distributional approximations like those used in softmax or auxiliary-variable approaches. The use of Gaussian pseudo-observations at fixed class targets (e.g., one-hot vectors transformed via ILR) simplifies the likelihood. The resulting model inherits the non-parametric flexibility of GPs while producing well-calibrated probabilities because the posterior is exact (in the full GP case) and the ILR transform preserves probability mass in a geometrically consistent manner.

## Foundational Learning
- **Isometric Log-Ratio (ILR) Transformation:** A bijection from the simplex to Euclidean space that uses an orthonormal basis. *Why needed:* It converts constrained probability vectors into unconstrained real vectors, enabling the use of standard GP regression with Gaussian likelihoods. *Quick check:* ILR is a specific case of log-ratio transforms used in compositional data analysis.
- **Simplex (Probability Simplex):** The set of vectors summing to one with non-negative entries. *Why needed:* Class probabilities live naturally on the simplex. *Quick check:* For \(K\) classes, the simplex is a \((K-1)\)-dimensional subset of \(\mathbb{R}^K\).
- **Gaussian Process Classification:** Non-parametric Bayesian approach using GP priors on latent functions. *Why needed:* Provides flexible, uncertainty-aware models for classification. *Quick check:* In binary GP classification, the likelihood is typically probit or logit, requiring approximation.
- **Conjugate Inference:** Analytic posterior computation without sampling or approximation. *Why needed:* Enables exact, efficient, and stable Bayesian updating. *Quick check:* Conjugacy occurs when prior and likelihood are from the same exponential family.
- **Probability Calibration:** Alignment between predicted probabilities and empirical frequencies. *Why needed:* Well-calibrated probabilities are crucial for decision-making under uncertainty. *Quick check:* Expected calibration error (ECE) bins predictions and compares accuracy vs. confidence.
- **Sparse Variational Gaussian Processes:** Uses a set of inducing points to approximate the full GP posterior. *Why needed:* Reduces computational complexity from \(O(n^3)\) to \(O(nm^2)\), where \(m\) is the number of inducing points. *Quick check:* The variational bound optimizes inducing point locations and hyperparameters.

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component Map:**  
Input Data -> ILR Transformation -> Latent GP (with Inducing Points) -> Gaussian Pseudo-Observations -> Inverse ILR -> Class Probabilities

**Critical Path:**  
1. Transform training labels (one-hot vectors) to ILR space.  
2. Place a GP prior on the latent function \(f: \mathbb{R}^d \to \mathbb{R}^{K-1}\).  
3. Approximate the GP posterior via sparse variational inference (optimize inducing points and ELBO).  
4. At test locations, draw latent samples from the approximate posterior and apply the inverse ILR to obtain predictive probabilities.

**Design Tradeoffs:**  
- **Reference Category:** The ILR requires choosing a reference category; this choice may affect posterior geometry and predictive performance.  
- **Inducing Point Budget:** More inducing points improve accuracy but increase computational cost; the optimal budget is dataset-dependent.  
- **Full vs. Sparse:** Full GP yields exact conjugate inference but scales cubically; sparse GP trades some exactness for scalability.

**Failure Signatures:**  
- Poor calibration (high ECE) when the ILR reference category is poorly chosen or when the sparse approximation is too coarse.  
- Degenerate or unstable posteriors if the GP hyperparameters (length scales, variance) are not properly optimized.  
- Memory exhaustion or excessive runtime when the number of classes \(K\) or dataset size \(n\) is very large, due to the \(K-1\) latent dimensions.

**Three First Experiments:**  
1. **ILR Reference Sensitivity:** Train the ILR-GP with all possible reference categories on 3 UCI datasets; compare accuracy, ECE, and log-likelihood.  
2. **Large-K Scalability:** Evaluate the sparse ILR-GP on CIFAR-100 (\(K=100\)) with varying inducing point budgets; measure runtime, memory, and ECE.  
3. **Statistical Significance:** Perform paired t-tests or Wilcoxon signed-rank tests on accuracy and ECE across 10 random train/test splits for UCI datasets, comparing ILR-GP against softmax GP and Dirichlet GP baselines.

## Limitations
- The effect of the ILR reference category choice on accuracy and calibration is not investigated.
- Scalability to many classes (\(K > 100\)) is claimed but not empirically validated; computational costs remain unknown.
- No statistical significance tests are provided for the reported improvements in accuracy and calibration.
