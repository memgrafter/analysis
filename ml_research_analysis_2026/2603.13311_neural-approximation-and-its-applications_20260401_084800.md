# Neural Approximation and Its Applications

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2603.13311
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.13311
- **Reference Count:** 19
- **Primary Result:** NeuApprox achieves 33.70 dB PSNR at 10% sampling for multispectral image inpainting, outperforming the best competing method by 1.62 dB.

## Executive Summary
Traditional multivariate function approximation relies on hand-crafted basis functions, which are limited in expressiveness and adaptability to data. This paper introduces Neural Approximation (NeuApprox), a paradigm that uses untrained neural networks as data-adaptive basis functions. The target function is expressed as a sum of block terms, each combining a neural basis function with a learnable coefficient tensor. Theoretically, NeuApprox can approximate any continuous multivariate function arbitrarily well. Empirical evaluations across diverse data types—multispectral images, light fields, videos, traffic data, and point clouds—show consistent and significant improvements over state-of-the-art methods in metrics like PSNR, SSIM, NRMSE, and RMSE.

## Method Summary
NeuApprox decomposes a target multivariate function into a sum of block terms. Each block consists of a neural basis function, implemented by an untrained neural network with fixed random weights, multiplied by a learnable coefficient tensor. The coefficients are optimized to fit the data, while the neural basis functions remain fixed. This separates representation (handled by the untrained network's functional space) from adaptation (handled by the linear coefficient tensor). The approach leverages the universal approximation capability of neural networks but avoids end-to-end training, aiming for efficiency and flexibility.

## Key Results
- Multispectral image inpainting at 10% sampling: NeuApprox reaches **33.70 dB PSNR** vs. 32.08 dB for the best competitor.
- Consistent gains across multiple tasks and metrics (SSIM, NRMSE, RMSE) on light field data, videos, traffic data, and point clouds.
- Demonstrated cross-domain applicability with superior compression and reconstruction quality.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The mechanism of NeuApprox hinges on two core principles: (1) untrained neural networks with random weights serve as a rich, data-adaptive basis function space, and (2) a learnable coefficient tensor linearly combines these basis functions to approximate the target multivariate function. The untrained network's fixed random initialization defines a deterministic nonlinear mapping from input coordinates to a feature space. This feature space is theoretically dense in the space of continuous functions (by the universal approximation theorem) given appropriate network architecture (width, depth, activation). The coefficient tensor optimization reduces to a convex problem (e.g., linear regression) because the basis functions are fixed. This separation avoids the non-convex optimization of end-to-end training, potentially leading to faster convergence and stability. Assumption: The network architecture satisfies the universal approximation conditions (e.g., sufficient width, bounded activation), and the coefficient tensor can capture the necessary multi-linear interactions. The paper provides a theoretical proof but the summary does not detail the exact assumptions (e.g., required width as a function of target function smoothness).

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component map:** Random weight initialization -> Untrained neural basis functions -> Coefficient tensor optimization -> Sum decomposition -> Reconstruction

**Critical path:** (1) Initialize network weights randomly; (2) Form neural basis functions; (3) Solve for optimal coefficient tensor given data; (4) Evaluate sum of basis-weighted terms.

**Design tradeoffs:** Untrained networks trade training cost for potential suboptimal basis; coefficient tensor size vs. approximation accuracy; network depth/width vs. memory.

**Failure signatures:** Poor reconstruction if network too narrow; coefficient optimization stuck in local minima if basis functions are linearly dependent; overfitting if coefficient tensor unregularized.
