# Increasing intelligence in AI agents can worsen collective outcomes

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2603.12129
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.12129
- **Reference count:** 27
- **Primary result:** Whether more sophisticated AI agents improve or degrade collective outcomes depends solely on a pre-deployable capacity-to-population ratio (C/N ≈ 0.5), not on absolute intelligence.

## Executive Summary
This paper studies competing AI agents in a resource-constrained environment, finding that increasing agent sophistication (via model diversity, reinforcement learning, and emergent tribal grouping) can paradoxically increase system overload under scarce resources. Using controlled experiments with seven small LLM-based agents, the authors identify a sharp crossover at C/N ≈ 0.5. Below this threshold (scarcity), sophistication amplifies correlated demand blocs, worsening overload, while tribal formation mitigates it by partitioning the population. Above this threshold (abundance), sophistication nearly eliminates overload, though tribes introduce inefficiency. Crucially, the outcome is determined by the simple, knowable C/N ratio rather than the agents' absolute intelligence level.

## Method Summary
The study uses a controlled experimental setup with N=7 LLM-based agents competing for a shared resource of capacity C. Each agent possesses a frozen pretrained LLM core, a scalar disposition \( p_i \), and a mechanism to decide whether to attempt resource access each round based on the LLM’s forecast of total demand. The experiment toggles four variables across five experimental levels (L1–L5): innate model diversity (nature), individual reinforcement learning (nurture), emergent tribal grouping (culture), and resource scarcity (C). Agents receive public demand history, compute an effective attempt probability, and are rewarded based on whether their action was correct given the total demand versus capacity. Primary metrics are the system overload fraction (rounds where demand > C) and individual win rates. The key manipulation compares L3 (diverse models, no RL), L4 (diverse + RL), and L5 (diverse + RL + tribal sensing) across varying C.

## Key Results
- A sharp crossover occurs at capacity-to-population ratio C/N ≈ 0.5, confirmed across N=7, 11, and 15.
- Under scarcity (C/N < 0.5), increased sophistication (diverse LLMs + RL in L4) increases system overload relative to simpler agents; tribal formation (L5) reduces it.
- Under abundance (C/N > 0.5), sophistication (L4) nearly eliminates overload, while tribes (L5) perform slightly worse due to granularity inefficiency.
- The critical transition point is arithmetical, determined by the largest emergent tribal bloc size first fitting within C.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1: Variance Amplification Under Scarcity
- **Claim:** Without tribal structure, diversity in LLM nature combined with reinforcement learning (nurture) increases the variance of total demand, directly driving system overload when C is scarce relative to N.
- **Mechanism:** Heterogeneous agents with distinct LLM forecasts \( p_{LLM} \) and individually adapting dispositions \( p_i \) can herd in correlated blocs. The variance of total demand scales with the square of the largest correlated bloc size. Without tribes, the entire population can act as one bloc (size N), leading to maximum possible variance (∝ N²).
- **Evidence anchors:** [abstract] "...when resources are scarce, AI model diversity and reinforcement learning increase dangerous system overload..."; [section, Page 4] "Without tribe formation... demand variance can in principle scale as high as N²..."
- **Break condition:** Tribal partitioning (L5) caps correlated bloc size.

### Mechanism 2: Tribal Partitioning Caps Correlated Bloc Size
- **Claim:** Emergent tribal grouping (L5) partitions the population into stable factions based on disposition similarity, limiting any single correlated demand bloc’s size and thus suppressing variance under scarcity.
- **Mechanism:** Agents sense and sort into tribes via a loyalty-defection mechanism tied to dispositional similarity. A stable partition (e.g., 3+3+1 for N=7) confines correlated action within tribes. The variance bound becomes the sum of squares of tribe sizes (e.g., 19), strictly less than N² (49).
- **Evidence anchors:** [abstract] "...tribe formation lessens this risk..."; [section, Page 4] "L5’s tribal mechanism partitions the population... capping the variance at 3² + 3² + 1² = 19..."; [section, Page 3, Figure 3] Shows stable 3+3+1 partition dynamics.
- **Break condition:** When C is large, fixed tribal blocs become too small to fully utilize capacity, causing under-use and higher relative overload.

### Mechanism 3: The C/N Crossover is an Arithmetic Threshold
- **Claim:** The switch from "sophistication harms" to "sophistication helps" is determined solely by the pre-deployable capacity-to-population ratio (C/N), with critical threshold ≈0.5.
- **Mechanism:** The crossover occurs where the largest expected tribal bloc size first fits within C. Under scarcity, variance reduction from tribes outweighs their mean-utilization cost. Under abundance, the problem shifts to tracking C closely; fixed tribal blocs create granularity inefficiency, making the more fluid L4 perform better.
- **Evidence anchors:** [abstract] "The crossover is arithmetical: it is where opposing tribes that form spontaneously first fit inside the available capacity."; [section, Page 3] "The crossover capacity C*/N≈0.5, confirmed at N=7, N=11, and N=15..."
- **Break condition:** The threshold might shift if initial \( p \) distribution or tribal rules change the typical largest bloc size.

## Foundational Learning

- **Concept: Capacity-to-Population Ratio (C/N)**
  - **Why needed:** This single pre-deployable ratio dictates whether intelligence (diversity+RL) helps or harms collective outcomes.
  - **Quick check:** Deploying 15 AI agents to compete for 6 resource units gives C/N = 0.4. Since this is below ≈0.5, tribal formation (L5) is expected to *decrease* overload compared to diverse agents without tribes (L4).

- **Concept: Disposition (`p`) as a Polarization Axis**
  - **Why needed:** The scalar \( p_i \in [0,1] \) modulates an agent’s tendency to follow its LLM’s forecast. It is the sole axis for tribal sorting; understanding this drives all group formation.
  - **Quick check:** With N=7 and evenly spaced initial \( p \), the most frequent L5 partition is two tribes of size 3 and one singleton. This partition maximizes the variance-capping benefit because it minimizes the sum of squares of bloc sizes (3²+3²+1²=19) while using all agents.

- **Concept: Reinforcement Learning as a Herding Amplifier**
  - **Why needed:** Individual RL (nurture) allows \( p_i \) to adapt. In homogeneous populations (L2), it causes convergence to similar \( p \), creating a single large correlated herd—worst-case for variance under scarcity. Diversity (nature) prevents full convergence but still allows multi-herd formation.
  - **Quick check:** Under very small C, L2 (identical LLMs + RL) has a larger maximum correlated bloc size (potentially N) than L4 (diverse LLMs + RL), where multiple smaller herds form. Thus, L2 is expected to produce *worse* overload.

## Architecture Onboarding

- **Component map:** LLM Core -> Disposition Filter -> Action Sampler -> Environment. Adaptation Module (for L2, L4, L5) updates p_i after rewards. Tribal Sensing & Loyalty Module (for L5) runs after adaptation to reassign tribes.

- **Critical path:** Each round: (1) LLM processes public demand history -> \( p_{LLM} \). (2) Agent applies \( p_i \) filter -> \( p_{eff} \). (3) Agent samples action \( a_i \). (4) Environment sums \( A = \sum a_i \), distributes rewards. (5) For L4/L5, update \( p_i \) via RL. (6) For L5, update tribal loyalties and handle defections. (7) Update demand history.

- **Design tradeoffs:**
  - **Model Diversity vs. Homogeneity:** Diversity breaks perfect herding but doesn't cap bloc size. Homogeneity maximizes herding risk.
  - **Individual RL vs. Fixed Disposition:** RL enables adaptation but can cause dangerous correlation. Fixed \( p=1 \) is safest under scarcity.
  - **Tribal Sensing vs. No Sensing:** Sensing creates beneficial variance-capping partitions under scarcity but causes granularity inefficiency under abundance. Adds computational overhead.
  - **Resource Headroom (C/N):** This environmental constraint inverts the value of all sophistication investments.

- **Failure signatures:**
  - **Collective failure:** Sustained overload fraction >> 0 when C/N < 0.5, especially with L4.
  - **Individual success within failure:** In L4/L5 scarcity regimes, agents aligned with the emergent majority bloc achieve win rates >80%.
  - **Tribal instability:** L5 fails to form stable 3+3+1 partitions (e.g., forms 4+3 or many singletons), reducing variance-capping benefit.

- **First 3 experiments:**
  1. **Baseline IID (L1):** Identical LLMs, \( p_i=1 \), no adaptation. Sweep C. Overload should match analytical Poisson-binomial for independent Bernoulli trials.
  2. **Add Homogeneous RL (L2):** Identical LLMs, \( p_i \) adapts via minimal RL. Sweep C. Expect *higher* overload than L1 at low C/N due to herding convergence.
  3. **Add Diversity Without RL/Tribes (L3):** Diverse LLMs (from 3 families), fixed \( p_i=1 \). Sweep C. Expect intermediate overload: better than L2 (no herding) but worse than L1 (heterogeneous correlations).

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- **Question 1:** Does LLM temperature affect the C/N ≈ 0.5 overload crossover?
- **Question 2:** How do richer action spaces and partial observability change the C/N crossover?
- **Question 3:** Do AI agents spontaneously form tribes without external sensing?
- **Question 4:** Does the C/N ≈ 0.5 crossover hold for extremely large N?

## Limitations
- The exact RL update rule (step size, direction) and tribal sensing parameters (loyalty ramp, defection threshold) are unspecified, making reproduction fragile.
- Prompt formatting (digit order, separators) and exact LLM-token mapping remain ambiguous.
- Generalizability beyond small N (≤15) and the specific LLM set used is untested.

## Confidence
- **C/N ≈ 0.5 crossover:** High (robust across multiple N and analytically motivated).
- **Variance amplification under scarcity:** Medium (theoretically sound, empirical magnitude depends on implementation details).
- **Tribal partitioning caps variance:** Medium (evident in figures, but partition stability sensitive to parameters).
- **Generalizability beyond small N & specific LLMs:** Low (scaling untested).
