# CitiLink-Summ: Summarization of Discussion Subjects in European Portuguese Municipal Meeting Minutes

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2602.16607
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.16607
- **Reference Count:** 28
- **Primary Result:** Introduced the first corpus for this task (2,880 hand-summarized discussion subjects); BART Large achieved ROUGE-L of 63.64, and PRIMERA achieved BERTScore of 85.79, establishing baseline benchmarks.

## Executive Summary
Municipal meeting minutes in European Portuguese are lengthy and structurally complex, making them difficult for citizens to navigate. A major obstacle to developing automatic summarization tools for this domain is the absence of a quality, manually annotated dataset. This paper addresses this gap by presenting CitiLink-Summ, the first corpus of 120 real municipal meeting minutes segmented into 2,880 discussion subjects, each paired with a manually written abstractive summary. The paper provides baseline results from fine-tuning models like BART and PRIMERA and evaluating LLMs via prompting, using both lexical and semantic metrics. This work establishes a foundational resource and performance benchmarks for future research on summarization in this under-resourced, high-value administrative domain.

## Method Summary
The authors constructed the CitiLink-Summ corpus from minutes of six Portuguese municipalities (2021-2024). Documents were manually segmented into topical "discussion subjects," and four annotators, under linguist supervision, wrote abstractive summaries following a strict multi-stage process with quality controls for relevance, cohesion, coherence, and length. The final corpus (2,880 instances) is split by municipality (60/20/20 train/val/test). For baseline experiments, discussion subjects were preprocessed with a hierarchical sliding-window chunking strategy to handle length, and chunk summaries were aggregated. Models fine-tuned included BART, BART Large, PTT5 (Portuguese T5), LED (Longformer), and PRIMERA. LLMs (Qwen2.5-1.5B-Instruct, Gemini-2.5-flash) were evaluated via few-shot prompting. Performance was measured using ROUGE, BLEU, METEOR (lexical), and BERTScore (semantic).

## Key Results
*   BART Large achieved the highest ROUGE-L score of **63.64** on the test set.
*   PRIMERA achieved the best BERTScore F1 of **85.79**.
*   The dataset exhibits **low extractive density** and **medium-to-high coverage**, confirming the summaries are highly abstractive.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1: Dataset as Primary Bottleneck Resolution
The creation of CitiLink-Summ directly solves the core problem of data scarcity. By providing 2,880 manually abstractive summaries, it supplies the essential supervised signal for training sequence-to-sequence models. The structured JSON format (`source`, `summary`) enables a clear input-output mapping where models learn to transform dense, formal administrative text into concise summaries. The multi-stage annotation and revision process was designed to ensure the summaries serve as a reliable gold standard for this complex text type.

### Mechanism 2: Enforced Abstraction Increases Utility and Challenge
The annotation workflow, with strict quality parameters and mandatory revisions, intentionally pushed annotators to reformulate information rather than copy sentences. This is evidenced by the corpus's low Density score, indicating rare use of contiguous extractive fragments. Consequently, models cannot rely on simple sentence selection; they must perform genuine semantic compression and paraphrasing. This design aligns with the end-goal of creating citizen-friendly summaries and presents a more meaningful and realistic challenge for NLP systems than extractive baselines.

### Mechanism 3: Multi-Metric Evaluation Captures Complementary Qualities
The benchmark uses both lexical (ROUGE, BLEU, METEOR) and semantic (BERTScore) metrics. Lexical metrics measure token-level recall of key terms, while semantic metrics assess meaning preservation via contextual embeddings. This combination mitigates the risk of a model optimizing for surface-level n-gram overlap at the expense of true meaning, or vice-versa. The fact that the "best" model varies slightly by metric (e.g., BART Large leads in ROUGE-L, PRIMERA in BERTScore) demonstrates the necessity of a multi-faceted evaluation to gauge different aspects of summary quality.

## Foundational Learning
*   **Concept: Domain-Specific NLP & Low-Resource Settings**
    *   **Why needed:** The task involves European Portuguese administrative text, a low-resource variant with specialized jargon and formal conventions. Standard pre-trained models may lack this domain knowledge.
    *   **Quick check:** Does a model fine-tuned on general Portuguese/newswire data capture the formulaic structures (e.g., "dispatch," "deliberation," "voting outcome") in municipal minutes?
*   **Concept: Abstractive vs. Extractive Summarization**
    *   **Why needed:** The dataset's low extractive density confirms the task requires generation (paraphrasing, synthesis), not just retrieval.
    *   **Quick check:** Which architectural components (e.g., BART's denoising objective) support abstractive generation, and how might they struggle with highly formal source text?
*   **Concept: Evaluation Metric Selection & Limitations**
    *   **Why needed:** Lexical metrics may penalize valid paraphrasing; semantic metrics may overlook factual precision. Understanding their biases is key to interpreting benchmarks.
    *   **Quick check:** What would it imply if a model achieves high BERTScore but low ROUGE-L on this dataset?

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component map:** Data Loading (JSON) -> Source/Target Extraction -> Chunking (if needed) -> Tokenization -> Model Fine-Tuning (Encoder-Decoder) -> Inference Generation -> Metric Calculation (ROUGE/BERTScore) -> Results Logging.

**Critical path:** Acquire dataset & code -> Set up environment -> Implement hierarchical chunking -> Fine-tune models (BART, PRIMERA, etc.) -> Generate test predictions -> Compute metrics -> Compare to Table 1 baselines.

**Design tradeoffs:**
*   **Chunk Size vs. Context Loss:** Smaller chunks fit all models but may lose intra-subject dependencies. Larger chunks preserve context but require long-document models (PRIMERA) or risk truncation. *Decision: Use model-native context length first; employ hierarchical chunking only for longer subjects.*
*   **Model Size vs. Resources:** BART Large/PRIMERA yield top scores but need more VRAM. PTT5 is Portuguese-specific but smaller. Qwen2.5-1.5B via prompting needs no fine-tuning but performs poorly. *Decision: Prototype with BART-base; for final model, use BART Large or PRIMERA if resources allow.*
*   **Fine-tuning vs. Prompting:** Fine-tuning adapts to domain jargon but needs labeled data. Few-shot prompting is flexible but showed lower performance. *Decision: Fine-tune an encoder-decoder; use LLMs for analysis or augmentation only.*

**Failure signatures:**
*   **High ROUGE, low BERTScore:** Output is overly extractive (copying n-grams without paraphrasing).
*   **Low scores across metrics:** Model failed to learn the task. Check loss curves, data loading, and tokenization.
*   **Incoherent/repetitive output:** Decoding issue (e.g., beam search too broad) or insufficient fine-tuning.
*   **Chunking artifacts:** Discontinuities in final summary indicate flawed aggregation logic.

**First 3 experiments:**
1.  **Reproduce Baseline:** Fine-tune `facebook/bart-large` on the official train/val/test split. Match or exceed reported BART Large scores (ROUGE-L ~63.64, BERTScore F1 ~86.28).
2.  **Ablation on Chunking:** For subjects >512 tokens, compare (a) hierarchical chunking, (b) simple truncation, and (c) using PRIMERA without chunking. Measure ROUGE-L delta and summary coherence (small human sample, n=20).
3.  **Theme Information Injection:** Prepend the discussion `theme` metadata to the source text during training/inference (e.g., `"Tema: {theme} {text}"`). Measure change in ROUGE-2 against the baseline to test if theme improves content selection.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
*   **Q1:** How can theme metadata, available during annotation but omitted in model training, be effectively integrated to improve summarization accuracy?
*   **Q2:** How well do automatic lexical and semantic metrics correlate with human judgments of summary quality for abstractive summaries in European Portuguese administrative texts?
*   **Q3:** Can cross-lingual transfer from English municipal meeting datasets enhance performance for European Portuguese, and what adaptation strategies are most effective?
*   **Q4:** What model architectures or training objectives best handle the high abstraction (low density) required for summarizing discussion subjects in municipal minutes?

## Limitations
*   **Chunking Implementation:** Exact sliding-window parameters (size, stride, aggregation method) are unreported, creating a critical reproduction gap.
*   **Fine-Tuning Protocol:** Key hyperparameters (epochs, batch size, learning rate, sequence lengths) are missing, hindering faithful baseline recreation.
*   **Evaluation Validity:** Reliance on automatic metrics without domain-specific human evaluation risks misalignment with true summary utility (e.g., factual accuracy, citizen readability).
