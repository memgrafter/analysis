# Do LLMs and VLMs Share Neurons for Inference? Evidence and Mechanisms of Cross-Modal Transfer

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID**: 2602.19058
- **Source URL**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.19058
- **Reference Count**: 40
- **Primary Result**: Over 50% of top-activated neurons during multi-step inference are shared between LLMs and LVLMs, enabling parameter-efficient cross-modal transfer via SNRF without multimodal fine-tuning.

## Executive Summary
This paper investigates whether large language models (LLMs) and vision-language models (LVLMs) share internal neural circuitry for reasoning. Through neuron-level activation profiling across model families (e.g., Qwen2.5-VL vs. Qwen2.5-Math), the authors find that more than half of the most active neurons during complex inference are shared, forming a modality-invariant subspace. Causal tests confirm these neurons are indispensable: ablating them collapses reasoning accuracy. Leveraging this, they propose Shared Neuron Low-Rank Fusion (SNRF), a method that transfers inference weights from an LLM to an LVLM by merging low-rank approximations of weight differences restricted to shared neurons. Without any multimodal fine-tuning, SNRF improves LVLM performance on math, science, and multimodal reasoning benchmarks while preserving visual perception capabilities. The work demonstrates that shared neurons provide an interpretable and efficient bridge for cross-modal transfer.

## Method Summary
The core method is Shared Neuron Low-Rank Fusion (SNRF). First, both a source LLM and target LVLM are profiled on an inference-specific corpus (e.g., math problems) to identify neurons with high activation during reasoning. The intersection of top-activated neurons across models defines a shared subspace. For each layer and projection (e.g., attention key, feed-forward up), the weight difference Δ = W_src – W_tgt is computed. A low-rank approximation Δ^(r) is obtained via truncated SVD applied only to the rows/columns corresponding to shared neurons. Finally, the target weights are updated: W_new = W_tgt + β · M_S(Δ^(r)), where M_S masks non-shared indices and β controls update strength. This selectively injects inference circuitry while leaving perception pathways unchanged, requiring no gradient-based training on multimodal data.

## Key Results
- Shared neurons constitute >50% of top-activated neurons during inference, forming a modality-invariant subspace concentrated in attention key/value projections.
- Ablating shared neurons in the LVLM drops reasoning accuracy to near zero, confirming their causal necessity; amplification reveals these neurons encode interpretable mathematical concepts.
- SNRF enhances LVLM reasoning on MathVista (+1.66 CoT, +2.0 Solution), ScienceQA (+7.64), and MMMU-Pro (+0.0627) while preserving or improving perception (MME +20.2, POPE stable).

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1: Identification of a Shared Inference Subspace
- **Claim**: LLMs and LVLMs share a substantial subset of neurons (>50% of top-activated) that form a modality-invariant inference subspace.
- **Mechanism**: Activation profiling identifies neurons consistently activated during multi-step inference tasks across both model types. The intersection of these neuron sets across contexts defines the shared subspace.
- **Core assumption**: Neurons activated for inference in text-only models correspond functionally to neurons activated for the same logical operations in multimodal models, despite different input modalities.
- **Evidence anchors**:
  - Abstract: "more than half of the top-activated neurons during multi-step inference are shared, forming a modality-invariant subspace."
  - Section 4.1: Formal definition N_shared := ⋂_{c∈C_text} N_c^ctx(M_text^0) ∩ ⋂_{c∈C_vl} N_c^ctx(M_vl^0).
  - Corpus: Weak direct external evidence; neighboring work supports neuron sharing across languages/tasks but not explicitly cross-modal.
- **Break condition**: Overlap drops below a functional threshold (e.g., <20% of top-activated neurons), indicating no stable shared subspace exists for transfer.

### Mechanism 2: Causal Role and Interpretability of Shared Neurons
- **Claim**: Shared neurons are causally necessary for inference and encode interpretable, concept-level semantics (e.g., mathematical reasoning).
- **Mechanism**: Activation amplification (boosting shared neuron outputs) systematically alters model generations toward concept-consistent tokens, while ablating them collapses inference performance.
- **Core assumption**: Amplifying a neuron's activation reveals its functional role by measuring token frequency shifts; necessity is shown by zeroing its parameters.
- **Evidence anchors**:
  - Abstract: "causal amplification shows these neurons encode consistent, interpretable math concepts and are functionally indispensable: ablating them drops accuracy to near zero."
  - Section 4.2: Deactivating shared neurons collapses performance to 0.0, whereas random ablations lead to notably smaller drops.
  - Section 4.2: Word clouds show neuron sensitivity to different parts of math inference (e.g., algebra, geometry).
- **Break condition**: Amplification fails to produce coherent concept shifts (token distributions unchanged) or ablation does not catastrophically degrade performance, suggesting neurons are not functionally critical.

### Mechanism 3: Efficient Cross-Modal Transfer via Low-Rank Fusion
- **Claim**: Transferring inference ability from an LLM to an LVLM can be achieved by selectively merging weight differences only within the shared neuron subspace via low-rank approximation.
- **Mechanism**: Compute inter-model weight delta Δ = W_src – W_tgt, take a low-rank (rank-r) approximation of Δ restricted to shared neuron indices, and add β-scaled updates to the target LVLM's weights. This injects inference signals without altering perception pathways.
- **Core assumption**: The weight difference within the shared subspace captures reusable inference circuitry; low-rank approximation preserves essential signal while being parameter-efficient.
- **Evidence anchors**:
  - Abstract: "SNRF identifies shared neurons, computes a low-rank approximation of weight differences, and injects updates only within the shared subspace."
  - Section 5: Formal update fW_{ℓ,p} = W_tgt_{ℓ,p} + β M_S_{ℓ,p}(Δ^(r)_{ℓ,p}).
  - Section 6.2: SNRF improves inference benchmarks (e.g., +2.0 MathVista Solution) while preserving perception (MME +20.2, POPE stable).
- **Break condition**: Low-rank truncation rank r too small to capture Δ_S causes degradation; or shared neuron masks incorrect (include non-transferable neurons), harming perception or inference.

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map**: Neuron Profiler → Shared Neuron Selector → Delta Computer → Low-Rank approximator → Fusion Applier → Evaluator
- **Critical path**: Profiling → Selection → Delta Computation → Low-Rank Fusion → Evaluation. Selection must precede fusion; profiling must use inference-specific corpus (not general text).
- **Design tradeoffs**:
  - Parameter efficiency vs. expressivity: Lower rank r saves parameters but may lose nuance; higher r risks overfitting.
  - Shared neuron purity vs. coverage: Strict intersection (large N_shared) ensures transferability but may miss model-specific refinements; loose inclusion risks harming perception.
  - Update strength β: Small β preserves target model behavior; large β may override useful LVLM-specific features.
- **Failure signatures**:
  - Catastrophic drop in perception metrics (MME, POPE) → shared neuron set likely includes visual pathway neurons; need stricter selection.
  - No gain on reasoning benchmarks → either no true shared subspace, or rank r too low, or β mis-scaled.
  - Training instability during merging → numerical issues from large Δ; recommend clipping or smaller β.
- **First 3 experiments**:
  1. **Baseline Profiling**: On a small inference corpus (e.g., 100 GSM8K problems), compute per-model top-activated neurons. Quantify overlap (Jaccard index) across layers/modules. Expect >50% overlap in attn.k, attn.v.
  2. **Ablation Sanity Check**: Zero all neurons in N_shared in the LVLM. Measure accuracy on MathVista and MME. Expect near-zero reasoning scores, stable perception.
  3. **SNRF Dry Run**: Apply SNRF with r=1, β=0.1 to one layer-module (e.g., attn.k in layer 10). Run a few validation examples manually to check output coherence vs. unmodified model. Should see improved reasoning without visual degradation.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
1. **Generalization beyond 8B scale and non-Qwen architectures?** Evidence limited to models up to 7B/8B from Qwen, LLaMA, InternVL. Scaling or different architectures (Mistral, Gemma) may alter representations.
2. **Why concentration in attn.k and attn.v?** Shared neurons peak in attention key/value projections, but the paper does not explain why. Targeted interventions and representational analysis needed.
3. **Transfer to non-mathematical reasoning?** Only math/science benchmarks tested. Other reasoning types (logical, commonsense, coding) may rely on different mechanisms.
4. **Sensitivity to detection corpus and threshold σ?** The construction of the inference corpus and the activation threshold σ are fixed but not analyzed; these could drastically affect shared neuron identification.
5. **Identification without a corresponding text-only model?** SNRF requires a source LLM; many LVLMs lack a closely related textual counterpart, limiting applicability.

## Limitations
- **Hyperparameter opacity**: Critical SNRF parameters (low-rank r, mixing coefficient β, activation threshold σ) are unreported, hindering reproducibility.
- **Corpus construction vagueness**: Details of the inference detection corpus (size, prompt templates, sampling strategy) are unspecified, risking misalignment in neuron profiling.
- **Architecture/model generality**: Evidence is limited to Qwen families; unknown if shared neurons exist in architectures with divergent vision encoders or different training objectives.

## Confidence
- **Shared neuron existence (≥50% overlap)**: **Medium** (strong internal profiling data but weak external validation; neighboring work supports cross-lingual/task sharing, not cross-modal).
- **Causal necessity of shared neurons**: **High** (ablation causing near-zero reasoning accuracy is a robust intervention result).
- **SNRF transfer efficacy**: **Medium** (benchmark gains reported, but without hyperparameter sweeps or cross-model tests, stability is uncertain).

## Next Checks
1. **Hyperparameter sensitivity sweep**: Systematically vary r (e.g., {1, 32, 64, 128}) and β (e.g., {0.1, 0.3, 0.5}) on a held-out validation set of MathVista; identify ranges where gains hold without perception degradation.
2. **Cross-architecture generalization test**: Apply SNRF to a non-Qwen pair (e.g., Llama-3-LLM → LLaVA-VLM). Re-measure neuron overlap and benchmark improvements; failure indicates architecture-specificity.
3. **Perception-reasoning neuron dissection**: Manually annotate shared neurons as "perception-related" vs. "reasoning-related" via activation patterns on visual vs. textual tasks. Ablate subsets separately to confirm reasoning neurons are perception-neutral.
