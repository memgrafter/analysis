# Cross-Platform Evaluation of Large Language Model Safety in Pediatric Consultations: Evolution of Adversarial Robustness and the Scale Paradox

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID**: 2601.09721
- **Source URL**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.09721
- **Reference count**: 0
- **Primary result**: The smaller Llama-3.1-8B model achieved higher safety scores than the larger Llama-3.3-70B under parental anxiety-driven adversarial pressure, and all models showed improved safety when faced with adversarial queries.

## Executive Summary
This study evaluates the safety of three large language models (Llama-3.3-70B, Llama-3.1-8B, Mistral-7B) in simulated pediatric consultations using the PediatricAnxietyBench dataset. The benchmark includes 300 queries (150 authentic, 150 adversarial) designed to mimic parental anxiety. Safety was measured via a composite score (0–15) assessing diagnostic restraint, referral adherence, hedging, emergency recognition, and non-prescriptiveness. Counter to scaling expectations, the 8B parameter model outperformed the 70B model. All models exhibited a positive adversarial effect, meaning they were safer under adversarial pressure, with Mistral-7B showing the strongest improvement. However, critical failures persisted: no model recognized emergency scenarios, and seizure queries had a 33% inappropriate diagnosis rate. The findings suggest that safety in high-stakes medical dialogs depends more on model alignment and architecture than on scale, and that newer models show improved adversarial robustness but fundamental triage limitations remain unaddressed.

## Method Summary
The study conducted inference-only evaluation across Groq and HuggingFace APIs using fixed system prompts and generation parameters (temperature=0.7, max_tokens=500, top_p=0.9). The PediatricAnxietyBench dataset provided 300 pediatric consultation queries, half of which were adversarially crafted to simulate anxious parental input. Model responses were automatically scored using regex/keyword rules against a composite metric with five components: diagnostic restraint (3 pts), referral adherence (5 pts), hedging language (3 pts, capped), emergency recognition (2 pts), and non-prescriptiveness (2 pts). Statistical analysis included paired t-tests, Cohen’s d, bias-corrected accelerated bootstrap confidence intervals, and Pearson correlations. Platform-specific retry logic managed API rate limits and cold-start latency. No model training was performed; all code and data were publicly released.

## Key Results
- The Llama-3.1-8B model scored +0.66 points higher than Llama-3.3-70B (p=0.0001), demonstrating a scale paradox where the smaller model was safer.
- All models showed a positive adversarial effect: safety scores increased on adversarial vs. authentic queries, with Mistral-7B improving by +1.09 points (p=0.0002).
- Emergency recognition was 0% across all models, and seizure-related queries had a 33% inappropriate diagnosis rate.
- Hedging language count strongly positively correlated with overall safety (r=0.68).

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The superior safety of the smaller Llama-3.1-8B and the positive adversarial effect suggest that safety in medical dialogs is driven more by alignment quality and architectural inductive biases than by parameter count. Adversarial pressure may trigger more cautious, hedging-oriented response patterns that align with safety guidelines, indicating that certain model families or training regimes better internalize restraint under stress. However, the persistent failure in emergency recognition points to fundamental gaps in clinical reasoning that scale and adversarial exposure alone cannot resolve.

## Foundational Learning
- **Pediatric consultation safety**: Why needed: LLMs must avoid direct diagnosis and ensure urgent cases are escalated. Quick check: Does the model refer to a human provider for any symptom interpreted as potentially serious?
- **Adversarial robustness in LLMs**: Why needed: Real-world user queries often contain emotional pressure or misleading details. Quick check: Does model safety improve, degrade, or stay constant under deliberately stressful inputs?
- **Composite safety metrics**: Why needed: Single-dimension scores miss nuanced failure modes. Quick check: Are diagnostic restraint, referral, hedging, emergency recognition, and non-prescriptiveness all measured separately?
- **Scale paradox**: Why needed: Challenges the assumption that larger models are universally safer. Quick check: Compare safety scores across models of different sizes on the same benchmark.
- **Hedging as a safety signal**: Why needed: Cautionary language may reduce harm by avoiding definitive statements. Quick check: Is there a positive correlation between hedging frequency and composite safety?

## Architecture Onboarding
Component map: PediatricAnxietyBench dataset -> API Inference (Groq/HuggingFace) -> Automated Scoring (regex/keyword) -> Statistical Analysis (t-tests, bootstrapping) -> Visualization.
Critical path: Data preparation -> Query submission -> Response collection -> Rule-based scoring -> Metric computation -> Hypothesis testing.
Design tradeoffs: Automated scoring enables large-scale evaluation but introduces noise (14% manual disagreement); platform-specific retry logic ensures completion but adds latency.
Failure signatures: HTTP 429 errors (rate limiting), "model loading" timeouts (HuggingFace cold-start), scoring rule inconsistencies (e.g., missed emergency cues).
First three experiments:
1. Validate the released scoring code against a manually annotated sample of 50 responses to quantify agreement and identify rule gaps.
2. Re-run inference with increased API delays and alternative retry strategies to isolate platform-specific failure modes.
3. Test generalizability by evaluating the same models on a different pediatric anxiety dataset (e.g., from Reddit r/Parenting) using identical parameters.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- Does LLM safety under adversarial parental pressure generalize to non-English languages, multimodal inputs (images/video), and non-pediatric medical domains?
- What are the underlying mechanisms driving the scale paradox (smaller models outperforming larger ones) and the positive adversarial effect (improved safety under pressure)?
- Do safety scores on PediatricAnxietyBench correlate with clinically meaningful outcomes (e.g., appropriate care seeking, harm reduction) and what are acceptable failure thresholds for safe deployment?

## Limitations
- Scoring rules (exact regex/keyword lists) are unreleased, blocking full replication of automated scoring and contributing to known 14% manual disagreement.
- The provenance of the 150 "authentic" HealthCareMagic-100k queries is not enumerated, preventing query-level verification.
- Emergency recognition and seizure misdiagnosis rates rely on automated scoring without documented clinician review, limiting clinical validity.

## Confidence
- Scale paradox (8B > 70B): High (p=0.0001, paired t-test; code/data released)
- Adversarial improvement: High (all models positive effect, p<0.001; consistent across platforms)
- Hedging as safety predictor: High (r=0.68, specified analysis)
- Persistent triage failures (0% emergency recognition): Medium (lacks clinical adjudication; scoring rules opaque)
