# Can vision language models learn intuitive physics from interaction?

## Quick Facts
- **ArXiv ID:** 2602.06033
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.06033
- **Reference count:** 40
- **Primary result:** Vision-language models (VLMs) do not acquire robust, generalizable intuitive physics from one-step reinforcement learning (RL) or supervised fine-tuning (SFT); both methods overfit to task-specific shortcuts despite near-ceiling within-task performance.

## Executive Summary
This study investigates whether vision-language models can learn generalizable intuitive physics through interaction. Models (Qwen3-VL-8B, Qwen2.5-VL-7B, Qwen3-VL-32B) were trained on block-tower tasks using either one-step reinforcement learning (GRPO/GSPO) or supervised fine-tuning (SFT). While both approaches achieved near-perfect performance on the training tasks, they failed to generalize to other physics tasks or real-world images. Linear probe analysis revealed that physical properties like stability are already decodable from the base model’s activations, suggesting a competence–performance gap: the models possess latent physical knowledge but cannot reliably apply it, especially out-of-distribution. The findings indicate that current interaction paradigms and fine-tuning strategies lead to shortcut learning rather than true physical intuition.

## Method Summary
The authors used three block-tower tasks: binary stability classification, horizontal displacement (x-only), and horizontal+vertical displacement (x-y). Models were adapted via parameter-efficient fine-tuning (QLoRA) with either SFT (demonstrations) or GRPO (one-step RL: generate a single response, compute reward, update policy). Evaluation included within-task test accuracy, cross-task generalization, transfer to real tower images (Lerer dataset), and linear probing of activations to measure decodability of physical properties. Training ran for 10k steps; larger models (32B) and alternative RL (GSPO) were also tested.

## Key Results
- Within-task performance reached near ceiling for both SFT and GRPO (e.g., ~0.97 accuracy on binary stability, ~20/20 reward on x-only).
- Neither method reliably generalized to other tasks; only subset relationships (e.g., x-y → x-only) showed weak transfer.
- Models failed to transfer to real-world tower images; those trained on action tasks performed no better than chance on binary stability with real images.
- Linear probes showed stability and block offset were highly decodable from base model activations, and fine-tuning did not substantially alter this decodability, indicating a gap between latent competence and actionable performance.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1: Interaction via one-step RL does not inherently yield generalizable physics
- **Claim:** Training VLMs with one-step reinforcement learning (GRPO) does not produce more generalizable physical intuitions compared to supervised fine-tuning (SFT).
- **Mechanism:** One-step RL provides limited interaction: models generate a single action sequence and receive a reward based on outcome. This narrow feedback loop encourages learning of task-specific policies rather than abstract physical principles. The model optimizes for the immediate reward signal, which can be exploited by shortcuts tied to the specific task's visual statistics.
- **Core assumption:** Generalizable intuitive physics requires exposure to diverse state transitions and multi-step consequences, which one-step RL does not offer.
- **Evidence anchors:** 
  - Abstract: "While learning from interaction allows models to improve their within-task performance, it fails to produce models with generalizable physical intuitions."
  - Section 4.2: "We find that no model reliably generalizes to all other tasks, regardless of if they are trained with interaction."
  - Corpus: Neighboring work "WoW: Towards a World omniscient World model Through Embodied Interaction" argues that authentic physical understanding requires embodied, multi-step interaction.
- **Break condition:** If interaction were extended to multi-step episodes with sequential decision-making and exploration, or if the environment offered richer physical dynamics, generalization might improve.

### Mechanism 2: Competence-performance gap in physical representation
- **Claim:** VLMs already encode physical properties (like stability and block offset) in their activations, but post-training methods fail to convert this latent competence into robust performance, especially out-of-distribution.
- **Mechanism:** Linear probes reveal that physical properties are highly decodable from model activations even in the base model, indicating representation of physical knowledge. However, fine-tuning (either SFT or RL) does not substantially alter these representations or bridge the gap to correct behavior on novel tasks. The model may rely on superficial heuristics during generation that do not generalize.
- **Core assumption:** Model performance on a task depends not only on having relevant knowledge in representations but also on the ability to access and apply that knowledge appropriately under varying conditions.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - Section 4.4: "We find that the binary stability of a tower is already trivially decodable in the base model, and it does not change in any substantial way through either fine-tuning method... This suggests that while the models have the competence to solve either task, this does not translate to good performance, hinting at shortcut learning."
  - Abstract: "Decodability analysis showed physical properties like stability and block offset were highly readable from activations even in the base model, indicating presence of competence but failure to convert it to performance."
- **Break condition:** If training were augmented with techniques that force the model to rely on these representations (e.g., adversarial training, distillation from probes), the performance gap might close.

### Mechanism 3: Overfitting to task-specific shortcuts due to limited data diversity
- **Claim:** Both SFT and RL overfit to spurious correlations and task-specific shortcuts present in the training data, preventing learning of general physical principles.
- **Mechanism:** The training datasets (synthetic block towers) have fixed visual statistics (camera angle, block colors, geometry). Models can achieve high reward by exploiting low-level cues (e.g., a few pixels along the center line) rather than learning physics-based reasoning. The reward functions and SFT targets are tailored to the specific task, further encouraging specialization.
- **Core assumption:** Generalization requires exposure to varied instances that break superficial correlations and force abstraction of underlying physics.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - Section 4.2: "Generalization was minimal and limited to subset relationships... We find no reliable generalization to other tasks."
  - Section 4.3: Models trained on binary stability do not perform at human level on real tower images, indicating reliance on synthetic distribution.
  - Section 4.5.2: Even with larger models (32B) and different RL algorithm (GSPO), generalization remains poor.
  - Corpus: Neighbor paper "Pixels to Principles: Probing Intuitive Physics Understanding in Multimodal Language Models" finds that MLLMs struggle on intuitive physics tasks, often relying on shortcuts.
- **Break condition:** If training data included extensive diversity (multiple viewpoints, lighting, block textures, physics parameters) and perhaps curriculum learning that gradually introduces complexity, models might be forced to learn more abstract physical rules.

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map:**  
  Pre-trained VLM (Qwen3-VL-8B, Qwen2.5-VL-7B, Qwen3-VL-32B) -> Parameter-Efficient Fine-Tuning (QLoRA adapters) -> Training loop (SFT cross-entropy or GRPO RL) -> Tasks (binary stability, x-only, x-y) -> Evaluation (test accuracy/reward, cross-task, real images, linear probes)

- **Critical path:**  
  1. **Data generation:** Create synthetic block tower images (256x256 RGB) from ThreeDWorld physics engine with fixed camera; generate prompts and optimal answers/targets for each task.  
  2. **Training loop:** For each batch of prompts:  
     - SFT: forward pass, compute cross-entropy loss on target tokens, update adapters.  
     - GRPO: generate N completions per prompt, compute task-specific reward per completion, normalize advantages, update adapters via GRPO loss.  
  3. **Periodic evaluation:** Every 2000 steps, evaluate on held-out test sets for all tasks; extract activations for linear probes.  
  4. **Analysis:** Compare within-task learning curves, cross-task generalization matrices, real-image transfer, and probe decodability across training methods and model sizes.

- **Design tradeoffs:**  
  - Model selection: Used 8B/7B models for GPU memory; 32B tested but showed no improvement (cost vs. capacity).  
  - Training duration: Fixed at 10k steps; longer caused overfitting (especially GRPO).  
  - Interaction depth: Only one-step RL (single action); multi-step is more expensive and may not scale with context length.  
  - Task design: Tasks share underlying physics but differ in output format and dataset (top block vs side block), allowing subset generalization tests but still narrow.  
  - Adapter rank: Rank 16 default; lower ranks (1,8) did not affect within-task learning or improve generalization.  
  - Vision encoder tuning: Included fine-tuning; ablation without vision fine-tuning showed similar failure to generalize, indicating vision encoder updates are not the bottleneck.

- **Failure signatures:**  
  - Within-task performance plateau: training reward/loss does not improve or test accuracy far below ceiling.  
  - Overfitting: training reward continues to increase while test reward decreases after a certain checkpoint (e.g., GRPO beyond 10k steps).  
  - No cross-task transfer: high within-task performance but near-baseline (e.g., ~0.5 for binary stability, ~0 for reward tasks) on other tasks.  
  - No real-image transfer: performance on Lerer dataset at or below random despite good synthetic performance.  
  - Decodability-performance mismatch: linear probes achieve high accuracy predicting physical properties, yet model outputs are incorrect on OOD tasks.  
  - Attention map anomalies: no meaningful change in attention patterns after fine-tuning (Fig. 12), suggesting models may not focus on relevant physical cues.

- **First 3 experiments:**  
  1. **Within-task learning curve:** Train Qwen3-VL-8B with GRPO and SFT on each of four tasks (binary-stability top block, x-only top block, x-only side block, x-y side block). Evaluate on held-out test instances from the same task every 2000 steps. Expected: Both methods reach near-ceiling performance (e.g., ~20 reward or ~0.97 accuracy) by 10k steps (see Figure 2 diagonal).  
  2. **Cross-task generalization matrix:** After 10k steps of training on a given task, evaluate the model on all four tasks. Compare performance heatmaps for GRPO and SFT. Expected: Only weak generalization along subset relationships (e.g., x-y side block → x-only side block; binary-stability top block ↔ x-only top block) but no transfer to different datasets (e.g., x-only top block → x-only side block) or across task types (e.g., x-only → binary-stability). See Figures 2 and 15.  
  3. **Real-world image transfer:** Take models trained on binary-stability top block and evaluate on 100 real block tower images from Lerer et al. (2016). Compare GRPO and SFT models, and also test models trained on other tasks. Expected: Models trained on binary-stability show some transfer (~0.6 accuracy) but remain below human average (~0.8). Models trained on action tasks (x-only, x-y) do not generalize to binary stability on real images (Figure 3 and 21). Additionally, test models trained on x-only top block on real images: no transfer (Figure 20).

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
The paper does not explicitly enumerate open questions beyond its stated limitations. However, based on the findings and discussion, implied open questions include: Whether multi-step interaction protocols could bridge the competence-performance gap; how dataset diversity quantitatively affects shortcut learning; and whether alternative architectures (e.g., diffusion-based planners) might better leverage latent physical representations. The authors note that their results are confined to Qwen VLMs and specific PEFT setups, leaving open the question of whether other model families (e.g., Gemini, Claude) exhibit the same gap.

## Limitations
- Interaction depth limited to single-step RL; multi-step interaction may be necessary for generalizable physics.
- Narrow task diversity and fixed synthetic visual statistics may encourage shortcut learning.
- Training instability observed (e.g., GRPO overfitting beyond 10k steps).
- Linear probe analysis may not fully capture the complexity of physical representations.
- Conclusions are restricted to the Qwen model family and specific PEFT setup; other architectures may behave differently.

## Confidence
- VLMs lack robust intuitive physics: **High**
- RL (GRPO/GSPO) does not yield better generalization than SFT: **High**
- Competence-performance gap (decodable but unusable knowledge): **Medium**
- Shortcut learning as primary failure mode: **Medium**
- Increased scale (32B) does not resolve generalization: **High**

## Next Checks
1. **Multi-step interaction protocol:** Replace one-step RL with multi-episode rollouts (e.g., 5–10 sequential block placements) to test if richer temporal credit assignment improves generalization.
2. **Dataset diversity ablation:** Systematically vary training data along axes like camera angle, block texture, and lighting to isolate which visual shortcuts models exploit and whether diversity forces abstraction.
3. **Probe-guided fine-tuning:** Introduce an auxiliary loss during training that directly optimizes for linear probe accuracy on the target physical property (e.g., stability) and measure if this closes the competence-performance gap and improves out-of-distribution performance.
