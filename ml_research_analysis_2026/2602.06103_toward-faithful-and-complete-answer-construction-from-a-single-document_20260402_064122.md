# Toward Faithful and Complete Answer Construction from a Single Document
## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2602.06103
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.06103
- **Reference count:** 28
- **Primary result:** EVE, a three-stage pipeline combining exhaustive multi-query extraction, validation via majority voting, and skeleton-guided enumeration, achieves up to 92% recall and 73.8% precision on STPA, improving recall by up to 24%, precision by 29%, and F1-score by 31% over single-pass baselines while increasing covered safety scenarios by orders of magnitude.

## Executive Summary
Large language models (LLMs) excel at statistical prediction but lack mechanisms ensuring completeness and faithfulness in high-rigor, single-document tasks, often omitting critical information or generating hallucinations. This paper introduces EVE, a structured framework that decomposes the task into extraction, validation, and enumeration stages. By performing independent multi‑query extraction and validation, EVE exponentially reduces the risk of omissions and false positives. A skeleton-guided enumeration then systematically covers all required output elements. Applied to automated System-Theoretic Process Analysis (STPA), EVE breaks the typical coverage‑accuracy trade‑off, achieving substantial gains in recall and precision. However, performance saturates around 92% recall and 73.8% precision due to inherent language ambiguity, suggesting fundamental limits of pure LLM‑based reasoning.

## Method Summary
EVE operates in three stages on a single technical document. **Stage 1 (Exhaustive extraction)** issues multiple independent extraction prompts to identify candidate answer elements, merging all results to maximize recall. **Stage 2 (Validation)** applies multiple independent validation prompts to each candidate, using majority voting to filter false positives and cluster synonymous aliases. **Stage 3 (Enumeration)** generates a reasoning skeleton that lists all validated components and their relationships; each skeleton node is expanded independently into a detailed description, ensuring exhaustive and structured output. The pipeline is model‑agnostic, using zero‑shot or few‑shot prompting without any training. Evaluation on a STPA dataset (100 patents) measures recall, precision, and F1‑score against ground‑truth annotations of unsafe control actions, comparing against single‑pass baselines.

## Key Results
- EVE improved recall by up to 24%, precision by 29%, and F1‑score by 31% relative to single‑pass baselines.
- Maximum achieved performance: 92% recall and 73.8% precision.
- Skeleton‑guided enumeration increased the number of covered safety scenarios by orders of magnitude, effectively breaking the coverage‑accuracy trade‑off.
- Performance plateaus due to inherent language ambiguity, not implementation limits.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1: Exhaustive Multi‑Query Extraction
**Claim:** Emitting multiple independent extraction queries exponentially reduces the probability of omitting relevant document elements by covering complementary perspectives.  
**Mechanism:** Perform \( M_e \) independent extraction queries on the same document. The union of results yields a candidate set \( C \). The probability that a true element \( e \) is missed by all attempts is \( \prod_{m=1}^{M_e} (1 - p_{i,e}) \), where \( p_{i,e} \) is the success probability of query \( i \). Thus, omission probability decays exponentially with \( M_e \).  
**Core assumption:** Queries have non‑overlapping failure modes and each has a non‑zero success probability \( (p_{i,e} > 0) \).  
**Evidence:** Section 3.1 describes the Exhaust Stage; Section 3.2 derives the \( P_E = 1 - \prod_{m=1}^{M_e}(1-p_{i,e}) \) formula.  
**Break condition:** Gains diminish when recoverable entities are mostly captured (saturation around \( M_e=3 \)); also fails if all queries share the same blind spot (e.g., semantically equivalent prompts).

### Mechanism 2: Majority Voting Validation
**Claim:** Aggregating multiple independent validation queries exponentially reduces the probability of accepting false positives (hallucinations).  
**Mechanism:** For each candidate \( e \in C \), run \( M_v \) independent validation queries (e.g., "Is \( e \) a valid answer based on the document?"). Accept \( e \) only if at least \( \lceil M_v/2 \rceil \) votes are positive. With per‑query error rate \( < 0.5 \), the overall error decays as \( P_{\text{error}} \leq 2 \exp\!\left( -\frac{M_v}{2}(1-2(1-p_{i,M}))^2 \right) \).  
**Core assumption:** Each validation query has independent error rate \( < 50\% \) (better than random) and errors are independent.  
**Evidence:** Section 3.1 defines the Validation Stage; Section 3.2 provides the error bound formula.  
**Break condition:** Validation queries are not truly independent (shared bias) or have error rate \( \ge 50\% \). Alias clustering (merging synonyms) remains weak.

### Mechanism 3: Skeleton‑Guided Enumeration
**Claim:** Decomposing a long‑form answer into a structured skeleton and expanding nodes independently systematically covers all required elements, overcoming truncation and ordering errors of a single pass.  
**Mechanism:** After validation, generate a skeleton (list of all validated components with required structure). Then, for each skeleton node, condition the LLM to generate a bounded description. This separates correctness (skeleton coverage) from capacity (per‑node generation).  
**Core assumption:** The skeleton correctly enumerates all top‑level elements and their dependencies; per‑node expansion is within the model’s capacity.  
**Evidence:** Abstract mentions "skeleton‑guided enumeration"; Section 3.3 explains the separation; Section 4 and Table 3 show orders‑of‑magnitude increase in enumerated scenarios.  
**Break condition:** Skeleton generation itself is incomplete or incorrect; combinatorial space may still be too large for practical scaling even with enumeration.

## Foundational Learning
- **Closed‑world assumption in document grounding:** EVE treats the document as containing all relevant information, differing from open‑world RAG. Quick check: If the document omits a critical safety requirement, EVE’s "complete" answer is still incomplete, highlighting its scope constraint.
- **Exponential error reduction via independent aggregation:** The theoretical guarantees rely on combining independent probabilities. Quick check: Why does adding a second extraction query often yield a larger marginal gain than adding a fourth? (Hint: the product term \( (1-p) \) compounds, making early reductions more impactful.)
- **Distinguishing recall‑oriented vs. precision‑oriented stages:** Extraction seeks high recall (accepts redundancy), validation seeks high precision (filters). Quick check: What happens if a strict validator is applied during extraction? It would drop true positives prematurely, breaking the pipeline’s error‑bounding property.

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component map:**  
Input (document \( D \), query templates \( Q_{\text{ext}}, Q_{\text{val}}, Q_f \), LLM instance, parameters \( M_e, M_v \)) → Stage 1: Exhaust (\( M_e \) independent \( Q_{\text{ext}} \) calls on \( D \) → candidate set \( C \)) → Stage 2: Validate (\( |C| \times M_v \) independent \( Q_{\text{val}} \) calls with voting → validated set \( V \)) → Stage 3: Enumerate (generate skeleton from \( V \), then \( |V| \times Q_f \) calls for node expansion → structured report \( R \)) → Output (\( R \)).

**Critical path:** The validation loop (\( C \rightarrow V \)) is the main scalability bottleneck due to its \( O(|C| \times M_v) \) LLM calls. The total call count grows quickly with \( M_e, M_v \) and candidate set size.

**Design tradeoffs:**  
- **Cost vs. Recall/Precision:** Increasing \( M_e, M_v \) improves metrics but adds API calls linearly/exponentially; diminishing returns appear beyond 2‑3 queries.  
- **Skeleton fidelity vs. coverage:** Overly rigid skeletons may miss valid paths; overly flexible ones become unmanageable.  
- **Generality vs. depth:** EVE is model‑agnostic but cannot leverage fine‑tuned domain expertise, possibly missing tacit knowledge.

**Failure signatures:**  
- **Saturation plateau:** Recall ~92%, precision ~74% indicates inherent language ambiguity, not implementation error.  
- **Precision drop with high \( M_e \):** Expected spurious candidates; if not recovered by validation, validation queries are ineffective.  
- **Low enumeration count:** Skeleton generation failed to list all validated components.  
- **Duplicate entities:** Alias merging weak, leading to multiple mentions of the same component.

**First 3 experiments:**  
1. **Extraction recall sweep:** Run Stage 1 only with \( M_e \in \{1,2,3,4\} \); plot recall vs. \( M_e \) to verify exponential omission reduction.  
2. **Validation precision/recall sweep:** Fix \( M_e=4 \), run Stages 1‑2 with \( M_v \in \{0,1,2,3,4\} \); plot precision/recall vs. \( M_v \) to verify voting effectiveness.  
3. **Enumeration scale test:** Run full pipeline (\( M_e=4, M_v=4 \)); compare number of enumerated UCAs to a single‑pass baseline; expect orders‑of‑magnitude increase.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
1. **Mitigating inherent semantic ambiguity:** How can the saturation limit (e.g., 92% recall) be pushed further? The paper suggests integrating formal methods but provides no empirical evaluation of hybrid LLM‑symbolic systems.  
2. **Robust alias clustering:** What is an automated, scalable method for merging synonymous entity mentions? Current contextual grounding is "not foolproof"; no alternative is proposed or quantitatively assessed.  
3. **Model‑framework compatibility:** Why does Qwen benefit disproportionately from multi‑query extraction/validation? This points to unexplored model‑pipeline interactions that may be predicted by model capabilities like instruction following or self‑consistency.  
4. **Generalizability of enumeration:** Does skeleton‑guided enumeration break the coverage‑accuracy trade‑off for all exhaustive listing tasks, or only for STPA’s structured permutation logic? The method’s reliance on a fixed combinatorial formula may not transfer to other domains.  
5. **Optimal budget allocation:** What is the ideal \( M_e, M_v \) configuration given document complexity and cost constraints? The paper observes a "sweet spot" at two queries but lacks a cost‑sensitive utility model or difficulty‑aware parameter selection.

## Limitations
- **Replication gaps:** Exact prompt templates, dataset schema, LLM generation parameters, and alias‑merging implementation are unspecified, hindering faithful reproduction.
- **Alias clustering weakness:** The paper identifies clustering as a critical yet under‑addressed limitation with no quantitative analysis of its downstream impact on precision.
- **Performance saturation:** Results plateau around 92% recall and 73.8% precision, attributed to inherent language ambiguity, but this boundary is not formally proven nor systematically mitigated.

## Confidence
- **High:** Core multi‑query mechanisms (exponential error reduction) are mathematically derived and empirically validated across multiple LLMs (Figures 3‑4, Tables 4‑6).
- **Medium:** Saturation limit claim; plateau is observed but not isolated from possible prompt‑engineering constraints or model capability ceilings.
- **Low:** Robustness of alias clustering; admitted as a key weakness with no quantitative failure analysis or alternative approach evaluated.
