# NN-OpInf: an operator inference approach using structure-preserving composable neural networks

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2603.08488
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.08488
- Reference count: 40
- Primary result: NN-OpInf uses structured neural operators to produce accurate and physically consistent reduced-order models for dynamical systems with non-polynomial nonlinearities, significantly outperforming polynomial-based operator inference in accuracy while preserving invariants like energy conservation.

## Executive Summary
Reduced-order modeling (ROM) is essential for efficient simulation of high-dimensional dynamical systems. Traditional polynomial-based operator inference (P-OpInf) struggles when the underlying dynamics involve non-polynomial nonlinearities. NN-OpInf addresses this by replacing polynomial expansions with composable neural network operators that are individually constrained to respect key algebraic structures (e.g., skew-symmetry for energy conservation, symmetric positive semi-definiteness for dissipation). This hybrid approach combines the expressiveness of neural networks with the stability guarantees of structure-preserving discretizations. The method trains on snapshot data projected onto a reduced basis, using a custom optimization procedure that mixes L-BFGS and Adam to handle non-convexity. Experiments on problems like nonlinear convection-diffusion-reaction demonstrate orders-of-magnitude improvement in predictive accuracy over P-OpInf, while physically important invariants remain conserved. The trade-off is higher offline training cost due to neural network optimization.

## Method Summary
NN-OpInf formulates the reduced-order model (ROM) as a sum of learned operator blocks: $\dot{\hat{x}} = \sum_{r=1}^M \hat{g}_r(\eta_r; w_r)$. Each block $\hat{g}_r$ is a neural network with a specific architecture designed to produce outputs with a prescribed algebraic structure (e.g., a skew-symmetric matrix, a symmetric positive semi-definite matrix, or an unstructured vector). The inputs $\eta_r$ may include the reduced state $\hat{x}$ and/or parameters $\mu$. Training data consist of full-order snapshots and approximated time derivatives, which are projected onto a POD basis and normalized via max-abs scaling. The loss is the relative mean squared error on reduced velocities, plus $\ell_2$ regularization. Optimization uses a hybrid schedule: initial and periodic L-BFGS phases for fast descent, interleaved with Adam to escape local minima. The framework is modular: users select operator types based on the physics of the problem (e.g., a skew operator for conservative advection, an SPSD operator for diffusion, and a constant or neural forcing term). This structure enforces invariants by construction while allowing complex non-polynomial dynamics through neural network expressivity.

## Key Results
- **Accuracy:** On nonlinear convection-diffusion-reaction, NN-OpInf with structured operators reduced relative error by an order of magnitude compared to P-OpInf.
- **Invariant preservation:** Energy-conserving dynamics (e.g., Burgers' with symmetric flux) are accurately captured by enforcing skew-symmetry, leading to stable long-term simulations.
- **Composability:** Combining heterogeneous operator blocks (SPSD, skew, forcing) successfully models parametric problems where each physical term has distinct structure.
- **Trade-off:** Offline training is substantially more expensive than P-OpInf due to non-convex optimization, but yields superior predictive performance for non-polynomial systems.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1
- **Claim:** NN-OpInf enforces key physical invariants (e.g., energy conservation) by constructing operators with prescribed algebraic structure (e.g., skew-symmetry, symmetric positive semi-definiteness) via tailored neural architectures.
- **Mechanism:** Each operator block is parameterized to guarantee its structure by construction. For example, a skew-symmetric operator outputs only strictly lower-triangular entries and assembles a skew matrix; an SPSD operator outputs a Cholesky factor. This embeds physical laws directly into the ROM's functional form.
- **Core assumption:** The system's essential physics can be decomposed into sub-operators, each possessing an identifiable algebraic structure even if the full right-hand side does not.
- **Evidence anchors:** Abstract emphasizes "enforcing local operator structure"; Section 4.1 shows parameterizations for linear and nonlinear operators; Table 1 and Section 6 experiments demonstrate that structured blocks yield accurate, stable ROMs (e.g., Burgers' with skew operator conserves energy).
- **Break condition:** If the true underlying operator structure is not represented by the implemented block types (e.g., a needed divergence-free constraint is missing), the method may fail to capture critical invariants.

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim:** The additive, composable operator framework allows NN-OpInf to model complex, non-polynomial dynamics by combining heterogeneous, specialized blocks, increasing expressiveness while maintaining local interpretability and structure.
- **Mechanism:** The ROM right-hand side is a sum of blocks $\hat{g}_r$, each with potentially different input dependencies, neural architectures, and structural constraints. This modularity enables mixing, for instance, an SPSD diffusion term, a skew-symmetric advection term, and an unstructured forcing term, each optimized for its role.
- **Core assumption:** A complex dynamics can be effectively partitioned into simpler sub-operators with distinct input/structure requirements, and that this decomposition is identifiable from data.
- **Evidence anchors:** Abstract mentions "supporting additive compositions of heterogeneous operators"; Section 4 motivates the modular structure by noting many discretizations yield RHS operators that are sums of structured terms; Section 6.2 explicitly composes SPSD, skew, and forcing terms for CDR.
- **Break condition:** If the true dynamics cannot be additively decomposed into components matching the implemented structured/unstructured types, or if the decomposition is non-unidentifiable without strong regularization, the model may not learn the correct behavior.

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim:** NN-OpInf's hybrid L-BFGS/Adam optimization strategy and max-abs data normalization improve training robustness and convergence for the resulting non-convex, high-dimensional inference problem compared to pure SGD.
- **Mechanism:** The alternating schedule uses L-BFGS (quasi-Newton, full-batch) to rapidly descend to a local minimum, then Adam (stochastic) to escape sharp minima and navigate plateaus. Max-abs normalization scales each variable by its maximum absolute value, preserving relative scales and keeping the loss geometry well-conditioned while ensuring that the algebraic structure imposed on normalized operators matches that on the original operators.
- **Core assumption:** The loss landscape, though non-convex, contains regions where curvature information from L-BFGS is beneficial, and max-abs scaling maintains a consistent norm across reduced and full spaces.
- **Evidence anchors:** Section 4.2.1 describes the hybrid optimizer; Section 4.2.3 details max-abs normalization; Section 6 results show NN-OpInf often surpasses prior NN-ROMs that suffered from poor convergence.
- **Break condition:** If the problem is extremely large (prohibiting full-batch L-BFGS), has many pathological minima, or max-abs scaling is inappropriate (e.g., near-zero but critical coefficients), training may fail.

## Foundational Learning
- **Reduced Basis Approximation (POD):** Needed because NN-OpInf operates in a low-dimensional subspace. Snapshots are projected onto an orthonormal basis to obtain reduced states and velocities. Quick check: Given snapshot matrix $X \in \mathbb{R}^{N \times N_t N_{\text{train}}}$ and POD basis $V \in \mathbb{R}^{N \times K}$, compute $\hat{X} = V^\top X$ and $\dot{\hat{X}} = V^\top \dot{X}$ for each training trajectory.
- **Algebraic Structures in Discretized Physics:** Needed because the novelty lies in enforcing algebraic properties (skew, SPSD) that reflect physical invariants. The choice of structure depends on the FOM's discrete operator characteristics. Quick check: For a semi-discrete diffusion operator that is symmetric positive definite, which NN parameterization from Table 1 would enforce this? (Answer: SpdOperator, outputting a Cholesky factor.)
- **Non-convex Neural Network Optimization:** Needed because unlike P-OpInf's convex least-squares, NN-OpInf solves a non-convex problem. Success hinges on initialization, optimizer schedule, regularization, and normalization. Quick check: Why is inferring an SPSD matrix via $\hat{A} = \hat{L}\hat{L}^\top$ non-convex in $\hat{L}$? (Answer: The mapping $\hat{L} \mapsto \hat{L}\hat{L}^\top$ is not convex; the set of Cholesky factors is not a convex set.)

## Architecture Onboarding
The NN-OpInf software architecture comprises:
- **Variables:** Manage data and normalization for inputs (reduced state $\hat{x}$, parameters $\mu$) and output (reduced velocity $\dot{\hat{x}}$). Handle train/validation splits and scaling (default: max-abs).
- **Operators:** PyTorch `nn.Module` subclasses implementing structured blocks (e.g., `SkewOperator`, `SpdOperator`). Each defines `forward(inputs_dict)` returning the operator evaluation and declares its input dependencies (e.g., acts on $\hat{x}$ and/or depends on $\mu$).
- **Model:** Aggregates a list of operators; its `forward` computes the sum $\sum_r \hat{g}_r(\eta_r)$. Manages scaling from variables and passes inputs to each operator.
- **Training loop:** Uses `nnopinf.training.train`. Constructs mini-batches from normalized variables, evaluates model, minimizes relative MSE loss plus $\ell_2$ weight decay. Employs hybrid L-BFGS/Adam schedule.

**Critical path:**
1. **Data Prep:** Obtain FOM snapshots $X$ and $\dot{X}$ for training parameters.
2. **Project:** Compute POD basis $V$ from training $X$; project data: $\hat{X}=V^\top X$, $\dot{\hat{X}}=V^\top \dot{X}$.
3. **Define Variables:** Create `Variable` objects for $\hat{x}$, $\mu$ (if parametric), and $\dot{\hat{x}}$; attach projected data and set normalization.
4. **Select & Compose Operators:** Choose structured operator blocks based on physics (e.g., `SpdOperator` for diffusion, `SkewOperator` for advection, `VectorOffsetOperator` for forcing); instantiate with appropriate input dependencies and architecture hyperparameters.
5. **Build Model:** `model = Model([op1, op2, ...])`.
6. **Configure & Train:** Adjust hyperparameters (epochs, learning rate); call `training.train(model, variables=[x_var, mu_var], y=y_var, training_settings=settings)`.
7. **Save & Deploy:** Use `model.save()` to export operators; at runtime, load operators, normalize inputs with stored factors, evaluate `model({'x': x_red, 'mu': mu})` to get $\dot{\hat{x}}$.

**Design tradeoffs:**
- Structured vs. Standard NN: Structured operators embed physical priors, improving stability and generalization with potentially fewer parameters, but are less expressive for components without clear structure. The "Standard" operator is maximally flexible but prone to instability and unphysical predictions.
- Composability vs. Identifiability: More operator blocks increase flexibility but introduce non-identifiability; careful design and regularization are needed.
- Training Cost vs. Accuracy: NN-OpInf has higher offline training cost than P-OpInf, traded for superior accuracy in non-polynomial regimes.

**Failure signatures:**
- Training instability: loss oscillates or diverges → check learning rate, initialization, normalization, model complexity.
- Poor generalization: low training error, high validation error → overfitting, insufficient data, mismatched physical structure.
- Physical law violation: e.g., energy grows despite skew-symmetry → structured operator misapplied, weights decayed off manifold, or scaling distortion.
- "Deep convergence" stagnation: error plateaus → optimizer trapped; try more L-BFGS, different ensemble, simpler composition.

**First 3 experiments:**
1. **Burgers' Equation (Reproductive, K=10):** Use a `SkewOperator` alone; expect energy-conserving accurate ROMs comparable to quadratic P-OpInf; also test vanilla `Standard` operator (since dynamics are quadratic).
2. **Nonlinear CDR (Parametric, K=20):** Compose `SpdOperator` (diffusion), `SkewOperator` (advection), and `VectorOffsetOperator` (forcing). Expect `NN-OpInf-PSD-f` to significantly outperform `P-OpInf-cAH` and unstable `NN-OpInf-NN`.
3. **2D Nonlinear Heat Conduction (Future-state, K=8):** Use `SpdOperator` + `VectorOffsetOperator`. Train on early time $[0,1]$, predict to $t=2.0$. Expect `NN-OpInf-SPSD-f` to maintain accuracy while other baselines fail.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
1. **Stochastic SR1 optimization:** Could improve convergence and robustness over the current hybrid L-BFGS/Adam? (Future work explicitly mentions investigating stochastic SR1.)
2. **Richer operator libraries:** Can additional structures (e.g., divergence-free, curl-free) be integrated to enforce invariants for PDEs like incompressible flow?
3. **Dynamics-constrained training:** Would backpropagation through time (BPTT) improve long-term stability and accuracy by enforcing trajectory consistency?
4. **Uncertainty quantification:** How to provide reliable error bars for NN-OpInf predictions? (Deterministic operators lack uncertainty estimates.)
5. **Multiphysics extensions:** Can the additive composition framework handle coupled multiphysics systems while preserving the distinct structures of each component?

## Limitations
- Exact FOM discretizations and data generation details (time steps, parameter splits) are not fully specified, hindering replication.
- Operator input dependencies (e.g., whether forcing depends on state or only parameters) remain ambiguous for some problems, affecting model formulation.
- Higher offline training cost and sensitivity to hyperparameters may limit scalability to very large systems or data-scarce regimes.

## Confidence
- **Structure preservation (invariant enforcement):** High – strong experimental evidence across multiple problems shows structured blocks reliably enforce properties like energy conservation.
- **Composability:** Medium-High – validated in parametric CDR, but general applicability depends on user's ability to choose correct decompositions for new systems.
- **Optimization robustness:** Medium – hybrid L-BFGS/Adam and max-abs scaling improve convergence in tested cases, but effectiveness for very stiff or high-dimensional problems is unproven.

## Next Checks
1. **Replicate Burgers' energy conservation:** Implement the symmetric flux discretization exactly and verify that a `SkewOperator`-only NN-OpInf ROM conserves discrete energy, using identical POD bases and training splits.
2. **Test on unseen non-polynomial PDE:** Apply NN-OpInf to an equation like Allen-Cahn (non-polynomial nonlinearity) with appropriate structured blocks (e.g., SPSD for diffusion) and compare accuracy against P-OpInf with high-degree polynomials.
3. **Ablation study on optimizer schedule:** On the CDR problem, train the same model under pure Adam, pure L-BFGS, and the hybrid schedule; compare final validation error, convergence speed, and rollout stability.
