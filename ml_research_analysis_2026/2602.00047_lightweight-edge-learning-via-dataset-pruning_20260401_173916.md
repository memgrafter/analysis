# Lightweight Edge Learning via Dataset Pruning
## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2602.00047
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.00047
- **Reference count:** 36
- **Primary result:** Dataset pruning on edge devices yields near-linear reductions in training time and energy with minimal accuracy loss, and importance-based selection significantly outperforms random pruning.

## Executive Summary
This paper addresses the high training cost of on-device learning on battery-powered edge devices. It introduces a dataset pruning framework that reduces training data volume in a single shot. Each device first scores all local samples by their average loss over a brief warm-up phase, then deterministically retains only the top ρ fraction of most important samples for the actual training. The method is lightweight, requires no communication, and is model-agnostic. Evaluated on CIFAR-10 with ResNet-34 and MobileNetV2, the approach achieves near-linear reductions in training time and energy proportional to the pruning ratio, while the accuracy drop is small. Importance-based pruning substantially outperforms random selection, especially at low pruning ratios, and the overhead of scoring is negligible. The work positions dataset pruning as a complementary paradigm for sustainable edge learning.

## Method Summary
The proposed framework operates in three stages on each edge device: (1) a scoring engine runs a small number of SGD iterations (T₀) on the full local dataset, accumulating the training loss for each sample to compute an average loss importance score; (2) a selection scheduler ranks samples by these scores and selects the top Mₖ = ⌊ρₖNₖ⌋ samples to form a pruned training set; (3) a training executor continues SGD for a fixed number of epochs exclusively on the pruned set, starting from the warm-up model. The entire process is one-shot and deterministic, incurring negligible overhead. The method is designed to scale training cost linearly with ρ, and it is agnostic to model architecture and without any inter-device communication.

## Key Results
- Near-linear scaling: Training time and energy drop proportionally with ρ (e.g., ~90% reduction at ρ=0.1).
- Minimal accuracy loss: At ρ=0.5, retains ~90% of full-data accuracy; importance-based pruning outperforms random by >12% at ρ=0.1 and ~10% at ρ=0.2.
- Negligible scoring overhead: The warm-up phase adds little extra cost.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1: Importance Scoring via Early Loss
The method assigns an importance score to each sample by measuring its average training loss over a short warm-up phase (T₀ SGD iterations). The core assumption is that samples with higher early loss are more informative for the optimization trajectory; they either belong to hard or crucial regions of the data manifold. This score serves as a lightweight, first-order proxy for a sample’s contribution to gradient updates. Evidence from the paper’s abstract and Section V-A supports this design. However, if T₀ is too short relative to dataset heterogeneity, scores may become noisy, leading to poor subset selection.

### Mechanism 2: Deterministic Top-ρ Selection
After scoring, the device ranks all samples by importance and deterministically retains the top ρ fraction. This constructs a compact coreset that aims to preserve the learning dynamics of the full dataset. The deterministic rule ensures predictable resource usage without runtime sampling overhead. The approach assumes that the highest-importance samples (per early loss) will yield an optimization trajectory close to that of the full dataset. Yet, at very low ρ (e.g., ρ < 0.2), even the selected samples may inadequately cover the data manifold, risking accuracy collapse, especially under non-IID distributions.

### Mechanism 3: Linear Cost Reduction via Subset Training
Training on the pruned subset reduces the number of SGD updates approximately by factor ρ, because the number of iterations Tₖ scales with the subset size Mₖ = ρₖNₖ (given fixed epochs Eₖ and batch size bₖ). Consequently, total FLOPs, wall-clock time, and energy consumption drop near-linearly with ρ. This relies on the assumption that per-iteration computational cost remains unchanged by pruning and that the one-shot overhead (warm-up, sorting) is negligible. The paper’s theoretical analysis in Section III-E and experimental measurements in Section V-D support this scaling. If selection overhead is not kept small, the scaling may become sub-linear.

## Architecture Onboarding
The framework comprises three components: **Scoring Engine** (runs warm-up SGD, computes per-sample average loss), **Selection Scheduler** (ranks scores, selects top Mₖ samples), and **Training Executor** (performs full training on pruned set). The critical path is sequential: Scoring → Selection → Training. Design tradeoffs include: selecting ρ (lower ρ reduces cost but may hurt accuracy), choosing T₀ (longer warm-up improves score reliability but adds overhead), and determinism (predictable vs. potential diversity loss). Failure signatures are: sharp accuracy drop when ρ is too low (especially with non-IID data), sub-linear cost scaling if overhead dominates, and selection bias toward certain classes in heterogeneous settings. The first validation experiments should: (1) evaluate accuracy vs. ρ and compare with random pruning; (2) measure actual training time and energy scaling with ρ; (3) isolate and quantify warm-up overhead to confirm it is negligible.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- **Adaptation to streaming data:** The current method assumes a static dataset and fixed pruning ratio. How can it be extended to continuously arriving data and dynamically adjust pruning based on time-varying energy harvesting and latency constraints?
- **Robustness to extreme non-IID data:** The paper tests only moderate data heterogeneity across devices. Under severe class imbalance per device, local importance scores may not reflect global utility, leading to biased coresets and degraded model performance.
- **Sensitivity to warm-up length:** The warm-up phase T₀ is fixed without analysis. Too short yields noisy scores; too long adds overhead. What is the minimal T₀ that provides reliable importance without negating the pruning gains?

## Limitations
- **Hyperparameter ambiguity:** The exact warm-up iterations (T₀) and full training schedule (learning rate, epochs) are unspecified, requiring assumptions that may affect reproducibility and result interpretation.
- **Break conditions untested:** The method’s performance under high dataset heterogeneity or very low ρ may degrade, and scaling assumptions could fail if selection overhead is non-negligible.
- **Weak external validation:** The core scoring mechanism (average loss as importance proxy) lacks strong support in prior literature; neighboring work focuses on communication or model efficiency, not data-centric scoring for edge training.
