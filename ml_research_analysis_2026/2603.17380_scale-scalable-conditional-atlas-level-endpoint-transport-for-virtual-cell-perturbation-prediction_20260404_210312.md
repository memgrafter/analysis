# SCALE:Scalable Conditional Atlas-Level Endpoint transport for virtual cell perturbation prediction

## Quick Facts
- **ArXiv ID:** 2603.17380
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.17380
- **Reference count:** 40
- **Primary result:** SCALE achieves 12.51× pretraining and 1.29× inference speedup over STATE, improves PDCorr by 12.02% and DE Overlap by 10.66% on Tahoe-100M, with gains attributed to adaptive condition aggregation, JiT endpoint training, and a Gaussian-control mixed prior.

## Executive Summary
Virtual cell perturbation prediction faces three core bottlenecks: inefficient infrastructure for atlas-scale data, unstable modeling in high-dimensional single-cell spaces, and evaluation protocols that prioritize reconstruction over biological fidelity. SCALE addresses these with a three-part solution: a BioNeMo-based scalable training and inference framework; a conditional transport model using a hierarchical set-aware encoder and endpoint-aligned supervision; and an evaluation protocol focused on biologically meaningful cell-level metrics. On the Tahoe-100M dataset, this yields substantial computational speedups and superior recovery of true perturbation effects compared to prior art, as measured by PDCorr and DE Overlap. Ablations confirm that the benefits stem from specific architectural and training choices rather than flow matching alone.

## Method Summary
SCALE integrates a scalable infrastructure, a specialized model architecture, and a rigorous evaluation protocol. The BioNeMo-based framework preprocesses single-cell atlases into condition-grouped LMDB shards and uses batch-aware sampling to maximize hardware utilization during distributed training. The model employs a hierarchical encoder: first applying intra-cell gene attention (LLaMA-style), then inter-cell DeepSets pooling to produce permutation-equivariant population embeddings. A conditional flow network, trained via Just-in-Time (JiT) parameterization, transports a control latent distribution to the predicted perturbed endpoint under observed conditions (cell type, perturbation, batch). Supervision is applied directly to the endpoint prediction (ˆZ₁) rather than intermediate velocities. The model is trained in two stages: first pretraining the autoencoder for reconstruction with MMD regularization, then training the transport network with paired endpoint supervision. Evaluation uses the Cell-Eval protocol, computing pseudobulk perturbation effect correlations (PDCorr) and differential expression gene overlap (DE Overlap) to assess biological fidelity.

## Key Results
- 12.51× speedup in pretraining and 1.29× in inference compared to STATE, driven by BioNeMo infrastructure optimizations.
- On Tahoe-100M, SCALE improves PDCorr by 12.02% and DE Overlap by 10.66% relative to STATE.
- Ablations identify key contributors: adaptive condition aggregation, JiT endpoint-aligned training (x-pred/x-loss), and a Gaussian-control mixed prior; flow matching alone is insufficient.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1
- **Claim:** A BioNeMo-based data loading and distributed training framework removes I/O and scheduling bottlenecks, enabling scalable pretraining and inference on atlas-sized perturbation datasets.
- **Mechanism:** Raw AnnData is preprocessed into condition-grouped LMDB shards to minimize random disk access. Batch-aware sampling groups perturbations by experimental condition to improve hardware utilization. The full stack runs within BioNeMo’s optimized runtime for efficient distributed execution.
- **Core assumption:** The dominant scalability bottleneck is data movement and framework overhead, not model compute, when processing highly heterogeneous single-cell perturbation atlases.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract] "BioNeMo-based training and inference framework... yielding 12.51× speedup on pretrain and 1.29× on inference."
  - [section 4.2] Table 2 reports native epoch time reduction (1298.43s → 243.00s) and cell-normalized throughput gain (21,584.40 → 27,818.20 cells/s).
- **Break condition:** If future datasets grow such that model compute (not I/O) becomes the primary bottleneck, or if BioNeMo’s ecosystem locks users into proprietary tooling that limits integration.

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim:** Formulating perturbation prediction as endpoint-aligned conditional transport in a hierarchical set-aware latent space yields stable training and stronger recovery of population-level perturbation effects.
- **Mechanism:** (a) A two-stage encoder applies intra-cell gene attention (LLaMA-style) then inter-cell DeepSets pooling, producing permutation-equivariant cell embeddings. (b) A conditional flow network (via JiT parameterization) transports the control latent distribution to the perturbed endpoint under observed conditions. Supervision is applied directly to the predicted perturbed endpoint (ˆZ₁).
- **Core assumption:** Optimizing for observed endpoints with a set-aware latent space better aligns with the unpaired, population-level nature of the task than reconstructing an unobserved intermediate trajectory or fitting per-cell averages.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [section 3.2] Describes hierarchical encoder: gene-wise self-attention → cell-wise DeepSets aggregation → fusion layer.
  - [section 3.3] Contrasts endpoint-oriented supervision with standard flow matching; defines JiT variants.
  - [section 4.2 (ablation)] Figure 3(b) shows x-pred/x-loss (0.8303 PDCorr) outperforms base flow (0.7088).
- **Break condition:** If latent interpolation introduces geometric shortcuts the model exploits, or if the hierarchical encoder fails to preserve fine-grained gene-gene interactions needed for subtle perturbations.

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim:** Evaluating with a cell-level protocol centered on biologically meaningful metrics (PDCorr, DE Overlap) rather than reconstruction error reveals true perturbation recovery and avoids the "mean-effect trap."
- **Mechanism:** Computes pseudobulk perturbation effects (Δx_λ) from predicted and ground-truth cell populations. Correlates predicted vs. true effects (PDCorr). Performs differential expression analysis and measures overlap of top DE genes (DE Overlap) and direction agreement (DirAgr). Uses same control cells for both to isolate prediction quality.
- **Core assumption:** MSE/MAE over all HVGs penalizes small shifts in unaffected background genes, while biological impact is concentrated in a sparse set of differentially expressed genes; thus, correlation-based and DE-based metrics better reflect virtual cell utility.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract] "evaluation on Tahoe-100M using biologically meaningful cell-level metrics... improves PDCorr by 12.02% and DE Overlap by 10.66%."
  - [section 4.2] Table 1 shows SCALE’s high PDCorr/DE Overlap despite higher MSE vs. STATE, with discussion linking this to mean-effect avoidance.
- **Break condition:** If Cell-Eval metrics become unstable across implementations or fail to correlate with downstream experimental validation.

## Foundational Learning
- **Neural Optimal Transport / Conditional Flow Matching**  
  *Why needed:* The core modeling primitive; frames perturbation prediction as learning a map between control and perturbed distributions. SCALE uses a flow-matching instantiation with JiT parameterization.  
  *Quick check:* Why is predicting the perturbed endpoint (ˆZ₁) more stable than predicting a velocity field (ˆU) in the latent space under paired supervision?
- **Set-Equivariant Neural Networks (DeepSets, Transformers without position)**  
  *Why needed:* Cellular populations are unordered sets; the encoder must be permutation-equivariant to avoid spurious ordering biases.  
  *Quick check:* How does the hierarchical encoder (gene-level attention + cell-level DeepSets) preserve both intracellular gene dependencies and population-level permutation symmetry?
- **Single-Cell Perturbation Evaluation (Cell-Eval protocol)**  
  *Why needed:* Standard regression metrics (MSE) are insufficient; need population-level correlation and differential expression recovery to assess biological fidelity.  
  *Quick check:* What is the difference between PDCorr and DE Overlap, and why might they dissociate from MSE?

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map:** Data Loader (LMDB shards) -> Encoder (gene attention + DeepSets) -> Transport Backbone (DiT blocks) -> Prediction Head (JiT x-pred) -> Decoder -> Evaluator (Cell-Eval)
- **Critical path:** LMDB batch -> Encoder(X₀) -> Z₀ -> sample t -> interpolate Zₜ -> Transport(Zₜ, t, C) -> predict ˆZ₁ -> Decode ˆX₁ -> Compare to ground-truth X₁ via L_x. AE losses run in parallel on X₀/X₁.
- **Design tradeoffs:**
  - Speed vs. Fidelity: BioNeMo optimizations trade simplicity for throughput; may obscure debugging.
  - Set Modeling Complexity: Hierarchical encoder vs. simpler cell-wise MLP; former preserves more structure but increases parameters and memory.
  - JiT Parameterization: x-pred/x-loss stable but may encourage shortcut learning; v-pred/v-loss could capture dynamics but harder to optimize.
  - Prior Distribution: Gaussian-control mix reduces shortcut risk but adds hyperparameter (mixing ratio); masking too destructive in latent space.
- **Failure signatures:**
  - Shortcut learning: High PDCorr but near-linear mapping from Z₀ to ˆZ₁; check latent interpolation plots.
  - Condition leakage: PCA of Z colored by batch/perturbation shows clustering by batch而非biology.
  - Metric-instability: Cell-Eval scores vary across runs with same seed; suspect nondeterministic CUDA or evaluation aggregation bugs.
  - Decoder collapse: ˆX₁ has near-zero variance across cells; check decoder gradient norms.
- **First 3 experiments:**
  1. Data pipeline sanity check: Load one LMDB shard, visualize distribution of cell counts per perturbation, confirm HVG selection yields 2000 genes, check log(1+𝑥)×10 scaling matches baseline.
  2. Minimal training run: Train on 1% of Tahoe-100M with 2 transformer blocks, no distributed setup. Verify: (a) L_x decreases, (b) PDCorr > 0.5 on validation, (c) condition tokens learned (embedding norms non-zero).
  3. Cell-Eval reproducibility: Take a published STATE checkpoint, generate predictions on Tahoe-100M test split using SCALE’s exact preprocessing and evaluator. Report PDCorr/DE Overlap; should match STATE’s numbers within ±0.02 if protocol aligned.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
1. For perturbation regimes with modest state shifts, is a multi-step flow-matching formulation preferable to a simpler direct endpoint prediction model?
2. Does the current flow-based conditional transport capture true causal perturbation structure, or does it exploit shortcuts due to the proximity of control and perturbed states?
3. How can we establish a fully standardized, reproducible evaluation protocol for single-cell perturbation prediction that eliminates hidden implementation biases?

## Limitations
- Infrastructure claims lack independent verification; speedups may be specific to BioNeMo’s ecosystem and not generalizable.
- Hyperparameters (layer dimensions, λ_MMD, mixing ratio) are undisclosed, hindering exact replication and tuning.
- Cell-Eval sensitivity is noted; metrics may vary with preprocessing choices (e.g., HVG selection, normalization), risking overfitting to protocol quirks.
- Long-term biological validity unproven; gains on Tahoe-100M may not transfer to unseen perturbation types (e.g., CRISPR knockdowns) without retraining.

## Confidence
- Mechanism 1 (BioNeMo scalability): Medium
- Mechanism 2 (Hierarchical encoder + JiT): High
- Mechanism 3 (Cell-level evaluation): Medium

## Next Checks
1. Infrastructure Independence Test: Reimplement SCALE’s data pipeline (LMDB sharding, batch-aware sampling) in pure PyTorch without BioNeMo. Measure epoch time/throughput on Tahoe-100M; if speedups vanish, BioNeMo is necessary; if comparable, claims overstate framework-specific advantage.
2. Encoder Invariance & Shortcut Probe: (a) Shuffle cell order in a batch—predictions must be identical. (b) Visualize latent Z₀→Z₁ interpolations; linear trajectories may indicate shortcutting. (c) Ablate DeepSets pooling (replace with mean); performance drop confirms set-aware design necessity.
3. Cell-Eval Robustness Audit: Implement Cell-Eval independently (version 0.6.6) from scratch. Run on published STATE predictions using SCALE’s exact preprocessing; if PDCorr/DE Overlap differ by >0.02, protocol drift is a risk. Also, test sensitivity to HVG count (1k–3k).
