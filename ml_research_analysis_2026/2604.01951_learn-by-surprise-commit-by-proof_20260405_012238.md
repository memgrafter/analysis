# Learn by Surprise, Commit by Proof

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID**: 2604.01951  
- **Source URL**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.01951  
- **Reference count**: 37  
- **Primary result**: LSCP reduces memorization (perturbation gap from ~11.6× to ~2.7–3.0× on Qwen3-14B) and generalizes across models to near-baseline gaps.

## Executive Summary
Language models cannot autonomously learn from new information; standard fine-tuning induces rote memorization and harms existing knowledge. LSCP addresses this with a self-gated post-training framework comprising three stages: (1) detect surprising passages via anomalously high per-token loss; (2) self-verify using generated Q&A chains to test consistency with existing knowledge; (3) adjust AdamW’s β2 parameter proportionally to verification depth (β2 = 0.999·r^k) for selective consolidation. On Qwen3-14B, normal fine-tuning produces a perturbation gap (paraphrase vs. original perplexity ratio) of 11.6±0.2×, indicating memorization, while all LSCP conditions achieve 2.7–3.0×, confirming semantic learning. The Q&A format alone (r=1.0) prevents memorization; β2 gating at r=0.98 further protects adjacent knowledge, improving accuracy from 90% to 93±7%. Across six models (8B–32B, four families), LSCP consistently yields near-baseline perturbation gaps (1.9–5.9×) versus normal fine-tuning’s 7.7–81.9×, demonstrating generalized efficacy in autonomous, verifiable knowledge acquisition.

## Method Summary
LSCP is a self-gated post-training framework that operates in three stages. First, it flags passages with anomalously high per-token loss (surprisal) relative to a reference corpus, indicating potential knowledge gaps. Second, for each flagged passage, the model generates a chain of epistemic-tagged Q&A pairs ([existing], [mechanism], [implication]) and sequentially validates each answer against its own knowledge; the number of steps survived before failure (k) represents the conviction depth. Third, the training process adjusts the AdamW β2 parameter to 0.999·r^k for each training item, where r controls the schedule; this dynamically modulates the optimizer’s variance lock, allowing stronger updates for more verified content while suppressing noise. The training data consists of these verified Q&A pairs alongside the original passage windows and strangeness records. All stages use the same model, ensuring autonomous operation without external verifiers.

## Key Results
- Normal fine-tuning on Qwen3-14B yields a perturbation gap of 11.6±0.2×, indicating severe memorization; LSCP reduces this to 2.7–3.0× across all conditions, confirming semantic learning.
- The Q&A format alone (r=1.0) prevents memorization; adjusting β2 (r=0.98) further protects adjacent knowledge, improving accuracy from 90% to 93±7%.
- Across six models (8B–32B, four families), LSCP achieves near-baseline perturbation gaps (1.9–5.9×) while normal fine-tuning produces gaps of 7.7–81.9×, demonstrating consistent generalization.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1: Surprisal-Based Detection
- **Claim**: Per-token surprisal (loss) signals knowledge gaps, enabling detection of unfamiliar passages without external labels.
- **Mechanism**: Compute mean surprisal over a passage window; flag if it exceeds µ + λσ from a reference corpus. The model’s forward pass provides this “free” salience signal.
- **Evidence**: Section 2.1 links high loss to parametric knowledge incompleteness.
- **Break condition**: Poor λ calibration causes false positives (known passages flagged) or false negatives (novel passages missed), derailing the pipeline.

### Mechanism 2: Self-Verification via Q&A Chains
- **Claim**: Self-generated Q&A chains verify whether surprising content is consistent with the model’s existing knowledge, separating novelty from noise.
- **Mechanism**: For each flagged passage, generate N Q&A pairs with epistemic tags. Validate each answer via the model’s own forward pass; record the step `k` where consistency fails. This `k` is the conviction depth.
- **Evidence**: Section 2.2 uses mechanism questions (e.g., “Does A actually cause B?”) to catch compositionally valid corruption.
- **Break condition**: Systematic model bias (e.g., overconfidence in a flawed worldview) may allow corrupt passages to achieve high `k` and bypass gating.

### Mechanism 3: β₂ Adjustment for Selective Consolidation
- **Claim**: Adjusting AdamW’s β₂ proportionally to conviction depth (β₂ = 0.999·rᵏ) selectively opens the “Variance Lock” for verified content, strengthening correct updates while suppressing noise.
- **Mechanism**: Lower β₂ reduces the second-moment estimate’s inertia, letting novel gradients have more influence. `k` scales the opening; `r` controls the schedule. β₂ resets after each item.
- **Evidence**: Section 2.3 explains how default high β₂ (≈0.999) downweights large gradients from novel data.
- **Break condition**: If `r` is too low (e.g., 0.9), even weakly verified items receive strong learning, risking contamination; if too high (e.g., 1.0), β₂ never drops, yielding no adjacent-knowledge protection.

## Foundational Learning
- **AdamW’s β₂ parameter**: Why needed: To understand how the optimizer’s variance estimate suppresses novel gradients and how β₂ adjustment gates learning strength. Quick check: What happens to the update magnitude when β₂ is lowered in AdamW?
- **Perturbation gap metric**: Why needed: Central experimental measure distinguishing memorization (high gap) from semantic learning (gap near baseline). Section 3.1 defines it as PPL(paraphrase)/PPL(original). Quick check: Why is a gap of 11.6× considered memorization while 2.7× is semantic?
- **Graduated accept vs. threshold accept**: Why needed: LSCP’s core design choice—modulating learning strength continuously via k (graduated) vs. binary filtering (threshold). Section 3.3 contrasts them. Quick check: What is the trade-off between graduated accept and threshold accept?

## Architecture Onboarding

**Component map**:  
Surprisal Calculator → Q&A Generator → Consistency Checker → β₂ Scheduler → Training Loop.  
Grounding Store is an external KV cache for {Pi, Si, di}.

**Critical path**:  
Forward pass (Stage 1) → Q&A generation (single call) → sequential consistency checks → determine `k` → train each item with scheduled β₂. All stages use the same model `Mθ` (per Section 2.2.3).

**Design tradeoffs**:  
- Verification thoroughness vs. cost: `N` scales with surprisal; more questions increase compute but improve discrimination.  
- Safety vs. learning: Lower `r` protects adjacent knowledge but may commit corrupt content if `k` is overestimated.  
- Graduated vs. threshold accept: Graduated maximizes learning (all `k>0` items trained weakly); threshold maximizes safety (only `k=N` items trained).

**Failure signatures**:  
- High **corrupt-adjacent error** (> baseline 90%) indicates β₂ gating too weak (`r` too high) or Stage 2 failing to detect corruption.  
- Very low **conviction depth `k`** across passages suggests Stage 2 is too conservative (e.g., prompt bottleneck).  
- **Perturbation gap** near Normal’s 11.6× signals memorization—likely Q&A format failed (check `r=1.0` condition).

**First 3 experiments**:  
1. **Baseline perturbation gap test**: Train on raw passage text (Normal) vs. Q&A pairs (`r=1.0`). Expect Normal ≈11× gap, Q&A ≈2.7×—isolates format effect.  
2. **`r`-sweep**: Run LSCP with `r ∈ {0.9, 0.95, 0.98, 1.0}`. Measure corrupt-adjacent accuracy and perturbation gap. Expect best protection at `r=0.98`.  
3. **Cross-model generalization**: Apply LSCP (`r=0.98`) to 6 models. Expect near-baseline gaps (1.9–5.9×) vs. Normal’s 7.7–81.9×, confirming Q&A-based training as primary anti-memorization mechanism.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
1. **Multi-model cross-verification for compositionally valid corruption?**  
   *Basis*: Section 6.1 notes single-model verification cannot catch claims that are compositionally false but all sub-claims true.  
   *Why unresolved*: Self-verification may miss false narratives that fit the model’s schema.  
   *Resolving evidence*: Build a benchmark of passages with compositionally false claims; test LSCP with ensembles of models vs. single-model.

2. **Dynamic surprisal threshold adaptation?**  
   *Basis*: Section 6.4 suggests adjusting λ as model evolves; Section 6.2 notes reference statistics drift.  
   *Why unresolved*: Static λ becomes miscalibrated as model learns, causing inefficiency or missed passages.  
   *Resolving evidence*: Run LSCP over multiple phases, periodically recompute µ/σ; compare flagged fraction and learning efficacy vs. static λ.

3. **Reliability of epistemic origin tags?**  
   *Basis*: Section 6.2 states tags are not formally verified; mistags can undermine conviction depth.  
   *Why unresolved*: Noisy tags degrade β₂ gating signal, potentially contaminating learning.  
   *Resolving evidence*: Manually annotate generated Q&A pairs with ground-truth tags; measure tag accuracy and correlate with learning outcomes.

4. **Generalization to non-propositional domains?**  
   *Basis*: Experiments focus on factual/mechanistic domains; Q&A format may not apply to creative writing, code, etc.  
   *Why unresolved*: Knowledge in these domains is not easily decomposable into verifiable claims, possibly leading to different dynamics.  
   *Resolving evidence*: Apply LSCP to creative writing and code corpora; measure perturbation gap, accuracy, human evaluations vs. normal fine-tuning.

5. **Self-extinguishing property with disjoint documents?**  
   *Basis*: The claim relies on coherent document streams with reinforcement; disjoint streams may not reduce surprisal over time.  
   *Why unresolved*: Persistent flagging could cause weight drift or adapter saturation in long-term learning.  
   *Resolving evidence*: Simulate two streams: coherent textbooks vs. random disjoint articles; track flagged fraction and weight norm changes over time.

## Limitations
- **Internal validation only**: All evidence is from self-referential experiments; no independent replication on standard benchmarks, and citation impact is negligible (avg 0.0 citations, max h-index 2).
- **High variance in reported improvements**: Accuracy gains (e.g., 93±7%) show large error bars, raising concerns about sample size and robustness.
- **Adversarial blind spot**: Self-verification may fail on compositionally valid corruption where individual claims are true but collectively false—untested.

## Confidence
- Memorization prevention (perturbation gap reduction): Medium  
- β₂ gating for adjacent knowledge protection: Low–Medium  
- Cross-model generalization: Medium  

## Next Checks
1. **Independent replication**: Re-run LSCP on a standard continual learning benchmark (e.g., LAMA with time cutoff), comparing memorization metrics to LoRA/full fine-tuning.
2. **Adversarial falsification test**: Construct synthetic passages with compositionally false claims (all sub-claims factually correct); measure whether conviction depth `k` falls below a safety threshold (e.g., `k < N/2`).
3. **Cross-verifier ablation**: Replace self-verifier `Mθ` with a separate model (larger sibling or human-labeled answers) in Stage 2; quantify impact on `k` distribution and final accuracy.
