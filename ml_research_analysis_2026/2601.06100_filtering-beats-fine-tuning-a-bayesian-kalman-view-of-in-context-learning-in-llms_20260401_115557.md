# Filtering Beats Fine Tuning: A Bayesian Kalman View of In Context Learning in LLMs
## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2601.06100
- **Source:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.06100
- **Reference count:** 33
- **Primary result:** In-context learning (ICL) in LLMs is driven by covariance collapse—a rapid reduction in posterior uncertainty induced by informative prompt tokens—which typically precedes convergence of the posterior mean, under a Bayesian filtering interpretation.

## Executive Summary
This paper provides a theoretical framework for in-context learning (ICL) in large language models, recasting it as Bayesian filtering over a low-dimensional latent adaptation state. The authors posit a linearized state-space model where task-specific parameters are expressed as a fixed pretrained base plus a low-rank adaptation. Under Gaussian assumptions, the posterior over this latent state evolves via a Kalman recursion. Key results prove that inference-time learning is governed by exponential covariance contraction (covariance collapse) provided the prompt tokens uniformly excite the adaptation subspace (observability). This yields stability guarantees and mean-square error bounds. Furthermore, common fine-tuning algorithms like gradient descent and natural gradient emerge as degenerate, noise-free limits of the filter. Minimal experiments on synthetic and toy settings corroborate the qualitative predictions, unifying ICL, parameter-efficient fine-tuning, and test-time learning under a single Bayesian perspective with explicit uncertainty dynamics.

## Method Summary
The method models the effective model parameters at token \(t\) as \(\theta_t = \theta_0 + B x_t\), where \(\theta_0\) are frozen pretrained weights, \(B \in \mathbb{R}^{D \times d}\) is a low-rank adaptation basis, and \(x_t \in \mathbb{R}^d\) is a latent task state. Each observed token induces a scalar loss \(\ell_t(\theta_t)\). Linearizing \(\ell_t\) around \(\theta_0\) yields an observation model \(y_t^{\text{obs}} = H_t x_t + v_t\), with \(H_t = \nabla_\theta \ell_t(\theta_0)^\top B\) and observation noise \(v_t\) subsuming linearization error. A (linear or extended) Kalman filter sequentially updates the posterior over \(x_t\): predict \(P_{t|t-1} = P_{t-1} + Q\), then update with gain \(K_t\) using \(H_t\) and observation noise covariance \(R_t\). The effective parameters for generation are \(\theta_0 + B \mu_T\) after processing the context. Scalable approximations (diagonal, ensemble) for the posterior covariance \(P_t\) are considered.

## Key Results
- Inference-time learning is driven by **covariance collapse**: rapid exponential contraction of the posterior uncertainty \(P_t\) induced by informative tokens, which typically occurs before the posterior mean \(\mu_t\) converges to the true task shift.
- The filtering dynamics are **stable** under a uniform observability condition on the token Jacobians \(H_t\), yielding exponential lower bounds on the information matrix growth and corresponding upper bounds on the covariance.
- Gradient-based fine-tuning (GD, natural gradient) and meta-learning updates arise as **singular limits** of the Kalman recursion when observation noise vanishes (\(R_t \to 0\)) and the prior covariance is static.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1
- **Claim:** Inference-time learning is driven by *covariance collapse*, a rapid contraction of epistemic uncertainty induced by informative tokens, which typically precedes convergence of the posterior mean.
- **Mechanism:** The posterior precision matrix \(\Lambda_t\) accumulates information via \(\Lambda_t = \Lambda_{t|t-1} + H_t^\top R_t^{-1} H_t\). If the sequence of token Jacobians \(H_t\) satisfies uniform observability (i.e., the accumulated information matrix \(W_{t,T} \succeq \alpha I\) over a finite horizon), the covariance \(P_t = \Lambda_t^{-1}\) decays exponentially, even while the mean \(\mu_t\) may still be converging.
- **Core assumptions:** The token Jacobians collectively excite the low-dimensional adaptation subspace \(\text{col}(B)\); higher-order/nonlinear residuals are well-modeled as Gaussian noise \(v_t\).
- **Evidence anchors:** [abstract] "inference-time learning is driven by covariance collapse"; [section 4.4] Lemmas on information accumulation and covariance contraction formalize \(W_{t,T} \succeq \alpha I\) and \(P_{t+T} \preceq (\Lambda_t + \alpha I)^{-1}\).
- **Break condition:** If prompts fail to uniformly excite \(\text{col}(B)\), \(W_{t,T}\) may be singular or ill-conditioned, halting guaranteed contraction and leading to ill-posed inference.

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim:** ICL corresponds to Bayesian inference over a *low-dimensional latent adaptation state* \(x_t\) that linearly perturbs the frozen pretrained parameters.
- **Mechanism:** The pretrained model \(\theta_0\) is fixed. Task-specific deviations are captured by \(\theta_t = \theta_0 + B x_t\), where \(B\) projects a small latent \(x_t\) into parameter space. Each token provides a linearized observation linking its loss gradient to \(x_t\) via \(H_t = \nabla_\theta \ell_t^\top B\). Bayesian updating with a Gaussian prior on \(x_t\) yields a Kalman recursion that sequentially refines the posterior.
- **Core assumptions:** The true task shift lies near \(\text{col}(B)\); the local linearization \(\ell_t(\theta_t) \approx \ell_t(\theta_0) + \nabla_\theta \ell_t^\top B x_t\) is accurate in a neighborhood of \(\theta_0\).
- **Evidence anchors:** [section 6.1] posits the low-dimensional state; [section 6.2] derives the scalar observation model.
- **Break condition:** If the true shift requires directions outside \(\text{col}(B)\) or linearization error \(v_t\) is large/non-Gaussian, the model cannot represent the necessary adaptation, leading to persistent bias.

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim:** Optimization-based fine-tuning (GD, natural gradient, meta-learning) emerges as a *singular, noise-free limit* of the Bayesian filtering dynamics, where epistemic uncertainty is ignored.
- **Mechanism:** The Kalman gain \(K_t = P_{t|t-1} H_t^\top (H_t P_{t|t-1} H_t^\top + R_t)^{-1}\) balances prior uncertainty and observation noise. As \(R_t \to 0\) with \(P_{t|t-1}\) fixed, \(K_t \to P_{t|t-1} H_t^\top (H_t P_{t|t-1} H_t^\top)^{-1}\), yielding a preconditioned gradient step on \(x_t\), equivalent to a natural gradient step on \(\theta_t\).
- **Core assumptions:** Observation noise vanishes (\(R_t \to 0\)) and prior covariance is static (or evolves slowly relative to information gain).
- **Evidence anchors:** [section 7.6/Theorem 4] shows the mean update converges to a gradient step with step size \(\eta = P_0\); [abstract] states gradient methods arise as singular limits.
- **Break condition:** The equivalence breaks if observation noise is non-negligible or prior covariance evolves significantly (\(Q > 0\)). In realistic regimes, the Kalman gain properly regularizes updates based on uncertainty—something fixed-step optimizers lack.

## Foundational Learning
- **Concept: Bayesian Filtering (Kalman Recursion)**
  - **Why needed:** Provides closed-form, sequential posterior updates under Gaussian assumptions, enabling explicit modeling of uncertainty dynamics that drive ICL.
  - **Quick check:** What is the role of the Kalman gain \(K_t\) in balancing prior belief and new token evidence?

- **Concept: Linearization and Low-Rank Subspaces**
  - **Why needed:** Reduces intractable high-dimensional parameter inference to a feasible low-dimensional state problem; connects to parameter-efficient fine-tuning (e.g., LoRA).
  - **Quick check:** How does the rank of \(B\) relate to the number of tokens needed for reliable covariance collapse?

- **Concept: Observability & Persistent Excitation**
  - **Why needed:** System-theoretic conditions guaranteeing that token observations sufficiently constrain the latent state, ensuring exponential covariance contraction.
  - **Quick check:** What property must the sequence of token Jacobians \(H_t\) have to satisfy uniform observability (\(W_{t,T} \succeq \alpha I\))?

## Architecture Onboarding
Frozen Pretrained Model -> Adaptation Subspace (B) -> Latent State Posterior (μ_t, P_t) -> Effective Parameters (θ_0 + B μ_t)

**Critical path:**
1. **Initialization:** Set prior \(P_0\) (diffuse or informed) and \(\mu_0 = 0\). Load frozen \(\theta_0\) and fixed \(B\).
2. **Per-token loop:** For each token in context:
   a. Forward pass with \(\theta = \theta_0 + B \mu_{t-1}\) to get logits/target token.
   b. Compute token loss \(\ell_t\) and gradient \(g_t = \nabla_\theta \ell_t\).
   c. Form observation \(H_t = g_t^\top B\) and set \(R_t\) (e.g., scalar or diagonal from Fisher).
   d. **Predict:** \(P_{t|t-1} = P_{t-1} + Q\).
   e. **Update:** Compute \(K_t = P_{t|t-1} H_t^\top (H_t P_{t|t-1} H_t^\top + R_t)^{-1}\). Then \(\mu_t \leftarrow \mu_{t-1} + K_t (y_t^{\text{obs}} - H_t \mu_{t-1})\) and \(P_t \leftarrow (I - K_t H_t) P_{t|t-1}\).
3. **Deployment:** Use \(\theta_{\text{eff}} = \theta_0 + B \mu_T\) for generation; or continue streaming updates.

**Design tradeoffs:**
- **\(B\) structure:** Random vs. learned? SVD of gradient corpus? Rank \(d\) trades expressivity vs. \(O(d^2)\) cost for \(P_t\).
- **Covariance representation:** Full matrix (clean but \(O(d^2)\)), diagonal (cheap but misses correlations), low-rank+diagonal, or ensemble (EnKF, scalable but stochastic).
- **\(R_t\) and \(Q\) specification:** \(R_t\) could be scalar, diagonal (from mini-batch Fisher), or learned. \(Q\) controls tracking for non-stationary tasks; \(Q=0\) assumes stationarity.
- **Linearization fidelity:** EKF recomputes \(H_t\) at current \(\mu_{t-1}\). Accuracy may degrade if \(\mu_t\) drifts far from 0. Consider multiple Gauss-Newton steps or UKF.

**Failure signatures:**
- **\(P_t\) divergence:** Eigenvalues explode → \(\Lambda_t\) not growing fast enough. Cause: observability violated, or \(Q\) too large relative to information rate.
- **\(P_t\) premature collapse:** All uncertainty vanishes → overfitting to early noisy tokens. Cause: \(R_t\) underestimated (over-confident linearization) or \(Q=0\) with non-stationary shift.
- **Oscillating \(\mu_t\):** \(K_t\) too large. Cause: \(P_{t|t-1}\) too large (\(Q\) too high) or \(R_t\) too small, causing aggressive updates.
- **No improvement over zero-shot:** \(H_t\) consistently near-zero. Cause: Tokens insensitive to \(\text{col}(B)\) (\(B\) misaligned) or gradients \(\nabla_\theta \ell_t\) orthogonal to \(\text{col}(B)\).

**First 3 experiments:**
1. **Reproduce synthetic linear regression:** Generate true \(\alpha^* \in \mathbb{R}^d\). For \(t=1\dots T\), sample \(h_t \sim \mathcal{N}(0,I)\) and \(y_t = h_t^\top \alpha^* + \varepsilon_t\), \(\varepsilon_t \sim \mathcal{N}(0,\sigma^2)\). Use linear Kalman: \(H_t = h_t^\top\), \(R = \sigma^2\), \(Q=0\), \(P_0 = \lambda I\). Plot \(\text{tr}(P_t)\) and \(\|\mu_t - \alpha^*\|\).
2. **Test observability sensitivity:** On a synthetic task, design "good" vs. "bad" prompts. In bad prompts, make \(H_t\) vectors nearly collinear or with small norm. Measure \(\lambda_{\min}(W_{t,T})\) and final \(\|\mu_T - \alpha^*\|\). Expect slow/no contraction for bad prompts.
3. **Compare to gradient-based fine-tuning:** On a small decoder-only transformer (e.g., GPT-2 small) with frozen body and low-rank \(B\) adapter on MLPs, run: (a) Kalman filtering on context tokens; (b) SGD fine-tuning of \(B\) on same context. Compare sample efficiency, stability (loss variance), and generalization to held-out prompt from same task. Expect Kalman to converge faster with less tuning sensitivity.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- **Question 1:** What conditions ensure exponential covariance contraction in extended Kalman filtering for nonlinear LLM loss observations?
  - **Basis in paper:** [Limitations] "Extending the theory to nonlinear observation operators and richer uncertainty representations is an important direction for future work."
  - **Why unresolved:** Current analysis is restricted to linear-Gaussian models; nonlinearities may disrupt contraction over long contexts.

- **Question 2:** How do structured covariance approximations (e.g., diagonal, low-rank, ensemble) affect the observability condition and covariance contraction rates?
  - **Basis in paper:** [Limitations] "maintaining even structured covariance approximations may be costly at scale"; [Conclusion] "Future work will explore scalable approximations."
  - **Why unresolved:** Full covariance is intractable for large models; impact of approximations on contraction guarantees is unknown.

- **Question 3:** Does covariance collapse—rapid uncertainty reduction preceding mean convergence—manifest in state-of-the-art large language models during few-shot ICL?
  - **Basis in paper:** [Abstract] "Minimal illustrative experiments corroborate the qualitative predictions"; [Conclusion] "Future work will explore ... empirical validation in large-scale foundation models."
  - **Why unresolved:** Validation is limited to synthetic/toy settings; unclear if separation of timescales holds for billion-parameter transformers on real tasks.

## Limitations
- The theory assumes linearized dynamics around \(\theta_0\), a low-dimensional adaptation subspace \(B\), Gaussian noise, and uniform observability of token Jacobians.
- Unknowns block faithful reproduction: construction of \(B\) (random, pre-trained, task-specific?), appropriate values for \(R_t\) and \(Q\), and precise definition of the scalar observation residual.
- Minimal experiments are on synthetic data and toy LLMs; scalability to large models and real-world tasks remains unproven.

## Confidence
- **Mechanism 1 (covariance collapse):** Medium. Exponential contraction proof requires strong observability; real token sequences may not satisfy it.
- **Mechanism 2 (low-dimensional latent state):** Medium. Aligns with LoRA but true task shift may lie outside \(\text{col}(B)\), causing bias.
- **Mechanism 3 (optimization as noise-free limit):** High. The limit derivation is mathematically rigorous under the Kalman model.

## Next Checks
1. **Synthetic observability stress test:** Generate token Jacobians \(H_t\) with controlled conditioning. Verify \(\text{tr}(P_t)\) decays exponentially iff the accumulated information matrix \(W_{t,T} \succeq \alpha I\). Correlate contraction rate with ICL performance.
2. **Ablation on \(B\) construction:** For a fixed LLM and task, compare ICL accuracy using \(B\) as (a) random orthonormal, (b) PCA of a gradient corpus, (c) LoRA‑trained. Track posterior uncertainty and sample efficiency.
3. **Kalman vs. SGD fine‑tuning on real prompts:** Across multiple tasks and model sizes, run EKF with diagonal covariance (tuned \(Q, R\)) against SGD on the same low‑rank adapter. Compare few‑shot performance, stability, and variance over random seeds.
