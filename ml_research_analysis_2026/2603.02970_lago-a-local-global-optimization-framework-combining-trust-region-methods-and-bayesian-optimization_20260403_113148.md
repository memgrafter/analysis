# LAGO: A Local-Global Optimization Framework Combining Trust Region Methods and Bayesian Optimization

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2603.02970
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.02970
- **Reference count:** 32
- **Primary result:** Hybrid BO-trust region method that reliably converges to global minima across non-convex problems, outperforming local methods and matching/beating BO-trust region hybrids in sample efficiency.

## Executive Summary
LAGO introduces a novel hybrid optimization framework that combines gradient-enhanced Bayesian Optimization (BO) for global exploration with a gradient-based trust region method (SR1) for local refinement. The core innovation lies in decoupling proposal generation: global candidates are obtained by maximizing the Expected Improvement acquisition function outside the current trust region, while local candidates arise from solving a deterministic quadratic subproblem inside the trust region. A lengthscale-based distance filter ensures that only sufficiently separated local evaluations are fed back into the global Gaussian process surrogate, preventing numerical instability from clustered points. This design allows aggressive local convergence without compromising global search. Empirical evaluations on a suite of synthetic benchmarks and a PDE-constrained optimization problem demonstrate that LAGO reliably reaches global minima, outperforms standard local optimizers on multimodal landscapes, and achieves sample efficiency comparable to or better than existing BO-trust region hybrids, striking an effective balance between exploration and exploitation.

## Method Summary
LAGO is a hybrid optimization algorithm that interleaves global Bayesian Optimization (BO) with a local trust region method (SR1). At each iteration, it generates two candidate points: a global candidate by maximizing the Expected Improvement (EI) acquisition function *outside* the current trust region ball, and a local candidate by solving a deterministic quadratic subproblem *inside* the trust region. The next evaluation is selected by comparing the global EI with the deterministic improvement predicted by the local quadratic model; if EI exceeds a threshold γ times the local improvement, the global point is evaluated, otherwise the local point is taken. To maintain a well-conditioned global Gaussian process (GP) surrogate, only local points that satisfy a lengthscale-based minimum-distance criterion (‖x−x_c‖ > νℓ) are incorporated into the GP's training set. This decoupling prevents the dense clustering of points during local exploitation from causing numerical instability in the GP, while still allowing aggressive local refinement. The GP is gradient-enhanced, using both function and derivative observations, and is periodically retrained with maximum likelihood estimation of hyperparameters.

## Key Results
- Reliably converges to global minima on synthetic benchmarks (Branin, Rosenbrock, Levy, Styblinski–Tang 2D/5D, Sphere) and a PDE-constrained problem across varying non-convexity and dimensionality.
- Outperforms standard local optimizers (e.g., L-BFGS with restarts) on multimodal problems.
- Matches or exceeds competing BO-trust region hybrids (e.g., TuRBO, TREGO, LABCAT) in sample efficiency while balancing exploration and exploitation.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1: Decoupled Proposal Generation
- **Claim:** Separating global BO acquisition maximization (outside the trust region) from local trust region steps (inside) prevents numerical instability in the global GP while allowing aggressive local refinement.
- **Mechanism:** The global GP surrogate is only used to propose points *outside* the current trust region ball. Local trust region steps solve a deterministic quadratic subproblem inside the ball, independent of the GP's acquisition function. This means dense clustering of points during local exploitation does not directly influence where the GP suggests the next *global* sample, avoiding the near-singular kernel matrices that occur when multiple near-identical points are added to the GP's conditioning set.
- **Core assumption:** The local trust region method (SR1) converges quickly in a neighborhood of a local minimizer even when not informed by the global GP's full prediction uncertainty, relying instead on local function/gradient values and a quadratic model.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract]: "LAGO separates global exploration from local refinement at the proposal level: the BO acquisition function is optimized outside the active trust region... This decoupling enables efficient local refinement without sacrificing global exploration."
  - [section 3]: "Global optimization... the acquisition function is optimized *outside* of the trust region." and "Local optimization... inside the trust region."
  - [corpus]: Corpus neighbors are unrelated to this specific optimization mechanism (federated learning, segmentation, etc.). No direct evidence.
- **Break condition:** If the trust region radius becomes too large relative to the lengthscale, global candidates may be proposed too close to the trust region boundary, potentially leading to clustering again. The algorithm mitigates this by resetting the radius to `ℓ/2` upon termination [page 9, 10].

### Mechanism 2: Selective Information Assimilation
- **Claim:** Incorporating local function/gradient evaluations into the global GP *only* when they satisfy a lengthscale-based minimum-distance criterion (`∥x−x_c∥ > νℓ`) prevents numerical ill-conditioning while preserving useful curvature information about the local basin.
- **Mechanism:** During local trust region steps, many points are generated in close proximity to the current best minimizer `x_c`. Adding all these points to the global GP's training set would create near-duplicate rows in the kernel matrix, causing near-singularity. The distance filter (7) acts as a thinning operator, keeping only points that are sufficiently spaced relative to the GP's learned lengthscale `ℓ`. This maintains a well-conditioned GP while still feeding it representative information about the local landscape to guide future global exploration when the trust region moves.
- **Core assumption:** The GP lengthscale `ℓ` is a reasonable proxy for the "scale of variation" of the function; points closer than `νℓ` are redundant for global modeling purposes, while those farther provide new information about the local basin's geometry.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract]: "local function and gradient evaluations are incorporated into the global gradient-enhanced Gaussian process only when they satisfy a lengthscale-based minimum-distance criterion, reducing the risk of numerical instability during the local exploitation."
  - [section 3]: "points close to the trust region center are retained in the GP conditioning dataset only when they satisfy a distance-based criterion relative to the GP lengthscale."
  - [Appendix A]: Experiment showing kernel condition number increases when the filter (`ν=0.1`) is disabled (`ν=0`), validating its role in stability.
- **Break condition:** If the lengthscale `ℓ` is severely underestimated during early stages, the filter may discard almost all local points, starving the global GP of local curvature information and potentially causing it to "forget" promising basins when the trust region relocates.

### Mechanism 3: Adaptive Selection via Predicted Improvement
- **Claim:** Comparing the global Expected Improvement (EI) against the local deterministic improvement (`I_t`) at each iteration provides an automated, data-efficient switch between exploration and exploitation without requiring explicit phase transitions.
- **Mechanism:** For a proposed global candidate `x_G`, the EI acquisition function estimates the probabilistic decrease in objective value if evaluated there, based on the GP's posterior mean and variance. For the local candidate `x_L = x_c + s_k`, the deterministic improvement `I_t = f(x_c) - m_q(s_k)` uses the local quadratic model's predicted decrease. The rule `EI(x_G) > γ I_t` selects the global point if its *expected* gain outweighs the *predicted certain* gain of the local step. This competition happens every iteration, allowing immediate exploitation once a local step's predicted improvement becomes competitive.
- **Core assumption:** The GP's EI prediction is a reliable proxy for the *true* expected improvement of a global candidate, and the local quadratic model `m_q` is accurate enough within the trust region to predict local decrease.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract]: "the next evaluation is selected based on predicted improvement: the global expected improvement versus the local deterministic improvement."
  - [section 3, Equation 6]: Explicit selection criterion: `if EI(x_G) > γ I_t then evaluate global else local`.
  - [section 4.1, Figure 3]:γ-ablation on Levy function shows performance sensitivity to this trade-off parameter, confirming its role in balancing exploration/exploitation.
- **Break condition:** If the GP posterior variance is too low (overly confident) in unexplored regions, EI may be erroneously low, causing prolonged exploitation even when a better global basin exists. Conversely, if the local quadratic model `m_q` is poor (e.g., ill-conditioned Hessian), `I_t` may be misleading, causing rejection of valid local steps.

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map:**
  1. **Global GP Surrogate:** Gradient-enhanced GP conditioned on dataset `D_t`. Provides `¯mf` and `σ` for EI, and posterior Hessian for trust region initialization.
  2. **Acquisition Optimizer:** Maximizes EI *outside* the current trust region ball `B(x_c, Δ)` to produce `x_G`.
  3. **Local Trust Region Module:** SR1 method with quadratic model `m_q` initialized from GP Hessian. Solves subproblem (2) inside `B(x_c, Δ)` to propose `s_k` (and `x_L`).
  4. **Selection Engine:** Computes `EI(x_G)` and `I_t`, applies threshold `γ` (Equation 6).
  5. **Assimilation Filter:** Applies criterion (7) to decide which local points (besides `x_c`) are added to `D_{t+1}` for GP retraining.
  6. **Trust Region Manager:** Updates `(x_c, Δ, H)` based on ρ_k and termination condition `∥s_k∥ ≤ ε_step`.
  7. **Stopping Monitor:** Tracks (C1) and (C2) for early termination.
- **Critical path:** Initialize `D_0` → Fit GP → Set `x_c` to best in `D_1` → Loop: Get `x_G` (global), solve trust region to get `s_k` (local) → Compare `EI(x_G)` vs `γ I_t` → If global: evaluate `(f,∇f)(x_G)`, update `D`, possibly reset TR; if local: evaluate `(f,∇f)(x_L)`, run SR1 step to update `(x_c, Δ, H)`, apply filter to `D` → Refit GP periodically → Check stopping.
- **Design tradeoffs:**
  - **γ tuning:** Higher `γ` favors local exploitation (faster convergence on unimodal/smooth problems but risks global stagnation); lower `γ` favors exploration (robust on multimodal but slower local convergence). Default 1 is a balance.
  - **Filter strength (ν):** Larger `ν` discards more local points, improving GP conditioning but may lose local curvature information; smaller `ν` risks ill-conditioning. 0.1 is default.
  - **GP retraining frequency:** More frequent hyperparameter fitting (lengthscale `ℓ`) adapts better to changing landscape but costs O(n³) time; less frequent risks using outdated lengthscale for filtering and acquisition.
- **Failure signatures:**
  - **GP Ill-conditioning:** Exploding condition numbers, especially after local steps. Manifest as unstable EI values or solver failures. Remedy: increase nugget `σ²` or `ν`.
  - **Trust Region Stagnation:** `ρ_k` consistently low, `Δ` shrinks repeatedly, `∥s_k∥` tiny. Indicates local model `m_q` is poor or `x_c` is near a flat region/non-smoothness.
  - **Global Surrogate Degradation:** GP mean `¯mf` develops large, unphysical oscillations or fails to capture known global minima. Often due to too few global points or poor lengthscale estimation.
  - **Oscillating Selection:** Rapid alternation between global and local steps without convergence, suggesting `γ` is poorly set or EI/local model are mis-calibrated.
- **First 3 experiments:**
  1. **Synthetic 2D multimodal (Styblinski-Tang):** Verify LAGO reliably finds global minimum vs. L-BFGS (stuck in local minima). Check that initial iterations select global candidates, then transition to local near basin.
  2. **Branin with filter ablation (`ν=0` vs. `ν=0.1`):** Monitor GP kernel condition number (as in Appendix A). Expect `ν=0` to show spikes in condition number after local steps, while `ν=0.1` maintains stability.
  3. **γ-ablation on Levy:** Run with `γ ∈ {0.1, 1, 10}`. Expect low `γ` to require more evaluations (more global steps), high `γ` to potentially converge to suboptimal local minima if global search stops too early.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
### Open Question 1
- **Question:** Can LAGO be extended to handle noisy objective and gradient evaluations?
- **Basis in paper:** [explicit] The authors state: "The proposed algorithm is currently developed for noise-free objective evaluations, and extending it to noisy settings is a natural direction for future work." (Page 7) and reiterate in the Conclusion: "While the BO component can accommodate noisy observations, the current trust region refinement assumes noise-free objectives; extending it to noisy settings ... will be explored."
- **Why unresolved:** The trust region improvement ratio (5) treats function and gradient values as deterministic, and the GP conditioning assumes noise-free derivatives. Noise would introduce uncertainty, leading to potentially erroneous acceptance/rejection decisions and numerical instability in the GP.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** A stochastic variant of the trust region subproblem that accounts for observation noise, e.g., via probabilistic improvement, and a robust GP inference that handles noisy gradients. Empirical validation on noisy benchmark functions would demonstrate viability.

### Open Question 2
- **Question:** How can LAGO be scaled to high-dimensional problems where BO typically struggles?
- **Basis in paper:** [explicit] The Conclusion acknowledges: "LAGO is best suited to low- to moderate-dimensional problems, where BO remains effective. ... extending it to ... high-dimensional problems will be explored in future work."
- **Why unresolved:** The gradient-enhanced GP inference scales cubicly with the number of points, and acquisition function optimization becomes increasingly challenging in high dimensions. The trust region method may also suffer from slower convergence and poor Hessian approximations.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Integration of scalable GP approximations (e.g., sparse, structural, or kernel interpolation) or alternative global surrogates that reduce dimensionality. Empirical studies on high-dimensional benchmarks (e.g., d > 50) would show if the local-global framework remains effective.

### Open Question 3
- **Question:** What theoretical convergence guarantees exist for the LAGO algorithm?
- **Basis in paper:** [inferred] The paper provides empirical results but no convergence analysis for the combined scheme. While BO and trust region methods individually have known properties (e.g., sublinear regret, convergence to stationary points), the adaptive selection between global and local candidates may disrupt these guarantees.
- **Why unresolved:** Without a convergence proof, it is unclear whether LAGO converges to a global optimum (under global concavity) or at least to a stationary point, and what the convergence rate is. This hinders understanding of its reliability and optimality.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** A theoretical analysis that merges BO’s information-theoretic regret bounds with trust region convergence theory, under assumptions on function smoothness, GP kernel, and selection parameter γ. Such a result would provide performance certificates.

### Open Question 4
- **Question:** Can the hyperparameters (γ, ν, ε_T, N, ε_step) be tuned automatically during optimization?
- **Basis in paper:** [inferred] The authors use fixed values (γ=1, ν=0.1, etc.) and note that γ controls the local-global trade-off, but no adaptive scheme is proposed. The stopping criterion depends on fixed N and ε_T.
- **Why unresolved:** Manual tuning may be necessary for different problem classes, contradicting the goal of a "hands-off" optimizer. Sensitivity to these parameters could affect robustness and sample efficiency.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Heuristic rules that adjust parameters based on observed dynamics (e.g., acceptance rates of local vs. global steps, GP conditioning). Comparative experiments on diverse benchmarks would show whether adaptation yields more consistent performance and reduces tuning effort.

## Limitations
- The exact values of baseline hyperparameters (e.g., TREGO nugget, BLOSSOM regret) are not fully disclosed; reproduction may differ slightly.
- The distance filter ν=0.1 relies on the GP lengthscale; if ℓ is poorly estimated early, useful curvature could be discarded, reducing global guidance.
- Gradient‑enhanced GPs scale as O(n³) and may become prohibitive beyond moderate dimensions; the paper only tests up to d=5 and a 2D PDE.
- The selection threshold γ=1 is shown to be sensitive on Levy; its generality across problem classes is unclear.

## Confidence
- *Decoupled proposal mechanism*: **High** – Algorithmic separation is clearly defined and stability tests (kernel condition number) support the claim.
- *Sample‑efficiency vs. BO‑trust‑region hybrids*: **Medium** – Benchmarks are standard but limited in number; baseline hyperparameters may not be optimally tuned.
- *Exploration‑exploitation balance*: **Medium** – γ‑ablation demonstrates trade‑off, yet default setting may fail on landscapes with different roughness.
