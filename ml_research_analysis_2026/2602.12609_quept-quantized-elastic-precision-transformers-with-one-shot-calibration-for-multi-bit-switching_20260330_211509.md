# QuEPT: Quantized Elastic Precision Transformers with One-Shot Calibration for Multi-Bit Switching

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2602.12609
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.12609
- Reference count: 8
- Improves accuracy by 6.2% over ERQ at W4A4 on ViT-S

## Executive Summary
This paper introduces QuEPT, a post-training elastic quantization framework that enables dynamic bit-width adaptation for Vision Transformers, Large Language Models, and Multi-modal LLMs. The key innovation is enabling real-time switching between different quantization bit-widths without repeated optimization through one-shot calibration. The method combines Multi-Bit Token Merging (MB-ToMe) for robust token feature fusion across bit-widths and Multi-Bit Cascaded Low-Rank Adapters (MB-CLoRA) for efficient parameter sharing. Extensive experiments show QuEPT achieves comparable or better performance than state-of-the-art methods while significantly reducing training time and storage costs.

## Method Summary
QuEPT performs block-wise optimization using a small calibration dataset to optimize LoRA parameters and weight clipping thresholds in a single pass. The method employs MB-ToMe to fuse token features by computing cosine similarity between high-bit and low-bit representations, selectively preserving robust tokens. MB-CLoRA implements cascaded low-rank adapters where lower bit-widths inherit parameters from higher bit-widths through partitioned LoRA matrices. During deployment, selecting the matching LoRA slice enables real-time switching to any predefined bit-width without re-optimization.

## Key Results
- ViT-S with weight-activation quantization improves accuracy by 6.2% over ERQ at W4A4
- LLaMA2-7B outperforms QuaRot by 7.07% in average accuracy across five benchmarks at W4A4
- LLaVA-OneVision achieves 1.5% higher accuracy than MBQ under W3A16 quantization and 4.3% under W4A8
- Training time reduced by more than half compared to ERQ and PTMQ
- Supports mixed-precision quantization via layer sensitivity data

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: Multi-Bit Token Merging (MB-ToMe) improves quantization robustness by selectively preserving high-precision token features while fusing lower-precision ones, reducing inter-bit conflict during multi-bit optimization.
- Mechanism: For each token, the method computes cosine similarity between 8-bit and 4-bit representations. Tokens with high similarity are deemed stable and retained in high-precision form; others are fused via weighted average across bit-widths.
- Core assumption: High-similarity tokens are more quantization-robust, so retaining them in high-precision form improves overall accuracy.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [section] "We first pre-calculate the token-wise cosine similarity between the features of the high-bit group X^H_k, and those of the low-bit group, X^L_k... By sorting the similarity scores... we then save the indices corresponding to the top p% of similarities as Φ."
  - [abstract] "To enhance accuracy and robustness, we introduce Multi-Bit Token Merging (MB-ToMe) to dynamically fuse token features across different bit-widths, improving robustness during bit-width switching."
- Break condition: If the similarity metric fails to capture quantization robustness, or if the top p% selection excludes too many important tokens, the merging strategy may degrade performance.

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: Multi-Bit Cascaded Low-Rank Adapters (MB-CLoRA) enable efficient parameter sharing across bit-widths by cascading LoRA ranks so lower bit-widths inherit parameters from higher ones, reducing storage and improving adaptation.
- Mechanism: LoRA matrices A and B are partitioned so that for a given bit-width b, the effective rank r_b is the sum of ranks from high, mid, and low groups: r_b = r_h if b in B_H, r_b = r_h + r_m if b in B_M, and r_b = r_h + r_m + r_l if b in B_L. Lower bit-widths thus include all higher-bit LoRA parameters.
- Core assumption: Lower bit-widths benefit from inheriting higher-bit adaptation parameters because they share similar error patterns but with larger quantization errors.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [section] "when processing low-bit-width b in B_L, the compensation matrix R_L = B[:,:r_h+r_m+r_l] A[:r_h+r_m+r_l,:] will contain all parameters of medium and high bitwidth... RH = B[:,:r_h] A[:r_h,:] and R_M = B[:,:r_h+r_m] A[:r_h+r_m,:] will appear as leading submatrices."
  - [abstract] "Additionally, we propose Multi-Bit Cascaded Low-Rank adapters (MB-CLoRA) to strengthen correlations between bit-width groups, further improve the overall performance of QuEPT."
- Break condition: If the cascaded rank allocation leads to rank saturation or parameter conflicts, or if the inherited parameters are not well-suited for lower bit-widths, performance may degrade.

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: One-shot calibration with block-wise reconstruction enables real-time switching between uniform and mixed-precision quantization without repeated optimization, by training a single model that can adapt to any predefined bit-width.
- Mechanism: The model calibrates LoRA parameters and weight clipping thresholds once using a small calibration dataset. During deployment, selecting the matching LoRA slice yields the quantized model for any target bit-width.
- Core assumption: A single calibration run can produce LoRA adapters robust enough across all target bit-widths to avoid the need for per-bit-width re-optimization.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "It can dynamically adapt to various predefined bit-widths by cascading different low-rank adapters, and supports real-time switching between uniform quantization and mixed precision quantization without repeated optimization."
  - [section] "Our method provides a unified multi-bit width quantization solution for various transformer models... This approach only requires one-shot calibration and can be deployed in real time to any target bit-width by selecting the appropriate LoRA, completely eliminating re-optimization."
- Break condition: If the calibration dataset is too small or unrepresentative, or if the model requires per-bit-width fine-tuning for best accuracy, one-shot calibration may yield suboptimal results.

## Foundational Learning

- Concept: Cosine similarity for feature comparison
  - Why needed here: Used to identify quantization-robust tokens by comparing high- and low-bit feature representations.
  - Quick check question: Given two feature vectors x and y, what is the formula for cosine similarity, and what range does it output?

- Concept: Low-Rank Adaptation (LoRA)
  - Why needed here: LoRA reduces quantization error by adding small, trainable low-rank matrices to frozen weights, enabling efficient fine-tuning without full model updates.
  - Quick check question: If the original weight matrix is W ∈ R^{p×q}, and LoRA uses A ∈ R^{r×q} and B ∈ R^{p×r}, what is the effective update applied to W during training?

- Concept: Quantization scaling and clipping
  - Why needed here: Essential for mapping continuous weights/activations to fixed-point integers; clipping parameters (α, β) bound the dynamic range to preserve precision.
  - Quick check question: Given a weight matrix W, clipping parameters α and β, and bit-width b, how is the quantization scale s_w computed in QuEPT?

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map:
  Input calibration dataset -> MB-ToMe (feature fusion) -> MB-CLoRA (LoRA adaptation) -> block-wise reconstruction -> output quantized model
  - LoRA parameters: A, B (shared across bit-widths)
  - Clipping parameters: α, β (optimized jointly)
  - Bit-width groups: B_L, B_M, B_H
  - Loss: Mean Absolute Error (MAE) across sampled bit-widths

- Critical path:
  1. Calibration data -> feature extraction
  2. MB-ToMe: token-wise similarity and selective fusion
  3. MB-CLoRA: cascaded LoRA application per bit-width
  4. Quantization: weight/activation scaling and clipping
  5. Loss computation and back-propagation into A, B, α, β

- Design tradeoffs:
  - Cascaded LoRA vs. independent LoRA: cascaded saves memory and encourages synergy but risks interference; independent is more modular but heavier.
  - Uniform vs. selective token merging: uniform is simpler but may lose critical high-bit information; selective preserves robust tokens but adds selection overhead.
  - One-shot calibration vs. per-bit optimization: one-shot is faster and simpler but may sacrifice peak accuracy.

- Failure signatures:
  - Performance drops at low bit-widths suggest insufficient LoRA rank or poor token selection.
  - High bit-width degradation indicates cascaded sharing is causing interference.
  - Inconsistent accuracy across bit-widths may reveal calibration dataset bias.

- First 3 experiments:
  1. Run MB-ToMe with random token selection vs. selective merging on a small ViT; compare accuracy at 4-bit to confirm the benefit of similarity-based selection.
  2. Compare cascaded LoRA vs. independent LoRA on LLaMA2-7B; measure accuracy at 4-bit and total parameter count to validate memory savings.
  3. Perform one-shot calibration on a ViT model, then deploy at 4-bit, 6-bit, and 8-bit without re-optimization; verify accuracy matches or exceeds baseline PTQ methods.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- Question: How does QuEPT perform on extremely low bit-widths (e.g., 2-bit) compared to specialized low-bit methods?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper states that performance at extremely low bit-widths remains constrained and requires further exploration.
- Why unresolved: The experiments only tested down to 3-bit quantization on ViTs and 4-bit on LLMs, leaving a gap in evaluation for 2-bit and lower.
- What evidence would resolve it: Systematic evaluation of QuEPT at 2-bit and 1-bit quantization on various transformer architectures, comparing accuracy and robustness to specialized low-bit quantization methods.

### Open Question 2
- Question: Can MB-ToMe and MB-CLoRA be effectively applied to non-transformer architectures like CNNs or RNNs?
- Basis in paper: [inferred] The method is specifically designed for transformers with attention mechanisms and dense inter-token dependencies, but no experiments or discussion exist for other architectures.
- Why unresolved: The paper only validates on transformer-based models (ViT, LLM, MLLM), leaving generalization to other network types untested.
- What evidence would resolve it: Performance evaluation of MB-ToMe and MB-CLoRA on CNNs, RNNs, or other neural architectures, measuring accuracy and efficiency gains compared to existing methods.

### Open Question 3
- Question: What is the impact of the one-shot calibration data size on final quantization accuracy?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The method uses small calibration datasets (1024 images for ViT, 128 samples for LLMs), but no ablation or sensitivity analysis on calibration data size is provided.
- Why unresolved: The paper does not explore how varying calibration data size affects accuracy, stability, or calibration time across different model sizes or tasks.
- What evidence would resolve it: Systematic experiments varying calibration dataset size (e.g., 64, 128, 256, 512, 1024 samples) and measuring the resulting accuracy and calibration overhead for different transformer models.

## Limitations

- The exact implementation details of MB-ToMe token similarity calculation and threshold selection are not provided
- MB-CLoRA rank allocation formula across different bit-width groups remains unclear
- Performance on extremely low bit-widths (2-bit and below) remains unexplored
