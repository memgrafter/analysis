# CAMMSR: Category-Guided Attentive Mixture of Experts for Multimodal Sequential Recommendation

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2603.04320
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.04320
- **Reference count:** 40
- **Primary result:** Achieves state-of-the-art performance on four datasets, with relative improvements up to 14.66% in NDCG@5 and 15.98% in MRR@5 compared to strongest baselines.

## Executive Summary
CAMMSR addresses multimodal sequential recommendation by adaptively fusing item features (ID, text, image) through a Category-guided Attentive Mixture of Experts (CAMoE). It uses an auxiliary category prediction task to assign context-sensitive modality weights, employs a modality swap contrastive learning objective to enhance cross-modal alignment, and replaces LayerNorm with the more efficient Dynamic Tanh. Experiments on four real-world datasets demonstrate significant gains over prior methods, along with robustness to category label noise and faster convergence.

## Method Summary
The model processes each item's ID, text (BERT-frozen), and image (ViT-frozen) embeddings. The core CAMoE module first computes attentive gating weights over a set of experts that process concatenated multimodal features. Concurrently, an auxiliary category prediction task on each modality's raw embedding yields a loss value; this loss is transformed into a modality-specific weight. The final item representation combines the raw embedding with the expert output, modulated by the category-guided weight. A Transformer encoder with Dynamic Tanh processes each modality's sequence separately. The final score for a candidate item is the sum of dot products between each modality's user sequence representation and the item's corresponding representation. An auxiliary contrastive loss is added by swapping items between pairs of modality sequences and enforcing similar sequence-level embeddings via InfoNCE.

## Key Results
- Relative improvements of up to **14.66% in NDCG@5** and **15.98% in MRR@5** over strongest baselines (p<0.05).
- Demonstrates **robustness to category label noise**; performance degrades gracefully as noise increases.
- Shows **faster convergence** compared to models using standard LayerNorm, attributed to Dynamic Tanh's efficiency.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1: Category-Guided Adaptive Modality Fusion
- **Claim:** The model dynamically allocates importance weights to different modalities per item based on its category, capturing context-sensitive user-modal interactions.
- **Mechanism:** For each modality *m* and item *i*, a binary cross-entropy loss `d_i^m` is computed from an auxiliary category classifier applied to the modality's raw item representation. A lower loss indicates the modality is more semantically aligned with the item's true category. The weight `w_i^m` is derived via `exp(d_i^m)/Σ_n exp(d_n^i)`. These weights gate the output of a Mixture-of-Experts (MoE) module, which combines multiple expert networks processing the concatenated multimodal features.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - Abstract: "adaptively allocates modality weights via an auxiliary category prediction task".
  - Section IV.B: Details equations (8-10) for computing `d_i^m` and `w_i^m`.
  - Corpus: Neighbors include general MoE works but lack this specific category-guided weighting integration.
- **Break condition:** If all `d_i^m` are nearly identical across modalities for most items, the weighting collapses to uniform averaging, losing adaptivity.

### Mechanism 2: Modality Swap Contrastive Learning for Cross-Modal Alignment
- **Claim:** Swapping representations between two modalities within a sequence and applying contrastive learning forces the model to learn representations where semantically corresponding items are aligned across modalities, strengthening synergistic signal extraction.
- **Mechanism:** For a modality pair (e.g., text and image), a augmented sequence view is created by randomly swapping the item representations at 50% of sequence positions (Eq. 17). Both original and swapped sequences are encoded by a Transformer. The InfoNCE loss (Eq. 18) is applied between the sequence-level embeddings of the two modalities for the **same** user sequence (positive) versus **different** sequences (negatives). This encourages embeddings invariant to which modality's representation is used at a position, as long as semantic content matches.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - Abstract: "modality swap contrastive learning objective to enhance cross-modal alignment".
  - Section IV.D: Details the swap operation `Swap(·)` and InfoNCE formulation.
  - Corpus: Neighbor "Multimodal Difference Learning..." uses contrastive learning but not swap augmentation; the specific swap mechanism is novel here.
- **Break condition:** If swapping corrupts semantic meaning beyond recovery (e.g., a book's text with a shoe's image), the contrastive objective yields noisy gradients.

### Mechanism 3: Dynamic Tanh (DyT) for Efficient Normalization
- **Claim:** Replacing LayerNorm with the computationally simpler Dynamic Tanh (learnable scaling of Tanh) maintains training stability while reducing overhead in the Transformer encoder.
- **Mechanism:** LayerNorm normalizes activations by mean and variance. DyT (Eq. 13) applies a per-channel learnable scalar `α` followed by Tanh: `DyT(x) = tanh(α * x)`. This mimics LayerNorm's effect of bounding outliers and stabilizing inputs, but without computing statistics.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - Abstract: "uses Dynamic Tanh as a computationally efficient alternative to LayerNorm".
  - Section IV.C: Presents Eq. (13-14) and states it "mimics the normalization and activation behavior of LayerNorm".
  - Corpus: Neighbor "Transformers without normalization" (Zhu et al., 2025) supports removing standard normalization.
- **Break condition:** If input distributions to Transformer blocks become highly biased or have extreme variances, DyT may fail to provide sufficient normalization compared to LayerNorm.

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map:**
  1.  Input/Init: ID embeddings (trainable), Text (BERT-frozen + linear), Image (ViT-frozen + linear). Add positional embeddings.
  2.  CAMoE: For each item & modality: Concatenate all modality reps -> K expert FFNs -> Attentive gating router (using `E^m` matrix) -> weighted sum `hat e_i^m`. Compute category prediction loss `d_i^m` on `e_i^m` -> get weight `w_i^m` -> final item rep `bar e_i^m = e_i^m + w_i^m * hat e_i^m`.
  3.  User Encoder: Per modality: `Z^m = Transformer(Dropout(DyT(S^m_bar)))`. Sequence-level rep `Z^m` (last token) is user interest under modality `m`.
  4.  Prediction: Final score `hat y_i = sum_m (Z^m * e_i^m)` (dot product).
  5.  Contrastive (aux): For each modality pair, swap half items between `S^m1` & `S^m2` -> get `Z_aug^m1`, `Z_aug^m2` -> Compute InfoNCE loss `L_m1,m2^MSL` between batch samples.
  6.  Loss: `L = L_SR + lambda * sum(L_pairs^MSL)`.
- **Critical path:** Item representations (`e_i^m`) -> CAMoE enrichment (`bar e_i^m`) -> Sequence construction (`S^m_bar`) -> User Transformer (`Z^m`) -> Scoring (`hat y_i`). Category prediction and contrastive are auxiliary.
- **Design tradeoffs:**
  - *CAMoE Complexity vs. Adaptivity:* More experts (K) increase capacity for cross-modal synergy but add routing cost and risk over-specialization.
  - *DyT vs. LayerNorm:* DyT trades statistical robustness for lower compute/memory (no stats storage). Performance is comparable but may be dataset-dependent (Home used stronger λ due to sparsity).
  - *Contrastive Scope:* Using all 3 modality pairs yields best performance but increases auxiliary cost. Pairs with larger semantic gaps (e.g., `<t,v>`) benefit most.
- **Failure signatures:**
  - *Category guidance ineffective:* `w/o CG` ablation performs similarly/better, indicating category labels are noisy or not predictive of modality utility.
  - *Contrastive collapse:* If λ is too high, `L_MSL` dominates and forces unrealistic alignment, harming main task performance (optimal λ is small, ~0.25).
  - *DyT instability:* Training unstable (loss spikes, NDCG drops) if input distributions to DyT are not centered, suggesting LayerNorm is necessary.
- **First 3 experiments:**
  1.  **Ablation - CAMoE Components:** Run `w/o Att` (linear router), `w/o CG` (equal weights), `r MoE` (vanilla MoE without cross-modal expert input), and `r CMoE` (prior cross-modal MoE). Verify each component contributes positively (Fig 2).
  2.  **Contrastive Pair Ablation:** Test all 7 combinations of modality pairs (`<v,t>`, `<id,t>`, `<id,v>`) for `L_MSL`. Confirm including all pairs is best and `<v,t>` is most critical single pair (Table IV).
  3.  **DyT Validation:** Directly compare `w/o DyT` (using LayerNorm) against full CAMMSR. Measure final NDCG/MRR and per-epoch training time to confirm DyT's efficiency claim and non-inferior performance (Fig 2, Table VI).

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- **Open Question 1:** Can the category-guided modality weighting be generalized to other contextual dimensions, such as temporal dynamics or user demographics, to further enhance adaptive fusion?
  - **Basis in paper:** [explicit] Conclusion identifies "generalizing the category guidance to other contextual dimensions" as a key future extension.
  - **Why unresolved:** Only validated on category; other signals unexplored.
  - **Resolution evidence:** Systematic experiments replacing/augmenting category guidance with other contexts (time intervals, user age groups) on diverse datasets, measuring performance and weight distributions.
- **Open Question 2:** How can synergy modeling between modalities be refined beyond CAMoE and contrastive learning to capture more nuanced and dynamic cross-modal interactions?
  - **Basis in paper:** [explicit] Conclusion calls for "developing more refined synergy modeling approaches".
  - **Why unresolved:** Current methods may miss higher-order or context-dependent synergies.
  - **Resolution evidence:** Introduce advanced synergy modules (e.g., cross-modal attention graphs, dynamic synergy routing) with ablations showing improvement over CAMMSR components, especially on datasets with diverse modality correlations.
- **Open Question 3:** Is the CAMMSR framework adaptable to sequential decision tasks outside conventional recommendation, such as in healthcare or education?
  - **Basis in paper:** [explicit] Conclusion suggests "adapting the framework to sequential decision tasks outside conventional recommendation settings".
  - **Why unresolved:** Evaluated only on product recommendation; transferability to other domains untested.
  - **Resolution evidence:** Apply CAMMSR (minimal adaptation) to sequential decision benchmarks in other domains (medical treatment sequences, learning paths) with comparisons to domain-specific SOTA.
- **Open Question 4:** How can label noise in category annotations be actively filtered to improve robustness of category-guided weighting, and what are the trade-offs?
  - **Basis in paper:** [explicit] Noise study states "developing effective label noise filtering mechanisms" is promising; suggests confidence-aware/self-correcting methods.
  - **Why unresolved:** No filtering implemented; potential for further robustness gains unexplored.
  - **Resolution evidence:** Integrate noise-filtering techniques (sample re-weighting, label smoothing based on modality consistency) into CAMMSR; experiments showing reduced degradation under noise and filtering impact on clean data.

## Limitations
- Attentive gating scope (full-catalog vs. batch-level) is undefined, impacting feasibility and gating dynamics.
- Category label processing (hierarchy/multi-label) is unclear; inconsistent handling could invalidate auxiliary loss signals.
- Novelty claims vs. prior MoE works are weak; neighbor corpus shows overlapping concepts; citation count is zero, limiting external validation.
- Dynamic Tanh's theoretical justification is thin; benefits may not generalize beyond reported datasets.

## Confidence
- **Performance claims (SOTA gains): High** (standard datasets, reported metrics, statistical tests).
- **Mechanism effectiveness (CAMoE, swap CL, DyT): Medium** (described design but limited independent verification).
- **Novelty/superiority over baselines: Low** (corpus indicates overlapping concepts; novelty of integration unproven).
