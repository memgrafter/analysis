# Towards Understanding Multimodal Fine-Tuning: Spatial Features
## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2602.08713  
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.08713  
- **Reference count:** 40  
- **Primary result:** Multimodal fine-tuning induces sparse, spatially selective features in the language backbone; ablating these causes large drops on spatial reasoning (-9 to -16 pts) with minimal effect on general VQA (<1 pt).

## Executive Summary
This paper investigates how a pretrained language model (LLaMA-3.1-8B) adapts to vision-language inputs during fine-tuning, focusing on the emergence of spatial reasoning capabilities. Using stage-wise model diffing with sparse autoencoders (SAEs), the authors identify features that reorient toward visual processing and are selectively recruited by spatial prompts. Through causal interventions (ablation) and attribution patching, they demonstrate these features are crucial for spatial accuracy and are mediated by a sparse set of mid-layer attention heads. The work provides a mechanistic, interpretable account of capability acquisition in multimodal fine-tuning.

## Method Summary
The analysis pipeline compares a text-only SAE (pretrained on LLaMA activations) with its version adapted on VLM (LLaVA-More) activations. Feature adaptation is quantified via decoder rotation (cosine similarity) and visual energy (activation preference for image tokens). Spatial features are isolated by comparing firing frequencies on base prompts versus spatial-filtered prompts, controlling for lexical artifacts with neutral prompts. Causal impact is tested by ablating features—removing their decoder direction from residual streams—and measuring accuracy changes on spatial (VSR) and general (VQAv2) benchmarks. Attribution patching traces these features to specific attention heads by comparing clean and corrupted runs (mean-visual-embedding corruption).

## Key Results
- Ablating spatial-selective features reduces VSR accuracy by 9–16 points, while general VQA accuracy changes by <1 point.
- These features exhibit high selective recruitment: odds ratios under spatial prompts reach up to 9.1.
- Attribution patching links them to a small set of mid-layer attention heads (e.g., L13H1), with consistent peaks across multiple spatial features.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
Multimodal fine-tuning causes representational rotation in the language backbone: a subset of features reorients toward visual input (high visual energy) and becomes specialized for spatial language. These features are causally necessary for spatial reasoning because they encode abstract spatial relations that are not reducible to lexical artifacts (validated via neutral prompts). Their effects are mediated through a sparse, identifiable set of attention heads that integrate visual and textual information in mid-layers.

## Foundational Learning
- **Sparse Autoencoders (SAEs):** Used to expand model activations into interpretable, nearly-independent features. *Why needed:* Decompose high-dimensional activations into human-understandable directions. *Quick check:* Feature activations should be sparse and decoders should reconstruct well (low FVU).
- **Visual Energy (Ev):** Measures a feature’s preference for image tokens vs. text tokens. *Why needed:* Isolate features that process visual information. *Quick check:* High Ev (>0) indicates visual preference; low Ev features are text-bound.
- **Decoder Cosine:** Cosine similarity between pretrained and adapted SAE decoder vectors for the same feature index. *Why needed:* Quantify representational rotation during fine-tuning. *Quick check:* Low cosine (e.g., bottom 25%) signals significant adaptation.
- **Dataset Shift (Δpf, Odds Ratio):** Compares feature firing frequency on spatial vs. base prompts. *Why needed:* Identify features selectively recruited by spatial language. *Quick check:* High odds ratio (>4) and positive Δpf indicate spatial selectivity; neutral prompt test rules out lexical triggers.
- **Attribution Patching:** Causal tracing method to assign patch importance (e.g., to attention heads). *Why needed:* Locate where spatial features influence the forward pass. *Quick check:* Clean run (spatial prompt) vs. corrupted run (mean visual embedding) should yield clear head-level attribution scores.
- **Lexical Artifact Control:** Testing features on prompts with spatial terms in neutral contexts. *Why needed:* Ensure spatial features encode relational meaning, not just keywords. *Quick check:* Features that drop out under neutral prompts are lexical confounders.

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component Map:**  
CLIP ViT-L/14-336 -> Image Patches -> Visual Projector -> LLaMA-3.1-8B (Transformer Layers) -> SAE per Layer -> Feature Selection -> Attribution Patching -> Ablation -> Evaluation (VSR, VQAv2)

**Critical Path:**  
1. Extract activations from VLM on VQAv2 training set.  
2. Adapt text-only SAEs to VLM activations (text-masked).  
3. Select rotated, visually energetic features.  
4. Filter spatial features via dataset shift + lexical control.  
5. Trace to heads with attribution patching.  
6. Ablate top spatial features and evaluate on VSR/VQA.

**Design Tradeoffs:**  
- *SAE fidelity vs. interpretability:* Higher k (feature count) improves reconstruction but may dilute interpretability; paper uses Top-K, k=50.  
- *Corruption strength in attribution:* Mean visual embedding suppresses spatial info but may over-smooth; zero/random baselines needed for sanity.  
- *Ablation scope:* Removing feature direction at all text tokens is drastic but necessary for causal effect; may introduce collateral damage.

**Failure Signatures:**  
- SAE adaptation fails (high FVU, unstable curves) → fall back to text-only SAEs for diffing.  
- Spatial feature set includes obvious lexical triggers (e.g., word “left”) → neutral prompt test filters them out.  
- Attribution scores are flat/noisy → check corruption method; verify mean embedding computed over diverse samples.  
- Ablation shows no VSR drop → ensure feature activates on test samples; use relation-specific subsets with enough samples.

**First Three Experiments:**  
1. Replicate SAE adaptation on 50k VQAv2 samples; plot per-layer FVU trajectories to confirm convergence.  
2. Compute visual energy and decoder cosine; verify bottom-25%/Ev>0 features are distributed in mid-layers (8–20).  
3. Run attribution patching for 5–10 top spatial features; confirm sparse head set (e.g., L13H1) emerges consistently across Methods A and B.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
### Open Question 1
- Question: Do the observed patterns of spatial feature emergence and their causal attribution to mid-layer attention heads generalize across different vision-language model architectures and scales?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The authors state: "our experiments are limited to a single model (LLaVA-More with a LLaMA-3.1-8B backbone); applying the method to other backbones and larger corpora will be key to assessing generality."
- Why unresolved: The study only examines one specific VLM configuration; different architectures (e.g., other LLMs, vision encoders) or larger models may exhibit different adaptation dynamics, so generalizability is unknown.
- What evidence would resolve it: Systematically replicate the stage-wise model diffing pipeline across multiple VLMs with varied backbones, vision encoders, and parameter scales, comparing the prevalence, layer distributions, and attention-head attribution of spatial features.

### Open Question 2
- Question: Can more detailed ablation and steering experiments establish a stronger causal link between the identified spatial features and spatial reasoning performance, and do these interventions generalize to unseen spatial relations?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The authors note: "more detailed ablation and steering studies are needed to fully validate causality."
- Why unresolved: Current ablation studies demonstrate correlation but not full causality; steering (e.g., enhancing feature activation) is not explored, and it is unclear whether spatial features are relation-specific or transferable.
- What evidence would resolve it: Conduct activation steering that selectively amplifies or suppresses individual spatial features across a diverse set of spatial relations, measuring consistent performance changes and assessing whether features generalize beyond the relations they were originally identified on.

### Open Question 3
- Question: How do other vision-grounded capabilities (e.g., OCR, object counting, attribute recognition) emerge during multimodal fine-tuning, and are they mediated by similar feature-level mechanisms (e.g., sparse sets of attention heads) as spatial reasoning?
- Basis in paper: [inferred] The paper includes a brief second case study on OCR features but does not fully analyze their causal attribution or compare mechanisms across capabilities. The authors frame their work as providing "a foundation for understanding and refining how pretrained language models acquire vision-grounded capabilities," implying broader investigation is needed.
- Why unresolved: The study focuses primarily on spatial reasoning; it remains unknown whether other multimodal skills follow similar patterns of feature adaptation and head specialization.
- What evidence would resolve it: Apply the same feature isolation and attribution patching pipeline to other multimodal tasks, comparing the number, layer distribution, and attention-head specialization of features associated with each capability to determine commonalities and differences.

### Open Question 4
- Question: What is the temporal dynamics of feature rotation and vision-preferring feature emergence throughout the multimodal fine-tuning process? Are there critical checkpoints where spatial capabilities become established?
- Basis in paper: [inferred] The paper uses stage-wise model diffing between pretrained and fully fine-tuned models but does not track intermediate stages. The introduction highlights the lack of understanding of "when vision-specific capabilities emerge."
- Why unresolved: The evolution of representational changes during fine-tuning is unknown; it is unclear whether feature rotations happen gradually, in discrete phases, or at specific stages.
- What evidence would resolve it: Perform stage-wise diffing at multiple checkpoints throughout fine-tuning (e.g., early, middle, late), tracking the number, rotation strength (decoder cosine), and modality preference (visual energy) of features over time, and correlate these trajectories with performance on spatial reasoning benchmarks.

## Limitations
- Exact hyperparameters for SAE fine-tuning (batch size, steps, λ, LR schedule) are unspecified.
- Attribution patching corruption details (mean visual embedding construction, sample size, layer) are missing.
- Spatial VQAv2 subset construction (keywords, sample count) is not provided, risking selection bias.
- Residual risk of lexical artifacts in spatial features despite neutral prompt controls.

## Confidence
- **Multimodal fine-tuning induces structured feature-level adaptations:** High (consistent SAE metrics across layers).  
- **Existence of spatially selective features:** Medium (sensitive to dataset shift and filter thresholds).  
- **Ablating these features drastically reduces VSR accuracy:** Medium (ablation scope may have indirect effects).  
- **Sparse mid-layer head attribution:** Medium (depends on corruption specifics; cross-method agreement varies).
