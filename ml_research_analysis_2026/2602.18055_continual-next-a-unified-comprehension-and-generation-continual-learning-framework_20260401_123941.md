# Continual-NExT: A Unified Comprehension And Generation Continual Learning Framework

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID**: 2602.18055
- **Source URL**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.18055
- **Reference count**: 40
- **Primary result**: MAGE achieves Avg.ACC 49.58, Forgetting 12.26, and New.ACC 59.79 on SEED-X, with significant reductions in hallucination and instruction unfollowing rates.

## Executive Summary
Continual-NExT introduces a unified continual learning framework for Dual-to-Dual multimodal large language models (MLLMs) that supports both comprehension and generation across text and image modalities. Existing CL methods target text-only generation; Dual-to-Dual MLLMs face catastrophic forgetting, hallucination, instruction unfollowing, and cross-modal transfer failure. The proposed MAGE (Mixture and Aggregation of General LoRA and Expert LoRA) separates parameter-efficient adaptation into General LoRA for input modality comprehension and Expert LoRA for output modality generation, combined with parameter-wise exponential moving average (PEMA) for stabilization. Experiments on SEED-X across six tasks show MAGE outperforms baselines, achieving Avg.ACC 49.58, Forgetting 12.26, and New.ACC 59.79, with markedly lower hallucination and instruction unfollowing rates. The framework also defines comprehensive evaluation metrics (Avg.ACC, Forgetting, New.ACC) and diagnostic metrics (Avg.HAL, Avg.IUF, Avg.OTH) to quantify error types.

## Method Summary
MAGE (Mixture and Aggregation of General LoRA and Expert LoRA) is a parameter-efficient continual learning method for Dual-to-Dual MLLMs. It inserts four LoRA adapters into the frozen LLM backbone: two General LoRAs (Image General for image input comprehension, Text General for text input comprehension) and two Expert LoRAs (Image Expert for image output generation, Text Expert for text output generation). During task-specific training, only the LoRAs matching the current task's input and output modalities are updated; all others remain frozen. All four LoRA deltas are summed in the forward pass, ensuring that frozen adapters preserve prior knowledge. After each batch, a parameter-wise exponential moving average (PEMA) computes Fisher information to estimate per-parameter importance, then updates an EMA snapshot with adaptive learning rates: important parameters change slowly (stability), unimportant ones adapt quickly (plasticity). The final model after all tasks uses the EMA weights.

The training pipeline uses SEED-X with frozen vision encoder and LLM, and only the LoRA adapters and input/output resampling connection layers are trainable. Hyperparameters: LoRA rank 32, dropout 0.05, learning rate 1e-4, batch size 16 (6 for HQEdit), mixed precision bf16, cosine schedule with warmup, DeepSpeed ZeRO stage 1. Tasks are learned sequentially: VQAv2, ImageNet, Flickr30k, RefCOCO, OCRVQA, HQEdit. After each task, evaluation on all previous test sets computes Avg.ACC, Forgetting, New.ACC.

## Key Results
- MAGE outperforms baselines with Avg.ACC 49.58, Forgetting 12.26, New.ACC 59.79.
- Diagnostic metrics improve: hallucination (Avg.HAL) and instruction unfollowing (Avg.IUF) are reduced.
- Cross-modal transfer is effective: after training OCRVQA (text→text), VQAv2 (image+text→text) accuracy recovers from 20.10 to 57.29.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
**Mechanism 1: Modality-specific LoRA separation**  
Claim: Distinct parameter subspaces handle input comprehension vs. output generation, enabling selective adaptation that mitigates forgetting.  
Mechanism: Four specialized LoRA adapters—Image General (input comprehension), Text General (input comprehension), Image Expert (output generation), Text Expert (output generation)—are inserted. During learning on a task, only adapters matching the task's input and output modalities are updated; others remain frozen.  
Core assumption: Comprehension and generation rely on separable weight subspaces; freezing irrelevant ones prevents interference.  
Evidence: Heatmap (Fig. 3) shows modality-specific discrepancy patterns; abstract states the separation; CL-MoE corroborates MoE for capability separation.  
Break condition: If the backbone lacks clear input/output separation, the selective freezing may fail.

**Mechanism 2: Frozen LoRA passive preservation**  
Claim: Keeping all four LoRA adapters in the forward pass, with only relevant ones updated, lets frozen adapters preserve prior knowledge without interfering.  
Mechanism: Forward pass sums base weights and all four LoRA deltas; frozen adapters contribute zero delta but remain in the computation graph, so when a similar task returns, their knowledge is immediately available.  
Core assumption: Frozen LoRA parameters do not drift and can be reactivated without re-learning.  
Evidence: Paper notes this design ensures cross-modal knowledge transfer; Table 3 shows performance recovery on VQAv2 after OCRVQA training.  
Break condition: Extreme modality shifts (e.g., new modality never seen before) cause dead-weight storage; summation might exceed model capacity.

**Mechanism 3: Parameter-wise EMA (PEMA) for stable regularization**  
Claim: PEMA uses per-parameter Fisher importance to modulate learning rates, reducing forgetting more effectively than layer-wise approaches.  
Mechanism: For each batch, PEMA computes the diagonal Fisher matrix (gradient variance) and a regularized inverse Hessian. It then updates an EMA snapshot with parameter-specific weights β_t = [∂L/∂θ_t + I][(θ_t − θ^*_{t−1})^T ˆH]^{-1}. Important parameters change slowly; unimportant ones adapt quickly.  
Core assumption: Diagonal Fisher approximates importance; per-parameter adaptation yields better stability-plasticity balance.  
Evidence: Ablation (Table 4) shows PEMA reduces Forgetting to 12.26 vs. 14.90 without it; Fig. 8 shows DEMA comparison.  
Break condition: Noisy Fisher estimates (small batches, high variance) lead to unreliable importance; diagonal ignores parameter interactions.

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component Map**: Frozen Backbone (SEED-X) -> Inserted LoRA Adapters (4 types) -> Connection Modules (input/output resamplers) -> PEMA -> Aggregation

**Critical Path**:  
1. Initialize four LoRA adapters with small random weights.  
2. For each new task, identify its input modalities and output modality.  
3. Freeze LoRAs whose modality does not match any current task modality.  
4. Train only unfrozen LoRAs and connection layers (1 epoch or until convergence).  
5. After each batch, accumulate squared gradients to estimate Fisher diagonal.  
6. Compute per-parameter β_t and update EMA snapshot θ^*_t.  
7. Proceed to next task.  
8. At test time after all tasks, use θ^*_final for evaluation.

**Design Tradeoffs**:  
- *Memory vs. modality coverage*: Four LoRA adapters increase memory by 4× LoRA storage; benefit: better separation.  
- *Granularity*: PEMA per-parameter updates are precise but require storing per-parameter Fisher (model size). DEMA is coarser but cheaper.  
- *Frozen LoRA participation*: Keeping all LoRAs in forward pass avoids re-routing but adds compute; unused LoRAs become dead weight.  
- *Epoch budget*: One epoch per task minimizes forgetting but may underfit larger datasets (e.g., HQEdit uses 10k steps).

**Failure Signatures**:  
- Sudden Avg.ACC drop after adding a task with a new output modality → insufficient parameter separation.  
- β_t values explode or vanish → Fisher estimation instability (check λ damping).  
- Avg.IUF increases → frozen LoRA insufficient to preserve instruction-following behavior.  
- Cross-modal transfer fails (e.g., training Task B does not improve Task A) → General LoRA not effectively shared.

**First 3 Experiments**:  
1. *Parameter pattern validation*: Replicate Fig. 3. Train VQAv2 then Flickr30k, visualize LoRA weight differences at shallow vs. deep layers; confirm modality clustering.  
2. *Ablation of selective freezing*: Compare (i) train all 4 LoRAs always, (ii) train only input-matching, (iii) only output-matching, (iv) MAGE (both). Measure forgetting on Task1 after Task2.  
3. *PEMA vs. DEMA under same FLOPs*: Implement DEMA from CIA; match total compute/params. Run three task orders; compare Avg.ACC, Forgetting, memory usage.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
**Open Question 1**: How can Dual-to-Dual MLLMs incrementally incorporate new modalities without catastrophic forgetting and linear parameter growth?  
*Basis*: Paper states that as modalities grow, LoRA experts increase linearly, leading to explosion.  
*Why unresolved*: MAGE does not provide a solution for >2 modalities; need shared experts or dynamic sparsity.  
*What evidence*: Extend to 3+ modalities with scalable mechanism.

**Open Question 2**: Is the separation of General LoRA (input comprehension) and Expert LoRA (output generation) the optimal parameter partitioning?  
*Basis*: Paper notes alternative decompositions (finer-grained, hierarchical, dynamic routing) are plausible but unexplored.  
*Why unresolved*: No comparative analysis of partitioning strategies; fixed split may not generalize.  
*What evidence*: Systematic ablation of different splits across diverse backbones and task distributions.

**Open Question 3**: Do the anti-forgetting benefits of MAGE hold for task sequences with high distribution shifts or conflicting objectives?  
*Basis*: Evaluation only on six tasks with three predefined orders; no antagonistic tasks tested.  
*Why unresolved*: Real-world CL involves arbitrary order and difficulty; current tests may underestimate forgetting.  
*What evidence*: Introduce adversarial sequences (e.g., alternating text-only and image-only generation) and measure forgetting, plasticity, transfer.

**Open Question 4**: Are the proposed Avg.HAL, Avg.IUF, and Avg.OTH metrics comprehensive for diagnosing multimodal continual learning failures?  
*Basis*: These metrics are derived from observed errors but classify "all other errors" into Avg.OTH without validation.  
*Why unresolved*: No human evaluation or comparison to other taxonomies; may miss nuanced failures.  
*What evidence*: Human annotations on outputs to assess correlation and refine metric definitions.

## Limitations
- **Generalization**: Method evaluated only on SEED-X; applicability to other Dual-to-Dual MLLMs not established.
- **Metrics underspecified**: CLIPScore for image generation lacks detail (model variant, reference use); diagnostic metrics (Avg.HAL/IUF/OTH) rely on Qwen3-VL classifier whose accuracy is not validated.
- **Robustness gaps**: PEMA's diagonal Fisher assumption may be brittle under noisy gradients; potential interference between summed LoRA streams not studied; task order diversity limited.

## Confidence
- Mechanism 1 (modality-specific LoRA separation): Medium
- Mechanism 2 (frozen LoRA preserves knowledge): Medium
- Mechanism 3 (PEMA effectiveness): Low
- Overall framework superiority: Medium
