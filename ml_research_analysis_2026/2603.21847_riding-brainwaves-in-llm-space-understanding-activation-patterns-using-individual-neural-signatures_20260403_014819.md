# Riding Brainwaves in LLM Space: Understanding Activation Patterns Using Individual Neural Signatures

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID**: 2603.21847
- **Source URL**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.21847
- **Reference count**: 19
- **Primary result**: Person-specific linear probes on frozen LLM activations achieve up to ninefold higher correlation with individual EEG power than population probes (high-gamma: ρ=0.183 vs 0.020, p<10^-4).

## Executive Summary
This study investigates whether frozen large language models (LLMs) encode individual neural signatures derived from EEG recordings. Using word-level EEG data from 30 participants reading naturalistic sentences, the authors extracted hidden states from Qwen 2.5 7B and LLaMA 3.1 8B and trained separate linear probes per person to predict EEG power features. These person-specific probes were compared against a single population probe trained on pooled data. The results show a substantial performance advantage for person-specific probes across all EEG features, with high-gamma power yielding a ninefold improvement (p<10^-4). A negative control (fixation count) showed no such advantage, confirming specificity to neural signals. The learned directions were temporally stable, concentrated in deep layers (peaking at Layer 24), and non-transferable across individuals. These findings demonstrate stable, person-specific neural patterns embedded in LLM activation spaces, opening avenues for personalized brain-LLM interfaces.

## Method Summary
The study used word-level EEG recordings from 30 participants engaged in naturalistic reading. For each word, hidden states were extracted from two 7B/8B parameter decoder-only LLMs (Qwen 2.5 7B, LLaMA 3.1 8B) using the exact textual input. Separate linear regression probes were trained for each participant to predict EEG power features (including high-gamma) from the activation vectors at all layers. A single population probe, trained on data from all participants combined, served as a baseline. To test specificity, a negative control probe predicted fixation count. Performance was evaluated on held-out data using Spearman’s rank correlation (ρ). Additional analyses included split-half reliability to assess temporal stability, cross-participant transfer tests to examine non-transferability, and layer-wise analyses to localize signal depth. Robustness checks controlled for word-level confounds such as frequency and length.

## Key Results
- Person-specific probes significantly outperformed the population probe for all EEG features; for high-gamma power, person-specific achieved ρ=0.183 vs population ρ=0.020 (ninefold improvement, p<10^-4).
- A negative control (predicting fixation count) showed no advantage for person-specific probes (p=0.360), confirming the effect is specific to neural signals.
- Person-specific directions were temporally stable (split-half cosine=0.824), non-transferable across individuals (self ρ=0.369 vs other ρ=0.143, p<10^-19), and peaked in deep layers (Layer 24).

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The paper establishes that person-specific neural directions exist in frozen LLM activations but does not propose a definitive mechanism. The observed stability and non-transferability suggest these directions capture idiosyncratic aspects of individual neural processing that are not shared at the population level. One plausible hypothesis is that LLMs, trained on vast and diverse language corpora, develop representations that resonate with personal linguistic experiences and cognitive styles, thereby reflecting individual neural patterns. However, the study cannot determine whether these directions play a causal role in language comprehension or are incidental byproducts of the models’ training objectives. The absence of behavioral or cognitive covariates prevents linking the directions to specific processes like semantic integration or attention, leaving the underlying reason for their existence an open question.

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component map**: Token IDs -> Token Embedding -> Positional Encoding -> [Transformer Block (masked multi‑head self‑attention -> LayerNorm -> Feed‑forward network -> LayerNorm)] repeated L times -> Layer‑wise hidden states -> Person‑specific Linear Probe -> EEG power prediction.

**Critical path**: For each word in a sentence, the token sequence up to that word is fed into the frozen LLM. The activation vector from a chosen layer (or all layers) is extracted. The person‑specific linear probe maps this vector to a vector of EEG power predictions. During training, the probe weights are optimized (e.g., via ridge regression) to maximize Spearman correlation with the actual EEG. The trained probe is evaluated on held‑out sentences.

**Design tradeoffs**:
- Decoder‑only architecture with causal attention aligns with incremental word‑by‑word EEG processing but cannot use bidirectional context.
- Freezing LLM weights tests pre‑trained knowledge and reduces overfitting on small EEG datasets, but may limit expressiveness compared to fine‑tuning.
- Linear probes are interpretable and computationally efficient, yet may miss nonlinear relationships.
- Examining all layers provides comprehensiveness at the cost of multiple comparisons; a focused layer selection reduces computational load but risks missing informative depths.
- 7B/8B models balance capability and resource demands; larger models might capture finer details but are more expensive to run.

**Failure signatures**:
- Person‑specific probes perform at chance or at the level of the population baseline.
- The negative control (fixation count) also shows a person‑specific advantage, indicating a non‑neural artifact.
- Probe performance is highly unstable across cross‑validation splits or random seeds.
- Cross‑participant transfer yields high correlation, contradicting the claim of person‑specificity.

**First experiments**:
1. Compare person‑specific linear probes against a single population probe across EEG features and model layers.
2. Conduct a negative control by predicting fixation count instead of EEG power using the same probing pipeline.
3. Test cross‑participant transfer: train on one participant’s data and evaluate on all others to assess generalizability.

## Limitations
- **Sample size & diversity**: Only 30 participants, limiting generalizability across demographics and neurodiverse/clinical populations.
- **Architectural scope**: Results are confined to 7B/8B decoder‑only models; scaling to larger sizes or encoder‑only architectures remains untested.
- **Interpretability & causality**: The functional role of person‑specific directions and their causal contribution to language processing are unclear; no behavioral or linguistic covariates were analyzed.

## Confidence
- Person‑specific probes outperform population: **High**
- Specificity to neural signals (not behavioral artifacts): **Medium**
- Non‑transferability across individuals: **High**
- Temporal stability & deep‑layer concentration: **Medium**

## Next Checks
1. **Diversity stress test**: Replicate with ≥100 participants spanning age, gender, and cultural backgrounds to assess demographic robustness.
2. **Scale & architecture sweep**: Test across model sizes (1B–70B) and types (decoder‑only, encoder‑decoder) to map scaling trends and architectural invariance.
3. **Cognitive validation**: Integrate word‑level behavioral measures (reading time, cloze probability, semantic surprisal) to test whether person‑specific directions mediate known EEG‑linguistic relationships.
