# Improving reasoning at inference time via uncertainty minimisation

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2603.07159
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.07159
- Reference count: 9
- Key outcome: Uncertainty minimization at the thought level outperforms greedy decoding on MATH500 and GSM8K using only 2-8 samples

## Executive Summary
This paper proposes a novel inference-time strategy for improving large language model reasoning by treating reasoning as uncertainty minimization at the level of individual thoughts rather than tokens. The approach samples multiple candidate continuations at each reasoning step and selects the one with highest self-certainty, computed from the model's internal predictive distribution without requiring external evaluators. Across Qwen and Llama models (0.5B-3B parameters) on MATH500 and GSM8K benchmarks, the method consistently outperforms greedy decoding and matches or exceeds self-consistency under equivalent token budgets.

The key insight is that correct reasoning paths exhibit higher self-certainty from early steps and stabilize quickly, allowing the method to be applied only to the first few reasoning steps while capturing most of the performance improvement. This makes it a computationally efficient inference-time scaling technique. The approach also generalizes effectively to low-resource languages like Danish, showing comparable relative gains.

## Method Summary
The proposed method treats reasoning as uncertainty minimization at the thought level, where each reasoning step is evaluated for its internal predictive certainty. At each step, the model samples multiple candidate continuations and selects the one with highest self-certainty—a metric derived from the model's internal probability distribution over possible next thoughts. This process occurs entirely within the model's internal computations without requiring external evaluators or extensive sampling across entire reasoning chains. By focusing on thought-level certainty rather than token-level confidence, the method identifies and follows reasoning paths that the model itself finds most internally coherent and likely, leading to improved final answer accuracy on mathematical reasoning benchmarks.

## Key Results
- Outperforms greedy decoding and matches/exceeds self-consistency on MATH500 and GSM8K using only 2-8 samples
- Applies effectively to both high-resource (English) and low-resource (Danish) language reasoning tasks
- Applying method to only first few reasoning steps captures most performance gains, reducing computational overhead
- Consistent improvements across Qwen (0.5B-3B) and Llama (1B-3B) model families

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The method works because reasoning processes have inherent uncertainty that can be measured and minimized at the thought level. By sampling multiple continuations at each reasoning step and selecting the most self-certain option, the model effectively performs an internal evaluation of reasoning quality without external supervision. The key mechanism is that correct reasoning paths tend to exhibit higher and more stable self-certainty from early steps, creating a self-reinforcing signal that guides the model toward successful reasoning trajectories. This eliminates the need for expensive external evaluators or exhaustive sampling across entire reasoning chains, while still achieving performance comparable to more resource-intensive methods.

## Foundational Learning
1. Self-certainty computation - understanding how to extract confidence metrics from internal probability distributions
   - Why needed: Provides the internal signal for selecting reasoning paths without external evaluation
   - Quick check: Verify that self-certainty correlates with actual reasoning success on validation set

2. Thought-level vs token-level uncertainty - recognizing that reasoning operates at discrete thought units rather than continuous token sequences
   - Why needed: Enables more meaningful uncertainty measurement aligned with actual reasoning structure
   - Quick check: Compare performance when applying uncertainty minimization at thought vs token level

3. Early-step signal stability - the observation that correct reasoning paths stabilize in self-certainty early in the process
   - Why needed: Allows truncation of uncertainty minimization to first few steps for efficiency
   - Quick check: Analyze self-certainty trajectories across correct vs incorrect reasoning paths

4. Sampling efficiency trade-offs - understanding how few samples (2-8) suffice when applied at thought level vs chain level
   - Why needed: Enables practical inference-time application without excessive computation
   - Quick check: Measure performance degradation as sample count decreases

5. Cross-linguistic reasoning patterns - recognizing that uncertainty minimization generalizes across languages
   - Why needed: Validates approach beyond English-centric benchmarks
   - Quick check: Test on multiple language pairs with varying resource availability

## Architecture Onboarding

Component Map: Model -> Sampling Engine -> Self-certainty Selector -> Reasoning Output

Critical Path: Input problem → Sampling multiple continuations → Self-certainty computation → Selection → Next reasoning step → Repeat until solution

Design Tradeoffs: Sampling 2-8 continuations balances computational cost against selection quality; thought-level vs token-level uncertainty measurement affects both accuracy and efficiency; early-step truncation trades some potential gains for significant computational savings.

Failure Signatures: Low self-certainty persisting across multiple steps may indicate model confusion or problem complexity beyond current capabilities; high self-certainty on incorrect paths suggests potential bias in the model's internal confidence calibration; performance degradation when applying to non-mathematical reasoning tasks.

## Limitations
- Performance on complex, multi-step reasoning tasks beyond mathematical problems remains untested
- Computational overhead of sampling multiple continuations could become prohibitive for larger models
- Reliance on internal predictive distributions may not generalize well across all model families and reasoning domains
- Assumption that early-step self-certainty correlates with final correctness may not hold for all problem types

## Confidence
High Confidence: Experimental methodology and benchmark results are sound and reproducible; early-step self-certainty correlation with correctness is well-supported.

Medium Confidence: Computational efficiency claims depend on implementation details; Danish generalization suggests broader applicability but needs more validation.

Low Confidence: Universal applicability as an "inference-time scaling technique" lacks sufficient evidence; paper doesn't address failure modes or complex reasoning tasks.
