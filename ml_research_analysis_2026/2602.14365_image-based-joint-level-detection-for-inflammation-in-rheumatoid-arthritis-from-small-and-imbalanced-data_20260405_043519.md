# Image-based Joint-level Detection for Inflammation in Rheumatoid Arthritis from Small and Imbalanced Data

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2602.14365  
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.14365  
- Reference count: 40  
- Primary result: 5-fold cross-validation F1-score 0.420 and Gmean 0.605, improving baseline ResNet-18 by 0.2 F1 and 0.25 Gmean, and approaching rheumatologist performance (average F1 0.305, Gmean 0.623).

## Executive Summary
Rheumatoid arthritis (RA) joint inflammation detection from RGB hand images is challenging due to scarce positive samples and extreme class imbalance. This work proposes a dual-encoder framework with separate global and local ResNet-18 branches, self-supervised pretraining on 11k healthy hand images using DINO, and fine-tuning with Focal Loss. Joint annotations are derived from ultrasound (GS>1 and PD>0). On a dataset of 222 hand images from 68 patients, 5-fold cross-validation yields F1-score 0.420 and Gmean 0.605, outperforming multiple baselines and achieving numbers close to rheumatologists who used palpation. The study demonstrates that carefully designed pretraining and imbalance handling can extract meaningful visual cues for RA from limited, imbalanced RGB data.

## Method Summary
The task is binary classification of joint-level inflammation in RA from RGB hand photos. Data comprises 222 images captured with iPhone 11, each annotated for 14 joints (excluding DIP) based on ultrasound. Preprocessing segments the hand using SAM2, detects joint landmarks via MediaPipe, and crops 224×224 patches around each joint while also resizing the full hand. The network contains two parallel ResNet-18 backbones: a Global Encoder processing the whole hand and a Local Encoder processing each joint patch. Both encoders are first pretrained from scratch on an external set of 11k healthy hand images using DINO’s self-supervised distillation objective; their weights are then frozen. A shared multi-layer perceptron (MLP) head takes the concatenated global feature vector (replicated per joint) and the corresponding local feature, producing an inflammation probability per joint. Fine-tuning employs Focal Loss (γ=2) to mitigate extreme imbalance and Adam (lr=1e-4). Evaluation uses patient-stratified 5-fold cross-validation, reporting F1-score and Gmean.

## Key Results
- 5-fold cross-validation F1-score 0.420 and Gmean 0.605.
- Improves baseline ResNet-18 by 0.2 F1 and 0.25 Gmean.
- Outperforms ResNet-50, Inception-ResNetv2, and vision transformers; performance close to rheumatologists (average F1 0.305, Gmean 0.623).

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1
*   **Claim:** Separate global and local encoders improve detection by capturing complementary hand-level and joint-level visual cues.
*   **Mechanism:** The Global Encoder processes the entire hand image to capture overall deformities and structural context (e.g., swelling patterns, hand alignment), while the Local Encoder processes cropped patches around each joint to focus on fine-grained, joint-specific textures and color changes. Their features are integrated to make a final joint-level prediction.
*   **Core assumption:** Inflamed joints present both localized symptoms (visible in the joint patch) and subtle global context (e.g., overall hand swelling), and a single-scale encoder cannot robustly learn both from small, imbalanced data.
*   **Evidence anchors:** [abstract] "The proposed framework employs separate global and local ResNet-18 encoders..."; [section 3.2] "To effectively capture the subtle visual cues of inflammation, our network consists of two parallel encoders... Global Encoder: Processes the full hand image... Local Encoder: Processes the cropped images of each joint region."; literature shows prior work focuses on radiographic or whole-hand analysis, not this dual-encoder joint-level RGB detection.
*   **Break condition:** If joint inflammation manifests purely as a global feature without local texture change, or vice versa, the dual-encoder design adds unnecessary complexity and may overfit.

### Mechanism 2
*   **Claim:** Self-supervised pretraining on a large, healthy hand dataset (11k images) using DINO learns robust, general hand anatomy representations that transfer effectively to the small, imbalanced RA detection task.
*   **Mechanism:** DINO is applied to the 11k healthy hand dataset. Both Global and Local Encoder backbones are pretrained to learn invariant features (e.g., shape, skin texture, bone structure) without disease labels. This creates a strong, general initialization. During fine-tuning, these pretrained weights are frozen, preventing catastrophic forgetting and overfitting on the small RA dataset.
*   **Core assumption:** The feature space of healthy hands is a valid and comprehensive prior for RA-affected hands; the key discriminative features for inflammation are deviations from this healthy manifold, which can be learned by a small adapter (the MLP header).
*   **Evidence anchors:** [abstract] "...self-supervised pretraining on 11k healthy hand images using DINO..."; [section 3.3.1] "We trained ResNet-18 backbones using DINO on the large-scale 11k-hands dataset..."; [section 4.4, Table 3] Ablation shows removing DINO pretraining ("w/o DINO pre-training") drops F1 from 0.420 to 0.352 and Gmean from 0.605 to 0.580.
*   **Break condition:** If the domain shift between healthy pretraining data (likely clean, well-lit, controlled) and the target RA data (home-captured, variable quality) is too large, the frozen features may be misaligned. Also, if inflammation cues are not captured by the self-supervised task (e.g., DINO's cropping/multi-crop strategy).

### Mechanism 3
*   **Claim:** Using Focal Loss instead of Binary Cross Entropy (BCE) during fine-tuning mitigates the extreme joint-level class imbalance (far more negative than positive joints) by down-weighting easy negative examples.
*   **Mechanism:** Focal Loss adds a modulating factor (1 - p_t)^γ to the standard cross-entropy loss, where p_t is the model's confidence for the true class. This factor exponentially reduces the loss contributed by well-classified examples (typically the abundant negative class), forcing the optimizer to focus training on the hard, misclassified, and rare positive (inflamed) examples.
*   **Core assumption:** The model quickly learns to predict the majority negative class; standard BCE loss is dominated by easy negatives, providing a weak learning signal for the minority positive class. Down-weighting these easy negatives is necessary to learn discriminative features for positives.
*   **Evidence anchors:** [abstract] "...fine-tuning with Focal Loss."; [section 3.3.2] "To deal with the severe data imbalance, we train the network using Focal Loss instead of standard Binary Cross Entropy (BCE)."; [section 4.4, Table 3] Ablation shows removing Focal Loss ("w/o Focal Loss") catastrophically drops Recall from 0.478 to 0.115 and F1 from 0.420 to 0.158, while Precision increases. This indicates the model collapses to predicting almost all negatives without Focal Loss.
*   **Break condition:** If γ is set too high, the model might focus excessively on a few hard/outlier positive examples, harming generalization. If the imbalance is not the primary difficulty (e.g., if features are simply not present), Focal Loss cannot conjure signal.

## Foundational Learning
*   **Concept: Class Imbalance in Medical Imaging**  
    Why needed here: The core data challenge is extreme joint-level imbalance (Figure 2). Understanding why accuracy is a misleading metric and the trade-off between Recall and Precision is critical to interpreting F1/Gmean results.  
    Quick check question: Why does a model that predicts "no inflammation" for every joint achieve >90% accuracy but be clinically useless?

*   **Concept: Self-Supervised Pretraining (DINO)**  
    Why needed here: The method's efficacy hinges on transferring knowledge from 11k unlabeled healthy hands to a tiny labeled RA set. One must understand how a model learns representations without labels (e.g., via invariance to augmentations, student-teacher distillation).  
    Quick check question: What visual invariance does DINO's multi-crop training encourage, and why would that be useful for recognizing inflamed joints?

*   **Concept: Focal Loss Mechanism**  
    Why needed here: It's the key imbalance-handling component. Understanding the (1-p_t)^γ term and its effect on the loss landscape is necessary to tune γ and diagnose performance (Table 3's precision/recall trade-off).  
    Quick check question: How does increasing the γ parameter in Focal Loss change the model's learning pressure on easy vs. hard examples?

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component map (plain text):**  
Raw image -> SAM2 segmentation & MediaPipe joint detection -> Cropped joint patches + full hand resize -> Global Encoder (ResNet-18) & Local Encoder (ResNet-18) -> Feature concatenation per joint -> MLP classification head -> Inflammation probability per joint

**Critical path:**  
1. **Phase 1 (Unfrozen):** Pretrain two ResNet-18 backbones from scratch on 11k healthy hand images using DINO's self-supervised loss.  
2. **Phase 2 (Frozen):** Load Phase 1 weights, freeze both ResNet-18 backbones, and train only the MLP classification head on the small RA dataset using Focal Loss.

**Design tradeoffs:**  
- *Parameter Efficiency vs. Adaptability:* Freezing backbones prevents overfitting and leverages powerful pretrained features but means the model cannot adapt the low/mid-level feature extractors to the specific visual signatures of RA inflammation (e.g., specific color shifts). All adaptation happens in the MLP head.  
- *Complexity vs. Signal:* The Global-Local design adds parameters and inference cost but is justified by the ablation (w/o Global/Local Encoder Gmean drops from 0.605 to 0.517). The signal from global context appears necessary.  
- *DINO vs. ImageNet Pretraining:* The ablation (w/o DINO) and ViT failure suggest the domain-specific (hand anatomy) pretraining from DINO is far superior to generic ImageNet pretraining for this task.

**Failure signatures:**  
- **Model Collapse (All Negatives):** As seen with ViT (Table 2). Symptoms: Recall ≈ 0, Precision undefined/0, F1 ≈ 0. Cause: Model unable to learn any signal from imbalanced small data; defaults to majority class. Fix: Use simpler architecture (ResNet vs. ViT), ensure correct pretraining/freezing, verify Focal Loss γ is not 0.  
- **High Precision, Extremely Low Recall:** As in "w/o Focal Loss" (Table 3). Symptoms: Precision ~0.465, Recall ~0.115. Cause: Model optimized for accuracy on majority class; ignores minority positives. Fix: Implement and tune Focal Loss (γ=2.0 in paper).  
- **Poor Pretraining Convergence:** If DINO loss on 11k hands does not decrease, global/local features remain random. Final performance will be poor regardless of fine-tuning strategy. Fix: Check DINO hyperparameters (learning rate, augmentations), data quality.

**First 3 experiments:**  
1. Replicate Ablation Core: Train the full proposed model. Then train the exact same model **without** the dual-encoder architecture (e.g., use only a single ResNet-18 on the full hand image). Compare F1/Gmean. This isolates the contribution of the global-local design.  
2. Test Focal Loss γ Sweep: Using the full proposed architecture, fine-tune with Focal Loss but sweep γ = {0.5, 1.0, 2.0, 5.0}. Plot Precision-Recall curves. Expected: γ=2.0 (paper) optimizes F1/Gmean balance for this imbalance level.  
3. Validate Pretraining Source: Replace the DINO-pretrained weights with standard ImageNet-pretrained ResNet-18 weights for both encoders (keeping everything else identical: frozen, Focal Loss). Measure drop in F1/Gmean. This quantifies the value of the domain-specific self-supervised pretraining.

## Limitations
- Small, single-center dataset (222 images, 68 patients) and single smartphone model limit generalizability to broader populations and imaging conditions.
- Ground truth annotations are based on ultrasound rather than visual inspection, creating a modality gap between the RGB input and the disease label.
- Frozen encoders after DINO pretraining may prevent adaptation to RA-specific visual cues; reported metrics (F1=0.420) are modest and lack detailed error analysis.

## Confidence
- **High Confidence:** The dual-encoder architecture with Focal Loss provides a measurable gain over a baseline ResNet-18 (Table 3 ablation).
- **Medium Confidence:** The specific value of domain-specific DINO pretraining over generic ImageNet pretraining is supported by the ViT failure and ablation, but could be confounded by architecture differences (ResNet vs. ViT).
- **Low Confidence:** The claim of "performance close to rheumatologists" is qualified by different diagnostic modalities (RGB vs. palpation) and absence of statistical significance testing; the clinical utility of the model remains unproven.

## Next Checks
1. External dataset validation: Test the trained model on an independent RA hand image dataset (different centers, cameras, demographics) to quantify generalization gaps and avoid overfitting to the 222-image cohort.
2. Encoder fine-tuning ablation: Unfreeze and fine-tune the last one or two ResNet-18 blocks during the Focal Loss stage (keeping earlier layers frozen) to measure whether limited adaptation improves performance beyond the frozen-header approach.
3. Clinical utility pilot: Conduct a reader study where rheumatologists review the model's false positives/negatives to assess whether the model's mistakes are clinically meaningful or capture non-visual ultrasound phenomena.
