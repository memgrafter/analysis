# Harmonizing Dense and Sparse Signals in Multi-turn RL: Dual-Horizon Credit Assignment for Industrial Sales Agents

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2603.01481
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.01481
- **Reference count:** 11
- **Primary result:** DuCA improves conversion rate by 6.82% relative, reduces inter-sentence repetition by 82.28%, lowers identity detection rate by 27.35%, and improves compliance by 7.61% over SFT.

## Executive Summary
Multi-turn reinforcement learning for industrial sales agents suffers from instability when combining dense turn-level linguistic rewards with sparse session-level business rewards, leading to gradient dominance and reward hacking. This paper introduces the Dual-Horizon Credit Assignment (DuCA) framework, which employs Horizon-Independent Advantage Normalization (HIAN) to separately normalize advantages from each reward horizon before fusion, ensuring balanced gradient contributions. DuCA also incorporates a gated fusion mechanism to suppress turn rewards when heuristic constraints (e.g., repetition, script similarity) are violated. Trained with a high-fidelity user simulator, DuCA significantly outperforms the GRPO baseline, achieving substantial gains in conversion, compliance, and human-likeness metrics. Ablation studies confirm the necessity of both decoupled normalization and the multi-turn interaction environment.

## Method Summary
DuCA extends Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO) with dual value heads that separately estimate advantages for turn-level and session-level rewards. For each horizon, Generalized Advantage Estimation (GAE) computes advantages; HIAN then normalizes these advantages within a mini-batch using their own mean and standard deviation, preventing the high-variance session-level advantages from dominating the gradient. The normalized advantages are fused with equal weights to produce the total advantage for the PPO surrogate objective. Additionally, a gated mechanism modulates turn-level rewards: if a response exceeds thresholds for repetition or verbatim similarity to a script, a penalty is applied instead of the length-based reward. This design mitigates reward hacking on dense heuristic signals. The policy is trained via multi-turn interactions with a persona-conditioned user simulator, enabling long-horizon credit assignment for business outcomes like conversion and compliance.

## Key Results
- DuCA achieves a 6.82% relative improvement in conversion rate over the GRPO baseline.
- Inter-sentence repetition is reduced by 82.28% and identity detection rate drops by 27.35%, indicating significantly less robotic and more human-like behavior.
- Compliance improves by 7.61% compared to the SFT baseline.
- Ablations show that removing either the decoupled normalization or the multi-turn environment degrades performance, underscoring the necessity of both components.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1
- **Claim:** Separately normalizing advantages from different reward horizons prevents gradient dominance, allowing both dense turn-level and sparse session-level signals to contribute meaningfully to policy updates.
- **Mechanism:** Dual value heads estimate advantages for each horizon (`A_turn`, `A_session`). HIAN normalizes these within a mini-batch using their own mean and standard deviation (`(A - μ) / σ + ε`). - **Core assumption:** The gradient suppression occurs during the scaling of the combined advantage before the policy update, where high-variance sparse rewards (`σ_session >> σ_turn`) cause `Δθ ∝ (A_turn + A_session) / √(σ_turn² + σ_session²)`, making the `A_turn` term negligible.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract] Explicitly states the method "separately normalizes advantages from turn-level and session-level rewards before fusion, ensuring balanced gradient contributions."
  - [section 3.4, Step 2] Provides the mathematical formulation of HIAN and the theoretical justification for gradient suppression in naive scalarization.
  - [corpus] Neighbors like "SCAR: Shapley Credit Assignment" and "Reinforcing Multi-Turn Reasoning... via Turn-Level Reward Design" indicate related interest in credit assignment, but do not directly validate HIAN's specific decoupled normalization mechanism.
- **Break condition:** If the per-horizon advantage distributions (`A_turn`, `A_session`) become highly correlated or their variances converge during training, the normalization would lose its disentangling effect, potentially reintroducing dominance.

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim:** A high-fidelity, persona-conditioned user simulator provides a stable, realistic, and safe environment for multi-turn exploration, enabling the learning of strategic long-horizon policies without online risks.
- **Mechanism:** The simulator `π_sim(ut|st, ψ)` generates user utterances conditioned on dialogue history `st` and a static persona `ψ`. This creates diverse, consistent user behaviors for the agent to interact with over many turns, generating trajectories `τ` that link early actions to final session rewards.
- **Core assumption:** The simulator's outputs are sufficiently realistic and correlated with real-world business outcomes (e.g., conversion) to make policy improvements in simulation transferable to production.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract] Mentions training with a "high-fidelity user simulator."
  - [section 3.2] Details the simulator's construction from 31k real dialogues and its conditioning on personas.
  - [Appendix B] Presents validation: a Turing test where experts misidentified 52% of human trajectories as simulator-generated, and a Pearson correlation ρ=0.9733 between simulated and real CVR across product categories.
- **Break condition:** Simulator fidelity degrades, either by failing to maintain persona consistency (low adherence score) or by diverging from real user behavior (low Sim-to-Real correlation). This would cause the policy to overfit to simulator quirks not present in reality.

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim:** A gated fusion mechanism for turn-level rewards prevents reward hacking on dense heuristic constraints (e.g., length, style) by suppressing rewards for responses that violate harder constraints (e.g., repetition, similarity to scripts).
- **Mechanism:** The final turn reward `r_turn = ⊮_valid(at) * r_len(at) + (1-⊮_valid(at)) * R_penalty`. The indicator `⊮_valid` is false if repetition exceeds δ₁ or similarity exceeds δ₂, triggering a constant penalty instead of the length reward.
- **Core assumption:** The gating criteria (repetition, verbatim similarity) are reliable proxies for "robotic" or "hacked" output that is fluent but strategically poor or non-compliant.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [section 3.3.1] Describes the gating mechanism and its components (`Rep(τ)`, `Sim(at, S)`, `r_len`, `R_penalty`).
  - [Appendix A.1.1] Provides exact formulas and thresholds (δ₁=0.4, δ₂=0.85, L_target=30 tokens, R_penalty=-2.0).
  - [Table 1 & 5] Show DuCA's dramatic reduction in Inter-R (82.28% vs GRPO) and IDR (27.35% vs GDPO), suggesting the mechanism successfully curbs repetitive/identifiable behavior.
- **Break condition:** Gating thresholds are poorly tuned. Too strict (high δ₁/δ₂) causes excessive penalties, stifling creativity; too lenient allows loopholes where the model satisfies `⊮_valid` while still producing low-quality, repetitive content.

Understanding how λ and γ control the bias-variance trade-off and credit assignment span is essential.   - *Quick check question:* Why would setting `γ_session < 1.0` for the session-level advantage be detrimental to learning a conversion-maximizing policy in a multi-turn sales dialogue?

- **Concept: Reward Shaping & Scalarization Pitfalls**
  - *Why needed here:* The entire paper addresses the failure mode of naive reward summation. An engineer must understand why merging dense/sparse signals into a single `R_total` causes gradient dominance or reward hacking, and how decoupling at the advantage level is a more principled solution than simply weighting the raw rewards.
  - *Quick check question:* In a naive scalarized reward `R = r_turn + R_session`, if `R_session` has a magnitude 10x larger than typical `r_turn` and high variance, what happens to the policy gradient's sensitivity to `r_turn`?

- **Concept: Multi-turn RL Credit Assignment**
  - *Why needed here:* The core research problem. The engineer must grasp that in long-horizon tasks, assigning credit for a final outcome (conversion) to much earlier linguistic turns is inherently difficult and prone to error. DuCA's approach is a specific solution to this general problem.
  - *Quick check question:* What is the key difference between "process-based credit assignment" (using a PRM) and "dual-horizon credit assignment" as proposed here?

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map:**
  1.  **User Simulator (`π_sim`)**: LLM-based, persona & history conditioned. Generates `u_t`.
  2.  **Agent Policy (`π_θ`)**: Target LLM being trained. Generates `a_t`.
  3.  **Dual Value Heads**: `V_ϕ_turn` and `V_ϕ_session` estimate state values for their respective reward streams.
  4.  **Reward Functions**: `r_turn(st,at)` (heuristic, gated), `R_session(τ)` (terminal, business/compliance).
  5.  **HIAN Module**: Performs independent mini-batch normalization on `A_turn` and `A_session`.
  6.  **Fusion & PPO**: Computes `A_total` and applies the clipped PPO surrogate objective.

- **Critical path:** Simulator generates `u_t` -> Agent generates `a_t` -> Environment computes `r_turn` & accumulates trajectory -> On terminal step `T`, compute `R_session` -> Both rewards feed their respective value heads & GAE to get `A_turn`, `A_session` -> HIAN normalizes -> Fuse to `A_total` -> PPO policy gradient update on `π_θ`.

- **Design tradeoffs:**
  - *Separate value heads vs. single head:* Separate heads allow tailored temporal discounting (`γ_turn=0.99` vs. `γ_session=1.0`) and prevent value function interference, but double the critic network size and inference cost.
  - *Fixed fusion weights (w_turn=w_session=1.0):* Simplicity and empirical success in the paper, but may not be optimal for all domains. Could learn these weights or tune via validation.
  - *Heuristic turn rewards:* Stable and interpretable, but may not capture all nuances of conversational quality. Relies on well-designed rules and thresholds.

- **Failure signatures:**
  - **Spiking or collapsing CVR during training** (Fig 3a): Indicates unstable learning; likely unbalance in advantage contributions or simulator issues.
  - **Explosion in Inter-R or Prefix-R** (Table 1,5): Classic reward hacking on turn-level signals; gating mechanism failing or weights mis-set.
  - **High IDR or low PATR**: Agent becomes detectable as non-human or fails to positively persuade; suggests over-optimization for compliance at cost of engagement.
  - **Simulator validation metrics drop** (Turing test score, ρ): Policy is overfitting to a flawed environment.

- **First 3 experiments:**
  1.  **Simulator Sanity Check:** Run the trained `π_θ` (even a random or SFT model) in the simulator for 100 trajectories. Compute the gating indicator `⊮_valid` rate, repetition rates (Intra/Inter-R), and persona adherence. Ensure basic interaction quality before RL.
  2.  **Validate HIAN Effect:** Train an ablation *without* HIAN (simple reward summation). Plot `A_turn` and `A_session` mean/variance per mini-batch over training steps. Expect to see `A_session`'s magnitude and variance dominate, causing `Â_turn`'s influence to vanish.
  3.  **Single-Turn Ablation:** Train the "w/o Multi-turn" variant. Compare its CVR and compliance dynamics to full DuCA (Fig 4). Expect strategic stagnation (low CVR) despite potentially high compliance from short-term gaming.

## Limitations
- **HIAN Dependency:** Assumes `A_turn` and `A_session` distributions remain statistically independent with distinct variances; if they become correlated or variances converge, the normalization loses disentangling effect.
- **Simulator Transfer Risk:** Fidelity metrics are proxy-based (e.g., Turing test, CVR correlation); real-world business outcomes may diverge, leading to overfitting to simulation artifacts.
- **Static Gate Thresholds:** Hand-tuned thresholds (`δ₁=0.4`, `δ₂=0.85`) may not generalize across product categories or user personas, potentially being too strict or too lenient.

## Confidence
- **HIAN Mechanism:** High. Strong theoretical justification and ablations confirm mitigation of gradient dominance.
- **Simulator Validity:** Medium. High proxy metrics exist, but sim-to-real transfer remains an assumption.
- **Gated Fusion:** High. Dramatic reductions in repetition and identity detection demonstrate effective mitigation of reward hacking.

## Next Checks
