# Pseudo Contrastive Learning for Diagram Comprehension in Multimodal Models

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2602.23589
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.23589
- Reference count: 29
- Primary result: Pseudo contrastive learning improves diagram image-text matching and VQA over CLIP without requiring editable source data, achieving Recall@1 up to 0.771, MRR 0.845, and BERTScore F1 0.648 on FlowVQA.

## Executive Summary
Standard vision-language models like CLIP struggle with fine-grained structural variations in diagrams, where minor visual changes can alter semantics. This is compounded by the lack of editable (vector) diagram data, which prior solutions require. This paper introduces pseudo contrastive learning: from raster diagram images, OCR extracts text to generate synthetic pseudo diagrams with random connections and structures, enabling rule-based creation of hard positive and negative samples for contrastive training. The method is evaluated on the FlowVQA flowchart dataset, showing significant improvements in image-text retrieval (Recall@1 up to 0.771, MRR 0.845) and visual question answering (BERTScore F1 0.648) over standard CLIP and hard-negative baselines, without needing original editable formats. The approach demonstrates that structural contrasts derived solely from raster data can endow multimodal models with structural awareness for diagram comprehension.

## Method Summary
The method consists of three stages. First, pseudo-diagram synthesis: OCR (Tesseract) extracts text labels and bounding boxes from a raster diagram. Nearby labels are grouped (nearest 3) into nodes, and random directed edges are added to form a graph, which is encoded as Mermaid code. This code is rendered to a raster image and paired with a caption generated by iterating over edges. Second, hard sample generation: on-the-fly rule-based edits are applied to the editable pseudo diagram to produce hard positives and negatives. Hard positives slightly modify the original (e.g., reversing overall flow direction, moving node positions). Hard negatives combine those positive edits with aggressive structural changes (reversing/removing individual arrows, swapping node labels in the caption). Third, structure-aware contrastive training: a CLIP model (ViT-L/14@336px) is trained with a combined loss = InfoNCE + λ_SC·L_SC, where L_SC aggregates similarities between the anchor and hard positives/negatives across image-image, text-text, and cross-modal pairs. The vision encoder is adapted via LoRA (r=8, α=16). For VQA, the finetuned vision encoder is inserted into LLaVA-v1.6-Mistral-7B, and the LLM is finetuned with LoRA on QA pairs.

## Key Results
- Image-text retrieval on FlowVQA: Recall@1 up to 0.771, MRR 0.845.
- Visual question answering: BERTScore F1 0.648.
- Significant gains over standard CLIP and hard-negative baselines, achieved without requiring editable source diagrams.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1
- **Claim:** Synthetic pseudo diagrams enable generation of hard positive/negative structural contrasts from raster-only data.
- **Mechanism:** OCR extracts text labels and positions. Random subsets of these labels are grouped into nodes (via nearest-neighbor proximity), and random directed edges are created between nodes. This produces semantically nonsensical but structurally valid diagram definitions (e.g., Mermaid code), which are rendered into images. Rule-based edits (e.g., reversing arrow direction, moving nodes) are applied to these renderings to create hard positives/negatives that differ only in specific structural attributes.
- **Core assumption:** Diagram renderers (e.g., Mermaid) can plausibly render arbitrary connections between extracted text labels, producing images whose structural variations are visually discernible and semantically meaningful to a VLM.
- **Evidence anchors:** Abstract: "Our approach introduces pseudo contrastive samples generated by a diagram renderer that creates synthetic diagrams using randomly picked text elements... without requiring any modification or editing of the original data." Section 3.1: random node/edge construction; renders pseudo diagrams alongside editable data.
- **Break condition:** If the target diagram domain's visual style (node shapes, arrow styles, layout conventions) is unsupported by the chosen renderer, the synthetic images may not share sufficient appearance with real diagrams, making structural contrasts ineffective.

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim:** Rule-based edits applied to pseudo diagrams generate targeted, structure-aware hard samples that force the model to learn fine-grained distinctions.
- **Mechanism:** Specific perturbations are applied to the editable pseudo diagram data. For hard positives, minor edits like reversing overall flow direction or moving node positions create visually similar images with subtle structural changes. For hard negatives, more aggressive edits (reversing individual arrows, removing arrows, swapping node labels in the caption) create mismatches between image structure and textual description.
- **Core assumption:** These predefined edit operations produce image-caption pairs where the positive pair shares semantic intent but differs in minor structure, and the negative pair shares superficial text/visual elements but has incompatible structure-meaning mappings. The contrastive loss then learns sensitivity to these structural attributes.
- **Evidence anchors:** Section 3.2 lists explicit rules; Figure 3 visualizes transformations.
- **Break condition:** If edit operations do not create sufficiently "hard" samples (too trivial or too drastic), or if generated captions do not accurately describe the edited structure, the contrastive signal becomes noisy.

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim:** Structure-aware contrastive loss (SC loss) explicitly optimizes the model to group hard positives and separate hard negatives across both image-image, text-text, and cross-modal similarities.
- **Mechanism:** The total loss combines standard CLIP's InfoNCE with an additional SC loss term. SC loss calculates similarity between the anchor and its generated hard positive samples (across vision and text modalities) as the numerator, and similarity to hard negative samples as the denominator. This forces the embedding space to cluster an image with its minimally-edited variants and push away images/captions with structurally incompatible variants.
- **Core assumption:** The model's representation space can be shaped by this multi-faceted similarity constraint to disentangle structural features from other semantic content, making structural differences a primary factor in similarity computation.
- **Evidence anchors:** Section 3.3 defines SC loss; Section 4.4 (Table 2) shows improved performance under hard-negative evaluation when using SC loss.
- **Break condition:** If hard positive/negative samples are low quality, SC loss may optimize a flawed objective and degrade performance.

## Foundational Learning
- **Optical Character Recognition (OCR)**
  - *Why needed:* OCR extracts text labels and positions from raster diagrams; inaccuracies corrupt node definitions and graph generation, leading to misleading contrastive samples.
  - *Quick check:* If OCR misses a key node label or mislocalizes a label, how would that affect the randomness and diversity of the generated pseudo-diagrams' structures?

- **Contrastive Learning with Hard Negatives**
  - *Why needed:* Standard CLIP's in-batch negatives are too easy for fine-grained diagram structure; understanding hard negative mining is crucial to grasp why synthetically created, structurally similar samples are more effective.
  - *Quick check:* Why might a randomly sampled negative be "easy" while a negative created by swapping two node labels be "hard"?

- **Diagram Rendering (Vector to Raster)**
  - *Why needed:* The renderer's style must match the distribution of real flowcharts (FlowVQA); a mismatch could make synthetic samples appear too synthetic and hinder transfer.
  - *Quick check:* If real flowcharts use a specific non-standard decision symbol (e.g., a hexagon), but Mermaid-cli only renders diamonds, how might this visual domain shift impact the model's ability to learn from the pseudo-diagrams?

## Architecture Onboarding
### Component Map
Input Raster Batch -> OCR Module (Tesseract) -> Pseudo-Diagram Synthesizer (groups text labels, creates random directed graphs, outputs Mermaid code and caption) -> Diagram Renderer (mermaid-cli, produces raster image and editable SVG) -> Hard Sample Generator (applies rule-based edits to both image and caption, yields hard positives/negatives) -> VLM (CLIP ViT-L/14@336px) -> Loss Module (L_total = L_CLIP + λ_SC * L_SC) -> Optimizer (LoRA)

### Critical Path
Raster Image → OCR → Text Labels → Random Graph Construction → Diagram Code → Renderer → Editable Image + Code → Rule-Based Edits → Hard Samples (Img/Caption) → Rasterization → VLM Encoding → Contrastive Loss → LoRA Update

### Design Tradeoffs
- **Synthetic Data Diversity vs. Plausibility:** Extreme randomness yields diverse structures but may create visually chaotic images unlike real diagrams. Node size N_n (set to 3) balances capturing local structure and graph complexity.
- **Hard Sample Generation Cost:** On-the-fly editing adds computational overhead but ensures constant novelty; precomputation would reduce epoch-level diversity.
- **Loss Weighting (λ_SC):** Set to 0.1; higher risks overfitting to artificial structural differences, harming general alignment.

### Failure Signatures
- Training instability or NaN loss, possibly due to absolute cosine similarity implementation or scaled gradients.
- Degraded zero-shot/coarse retrieval performance if over-specialization to synthetic patterns occurs.
- Poor VQA transfer if learned features do not generalize to original FlowVQA questions.

### First 3 Experiments
1. **OCR Output Sanity Check:** Manually verify OCR output on a subset of FlowVQA images—confirm all node labels are extracted and grouped correctly via proximity heuristics. Adjust grouping distance thresholds if node clusters are incoherent.
2. **Renderer Output Validation:** Render a few pseudo-diagrams from OCR outputs. Visually inspect for flowchart plausibility and stylistic match to FlowVQA. If mismatched (e.g., wrong node shapes), tweak Mermaid themes or switch renderers.
3. **Hard Sample Qualitative Inspection:** Generate triplet samples (anchor, hard positive, hard negative). Ensure hard positives differ only in intended edits (e.g., reversed flow) and hard negatives are semantically plausible but structurally inconsistent. Quantify difficulty by measuring anchor-negative similarity distributions before training.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- **Open Question 1:** For which diagram types or visual styles does the pseudo diagram generation method fail to produce structurally meaningful training samples? *Basis:* Authors note the method relies on similar appearance between target diagrams and renderer output; not fully applicable when target appearance differs from unsupported renderer styles. *Why unresolved:* Evaluated only on flowchart images rendered via Mermaid; boundary conditions for mismatch (hand-drawn, legacy formats, domain-specific symbols) remain unexplored. *Resolving evidence:* Systematic evaluation across diverse diagram datasets (UML, electrical schematics, scientific figures) using multiple renderers, measuring performance when styles diverge.
- **Open Question 2:** How does OCR accuracy quantitatively affect pseudo diagram quality and downstream VLM performance? *Basis:* Authors identify impact of OCR quality as currently unclear. *Why unresolved:* Single OCR engine used without analyzing error propagation; sensitivity to OCR mistakes unknown. *Resolving evidence:* Controlled experiments injecting synthetic OCR errors or comparing multiple OCR engines, correlating OCR error metrics with changes in retrieval Recall@1 and VQA BERTScore.
- **Open Question 3:** Can the approach generalize to diagram domains beyond flowcharts (e.g., scientific diagrams, technical schematics)? *Basis:* Conclusion suggests extending to wider diagram types. *Why unresolved:* All experiments on FlowVQA, a flowchart-specific dataset; no evidence for other domains with different structural semantics. *Resolving evidence:* Apply pipeline to other diagram VQA/retrieval benchmarks (e.g., scientific paper diagrams, CLEVR-diagrams) and report improvements over baselines.
- **Open Question 4:** Do diagram semantics rely on non-textual visual elements (icons, colors, layout conventions) ignored by OCR-based synthesis, creating a semantic ceiling? *Basis:* Inferred; method uses only OCR text, assuming structural variations in connections/flow are primary carriers, but diagrams also encode meaning in non-textual features. *Why unresolved:* No ablation of non-textual visual information; unclear if improvements are limited to connection patterns or fail on cues like node shapes/colors. *Resolving evidence:* Synthetic experiments that preserve or remove non-textual semantics (identical text but varying shapes/colors to change meaning) and measure accuracy on questions depending solely on those cues.

## Limitations
- The method's effectiveness depends on the diagram renderer's visual style matching real flowcharts; domain shifts (e.g., hand-drawn, custom symbols) may render synthetic samples ineffective.
- Heavy reliance on OCR; errors in text extraction or bounding boxes can corrupt pseudo-diagram generation and degrade training signal.
- Unclear calibration of hyperparameters (node grouping count, edge counts, edit difficulty) and qualitative aspects of hard negative generation, which may produce insufficiently challenging samples.

## Confidence
- Synthetic pseudo-diagram utility: Medium
- Hard sample quality: Low
- Structure-aware loss benefit: High
