# Learning Structured Reasoning via Tractable Trajectory Control

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID**: 2603.01641  
- **Source URL**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.01641  
- **Reference count**: 40  
- **Primary result**: Ctrl-R improves reasoning accuracy by +1.38% for Qwen3-1.7B (over DAPO) and +2.60% for Qwen2.5-VL-7B (over GRPO), outperforming natural language guidance and reward shaping baselines.

## Executive Summary
Ctrl-R addresses the lack of diverse reasoning behaviors in reinforcement learning (RL)-trained language models by introducing tractable trajectory control. It distills a hidden Markov model (HMM) from the initial policy and encodes desired reasoning structures (e.g., backtracking, induction) as deterministic finite automata (DFAs). A guidance function computed with the HMM and DFA softly biases the next-token distribution toward satisfying the chosen structure while preserving a valid probability distribution. This enables exact importance sampling for unbiased policy gradient updates despite off-policy rollouts. A power-scaling factor on the importance weights selectively amplifies learning signals from exploratory trajectories. On mathematical reasoning benchmarks (AIME'24, MATH500, etc.), Ctrl-R yields consistent gains and surpasses natural language guidance and reward shaping approaches.

## Method Summary
Ctrl-R operates on base language models (e.g., Qwen3, Qwen2.5-VL) that are first pre-trained with an RL method (DAPO for LMs, GRPO for VLMs). The method begins by distilling a small HMM from the initial policy using a large set of prefix-suffix pairs from the RL training data. For a sampled reasoning structure \( \alpha \), a DFA is constructed from a predefined set of lexical keyphrases. At each generation step, the guidance function \( \gamma(\alpha \mid x_{<t}, x_t) \) estimates the marginal probability of eventually satisfying the DFA constraint; this is computed efficiently via dynamic programming over the HMM and DFA. The behavior policy is \( \mu_\alpha(x_t \mid x_{<t}) \propto \pi_{\text{old}}(x_t \mid x_{<t}) \cdot \gamma(\alpha \mid x_t, x_{<t}) \), normalized to sum to one. Rollouts are collected from \( \mu_\alpha \), and per-token importance weights \( w_t = \pi_{\text{old}}(x_t \mid x_{<t}) / \mu_\alpha(x_t \mid x_{<t}) \) are multiplied to a trajectory weight \( w \). The policy loss uses a power-scaled weight \( w^\beta \) (default \( \beta = 0.2 \)) to balance exploratory signals and stability. The policy is updated with standard clipped PPO/GRPO objectives, and \( \pi_{\text{old}} \) is periodically refreshed from the current policy.

## Key Results
- Ctrl-R achieves +1.38% average accuracy on AIME’24/MATH500 for Qwen3-1.7B over a strong DAPO baseline.
- For Qwen2.5-VL-7B, Ctrl-R gains +2.60% over GRPO on mathematical VLM benchmarks.
- The method significantly outperforms natural language guidance and reward shaping baselines, and analysis confirms internalization of effective reasoning patterns rather than superficial keyword use.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1: Tractable steering toward target reasoning patterns
Ctrl-R computes a guidance function \( \gamma(\alpha \mid x_{<t}, x_t) \) that estimates the probability of fulfilling a reasoning structure \( \alpha \) encoded as a DFA. The HMM, distilled from the initial policy, models the token distribution, and the DFA captures lexical patterns. Their combination allows efficient marginalization via the forward algorithm, yielding a soft bias without breaking the probability distribution. The core assumption is that keyphrases reliably indicate reasoning behaviors and the HMM accurately approximates the policy’s token probabilities.

### Mechanism 2: Exact importance sampling for unbiased updates
Because \( \mu_\alpha \) is a normalized product of \( \pi_{\text{old}} \) and \( \gamma \), its probability for any token sequence can be computed exactly. This permits exact importance weights \( w_t = \pi_{\text{old}} / \mu_\alpha \) and a trajectory weight \( w = \prod w_t \). Consequently, the off-policy policy gradient \( \mathbb{E}_{\tau \sim \mu_\alpha}[ w \nabla \log \pi_\theta ] \) remains unbiased. The validity of \( \mu_\alpha \) as a probability distribution ensures stable optimization.

### Mechanism 3: Power-scaling for selective learning from exploratory rollouts
Applying a power \( \beta \in [0,1] \) to the importance weight \( w^\beta \) downweights trajectories with extreme weights (either too close to on-policy or too distorted). Empirically, intermediate weights correspond to trajectories that exhibit the guided reasoning pattern while remaining of sufficient quality. Setting \( \beta = 0.2 \) selectively amplifies these informative rollouts, leading to better performance than full correction (\( \beta=1 \)) or no correction (\( \beta=0 \)).

## Foundational Learning

### Off-Policy Reinforcement Learning and Importance Sampling
**Why needed here**: The guided rollouts come from a behavior policy \( \mu_\alpha \) that differs from the target policy \( \pi_\theta \). Importance sampling corrects this distribution shift to compute an unbiased policy gradient.  
**Quick check**: In \( \nabla_\theta J(\pi_\theta) = \mathbb{E}_{\tau \sim \mu}[ w \nabla_\theta \log \pi_\theta(a|s) ] \), what does \( w \) represent and why is it necessary?

### Constrained Decoding in Language Generation
**Why needed here**: Reasoning structures are enforced as lexical constraints during token generation, but hard constraints can produce invalid distributions. Ctrl-R uses soft probabilistic guidance to maintain a valid probability measure.  
**Quick check**: How does soft guidance (multiplying by \( \gamma \)) differ from hard token blocking (e.g., banning certain tokens) in terms of preserving a normalized distribution?

### Hidden Markov Models for Sequence Marginalization
**Why needed here**: The guidance function \( \gamma \) requires marginalizing over all possible future continuations to estimate the probability of satisfying a DFA constraint. An HMM approximation makes this tractable.  
**Quick check**: Why can an HMM combined with a DFA compute \( \gamma \) efficiently, while a neural language model cannot directly marginalize over exponentially many futures?

## Architecture Onboarding

### Component map
The system consists of: a base policy \( \pi_\theta \); an HMM distiller that fits an HMM to \( \pi_{\theta_0} \); a DFA generator that creates an automaton from keyphrases; a guidance module \( \gamma \) that combines HMM and DFA to compute marginal probabilities; a behavior policy \( \mu_\alpha \) that multiplies \( \pi_{\text{old}} \) with \( \gamma \) and normalizes; a rollout sampler that draws trajectories from \( \mu_\alpha \); an importance calculator that produces per-token weights \( w_t = \pi_{\text{old}} / \mu_\alpha \) and aggregates to \( w \); a policy optimizer that applies the Ctrl-R loss with power-scaling factor \( \beta \); and a proximal policy updater that periodically sets \( \pi_{\text{old}} = \pi_\theta \). The flow is: \( \pi_{\theta_0} \xrightarrow{\text{distill}} \text{HMM} \); keyphrases \( \xrightarrow{\text{generate}} \text{DFA} \); HMM + DFA + \( \pi_{\text{old}} \xrightarrow{\gamma} \mu_\alpha \xrightarrow{\text{rollouts}} \text{trajectories} \); \( \pi_{\text{old}} \) & \( \mu_\alpha \xrightarrow{\text{IS}} w \xrightarrow{\text{loss}} \pi_\theta \); periodic \( \pi_\theta \rightarrow \pi_{\text{old}} \).

### Critical path
1. At training start, distill HMM from the initial policy \( \pi_{\theta_0} \).
2. For each RL iteration:
   - Sample a reasoning structure \( \alpha \).
   - Initialize \( \pi_{\text{old}} \) as a snapshot of the current \( \pi_\theta \).
   - Generate trajectories using \( \mu_\alpha \), compute rewards and group-relative advantages.
   - Compute per-token importance weights \( w_t = \pi_{\text{old}} / \mu_\alpha \) and trajectory weight \( w = \prod w_t \).
   - Update \( \pi_\theta \) with the Ctrl-R loss \( -\mathbb{E}[ w^\beta \cdot \text{PG-clipped} ] \).
   - After several steps, refresh \( \pi_{\text{old}} \) from the updated \( \pi_\theta \).

### Design tradeoffs
- **HMM state count**: More states improve the approximation of \( \pi_{\text{old}} \) but increase compute and risk overfitting to the prefix distribution.
- **Lexical pattern coverage**: Incomplete keyphrase lists cause false negatives; overly broad lists may introduce noise.
- **Power-scaling factor \( \beta \)**: Lower values emphasize exploration but risk learning from noisy rollouts; higher values reduce exploration and may underutilize guidance.
- **Update frequency of \( \pi_{\text{old}} \)**: Frequent updates reduce distribution shift but add overhead; infrequent updates can cause large importance weight variance.

### Failure signatures
- Low occurrence of target reasoning patterns in rollouts (check \( \gamma \) values and HMM accuracy).
- Importance weight histogram dominated by extremely small (\( <10^{-6} \)) or large (\( >10^3 \)) values, indicating misaligned behavior policy.
- No improvement or performance drop compared to a no-guidance baseline across multiple \( \beta \) settings.
- Training instability (loss spikes, NaNs) possibly due to extreme importance weights or \( \gamma \) producing near‑zero probabilities.

### First 3 experiments
1. **HMM validation**: Generate many trajectories from \( \mu_\alpha \) and measure the fraction that satisfy \( \alpha \) (by keyphrase match). Compare to \( \pi_{\text{old}} \) rollouts; expect a significant increase.
2. **Ablation on \( \beta \)**: Train with \( \beta \in \{0, 0.2, 1.0\} \) on a small dataset. Plot accuracy and pattern usage over steps; \( \beta=0.2 \) should give the best trade‑off.
3. **Importance weight distribution**: Collect logs of per‑token weights and plot the histogram of \( \log w \). A healthy spread indicates effective guidance; overly concentrated (near 0 or 1) distribution suggests too weak or too strong guidance.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
1. *How does the choice and coverage of lexical keyphrases impact generalizability to reasoning structures not explicitly listed?* The paper uses a fixed set from prior work; it is unclear whether other important patterns are missed or whether the current set is sufficient across domains. Systematic ablation studies varying keyphrase sets would help resolve this.
2. *What theoretical principles determine the optimal power‑scaling factor \( \beta \), and can it be adaptively tuned?* The choice \( \beta=0.2 \) is empirical. Deriving bias‑variance trade‑off bounds and testing adaptive schemes based on trajectory statistics could improve robustness.
3. *Does task‑specific HMM distillation limit applicability to out‑of‑distribution problems?* The HMM is overfitted to the RL training distribution. Testing on held‑out problem distributions and comparing to generalized HMMs would clarify transferability.
4. *Can Ctrl-R guide reasoning structures lacking clear lexical manifestations?* The method relies heavily on lexical patterns; applying it to domains like program synthesis or proof verification where reasoning is more symbolic would require alternative constraint representations.

## Limitations
- Major uncertainties remain in **HMM fidelity** (the exact number of hidden states, training optimizer, and iterations are unreported).
- **DFA pattern coverage** is incomplete: the precise regex/keyphrase definitions beyond Table 1 are not provided, limiting reproducibility and potentially missing relevant patterns.
- The **sampling distribution over reasoning structures \( \alpha \)** during rollout is unspecified, which may affect the diversity and balance of guided patterns.

## Confidence
- **Tractability of guidance (Mechanism 1)**: Medium – algorithmic description is present but lacks external validation; corpus evidence weak.
- **Exact importance sampling (Mechanism 2)**: High – mathematically sound and explicitly implemented; depends on correct normalization.
- **Power‑scaling selective learning (Mechanism 3)**: Medium – empirical support in Section 6, but optimal \( \beta \) may be task‑specific and sensitive to HMM mis‑specification.

## Next Checks
1. **HMM‑\( \pi_{\text{old}} \) alignment**: Generate 10 k prefixes from \( \pi_{\text{old}} \). For each prefix, compare the HMM’s next‑token distribution to \( \pi_{\text{old}} \) using KL divergence. Target median KL < 0.1 nats; higher indicates miscalibrated guidance.
2. **DFA pattern audit**: Build the DFA from the paper’s keyphrase list. Annotate 500 human‑verified reasoning traces. Compute recall of target patterns (e.g., backtracking) by the DFA. Target > 90% recall; lower suggests incomplete pattern coverage.
3. **\( \beta \)-ablation on proxy task**: Train Ctrl‑R on a small subset (e.g., 500 problems) with \( \beta \in \{0, 0.2, 1.0\} \). Measure both frequency of guided patterns in rollouts and final accuracy. Expected result: \( \beta=0.2 \) maximizes pattern usage *and* accuracy; if \( \beta=0 \) or \( \beta=1 \) dominates, guidance or IS correction is flawed.
