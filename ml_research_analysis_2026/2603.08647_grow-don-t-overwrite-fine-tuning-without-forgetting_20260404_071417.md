# Grow, Don't Overwrite: Fine-tuning Without Forgetting

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2603.08647  
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.08647  
- **Reference count:** 40  
- **Primary result:** Matches standard fine-tuning on downstream tasks while nearly eliminating catastrophic forgetting on original tasks, using only ~60% of original model parameters trained.  

## Executive Summary
Catastrophic forgetting—where fine-tuning a pre-trained model on a new task degrades its original capabilities—poses a major challenge for adapting large language models. This paper introduces a function-preserving expansion method that grows the hidden dimension of MLP submodules by duplicating up-projection weights and scaling down-projection weights by 1/2, ensuring the expanded model is mathematically identical to the original at initialization. By freezing all original parameters and training only the newly added ones, the method allows the model to acquire new skills without overwriting prior knowledge. Experiments show it achieves comparable downstream task performance (e.g., translation, QA) while almost perfectly preserving baseline abilities (e.g., WinoGrande), with significant parameter efficiency.  

## Method Summary
The core technique expands each MLP’s hidden dimension by a factor *k* (default 2) via horizontal concatenation of the up-projection matrix `W^{(1)}` (*k* copies) and vertical stacking of the down-projection matrix `W^{(2)}` scaled by `1/*k*`. This guarantees functional equivalence at initialization: `ReLU(X[W^{(1)}…W^{(1)}]) * [1/k·W^{(2)}; …; 1/k·W^{(2)}] = ReLU(XW^{(1)})W^{(2)}`. Two training variants are proposed: **G-Freeze** (train only the new weights in both projections) and **G-Train** (train the entire expanded up-projection, freeze down-projection and other originals). For further efficiency, only a subset of layers (e.g., 9–10 out of ~18) is expanded, selected by ranking layers according to the magnitude of weight updates in a preliminary standard fine-tuning run.  

## Key Results
- Downstream task performance matches standard fine-tuning (e.g., BLEU for translation, accuracy for QA).  
- Original task capabilities nearly perfectly preserved: WinoGrande scores remain stable; function vector cosine similarity reaches 0.95 vs. 0.28 for standard fine-tuning; 5 of 10 causal attention heads retained.  
- Parameter efficient: expanding all layers trains ~60% of original model’s parameters; expanding only 9–10 layers reduces this to ~30%.  

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1: Function-Preserving Initialization
- **Claim:** The expanded model produces identical outputs to the original at initialization, preventing immediate representational drift.  
- **Mechanism:** By horizontally concatenating `W^{(1)}` *k* times and vertically stacking `W^{(2)}` scaled by `1/*k*`, the composition simplifies exactly to the original linear transformation due to distributivity.  
- **Core assumption:** ReLU activation output *Y* is identical before and after expansion; scaling in down-projection perfectly counteracts the wider internal representation `[Y … Y]`.  
- **Evidence anchors:** Abstract guarantees mathematical identity; Section 3 provides formal proof; neighboring papers discuss forgetting but not this specific expansion.  
- **Break condition:** Incorrect scaling factor or imprecise duplication breaks output equality at initialization.  

### Mechanism 2: Freezing Original Parameters
- **Claim:** Freezing all original parameters isolates new learning to the added capacity, preventing overwriting of pre-trained knowledge.  
- **Mechanism:** Training algorithm (`G-Freeze`) computes gradients and updates only newly introduced weights (`\hat{W}^{(1)}`, `\hat{W}^{(2)}`); original parameters remain fixed.  
- **Core assumption:** Catastrophic forgetting is primarily caused by gradient-based overwriting; immutable parameters preserve their contribution to the forward pass.  
- **Evidence anchors:** Abstract states freezing; Section 3 defines strategy; Section 4.1 shows near-perfect retention while matching SFT on new tasks.  
- **Break condition:** Implementation error allowing gradient flow to original parameters; insufficient capacity in expanded subnetwork for new task.  

### Mechanism 3: Preserving Latent Functional Circuits
- **Claim:** The method preserves latent functional circuits (e.g., “function vectors”) underlying baseline capabilities.  
- **Mechanism:** Growing *inside* existing MLPs and preserving down-projection’s role maintains internal activation patterns for original tasks; expanded pathways for new learning are additive and non-disruptive.  
- **Core assumption:** Forgetting manifests as divergence in internal representations; high cosine similarity of function vectors (summed activations of key causal attention heads) indicates intact circuits.  
- **Evidence anchors:** Abstract reports FV cosine similarity 0.95 vs. 0.28; Section 4.5 details retention of 5 causal heads; Section B describes FV computation.  
- **Break condition:** Identified causal heads and FV may not fully capture all pre-trained knowledge; forgetting could occur for unmeasured skills.  

## Foundational Learning
- **Linear Algebra for Function-Preserving Transformations**  
  *Why needed:* The proof of output invariance relies on matrix concatenation and scalar multiplication properties. Dimensional correctness is critical: `(s×h) * (h×2p) → (s×2p)` then `(s×2p) * (2p×h) → (s×h)`.  
  *Quick check:* If `Y = ReLU(XW^{(1)})`, show mathematically why `Y W^{(2)} = [Y Y] * [0.5·W^{(2)}; 0.5·W^{(2)}]`.  

- **Catastrophic Forgetting in Gradient-Based Learning**  
  *Why needed:* Freezing parameters assumes overwriting is the primary cause. Understanding this phenomenon justifies the parameter isolation strategy.  
  *Quick check:* In standard fine-tuning, why does minimizing loss on new data `D_T` cause performance on pre-training data `D_{pt}` to degrade?  

- **Transformer MLP Submodule Architecture**  
  *Why needed:* The method modifies only MLP up/down projections; knowing data flow `X → W^{(1)} → ReLU → W^{(2)} → output` is essential for correct implementation and efficiency analysis.  
  *Quick check:* What are the input and output dimensions of the MLP’s up-projection and down-projection layers in a standard Transformer?  

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map:**  
  Original: `W^{(1)} (h×p) → ReLU → W^{(2)} (p×h)`  
  Expanded: `\hat{W}^{(1)} ([W^{(1)}|W^{(1)}], h×2p) → ReLU → \hat{W}^{(2)} ([0.5·W^{(2)};0.5·W^{(2)}], 2p×h)`  
  Frozen: All original parameters (`W^{(1)}`, `W^{(2)}`, attention, LayerNorm, embeddings).  
  Trainable: New columns in `\hat{W}^{(1)}` and new rows in `\hat{W}^{(2)}` (G-Freeze); entire `\hat{W}^{(1)}` (G-Train).  

- **Critical path:**  
  1. Input `X` (batch×seq_len×h) enters MLP.  
  2. Up-projection: `X @ \hat{W}^{(1)}` yields `(…,2p)` tensor; first `p` columns = `X@W^{(1)}`, second `p` columns identical.  
  3. ReLU applies element-wise; both copies receive identical activations.  
  4. Down-projection: `(ReLU(...)) @ \hat{W^{(2)}}`; each `p`-wide block multiplies by `0.5·W^{(2)}`, summing to original `Y@W^{(2)}`.  
  5. Invariant: At initialization, expanded MLP output is bitwise identical to original MLP output.  

- **Design tradeoffs:**  
  - *Expansion factor k:* Paper uses *k*=2 as optimal; higher *k* adds capacity but increases trainable parameters (∝ `(k-1)/k`).  
  - *Variant selection:* G-Freeze maximizes retention; G-Train used for complex tasks (e.g., MathQA) for plasticity, risking minor forgetting.  
  - *Selective expansion:* Growing only 9–10 layers reduces trainable params from ~60% to ~30% with minimal loss; heuristic ranks layers by magnitude of weight changes in preliminary SFT.  

- **Failure signatures:**  
  1. **Immediate output mismatch:** Expanded model’s logits differ from original’s on validation set before training → bug in duplication/scaling.  
  2. **Degradation on proxy task:** WinoGrande accuracy drops significantly while downstream accuracy rises → insufficient expansion, inadvertent training of original params, or task conflict.  
  3. **Poor convergence on new task:** Downstream performance lags behind SFT → too few layers grown, suboptimal layer selection, or G-Freeze lacks capacity (try G-Train).  

- **First 3 experiments:**  
  1. **Function Preservation Unit Test:** Implement expansion for a single MLP, pass random input `X`, assert `output_original` and `output_expanded` numerically identical before training.  
  2. **Reproduce Figure 2:** Apply method to Gemma-1B on French translation (mtnt); plot downstream (BLEU) and proxy (WinoGrande) metrics for SFT vs. G-Freeze. Expect SFT to collapse proxy, G-Freeze to maintain it.  
  3. **Selective Layer Expansion Ablation:** Use layer-ranking heuristic to select top-N layers (N=1,3,5,7,10,15,all). Train each on a task (e.g., SciTail), plot downstream vs. proxy performance vs. N. Expect downstream performance to scale with N, proxy to remain stable for G-Freeze.  

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- **What is the optimal method for selecting which layers to expand for a given downstream task?**  
  *Basis:* Authors hypothesize performance could be optimized with more sophisticated skill localization techniques (Section 4.2).  
  *Why unresolved:* Current heuristic (ranking by weight update magnitude after SFT) is ad-hoc and may not generalize.  
  *Resolution evidence:* Develop and evaluate advanced skill localization (gradient-based saliency, activation clustering, task-probing) across diverse tasks and model sizes.  

- **Why does expanding MLP submodules outperform expanding attention mechanisms in preventing catastrophic forgetting?**  
  *Basis:* Section C.3 empirically shows MLP growth surpasses expanding attention head dimension or count, but no theoretical justification provided.  
  *Why unresolved:* Understanding superiority could inform future architecture design and reveal knowledge storage/update mechanisms in Transformers.  
  *Resolution evidence:* Mechanistic interpretability studies (causal mediation analysis, function vector probing) comparing roles of MLP and attention layers.  

- **Can the expansion method be synergistically combined with parameter-efficient fine-tuning (PEFT) techniques like LoRA?**  
  *Basis:* Authors note in Section 5 that method is orthogonal to PEFT and can be combined, but no experiments test this.  
  *Why unresolved:* PEFT methods may not address forgetting; combination could yield benefits or conflicts.  
  *Resolution evidence:* Implement hybrid models with expansion + PEFT (e.g., LoRA on expanded MLPs), evaluate on benchmarks for downstream performance and retention.  

- **How effective and scalable is the method in continual learning settings with sequential fine-tuning on multiple tasks?**  
  *Basis:* All experiments involve single-task fine-tuning; multi-task or lifelong learning scenarios unaddressed.  
  *Why unresolved:* Continual learning exacerbates forgetting; per-task expansion might cause parameter bloat, interference, or degradation over many tasks.  
  *Resolution evidence:* Experiments with sequence of fine-tuning tasks (e.g., 5–10 diverse tasks), measure forgetting on all previous tasks after each new task, analyze parameter growth and stability.  

## Limitations
- Bias term handling in expanded MLPs (duplicate? scale?) unspecified; may break function-preserving property.  
- Selective expansion heuristic (layer ranking by weight update magnitude) lacks precise definition (metric, aggregation).  
- Exact training step counts per task not reported; requires preliminary SFT to determine convergence.  
- Evaluation of retention limited to WinoGrande; broader capability preservation not tested.  

## Confidence
- **Function-preserving expansion:** High (mathematical proof solid; bias handling introduces minor risk).  
- **Forgetting prevention via parameter freezing:** Medium (empirical support strong; causal claim may not generalize across tasks).  
- **Latent circuit preservation (FV similarity):** Medium (probe limited to one task; FV may not capture all pre-trained knowledge).
