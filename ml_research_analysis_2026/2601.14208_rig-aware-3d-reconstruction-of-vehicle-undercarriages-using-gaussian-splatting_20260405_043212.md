# Rig-Aware 3D Reconstruction of Vehicle Undercarriages using Gaussian Splatting

## Quick Facts
*   **arXiv ID:** 2601.14208
*   **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.14208
*   **Reference count:** 20
*   **Primary result:** An end-to-end pipeline using a synchronized three-camera rig and rig-aware Structure-from-Motion (SfM) to seed 3D Gaussian splatting produces interactive, photorealistic 3D models of vehicle undercarriages from drive-over video, achieving high-quality reconstruction (PSNR >30 dB) in minutes and real-time rendering.

## Executive Summary
Labor-intensive manual vehicle undercarriage inspection is automated by developing a complete reconstruction pipeline from a drive-over video captured by a fixed three-camera rig. The core contribution is a rig-aware SfM system that overcomes the challenges of wide-angle distortion, low parallax, and precise multi-view correspondence. This is achieved through one-time full camera calibration, sub-frame video synchronization, and a constrained matching strategy that leverages a learned feature matcher (DISK+LightGlue) while enforcing known rig geometry during bundle adjustment. The resulting high-quality sparse point cloud and camera poses initialize 3D Gaussian splatting, enabling fast, photorealistic novel-view synthesis for inspection.

## Method Summary
The pipeline begins with a one-time calibration using a ChArUco board to fit a full 8-parameter OpenCV distortion model for each wide-angle lens. Raw video streams are synchronized by estimating and correcting per-frame vertical shifts via phase correlation and aligning their motion profiles. From synchronized videos, sharp frame triplets are selected, undistorted, and processed. Features are extracted with DISK, and matches are computed only under a constrained policy (temporal window ±5, only left-center and center-right pairs) using LightGlue, with geometric verification. Incremental SfM is performed in GLOMAP, augmented by a bundle adjustment regularization term that tightly enforces the known, fixed extrinsics between the three cameras (e.g., ±0.31m lateral offset from center). Finally, 3D Gaussian splatting is initialized from the sparse point cloud (Gaussian means = point positions) and optimized to minimize photometric loss against the input images.

## Key Results
*   The rig-aware SfM produces a dense sparse point cloud (427,299 points) with very low reprojection error (0.49 pixels).
*   The seeded 3D Gaussian splatting achieves high fidelity (PSNR 30.66 dB, SSIM 0.92, LPIPS 0.19) and converges quickly (~8-10 minutes).
*   The final model renders in real-time at over 130 FPS, enabling interactive inspection.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1
*   **Claim:** Precise camera calibration corrects severe wide-angle distortion, providing a geometrically accurate foundation for SfM.
*   **Mechanism:** A one-time calibration uses a ChArUco board to fit an eight-parameter Full OpenCV camera model (including higher-order radial and tangential terms). This model is applied to undistort all subsequent frames before feature extraction, ensuring that detected features lie in a rectified, linear imaging space. Accurate intrinsics minimize reprojection error and prevent bundle adjustment drift.
*   **Core assumption:** The distortion model is stable and generalizes from the calibration board to the undercarriage scene.
*   **Evidence anchors:**
    *   [abstract]: "integrating precise camera calibration... to overcome the challenges of wide-angle lens distortion"
    *   [section III-A]: Details the ChArUco-based calibration process, frame curation via Laplacian variance, and optimization of the Full OpenCV model.
    *   [corpus]: Neighboring papers (e.g., "Drive-Through 3D Vehicle Exterior Reconstruction") also identify distortion as a primary challenge, supporting the mechanism's relevance.
*   **Break condition:** Calibration fails if the ChArUco board is not swept through a sufficiently diverse range of poses (roll, pitch, yaw, distance), leading to an under-constrained model and residual distortion in the point cloud.

### Mechanism 2
*   **Claim:** Sub-frame synchronization via global motion alignment ensures spatial-temporal correspondence across the three camera streams, which is critical for coherent multi-view feature matching.
*   **Mechanism:** Vertical frame-to-frame shifts are estimated using phase correlation on preprocessed (blurred, CLAHE, Laplacian) frames. The center camera is a temporal reference, and offsets for left/right cameras are found by minimizing L1 loss between their shift sequences. This aligns the videos so that features from a single physical point on the undercarriage appear at matching times in all three views.
*   **Core assumption:** The dominant motion in the drive-over sequence is vertical (along the vehicle's length), and its profile is consistent across all three cameras after accounting for temporal offset.
*   **Evidence anchors:**
    *   [abstract]: "synchronized video streams" as part of overcoming low-parallax scenes.
    *   [section III-B]: Describes the two-stage alignment (phase correlation for shift estimation, L1 loss for offset search) and shows the reduction in vertical motion difference from ~774 to ~22 pixels.
    *   [corpus]: The neighbor "Drive-Through 3D Vehicle Exterior Reconstruction" also highlights temporal synchronization as a key challenge in dynamic drive-through scenes.
*   **Break condition:** The motion profile is not predominantly vertical (e.g., significant lateral vehicle sway) or the cameras have strongly differing perspectives, making the shift signals non-alignable via a single scalar offset.

### Mechanism 3
*   **Claim:** Rig-aware bundle adjustment with constrained matching and learned features (DISK+LightGlue) generates a high-quality sparse point cloud from a low-parallax, wide-angle setup.
*   **Mechanism:**
    1.  **Constrained Matching:** Only image pairs within a tight temporal window (i=±5) and specific cross-camera pairs (L↔C, C↔R) are considered, avoiding the extreme baseline between L and R.
    2.  **Learned Matcher:** DISK provides robust keypoints in challenging lighting/texture; LightGlue uses attention to find accurate matches within the constrained pairs, producing few outliers.
    3.  **Rig-Aware BA:** The known fixed extrinsics between cameras (e.g., tL-C = [-0.31,0,0]^T m) are added as a regularization term in the bundle adjustment objective, penalizing deviations from the rig geometry and preventing inter-camera drift.
*   **Core assumption:** The physical rig geometry is rigid and precisely known, and the constrained matching strategy does not exclude necessary correspondences for a coherent reconstruction.
*   **Evidence anchors:**
    *   [abstract]: "constrained matching strategy with learned components... and strong geometric priors from the camera rig"
    *   [section III-C]: Explicitly defines the constrained pair classes, the DISK/LightGlue workflow, and the rig-based pose prior added to the BA cost function.
    *   [Table I]: Ablations show that removing custom matching or pose priors leads to degenerate point clouds (e.g., - Custom Matching*: 0 points).
*   **Break condition:** The true inter-camera baselines differ significantly from the prior values (e.g., due to rig flexing), or the temporal window is too narrow to find matches for very slow vehicle speeds, leading to insufficient triangulation.

## Foundational Learning
*   **Concept: Photogrammetry & SfM Fundamentals (Triangulation, Bundle Adjustment)**
    *   *Why needed here:* The entire pipeline seeds Gaussian splatting with a sparse point cloud and camera poses from SfM. Understanding how point-cloud density, reprojection error, and drift arise from matching and BA is key to diagnosing failures.
    *   *Quick check question:* Why does low parallax specifically degrade the condition number of the triangulation problem, leading to unstable 3D point positions?
*   **Concept: Camera Model & Distortion (Radial/Tangential Parameters)**
    *   *Why needed here:* The 160° FOV lenses introduce extreme non-linear distortion. The pipeline's success hinges on accurately modeling and removing this distortion before feature matching. Misunderstanding distortion models leads to incorrect rectification.
    *   *Quick check question:* How does uncorrected radial distortion cause straight lines (e.g., chassis rails) to appear curved in the point cloud, and why does this confuse matchers?
*   **Concept: 3D Gaussian Splatting Dependency on SfM Initialization**
    *   *Why needed here:* 3D-GS uses the SfM point cloud to initialize Gaussian means and opacities. A degenerate or sparse initialization (from failed SfM) leads to "floaters," missing geometry, or slow/no convergence, regardless of the GS optimizer.
    *   *Quick check question:* If the SfM point cloud has correct camera poses but is 10x sparser than ideal, what artifacts would you expect in the final GS render, and why?

## Architecture Onboarding
*   **Component map:** Calibration -> Synchronization -> SfM -> Gaussian Splatting
*   **Critical path:** Calibration → Synchronization → SfM → Gaussian Splatting. A failure in any upstream module (e.g., bad calibration) propagates downstream, causing GS failure.
*   **Design tradeoffs:**
    *   **Matching:** Constrained window size (W=±5) trades recall (missing matches for very slow motion) for precision (fewer outliers).
    *   **Rig priors:** Strong regularization prevents drift but assumes perfect rig knowledge; a small error in baseline distance could bias the entire reconstruction.
    *   **Speed vs. Quality:** Using u8192 DISK features and LightGlue is computationally heavier than SIFT but yields denser, more accurate matches critical for low-parallax scenes.
*   **Failure signatures:**
    *   **Calibration failure:** Undistorted images still show bent lines; high RMS error (>2px) on ChArUco reprojection.
    *   **Sync failure:** Visible "ghosting" or double edges in GS renders when the vehicle moves; L1 loss curve has no clear minimum.
    *   **SfM failure:** Sparse point count <100k for a full undercarriage; highly elongated covariance in point cloud along vehicle length; Table I metrics collapse (e.g., Reprojection Error >1px, Sparse Points near zero).
    *   **GS failure:** Hazy renders, "floater" artifacts detached from the main model, low PSNR (<25 dB), training does not converge (loss plateaus at high value).
*   **First 3 experiments:**
    1.  **Validate Calibration Independently:** Run the calibration module on a held-out ChArUco video. Check RMS reprojection error (<1px) and visually inspect 5+ undistorted images for straight lines.
    2.  **Test Synchronization on Synthetic Offset:** Apply a known integer frame offset (e.g., +10 frames) to one video stream. Run the sync module and verify it recovers the exact offset. Then test with sub-frame offsets via interpolation.
    3.  **Run SfM with/without Rig Priors:** On a single vehicle, execute the full pipeline. Then, rerun the BA stage with the `- Pose Priors*` ablation (remove rig regularization term). Compare the resulting point cloud's spatial coherence (visual) and reprojection error. The rig-aware version should show a tighter, more planar undercarriage structure.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
### Open Question 1
*   **Question:** Can the rig-aware SfM pipeline be directly applied to other industrial inspection scenarios with different camera rig configurations (e.g., varying baselines, number of cameras) without retraining or significant parameter adjustments?
*   **Basis in paper:** [inferred] The conclusion suggests potential applications in other industrial inspection settings, but the method is only evaluated on vehicle undercarriages using a fixed three-camera rig with specific geometry.
*   **Why unresolved:** The pipeline’s design—especially the constrained matching strategy and rig-aware bundle adjustment—is tightly coupled with the specific rig parameters (62 cm baseline, 12–30 cm height to undercarriage). Generalization to other geometries (e.g., larger baselines for ship hulls or different motion patterns) has not been tested.
*   **What evidence would resolve it:** A study on 2–3 additional industrial datasets with diverse rig setups, measuring reconstruction quality (point cloud density, reprojection error, PSNR) after only adjusting the rig pose priors in bundle adjustment, to determine required modifications.

### Open Question 2
*   **Question:** How tolerant is the final reconstruction to inaccuracies in the known camera rig geometry (e.g., baseline error, orientation drift) used as priors during bundle adjustment?
*   **Basis in paper:** [explicit] The rig-aware bundle adjustment explicitly enforces relative camera poses from the rig schematics (e.g., tL-C = [−0.31,0,0]^T) to prevent drift, but no robustness analysis is provided.
*   **Why unresolved:** Real-world rigs may experience mechanical shifts or calibration drift; the paper assumes perfect priors, but the sensitivity of the sparse point cloud and Gaussian splatting quality to small perturbations (e.g., ±1 cm baseline) is unknown.
*   **What evidence would resolve it:** Inject controlled synthetic errors into the rig priors (e.g., varying baseline by ±0.5–2 cm, rotating one camera by ±0.5°) and measure changes in SfM metrics (reprojection error, point cloud completeness) and final Gaussian Splatting metrics (PSNR, SSIM) to establish error budgets.

### Open Question 3
*   **Question:** What is the maximum sub-frame synchronization error between camera streams that the constrained matching strategy can tolerate before degrading the sparse point cloud quality?
*   **Basis in paper:** [explicit] The synchronization method aligns videos with up to ±35 frame offsets and uses a 5-frame temporal window (W_5(i)) for constrained matching, but no correlation between residual sync error and matching success is quantified.
*   **Why unresolved:** The heuristic window size (5 frames) and reliance on precise sync imply vulnerability to residual misalignment; the threshold at which matches become erroneous or insufficient for robust triangulation is not established.
*   **What evidence would resolve it:** Systematically introduce known post-sync offsets (e.g., 0.1, 0.5, 1.0 frames) into already synchronized videos, then compute match inlier rates, trianguable point count, and reprojection error to identify a critical degradation point.

### Open Question 4
*   **Question:** Would alternative learned feature extractors and matchers (e.g., SuperPoint + SuperGlue, or LoFTR) improve sparse point cloud quality in this low-parallax, wide-angle regime compared to DISK + LightGlue?
*   **Basis in paper:** [explicit] The pipeline uses DISK and LightGlue, with an ablation showing that replacing them with SIFT degrades results. However, other modern learned features are not compared.
*   **Why unresolved:** The paper attributes success to DISK+LightGlue but does not benchmark against other state-of-the-art pairs that may better handle extreme distortion or repetitive textures common in undercarriages. The constrained matching strategy’s interaction with different features is unexplored.
*   **What evidence would resolve it:** Integrate other feature-matcher pairs into the same pipeline (keeping calibration, sync, and BA identical) and compare sparse point cloud metrics (point count, track length, reprojection error) and downstream Gaussian splatting performance to determine if DISK+LightGlue is optimal or if alternatives excel in specific metrics.

## Limitations
*   The synchronization method assumes vehicle motion is predominantly vertical; significant lateral sway or non-rigid motion could break the single-scalar offset model.
*   The rig-aware bundle adjustment assumes perfect prior knowledge of the rigid camera geometry; any flexing or error in the baseline measurement would bias the reconstruction.
*   The pipeline's robustness to varied vehicle types, undercarriage textures (e.g., mud), lighting conditions, and extreme speeds is not evaluated, as testing is on a specific dataset.

## Confidence
*   **High Confidence:** The calibrated Full OpenCV model effectively corrects wide-angle distortion. This is a standard, well-validated photogrammetric process.
*   **Medium Confidence:** The rig-aware synchronization and constrained matching strategy successfully produce a coherent, high-quality sparse point cloud *under the stated assumptions* (vertical motion, correct rig priors). The ablations in Table I support this for their test data.
*   **Low Confidence:** The pipeline is generally robust to variations in vehicle type, undercarriage texture, or lighting conditions. The paper's evaluation is on a specific dataset; boundary cases are not explored.

## Next Checks
1.  **Sync Robustness Test:** Apply controlled synthetic temporal offsets (integer and sub-frame) and synthetic lateral sway (via frame shearing) to a synchronized test sequence. Measure if the sync module recovers the correct offset under sway and quantify the resulting SfM point cloud degradation.
2.  **Rig Prior Sensitivity Analysis:** Intentionally perturb the known rig baseline (e.g., ±2cm, ±5cm) in the BA regularization term. Reconstruct the same vehicle and measure the change in final SfM reprojection error, sparse point cloud structure, and final Gaussian Splatting PSNR/SSIM.
3.  **SfM Seed Failure Propagation:** Artificially degrade the SfM output (e.g., randomly subsample the point cloud by 90%, add Gaussian noise to camera poses). Feed these corrupted seeds into the Gaussian Splatting pipeline. Monitor training stability, final rendering metrics, and visualize artifacts to characterize the failure modes of 3D-GS when seeded with poor SfM data.
