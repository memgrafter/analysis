# DISCOVER: A Solver for Distributional Counterfactual Explanations
## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID**: 2603.16436  
- **Source URL**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.16436  
- **Reference count**: 40  
- **Primary result**: DISCOVER achieves the lowest output-side Wasserstein distance (OT(y)) in all non-differentiable settings and competitive input-side distances (OT(x)) across five tabular datasets.

## Executive Summary
DISCOVER is a solver for distributional counterfactual explanations (DCE) that extends the approach to black-box tabular models. It replaces gradient-based optimization with a sparse propose-and-select search, decomposing the global optimal transport objective to identify influential samples and constrain edits to a top-k set. Candidate counterfactual distributions are generated using modular optimizers guided by an input-side OT gradient cone, without requiring model gradients. Evaluated on multiple datasets and predictors—including non-differentiable models—DISCOVER demonstrates robust dual alignment, lowering output-side transport cost consistently while maintaining competitive input-side costs. Ablations validate the contributions of its sparsity mechanism and geometric guidance.

## Method Summary
DISCOVER solves the DCE problem by iteratively improving a factual input distribution \(X'\) to satisfy certified chance constraints on input and output Wasserstein distances. It first decomposes the global transport objective into per-sample contributions to rank rows by impact and selects a sparse top-k set for editing. For these rows, it generates \(M\) candidate distributions via a propose-and-select loop: an OT-guided cone sampler perturbs features along directions derived from the input-side transport gradient, ensuring edits remain plausible. Each candidate is evaluated on the combined objective \(Q\) (weighted input/output OT costs) and the best is accepted, with monotonic improvement guaranteed per iteration. The process repeats, updating the trade-off parameter \(\eta\) until convergence or certification.

## Key Results
- DISCOVER attains the lowest output-side Wasserstein distance (OT(y)) across all non-differentiable model settings (e.g., Random Forest, XGBoost).
- It achieves competitive input-side distances (OT(x)) and overall objective \(Q\) values, often matching or outperforming gradient-based DCE baselines on differentiable models.
- Ablations confirm that the top-k sparsity mechanism improves stability and that OT-guided cone sampling accelerates convergence compared to random proposals.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
DISCOVER’s effectiveness stems from three interconnected mechanisms.  
First, it exploits a per-sample decomposition of the global transport objective to compute impact scores, concentrating edits on a small set of influential individuals. This sparse intervention reduces the effective search space and targets changes where they most improve the objective.  
Second, it replaces gradient descent with a derivative-free propose-and-select paradigm. Candidate distributions are generated by modular optimizers (e.g., Monte Carlo, Genetic) that edit only the selected rows. Each candidate is evaluated using the certified objective, including feasibility checks via upper confidence limits, guaranteeing non-increasing \(Q\) per step.  
Third, guidance for proposals comes from an input-side OT gradient \(\nabla_X SW_2(X, X')\), computed without querying the predictor. A cone-sampling primitive perturbs features within a half-angle \(\phi\) around the negative gradient, biasing proposals toward low-distortion regions while preserving plausibility. This geometric prior stabilizes optimization for black-box models.

## Foundational Learning
- **Optimal Transport (OT)**: Needed to define the DCE objectives \(Q_x\) and \(Q_y\) as empirical OT costs, with transport plans coupling samples between distributions.  
  *Quick check*: How does the optimal transport plan \(\mu^{(k)}_{ij}\) assign mass between factual and counterfactual samples under a projection \(\theta_k\)?
- **Sliced Wasserstein Distance**: Approximates high-dimensional input distribution discrepancy by averaging 1D Wasserstein distances over random projections.  
  *Quick check*: Why does increasing the number of projections \(N\) improve the \(SW_2\) approximation, and how does this affect computational complexity?
- **Chance Constraints and Upper Confidence Limits (UCL)**: Provide finite-sample guarantees that the counterfactual distribution meets distance thresholds with probability \(1-\alpha/2\).  
  *Quick check*: What is the effect of the trimming constant \(\delta\) on the tightness of the UCLs?

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component map**: Impact Scorer -> Top-k Selector -> OT Guidance Calculator -> Proposal Generator (modular optimizer + cone sampler) -> Objective Evaluator -> Selection Loop.  
**Critical path**:  
1. Initialize \(X \gets X'\).  
2. Compute \(Q\), \(SW_2\), \(W_2\), UCLs, update \(\eta\).  
3. Compute per-row \(q_i\); select top-\(k\) set \(I\).  
4. Compute guidance \(g = \nabla_X SW_2(X, X')\).  
5. Generate \(M\) candidates by editing rows in \(I\) via cone sampler.  
6. Evaluate \(Q\) on all candidates + no-op; choose best as new \(X\).  
7. Repeat steps 2–6 for \(T\) iterations; final certification check.  
**Design tradeoffs**:  
- \(k\) (top-\(k\)): Smaller \(k\) improves stability but may require more iterations; larger \(k\) accelerates alignment but increases variance.  
- \(\phi\) (cone half-angle): Narrower \(\phi\) enforces transport-consistent moves; wider \(\phi\) boosts exploration but risks distortion.  
- \(M\) (candidates): Larger \(M\) improves proposal coverage at linear cost; \(M=1\) reverts to greedy descent.  
- Optimizer choice: Monte Carlo is simple and fast; Genetic adds recombination for diversity but with overhead.  
**Failure signatures**:  
- Certification failure (final \(SW_2\) or \(W_2\) exceeds UCLs) due to overly aggressive \(k\) or poor guidance tuning.  
- Unstable trajectories (oscillating OT distances) often from large \(\phi\) or insufficient \(M\).  
- Stagnation (early \(Q\) plateau) may indicate static top-\(k\) set or exhausted guidance.  
- Constraint violations in categorical edits if decoding step ignores actionability.  
**First 3 experiments**:  
1. **Main evaluation**: DISCOVER vs. AReS, GLOBE, DiCE, DCE on five datasets; DISCOVER achieves lowest \(OT(y)\) in all non-differentiable settings.  
2. **Ablation of top-\(k\) budget**: Setting \(k = n\) (edit all samples) increases instability and worsens final \(OT(x)\), validating sparsity.  
3. **Ablation of OT-guided cone sampling**: Replacing cone sampler with uniform random proposals while keeping other components fixed results in slower, noisier convergence (higher variance in \(OT(x)\)).

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
1. **Scalability to large datasets**: How does DISCOVER perform on tabular data with >100k samples? The paper only tests datasets with <10k samples; large-scale empirical validation is missing.  
2. **Convergence guarantees**: Does the propose-and-select scheme provably converge to a feasible solution of the original DCE problem? Only monotonicity per iteration is proven; overall convergence remains open.  
3. **Hyperparameter sensitivity**: How sensitive is DISCOVER to hyperparameters (\(\phi\), \(\lambda_{\text{max}}\), \(r\), \(\tau\)) and can they be set automatically? No systematic study is provided; manual tuning is required.

## Limitations
- Exact hyperparameters (e.g., \(k\), \(M\), cone angle \(\phi\)) are not detailed in the paper text, hindering exact replication.  
- Reproducibility depends on code availability; if unreleased, independent reimplementation is non-trivial due to complex OT decompositions and cone sampling.  
- The core assumption that a small subset of samples drives global OT may fail for certain data distributions, limiting the top-\(k\) approach.  
- Input-side gradient guidance can become uninformative near convergence (flat gradients), potentially leading to random walks and slow final refinement.

## Confidence
- **Optimal transport minimization**: High – DISCOVER consistently achieves the lowest \(OT(y)\) across all non-differentiable settings in reported experiments.  
- **Sparse top-\(k\) mechanism**: Medium – Ablations show improved stability, but sensitivity to \(k\) and risk of excluding critical samples are not fully explored.  
- **OT-guided cone proposals**: Medium – Ablation confirms benefit over random proposals; performance likely depends on proper tuning of \(\phi\) and may degrade in flat-gradient regions.
