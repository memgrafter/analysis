# Functional Central Limit Theorem for Stochastic Gradient Descent
## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2602.15538
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.15538
- Reference count: 40
- Primary result: Proves a functional central limit theorem for SGD on convex objectives, showing that the rescaled trajectory converges to a diffusion process; the asymptotic variance of the last iterate is strictly larger than that of the empirical risk minimizer.

## Executive Summary
The paper establishes a functional central limit theorem (FCLT) for stochastic gradient descent (SGD) minimizing a convex objective Φ(θ) = E[ϕ(X,θ)]. Under a step-size schedule t_n = δ/n with δ > 1/λ_min (where λ_min is the smallest eigenvalue of the Hessian ∇²Φ(θ*)), and assuming a unique minimizer θ*, local strong convexity, bounded gradient noise, and continuity of the noise covariance at θ*, the rescaled process Y^n_t = √n(θ_{⌊nt⌋} - θ*) converges in distribution in the Skorokhod space C_0((0,∞)) to a diffusion Y satisfying

dY_t = t^{-1}(I - δ∇²Φ(θ*)) Y_t dt + δΓ^{1/2} dB_t,

where Γ is the covariance of the gradient noise at θ*. This functional limit captures the entire fluctuating trajectory around θ* and holds even for non-smooth global objectives (e.g., geometric median), unlike classical CLTs that rely on global smoothness. As a corollary, √n(θ_n - θ*) converges in distribution to N(0, Σ) with

Σ = δ ∫_0^∞ e^{t/δ} e^{-t∇²Φ(θ*)} Γ e^{-t∇²Φ(θ*)} dt.

Moreover, the asymptotic variance Σ is strictly larger than that of the empirical risk minimizer (ERM), which attains the Cramér-Rao bound Δ = ∇²Φ(θ*)^{-1} Γ ∇²Φ(θ*)^{-1}. The paper’s analysis combines local quadratic approximations, martingale techniques, and functional CLT criteria for Markov processes, providing a rigorous foundation for understanding SGD’s long-term stochastic behavior.

## Method Summary
The method treats SGD as a stochastic approximation of the Robbins–Monro type. Near the unique minimizer θ*, the objective is locally strongly convex, allowing a second-order expansion: G(θ) ≈ ∇²Φ(θ*)(θ - θ*). The update becomes θ_n - θ* ≈ (I - t_n ∇²Φ(θ*))(θ_{n-1} - θ*) + t_n ε_n, where ε_n is a mean-zero martingale difference with covariance Γ at θ*. With t_n = δ/n, the authors define the continuous-time rescaling Y^n_t = √n(θ_{⌊nt⌋} - θ*) and interpolate linearly to lie in C_0((0,∞)). They prove tightness of {Y^n} using quadratic growth and martingale bounds. Then they verify that the conditional drift and diffusion coefficients converge uniformly on compacts to those of the candidate diffusion Y, whose generator is G_t f(y) = t^{-1} y^⊤ (I - δ∇²Φ(θ*))∇f(y) + (δ²/2) Tr(Γ ∇²f(y)). By a functional CLT for Markov chains (e.g., Stroock–Varadhan), Y^n ⇒ Y. The asymptotic variance Σ is obtained by solving the Lyapunov equation for the stationary covariance of the diffusion. The overall approach is non-parametric regarding the global shape of Φ, relying only on local properties, which extends applicability to non-smooth problems like the geometric median.

## Key Results
- **Theorem 1** (FCLT): For t_n = δ/n with δ > 1/λ_min(∇²Φ(θ*)), the rescaled trajectory Y^n_t converges in distribution in C_0((0,∞)) to the diffusion Y solving dY_t = t^{-1}(I - δ∇²Φ(θ*)) Y_t dt + δΓ^{1/2} dB_t.
- **Corollary 1** (Last-iterate CLT): √n(θ_n - θ*) → N(0, Σ) with Σ = δ ∫_0^∞ e^{t/δ} e^{-t∇²Φ(θ*)} Γ e^{-t∇²Φ(θ*)} dt.
- **Proposition 3** (Variance gap): Σ - Δ is positive semidefinite, where Δ = ∇²Φ(θ*)^{-1} Γ ∇²Φ(θ*)^{-1} is the asymptotic variance of the empirical risk minimizer; thus SGD’s last iterate has strictly larger asymptotic variance.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The functional CLT succeeds due to three interconnected reasons: First, rescaling by √n and embedding the discrete updates into continuous time yields a non-trivial limiting process that retains temporal correlations; this avoids trivial concentration and provides a diffusion approximation. Second, the local quadratic approximation of Φ around θ* together with the martingale difference property of gradient noise linearizes the dynamics, producing a stochastic recurrence whose rescaling yields an SDE with explicit time-dependent drift and constant diffusion. Third, the condition δ > 1/λ_min ensures that the eigenvalues of I - δ∇²Φ(θ*) have negative real parts, making the drift confining and guaranteeing the existence of a stationary covariance Σ; this also explains why Σ strictly dominates Δ. These mechanisms rely fundamentally on: a unique minimizer, local strong convexity (positive definite Hessian), bounded second moments of the gradient noise, and continuity of the noise covariance at θ*. If any of these fail—e.g., if the Hessian is singular, the noise covariance is discontinuous, or the step-size is too small—the diffusion limit may break down or become degenerate.

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component map**: SGD iterator -> Step-size scheduler -> Noise model -> Rescaling layer -> Limiting diffusion generator

**Critical path**:
1. Verify local strong convexity at θ* and compute λ_min(∇²Φ(θ*)).
2. Ensure gradient noise variance is bounded (Assumption 4) and continuous near θ* (Assumptions 5–7).
3. Set δ > 1/λ_min; compute Γ = Cov(g(X,θ*)).
4. Check that ∑ t_n = ∞ and ∑ t_n² < ∞ (satisfied by t_n = δ/n).
5. Prove tightness of {√n(θ_n - θ*)} (Theorem 2) using quadratic growth and martingale concentration.
6. Show uniform convergence of conditional moments to identify the drift and diffusion coefficients (Proposition 2).
7. Apply the functional CLT for Markov chains (e.g., Stroock-Varadhan) to conclude Y^n ⇒ Y.

**Design tradeoffs**:
- Larger δ increases the asymptotic variance Σ but accelerates initial convergence; δ ≤ 1/λ_min leads to a non-confining drift and potential instability.
- Weaker noise moment assumptions (e.g., only bounded second moment) demand more sophisticated uniform integrability arguments (Proposition 2) and may complicate verification.
- The FCLT is asymptotic; it provides no finite-sample error bounds, so practical step-size selection must rely on heuristics or non-asymptotic theory.

**Failure signatures**:
- Failure of Assumption 3 (quadratic growth) may prevent almost sure convergence of θ_n to θ*, invalidating the local analysis.
- Singular Hessian (λ_min = 0) makes I - δ∇²Φ(θ*) have an eigenvalue at or above 1, causing the drift to be non-contracting and potentially leading to a degenerate or divergent limit.
- Discontinuity of the noise covariance Σ(θ) at θ* can break the uniform convergence needed for the diffusion coefficient, jeopardizing the functional limit.
- Step-size with δ ≤ 1/λ_min may cause tightness to fail, resulting in a different limiting process or non-convergence.

**First 3 experiments**:
1. **Step-size sweep**: For logistic regression, run SGD with t_n = δ/n across δ values spanning below and above 1/λ_min. Estimate Σ from the empirical covariance of √n(θ_n - θ*) and compare to the theoretical Σ. Observe Σ increasing with δ and diverging as δ approaches 1/λ_min from above.
2. **Non-smooth test**: Apply SGD to geometric median estimation (Φ(θ)=E[‖X-θ‖], non-smooth at the minimizer). Compute the rescaled trajectory Y^n_t and check its sample path properties (e.g., autocorrelation, marginal distributions) against a simulated diffusion with SDE (6). Validate that the functional limit holds despite non-smoothness.
3. **ERM vs SGD variance gap**: For a strongly convex Φ, obtain the ERM θ̂_n (e.g., via batch optimization) and compare its asymptotic covariance Δ to the SGD Σ estimated from the average of many SGD runs. Confirm that Σ - Δ is positive semidefinite by verifying all eigenvalues are non-negative, and quantify the variance increase as δ grows.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
1. **FCLT for Polyak-Ruppert averaging and larger step-sizes**  
   The paper notes that extending the asymptotic theory to algorithms using larger step sizes (e.g., t_n ≍ n^{-α} with α > 1/2) and Polyak-Ruppert averaging is open. Such methods achieve asymptotic efficiency for point estimation (matching ERM variance), but the long-term trajectory behavior under a functional limit remains unknown. Resolving this would require defining appropriate resealings for averaged iterates, proving tightness, and identifying the limiting diffusion, likely with a modified drift structure.

2. **Necessity of δ > 1/λ_min**  
   Theorem 3 requires δ > 1/λ_min to ensure a confining drift. It is unclear whether this condition is necessary or can be relaxed. A counterexample for δ ≤ 1/λ_min or an extension using stronger assumptions (e.g., global strong convexity) would clarify its role.

3. **FCLT under infinite-variance noise**  
   The current FCLT assumes finite second moments (bounded noise variance). However, recent work [8] shows a pointwise CLT for SGD under heavy-tailed noise with infinite variance, yielding a non-Gaussian stable limit. A functional CLT in that regime would likely involve a Lévy-driven process and possibly a different topology (e.g., Skorokhod with jumps). Proving tightness and identifying the limit under appropriate moment conditions remains open.

4. **Extension to non-convex objectives**  
   All assumptions include convexity. For non-convex objectives (e.g., deep learning), it is unknown whether an FCLT holds near local minima under SGD. Non-convex landscapes introduce saddle points and multiple minima; the convergence proofs rely on convexity inequalities (e.g., Robbins-Siegmund). A local analysis assuming strong convexity only in a neighborhood of a local minimum, together with a high-probability bound that iterates stay in that neighborhood, could potentially yield a conditional FCLT. Alternatively, projecting SGD onto the attraction region might be feasible.

## Limitations
- The step-size condition δ > 1/λ_min depends on the minimal curvature of Φ at θ*, which is usually unknown and may be zero or very small, limiting practical applicability.
- The framework requires local strong convexity and a twice-differentiable Hessian at the minimizer, which conflicts with the claim of handling non-smooth problems like the geometric median unless a hidden smoothness is invoked.
- The asymptotic analysis provides no finite-sample guarantees; real-world use of SGD must rely on heuristics or non-asymptotic bounds.
