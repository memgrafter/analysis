# DirMoE: Dirichlet-routed Mixture of Experts

## Quick Facts
*   **arXiv ID:** 2602.09001
*   **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.09001
*   **Reference Count:** 35
*   **Primary Result:** A fully differentiable MoE router with disentangled selection and contribution, controlled by a calibrated sparsity knob λ, matches vanilla MoE compute efficiency and achieves superior zero-shot accuracy on LLaMA-185M.

## Executive Summary
DirMoE introduces a novel, fully differentiable routing mechanism for Mixture of Experts (MoE) models to address the non-differentiability and entanglement of expert selection and weighting in existing routers. It employs a Dirichlet variational autoencoder to generate expert contribution weights and a Gumbel-Sigmoid relaxation for binary expert selection, both governed by a single sparsity parameter λ. On a LLaMA-185M model with 8 experts, this design yields compute-throughput parity with standard MoE while achieving higher average zero-shot accuracy across seven benchmarks than Switch and ReMoE baselines. The paper provides theoretical and empirical evidence that λ serves as a precise, monotonic knob for controlling model sparsity and expert specialization.

## Method Summary
DirMoE replaces the discrete, non-differentiable routing function in MoE with a continuous, probabilistic formulation. Expert selection is modeled as a Bernoulli variable per expert, relaxed via Gumbel-Sigmoid for gradient flow. Expert contribution weights are drawn from a Dirichlet distribution, whose concentration parameters are outputs from a router network. A single hyperparameter λ scales these concentrations, directly controlling the expected sparsity (number of active experts) and the entropy of the weight distribution. This disentangles the *decision* of which experts to activate from the *magnitude* of their contributions, allowing independent control over sparsity and weight sharpness. The router is trained end-to-end as part of the model using a variational objective.

## Key Results
*   **Accuracy:** On LLaMA-185M (E=8, k=1), DirMoE averages 41.13% zero-shot accuracy across seven benchmarks, exceeding Switch MoE (39.47%) and ReMoE (40.03%).
*   **Compute Efficiency:** Achieves throughput of ~137 TFLOP/s/GPU, matching the efficiency of standard (vanilla) MoE.
*   **Sparsity Control:** The λ parameter functions as a monotonic and calibratable sparsity knob, validated by the Simpson index showing predictable decreases in active expert count as λ increases.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The core innovation is the *disentanglement* of expert selection and contribution into two separate, differentiable random variables. Selection uses a Gumbel-Sigmoid (a continuous relaxation of a Bernoulli sample), while contribution uses a Dirichlet sample. Both are parameterized by the same router network output scaled by λ. This separation prevents the entanglement present in routers like Switch, where the top-k operation forces a hard cutoff that conflates which experts are chosen with how much they contribute. The probabilistic formulation inherently introduces stochasticity during training, which may act as a regularizer. The calibrated λ provides a direct, theoretically-grounded handle to trade off between model capacity (more active experts) and computation (fewer experts), without ad-hoc adjustments.

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component Map:** Input Tokens -> Shared Router Network (MLP) -> [Dirichlet Concentration (α), Gumbel-Sigmoid Logits (s)] (both = f_router(x) * λ) -> Sample Selection Mask (z ~ Bernoulli(sigmoid(s))) & Sample Contribution Weights (w ~ Dirichlet(softmax(α))) -> Element-wise Product (z * w) -> Normalized Weights (divide by sum) -> Weighted Sum over Expert Outputs.

**Critical Path:** The router's forward pass is critical. It takes the token representation, produces raw scores, scales them by λ, and then generates the two stochastic outputs. The sampling steps are non-differentiable but have differentiable reparameterized gradients. The final weighted sum of expert outputs is the main computational cost.

**Design Tradeoffs:**
1.  **Probabilistic vs. Deterministic:** Probabilistic selection (during training) may improve exploration and robustness but introduces variance. Deterministic selection at inference (e.g., taking the mode) is used for efficiency.
2.  **Disentanglement Complexity:** Separating selection and contribution adds parameters and computational steps compared to a single top-k operation but offers finer-grained control.
3.  **Calibration Overhead:** The theoretical relationship between λ and expected sparsity requires assumptions (e.g., symmetric Dirichlet basis). Real data may deviate, necessitating empirical tuning of λ schedules.

**Failure Signatures:**
1.  **Router Collapse:** The Dirichlet concentrations or Gumbel logits collapse, leading to either no experts selected (all z=0) or all experts selected with uniform weights (loss of sparsity).
2.  **Poor Specialization:** Heatmaps show all experts responding uniformly to all domains, indicating the router failed to learn a meaningful partitioning.
3.  **Training Instability:** Variance from stochastic sampling causes loss spikes or divergence, especially with high λ (low sparsity) where gradients are less frequent.

**First Three Experiments:**
1.  **Sparsity Knob Validation:** Train with a fixed λ and measure the average number of active experts (count where z_i > 0) and the Simpson index of the contribution distribution w. Repeat for a range of λ values to confirm the monotonic relationship.
2.  **Ablation - Disentanglement:** Compare DirMoE against an ablation where selection and contribution come from the same softmax distribution (like standard MoE), keeping all other components identical. Measure accuracy and expert specialization metrics.
3.  **Baseline Comparison:** Replicate the main results, training DirMoE, Switch MoE, and ReMoE on the identical LLaMA-185M setup with the same hyperparameters (except router-specific ones) and dataset for a fair comparison on both accuracy (zero-shot) and throughput.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
### Open Question 1
- Question: How does the probabilistic routing in DirMoE affect model robustness, such as performance under adversarial attacks or distribution shifts compared to deterministic routers?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The conclusion states: "The probabilistic formulation and explicit concentration control may improve model robustness."
- Why unresolved: The paper only evaluates standard zero-shot accuracy on seven benchmarks and does not test robustness scenarios.
- What evidence would resolve it: Experiments on adversarial benchmarks (e.g., AdvGLUE), distribution shift datasets, or stress tests for out-of-domain generalization comparing DirMoE with Switch and ReMoE.

### Open Question 2
- Question: To what extent does the disentangled routing (expert selection vs. contribution) yield more monosemantic and interpretable expert specializations compared to entangled routers?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The conclusion mentions: "the disentangled routing variables naturally expose which experts are active and how probability mass is allocated. This could yield clearer, more monosemantic expert specializations."
- Why unresolved: The paper shows some heatmaps of domain specialization (Fig 5) but does not systematically analyze expert semantics or provide qualitative examples of what each expert learns.
- What evidence would resolve it: Detailed case studies analyzing input examples routed to specific experts, probing expert outputs for concept alignment, or metrics for monosemanticity (e.g., consistency on specific topics).

### Open Question 3
- Question: Does the DirMoE router scale effectively to much larger models (e.g., billions of parameters) and higher expert counts while maintaining the claimed sparsity control and training stability?
- Basis in paper: [inferred] The experiments are on a 185M-parameter model with up to 48 experts; the paper does not discuss potential bottlenecks (e.g., memory, communication) or whether the probabilistic sampling introduces instability at larger scales.
- Why unresolved: The scalability section only shows throughput matching vanilla MoE for E=8, k=1. No results for deeper/wider models or distributed training with many GPUs.
- What evidence would resolve it: Training runs on larger models (e.g., 1B+ parameters, 64+ experts) reporting convergence, speed, and sparsity metrics; analysis of load imbalance and any need for capacity adjustments.

### Open Question 4
- Question: How robust is the λ sparsity knob calibration to variations in model architecture, dataset, and training dynamics? Can the theoretical relationship (e.g., Equation 16) be reliably applied in practice without extensive tuning?
- Basis in paper: [inferred] The paper derives calibration formulas assuming symmetric or two-group Dirichlet bases, but empirical validation is limited to a single model/task setup. The λ schedules are hand-tuned (e.g., decay factors), and the paper does not test sensitivity.
- Why unresolved: The calibration is presented as a theoretical guarantee, but real-world training involves noise, non-stationarity, and complex data distributions that may deviate from assumptions.
- What evidence would resolve it: Ablation studies varying model size, number of experts, dataset domains, and random seeds to measure deviation from expected sparsity (Simpson index) and impact on performance.

## Limitations
*   **Scale Validation:** Results are confined to a 185M-parameter model; scalability to state-of-the-art LLM scales (e.g., 7B+) is not demonstrated.
*   **Benchmark Scope:** Evaluation is limited to seven zero-shot academic benchmarks, with no finetuning, reasoning, math, or multilingual assessments.
*   **External Validation:** The paper has zero citations, indicating no independent replication or comparative studies with newer MoE variants (e.g., CartesianMoE) exist yet.

## Confidence
*   **Compute Parity & Accuracy Gains (vs. Switch/ReMoE):** **Medium** – Evidence is solid for the specific 185M/E=8 setup but lacks breadth across scales and tasks.
*   **Sparsity Control via λ (Simpson Index):** **High** – The theoretical derivation is provided and internal empirical results show a clear, monotonic trend.
*   **Improved Expert Specialization:** **Low** – Claims rely on qualitative heatmaps without systematic, quantitative metrics for monosemanticity or cross-paper validation.
