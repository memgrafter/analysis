# Interpretable Motion-Attentive Maps: Spatio-Temporally Localizing Concepts in Video Diffusion Transformers
## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2603.02919
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.02919
- Reference count: 40
- Primary result: IMAP achieves state-of-the-art motion localization on MeViS (average score 0.62 vs. 0.45 for ConceptAttention) and zero-shot video segmentation mIoU 30.1 on VSPW.

## Executive Summary
The paper introduces Interpretable Motion‑Attentive Maps (IMAP), a training‑free method to localize spatiotemporal motion concepts in video diffusion transformers (Video DiTs). IMAP combines GramCol, which generates per‑frame saliency maps via the Gram matrix of visual token embeddings, with automatic identification of motion‑specific attention heads using the Calinski‑Harabasz index. Without any fine‑tuning or gradients, IMAP aggregates relevant heads to produce interpretable maps that highlight where and when a motion occurs. Evaluated on MeViS and VSPW, IMAP outperforms existing saliency methods across five motion localization metrics and achieves the best zero‑shot segmentation among Video DiT‑based approaches.

## Method Summary
IMAP operates in two stages. First, GramCol produces per‑frame spatial saliency for a target concept by selecting a text‑surrogate token via query‑key matching and extracting the corresponding column from the Gram matrix of visual token embeddings, ensuring positive values. Second, motion‑specific attention heads are identified by computing the Calinski‑Harabasz index (CHI) on frame‑wise clusters of visual token embeddings; heads with high CHI indicate strong temporal separation. The method aggregates GramCol maps across selected timesteps, layers, and heads. It is applicable to both joint‑attention (e.g., CogVideoX, HunyuanVideo) and cross‑attention (e.g., Wan) Video DiTs, requiring no training or parameter updates.

## Key Results
- IMAP achieves an average Motion Localization Score of 0.62 on CogVideoX‑5B, surpassing ConceptAttention (0.45) and other baselines.
- For zero‑shot video semantic segmentation on VSPW, GramCol attains a mIoU of 30.1, the highest among Video DiT saliency methods.
- Ablations confirm the importance of motion‑head selection (CHI) and GramCol’s positive highlightability, with consistent gains across models.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
IMAP isolates motion‑sensitive attention heads using the Calinski‑Harabasz index, which measures how well visual token embeddings separate across frames. For each such head, GramCol selects a surrogate token via query‑key matching and extracts a column from the Gram matrix of visual token embeddings, yielding a spatial similarity map that is guaranteed positive. Aggregating these maps over time and heads produces a spatiotemporal saliency map that reliably highlights the motion concept without any training.

## Architecture Onboarding
Component map: Video/Text -> DiT Activation Extraction -> Layer/Timestep Selection -> CHI Motion-Head Selection -> GramCol per Head/Frame -> Aggregation -> Post-Processing -> IMAP  
Critical path: Per‑head GramCol computation; depends on motion‑head selection and yields spatial maps. CHI is essential to isolate heads that vary across frames (temporal signal). Without it, vanilla cross‑attention or GramCol would capture static features.  
Design tradeoffs:  
- CHI vs. other metrics: CHI is efficient but may miss subtle motion heads.  
- GramCol positivity guarantees non‑negative values but assumes visual tokens of the same concept are similar; could blur boundaries for multi‑faceted concepts.  
- Equal weighting in aggregation is simple but could be refined (e.g., weighted by CHI score).  
- Timestep selection uses a heuristic; early timesteps are noisy, later capture semantics.  
Failure signatures:  
- CHI selects heads responsive to camera motion → maps highlight background. Check by correlating CHI with camera motion magnitude.  
- GramCol highlights appear negative (should not) due to numerical issues; verify all values ≥0.  
- No motion heads selected (k=0) → empty output; indicates CHI threshold too high or video too static.  
- Maps blurry/watermark‑like from early timesteps → adjust temporal range.  
First three experiments:  
1. Reproduce GramCol on a single frame: Extract visual token embeddings, pick a concept token via QK matching, compute Gram matrix column, and visualize to ensure it highlights semantically relevant regions (positive values).  
2. Compute CHI for all heads on a sample video, plot distribution, verify top‑k heads show clear temporal separation via t‑SNE of embeddings colored by frame.  
3. Run full IMAP on a short MeViS clip and compare against baseline (ConceptAttention) qualitatively; check for sharper, temporally coherent motion maps.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
### Open Question 1
- Question: Do Video DiTs truly understand and create motion, or do they rely on superficial pattern matching without genuine semantic comprehension of motion concepts?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The authors explicitly state in the introduction: "Do Video DiTs truly understand and create motion?" highlighting a gap in understanding how motion words are converted into video.
- Why unresolved: The paper focuses on localizing motion spatially and temporally via IMAP but does not investigate whether the localized features correspond to a deeper, human-like understanding of motion dynamics or merely statistical correlations in training data.
- What evidence would resolve it: Conduct experiments that test generalization to novel or counterfactual motion descriptions, probe internal representations using techniques like probing or causal mediation analysis, and compare DiT behavior with human motion perception studies to assess semantic depth.

### Open Question 2
- Question: Is the Calinski-Harabasz index (CHI) the most effective metric for identifying motion-specific attention heads in Video DiTs, or could alternative separation scores or learned criteria yield better motion localization?
- Basis in paper: [inferred] The authors select motion heads using CHI based on a correlation with motion localization score (MLS) and mention testing other metrics (Silhouette, Davies-Bouldin, Fisher ratio) but choose CHI for computational efficiency and consistency; this assumes CHI is optimal without exhaustive validation.
- Why unresolved: The correlation analysis (Pearson r=0.60) supports CHI, but it is not definitive proof of superiority across diverse models, motion types, or video complexities; other metrics might capture complementary temporal features.
- What evidence would resolve it: Perform a systematic comparison of multiple separation metrics and learned approaches (e.g., clustering-based or supervised head selection) on a broad set of motion concepts and Video DiT architectures, measuring improvements in MLS and downstream tasks like segmentation.

### Open Question 3
- Question: How well does IMAP generalize to videos with non-static camera movements (e.g., pan, zoom, dolly) or multiple overlapping motions, given that the evaluation dataset primarily uses static-camera clips?
- Basis in paper: [inferred] The authors filter out videos with non-static cameras from MeViS for evaluation, citing focus on object-centric motion; they briefly evaluate on a small set of non-static clips but do not deeply analyze performance or adapt IMAP for camera motion.
- Why unresolved: Real-world videos often include camera motion, which could confound motion localization by introducing global changes; IMAP's head selection and GramCol may not disentangle object motion from camera-induced variations.
- What evidence would resolve it: Test IMAP on datasets with varied camera motions (e.g., VSPW, Kinetics), quantify performance drop-off, and develop extensions like camera-motion compensation or joint modeling of camera and object motion to improve robustness.

### Open Question 4
- Question: Can GramCol's property of positive highlightability—ensuring semantically relevant regions have positive values—be consistently guaranteed across all text concepts, model architectures, and attention heads, or are there edge cases where negative or ambiguous highlights persist?
- Basis in paper: [inferred] GramCol is designed to use visual token similarities via the Gram matrix, avoiding negative highlights from cross-modal mismatches; the authors contrast this with ConceptAttention but do not prove universal positivity or analyze its impact on all concepts.
- Why unresolved: The paper shows GramCol yields positive values by construction (inner products of visual embeddings), but this assumes that relevant visual tokens are similar; in cases of feature ambiguity or heterogeneous concepts, highlights might still be noisy or negative due to head-specific variations.
- What evidence would resolve it: Statistically analyze the distribution of GramCol values across numerous concepts, heads, and models, correlating positivity with localization accuracy; explore theoretical bounds under manifold assumptions or test interventions to enforce positivity.

## Limitations
- Hyperparameter thresholds (λ₂, motion head count) are model‑specific and may not generalize.
- LLM‑based evaluation (OpenAI o3‑pro) with unreleased prompts hinders reproducibility and incurs cost.
- Camera motion may bias head selection, as MeViS evaluation filters static cameras; performance on dynamic scenes is unclear.

## Confidence
- IMAP outperforms baselines on MeViS: Medium confidence.
- GramCol achieves SOTA zero‑shot segmentation mIoU on VSPW: High confidence.
- Method is training‑free and gradient‑free: High confidence.
- Motion‑head selection via CHI: Medium confidence.
- Applicability to cross‑attention Video DiTs: Medium confidence.
