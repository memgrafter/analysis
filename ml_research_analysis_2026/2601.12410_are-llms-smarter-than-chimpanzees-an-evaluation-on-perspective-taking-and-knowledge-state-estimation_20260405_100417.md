# Are LLMs Smarter Than Chimpanzees? An Evaluation on Perspective Taking and Knowledge State Estimation

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2601.12410
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.12410
- **Reference count:** 35
- **Primary result:** Even the best LLMs (Claude-4.5-Opus) lag significantly behind human performance on story-based knowledge state estimation tasks, with scaling and basic reasoning providing minimal gains.

## Executive Summary
This paper evaluates large language models (LLMs) on their ability to infer characters’ knowledge states within narratives—a core component of theory of mind that distinguishes humans from other primates. Using two novel tasks, Knowledge Prediction (KNP) and Implausible Knowledge Detection (IKD), the authors test state-of-the-art models on datasets derived from human stories. In zero-shot settings, all models perform near random chance. The top model, Claude-4.5-Opus, achieves 69% accuracy on KNP compared to a human baseline of 92%, and 68% accuracy on IKD classification versus 76% for humans, with a localization Intersection-over-Union (IoU) of 0.59 against 0.72 for humans. Simple prompting interventions that explicitly focus on knowledge states improve scores by 12–15 percentage points but still fall short of human capability. The findings suggest current LLMs remain deficient in tracking and utilising perspective-dependent knowledge, a foundational aspect of social cognition.

## Method Summary
The study employs two experimental tasks constructed from a corpus of 500 human-written short stories. The **Knowledge Prediction (KNP)** task requires models to predict a character’s next action based solely on that character’s accessible knowledge, ignoring information known to other characters or the reader. The **Implausible Knowledge Detection (IKD)** task requires models to identify plot holes by spotting instances where a character acts on information they could not plausibly possess. For IKD, evaluation includes both classification accuracy and localization (pinpointing the exact implausible knowledge span) measured by IoU. Multiple LLMs are evaluated in a zero-shot manner without in-context examples. An additional prompting experiment tests whether explicitly instructing the model to "focus on the character's knowledge state" improves performance.

## Key Results
- All tested LLMs performed near random chance (~50%) on KNP and IKD in standard zero-shot prompting.
- The best model, Claude-4.5-Opus, achieved 69% on KNP vs. 92% for humans, and 68% classification accuracy on IKD vs. 76% for humans (localization IoU: 0.59 vs. 0.72).
- Knowledge-focused prompting yielded a 12–15 percentage point improvement but still left models trailing human performance by a large margin.
- Increased model scale and the use of extended reasoning chains produced only minimal performance gains on these tasks.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

The tasks isolate the cognitive process of maintaining and consulting a perspective-specific knowledge base. Success requires: (1) correctly representing the knowledge state of a specific agent within a narrative, (2) holding that representation constant while reasoning about the agent's actions or the plausibility of events, and (3) suppressing the model's own "omniscient" knowledge of the full story. The poor zero-shot performance suggests LLMs, trained to generate the most statistically probable next token across the entire text, fail to reliably decouple an agent's limited perspective from the global narrative truth. The modest gains from knowledge-focused prompting indicate that models possess some latent ability to engage in this decoupling when explicitly directed, but the capability is not robust or automatic.

## Foundational Learning
The evaluation rests on several cognitive and computational concepts:
- **Theory of Mind (ToM):** The ability to attribute mental states (beliefs, desires, knowledge) to oneself and others. *Why needed:* The KNP and IKD tasks are specific tests of "knowledge state tracking," a ToM subclass. *Quick check:* Can the model distinguish what *Agent A* knows from what *Agent B* or the reader knows?
- **Knowledge State Tracking (KST):** Maintaining an up-to-date representation of an entity's information. *Why needed:* The core challenge is not general knowledge but the *current, accessible* knowledge of a story character. *Quick check:* Does the model's prediction change if the character is alone versus with an informant?
- **Counterfactual Reasoning:** Evaluating "what if" scenarios based on alternative knowledge sets. *Why needed:* IKD requires spotting contradictions between a character's action and their *hypothetical* knowledge state. *Quick check:* Can the model identify an action that would only make sense if the character had witnessed an off-screen event?
- **Grounding to Perspective:** Aligning reasoning to a specific, limited viewpoint. *Why needed:* LLMs are inherently "bird's-eye" narrators; this task forces a " frog's-eye" perspective. *Quick check:* Does the model's answer rely on facts the character has not yet been exposed to?
- **Narrative Comprehension:** Understanding plot, causality, and information flow in stories. *Why needed:* The tasks are embeddedin realistic story contexts. *Quick check:* Can the model follow the sequence of events and information disclosure?
- **Zero-shot Generalisation:** Applying a learned capability to a novel task without task-specific examples. *Why needed:* The evaluation tests inherent capability, not fine-tuned skill. *Quick check:* Does performance drop drastically without demonstrations?

## Architecture Onboarding
*[No explicit architectural analysis is provided in the paper. The following is a generic mapping based on standard transformer-based LLM processing for such a task.]*

**Component Map:**
Input Text (Story) -> Token Embedding -> Transformer Layers (Self-Attention, Feed-Forward) -> Final Hidden State -> Output Head (Token Logits)

**Critical Path:** The story text, including character dialogue and actions, flows through the embedding layer into the transformer stack. The self-attention mechanism is the critical component, as it must learn to weight tokens based on their relevance to a specific character's information access (e.g., what was said in a private conversation vs. a public announcement). The final layer's representation must then be decoded to either generate a plausible action (KNP) or assess the consistency of a marked span (IKD).

**Design Tradeoffs:**
- **Scale vs. Efficiency:** Larger models have more parameters to potentially learn nuanced perspective representations but are computationally expensive and, per this paper, do not reliably translate to better KST.
- **Context Window:** A longer context window allows more story information to be processed, but risks the model amalgamating all information rather than segmenting it by source/agent.
- **Objective Function:** Standard next-token prediction does not explicitly reward maintaining separate knowledge states. A more suitable objective might involve contrastive learning between different characters' perspectives.

**Failure Signatures:**
- **"Omniscient contamination":** The model's prediction for a character incorporates information only the narrator or another character knows.
- **Random-like performance:** Scores clustering around 50% for binary classification, indicating inability to capture the task's statistical pattern beyond chance.
- **Inconsistent sensitivity:** The model correctly answers some cases with clear knowledge splits but fails on subtler ones, suggesting brittle understanding.

**First Three Experiments:**
1. **Ablate context:** Present the story without the final paragraph/action and see if the model can predict the action based solely on the preceding knowledge state.
2. **Swap knowledge frames:** Replace a character's private information with publicly known information in the prompt and measure the change in prediction plausibility.
3. **Probe attention weights:** Analyze attention patterns between tokens representing knowledge sources (e.g., "whispered," "thought") and tokens representing a character's subsequent actions to see if the model focuses on appropriate knowledge channels.

## Limitations
- **Dataset Scope:** The 500-story dataset, while novel, is small and may not capture the full diversity of real-world knowledge state scenarios.
- **Indirect Chimpanzee Comparison:** The title's comparison is conceptual; the paper does not include or test data from non-human primates, so the claim about LLMs being "less capable than chimps" is an inference.
- **Zero-Shot Focus:** The primary evaluation is zero-shot; the potential for significant improvement via targeted fine-tuning or more sophisticated prompting (e.g., multi-step reasoning chains) is not fully explored beyond a simple instruction.

## Confidence
- **Claim:** Current LLMs significantly underperform humans on story-based knowledge state estimation tasks. **Label:** High (Consistent gap across two tasks with a robust human baseline).
- **Claim:** Model scaling and extended reasoning yield minimal improvements on these tasks. **Label:** Medium (Tested multiple models, but limited to specific architectures and prompting strategies; fine-tuning remains unexamined).
- **Claim:** LLMs are inherently deficient in theory of mind relative to chimpanzees. **Label:** Low (Comparison is indirect and based on an assumed cognitive hierarchy; no empirical data from chimpanzees is presented).

## Next Checks
1. **Scale and Curate Data:** Replicate the study with a substantially larger (>5,000 samples) and adversarially curated dataset that covers a wider range of knowledge state configurations and story genres.
2. **Direct Cross-Species Comparison:** Design and run an identical experimental protocol with chimpanzee subjects (or other primates) to obtain empirical data for a direct performance comparison.
3. **Advanced Intervention Testing:** Systematically evaluate the impact of fine-tuning on KST tasks and advanced reasoning prompts (e.g., chain-of-thought, self-consistency, explicit agent simulation) to robustly challenge the scaling hypothesis and identify the ceiling for this capability.
