# Atomic-SNLI: Fine-Grained Natural Language Inference through Atomic Fact Decomposition

## Quick Facts
- **ArXiv ID:** 2601.06528
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.06528
- **Reference count:** 18
- **Primary result:** Training on Atomic-SNLI improves atomic-level accuracy on multi-fact hypotheses by up to 10% (DeBERTa-v3-xsmall) while maintaining sentence-level performance.

## Executive Summary
The paper introduces Atomic-SNLI, a dataset that decomposes SNLI hypotheses into atomic facts and enriches them with automatically generated examples via linguistically guided pipelines. This addresses the lack of interpretability in sentence-level NLI and poor model performance on fine-grained atomic reasoning. Fine-tuning standard NLI models on Atomic-SNLI yields significant gains in atomic-level accuracy for hypotheses with multiple facts, and probability summation for aggregating atomic predictions provides robust and balanced sentence-level performance.

## Method Summary
Atomic-SNLI is constructed by decomposing each SNLI hypothesis into minimal atomic facts using DECMODEL. Each atomic fact inherits the original hypothesis label. The dataset is enriched with label-specific strategies: entailment examples are taken directly from SNLI entailment pairs and filtered by confidence; neutral examples include direct SNLI neutral pairs and BM25-retrieved candidates; contradiction examples include direct SNLI contradictions and LLM-generated minimal edits validated by an ensemble NLI model. The resulting atomic pairs fine-tune pre-trained NLI models (DeBERTa, ELECTRA, BERT). At test time, a hypothesis is decomposed, each atomic fact is classified, and the sentence-level label is obtained by summing class probabilities across atoms and taking the argmax.

## Key Results
- DeBERTa-v3-xsmall trained on Atomic-SNLI achieves +10.07% accuracy and +8.16% F1 on 3-fact hypotheses compared to SNLI-only training.
- Probability summation yields more balanced precision and recall across entailment, neutral, and contradiction than strict logical rules.
- The approach maintains strong sentence-level performance while enabling interpretable, fine-grained reasoning.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1: Decomposition for Interpretability
**Claim:** Decomposing hypotheses into atomic facts yields interpretable, fine-grained NLI judgments and enables compositional reasoning.  
**Mechanism:** Each hypothesis is split into minimal, self-contained propositions. A model independently classifies the premise-atomic fact relationship. The final label is derived by aggregating these local decisions (e.g., probability summation). This bottom-up composition makes the model's reasoning traceable.  
**Core assumption:** Atomic facts are semantically independent and collectively exhaustive for the hypothesis.  
**Evidence:** The paper states that atomic-level NLI addresses interpretability by decomposing complex reasoning (abstract, section 3.2). However, the independence assumption is often violated.

### Mechanism 2: Atomic Training Improves Fine-Grained Reasoning
**Claim:** Training on Atomic-SNLI significantly improves a model's fine-grained reasoning, especially for hypotheses with multiple atomic facts.  
**Mechanism:** Atomic-SNLI provides direct supervision at the atomic level by pairing each premise with individual atomic facts and their labels. The construction uses direct extraction, retrieval, and LLM generation to ensure coverage of all three NLI relations. Models fine-tuned on this data learn precise fact-premise alignment, transferring to better performance on complex hypotheses.  
**Core assumption:** Gains on atomic tasks translate to improved sentence-level performance; automatically generated atomic examples are of sufficient quality.  
**Evidence:** Models trained on Atomic-SNLI consistently outperform SNLI-trained counterparts for 2- and 3-fact hypotheses (section 5.2, Table 4). The benefit diminishes for 4- and 5-fact cases due to data sparsity.

### Mechanism 3: Soft Aggregation for Robustness
**Claim:** Soft probability summation for aggregating atomic predictions yields more balanced and robust sentence-level NLI than strict logical rules.  
**Mechanism:** Instead of deterministic rules (e.g., all atoms must be entailed), the method sums the class-probability distributions predicted for each atomic fact and selects the label with the highest total score. This smooth aggregation tolerates individual atomic errors and retains uncertainty information.  
**Core assumption:** The model's probability outputs are reasonably calibrated; summing independent (or weakly dependent) atomic confidences approximates the joint likelihood.  
**Evidence:** Probability summation maintains balanced precision and recall across all three NLI classes, while logical rules show extreme patterns (e.g., entailment precision 100% but recall 20.69% for 3-fact) (section 5.3, Tables 5 and 7).

## Architecture Onboarding
### Component Map
SNLI Input -> Decomposition Engine (DECMODEL) -> Label-Specific Generators (Entailment, Neutral, Contradiction) -> Dataset Compiler -> Model Trainer -> Inference Aggregator -> Evaluation Module

### Critical Path
Load SNLI -> decompose hypotheses (DECMODEL) -> generate neutral & contradiction examples -> assemble atomic training set -> fine-tune base model -> for test, decompose hypotheses -> predict atomic labels -> aggregate -> evaluate metrics.

### Design Tradeoffs
- Data coverage vs. noise: Retrieval and LLM generation increase diversity but risk irrelevant/ungrammatical outputs; confidence thresholds (τ>0.5) mitigate noise at cost of recall.
- Aggregation strictness: Logical rules are interpretable but brittle; probability summation is error-tolerant but blurs crisp boundaries.
- Model capacity: Smaller models show larger absolute gains, possibly because they benefit more from focused atomic training.
- Decomposition dependency: The pipeline relies on DECMODEL; any decomposition errors propagate.
- Long-tail complexity: Very few training examples for 4+ atomic facts limit learning deep composition.

### Failure Signatures
- Accuracy drops for 4- and 5-fact hypotheses (Table 4).
- Under logical rules, extreme precision-recall imbalance (e.g., entailment precision 100%, recall 20.69% for 3-fact).
- High rejection rate in LLM contradiction generation (if many candidates fail τ_c>0.5).
- Average "Premise Use" shows many atomic hypotheses per original premise, raising potential data leakage concerns.
- If confidence thresholds are not tuned, atomic training set may become too small or too noisy.

### First 3 Experiments
1. Reproduce atomic-level gap: Evaluate a pretrained NLI model on SNLI test after decomposing each hypothesis with DECMODEL. Compute atomic-level accuracy and compare to sentence-level accuracy (expected ~0.7% gap as in Table 2).
2. Train on Atomic-SNLI: Fine-tune DeBERTa-v3-xsmall on Atomic-SNLI training split (3 epochs, lr=5e-5, batch=32). Evaluate on 2-fact and 3-fact test subsets using probability summation. Compare to SNLI-trained counterpart; expect significant gains (e.g., +10% accuracy for 3-fact).
3. Aggregation comparison: Using same atomic predictions from experiment 2, apply both logical rules and probability summation. Compute class-wise precision/recall. Observe summation yields more balanced metrics, while logical rules produce extreme patterns.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
Open Question 1: Why do state-of-the-art NLI models exhibit weaker performance on atomic-level inference compared to sentence-level tasks, even when the atomic facts are derived from the same hypothesis? The paper identifies this as a critical gap but does not diagnose the underlying cause, leaving open whether it stems from training data biases, architectural limitations, or misalignment between the standard NLI objective and compositional reasoning. Evidence to resolve: Ablation studies isolating factors like model architecture, training objectives, and lexical/syntactic complexity of atomic facts; error analysis categorizing specific failure modes.

Open Question 2: How does the probability summation aggregation method for combining atomic predictions mitigate the brittleness of strict logical rules, and what does this reveal about the relationship between model confidence and compositional reasoning? The paper shows empirical benefit but does not theoretically analyze why summation yields more balanced precision/recall. Evidence to resolve: Controlled experiments measuring how atomic prediction confidence distributions correlate with final sentence-level correctness; probing whether summed probabilities align with a valid probability semantics for compositional operators.

Open Question 3: To what extent does the quality and diversity of the generated atomic examples (especially for neutral and contradiction) limit the generalizability of models trained on Atomic-SNLI to out-of-distribution or real-world NLI scenarios? The automatic generation strategies may introduce distributional shifts. Evidence to resolve: Cross-dataset evaluation on a manually created atomic NLI test set (e.g., from fact-checking or QA domains); human evaluation of generated atomic facts for naturalness and coverage of logical phenomena.

Open Question 4: Does the assumption of independence between atomic facts in the aggregation rules hold for real-world hypotheses, and how do inter-fact dependencies affect final NLI judgments? The framework treats atomic facts as independent evidence units, but in practice facts often share entities, predicates, or constraints. Evidence to resolve: Developing a model that explicitly reasons over a graph of atomic facts with learned dependency edges; evaluating on multi-fact hypotheses with clear dependencies; comparing independent vs. joint modeling.

Open Question 5: Can the atomic decomposition and training methodology of Atomic-SNLI be effectively scaled to longer, more complex textual inputs (e.g., paragraphs or documents) for fine-grained entailment, and what new challenges arise in decomposition and composition at that granularity? The dataset is built from sentence-level hypotheses with max 5 atomic facts. Evidence to resolve: Extending the pipeline to multi-sentence documents, creating a document-level atomic NLI dataset; benchmarking decomposition quality and aggregation accuracy as atom count increases.

## Limitations
- Decomposition quality hinges on DECMODEL; invalid or non-minimal atomic facts could degrade training and evaluation.
- Construction pipelines rely on fixed confidence thresholds (τ = 0.5) without human validation, likely introducing label noise.
- Test set contains very few 4- and 5-fact hypotheses, making conclusions about deeper composition statistically weak.
- Probability summation assumes atomic predictions are independent and well-calibrated; correlations among facts from the same premise are ignored.

## Confidence
- Atomic decomposition enables interpretable reasoning: **Medium**
- Atomic-SNLI training improves fine-grained reasoning: **Medium-High** for 2–3 fact cases, **Low** for ≥4 fact cases
- Probability summation yields balanced class performance: **Medium**
