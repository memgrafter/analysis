# Scalable Learning of Multivariate Distributions via Coresets
## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2603.19792
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.19792
- **Reference count:** 40
- **Primary result:** First coreset method for multivariate conditional transformation models (MCTMs) with theoretical size `O(J²d² polylog(Jd)/ε²)` and demonstrated scalability on datasets up to `n=300k`.

## Executive Summary
This paper introduces the first coreset construction for multivariate conditional transformation models (MCTMs), a flexible framework for complex multivariate distributions. The method combines `ℓ₂` leverage score sampling with a convex hull augmentation to stabilize the problematic logarithmic terms in the MCTM log-likelihood. It yields a small, weighted subset of the data that approximates the full objective within a `(1±ε)` multiplicative factor with high probability. The approach enables scalable and stable inference on massive datasets, reducing computation from hours to seconds while preserving dependency structure.

## Method Summary
The coreset is built in two stages. First, a sensitivity-based sampling scheme uses `ℓ₂` leverage scores of a constructed matrix `B` (encoding the quadratic part of the negative log-likelihood) to sample `k₁` points with probabilities proportional to `uᵢ + 1/n`, where `uᵢ` is the leverage score. Second, to handle the unstable negative logarithmic term, the method restricts the optimization domain and deterministically includes all points on the convex hull of a transformed data representation `a′`. The final coreset is the union of these two sets with appropriately assigned weights.

## Key Results
- Theoretical coreset size is `O(J²d² polylog(Jd)/ε²)` with matching lower bounds `Ω(dJ²)`.
- On 14 simulated 2D distributions, the `ℓ₂`-hull method significantly outperforms uniform and pure `ℓ₂` sampling in log-likelihood ratio, parameter error, and dependency structure preservation.
- On real-world datasets (Covertype, stock returns), it reduces fitting time from hours to seconds while maintaining accuracy, demonstrating scalable stable inference for big-data distribution modeling.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1
The squared (quadratic) part of the MCTM negative log-likelihood is expressed as `||Bθ||₂²`. `ℓ₂` leverage score sampling of rows of `B` provides an ε-subspace embedding, preserving this quadratic form for all θ. This is effective because the squared term dominates the optimization, and leverage scores accurately measure row importance for `ℓ₂` norm preservation. The guarantee requires that the effective rank of `B` is not excessively high.

### Mechanism 2
The positive logarithmic part `f₂` is bounded by a constant times the squared part plus a uniform term. Using the sensitivity sampling framework, the total sensitivity is shown to be bounded by `γ(uᵢ + 1/n)`, where `uᵢ` are the `ℓ₂` leverage scores from Mechanism 1 and `γ` depends on a Lipschitz constant `c`. Sampling proportional to `uᵢ + 1/n` thus yields a coreset for `f₂` with error relative to `f₁`.

### Mechanism 3
The negative logarithmic part `f₃` has unbounded sensitivity because `-log(⟨ϱⱼ, a′ᵢⱼ⟩) → ∞` as the inner product approaches zero. The method stabilizes this by (a) restricting optimization to a domain `D(η)` where inner products exceed `η`, and (b) deterministically including all extreme points of the convex hull of `{a′ᵢⱼ}` in the coreset. This bounds sensitivity and ensures representation of geometric extremes that could cause instability.

- **Why needed:** It generalizes leverage scores to arbitrary loss functions (here the log parts). The total sensitivity dictates the coreset size.

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component map:**
Raw data Y ∈ ℝ^{n×J} -> Basis transform (compute a(y), a′(y)) -> Construct matrix B (for squared part) and keep A′ (for log parts) -> Approximate ℓ₂ leverage scores uᵢ for rows of B -> Sample k₁ points with pᵢ ∝ (uᵢ + 1/n), weight wᵢ = 1/(k₁pᵢ) -> Compute ε/J‑kernel of {a′ᵢⱼ} (convex hull points) -> Add k₂ hull points with weight 1 -> Merge into final coreset (size k = k₁ + k₂) -> Fit MCTM by optimizing weighted log‑likelihood Σ wᵢ log f_θ(yᵢ).

**Critical path:**
正确 construction of the block matrix B is mathematically precise; errors break the ℓ₂ guarantee. Leverage score approximation must be within a constant factor, typically via Johnson‑Lindenstrauss sketching. The convex hull algorithm must respect the kernel tolerance ε/J; too loose loses stabilization, too tight increases hull size. The MCTM optimizer must accept sample weights.

**Design tradeoffs:**
- **k₁ vs. k₂:** k₁ (sensitivity sampling) forms the bulk of the coreset; k₂ (hull) handles extremes. k₂ is typically small (~O(1/η²) worst‑case). For high‑d or heavy‑tailed data, increase k₂.
- **η vs. accuracy:** η = Θ(ε) in theory; smaller η improves fidelity but enlarges hull. Fixed η=2ε worked for most DGPs but failed for skew‑t (dense hull).
- **Speed vs. guarantee:** Theoretical sample size is large (O(J²d² polylog/ε²)). Empirically, smaller k is used accepting “good enough” approximation, trading theoretical guarantee for practical scalability.

**Failure signatures:**
1. **Divergence during fitting:** Missing hull points cause inner products ⟨ϱⱼ, a′ᵢⱼ⟩ to approach zero, triggering log explosion and NaNs.
2. **Poor λ error:** Coreset fails to capture dependence structure. Likely hull insufficient or sensitivity sampling missed important rows. Visualize coreset scatter to check if extreme margins are spanned.
3. **High variance across runs:** If k is too small, randomness in sensitivity sampling dominates. Increase k or use median over multiple runs.
4. **Worse than uniform:** Especially on heavy‑tailed or dense‑hull data (skew‑t, t‑copula). Increase hull size or preprocess (e.g., robust scaling).

**First 3 experiments:**
1. **Bivariate normal (DGP 1):** Baseline. ℓ₂‑hull should match ℓ₂‑only and uniform since distribution is simple. Verify log‑likelihood ratio ~1 for k=100.
2. **Geometric Mixed Distribution (DGP 4):** Complex multimodality + cross‑pattern. ℓ₂‑hull should significantly beat ℓ₂‑only and uniform in λ error and likelihood ratio at k=30,100.
3. **Covertype (real, n=300k, J=10):** Stress test scalability. Full fitting crashes/too slow. ℓ₂‑hull should fit in seconds with competitive log‑likelihood ratio vs uniform. Measure runtime reduction.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- Can the coreset size be improved by using adaptive sketching or data-dependent bounds?
- How does the method extend to deeper transformation models or non‑smooth losses?
- What are the practical limits of the convex hull approach in higher dimensions (d > 2)?

## Limitations
- Theoretical sample size may be pessimistic; empirical results use smaller `k` without formal guarantee, suggesting loose constants or unexamined data-dependent rank structure.
- Convex hull augmentation could become prohibitive for high `d` or dense extreme points (e.g., heavy‑tailed `t`‑copula); paper only tests `d≤2`.
- The combined `ℓ₂` + sensitivity + hull approach is tailored to MCTMs’ specific decomposition and not directly applicable to other unstable losses.

## Confidence
- **Coreset size guarantee (theoretical):** Medium (proof seems sound but constants loose; no empirical validation of the `Ω(dJ²)` lower bound).
- **Convex hull stabilization for log-terms:** Low (only `d≤2` empirical validation; high‑d behavior extrapolated from worst‑case geometry, not tested).
- **Empirical scalability & accuracy claims:** Medium (strong results on 14 synthetic + 2 real datasets, but limited to `J≤20`, `d≤2`; no ablation on hull size vs. `d`).

## Next Checks
1. **High-dimensional stress test:** Apply to synthetic MCTMs with `d=50` (e.g., sparse ϱ), measure hull size growth and coreset accuracy vs. `k`. Expected: hull size explodes, `ℓ₂`‑only coreset degrades.
2. **Comparison against alternative coresets:** Benchmark against MMD‑based or `k`‑MEANS coresets for density estimation on the same 2D DGPs, assessing log‑likelihood approximation per sample. Expected: `ℓ₂`‑hull dominates for MCTM‑specific loss but may underperform on pure distribution distance.
3. **Hyperparameter sensitivity analysis:** Systematically vary `η` (domain shift) and `ε` (kernel tolerance) on a heavy‑tailed DGP (e.g., `t`‑copula with `ν=3`), tracking hull size and log‑likelihood error. Expected: small `η` needed for accuracy, but hull size grows rapidly—trade‑off curve not characterized in paper.
