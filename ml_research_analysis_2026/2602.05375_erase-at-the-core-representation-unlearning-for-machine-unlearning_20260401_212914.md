# Erase at the Core: Representation Unlearning for Machine Unlearning

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2602.05375
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.05375
- **Reference count:** 21
- **Primary result:** EC substantially reduces representational similarity to the original model (CKA down to 38.68, IDI to 0.051 on ImageNet-1K Random-100) while preserving retain accuracy (72.63%), achieving the highest harmonic mean (85.75) among utility-preserving unlearning methods.

## Executive Summary
Approximate machine unlearning often suffers from *superficial forgetting*: models show near‑zero forget‑set accuracy at the logits level yet retain strong class‑discriminative information in intermediate feature representations. This paper proposes **Erase at the Core (EC)**, a framework that enforces forgetting throughout the entire network hierarchy. EC attaches auxiliary modules to intermediate layers, applies multi‑layer contrastive unlearning on the forget set alongside cross‑entropy on the retain set, and uses layer‑wise weighted losses. Experiments on ImageNet‑1K and CIFAR‑100 demonstrate that EC dramatically lowers representational similarity metrics (CKA, IDI) while maintaining high retain accuracy. Moreover, EC serves as a plug‑in, boosting the representation‑level forgetting of existing unlearning methods without sacrificing their utility.

## Method Summary
EC targets the problem of superficial forgetting by directly intervening on intermediate feature representations. The method involves three main steps: (1) attach lightweight EC modules (a sequence of convolutional blocks followed by a classifier) after selected intermediate stages of a pre‑trained backbone (e.g., ResNet‑50); (2) pre‑train the convolutional blocks of these modules using Supervised Contrastive Learning on the full dataset while freezing the backbone; (3) jointly train the backbone, final classifier, and all EC modules with a total loss that aggregates, at each supervision point, a contrastive unlearning loss (maximising similarity between forget and retain embeddings) and a cross‑entropy loss on the retain set. Layer‑specific weights increase with depth to emphasise high‑level features, and two hyperparameters (λ_CU, λ_CE) balance forgetting strength against retain utility. Exact forgetting class indices for ImageNet experiments are not disclosed, and full SupCon pre‑training details (e.g., temperature) remain unspecified; nevertheless, the reported protocol enables a minimum viable reproduction.

## Key Results
- On ImageNet‑1K Random‑100, EC reduces CKA to 38.68 and IDI to 0.051 while retaining 72.63% accuracy on the retain set.
- EC attains a harmonic mean (H‑Mean) of 85.75, the highest among utility‑preserving unlearning methods.
- As a plug‑in, EC improves CKA/IDI for baselines such as DUCK and COLA while preserving their retain performance.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1: Multi‑layer contrastive unlearning
- **Claim:** EC achieves representation‑level forgetting by applying contrastive unlearning objectives at multiple intermediate layers, pushing forget‑set embeddings toward the retain‑set manifold across the network hierarchy.
- **Mechanism:** For each attached EC module, the contrastive unlearning loss (Ll_CU) operates on L2‑normalised embeddings. It maximises cosine similarity between forget and retain samples, thereby diffusing forget‑class structure into the retain manifold and erasing class‑specific information at that depth.
- **Core assumption:** Intermediate layers retain linearly separable structure for forget classes even after logit‑level forgetting (“superficial forgetting”); direct supervision at those layers is required to alter representations.
- **Evidence anchors:** Abstract and Section 3.3 describe multi‑layer extension of the contrastive unlearning loss; corpus literature recognises superficial forgetting.
- **Break condition:** If the retain‑set manifold lacks diversity, contrastive diffusion may fail to fully disrupt forget‑class clustering, leaving residual similarity. The ablation “w/o layer‑wise ce” shows higher CKA/IDI when retain preservation is weaker at intermediate layers.

### Mechanism 2: Deep supervision with depth‑weighted losses
- **Claim:** Deep supervision with layer‑wise weights ensures forgetting signals propagate effectively, with deeper layers (encoding high‑level features) contributing more strongly.
- **Mechanism:** Total loss aggregates per‑layer contrastive unlearning and cross‑entropy losses with weights w_l. Weights increase with depth (e.g., (0.2, 0.4, 0.8, 1.0)), emphasising deeper layers where class‑discriminative features are stronger, while still maintaining utility via cross‑entropy at all layers.
- **Core assumption:** Deeper convolutional stages encode increasingly class‑specific representations; weighting them more amplifies forgetting where it matters most without losing utility from lower‑level features.
- **Evidence anchors:** Section 3.3 explains the weighting strategy; Section 4.3 (Table 8) shows (0.2,0.4,0.8,1.0) yields best H‑Mean; uniform or inverted weights degrade performance.
- **Break condition:** Over‑weighting deeper layers may cause excessive representational drift, harming retain accuracy; under‑weighting may leave high‑level features unchanged. The “w/o layer‑wise ce” ablation indicates cross‑entropy at intermediate layers stabilises training.

### Mechanism 3: Attachable EC modules as stabilisers
- **Claim:** Attachable EC modules provide a dedicated pathway for multi‑layer supervision without disrupting the backbone’s feature extraction capability.
- **Mechanism:** EC modules (Conv blocks + FC classifier) are inserted after intermediate backbone stages. The Conv blocks are pre‑trained via supervised contrastive learning while the backbone is frozen. During unlearning, these modules transform intermediate features before loss computation, acting as learned adapters that can be updated jointly with the backbone.
- **Core assumption:** The pre‑trained backbone features are useful but need targeted modification at multiple depths; EC modules allow per‑layer transformation without corrupting backbone weights prematurely.
- **Evidence anchors:** Section 3.2 details the attachment and pre‑training; Table 4 shows removal (“w/o EC modules”) leads to unstable optimisation (negative IDI), proving their stabilising role.
- **Break condition:** If EC modules are too shallow or misaligned with backbone stages, they may fail to capture layer‑specific representations effectively. Overlapping supervision signals (e.g., adding extra supervision within backbone blocks) can constrain representational reorganisation.

## Foundational Learning

### Concept: Supervised Contrastive Learning (SupCon)
- **Why needed here:** EC modules are pre‑trained with SupCon to initialise them as discriminative feature transformers before unlearning begins, ensuring they produce meaningful embeddings for the contrastive unlearning loss.
- **Quick check question:** How does SupCon differ from standard contrastive loss, and why is it suitable for pre‑training classification‑oriented auxiliary modules?

### Concept: Deep Supervision
- **Why needed here:** EC applies losses at multiple intermediate layers; understanding deep supervision (from Lee et al., 2015) is key to grasping why multi‑layer signals improve optimisation and feature learning in deep networks.
- **Quick check question:** What are the trade‑offs of applying supervision at multiple depths versus only at the output layer in terms of gradient flow and feature collapse?

### Concept: Representation Similarity Metrics (CKA, IDI)
- **Why needed here:** EC’s goal is representational forgetting, which requires metrics beyond logit accuracy. CKA measures feature similarity to the original model; IDI quantifies residual mutual information with forget labels relative to a retrained baseline.
- **Quick check question:** Why are CKA and IDI considered more stringent tests of unlearning completeness than forget‑set accuracy, and what do their values indicate about superficial versus core forgetting?

## Architecture Onboarding

**Component map:**  
Backbone (e.g., ResNet‑50 stages 1‑4) → EC modules attached after stages 1‑3 → final classifier.  
Each EC module: Conv blocks (pre‑trained with SupCon) → FC classifier.  
Losses: per‑layer contrastive unlearning (Ll_CU) + per‑layer cross‑entropy (Ll_CE) with weights w_l.

**Critical path:**  
1. Pre‑train EC module Conv blocks with SupCon on full dataset, backbone frozen.  
2. Jointly train backbone, final classifier, and all EC modules with total loss L_total = Σ w_l (λ_CU * Ll_CU + λ_CE * Ll_CE).  
3. The final classifier is also updated via cross‑entropy loss on the retain set (at the final layer).

**Design tradeoffs:**  
- **Loss weighting (w_l):** Higher weights on deeper layers improve forgetting but risk utility loss if too aggressive; default (0.2, 0.4, 0.8, 1.0) balances both.  
- **λ_CU vs λ_CE:** λ_CU controls forgetting strength; λ_CE controls retain utility. (1.5, 1.5) works well but may need adjustment per dataset/architecture.  
- **EC module depth:** More Conv blocks per module increase capacity but also computational cost.

**Failure signatures:**  
- **High CKA/IDI** with acceptable RA/TRA → superficial forgetting persists; increase λ_CU or adjust w_l to emphasise deeper layers.  
- **RA/TRA drops sharply** → over‑forgetting; reduce λ_CU, increase λ_CE, or re‑evaluate w_l distribution.  
- **Training instability / negative IDI** → likely missing EC modules; ensure they are attached and pre‑trained.

**First 3 experiments:**  
1. **Layer‑wise CKA validation:** After training EC, compute CKA between original and unlearned models at backbone layers 4.0, 4.1, 4.2. Expect a monotonically decreasing CKA with depth compared to baselines.  
2. **Ablation of layer weights:** Run with w_l = (0.5, 0.5, 0.5, 0.5) and (1.0, 0.8, 0.4, 0.2). Harmonic mean should drop significantly versus default, confirming deeper weighting is critical.  
3. **Plug‑in test:** Apply EC modules to a baseline like DUCK. Expect improved CKA/IDI while retain accuracy remains similar or slightly improves.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
1. **Theoretical certification:** What framework can certify complete removal of forget‑set information from all intermediate layers of an EC‑unlearned model? (Basis: the conclusion states formal erasure guarantees remain open; evidence needed: a formal proof or bounds showing feature distributions match a retrained baseline.)
2. **Generalisation to other architectures:** Does EC’s multi‑layer supervision remain effective and efficient for Vision Transformers or graph neural networks? (Basis: authors mention extending EC to more diverse architectures as future work; evidence needed: successful adaptation with similar CKA/IDI improvements.)
3. **Stabilisation mechanism:** What causes the stabilisation effect of EC modules that prevents over‑forgetting (e.g., negative IDI) when they are removed? (Basis: ablation shows negative IDI without EC; evidence needed: diagnostic experiments comparing training dynamics like gradient norms or feature distributions.)
4. **Compatibility with unlearning baselines:** What determines whether EC’s plug‑in integration preserves or harms retain set accuracy across different unlearning baselines? (Basis: Table 7 shows mixed effects when combining with DUCK vs. CU; evidence needed: a controlled study of loss interactions.)

## Limitations
- Exact forget class indices and seeds for ImageNet-1K Random‑100/Top‑100 are unreported, hindering exact result replication.
- Supervised Contrastive pre‑training details (augmentation pipeline, temperature) are underspecified.
- Evaluation is limited to a small set of baselines (2022‑2024); newer unlearning methods or larger models are not considered.

## Confidence
- **Mechanism 1** (Multi‑layer contrastive unlearning): Medium
- **Mechanism 2** (Deep supervision with depth‑weighted losses): High
- **Mechanism 3** (Attachable EC modules): High
- **Overall claim** (EC mitigates superficial forgetting): Medium‑High
