# Factorized Neural Implicit DMD for Parametric Dynamics
## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2603.10995
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.10995
- **Reference count:** 28
- **Primary result:** A factorized neural implicit DMD (INR-DMD) decouples spatial Koopman modes and temporal eigenvalues as physics-conditioned neural fields, enabling stable long-horizon rollouts, parameter generalization, and spectral analysis with superior accuracy and efficiency on parametric PDE benchmarks.

## Executive Summary
This paper presents INR-DMD, a method for learning the dynamics of parametric partial differential equations (PDEs) by representing Koopman operator eigenfunctions (spatial modes) and eigenvalues (temporal dynamics) as separate neural networks conditioned on physical parameters. This factorization isolates spatial patterns from their temporal evolution, allowing the system to be expressed as a low-rank linear expansion that is stable under repeated application. The approach incorporates stage-wise deflation to enforce mode orthogonality and a hybrid loss combining short- and long-horizon prediction objectives. Evaluated on Burgers' equation, 2D Navier–Stokes, Kármán vortex street, and parameterized airfoil flow, INR-DMD achieves lower errors, fewer parameters, and significantly faster inference compared to baselines like KAE, FNO, and RKN. Ablations confirm the necessity of deflation, orthogonality constraints, and multi-horizon losses for clean spectral decomposition and stable long-term predictions.

## Method Summary
INR-DMD learns a Koopman-based reduced-order model where the state is approximated by a linear combination of spatial basis functions multiplied by temporal exponentials. The spatial basis Φ(x;ξ) and the diagonal matrix of eigenvalues Λ(ξ) are each represented by a separate multi-layer perceptron (MLP) that takes the spatial coordinate x and a physics parameter vector ξ as input. This factorization enables continuous evaluation in space and smooth interpolation across parameter values. During training, modes are learned sequentially. For each new mode, a raw candidate is computed and then deflated—orthogonalized against all previously learned modes—by subtracting its projection onto the existing basis. Only the networks for the current mode are trained, using a loss that combines a one-step prediction error and a multi-step autoregressive rollout loss. At inference, given a new parameter ξ and an initial snapshot, modal coefficients are obtained via a least-squares solve, and future states are generated by repeatedly multiplying by Λ(η).

## Key Results
- Achieves a relative MSE of 1.35×10⁻⁴ on Burgers' equation (vs. 1.35×10⁻³ for P-DMD) with 60× faster inference.
- Attains the lowest error and fastest inference across all tested benchmarks (Navier–Stokes, Kármán vortex street, airfoil flow) compared to KAE, FNO, and RKN.
- Ablations show that removing stage-wise deflation, orthogonality constraints, or long-horizon losses leads to dramatic increases in error (e.g., from 8.093e-7 to 1.819e-4 without deflation) and unstable rollouts.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1
- **Claim:** Decoupling spatial modes and temporal evolution into separate, physics-conditioned neural fields enables stable long-term rollouts and parameter generalization.
- **Mechanism:** The method represents the Koopman expansion u(x, t;ξ) ≈ Φ(x;ξ)ᵀ Λ(ξ)ᵗ z by parameterizing Φ(x;ξ) and Λ(ξ) with independent networks. This isolates how a flow pattern depends on space and configuration ξ from how its amplitude evolves in time. Future states are generated by matrix multiplication with Λ(η), a stable, linear operation after an initial projection.
- **Core assumption:** The dominant dynamics can be captured by a low-rank linear Koopman operator whose eigenfunctions vary smoothly with ξ and whose eigenvalues depend only on ξ.
- **Evidence anchors:** [abstract] decoupling enables stable long-horizon rollouts, generalization, spectral analysis; [section 3.3] explicit formulation of Φθ(x;ξ) and Λη(ξ) as neural fields; [corpus] weak direct support from neighboring neural operator and Koopman literature.
- **Break condition:** If the true dynamics require a highly nonlinear or non-separable representation, the factorized form becomes too restrictive, causing large approximation errors that destabilize rollouts.

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim:** Stage-wise deflation with enforced orthogonality prevents mode collapse and correlation, yielding clean, interpretable spectral decompositions.
- **Mechanism:** Modes are learned sequentially. When training mode p, all earlier networks are frozen. The raw candidate mode ˜ϕₚ is deflated by subtracting its projection onto the span of Φ<p, guaranteeing orthogonality by construction. The model is then trained to minimize loss using only mode p's contribution, avoiding mode competition.
- **Core assumption:** True Koopman eigenfunctions for dominant dynamics are mutually orthogonal under the data distribution; enforcing this during learning separates distinct physical patterns.
- **Evidence anchors:** [section 3.4.2] deflation strategy enforces orthogonality by construction; [section 4.5] ablation "w/o Deflation" shows correlated modes and large error increase; [corpus] neighboring work on DMD discusses eigenfunctions but not this sequential deflation procedure.
- **Break condition:** If true eigenfunctions are non-orthogonal (due to operator non-normality or finite-data effects), strict orthogonality may force modes to be linear combinations that misrepresent coherent structures, harming interpretability and generalization.

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim:** A hybrid loss combining short-horizon DMD loss and long-horizon OptDMD-style loss directly penalizes error accumulation, stabilizing multi-step predictions.
- **Mechanism:** The loss L = αL_short + βL_long mixes a one-step prediction error with a loss evaluated on an autoregressive rollout from the initial condition. This encourages both local accuracy and consistency when applying the learned linear operator repeatedly.
- **Core assumption:** Minimizing multi-step prediction error yields stable long-term rollouts, whereas one-step-optimized models overfit transient details and accumulate error when used autoregressively.
- **Evidence anchors:** [section 3.4.1] mixed objective introduced; [section 4.5] "w/o Long Horizon" ablation shows dramatic degradation in long-horizon stability; [corpus] related work on autoregressive PDE models addresses error accumulation but not via explicit Koopman-structured multi-step losses.
- **Break condition:** Improper weighting (e.g., β too low) can cause overfitting to short-term transitions, as suggested by the sensitivity sweep in Fig. 6.

## Foundational Learning
- **Concept: Koopman Operator Theory & Dynamic Mode Decomposition (DMD)**
  - *Why needed:* The entire method is a neural parameterization of a Koopman-based reduced-order model. Understanding that nonlinear dynamics can be lifted to a linear Koopman operator and approximated via DMD is fundamental to grasping the model's objective and the meaning of Φ (modes) and Λ (eigenvalues).
  - *Quick check:* Given snapshots x₀, x₁, x₂, what does the DMD operator A approximate, and how are its eigenvalues related to the system's dominant frequencies and growth/decay rates?

- **Concept: Implicit Neural Representations (INRs) / Neural Fields**
  - *Why needed:* The model uses MLPs to represent continuous functions Φ(x;ξ) and Λ(ξ). This allows evaluation at arbitrary spatial coordinates and physics codes, enabling generalization to new geometries and parameters without retraining on a fixed grid.
  - *Quick check:* How does a coordinate-based MLP (like an INR) differ from a CNN trained on a fixed grid, and why is the former beneficial for parameterized PDE families?

- **Concept: Spectral Decomposition & Orthogonality in Reduced-Order Modeling**
  - *Why needed:* The method aims to extract spatial modes that are mutually orthogonal and each associated with a distinct temporal frequency. This interpretable spectral structure is key for analysis and stable multi-mode composition.
  - *Quick check:* Why might learning all Koopman modes jointly lead to correlated, redundant bases, and how does sequential deflation address this?

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map:**  
  Input: (spatial coordinate x, physics code ξ) -> Φ-network (MLP) -> spatial basis matrix Φ(x;ξ) [r × 2]  
  Input: (physics code ξ) -> Λ-network (MLP) -> eigenvalue vector Λ(ξ) [r complex]  
  Projection: (snapshots u(t), Φ(x;ξ)) -> compute Φ†(ξ) -> modal coefficients z(t)  
  Prediction: z(t₀), Λ(ξ), Φ(x;ξ) -> Λ(ξ)^(t-t₀) -> û(x,t) = Φ(x;ξ) · diag(Λ(ξ)^(t-t₀)) · z(t₀)

- **Critical path:**  
  1. Data preparation: Generate trajectories for a set of physics codes ξ, storing full-resolution fields u(x,t;ξ).  
  2. Mode 1 initialization: Train Φ₁, λ₁ networks from scratch on full loss L = αL_short + βL_long.  
  3. Deflation loop for Modes 2..r: For each new mode p, evaluate frozen Φ<p to compute projection operator, compute deflated raw mode ˜ϕₚ, update only networks for mode p.  
  4. Inference: For new ξ and initial u(x,t₀), compute z(t₀) = Φ†(ξ)u(t₀) via least-squares; then evaluate Φ(x;ξ) and Λ(η) and apply Λ(η)^k for k-step rollout.

- **Design tradeoffs:**  
  - *Model capacity:* Wider/deeper networks for Φ/Λ can capture more complex mode variations with ξ but risk overfitting and slower training. Default: Φ uses 128×3 MLP, Λ uses 64×3 MLP.  
  - *Loss weighting (α, β):* Balance between short- and long-horizon loss is critical. Sweeping is recommended; typical values α=0.1–0.9, β=0.05–0.95 depending on dynamics stiffness.  
  - *Deflation vs. joint ortho-regularization:* Stage-wise deflation is empirically superior to joint training with an orthogonality regularizer.

- **Failure signatures:**  
  - *Diverging long-horizon rollout:* Error explodes after a few steps. Likely causes: missing long-horizon loss (β≈0), insufficient rank r, or unstable |λ|>1 not regularized.  
  - *Noisy, correlated basis functions:* Indicates failed deflation (e.g., gradients leaking through frozen networks, incorrect deflation step). Ensure stop-gradient on Φ<p and Φ†<p>.  
  - *Poor generalization to unseen ξ:* Check that ξ is properly normalized and training covers parameter space diversity. Overfitting to training ξ may occur if code space is sparse.

- **First 3 experiments:**  
  1. Burgers' equation (1D, viscosity parameter ν): Validates factorization; single parameter, clear dissipation; expected accurate shock/smoothing with few modes (r=2).  
  2. Double shear layer (2D, initial shear separation parameter): Tests generalization across initial conditions in a nonlinear, chaotic flow; requires more modes (r=6) to capture vortex merging; validates mode orthogonality and long-term stability.  
  3. Kármán vortex street (2D, obstacle x-position parameter): Tests generalization across boundary conditions (cylinder location); challenges: geometry change alters boundary constraints; expected Φ(x;ξ) adapts to cylinder position; ablation impact critical for separating shedding frequencies.

## Limitations
- Assumes dominant dynamics are well-approximated by a low-rank, *linear* Koopman operator with *orthogonal* eigenfunctions under the data distribution; this may fail for strongly nonlinear, non-normal, or high-frequency dynamics.
- Generalization to *unseen physics parameters* ξ is demonstrated, but robustness to *unseen initial conditions* or radically different boundary conditions is not explicitly tested.
- Enforced orthogonality is a design choice, not a guaranteed property of true Koopman eigenfunctions; in systems with non-orthogonal eigenfunctions, learned modes may be physically misleading.
- Per-inference projection step (least-squares solve) has a cost that scales with snapshot batch size and mode count; wall-clock comparisons to amortized surrogates like FNO are not detailed.

## Confidence
- **Factorization enables stable long-horizon rollouts:** High. Supported by ablations and consistent error reductions across multiple chaotic benchmarks.
- **Stage-wise deflation yields clean, orthogonal modes:** Medium. Ablations confirm necessity, but interpretability of orthogonal modes as true physical structures is not independently validated.
- **Parameter generalization performance (superior accuracy & efficiency):** High for relative comparisons within benchmark suite; Medium for absolute claim of "superior" given competitive neighbor papers.
