# AgriPath: A Systematic Exploration of Architectural Trade-offs for Crop Disease Classification
## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2603.13354
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.13354
- Reference count: 18
- Primary result: CNNs excel in lab settings but fail in field; contrastive VLMs offer robust cross-domain performance; generative VLMs tolerate distributional shifts at the cost of parse failures.

## Executive Summary
The paper addresses unreliable crop disease detection across lab and field conditions by systematically comparing CNNs, contrastive VLMs, and generative VLMs. Using the new AgriPath-LF16 dataset (30k images, 16 crops, 41 diseases) with explicit lab/field separation, findings show CNNs achieve high lab accuracy (F1 0.973) but collapse under domain shift (field F1 0.045). Contrastive VLMs (e.g., CLIP) provide stable cross-domain robustness, while generative VLMs (e.g., Qwen2.5-VL) show greatest resilience but introduce parse failures like hallucinations and empty outputs. The study concludes that model selection must align with deployment context: CNNs for controlled environments, contrastive VLMs for balanced robustness, and generative VLMs for flexible, domain-tolerant applications despite generation risks.

## Method Summary
The study conducts a controlled comparison of three architecture families—CNNs (ResNet), contrastive VLMs (CLIP), and generative VLMs (Qwen2.5-VL)—under unified training and evaluation protocols. All models are trained on the AgriPath-LF16 training split (lab-only) and evaluated on both lab and field test sets. Performance is measured via macro-F1 score for classification accuracy and Parse Success Rate (PSR) to quantify valid output generation. This design isolates the impact of architectural design on domain generalization and output reliability.

## Key Results
- CNNs achieve peak lab accuracy (macro-F1 0.973) but suffer catastrophic domain collapse when applied to field data (field F1 0.045).
- Contrastive VLMs maintain stable cross-domain performance with robust zero-shot transfer, showing minimal degradation between lab and field conditions.
- Generative VLMs demonstrate the strongest resilience to distributional variation but exhibit non-trivial parse failures (39.7% empty outputs, 12.69% hallucinations), impacting deployment reliability.

## Why This Works
The performance differences stem from inherent architectural biases: CNNs overfit to lab-specific textures and backgrounds due to high-capacity supervised learning; contrastive VLMs learn semantically aligned image-text representations that generalize across domains via contrastive objectives; generative VLMs, trained on diverse internet data, inherently model broader distributional shifts but lack constraints for structured output, leading to hallucinations. The explicit lab/field split in AgriPath-LF16 reveals these trade-offs clearly.

## Foundational Learning
- **Domain Shift**: Covariate mismatch between training and deployment environments causes model failure; needed to understand generalization limits. Quick check: Compare performance on in-distribution vs. out-of-distribution test sets.
- **Contrastive Learning**: Aligns image and text embeddings in a shared space; needed for zero-shot transfer. Quick check: Evaluate CLIP-style model on novel categories without fine-tuning.
- **Generative VLMs**: Autoregressive models that generate text from image prompts; needed for flexible reasoning but prone to unconstrained outputs. Quick check: Measure parse failure rates on agricultural queries.
- **Parse Success Rate (PSR)**: Metric for valid, non-hallucinated outputs; needed to assess deployment safety. Quick check: Compute % of responses that match expected format and content.
- **Background Bias**: Models relying on non-symptomatic contextual cues; needed to diagnose domain collapse. Quick check: Occlude backgrounds in images and observe accuracy changes.

## Architecture Onboarding
Component map: Input Image -> CNN Backbone (ResNet) / Image Encoder (CLIP, Qwen2.5-VL) -> Task Head (Linear Classifier) / Text Decoder (Qwen2.5-VL) -> Output (Class Label / Generated Text). Critical path: For CNNs, feature extraction -> classification; for contrastive VLMs, image-text similarity scoring; for generative VLMs, multimodal prompt -> autoregressive text generation. Design tradeoffs: CNNs offer high accuracy but low robustness; contrastive VLMs balance accuracy and robustness with moderate compute; generative VLMs maximize robustness at cost of parse reliability and higher inference cost. Failure signatures: CNNs fail on field images (sharp F1 drop); generative VLMs produce empty or hallucinated outputs (low PSR). First three experiments: 1) Train ResNet on AgriPath-LF16 lab set, evaluate on lab and field to quantify domain collapse. 2) Zero-shot test CLIP on both sets by embedding class names, measure macro-F1. 3) Prompt Qwen2.5-VL with disease queries, compute PSR and F1 on both sets, analyze failure modes.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
### Open Question 1
- Question: How can the under-representation of field-sourced samples for certain crop-disease pairs in AgriPath-LF16 be addressed to better characterize model robustness under real-world variability?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] "Future work should expand field data collection to reduce domain imbalance and increase environmental coverage."
- Why unresolved: The current dataset exhibits imbalance with limited field data for some classes, constraining the evaluation of robustness across diverse deployment conditions.
- What evidence would resolve it: Construct a larger, balanced dataset with expanded field imagery across all crop-disease pairs, then conduct controlled experiments comparing model performance (macro-F1, PSR) under domain shift to quantify robustness improvements.

### Open Question 2
- Question: What is the impact of integrating multimodal agricultural metadata (e.g., region, climate, soil) on the reliability and hallucination rates of generative VLMs in crop disease classification?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] "Integrating contextual metadata into generative VLM pipelines may improve reliability and reduce hallucinations through agricultural grounding."
- Why unresolved: Current models lack such metadata, and generative VLMs show non-trivial hallucination rates (e.g., 39.7% empty, 12.69% hallucinations), limiting deployment safety.
- What evidence would resolve it: Develop a VLM variant that incorporates metadata as auxiliary inputs, train on a metadata-augmented version of AgriPath-LF16, and compare Parse Success Rate (PSR) and F1 against baselines to assess reductions in invalid outputs.

### Open Question 3
- Question: How can temporal information from longitudinal imagery be leveraged to improve the accuracy and robustness of crop disease classification models, particularly for diseases with dynamic progression?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] "Temporal modelling of disease progression using longitudinal imagery is another promising direction."
- Why unresolved: The dataset is static, omitting disease progression over time, which is critical for accurate diagnosis in dynamic field conditions.
- What evidence would resolve it: Collect time-series image sequences for key crop-disease pairs, design architectures (e.g., video models, temporal attention) to capture progression, and evaluate on progression-aware classification tasks, measuring gains in cross-domain F1.

### Open Question 4
- Question: Can reasoning-aware fine-tuning techniques that emphasize localisation of symptomatic regions reduce the reliance of models on background cues and enhance cross-domain robustness in crop disease classification?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] "reasoning-aware fine-tuning that promotes localisation of symptomatic regions and suppresses background cues may improve robustness."
- Why unresolved: CNNs show severe domain collapse (e.g., lab-to-field F1 drop from 0.968 to 0.045), possibly due to overfitting to lab-specific backgrounds or local textures; VLMs may still suffer from similar biases.
- What evidence would resolve it: Implement fine-tuning with attention regularization or region-based losses to focus on disease symptoms, then test lab-field generalisation (F1 metrics) compared to standard fine-tuning, using failure analysis to verify reduced background reliance.

### Open Question 5
- Question: What are the practical challenges and trust calibration issues when deploying crop disease detection systems in real-world farming scenarios, and how can model outputs be designed to support farmer decision-making effectively?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] "real-world farmer-facing evaluations would clarify usability, trust calibration, and practical performance beyond benchmarks."
- Why unresolved: Current evaluations rely on standardized benchmarks like AgriPath-LF16, but real-world deployment involves human factors like trust, interpretability, and integration into farming workflows that are untested.
- What evidence would resolve it: Conduct field studies with farmers using prototype systems, collect qualitative and quantitative data on trust, usability, and decision accuracy, and correlate with benchmark metrics to identify gaps and design improvements (e.g., confidence scores, explanations).

## Limitations
- Dataset scope: AgriPath-LF16’s 30k images across 16 crops/41 diseases may not capture global agronomic diversity; field conditions lack controlled environmental variance.
- Deployment practicality: Compute cost, latency, and parse failure impact of generative VLMs on edge devices are unquantified.
- Metric interpretation: Parse Success Rate (PSR) definitions and failure causes require granular error analysis beyond aggregate scores.

## Confidence
- CNN domain collapse: High (aligns with covariate shift literature; lab-to-field drop is expected).
- Contrastive VLM robustness: Medium (zero-shot transfer proven in general vision, but disease-specific efficacy needs domain validation).
- Generative VLM resilience vs. parse failures: Low (hallucination rates in agricultural contexts uncharacterized; risk-reward trade-off speculative without failure-mode statistics).

## Next Checks
