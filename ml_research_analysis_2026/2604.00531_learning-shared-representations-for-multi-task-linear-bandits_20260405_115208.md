# Learning Shared Representations for Multi-Task Linear Bandits

## Quick Facts
- **ArXiv ID:** 2604.00531
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.00531
- **Reference count:** 26
- **Primary result:** Proposes an OFUL algorithm for multi-task linear bandits with shared low‑rank structure achieving cumulative regret Õ(√drNT), improving over Õ(dT√N) for independent tasks.

## Executive Summary
The paper addresses the problem of multi‑task linear bandits where T tasks share a common low‑dimensional latent representation of dimension r. It introduces a three‑phase algorithm: (1) a random exploration phase to gather data, (2) a spectral initialization step that estimates the shared low‑rank matrix B* from truncated aggregated data, and (3) an Optimism in the Face of Uncertainty Linear (OFUL) phase that uses a novel confidence set construction leveraging the low‑rank structure. The analysis yields a cumulative regret bound of Õ(√drNT) under assumptions of Gaussian exploration features, sub‑Gaussian noise, and a small noise‑to‑signal ratio. This bound improves over the independent‑task baseline Õ(dT√N) when the latent dimension r is much smaller than d and T is large. Simulations on synthetic data confirm sublinear per‑task regret and demonstrate the benefits of sharing structure across tasks.

## Method Summary
The proposed OFUL algorithm proceeds in three stages. First, for N₁ rounds, each task selects actions randomly from a Gaussian distribution N(0, I_d) and observes rewards. Second, the exploration data from all tasks are aggregated into a matrix Θ̆ after element‑wise truncation of the rewards using a data‑dependent threshold α; the top‑r left singular vectors of Θ̆ serve as an estimate ̂B of the shared basis B*. Third, for the remaining N‑N₁ rounds and each task, the algorithm constructs a confidence ellipsoid β_{n,t} around the task‑specific parameter estimate θ̂_t = ̂B ŵ_t, where ŵ_t is obtained by ridge regression of the task’s rewards onto ̂B. Actions are chosen by maximizing the inner product over β_{n,t} and the feasible action set (assumed bounded). Regularization (λ) and truncation ensure numerical stability and theoretical guarantees.

## Key Results
- Theoretical guarantee: Cumulative regret Õ(√drNT) under suitable conditions, a significant improvement over the Õ(dT√N) bound for solving tasks independently.
- Empirical validation: Simulations show sublinear per‑task regret; performance improves as latent dimension r and feature dimension d decrease, and as number of tasks T increases.
- Confidence set innovation: The radius scales with √r (the latent dimension) rather than √d, exploiting the shared low‑rank structure.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1
- **Claim:** Spectral initialization on truncated aggregated data provides a sufficiently accurate estimate of the shared low‑rank feature matrix B*.
- **Mechanism:** During exploration, each task collects (x, y) pairs. The algorithm aggregates all data into a matrix Θ̆ = (1/N₁) Σ_t Σ_n x_{n,t} y_{n,t} e_t^⊤ after element‑wise truncation of y values based on a data‑dependent threshold α. The top‑r left singular vectors of Θ̆ are taken as ̂B.
- **Core assumption:** Feature vectors x_{n,t} are i.i.d. Gaussian during exploration (Assumption II.2 extended), and noise is sub‑Gaussian. This ensures truncated summands are sub‑Gaussian, enabling concentration.
- **Evidence anchors:** [section: "Spectral Initialization" (lines 6‑10 of Algorithm III.1) and Proposition III.1]; [section: Appendix B explains truncation converts summands to sub‑Gaussian for better concentration].
- **Break condition:** The guarantee fails if the noise‑to‑signal ratio (NSR = Tσ² / σ*_min²) is too high or if exploration samples N₁T are insufficient relative to d, r, κ, µ (see condition in Proposition III.1).

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim:** A task‑specific confidence ellipsoid can be constructed that contains θ*_t with high probability, and its size scales with √r instead of √d by leveraging the shared low‑rank structure and the ̂B estimate.
- **Mechanism:** For each task t after exploration, the confidence set β_{n,t} is defined using the norm ∥θ - θ̂_{n,t}∥_{V̄_{n,t}}, where V̄_{n,t} = λI + Σ_{m} x_{m,t} x_{m,t}^⊤. The center θ̂_{n,t} = ̂B ŵ_{n,t} uses the ridge regression solution in the projected r‑dimensional space (ŵ_{n,t} = (̂B^⊤ V̄_{n,t} ̂B)^{-1} ̂B^⊤ Φ_{n,t}^⊤ Y_{n,t}). The radius includes terms dependent on ∥̂B - B*∥ and σ*_max/σ*_min.
- **Core assumption:** Assumptions II.1 (low‑rank model), II.2 (sub‑Gaussian noise), II.3 (bounded task coefficients for incoherence). The confidence set proof relies on the error decomposition in Equation (3) and bounding mis‑specification error from ̂B.
- **Evidence anchors:** [section: Equation (2) defines β_{n,t}; Theorem III.2 gives the guarantee]; [section: The remark after Theorem III.2 explicitly notes the radius scales with √r vs. √d in standard OFUL [12]].
- **Break condition:** The confidence set guarantee requires N₁T large enough to bound subspace distance SD(̂B, B*) ≤ δ₀ (from Proposition III.1). If δ₀ is large, the mis‑specification terms in the radius (e.g., (1+δ₀) factor and terms proportional to δ₀) degrade the bound.

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim:** The integrated algorithm (random exploration → spectral initialization → OFUL with the novel confidence set) achieves cumulative regret Õ(√drNT), improving over independent OFUL's Õ(dT√N) when r ≪ d and T is large, under a small noise‑to‑signal ratio (NSR).
- **Mechanism:** The exploration phase collects diverse data for spectral initialization. The OFUL phase then for each task t selects actions by maximizing ⟨x, θ⟩ over x ∈ X and θ ∈ β_{n,t}. The regret analysis decomposes into exploration regret R₁ and learning regret R₂. The low‑dimensional representation allows per‑task uncertainty to be characterized by r‑dimensional terms in V_{n,t} = ̂B^⊤ V̄_{n,t} ̂B, leading to the √(dr) scaling instead of √(d²T).
- **Core assumption:** All prior assumptions plus that the number of post‑exploration rounds (N‑N₁) is large enough for the OFUL phase to dominate, and the NSR is small enough that the √(NSR) term in the final bound (Theorem III.3) does not overwhelm the √(drN T) scaling.
- **Evidence anchors:** [abstract: "cumulative regret bound of Õ(√drNT)" and comparison to Õ(dT√N)"]; [section: Theorem III.3 states the bound and its dependence on NSR, κ, etc.].
- **Break condition:** The regret improvement over independent learning requires r ≪ min{d, T} and a small NSR. If NSR is large, the term √(NSR) in the bound can make the constant large, potentially negating the benefit. Also, the condition N₁T > C µ² κ² L² d (N‑N₁)(rκ²+NSR) must hold; if N₁ too small or N‑N₁ too large, the sample complexity may not be met.

## Foundational Learning
- **Concept: Low‑rank matrix factorization (Θ* = B*W*)**  
  - **Why needed here:** Encodes the core assumption that T tasks share a common r‑dimensional subspace (via B*) and have task‑specific coefficients (W*). This structure enables sample efficiency gains.  
  - **Quick check:** If Θ* is rank r, what does that imply about the relationship between the reward parameter vectors {θ*_t}?

- **Concept: Spectral initialization for non‑convex problems**  
  - **Why needed here:** Directly optimizing the least‑squares loss over (B, W) is non‑convex. A good initial point near (B*, W*) ensures subsequent ridge regression in the projected space is accurate. Truncation is used to get sub‑Gaussian concentration.  
  - **Quick check:** Why is truncation of y‑values applied before computing the SVD for ̂B?

- **Concept: Confidence ellipsoids in linear bandits (OFUL principle)**  
  - **Why needed here:** The algorithm must balance exploration and exploitation. The confidence set must contain θ*_t with high probability to ensure optimism is justified. The construction here adapts the standard approach [12] to the low‑rank setting by using the projected design matrix.  
  - **Quick check:** In the standard OFUL confidence set, what norm is used and why? How does this paper’s construction (Equation 2) differ in scale?

## Architecture Onboarding
Component map: Exploration Module (random Gaussian actions) -> Spectral Initialization Module (truncated aggregation, top‑r SVD) -> OFUL Phase (per‑task confidence ellipsoid construction and action selection)

Critical path: The algorithm first performs N₁ rounds of random exploration for all tasks. Then it computes ̂B from the aggregated truncated data. After that, for each task and each subsequent round (n = N₁+1 to N), it updates the confidence set based on past data and selects actions by maximizing over the ellipsoid and the bounded action set. The OFUL phase is sequential and task‑specific, with updates depending on the shared ̂B.

Design tradeoffs:
- Gaussian exploration vs. realism: Theoretical guarantees require Gaussian x_{n,t} during exploration for spectral initialization concentration. In practice, random actions from a bounded set might be used, but the theory may break down. Tradeoff: sample efficiency (analysis) vs. practical action space constraints.
- Truncation parameter α: Chosen as data‑dependent (α = 9κ²µ² * average y²). Too small discards useful data; too large yields insufficient sub‑Gaussianity.
- Regularization λ: Affects both initialization (through V̄_{n,t}) and final confidence sets. Larger λ improves conditioning but adds bias.

Failure signatures:
1. **Subspace estimation failure**: SD(̂B, B*) > δ₀. Symptoms: per‑task regret grows superlinearly; confidence sets fail to contain θ*_t (if simulator available). Cause: N₁T too small, NSR too high, or feature distribution deviates from Gaussian.
2. **Confidence set failure**: θ*_t ∉ β_{n,t} for some t,n. Symptoms: algorithm becomes over‑optimistic, selects suboptimal actions, regret increases. Cause: ̂B inaccurate (see 1) or noise variance underestimated, or log‑det term grows too fast.
3. **Regret scaling mismatch**: Observed per‑task regret scales worse than √N. Symptoms: plots show linear or near‑linear growth. Cause: latent dimension r not small enough, T not large enough to see benefit; NSR large; or algorithm parameters (N₁, λ) not tuned.

3. Vary N₁ (exploration rounds): Fix d=100, r=2, T=100, total N=600. Test N₁ = {10,20,40}. Expect a U‑shaped curve: too small N₁ → bad ̂B, high regret; too large N₁ → less data for OFUL phase, higher regret. Find optimal N₁ ~ O(r log d / σ*_min²) from theory.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- **Open Question 1:** Can the theoretical guarantees be extended beyond Gaussian feature vectors and sub‑Gaussian noise in the exploration phase? The remark after Theorem III.2 explicitly states that relaxing the Gaussian model for the initial exploration phase is part of future work. Current proofs rely critically on Gaussianity for concentration.
- **Open Question 2:** How does the algorithm’s cumulative regret behave when the noise‑to‑signal ratio (NSR) is large? The theoretical bound scales with √NSR, but experiments only consider small NSR (e.g., variance 0.01). The regime of large NSR remains unexplored.
- **Open Question 3:** Is the dependence of the confidence set radius on problem‑dependent constants like the incoherence parameter µ and condition number κ tight? The proof may be loose; no lower bound demonstrates necessity of these factors.
- **Open Question 4:** Would an adaptive exploration strategy during the initial phase reduce the required number of exploration rounds N₁ compared to uniform random exploration? The algorithm uses a fixed predetermined N₁; adaptive methods could focus sampling on directions that most reduce uncertainty in B*.

## Limitations
- Theoretical guarantees rely on strong assumptions: Gaussian feature distribution during exploration, sub‑Gaussian noise with known variance, small NSR, and exact knowledge of incoherence parameters κ, µ.
- The constant C in the sample complexity condition N₁T > C µ²κ²L²d(N‑N₁)/(rκ²+NSR) is unspecified, making it difficult to predict when spectral initialization succeeds in practice.
- The truncation parameter C̃ = 9κ²µ² may be overly conservative, and hyperparameters (λ, L) are assumed known and perfectly tuned.

## Confidence
- Spectral initialization accuracy: Medium (proof holds under strong assumptions; unknown constants and sensitivity to non‑Gaussian features reduce practical confidence).
- Confidence set scaling (√r vs √d): Medium (relies on accurate ̂B; mis‑specification terms may dominate if initialization error is large).
- Overall regret bound Õ(√drNT): Medium (conditional on small NSR and sample‑complexity condition; bound’s constant involves unspecified µ, κ, C).
