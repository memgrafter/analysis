# A visual observation on the geometry of UMAP projections of the difference vectors of antonym and synonym word pair embeddings

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2603.24150
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.24150
- Reference count: 9
- Primary result: UMAP projections (Euclidean) of difference vectors reveal a persistent "swirl" pattern that separates real antonym/synonym pairs from shuffled controls, enabling near state-of-the-art transductive classification (F1 up to 0.9643).

## Executive Summary
This study investigates how antonymy and synonymy are geometrically represented in word embedding spaces. By analyzing difference vectors (word2 - word1) from antonym and synonym pairs, the authors observe a consistent "swirl" pattern in 2D UMAP projections when using Euclidean distance and including shuffled control pairs. This pattern reliably separates semantically related pairs from random pairings across multiple embedding models (word2vec, GloVe, BERT, OpenAI text-embedding). The structure supports a transductive classifier that clusters the projected vectors and assigns labels based on training set votes, achieving performance near the state of the art on the Antonym-Synonym Differentiation task and significantly outperforming classifiers using raw embeddings.

## Method Summary
The authors used the Stuttgart dataset of antonym and synonym word pairs, generating shuffled control pairs by recombining words within each relation type. For each embedding model, they extracted vectors for individual words, computed difference vectors (E2 - E1), and applied UMAP with Euclidean distance (typically n_neighbors=30, min_dist=0.1) to project all difference vectors into 2D. The resulting point cloud exhibited a swirl pattern that separated real pairs from shuffled ones. For classification, they employed a transductive approach: after projection, they discarded shuffled points, clustered the remaining real pairs (using KMeans or Spectral clustering), assigned cluster labels via majority vote from the training set, and evaluated on the test set under both lexical (no word overlap) and Stuttgart predefined splits.

## Key Results
- A persistent swirl pattern emerges in 2D UMAP projections (Euclidean metric) of difference vectors across word2vec, GloVe, BERT, and OpenAI text-embedding models, cleanly separating real antonym/synonym pairs from shuffled controls; the pattern vanishes with cosine metric, concatenation vectors, or PCA/t-SNE (except for the largest models).
- Including shuffled pairs is crucial; without them the swirl becomes diffuse and less coherent for most models.
- The transductive classifier (UMAP projection + clustering) achieves near state-of-the-art F1 scores on the Antonym-Synonym Differentiation task (e.g., 0.9643 lexical split with text-embedding-3-large), significantly outperforming classifiers on raw embeddings.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
**Mechanism 1**: Euclidean distance in UMAP reveals the swirl because it preserves both magnitude and directional variance of difference vectors, which may encode a "salient dimension" for antonymy. Cosine similarity normalizes magnitude and collapses this structure. The swirl emerges as a low-dimensional manifold approximation of the high-dimensional distribution shaped by the semantic constraint (single differing dimension) and the vector difference operation. *Break condition*: Swirl disappears if cosine distance is used in UMAP, if concatenation vectors are projected, or if PCA/t-SNE is used (except for text-embedding-3-large).

**Mechanism 2**: The difference vector operation creates a point cloud where the constrained variance of real pairs (primarily along a limited set of dimensions) versus the broader, unconstrained distribution of shuffled pairs becomes topologically separable under Euclidean UMAP as a swirl. *Break condition*: Swirl degrades without shuffled pairs or when using concatenation vectors.

**Mechanism 3**: The transductive classifier succeeds because the swirl provides a clear, clusterable manifold where the class boundary is primarily topological (loop-based) rather than linear. Simple clustering in 2D can exploit this pre-existing separation, bypassing the need for complex high-dimensional classifiers. *Break condition*: Without the swirl (e.g., wrong metric), transductive performance drops to near chance.

## Foundational Learning
- **Euclidean vs. Cosine Distance in High-D Spaces**: Euclidean captures absolute vector differences (including magnitude), while cosine focuses on orientation. The swirl's metric sensitivity suggests magnitude information in difference vectors is crucial for the effect. *Quick check*: If two difference vectors point in nearly the same direction but have different magnitudes, Euclidean distance will be larger for the larger magnitude, while cosine distance will be near zero; which metric would place them closer in UMAP's neighbor graph if magnitude matters?
- **UMAP's Manifold Assumption and Metric Sensitivity**: UMAP assumes data lies on a manifold and preserves local topology; the chosen metric defines the notion of "closeness" and thus the manifold structure. *Quick check*: Changing the metric alters the nearest neighbor graph, which most directly changes the global topology of the final low-dimensional projection.
- **Difference Vectors as Semantic Relation Encoders**: In word2vec-like spaces, analogies are often solved by vector arithmetic (king - man + woman ≈ queen), indicating difference vectors encode relational information. For synonyms, the difference vector should be small and sparse (many dimensions cancel); for antonyms, it may align with a salient semantic axis. *Quick check*: Would you expect synonym pairs to have smaller average magnitude difference vectors than random word pairs? ## Architecture Onboarding
**Component map**: Data Loader -> Embedding Retriever -> Vector Transformer -> Projector -> Label Applicator -> Transductive Classifier

**Critical path**: Word Pair -> Embeddings -> Difference Vectors -> UMAP(Euclidean) -> 2D Plot / Cluster Input

**Design tradeoffs**:
- Metric choice: Euclidean vs. cosine is the most critical switch; enables/disables the swirl.
- Control pairs: Including shuffled pairs sharpens the swirl but changes the classification task to "real vs. random" rather than pure antonym/synonym.
- Transductive vs. Inductive: Transductive leverages the full test set structure during projection; not comparable to standard inductive baselines but demonstrates geometric separability.
- Model choice: Larger, newer models (text-embedding-3-large) show more robust swirls even with PCA/t-SNE, suggesting scale affects geometry.

**Failure signatures**:
- No swirl appears: Check if UMAP metric is cosine, if using concatenation vectors, or if using PCA/t-SNE (except for largest models).
- Swirl is noisy/diffuse: Check if shuffled control pairs are included; remove them to see if structure degrades.
- Classification fails: Ensure transductive setup uses the same UMAP projection for train and test (all points projected together). If using an inductive classifier on raw vectors, expect near-chance performance for smaller models.

**First 3 experiments**:
1. **Replicate Swirl Base Case**: On BERT-base context-free embeddings, generate difference vectors for all 4 datasets (antonym, synonym, shuffled antonym, shuffled synonym). Run UMAP with n_neighbors=30, min_dist=0.1, metric='euclidean'. Verify the 4-strand swirl pattern appears.
2. **Metric Ablation**: Repeat Experiment 1 but change UMAP metric to 'cosine'. Document that the swirl collapses into a less separable blob.
3. **Shuffle Necessity Check**: Repeat Experiment 1 but exclude the shuffled datasets (only plot real antonym/synonym points). Observe the structure becomes a noisier, less coherent spiral (for word2vec/BERT), confirming the shuffled pairs' role in defining the strands.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
1. What underlying geometric or training-induced property of word embeddings produces the persistent "swirl" pattern in Euclidean UMAP projections of difference vectors for antonym/synonym pairs?
2. Why does the swirl pattern depend on using Euclidean distance rather than cosine similarity, and what does this metric sensitivity imply about how semantic differences are represented in embeddings?
3. Is the swirl pattern a universal feature across languages and embedding models, or is it specific to the Stuttgart dataset and the models tested?
4. How do shuffled control pairs contribute to the clarity of the swirl pattern, and what does their effect reveal about the statistical properties distinguishing real semantic pairs from random ones?

## Limitations
- Exact Stuttgart dataset splits (lexical and predefined) are unspecified, blocking direct F1 replication.
- Shuffled pair generation details (sampling scheme, duplicates) are unreported, affecting projection manifold.
- BERT embedding extraction method (input layer vs. pooled) is ambiguous; wrong choice alters vectors.
- UMAP's known artifact risk raises possibility that the swirl is a projection-specific phenomenon rather than a semantic signal.
- Transductive evaluation uses test data during projection, potentially inflating performance relative to standard inductive benchmarks.

## Confidence
- Swirl pattern emergence (Euclidean UMAP separates real/shuffled): Medium (consistent but metric-sensitive and artifact-prone).
- Difference vector criticality (concatenation fails, shuffled needed): High (strong ablation evidence).
- Transductive classifier performance (near SOTA): Medium (robust scores but non-standard evaluation limits comparability).
