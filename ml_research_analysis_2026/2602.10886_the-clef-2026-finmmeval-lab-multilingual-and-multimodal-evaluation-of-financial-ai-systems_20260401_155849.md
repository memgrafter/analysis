# The CLEF-2026 FinMMEval Lab: Multilingual and Multimodal Evaluation of Financial AI Systems

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID**: 2602.10886
- **Source URL**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.10886
- **Reference count**: 40
- **Primary outcome**: Introduces FinMMEval Lab, the first multilingual and multimodal evaluation framework for financial LLMs, with three tasks and public datasets covering seven languages.

## Executive Summary
The CLEF-2026 FinMMEval Lab paper addresses a critical gap in financial AI evaluation: existing benchmarks are mostly monolingual, text-only, and narrow. It introduces a comprehensive, multilingual, and multimodal assessment framework designed to measure financial AI capabilities across three progressive dimensions—conceptual understanding (through multilingual exam QA), cross-lingual reasoning (using financial reports and news in multiple languages), and decision-making (simulated trading with evidence-grounded rationales). The framework spans seven languages and multiple data modalities, and it provides standardized metrics to encourage rigorous, globally inclusive research. By structuring evaluation from knowledge to action, FinMMEval aims to ensure that financial AI systems are not only accurate but also capable of integrating diverse information sources and making justifiable decisions under uncertainty. The release of datasets and evaluation protocols is expected to catalyze progress in multilingual financial NLP and multimodal reasoning.

## Method Summary
The paper outlines three core tasks. **Task 1 (Financial Exam QA)** uses multiple-choice questions from professional exams (CFA, CPA, EFPA, etc.) in seven languages to assess foundational financial knowledge. **Task 2 (Multilingual Financial QA, PolyFiQA)** provides English financial reports (10-K/10-Q) paired with news articles in five languages (Chinese, Japanese, Spanish, Greek, English) and asks analytical questions, testing the model’s ability to integrate multimodal and cross-lingual information. **Task 3 (Financial Decision Making)** presents a daily JSON context (prices, news, momentum, filings) for specific equities (BTC, TSLA) and requires a Buy/Hold/Sell action along with a concise rationale citing evidence. Evaluation metrics are accuracy for Task 1, ROUGE-1 (primary) with BLEURT and factual consistency for Task 2, and cumulative return and Sharpe ratio for Task 3. The framework is intended as a shared benchmark; datasets and code will be publicly released.

## Key Results
- Public release of multilingual and multimodal financial datasets covering seven languages.
- Defined a hierarchical evaluation suite linking conceptual QA, cross-lingual reasoning, and evidence-based trading.
- Established metrics (accuracy, ROUGE-1, cumulative return, Sharpe ratio) to measure progress along this spectrum.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1: Layered task design scaffolds evaluation from conceptual understanding to real-world action
Claim: The hierarchical tasks isolate and test progressively more complex cognitive capabilities, preventing conflation of skill deficits.
Mechanism: By structuring tasks as a hierarchy—Exam QA → PolyFiQA → Decision Making—the framework measures foundational knowledge recall, cross-lingual/multimodal analytical integration, and reasoning-to-action synthesis separately.
Core assumption: Financial AI proficiency requires separable competencies in knowledge, cross-context reasoning, and action, not a monolithic ability.
Evidence anchors: Abstract: "three interconnected tasks that span financial understanding, reasoning, and decision-making"; Section 1: "Together, these tasks establish a multi-layered evaluation framework that bridges understanding, reasoning, and decision-making".
Break condition: If performance on Task 1 strongly correlates with Task 3 across models, the separable-skill assumption may be invalid; capabilities could be entangled.

### Mechanism 2: Multilingual and multimodal data integration tests model generalization beyond surface-level text processing
Claim: PolyFiQA forces models to reconcile information across languages and modalities, measuring true cross-lingual financial reasoning.
Mechanism: PolyFiQA inputs combine structured numeric trends from filings and unstructured news in various languages; models must align numbers with narratives across linguistic boundaries, avoiding simple translation shortcuts.
Core assumption: Robust financial reasoning requires integrating heterogeneous, linguistically diverse information as encountered in global markets.
Evidence anchors: Section 2: "This task introduces the first cross-lingual financial reasoning benchmark"; Section 4: "challenges systems to integrate financial reports and news across languages for complex analytical QA".
Break condition: If models achieve high PolyFiQA scores via translation-based solutions (e.g., translating all inputs to English), the multilingual integration test fails; metrics like ROUGE-1 with source citation are needed to detect this.

### Mechanism 3: The trading simulation grounds evaluation in a closed-loop, evidence-constrained action space, penalizing hallucination and encouraging risk-aware reasoning
Claim: Task 3 ties actions to observable context and evaluates profitability and stability, ensuring decisions are actionable and justifiable.
Mechanism: Each prediction must include a ≤50-word rationale explicitly citing provided evidence (prices, news, filings). Metrics (Cumulative Return, Sharpe Ratio, Max Drawdown) force a balance of return and risk.
Core assumption: Financial AI value lies in actionable, justifiable decisions, not just accurate predictions or summaries.
Evidence anchors: Section 5: "Each prediction must be accompanied by a concise textual rationale (≤50 words) that explicitly cites the supporting evidence"; Section 5: "evaluate... profitability, stability, and risk control using... Cumulative Return, Sharpe Ratio".
Break condition: If models generate plausible rationales not grounded in the provided context (detected via evidence retrieval evaluation), the reasoning-to-action link is broken.

## Foundational Learning

### Cross-lingual financial terminology and regulatory variance
Why needed: Tasks 1 and 2 require understanding that financial terms (e.g., "assets under management", "activos bajo gestión", "资产管理") may map to subtly different regulatory frameworks or calculation methods across jurisdictions; literal translation can cause errors.
Quick check: Given a Spanish EFPA question about "renta variable" and a Chinese CPA question about "股权投资", what are the key conceptual overlaps and divergences in how these terms are treated in their respective exam contexts?

### Multimodal data fusion for financial documents
Why needed: PolyFiQA requires aligning structured numeric trends (from 10-K/10-Q) with unstructured multilingual news; models must connect a revenue figure mentioned in a filing with a news article discussing the same performance.
Quick check: How would you design a data loader that preserves the alignment between a quarterly revenue figure in a 10-Q excerpt and a Spanish news article discussing that same quarter’s performance?

### Sequence-to-action modeling with risk constraints
Why needed: Task 3 transforms sequential market context into discrete actions (Buy/Hold/Sell) with risk-adjusted evaluation; this sequence-to-action paradigm differs from classification or QA as output must optimize cumulative metrics (CR, Sharpe) over a trajectory.
Quick check: Why is accuracy an insufficient metric for Task 3, and how does a high cumulative return with a low Sharpe Ratio indicate a failure mode?

## Architecture Onboarding

### Component Map
Data ingestion module -> Reasoning engine (LLM backbone with optional retrieval) -> Task-specific head (QA for Tasks 1-2 or Action decoder for Task 3) -> Output. Separate modules: Evaluation suite (computes metrics) and Evidence checker (validates rationales).

### Critical Path
Data loading -> Context embedding -> Task-specific head (QA or action) -> Output generation. For Task 3, the path is sequential: each day’s prediction depends on the previous context state (growing JSON), requiring a loop over dates before metric calculation.

### Design Tradeoffs
- ROUGE-1 (Task 2) favors lexical overlap but may penalize valid paraphrasing; BLEURT can provide semantic similarity.
- Using a single representative price per day (Task 3) simplifies simulation but loses intraday volatility; trade-off between realism and reproducibility.
- Publicly available datasets ease adoption but may have been seen during LLM pretraining, risking data leakage; future iterations need dynamically generated, time-sealed data.

### Failure Signatures
- Task 1: High accuracy on English CFA but near-random on Hindi BBF → poor low-resource language handling.
- Task 2: High ROUGE-1 but low factual consistency → models extracting incorrect numeric details from cross-lingual docs.
- Task 3: High cumulative return but extreme max drawdown (>30%) or low Sharpe ratio → excessive risk; always-Buy baseline may yield decent CR but poor Sharpe.

### First 3 Experiments
1. **Baseline replication**: Implement zero-shot prompting for each task using a generalist LLM (e.g., Llama-3) to establish a performance floor and identify modality/language gaps.
2. **Error analysis by language/tier**: For Tasks 1–2, break down accuracy/ROUGE by language and difficulty (Easy/Expert) to pinpoint weak spots (e.g., Greek financial terminology).
3. **Ablation on multimodal signals**: For Task 3, run with (a) prices only, (b) news only, (c) both to quantify added value of textual context in driving actions.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
1. **How can FinMMEval be extended to include evaluation for additional low-resource languages beyond the current seven, and what challenges might arise in data collection and model generalization?**  
   *Basis:* Section 6 states future editions will expand language coverage.  
   *Why unresolved:* Low-resource languages suffer from data scarcity and cultural nuances, potentially leading to biased or underperforming models.  
   *Resolving evidence:* Development of new multilingual financial datasets for underrepresented languages and systematic benchmarking of LLMs to identify adaptation needs.

2. **What methodologies should be developed to effectively integrate and evaluate multimodal financial data such as charts, graphs, and regulatory filings within the FinMMEval framework?**  
   *Basis:* Section 6 mentions expanding to modalities like charts and regulatory filings.  
   *Why unresolved:* Current tasks focus on text and simple price data; interpreting visual and complex structured documents is not evaluated.  
   *Resolving evidence:* Creation of benchmark datasets with multimodal financial documents (e.g., charts, filings) and corresponding QA or decision-making tasks, and validation of new metrics for cross-modal reasoning.

3. **How does the performance of financial LLMs in decision-making tasks differ between the current pre-specified time window evaluation and dynamic real-time market conditions?**  
   *Basis:* Inferred from Task 3's fixed time window vs. mention of future "dynamic real-time evaluation scenarios".  
   *Why unresolved:* Static evaluations may not account for market dynamics like volatility spikes, information delay, and continuous adaptation.  
   *Resolving evidence:* Conduct a live or simulated real-time trading experiment comparing model performance under dynamic conditions versus the existing protocol, measuring profitability and risk metrics.

4. **Are existing metrics like ROUGE-1 adequate for assessing the quality of multilingual financial question answering, or should factuality and cross-lingual semantic equivalence be prioritized?**  
   *Basis:* Inferred from Task 2’s reliance on ROUGE-1 as primary metric, with secondary measures hinting at shortcomings.  
   *Why unresolved:* ROUGE-1’s lexical focus may not capture semantic accuracy across languages, especially with financial jargon and translations.  
   *Resolving evidence:* Correlate ROUGE scores with human judgments of answer correctness in multiple languages, and develop enhanced metrics that incorporate factual verification and semantic similarity.

## Limitations
- Datasets and evaluation code are not yet public, making exact replication impossible; key details on splits, preprocessing, and language-specific sources remain unspecified.
- Task 3 simulation mechanics (slippage, execution prices, shorting rules, momentum derivation) are undefined, hindering faithful implementation.
- The core assumption of separable skills (hierarchical scaffolding) is untested; strong correlations between Task 1 and Task 3 performances could invalidate the design.

## Confidence
- Framework novelty/comprehensiveness: Medium
- Hierarchical task design scaffolds separable skills: Medium
- PolyFiQA tests genuine cross-lingual reasoning: Medium
- Trading simulation grounds decisions in evidence: Medium
