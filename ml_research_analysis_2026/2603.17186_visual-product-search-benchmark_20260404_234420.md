# Visual Product Search Benchmark

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2603.17186  
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.17186  
- **Reference count:** 39  
- **Primary result:** Nyris's GEM v5.1 achieved the highest overall performance with 58.3% R@1, 74.3% R@5, and 49.5% mAP@20 average across eight industrial and public datasets.

## Executive Summary
This paper presents a comprehensive benchmark for visual product search, evaluating embedding models on eight datasets (four industrial, four public) using a unified image-to-image retrieval protocol without post-processing. Domain-specialized models like Nyris's GEM v5.1 substantially outperform general foundation models on industrial data, with a 63.4% vs 26.4% R@1 gap on Clips-and-Connectors v1. Conversely, general models such as Gemini Embedding 2 and SigLIP2 excel on public benchmarks but collapse on industrial sets (e.g., SigLIP2 0% R@1 on DIY v1). The results highlight that industrial instance-level retrieval requires fine-grained visual discrimination under domain shift, which current general-purpose models lack. The benchmark isolates embedding quality, emphasizing the need for domain-specialized training or adaptation.

## Method Summary
The evaluation employs a strict image-to-image retrieval protocol: for each query image, the system retrieves the nearest gallery embeddings using exact dot-product search (cosine similarity after L2 normalization) with no post-processing, fine-tuning, or dataset-specific adaptation. Models are accessed via their official interfaces (open-source checkpoints or commercial APIs) and use native preprocessing (fixed or adaptive input resolution). Eight datasets are used—four proprietary industrial (Clips-and-Connectors v1, Furniture v1, DIY v1, Automotive v1) and four public (SOP, Products-10K, ILIAS, and one more). Protocols vary: intra-retrieval (query and gallery drawn from same split, self-excluded) for SOP and Products-10K; inter-retrieval for ILIAS with a 5M distractor gallery. Primary metrics are Recall@1, Recall@5, and mAP@20 (mAP@1000 for ILIAS). Gallery embeddings are indexed with Qdrant (or FAISS for open-source) using exact search. Results are averaged across datasets to compare model performance.

## Key Results
- Nyris GEM v5.1 achieves the best overall average: 58.3% R@1, 74.3% R@5, 49.5% mAP@20.
- On the challenging Clips-and-Connectors v1 industrial dataset, GEM v5.1 reaches 63.4% R@1, while the strongest general model (DINOv3) scores only 26.4% R@1.
- General multimodal models fail catastrophically on some industrial sets: SigLIP2 registers 0% R@1 on DIY v1, and Gemini Embedding 2 shows severe degradation on multiple industrial datasets.
- Domain-specific AEM v1 attains 32.5% R@1 on Automotive v1, surpassing general models but remaining far behind GEM v5.1.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1: Domain-Specialized Training for Fine-Grained Discrimination
**Claim:** Vision-only models trained on industrial data (e.g., GEM v5.1, AEM v1) achieve higher instance-level retrieval accuracy because their training objectives and data simulate production constraints—high intra-class similarity, domain shift between synthetic catalog and real query images, and heterogeneous backgrounds—forcing the embedding space to encode subtle geometric and textural details.
- **Core assumption:** Industrial retrieval requires sensitivity to minute visual differences (e.g., screw thread patterns, connector geometry) that are under-represented in generic pretraining corpora.
- **Evidence anchors:** 
  - [abstract] "domain-specific AEM v1 reached 32.5% R@1 on automotive data... General models like Gemini Embedding 2 excelled on public benchmarks but showed severe degradation on industrial sets, with SigLIP2 scoring 0% R@1 on DIY v1."
  - [section 4.3] "nyris General Embedding Model v5.1... is trained to handle a wide variety of product categories while retaining the ability to distinguish between visually similar instances... heavily optimized for versatile visual search scenarios characterized by heterogeneous capture conditions."
- **Break condition:** If domain-specific training data does not cover the visual variance of deployed queries (e.g., new lighting, occlusion), performance regresses toward general baselines.

### Mechanism 2: General Multimodal Models' Semantic Bias
**Claim:** Multi-modal foundation models (e.g., Gemini Embedding 2, SigLIP2) suffer performance collapse on industrial datasets because their representation learning is optimized for semantic alignment with text, not for instance-level visual equivalence under distribution shift.
- **Mechanism:** Web-scale image-text pairs drive representation learning via co-occurrence statistics. In industrial settings, text metadata is often sparse (e.g., part numbers) and visual similarity between instances is high while semantic labels are identical. The embedding space lacks resolution to separate visually near-identical items without strong linguistic cues.
- **Core assumption:** Language supervision introduces a bias toward semantic rather than instance-level matching, detrimental when textual descriptors are uninformative.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract] "General models like Gemini Embedding 2 excelled on public benchmarks but showed severe degradation on industrial sets... highlighting significant gaps in fine-grained retrieval under domain shift."
  - [section 2.2] "Unified embedding models are often designed for semantic and multi-modal retrieval... their effectiveness for fine-grained, instance-level image retrieval under realistic industrial constraints remains an open question."
  - [section 7] "On Clips-and-Connectors v1, GEM v5.1 achieves 63.4 R@1, substantially outperforming the strongest general-purpose baseline, DINOv3 ViT-L/16, which achieves 26.4 R@1."
- **Break condition:** If industrial datasets included rich, consistent textual descriptions for every SKU, the gap might narrow as language supervision provides discriminative signal.

### Mechanism 3: Evaluation Without Post-Processing Exposes Embedding Limitations
**Claim:** The absence of post-processing (re-ranking, geometric verification, metadata filtering) isolates the embedding model's intrinsic discrimination capability, revealing that modern foundation models lack sufficient invariance to intra-class variation and sensitivity to instance-level details required for industrial retrieval.
- **Mechanism:** Forcing reliance solely on global embedding nearest-neighbor behavior, failure of general models indicates their embeddings collapse visually distinct instances into similar vectors when faced with domain-specific appearance variations (e.g., reflections on metal parts, cluttered workshop backgrounds).
- **Core assumption:** Industrial deployment often cannot support heavy post-processing due to latency or catalog dynamism, making embedding quality paramount.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract] "Evaluation is conducted without post-processing, isolating the retrieval capability of each model."
  - [section 5] "No model-specific post-processing, fine-tuning, or dataset-dependent adaptation is applied during evaluation."
  - [section 7] "On DIY v1, SigLIP2 SO400M achieves 0.0 R@1, despite strong performance on public datasets."
- **Break condition:** Introduction of domain-adaptive post-processing (e.g., local feature re-ranking) could mitigate embedding deficiencies, reducing observed performance gaps.

## Foundational Learning
- **Concept: Metric Learning with Hard Negative Sampling**  
  - **Why needed here:** Instance-level retrieval requires embeddings where distances reflect exact instance equivalence, not semantic similarity. This demands training strategies that enforce separation between highly similar instances (e.g., two bolts differing only in thread pitch).  
  - **Quick check:** How would you construct a training batch for a model like GEM v5.1 to ensure it learns to distinguish between two visually identical fasteners with different part numbers?

- **Concept: Domain Shift and Covariate Generalization**  
  - **Why needed here:** Industrial queries exhibit strong distributional differences from canonical catalog images (synthetic CAD renders vs. user-captured workshop photos). Models must embed both into a shared space where true matches remain nearest.  
  - **Quick check:** What visual factors contribute to the domain shift between Clips-and-Connectors' gallery (CAD renders) and queries (real photos), and how might a model invariance to these be learned?

- **Concept: Open-Set Recognition vs. Closed-Set Classification**  
  - **Why needed here:** Product catalogs are dynamic; new SKUs appear continuously. A retrieval system must handle unseen instances without retraining, unlike a classifier with a fixed label set.  
  - **Quick check:** Why is a nearest-neighbor search over embeddings inherently more suitable for a continuously evolving catalog than a softmax classifier?

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map:** Input Image → Native Pre-processing (per model) → Embedding Model (vision encoder only) → L2 Normalization → Dot Product Similarity Search (exact) → Ranked Gallery List.
- **Critical path:** The image flows through the model's native preprocessing into the vision encoder, producing an embedding that is normalized and compared against a pre-indexed gallery via exact dot-product nearest-neighbor search.
- **Design tradeoffs:**
  - Vision-only vs. Multimodal: Vision-only models (DINO, GEM) avoid dilution from text supervision but cannot leverage descriptions. Multimodal models (Gemini, SigLIP) can use text but may degrade on pure visual tasks.
  - Fixed vs. Adaptive Input Resolution: Variable-resolution models (Jina Embeddings v4) preserve details but increase compute; fixed-size models (DINOv2 at 224×224) are simpler but may lose fine details.
  - Embedding Dimensionality: Higher dimensions (Gemini 3072, Jina 2048) capture nuance but increase storage/latency; Nyris models use 768-dim, prioritizing efficiency.
- **Failure signatures:**
  - Complete collapse on a dataset: SigLIP2's 0% R@1 on DIY v1 suggests insensitivity to tool/hardware structure.
  - Large intra-dataset variance: Strong performance on SOP but poor on Clips-and-Connectors indicates reliance on semantic cues absent in fine-grained industrial data.
  - Poor ranking quality (mAP@20 << R@1): Would indicate embedding space congestion—model retrieves one correct neighbor but ranks remaining relevant instances poorly.
- **First 3 experiments:**
  1. **Protocol replication:** Run the exact image-to-image protocol on a small public subset (e.g., SOP test split) for DINOv2 to validate the pipeline—embedding extraction, indexing, exact search, metric calculation—matches reported numbers.
  2. **Failure case deep-dive:** For SigLIP2's catastrophic failure on DIY v1, visualize top-5 retrievals for 20 queries. Inspect if failures stem from background clutter, scale variation, or fundamental shape confusion.
  3. **Embedding space analysis:** For a pair of confusing instances in Clips-and-Connectors, compute t-SNE/PCA of embeddings from GEM v5.1 vs. DINOv3. Quantify inter-instance vs. intra-instance distance distributions to confirm specialization leads to tighter instance clusters.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
1. **What visual characteristics in industrial data cause general vision-language models to fail, and how can architectures be made robust?**  
   Basis: SigLIP2 scores 0% R@1 on DIY v1; authors note sensitivity to "structured mechanical components and repetitive visual patterns." Failures are documented but not analyzed; we lack actionable insights.  
   Resolution evidence: Systematic ablation correlating performance with visual attributes, feature visualizations, and error case clustering.

2. **What is the trade-off between domain specialization and generalization in industrial retrieval, and can few-shot learning reduce data requirements?**  
   Basis: Domain-specific AEM v1 outperforms general models on Automotive v1 (32.5% vs 28.2% R@1) but doesn't generalize; general industrial GEM v5.1 works across domains but still trained on industrial data. Quantifying needed domain data and few-shot adaptation remains open.  
   Resolution evidence: Controlled experiments varying fine-tuning data amount across multiple domains, comparing to few-shot and augmentation methods.

3. **How does retrieval performance correlate with end-to-end system success when combined with realistic post-processing (re-ranking, metadata fusion)?**  
   Basis: Evaluation isolates retrieval stage: "without the influence of downstream re-ranking, decision logic, or business rules." Pure retrieval metrics may not predict operational outcomes; post-processing could recover or amplify errors.  
   Resolution evidence: End-to-end evaluations on industrial workflows, measuring correlation between retrieval scores and final identification accuracy.

4. **What are the dominant failure modes (e.g., confusion between similar variants, lighting sensitivity) in industrial instance retrieval?**  
   Basis: Future Work section calls for "deeper analysis of failure modes, fine-grained visual confusions, and domain-specific challenges." Aggregate metrics hide error patterns; understanding confusions is key to targeted development.  
   Resolution evidence: Qualitative analysis of top-k errors, clustering by visual attributes, and user studies on critical error types.

## Limitations
- **Proprietary data and models:** Industrial datasets (Clips-and-Connectors v1, Furniture v1, DIY v1, Automotive v1) and Nyris models (GEM v5.1, AEM v1) are not public, preventing independent verification of core results.
- **Commercial API drift:** Models accessed via paid APIs (Gemini, Cohere, Vertex AI) may have changed since evaluation, introducing potential performance drift.
- **Implementation ambiguities:** Key details for some open-source baselines (e.g., PE-Core-L/14) are missing, risking replication errors.
- **Domain coverage:** The benchmark covers only four industrial domains; it is unclear whether conclusions generalize to other product categories or capture conditions.

## Confidence
- **Domain-specialized superiority:** Medium. Large performance gaps are reported but rely on in-house data; independent checks on public industrial benchmarks are lacking.
- **General-model degradation:** Medium. Catastrophic failures (e.g., SigLIP2 0% R@1 on DIY v1) are plausible but could be influenced by dataset quirks or preprocessing mismatches.
- **No post-processing isolates embedding ability:** High. The protocol is logically sound and uniformly applied, though real-world pipelines often include re-ranking that could alter outcomes.
