# CAPITU: A Benchmark for Evaluating Instruction-Following in Brazilian Portuguese with Literary Context

## Quick Facts
- **ArXiv ID:** 2603.22576
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.22576
- **Reference count:** 38
- **Primary result:** CAPITU provides the first native Portuguese instruction-following benchmark with automatic verification; evaluation of 18 models shows Sabiazinho-4 achieves 87.0% accuracy at $0.13, outperforming Claude-Haiku-4.5 (73.5%, $1.12) by 8.6x on literary tasks.

## Executive Summary
CAPITU addresses the lack of evaluation benchmarks for Portuguese instruction-following by grounding tasks in eight canonical Brazilian literary works. It defines 59 instruction types—including Portuguese-specific morphological constraints—that are automatically verifiable, enabling scalable and objective assessment of 18 LLMs. Results reveal that top reasoning models like GPT-5.2 achieve near-perfect compliance (98.5% strict accuracy), while a small Portuguese-specialized model (Sabiazinho-4, 87.0%) delivers strong performance at dramatically lower cost than generalist models. Multi-turn evaluation uncovers significant variation in constraint persistence (60–96% accuracy), with notable drops for harder constraints, highlighting challenges in conversational state maintenance.

## Method Summary
The benchmark comprises 500 evaluation instances: 200 single-turn prompts and 100 three-turn dialogues. Each prompt is derived from one of eight Brazilian literary works and includes one or more of 59 instruction types across seven categories (e.g., word counts, pattern inclusion, forbidden words, formatting). All instructions are paired with deterministic verification functions (string operations, regex, lexicon lookup) to score model responses without human judges. The evaluation pipeline selects random works, themes, and task templates, samples instructions by difficulty, and generates prompts. Models are queried with temperature 0.0; responses are checked against the original and preprocessed variants (e.g., markdown stripped) for strict and loose accuracy. An optional coherence score uses GPT-4o-mini with literary metadata. The full suite is publicly released.

## Key Results
- Sabiazinho-4 (87.0% strict accuracy, $0.13) outperforms Claude-Haiku-4.5 (73.5%, $1.12) by 8.6x in cost efficiency.
- GPT-5.2 with reasoning achieves near-perfect compliance (98.5% strict accuracy), setting a high bar.
- Multi-turn conversation accuracy ranges from 60% to 96%, with significant constraint decay by Turn 3 on harder tasks (e.g., exact word count, morphological limits).

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1: Automatic Verification
CAPITU's deterministic verification functions enable objective, scalable evaluation of instruction-following without human judges. All 59 instruction types are implemented with programmatic checks (string operations, regular expressions, lexicon matching) that pass/fail model responses. This eliminates subjectivity and reduces cost. The core assumption is that surface-level formal properties (word counts, suffix matches, etc.) are sufficient proxies for instruction compliance. Evidence includes the benchmark's design and a manual audit that found ~8% false negatives but no false positives, validating the checks' soundness.

### Mechanism 2: Portuguese-Specific Morphological Constraints
By including constraints that target uniquely Portuguese morphological patterns (e.g., diminutive -inho/-inha, gerund -ando/-endo/-indo, adverbial -mente), CAPITU tests language-specific competence that generic or translated benchmarks miss. Instruction templates explicitly require models to include a minimum/maximum number of words with these suffixes, forcing generation of productive Portuguese morphological forms within the literary context. The core assumption is that suffix-based constraints are meaningful indicators of a model's ability to control Portuguese morphology; they cannot be trivially satisfied without generating actual Portuguese words. Evidence includes the benchmark's taxonomy and linguistic motivation, supported by similar approaches in other language-specific benchmarks.

### Mechanism 3: Literary Grounding for Cultural Authenticity
Grounding prompts in eight canonical Brazilian literary works ensures that instruction-following is assessed within linguistically rich, culturally meaningful contexts, avoiding the bias of generic or translated prompts. Each prompt references characters, themes, and settings, requiring models to produce analyses or responses that are contextually appropriate and demonstrate understanding of Brazilian literature. The core assumption is that performance on these tasks correlates with real-world Portuguese language proficiency better than artificial prompts. Evidence includes the selection of diverse canonical works and the benchmark's design, aligned with a broader trend toward culturally-grounded evaluation.

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component map:**  
Literary corpus, Instruction taxonomy, Task templates -> Composition engine -> Prompt -> Model -> Response -> Verification module -> Evaluation metrics -> (Optional) Coherence judge -> Output

**Critical path:**  
1. Choose literary work, author, theme, and task template.  
2. Sample instruction type(s) based on difficulty and apply parameter generation.  
3. Assemble full prompt with cumulative instructions for multi-turn if applicable.  
4. Query target model, obtain response.  
5. Run verification functions for each instruction; record pass/fail.  
6. Compute accuracy metrics; optionally run coherence scoring.  
7. Aggregate across models for analysis.

**Design tradeoffs:**  
- Automatic verification vs. inability to judge subtle quality.  
- Surface-level checks vs. deep semantic correctness.  
- Literary grounding vs. risk of model memorization.  
- Single vs. multi-turn evaluation: multi-turn provides realistic test but reveals constraint decay.

**Failure signatures:**  
- Exact count failures: responses off by few words (e.g., 148 vs 180).  
- Formatting artifacts: markdown headers/preambles breaking start-word or acrostic constraints.  
- Morphological limit violations: exceeding allowed suffix counts, especially gerunds.  
- Constraint forgetting: multi-turn accuracy drops after turn 2.  
- Verification bugs: bullet list check failing when item count below a threshold, first-person false positives on contractions.  
- Coherence judge overgenerous: all models scored >0.91, indicating limited discriminative power.

**First 3 experiments:**  
1. Single-turn evaluation: Run 200 prompts (easy/medium/hard) through all 18 models; compute strict, loose, instruction-level accuracy, coherence, and cost.  
2. Multi-turn evaluation: Conduct 100 three-turn dialogues per model; measure per-turn and conversation accuracy to assess constraint persistence.  
3. Category and difficulty analysis: Break down results by instruction category (Count, Words, Pattern, Forbidden, Structure, Punctuation, Format) and difficulty level; identify hardest instructions (e.g., exact_word_count, pattern:terminacao_inho_inha_min) and examine qualitative error cases.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
### Open Question 1
**Question:** What architectural or training modifications can significantly reduce multi-turn constraint degradation (20–40% drops on hard turns) without sacrificing coherence or efficiency?  
**Basis in paper:** Future work mentions investigating techniques to improve multi-turn constraint persistence. Results show conversation accuracy ranging from 60% to 96% across models, with notable drops at Turn 3.  
**Why unresolved:** Persistent degradation limits reliability in iterative applications. Understanding whether this stems from attention limitations, training objectives, or context management is crucial for robust dialogue systems.  
**What evidence would resolve it:** Controlled experiments isolating factors like context window size, attention sparsity, constraint-aware fine-tuning, and explicit constraint reminder prompting on multi-turn benchmarks; analysis of failure modes per turn.

### Open Question 2
**Question:** How reliable is GPT-4o-mini as a coherence judge for Brazilian Portuguese literary responses, and to what extent do its scores correlate with expert human assessment?  
**Basis in paper:** Limitation: "We use GPT-4o-mini as a coherence judge without human validation of the resulting scores." High, undifferentiated coherence (91–99%) suggests limited discriminative power.  
**Why unresolved:** The final score combines instruction compliance and coherence. If coherence scoring is unreliable, the benchmark may not penalize degenerate but mechanically compliant responses.  
**What evidence would resolve it:** Human evaluation by multiple Portuguese literary experts on a stratified sample of responses; statistical correlation and disagreement analysis to identify judge biases or lack of nuance.

### Open Question 3
**Question:** Are suffix-based morphological verifications (e.g., counting -inho/-inha words) a valid proxy for syntactic competence in Portuguese, or are models exploiting superficial pattern matching without semantic understanding of diminutives?  
**Basis in paper:** Verification accepts any suffix match regardless of semantic validity. Manual audit found no false positives, but this may reflect model output rather than verification soundness. Morphological constraints remain among the hardest (84.8% avg).  
**Why unresolved:** If models succeed via surface pattern matching, the benchmark may not test true morphological knowledge. Conversely, if they produce real words, it suggests learned productive morphology. Distinguishing is key for linguistic evaluation.  
**What evidence would resolve it:** Linguistic analysis of satisfied responses: proportion of true diminutives vs. accidental suffix matches; semantic appropriateness of diminutive use; comparison with a semantically-grounded verification variant requiring valid diminutive contexts.

### Open Question 4
**Question:** Why do LLMs achieve such low accuracy on exact word count constraints (21.1% average), and can generation algorithms incorporate reliable, real-time token counting to meet precise length requirements?  
**Basis in paper:** "The hardest instruction (exact_word_count at 21.1%)" and error analysis confirms "models lack reliable token-counting mechanisms during decoding."  
**Why unresolved:** Exact counts are fundamental for controlled generation (e.g., summaries, legal documents). This failure points to a gap between training objectives and precise output control.  
**What evidence would resolve it:** Ablation studies with modified decoding (e.g., constrained beam search with length penalties, external counters), analysis of error distribution (under/over count, off-by-one patterns), and comparison of models with built-in counting modules versus standard autoregressive decoders.

## Limitations
- Tokenization and whitespace splitting rules for word counting are underspecified, leading to potential variance in exact-word-count verification.
- The specific instruction combinations for medium/hard difficulties are not detailed in the paper, requiring repository access for full reproducibility.
- Coherence evaluation relies on GPT-4o-mini without validation against human judges, and the exact prompt/metadata used is not fully documented.
- Risk of model memorization of canonical literary works may inflate performance despite analysis-oriented tasks.

## Confidence
- Automatic verification objectivity: High
- Portuguese-specific constraint validity: Medium
- Literary grounding authenticity: Medium
- Multi-turn constraint decay: High

## Next Checks
1. **Tokenization sensitivity test**: Re-run the evaluation using alternative whitespace/punctuation splitting rules (e.g., NLTK vs. simple `split()`) and quantify the variance in strict accuracy, especially for `exact_word_count` instructions.
2. **Instruction combination audit**: Extract the full list of instruction type combinations used for "medium" and "hard" prompts from the released repository. Compare their distribution and constraint cardinality against the paper’s examples to confirm intended difficulty gradient.
3. **Coherence judge calibration**: Replicate the coherence scoring using the exact judge prompt and model version (GPT-4o-mini) with fixed sampling parameters. Measure inter-annotator agreement (e.g., quadratic weighted kappa) across a sample of 100 responses to assess discriminative power.
