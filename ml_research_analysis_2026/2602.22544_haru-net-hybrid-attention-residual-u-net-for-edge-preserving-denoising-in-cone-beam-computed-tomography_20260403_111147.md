# HARU-Net: Hybrid Attention Residual U-Net for Edge-Preserving Denoising in Cone-Beam Computed Tomography

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2602.22544
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.22544
- **Reference count:** 13
- **Primary result:** HARU-Net achieves state-of-the-art CBCT denoising (PSNR 37.52 dB, SSIM 0.9557, GMSD 0.1084) with lower computational cost (40.76 GMACs/patches, ~2 min/scan) by embedding hybrid attention in skip connections and the bottleneck.

## Executive Summary
Low-dose cone-beam computed tomography (CBCT) suffers from significant noise, but acquiring high-quality paired training data is ethically and practically challenging. HARU-Net addresses this by introducing a Hybrid Attention Residual U-Net. It integrates Hybrid Attention Blocks (HABs) within skip connections to refine transmitted features and a Residual Hybrid Attention Group (RHAG) at the bottleneck for global context modeling, all built on a stable residual convolutional backbone. Trained on cadaver-derived data with simulated noise, the model outperforms transformer-based competitors SwinIR and Uformer in edge-preserving metrics while being more computationally efficient.

## Method Summary
HARU-Net is a U-Net architecture where the encoder and decoder consist of standard residual convolutional blocks for stable local feature extraction. The key innovations are placed in the skip pathways and bottleneck: each encoder output is refined by a Hybrid Attention Block (combining windowed self-attention and channel attention) before being concatenated with the decoder. The bottleneck employs a Residual Hybrid Attention Group—a series of six HABs with residual connections—to strengthen long-range dependencies in the deepest, most compressed representation. The network is trained using mean squared error loss on 256×256 patches extracted from pre-segmented tissue regions of high-dose (clean) and synthetically noisy CBCT volumes.

## Key Results
- Quantitative SOTA: PSNR 37.52 dB, SSIM 0.9557, GMSD 0.1084 on the authors' cadaver dataset.
- Efficiency: 40.76 GMACs per patch and approximately 1.985 minutes for full-volume inference (512³) on an RTX 2080 Ti.
- Outperforms SwinIR and Uformer in edge preservation (lower GMSD) with fewer computations.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1: Selective Feature Refinement in Skip Connections via Hybrid Attention Blocks (HABs)
- **Claim:** HABs placed in skip connections selectively emphasize salient anatomical features (e.g., bone boundaries) while suppressing noise, preventing noisy encoder outputs from being directly passed to the decoder.
- **Mechanism:** The HAB applies window-based self-attention to capture local spatial patterns within partitioned windows and channel attention to re-weight feature maps globally. This hybrid operation refines feature tensors before concatenation, activating diagnostically relevant pixels more strongly than background noise.
- **Evidence anchors:** [abstract] states the blocks "selectively emphasize salient anatomical features"; [section: Hybrid Attention Transformer Block] says HABs are used "to refine and enhance the core feature representations transmitted between the encoder and decoder stages."
- **Break condition:** If attention maps fail to distinguish fine edges from high-frequency noise (e.g., due to homogenized training data), the HAB may blur subtle details or inadequately suppress noise, harming SSIM/GMSD.

### Mechanism 2: Global Context Modeling at the Deepest Representations via Residual Hybrid Attention Group (RHAG)
- **Claim:** The RHAG at the bottleneck strengthens long-range dependencies and global contextual modeling in the deepest feature maps, enabling coherent reconstruction of large-scale structures from noisy inputs.
- **Mechanism:** A sequence of six HAB blocks arranged residually repeatedly applies hybrid attention to the bottleneck maps, allowing iterative refinement of global relationships across the entire latent representation, compensating for spatial resolution loss from downsampling.
- **Evidence anchors:** [abstract] mentions the group "strengthen[s] global contextual modeling and long-range feature interactions"; [section: RHAG and Bottleneck] states it "enables the modeling of long-range contextual dependencies at a relatively low additional computational cost."
- **Break condition:** Under extreme or spatially heterogeneous noise, the RHAG may propagate incorrect global associations (e.g., treating a noise cluster as structure), introducing artifacts. Computational cost could also become prohibitive in 3D.

### Mechanism 3: Stable Feature Extraction via Residual Convolutional Blocks
- **Claim:** Residual convolutional blocks throughout the encoder and decoder enable deeper, more stable gradient flow and robust local feature extraction, forming a stable backbone that prevents optimization issues when hybrid attention modules are inserted.
- **Mechanism:** Each convolutional stage uses a skip connection (1×1 conv) to add the input to the output of two 3×3 conv-LeakyReLU layers. This identity mapping eases gradient propagation and ensures local feature extraction remains reliable even when attention modules re-weight features.
- **Evidence anchors:** [abstract] notes the blocks "facilitate deeper, more stable feature extraction"; encoder/decoder sections describe the residual design for "stable gradient flow and effective feature propagation."
- **Break condition:** Improper residual scaling or missing LayerNorm around the add operation could cause gradient explosion or signal proliferation, destabilizing training when combined with dynamic attention computations.

## Foundational Learning
- **Concept: CBCT Noise Modeling (Additive Gaussian Approximation)**  
  *Why needed here:* To synthesize realistic supervised training pairs from high-dose cadaver scans, the paper models quantum (Poisson) and electronic noise as additive Gaussian components (Eq. 1).  
  *Quick check:* Why is approximating quantum noise as additive Gaussian valid for reconstructed CBCT, and what are the limitations of this approximation at very low doses?

- **Concept: Windowed Self-Attention (Swin Transformer)**  
  *Why needed here:* Standard self-attention is computationally infeasible for high-resolution 2D patches. Windowed attention partitions the feature map into local windows, making attention tractable while allowing long-range context via shifted windows across layers.  
  *Quick check:* How does shifted windowing enable cross-window information flow, and what is the trade-off between window size and model capacity vs. computation?

- **Concept: Morphological Image Processing for ROI Segmentation**  
  *Why needed here:* The dynamic patching strategy must avoid empty air regions to maximize training efficiency. The pipeline (K-means, dilation, contour-based hole-filling) creates a clean binary mask of tissue.  
  *Quick check:* Why is hole-filling via contour hierarchy necessary after K-means and dilation, and what anatomical artifacts might this process incorrectly include or exclude?

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map:** Input 256×256 noisy tissue patch → Encoder (4 stages: Residual Conv Block → 4×4 strided downsampling, channels 64→128→256→512) → Skip outputs each pass through a Hybrid Attention Block (HAB) → Bottleneck: 1×1 conv to 1024 channels → Residual Hybrid Attention Group (6× HAB with residuals) → 1×1 conv to 512 channels → Decoder (4 stages: transposed 2×2 upsampling → concatenate with *HAB-refined* skip features → Residual Conv Block) → Output Head (1×1 conv).
- **Critical path:** Dataset preprocessing (tissue segmentation via K-means/morphology → patching) is a prerequisite. The model expects pre-segmented tissue patches; unprocessed patches with large air regions degrade performance and waste computation. Training stability depends on proper LayerNorm placement and residual scaling around HAB modules.
- **Design tradeoffs:**  
  - HABs are only in skip connections and bottleneck, not every encoder/decoder block: reduces cost vs. a full transformer but may limit early-layer attention.  
  - Patch size (256×256): balances context and memory; limits modeling of very large structures, requiring overlapping aggregation for full volumes.  
  - RHAG depth (6 HABs): chosen for cost-performance balance; more could improve global modeling but increase computation.
- **Failure signatures:**  
  - Blurred edges / over-smoothing: HABs under-activated or channel attention suppressing high-frequency channels; check attention heatmaps, adjust channel reduction ratio.  
  - Residual-like artifacts / checkerboard: Insufficient refinement after transposed upsampling; decoder residual blocks may need more layers.  
  - Slow inference (>2min/scan): RHAG and HAB FLOPs dominate; consider efficient attention variants or pruning for deployment.  
  - Training instability (NaNs / loss spikes): Likely uninitialized LayerNorm or projection layers; verify initialization and use gradient clipping.
- **First 3 experiments:**  
  1. **Baseline replication:** Train a ResU-Net (HARU-Net minus HAB/RHAG) on the same data to isolate the attention modules' contribution; expect a significant PSNR drop (~2 dB) per Table 1.  
  2. **Ablation: HAB placement:** Move one HAB from the skip connection into an encoder block. Hypothesis: skip connection placement is more effective for feature transmission than internal encoder attention.  
  3. **Noise robustness curve:** Train HARU-Net with varying simulated quantum noise standard deviation (σ_q). Evaluate on unseen noise levels to test generalization beyond the training distribution.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- **Cross-vendor generalizability:** The model was trained/evaluated only on data from a J. Morita Accuitomo 170 system. Performance on CBCT from other manufacturers is unknown and is a key limitation.
- **Real-time deployment:** Full-volume inference takes ~2 minutes, falling short of real-time clinical use. Model compression, pruning, and efficient attention are needed.
- **3D extension:** The current 2D slice-based approach misses inter-slice context. Adapting HARU-Net for volumetric (3D) denoising is future work.
- **Reduced dependence on paired data:** Supervised training requires clean references, which are hard to obtain for patients. Integration with self-supervised or physics-informed learning could mitigate this.

## Limitations
- Single-site, single-vendor dataset (21 hemimandible volumes) limits generalizability to other anatomies and scanners.
- Noise simulation approximates CBCT noise as additive Gaussian; validity at very low doses is unclear.
- Critical architectural details (exact channel counts, HAB window size, MLP ratio) are omitted, hindering exact replication.
- Ablation evidence is insufficient; the isolated contributions of HABs, RHAG, and residual blocks are not rigorously quantified.
- Edge preservation relies solely on GMSD; no clinician assessment or fine-edge-specific metrics are reported.

## Confidence
- **Overall SOTA performance:** Medium (strong on reported dataset but no external validation).
- **HABs in skip connections:** Medium-Low (plausible mechanism inferred from architecture and results, not directly evidenced).
- **RHAG at bottleneck:** Medium-Low (global modeling expected, specific design benefit unproven by ablation).
- **Residual convolutional blocks:** High (residual learning is a well-established stabilizer).
- **Noise simulation validity:** Low (approximation not validated against real low-dose noise statistics).
