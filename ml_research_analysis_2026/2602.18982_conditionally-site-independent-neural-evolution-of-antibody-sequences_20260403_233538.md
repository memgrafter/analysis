# Conditionally Site-Independent Neural Evolution of Antibody Sequences

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2602.18982
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.18982
- **Reference count:** 40
- **Primary result:** CoSiNE achieves state-of-the-art zero-shot variant effect prediction on four DMS benchmarks (Spearman up to 0.613 for expression, 0.536 for binding) and enables guided optimization of antibody binding affinity via Guided Gillespie sampling.

## Executive Summary
This paper introduces CoSiNE, a neural continuous-time Markov chain model for antibody evolution that conditions site-specific mutation rates on the full parental sequence. This design relaxes the independent-sites assumption to capture context-dependent epistasis while remaining tractable. CoSiNE disentangles selection from somatic hypermutation biases via a log-likelihood ratio with a Thrifty SHM model. At inference, Guided Gillespie sampling steers generation toward desired properties like high antigen binding without retraining. The method sets new benchmarks for zero-shot variant effect prediction and demonstrates effective affinity maturation guided by a classifier.

## Method Summary
CoSiNE models antibody maturation as a continuous-time Markov process where transition rates for each site depend on the full parent sequence. The model uses a pretrained ESM2 protein language model backbone to encode the parent sequence, followed by per-site heads that output `|A|×|A|` rate matrices (off-diagonals via softplus, diagonals set to negative row sum). The transition likelihood over branch length `t` factorizes across sites: `pθ(y|x, t) = Πℓ exp(tQθ(x)ℓ)xℓ,yℓ`. Training minimizes negative log-likelihood on ~2M parent-child transitions from clonal trees, optionally with SNR weighting. For selection scoring, a log-likelihood ratio between CoSiNE and a SHM-only model (Thrifty) estimates selective advantage. Guidance uses a Taylor-approximated transformation of the rate matrix based on gradients from a binding affinity predictor, enablingClassifier-guided Gillespie sampling.

## Key Results
- CoSiNE surpasses ESM2, ProGen2, and DASM in zero-shot variant effect prediction on FLAb2 DMS datasets, with top Spearman correlations of 0.613 (expression) and 0.536 (binding).
- The selection score (log-likelihood ratio with Thrifty) correlates better with experimental fitness than raw model likelihood, successfully disentangling selection from SHM bias.
- Guided Gillespie sampling optimizes predicted binding affinity from naive seeds more effectively than unguided sampling and baselines, while preserving structural quality (pLDDT) and humanness (OASis). Empirically, it yields sequences closer to real evolutionary processes than naive matrix exponentiation.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1
**Claim:** CoSiNE models antibody affinity maturation as a continuous-time Markov chain (CTMC) where site-specific mutation rates are conditioned on the full sequence via a neural network, enabling a first-order approximation to sequential point mutation with epistasis.
**Mechanism:** Given a parent sequence `x`, a neural network outputs `L` site-specific rate matrices `Qθ(x)ℓ`. The transition probability to a child `y` over branch length `t` is factorized as `pθ(y|x, t) = Πℓ exp(tQθ(x)ℓ)xℓ,yℓ`. This relaxes the classical independent-sites assumption by making rates context-dependent, capturing some epistatic interactions while maintaining tractability.
**Core assumption:** The factorized likelihood provides a first-order approximation to the true sequential mutation process, with approximation error bounded quadratically by branch length `t` (Proposition 4.1).
**Evidence anchors:** Abstract and Section 4 state the approximation and error bound; Proposition 4.1 proves `∥P(·|x,t) - pθ(·|x,t)∥₁ ≤ (λt)²` under a condition on single-mutant rates.
**Break condition:** The approximation error becomes dominant for long branch lengths (`t` large), where higher-order epistatic effects degrade performance. This is mitigated in affinity maturation data where selected lineages typically have short branches.

### Mechanism 2
**Claim:** CoSiNE disentangles selective fitness from somatic hypermutation (SHM) biases by taking a log-likelihood ratio with a pre-trained, selection-free SHM model (Thrifty), yielding a selection score correlated with experimental fitness.
**Mechanism:** Following mutation-selection theory, observed rates `Qxy ∝ µxy * P_fix(x→y)`, where `µxy` is SHM rate and `P_fix` is fixation probability. CoSiNE’s small-`t` likelihood approximates `Qxy`; Thrifty estimates `µxy`. The selection score `score(x→y) = log pθ(y|x,t) - log q(y|x,t)` cancels SHM biases, leaving a signal proportional to `log P_fix(x→y)` and thus selective advantage.
**Core assumption:** Thrifty accurately models the neutral SHM process; the small-`t` approximation holds for `t=0.2`; fixation probability is monotonic in selective advantage.
**Evidence anchors:** Abstract mentions explicit disentanglement; Section 4.1 defines the score; Section 5.3 (Figure 4) shows improved Spearman with the selection score vs. raw likelihood.
**Break condition:** Thrifty is misspecified for the germline/species (e.g., mouse Adams dataset), leading to imperfect cancellation. The small-`t` approximation fails for long branches, contaminating the selection signal.

### Mechanism 3
**Claim:** Guided Gillespie sampling steers CoSiNE’s evolutionary trajectories toward sequences with desired properties (e.g., high antigen binding) by modulating the generator rate matrix using a classifier score, without retraining the model.
**Mechanism:** At each Gillespie step, the rate for a potential mutant `y` (Hamming distance 1) is scaled by a guidance factor derived from property predictor `µθz(·)`. Under Taylor-approximated guidance (TAG), the guided rate is `(Qγ_z)x,y ≈ [2Φ((∇ₓµθz(x)ᵀ(y-x))/σθz(x))]^γ * Qx,y`, where `γ` controls strength. This alters jump chain probabilities to favor beneficial mutations.
**Core assumption:** `µθz` provides a differentiable, locally linear fitness landscape; variance `σθz` is approximately constant; the guided matrix remains a valid CTMC.
**Evidence anchors:** Abstract describes guided sampling; Section 4.2 derives TAG (Eqs. 8–9) and Algorithm S2; Section 5.4 (Figures 5–6) shows affinity shifts while preserving quality and humanness.
**Break condition:** Predictor is uninformative (high `σθz`) or uncalibrated; linear approximation fails for large mutations/non-smooth landscapes; over-guidance (`γ` too high) produces unnatural sequences.

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component map:** Input sequence (heavy + `<chain_break>` + light) -> ESM2-150M backbone -> Per-site linear heads (softplus) -> Site-specific rate matrices `Qθ(x)ℓ` -> Factorized likelihood `Πℓ exp(tQθ(x)ℓ)` (training) or Gillespie sampler (inference). Guidance module: external predictor `µθz(·)` -> gradient `∇ₓµθz(x)` -> TAG rates `(Qγ_z)` -> Guided Gillespie.

**Critical path:**
1. **Data prep:** Process BCR sequences from 5 sources; infer clonal families; extract parent-child pairs with branch lengths; filter/align sequences.
2. **Training:** Replace ESM2 head with per-site rate matrices; train with AdamW (lr=2.5e-4, warmup 5k, decay power=2.0, weight decay 0.01, clip 1.0); minimize NLL on transitions.
3. **Selection scoring:** For DMS mutant `(x,y)`, compute `log pθ(y|x,0.2) - log q(y|x,0.2)` using Thrifty for `q`.
4. **Guided sampling:** Start from naive `x`; loop: compute `∇ₓµθz(x)`; derive `(Qγ_z)` via TAG; Gillespie step; update `x`; repeat until cumulative `t`.

**Design tradeoffs:**
- First-order CTMC approx vs. full sequential: Factorized likelihood enables efficient training but error grows as `O(t²)`; Gillespie reduces sampling error but requires recomputing `Qθ(x)` each step.
- Context window: Conditioning on full sequence captures long-range epistasis but increases compute; could truncate to CDRs.
- Guidance exactness: TAG is 500–900× faster than exact guidance with no significant fitness loss, but assumes local linearity.
- Indel omission: Model assumes equal-length sequences; future work needs indel rates.

**Failure signatures:**
- Poor VEP correlation on long-branch assays indicates misspecification; `t=0.2` may exceed first-order regime.
- Guided samples lose humanness (low OASis) if guidance strength `γ` is too high.
- Training instability if per-site rate distributions diverge; monitor on held-out PCPs.
- SHM correction fails if Thrifty is misapplied (e.g., wrong germline mapping).

**First 3 experiments:**
1. **Synthetic validation:** Train on codon-level sequences with known `Q_true` (varying epistasis). Verify Gillespie samples match exact `P(y|x,t)` better than matrix-exp samples; confirm KL error scales with `t²`.
2. **DMS zero-shot VEP:** Evaluate selection score Spearman on FLAb2 benchmarks. Ablate paired-chain input to quantify inter-chain epistasis. Compare against ESM2, ProGen2, DASM.
3. **Guided affinity maturation:** From naive OAS seeds, sample leaves via Guided Gillespie (`γ=5`). Measure shift in predicted binding (oracle) vs. unguided/natural binders. Verify structural (pLDDT) and humanness (OASis) preservation.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
### Open Question 1
To what extent does the first-order approximation error affect performance on real clonal trees with varying branch lengths, and can this error be mitigated for longer evolutionary distances?
- **Basis:** Paper proves quadratic error bound and notes potential misspecification on longer branches, but empirical validation is limited.
- **Why unresolved:** Theoretical bound assumes idealized conditions; impact across diverse real trees is unknown.
- **Resolution evidence:** Systematic evaluation on trees with known ground truth or high-quality phylogenies, comparing CoSiNE probabilities to exact CTMC simulations.

### Open Question 2
Can CoSiNE be extended to incorporate insertions and deletions (indels) while preserving tractability and biological realism?
- **Basis:** Paper explicitly acknowledges ignoring indels as a key limitation.
- **Why unresolved:** Current framework assumes point mutations only; indels require variable-length state space, breaking factorization.
- **Resolution evidence:** Develop a variant modeling indel events (e.g., hierarchical rate matrices) and validate on datasets with observed indels.

### Open Question 3
What factors determine when inter-chain conditioning outperforms single-chain conditioning, and how does training data composition influence this?
- **Basis:** Section C.6 notes paired conditioning sometimes underperforms single-chain, without investigation.
- **Why unresolved:** Cause unclear—may be noisy inter-chain dependencies, model capacity, or task relevance.
- **Resolution evidence:** Controlled experiments varying paired/single-chain proportions in training; measure performance on paired vs. single-chain DMS; analyze learned representations.

### Open Question 4
How well does CoSiNE scale to full-length proteins (>500 residues) while modeling long-range epistasis, and what approximations are necessary?
- **Basis:** Paper notes limitations are less concerning for antibodies but pose greater challenges generalizing to all proteins.
- **Why unresolved:** Current design for ~120-residue antibodies; longer sequences increase state space exponentially.
- **Resolution evidence:** Apply CoSiNE to longer protein families using approximate inference (sparse attention, hierarchical decomposition); evaluate performance and runtime scaling.

## Limitations
- **Hyperparameter uncertainty:** Exact training details (lr schedule nuances, weight decay exclusions) require empirical tuning for 1M-step runs.
- **Thrifty SHM integration:** Unclear specifics (version, codon backtranslation for non-human germlines, multihit correction) risk selection score miscalibration.
- **Guidance oracle details:** Unpublished RNN weights, MC dropout setup, and thresholding strategy hinder faithful replication of Guided Gillespie.

## Confidence
- **CTMC approximation mechanism:** High (Proposition 4.1 proof + synthetic validation).
- **Selection score disentanglement:** Medium (strong DMS correlations but depends on correct Thrifty implementation).
- **Guided sampling mechanism:** Medium (empirical affinity gains but assumes local linearity and well-calibrated oracle).
- **Overall empirical results:** Medium (SOTA on benchmarks but limited independent citations/verification).
