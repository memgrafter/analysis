# Learning from Mistakes: Negative Reasoning Samples Enhance Out-of-Domain Generalization

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2601.04992
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.04992
- **Reference count:** 40
- **Primary result:** Negative-only supervised fine-tuning (SFT) outperforms positive-only SFT on out-of-domain reasoning tasks (average gain +11.97% for Qwen2.5-7B), and the proposed GLOW weighting further improves OOD performance by 5.51% over positive-only and raises MMLU from 72.82% to 76.47% as an RL initialization.

## Executive Summary
This paper investigates the role of negative reasoning trajectories—chain-of-thought samples with incorrect final answers—in improving out-of-domain (OOD) generalization of large language models. While standard supervised fine-tuning (SFT) discards such negatives, the authors show they contain diverse intermediate reasoning patterns that act as a regularizer, slowing loss descent and increasing policy entropy to encourage broader exploration. Building on this, they propose Gain-based LOss Weighting (GLOW), which adaptively upweights samples with low inter-epoch loss reduction to prioritize underexplored reasoning modes. Experiments on Qwen2.5 and Llama3.1 models demonstrate that negative-only SFT surpasses positive-only on OOD benchmarks, and GLOW applied to mixed data yields additional gains. The work highlights the value of learning from mistakes and offers a practical method to harness negative samples.

## Method Summary
The authors generate chain-of-thought (CoT) trajectories by distilling from a teacher model (e.g., Qwen3-8B) on math and multi-task knowledge datasets. Each trajectory is labeled positive if the final answer matches the ground truth, otherwise negative. Three SFT regimes are compared: positive-only, negative-only, and mixed (all). The key innovation is GLOW, a gain-based loss weighting scheme: for each sample, inter-epoch loss reduction Δ is computed, and weight w = α(1 - σ(βΔ)). Samples with small Δ (low gain) are upweighted to focus optimization on undercovered patterns. Training uses next-token prediction for 20 epochs with standard hyperparameters (batch 128, cosine LR, peak 5e-5). Models are evaluated on in-domain and OOD benchmarks using greedy and sampling decoding.

## Key Results
- Negative-only SFT yields substantial OOD generalization improvements; e.g., average gain of +11.97% on general reasoning tasks for Qwen2.5-7B compared to positive-only.
- GLOW applied to mixed data further improves OOD performance by 5.51% over positive-only SFT and raises MMLU accuracy from 72.82% to 76.47% when used as RL initialization.
- Negative trajectories increase policy entropy during the thinking phase by 35.67%, encouraging exploration, while maintaining decisive answer commitment.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1: Negatives as a regularizer
Negative trajectories contain diverse error patterns that expose the model to heterogeneous failure regimes. This diversity slows early loss reduction compared to homogeneous positives, forcing the model to learn invariant features that transfer across distributions. Many negatives possess partially valid intermediate reasoning, making them learnable rather than pure noise. If negatives are predominantly nonsensical, the regularization effect vanishes.

### Mechanism 2: Increased policy entropy
Training on negatives leads to higher token-level entropy during the thinking span of generated responses, while answer-span entropy drops sharply at commitment. This encourages broad exploration of reasoning paths, improving multi-sample efficiency (pass@k) and OOD performance. Excessive entropy without eventual commitment would harm coherence, but the observed pattern is beneficial.

### Mechanism 3: GLOW’s gain-based weighting
GLOW upweights samples with low inter-epoch loss reduction, which correspond to undercovered reasoning modes. Theoretically, this yields a positive semidefinite augmentation of the Fisher matrix, improving conditioning on low-curvature subspaces under a coverage assumption. Empirically, GLOW upweights many negatives early in training and consistently boosts OOD metrics. However, the coverage assumption may fail if low-gain samples are outliers, leading to instability.

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component map:** Teacher distillation -> Data pipeline (labeling) -> SFT trainer (with GLOW weighting) -> Evaluation harness.

**Critical path:** To reproduce GLOW: a) Distill ≥5k CoT trajectories from a teacher on the target task; b) Split into positive/negative sets; c) Initialize an SFT trainer and modify the loop to log per-sample loss; d) At epoch boundaries compute gain Δ and update weights via Eq. (4) for the next epoch; e) Train for 20 epochs with cosine LR and batch size 128; f) Evaluate on all benchmarks. Accurate loss logging and consistent shuffling are key.

**Design tradeoffs:** GLOW adds minimal compute overhead (storing loss tensors) but early Δ estimates are noisy. Hyperparameters α (scale) and β (sigmoid slope) control reweighting intensity; α∈[0.8,1.5], β≈12 work well. Higher β focuses on extremely low-gain samples but may amplify noise; α scales weight magnitude.

**Failure signatures:** 
1. The fraction of negatives among top-weighted samples collapses to 0% or 100% permanently, indicating broken gain computation.
2. Training loss becomes unstable or diverges after the first reweighting, often due to too large α.
3. OOD performance does not improve relative to full-SFT, possibly because the negative set lacks diversity or coverage assumption fails.

**First 3 experiments:**
1. Replicate positive-only vs negative-only SFT on Qwen2.5-7B with math data; confirm negative-only outperforms on OOD benchmarks (Table 1).
2. Run GLOW on mixed data for 20 epochs, varying α and β; measure average OOD gain to identify stable configurations (Figure 4).
3. Compute policy entropy for thinking and answer spans on an OOD set; verify GLOW increases thinking entropy and preserves answer-span decisiveness (Table 5).

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
1. **Interaction with RL post-training:** How do negative reasoning trajectories affect reinforcement learning stages compared to standard positive-only SFT initialization? The paper only evaluates SFT and one fixed RL initialization; RL dynamics remain unexplored.
2. **Causal mechanism of diversity:** What is the causal link between error pattern diversity in negatives and enhanced OOD generalization? The paper hypothesizes that diversity compels invariant feature learning but does not isolate specific patterns or manipulate diversity directly.
3. **Comparison to other dynamic weighting:** Does gain-based loss weighting in GLOW offer advantages over focal loss, curriculum learning, or uncertainty-based reweighting? The paper does not benchmark against these alternatives.
4. **Generalization beyond text-only CoT:** To what extent do the benefits of negative samples and GLOW extend to larger models, multimodal settings, and tool-augmented reasoning? Experiments are limited to text-only math and MMLU data with models up to 32B.

## Limitations
- The exact teacher prompting, decoding strategy, and number of sampled trajectories for generating negative trajectories are unspecified; additionally, the teacher model Qwen3-8B may not be publicly available, requiring approximations.
- GLOW implementation details (loss logging frequency, epoch vs. step weight updates) and hyperparameter selection rationale are not fully elaborated.
- The evaluation codebase and exact test split versions are not provided; minor differences in evaluation could affect reported metrics.
