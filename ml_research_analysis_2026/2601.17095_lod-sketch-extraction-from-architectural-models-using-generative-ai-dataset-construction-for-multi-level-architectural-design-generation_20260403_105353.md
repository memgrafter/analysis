# LoD Sketch Extraction from Architectural Models Using Generative AI: Dataset Construction for Multi-Level Architectural Design Generation

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2601.17095
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.17095
- Reference count: 2
- Primary result: Proposes an automatic framework to generate geometrically consistent multi-LoD sketches from high-detail models, constructing a dataset of ~75,600 aligned images across LoD1-3 with strong consistency metrics (SSIM >0.73, HD ~25-61% of diagonal).

## Executive Summary
The paper addresses the challenges of manual, inconsistent Level-of-Detail (LoD) modeling and the lack of paired training data for multi-LoD architectural design generation. It introduces a three-stage automatic framework that transforms high-detail 3D models into geometrically consistent sketches across LoD1–LoD3 by integrating computer vision and generative AI. The pipeline first extracts full-detail LoD3 sketches using gradient-based edge detection, then reduces detail to LoD2 via Stable Diffusion XL with a T2I-Adapter, and finally abstracts to LoD1 using a dual-ControlNet conditioned on sketches and depth. This yields a dataset of approximately 75,600 aligned LoD images, demonstrating strong geometric consistency as measured by SSIM and Hausdorff distance.

## Method Summary
The method constructs a multi-LoD sketch dataset by processing high-detail architectural renderings through three progressive stages. Stage 1 extracts LoD3 sketches from rendered images via grayscale conversion, Sobel gradient magnitude computation, blending with the original to suppress shadows, contrast adjustment, and morphological closing to produce clean structural lines. Stage 2 reduces LoD3 to LoD2 by feeding the original LoD3 image into Stable Diffusion XL conditioned by a T2I-Adapter; the generated simplified RGB image is then fed to the same sketch extractor. Stage 3 abstracts LoD2 to LoD1 using a dual-ControlNet that jointly conditions on the LoD2 sketch and an estimated depth map (via MiDaS) to produce shoebox volumes; the resulting image undergoes Canny edge detection and line refinement. The pipeline yields aligned LoD1, LoD2, LoD3 sketch pairs.

## Key Results
- Constructed a dataset of ~75,600 aligned images across LoD1–LoD3.
- Achieved SSIM of 0.7319 (LoD3→LoD2) and 0.7532 (LoD2→LoD1).
- Normalized Hausdorff distances: 25.1% (LoD3→LoD2) and 61.0% (LoD2→LoD1) of image diagonal, with the higher HD reflecting intentional volumetric simplification while preserving structural integrity.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1: Full-detail LoD3 extraction
Claim: Structural edges can be separated from illumination and texture noise using gradient analysis and morphological operations. Mechanism: Convert input to grayscale, compute Sobel gradient magnitude M, invert to brighten edges, blend with original grayscale using coefficient α to control shading retention, apply contrast adjustment with coefficient β for line thickness, and finally use Black-Hat morphology to darken fine lines uniformly. Core assumption: Structural edges have distinct gradient signatures. Evidence anchors: Section 3.1 describes the exact formula. Break condition: Architectural textures (e.g., brick) may produce gradients comparable to structural edges, causing texture lines to persist if α/β are mis-tuned.

### Mechanism 2: Detail reduction via intermediate RGB generation (LoD3→LoD2)
Claim: Mapping from detailed to simplified representation is more stable in the RGB image domain than directly between sketches. Mechanism: Use Stable Diffusion XL as generator, modulated by a T2I-Adapter (soft conditioning) to transform the LoD3 render into a simplified LoD2-style RGB image. Text prompts provide semantic guidance. The resulting image is fed into the Stage 1 sketch extractor. Core assumption: RGB images retain lighting/material cues that help the model learn fine-to-coarse transformation. Evidence anchors: Section 3.2 explains the preference for T2I-Adapter over ControlNet due to its softer conditioning. Break condition: Semantic drift (e.g., window placement changes, merging of building masses) in the generated LoD2 RGB would propagate to the LoD2 sketch.

### Mechanism 3: Volumetric abstraction via dual-ControlNet (LoD2→LoD1)
Claim: Combining precise 2D structural constraints (LoD2 sketch) and coarse 3D spatial relationships (depth) correctly simplifies building volumes into shoeboxes. Mechanism: Two ControlNet branches (Sketch and Depth) inject features into Stable Diffusion's UNet. The Sketch branch preserves global contours; the Depth branch maintains correct depth ordering. At inference, depth is estimated using MiDaS if ground truth is unavailable. Core assumption: The combination is necessary to remove fine details while maintaining aligned volumes. Evidence anchors: Section 3.3 details the dual-ControlNet injection and MiDaS usage. Break condition: Inaccurate depth maps (e.g., on complex façades) can impose wrong volumetric constraints, producing misaligned LoD1 shapes.

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component map**: Input (high-detail 3D model → rendered RGB 512×512) → Stage1 (grayscale → Sobel gradient → blend α → contrast β → Black-Hat → LoD3 sketch) → Stage2 (LoD3 image → Stable Diffusion XL + T2I-Adapter → LoD2 RGB → Stage1 extractor → LoD2 sketch) → Stage3 (LoD2 sketch + depth (MiDaS/GT) → dual-ControlNet (Sketch+Depth) → LoD1 RGB → Canny + line refinement → LoD1 sketch) → Output (aligned LoD1-3 sketches).

**Critical path**: Stage 1 must first produce clean LoD3 sketches; errors propagate downstream. Stage 2's T2I-Adapter training stability is a bottleneck; Stage 3 depends on reliable depth estimation (MiDaS). The pipeline is sequential and state-dependent.

**Design tradeoffs**: 
- T2I-Adapter vs. ControlNet in Stage 2: Adapter offers flexibility for detail suppression but may under-constrain geometry; ControlNet would preserve edges too rigidly, hindering detail removal.
- Depth source in Stage 3: MiDaS (no GT) enables broad application but introduces estimation error; GT depth ensures accuracy but requires paired depth data.
- Reusing Stage 1's sketch extractor for all stages ensures consistent line style but may not be optimal for LoD1's extreme simplification.

**Failure signatures**:
- High SSIM but high HD in LoD2→LoD1: Expected if simplification is intentional; but if SSIM also drops (<0.6), indicates structural corruption.
- Inconsistent line connectivity in Stage 1 outputs (broken contours) leading to misinterpretation in later stages.
- Semantic drift in Stage 2: generated LoD2 RGB shows altered rooflines or merged buildings → inherited errors.
- Depth-ControlNet conflict: LoD1 volumes correct but misaligned with LoD2 sketch outline due to incorrect depth constraints.

**First 3 experiments**:
1. **Stage 1 parameter robustness sweep**: Render a single building at LoD3. Vary α (0.0–1.0) and β (−0.5–0.5). Evaluate line continuity vs. texture retention via human inspection or edge density metrics. Identify robust parameter region.
2. **Stage 2 model sanity check**: Train T2I-Adapter on a small subset (5 models). Generate LoD2 images for unseen LoD3 renders. Visually confirm removal of windows/textures without altering building footprint/roof shape. Measure SSIM between generated LoD2 and target LoD2 RGB (if available).
3. **Depth estimation validation for Stage 3**: Run MiDaS on 50 LoD2 sketches. Compare estimated depth vs. GT depth (from render). Compute depth error metrics (Abs Rel, Sq Rel). Identify failure cases (e.g., shallow façades, glass walls) where depth error >15% may jeopardize LoD1 generation.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- How does the proposed LoD extraction framework compare to existing LoD abstraction methods in terms of geometric consistency and semantic preservation? (The paper states future work will focus on broader benchmarking against alternative methods.)
- To what extent do the generated LoD sketches align with human judgments of architectural abstraction quality and hierarchical coherence? (The paper identifies conducting perceptual evaluations as needed.)
- How sensitive is the quality of the final LoD1 sketches to errors in depth maps estimated by MiDaS, given the framework's reliance on this auxiliary input when ground truth depth is unavailable? (The paper mentions analyzing auxiliary input reliability as future work.)

## Limitations
- Unvalidated performance on real-world building images; the dataset uses rendered models only.
- Unquantified failure rate of Stage 3 due to MiDaS depth errors on complex façades.
- Uncertainty whether Stage 2 preserves precise topological relationships or only approximates massing.
