# PRISM: Probability Reallocation with In-Span Masking for Knowledge-Sensitive Alignment

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2604.01682
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.01682
- **Reference count:** 13
- **Primary result:** PRISM improves factual aggregates (HHEM, PopQA, TriviaQA) across Qwen3-4B/8B and Llama 3.1-8B while maintaining competitive general capabilities (MMLU, GSM8K, HumanEval, IFEval) via a risk-gated auxiliary loss that selectively penalizes high-confidence predictions on fact-critical spans.

## Executive Summary
Standard supervised fine-tuning (SFT) with token-level hard labels can reinforce overconfident hallucinations on unsupported facts. PRISM addresses this by augmenting SFT with coarse atomic fact and cross-sentence dependency annotations. It introduces a lightweight, differentiable risk-gated objective that selectively reduces model probability on risky, fact-critical tokens—without specifying alternatives—using a model-aware consistency gate to ensure corrections stay aligned with the provided target. Evaluations show consistent factual gains over vanilla SFT and strong baselines (SEAL, NOVÁ), with a trade-off controlled by an auxiliary weight λ (optimal ≈0.1). Ablations confirm that both probability reallocation and knowledge masking are necessary components.

## Method Summary
PRISM takes standard SFT pairs (instruction `x`, response `y`). Each response is preprocessed to produce: (1) per-token `fact-active mask` marking spans aligned to atomic propositions; (2) per-token `support weight` `w_t` derived from sentence-level factuality risk scores propagated along a dependency graph. During training, the standard cross-entropy loss `L_SFT` is applied to all valid tokens. An auxiliary complement loss `L_comp` is activated only on fact-critical positions where: (a) the target token is the model's current top-1 prediction, and (b) after redistributing probability mass by factor `w_t`, the target would remain top-1. The combined loss `L = L_SFT + λL_comp` is optimized with AdamW. This selectively suppresses overconfidence on risky facts without altering the target sequence.

## Key Results
- PRISM achieves consistent improvements on factual benchmarks (HHEM, PopQA, TriviaQA) across Qwen3-4B/8B and Llama 3.1-8B, outperforming both vanilla SFT and recent baselines SEAL/NOVÁ.
- A moderate auxiliary weight `λ=0.1` balances factual correction and capability preservation; higher `λ` degrades general performance (MMLU, GSM8K, HumanEval, IFEval).
- Ablations confirm both the model-aware consistency gate and knowledge masking (`m_fact_t`) are necessary for optimal performance; removing either reduces factual gains.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1: Selective Probability Suppression on Risky Tokens
For tokens within fact-critical spans (`m_fact_t=1`), when the target token `y_t` is the model's top-1 prediction and would remain so after redistribution, a complement loss `L_comp` decreases `p_θ(y_t|x, y_{<t})`. This causes probability mass to reallocate to other tokens according to the model's existing distribution, mitigating over-imitation without specifying alternatives. The penalty strength is modulated by risk weight `w_t` (lower for riskier sentences) and auxiliary weight `λ`. **Break condition:** If `y_t` is not top-1 or redistribution would dethrone it, `L_comp` is not applied, so no correction occurs even for risky tokens.

### Mechanism 2: Model-Aware Consistency Gating
The gate ensures the reallocation objective does not push the model's sequence preference away from the provided target. After computing the intended redistribution (reducing `p_θ(y_t)` by factor `w_t`), it checks whether `y_t` would still be the top-1 token post-redistribution. Only if true (`g_keep_t=1`) is `L_comp` activated. This prevents gradient updates that inadvertently optimize toward a different continuation. **Break condition:** If model confidence is very high and competing probabilities are non-trivial, `g_keep_t` may frequently be 0, suppressing the auxiliary update even on risky tokens, limiting correction strength.

### Mechanism 3: Cross-Sentence Risk Propagation
Sentence-level risks (`r_j`) are propagated along dependency edges (`E`), so a sentence's effective risk `˜r_j` is the maximum of its own risk and those of all supporting sentences. This `˜r_j` maps to `w_t = 1 - ˜r_{sid(t)}`, causing tokens in dependent sentences to inherit higher risk if they rely on unsupported premises. This mitigates cascading hallucinations in multi-sentence generation. **Break condition:** Noisy or missing dependency annotations cause inaccurate `˜r_j`, either failing to propagate risk (false negatives) or incorrectly penalizing supported facts (false positives).

## Foundational Learning
- **Concept: Autoregressive Training with Token-Level Cross-Entropy**  
  Why needed: PRISM modifies standard SFT; understanding the base gradient effect on high-likelihood tokens is prerequisite to seeing why over-imitation occurs.  
  Quick check: In SFT, what gradient does a token with `p_θ(y_t) ≈ 1` produce, and why might this reinforce unsupported facts beyond the model's pretraining knowledge?

- **Concept: Model Probability Distribution and Top-1 Preference**  
  Why needed: The consistency gate (`g_keep_t`) explicitly uses `argmax` and a post-redistribution inequality, relying on interpreting `p_θ` as the model's belief.  
  Quick check: If `p_θ(y_t)=0.9` and all others sum to `0.1`, what condition on `w_t` would cause `g_keep_t=0`? Intuitively, how does `w_t` affect the redistribution's impact on the top-1 status?

- **Concept: Atomic Fact Decomposition and Fact-Relation Graphs**  
  Why needed: PRISM's supervision requires coarse spans (`m_fact_t`) and dependencies (`E`) derived from atomic propositions.  
  Quick check: Given two sentences where sentence 2 depends on sentence 1, with `r_1=0.8` (risky) and `r_2=0.2`, what is `˜r_2`? How does this affect `w_t` for tokens in sentence 2?

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component map:**  
Input (SFT pair `(x, y)`) -> Fact Extractor (atomic facts `F`, dependency graph `E`) -> Risk Mapper (`˜r_j` via Eq.1, `w_t` via Eq.2) -> Training Loop (compute `p_θ`, gates `g_pref_t`, `g_keep_t`, activation `α_t`, apply `L_SFT` + `λL_comp`) -> Optimizer (AdamW)

**Critical path:** Fact extraction → `w_t` mapping → per-step gating (`α_t`) → selective `L_comp` application. Failures in fact extraction or gating directly break selective correction.

**Design tradeoffs:**  
- `λ` strength: Higher `λ` increases factual correction but risks degrading general capabilities; `λ=0.1` recommended.  
- Gate precision vs. coverage: Strict `g_keep_t` ensures target alignment but may skip many risky tokens; looser gates risk target mismatch.  
- Annotation cost: Requires atomic fact and dependency extraction, which may be noisy or expensive.

**Failure signatures:**  
- Over-correction: General capability drops (e.g., IFEval, MMLU) with high `λ` (≥0.5). Monitor common average vs. factual average.  
- No effect: `α_t` frequently 0, e.g., if fact spans are too conservative (`m_fact_t` sparse) or `g_keep_t` overly strict. Check `L_comp` activation rate.  
- Inconsistent factuality: If dependency propagation is broken (e.g., `E` empty), later sentences won't inherit risk, allowing cascading hallucinations.

**First 3 experiments:**  
1. Sweep `λ` on a single backbone (e.g., Qwen3-4B) to verify the trade-off curve; select `λ` that improves Factual Avg with minimal Common Avg drop.
2. Component ablation: disable model-aware gate (keep only `w_t` weighting) and disable knowledge mask (`m_fact_t` always 1) to confirm both are needed.
3. Cross-backbone replication (e.g., Qwen vs. Llama) to check robustness of gains; ensure identical training data and preprocessing across baselines.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
1. **How can the reliability of the fact extraction and verification pipeline be improved?**  
   Basis: Section 6 (Conclusion) explicitly mentions future work on improving reliability.  
   Why unresolved: The pipeline discards parsing failures, but remaining atomic fact and dependency annotations may still be noisy, degrading training signal quality.  
   Evidence needed: Evaluate alternative fact extraction models (e.g., larger LLMs, specialized parsers), introduce human-in-the-loop verification for a subset, or develop noise-robust objectives that account for annotation uncertainty.

2. **Can adaptive scheduling of λ further improve the factuality-capability trade-off?**  
   Basis: Section 6 suggests exploring more adaptive ways to schedule the auxiliary signal across domains and training stages.  
   Why unresolved: Ablations use a single fixed `λ` (e.g., 0.1); optimal `λ` may vary with data distribution or training progress.  
   Evidence needed: Implement dynamic `λ` strategies (instance-level risk-based, curriculum-based, RL-optimized) and compare against fixed `λ` on factual and common benchmarks.

3. **How does base model factuality moderate PRISM's effectiveness?**  
   Basis: Inferred from Table 1: Qwen3-4B (high baseline HHEM ~94.5) shows small PRISM gains, while Llama 3.1-8B (lower baseline ~88.1) shows larger improvements; authors note "ceiling effects."  
   Why unresolved: Only three backbones tested; correlation between baseline factuality and marginal benefit not systematically analyzed.  
   Evidence needed: Evaluate PRISM on a wider range of models with varying pretraining data and scales; statistically correlate baseline factuality (e.g., HHEM) with relative PRISM gains to test for inverse relationships.

4. **What is the precise theoretical interaction between knowledge masking and model-aware reallocation?**  
   Basis: Inferred from Table 3; authors state they "play complementary roles" but lack explanatory analysis.  
   Why unresolved: No dissection of optimization dynamics, gradient interactions, or how each affects the decision boundary on fact-critical spans.  
   Evidence needed: Analyze gradient norms and directions at factual positions for each ablated variant; study probability mass shifts; visualize attention or confidence changes to understand how masking constrains updates and how gating refines them.

## Limitations
- **Underspecified annotation pipeline:** Exact atomic fact extraction model, its training data, and heuristic for dependency edges are not detailed, directly affecting `m_fact_t` and `w_t` fidelity.
- **Unclear risk scoring:** Per-sentence risk score `r_j` is not concretely defined (e.g., NLI threshold, self-consistency metric), hindering faithful reproduction of `w_t`.
- **Generalization constraints:** Results are on curated `lmsys-chat-1m-clean` with preprocessed annotations; effectiveness on noisier real-world dialogues or domains without clear sentence dependencies is unknown.

## Confidence
- **Factual improvement across models (Table 1):** **High** (consistent gains across three distinct backbones on multiple factual benchmarks)
- **Mechanism efficacy (selective reallocation, gating, propagation):** **Medium** (relies on internal components whose exact implementation and failure modes are not fully disclosed; ablation evidence is internal)
- **Capability preservation trade-off (λ sweep):** **High** (clear ablation showing `λ=0.1` as robust sweet spot; higher λ degrades common averages)
- **Necessity of both masking and gating (Table 3):** **High** (ablations confirm joint requirement; performance drops when either disabled)

## Next Checks
1. **Validate fact extraction fidelity:** Run the described three-stage pipeline on 200 random `lmsys-chat-1m-clean` instances. Manually verify (a) span alignment `m_fact_t` covers atomic propositions without over-marking, and (b) dependency edges `E` reflect logical support. Report precision/recall against human annotations.
2. **Audit `g_keep_t` activation dynamics:** During training on Qwen3-4B, log per-batch: fraction of fact-active tokens where `g_pref_t=1`, and among those, fraction where `g_keep_t=1`. If `g_keep_t` is nearly always 0 or 1, the gate is too strict or ineffective; adjust `w_t` scaling and re-evaluate factual gains.
3. **Test out-of-distribution robustness:** Apply a saved `λ=0.1` PRISM checkpoint (trained on chat data) to a held-out factual QA dataset (e.g., TruthfulQA) *without* re-annotating. Compare factual accuracy against vanilla SFT; failure indicates overfitting to annotation heuristics.
