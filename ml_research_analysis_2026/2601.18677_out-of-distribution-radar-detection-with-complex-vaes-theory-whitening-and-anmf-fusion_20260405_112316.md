# Out-of-Distribution Radar Detection with Complex VAEs: Theory, Whitening, and ANMF Fusion

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID**: 2601.18677
- **Source URL**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.18677
- **Reference count**: 40
- **Primary result**: A complex-valued VAE trained on clutter-plus-noise, combined with local whitening and decision-level fusion with ANMF, achieves higher detection probability than classical adaptive detectors in non-Gaussian maritime radar clutter, with gains up to ~2 dB and improved robustness across Doppler bins.

## Executive Summary
The paper introduces a framework for weak target detection in non-Gaussian, range-varying maritime radar clutter using a complex-valued variational autoencoder (CVAE). The CVAE is trained solely on clutter-plus-noise (H0) and detects targets as out-of-distribution samples via reconstruction error. Inputs are complex I/Q radar snapshots or Doppler profiles. Local whitening using neighboring range covariances enhances performance, particularly off the clutter ridge. The CVAE score is calibration per Doppler bin via probability integral transform (PIT) to achieve constant false-alarm rate (CFAR) and fused with an adaptive matched filter (ANMF) using a weighted log-p combination. Evaluations on simulated compound-Gaussian clutter and real CSIR sea-clutter data show that the fused CVAE-ANMF scheme outperforms classical detectors (MF, NMF, AMF-SCM, ANMF-Tyler) with improved detection probability and homogeneity across Doppler frequencies.

## Method Summary
The method proceeds as follows: (1) Preprocess radar data: extract slow-time vectors per range gate; optionally apply local whitening using a covariance estimate from adjacent ranges (regularized) before computing Doppler profiles. (2) Train a complex-valued VAE on H0 profiles. The encoder outputs a complex mean \(\mu\), a real scale \(\sigma\), and a complex pseudo-variance \(\delta\); the decoder reconstructs the input. The loss is the \(\beta\)-ELBO (reconstruction + closed-form KL). (3) For detection, compute the reconstruction error \(\|z-\hat{z}\|^2\) for a test sample. Calibrate this raw score per Doppler bin using the empirical CDF of H0 reconstruction errors (from a held-out set) to obtain a p-value via the probability integral transform (PIT). (4) Similarly, compute an ANMF statistic using secondary data (SCM or Tyler’s estimator) and calibrate it per Doppler bin via PIT to get a second p-value. (5) Fuse the two p-values: for each Doppler bin, compute a weighted log-p score \(S = - (w_b \log p_{\text{ANMF}} + (1-w_b) \log p_{\text{CVAE}})\), where weights \(w_b\) may be derived from the spread of CVAE p-values under H0 or a Doppler prior. The fused score distribution under H0 is used to set a threshold for the desired false-alarm rate.

## Key Results
- The CVAE (especially with local whitening) achieves higher detection probability than classical adaptive detectors (ANMF-Tyler, AMF-SCM) at \(P_{fa}=10^{-2}\) in simulated compound-Gaussian clutter.
- On real CSIR sea-clutter data, local whitening enables the CVAE to complement ANMF-Tyler, notably improving detection off the clutter ridge where classical detectors struggle.
- Decision-level fusion via weighted log-p aggregation yields robust performance in heavy-tailed clutter, provides empirical \(P_{fa}\) control, and delivers up to \(\sim\)2 dB gain with more homogeneous \(P_d\) across Doppler bins and SNR.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The CVAE learns a compact latent representation of the clutter-plus-noise distribution from training data. A target signal is an out-of-distribution sample for this model, producing a large reconstruction error. Per-Doppler PIT calibration transforms raw reconstruction errors into uniform p-values under H0, enabling CFAR thresholding. Local whitening preprocesses slow-time vectors to have approximately white, unit-variance clutter in each range, which standardizes the input to the CVAE and improves its modeling accuracy, particularly when clutter statistics vary with range. Fusion with ANMF combines the strengths of both detectors: ANMF is optimal for Gaussian clutter, while the CVAE captures non-Gaussian structure. The weighted log-p combination uses the CVAE’s confidence (e.g., via p-value dispersion) to modulate the contribution of the ANMF score, effectively down-weighting the ANMF when its assumptions are violated (heavy tails) and thereby maintaining robustness and CFAR compliance.

## Foundational Learning
- **Variational Autoencoders (VAEs)**: generative models that learn a latent distribution; needed to model complex clutter distributions and compute reconstruction-based OOD scores. Quick check: understand ELBO, reparameterization trick, and how latent space structure affects generation.
- **Out-of-Distribution (OOD) Detection**: identifies samples that differ from training data; core mechanism here. Quick check: know common OOD metrics (reconstruction error, likelihood) and their limitations.
- **Complex-Valued Neural Networks**: preserve phase information in I/Q data; crucial for radar Doppler processing. Quick check: review complex activations (modReLU, CReLU) and complex backpropagation.
- **Whitening and Covariance Estimation**: normalizes data to have identity covariance; mitigates range-varying clutter. Quick check: understand whitening transform, matrix square root, and regularization of sample covariance.
- **Adaptive Matched Filtering (ANMF)**: classical detector that adapts to clutter covariance using secondary data. Quick check: derive ANMF statistic and its CFAR property under Gaussian clutter.
- **Probability Integral Transform (PIT)**: converts any continuous random variable to uniform via its CDF; used for score calibration. Quick check: recall that if \(F\) is the true CDF, then \(F(X) \sim \text{Uniform}(0,1)\).

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component map**  
Input (complex I/Q snapshots) -> Optional: Local Whitening -> CVAE (Encoder -> Latent Sampling -> Decoder) -> Reconstruction Error -> PIT Calibration -> CVAE p-value  
Input -> ANMF (Secondary Covariance Estimation -> ANMF Statistic) -> PIT Calibration -> ANMF p-value  
(CVAE p-value, ANMF p-value) -> Weighted Log-p Fusion -> Fused Score -> Thresholding -> Detection Decision

**Critical path**  
1. Acquire radar I/Q samples per range gate.  
2. (If whitening) For each range, compute sample covariance from adjacent ranges, regularize, whiten slow-time vectors.  
3. Form Doppler profiles via DFT (or use raw snapshots).  
4. CVAE inference: encode, sample latent, decode, compute reconstruction error (squared L2 norm).  
5. Per-Doppler calibration: map raw error to p-value using H0 empirical CDF.  
6. Similarly for ANMF: estimate clutter covariance from secondary data, compute ANMF statistic, calibrate per Doppler.  
7. Fuse p-values: \(S = - (w_b \log p_{\text{ANMF}} + (1-w_b) \log p_{\text{CVAE}})\) with Doppler-dependent weights \(w_b\).  
8. Threshold \(S\) using H0 fused score distribution to achieve target \(P_{fa}\).

**Design tradeoffs**  
- CVAE capacity vs. overfitting: Larger networks model complex clutter but risk overfitting, reducing OOD sensitivity.  
- Local whitening window: Larger neighborhood improves covariance estimate but may smooth range variations; too small yields noisy estimates.  
- Fusion weight selection: Fixed weights (e.g., equal) are simple but may not exploit complementarity; adaptive weights risk breaking CFAR if mis-calibrated.  
- Per-Doppler calibration: Accounts for Doppler-dependent statistics but requires sufficient H0 samples per bin; global calibration may be insufficient.

**Failure signatures**  
- CVAE reconstruction error on H0 is high or non-uniform across Doppler: PIT calibration fails, causing \(P_{fa}\) drift. Check H0 score histograms and uniformity of p-values.  
- Whitening instability: Condition number of local covariance is high; leads to amplified noise. Increase ridge regularization or neighborhood size.  
- Fusion degradation: Fused performance worse than individual detectors; likely due to poor weight choices. Test simple average as baseline.  
- ANMF covariance misestimation: With limited secondary data, SCM becomes ill-conditioned; Tyler’s estimator is more robust but slower.

**Exactly 3 first experiments**  
1. **Baseline CVAE without whitening**: Train CVAE on raw snapshots/Doppler profiles; evaluate \(P_d\) vs. SNR on simulated compound-Gaussian clutter. Verify that CVAE alone outperforms or matches ANMF-Tyler.  
2. **Whitening parameter sweep**: Vary number of adjacent ranges (\(N_{adj}\)) and ridge regularization (\(\epsilon_{ridge}\)) on real CSIR data; measure \(P_d\) improvement and stability. Identify parameter regime where whitening helps without causing numerical issues.  
3. **Fusion weight ablation**: Compare (a) equal weighting (\(w_b=0.5\)), (b) proposed sigmoid-weighted log-p, (c) data-driven weight learning (via grid search on validation H0). Assess \(P_d\) homogeneity across Doppler and robustness to clutter heaviness.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
1. How can the proposed CVAE-based detection framework be extended from 1D Doppler profiles to 2D range-Doppler patches or multi-channel array data while preserving detection performance and CFAR compliance?
2. Can the fusion weights in the weighted log-p combination be learned directly from H0 data to optimize detection performance while preserving CFAR properties?
3. How can the CVAE model and its null calibration be adapted online to track slowly varying clutter environments without requiring full retraining or manual recalibration?
4. What theoretical conditions ensure that the per-Doppler PIT calibration of the CVAE reconstruction error yields exact CFAR, and how do model misspecification and finite samples affect this property?

## Limitations
- **Reproducibility gaps**: Critical hyperparameters (CVAE architecture, training settings, whitening/fusion parameters) are unspecified, forcing arbitrary choices that may significantly impact performance.
- **Generalizability**: Evaluation relies on only two CSIR X-band sea-clutter scenes; transferability to other radar bands, sea states, or geographies remains unproven.
- **Theoretical assurances**: The per-Doppler PIT calibration lacks formal analysis; its CFAR guarantee under model misspecification and finite samples is uncertain.

## Confidence
- **CVAE outperforms classical detectors in simulated compound-Gaussian clutter**: Medium.
- **Local whitening substantially improves CVAE performance, especially off-clutter-ridge**: Medium.
- **Decision-level fusion (CVAE+ANMF) yields robust, homogeneous \(P_d\)**: Medium.

## Next Checks
1. **Ablation study on CVAE design choices**: Systematically vary latent dimension, network depth, and \(\beta\)-ELBO weight; measure \(P_d\) degradation to identify critical hyperparameters and establish robustness bounds.
2. **Cross-dataset validation**: Apply the CSIR-trained CVAE (without retraining) to an independent sea-clutter dataset (e.g., OMAR or Australian data); quantify \(P_d/P_{fa}\) consistency and domain shift effects.
3. **Sensitivity analysis of whitening and fusion parameters**: Sweep local whitening parameters (\(N_{adj}\), \(\epsilon_{ridge}\)) and test alternative fusion schemes (equal weighting, majority vote) versus proposed weighted log-p; report performance variance to assess practical resilience.
