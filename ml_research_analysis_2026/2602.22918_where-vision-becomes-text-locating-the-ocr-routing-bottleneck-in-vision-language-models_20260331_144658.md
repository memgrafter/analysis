# Where Vision Becomes Text: Locating the OCR Routing Bottleneck in Vision-Language Models

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2602.22918
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.22918
- **Reference count:** 5
- **Primary result:** Vision-language models exhibit architecture-dependent OCR bottlenecks (mid-depth for DeepStack, early for single-stage), the OCR signal is low-dimensional (PC1=72.9% variance), and counterintuitively, removing OCR subspaces in Qwen3-VL-4B boosts object-counting by up to 6.9%.

## Executive Summary
This paper investigates how vision-language models (VLMs) process textual information in images, identifying the layers where optical character recognition (OCR) becomes separable from other visual features. Using a method that compares activations between original images and versions with text removed via inpainting, the authors compute activation differences and apply PCA to isolate OCR-related subspaces. They find that the bottleneck layer—where OCR information is most critical and concentrated—varies systematically with the model architecture: DeepStack models like Qwen show a mid-depth bottleneck (~50% depth), whereas single-stage projection models like Phi-4 and InternVL show an early bottleneck (6–25% depth). The OCR signal is remarkably low-dimensional, with the first principal component explaining 72.9% of variance, and these PCA directions transfer across OCR datasets. Notably, removing the OCR subspace in the modular Qwen3-VL-4B model improves object-counting performance by up to 6.9 percentage points, suggesting that OCR processing can interfere with other visual capabilities.

## Method Summary
The core method uses paired images: original images and text-inpainted versions (text removed). For each model, forward passes extract per-layer residual activations for both conditions. Activation differences per layer (`Δhℓ = h_original − h_inpainted`) are computed over a training set (e.g., 315 images from EgoTextVQA). PCA on these differences per layer yields principal components (PCs) representing the "OCR signal." To localize the bottleneck, the authors sweep across layers, projecting out the top *N* PCs (usually *N*=5) from activations and measuring the resulting drop in OCR accuracy on a test set. The layer causing maximal accuracy drop identifies the bottleneck. The same PCs are then used to intervene on other tasks (counting, spatial reasoning) to assess interference and cross-dataset transferability. Controls include random masking and alternative inpainting tovalidate that differences capture OCR-specific signal rather than artifacts.

## Key Results
- **Architecture-dependent bottleneck:** DeepStack (Qwen) peaks at mid-depth (~50%), single-stage (Phi-4, InternVL) at early layers (6–25%).
- **Low-dimensional OCR signal:** PC1 captures 72.9% of variance in activation differences; removing top 3–5 PCs strongly suppresses OCR.
- **Interference effect:** Removing OCR subspaces in Qwen3-VL-4B improves object-counting by up to +6.9 percentage points, with only mild degradation on spatial tasks.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1: Architecture determines bottleneck location
- **Claim:** The location of the OCR processing bottleneck is determined by the vision-language integration strategy of the model architecture.
- **Mechanism:** By comparing activations between original and text-removed images, the study computes activation differences (`Δhℓ`). PCA on these differences isolates directions corresponding to "text presence." The layer-wise impact of removing these directions (via projection) on OCR accuracy reveals where OCR information is most critical and separable. A sharp drop in OCR accuracy upon intervention pinpoints the bottleneck.
- **Core assumption:** Activation differences between original and text-removed images (`original − inpainted`) primarily capture OCR-specific signal, not general image perturbation artifacts. This is validated by control experiments with random masking showing minimal effect.
- **Evidence anchors:** DeepStack models (Qwen) show peak sensitivity at mid-depth (~50%) for scene text, while single-stage projection models (Phi-4, InternVL) peak at early layers (6–25%); section 3.4 describes the PCA-based intervention method; corpus neighbors focus on OCR-enhanced VLMs but do not analyze internal routing bottlenecks.
- **Break condition:** If vision-language fusion strategy changes (e.g., adding cross-attention at multiple depths), the bottleneck location would shift accordingly. The mechanism would fail if activation differences did not correlate causally with OCR capability.

### Mechanism 2: OCR signal occupies a low-dimensional subspace
- **Claim:** OCR-related information in the residual stream occupies a remarkably low-dimensional subspace.
- **Mechanism:** PCA on activation deltas shows that the first principal component (PC1) captures 72.9% of the variance. Removing just the top 3–5 PCs strongly suppresses OCR accuracy, indicating that the "OCR signal" is concentrated in a few dominant directions rather than distributed across many neurons.
- **Core assumption:** The dominant variance in `original − inpainted` activations corresponds to the core OCR processing pathway. This assumes that other semantic changes between original and inpainted images (e.g., texture, context) are negligible or orthogonal.
- **Evidence anchors:** PC1 captures 72.9% of variance; removing PC1–PC3 achieves strong OCR suppression; control with different inpainting methods yields similar PCA variance (68–73%), supporting robustness.
- **Break condition:** If OCR processing were highly distributed or entangled with other features, PCA would not show such concentration. The mechanism would break if inpainting artifacts dominated the activation differences.

### Mechanism 3: OCR removal can improve other tasks via interference
- **Claim:** In models with relatively separable OCR circuits (e.g., DeepStack Qwen), OCR processing can interfere with other visual tasks (e.g., object counting), so targeted removal can improve non-OCR performance.
- **Mechanism:** Interventions that remove OCR-related subspaces at the identified bottleneck layer (e.g., L17 in Qwen3-VL-4B) sometimes improve CountBench scores (+6.9pp). This suggests that the OCR pathway competes for representational capacity or activations in the residual stream, and its removal frees resources for other visual processing.
- **Core assumption:** The residual stream has limited capacity, and features for different tasks (OCR vs. counting) can interfere. The observed improvement is causal from OCR removal, not from other intervention side-effects.
- **Evidence anchors:** Qwen3-VL-4B shows CountBench improvement (+4.3pp) with L17–19_pc5 intervention, while spatial reasoning degrades only mildly (−2pp); head ablation of OCR-selective heads (93 heads) improves CountBench (+5.0pp), directionally consistent.
- **Break condition:** The interference effect is not universal—InternVL degrades on all tasks when OCR is removed, suggesting tight coupling. The mechanism holds only when OCR is processed in a relatively separable module; it would break if OCR were perfectly integrated or if counting relied on text.

## Foundational Learning

### Concept 1: PCA for subspace identification
- **Why needed:** To isolate the low-dimensional directions in the residual stream that encode OCR information from the activation differences between original and inpainted images.
- **Quick check:** If PC1 explains 73% of variance in activation differences, what does that imply about the geometry of the OCR representation in the residual stream?

### Concept 2: Vision-language integration architectures
- **Why needed:** To understand why the bottleneck layer varies across models (DeepStack vs. single-stage projection), which is central to the paper's cross-architecture comparison.
- **Quick check:** Why might injecting visual features at multiple depths delay the emergence of a clear OCR bottleneck compared to single-stage injection?

### Concept 3: Causal intervention via activation patching
- **Why needed:** The method uses activation patching (restoring "clean" activations) to test causality: removing PCA-projected directions from the residual stream to see the effect on OCR accuracy.
- **Quick check:** What is the key assumption when using `h_original − h_inpainted` as the "OCR direction," and how is it validated?

## Architecture Onboarding

**Component map:**  
Image → ViT → Visual tokens.  
DeepStack (Qwen): Visual tokens from multiple ViT blocks (e.g., blocks 8, 16, 24) are injected into early LLM layers (0, 1, 2). LLM layers 0–36 process fused stream.  
Single-stage (Phi-4, InternVL): All visual tokens projected once at Layer 0 input, then processed through all LLM layers.  
Intervention: Per-layer residual stream hooks → Project out top-N PCs from activation deltas → Forward pass with modified activations.  
Output: Text generation.

**Critical path:**  
1. Prepare paired dataset (original + text-inpainted images).  
2. Run forward passes on both to extract per-layer residual activations for all tokens.  
3. Compute `Δhℓ` across a training split.  
4. Apply PCA per layer to get PCs.  
5. For evaluation, run intervention: `h_intervened = h − Σ_{i=1}^N (h·pc_i) pc_i` at target layers.  
6. Generate outputs and evaluate on OCR + retention benchmarks.  
7. Identify bottleneck layer(s) where OCR drops maximally.

**Design tradeoffs:**  
- PCA component count (*N*): Small *N* may not fully suppress OCR; large *N* risks collateral damage. Paper finds top 3–5 PCs effective.  
- Intervention scope: Single-layer vs. multi-layer interventions. Multi-layer (e.g., L16–20) can improve trade-offs but may over-remove.  
- Alpha (α): Paper fixes α=1 (full projection). Partial removal (α<1) could yield smoother effects but is not explored.  
- Dataset choice for PCA training: EgoTextVQA (scene text) used; transfer to document datasets (OCRBench, InfoVQA) works for directions but bottleneck layer may shift.

**Failure signatures:**  
- Artifact contamination: If inpainting introduces textures/patterns correlated with text regions, PCA may capture artifacts, not OCR. Validated by random-box control (minimal effect) and multiple inpainting methods (similar results).  
- Wrong bottleneck identification: If intervention at suspected layer does not drop OCR but does harm retention, you are likely intervening on non-bottleneck layers. Compare against control layers (e.g., L17–20 in InternVL should have minimal effect).  
- Non-transferable directions: If PCA directions trained on one dataset fail to suppress OCR on another, the model may have dataset-specific OCR circuits. Paper finds good transfer, but failure would indicate need for dataset-specific PCA.

**First 3 experiments:**  
1. **Replicate bottleneck localization:** For Qwen3-VL-4B, run PCA on EgoTextVQA deltas, plot OCR accuracy vs. layer for interventions removing PC1–5. Expect peak sensitivity at L16–L20.  
2. **Cross-architecture comparison:** Repeat for Phi-4. Expect early bottleneck (L3–L9). Confirm that intervention at DeepStack-equivalent layers (L17) has minimal effect on Phi-4, proving architecture dependence.  
3. **Interference test:** Apply Qwen3-VL-4B's best intervention (e.g., L17–19_pc5) to CountBench and RealWorldQA. Expect CountBench improvement (+4.3pp) and mild RWQ drop (−1 to −2pp). If both drop sharply, you may be over-removing (e.g., using pc10 instead of pc5).

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1: Does the bottleneck hypothesis generalize to MoE and OCR-specialized models?
- **Basis:** The Future Directions section explicitly states: "Extending this framework to Mixture-of-Experts (MoE) architectures, where OCR routing may involve expert selection rather than subspace routing, and to OCR-specialized models [...] would test the generality of the bottleneck hypothesis."
- **Why unresolved:** The study only validates the method on five dense-attention VLMs from two integration strategies (DeepStack, single-stage). MoE models may route OCR via expert activation rather than a contiguous subspace, while OCR-specialized models might bypass standard routing entirely, potentially invalidating the bottleneck concept.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Apply the same PCA-based intervention to MoE-based VLMs (e.g., Mixtral-integrated models) and OCR-specialized models. If concentrated sensitivity layers still appear, the hypothesis generalizes; if not, routing mechanisms fundamentally differ.

### Open Question 2: Why does the peak sensitivity layer shift across OCR datasets?
- **Basis:** Section 4.5 notes: "the max-sensitivity layer may shift across datasets because different text types (scene text vs. document layout) may emphasize different decoding stages." The paper observes this shift (e.g., Qwen3-VL-4B peaks at L18 for EgoTextVQA vs. L30 for OCRBench) but does not investigate the causes.
- **Why unresolved:** Without linking dataset properties to bottleneck location, the finding remains descriptive. Understanding this would clarify how text complexity influences internal processing stages and whether bottleneck localization is task- or data-dependent.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Analyze correlations between dataset metadata (text area ratio, character diversity, layout regularity) and measured bottleneck layers across multiple datasets. Controlled experiments with synthetic text systematically varying these properties could isolate causal factors.

### Open Question 3: Do other visual capabilities also reside in low-dimensional subspaces?
- **Basis:** The Discussion suggests that the low-dimensional OCR signal implies multimodal capabilities may organize into separable subspaces, with implications for steering. However, only OCR is characterized geometrically; other tasks are evaluated only for collateral damage from OCR removal.
- **Why unresolved:** It is unclear whether spatial or counting skills are similarly low-dimensional or whether they overlap with OCR subspaces. This limits the applicability of subspace interventions for interpretability and control.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Compute PCA on activation differences for spatial tasks (using EmbSpatial perturbations) and counting (using CountBench with object manipulations). If those signals also concentrate in few PCs, targeted subspace removal/isolation should be possible, tested via retention evaluations.

### Open Question 4: What specific computations cause shared representations between OCR and general visual processing?
- **Basis:** Table 2 shows L18_pc5 harms RealWorldQA (−13pp) while L17/L19 preserve it, yet L17–L19_pc5 improves counting (+4.3pp). This indicates L18 encodes representations detrimental to counting but useful for general VQA. The paper hypothesizes interference but does not dissect L18's role.
- **Why unresolved:** Without identifying whether L18 involves attention patterns, feature amalgamation, or competing representational slots, the mechanism of interference remains speculative. This limits understanding of how capabilities interact in transformer layers.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Perform mechanistic analysis on L18: visualize attention maps for OCR vs. non-OCR inputs, ablate neurons or heads selectively, and probe for features related to both text and spatial aggregation. Causal tracing from L18 to downstream task heads could identify critical pathways that are disrupted or cleared by OCR removal.

## Limitations
- Token pooling strategy for activation differences (mean vs. last token) is unspecified, potentially affecting PCA composition and bottleneck localization.
- Exact EgoTextVQA train/test split and prompt format are not disclosed, introducing reproducibility uncertainties.
- PCA subspace removal methodology (per-layer vs. joint across layer range) is ambiguous, which could alter intervention effects.

## Confidence
- **Bottleneck localization** (architecture-dependent mid vs. early layers): Medium – robust to inpainting method but sensitive to unspecified pooling/split choices.
- **Low-dimensional OCR signal** (PC1 variance >70%): High – consistently observed across controls; PCA transfer validates orthogonality to artifacts.
- **OCR removal improves counting** (+6.9pp): Medium – effect specific to DeepStack Qwen; fails in tightly coupled models (InternVL) – suggests conditional modularity.

## Next Checks
1. **Replicate bottleneck with alternative inpainting** (SDXL inpainting, Gaussian blur) – if location shifts, artifact risk is high.
2. **Vary pooling strategy** (image-tokens only, CLS token) – if bottleneck layers move significantly, the Δhℓ composition is unstable.
3. **Test interference on diverse counting tasks** (TallyQA, COCO-Text count) – if improvement holds only on CountBench, the effect may be benchmark-specific.
