# BiT-MCTS: A Theme-based Bidirectional MCTS Approach to Chinese Fiction Generation
## Quick Facts
- **ArXiv ID:** 2603.14410
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.14410
- **Reference count:** 40
- **Primary result:** A "climax-first, bidirectional expansion" strategy using MCTS substantially improves narrative coherence, structure, and length in long-form Chinese fiction generation versus strong baselines.

## Executive Summary
Generating long-form fiction from open-ended themes remains a challenge for LLMs, often resulting in poor global structure and limited narrative diversity. This paper introduces BiT-MCTS, a framework that operationalizes Freytag’s Pyramid by first extracting a core conflict and generating a concrete climactic event, which serves as a fixed narrative anchor. Using bidirectional Monte Carlo Tree Search (MCTS)—expanding forward for falling action and backward for rising action—the system constructs structured plot outlines. After a two-step refinement process, the polished outline drives segmented story realization. Evaluated on a Chinese theme corpus with three LLM backbones (GPT-5-mini, Gemini-2.5-Flash, DeepSeek-V3), BiT-MCTS achieves significantly higher win rates in automated and human evaluations, particularly in plot structure, emotional resonance, and thematic depth, while generating far longer coherent narratives.

## Method Summary
BiT-MCTS is a four-stage pipeline. First, an LLM extracts a core dramatic conflict from the theme and generates multiple candidate climax events, selecting the best via a screening LLM. Second, bidirectional MCTS uses the chosen climax as a root node: a forward pass explores falling action/resolution, and a backward pass (starting from the forward result) explores rising action/exposition. Each MCTS phase uses a UCT selection strategy, cached expansions (K=4 candidates per node), a guided simulation with early stopping (accepting extensions only if an LLM-based reward score does not decrease), and backpropagation. Third, outline refinement adds tailored opening/closing scenes and employs an LLM self-critic to revise the full outline for coherence and pacing. Finally, segmented generation produces the beginning, body, and ending in three separate LLM calls, each conditioned on the refined full outline.

## Key Results
- Automated LLM-as-judge win rates averaged 47.2–58.5% for BiT-MCTS versus 16.8–31.5% for strong baselines across 10 narrative dimensions.
- Human judges awarded a 43.82% average win rate, with leads in emotional resonance, plot structure, character development, and theme.
- Generated stories were substantially longer; e.g., with GPT-5-mini, BiT-MCTS produced 58,657 tokens versus 35,302 for the best baseline.
- Ablations showed near-100% performance loss when disabling bidirectional search ("-Bidirectional") or swapping the order ("-Order Swapped"), and a 94.0% loss for "-Refinement".

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1: Climax-First Anchoring
- **Claim:** Generating an explicit climax first, based on the theme's core conflict, provides a strong narrative anchor that improves global coherence and thematic focus.
- **Mechanism:** An LLM extracts a core conflict and generates a concrete climactic event, which becomes the root node for subsequent bidirectional plot expansion. This ensures all rising and falling actions are logically oriented toward a pre-defined, theme-relevant peak, as per Freytag’s Pyramid.
- **Core assumption:** A climactic event crystallizing the central conflict is the most effective fixed point for structuring a coherent narrative arc.
- **Evidence anchors:** The pipeline explicitly starts with conflict and climax generation; the climax anchors both MCTS phases. The necessity of a *fixed* anchor is implied by the catastrophic failure of order-swapped ablations.

### Mechanism 2: Bidirectional MCTS with Ordered Expansion
- **Claim:** Bidirectional Monte Carlo Tree Search—expanding backward for rising action and forward for falling action—produces more coherent and diverse plot outlines than unidirectional or sequential planning.
- **Mechanism:** Two separate MCTS phases use the climax as the root. Forward MCTS explores consequences (falling action/resolution), then backward MCTS explores antecedents (rising action/exposition), using the forward result as its root. An LLM-based reward function evaluates partial plot prefixes for coherence, creativity, and thematic alignment, guiding search via UCT. The forward-first ordering constrains backward generation, reducing incoherent setups.
- **Core assumption:** Planning the aftermath (falling action) before the cause (rising action) creates a stronger causal chain, as the consequences of the climax are known and can inform plausible backstory.
- **Evidence anchors:** The paper defines forward then backward expansion. Ablations show "-Bidirectional" causes 100% average loss, and "-Order Swapped" (backward then forward) causes 97.25% loss, confirming the critical necessity and specific ordering.

### Mechanism 3: Outline Refinement and Segmented Generation
- **Claim:** A two-step outline refinement followed by segmented generation transforms a rough MCTS outline into a long, coherent, and stylistically consistent fiction by fixing logical gaps and managing context limits.
- **Mechanism:** First, tailored opening and closing scenes are generated to bookend the core conflict. Second, an LLM self-critic reviews the full outline for contradictions and pacing issues, proposing reorderings/insertions/deletions. Finally, the polished outline is realized in three sequential LLM calls (beginning, body, ending), each conditioned on the full outline to maintain consistency.
- **Core assumption:** A structured outline is necessary for long-form coherence, but raw MCTS outputs require global polish; segmented generation prevents context collapse and allows segment-specific control.
- **Evidence anchors:** The refinement stage is explicitly described. Ablation "-Refinement" yields 94.0% average loss rate. Length analysis shows far longer coherent stories, supporting the efficacy of the outline-driven segmented approach.

## Foundational Learning
- **Concept: Freytag’s Pyramid**
  - *Why needed here:* Provides the theoretical narrative structure (exposition, rising action, climax, falling action, resolution) that the entire BiT-MCTS pipeline operationalizes. The "climax-first, bidirectional expansion" is a direct implementation.
  - *Quick check:* Given a theme and a generated climax, can you map which parts of a bidirectional outline correspond to rising action vs. falling action?
- **Concept: Monte Carlo Tree Search (MCTS)**
  - *Why needed here:* The core search algorithm for exploring the vast narrative space. Understanding selection (UCT), expansion, simulation, and backpropagation is key to grasping how plot outlines are systematically built and evaluated.
  - *Quick check:* How does the UCT formula balance exploring new plot directions vs. exploiting promising ones in the narrative tree?
- **Concept: LLM-as-Judge / Reward Function**
  - *Why needed here:* The evaluator `R(·)` is an LLM prompted to score partial plot outlines across 10 literary dimensions. This scalar reward drives the MCTS search toward high-quality, coherent structures.
  - *Quick check:* What are the key dimensions (e.g., causal/temporal relationship, theme exploration) the reward function uses, and why are they chosen over simple fluency metrics?

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map:**
  Theme → Conflict Processor → Climax Generator → Forward MCTS Engine → Backward MCTS Engine → Rough Outline → Outline Refiner (Opening/Closing + Self-Critic) → Refined Outline → Segmented Generator (Beginning, Body, Ending) → Full Fiction
- **Critical path:** The user theme generates a core conflict and a single climactic event (root node). A forward MCTS from this root explores falling action to produce a post-climax segment. A *new* backward MCTS is initialized with this post-climax segment as its root and explores rising action, generating a pre-climax segment. The concatenated pre-climax + (climax embedded in post-climax?) + post-climax forms the rough outline. *Correction:* The climax is the root for forward MCTS. The forward result (a sequence ending with climax and falling action) becomes the root for backward MCTS, which prepends rising action events. The complete outline is the backward-generated prefix plus the forward-generated suffix, with the climax situated at the pivot.
- **Design tradeoffs:**
  - *Bidirectional vs. Unidirectional:* Bidirectional is more complex but enforces a strong causal anchor via a fixed climax. Unidirectional (forward-only from premise) risks meandering or incoherent backstory.
  - *Early Stopping vs. Fixed Rollout:* Early stopping (accepting extensions only if reward doesn't decrease) is cheaper and empirically better than forcing full-depth rollouts, but may terminate too early in some narratives.
  - *Outline-driven vs. Direct Generation:* Outline-first adds stages but solves long-horizon coherence; direct generation is simpler but prone to structural failure.
- **Failure signatures:**
  - Incoherent causal chain: Events before and after the climax don't logically connect.
  - Thematic drift: Generated plot explores irrelevant sub-themes; low scores in "Theme Exploration".
  - Repetitive or formulaic plots: Low "Creativity" and "Diversity" scores, often from MCTS converging on a local optimum.
  - Short & incomplete stories: If MCTS terminates too early or refinement removes too much content.
- **First 3 experiments:**
  1. **Ablation: Disable MCTS (direct outline generation).** Expect severe drop in plot structure, coherence, and length. Validates search necessity.
  2. **Ablation: Swap Bidirectional Order (backward then forward).** Expect large performance drop, confirming "effect-before-cause" ordering is critical.
  3. **Stress Test: Provide a deeply abstract theme (e.g., "the void").** Observe if climax extraction fails or bidirectional search produces irrelevant branches. Tests limits of theme-to-conflict translation.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- **O1: Cross-lingual/cultural generalizability.** Does BiT-MCTS maintain effectiveness for non-Chinese languages and cultural themes? Future work is planned for other languages.
- **O2: Computational cost reduction.** Can MCTS cost be reduced without sacrificing quality? The paper notes unaddressed efficiency techniques (progressive widening, learned priors).
- **O3: Judge model bias.** To what extent does reliance on a single automatic judge (Qwen3-Max) bias results? The paper acknowledges potential bias from using one external model.
- **O4: Length-quality trade-off.** How does story length affect perceived quality? The paper did not conduct controlled studies to disentangle length from quality improvements.

## Limitations
- Exact prompts (self-critic, reward function) and the Chinese theme corpus are unreleased, blocking exact replication.
- Evaluation relies heavily on a single automatic judge (Qwen3-Max), risking model-specific bias.
- Generalizability beyond the evaluated Chinese theme set and to non-Freytag narrative structures is unestablished.

## Confidence
- **High:** Empirical improvements (win rates, length) and necessity of bidirectional MCTS (ablation results) are strongly supported.
- **Medium:** Mechanistic claims (climax as anchor, specific ordering) are supported by ablations and theory but may be sensitive to prompt/reward design.
- **Low:** Generalizability across languages and narrative structures is unproven.

## Next Checks
1. **Stress-test the climax anchor:** Run the pipeline on highly abstract themes (e.g., "entropy", "the void") and measure coherence/win rates. A significant drop would challenge the core assumption.
2. **Prompt reconstruction audit:** Systematically vary the missing self-critic and reward prompts using open-source reimplementations to determine which design choices are critical.
3. **Cross-judge consistency test:** Re-evaluate a sample of stories using 3+ diverse judge models (e.g., GPT-4, Claude, Llama). High variance indicates non-robust judgments.
