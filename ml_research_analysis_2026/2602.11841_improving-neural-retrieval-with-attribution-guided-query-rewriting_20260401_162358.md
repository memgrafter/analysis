# Improving Neural Retriever with Attribution-Guided Query Rewriting
## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2602.11841
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.11841
- **Reference count:** 40
- **Primary result:** Attribution-guided LLM rewriting improves retrieval on ambiguous queries, with nDCG@10 gains up to 10% over original queries and up to 22% over LLM-only baselines.

## Executive Summary
Neural retrievers often struggle with ambiguous or underspecified queries. This paper introduces attribution-guided query rewriting, which uses token-level attributions from Integrated Gradients to steer an LLM in clarifying problematic tokens while preserving the original intent. By computing attributions on the retriever’s own top‑k results, the method generates a rewritten query that better matches the retriever’s behavior. Evaluated on BEIR datasets with SPLADE and TCT‑ColBERT, the approach yields consistent and significant gains, particularly for queries expressing implicit information needs. The one‑shot, model‑agnostic design is efficient and avoids error propagation.

## Method Summary
The method requires no retriever training. For each query, a fixed neural retriever first obtains the top‑5 documents. Integrated Gradients is applied to the retriever’s scoring function to estimate each query token’s contribution to the relevance scores of those documents. The per‑document attributions are averaged and normalized per query to produce a guidance score α_i for every token. These token‑score pairs are embedded in a structured prompt for an LLM (Mistral‑7B‑Instruct), instructing it to preserve high‑attribution tokens and clarify low‑ or negative‑attribution ones. The LLM produces a single rewritten query, which the same retriever then processes to generate the final ranking. The entire pipeline operates in one shot and is compatible with any differentiable neural retriever.

## Key Results
- nDCG@10 gains up to 9% over original queries for SPLADE and 10% for TCT‑ColBERT.
- Relative improvements over LLM‑only rewriting reach up to 22% (SPLADE) and 16% (TCT‑ColBERT).
- Largest benefits observed on datasets with implicit information needs (e.g., SciFact).

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1
- **Claim:** Token‑level attributions computed via Integrated Gradients (IG) identify query tokens that are weak, ambiguous, or misleading for the specific neural retriever.
- **Mechanism:** For a query, the top‑k documents retrieved by the base retriever are used as targets. IG is applied to estimate each token’s contribution to the relevance score s(q, d) for each document d. These per‑document attributions are averaged to produce a single attribution score α_i per token t_i. Low/negative α_i tokens are those that contribute weakly or negatively to the scores of the currently retrieved documents.
- **Core assumption:** A token’s average attribution across the top‑k documents is a valid proxy for its utility in guiding the retriever toward relevant content for that specific query.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract] "compute token‑level attributions from the retriever using Integrated Gradients on top‑k retrieved documents"
  - [section 3.2] Explicit formula: α_i = (1/k) ∑_{d∈D_top_q} IG(t_i, s(q, d)) and rationale: "Tokens with low or negative α_i are considered as candidates for clarification."
  - [corpus] Corpus signals show related work on explainable IR and attribution methods, but direct evidence for this specific aggregation strategy’s effectiveness is primarily from the paper’s own experiments.
- **Break condition:** If the averaged attribution scores do not correlate with human judgment of token importance or with retrieval performance when tokens are manipulated (e.g., removing low‑attribution tokens harms results).

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim:** Providing the LLM with raw tokens and their attribution scores as soft guidance enables it to rewrite the query in a way that preserves high‑utility tokens while clarifying low‑utility ones, leading to a more retriever‑effective query.
- **Mechanism:** The LLM is prompted with the original query, the full list of tokens paired with their α_i scores, and instructions to preserve high‑attribution tokens and clarify low/negative ones. The LLM generates a new query q’ in one shot. This uses the LLM’s semantic and linguistic capabilities to perform clarification (e.g., expanding acronyms, disambiguating terms) guided by the retriever‑specific signal.
- **Core assumption:** The LLM can correctly interpret the continuous attribution scores as priorities for preservation vs. modification and will follow the instruction to avoid adding new concepts.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract] "prompt an LLM with these scores to clarify low‑attribution tokens while preserving intent."
  - [section 3.3] The full prompt structure (Listing 1) explicitly instructs: "Tokens with high attribution scores should be preserved or emphasized," and "Tokens with low or negative attribution scores may be clarified, specified, or disambiguated."
  - [section 5.2] Qualitative example shows LLM expanding "chicken nuggets" to "ingredients in chicken nuggets" when guided, whereas LLM‑only produces a weaker rewrite.
- **Break condition:** If the LLM frequently ignores the attribution guidance (e.g., modifies high‑attribution tokens, fails to clarify low‑attribution ones) or consistently adds spurious concepts, the method fails.

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim:** The one‑shot closed‑loop design (compute attributions → rewrite → re‑retrieve) improves retrieval without retriever modification or iterative error propagation, making it efficient and deployable.
- **Mechanism:** The process runs a single cycle: attributions are computed on the initial top‑k results, the LLM rewrites once, and the fixed retriever R processes q’. No further rewriting or feedback loops are used. This avoids compounding errors from multiple LLM generations and keeps computational cost low.
- **Core assumption:** A single, attribution‑guided rewrite is sufficient to resolve the primary ambiguities or underspecifications causing the initial retrieval failure.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract] "closer this loop" and "one‑shot closed loop setting (one attribution step and one rewrite per query)."
  - [section 3.3] "We operate in a one‑shot closed‑loop setting... which keeps the method efficient and avoids propagating errors across multiple rewriting rounds."
  - [section 5] Results show consistent gains from this single‑step approach over baselines, implying one step is often enough.
- **Break condition:** If problems requiring multi‑hop disambiguation or iterative refinement are common, the one‑shot approach would yield limited gains compared to a multi‑step process.

## Foundational Learning

  - *Why needed here:* Core to generating the token‑level guidance signal. Must understand how IG computes path integrals between a baseline and input to attribute model output to input features.
  - *Quick check question:* How does summing attributions along a straight‑line path from a baseline embedding to the actual query embedding approximate the token’s contribution to the retriever’s score?
- **Concept: Prompt Engineering for Controlled Text Generation**
  - *Why needed here:* The LLM’s output quality hinges on the prompt’s ability to convey the attribution scores as a soft constraint. Requires understanding how to structure instructions, examples, and numerical guidance for an LLM.
  - *Quick check question:* In the provided prompt, what are the key syntactic and semantic elements used to translate continuous attribution scores into actionable rewriting guidelines for the LLM?

## Architecture Onboarding
### Component Map
Original Query → Retriever (top‑k) → Attribution Engine → Prompt Formatter → LLM Rewriter → Rewritten Query → Retriever (final)

### Critical Path
Query → Retriever (top‑k) → Attribution (per token) → Prompt → LLM → Rewritten Query → Retriever (final)

### Design Tradeoffs
- Model‑agnostic vs. Retriever‑dependent: Works with any R but requires running R twice per query (initial retrieval + final retrieval).
- Efficiency vs. Guidance Granularity: Using top‑k=5 documents balances stability and cost; more docs improve signal robustness but increase IG computation linearly.
- One‑shot vs. Iterative: One‑shot avoids error accumulation but may miss complex reformulation needs; iterative could help but risks degeneration.

### Failure Signatures
- High variance in α_i scores across top‑k docs, leading to noisy guidance.
- LLM generates queries longer than 120 tokens (if decoder constraints fail) or introduces unrelated entities.
- Gains are negative on datasets where the original query is already highly specific (e.g., NFCorpus short queries), suggesting over‑rewriting.

### First 3 Experiments
1. **Attribution sanity check:** For a known query (e.g., from the paper’s example), compute α_i manually for a few tokens. Verify that content words (e.g., "chicken", "nuggets") get higher scores than function words (e.g., "what", "is").
2. **LLM prompt adherence test:** Feed the LLM a prompt with artificially assigned attribution scores (e.g., mark a key token as very low). Check if the generated rewrite removes or clarifies that token against instructions.
3. **Baseline replication:** Re‑run the "LLM‑only" and "Original Query" baselines on a small subset of SciFact to confirm the paper’s trend (LLM‑only may not help or hurt; GLLM should improve).

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- **How does attribution‑guided query rewriting generalize across different neural retrieval architectures and benchmark collections?**  
  Basis: Future work includes exploring additional neural retrievers and benchmark collections (Conclusion).  
  Why unresolved: The study evaluated only SPLADE and TCT‑ColBERT on three BEIR datasets; broader generalizability is unknown.  
  Evidence: Systematic evaluation on a wider range of retrievers (dense, hybrid, sparse) and the remaining BEIR collections to test consistency.

- **Can iterative and multi‑stage rewriting strategies yield further improvements beyond the one‑shot approach?**  
  Basis: Future work plans to investigate iterative and multi‑stage rewriting (Conclusion).  
  Why unresolved: The current method uses a single attribution and rewrite step; repeated refinement might help or cause error propagation.  
  Evidence: Implement multi‑round rewriting with recomputed attributions after each iteration and compare performance against the one‑shot baseline.

- **How effective is attribution‑guided query rewriting in cross‑lingual and domain‑specific retrieval scenarios?**  
  Basis: Future work aims to assess cross‑lingual and domain‑specific scenarios (Conclusion).  
  Why unresolved: Experiments were limited to English BEIR datasets; applicability to other languages or specialized domains is unclear.  
  Evidence: Test on multilingual benchmarks (e.g., XBEIR) and domain‑specific collections (e.g., legal, biomedical) to measure robustness.

- **How do alternative attribution methods (e.g., Integrated Gradients vs. other gradient‑based or perturbation‑based techniques) interact with LLM‑guided rewriting?**  
  Basis: Future work includes studying alternative attribution methods and their interaction with LLMs (Conclusion).  
  Why unresolved: Only Integrated Gradients was used; other methods might provide more accurate or stable token scores.  
  Evidence: Replace IG with methods like DeepLIFT, LIME, or SHAP and compare rewriting effectiveness and stability across runs.

## Limitations
- Attribution normalization method is unspecified, altering guidance strength.
- TCT‑ColBERT fine‑tuning protocol on BEIR datasets is undocumented, affecting baseline reproducibility.
- LLM prompt adherence relies on a single example; robustness to varied query types or LLMs is unknown.
- Generalizability beyond BEIR and the specific retrievers/LLM (Mistral‑7B) is not established.
