# CORE: Comprehensive Ontological Relation Evaluation for Large Language Models

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2602.06446
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.06446
- **Reference count:** 17
- **Primary result:** State-of-the-art LLMs exhibit a severe, SAFETY-CRITICAL failure to recognize unrelatedness, showing near-perfect accuracy on related pairs (86.5–100%) but collapsing to 0–41.35% on unrelated pairs while maintaining high confidence (~92–94%).

## Executive Summary
This paper introduces CORE, a rigorously validated benchmark designed to measure a critical yet overlooked capability of large language models (LLMs): distinguishing meaningful semantic relations from genuine unrelatedness. Evaluating 29 leading LLMs reveals a stark performance asymmetry. Models excel at identifying related entity pairs but catastrophically fail on unrelated pairs, often fabricating plausible but incorrect relations with high confidence. This "semantic collapse" indicates a fundamental flaw in LLMs' ontological reasoning, with significant implications for safety, reliability, and truthfulness in real-world applications. A full-scale evaluation on a 225K-question dataset spanning 74 disciplines shows accuracy plummeting to ~2%, highlighting a vast, under-evaluated frontier in LLM capability.

## Method Summary
The study evaluates LLMs on a binary classification task: given two entities and four candidate relations, select the correct relation or "unrelated." The core evaluation uses a balanced, human-validated 203-question benchmark covering 24 relation types plus unrelated pairs. An additional large-scale corpus of 225K multiple-choice questions spans 74 academic disciplines. Models are prompted uniformly with a structured JSON output requirement. Key metrics include standard accuracy, balanced accuracy, Expected Calibration Error (ECE), Overconfident Error Rate (OER), and Semantic Collapse Rate (SCR)—the rate at which unrelated pairs are assigned any specific relation. Proprietary models are queried via API with deterministic settings; open-source models run locally. A human baseline (n>1000) is collected for comparison.

## Key Results
- **Performance Asymmetry:** LLMs achieve near-ceiling accuracy on related pairs (86.5–100%) but disastrously low accuracy on unrelated pairs (0–41.35%).
- **Severe Miscalibration:** Confidence remains high (~92–94%) on unrelated items, causing ECE to increase 2–4× compared to related items.
- **Systematic Fabrication:** The average Semantic Collapse Rate is 37.6%, meaning models routinely invent spurious relations for truly unrelated entity pairs.
- **Full-Scale Collapse:** On the 225K-question dataset, overall accuracy drops to approximately 2%, underscoring the severity of the failure mode.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The CORE framework exposes a fundamental divergence in how LLMs process related versus unrelated pairs. For related pairs, models leverage learned co-occurrence and relational patterns to achieve high accuracy. For unrelated pairs, the absence of a true relation conflicts with the model's inherent drive to produce coherent, plausible narratives. This leads to "semantic collapse"—the model fabricates a relation where none exists, driven by pattern-matching and text generation objectives rather than a true capacity for recognizing null or negative relational facts. The high confidence coupled with low accuracy reveals that the models' internal certainty is anchored in surface coherence, not factual grounding.

## Foundational Learning
- **Semantic Relation Typology:** Understanding predefined relation types (e.g., *Part-of*, *Used-for*) is necessary to structure the evaluation task. **Quick check:** Can you list 5 distinct ontological relations and provide an example entity pair for each?
- **Confidence Calibration:** Models must align predicted confidence with actual accuracy. Miscalibration on unrelated pairs is a key failure signature. **Quick check:** If a model is 90% confident on a set of questions but only 40% correct, what is the Expected Calibration Error contribution for that bin?
- **Confabulation/Fabrication:** The tendency to generate plausible but incorrect information, especially on "none of the above" or unrelated options. **Quick check:** Given the unrelated pair (hospital, flying), does a high-confidence selection of a specific relation indicate confabulation?
- **Balanced Benchmark Design:** Ensuring equal representation of relation types and the unrelated class prevents skewed evaluation. **Quick check:** Why is it insufficient to evaluate only on positive (related) examples?
- **Human-AI Discrepancy Analysis:** Comparing model performance to human baselines isolates capabilities unique to human reasoning (e.g., meta-cognitive awareness of unrelatedness). **Quick check:** Humans scored 95.1% on unrelated pairs; what does the >50-point gap with LLMs imply?
- **Scale-Generalization Trade-off:** Evaluating on a small, balanced benchmark versus a large, diverse corpus tests robustness across domains. **Quick check:** How does performance on 203 carefully curated items differ from performance on 225K discipline-specific items?

## Architecture Onboarding
The CORE evaluation system is a pipeline for stress-testing LLM ontological reasoning:
**Prompt Template -> LLM Inference -> JSON Parser -> Metrics Calculator**
The critical path flows from dataset curation (ensuring balanced relation types) through standardized prompting and deterministic decoding, to the extraction of answers and confidences, culminating in the computation of accuracy, calibration, and collapse metrics. A key design tradeoff is between benchmark size/diversity (225K) and rigorous human validation/balance (203-question core). The system fails silently when models output non-JSON or when confidence values are uncalibrated. The primary failure signature is high mean confidence coupled with very low accuracy specifically on the "unrelated" class.

**First Three Experiments:**
1. **Baseline Run:** Execute the 203-question benchmark on 3-5 models (e.g., GPT-4, Claude 3, Llama 3) using the provided prompt. Verify the replication of the reported accuracy gap between related and unrelated pairs.
2. **Calibration Diagnosis:** For the same runs, compute ECE separately for related and unrelated bins. Confirm that ECE for unrelated items is at least 2× higher than for related items.
3. **Failure Mode Inspection:** Manually examine 20 high-confidence, incorrect predictions on unrelated pairs. Categorize the fabricated relations (e.g., spurious thematic links, verb-driven constructs) to characterize the nature of the semantic collapse.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
None explicitly stated by the authors. The reproduction notes identify implicit open questions regarding inference parameters and dataset access.

## Limitations
- **Unknown Inference Parameters:** Exact API parameters (temperature, max_tokens) for proprietary models in the "deterministic" setting are not detailed, requiring lookup from provider documentation.
- **Dataset Accessibility:** Public access is confirmed only for the 203-question benchmark; the full 225K corpus and its 74-discipline partition are not guaranteed to be publicly available.
- **Generalization Limits:** The benchmark's 24 relation types may not fully capture the diversity of the 74 disciplines in the large corpus; the human baseline is likely English-centric.

## Confidence
- **Claim 1 (Existence of stark performance asymmetry on the 203-question benchmark):** High confidence. The result is clear, reproducible on the public benchmark, and central to the paper.
- **Claim 2 (Systematic miscalibration and semantic collapse on unrelated pairs):** Medium confidence. Depends on the benchmark's balance and specific implementation of SCR; full-dataset trends may vary.
- **Claim 3 (Severe performance degradation to ~2% on the full 225K dataset):** Low confidence. Relies on authors' unreported results; no direct access to the full dataset for independent verification.
