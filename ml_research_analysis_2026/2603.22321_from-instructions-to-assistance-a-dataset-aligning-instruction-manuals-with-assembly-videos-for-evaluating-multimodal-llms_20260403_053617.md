# From Instructions to Assistance: a Dataset Aligning Instruction Manuals with Assembly Videos for Evaluating Multimodal LLMs

## Quick Facts
*   **arXiv ID:** 2603.22321
*   **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.22321
*   **Reference Count:** 40
*   **Primary Result:** Introduced M2AD, a dataset of 1,228 annotations aligning IKEA manuals with videos, to show current MLMs achieve modest (~56-79%) zero-shot performance on three procedural task understanding benchmarks, with performance limited by architectural constraints on multi-image and interleaved reasoning.

## Executive Summary
This paper introduces the Manual-to-Action Dataset (M2AD) to evaluate multimodal large language models (MLMs) on a real-world technical assistance scenario: understanding procedural tasks like furniture assembly. M2AD precisely aligns steps from IKEA assembly instruction manuals with corresponding video segments from assembly demonstrations across 53 furniture items. Using this dataset, the authors conduct zero-shot evaluations on consumer hardware for three tasks: step completion detection (is a given step done?), step detection (does this video segment match this step?), and step identification (which step is shown?). Results indicate that while models like LLaVa-Video and Qwen2-VL perform best on the binary tasks (accuracy ~56-59%), and MolMo surprisingly excels at multi-class identification (79.6%), overall performance remains limited. The authors attribute these modest results to current MLM architectural constraints in processing sequential visual information and interleaved text-image contexts, highlighting a gap for models that can robustly integrate manuals with video for real-time assistance.

## Method Summary
The core methodological contribution is the construction of M2AD. The authors developed a pipeline to automatically and manually align frame-level video data from IKEA assembly demonstrations with the discrete, sequential steps depicted in corresponding PDF instruction manuals. This results in 1,228 timestamped annotations linking a manual step (including its visual and textual description) to a video segment showing its execution. This aligned dataset enables three zero-shot evaluation tasks formulated as binary classification or multi-class identification problems. Models are given multimodal inputs (e.g., a query step manual page + a video clip) and must produce a textual judgment, with accuracy against the human annotation as the metric. All evaluations are run on consumer-grade hardware without task-specific fine-tuning.

## Key Results
*   **Task 1 (Step Completion) & Task 2 (Step Detection):** Models with stronger video understanding capabilities, specifically LLaVa-Video and Qwen2-VL, lead with accuracies around 56-59%, though still only slightly above random baselines.
*   **Task 3 (Step Identification):** MolMo achieves a substantially higher 79.6% accuracy. The authors attribute this outlier performance to the model's spatial reasoning strengths when provided with concatenated image inputs (multiple manual frames stitched together) and prompted with spatial references ("left," "right").
*   **Overall Trend:** Performance across all tasks is modest, suggesting current MLMs struggle to robustly integrate the sequential, multi-modal information (text + image sequences) required for procedural understanding. The best results for tasks requiring temporal video reasoning come from models with explicit video architectures, while a model without native interleaved text-image support (MolMo) excels at a task leveraging static spatial layout.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The paper's evidence suggests the mechanism for success on these tasks is highly model-architecture dependent. For temporal video reasoning (Tasks 1 & 2), models with dedicated video encoders (LLaVa-Video, Qwen2-VL) perform best, indicating the importance of processing temporal visual features. For spatial layout reasoning over static manual pages (Task 3), MolMo's strength appears linked to its pointing and spatial reasoning capabilities, which are effectively triggered by the "concatenated image + spatial prompt" input strategy. This reveals a split: temporal video understanding and spatial diagram understanding may currently be separate competencies, and the paper's evaluation framework successfully probes both. The overarching "why" for modest performance is that no single architecture seamlessly handles the full challenge: integrating a sequential textual/visual manual with a sequential video demonstration.

## Foundational Learning
*   **Multimodal Alignment:** The core challenge of creating joint representations for text and vision. *Why needed:* M2AD requires matching a textual/visual manual step to a video segment, testing if models align cross-modal content. *Quick check:* Does model performance correlate with its pre-training objective's emphasis on image-text contrastive learning?
*   **Instruction Following:** The model's ability to parse and execute a query expressed in natural language. *Why needed:* All three tasks are framed as instruction-following problems (e.g., "Is this step shown?"). *Quick check:* Do models fail more on complex phrasings of the same underlying question?
*   **Spatial Reasoning:** Understanding relative positions ("left," "right") and object layouts within an image. *Why needed:* Critical for interpreting IKEA manuals and MolMo's success suggests spatial prompts are a powerful, under-explored interface. *Quick check:* Does removing spatial descriptors from Task 3 prompts collapse MolMo's performance?
*   **Temporal Video Understanding:** Extracting meaning from a sequence of frames, not just individual images. *Why needed:* Tasks 1 & 2 require judging actions over time in assembly videos. *Quick check:* Do models process each frame independently (image-based) vs. as a coherent sequence (video-based)?
*   **Zero-Shot Generalization:** Evaluating a model on a new task without task-specific gradient updates. *Why needed:* The paper's evaluation protocol tests raw model reasoning abilities on a novel domain (furniture assembly). *Quick check:* Would a few in-domain examples drastically improve scores, indicating poor inherent generalization?

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component Map:** Input (Manual Page(s) + Video Clip) -> Modality-Specific Encoder (Image/Frame Encoder, Text Encoder) -> Multimodal Fusion Layer -> LLM/MLLM Reasoner -> Text Output (Yes/No/Step Number)

**Critical Path:** The most challenging and performance-determining segment is the **Multimodal Fusion Layer -> LLM Reasoner** path. It must integrate: (1) the *static, symbolic* information from the manual (sequence of diagrams/text) with (2) the *dynamic, procedural* information from the video (actor's actions over time). The fusion must support both spatial layout matching (manual diagrams vs. video snapshot) and temporal action matching (manual step description vs. video segment).

**Design Tradeoffs:**
1.  **Input Format:** Concatenated static images (favors spatial layout reasoning, as with MolMo) vs. true interleaved text-image sequences or video tensors (favors temporal reasoning, as with LLaVa-Video/Qwen2-VL).
2.  **Sequence Handling:** Treating video as a list of frames processed by an image encoder vs. using a dedicated video encoder that captures motion.
3.  **Context Window:** Long manuals require many image frames; long videos require many video frames or temporal subsampling. Models must compress or select relevant information from a long context.

**Failure Signatures:**
*   **Temporal Confusion:** Incorrect step completion/detection due to inability to order actions or distinguish similar-looking steps at different assembly stages.
*   **Spatial Mismatch:** Failing to align a manual diagram's perspective or part layout with the corresponding video view.
*   **Instruction Misinterpretation:** Failing on the binary tasks due to not understanding the query's logical form (e.g., conflating "is this *step* shown?" with "is this *part* shown?").
*   **Context Window Limit:** Truncating early manual pages or video segments, losing crucial sequential information.

**First 3 Experiments:**
1.  **Replicate MolMo's Success:** Test MolMo's concatenated-image + spatial prompting strategy on other models (LLaVa, Qwen2-VL) for Task 3. Does the input format transfer the advantage?
2.  **Perspective Ablation:** Isolate and compare model performance on the egocentric vs. exocentric video subsets within M2AD to quantify perspective impact.
3.  **Architectural Intervention:** Fine-tune a model with native interleaved support (e.g., a small Flamingo-style model) on a *subset* of M2AD using the *concatenated-image* input format. Does it gain MolMo's spatial advantage while retaining some interleaved processing strength?

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
### Open Question 1
- Question: How can pre-training and fine-tuning strategies be enhanced to improve multimodal alignment and instruction-following capabilities in MLMs for technical assistance?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The authors state in the conclusion: "One promising direction is to look deeper into pre-training or fine-tuning approaches for multimodal models to understand and improve the alignment between modalities and the instruction following aspect."
- Why unresolved: Current models show limited performance in tasks requiring integration of visual sequences and textual manuals, suggesting poor multimodal alignment and instruction adherence.
- What evidence would resolve it: Comparative studies of different pre-training objectives or fine-tuning datasets on M2AD-like benchmarks, measuring improvements in step completion, detection, and identification tasks, with ablation on modality interaction mechanisms.

### Open Question 2
- Question: To what extent can MLMs reduce reliance on detailed annotations by autonomously extracting relevant information from raw multimodal inputs for procedural task understanding?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The authors aimed to assess "to which extent the reasoning abilities of MLMs can be used to reduce the need for detailed labelling" and note that M2AD uses a minimal annotation approach.
- Why unresolved: Models performed modestly, and the paper highlights that performance is limited by architectural constraints, indicating current MLMs may not fully leverage raw data without structured labels.
- What evidence would resolve it: Zero-shot or few-shot experiments where models are provided only raw video clips and manual pages (without temporal step annotations) and evaluated on their ability to infer completion, detect steps, and identify step numbers, with success measured by accuracy on the three defined tasks.

### Open Question 3
- Question: Why did MolMo achieve high accuracy in step identification through spatial reasoning on concatenated images despite lacking native interleaved text-image support, and can this approach be generalized to other models?
- Basis in paper: [inferred] Results show MolMo unexpectedly excelled at step identification (79.6% accuracy) using concatenated images and spatial references (e.g., "left"/"right"), while other models with native interleaved support performed poorly. The authors attribute this to spatial reasoning and MolMo's pointing ability.
- Why unresolved: It is unclear whether MolMo's success stems from its architectural design, the concatenation+sp prompting approach, or unique features like pointing, and whether this is transferable to models without such traits.
- What evidence would resolve it: Ablation studies comparing concatenated vs. interleaved inputs across multiple models, testing spatial prompting strategies, and evaluating the impact of MolMo's pointing mechanism through controlled modifications or fine-tuning on M2AD.

### Open Question 4
- Question: How does the perspective (egocentric vs. exocentric) of input videos affect MLM performance on procedural task understanding?
- Basis in paper: [inferred] M2AD includes both egocentric and exocentric views to address perspective diversity, but results do not differentiate performance by perspective. The authors note perspective complexities but do not analyze its impact.
- Why unresolved: It is unknown whether models perform differently on egocentric versus exocentric videos, which is critical for designing VR/AR-assisted systems versus external camera setups.
- What evidence would resolve it: Subset analysis of M2AD results separating egocentric and exocentric samples, or a controlled study with matched tasks in both perspectives to measure accuracy differences in step completion, detection, and identification tasks.

### Open Question 5
- Question: Do MLMs generalize across diverse technical domains (e.g., furniture assembly vs. vehicle repair or healthcare procedures) when evaluated on multimodal procedural tasks?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The conclusion mentions extending evaluation "to a broader range of technical and domain-specific settings, including other domains such as vehicles repair, healthcare and programming."
- Why unresolved: M2AD is specific to IKEA furniture assembly; it is unclear if performance trends and architectural limitations observed generalize to domains with different visual, procedural, and instructional characteristics.
- What evidence would resolve it: Development of analogous datasets for other domains (e.g., repair manuals, medical procedures) and benchmarking the same MLMs to compare accuracy, error patterns, and the influence of domain-specific factors like object variability or instruction clarity.

## Limitations
*   **Dataset Scale & Diversity:** M2AD covers only 53 IKEA items (~1.2k annotations), limiting generalizability across furniture complexity types and manual styles.
*   **Evaluation Scope:** Tests only three zero-shot tasks on consumer hardware; it does not assess real-time interaction, error recovery, or temporal coherence over long sequences.
*   **Inferred Architectural Claim:** The conclusion that performance is "limited by architectural constraints" is based on comparative results, not direct ablation studies modifying model architectures.

## Confidence
*   **Dataset Construction & Alignment:** High. The annotation pipeline and step-video alignment methodology are clearly described and validated.
*   **Reported Model Performance Results:** Medium. Specific models and tasks are tested, but the model set is limited, hardware constraints may cap performance, and no statistical significance tests are reported.
*   **Architectural Limitation Inference:** Medium. The inference is plausible given the performance patterns (e.g., MolMo's outlier success with a non-standard input), but lacks direct evidence from controlled architectural modifications or a broader model survey.

## Next Checks
1.  **Replicate MolMo's Outlier Performance:** Conduct controlled ablations to determine if its high step identification accuracy is due to the concatenated-image+spatial-prompt input format, its inherent spatial reasoning/pointing ability, or both. Test this input strategy on other models.
2.  **Evaluate Newer Native Interleaved Models:** Benchmark state-of-the-art models with native support for interleaved text-image sequences (e.g., GPT-4V, Claude 3, latest InternVL) on M2AD to verify if the performance gap on tasks requiring manual-video integration persists.
3.  **Assess Dataset Diversity Bias:** Perform a fine-grained error analysis correlating model accuracy with furniture category (chair, shelf, table), manual diagram style, and video perspective (egocentric/exocentric) to identify specific biases or failure modes within M2AD.
