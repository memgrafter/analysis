# Unlearning Evaluation through Subset Statistical Independence
## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID**: 2603.00587  
- **Source URL**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.00587  
- **Reference count**: 31  
- **Primary result**: Split-half Dependence Evaluation (SDE) achieves F1 scores above 0.85–0.99 on CIFAR10-ResNet18 for forgetting ratios 5%–20%, substantially outperforming distribution-based metrics and providing clearer signals for unlearning efficacy.

## Executive Summary
Machine unlearning evaluation traditionally relies on retraining a reference model or membership inference attacks, both of which are impractical or unreliable in realistic settings. This paper introduces Split-half Dependence Evaluation (SDE), a novel framework that determines whether a data subset was used during training by measuring statistical dependence in model activations via the Hilbert-Schmidt Independence Criterion (HSIC). By splitting a subset into two halves and computing HSIC between their activations, SDE detects the co-adaptation imprint left by training. Extensive experiments on classification and diffusion models show SDE reliably distinguishes in-training from out-of-training subsets and correctly assesses unlearning methods where conventional metrics fail, offering a robust, reference-model-free evaluation tool.

## Method Summary
SDE evaluates a target subset \(S_{\text{tar}}\) by comparing the distribution of split-half HSIC values against reference distributions from known in-training (\(S_{\text{IT}}\)) and out-of-training (\(S_{\text{OOT}}\)) subsets. For a given model, a subset is randomly split into halves \(S_1, S_2\). Over \(T=200\) shuffles of \(S_2\), HSIC between activations of \(S_1\) and shuffled \(S_2\) is computed using a Gaussian RBF kernel (bandwidth \(\sigma = \sqrt{d}\), \(d\) = activation dimension). This yields a distribution \(H(S, h)\). Jensen-Shannon divergence (JSD) between \(H(S_{\text{tar}}, h)\) and each reference distribution is calculated; \(S_{\text{tar}}\) is classified as out-of-training if it is closer to \(H(S_{\text{OOT}}, h)\). The out-of-training rate (OTR) aggregates decisions over multiple subsets from a forgetting set \(D_f\). The method requires only the model, target subset, and two reference subsets, avoiding retrained models or auxiliary classifiers.

## Key Results
- SDE achieves F1 scores >0.85 on CIFAR10-ResNet18 across forgetting ratios (5%–20%) and subset sizes ≥1000, with performance declining for smaller subsets (<800 samples).
- SDE significantly outperforms distribution distance metrics (MMD, Wasserstein) in distinguishing in- vs. out-of-training subsets under all tested conditions.
- For unlearning evaluation, SDE’s OTR correctly identifies successful unlearning (Random-label, OTR ~87–99%) and failure (Unroll, OTR ~0–4%) where conventional metrics like attack success rate (ASR) provide ambiguous signals.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1
- **Claim**: In-training subsets exhibit higher statistical dependence between split halves of model activations than out-of-training subsets, due to shared influence from gradient updates during training.
- **Mechanism**: Randomly split a candidate subset \(S\) into \(S_1\) and \(S_2\). Compute HSIC between \(h(S_1)\) and \(h(S_2)\). Training-induced co-adaptation yields non-zero dependence for \(S \subset D_{\text{tr}}\), while out-of-training samples remain independent.
- **Core assumption**: The shared parameter shift \(\Delta\theta_S\) from samples in \(S\) induces detectable dependency across the split halves.
- **Evidence anchors**:
  - [abstract] "propose a tailored use of the Hilbert–Schmidt Independence Criterion to assess whether the model outputs on a given subset exhibit statistical dependence"
  - [section 3.1] Figure 1 shows significantly higher \(H(S_{\text{IT}}, h_{\text{or}})\) vs \(H(S_{\text{OOT}}, h_{\text{or}})\) with \(p=4.73\times10^{-11}\), but overlap under \(h_{\text{rand}}\) (\(p=0.0795\)).
  - [section 4.1] F1 scores >0.85 on CIFAR10-ResNet18 for \(R\in\{5\%,20\%)\) and \(|S|\ge1000\).
- **Break condition**: Subset size too small (e.g., \(|S|=400\) on SVHN-AllCNN yields F1~0.62), poorly chosen kernel bandwidth \(\sigma\), or insufficient training progress (e.g., <20% epochs reduces F1 below 0.7).

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim**: Split-half HSIC provides a robust, distribution-free signal of training participation that does not require retrained reference models or auxiliary classifiers.
- **Mechanism**: By measuring dependence *within* a subset, SDE avoids reliance on per-sample cues (loss/confidence) that weaken after unlearning and sidesteps need for shadow models or training statistics.
- **Core assumption**: The split-half dependence captures a property intrinsic to the model–data interaction that persists unless the subset’s influence is removed.
- **Evidence anchors**:
  - [abstract] "does not rely on auxiliary classifiers or additional model training, and operates on data subsets"
  - [section 4.2] SDE outperforms MMD and Wasserstein distance across subset sizes and forgetting ratios (Table 3).
  - [section 4.3] SDE’s OTR clearly distinguishes effective (Random-label) vs ineffective (Unroll) unlearning, while ASR shows ambiguous signals.
- **Break condition**: Reference sets \(S_{\text{IT}}, S_{\text{OOT}}\) not properly constructed or unrepresentative; model architecture changes between reference and target evaluation.

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim**: Comparing the HSIC distribution of a target subset against reference distributions via Jensen–Shannon Divergence (JSD) yields a reliable binary decision on unlearning success.
- **Mechanism**: Compute \(D(S_{\text{tar}}, S_{\text{IT}}, h)\) and \(D(S_{\text{tar}}, S_{\text{OOT}}, h)\) as JSD between HSIC distributions (Eq. 3). Classify \(S_{\text{tar}}\) as out-of-training if closer to \(S_{\text{OOT}}\) (Eq. 2).
- **Core assumption**: The JSD comparison threshold is stable across different reference set instances and model checkpoints.
- **Evidence anchors**:
  - [section 3.2] Introduces the classification rule; Figure 2 shows \(H(S_{\text{tar}}, h_{\text{re}})\) closer to \(S_{\text{OOT}}\) than \(S_{\text{IT}}\) after retraining.
  - [section 4.3] OTR rates for Retrain (~87–99%) vs Unroll (~0–4%) consistently align with expected unlearning quality.
  - [section 5] Notes that reference set selection affects performance (missing bars in Figure 4) and may require optimization.
- **Break condition**: Reference set size too small (\(|S_{\text{IT}}|, |S_{\text{OOT}}|<400\)), or JSD threshold mis-specified; catastrophic forgetting representations mixing.

## Foundational Learning
- **Concept: Machine Unlearning Evaluation Goals**  
  *Why needed*: Understanding why unlearning evaluation must verify data removal without retraining, and the limitations of membership inference and utility metrics.  
  *Quick check*: Why are retraining-based evaluations considered impractical in deployment, and how does SDE’s subset-level approach address this?

- **Concept: Hilbert–Schmidt Independence Criterion (HSIC)**  
  *Why needed*: Core metric for capturing nonlinear dependencies in high-dimensional activations without density estimation; requires understanding kernel choice and bandwidth.  
  *Quick check*: How does HSIC differ from distribution distance metrics like MMD, and why is it more robust to subset size variations?

- **Concept: Statistical Hypothesis Testing in Evaluation**  
  *Why needed*: The paper uses Mann–Whitney U-test to establish that in-training HSIC distributions are stochastically larger than out-of-training; key for rejecting null hypothesis of indistinguishable distributions.  
  *Quick check*: What does a \(p\)-value <0.01 in the U-test imply about the relationship between \(H(S_{\text{IT}}, h)\) and \(H(S_{\text{OOT}}, h)\), and why is a one-sided test appropriate?

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map**:  
  Model -> Split-half sampler -> HSIC calculator -> Reference manager -> Comparator -> Aggregator  
  (Inputs: model \(h\), target subset \(S_{\text{tar}}\), reference subsets \(S_{\text{IT}}, S_{\text{OOT}}\))

- **Critical path**:  
  1. For given \(h_{\text{un}}\) and \(D_f\), sample \(m\) target subsets \(S_{\text{tar}}\).  
  2. For each \(S_{\text{tar}}\), compute \(H(S_{\text{tar}}, h_{\text{un}})\) distribution via split-half HSIC (200 shuffles).  
  3. Precompute \(H(S_{\text{IT}}, h_{\text{un}})\) and \(H(S_{\text{OOT}}, h_{\text{un}})\) for reference sets.  
  4. Compute \(D(S_{\text{tar}}, S_{\text{IT}})\) and \(D(S_{\text{tar}}, S_{\text{OOT}})\) using JSD.  
  5. Label \(S_{\text{tar}}\) as *out-of-training* if \(D(S_{\text{tar}}, S_{\text{OOT}}) < D(S_{\text{tar}}, S_{\text{IT}})\).  
  6. OTR = fraction of \(S_{\text{tar}}\) labeled out-of-training.

- **Design tradeoffs**:  
  • Kernel bandwidth \(\sigma\): \(\sigma=\sqrt{d}\) generally works but may fail on high-dim generative features; median heuristic more adaptive but adds compute.  
  • Reference set size: Larger improves stability but requires audit storage; 1000 samples used in paper.  
  • Layer choice: Deeper layers (penultimate) work best; earlier layers may give weak signals but still usable with high-quality references.  
  • Subset size: \(|S|\ge1000\) recommended; smaller subsets increase variance and degrade F1.

- **Failure signatures**:  
  • Overlap of \(H(S_{\text{IT}}, h)\) and \(H(S_{\text{OOT}}, h)\) distributions (non-significant Mann–Whitney test, \(p\ge0.01\)).  
  • Inconsistent layer effectiveness: missing bars in Figure 4 indicate reference sets fail to produce clear separation for certain layers.  
  • High variance in OTR across random seeds (e.g., Sparsity method shows ±20% swings in Table 4).  
  • Sensitivity to \(\sigma\): F1 drops sharply outside effective bandwidth range (Figure 3).

- **First 3 experiments**:  
  1. **Controlled evaluation on retrained models**: Vary forgetting ratio \(R\), subset size \(|S|\), layer depth, and training epoch to establish baseline distinguishability (section 4.1).  
  2. **Comparison to distribution metrics**: Benchmark SDE vs MMD/Wasserstein on CIFAR10-ResNet18 across all conditions (section 4.2, Table 3).  
  3. **Unlearning method assessment**: Apply SDE to common unlearning algorithms (Random-label, Unroll, Sparsity, SalUn) and compare OTR with utility/ASR (section 4.3, Table 4).

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- **How can kernel bandwidth \(\sigma\) and kernel function choices be adaptively optimized?**  
  *Basis*: The authors identify that \(\sigma\) critically affects F1 and that the \(\sqrt{d}\) heuristic performed poorly in diffusion experiments.  
  *Why unresolved*: No principled framework for selecting \(\sigma\) or kernels that works consistently across classification and generative models.  
  *What evidence would resolve*: Systematic study of \(\sigma\) strategies (cross-validation, meta-learning) and kernel variants across architectures, with theoretical analysis linking representation characteristics to optimal parameters.

- **What are optimal strategies for constructing reference sets (\(S_{\text{IT}}\) and \(S_{\text{OOT}}\))?**  
  *Basis*: Discussion notes reference set choice affects performance, citing a missing bar in Figure 4 where random references failed the U-test.  
  *Why unresolved*: Paper uses random reference sampling with no systematic validation or guidelines.  
  *What evidence would resolve*: Formal criteria (e.g., maximizing HSIC gap) or algorithmic approaches (e.g., clustering) tested across datasets, with sensitivity analysis showing improved robustness.

- **How can SDE distinguish intentional unlearning from natural forgetting?**  
  *Basis*: Authors caution that SDE may capture general forgetting, not solely unlearning, and note distinguishing these is "non-trivial."  
  *Why unresolved*: No address of whether dependency reduction could stem from non-unlearning factors, risking false positives.  
  *What evidence would resolve*: Controlled experiments with models undergoing natural forgetting (e.g., sequential tasks) vs explicit unlearning, measuring SDE signals alongside temporal markers.

- **Why do conventional unlearning metrics diverge from SDE’s OTR, and how should evaluation be rethought?**  
  *Basis*: Discussion highlights a "critical discrepancy" where methods like Unroll appear effective by conventional metrics but show low OTR.  
  *Why unresolved*: No analysis of root causes or proposal for a unified evaluation standard.  
  *What evidence would resolve*: Analytical studies linking conventional metrics to statistical dependence measures, plus benchmark suites comparing methods across SDE and existing metrics to derive a consensus protocol.

- **Can SDE be extended to quantify the degree of unlearning?**  
  *Basis*: Authors suggest SDE currently uses binary comparison via JSD and propose quantifying unlearning by comparing HSIC values across unlearned models.  
  *Why unresolved*: No operationalization or evaluation of potential continuous metrics (e.g., normalized HSIC reduction).  
  *What evidence would resolve*: Development of a continuous unlearning score derived from HSIC distributions, validated by correlating with ground-truth forgetting ratios and unlearning algorithm strengths.

## Limitations
- Hyperparameter sensitivity: HSIC results depend on kernel bandwidth \(\sigma\) and subset size \(|S|\); poor choices can degrade F1 below 0.7. Reference set quality is also critical and not systematically addressed.
- Scalability: Split-half HSIC computation is \(O(|S|^2 d)\), making evaluation expensive for large subsets; no efficient approximations provided in main method.
- Generality: Experiments limited to CNNs and diffusion models; no evaluation on transformers or graph networks. Theoretical assumptions about gradient-induced dependence may not hold for all unlearning methods.

## Confidence
- **Cluster A (HSIC distinction between in/out subsets)**: High. Consistent F1>0.85 in controlled experiments across forgetting ratios 5–20% and \(|S|\ge1000\).
- **Cluster B (SDE superiority over distribution metrics)**: High. Direct benchmarks in Table 3 show SDE outperforming MMD/Wasserstein in nearly all settings.
- **Cluster C (OTR signals for unlearning methods)**: Medium. OTR aligns with expected quality on four methods, but limited method diversity and high variance for some algorithms (e.g., Sparsity).

## Next Checks
1. **Hyperparameter sweep**: Run SDE on CIFAR10-ResNet18 with \(\sigma \in \{\sqrt{d}, \text{median heuristic}, 2\sqrt{d}\}\) and \(|S| \in \{200,500,1000,2000\}\) to map performance landscapes and propose robust defaults.
2. **Reference set robustness**: For a fixed unlearned model, repeat OTR computation with 20 independently sampled reference sets (sizes 400–2000) and compute variance; identify minimal reference size that keeps JSD classification stable (CV <10%).
3. **Cross-architecture test**: Apply SDE to unlearn BERT (text classification) and ViT (image classification) using standard unlearning methods; verify that in-training HSIC remains significantly higher than out-of-training.
