# Finding the Cracks: Improving LLMs Reasoning with Paraphrastic Probing and Consistency Verification

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2602.11361  
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.11361  
- **Reference count:** 40  
- **Primary result:** PPCV improves Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct by +10.8% on GSM8K (88.24% vs. 77.40% CoT), +21.7% on GSM-Hard, and +19.0% on Math500.

## Executive Summary
Large language models (LLMs) often produce erroneous reasoning chains due to hallucinations and error accumulation, especially on complex multi-step problems like mathematics. This paper introduces the Paraphrastic Probing and Consistency Verification (PPCV) framework, an inference-time method that does not require model training. PPCV first identifies a single "critical token"—an intermediate token whose value heavily influences subsequent reasoning steps—by detecting token prediction mismatches when the input question is paraphrased. It then samples alternative tokens at that position, generates new reasoning paths for both the original and paraphrased questions, and selects the final answer based on consistency across the paraphrased outputs. On Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct, PPCV yields large absolute gains on GSM8K, GSM-Hard, and Math500, demonstrating that targeted correction and paraphrase-based robustness significantly enhance LLM reasoning.

## Method Summary
PPCV operates in two stages. **Stage 1 (Paraphrastic Probing):** The model generates a greedy-decoded chain-of-thought (CoT) for the original question. It then produces *N* semantically equivalent paraphrases of that question. For each position in the initial reasoning chain, the model computes its token prediction distribution when conditioned on each paraphrased question (concatenated with the reasoning prefix). A position is flagged as a candidate critical token if the top-1 predicted token under any paraphrase differs from the token in the original greedy path. A verifier score Δ quantifies the magnitude of this divergence, and the token with the maximum Δ is selected as the final critical token *a_c*. **Stage 2 (Consistency Verification):** The model samples *K* alternative tokens at the *a_c* position using top-K sampling on the original question. For each alternative token, the initial reasoning is truncated and extended with that token. Then, for the original question and all paraphrases, the model generates complete greedy-decoded reasoning paths from these new prefixes. The final answer from each rollout is extracted. The answer corresponding to the alternative token that achieves the highest consistency score—the count of paraphrases that yield the same final answer as the original question—is selected. Ties are broken using similarity-weighted consistency that discounts near-identical paraphrases.

## Key Results
- On Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct, PPCV achieves **88.24%** on GSM8K vs. **77.40%** for standard CoT (+10.8% absolute).
- On GSM-Hard, PPCV reaches **49.73%** vs. **28.00%** for CoT (+21.7% absolute).
- On Math500, PPCV scores **50.00%** vs. **31.00%** for CoT (+19.0% absolute).
- Similar improvements are observed across other model sizes and on ARC-Challenge.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1
- **Claim:** Critical tokens—intermediate tokens that disproportionately influence subsequent reasoning—can be identified by measuring divergence in the LLM's token predictions when the original question is paraphrased.
- **Mechanism:** The LLM first generates a greedy-decoded reasoning path for the original question. For each token position in this path, the model's next-token distribution is conditioned on concatenated paraphrased variants of the original question. A position is flagged as a candidate critical token if the top-1 predicted token for that position under the paraphrase condition differs from the token in the original path. A verifier score (Δ) quantifies the magnitude of this divergence, and the token with the maximum Δ is selected as the final critical token.
- **Core assumption:** Tokens whose prediction is highly sensitive to semantically equivalent surface forms (paraphrases) occupy a pivotal position in the reasoning chain, such that their value strongly constrains or misguides subsequent steps.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract] "identify critical tokens—positions in the reasoning chain that heavily influence subsequent steps—using mismatches in token predictions between the original and paraphrased questions."
  - [section 3.1] "Positions where the predicted top-1 token diverges from the expected token are flagged as potential pivotal points, as these positions are sensitive to paraphrased inputs and can trigger a pivot in the reasoning trajectory."
  - [corpus] Weak direct evidence; related work on paraphrastic variability exists, but the specific probing mechanism is novel to this paper.
- **Break condition:** If paraphrasing the question does not meaningfully alter the token distribution at any position (low Δ scores), no critical token is found, and the method may fall back to a baseline (e.g., vanilla CoT or Self-Consistency).

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim:** Replacing a critical token with a set of plausible alternatives can generate multiple diverse reasoning paths, some of which correct the error introduced by the original token and lead to the correct final answer.
- **Mechanism:** After identifying the critical token *a_c*, the model samples *K* alternative tokens (via top-K sampling) at that position. For each alternative, the initial reasoning path is truncated and extended with the new token. New full reasoning trajectories are then generated (greedy decode) for both the original and each paraphrased question. This process explores alternative continuations from a known failure point.
- **Core assumption:** The space of plausible alternative tokens at a critical position contains at least one token that, when substituted, puts the model on a trajectory toward a correct solution. This assumes the error is localized and not pervasive throughout the entire chain.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [section 2] "replacing critical tokens can refine reasoning trajectories."
  - [section 3.2] "We generate a set of alternative tokens... and roll out new reasoning paths for both the original and paraphrased questions."
  - [section 2, Figure 3] Case study showing substitution (e.g., "woman" → "remaining") correcting an erroneous path.
- **Break condition:** The new rollouts from all alternative tokens (and across paraphrases) consistently produce the same incorrect answer, indicating the identified token may not be the root cause or that the error is non-local.

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim:** Final answers derived from reasoning paths that are robust across paraphrased versions of the question (i.e., yield the same final answer) are more likely to be correct than answers that vary.
- **Mechanism:** For each set of rollouts generated from a specific alternative token (across original + paraphrased questions), a consistency score *c_k* is computed as the count of paraphrase variants that produce the same final answer as the rollout for the original question. The answer corresponding to the alternative token with the highest consistency score is selected. A similarity-weighted variant dampens the influence of paraphrases that are too close to the original.
- **Core assumption:** A correct reasoning chain is semantically grounded and therefore resilient to superficial changes in problem phrasing; incorrect chains are more brittle and sensitive to such perturbations.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract] "selecting the final answer based on consistency across the paraphrased outputs."
  - [section 3.2] "trajectories leading to correct answers are robust to paraphrastic perturbations... more than 90% of rollouts with correct answers achieve a consistency score of at least 1, whereas this proportion drops to around 30% for rollouts with incorrect answers."
  - [section 3.2, Figure 6d] Density plot showing a clear separation in consistency scores between correct and incorrect rollouts on GSM8K.
- **Break condition:** Multiple alternative tokens yield the same (and potentially high) consistency score, creating a tie that requires a heuristic (similarity weighting) to break, which itself may not guarantee correctness.

## Foundational Learning
- **Concept: Chain-of-Thought (CoT) Reasoning**
  - *Why needed here:* PPCV operates on an initial CoT trajectory. Understanding CoT's generation (greedy vs. sampled) and its error modes (error accumulation) is essential to grasp why the initial path is often flawed and needs correction.
  - *Quick check question:* What is a key limitation of greedy-decoded Chain-of-Thought that PPCV explicitly targets?
- **Concept: Token-Level Prediction & Logits**
  - *Why needed here:* The first stage hinges on comparing the model's predicted top-1 token distribution (logits) at specific positions under different input conditions (original vs. paraphrased question).
  - *Quick check question:* During paraphrastic probing, what specific signal at a token position indicates it might be critical?
- **Concept: Paraphrase Semantics & Diversity**
  - *Why needed here:* The method's efficacy depends on generating paraphrases that are semantically equivalent but lexically diverse enough to probe the model's internal sensitivity. Understanding the trade-off is key.
  - *Quick check question:* Why is controlling paraphrase diversity (via something like APE) important for the probing stage?
- **Concept: Consistency-Based Selection**
  - *Why needed here:* The final answer aggregation is a form of ensemble method where agreement (consistency) across semantically perturbed inputs is the selection criterion, differing from majority voting over identical inputs.
  - *Quick check question:* How does "paraphrase consistency" differ from the "majority voting" used in Self-Consistency?

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map:**
  Paraphrase Generator -> Initial Rollout Module -> Token Influence Analyzer -> Critical Token Verifier -> Alternative Token Sampler -> Parallel Rollout Engine -> Consistency Scorer & Selector
- **Critical path:** Paraphrase Generation -> Initial Rollout -> Token Mismatch Detection -> Δ-verification -> Alternative Sampling -> Parallel Rollouts (Original + All Paraphrases) -> Consistency Scoring -> Final Answer.
- **Design tradeoffs:**
  - **Latency vs. Accuracy:** Performance scales with number of paraphrases (N) and alternative tokens (K), but T4 (parallel rollouts) dominates cost. Trade-off is adjustable (see Appendix G).
  - **Paraphrase Quality vs. Diversity:** High diversity improves probing sensitivity but risks semantic drift. The APE prompt is designed to balance this.
  - **Single vs. Multi-Critical Token:** The paper presents the single-token case for clarity. Extending to multiple tokens increases search space exponentially (product of K choices per position).
  - **Consistency Heuristic:** The similarity-weighted consistency adds a tunable hyperparameter (λ) to discount near-identical paraphrases.
- **Failure signatures:**
  - **No critical token found:** All Δ scores below threshold → fallback to CoT or Self-Consistency.
  - **Low consistency spread:** Most or all *c_k* scores are 0 or 1, providing no discriminative signal.
  - **Paraphrase degradation:** Generated paraphrases alter core logic/numbers, invalidating the equivalence assumption (mitigated by APE but not guaranteed).
  - **Non-local errors:** The true error source is not captured by a single token position, so correcting *a_c* does not fix downstream steps.
- **First 3 experiments:**
  1. **Replication of Main Result:** Run PPCV on Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct with GSM8K (N=4, K=10). Verify reported ~88.2% pass@1 against CoT baseline 77.4%.
  2. **Ablation - CriticalToken Source:** Replace the Δ-verified critical token with a randomly selected token from the same early reasoning segment. Expect significant performance drop (as in Table 3), confirming the verifier's utility.
  3. **Ablation - Consistency Mechanism:** Replace paraphrase consistency with majority voting over rollouts from *only the original question* (same total sample count). Expect performance drop (as in Table 4), proving the value of paraphrase-based robustness.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
### Open Question 1
- **Question:** Does PPCV generalize to non-mathematical reasoning tasks?
- **Basis in paper:** [explicit] The experimental evaluation (Section 4.1) is limited to math and ARC, leaving other reasoning domains untested.
- **Why unresolved:** Paraphrastic probing may not work for domains without clear formal steps; its sensitivity to surface forms is unknown in such contexts.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Run PPCV on diverse reasoning benchmarks (e.g., LogiQA, CommonsenseQA, HellaSwag) and assess performance gains.

### Open Question 2
- **Question:** How does paraphrase quality affect PPCV?
- **Basis in paper:** [explicit] Paraphrases are generated via APE but the paper does not analyze their impact (Section 3.1).
- **Why unresolved:** Critical token identification relies on token mismatches; poor or uniform paraphrases may weaken the signal, while overly divergent ones could alter problem semantics.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Vary paraphrase generation methods (e.g., different prompts or models) and correlate metrics like BLEU or semantic similarity with both identification accuracy and final answer accuracy.

### Open Question 3
- **Question:** How does PPCV perform when multiple critical tokens are corrected?
- **Basis in paper:** [explicit] Section 3.3 notes the framework extends to multiple critical tokens but only single-token results are reported.
- **Why unresolved:** Many errors may stem from several bad tokens; fixing one might not suffice, and additive effects or interference are unknown.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Implement PPCV with 2, 3, … critical tokens, measure accuracy versus computational cost, and explore heuristics for selecting which tokens to replace.

- Critical token selection: Ambiguous whether rollout‑based filters (correctness < 5%, subsequent < 10%) are used; only Δ ranking described.
- Underspecified: top‑K temperature, embedding model for similarity weighting, selection of 4/5 paraphrases.
- Scope: Single‑token correction; multi‑token or non‑local errors not addressed. Latency scales with N·K.

- **Overall gains:** Medium. Single‑model results; no variance reported.
