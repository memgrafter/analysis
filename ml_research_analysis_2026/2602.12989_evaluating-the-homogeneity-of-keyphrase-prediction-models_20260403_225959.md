# Evaluating the Homogeneity of Keyphrase Prediction Models

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2602.12989
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.12989
- **Reference count:** 0
- **Primary result:** Generative models, particularly those augmented for absent keyphrase generation, exhibit higher homogeneity on semantically similar document pairs (KP20k), while extraction methods dominate on lexically similar pairs (Inspec); this challenges the assumption that generative models are inherently more homogeneous.

## Executive Summary
The paper investigates homogeneity—consistency of predictions across similar documents—in keyphrase prediction models. It constructs two evaluation datasets: (1) Inspec pairs formed by GPT-4o reformulations (high lexical overlap, mostly present keyphrases) and (2) KP20k pairs selected via shared keyphrase Jaccard (semantic similarity, generic/absent keyphrases). Homogeneity is measured via Hooper’s CP (exact keyphrase Jaccard) and Rodgers’ CP (word-level Jaccard). Results show that on Inspec, extraction methods (TF-IDF: 49.3%, MultipartiteRank: 46.4%) outperform most generative models, with TGRF (57.6%) a notable exception despite generating few absent keyphrases. On KP20k, augmented generative models (e.g., One2Set-60p at 16.5% Hooper’s) achieve significantly higher homogeneity than both extraction (TF-IDF: 6.3%) and base generative models. The study concludes that generating absent keyphrases harms homogeneity for explicit lexical links but benefits implicit, generic links, complicating the view of generative models as universally more consistent.

## Method Summary
The authors evaluate keyphrase prediction models by measuring their homogeneity on pairs of similar documents. Two pair-generation strategies are employed: (a) GPT-4o reformulation of Inspec documents (back-translation and paraphrasing) with filtering to preserve keyphrases and maintain length similarity; (b) selection of document pairs from the KP20k test set with a keyphrase Jaccard similarity of at least 50%. Models include extraction baselines (TF-IDF, MultipartiteRank) and various generative architectures (CopyRNN, CorrRNN, TG-Net, TGRF, BART, One2Set) plus augmentation variants (BART-60p, One2Set-60p) trained on KP20k. Homogeneity is quantified by Hooper’s CP (overlap of predicted keyphrase sets) and Rodgers’ CP (token-level overlap). The analysis correlates these scores with document similarity metrics (ROUGE-1) and keyphrase types (Present/Reordered/Missing/Unseen). The study highlights that extraction excels when keyphrases are present in both documents, while generation with absent-keyphrase capability shines when links are implicit and generic.

## Key Results
- On lexically similar Inspec pairs, TF-IDF (49.3% Hooper’s) and MultipartiteRank (46.4%) outperform 7 of 8 generative models; TGRF achieves 57.6% but generates few absent keyphrases.
- On semantically similar KP20k pairs, augmented generative models lead: One2Set-60p (16.5% Hooper’s) and BART-60p (+3.6 points over base BART) surpass extraction methods (e.g., TF-IDF: 6.3%) and base generators.
- Augmentation that increases absent keyphrase generation improves homogeneity on KP20k but harms it on Inspec, demonstrating a trade-off tied to the nature of document similarity.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1: Generative models bridge semantic gaps via absent keyphrases
- **Claim:** For documents covering the same concepts, a generative model can abstract beyond surface forms by predicting shared absent keyphrases (e.g., “deep learning”) even if only one document contains the exact phrase. An extraction model, constrained to source text spans, fails to produce identical keyphrases when they are not lexically present in both.
- **Core assumption:** Semantic similarity often involves concepts not explicitly co-occurring; generation overcomes this limitation.
- **Evidence:** The paper argues that only generative models can generate absent keyphrases, enabling homogeneity where extraction fails (Sec. 2). This is supported by the superior performance of augmented generative models on KP20k pairs where shared keyphrases are frequently absent (Sec. 4.2).

### Mechanism 2: Extraction excels under high lexical overlap
- **Claim:** On reformulated document pairs with high lexical similarity and shared present keyphrases, extraction methods achieve higher homogeneity than most generative models.
- **Mechanism:** Extraction models directly copy spans from nearly identical source texts, guaranteeing identical predictions. Generative models, despite having the correct keyphrases available in the text, may generate different surface expressions or select alternative keyphrases due to free-indexing and a large output vocabulary.
- **Evidence:** On Inspec pairs, TF-IDF (49.3% Hooper’s) and MultipartiteRank (46.4%) outperform the majority of generative models (Sec. 4.1). TGRF, which minimizes absent keyphrase generation, is the top performer but still lags behind extraction in absolute terms.

### Mechanism 3: Augmentation improves implicit-link homogeneity
- **Claim:** Self-compositional data augmentation that increases a model’s propensity to generate absent keyphrases enhances homogeneity on semantically similar, implicitly linked pairs (KP20k).
- **Mechanism:** Synthetic training documents created by combining keyphrases teach the model to abstractly associate broad concepts with documents, reducing variability when forecasting keyphrases for documents sharing generic, often absent, topics.
- **Evidence:** BART-60p improves by 3.6 points over base BART, and One2Set-60p by 17 points, on Hooper’s CP (Sec. 4.2). These augmented models, trained on data mimicking the KP20k scenario, become more consistent for implicit links.

## Foundational Learning
- **Homogeneity Metrics (Hooper’s CP, Rodgers’ CP)**  
  *Why needed:* They define consistency between predictions for similar documents; Hooper’s uses exact keyphrase Jaccard (strict), Rodgers’ uses token-level Jaccard (lenient).  
  *Quick check:* If Model A predicts ["neural networks", "AI"] and Model B predicts ["artificial intelligence", "networks"], Hooper’s = 0%, Rodgers’ ≈ 66.7%.

- **PRMU Classification (Present, Reordered, Missing, Unseen)**  
  *Why needed:* Categorizes keyphrases based on their presence and form in the source text; crucial for understanding why models succeed or fail on different pair types (e.g., many Unseen in KP20k).  
  *Quick check:* For a keyphrase “machine learning model”, if “machine” and “learning” appear but not consecutively and “model” is missing, classify as Reordered.

- **Explicit vs. Implicit Document Links**  
  *Why needed:* The central framing: explicit links (high lexical overlap) favor extraction; implicit links (semantic/generic) favor generation. Guides dataset design and hypothesis.  
  *Quick check:* Which dataset represents a more implicit link? KP20k pairs, because shared keyphrases are often absent/unseen and generic.

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component map:**  
Pair Generation Module → Keyphrase Prediction Engine → Scoring & Analysis Module

**Critical path:**  
1. Generate document pairs (reformulations or shared-keyphrase selection).  
2. Run all keyphrase prediction models on each document.  
3. Compute Hooper’s and Rodgers’ CP per pair.  
4. Aggregate scores, correlate with properties (ROUGE-1, PRMU categories).

**Design tradeoffs:**  
- *Reformulation vs. shared-keyphrase pairs:* Reformulations offer controlled lexical variation but rely on LLM quality and filtering heuristics; shared-keyphrase pairs use natural links but introduce selection bias and generic noise.  
- *Extraction vs. generation:* Extraction is deterministic and fast but limited to source vocabulary; generation is flexible but introduces stochasticity and vocabulary bloat, potentially harming lexical consistency.  
- *Metric choice:* Hooper’s CP is stricter; a model may achieve high Rodgers’ (token overlap) while low Hooper’s (phrase mismatch) if it uses synonyms.

**Failure signatures:**  
- High Rodgers’ + low Hooper’s → model generates semantically similar but lexically different phrases.  
- Extraction models performing poorly on KP20k → inability to produce absent/generic keyphrases.  
- Low correlation with ROUGE-1 for generative models → internal variability is the primary cause of low homogeneity, not just lexical differences.  
- Augmented models underperforming on Inspec → augmentation for absent phrases harms performance on present-keyphrase-dominant pairs.

**First 3 experiments:**  
1. **Baseline Homogeneity Test:** Run all models on the 280 Inspec reformulation pairs. Expect extraction methods (TF-IDF, MultipartiteRank) to lead; TGRF should be the best among generative models.  
2. **Implicit Link Test:** Run all models on the 145 KP20k shared-keyphrase pairs. Expect augmented generative models (One2Set-60p, BART-60p) to outperform extraction and base generative models.  
3. **Ablation on Augmentation:** Compare base vs. augmented models (e.g., BART vs. BART-60p) on both test sets to confirm that augmentation harms explicit-link homogeneity but benefits implicit-link homogeneity.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
1. **How can larger and more diverse evaluation pairs of similar documents be constructed?** The current evaluation sets (280 Inspec, 145 KP20k pairs) are too small for statistically significant conclusions; scalable automated methods (e.g., advanced LLM-based reformulation or shared-concept mining) are needed.  
2. **Do hybrid systems (extraction + generation) achieve higher homogeneity?** The authors suggest extracting present keyphrases first and using them as context for generating absent ones as a promising direction, but hybrids remain unevaluated.  
3. **To what extent do the results generalize beyond computer science?** The study is limited to CS datasets (Inspec, KP20k); domain differences (e.g., biomedical, legal, news) could alter the extraction vs. generation trade-offs.  
4. **How does annotation source (professional indexers vs. authors) affect measured homogeneity?** The paper compares indexer-assigned (Inspec) and author-assigned (KP20k) keyphrases but cannot isolate the effect of annotation style due to confounding dataset differences.

## Limitations
- Missing details on GPT-4o prompts, filtering thresholds, and precise reformulation quality metrics hinder exact replication of the Inspec pairs.  
- Training hyperparameters, data splits, and model checkpoints for generative models (especially BART-60p/One2Set-60p) are not provided, obstructing faithful reimplementation.  
- Tokenization/preprocessing for Hooper’s and Rodgers’ CP (e.g., lowercasing, stemming) is unspecified, risking metric mismatch.

## Confidence
- Generative models more homogeneous on implicit links (KP20k): **Medium** (trend is logical but exact replication uncertain).  
- Extraction methods more homogeneous on explicit links (Inspec): **Medium** (intuitive but relies on unreproducible dataset construction).  
- Augmentation improves implicit-link homogeneity but harms explicit-link: **Low** (depends on unreleased training setups and potential dataset biases).
