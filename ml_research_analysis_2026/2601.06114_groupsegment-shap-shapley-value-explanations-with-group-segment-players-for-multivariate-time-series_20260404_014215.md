# GroupSegment-SHAP: Shapley Value Explanations with Group-Segment Players for Multivariate Time Series

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2601.06114
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.06114
- **Reference count:** 40
- **Primary result:** GroupSegment-SHAP (GS-SHAP) improves deletion-based faithfulness (ΔAUC) by ~1.7× on average compared to prior time-series SHAP baselines while reducing wall-clock runtime by ~40% under matched perturbation budgets.

## Executive Summary
GroupSegment-SHAP (GS-SHAP) addresses the fragmentation problem in explaining multivariate time series models, where standard SHAP methods treat each time step and variable as independent players, breaking apart coupled spatiotemporal patterns. GS-SHAP constructs explanatory units by first grouping variables via HSIC-based spectral clustering to preserve nonlinear cross-variable dependencies, and then segmenting time via MMD-based change-point detection to capture distributional regime shifts. The Cartesian product of these groups and segments defines a minimal set of "group-segment players." Shapley values are computed over this player set, yielding explanations that retain the model's native spatiotemporal interactions. Across four diverse domains, GS-SHAP demonstrates superior faithfulness in deletion-based evaluations and improved computational efficiency.

## Method Summary
GS-SHAP proceeds in two offline and one online phase. First, **feature grouping** computes an HSIC affinity matrix from training data, applies spectral clustering via the Laplacian's eigengap to partition variables into groups *G* where within-group nonlinear dependence is high. Second, for each feature group *Gₖ*, **temporal segmentation** recursively applies an unbiased MMD two-sample test with permutation-calibrated thresholds to split the time axis into intervals *S⁽ᵏ⁾* where the joint distribution shifts, respecting a minimum segment length *Lₘᵢₙ*. For a new input instance *X*, the online phase constructs the player set *P* as all pairs *(Gₖ, Sⱼ⁽ᵏ⁾)*, each covering a contiguous block of cells. Shapley values are approximated by *M* random permutations: for each permutation, excluded players are masked by replacing their cell values with feature-wise means from a background dataset, the model is queried, and marginal contributions are averaged. The resulting attributions can be aggregated to cell-level maps or used directly for interpretation.

## Key Results
- **Faithfulness:** GS-SHAP improves deletion-based ΔAUC by approximately 1.7× on average over time-series SHAP baselines (KernelSHAP, TimeSHAP, SequenceSHAP).
- **Efficiency:** Under matched perturbation budgets, GS-SHAP reduces wall-clock runtime by about 40% on average due to fewer players.
- **Interpretability:** A financial case study demonstrates regime-specific interactions among key market variables (e.g., volatility, volume) during high-volatility periods.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1: HSIC-Based Feature Grouping Preserves Nonlinear Variable Dependencies
- **Claim:** HSIC-based feature grouping creates explanatory units that preserve nonlinear cross-variable dependencies, preventing fragmentation of joint variable patterns.
- **Mechanism:** Computes an HSIC affinity matrix from training data to measure pairwise nonlinear dependence. Spectral clustering on the normalized Laplacian partitions variables into groups where within-group HSIC is high. These groups become the feature-axis component of players.
- **Core assumption:** Predictive signals in multivariate time series often arise from subsets of variables exhibiting strong, nonlinear co-variation, not from individual variables acting independently.
- **Evidence anchors:** 
  - [abstract] "GroupSegment-SHAP constructs explanatory units by combining HSIC-based feature groups with MMD-based temporal segments."
  - [section 3.2] Details the HSIC affinity matrix construction, eigengap selection, and spectral clustering to derive groups G.
- **Break condition:** If the true predictive structure relies on linear dependencies or dependencies are extremely localized and non-overlapping, grouping may misalign with the model's used structure, harming faithfulness.

### Mechanism 2: MMD-Based Temporal Segmentation Captures Distributional Regime Changes
- **Claim:** MMD-based temporal segmentation identifies data-driven intervals where the joint distribution shifts, preserving temporal regime changes as coherent time-axis units.
- **Mechanism:** Recursively applies an unbiased MMD two-sample test to candidate split points within an interval. A split is accepted if the MMD statistic exceeds a permutation-calibrated threshold τ. This yields group-specific, non-overlapping temporal segments S^(k).
- **Core assumption:** Predictive models for time series often exploit distinct temporal regimes (e.g., high-volatility vs. stable periods), and these regimes correspond to detectable distributional shifts in the multivariate feature space.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract] "...MMD-based temporal segments. This preserves coupled spatiotemporal patterns as single players."
  - [section 3.3] Describes the recursive MMD-based change-point detection, the use of a permutation test for threshold τ, and group-specific segmentation.
- **Break condition:** If regime shifts are gradual, non-stationary without discrete breaks, or too subtle for the chosen MMD kernel/bandwidth/α, segmentation may over-fragment or under-segment, distorting the temporal explanation units.

### Mechanism 3: Cartesian Product of Groups and Segments Prevents Attribution Fragmentation
- **Claim:** Constructing players as the Cartesian product of HSIC feature groups and MMD temporal segments (group-segment players) prevents attribution fragmentation by treating coupled spatiotemporal patterns as indivisible units.
- **Mechanism:** For each feature group G_k and each of its temporal segments S^(k)_j, define a player p_(k,j) covering exactly the cells (t,d) where d∈G_k and t∈S^(k)_j. This ensures every cell belongs to one player and Shapley values are computed over this player set P.
- **Core assumption:** The model's predictive rationale is best explained by interactions that are *jointly* multivariate and temporal; isolating the feature or time axis discards the true explanatory unit.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract] "...group-segment players...preserves coupled spatiotemporal patterns as single players."
  - [section 3.4] Formally defines the player set P and proves each cell is assigned uniquely, preventing overlap.
  - [section 4.2] Deletion experiments (Figure 3) show GS-SHAP achieves higher ΔAUC than axis-separated baselines, supporting the claim that joint units yield more faithful explanations.
- **Break condition:** If the model's decisions are primarily driven by single variables at specific times, grouping and segmenting may combine irrelevant cells into a single player, aggregating opposite contributions and obscuring the true signal.

## Foundational Learning
- **Concept: Shapley Values**
  - **Why needed here:** GS-SHAP's core output is a Shapley value φ_p for each group-segment player. Understanding the axiomatic foundation (efficiency, symmetry, dummy, additivity) is crucial to grasp why this attribution scheme is used and what its guarantees (and limitations) are.
  - **Quick check question:** If a player's Shapley value is positive, what does that imply about the model's output when that player's information is present versus when it is replaced by the baseline?

- **Concept: Kernel-Based Dependence Measures (HSIC, MMD)**
  - **Why needed here:** HSIC (Hilbert-Schmidt Independence Criterion) drives feature grouping; MMD (Maximum Mean Discrepancy) drives temporal segmentation. Both are nonparametric, kernel-based tests that capture complex, nonlinear distributional differences beyond simple summary statistics.
  - **Quick check question:** Why might HSIC be preferred over Pearson correlation for grouping variables in a time series where relationships could be nonlinear?

- **Concept: Model-Agnostic Post-Hoc Explanations & Perturbation Baselines**
  - **Why needed here:** GS-SHAP is a model-agnostic explainer applied to a trained BiLSTM. It uses perturbation (masking) to estimate marginal contributions. The choice of baseline (mean, zero, noise) for masked regions directly impacts the generated explanations and their stability.
  - **Quick check question:** How does the choice of background set (used to compute feature-wise means for masking) affect the computed Shapley values, as hinted in the robustness analysis (Section 4.4)?

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map:**
  Trained model f(·) & input X & background D_train -> HSIC grouping (offline) -> Feature groups G -> MMD segmentation (online per X) -> Temporal segments S^(k) per group -> Player set P = {p_(k,j)} -> M permutations & mean-masking -> Model queries f(·) -> Marginal contributions -> Averaged φ_p -> Cell-level maps

- **Critical path:** The correctness of the final φ_p depends on the sequential accuracy of: (a) HSIC groups reflecting true variable dependencies, (b) MMD segments reflecting true temporal regimes for each group, (c) the permutation sampling adequately approximating the Shapley value over the defined player space P.

- **Design tradeoffs:**
  - **Segmentation granularity:** Minimum segment length L_min trades off between capturing short-lived patterns (low L_min) and avoiding noisy, uninterpretable fragments (high L_min). See Table 3.
  - **Player space size |P|:** More groups (K) and more segments per group (J_k) increase |P|, which improves structural fidelity but increases variance of Shapley estimation for a fixed M (perturbation budget). GS-SHAP's grouping aims to reduce |P| vs. cell-level players.
  - **Masking baseline:** Mean replacement is default but zero/noise baselines change ΔAUC by ~4% (Table 4). Choice affects the "missingness" semantics.

- **Failure signatures:**
  - **Over-segmentation:** Very many tiny segments with low |S^(k)_j|. This increases |P| excessively, making Shapley estimates high-variance and potentially less faithful. Diagnose by checking distribution of segment lengths.
  - **Poor grouping:** HSIC matrix shows weak block structure; groups are essentially random. This will cause GS-SHAP performance to approach "no grouping" baselines. Diagnose by inspecting the reordered HSIC similarity matrix.
  - **Mismatched regime detection:** MMD fails to find meaningful splits (only one segment per group) or finds splits that do not correspond to known/expected regime changes in the domain. Diagnose by visualizing segment boundaries on raw time series.

- **First 3 experiments:**
  1. **Grouping Quality Sanity Check:** Compute the HSIC affinity matrix on a small, synthetic multivariate time series with known dependent groups (e.g., two triads of variables with nonlinear coupling). Run spectral clustering and verify that known groups are recovered (high intra-group HSIC, low inter-group HSIC). This validates the HSIC pipeline.
  2. **Segmentation Sensitivity Sweep:** On a single dataset (e.g., ETTm1), run GS-SHAP while varying L_min over a range (e.g., [4, 6, 8, 10, 12, 16] as in Table 3). Plot ΔAUC vs. L_min. Identify the "elbow" where faithfulness peaks before degrading due to over/under-segmentation.
  3. **Baseline Comparison Under Fixed Player Count:** To isolate the effect of *player definition* from *player count*, compare GS-SHAP against a baseline that constructs a similar number of players but defined differently (e.g., fixed, non-overlapping time windows of average size matching GS-SHAP's |P|/D). This tests whether the *structure* of group-segment players is beneficial, not just having fewer players.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- **Question 1:** To what extent do GS-SHAP's data-driven group-segment players align with domain-specific, semantically meaningful units (e.g., physiological states in healthcare or economic regimes in finance)?
- **Question 2:** How sensitive are GS-SHAP's faithfulness gains and computational efficiency to the choice of hyperparameters (e.g., minimum segment length `L_min`, MMD significance level `α`, HSIC eigengap threshold) and kernel choices (RBF bandwidth for HSIC/MMD)?
- **Question 3:** Can the regime-specific pattern detection shown in the S&P500 case study be generalized to other domains (e.g., identifying "stable" vs. "fault-prone" regimes in industrial sensor data or "normal" vs. "arrhythmia" periods in ECG signals)?
- **Question 4:** Is the observed improvement in deletion-based faithfulness (ΔAUC) primarily driven by the *grouping* of features (via HSIC) or the *temporal segmentation* (via MMD), or by their specific *intersection* as group-segment players?
- **Question 5:** For long input sequences (e.g., PTB-XL with `T=1000`), does GS-SHAP's computational advantage over methods like TimeSHAP persist, and how does its runtime scale with sequence length `T` and number of variables `D` compared to the theoretical reduction in player count?

## Limitations
- **Undisclosed masking background:** The exact composition (size, sampling) of the background dataset used for mean replacement is not specified, which can crucially affect ΔAUC and robustness metrics.
- **Hyperparameter sensitivity:** MMD segmentation parameters (threshold α, minimum segment length L_min) are reported per dataset but lack a clear selection protocol, risking overfitting to each test set; HSIC grouping's sensitivity to subsample size and kernel bandwidth is unexplored.
- **Ablation gap:** The unique contribution of the joint group-segment player construction (vs. grouping *or* segmentation alone) is not fully isolated in the experimental design.

## Confidence
- **Mechanism 1 (HSIC grouping):** Medium. HSIC captures nonlinear dependence, but evidence that it yields optimal explanation players is indirect; no ablation on grouping quality vs. faithfulness.
- **Mechanism 2 (MMD segmentation):** Medium. MMD is a valid distribution test, yet recursive splitting per group may produce unstable segments if kernel/α are misset; paper lacks sensitivity analysis.
- **Mechanism 3 (Joint players prevent fragmentation):** High. Strong empirical support from consistent ΔAUC improvements across four diverse domains, though gains may partly stem from reduced player count.
- **Overall empirical claims (ΔAUC ↑1.7×, runtime ↓40%):** High. Reported numbers are clear and reproduced internally, but external replication is pending due to undisclosed details.

## Next Checks
1. **HSIC grouping robustness:** Using synthetic multivariate series with known ground-truth variable clusters (e.g., nonlinear coupled triads), vary subsample size (N=500–5000) and kernel bandwidth strategies (median vs. fixed). Measure cluster purity (ARI/NMI) and resulting ΔAUC to determine sensitivity.
2. **MMD segmentation parameter sweep:** For each dataset, systematically vary α ∈ {0.001, 0.01, 0.05} and L_min ∈ {0.05T, 0.1T, 0.2T}. Record player count distribution, segment length statistics, and ΔAUC. Identify parameter regions where faithfulness peaks and is stable.
3. **Player count isolation study:** Construct an alternative baseline that yields a similar number of players as GS-SHAP by dividing time into non-overlapping windows of average length matching GS-SHAP's segments, and grouping features either randomly or via HSIC but without temporal segmentation. Compare ΔAUC under identical M, background, and masking to verify the benefit of the Cartesian product structure.
