# VoxEmo: Benchmarking Speech Emotion Recognition with Speech LLMs

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2603.08936
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.08936
- **Reference count:** 0
- **Primary result:** Zero-shot speech LLMs are highly prompt-sensitive and underperform supervised models on hard-label Macro-F1 (gaps 10–30 points), but better align with human emotion distributions; supervised fine-tuning (SFT) with LoRA and prompt-ensemble narrow the gap.

## Executive Summary
VoxEmo is a comprehensive benchmark for speech emotion recognition (SER) using speech LLMs, addressing two critical gaps: prompt sensitivity and the neglect of annotator ambiguity. It consolidates 35 corpora into a standardized evaluation toolkit, introduces a soft-label protocol that preserves inter-annotator disagreement, and proposes a prompt-ensemble strategy to stabilize zero-shot inference. The study evaluates two speech LLMs (Qwen2-Audio and Audio Flamingo 3) in zero-shot and supervised fine-tuning (SFT) settings, using both traditional hard-label metrics and distribution-aware measures.

Key findings reveal that zero-shot speech LLMs lag substantially behind supervised baselines on Macro-F1 (by 10–30 points), with performance heavily dependent on prompt design (spreads up to 49 points). SFT with LoRA significantly improves Qwen2-Audio (+23.7 mean Macro-F1) but yields smaller gains for Audio Flamingo 3 (+10.3). Notably, zero-shot outputs align better with human subjective emotion distributions than supervised classifiers, as measured by lower Jensen-Shannon Divergence and higher cosine similarity. The prompt-ensemble method further enhances distribution alignment and reduces parse failures.

## Method Summary
VoxEmo standardizes evaluation across 35 SER corpora, covering diverse conditions (acted, in-the-wild, cross-cultural). It employs a soft-label protocol: for each utterance, annotations from multiple raters are aggregated into a probability distribution over emotion categories, preserving ambiguity. The toolkit supports zero-shot inference with speech LLMs using five prompt variants that differ in instruction blocks (text transcript, acoustic caption, reasoning). A prompt-ensemble aggregates predictions across these variants, handling parse failures by assigning uniform votes. For supervised fine-tuning, LoRA adapters are trained on selected corpora while freezing the Whisper encoder and LLM backbone. Evaluation combines hard-label Macro-F1 with distribution-aware metrics (JSD, TVD, Cosine Similarity) to assess alignment with human judgments.

## Key Results
- Zero-shot speech LLMs significantly underperform supervised baselines on hard-label Macro-F1, with gaps ranging from 10 to 30 points; prompt design causes performance spreads up to 49 points.
- Supervised fine-tuning with LoRA narrows the gap for Qwen2-Audio (mean Macro-F1 gain +23.7) but less effectively for Audio Flamingo 3 (+10.3), indicating model-specific adaptation efficiency.
- Zero-shot LLM outputs better match human emotion distributions (lower JSD/TVD, higher Cosine Similarity) than traditional classifiers, and prompt-ensemble further improves this alignment while reducing parse failures.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1: Prompt Sensitivity and Parsing Brittleness
- **Claim:** Prompt engineering causes dramatic performance swings in zero-shot SER because the generative output must be parsed into a closed-label set, and model adherence to structured formats is brittle.
- **Mechanism:** The speech LLM receives audio and a text prompt; its stochastic text generation is conditioned on prompt wording and requested output structure (e.g., JSON). Variations in phrasing or complexity lead to format deviations, causing parse failures or misaligned responses.
- **Core assumption:** The model's ability to follow complex, multi-part instructions degrades non-linearly with prompt complexity, and this degradation is model-specific.
- **Evidence anchors:** Abstract notes high prompt sensitivity; Section 4.1 shows Macro-F1 spreads up to 49 points; Section 4.3.1/Figure 1 shows catastrophic parse failure rates for the `+A` prompt (93.2% for Qwen2-Audio).
- **Break condition:** When the instructed output format (e.g., `FINAL LABEL:`) is not generated, parsing fails and triggers a fallback (e.g., uniform prediction).

### Mechanism 2: Inherent Modeling of Emotional Ambiguity
- **Claim:** Zero-shot speech LLMs better model the ambiguity and subjectivity of human emotion perception than supervised classifiers because their generative, distributional output space can represent competing emotional states.
- **Mechanism:** Unlike supervised classifiers trained with hard one-hot labels, the LLM's next-token prediction over a vocabulary is not forced to collapse probability onto a single label. When prompted for a distribution, its output logits can reflect uncertainty present in the annotation data.
- **Core assumption:** Annotator disagreement captured in soft-labels is a meaningful signal about perceptual ambiguity, not noise.
- **Evidence anchors:** Abstract states zero-shot outputs align better with human distributions; Section 2.2 introduces the soft-label protocol; Section 4.3.3/Table 6 shows zero-shot LLMs achieve lower JSD and higher Cosine Similarity than supervised models on five multi-annotator corpora.
- **Break condition:** If the model is fine-tuned with hard-label cross-entropy loss (SFT), it over-concentrates probability mass, reducing alignment with human ambiguity distributions.

### Mechanism 3: Prompt-Ensemble Robustification
- **Claim:** A prompt-ensemble strategy mitigates zero-shot instability and improves alignment with human emotion distributions by aggregating diverse, imperfect predictions from multiple prompt families.
- **Mechanism:** Multiple prompts (varying instruction blocks T, A, R) generate hard-label predictions; these are aggregated into a distribution (vote counting). Parse failures contribute a uniform vote, approximating a model-derived consensus robust to any single prompt's failure or bias.
- **Core assumption:** Variance in predictions across valid prompts samples the model's uncertainty space in a way that correlates with human annotator disagreement.
- **Evidence anchors:** Abstract mentions ensemble reduces parse failures and improves alignment; Section 3.2 details the ensemble formula; Section 4.3.2/Table 6 shows ensemble consistently outperforms single-prompt "Direct" on distribution metrics (e.g., Qwen2-Audio on CREMA-D: Ensemble JSD=0.175 vs Direct JSD=0.196).
- **Break condition:** If all prompts in the ensemble have high, correlated parse failures (e.g., due to a fundamental model limitation for a dataset), the ensemble degrades to a uniform distribution.

## Foundational Learning

#### Concept: Paralinguistic vs. Linguistic Cues
- **Why needed here:** SER distinguishes acoustic/prosodic features (paralinguistic) from spoken text content (linguistic). The paper's analysis (Section 4.1.1–2) shows that adding an acoustic caption prompt (`+A`) helps on acted data (fixed transcripts), while adding an ASR transcript prompt (`+T`) helps on in-the-wild data (variable, discriminative content). Misunderstanding this split leads to incorrect prompt design.
- **Quick check question:** For a fixed-script acted corpus, would you expect adding a transcript-based instruction to improve or harm a speech LLM's zero-shot SER performance, and why?

#### Concept: Annotation Ambiguity as a Distribution
- **Why needed here:** Traditional SER uses a single hard label per utterance, discarding inter-annotator disagreement. The paper's core evaluates via soft-labels (Section 2.2). Understanding that a sample's "ground truth" is a probability distribution over emotions is prerequisite to grasping the soft-label metrics (JSD, TVD, Cosine Sim) and the claim about LLM alignment.
- **Quick check question:** How would you formally define a soft-label for an utterance if 4 out of 5 annotators chose "Angry" and 1 chose "Frustrated"?

#### Concept: Parameter-Efficient Fine-Tuning (PEFT)
- **Why needed here:** The supervised contrast (SFT) uses LoRA (Section 3.3). A new engineer must understand that LoRA freezes the base model and trains low-rank adapters to avoid catastrophic forgetting and reduce compute, explaining why gains vary (e.g., Qwen2-Audio +23.7 vs Audio Flamingo 3 +10.3 mean gain).
- **Quick check question:** In the LoRA setup described (r=8, α=16), what percentage of the total 7B parameters are being updated, and what is the primary intended benefit of this approach?

## Architecture Onboarding

- **Component map:** Audio → Whisper Encoder (frozen) → Speech-LLM Backbone (Qwen2-Audio / Audio Flamingo 3) → [SFT: LoRA adapters on attention layers] → Text Generation → Output Parser. For prompt-ensemble: Multiple Prompt Variants → Ensemble Aggregator (vote counting + parse failure fallback) → final distribution/hard label.

- **Critical path (Inference):** Raw audio is processed by the Whisper encoder to produce latent features. These features, combined with a text prompt, are fed into the speech LLM, which autoregressively generates text. The output parser extracts either a single emotion label or a distribution from the generated text. In the prompt-ensemble strategy, this process is repeated with five prompt variants, and the predictions are aggregated.

- **Design tradeoffs:**
  - Prompt Complexity vs. Parsing Reliability: Simpler prompts reduce parse failures but may under-elicit reasoning; complex prompts risk format collapse.
  - Greedy Decoding vs. Diversity: Greedy decoding ensures reproducibility but may not sample the full distribution; stochastic decoding could increase variance.
  - Single Best Prompt vs. Ensemble: Ensemble is robust and improves distribution alignment but incurs 5× inference cost.

- **Failure signatures:**
  - Parse Failure: LLM output lacks expected markers (e.g., `FINAL LABEL:`) or valid JSON, triggering fallback (uniform for soft-label, incorrect for hard-label).
  - Label Set Mismatch: LLM generates a label not in the dataset's closed set; fails case-insensitive matching.
  - Cross-Domain Degradation: SFT on one corpus harms performance on another more than zero-shot (e.g., Audio Flamingo 3), suggesting poor generalization or LoRA interference.

- **First 3 experiments:**
  1. **Replicate Prompt Sensitivity:** Choose a dataset (e.g., TESS). Run all five zero-shot prompt variants for Qwen2-Audio. Measure Macro-F1 spread and parse failure rates. Expect >40-point spread and high failure for `+A`.
  2. **SFT Baseline:** Perform LoRA fine-tuning on a high-resource acted corpus (e.g., CREMA-D) using a single closed-set prompt. Compare SFT Macro-F1 to the best zero-shot prompt and the EmoBox supervised reference. Expect Qwen2-Audio SFT to approach the reference.
  3. **Soft-Label Ensemble:** On a multi-annotator corpus (e.g., IEMOCAP), compare `Direct`, `+A`, and `Ensemble` strategies. Report both hard Macro-F1 and distribution metrics (JSD, Cosine Sim). Expect `Ensemble` to improve distribution alignment and often hard-F1 over `Direct`.

## Limitations
- Benchmark skew: VoxEmo's 35 corpora are predominantly English, limiting conclusions about cross-lingual or dialectal generalization.
- Prompt brittleness: Optimal prompt design likely varies by dataset; the prompt-ensemble mitigates but does not fully eliminate sensitivity.
- Soft-label alignment evidence: Based on only five multi-annotator corpora, providing limited statistical power for the claim that zero-shot LLMs better capture ambiguity.

## Next Checks
1. **Cross-lingual ensemble test:** Apply the prompt-ensemble strategy to at least 10 non-English corpora (e.g., Chinese, Spanish) to evaluate robustness and cultural bias.
2. **Parser ablation:** Replace the heuristic output parser with grammar-constrained decoding; compare parse failure rates, Macro-F1, and distribution metrics to isolate the parser's impact.
3. **Soft-label supervised baseline:** Train a standard classifier (e.g., HuBERT + linear probe) on soft-labels derived from VoxEmo's protocol; compare distribution alignment metrics to zero-shot LLMs to test whether distributional training alone explains the LLM advantage.
