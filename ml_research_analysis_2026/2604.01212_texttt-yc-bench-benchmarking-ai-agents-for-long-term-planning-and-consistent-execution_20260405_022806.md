# $\texttt{YC-Bench}$: Benchmarking AI Agents for Long-Term Planning and Consistent Execution

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2604.01212
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.01212
- Reference count: 15
- Primary result: Only 3 of 12 models (Claude Opus 4.6, GLM-5, GPT-5.4) exceeded the $200K starting capital, with Opus 4.6 averaging $1.27M and GLM-5 achieving $1.21M with 11× lower cost.

## Executive Summary
YC-Bench is a novel benchmark designed to evaluate the long-term planning and consistent execution capabilities of large language model agents. The environment simulates running a startup over one year, requiring agents to accept tasks, assign employees, manage cash flow, and build client trust while contending with adversarial clients that inflate work requirements and compounding payroll growth. Due to a 20-turn context window, agents must rely on a persistent scratchpad for long-term memory. Testing twelve models on three seeds each revealed that only Claude Opus 4.6, GLM-5, and GPT-5.4 succeeded in growing capital beyond the initial $200K. Scratchpad usage emerged as the strongest predictor of success, while the primary failure mode—adversarial client misidentification—accounted for 47% of bankruptcies. Additional failures included over-parallelization and reasoning-execution gaps, highlighting the challenge of maintaining coherent long-horizon strategies.

## Method Summary
Agents interact with the YC-Bench environment through a command-line interface, issuing actions such as `task browse`, `client accept`, and `employee assign` each turn. The simulation spans one year with hundreds of turns; each turn, the agent can take multiple commands before advancing time with `sim resume`. The environment is a partially observable Markov decision process where hidden adversarial clients inflate work after acceptance, and payroll increases with each completed task, creating financial pressure. A 20-turn context limit forces agents to use a persistent scratchpad, which is reinjected each turn, to retain critical strategic information. The evaluation involves twelve state-of-the-art LLMs, each run with three random seeds, with primary metric being final funds. Secondary metrics include bankruptcy rate, scratchpad usage frequency, and breakdown of failure modes.

## Key Results
- Scratchpad usage is the strongest predictor of success; models with higher writes per 100 turns achieve significantly higher final funds, while models with near-zero usage consistently fail.
- Adversarial client misidentification is the leading cause of bankruptcy (47% of runs); two-thirds of all runs make no mention of "blacklisting" adversarial clients in their scratchpad.
- Over-parallelization (excessive concurrent tasks relative to employee capacity) and reasoning-execution gaps (failure to act on identified problems) are widespread secondary failure modes.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1
- **Claim:** Persistent scratchpad usage enables long-horizon coherence by allowing the agent to retain and retrieve critical strategic information across context window truncations.
- **Mechanism:** The environment imposes a 20-turn context limit, forcing the agent to explicitly write key insights (e.g., client reliability, employee strengths, strategic rules) to a persistent scratchpad that is reinjected each turn. This compensates for the loss of historical conversation data.
- **Core assumption:** The agent can correctly identify which information is strategically valuable for long-term decision-making and consistently update/consult the scratchpad.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - Abstract: "Scratchpad usage, the sole mechanism for persisting information across context truncation, is the strongest predictor of success."
  - Section 3: "The scratchpad is the agent’s sole mechanism for retaining information across context truncation... tests whether agents can autonomously determine what information is worth persisting."
- **Break condition:** The agent fails to write to the scratchpad, writes stale/irrelevant information, or does not consult its own notes before acting, leading to repeated mistakes (e.g., re-accepting adversarial clients).

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim:** Successful detection and avoidance of adversarial clients is the primary determinant of avoiding catastrophic failure, accounting for 47% of bankruptcies.
- **Mechanism:** A subset of clients secretly inflates work quantity after task acceptance. The agent must infer this hidden property by observing repeated task failures linked to specific clients, then explicitly blacklist them in the scratchpad to prevent future acceptance.
- **Core assumption:** The agent can correlate patterns of failure (missed deadlines) with the responsible client and maintain a reliable blacklist over hundreds of turns.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - Abstract: "adversarial client detection is the primary failure mode, accounting for 47% of bankruptcies."
  - Section 4.2: "Half of all the models accept adversarial tasks at a rate higher than the natural market share... two-thirds of all runs make no mention of 'blacklisting' any adversarial client."
  - Section 5: "Opus... writes explicit guidelines to never accept tasks from those clients."
- **Break condition:** The agent does not track per-client failure rates, accepts tasks from clients with a history of failure, or fails to record blacklist decisions persistently, leading to repeated scope-creep failures.

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim:** Correct management of concurrent task commitment relative to employee throughput prevents understaffing failures and controls payroll growth.
- **Mechanism:** Employees split their productivity across all active tasks (throughput = rate / N). Accepting too many tasks concurrently without sufficient employee assignment guarantees failure. Furthermore, each completed task triggers a salary bump, so unnecessary concurrency inflates payroll without proportional reward.
- **Core assumption:** The agent can accurately calculate required work against employee rates and deadlines, and enforce a "one-task-at-a-time" or limited-concurrency policy.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - Section 4.2: "over-committed employees to tasks" is a listed failure mode.
  - Section 5: "Sonnet... averages 7.23 concurrent active tasks (max 16)... 41% [of failures] result from understaffing."
  - Section 5: "Flash... executes an identical 4-command cycle... accepts tasks without inspection."
- **Break condition:** The agent accepts tasks without inspecting work requirements, assigns insufficient employees, or runs too many tasks concurrently, causing missed deadlines and cascading payroll costs.

## Foundational Learning
- **Partially Observable Markov Decision Process (POMDP)**  
  Why needed: The environment contains hidden state (adversarial client status, true employee productivity) that must be inferred from observations over time; success depends on maintaining a belief state.  
  Quick check: If a client's tasks repeatedly fail despite assigning all employees, what hidden attribute should the agent infer, and what persistent record should it create?

- **Compounding Financial Dynamics**  
  Why needed: Payroll grows monotonically with each completed task, while prestige and trust decay create moving targets; short-term profit can lead to long-term insolvency if not carefully managed.  
  Quick check: How does completing a high-reward task today potentially reduce profitability six months later, even if successful?

- **Trust-as-a-Limited-Resource Game**  
  Why needed: Trust with a client reduces future workload and unlocks high-reward tasks, but working for one client decays trust with others; this creates a non-linear optimization problem with strategic trade-offs.  
  Quick check: Why might focusing on a single client early be more profitable than diversifying across many low-trust clients in the first 60 days?

## Architecture Onboarding
Component map:
Agent Loop -> CLI Interface (sends commands, receives observations) -> Environment State (updates) -> observations fed back to Agent Loop; Scratchpad is a persistent module read/written by Agent Loop each turn.

Critical path:
1. **Calibration Phase (Turns 1–10):** Use `task inspect` and `client history` to build baseline productivity and failure models.
2. **Trust Snowball (Turns 10–60):** Select 1–2 clients, complete tasks consistently to build trust, unlocking lower-work, higher-reward tasks.
3. **Adversarial Avoidance (Ongoing):** Maintain and obey a client blacklist in scratchpad; never accept from blacklisted clients.
4. **Payroll Management:** Limit concurrent tasks to what 8 employees can handle; prioritize high reward/workload ratio tasks.

Design tradeoffs:
- **Context window size (K):** Larger K improves situational awareness but reduces action efficiency (models issue fewer commands per turn; see Appendix E).
- **Scratchpad curation:** Overwriting loses history; appending creates clutter. Need structured formats (e.g., lists, tables) for quick retrieval.
- **Inspection cost:** `task inspect` is cheap but time-consuming in-turns; must balance thoroughness vs. throughput.

Failure signatures:
- **High concurrency:** `task list` shows >3 active tasks consistently; likely understaffing failures.
- **Scratchpad stagnation:** No updates for >10 turns; stale financial data; no client policy.
- **Adversarial acceptance:** `client history` shows repeated failures with same client; scratchpad lacks blacklist.
- **Aware inaction:** Scratchpad correctly diagnoses problems (e.g., "runway low") but subsequent actions don't change.

First 3 experiments:
1. **Scratchpad Utilization:** Instrument every turn to log whether `scratchpad write/append` was called and whether the content was strategic (e.g., rules, lists) vs. episodic. Correlate with final funds.
2. **Client Selection Policy:** Parse scratchpad for "whitelist"/"blacklist" mentions. Compare acceptance rate of adversarial clients for models with/without explicit lists.
3. **Throughput Calculation:** For each accepted task, compute `(assigned employees * domain rate) / work_quantity`. Flag tasks where this ratio < 1.0 before acceptance. Measure how often agent performs this check.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- **Dynamic Hiring/Firing:** How does introducing dynamic hiring and firing of employees change long-term planning dynamics and success rates?  
  *Basis:* The paper explicitly notes employees cannot be hired or fired as a simplifying limitation for future work.  
  *Why unresolved:* The fixed workforce removes a major strategic degree of freedom; it is unclear whether models could handle additional optimization complexity.  
  *Evidence:* Extend YC-Bench to allow hiring/firing with associated costs and skill distributions, then compare agent performance, payroll management, and failure modes against the fixed-roster baseline.

- **Exogenous Disruptions:** How resilient are agents to random exogenous disruptions (e.g., market crashes, client cancellations) beyond the deterministic adversarial clients?  
  *Basis:* The paper explicitly states disruptions are limited to adversarial clients rather than random exogenous events as a simplification for future work.  
  *Why unresolved:* Real-world businesses face unpredictable external shocks; the current POMDP dynamics are fully deterministic once adversarial status is fixed, potentially under-testing adaptive planning.  
  *Evidence:* Introduce stochastic events (e.g., random task reward changes, sudden client dropouts) into YC-Bench and measure agent performance variance, recovery speed, and bankruptcy rates across seeds.

- **Scratchpad Utilization Variance:** Why do models exhibit such dramatically different scratchpad utilization frequencies and strategies despite its strong correlation with success?  
  *Basis:* Scratchpad writes per 100 turns range from 0.0 to 5.6 across models, and usage is the "strongest predictor of success", but the paper does not explain why some models use it prolifically while others barely do.  
  *Why unresolved:* The system prompt explicitly encourages scratchpad use, yet behavioral patterns vary widely; this may reflect underlying architectural differences, training objectives, or deliberation-action coupling not analyzed.  
  *Evidence:* Conduct a qualitative and quantitative analysis of scratchpad content across successful vs. failing runs (e.g., rule specificity, update frequency, content longevity) and correlate with final funds to isolate effective usage patterns.

- **Adversarial Identification Challenges:** What specific inference or credit-assignment challenges make adversarial client identification (a hidden POMDP state) so difficult, leading to 47% of bankruptcies?  
  *Basis:* Adversarial client misidentification is the "primary failure mode" and models accept adversarial tasks at rates near or above the market share, but the paper does not dissect the sample complexity or delayed feedback issues.  
  *Why unresolved:* While the environment requires learning client reliability from repeated task outcomes, the paper does not analyze how many failures are needed for detection, whether models confuse noise with adversarial patterns, or how trust decay interacts with this learning.  
  *Evidence:* Ablate the environment by providing intermediate reliability signals (e.g., partial work inflation hints) or vary the adversarial fraction to measure detection thresholds and learning curves across models.

- **Cost-Efficiency Gaps:** What drives the large cost-efficiency gaps (e.g., Kimi-K2.5 vs. Claude Opus 4.6) that are not explained by final revenue alone?  
  *Basis:* Cost-efficiency is reported, showing Kimi-K2.5 generates 2.5× more revenue per dollar than the next model, while top performers like Opus lag significantly despite higher revenue; the paper does not analyze causes such as token efficiency or action granularity.  
  *Why unresolved:* The token and runtime statistics are provided without correlating them to decision quality; it is unclear whether high-cost models use more verbose reasoning, make more tool calls, or have less efficient action bundling.  
  *Evidence:* Correlate per-turn token count, command execution rates (Cmd/Turn), and average concurrency (Avg Conc.) with final funds and cost metrics across all runs to identify the operational factors behind cost-performance trade-offs.

## Limitations
- The exact mathematical model for adversarial client inflation (scope creep) and the seeding mechanism for environmental variability are unspecified, hindering faithful replication.
- The benchmark simplifies payroll dynamics (e.g., fixed salary bumps) and prohibits hiring/firing, omitting key real-world financial and operational complexities.
- The environment is fully deterministic once adversarial status is fixed, lacking exogenous randomness that could better test adaptive planning to unforeseen disruptions.

## Confidence
- Scratchpad efficacy: High
- Adversarial client impact: High
- Over-parallelization failure: Medium
- GLM-5 cost advantage: Medium
- Reasoning-execution gap: Medium
