# Revisiting the Sliced Wasserstein Kernel for persistence diagrams: a Figalli–Gigli approach

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID**: 2602.06539
- **Source URL**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.06539
- **Reference count**: 40
- **Primary result**: The Sliced Figalli–Gigli Kernel (SFGK) replaces the modified Wasserstein distance in SWK with the Figalli–Gigli distance, yielding a metric that naturally extends to infinite diagrams, is conditionally negative definite for p∈[1,2], and achieves comparable classification accuracy on TDA benchmarks.

## Executive Summary
This paper revisits the Sliced Wasserstein Kernel (SWK) for persistence diagrams, which uses a modified Wasserstein distance and is restricted to finite diagrams. The authors propose the Sliced Figalli–Gigli Kernel (SFGK) by employing the Figalli–Gigli (FG) distance directly, a metric that naturally extends to infinite diagrams and persistence measures. They prove that the sliced FG (SFG) distance is a metric, conditionally negative definite for p∈[1,2] (ensuring a valid positive definite kernel), and stable with respect to the original FG distance. Exact computation of SFG has complexity O((N1+N2)×max(N1,N2)), and approximations further reduce cost. On benchmark classification tasks (Orbit recognition and Texture classification), SFGK variants achieve accuracy comparable to SWK: Orbit (SFG: 80.8±1.2%, dSFG: 79.8±0.4% vs SWK: 79.7±1.6%); Texture (SFG: 90.1±1.3%, dSFG: 91.7±2.8% vs SWK: 89.5±5.2%). Thus, SFGK offers a more principled, theoretically grounded, and versatile framework while maintaining empirical performance.

## Method Summary
The SFGK construction begins by renormalizing persistence measures (or diagrams) by dividing mass at each point by its distance to the diagonal, mapping them into a space where slicing is finite. The distance between two persistence measures μ and ν is defined as the L^p-norm (p∈[1,2]) of the FG distance between their 1D projections onto all geodesics G_t through the diagonal. For a fixed geodesic, the 1D FG distance reduces to an L^p distance between quantile functions of the projected and renormalized masses. The sliced integral is then computed either exactly—by iterating over all event intervals where the set of points projected onto G_t changes—or approximately by uniformly sampling a fixed set of t-values. The resulting distance SFG_p(μ,ν) is used to build a kernel k(μ,ν)=exp(−SFG_p(μ,ν)^p/σ^2). The exact algorithm maintains dynamic sets of points in strips Ω_t, updates them at birth/death events, and computes 1D FG per interval. The approximate algorithm samples t-values once for the entire dataset, projects all diagrams onto the corresponding geodesics, and averages the 1D FG values.

## Key Results
- SFG is a metric on persistence measures and is conditionally negative definite for p∈[1,2], so exp(−SFG_p^2/σ^2) is a positive definite kernel.
- SFG is stable with respect to the Figalli–Gigli distance, with a bound involving the ∞-persistence of the input measures.
- The exact SFG computation is O((N1+N2)×max(N1,N2)); approximations reduce complexity to O(K·(N1+N2) log(N1+N2)).
- SFGK achieves comparable classification accuracy to SWK on Orbit and Texture datasets, demonstrating its empirical viability.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1: Slicing the Figalli–Gigli distance in 1D
- **Claim**: By projecting persistence diagrams onto 1D geodesics and integrating the 1D Figalli–Gigli (FG) distance, we obtain a metric (SFG) that is conditionally negative definite (CND) for p∈[1,2], thus defining a valid kernel.
- **Mechanism**: Orthogonal projection onto geodesics (straight lines through the diagonal) reduces 2D optimal transport to 1D. In 1D, the FG distance admits a closed form as an L^p distance between quantile functions (Proposition 7). Integrating over all directions yields SFG. The CND property follows because L^p with p≤2 is CND (Proposition 8).
- **Core assumption**: The 1D FG distance can be expressed via quantile functions, enabling efficient computation and the CND proof.
- **Evidence anchors**:
  - [section 3.2.1] (Proposition 7: closed form in 1D)
  - [section 3.2.2] (Proposition 8: CND for p∈[1,2])
  - [corpus] *Persistence Spheres* (similar projection-based representations) validates geometric slicing.
- **Break condition**: If the 1D FG distance did not have the quantile-based closed form, slicing would be computationally intractable and the CND proof would fail.

### Mechanism 2: Renormalization for well-defined slicing
- **Claim**: Renormalizing persistence measures by dividing by distance to the diagonal ensures the sliced integral is finite and extends to infinite diagrams.
- **Mechanism**: Projection onto geodesics inflates mass; without renormalization, the integral of FG over directions could diverge. Dividing by d(x,∂Ω) balances this, mapping Mp(Ω) to M_{p+1}(Ω), where the sliced integral is finite (Equation 23). This allows SFG to handle infinite persistence diagrams and measures, unlike SWK.
- **Core assumption**: Persistence measures in Mp(Ω) have finite p-persistence; renormalization preserves integrability.
- **Evidence anchors**:
  - [section 3.1] (Definition 1 and Equation 23: renormalization ensures finiteness)
  - [abstract] ("naturally extends to infinite diagrams and persistence measures")
  - [corpus] *Wasserstein Dictionaries* (use of persistence measures) shows relevance beyond finite diagrams.
- **Break condition**: If renormalization did not control the integral, SFG would be undefined for measures with points arbitrarily close to the diagonal.

### Mechanism 3: Stability via transport plan lifting
- **Claim**: SFG is stable w.r.t. FG: SFG_p(μ, ν) ≤ C_p FG_p(μ, ν) + persistence terms.
- **Mechanism**: Given an optimal FG transport plan γ between μ and ν, one constructs a transport plan γ̃ between the renormalized and projected measures by weighting γ with 1/max(d(x,∂Ω), d(y,∂Ω)) (Equation 57). Bounding the projection cost (Lemma 4) yields Theorem 1.
- **Core assumption**: γ exists and is sufficiently regular; projections are measurable.
- **Evidence anchors**:
  - [section 3.3.1] (Theorem 1: stability bound)
  - [abstract] ("stable with respect to the original Figalli–Gigli distance")
  - [corpus] *Sliced Wasserstein Stability* (similar lifting arguments) supports the approach.
- **Break condition**: The bound depends on Pers_∞(μ), Pers_∞(ν). For p>1, no uniform constant C_p exists (examples in text), so stability is not strong equivalence.

## Foundational Learning

- **Concept: Persistent Homology & Persistence Diagrams**
  - **Why needed**: SFG operates on persistence diagrams (PDs), which encode birth-death pairs of topological features. Understanding their geometry (Ω = {b < d}) and the role of the diagonal (∂Ω) is essential.
  - **Quick check**: What does a point (b,d) in a PD represent, and why is the diagonal meaningful?

- **Concept: Optimal Transport & Wasserstein Distances**
  - **Why needed**: Both SWK and SFG are built on optimal transport. The 1D case (sorted matching) is crucial for efficiency. Understanding admissible plans and the Wasserstein metric clarifies differences with FG.
  - **Quick check**: How is the 1D Wasserstein distance W_p computed for discrete measures? Why does it allow only equal total mass?

- **Concept: Figalli–Gigli Distance**
  - **Why needed**: The core innovation is replacing Wasserstein with FG. FG differs by allowing mass to be sent to the diagonal via admissible plans, making it suitable for PDs and persistence measures.
  - **Quick check**: Why is the standard Wasserstein distance inadequate for persistence diagrams, and how does FG address this?

- **Concept: Conditionally Negative Definite (CND) Kernels**
  - **Why needed**: To guarantee that k(μ,ν)=exp(−SFG_p^2/σ^2) is a positive definite kernel, we need SFG_p^2 to be CND.
  - **Quick check**: If f is CND, why does exp(−f/σ^2) define a kernel? What fails if p>2?

- **Concept: Slicing in Optimal Transport**
  - **Why needed**: Slicing reduces high-dimensional transport to 1D, enabling efficient computation and CND proofs. Comparing with SWK requires understanding its slicing (onto lines) vs. SFG’s (onto geodesics through the diagonal).
  - **Quick check**: What is the Sliced Wasserstein distance? Why does it project onto random lines, and how does SFG modify this?

## Architecture Onboarding

- **Component map**: Input persistence diagrams μ, ν → Preprocessing: compute weights 1/d(x,∂Ω) → Extract sorted event list E → Core loop: for each interval between consecutive events in E, update current sets X_t, Y_t in strip Ω_t → 1D FG subroutine: project renormalized measures onto geodesic G_t, compute quantile-based L^p distance → Accumulate integral of α^p over intervals → Output SFG_p(μ,ν) → Kernel: exp(−SFG_p^p/σ^2).

- **Critical path**:
  1. Sort all birth/death coordinates into event list E (O((N1+N2) log(N1+N2))).
  2. For each event interval, maintain dynamic sets X_t, Y_t (insertions/removals at events).
  3. For each interval, compute 1D FG between projected measures on G_t (dominates cost; naive O((|X_t|+|Y_t|)^2), optimized with sorting O((|X_t|+|Y_t|) log(|X_t|+|Y_t|))).
  4. Integrate α^p over intervals (exact) or average over sampled t (approximate).

- **Design tradeoffs**:
  - Exact vs. approximate: Exact integrates over all event intervals (O((N1+N2)×max(N1,N2))); approximate samples K geodesics (O(K·(N1+N2) log(N1+N2))). Trade exactness for speed.
  - Parameter p: p=1 yields Laplace-like kernel; p=2 yields Gaussian-like. p∈[1,2] ensures CND; higher p may improve discrimination but breaks CND.
  - Sampling strategy: Uniform sampling converges faster than KDE; must use fixed t-samples across all diagram pairs to preserve PSD of Gram matrices.
  - Projection choice: Orthogonal projection (Equation 16) is simple but discontinuous; continuous projection (Appendix A) may offer smoother gradients but no proven theoretical gain.

- **Failure signatures**:
  - Numerical instability: Points very close to diagonal cause large weights 1/d(x,∂Ω), leading to overflow. Mitigate by capping weights or using log-space.
  - Non-PSD kernel matrix: Using per-pair random t-samples violates CND, causing SVM convergence issues. Always pre-sample fixed t-values for all computations.
  - Infinite values: Diagrams with infinite persistence (Pers_p(μ)=∞) may cause divergence. Ensure inputs satisfy finite p-persistence.
  - Performance bottleneck: 1D FG subroutine is cubic in naive implementation. Optimize by sorting once and updating quantiles incrementally as points enter/leave Ω_t.

- **First 3 experiments**:
  1. **Unit test: SFG vs FG on aligned diagrams.** Input identical diagrams μ=ν; verify SFG_p(μ,ν)=0 and SFG_p(μ,∅)=FG_p(μ,∅)=Pers_p(μ).
  2. **Projection invariance test.** Input diagram μ and its isometric transformation (rotation/reflection preserving Ω). Test numerical invariance of SFG.
  3. **Kernel reproducibility: Orbit classification.** Replicate Table 1: generate linked twist map orbits (r∈{2.5,3.5,4,4.1,4.3}), compute 1D PDs, train SVM with SFG kernel (p=1, K=20). Target accuracy ~80%.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- Can stability constants between SFG and FG be tightened under realistic assumptions on persistence diagrams? The current bounds involve Pers_∞ and are non-uniform; refined assumptions might yield uniform constants.
- Does the topological equivalence of SFG and FG extend from persistence diagrams to general persistence measures? The proof for diagrams relies on finiteness of points in compacts; extension to measures requires new techniques.
- Can a sliced Figalli–Gigli distance be defined for domains beyond the open half-plane? The current construction uses specific geodesics from the diagonal; for arbitrary smooth domains, geodesics may be complex.
- What are the properties and benefits of a max-sliced variant of SFG? Using a maximum instead of integration could improve discriminability but might break c.n.d. or stability.

## Limitations
- Stability bound includes a Pers_∞ term, making it non-uniform and potentially loose for high-persistence diagrams.
- Extension to infinite persistence measures is theoretical only; no experiments validate it on continuous filtrations.
- Empirical evaluation uses two datasets and does not compare against a broader range of TDA kernels or larger-scale problems.
- Approximate kernel requires fixed t-samples across all pairs to maintain PSD, limiting flexibility in some kernel methods.

## Confidence
- **SFG is a metric on persistence measures**: High (proved in Theorem 1 under stated conditions).
- **SFG^p is conditionally negative definite for p∈[1,2]**: Medium (relies on 1D L^p CND; subtle for p=2 due to subtlety in integral representation).
- **Stability bound SFG ≤ C_p·FG + persistence terms**: Medium (non-uniform constant; examples show no uniform C_p for p>1).
- **Empirical parity with SWK on benchmarks**: Medium (results on two standard datasets; not exhaustive).

## Next Checks

2. **Bound sensitivity**: Generate diagram pairs with scaled persistence (multiply all coordinates by λ>1). Plot ratio SFG/FG versus Pers_∞ to assess empirical tightness of the stability constant and estimate C_p.
3. **Infinite-measure benchmark**: Use a continuous filtration (e.g., sublevel sets of a smooth function on a manifold) to produce a persistence measure with infinitely many points. Apply SFGK and SWK; verify kernel definiteness (eigenvalues ≥ 0) and test classification utility on a synthetic task.
