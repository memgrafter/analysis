# Stop Preaching and Start Practising Data Frugality for Responsible Development of AI

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2602.19789
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.19789
- **Reference Count:** 40
- **Primary Result:** Pruning 25–35% of ImageNet-1K using state-of-the-art coresets reduces training time by up to 40% and energy by up to 33% with negligible accuracy loss.

## Executive Summary
This paper confronts the persistent gap between advocating for data frugality in AI and its actual practice, which leads to substantial, often unaccounted environmental costs. The authors demonstrate that applying coreset-based subset selection to large datasets like ImageNet-1K can significantly reduce the computational energy required for model training while maintaining performance. Empirical results show that pruning a quarter to a third of ImageNet-1K yields energy savings of up to one-third. Furthermore, they show coresets can be used to mitigate representational bias by rebalancing sample groups. To close the "preach-practice gap," the paper argues for institutional changes: mandating resource reporting (energy, dataset size) and redesigning benchmarks to prioritize efficiency alongside accuracy.

## Method Summary
The core method involves using a coreset selection algorithm to identify a small, representative subset of a large training dataset (e.g., ImageNet-1K). This subset is then used to train a model (e.g., ResNet-50, Swin-T) instead of the full dataset. The experimental procedure measures the trade-off between accuracy and resource consumption (training time, energy in kWh). Specific coreset methods evaluated include Dyn-Unc and InfoMax. The paper also adapts coreset objectives for bias mitigation by forcing equal or proportional representation of predefined groups (e.g., in a synthetic Colour-MNIST dataset). The overarching proposal is to make reporting on these resource metrics mandatory for publication and to incorporate them into benchmark leaderboards.

## Key Results
- Downstream training on ImageNet-1K (2017–2025) is estimated to have consumed ~5.46 GWh of energy (2,429 tCO₂e).
- Pruning 25–35% of ImageNet-1K with SOTA coresets reduced per-epoch training time by 24–40% and energy by 24–33% for ResNet-34/50 and Swin-T, with less than 1% Top-1 accuracy drop.
- On a biased Colour-MNIST dataset, a balanced coreset method significantly improved performance on minority-group ("conflicting") samples compared to random sampling from the biased full set.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1
*Claim:* Pruning a dataset with a coreset method can reduce the energy consumption of model training with minimal accuracy loss.
*Mechanism:* A coreset selects a small, representative subset. Training on this subset requires fewer gradient updates and less data loading per epoch, directly lowering active GPU/CPU time and electricity draw.
*Core Assumption:* The selected subset preserves the essential learning signal (e.g., gradient dynamics) for the target model and task.
*Evidence Anchors:* Abstract states coresets reduce training energy with little accuracy loss. Section 4.2 reports 24–40% time reduction and 24–33% energy reduction for 25% pruning. Neighboring work on "Frugal Machine Learning" aligns with the resource-aware goal.
*Break Condition:* The computational overhead of constructing the coreset (e.g., computing scores for all samples) exceeds the total training energy saved over all subsequent reuses.

### Mechanism 2
*Claim:* Coreset selection can mitigate representational bias by rebalancing group distributions within the training subset.
*Mechanism:* The coreset objective is modified to enforce equal or proportional representation of predefined groups (e.g., demographic attributes). This forces the model to learn from a balanced distribution, reducing reliance on spurious correlations tied to the majority group.
*Core Assumption:* The primary dataset bias is correctable by adjusting sample counts at the data level, and task labels remain valid for the rebalanced subset.
*Evidence Anchors:* Abstract mentions bias mitigation via rebalancing. Section 4.3 experiments on Colour-MNIST show balanced sampling improves minority-group accuracy under strong bias. Neighboring bias literature discusses dataset harms but not coreset remediation.
*Break Condition:* The bias source is not primarily sample imbalance (e.g., label bias), or removing majority samples discards critical task-relevant variation.

### Mechanism 3
*Claim:* Institutionalizing data frugality via mandatory reporting and resource-aware benchmarking can close the "preach-practice gap".
*Mechanism:* Making resource metrics (kWh, dataset size) a required part of paper submission and benchmark leaderboards reframes "progress" to include efficiency. This makes environmental costs visible and rewards data-efficient submissions.
*Core Assumption:* The ML research community's evaluation norms can be shifted by conference/journal policies and platform design.
*Evidence Anchors:* Abstract outlines recommendations for mandatory reporting. Section 6 proposes actions for platforms (e.g., CVPR compute reporting) and policies. Neighboring work by McCoy et al. (2025) advocates for "capability-per-resource" metrics.
*Break Condition:* Reporting is gamed without auditing, or the community disregards the new metrics, maintaining performance-only leaderboards.

## Foundational Learning
- **Concept: Coreset Selection**
  *Why needed:* The paper's central technical proposal. A coreset is a weighted subset designed to approximate the full dataset's learning objective, enabling data reduction without catastrophic forgetting.
  *Quick Check:* What is the difference between a heuristic data pruning method and a coreset with approximation guarantees?

- **Concept: Data Lifecycle & Embodied/Operational Impacts**
  *Why needed:* To move beyond focusing only on model training energy. The paper frames data as having its own lifecycle with costs (creation, storage, distribution) that data frugality aims to reduce.
  *Quick Check:* Give one example of an embodied impact and an operational impact related to a dataset like ImageNet-1K.

- **Concept: Performance-per-Resource Metric (Data-Pareto)**
  *Why needed:* The core normative argument. Shifting from a single metric (accuracy) to a multi-objective frontier (accuracy vs. dataset size vs. energy) changes optimization targets.
  *Quick Check:* Why does reporting only final accuracy on a fixed dataset hide the resource cost of achieving that accuracy?

## Architecture Onboarding
Component Map:
Coreset Selector Module -> Training Pipeline (with subset) -> Energy & Carbon Tracker -> Evaluation Harness
(Optional: Bias Audit Module attached to Coreset Selector for rebalancing)

Critical Path:
1. Define experiment (dataset, model, coreset method, prune ratio)
2. Run coreset selection to generate training indices
3. Train model on coreset while tracking energy per epoch
4. Evaluate model on full validation set
5. Analyse accuracy vs. energy/dataset size trade-off
6. For bias: repeat with balanced coreset objective and compare group metrics.

Design Tradeoffs:
- **Coreset Construction Cost:** Gradient-based methods have high upfront compute, amortized over many training runs. Heuristic methods (random, forgetting) are cheaper for one-off experiments but less optimal.
- **Bias Correction vs. General Performance:** A coreset optimised for group balance may under-represent rare task-relevant modes, potentially harming overall accuracy.
- **Architecture Sensitivity:** The same coreset method (e.g., Dyn-Unc) can perform differently on different architectures (Swin-T vs. ResNet), suggesting selectors may need to be model-aware.

Failure Signatures:
1. **No Energy Savings:** Observed reduction is much less than the prune ratio (e.g., 25% prune -> <20% save). Likely cause: I/O bottleneck or high coreset overhead.
2. **Accuracy Drop:** Model performance degrades substantially (>1-2%) vs. full-data training. Indicates coreset failed to preserve learning signal.
3. **Bias Amplification:** After rebalancing, minority-group performance does not improve or worsens. May indicate bias not solvable by simple sampling.

First 3 Experiments:
1. **Replicate Pruning Gain:** Train ResNet-50 on full ImageNet-1K and a 25% random subset for 10 epochs. Measure time/kWh per epoch. Expect ~30% reduction.
2. **Measure Coreset Overhead:** Run Dyn-Unc on ImageNet-1K for ResNet-50. Record total time/kWh for selection. Compare one-time cost to cumulative savings over 300 epochs of training on the 25% subset.
3. **Test Bias Mitigation:** Replicate Colour-MNIST experiment with 0.99 bias strength. Compare balanced vs. random sampling for a small data budget (e.g., 500 samples). Measure conflicting accuracy; expect balanced to gain.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
1. **Net environmental impact with coreset overhead:** What is the total energy cost when including the one-time computational overhead of coreset construction? The paper acknowledges this upfront cost must be amortized but does not quantify the break-even point for typical reuse scenarios. Evidence needed: A lifecycle assessment comparing (coreset construction + repeated training on coreset) vs. (repeated training on full data) across varying numbers of runs.
2. **Scalability to non-i.i.d. modalities:** How do coreset methods perform on tokenized text for LLMs or multimodal streams where preserving rare modes/temporal dependencies is critical? The paper's experiments are limited to image classification. Evidence needed: Empirical studies applying coresets/condensation to LLM pretraining datasets (e.g., The Pile), measuring performance retention (perplexity) and rare skill preservation.
3. **Rebound effects:** Do efficiency gains from data frugality lead to net systemic reductions in AI's footprint, or do they trigger increased experimentation and scale? The paper cites rebound effect literature but does not investigate it. Evidence needed: Longitudinal/meta-analytic studies tracking aggregate compute in subfields before/after adopting data-efficient methods, controlling for model scaling.
4. **Standardized reporting protocols:** What low-overhead metrics and protocols can make a dataset's full lifecycle environmental cost visible and comparable? The paper advocates mandatory reporting but lacks a concrete, minimal schema. Evidence needed: A community-backed proposal (e.g., from a major conference) defining core required metrics (embodied energy, training energy, storage energy, reuse factor) with standardized templates.
5. **Unintended fairness consequences:** Can bias mitigation via rebalancing coresets introduce new unfairness or harm subgroups not explicitly targeted? The paper's positive result is on a single synthetic bias. Evidence needed: Systematic studies on real-world datasets with multiple sensitive attributes, evaluating fairness metrics across *all* subgroups after rebalancing for one specific proxy.

## Limitations
- **Scaling Uncertainty:** The reported energy gains are from controlled, single-architecture training runs. Effectiveness in multi-stage pipelines (pre-training → fine-tuning) where data is reused is not quantified.
- **Hardware & Measurement Consistency:** Reported savings depend on specific hardware (DGX A100) and tools (Carbontracker). Generalizability to other setups (consumer GPUs, cloud instances) is unclear; location-specific carbon intensity is anonymized.
- **Bias Mitigation Generalizability:** Positive bias mitigation results are from a synthetic Colour-MNIST setting. Transfer to natural, entangled biases in large datasets like ImageNet is unproven and may involve significant accuracy trade-offs.

## Confidence
- **Pruning Reduces Training Energy:** High. Direct empirical evidence from Section 4.2 with specific percentages and models. Clear break condition identified (overhead vs. savings).
- **Coresets Mitigate Representational Bias:** Medium. Evidence is limited to a controlled, synthetic bias scenario (Section 4.3). Application to real-world, multi-attribute biases is an extrapolation.
- **Institutional Changes Close the Gap:** Medium. This is a normative, systemic claim supported by logical argument and analogous proposals (corpus), but it lacks direct empirical validation within the paper.

## Next Checks
1. **Replicate Core Pruning Gain:** Train ResNet-50 on full ImageNet-1K vs. a 25% random subset for 10 epochs. Measure per-epoch time and energy. Verify a ~25-30% reduction to establish the baseline frugality signal.
2. **Measure Coreset Overhead in Practice:** Implement a gradient-based coreset (e.g., Dyn-Unc). Quantify kWh for the selection phase on ImageNet-1K. Compare this one-time cost to cumulative kWh saved over 300 epochs of training on the selected subset to find the break-even point.
3. **Test Bias Mitigation on a Real Dataset:** Apply a balanced coreset objective to a biased dataset with known group labels (e.g., a FairFace subset).
