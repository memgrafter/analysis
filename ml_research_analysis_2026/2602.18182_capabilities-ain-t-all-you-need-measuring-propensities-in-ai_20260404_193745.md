# Capabilities Ain't All You Need: Measuring Propensities in AI

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2602.18182
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.18182
- Reference count: 40
- Primary result: Introduces a bilogistic IRT framework to measure AI propensities independently, showing predictive power for held-out tasks and AUROC gains when combined with capabilities.

## Executive Summary
This paper argues that evaluating AI systems requires more than capability metrics; it introduces a bilogistic item response theory (IRT) framework to measure latent propensities. Applying the framework to six large language model families across four propensity dimensions, the authors demonstrate that (1) measured propensities predict performance on held-out tasks, (2) combining propensities with capabilities improves AUROC by 0.02–0.03 over capabilities alone, and (3) systematic differences in propensities exist across models. The findings suggest propensities offer an independent, measurable contribution to AI evaluation.

## Method Summary
The authors develop a bilogistic IRT model to quantify AI propensities. They collect response data from six LLM families on a task suite, fit the IRT model to estimate model-specific propensity parameters ("ideal bands"), and validate these propensities by predicting held-out task performance. Additionally, they test whether adding propensity scores to capability-based classifiers enhances discrimination (measured via AUROC). The method provides a formal, quantitative approach to separate propensity from capability.

## Key Results
- Measured propensities predict held-out task performance.
- Combining propensities with capabilities improves AUROC by 0.02–0.03 over capabilities alone.
- The analysis reveals systematic differences in propensities across the six LLM families studied.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The bilogistic IRT framework works because it models the probability of a correct response as a function of both task-specific parameters (difficulty, discrimination) and a latent model-specific propensity parameter. By fitting across many models and tasks, the framework isolates the propensity dimension that is independent of overall capability. This separation enables measuring how a model's inherent tendencies (e.g., safety, reasoning style) differ from its sheer performance ability, thus capturing an orthogonal evaluation axis. The resulting propensity estimates can then be used to predict novel tasks or augment capability classifiers.

## Architecture Onboarding
The measurement pipeline consists of: Data Collection (gather model outputs on diverse tasks) → IRT Fitting (estimate model propensity parameters using bilogistic IRT) → Propensity Scoring (assign propensity scores to models) → Evaluation (predict held-out tasks and combine with capabilities for classification).  

**Critical Path:** Data collection must precede model fitting; fitted parameters are required for scoring; scoring enables evaluation.  

**Design Tradeoffs:** Selecting tasks involves a trade-off between diversity (improves generalizability) and noise (harms measurement precision). The bilogistic model adds complexity over simpler IRT variants but captures both discrimination and difficulty, improving fit at the cost of interpretability. Use of proprietary model outputs hinders reproducibility.  

**Failure Signatures:** Poor IRT model fit (e.g., high residual deviance), non-identifiable parameters (e.g., all models assigned similar propensities), or negligible AUROC improvement when adding propensities.  

**First Experiments:**  
1. Validate IRT parameter recovery on synthetic data where true propensities are known.  
2. Run the full pipeline on a minimal subset (e.g., 2 models, 20 tasks) to assess stability of propensity estimates across random seeds.  
3. Perform an ablation test: remove propensity features from the combined classifier and measure the resulting AUROC drop.

## Limitations
- The bilogistic IRT framework is underspecified and relies on proprietary data, hindering replication and broad application.
- The statistical significance and practical importance of the modest AUROC gains (0.02–0.03) are not established.
- The study's scope is limited to six LLM families; generalizability to other architectures (e.g., encoder-decoder, multimodal models) remains unknown.

## Confidence
- Propensities predict held-out performance: **Medium**
- Combining propensities with capabilities improves AUROC: **Low–Medium**
- Reveals systematic model differences: **Medium**
