# LeakBoost: Perceptual-Loss-Based Membership Inference Attack

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2602.05748
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.05748
- **Reference count:** 34
- **Primary result:** Boosts membership inference attacks by synthesizing interrogation images via perceptual-loss optimization, improving AUC from ~0.53–0.62 to 0.81–0.88 and TPR@1%FPR from ≤1.3% to 11.8–20.15% on ViT-4 and AlexNet, especially with gradient-based detectors like GLiR.

## Executive Summary
LeakBoost introduces a novel interrogation technique to enhance white-box membership inference attacks (MIAs). Unlike static-output methods, it actively probes a target model's internal representations by synthesizing an “interrogation image” that maximizes perceptual loss (activation-space distance) relative to a query image. This amplified image is then fed to existing MIA detectors, most notably gradient-based ones like GLiR. The method yields dramatic improvements on ViT-4 and AlexNet across CIFAR-10 and CIFAR-100, raising AUC from ~0.53–0.62 to 0.81–0.88 and TPR@1%FPR from ≤1.3% to 11.8%–20.15%. Gains are highly sensitive to hyperparameters (short, low-learning-rate optimization, deeper layers) and detector type, revealing architectural differences in privacy leakage.

## Method Summary
LeakBoost acts as a wrapper around existing white-box MIA detectors. Given a target model and a query image, it first records the model's intermediate activations for that image. It then initializes a random noise image (within valid pixel range) and performs gradient-based optimization (Adam) to minimize the sum of MSE losses between the synthetic image's activations and the query's activations across a selected set of layers (perceptual loss), clipping after each step to the training data range. The resulting “interrogation image” is submitted to the detector (e.g., GLiR) to compute a membership score. Success hinges on architecture-specific hyperparameters: optimization steps (typically 80–120), learning rate (low, ~0.05), and layer groups (mid/late for ViT/AlexNet), tuned via validation.

## Key Results
- AUC improves from 0.53–0.62 to 0.81–0.88; TPR@1%FPR increases from ≤1.3% to 11.8%–20.15% on ViT-4 and AlexNet.
- Gains concentrate on gradient-based detectors (e.g., GLiR); minimal benefit for activation-based or robustness-based detectors.
- Performance is highly sensitive to hyperparameters: short runs (80–120 steps) with low learning rates (0.05) and deeper layers yield best results.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1: Amplifying Representational Differences via Perceptual Loss
Optimizing an input to match a query's internal activations via perceptual loss pushes the synthetic image toward regions of the input space where the model's responses diverge most clearly based on membership status. This “interrogation” treats the target model as a fixed feature extractor, leveraging the assumption that membership induces detectable, consistent differences in activation patterns. By minimizing activation-space distance (weighted MSE over selected layers), the synthetic image accentuates these discrepancies, making them more salient to downstream detectors. Evidence includes the optimization loop in Section 5.2 and prior noise-probe work.

### Mechanism 2: Synergy with Gradient-Based Detectors
The interrogation directly manipulates the local gradient landscape, strongly benefiting gradient-based detectors like GLiR. GLiR’s membership statistic is a log-likelihood ratio on gradient vectors. Short, low-learning-rate optimization alters the input to amplify separability of gradient distributions between members and non-members. This synergy arises because interrogation transiently modifies model sensitivity—a signal GLiR explicitly models. Detectors relying on static signals (e.g., final loss) or non-gradient features see minimal gains.

### Mechanism 3: Architectural Dependence of Leakage Amplification
Improvement magnitude varies across architectures due to differences in representational memorization and optimization dynamics. Deeper layers in ViT-4 and AlexNet exhibit stronger, more separable memorization, so interrogating these layers optimally amplifies the signal. Deeper CNNs (ResNet-18, DenseNet) show minor gains, possibly due to distributed memorization or destructive interference with detector assumptions. Sensitivity analyses confirm that the optimal interrogation regime is architecture-specific.

## Architecture Onboarding
### Component Map
Target Model (Tθ) -> Interrogation Module -> Detector Module -> Score -> Threshold  
Supporting: Configuration Manager (stores hyperparameters).

### Critical Path
1. Receive query image x.  
2. Target model forward pass: store intermediate activations hℓ for chosen layers.  
3. Interrogation: Initialize random noise x_g within valid range; run Adam optimization for T steps to minimize perceptual loss Σ λℓ MSE(eℓ(x_g), hℓ), clipping after each step.  
4. Submit x_g to existing white-box MIA detector (e.g., GLiR).  
5. Detector computes membership score s(x).  
6. Apply threshold γ (determined at desired FPR) to decide membership.

### Design Tradeoffs
- **Detector Choice:** Use GLiR for strongest, most consistent gains; SIF/LAEQ/IA yield minimal benefit.
- **Layer Selection:** For ViT/AlexNet, target “Mid” or “Late” layers; for ResNet/DenseNet gains are small even with “Late” layers. “All” layers often dilutes signal.
- **Optimization Regime:** Short runs (80–120 steps) with low learning rate (0.05) are optimal; longer/higher-LR runs degrade performance (ViT) or cause instability (AlexNet).
- **Clipping:** Enabling clip-to-training-range is recommended but impact is marginal.

### Failure Signatures
- **AUC/TPR drop vs. baseline:** Likely causes: too many optimization steps, wrong layer group, or using a non-gradient-based detector.
- **Extreme variance in TPR@1%FPR across runs:** Indicates high hyperparameter sensitivity; requires careful validation tuning (common for AlexNet-CIFAR-100).
- **No improvement on deep CNNs:** Expected for ResNet/DenseNet; does not indicate implementation error.

### First 3 Experiments
1. Replicate baseline GLiR on ViT-4 CIFAR-10 to establish TPR@1%FPR ~1% (AUC ~0.53).
2. Apply LeakBoost interrogation with best ViT config (Late layers, 80 steps, LR=0.05, clip=True) and verify TPR@1%FPR ~12% (AUC ~0.81).
3. Test hyperparameter sensitivity on AlexNet CIFAR-100: sweep steps (80,120,200) and LR (0.05,0.1) with Mid/Late layers; expect peak at 120 steps, LR=0.05 and high variance.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
1. **Generalization to non-image domains** (text, audio). Future work; requires adapting perceptual loss to other modalities.
2. **Impact of defenses** (differential privacy, adversarial training). Only a simple activation-obfuscation defense was tested; DP and adversarial training remain unexplored.
3. **Detector properties driving responsiveness** to LeakBoost. GLiR benefits greatly while others do not; unclear whether gradient dependence or other factors cause this.
4. **Architectural reasons for success variance** (ViT/AlexNet vs. ResNet/DenseNet). No ablation on specific components (e.g., residuals, attention) to explain.

## Limitations
- **Implementation Gaps:** Exact layer definitions, GLiR gradient estimation details, and clipping bound computation are unspecified, hindering exact replication.
- **Detector-Agnosticity Overstated:** Gains are strongly concentrated on gradient-based detectors; limited applicability to other white-box signals.
- **Architectural Dependency:** Performance varies widely across architectures (strong on ViT/AlexNet, weak on ResNet/DenseNet), restricting predictive power for new models.

## Confidence
- **Core Performance Boost** (AUC/TPR gains on ViT-4, AlexNet): High  
- **Gradient-Detector Synergy Mechanism:** High  
- **Architectural & Hyperparameter Sensitivity:** High  
- **Detector-Agnosticity / Broad Applicability:** Low  

## Next Checks
1. **Layer selection replication:** For ViT-4 CIFAR-10, independently sweep layer groups (Early/Mid/Late/All) with fixed optimal hyperparameters (80 steps, LR=0.05) to confirm the “Late layers” peak and quantify the performance drop with “All” layers.
2. **Optimization sensitivity verification:** On AlexNet-CIFAR-100, perform a fine-grained sweep over steps (40, 80, 120, 200) and learning rates (0.01, 0.05, 0.1) to reproduce the reported high variance and identify the precise optimal regime (likely 120 steps, LR=0.05).
3. **Detector specificity test:** Apply the best LeakBoost configuration (ViT-4, Late layers) to non-gradient detectors (SIF, LAEQ) and compare the TPR@1%FPR gain against the GLiR baseline. Expect negligible improvement for non-gradient detectors.
