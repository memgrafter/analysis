# MJ1: Multimodal Judgment via Grounded Verification

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2603.07990
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.07990
- Reference count: 40
- Primary result: MJ1 achieves 77.0% accuracy on MMRB2, surpassing all API and open-source models; a simple structured prompt alone yields gains of +3.8 (Image Editing) and +1.7 (Multimodal Reasoning) on the same benchmark.

## Executive Summary
Multimodal judges (vision-language models) often struggle to ground their decisions in visual evidence, leading to position biases and inaccuracies. MJ1 addresses this by introducing a grounded verification chain: a structured reasoning process that breaks judgment into five stages—Observations, Claims, Verification, Evaluation, and Scoring. Additionally, a counterfactual consistency reward penalizes the model if its verdict does not invert when the two responses are swapped, directly reducing position bias. Even without training, the structured prompt improves the base model’s accuracy on MMRB2 noticeably. After a two‑phase training pipeline (cold‑start SFT followed by GRPO with composite rewards) on 70K samples, MJ1 (using Qwen3‑VL‑30B‑A3B, 3B active parameters) reaches 77.0% accuracy on MMRB2, outperforming all API and open‑source competitors including Gemini‑3‑Pro. The consistency reward also reduces positional bias from a 2:1 preference ratio to near parity.

## Method Summary
MJ1 builds on the Qwen3‑VL‑30B‑A3B model. At inference time, it employs a structured prompt (Fig. 7) that forces the model to output a five‑stage reasoning chain: first observe visual content, then make comparative claims about both responses, verify each claim against the images, evaluate based on defined criteria, and finally assign scores. No training is required for this step, yet it already enhances grounding and accuracy.

Training proceeds in two phases. First, a cold‑start supervised fine‑tuning (SFT) stage trains the model to produce the full chain. The authors mention “10k distilled reasoning traces” but also refer to a 70K sample mix from Rapidata Coherence, EditReward, and RLAIF‑V; the exact distillation process is unspecified. SFT runs for 5 epochs with learning rate 5e‑5 and batch size 16. Second, reinforcement learning with Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO) is applied. For each prompt, the model generates 32 responses; another 32 are produced with the responses flipped (R_A and R_B swapped). A composite reward is computed per generated output: R_format (XML tag compliance), R_correct (preference accuracy against the ground truth), and R_cons (counterfactual consistency: 1 if the verdict correctly inverts upon flip, else 0). The flip requires swapping all references to R_A and R_B throughout the chain; the model then regenerates only the Evaluation and Scoring sections from the point after Verification. GRPO uses group‑wise advantages, LoRA adapters (rank 64), and a cosine learning rate decay from 5e‑5 to 1e‑7 for one epoch with a maximum context of 6144 tokens.

## Key Results
- Structured prompting (without training) improves base accuracy on MMRB2 by +3.8 points on Image Editing and +1.7 points on Multimodal Reasoning.
- After full training, MJ1 (3B active parameters) achieves 77.0% accuracy on MMRB2, surpassing all API and open‑source models, including Gemini‑3‑Pro.
- The counterfactual consistency reward reduces positional bias from a 2:1 preference ratio to near parity.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
MJ1’s structured chain enforces visual grounding by requiring the model to explicitly observe the images, formulate claims tied to visual details, and verify those claims before passing judgment. This stepwise decomposition prevents the model from relying solely on textual cues or superficial patterns. The counterfactual consistency reward directly attacks position bias: when the order of the two responses is flipped, the correct verdict should invert. By rewarding such consistency, the model learns to base its decision on content rather than on the responses’ positions. Together, these mechanisms encourage the model to engage in genuinely multimodal reasoning and yield more robust, accurate judgments.

## Foundational Learning
- **Multimodal Judgment**: The task of selecting the better between two AI‑generated outputs (text + images) given a user prompt.
  - Why needed: MJ1 targets this task, which is benchmarked by MMRB2.
  - Quick check: Can a human decide which of two image‑text responses better fulfills a prompt?
- **Position Bias**: The tendency to favor a response because it appears first (or second) rather than because of its intrinsic quality.
  - Why needed: A core failure MJ1 addresses; many judges exhibit strong position bias.
  - Quick check: Swap the two responses; does the model’s choice flip?
- **Grounded Verification Chain**: A fixed reasoning template: Observations → Claims → Verification → Evaluation → Scoring.
  - Why needed: Forces the model to explicitly ground each claim in visual evidence, improving interpretability and accuracy.
  - Quick check: Does the model cite specific visual elements (e.g., object positions, colors) in its verification?
- **Counterfactual Consistency Reward**: An RL reward that gives a positive signal if, upon swapping the two responses, the verdict correctly inverts.
  - Why needed: Directly penalizes position‑dependent reasoning and encourages evidence‑based decisions.
  - Quick check: After flip, does the score assignment swap from (A > B) to (B > A)?
- **GRPO (Group Relative Policy Optimization)**: An RL algorithm that generates multiple outputs per prompt and computes relative advantages within the group.
  - Why needed: Enables stable and sample‑efficient training with sparse, composite rewards.
  - Quick check: Can the update rule compare several candidate responses and boost those with higher relative rewards?
- **LoRA (Low‑Rank Adaptation)**: A parameter‑efficient fine‑tuning method that updates low‑rank matrices on top of frozen pretrained weights.
  - Why needed: Allows fine‑tuning a 30B model with only 3B active parameters, reducing memory and compute.
  - Quick check: Does adding LoRA adapters with rank 64 yield comparable performance to full fine‑tuning in preliminary tests?

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component Map** (plain text):
Input (prompt p, R_A, R_B) → Structured Prompt (XML template with chain) → Qwen3‑VL‑30B‑A3B (with LoRA adapters) → Output chain (Observations, Claims, Verification, Evaluation, Scores). For training: 70K dataset → SFT (distilled chains) → GRPO (generation groups + flip) → Updated LoRA weights.

**Critical Path**: The inference path follows the five stages in strict order: first, the model describes observable image content; second, it makes comparative claims about both responses; third, it verifies each claim against the images; fourth, it evaluates based on criteria; fifth, it emits final boxed scores. Each stage depends on the previous.

**Design Tradeoffs**:
- XML format provides clear parsing boundaries but is brittle; any deviation yields zero R_format.
- Composite reward (format + correctness + consistency) must be balanced; over‑weighting R_cons might encourage superficial flips.
- LoRA reduces memory footprint but may limit the model’s capacity to learn complex multimodal interactions.
- Flip mechanism doubles generation cost per prompt but is essential for counterfactual signal.

**Failure Signatures**:
- Parsing errors: missing tags, malformed `\boxed{score_A, score_B}`.
- Lack of visual grounding: chain contains no image references; performance does not degrade on blank/shuffled images.
- Reward hacking: model learns to produce correct verdict without proper verification (e.g., generic statements that always invert).
- Overfitting: high training reward but poor generalization to MMRB2.

**First Three Experiments**:
1. Replicate Table 1: Run the base model with the MJ1 structured prompt on a small MMRB2 subset; confirm improvements of +3.8% (Editing) and +1.7% (Reasoning).
2. Manual flip audit: Inspect 10 flipped examples; verify that all references to R_A and R_B in Claims/Verification are swapped, that regeneration starts after the Verification block, and that final scores invert correctly; compute R_cons agreement.
3. Grounding stress test: Evaluate MJ1 on MMRB2 subsets with shuffled images and blank images; expect a substantial accuracy drop compared to the original images, confirming reliance on visual evidence.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
1. **Can MJ1 achieve high counterfactual consistency without genuine visual grounding (“consistency theater”)?**  
   The authors do not formally rule out that a model might generate internally coherent but image‑independent outputs that happen to flip correctly and thus earn high R_cons. If such shortcuts exist, MJ1’s claimed grounding advantage is unsupported. Resolution: test MJ1 on samples with deliberately mismatched images and fixed reasoning; high R_cons despite misalignment would indicate exploitation.

2. **Does the counterfactual consistency reward accelerate grounding only on image‑critical samples?**  
   The paper acknowledges that on purely text‑discriminable samples the flip reward provides no additional signal. Without stratification, the scope of R_cons’s benefit is unclear and may be limited to a subset of MMRB2. Resolution: annotate an MMRB2 subset for whether correct judgment requires visual evidence; measure marginal improvements in accuracy and R_cons when enabling the reward on each stratum.

3. **Does MJ1’s grounded verification chain transfer to multimodal judgment beyond static images?**  
   All evaluation is confined to MMRB2’s four image‑based subtasks (T2I, Editing, Interleaved, Reasoning); no experiments on video, 3D, or other modalities are reported. The claim of “substantial improvement” may not generalize. Resolution: replicate the SFT+GRPO pipeline on additional multimodal benchmarks (e.g., VideoRewardBench, 3D‑MVBench) and compare performance.

4. **Are MJ1’s gains due to the training recipe or specific to the Qwen3‑VL‑30B‑A3B architecture?**  
   The study uses a single MoE‑based VLM; no ablations with different base models (dense vs. MoE) or varying parameter counts are conducted. Without cross‑architecture validation, it is unclear if the observed “recipe > scale” conclusion holds broadly. Resolution: apply the identical SFT+GRPO pipeline to multiple open‑source VLMs (e.g., LLaVA‑NeXT, InternVL2, a dense variant of Qwen) and report MMRB2 accuracy and training dynamics.

## Limitations
- Construction of the “10k distilled reasoning traces” for SFT is unspecified (subset? synthetic? teacher model?), blocking exact reproduction.
- Flip mechanism details (truncation point, provided context for regenerating Evaluation/Scoring) are ambiguous and may affect R_cons computation.
- Results are limited to a single MoE‑based VLM (Qwen3‑VL‑30B‑A3B) and static image tasks; generalizability to other architectures, model sizes, or modalities (video, 3D) is unknown.
- Strict XML format can cause parsing failures if the model deviates, leading to zero reward and potential training instability.
- The counterfactual reward might be gamed without true grounding (“consistency theater”).
