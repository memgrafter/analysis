# From Topic to Transition Structure: Unsupervised Concept Discovery at Corpus Scale via Predictive Associative Memory

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2603.18420
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.18420
- Reference count: 8
- Primary result: Discovered recurrent cross-author transition-structure concepts in literature by clustering passages based on temporal co-occurrence, with high selectivity on unseen novels (e.g., Pride and Prejudice: 66.5% concentration in top 5 clusters).

## Executive Summary
This paper introduces Predictive Associative Memory (PAM), an unsupervised method for discovering functional narrative structures in large text corpora. Unlike traditional topic modeling that groups by semantic content, PAM uses temporal co-occurrence patterns within books to identify passages that play similar roles in local narrative sequences. By training a contrastive model on pairs of passages that appear near each other, and imposing a capacity constraint, the method compresses specific co-occurrences into generalizable transition-structure concepts that generalize across authors and genres. Evaluation on 9,766 Project Gutenberg texts shows that clusters in the resulting association space group by narrative function, register, and literary tradition, and transfer effectively to unseen novels, demonstrating a new way to extract structural regularities from sequential data.

## Method Summary
The pipeline begins by chunking each book into 50-token passages with 15-token overlap, then embedding them using a pre-trained BGE model. Co-occurrence pairs are generated for passages within ±15 chunks in the same book. A 4-layer MLP (29.4M parameters) is trained via symmetric contrastive loss (InfoNCE) to map these embeddings into an association space where temporally linked passages are closer. The model is trained for 150 epochs with AdamW and cosine LR, targeting a top-1 batch accuracy of ~42.75%—a regime where capacity is insufficient to memorize all pairs, forcing compression into recurring patterns. Finally, k-means clustering (k=50–2000) is applied to the association-space embeddings, and clusters are filtered for book diversity and low positional bias.

## Key Results
- At k=100, clusters average 4,508 books (46% of the corpus), indicating high cross-book coverage.
- Association-space clusters coherently group passages by narrative function (e.g., confrontation, investigation) and literary tradition, whereas BGE clusters remain topical.
- Unseen novels show high selectivity: Pride and Prejudice activates 80/100 clusters with 66.5% of its passages concentrated in the top 5, demonstrating strong transfer.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1
- **Claim:** Temporal co-occurrence within texts discovers recurrent transition-structure concepts; passages with similar transition structure cluster together.
- **Mechanism:** Passages that reliably co-occur within a local window share a "relational signature" in transition space. A contrastive model trained to cluster these pairs learns to map passages with similar local narrative neighborhoods together, regardless of surface vocabulary.
- **Core assumption:** Recurrent narrative/functional patterns (e.g., "confrontation," "investigation") manifest as stable co-occurrence relationships across thousands of independent texts, providing a signal that transcends topic.
- **Evidence anchors:** [abstract] "temporal co-occurrence within texts discovers... recurrent transition-structure concepts... passages with similar transition structure cluster together." [section 1] "passages that reliably co-occur within a local window share transition structure... even when their vocabulary, genre, and century differ entirely." [corpus] 373M co-occurrence pairs from 9,766 texts provide sufficient independent sequences for pattern recurrence.
- **Break condition:** If the corpus lacks sufficient recurrent structural patterns, the co-occurrence signal may not converge to meaningful concepts.

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim:** Severe capacity constraint (42.75% training accuracy) forces the model to compress specific co-occurrences into generalizable structural patterns rather than memorizing individual pairs.
- **Mechanism:** The 29.4M-parameter MLP cannot memorize 373M unique pairs. Multi-epoch contrastive training under this bottleneck acts as a consolidation mechanism, extracting regularities that recur across many books. The model learns a transformation that maps embeddings into a space where structurally similar passages (across authors) are closer.
- **Core assumption:** Compression pressure differentiates this from PAM (episodic recall) and AAR (corpus-specific retrieval). Lower accuracy reflects successful generalization beyond specific instances.
- **Evidence anchors:** [section 3.5] "Under severe capacity constraint... specific associations get compressed into structural patterns... multi-epoch contrastive training under bottleneck provides consolidation." [section 6.2] "PAM memorises... AAR reaches ∼97%... present work reaches 42.75%, which forces something different: pattern extraction." [corpus] The same architecture at 2K scale reached 51.0% accuracy and produced more author-tradition clusters, while 10K scale at lower accuracy produced broader cross-author concepts—consistent with compression shifting behavior.
- **Break condition:** If capacity is too high, memorization may dominate; if too low, useful patterns may not emerge. The 42.75% point is a single observed regime, not a validated optimum.

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim:** The resulting concepts transfer inductively to unseen novels because they capture recurrent, domain-wide structural regularities, not contingent co-occurrences.
- **Mechanism:** A narrative function like "solitary journey with introspection" appears thousands of times across the training corpus. The model encodes this as a mode in transition space. An unseen novel containing such a passage will map near this mode's centroid, even with different vocabulary. This contrasts with AAR's failure, where associations are specific to a particular corpus's contingent links.
- **Core assumption:** Literary narrative structure possesses sufficiently conserved functional patterns that recurrence outweighs contingency.
- **Evidence anchors:** [abstract] "unseen novels exhibit high selectivity... Pride and Prejudice activates 80 of 100 clusters with 66.5% concentration... association-space clusters group by function, register, and literary tradition." [section 5.4] PAM concentrates unseen novels into selective, coherent subsets while BGE saturates clusters. Assignments track structural role across vocabulary shifts. [corpus] The five unseen novels are canonical works from different traditions; their clean assignment to pre-computed clusters without retraining indicates the concepts are corpus-wide, not training-text-specific.
- **Break condition:** In domains without recurrent structural patterns, transfer may fail. Also, if unseen texts are stylistically alien to the training corpus, concept matching may degrade.

## Foundational Learning
- **Concept: Contrastive learning with in-batch negatives**
  - **Why needed here:** The method trains the MLP via symmetric InfoNCE loss, where positives are temporal co-occurrence pairs and negatives are all other passages in the batch. This shapes the association space so that transitionally linked passages are closer than unrelated ones.
  - **Quick check question:** How does using in-batch negatives (where each batch contains many unrelated passages) differ from a sampled negative strategy in forcing the model to learn general transition structure rather than batch artifacts?
- **Concept: Capacity constraint & generalization**
  - **Why needed here:** The 29.4M-parameter model must compress 373M training pairs. The gap between model capacity and data size is hypothesized to force extraction of recurrent patterns rather than memorization. Training accuracy (42.75%) is an empirical indicator of this compression regime.
  - **Quick check question:** If we doubled the model size and achieved 80% training accuracy on the same data, would we expect the clusters to become more topical, more author-specific, or both? Why?
- **Concept: Temporal co-occurrence as a structural signal**
  - **Why needed here:** Unlike semantic similarity, temporal proximity within a text links passages that play similar roles in the local narrative sequence, even if semantically dissimilar.
  - **Quick check question:** Why does a 15-chunk window (~525 tokens) capture "local narrative neighborhood" effectively? What might happen if the window were 2 chunks or 100 chunks?

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component map:** Chunker → Embedder → Association model → Pair generator → Trainer → Clusterer

**Critical path:**
1. Corpus ingestion → chunking → embed all passages (precomputed BGE).
2. Generate all within-book co-occurrence pairs for training.
3. Train association model until convergence (monitor batch accuracy).
4. Transform all passages through trained `f(·)`.
5. Run k-means at each k on transformed embeddings.
6. Apply quality filters (min book diversity, max single-book dominance) per cluster.
7. (Optional) Label clusters via LLM inspection of top passages.

**Design tradeoffs:**
- Chunk size vs. context: Smaller chunks (50 tokens) capture single narrative beats but lose broader context; larger chunks may blur function boundaries. Overlap (15 tokens) prevents content fragmentation.
- Window size vs. scope: 15-chunk window balances local coherence and cross-scene linking. Too narrow → only adjacent sentences; too wide → mixes distinct narrative units.
- Capacity vs. concept granularity: More parameters or fewer epochs may yield author/tradition-specific clusters (less compression). Smaller capacity or more data forces broader cross-author concepts.
- Normalization scheme: L2-normalizing both input (BGE) and output (`f`) ensures contrastive loss operates on directional similarity, preserving semantic anchor while learning associative perturbation.

**Failure signatures:**
- Positional bias: Clusters with mean normalized passage position near 0.0 or 1.0 (start/end of book). Check: compute mean/std of position per cluster.
- Book concentration: Clusters where >10% passages from a single book. Indicates failure to generalize across texts.
- Low book diversity: Clusters drawing from <~1000 books at k=100 suggest overfitting to specific authors/traditions.
- Temporal shuffle sensitivity: If shuffling passage order within books does not drastically degrade cluster coherence (measured by cross-boundary recall), the signal may be embedding geometry artifact.
- Context-enriched baseline failure: If simple window-averaged embeddings produce similarly broad clusters, the learned transformation may not be necessary.

**First 3 experiments:**
1. **Reproduce training accuracy curve:** Train on a small pilot (e.g., 500 books). Verify model reaches ~40-50% top-1 in-batch accuracy by epoch 100-150. If accuracy is >80%, capacity is likely sufficient for memorization—reduce model size or increase data.
2. **Validate cluster diversity:** At k=100 on pilot, compute average distinct books per cluster. Expect >1,000 books per cluster at 10K scale. If <200, compression may be insufficient or window too narrow.
3. **Direct comparison with BGE:** Cluster raw BGE embeddings on same pilot. Sample 20 clusters each from PAM and BGE. Inspect: do PAM clusters mix topics but share functional register (e.g., "confrontation" across genres)? Do BGE clusters mix functions but share topics (e.g., all "fear" passages)? This sanity-check confirms the structural vs. topical split.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- What compression ratio (training accuracy) optimally balances concept specificity and generalizability?
- Do human annotators perceive the discovered concepts as coherent structural units distinct from topical groupings?
- Do transition-structure concepts improve performance on downstream NLP tasks compared to topical embeddings?
- Does temporal co-occurrence under compression discover analogous transition-structure concepts in non-literary sequential domains?
- Is the compression-induced concept formation mechanism analogous to sleep replay consolidating episodic memories into semantic structures in biological systems?

## Limitations
- Hyperparameters (learning rate, batch construction) not fully specified, hindering exact reproduction.
- Evaluation limited to 5 unseen novels and LLM-based cluster labeling; no human-annotated ground truth.
- Corpus restricted to pre-1900 English prose; applicability to other domains or modern text remains untested.

## Confidence
- Transition-structure discovery: Medium
- Capacity-driven compression: Medium
- Unseen-novel transfer: Medium-High
