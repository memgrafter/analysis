# Maximum-Entropy Exploration with Future State-Action Visitation Measures
## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2603.18965
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.18965
- **Reference count:** 40
- **Primary result:** Introduces a maximum-entropy RL intrinsic reward that maximizes the entropy of the discounted conditional distribution of state-action features visited in future steps, achieving higher within-trajectory feature diversity and faster exploration convergence.

## Executive Summary
The paper proposes a novel intrinsic reward for maximum-entropy reinforcement learning that encourages agents to maximize the entropy of the discounted conditional distribution of state-action features visited in the remainder of an episode. This objective is theoretically grounded as a lower bound on the marginal state-action visitation entropy and corresponds to the unique fixed point of a γ‑contractive operator, enabling efficient off‑policy temporal‑difference learning. The method augments Soft Actor‑Critic with a learned model of the conditional feature distribution and is evaluated on Minigrid benchmarks, where it yields increased conditional feature entropy, faster exploration, and comparable task performance relative to other maximum‑entropy approaches.

## Method Summary
The approach enhances Soft Actor‑Critic (SAC) with an intrinsic reward derived from a conditional visitation distribution over features. Let z = h(s,a) denote a feature mapping (e.g., agent grid position). The agent learns a parametric model q_ψ(z|s,a) to approximate the discounted conditional distribution of features visited from (s,a). The intrinsic reward is defined as R_int(s,a) = log q*(z) − log q_ψ(z|s,a), where q* is a uniform prior. The model q_ψ is trained off‑policy using N‑step transitions from a replay buffer: for each starting (s_0,a_0), samples of z are drawn from a mixture that combines future feature observations (h(s_Δ,a_Δ)) with probability (1−γ)γ^{Δ−1} for Δ ≤ N and bootstrapped predictions from q_ψ(s_N,a_N) with probability γ^N. This mixture corresponds to applying the conditional visitation operator (P^π)^N to q_ψ. The intrinsic reward augments environment returns for both critic and actor updates, promoting policies that yield diverse futures within each trajectory.

## Key Results
- The proposed method (CV) attains higher feature entropy **within individual trajectories** (conditional entropy) compared to methods that maximize marginal visitation entropy, while marginal entropy is slightly reduced.
- CV demonstrates faster convergence for exploration‑only agents on several Minigrid benchmarks.
- Final task performance (expected return) of CV is comparable to other maximum‑entropy methods (SAC with policy entropy, marginal visitation), but does not clearly surpass them.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1: Conditional Entropy Maximization for Within‑Trajectory Diversity
The intrinsic reward R_int(s,a) = −KL[q_π(z|s,a)∥q*] directly encourages actions that lead to diverse futures *from the current state*, rather than merely covering the whole state space over the long‑run distribution. This yields higher conditional entropy within each trajectory. Theoretically, this objective lower bounds the marginal visitation entropy (Theorem 3.2), but the bound may loosen under distribution shift (e.g., non‑uniform initial states), explaining the observed trade‑off where marginal entropy is slightly reduced.

### Mechanism 2: γ‑Contractive Operator Enables TD Learning
The conditional visitation distribution q_π(z|s,a) is the unique fixed point of the operator P^π defined by (P^π q)(z|s,a) = E_{s',a'∼π}[ (1−γ)h(z|s',a') + γ q(z|s',a') ]. Theorem 4.4 proves P^π is γ‑contractive in the ¯L_n‑norm, which justifies bootstrapping and off‑policy estimation. Hence, q_ψ can be trained by minimizing the residual ||P^π q_ψ − q_ψ||_n via a surrogate cross‑entropy loss using samples from (P^π)^N q_ψ.

### Mechanism 3: Off‑Policy Implementation via N‑Step Mixture
In practice, the expectation in P^π is approximated using an N‑step mixture: with probability (1−γ)γ^{Δ−1} draw z = h(s_Δ,a_Δ) from a future step, and with probability γ^N sample from q_ψ(s_N,a_N). This yields an unbiased estimate of (P^π)^N q_ψ under on‑policy data and a stationary distribution assumption. The intrinsic reward computed from these samples is plugged into SAC updates. Implementation shortcuts (target networks, dropping importance weights) introduce bias but improve stability.

## Architecture Onboarding

### Component map
Replay Buffer -> (provides N‑step transitions for) -> q_ψ update and Q_ϕ update
q_ψ -> (computes intrinsic reward for) -> Q_ϕ and π_θ updates
Q_ϕ -> (provides value estimates for) -> π_θ update
π_θ -> (generates actions for) -> Environment -> (feeds into) -> Replay Buffer

### Critical path
1. Collect interactions with current π_θ, store N‑step sequences in replay buffer.
2. Update q_ψ: For each starting (s_0,a_0) in batch, sample z from mixture of future features and bootstrapped q_ψ; minimize cross‑entropy −log q_ψ(z|s_0,a_0).
3. Compute R_int(s_0,a_0) = log q*(z) − log q_ψ(z|s_0,a_0); augment reward r_0 ← r_0 + λ_R·R_int.
4. Update Q_ϕ: Minimize MSE between Q_ϕ(s_0,a_0) and y = r_0 + γ(Q_ϕ'(s_1,a_1') − λ_SAC log π_θ(a_1'|s_1)).
5. Update π_θ: Maximize E[Q_ϕ(s,a) − λ_SAC log π_θ(a|s)].
6. Polyak update target networks (Q_ϕ', q_ψ').

### Design tradeoffs
- *N‑step length*: Larger N reduces bootstrap bias in q_ψ training but increases variance and memory; helps stabilize when γ is high.
- *Feature space Z*: Must be low‑dimensional for density modeling; discrete grid positions work for Minigrid but may not scale to high‑dimensional or continuous features.
- *Policy regularization λ_SAC*: Critical to prevent policy collapse; if too low, policy becomes deterministic despite intrinsic reward.
- *Importance weights*: Full importance sampling is unbiased but high variance; the implementation neglects some ratios for variance reduction, introducing dependence on the behavior policy β.

### Failure signatures
- q_ψ becomes deterministic (low output entropy): intrinsic rewards vanish, exploration stops. Monitor conditional entropy.
- Q‑values diverge or become NaN: likely γ too close to 1 without sufficient N, or intrinsic reward scale λ_R too high; clipping helps.
- Policy π_θ collapses to near‑deterministic despite λ_SAC > 0: q_ψ may be inaccurate or feature space uninformative.
- No improvement in marginal entropy despite high conditional entropy: the lower bound from Theorem 3.2 may be loose under distribution shift, indicating that conditional optimization sacrifices marginal coverage.

### First 3 experiments
1. **Baseline Comparison**: Run CV vs. MV vs. SAC‑only on SimpleCrossingS11N5. Verify CV achieves higher conditional entropy (as in Fig 3) but slightly lower marginal entropy (Fig 2).
2. **Ablation on N**: For CV on ObstructedMaze‑Full, vary N ∈ {1,5,10}. Expect larger N to stabilize training for γ=0.99, but possibly slow learning if N too large.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
Which exploration objective (marginal vs. conditional visitation entropy) is theoretically optimal for different MDP characteristics, and how does it affect sample efficiency and final control performance? The authors state that the exploration objective remains unclear and environment‑dependent; a theoretical framework linking MDP properties to objective benefits is needed.

### Open Question 2
How can the feature mapping *h* be learned automatically to maximize exploration effectiveness in complex environments? The paper identifies reliance on hand‑crafted features as a key limitation; learned representations could adapt to task‑relevant structure.

### Open Question 3
Can more expressive density models (e.g., diffusion models, normalizing flows) improve estimation of conditional visitation distributions and lead to more stable or sample‑efficient exploration? The current categorical model restricts the model class; alternative models are proposed for future work.

### Open Question 4
Why does learning the conditional visitation model become unstable for discount factors γ close to 1, and how can we ensure stable off‑policy learning without relying on large N‑step returns? The paper observes instability near γ=1 and mitigates by increasing N, but offers no deeper analysis or alternative solutions.

## Limitations
- Missing architectural and hyperparameter details hinder exact reproduction; the method relies on low‑dimensional discrete features (grid positions) and may not scale to high‑dimensional or continuous spaces.
- Practical approximations (target networks, dropping importance weights) may introduce bias into the learned conditional model.
- Instability for γ close to 1 is mitigated by increasing N‑step length, but the trade‑off is not fully characterized and increases computational cost.
- The theoretical lower bound on marginal entropy (Theorem 3.2) may become loose under non‑uniform initial state distributions, potentially undermining the motivation for the conditional objective.

## Confidence
- **Lower bound guarantee (Theorem 3.2):** High (proof sound, but practical tightness uncertain under distribution shift)
- **Contraction property (Theorems 4.4, 4.5):** High (rigorous establishment)
- **Empirical conditional entropy improvement:** Medium (confirmed on several Minigrid tasks, but limited benchmark)
- **Task performance parity:** Medium (matches other MaxEnt methods, but no clear advantage; sensitivity to q_ψ errors not deeply analyzed)

## Next Checks

2. **Interaction of N and γ:** In ObstructedMaze‑Full, vary N ∈ {1,5,10} and γ ∈ {0.9,0.95,0.99}. Monitor stability (q_ψ loss variance, entropy) and final conditional entropy. Expect larger N to stabilize higher γ.
3. **Scalability of feature representation:** Replace one‑hot positions with a learned state embedding (e.g., CNN output). Train q_ψ as a continuous density estimator (e.g., mixture of Gaussians). Compare CV to MV on a complex Minigrid task (e.g., MultiRoom) to assess robustness to richer features.
