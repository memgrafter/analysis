# From Causal Discovery to Dynamic Causal Inference in Neural Time Series

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2603.20980
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.20980
- **Reference count:** 40
- **Primary result:** DCNAR produces more stable and interpretable dynamic causal inferences than coefficient-based or structure-free alternatives while maintaining comparable predictive distribution accuracy.

## Executive Summary
This paper tackles the problem of dynamic causal inference in settings where the underlying causal network structure is unknown, a common scenario in scientific domains. It introduces Dynamic Causal Network Autoregression (DCNAR), a two-stage neural framework. First, a neural additive autoregressive model discovers a sparse directed causal network from multivariate time series. Second, this learned network acts as a structural prior for a time-varying network autoregression, enabling the estimation of dynamic causal influences. Evaluated on multi-country panel data using behavioral diagnostics—such as impulse response stability and counterfactual coherence—rather than predictive accuracy alone, DCNAR yields smoother, more sign-consistent dynamic responses and bounded counterfactual trajectories compared to methods like Ridge VAR, TV-VAR, or LSTMs.

## Method Summary
DCNAR operates in two sequential stages. In the discovery stage, a Neural Additive Vector Autoregression (NAVAR) model decomposes each variable's forecast into additive contributions from lagged values of all other variables using small neural networks. Sparsity is induced via ℓ₁ regularization on component outputs, and a necessity-based edge ablation procedure prunes edges whose removal does not significantly degrade out-of-sample forecast performance, yielding a sparse directed adjacency matrix bG. In the inference stage, this bG serves as a fixed structural prior for a time-varying Network Autoregression (tvNAR). The tvNAR estimates smooth time-varying nodal influence parameters Λ(t) via kernel smoothing, but only for edges present in bG, drastically reducing dimensionality. The resulting time-varying state-space matrix A(t) = (bG + I)Λ(t) is then used to compute dynamic causal quantities like impulse response functions and counterfactual trajectories.

## Key Results
- DCNAR yields impulse responses that are smoother, sign-consistent, and bounded, unlike the oscillatory or explosive behavior seen in TV-VAR and LSTM baselines.
- Counterfactual trajectories under interventions remain interpretable and do not diverge unexpectedly.
- Predictive distribution accuracy (CRPS) and calibration are comparable to baselines, demonstrating that behavioral improvements do not come at a severe predictive cost.
- The framework's advantages hold across panels of differing temporal lengths, suggesting robustness to data limitations.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
DCNAR’s efficacy stems from three intertwined mechanisms. First, the NAVAR-based discovery phase leverages the predictive fidelity of neural additive models to infer a sparse directed graph. By enforcing sparsity through ℓ₁ regularization and then pruning edges based on their necessity for out-of-sample forecasting (via loss differentials and Diebold–Mariano tests), it derives a structural prior that aggregates Granger-causal signals while filtering out spurious dependencies. This prior is treated as a testable hypothesis rather than a ground truth. Second, imposing this fixed, sparse topology as a constraint on the time-varying coefficient estimation in tvNAR prevents overfitting and pathological dynamics, especially in short panels. The reduction in free parameters from O(N²pT) to O(|E|T) allows kernel smoothing to produce smooth, stable time paths for nodal influences. Third, the entire pipeline is explicitly optimized for behavioral diagnostics (e.g., impulse response stability, counterfactual coherence) rather than pure point forecast accuracy. The structural prior inherently discourages high-frequency coefficient fluctuations that would yield erratic causal responses, aligning the model’s dynamic behavior with scientific expectations of smooth, interpretable influence propagation.

- **Concept: Granger-causal structure learning for time series**

  - **Quick check:** How does ℓ₁ regularization on NAVAR's additive component functions encourage sparsity in the final causal graph, and why is this preferable to thresholding linear VAR coefficients?

  - **Why needed:** tvNAR estimates smooth nodal influence paths Λ(t); understanding local kernel-weighted regression is key to grasping how temporal variation is regularized.
  - **Quick check:** In tvNAR, why is kernel smoothing applied over a normalized time index τ, and how does this handle unbalanced panels?
- **Concept: Behavioral evaluation of dynamic causal models**
  - **Why needed:** DCNAR's value proposition is superior causal interpretability, not forecast accuracy; impulse response functions and counterfactual simulation are the primary validation tools.
  - **Quick check:** What specific qualitative properties (e.g., sign consistency, boundedness) of impulse responses are used to judge a model's scientific plausibility, and why might a model with better CRPS fail these tests?

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map:** Input (multivariate panel time series) -> NAVAR discovery (per-variable additive NNs -> causal score matrix S -> edge ablation -> binary adjacency bG) -> tvNAR inference (constrained time-varying coefficient estimation via kernel smoothing) -> Output tools (time-varying impulse responses, counterfactual trajectories)
- **Critical path:** NAVAR training produces S; edge ablation converts S to bG; tvNAR estimation uses bG as a fixed prior to compute Λ(t); behavioral diagnostics are derived from A(t) = (bG + I)Λ(t). The structural prior bG is the single coupling point; errors here propagate downstream.
- **Design tradeoffs:**
  - *Expressivity vs. Stability:* NAVAR's neural components capture nonlinearities but risk overfitting; ℓ₁ and edge ablation sacrifice some true edges for a cleaner, more stable prior.
  - *Static prior vs. Dynamic truth:* A fixed bG cannot capture time-varying structural breaks; DCNAR accepts this to stabilize estimation in short panels.
  - *Prediction vs. Causation:* The framework does not optimize hyperparameters for CRPS; it accepts modest predictive trade-offs for interpretable dynamics.
- **Failure signatures:**
  - **Stage I:** bG is extremely dense or varies wildly across cross-validation folds.
  - **Stage II:** tvNAR coefficient paths Λ(t) exhibit high-frequency oscillations despite kernel smoothing.
  - **Outputs:** Impulse responses show sign reversals within a few lags; counterfactual trajectories explode or collapse.
  - **Calibration mismatch:** Good CRPS but poor 90% interval coverage (as in LSTM baseline).
- **First 3 experiments:**
  1. **Discovery sanity check:** Visualize the learned bG (e.g., heatmap) and its edge count distribution across countries. Is it sparse and interpretable?
  2. **Dynamic coherence test:** Plot Λ(t) for key nodes. Are the time-varying influence parameters smooth? Compare against unconstrained TV-VAR coefficients.
  3. **Behavioral comparison:** For a fixed country and shock variable, overlay impulse responses from DCNAR, Ridge VAR, TV-VAR, and LSTM. Assess for oscillations, sign consistency, and decay patterns.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- **Question 1:** How do errors in the initial causal discovery stage propagate to affect the temporal stability and sign-consistency of DCNAR's impulse response functions?
- **Question 2:** Can DCNAR's learned structural prior generalize to time series with non-annual frequencies or irregular sampling, where lag structures and temporal dependencies may differ fundamentally?
- **Question 3:** What quantitative metrics best capture the "stability" and "interpretability" of impulse responses and counterfactual trajectories for objective model comparison in dynamic causal inference?
- **Question 4:** To what extent does DCNAR's assumption of a static learned network (bG) limit its capacity to model systems where the underlying causal structure itself evolves over time?

## Limitations
- Hyperparameters (NAVAR architecture, ℓ₁ weight, kernel bandwidth) and exact data preprocessing details are undisclosed, hindering exact replication.
- The fixed structural prior bG cannot model time-varying structural breaks, potentially missing regime shifts in dynamic systems.
- Behavioral diagnostics rely on qualitative assessment (smoothness, boundedness) without standardized numerical metrics or ground truth, making claims of "scientific validity" weakly supported.

## Confidence
- **Claim:** DCNAR learns a sparse directed causal network from short, heterogeneous panel time series.
  -> **Confidence:** Medium (plausible mechanism, but hyperparameter sensitivity and lack of direct evidence on short panels create uncertainty).
- **Claim:** Treating the learned network as a fixed structural prior stabilizes time-varying network autoregression in data-limited regimes.
  -> **Confidence:** Medium (theoretical rationale and empirical results are promising, but static prior limitation is a significant untested break condition).
- **Claim:** Evaluating via behavioral diagnostics rather than predictive accuracy yields scientifically valid dynamic causal inferences.
  -> **Confidence:** Medium-Low (conceptually sound, but without ground truth, the claim remains weakly supported).
