# A Tale of Two Graphs: Separating Knowledge Exploration from Outline Structure for Open-Ended Deep Research

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2602.13830
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.13830
- **Reference count:** 40
- **Primary result:** DualGraph achieves state-of-the-art performance on OEDR benchmarks, reaching a 53.08 RACE score with GPT-5, converging faster and producing higher-quality reports.

## Executive Summary
Open-Ended Deep Research (OEDR) tasks require agents to iteratively search, connect, and synthesize evidence into comprehensive reports. Existing agents suffer from "lost-in-the-middle" failures or rely on outline-centric planning with weak supervision for identifying knowledge gaps. This paper introduces DualGraph, a novel architecture that separates the agent’s evolving knowledge (Knowledge Graph, KG) from its report structure (Outline Graph, OG). The KG stores fine-grained entities and relations; its topology is analyzed together with OG structural signals to generate targeted search queries, enabling efficient and comprehensive iterative exploration. Evaluations on DeepResearch Bench, DeepResearchGym, and DeepConsult show consistent superiority over state-of-the-art baselines. With GPT-5, DualGraph attains a 53.08 RACE score and demonstrates faster convergence (fewer iterations) and higher-quality intermediate outlines, validating the dual-graph design.

## Method Summary
DualGraph orchestrates a co-evolution process between two graphs: the Outline Graph (OG) defines the hierarchical report structure and tracks which evidence supports each section; the Knowledge Graph (KG) represents atomic entities, concepts, and relations grounded in retrieved evidence. Each iteration begins by generating search queries from two sources: (1) KG-driven “enrichment” and “exploration” chains that target weakly supported or missing edges based on topological signals (semantic similarity, stochastic block model cross-community probabilities, structural-hole bridging); and (2) OG-driven queries for sections lacking citations. After deduplication, the queries are issued to a web search pipeline; retrieved evidence is extracted, added to the Evidence Bank, and used to update both graphs. KG updates involve node/edge extraction, canonical merging, semantic clustering, and community detection (Leiden). OG updates revise the outline structure and attach new citations, enforcing citation persistence to maintain a traceable narrative. An early stopping evaluator scores the OG on six dimensions (instruction following, depth, breadth, balance, support, insightfulness); when all exceed thresholds, the loop terminates and a report writer synthesizes the final document using only cited evidence. This dual-graph separation enables systematic gap discovery and coherent report growth, mitigating the “lost-in-the-middle” issue.

## Key Results
- DualGraph significantly outperforms prior state-of-the-art agents across DeepResearch Bench, DeepResearchGym, and DeepConsult, achieving a RACE score of 53.08 with GPT-5.
- Ablations reveal that the KG component reduces iterations (3.20 vs. 3.81) and improves intermediate outline quality (depth, breadth, support).
- The system increases effective citation counts (+13.59 with GPT-5) and yields gains in report breadth and depth, albeit with a slight reduction in citation accuracy.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1
- **Claim:** The Knowledge Graph (KG) enables systematic identification of missing or weakly supported relations, driving targeted exploration.
- **Mechanism:** KG topology analysis flags gaps by combining semantic similarity (core entity–concept pairs), stochastic block model (SBM) inter-community link probabilities/uncertainty, and structural-hole bridging scores. These signals generate "exploration" search chains for missing edges and "enrichment" chains for weakly supported edges.
- **Core assumption:** A dense, well-connected KG with canonically merged nodes accurately represents the research state; missing links or low-evidence edges correspond to meaningful knowledge gaps.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract] "analyzing the KG topology together with structural signals from the OG... generate targeted search queries"
  - [section 3.4] Details enrichment/exploration chain construction; SBM and bridging scores compute cross-community link candidates.
  - [corpus] Weak: neighbors discuss KG-guided retrieval but not explicit gap-discovery via topology (e.g., SciRAG uses citation graphs; WebWeaver uses dynamic outlines without separate KG).
- **Break condition:** If KG extraction is error-prone (e.g., incorrect node/edge canonicalization), gap signals become noisy, leading to irrelevant queries.

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim:** The Outline Graph (OG) provides structural context that prioritizes KG-driven gaps and ensures report coherence, preventing the agent from overlooking planned but unincorporated aspects.
- **Mechanism:** OG sections without citations flag under-researched topics. When merged with KG-derived chains, OG context broadens query coverage beyond already-grounded KG nodes, capturing query-space areas not yet in the KG.
- **Core assumption:** A hierarchical outline effectively scaffolds report structure; uncited sections indicate legitimate knowledge needs even if not yet reflected in the KG.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract] "KG reveals missing or underexplored relations, while the OG provides structural context to prioritize and guide search"
  - [section 3.3] "Since KG-driven search chains are largely related to already grounded nodes... they can miss parts of the query space that have not yet been incorporated into KG. We therefore provide both the KG-derived search chains and the updated OG"
  - [corpus] WebWeaver (neighbor) also uses dynamic outlines but lacks a separate KG; its queries derive from outline alone, implying weaker gap supervision.
- **Break condition:** If OG contains inaccurate or overly narrow sections, it may misprioritize KG gaps or introduce redundant queries.

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim:** Co-evolution of KG and OG over iterations creates a closed loop where new evidence refines both knowledge representation and report structure, leading to faster convergence and higher-quality outlines.
- **Mechanism:** Each iteration: (1) new evidence updates KG (node/edge extraction, merging, community detection); (2) OG is revised with citations and structural changes, enforced with citation persistence; (3) gap analysis combines updated KG and OG to produce next queries. Early stopping judges OG completeness.
- **Core assumption:** Iterative refinement with persistent citations prevents evidence loss and allows progressive deepening; KG topology changes meaningfully signal knowledge completeness.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract] "co-evolves an Outline Graph and a Knowledge Graph... new evidence continuously reshaping both"
  - [section 3.3] "DualGraph enforces citation persistence... keeping outline evolution synchronized with accumulated evidence"
  - [section 4.3] Ablation shows DualGraph terminates in fewer iterations (3.20 vs 3.81) and produces higher-quality intermediate outlines (Figure 3, 4).
  - [corpus] A-MEM and MemGPT maintain memory but not dual-graph co-evolution for OEDR.
- **Break condition:** If iteration budget is too low, KG/OG may be underdeveloped; if too high, diminishing returns or KG noise accumulation may occur.

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component map:**
- Outline Graph (OG): Hierarchical tree (sections/subsections) with semantic descriptions and citation sets `C_i`; updated via `UPDATEOG`.
- Knowledge Graph (KG): Directed edge-centric graph; nodes are core entities/concepts, edges are relations with evidence IDs; updated via `UPDATEKG` (extraction → merging → clustering → community detection).
- Evidence Bank: Stores citable evidence units (`EN#`) with unique IDs, retrieved via `SEARCH` (Bing → URL filter → crawl → evidence extraction).
- Search Chain Generator: `GENFROMKG` produces enrichment/exploration chains; `GENFROMOG` produces outline-gap queries; merged/deduped into next queries.
- Early Stopping Evaluator: Scores OG on six dimensions (instruction following, depth, balance, breadth, support, insightfulness); stops when all exceed thresholds.
- Report Writer: `WRITEREPORT` synthesizes section-by-section using only cited evidence from bank.

**Critical path:** User query → initial `CREATEOUTLINE` → `SEARCH` → `BUILDKG` → loop: `GENFROMKG`/`GENFROMOG` → `SEARCH` → `UPDATEKG` + `UPDATEOG` → `EARLYSTOP`? → `WRITEREPORT`.

**Design tradeoffs:**
- **KG overhead vs. coverage:** KG maintenance adds ~9% tokens but increases effective citations and improves breadth/depth; trade-off is slight citation accuracy drop.
- **Early stopping thresholds:** Too lenient → redundant searches; too strict → premature termination. Benchmarks use preset thresholds tuned on validation.
- **Query budget:** Max 10 from KG, 10 from OG per iteration. Larger budget improves coverage but increases cost; smaller risks missing gaps.

**Failure signatures:**
- **KG extraction failures:** Duplicate concept nodes not merged → fragmented evidence; missed core entities → incomplete gap analysis. Check: Post-merge node count growth per iteration (should plateau).
- **OG citation drift:** Without persistence, section edits lose citations → unsupported claims. Check: Citation ID consistency across `UPDATEOG` calls.
- **Early stopping misjudgment:** OG scores high but KG still has many weak/exploration edges → report lacks depth. Check: KG edge density and enrichment chain quota utilization at termination.
- **Search query redundancy:** Poor deduplication → repeated retrieval. Check: Overlap ratio between consecutive `Q_t` sets.

**First 3 experiments:**
1. **Baseline replication:** Run `DualGraph w/o KG` with identical initial OG, queries, and retrieved evidence as `DualGraph`. Compare RACE/FACT to isolate KG contribution.
2. **Ablation of gap discovery:** Disable KG enrichment/exploration; only use `GENFROMOG` queries. Measure iteration count and intermediate outline quality (Insight/Depth/Breadth/Support).
3. **KG update analysis:** Log per-iteration KG metrics: new nodes/edges added, merged node count, community count, enrichment chain selection rate. Correlate with final report quality to validate gap-signal efficacy.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
### Open Question 1
**Question:** How does the DualGraph architecture scale to Open-Ended Deep Research tasks that involve multimodal evidence (e.g., images, videos, charts) beyond text-centric sources?  
**Basis in paper:** The paper focuses on text-centric deep research benchmarks; effectiveness in multimodal settings remains unexplored.  
**Why unresolved:** Current KG construction and evidence extraction pipeline is designed for textual units. Integrating visual or structured data requires new node/edge types, grounding mechanisms, and topological analysis methods that preserve the separation of knowledge (KG) and structure (OG).  
**What evidence would resolve it:** A) A multimodal extension of the KG schema (e.g., visual concept nodes, image-to-text relation edges). B) Benchmarks with mixed-modality source material and evaluation metrics that assess cross-modal synthesis quality. C) Ablation studies isolating the contribution of multimodal KG nodes versus text-only KG.

### Open Question 2
**Question:** To what extent can the robustness and accuracy of the Knowledge Graph be decoupled from the performance of the underlying LLM used for node/edge extraction and semantic merging?  
**Basis in paper:** The KG quality depends on the underlying LLM’s ability to accurately abstract entities and relations from retrieved evidence. Imperfect extractions may propagate errors.  
**Why unresolved:** The KG is LLM-generated and continuously updated. Errors in extraction (missing nodes, spurious edges, incorrect relations) or merging (over-aggressive canonicalization) could distort topological signals that drive gap discovery, leading to suboptimal or erroneous search chains.  
**What evidence would resolve it:** A) Controlled noise-injection experiments where known gold-standard KG structures are perturbed to simulate LLM errors, measuring sensitivity of final report quality. B) Comparison across multiple LLM families (open vs. proprietary, varying sizes) to isolate impact of extraction fidelity on exploration efficiency. C) Development and evaluation of post-hoc KG validation or human-in-the-loop correction loops.

### Open Question 3
**Question:** Are the specific topological signals used for knowledge gap discovery (SBM probability, structural-hole bridging, cross-community exposure) optimal, or could alternative or learned graph metrics better identify high-value missing relations for complex research queries?  
**Basis in paper:** DualGraph uses a Stochastic Block Model (SBM) to estimate prior probability and uncertainty of cross-community links, and cross-community bridging and structural-hole cues. These signals balance high-confidence completion with high-value exploration.  
**Why unresolved:** The current signals are hand-designed heuristics. Their effectiveness may be domain-dependent, and they may not capture all valuable knowledge gaps (e.g., gaps in temporal sequences, causal chains, contradictory evidence). A learned scoring function could adapt to evolving KG topology and the specific query.  
**What evidence would resolve it:** A) Ablation study removing or substituting individual graph signals (SBM, bridging, similarity) and measuring impact on final report comprehensiveness and insight. B) Training a lightweight graph neural network on successful past trajectories to predict which missing edges yield highest marginal utility when searched, compared to the heuristic approach. C) Qualitative analysis of failed cases where topological signals missed a critical relation.

### Open Question 4
**Question:** Can the KG's explicit representation of evidence-grounded relationships be leveraged to create more meaningful and earlier self-termination criteria than the current Outline Graph (OG)-centric scoring?  
**Basis in paper:** Self-termination is based solely on OG quality dimensions (depth, breadth, support, etc.). The KG provides a complementary, quantifiable view of knowledge saturation (e.g., density of core-entity connections, closure of cross-community links, diminishing returns on new edge additions).  
**Why unresolved:** Using KG metrics might allow earlier, more reliable detection of diminishing returns, reducing redundant search, but this is not explored.  
**What evidence would resolve it:** A) Correlation analysis between KG topological metrics (e.g., global clustering coefficient, new edge rate per iteration) and eventual utility of additional search rounds. B) Implementation of a hybrid termination criterion (OG score + KG saturation metric) and comparison to current OG-only method in terms of iterations to convergence and final quality. C) User studies to assess if earlier termination based on KG saturation produces equally complete reports.

### Open Question 5
**Question:** How does the separation of concerns in DualGraph (KG for knowledge, OG for narrative) affect the agent's ability to detect and resolve contradictions or inconsistencies across sources compared to outline-centric or monolithic memory approaches?  
**Basis in paper:** The KG provides a "single source of truth," but the paper does not detail a specific contradiction detection mechanism beyond missing links. The impact of unified relation storage on identifying conflicting evidence (e.g., two sources supporting opposite relations for the same node pair) and how that triggers targeted resolution searches is not explored.  
**Why unresolved:** Unified relation storage could help identify conflicts, but no explicit module or evaluation on contradictory material is presented.  
**What evidence would resolve it:** A) Case studies or error analysis on benchmark reports showing how contradiction detection (e.g., via opposing edges between the same nodes with different evidence) was performed and resolved. B) A specific module or prompt design within the KG update phase for conflict identification and resolution planning. C) Comparative evaluation on datasets known to contain contradictory source material, measuring factual accuracy and neutrality of the final report.

## Limitations
- **Implementation details omitted:** The paper does not provide key prompts (evidence extraction, outline update, early stopping evaluation), hyperparameter values (enrichment threshold, early stopping thresholds, clustering parameters), or exact query budget allocation, hindering faithful reproduction and introducing variability.
- **Reliance on external APIs and KG noise:** Dependence on Bing and OpenAI APIs, combined with potential noise from LLM-based extraction and merging errors, threatens robustness and reproducibility.
