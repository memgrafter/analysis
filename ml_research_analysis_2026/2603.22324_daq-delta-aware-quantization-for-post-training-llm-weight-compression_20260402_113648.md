# DAQ: Delta-Aware Quantization for Post-Training LLM Weight Compression
## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2603.22324
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.22324
- Reference count: 33
- Primary result: DAQ recovers lost style-specific capability after FP8 quantization, slightly exceeding the unquantized post-trained model, while standard MSE-based methods degrade performance.

## Executive Summary
Standard post-training quantization (PTQ) for large language models (LLMs) typically minimizes reconstruction error (MSE) between quantized and original weights. This approach is base-model-agnostic and inadvertently treats small-magnitude parameter updates from fine-tuning (ΔW) as noise, erasing them during optimization. Delta-Aware Quantization (DAQ) replaces the reconstruction loss with delta-aware metrics—Sign Preservation Rate and Cosine Similarity—directly optimizing the directional fidelity of ΔW using only the base and post-trained weight matrices. DAQ is data-free, requiring no calibration data. Experiments on DeepSeek-V3 after stylistic supervised fine-tuning show that DAQ restores the style-specific metric from 1.08 (AbsMax FP8) to 1.72, surpassing the unquantized post-trained model (1.71), while maintaining general performance around 1.44. In contrast, MSE-guided scale search further degrades style to 0.26. The work demonstrates that preserving the post-training increment is essential for quantized aligned models.

## Method Summary
DAQ formulates PTQ as a data-free, layer-wise optimization problem that explicitly accounts for the parameter delta ΔW = W_post - W_base. For each weight matrix, a quantization operator with a scale parameter s maps the post-trained weights to quantized-dequantized values. Instead of minimizing MSE between quantized and original weights, DAQ maximizes a delta-aware metric M(ΔW_post, ΔW_quant) such as Sign Preservation Rate (fraction of matching signs) or Cosine Similarity (angular alignment). An efficient coarse-to-fine grid search over scaling factors s (Algorithm 1) finds the optimal per-layer scale. The quantized weights are then stored along with their inverse scales for deployment. By operating solely on the weight tensors, DAQ avoids any dependence on input data or activation statistics.

## Key Results
- DAQ (SignRate or CosSim) recovered the style metric to ~1.72, slightly above the unquantized post-trained model (1.71), while standard AbsMax FP8 dropped to ~1.08.
- MSE-guided scale search (same framework but optimizing -MSE) further degraded style to 0.26, confirming the bias of reconstruction loss against small ΔW.
- General performance remained stable (~1.44) across DAQ and baselines, indicating that delta-aware optimization preserves style without sacrificing overall capability.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1
- **Claim:** Standard MSE-based quantization optimization disproportionately corrupts small-magnitude post-training deltas (ΔW) by biasing weights toward the base model.
- **Mechanism:** Minimizing reconstruction error (MSE) between quantized weights and the post-trained weights is mathematically equivalent to minimizing the MSE of the quantized delta (∥ΔW_quant - ΔW_post∥² = ∥W_quant - W_post∥²). This formulation is blind to the base model, treating the post-trained weight as the sole target. For a weight with a large base component and a small ΔW, the nearest quantization point (minimizing MSE) often corresponds to the base weight, erasing the ΔW.
- **Core assumption:** Post-training knowledge is encoded in small, directional updates relative to a large, stable base model.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract]: "reconstruction-based optimization is base-model-agnostic and treats ΔW as noise."
  - [section 2.3, Eq 7]: "MSE-based optimization is entirely base-model-agnostic... cannot distinguish between quantization errors that preserve the fine-tuning direction and those that reverse it."
  - [section 3.3]: MSE-guided scale search degraded the style metric (e.g., to 0.260) while reducing ΔW L2 norm.
- **Break condition:** If the post-training delta (ΔW) is large relative to base weights (e.g., after extensive full-parameter fine-tuning), the MSE objective may no longer systematically erase ΔW.

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim:** Optimizing delta-aware metrics (Sign Preservation Rate, Cosine Similarity) directly preserves the directional fidelity of ΔW, thereby protecting post-training knowledge.
- **Mechanism:** DAQ defines the optimization target on the delta vector itself (ΔW_quant vs. ΔW_post). The Sign Preservation Rate ensures the sign (direction) of each update is kept. Cosine Similarity ensures the overall angular alignment between the quantized and original delta vectors is maximized, accounting for relative magnitude. This explicitly counteracts the regularization bias that smooths small updates.
- **Core assumption:** The direction of ΔW is a sufficient proxy for preserving the behavioral changes introduced by post-training.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract]: "DAQ replaces reconstruction loss with delta-aware metrics—Sign Preservation Rate and Cosine Similarity—directly optimizing the directional fidelity of ΔW."
  - [section 2.3]: Definitions of SignRate and CosSim as delta-aware metrics.
  - [section 3.4]: DAQ (using Sign or CosSim) recovered the style metric (e.g., to 1.718-1.761), slightly exceeding the unquantized post-trained model (1.71), while maintaining general performance.
- **Break condition:** If ΔW magnitude is very large, sign/cosine metrics become less discriminative as quantization noise is unlikely to flip direction; intermediate checkpoints may be needed as the base.

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim:** The DAQ framework is data-free because its objective depends only on the base and post-trained weight matrices, not on input activations or calibration data.
- **Mechanism:** The optimization problem (Eq. 3, 5) uses only W_base and W_post to compute ΔW and evaluate M(ΔW_post, ΔW_quant). No forward passes on representative data are required to estimate activation statistics or Hessians, unlike GPTQ or AWQ.
- **Core assumption:** The sole requirement for preserving post-training knowledge is to maintain the parameter increment ΔW; activation statistics are secondary or unnecessary for this specific goal.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract]: "requiring only the base and post-trained weight matrices."
  - [section 1]: "DAQ is entirely data-free: unlike calibration-based methods such as GPTQ and AWQ, it requires no representative input samples, no activation statistics, and no Hessian estimation."
  - [section 2.4]: Scale search is performed on the weight tensors themselves using ΔW.
- **Break condition:** If preservation of post-training behavior critically depends on intricate activation distributions (e.g., for certain attention mechanisms), a data-free weight-only approach may be insufficient.

## Foundational Learning
- **Parameter Delta (ΔW = W_post - W_base)**  
  *Why needed:* Central object of optimization; post-training introduces small, directional updates relative to a stable base model.  
  *Quick check:* Why is a small ΔW more vulnerable to quantization noise than large base weights? Because quantization error is absolute, so relative error is larger for small magnitudes, and MSE minimization pulls small values toward zero.

- **Implicit Regularization via Quantization**  
  *Why needed:* Explains the failure mode of MSE quantization; the noise acts as a regularizer that shrinks small-magnitude parameters, effectively pushing them toward the base distribution.  
  *Quick check:* How does minimizing reconstruction error implicitly push weights toward a prior (the base model)? It minimizes distance to the post-trained weight, which for a small ΔW is closer to the base weight than alternative quantization points, thus erasing the delta.

- **Directional Fidelity Metrics (Sign Rate, Cosine Similarity)**  
  *Why needed:* Replacement objectives that explicitly protect the direction of ΔW. Sign Rate is a binary measure of sign agreement; Cosine Similarity measures angular alignment, accounting for magnitude.  
  *Quick check:* What is the key difference between what the MSE metric optimizes and what the Cosine Similarity metric optimizes? MSE optimizes absolute difference (magnitude and direction), while Cosine Similarity optimizes relative direction regardless of magnitude scaling.

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map:**  
  Base & Post-trained Checkpoints -> Delta Computation Module -> Quant-Dequant Operator (parameterized by scale s) -> Metric Evaluator -> Coarse-to-Fine Scale Searcher -> Quantized Weight Writer

- **Critical path:**  
  1. Load base and post-trained checkpoints (W_base, W_post).  
  2. For each layer, compute ΔW = W_post - W_base.  
  3. Initialize default scale s0 = max(|W_post|) / Q_max (Q_max = FP8 max).  
  4. Perform coarse search: select α values in [α_min, α_max] (e.g., 5 points), set s = α * s0, compute ΔW_quant via quantize-dequantize with s, evaluate metric M(ΔW, ΔW_quant).  
  5. Perform fine search: around best coarse α, sample additional points (e.g., 10), evaluate M, pick s*.  
  6. Store s* and quantized weights (Quant(W_post, s*)).  
  7. After all layers, construct the quantized model with per-layer scales.

- **Design tradeoffs:**  
  • *Metric choice:* SignRate yields higher peak style scores but can be non-monotonic; CosSim is more stable but slightly lower. Hybrid metrics possible.  
  • *Granularity:* Per-channel scaling improves delta preservation but increases storage overhead.  
  • *Search cost:* Coarse-to-fine grid search is cheap per layer but linear in layer count; parallelism possible.  
  • *Base model assumption:* Assumes W_base is the correct reference; using an intermediate checkpoint might help for aggressive fine-tuning.

- **Failure signatures:**  
  • Style metric collapses (<1.0) while General remains stable → ΔW directional fidelity destroyed; likely used MSE or poor scale.  
  • Both Style and General drop → catastrophic quantization (e.g., extreme scale, format error).  
  • SignRate very low (<60%) but CosSim moderate → many sign flips, likely altered behavior.  
  • Optimal scale s* at search boundaries (α_min or α_max) → search range too narrow; expand.

- **First 3 experiments:**  
  1. Replicate Table 2: Quantize DeepSeek-V3 post-trained model with AbsMax FP8 (block/channel). Confirm style drop from ~1.71 to ~1.08/1.32.  
  2. Replicate Table 3: Apply DAQ's scale search framework but optimize -MSE. Expect further style degradation (e.g., <0.5).  
  3. Apply DAQ with SignRate and CosSim objectives (Tables 4/5). Expect style recovery to ~1.7+ and stable general metric (~1.44). Verify that CosSim search yields highest CosSim.

## Limitations
- Undisclosed evaluation rubrics for Style/General score prevent exact replication of key results.
- Experiments limited to a single model (DeepSeek-V3) and one fine-tuning scenario (stylistic SFT); generalization to other architectures or tasks is unknown.
- Theory assumes small ΔW; the break point where ΔW becomes large enough for MSE to be effective is not investigated.

## Confidence
- MSE quantization bias: High  
- Delta-aware metric efficacy: Medium  
- Superiority over unquantized: Low

## Next Checks
1. Cross-model replication: Apply DAQ to another LLM (e.g., Llama 3) with a small-ΔW fine-tuning task, verify delta metrics predict task performance and DAQ recovers style.
2. ΔW magnitude ablation: Interpolate between base and post-trained weights (varying ΔW size). Quantize with DAQ and MSE methods to identify the regime where DAQ's advantage diminishes.
3. Layer-wise correlation analysis: Compute per-layer SignRate/CosSim for various quantizers. Correlate these with layer-wise contributions to Style/General scores to test if delta fidelity predicts capability retention.
