# PICon: A Multi-Turn Interrogation Framework for Evaluating Persona Agent Consistency

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2603.25620
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.25620
- **Reference count:** 40
- **Primary result:** No evaluated persona agent matched human performance across internal, external, and retest consistency dimensions, with fine-tuned models showing particularly low cooperativeness and coverage.

## Executive Summary
PICon introduces a systematic, black-box framework for evaluating the consistency of LLM-based persona agents in multi-turn dialogues. It operationalizes interrogation principles through a three-stage pipeline: generating logically chained follow-up questions to test internal consistency, using real-time web verification of extracted factual claims for external consistency, and re-asking initial demographic questions to measure retest stability. In a comparative evaluation of seven persona agent groups (e.g., Character.ai, fine-tuned open-source models) against 63 human participants, all agents fell short of the human baseline. The results reveal fundamental challenges in maintaining coherent, fact-grounded, and stable personas, especially for fine-tuned models that exhibit low cooperativeness under adversarial probing.

## Method Summary
The framework comprises three interconnected stages. First, a "Get-to-Know" phase collects baseline demographic answers. Second, the Main Interrogation loop runs for a fixed number of turns (default 50): the persona responds, an Entity & Claim Extractor identifies searchable entities and atomic factual claims from the response, a web search retrieves evidence for those claims, and a Questioner (using the dialogue history and extracted claims) generates the next logically chained follow-up question. Third, the Retest stage re-poses the initial demographic questions. An Evaluator then ingests the full interrogation log and extraction sets to compute three scores: Internal Consistency (IC) as a harmonic mean of cooperativeness and non-contradiction rate, External Consistency (EC) as a harmonic mean of coverage and non-refutation rate, and Retest Consistency (RC) via semantic equivalence between original and retest answers.

## Key Results
- **Human baseline scores:** IC=0.90, EC=0.66, RC=0.94 across 63 participants.
- **Top agent performance:** Best systems (Character.ai, Human Simulacra, Li et al.) achieved IC=0.71–0.79 and EC=0.59–0.71, lagging notably in internal and external consistency.
- **Critical failure modes:** Fine-tuned open-source models (OpenCharacter, Consistent LLM) showed extremely low cooperativeness (e.g., OpenCharacter Scoop=0.11), leading to very low IC. Inter-session retest consistency varied widely (e.g., Character.ai RC=0.55±0.22), indicating fundamental instability even with greedy decoding.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1: Logically Chained Follow-up Questioning
- **Claim:** Exposes internal contradictions that isolated pairwise checks miss by forcing the persona to sustain a coherent narrative across accumulated dialogue history.
- **Mechanism:** The Questioner generates each follow-up question based on logical implications of the preceding response (e.g., if persona states job title, next question asks for manager's name). Multi-hop contradictions surface only after several turns.
- **Core assumption:** A fabricated or unstable persona will eventually produce mutually incompatible statements under sustained, logically connected probing.
- **Evidence anchors:** [abstract] "logically chained follow-up questioning (internal consistency)"; [section 3.2] "Questioner generates a follow-up question qt derived from the logical implications of the preceding response"; [section 4.2] "Prior consistency evaluations... can miss contradictions that only surface when statements are accumulated across many turns."
- **Break condition:** If the persona evades with non-substantive answers (low cooperativeness), contradictions cannot be detected, yielding a false sense of consistency. The harmonic mean of cooperativeness and non-contradiction rate prevents this.

### Mechanism 2: Real-time Web Verification of Claims
- **Claim:** Grounds external consistency in objective, real-world facts by verifying extracted atomic claims against publicly available information.
- **Mechanism:** The Entity & Claim Extractor identifies web-searchable entities (e.g., institutions, locations) and generates atomic factual claims from each response. The Questioner performs a web search and poses a confirmation question. The Evaluator labels each claim as supported, refuted, or NEI (not enough information) based on retrieved evidence.
- **Core assumption:** For claims about real-world entities within the evaluation scope, publicly available web information exists to verify or refute them. The extraction and verification process penalizes agents that avoid concrete details or generate false specifics.
- **Evidence anchors:** [abstract] "real-time web verification of claims (external consistency)"; [section 3.2] "Entity & Claim Extractor identifies web-searchable entities... retrieves evidence via web search"; [section 3.3] "Coverage captures the agent’s ability to ground its responses in specific, verifiable factual details."
- **Break condition:** The mechanism depends on the availability of searchable public information. Claims about private, hyper-local, or poorly documented facts will be labeled NEI, reducing coverage and preventing a definitive external consistency assessment.

### Mechanism 3: Re-asking Initial Questions for Retest Stability
- **Claim:** Re-posing the same demographic questions after intervening dialogue reveals whether core identity attributes drift over a single session, even when prior context is available.
- **Mechanism:** After the Main Interrogation, the Retest stage re-asks the initial "Get-to-Know" questions. The Evaluator checks for approximate semantic equivalence between original and retest answers.
- **Core assumption:** A coherent persona should provide stable answers to invariant biographical questions regardless of intervening dialogue. Instability indicates the persona is not robustly grounded.
- **Evidence anchors:** [abstract] "repeated questioning (retest consistency)"; [section 3.2] "Retest... the initial questions Qpre from the get-to-know phase are re-asked"; [section 4.2] "Character.ai, OpenCharacter, and Consistent LLM scored well below the ceiling despite having access to their prior answers."
- **Break condition:** If the persona simply repeats memorized prior answers verbatim without understanding, it may score perfectly on retest while still being inconsistent internally or externally. Retest consistency alone is insufficient.

## Foundational Learning
- **Concept: Black-box, response-only evaluation**  
  *Why needed here:* PICon evaluates agents without access to internal states or training data, mimicking real-world deployment. Engineers must infer consistency solely from I/O transcripts, requiring robust extraction and verification from natural language.  
  *Quick check:* If an agent's system prompt or training data is hidden, what observable evidence would you use to judge if it maintains a consistent persona?

- **Concept: Harmonic mean scoring for multi-faceted metrics**  
  *Why needed here:* IC and EC are harmonic means (e.g., IC = 2·Scoop·Snc/(Scoop+Snc)). This penalizes extreme imbalance (e.g., high non-contradiction but low cooperativeness). Arithmetic mean would misleadingly reward evasion.  
  *Quick check:* Why would an agent that says "I don't know" to every question receive a near-zero IC score under harmonic mean, but a ~0.5 score under arithmetic mean?

- **Concept: Interrogation-inspired adversarial probing**  
  *Why needed here:* The framework's power comes from structured, logical drilling, not random QA. Engineers must design the Questioner to chain queries and the Extractor to target verifiable specifics, moving beyond superficial chit-chat consistency.  
  *Quick check:* How does asking "What is your job title?" followed by "What is your manager's name?" create a stronger consistency test than asking both in isolation?

## Architecture Onboarding

**Component map:**  
Get-to-Know (Q_pre) → Main Loop: Persona answers → Extractor (GPT-5.1) updates claim set E_t → Web search tool verifies claims → Questioner (GPT-5) uses dialogue history & E_t to generate q_t → (repeat T turns) → Retest (re-ask Q_pre) → Evaluator (Gemini-2.5-Flash) consumes full log H and all E_t to output IC, EC, RC.

**Critical path:**  
1. Pre-interrogation: Ask fixed demographic questions Q_pre.  
2. For each turn t=1..T:  
   a. Persona responds.  
   b. Extractor identifies NEW, atomic, verifiable entity-claim pairs from response, deduplicated against prior E_t.  
   c. Web search retrieves evidence for extracted claims.  
   d. Questioner generates next question based on logical implications of response and extracted claims.  
3. Post-interrogation: Re-ask Q_pre.  
4. Evaluator computes:  
   - IC: harmonic mean of cooperativeness (Scoop) and non-contradiction rate (Snc).  
   - EC: harmonic mean of coverage (¯p) and non-refutation rate (c).  
   - RC: semantic equivalence between original and retest answers.

**Design tradeoffs:**  
- Black-box vs. Coverage: Black-box evaluation is universally applicable but limited to surface responses; cannot probe internal representations.  
- Web verification depth vs. Cost: More claims per turn increase coverage but multiply search costs and noise; Extractor's F1=0.77 is critical.  
- Turn count (T): More turns increase stress-testing but risk context overflow and persona fatigue; paper finds relative rankings stable from 25–75 turns; default T=50 balances feasibility and depth.

**Failure signatures:**  
- Low IC with high Snc: Indicates low cooperativeness (evasion/irrelevance). Example: OpenCharacter (IC=0.16, Snc=0.54, Scoop=0.11).  
- Low EC with high ¯p: Indicates low coverage; agent makes few verifiable claims. Example: Consistent LLM (EC=0.30, ¯p=1.00, c=0.18).  
- High discarded rate (>0.5): Extractor generates claims persona later rejects in confirmation questions, suggesting unrealistic or misattributed entities.  
- Large session-to-session RC variance: Inherent instability; same persona definition yields different outputs across runs (e.g., Character.ai inter-session RC=0.55±0.22).

**First 3 experiments:**  
1. **Sanity check on human baseline:** Run PICon on 5–10 real humans. Expect IC~0.90, EC~0.66, RC~0.94. If far lower, inspect Questioner (overly adversarial?) or Evaluator (conflict rules too strict?).  
2. **Component validation:** Isolate the Extractor. Feed it 50 human responses from a known persona. Manually check precision/recall against gold extractions. Target F1 >0.75. If recall low, Extractor misses claims; if precision low, it generates spurious entities.  
3. **End-to-end smoke test on a simple persona:** Use a fine-tuned model with rigid profile (e.g., "I am a 30-year-old teacher in Paris"). IC should be high, EC moderate, RC near 1.0. If RC low, check context window issues or prompt leakage.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- **Question:** How can consistency evaluation be extended to assess subjective dimensions of persona fidelity, such as stylistic coherence and personality trait stability, which are currently excluded from PICon's scope?
- **Basis:** Limitations state PICon focuses on asserted content; stylistic coherence and personality stability are separate, non-logical dimensions. Evaluation scope excludes speaking style, preferences, values, personality as they "do not amount to logical contradiction."
- **Why unresolved:** PICon narrows scope to logically determinable content for reproducibility, but holistic persona fidelity likely requires these nebulous dimensions. No principled, operationalized framework exists.
- **Resolution evidence:** A follow-up study defining measurable, inter-annotator reliable criteria for stylistic/personality consistency (e.g., via validated LLM-as-judge rubrics) and showing correlation with downstream task performance or human "persona realism" judgments.

### Open Question 2
- **Question:** What architectural or training modifications can mitigate the fundamental instability in persona agents revealed by inter-session retest consistency analysis, where the same definition yields inconsistent responses across runs even with greedy decoding?
- **Basis:** Section 4.3 reports wide inter-session RC variance; greedy decoding did not consistently improve stability, indicating input ordering alone can destabilize responses.
- **Why unresolved:** Paper identifies problem but not root causes (prompt phrasing sensitivity, lack of grounding during inference, architectural biases in fine-tuned models). Critical barrier to reliable deployment.
- **Resolution evidence:** Ablation experiments isolating factors like prompt template design, context window management, RAG vs. fine-tuning, measuring RC across conditions to pinpoint instability sources.

### Open Question 3
- **Question:** To what extent does reliance on publicly searchable web evidence for external consistency systematically under-estimate true external consistency for claims about private, local, or temporal experiences?
- **Basis:** Limitations acknowledge evidence limited to public web info; local/private facts may be NEI, reducing coverage. Table 3 shows human baseline coverage only ~0.5, suggesting a ceiling effect.
- **Why unresolved:** EC score (harmonic mean of non-refutation rate and coverage) conflates agent's ability to generate verifiable facts with fact verifiability itself. Low coverage may reflect framework constraints, not persona flaws, making cross-agent EC comparisons misleading for personal domains.
- **Resolution evidence:** Controlled study verifying a subset of claims against a private ground-truth knowledge base (e.g., participant's pre-collected personal history) alongside web verification. Compare EC scores from both methods to quantify public-only bias.

### Open Question 4
- **Question:** Why do fine-tuned persona models exhibit dramatically lower cooperativeness under chained interrogation compared to prompting/RAG-based systems, and is this an inherent trade-off of the fine-tuning objective?
- **Basis:** Figure 2/Tables 2–3 show fine-tuned models have lowest IC/EC, primarily due to extremely low cooperativeness (OpenCharacter: 0.11, Consistent LLM: 0.20) and coverage. They maintain moderate non-contradiction but collapse into irrelevant/generic responses under logical pressure.
- **Why unresolved:** Paper observes pattern but does not hypothesize/test why fine-tuning leads to evasion. May be that fine-tuning on static persona dialogue does not train handling novel, probing question chains, encouraging a "safemode" of evasion.
- **Resolution evidence:** Error analysis of fine-tuned model failure modes (refusals, topic shifts, hallucinations). Compare fine-tuning datasets (dialogue diversity, question types) with PICon interrogation style. Train controlled fine-tuned model with adversarial, chained QA in fine-tuning set and measure cooperativeness changes.

### Open Question 5
- **Question:** How does PICon's performance and observed agent failures generalize beyond English-speaking, Western-educated populations, given the human baseline was recruited via snowball sampling with a Korean skew and uses World Value Survey questions?
- **Basis:** Human participants "predominantly in their 20s–40s, diverse nationalities but skewed Korean due to snowball sampling." Pre-defined demographic questions from World Value Survey have known cultural biases. Framework and agents largely developed/tested in English/Western contexts.
- **Why unresolved:** Persona consistency likely culturally contingent (norms around privacy, self-disclosure, factual precision vary). Human baseline may not represent universal consistency standard; interrogation logic (e.g., drilling for proper nouns) may be culturally specific. Applicability to non-Western personas or non-English interactions is open.
- **Resolution evidence:** Replicate study with humans and persona agents designed for non-Western cultural contexts (locally relevant demographic questions/personas). Cross-cultural analysis of consistency scores, failure modes, and cooperativeness to identify cultural biases in protocol.

## Limitations
- **Web verification scope:** EC relies on publicly searchable facts; private/hyper-local claims marked NEI, reducing coverage and potentially under-scoring agents with valid non-public knowledge.
- **Black-box evaluation:** Only assesses outputs, not internal states; instability sources (prompt leakage, context limits) remain inferred.
- **Component biases:** Extractor (F1=0.77) and Evaluator (Gemini-2.5-Flash) may introduce systematic errors; performance across diverse personas (non-Western, fictional) untested.

## Confidence
- **High:** Methodology soundness (three-stage pipeline) and human baseline scores (IC=0.90, EC=0.66, RC=0.94) from 63 participants.
- **Medium:** Agent comparisons (e.g., Character.ai’s IC=0.79) due to potential unmeasured biases in GPT-5 Questioner/Gemini Evaluator.
- **Low:** Generalizability beyond the 7 tested agents and 50-turn default; inter-session RC variance suggests high instability but root causes are speculative.

## Next Checks
1. **Extractor cross-persona validation:** Manually annotate 100 responses from 5 diverse personas (e.g., historical figures, fictional characters) to measure precision/recall drift beyond reported F1.
2. **NEI claim audit:** Sample 200 NEI-labeled external verification cases to quantify how often NEI reflects truly unverifiable claims vs. Extractor/Questioner overreach.
3. **Turn elasticity test:** Run 10 sessions per agent at 25, 50, 100 turns to confirm ranking stability and identify if longer dialogues systematically lower IC/EC (e.g., context window degradation).
