# Exposing Diversity Bias in Deep Generative Models: Statistical Origins and Correction of Diversity Error

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2602.14682  
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.14682  
- **Reference count:** 40  
- **Primary result:** Systematic downward diversity bias in deep generative models is exposed via reference-free entropy scores (Vendi, RKE); finite-sample entropy underestimation is proven, and entropy-based correction (regularization, guidance, projection) improves diversity with minimal fidelity loss.

## Executive Summary
This paper reveals that deep generative models (GANs, diffusion) consistently produce less diverse samples than real data across multiple datasets, as measured by reference-free entropy metrics (Vendi, RKE). The root cause is identified as a finite-sample bias: the expected kernel entropy of empirical samples underestimates the true population value and increases monotonically with sample size. Theoretically, the authors prove this monotonicity and establish a projection principle: moving a learned distribution into higher entropy regions (assuming the real distribution lies there) non-increases common divergence measures (MMD, KL). Empirically, entropy-based interventions—such as training-time regularization, inference-time guidance (SPARKE), and post-hoc reweighting—effectively close the diversity gap while preserving or slightly improving fidelity metrics like Frechet Distance.

## Method Summary
The study evaluates generative models (DiT, StyleGAN-XL, LDM) on ImageNet, FFHQ, and MS-COCO using DINOv2 embeddings. Diversity is quantified via Vendi (exponential von Neumann entropy of normalized kernel matrix) and RKE (inverse squared Frobenius norm). The core hypothesis is that models trained on finite datasets inherit an entropy underestimation bias. To correct this, the authors propose three interventions: (1) adding an entropy regularizer to the training objective, (2) applying gradient-based entropy guidance during sampling, and (3) reweighting generated samples post-hoc via convex projection onto an entropy superlevel set. Theoretical backing includes a proof that expected log-Vendi increases with sample size and that projection onto convex high-entropy sets reduces divergence under assumptions.

## Key Results
- Generated samples exhibit significantly lower Vendi/RKE scores than real test data across all evaluated models and datasets.
- Expected log-Vendi of real data increases monotonically with sample size, confirming finite-sample entropy underestimation.
- Entropy-based mitigation (regularization, guidance, projection) consistently raises diversity metrics (Vendi/RKE) while maintaining or improving fidelity (FD/KD).

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Finite-Sample Entropy Underestimation
- **Claim:** For i.i.d. samples from the true data distribution, the expected log-Vendi (or kernel entropy) is strictly less than the population value and increases monotonically with sample size.
- **Mechanism:** The empirical kernel covariance matrix is a low-rank, noisy approximation of the population operator; its von Neumann entropy is biased downward due to Jensen’s inequality applied to the random subset eigenvalue distribution.
- **Evidence:** Proposition 1 proves monotonicity; Figure 3 shows Vendi scores rising with n on real datasets.

### Entropy Superlevel Set Projection Principle
- **Claim:** If the true data distribution lies within a convex entropy superlevel set \(C_\rho = \{Q: H_{\text{VNE}}(Q) \geq \rho\}\), then projecting a learned distribution onto this set non-increases its MMD or KL divergence to the true distribution.
- **Mechanism:** Convexity of entropy superlevel sets (due to entropy’s concavity) ensures that any Hilbertian (MMD) or I-projection (KL) moves the learned distribution toward the true distribution when the latter is inside the set.
- **Evidence:** Theorem 1 formalizes the principle; Figure 5 illustrates the geometric argument.

### Entropy-Based Regularization/Guidance
- **Claim:** Differentiable entropy regularizers (e.g., inverse-RKE) during training or gradient-based guidance during inference increase the entropy of the generated distribution, thereby reducing diversity error with minimal fidelity cost.
- **Mechanism:** Gradients of entropy metrics push model parameters or samples toward higher-entropy regions of the embedding space.
- **Evidence:** Section 6.1 describes methods; Figures 1 & 7 and Table 1 show improved Vendi/RKE and stable FD/KD for DiT/StyleGAN with interventions.

## Foundational Learning

- **Kernel-based entropy (Vendi/RKE)**  
  Why needed: These reference-free metrics quantify sample diversity without requiring a reference dataset.  
  Quick check: *How does the Vendi score relate to the eigenvalues of the normalized kernel matrix?*  
  → Vendi = exp(∑ λ_i log λ_i), where λ_i are eigenvalues of the normalized kernel matrix.

- **Finite-sample bias in plug-in estimators**  
  Why needed: The paper extends the classic downward bias of Shannon entropy estimators to kernel entropy, explaining the origin of diversity loss.  
  Quick check: *Why is the plug-in Shannon entropy estimator downward biased?*  
  → Because entropy is concave; by Jensen’s inequality, the expected log-likelihood of an empirical distribution underestimates the true entropy.

- **Convex duality of entropy superlevel sets**  
  Why needed: The projection principle relies on the convexity of sets defined by minimal entropy to guarantee divergence reduction.  
  Quick check: *Why is the set \(\{Q: H(Q) \geq \rho\}\) convex when \(H\) is concave?*  
  → Superlevel sets of a concave function are convex; for any two distributions with entropy ≥ ρ, any mixture also has entropy ≥ ρ by concavity.

## Architecture Onboarding

### Component map
Training Data -> Generative Model (Qθ) -> Metrics Compute (Vendi/RKE/FD/KD) -> if diversity gap, apply: (a) Retrain with entropy regularization, (b) Inference guidance (SPARKE), or (c) Post-hoc reweighting (VNE projection).  
Key components: Evaluator (embedding extraction, kernel computation), Regularization module (adds -λ H_VNE to loss), Guidance module (gradient steps on samples), Post-hoc projector (solves weighted MMD + entropy objective).

### Critical path
1. Collect real dataset embeddings (DINOv2).  
2. Train or load pretrained generative model Qθ.  
3. Compute diversity and fidelity metrics on generated samples.  
4. If Vendi/RKE below real-data curve, apply mitigation (retrain, guide, or reweight).  
5. Re-evaluate to confirm improved diversity without large fidelity drop.

### Design tradeoffs
- **Exact Vendi vs. RKE**: Vendi requires O(n³) eigendecomposition but captures full spectrum; RKE is O(n²) via Frobenius norm but less sensitive.
- **Training-time vs. inference-time**: Regularization requires full retrain; guidance is plug-and-play but adds per-sample overhead.
- **Fidelity-diversity balance**: Theorem 1 guarantees no worse divergence if P_data in entropy set; in practice, small fidelity loss is common but often offset by gains in recall/coverage.

### Failure signatures
- Vendi/RKE do not increase after regularization/guidance (λ or η too small, poor kernel).
- FD/KD degrade significantly after intervention (λ or η too large, violating set assumption).
- Post-hoc reweighting yields extreme weights (feasibility region too tight, poor initialization).

### First 3 experiments
1. **Finite-sample bias check**: Compute Vendi/RKE on real dataset at increasing n (1k–20k); confirm monotonic increase.
2. **Model comparison**: Evaluate pretrained generative models (DiT, StyleGAN-XL) at fixed n (e.g., 10k); expect generated Vendi/RKE below real-data curve.
3. **Mitigation test**: Apply SPARKE to DiT or Projected-Vendi reweighting to BigGAN samples; measure ΔVendi/RKE and ΔFD/KD; expect diversity up, fidelity stable.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
1. **Scalable entropy projection**: Can we design algorithms for exact VNE projection that avoid O(n²) eigendecomposition? The paper notes computational challenges and leaves approximation methods without guarantees.
2. **Generalization beyond images**: Does the diversity bias extend to text, audio, or other architectures? Current evidence is limited to vision datasets and models.
3. **Theoretical guarantees for guidance**: What convergence/accuracy bounds exist for entropy-guided sampling (e.g., SPARKE)? The projection principle applies to distributions, but guidance is a heuristic without formal analysis.

## Limitations
- **Kernel/embedding dependence**: Vendi/RKE rely on DINOv2 and a fixed σ=35; results may not generalize to other features or bandwidths.
- **Projection assumption**: The guarantee requires P_data to lie in the chosen entropy superlevel set; true entropy is unknown and could violate this.
- **Hyperparameter sensitivity**: Regularization strength λ and guidance step η require tuning; minimal fidelity loss reported may not hold universally.

## Confidence
- **Finite-sample bias**: **High** – theorem and experiments align across datasets.
- **Projection principle**: **Medium** – theoretically sound but assumptions (P_data in set) not directly validated.
- **Mitigation efficacy**: **Medium** – empirical gains shown, but scalability and robustness need broader study.

## Next Checks
1. **Feature robustness**: Test Vendi/RKE with CLIP and ResNet embeddings, and vary kernel bandwidths across datasets; verify bias persists.
2. **True entropy measurement**: Estimate population entropy for ImageNet/FFHQ using N≥100k samples; confirm it exceeds model entropies.
3. **Hyperparameter sweep**: Systematically vary λ and η for DiT/StyleGAN (5 seeds each); map fidelity-diversity tradeoff curves and identify robust regimes.
