# Not All Steps are Informative: On the Linearity of LLMs' RLVR Training
## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2601.04537  
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.04537  
- **Reference count:** 14  
- **Primary result:** RLVR training exhibits near-linear weight and log-prob evolution (R² >0.7 / ~0.9), enabling extrapolation methods that achieve up to 6.1× speedup with matching or superior performance.

## Executive Summary
This paper reveals that during Reinforcement Learning from Verifiable Rewards (RLVR) for LLMs, model weights and token log-probabilities evolve almost linearly with training steps across diverse models (1.5B–8B) and algorithms (GRPO, Reinforce++, GSPO). This linearity implies most training steps contribute minimally to performance. Leveraging this, the authors propose Weight Extrapolation, Logits Extrapolation, and RL-Extra—an iterative scheme alternating RL updates with extrapolation—that significantly reduces required steps (up to 600 fewer) while matching or exceeding standard RL performance on math and coding benchmarks.

## Method Summary
The work accelerates RLVR by exploiting observed linear weight/logit dynamics. Standard RLVR (e.g., GRPO) is first run for an initial segment, with periodic checkpoints saved. **Weight Extrapolation** linearly projects weights forward: `W_t' = W_t0 + β(W_t1 - W_t0)` for `β>1`. **Logits Extrapolation** similarly projects future token logits. **RL-Extra** cycles between `m` steps of normal RL updates and `n` steps of weight extrapolation, effectively skipping intermediate updates while staying within the linear regime. Performance is evaluated on AIME24/25, MATH500, and LiveCodeBench using Pass@k/Avg@k metrics.

## Key Results
- Weights and log-probs show strong linearity (R² >0.7 for >80% of weights, ~0.9 for log-probs) across 1.5B–8B models and three RL algorithms.  
- Weight Extrapolation matches standard RL performance with up to 600 fewer steps.  
- Logits Extrapolation outperforms continued RL by up to 3% on AIME and LiveCodeBench.  
- RL-Extra achieves up to 6.1× speedup; on AIME24, it reaches 0.40 accuracy with 900 steps vs. >1100 for standard RL, and with aggressive settings matches 1100-step performance in only 180 steps.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The linearity is attributed to the Adam optimizer maintaining stable gradient distributions and update directions over many steps, preventing large deviations. However, non-linearities emerge at entropy collapse when the model's output distribution becomes overly deterministic, breaking the linear trend. Extrapolation methods succeed because they advance the model along the established linear trajectory without the small, redundant updates that fill the intermediate steps.

## Foundational Learning
- **RLVR (Reinforcement Learning from Verifiable Rewards)**: Fine-tunes LLMs using scalar rewards (e.g., math correctness) via policy gradient methods. *Why needed*: Enables domain-specific improvement without human-labeled data. *Quick check*: Can implement a simple GRPO loop with rollout sampling and reward scaling.  
- **Linear Training Dynamics**: Model parameters evolve as a near-linear function of step count. *Why needed*: Foundation for extrapolation-based acceleration. *Quick check*: Fit a line to weight snapshots at different steps; compute R².  
- **Adam Optimizer**: Adaptive moment estimation updates weights using moving averages of gradients and squared gradients. *Why needed*: Its stability is cited as cause of linear weight evolution. *Quick check*: Monitor gradient norm variance across RL steps; low variance correlates with linearity.  
- **Model Checkpoints**: Saved model states at specific training steps. *Why needed*: Required to compute extrapolation directions (`W_t1 - W_t0`). *Quick check*: Verify saved checkpoints contain identical parameter keys and recover exact training state.  
- **Token Log-Probabilities**: Log-softmax outputs over the vocabulary. *Why needed*: Show even higher linearity than weights, enabling Logits Extrapolation. *Quick check*: For a fixed prompt, compare logit vectors between checkpoints; compute cosine similarity of difference vectors.  
- **Benchmark Evaluation (AIME, MATH, LiveCodeBench)**: Standardized tests for reasoning and coding. *Why needed*: Measure generalization beyond training distribution. *Quick check*: Implement evaluation with `vllm` or similar for fast sampling; use exact match or pass@k metrics.

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component map**: Base Model -> RL Trainer (GRPO/Reinforce++) -> Periodic Checkpoint Saver -> Extrapolator (Weight/Logits) -> Evaluation Pipeline

**Critical path**: 
1. Initialize base model and RL trainer with hyperparameters (LR, batch size, rollout N).  
2. Run training loop, saving checkpoints at fixed intervals (e.g., every 50–100 steps).  
3. Select two checkpoints (`t0`, `t1`) within linear regime.  
4. Compute extrapolated parameters/logits for target step `t'` using linear projection.  
5. Either evaluate directly (extrapolation-only) or resume training from extrapolated state (RL-Extra cycles).

**Design tradeoffs**:  
- Checkpoint frequency: Too sparse risks missing linear regime; too frequent increases storage/compute.  
- Extrapolation factors (β, α): Larger values achieve bigger speedups but risk exiting linear regime and performance collapse.  
- RL-Extra cycle lengths (`m`, `n`): More RL steps (`m`) maintain stability; more extrapolation steps (`n`) increase speedup but may accumulate error.

**Failure signatures**:  
- Sharp drop in benchmark performance when extrapolating beyond linear horizon (e.g., β > ~1.5 for weight extrapolation).  
- Divergence in evaluation metrics (Pass@k) between extrapolated model and a normally trained checkpoint at the same nominal step.  
- Non-linear R² (<0.5) for a significant fraction of parameters, indicating deviation from linear trend.

**First three experiments**:  
1. **Replicate baseline RLVR**: Train DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Qwen-1.5B with GRPO for ~1200 steps; save checkpoints every 100 steps; evaluate on AIME24 to reproduce performance curve.  
2. **Validate linearity**: Randomly sample 0.1% of weights (or all logits) from checkpoints; fit linear model step→parameter; report R² distribution.  
3. **Test single-step extrapolation**: From checkpoints at steps 0 and 300, compute weight extrapolation for step 600 (β=2); evaluate on MATH500; compare to true 600-step checkpoint.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- **Open Question 1**: What causes the strong non-linearity observed at entropy collapse during RLVR training?  
- **Open Question 2**: How do RLVR dynamics differ when using gradient accumulation for large parameter updates instead of frequent incremental steps?  
- **Open Question 3**: Does the linearity phenomenon generalize to ultra-large models (>30B) and sparse architectures like Mixture-of-Experts?  
- **Open Question 4**: Is linearity stable in RLVR with complex multi-turn interactions?  
- **Open Question 5**: Can RLVR linearity be effectively leveraged in large-scale industrial deployments?

## Limitations
- Checkpoint sampling methodology (layer distribution, random seed) for linearity analysis is unspecified, making exact replication of R² statistics difficult.  
- Rollout configuration details (e.g., number of responses per prompt, length scheduling) are not fully disclosed; these may influence linear dynamics and extrapolation boundaries.  
- Extrapolation robustness is sensitive to β/α selection; the paper provides limited guidance for adapting to new models or tasks outside the tested 1.5B–8B dense transformers on math/coding.

## Confidence
| Claim                               | Confidence |  
|-------------------------------------|------------|  
| Linearity of weights/log-probs      | High       |  
| Weight/Logits Extrapolation gains   | Medium     |  
| RL-Extra speedup (up to 6.1×)       | Medium     |  

**Rationale**: Linearity is empirically robust across three model scales and three RL algorithms (R² >0.7 / ~0.9). Extrapolation and RL-Extra show clear improvements but are sensitive to hyperparameters (β, cycle lengths) and may fail if linearity is violated (e.g., near entropy collapse).
