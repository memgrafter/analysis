# Speech Codec Probing from Semantic and Phonetic Perspectives
## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2603.10371
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.10371
- **Reference count:** 0
- **Primary result:** Speech tokenizers for multimodal LLMs primarily encode phonetic, not lexical-semantic, structure.

## Executive Summary
This paper evaluates whether modern speech codecs, which discretize audio for multimodal LLMs, capture lexical-semantic meaning. Using a suite of probing methods—word-pair distance analysis, articulatory phonetic probing via Vocal Tract Distance (VTD), and cross-modal Centered Kernel Alignment (CKA)—the authors examine four codecs (EnCodec, DAC, MIMI, MIMO). Results consistently reveal a dominance of phonetic information over semantic structure. The phonetic signal is shown to be grounded in physiological speech production mechanisms through correlation with rt-MRI data. Cross-modal alignment between speech token representations and text-derived token spaces is low, even for codecs with a "semantic" layer. The findings imply that current tokenizers inherit biases from their training objectives and require explicit semantic constraints for effective multimodal integration.

## Method Summary
The probing pipeline uses LibriSpeech audio aligned to word timestamps via Montreal Forced Aligner (MFA). For each codec, latent features are extracted from every quantizer layer for each word segment. Word-pair analysis computes normalized Euclidean distances between synonyms (semantic) and near-homophones (phonetic) across layers. Articulatory grounding employs Projection Weighted Canonical Correlation Analysis (PWCCA) between codec features and time-aligned Vocal Tract Distance (VTD) features from a 75-speaker rt-MRI dataset. Cross-modal alignment measures CKA between pre-LLM speech token representations and corresponding text token embeddings from the integrated LLM (e.g., Moshi for MIMI), with a random-permutation baseline to control for dimensionality effects.

## Key Results
- **Phonetic dominance:** Near-homophone word-pair distances significantly exceed synonym distances across most layers for all four codecs, indicating phonetic encoding overshadows lexical-semantic structure.
- **Articulatory grounding:** Positive PWCCA correlation between codec features and VTD confirms the phonetic information is tied to speech production dynamics, not merely acoustic similarity.
- **Cross-modal misalignment:** CKA scores between speech and text token spaces are low (MIMI: 0.329, MIMO: 0.122) and show minimal improvement over random-permutation baselines, revealing poor structural alignment.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
**Mechanism 1: Phonetic Encoding Dominance**  
Claim: Speech codecs encode a higher proportion of phonetic information relative to lexical-semantic information.  
Mechanism: Layer-wise Euclidean distance analysis on pre-defined word pairs shows larger relative distances for near-homophones than synonyms across layers.  
Core assumption: Relative distance in the representational space reflects the relative importance of the underlying attribute.  
Evidence anchors: Consistent separation in Fig. 2 for all codecs; analog to SSL probing in Choi et al. [14].  
Break condition: If synonym distances were consistently and significantly smaller than near-homophone distances, the phonetic dominance claim would be invalidated.

**Mechanism 2: Articulatory Grounding**  
Claim: The phonetic information encoded by codecs is grounded in physiological speech production mechanisms.  
Mechanism: PWCCA correlation between codec layer features and time-aligned VTD from rt-MRI. High positive correlation indicates sensitivity to articulatory shape changes.  
Core assumption: VTD is a valid, low-level proxy for phonetic articulation.  
Evidence anchors: Positive VTD correlation curves in Fig. 3; high VTD correlation in MIMI's first "semantic" layer shows WavLM's prior is phonetic.  
Break condition: Low, flat, or negative PWCCA scores across layers would disconfirm production-grounded encoding.

**Mechanism 3: Cross-modal Misalignment**  
Claim: The phonetic bias leads to structural misalignment with text-derived semantic token spaces in MLLMs.  
Mechanism: CKA measures linear similarity between speech token latent space and text token latent space. Low scores, especially after permutation baseline correction, indicate dissimilar geometries.  
Core assumption: For effective multimodal integration, corresponding content should occupy similar representation geometries.  
Evidence anchors: Low absolute and baseline-corrected ΔCKA in Fig. 4; MIMI's higher absolute CKA attributed to low dimensionality.  
Break condition: CKA near 1.0 with large Δ would indicate strong alignment.

## Foundational Learning
**Concept: Semantic vs. Phonetic Information Distinction**  
Why needed: The core argument hinges on separating lexical meaning (synonymy) from production-based identity (homophony). Confusing these distorts probe interpretation.  
Quick check: If two words sound almost identical (near-homophones), should a "semantic" speech tokenizer place their representations closer together or further apart than two synonyms?

**Concept: Probing via Representational Similarity Analysis (RSA)**  
Why needed: The primary evidence uses distance-based probing. Understanding that we compare *relative* distances between pre-defined pairs to infer preserved structure is essential.  
Quick check: In a distance-based probe, what does it mean if the distance between synonym pairs is larger than the distance between random word pairs?

**Concept: Articulatory Grounding via rt-MRI**  
Why needed: Validity of Mechanism 2 depends on accepting VTD as a truthful measure of phonetic content, linking abstract representation to a physical cause.  
Quick check: How does correlating a codec's representation with a measure of vocal tract shape provide stronger evidence for phonetic encoding than showing it clusters homophones?

## Architecture Onboarding
Component map: LibriSpeech/rt-MRI input -> MFA word alignment -> Codec encoder -> Accumulated layer features -> Metrics (Word-pair Euclidean, VTD PWCCA, Cross-modal CKA) -> Analysis.
Critical path: For a new codec: (a) implement word-pair distance analysis; (b) if resources allow, run VTD correlation; (c) if intended for MLLMs, perform cross-modal CKA.
Design tradeoffs: probing depth vs. compute; MFA alignment fidelity; synonym/homophone dataset coverage; VTD data scarcity (75 speakers); CKA requires compatible LLM checkpoints.
Failure signatures: 1) Fading effect: distances converge to random baseline early (EnCodec/DAC). 2) Early convergence: all information in early layers (MIMI). 3) High CKA with low Δ: alignment due to shared dimensionality, not semantics.
First 3 experiments: 1) Replicate word-pair analysis on LibriSpeech with MFA/WordNet. Success: homophone distance > synonym distance in mid-layers. 2) Perform VTD correlation if rt-MRI available. Success: positive PWCCA in deeper layers. 3) Compute baseline-corrected CKA on paired LibriSpeech. Success: ΔCKA > 0.05.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
1. How can future speech tokenizers be designed with explicit semantic objectives to better align with text-derived semantics? The paper suggests adding constraints to bring synonymous words closer but provides no validated training paradigm.
2. Would distilling from models with genuine text-semantic understanding (e.g., LLM text embeddings or cross-modal encoders) yield better lexical-semantic alignment than distillation from SSL speech models like WavLM? No comparison is presented.
3. Do the observed phonetic dominance and semantic misalignment generalize beyond English and beyond the four studied codecs? The study is English-only and model-limited; cross-lingual and architectural variations are unexamined.
4. Is the low cross-modal CKA fundamentally due to the tokenizers' lack of semantic encoding, or could it be an artifact of CKA's sensitivity to dimensionality mismatches or non-linear projections in MLLMs? The metric's suitability for cross-modal alignment post-projection is questioned.
5. To what extent does the residual acoustic information in deeper codec layers interfere with any semantic signal, and could a different layering or disentanglement strategy preserve more semantics? It is unclear if phonetic dominance is unavoidable under standard RVQ residual objectives.

## Limitations
- VTD upsampling method (to match codec frame rate) is unspecified, potentially biasing PWCCA.
- Details for extracting text embeddings in CKA (specific LLM layer, tokenizer) are ambiguous.
- The rt-MRI dataset is small (75 speakers), limiting generalizability of articulatory grounding.
- Forced alignment errors may contaminate word-pair distance measurements.

## Confidence
- **Phonetic dominance** (Mechanism 1): High. Clear and consistent separation of near-homophone vs. synonym distances across layers in all four codecs.
- **Articulatory grounding** (Mechanism 2): Medium. Positive VTD correlation observed, but dependent on upsampling choices and a small rt-MRI sample.
- **Cross-modal misalignment** (Mechanism 3): Medium. Low ΔCKA suggests misalignment, yet uncertainty in text representation extraction and limited codec-LLM pairs.

## Next Checks
1. **VTD upsampling robustness:** Repeat PWCCA using linear, cubic spline, and nearest-neighbor upsampling to test stability of the articulatory grounding claim.
2. **Text embedding layer sweep:** Compute CKA across multiple layers of the integrated LLM; verify if low alignment holds regardless of layer choice.
3. **Novel codec probing:** Apply the word-pair distance analysis to a recent semantic-focused codec (e.g., SpeechTokenizer) to test the phonetic dominance hypothesis.
