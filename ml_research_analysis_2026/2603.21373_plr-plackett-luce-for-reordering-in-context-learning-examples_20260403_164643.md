# PLR: Plackett-Luce for Reordering In-Context Learning Examples

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID**: 2603.21373
- **Source URL**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.21373
- **Reference count**: 31
- **Primary result**: PLR improves in-context learning by learning a distribution over example orderings, achieving consistent accuracy gains on classification and reasoning benchmarks, especially on open-ended reasoning tasks where label-based methods fail.

## Executive Summary
PLR tackles the challenge of finding optimal orderings of in-context examples for large language models. Since exhaustive permutation search is intractable, PLR introduces a learnable Plackett-Luce distribution over permutations. It samples candidate orderings using Gumbel perturb-and-sort and updates the distribution to concentrate probability on permutations that yield high accuracy when used as context. The method is evaluated on five text classification datasets (MR, NEWS, SST5, TREC, SUBJ) and three reasoning datasets (GSM8K, DeepMath, Math500) using Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct and Llama3.1-8B-Instruct, with shot counts *k* ∈ {4,8,16,32}. PLR variants (EMA, MLE, mixture) consistently outperform baselines like Top-K and label-based ordering methods. Notably, on SUBJ, PLR-4 achieves 85.98% accuracy at 32 shots versus 82.32% for Top-K; on GSM8K, PLR-4 gains 4.83% over Top-K at 4 shots. Crucially, PLR succeeds on open-ended reasoning tasks where methods that rely on label distributions fail, demonstrating the broad applicability of the approach.

## Method Summary
PLR models the permutation distribution using a Plackett-Luce model, where each example is assigned a utility score. The probability of a permutation is proportional to the product of normalized utilities in the order of appearance. To sample permutations efficiently, PLR adds independent Gumbel noise to the utilities and sorts the resulting values, a technique that yields exact samples from the Plackett-Luce distribution and is differentiable. Given a sampled ordering, it is used as the context for the target LLM, and the model's accuracy on a validation set (or a reward signal) is computed. The utilities are then updated—via methods such as exponential moving average (EMA), maximum likelihood estimation (MLE), or a mixture—to increase the probability of orderings that achieve high accuracy. This iterative process allows PLR to focus probability mass on effective permutations without enumerating the full permutation space.

## Key Results
- On the SUBJ dataset, PLR-4 achieves 85.98% accuracy at 32 shots, surpassing Top-K's 82.32%.
- On GSM8K, PLR-4 gains 4.83% accuracy over Top-K at 4 shots.
- PLR consistently outperforms baselines across all five classification benchmarks and demonstrates significant improvements on all three reasoning tasks, particularly where label-dependent ordering methods fail.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The Plackett-Luce formulation provides a principled, tractable way to represent and sample from a distribution over permutations. By using Gumbel perturb-and-sort, PLR obtains differentiable samples, enabling gradient-based optimization of the utilities towards orderings that maximize validation accuracy. This approach efficiently explores the permutation space, concentrating probability on high-performing orderings without exhaustive search. The success on open-ended reasoning tasks suggests that modeling latent utilities—rather than relying on superficial label statistics—captures more robust notions of example relevance for complex inference.

## Foundational Learning
- **Plackett-Luce model**: A discrete choice model that defines a probability distribution over permutations. *Why needed?* It offers a flexible, mathematically convenient way to parameterize distributions over the combinatorial space of orderings. *Quick check*: Given utilities \(u_i\) for items \(i=1..n\), write the probability of permutation \(\pi\) under the Plackett-Luce model.
- **Gumbel perturb-and-sort**: A method to sample from the Plackett-Luce distribution by adding i.i.d. Gumbel noise to utilities and sorting. *Why needed?* It makes sampling differentiable, allowing gradient-based learning of utilities. *Quick check*: Explain why adding Gumbel noise before sorting yields a sample from the Plackett-Luce distribution.
- **In-context learning (ICL)**: The paradigm where a language model processes a sequence of labeled examples before making a prediction, without parameter updates. *Why needed?* The order of examples in the context can dramatically affect model performance; optimizing this ordering can improve ICL accuracy. *Quick check*: Provide an intuitive example of how the order of in-context examples might influence a model's prediction.

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map**: Utility Scorer → Plackett-Luce Parameterization → Gumbel Perturbation & Sorting → Permutation Sampler → LLM (ICL) → Accuracy Evaluator → Parameter Update (EMA/MLE) → Utility Scorer (cycle)
- **Critical path**: Initialize utility model; repeat: sample permutations via Gumbel sort; run ICL with each permutation on validation data; compute accuracy reward; update utilities to increase probability of high-reward permutations; until convergence or budget exhausted.
- **Design tradeoffs**: Exploration (Gumbel temperature) vs. convergence speed; computational cost of evaluating many permutations on the LLM; bias-variance tradeoff in reward estimation from finite validation samples; model capacity of utility scorer vs. overfitting.
- **Failure signatures**: Mode collapse (probability mass concentrates on a single possibly suboptimal permutation); utility model fails to capture meaningful example qualities leading to poor rankings; high variance in reward estimates causing unstable or stalled learning.
- **First experiments**: (1) Verify PLR can recover a known optimal ordering in a synthetic task with ground-truth utilities. (2) Compare convergence and final performance against random permutation search and a greedy heuristic that iteratively adds best next example. (3) Ablate the Gumbel noise (use deterministic sorting) to measure impact on exploration and final utility.

## Limitations
- **Model & task scope**: Evaluations restricted to two instruction-tuned models and eight tasks; generalizability to other architectures, domains, or longer contexts is unknown.
- **Ablation depth**: The paper does not isolate why PLR excels on reasoning tasks; the interplay between latent utility modeling and task type remains unexplained.
- **Baseline gaps**: No direct comparisons with recent ordering methods like OptiSeq or influence-based selection, leaving relative performance unclear.

## Confidence
- **Classification improvements**: High
- **Reasoning task gains & label-method failure**: Medium
- **Computational efficiency claims**: Low

## Next Checks
1. Test PLR on at least three additional model families (e.g., Mistral, Phi, GPT-series) and two non-English tasks to probe generalizability.
2. Perform mechanistic ablations on reasoning tasks by varying utility function design (label-dependent vs. label-agnostic) and Gumbel temperature to understand success factors.
