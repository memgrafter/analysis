# When Humans Judge Irises: Pupil Size Normalization as an Aid and Synthetic Irises as a Challenge

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2601.06725
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.06725
- **Reference Count:** 16
- **Primary Result:** Nonlinearly deforming both images in a pair to a middle pupil size improved human iris verification accuracy by 7 percentage points (76.4% → 83.4%), while StyleGAN synthetic same-eye pairs were misjudged as "different" significantly more often than authentic same-eye pairs.

## Executive Summary
This study examines two key challenges for human forensic iris verification through online experiments: the impact of varying pupil sizes and the introduction of high-fidelity synthetic iris images. For pupil size variation, normalizing images to a common, intermediate pupil size using a nonlinear deformation model (EyePreserve) significantly boosted human accuracy. Conversely, for synthetic irises generated by StyleGAN, human accuracy for genuine (same-eye) pairs dropped markedly compared to authentic pairs, while impostor pair accuracy remained high. The findings suggest that while geometric alignment aids human comparison, subtle perceptual gaps persist between state-of-the-art synthetic irises and their authentic counterparts.

## Method Summary
The study utilized the Notre Dame iris dataset, pre-filtered using the HDBIF matcher (genuine scores < 0.35, impostor > 0.35) and ISO/IEC 19794-6 quality standards. For Scenario I (pupil size), 140 image pairs were presented to 100 subjects after applying either Daugman's linear rubber-sheet model or the nonlinear EyePreserve autoencoder to deform both images to target pupil sizes (small, middle, large). For Scenario II (synthetic irises), 80 pairs (40 authentic, 40 synthetic) from StyleGAN-generated images were shown to 163 subjects. All experiments were conducted online via Qualtrics, with each pair displayed for 6 seconds and responses collected on a 5-point Likert scale.

## Key Results
- Deforming both images to a middle pupil size with a nonlinear model yielded the highest human accuracy (83.4%), a significant 7-point gain over original images (76.4%).
- For synthetic genuine pairs, human accuracy (57.55%) was significantly lower than for authentic genuine pairs (75.00%), with p < 0.0001.
- Deformation to a large pupil size reduced accuracy compared to original images, while the nonlinear model outperformed the linear model specifically for middle-size alignment.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1: Pupil Size Normalization
- **Claim:** Aligning both images to a middle pupil size via nonlinear deformation improves human accuracy by standardizing the geometric distortion of iris texture.
- **Mechanism:** The deformation resamples the annular iris texture to a common geometry, reducing confounding scaling differences and allowing focus on stable textural features (crypts, furrows). This aligns with the biological non-uniform dilation of the iris.
- **Core Assumption:** Human comparison relies on matching localized texture patterns, which are geometrically distorted by pupil size changes.
- **Evidence Anchors:** [abstract, section 5.2]; nonlinear middle-size alignment improved from 76.4% to 83.4%. Deformation to a large size was detrimental.

### Mechanism 2: Synthetic Genuine Pair Deficit
- **Claim:** Humans judge same-eye synthetic iris pairs (StyleGAN) as "different" more often than authentic same-eye pairs.
- **Mechanism:** Even high-fidelity synthetic irises may not fully replicate subtle, identity-preserving micro-structures and texture statistics present in authentic samples. Humans detect perceptual discrepancies that automated HDBIF scoring overlooks.
- **Core Assumption:** Human examiners leverage perceptual cues beyond the feature space used to train/evaluate the synthetic generator.
- **Evidence Anchors:** [abstract, section 5.3 & Table 2]; synthetic genuine accuracy (57.55%) vs. authentic (75.00%), p<0.0001, despite higher HDBIF scores for synthetic pairs.

### Mechanism 3: Model-Specific Alignment Efficacy
- **Claim:** The nonlinear (EyePreserve) model is superior for middle-pupil alignment, while the linear model is slightly better for extreme alignments.
- **Mechanism:** The nonlinear model better captures biological muscle constriction/dilation, which is critical for bidirectional, moderate deformations to an intermediate size. Simpler linear warping may suffice for unidirectional, extreme deformations by introducing fewer interpolation artifacts in those specific cases.
- **Core Assumption:** Biological accuracy of deformation matters most for moderate, bidirectional warping.
- **Evidence Anchors:** [section 5.2]; For middle-size: nonlinear (90.30% genuine) > linear (85.30% genuine). For overall accuracy with various targets, linear was slightly higher (79.67% vs. 77.17%).

  - **Why needed here:** The study depends on understanding that the annular iris texture's apparent geometry changes with pupil size, distorting crypts and furrows and complicating comparison.
  - **Quick Check:** "What happens to the apparent spacing and polarity of iris furrows when the pupil dilates versus constricts?"
- **Concept:** Synthetic Media & Fidelity Metrics
  - **Why needed here:** To interpret why synthetic irises fool some metrics (HDBIF) but not humans, one must distinguish between algorithmic biometric matching scores and human perceptual realism.
  - **Quick Check:** "If a generative model creates two 'same identity' images that score highly on an automated matcher, what perceptual cue might a human examiner still notice?"
- **Concept:** Deformation Models (Linear vs. Nonlinear)
  - **Why needed here:** The core intervention is deformation; understanding that linear scales uniformly while nonlinear varies locally to mimic muscle contraction is key to interpreting accuracy differences.
  - **Quick Check:** "Why is a 'rubber sheet' linear model insufficient to model real iris deformation during constriction?"

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component Map:** 
  Data Source & Filtering (Notre Dame + HDBIF) -> Pupil Size Metric (PIR calculation) -> Deformation Engine (EyePreserve or Daugman's model) -> Pair Generator (genuine/impostor variants) -> Synthetic Generator (StyleGAN) -> Experiment Interface (Qualtrics) -> Analysis Pipeline (χ² tests).
- **Critical Path:** Data Selection -> Deformation/Synthesis -> Pair Categorization -> Human Experiment Execution -> Response Aggregation -> Statistical Comparison.
- **Design Tradeoffs:** 
  - *Deformation model:* Nonlinear is biologically plausible and best for middle alignment but computationally heavier; Linear is faster and slightly better for extreme alignments.
  - *Synthetic data:* StyleGAN provided better same-eye separation than diffusion in feature space, but human accuracy suggests remaining perceptual fidelity gaps.
  - *Deployment:* Online (Qualtrics) enables scalability but sacrifices control over display conditions and participant environment.
- **Failure Signatures:** 
  - Accuracy collapse for pairs with very large initial PIR differences (>0.4 delta) even after deformation.
  - Systematically lower "Same" confidence ratings for synthetic genuine pairs vs. authentic.
  - High inter-subject variability on borderline pairs (e.g., PIR ~0.45 vs 0.48).
- **First 3 Experiments:**
  1. Replicate Scenario I's core finding: test middle-size nonlinear deformation vs. original on a new subject cohort to confirm the ~7 percentage point boost.
  2. Direct Model Comparison: test nonlinear (EyePreserve) vs. linear on the same set of middle-sized target pairs to verify the reported 5-point genuine accuracy gap (90.3% vs 85.3%).
  3. Synthetic Fidelity Probe: present triads (authentic A/authentic B, synthetic A/synthetic B, authentic A/synthetic B) for same-eye identities to isolate whether the "authentic" or "synthetic" image in a cross-type pair drives the "different" judgment.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
### Open Question 1
- **Question:** Why do humans misclassify same-eye synthetic iris pairs as different at a significantly higher rate (57.55% accuracy) compared to authentic same-eye pairs (75.00% accuracy)?
- **Basis in paper:** The paper explicitly states the significant drop (p<0.0001) but does not explain the underlying perceptual cause.
- **Why unresolved:** The root cause—whether subtle texture differences, missing features, or perceptual artifacts—is unknown and critical for improving synthetic iris generation and forensic training.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Psychophysical experiments using masked/altered images to isolate specific regions, and quantitative texture analysis (e.g., local phase quantization) comparing correctly vs. incorrectly judged synthetic pairs.

### Open Question 2
- **Question:** Would performance findings generalize to trained forensic iris examiners, especially for synthetic genuine pairs?
- **Basis in paper:** The study recruited non-expert students, staff, and online participants; the paper notes a human expert may review algorithm outputs but did not test experts.
- **Why unresolved:** Expert examiners have extensive experience and may use different strategies; their performance on these specific challenges is unknown and vital for real-world forensics.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Replication of both scenarios with a cohort of certified forensic iris examiners, comparing accuracy, confidence, and decision times against the novice sample.

### Open Question 3
- **Question:** Is aligning to the pair's average pupil size ("middle") optimal, or could other canonical sizes yield higher accuracy? Does optimal size differ between deformation models?
- **Basis in paper:** The paper reports that deforming to a middle size gave highest accuracy but only tested that specific definition (pair average); it does not claim global optimality.
- **Why unresolved:** A data-driven search over a range of target pupil sizes might find a better trade-off between discriminator retention and artifact minimization.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** An expanded experiment where genuine pairs are aligned to various predefined population-based pupil sizes for both deformation models, measuring human accuracy for each target to locate performance peaks.

### Open Question 4
- **Question:** How do state-of-the-art automated iris matchers perform on the exact same challenging sets used here, and where do human and algorithm errors diverge?
- **Basis in paper:** The study focuses solely on human performance; no algorithmic comparison on the same test pairs is provided.
- **Why unresolved:** Forensic workflows may combine human and algorithmic judgments; knowing comparative strengths on these specific challenges is needed for hybrid systems.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Evaluating top-performing iris matchers (e.g., NIST IRIS participants) on the identical image pairs from Scenarios I and II, followed by error analysis to identify conditions where humans outperform algorithms.

## Limitations
- **HDBIF Matcher Specifics:** Exact implementation, segmentation, and normalization steps for the HDBIF score threshold (0.35) are unreported, risking pair selection mismatch.
- **Synthetic Generation Parameters:** StyleGAN architecture, training details, and precise ISO/IEC 19794-6 quality thresholds are not provided, hindering faithful replication.
- **Experiment External Validity:** Online deployment (Qualtrics) lacks control over display calibration and participant environment; subject screening criteria and demographics are unspecified.

## Confidence
- **Pupil Normalization Efficacy (Middle-size, Nonlinear):** High. The 7-point gain is statistically robust and physiologically plausible.
- **Nonlinear Model Superiority for Middle Alignment:** Medium. The 5-point gap is significant but may be confounded by unseen EyePreserve artifacts.
- **Synthetic Genuine Pair Deficit:** High. The large, highly significant drop (p<0.0001) is consistent with known fidelity gaps.
- **Generalizability to Real Forensics:** Low. Brief, online crowdsourced judgments differ from controlled expert examination protocols.

## Next Checks
1. **Artifact–Performance Correlation:** Visually inspect a stratified sample of deformed images (especially "large" target) for blurring/hallucination; correlate artifact severity metrics with individual subject error rates.
2. **Perceptual–Algorithmic Fidelity Probe:** Compute HDBIF scores for all synthetic pairs used in Scenario II; plot human accuracy against HDBIF score distributions to isolate whether discrepancies stem from perceptual gaps or score threshold misalignment.
3. **Deformation Limit Test:** Construct genuine pairs with extreme initial pupil-to-iris ratio (PIR) differences (>0.4); test whether middle-size nonlinear deformation still improves accuracy or hits a breakpoint where distortion overwhelms alignment benefits.
