# Radial-VCReg: More Informative Representation Learning Through Radial Gaussianization
## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2602.14272
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.14272
- **Reference count:** 40
- **Primary result:** Radial-VCReg augments VCReg with a radial Gaussianization loss aligning feature norms to the Chi distribution, yielding ~1.8% higher linear probe accuracy on CIFAR-100 (65.99% vs 64.23% with ResNet18, d=512) and consistent gains across benchmarks; performance correlates with Chi alignment.

## Executive Summary
Radial-VCReg introduces a radial Gaussianization loss that explicitly shapes the distribution of feature norms to follow the Chi distribution—a key property of high-dimensional Gaussians. This extends VCReg, which only enforces second-order statistics (mean and covariance), by addressing the radial dimension. Theoretically, Radial-VCReg can transform a broader class of distributions (all elliptically symmetric ones) toward Gaussianity compared to VCReg. Empirically, the method reduces Wasserstein distance to the standard normal on synthetic non-elliptical data and achieves consistent improvements in linear probe accuracy on CIFAR-100 (by ~1.8%), ImageNet-10, and CelebA. The correlation between downstream performance and the alignment of radius distribution to the Chi distribution supports the proposed mechanism.

## Method Summary
Radial-VCReg extends the VICReg self-supervised learning framework by adding a radial Gaussianization loss term. Given a batch of representations \(Z \in \mathbb{R}^{N \times d_{\text{out}}}\), the loss computes the norms \(r_i = \|z_i\|\) and estimates the KL divergence between the empirical distribution of sorted radii and the Chi distribution \(\chi(d_{\text{out}})\). This is achieved via a cross-entropy term (encouraging mode/mean matching) and an m-spacing entropy term (encouraging spread). The total loss combines VICReg’s invariance (MSE between positive pairs), variance (ensuring each dimension has sufficient variance), and covariance (decorrelating dimensions) losses, plus the radial loss applied to each view. Training uses LARS for ResNet and AdamW for ViT, with standard augmentations and a 3-layer MLP projector. Evaluation is performed by linear probing on frozen representations, yielding significant gains over VICReg on multiple datasets.

## Key Results
- Linear probe on CIFAR-100: 65.99%±0.08 (Radial-VICReg, ResNet18, d=512) vs 64.23%±0.10 (VICReg).
- On synthetic X-distribution, Radial-VCReg reduces Wasserstein distance to \( \mathcal{N}(0,I) \) more effectively than VCReg.
- Consistent 1-2% improvements on ImageNet-10 and CelebA multi-label classification; performance positively correlates with lower Wasserstein distance between empirical radii and \(\chi(d)\).

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1
- **Claim:** Radial-VICReg reduces higher-order dependencies in learned features more effectively than VICReg by aligning feature norms to the Chi distribution.
- **Mechanism:** For a d-dimensional isotropic Gaussian, feature norms follow a Chi distribution with d degrees of freedom. Radial-VICReg explicitly minimizes the KL divergence between the empirical radius distribution and the Chi distribution via a cross-entropy term (encouraging mode/mean matching) and an entropy term (encouraging spread). This radial constraint is added to the VICReg invariance, variance, and covariance losses.
- **Core assumption:** The data representation can be Gaussianized by spherical whitening (covariance regularization) followed by radial shaping; this is a necessary (but not sufficient) condition for full Gaussianity under ellipticity.
- **Evidence anchors:** [abstract] "augments VCReg with a radial Gaussianization loss that aligns feature norms with the Chi distribution—a defining property of high-dimensional Gaussians." [section 2.3] Derivation of the radial Gaussianization loss \( r(Z;\beta_1,\beta_2) \) as a KL estimator between empirical radii and \(\chi(d_{\text{out}})\). [corpus] Neighbors focus on covariance-based regularization (e.g., "Variance-Covariance Regularization") but do not directly address radial shaping; evidence here is contextual rather than direct.
- **Break condition:** If the underlying distribution is not elliptically symmetric (e.g., sunshine distribution in Appendix 8.1), radial Gaussianization alone cannot achieve full Gaussianity, though it may still improve over VICReg.

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim:** Radial-VCReg theoretically Gaussianizes a strictly broader class of distributions than VCReg.
- **Mechanism:** VCReg whitens features (enforcing zero mean, identity covariance), which only transforms elliptically symmetric distributions to spherically symmetric ones. Radial-VCReg additionally re-maps the radius via a quantile transform to match the Chi CDF, which can transform *any* distribution with identity covariance toward a Gaussian—provided the whitening step is valid.
- **Core assumption:** Whitening (covariance regularization) is feasible and meaningful; the radial transform defined in Eq. (10) is applied post-whitening.
- **Evidence anchors:** [abstract] "We prove that Radial-VCReg transforms a broader class of distributions towards normality compared to VCReg." [section 7] Proposition 1 and proof: \( \mathcal{F}_{\text{VCReg}} \subsetneq \mathcal{F}_{\text{Radial-VCReg}} \) because VCReg only Gaussianizes Gaussian distributions, while Radial-VCReg can Gaussianize all elliptically symmetric distributions. [corpus] Weak; neighboring papers do not discuss set inclusions of Gaussianizable distributions.
- **Break condition:** The hold set \( \mathcal{F}_{\text{Radial-VCReg}} \) assumes distributions are *elliptically symmetric* after whitening. Non-elliptical distributions (like X-distribution) may be *pushed* toward Gaussianity but are not guaranteed to reach it.

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim:** Better alignment of the radius distribution with the Chi distribution correlates with higher downstream linear probe accuracy.
- **Mechanism:** The radial loss encourages features to occupy the high-dimensional Gaussian "thin shell," increasing feature diversity and entropy. This mitigates collapse modes where features have low norm variance. Improved Chi alignment (lower Wasserstein distance) indicates reduced higher-order dependencies, yielding more informative representations.
- **Core assumption:** Linear probe accuracy is a valid proxy for representation quality; Chi distribution is the correct target for radius in high-dim Gaussians.
- **Evidence anchors:** [abstract] "Performance correlates with closer alignment of the radius distribution to the Chi distribution." [section 2] Figure 2d: Scatter plot shows validation accuracy increases as Wasserstein distance to \(\chi\) decreases across hyperparameter sweeps. [section 4] Tables 1–3: Radial-VICReg consistently outperforms VICReg on CIFAR-100, ImageNet-10, and CelebA. [corpus] Weak; neighboring papers do not measure radius distribution alignment or its correlation with accuracy.
- **Break condition:** Correlation is not causation; other factors (e.g., optimizer interactions) could explain gains. The relationship may not hold for all architectures/datasets.

## Foundational Learning
- **Concept: Mutual Information Maximization (InfoMax)**
  - *Why needed here:* Self-supervised learning aims to maximize mutual information \( I(Z;Z') \) between augmented views. This requires both invariance (positive pairs close) and high marginal entropy (diverse features). Radial-VICReg directly targets the entropy term via radial shaping.
  - *Quick check question:* Why does maximizing \( H(Z) \) alone risk collapse if not paired with invariance regularization?
- **Concept: Maximum Entropy Distributions**
  - *Why needed here:* Gaussian is the maximum entropy distribution for fixed mean/covariance. Aligning radii to Chi (a property of Gaussians) is a step toward achieving maximum entropy in high dimensions.
  - *Quick check question:* Given fixed variance, which distribution maximizes entropy: Gaussian, uniform on a sphere, or Laplace?
- **Concept: Elliptically Symmetric Distributions (ESD) & Whitening**
  - *Why needed here:* Whitening (VCReg) transforms any ESD to a spherically symmetric one. Radial shaping then completes the Gaussianization *if* the post-whitening distribution is ESD. This theory justifies the two-step approach.
  - *Quick check question:* Does whitening a uniform distribution on a sphere yield another spherical distribution? Why or why not?

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map:**
  1. **Backbone** (ResNet18, ViT): Processes input `x` → feature map.
  2. **Projector** (MLP, usually 3-layer): Maps backbone output `h(x)` to representation `z ∈ ℝ^d_out`.
  3. **Loss modules:**
     - Invariance: `s(Z,Z') = mean(||z_i - z'_i||²)`.
     - Variance: `v(Z) = mean_j max(0, γ - sqrt(Var(z_j)+ε))`.
     - Covariance: `c(Z) = mean_{i≠j} [Cov(Z)_{i,j}²]`.
     - Radial Gaussianization: `r(Z;β1,β2)` (Eqs. 6–7), using cross-entropy and m-spacing entropy estimators on sorted radii `||z_i||`.
  4. **Aggregator:** `L = λ1 s + λ2(v(Z)+v(Z')) + λ3(c(Z)+c(Z')) + r(Z) + r(Z')`.
- **Critical path:**
  1. Forward pass: `x → t(x), t'(x) → z, z'`.
  2. Compute `s, v, c` on `Z, Z'`.
  3. Compute radii `r_i = ||z_i||`, sort, compute `r(Z)` via Eq. (6–7).
  4. Backpropagate total loss `L`.
- **Design tradeoffs:**
  - **β1 vs β2:** β1 controls cross-entropy (mode alignment), β2 controls entropy (spread). Table 4 shows β2>0 can hurt performance if unaccompanied by sufficient β1.
  - **m-spacing hyperparameter:** Larger `m` reduces entropy estimator variance but increases bias; default often `m=⌊√N⌋`.
  - **Computational cost:** Sorting radii each forward pass is `O(N log N)`. For large batches, approximate quantile estimators could replace sorting.
  - **Projector dimension dependence:** Gains are larger at `d=512` than `d=2048/8192` (Tables 1–2), possibly because Chi approximation improves in lower dimensions.
- **Failure signatures:**
  - **Radius distribution mismatch:** High Wasserstein distance to `χ(d_out)` despite training (inspect histogram of `||z_i||`).
  - **Degraded invariance:** If `λ1` (invariance weight) is too low relative to radial loss, positive pairs may diverge.
  - **Entropy-only collapse:** Setting `β2>0` with `β1=0` (Table 4: Radial ENT) can increase norm variance without aligning to Chi, harming accuracy.
- **First 3 experiments:**
  1. **Synthetic X-distribution** (section 3, Fig. 1): Optimize samples from non-elliptical `X` with Radial-VCReg; verify points become spherical and Wasserstein distance to `N(0,I)` decreases vs VCReg.
  2. **CIFAR-100 linear probe** (Table 1): Pretrain ResNet18 with `d_out=512`, compare Top-1 accuracy of Radial-VICReg vs VICReg; expect ~1.5% gain.
  3. **Radius distribution analysis** (Fig. 2): Plot histogram of `||z_i||` after pretraining; compute `W1(pr, χ(d_out))`. Expect Radial-VICReg to yield distribution much closer to Chi than VICReg.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- **Question:** What constraints beyond Radial-VCReg ensure exact Gaussianity for arbitrary feature distributions?
  - *Basis:* The paper states Radial-VCReg is not sufficient for perfect Gaussianity; the Sunshine distribution (Figure 3a) remains non-Gaussian despite Chi radius.
  - *Why unresolved:* Radial-VCReg only transforms elliptically symmetric distributions; non-elliptical data still contain higher-order dependencies. What additional statistical constraints are necessary and sufficient?
  - *What evidence:* Derive a regularization term that, combined with Radial-VCReg, provably Gaussianizes non-elliptical distributions (e.g., Sunshine) and verify via Wasserstein distance.

- **Question:** Under what conditions (data, architecture, training) does Radial-VCReg provide the largest accuracy gains over VCReg?
  - *Basis:* Tables 1-3 show 1-2% improvements on CIFAR-100 and ImageNet-10 but not uniformly; no analysis correlates gains with feature or dataset properties.
  - *Why unresolved:* Without predictive understanding, practitioners cannot know when to use Radial-VCReg. Possibly gains correlate with initial non-Gaussianity or task difficulty, but this is untested.
  - *What evidence:* Large-scale study measuring feature statistics (e.g., kurtosis, radius distribution shape) across many datasets/architectures and correlating with downstream performance changes from adding radial loss.

- **Question:** How to set hyperparameters β1, β2, and m for radial Gaussianization without costly sweeps? Currently optimal β1≠β2, and m is fixed arbitrarily.
  - *Basis:* Section 10 reports hyperparameter sweeps; Figure 5 shows best β1 often ≠ β2, contrary to theory (β1=β2=1). The spacing parameter m is never analyzed.
  - *Why unresolved:* Sweeping is computationally expensive and yields inconsistent optimal settings; the effect of weighting and m on the objective and final representations is unclear, hindering practical adoption.
  - *What evidence:* Theoretical analysis of bias-variance trade-offs for the m-spacing estimator as a function of batch size and dimension; propose an adaptive rule (e.g., set m∝√N) and validate on multiple benchmarks against extensive sweeps.

## Limitations
- Theoretical guarantee limited to elliptically symmetric distributions post-whitening; non-elliptical data may not fully Gaussianize.
- Correlation between Chi alignment and accuracy is not causal; optimizer interactions may contribute to observed gains.
- Hyperparameter sensitivity (β1, β2, m-spacing, output dimension) requires extensive tuning; optimal values are inconsistent across settings.

## Confidence
- **Broader Gaussianization:** High
- **Downstream gains:** Medium
- **Chi alignment necessity:** Medium
