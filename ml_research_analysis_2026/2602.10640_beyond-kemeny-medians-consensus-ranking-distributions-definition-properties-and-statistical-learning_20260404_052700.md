# Beyond Kemeny Medians: Consensus Ranking Distributions Definition, Properties and Statistical Learning
## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2602.10640
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.10640
- Reference count: 40
- Primary result: Introduces Consensus Ranking Distributions (CRDs) and the COAST algorithm to approximate complex ranking distributions via a sparse mixture of local Kemeny medians, achieving lower optimal transport distortion than a global median.

## Executive Summary
The paper addresses the limitations of using a single Kemeny median to summarize multimodal or complex ranking distributions. It proposes the Consensus Ranking Distribution (CRD), a parsimonious mixture model that places Dirac masses at local Kemeny medians defined on a partition of the symmetric group. A key theoretical contribution shows that the optimal transport distortion (using Kendall τ distance) depends solely on local pairwise probabilities within cells. This insight leads to the COAST algorithm—a greedy top-down binary tree method that recursively splits the ranking space via admissible pairwise comparisons to minimize empirical distortion. The authors prove that the CRD distortion is bounded by the intra-cell variability and demonstrate empirically that COAST effectively recovers mixture modes, achieves significantly lower distortion than a global median, and enables practical applications such as anomaly detection via local depth.

## Method Summary
A CRD approximates a ranking distribution \(P\) by a mixture \(\sum_{C \in \mathcal{P}} \alpha_C \delta_{\sigma^*_C}\), where \(\mathcal{P}\) partitions the symmetric group \(\mathfrak{S}_n\), \(\alpha_C = P(\Sigma \in C)\), and \(\sigma^*_C\) is the Kemeny median of the conditional distribution \(P(\cdot \mid \Sigma \in C)\). The optimal transport distortion \(W_{d_\tau}(P, P^*_\mathcal{P})\) is bounded by the expected intra-cell variability \(\mathbb{E}_\mathcal{P}(\mathcal{P}) = \sum_{C \in \mathcal{P}} P(C) V(C)\), with \(V(C)\) being the minimal average Kendall distance within cell \(C\). Critically, both \(V(C)\) and the learning criterion can be expressed in terms of local pairwise probabilities \(p_{i,j}(C) = P(\Sigma(i) < \Sigma(j) \mid \Sigma \in C)\). The COAST algorithm learns \(\mathcal{P}\) by recursively splitting cells: at each step, it selects an admissible pair \((i,j)\) that minimizes the empirical analogue of the dispersion measure, i.e., \(\hat{P}_N(C^{(0)}_{i,j}) \hat{V}'_N(C^{(0)}_{i,j}) + \hat{P}_N(C^{(1)}_{i,j}) \hat{V}'_N(C^{(1)}_{i,j})\), where \(C^{(0)}_{i,j}\) and \(C^{(1)}_{i,j}\) are the two child cells defined by the order of \(i\) and \(j\). The process stops when all leaves have empirical variability below a threshold \(\epsilon\), and the final CRD uses the empirical local medians of each leaf.

## Key Results
- Defines CRD as a parsimonious mixture of Dirac masses at local Kemeny medians, balancing model complexity and distortion via a partition of \(\mathfrak{S}_n\).
- Shows that the Wasserstein distortion under Kendall τ distance depends exclusively on local pairwise probabilities, enabling tractable computation and learning.
- Introduces COAST, a greedy top-down binary tree algorithm that uses admissible pairwise splits to minimize empirical dispersion, with a fast heuristic based on pairwise imbalance.
- Provides a distortion bound \(W_{d_\tau}(P, P^*_\mathcal{P}) \leq \sqrt{2\mathbb{E}_\mathcal{P}(\mathcal{P})}\) and a generalization bound (Proposition 2) for the empirical criterion.
- Empirical evaluation on synthetic mixtures shows COAST recovers true modes, reduces distortion substantially compared to a global median, and facilitates anomaly detection via local Kendall depth.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1
**Claim:** A ranking distribution can be effectively approximated by a sparse mixture of Dirac masses placed at *local ranking medians* defined on a partition of the symmetric group, rather than a single global median.  
**Mechanism:** The process defines a **Consensus Ranking Distribution (CRD)** as \( P^*_\mathcal{P} = \sum_{C \in \mathcal{P}} \alpha_C \delta_{\sigma^*_C} \), where \(\mathcal{P}\) is a partition of \(\mathfrak{S}_n\), \(\alpha_C = P(\Sigma \in C)\), and \(\sigma^*_C\) is the Kemeny median (w.r.t. Kendall \(\tau\)) of the conditional distribution \(P(\cdot \mid \Sigma \in C)\). This trades off dimensionality reduction (few cells) against distortion (fit to the true \(P\)).  
**Core assumption:** The partition \(\mathcal{P}\) can be chosen such that the **intra-cell variability** \(V(C) = \min_{\sigma \in \mathfrak{S}_n} L_{P_C}(\sigma)\) is low for all cells, meaning rankings within a cell are similar enough to be well-summarized by a single median.  
**Evidence anchors:**  
  - [abstract] Introduces CRD as a "parsimonious mixture model placing Dirac masses at local ranking medians defined on cells of a partition."  
  - [section 3.1] Defines local median \(\sigma^*_C\) and CRD \(P^*_\mathcal{P}\). Proposition 1 bounds the Wasserstein distortion \(W_d(P, P^*_\mathcal{P})\) by the expected intra-cell variability \(\mathbb{E}_\mathcal{P}(\mathcal{P}) = \sum_{C \in \mathcal{P}} P(C) V(C)\).  
**Break condition:** The approximation fails if the partition is too coarse (high intra-cell variability \(V(C)\)) leading to excessive distortion, or too fine (many cells) losing the dimensionality reduction benefit. The bound in Proposition 1 becomes loose if cells are not homogeneous.

### Mechanism 2
**Claim:** When using the Kendall \(\tau\) distance as the cost function, the **optimal transport distortion** (Wasserstein metric) of a CRD can be expressed solely in terms of **local pairwise probabilities** \(p_{i,j}(C) = P(\Sigma(i) < \Sigma(j) \mid \Sigma \in C)\), avoiding the need to work directly with the full distribution on \(\mathfrak{S}_n\).  
**Mechanism:** The distortion \(W_{d_\tau}(P, P^*_\mathcal{P})\) and the criteria \(\mathbb{E}_\mathcal{P}(\mathcal{P})\), \(\mathbb{E}'_\mathcal{P}(\mathcal{P})\) used for learning can be bounded by sums over pairs \((i,j)\) of terms like \(\min\{p_{i,j}(C), 1-p_{i,j}(C)\}\) or \(p_{i,j}(C)(1-p_{i,j}(C))\). This reduces the problem to estimating these pairwise marginals within cells.  
**Core assumption:** The Kendall \(\tau\) distance's structure allows the transport cost to decompose into pairwise disagreements, and the CRD's distortion is dominated by these local pairwise marginals. This holds generally, not requiring stochastic transitivity.  
**Evidence anchors:**  
  - [abstract] States: "the optimal transport distortion (using Kendall \(\tau\) distance) is shown to depend solely on local pairwise probabilities."  
  - [section 3.1 & A.1] Provides the bounds \(V(C) \leq V''(C) := \sum_{i<j} \min\{p_{i,j}(C), 1-p_{i,j}(C)\}\) and \(V'(C) = \sum_{i<j} p_{i,j}(C)(1-p_{i,j}(C))\). The appendix (A.1) shows how local pairwise probabilities characterize distributions on cells defined by pairwise comparisons.  
**Break condition:** If the local pairwise probabilities \(p_{i,j}(C)\) cannot be estimated accurately (e.g., because cells \(C\) are too small, leading to high variance in empirical estimates), the learned CRD may have uncontrolled distortion. The theoretical guarantees (Proposition 2) require a minimum cell probability mass \(\chi(\Pi_K) > 0\).

### Mechanism 3
**Claim:** The **COAST algorithm** learns an effective CRD by recursively applying a **greedy top-down binary splitting** rule on the ranking space, guided by an empirical estimate of the pairwise-based distortion criterion \(\mathbb{E}'\).  
**Mechanism:** Starting from \(\mathcal{P}_1 = \{\mathfrak{S}_n\}\), at each leaf node (cell) \(C\) with empirical variability \(\hat{V}'_N(C) > \epsilon\), the algorithm selects an **admissible splitting pair** \((i,j)\) (compatible with the partial order defining \(C\)) that minimizes the empirical objective:  
\(\hat{P}_N(C^{(0)}_{i,j}) \hat{V}'_N(C^{(0)}_{i,j}) + \hat{P}_N(C^{(1)}_{i,j}) \hat{V}'_N(C^{(1)}_{i,j})\)  
where \(C^{(0)}_{i,j} = \{\sigma \in C : \sigma(i) < \sigma(j)\}\), \(C^{(1)}_{i,j} = C \setminus C^{(0)}_{i,j}\). This aims to maximize the reduction in the expected pairwise-based dispersion \(\mathbb{E}'\). The process stops when all leaves have variability below \(\epsilon\).  
**Core assumption:** Greedily minimizing the empirical intra-cell dispersion \(\hat{\mathbb{E}}'(\mathcal{P})\) at each step leads to a partition \(\mathcal{P}\) with small true distortion \(\mathbb{E}'(\mathcal{P})\) and good generalization. The splitting rules based on single pairs are expressive enough to capture the modes of multimodal distributions.  
**Evidence anchors:**  
  - [section 4.1] Derives the splitting criterion from minimizing a local version of \(V'(C)\) and describes the COAST pseudocode. It notes that the criterion depends only on local pairwise probabilities.  
  - [section 4.2 & Appendix B] Empirical results show COAST recovers modes of mixture models and achieves lower distortion than a global median. The experiments compare two splitting rules: the exact distortion-minimizing rule and a faster "balanced size" heuristic based on \(|p_{i,j}(C)-0.5|\).  
**Break condition:** The greedy criterion may get stuck in a local optimum. The splitting rule may fail if no admissible pair significantly reduces variability (e.g., all remaining pairs have \(p_{i,j}(C)\) very close to 0 or 1, indicating the cell is already nearly unimodal). Computational complexity is high (\(O(\#I(C)^4)\)) for the exact rule in large cells.

## Foundational Learning
- **Kendall τ distance and ranking risk**  
  **Why needed:** It is the fundamental metric defining consensus (Kemeny medians) and the transport cost for the Wasserstein distortion. Understanding \(d_\tau(\sigma, \sigma') = \sum_{i<j} \mathbb{I}\{(\sigma(i)-\sigma(j))(\sigma'(i)-\sigma'(j)) < 0\}\) is essential to grasp the loss functions \(L_P(\sigma)\) and \(V_P\).  
  **Quick check:** Given two rankings \(\sigma_1, \sigma_2\) on 4 items, how would you compute \(d_\tau(\sigma_1, \sigma_2)\)? What does a Kendall distance of 0 imply about the two rankings?

- **Wasserstein (optimal transport) metric between ranking distributions**  
  **Why needed:** It provides the distortion measure \(W_d(P, P')\) used to evaluate how well a CRD \(P^*_\mathcal{P}\) approximates the true distribution \(P\). It generalizes the concept of average distance to a median.  
  **Quick check:** Why is \(W_{d_\tau}(P, \delta_{\sigma^*})\) equal to the ranking risk \(L_P(\sigma^*)\)? How does a coupling \((\Sigma, \Sigma')\) relate to computing this metric?

- **Stochastic transitivity (SST)**  
  **Why needed:** It identifies conditions under which the Kemeny median is unique and has a simple form (Copeland ranking). It is a key property of common parametric models (Mallows, Plackett-Luce) and relates to the "low-noise" condition for fast statistical rates.  
  **Quick check:** State the SST condition for three items \(i,j,k\). If \(p_{i,j} > 0.5\) and \(p_{j,k} > 0.5\), what must be true about \(p_{i,k}\) under SST? What does the SST condition intuitively mean about preferences?

- **U-statistics and concentration for dependent data**  
  **Why needed:** The empirical distortion criterion \(\hat{\mathbb{E}}'_N(\mathcal{P})\) is a U-statistic of degree 2. The generalization bound in Proposition 2 relies on concentration inequalities for U-statistics to control deviations between \(\hat{\mathbb{E}}'_N(\mathcal{P})\) and \(\mathbb{E}'(\mathcal{P})\).  
  **Quick check:** What is a U-statistic? Why is \(\hat{\mathbb{E}}'_N(\mathcal{P})\) a U-statistic? What property of the kernel \(\Phi_\mathcal{P}\) is needed for Hoeffding-type bounds?

- **Recursive partitioning / decision trees for non-Euclidean data**  
  **Why needed:** COAST adapts the classic top-down tree-building paradigm to the discrete space \(\mathfrak{S}_n\) using binary splits defined by pairwise comparisons. This is the core learning algorithm.  
  **Quick check:** In a standard CART tree for real data, splits test "feature \(x_j \leq c\)". What is the analogue in COAST? What makes a splitting pair \((i,j)\) "admissible" for a cell \(C\)?

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map:**  
  The COAST system comprises four sequential components: Input Handler → Tree Builder → Local Aggregator → CRD Composer. The Input Handler ingests the ranking dataset and hyperparameters. The Tree Builder recursively partitions the ranking space using admissible pairwise splits. For each resulting leaf cell, the Local Aggregator computes the empirical Kemeny median. Finally, the CRD Composer assembles the mixture model and optionally prunes the tree.

- **Critical path:** The algorithm's runtime is dominated by the recursive splitting loop inside the Tree Builder. Evaluating one candidate pair \((i,j)\) on a cell \(C\) requires computing empirical variability \(\hat{V}'_N\) for both children, which naively is \(O(N_C^2)\). The number of admissible pairs \(\#\mathcal{J}(C)\) can be up to \(O(n^2)\) initially, leading to high cost for the exact splitting rule. The "balanced size" heuristic reduces per-pair cost to \(O(N_C)\) by avoiding full variability computation.

- **Design tradeoffs:**  
  - **Splitting rule:** Exact minimization of \(\hat{\mathbb{E}}'\) is theoretically sound but computationally heavy (\(O(\#\mathcal{J}(C) \cdot N_C^2)\)). The "balanced size" heuristic (minimizing \(|p_{i,j}(C)-0.5|\)) is much faster (\(O(\#\mathcal{J}(C) \cdot N_C)\)) and empirically similar in reducing distortion, but lacks a formal optimality guarantee.  
  - **Stopping threshold \(\epsilon\):** Smaller \(\epsilon\) yields more cells (low bias, high variance), while larger \(\epsilon\) yields fewer cells (high bias). Pruning with a penalty \(\lambda\) is preferred over a fixed \(\epsilon\).  
  - **Local median algorithm \(\mathcal{A}\):** Must be chosen based on \(n\) and cell size \(N_C\). Exact Kemeny is NP-hard; heuristics (Borda count, Markov chain) may be needed for large cells, introducing approximation bias.  
  - **Tree depth limit:** A hard limit can prevent degenerate splits (e.g., cells with \(N_C=1\)).

- **Failure signatures:**  
  - **High final distortion:** \(\mathbb{E}'(\hat{\mathcal{P}})\) remains large relative to \(V'_P\), indicating partitions do not capture the underlying modes.  
  - **Poor mode recovery:** Learned local medians \(\hat{\sigma}^*_C\) do not align with true component centers, possibly due to insufficient data per cell or a misaligned splitting criterion.  
  - **Computational bottleneck:** Runtime explodes for large \(n\) (e.g., \(n>100\)) with the exact splitting rule due to \(O(n^2)\) candidate pairs and \(O(N_C^2)\) variability computations.  
  - **Empty child nodes:** If a candidate pair \((i,j)\) is incompatible with the partial order of cell \(C\) (violates transitivity), one child may be empty, wasting computation.  
  - **Unstable partitions:** Small dataset changes cause large tree structure changes, suggesting cells are too small or the splitting rule is overly sensitive to noise.

- **First 3 experiments:**  
  1. **Synthetic Mixture Recovery:** Generate rankings from a known \(K\)-component Mallows mixture. Run COAST with varying \(\epsilon\) or \(\lambda\). Evaluate: (a) distortion \(\hat{\mathbb{E}}'(\hat{\mathcal{P}})\) vs. iteration, (b) distance between learned local medians \(\hat{\sigma}^*_C\) and true component medians \(\sigma^*_k\). Verify distortion decreases and stabilizes near the true \(K\).  
  2. **Splitting Rule Comparison:** On the same synthetic data, run COAST with the exact criterion and the balanced size heuristic. Compare (a) final distortion, (b) number of iterations to plateau, (c) wall-clock time. This validates the heuristic's trade-off.  
  3. **Local Depth for Anomaly Detection:** From a mixture training set, learn a CRD with \(K\) leaves. For a test set containing inliers and outliers, compute local Kendall depth \(D_{\hat{P}_C}(\sigma) = \sum_{i<j} \hat{p}^{-1}_{\sigma(i), \sigma(j)}(C)\) within the assigned leaf \(C\). Plot depth distributions for inliers vs. outliers; expect clear separation for optimal \(K\), degrading if \(K\) is too small/large.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
### Open Question 1
- **Question:** For which classes of ranking distributions does the computationally efficient splitting rule (based on \(|p_{i,j}(C)-1/2|\)) achieve a distortion within a constant factor of the optimal rule (minimizing \(\mathbb{E}'(\mathcal{P})\))?
- **Basis in paper:** [explicit] Remark 3 states that finding conditions for quasi-optimality of this rule remains an open question, despite empirical evidence of comparable performance.
- **Why unresolved:** The theoretical relationship between pairwise probability imbalance and distortion reduction is unknown; this limits understanding of when the approximate method can be safely used.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Proof that for SST or Mallows-like distributions, the approximation ratio is bounded; or counterexamples showing unbounded error.

### Open Question 2
- **Question:** How can the hyperparameters (\(\epsilon\), \(\lambda\)) in COAST be chosen data-adaptively without prior knowledge, ensuring near-optimal bias-variance trade-off?
- **Basis in paper:** [inferred] The algorithm requires a stopping threshold \(\epsilon\) or pruning penalty \(\lambda\), but the paper admits difficulty in choosing \(\epsilon\) and leaves \(\lambda\) selection underspecified despite presenting a theoretical model selection framework.
- **Why unresolved:** The theoretical penalty depends on an unknown oracle complexity; no practical data-driven selector (e.g., stability selection, information criterion) is proposed or evaluated.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Derivation of a computable penalty term based on partition class complexity; extensive experiments comparing selectors across diverse ranking distributions.

### Open Question 3
- **Question:** Does the COAST algorithm consistently estimate the number of modes in a mixture ranking distribution as the sample size grows?
- **Basis in paper:** [inferred] While the paper shows COAST can recover modes empirically (plateau in distortion), there is no statistical guarantee that the selected number of leaves converges to the true number of components, nor bounds on partition recovery error.
- **Why unresolved:** Greedy top-down splitting may commit to errors early; distortion bounds do not imply consistency of the partition structure.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Consistency proof under mixture assumptions (e.g., separation conditions between components); finite-sample bounds on mis-clustering probability.

### Open Question 4
- **Question:** Can local aggregation via CRDs reduce discriminatory bias compared to a global Kemeny median when rankings encode protected group preferences?
- **Basis in paper:** [inferred] The paper's Impact Statement acknowledges societal consequences but does not address fairness. Given that ranking aggregation is used in hiring and recommendations, understanding bias propagation is critical. Local medians could either isolate or amplify biases depending on cluster alignment with groups.
- **Why unresolved:** No analysis of how local vs. global summarization affects group-level fairness metrics (e.g., rank correlation with protected attributes).
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Theoretical analysis linking CRD clustering to fairness measures; empirical evaluation on biased datasets (e.g., synthetic rankings with known group effects) comparing disparity metrics.

## Limitations
- The theoretical distortion bound (Proposition 1) is likely loose; its empirical tightness across distributions is unknown.
- Greedy top-down partitioning is not globally optimal and may yield suboptimal partitions for complex multimodal structures; no consistency guarantees are provided.
- The fast "balanced size" splitting heuristic lacks theoretical justification for its distortion performance, limiting understanding of its applicability.
- Hyperparameter selection (\(\epsilon\), \(\lambda\)) remains largely manual and may require domain-specific tuning; no automatic procedure is validated.
- Computing exact Kemeny medians is NP-hard; for large cells, heuristic local medians introduce approximation error.

## Confidence
- **Theoretical foundations (Props 1 & 2, pairwise reduction):** High – rigorous proofs with clear assumptions.
- **COAST's practical effectiveness (mode recovery, distortion reduction):** Medium – supported by synthetic experiments but lacks real-world validation.
- **Heuristic efficiency (balanced size rule):** Medium – empirical comparisons show promise; no large-scale or theoretical optimality results.
