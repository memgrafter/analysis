# KLong: Training LLM Agent for Extremely Long-horizon Tasks

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2602.17547  
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.17547  
- **Reference count:** 40  
- **Primary result:** KLong (106B) achieves state-of-the-art on PaperBench with an average score of 62.59, outperforming larger closed-source models and generalizing to multiple agent benchmarks.

## Executive Summary
KLong is an open-source LLM agent designed for extremely long-horizon tasks such as research paper reproduction, which exceed typical context windows and require thousands of steps. It introduces two key techniques: (1) trajectory-splitting supervised fine-tuning that preserves essential early context while progressively truncating later context, and (2) progressive reinforcement learning that stages training with increasing timeouts. The training data is generated by Research-Factory, an automated pipeline that distills trajectories from Claude 4.5 Sonnet and constructs evaluation rubrics. KLong (106B) achieves significant improvements on PaperBench (62.59 average score) and demonstrates strong generalization to SWE-bench Verified, Terminal-Bench Hard, and SEC-bench.

## Method Summary
The training pipeline combines three stages: (a) a comprehensive SFT phase on a broad dataset to impart general skills; (b) trajectory-splitting SFT that handles long trajectories by pinning the initial prefix (paper content) and splitting the remainder into overlapping sub-trajectories; and (c) progressive RL where timeouts are gradually increased across stages, allowing the agent to learn from denser reward signals initially and then master longer horizons. The data is produced by Research-Factory, which collects papers, automatically generates rubric trees from official code, and distills high-quality trajectories using Claude 4.5 Sonnet with rejection sampling. The system runs in a sandboxed environment with pre-installed research packages and a judge model for evaluation during training.

## Key Results
- PaperBench average score: **62.59**, surpassing Kimi K2 Thinking (1T) by 11.28%.
- Improved generalization: SWE-bench Verified pass rate ↑ from 60.8% to 62.8%; Terminal-Bench Hard success ↑ from 14.58% to 16.67%; SEC-bench overall success ↑ from 5.0% to 7.67%.
- Ablations: trajectory-splitting SFT alone yields +17.29 points; adding progressive RL provides an additional +6.67 points.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1: Trajectory-splitting Supervised Fine-Tuning
- **Claim:** Enables training on trajectories that exceed the model's context window while preserving critical early context (paper reading).
- **Mechanism:** Splits long trajectories into overlapping sub-trajectories of length ≤ L_max. Each sub-trajectory starts with the same pinned prefix containing the task specification and paper content. Later context is progressively truncated, and an overlap O maintains continuity between consecutive sub-trajectories.
- **Core assumption:** The initial paper-reading phase provides essential global information that must be retained in full for every learning step, while later steps can be truncated as needed.
- **Evidence anchors:** [abstract] describes the approach; [section 2.3] provides formal definitions and Figure 4 visualizes it. Neighboring works (PDC & DM-SFT) explore SFT techniques but do not address extreme horizon splitting.
- **Break condition:** If the early context is not actually necessary in every step, fixed pinning wastes context capacity and may hinder learning of later-phase decision-making.

### Mechanism 2: Progressive Reinforcement Learning with Timeout Curriculum
- **Claim:** Stabilizes and improves RL on extremely long-horizon tasks by gradually increasing the timeout across training stages, providing a curriculum from short to long horizons.
- **Mechanism:** Training is scheduled into M stages with increasing timeouts T(1) < T(2) < ... < T(M). At stage m, rollouts are force-terminated at T(m), yielding truncated trajectories that are further split. The policy optimizes via PPO on these sub-trajectories with group-relative advantages.
- **Core assumption:** Shorter horizons yield denser, less delayed reward signals that serve as a curriculum; longer horizons are needed for full task mastery but are too sparse early on.
- **Evidence anchors:** [abstract] mentions progressive RL; [section 2.4] defines timeouts and splitting within RL. Neighbor papers (Enhancing Agentic RL with Progressive Reward Shaping, PilotRL, YODA) explore progressive/curriculum RL, supporting plausibility.
- **Break condition:** If the optimal policy requires full-horizon exploration from the start, staged timeouts may prevent learning necessary long-range dependencies.

### Mechanism 3: Research-Factory Automated Distillation Pipeline
- **Claim:** Scales high-quality training data for research reproduction by automating paper collection, rubric construction, and trajectory distillation from Claude 4.5 Sonnet.
- **Mechanism:** Search agent collects papers from top conferences; evaluation agent analyzes papers and official code to generate structured rubric trees; trajectories are distilled via Claude 4.5 Sonnet (Thinking) with rejection sampling based on quality and judge scores. Official GitHub URLs are blacklisted to prevent cheating.
- **Core assumption:** Claude 4.5 Sonnet (Thinking) produces near-optimal demonstrations, and automatically constructed rubrics reliably evaluate reproduction quality.
- **Evidence anchors:** [abstract] describes Research-Factory; [section 2.2] gives full pipeline details including filtering and blacklist. Evidence from neighbors is weak; they focus on training methods, not data distillation.
- **Break condition:** If Claude 4.5 Sonnet's demonstrations are suboptimal or rubrics misalign with true scientific criteria, the training data will be flawed, limiting performance.

## Architecture Onboarding
### Component map
Research-Factory (data generation) -> Trajectory processor (splitting) -> Training orchestrator (SFT + RL scheduler) -> Infrastructure (sandbox, scaffolding, judge model)

### Critical path
1. Implement baseline SFT without splitting; verify model can solve short-horizon agent tasks.
2. Add trajectory-splitting function; validate that sub-trajectories retain the pinned prefix and have correct overlap.
3. Set up single-stage RL with fixed timeout (e.g., 2h) and judge model; confirm reward signals correlate with task success.
4. Introduce progressive timeout scheduling; monitor performance improvements across stages.
5. Integrate Research-Factory data generation for PaperBench; run full training pipeline.

### Design tradeoffs
- Splitting overlap size: Larger overlap better preserves continuity but reduces unique context per sub-trajectory, slowing convergence; smaller overlap risks context fragmentation.
- Timeout stage schedule: Fewer stages reduce training time but may cause abrupt difficulty jumps; more stages require more rollouts and infrastructure time.
- Judge model choice: Using open-source gpt-oss-120b lowers cost and avoids overfitting to official judge but may produce reward signals that misalign with final evaluation; tradeoff between training stability and benchmark validity.

### Failure signatures
- SFT: Assistant turns fail to increase after adding splitting (indicates splitting is breaking learning signal).
- RL: Reward plateaus or decreases across stages; check advantage normalization, KL penalty β, and that judge scores are meaningful.
- Infrastructure: Rollout nodes idle during evaluation; verify partial rollout logic and priority queue implementation.
- Generalization: Gains on PaperBench but regression on other benchmarks; suspect overfitting to training distribution or rubric overfitting.

### First 3 experiments
1. *Trajectory splitting unit test*: Take a synthetic 1,000-step trajectory, apply splitting with L=512, O=128. Assert each sub-trajectory starts with identical prefix and consecutive sub-trajectories overlap by exactly O steps.
2. *SFT cold-start ablation*: Train base model with standard SFT vs. trajectory-splitting SFT on 100 trajectories. Compare assistant turn counts and success on a held-out short-horizon task (e.g., SWE-bench Lite).
3. *Single-stage RL pilot*: Run RL with fixed timeout T=1h on 10 PaperBench tasks. Verify judge score improves over 3 PPO epochs and that rollout times respect the timeout constraint.

## Limitations
- **Data provenance:** The Research-Factory data (Claude-4.5-Sonnet-distilled trajectories) is unreleased, preventing audit for bias or suboptimal demonstrations; rubric quality is also unverified.
- **Hyperparameter gaps:** Exact progressive RL schedule (timeouts per stage, KL coefficient β), splitting parameters (overlap O, sub-trajectory length L), and judge prompts are undisclosed, hindering precise replication.
- **Infrastructure opacity:** Sandbox specifics (Kubernetes scale, sidecar management of 25k+ Docker images) and the 80+ pre-installed packages are not detailed, making scale reproduction uncertain.

## Confidence
- **High confidence:** Reported main results (PaperBench 62.59) and ablation gains (+17.29 from SFT splitting, +6.67 from RL) are explicitly quantified and reproducible given access to the same data and infrastructure.
- **Medium confidence:** Mechanism claims (pinned prefix necessity, curriculum benefit) are theoretically plausible and supported by neighbor papers on progressive RL, but lack direct ablation of the pinning decision itself.
- **Low confidence:** Long-term reliability of the automated distillation pipeline; assumes Claude 4.5 Sonnet provides near-optimal teacher trajectories without verification on unseen papers.

## Next Checks
1. **Trajectory-splitting logic audit:** Implement the splitting algorithm on a long synthetic trajectory. Verify that pinned prefixes are identical across splits, overlaps are exact, and truncation is progressive. Test if model performance degrades with smaller/larger overlaps.
2. **RL progression necessity test:** Run progressive RL with a *single long timeout* (e.g., 6h) vs. the staged schedule (2h→4h→6h). If staged training outperforms, it validates the curriculum hypothesis; if not, the timeout schedule may be non-essential.
3. **Judge alignment check:** Compare reward correlations between the training judge (gpt-oss-120b) and final eval judge (o3-mini) on a held-out set of trajectories. Low correlation would indicate reward hacking or misalignment, threatening RL validity.
