# GEM: Guided Expectation-Maximization for Behavior-Normalized Candidate Action Selection in Offline RL

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2603.23232
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.23232
- **Reference count:** 40
- **Primary result:** Introduces a candidate-based selection interface with behavior-normalized support that stabilizes risk control and enables increasing candidate budget to improve returns on multimodal offline RL tasks without retraining.

## Executive Summary
Offline reinforcement learning suffers from distributional shift when deploying policies trained on static datasets, particularly in environments with multimodal action landscapes where standard unimodal policies collapse toward poorly supported actions. GEM addresses this by decoupling training from inference via a propose–select interface. It trains a multimodal Gaussian Mixture Model (GMM) actor using advantage-weighted EM updates to preserve distinct modes while shifting mass toward high-value regions, a critic ensemble for conservative value estimates, and a separate behavior model to quantify support. At inference, for each state it generates a candidate set (an anchor plus samples) and scores each candidate using a conservative lower-confidence bound combined with a behavior-normalized support term, where the support log-likelihood is standardized within the candidate set. This yields an explicit, stable trade-off between pessimism and adherence to the data distribution, allowing the candidate budget to be increased safely at test time to boost performance. On D4RL benchmarks, GEM matches strong baselines overall and achieves substantial gains on multimodal tasks (e.g., +32.55 on Hopper-medium, +82.76 on Maze2D-medium vs IQL), while maintaining low out-of-support selection rates even as candidate budget grows.

## Method Summary
GEM operates in two phases. During training, three models are learned offline: (1) a critic ensemble `{Q_i}` via TD learning with Polyak averaging; (2) a value network `V` trained by expectile regression to provide a robust baseline; (3) a GMM actor `π_θ` updated via a guided EM-style procedure where dataset actions are reweighted by `exp(βA)` (with `A = Q_min - V`) and responsibilities are computed from current mixture log-joints; and (4) a separate GMM behavior model `µ_φ` fitted by maximum likelihood on dataset actions. At inference, for a given state `s`, the interface constructs a candidate set: an anchor action from the most-weighted actor component mean and `N` samples drawn from either the actor or behavior GMM. Each candidate `a` is scored as `LCB_λ(s,a) + wp * z_score(log µ_φ(a|s))`, where `LCB_λ = mean(Q_i) - λ * std(Q_i)` and the `z_score` standardizes the behavior log-likelihood across the candidate set. The top candidate (or a smoothed mean of top-`k`) is executed. The support normalization and LCB terms together mitigate extreme-value amplification when `N` is large, enabling a compute-quality trade-off without retraining.

## Key Results
- Matches or exceeds strong offline RL baselines on D4RL locomotion and maze tasks, with large improvements on multimodal benchmarks: +32.55 normalized score on Hopper-medium and +82.76 on Maze2D-medium compared to IQL.
- Ablations confirm that both the behavior-normalized support term and the ensemble lower-confidence bound are necessary to keep support violations and collapse distances low when scaling the candidate budget `N`.
- Increasing `N` consistently raises returns across suites while maintaining low out-of-support selection rates, demonstrating a usable inference-time compute knob. Deployment cost scales with memory (parallel scoring) rather than latency, unlike iterative diffusion planners.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
**Claim:** Standardizing the behavior log-likelihood within the candidate set yields a stable, comparable support weight across states and candidate budgets.  
**Mechanism:** The interface computes a `z-score` of `log µ_φ(a|s)` over the candidate set at each state, removing affine scale differences in raw log-likelihood magnitudes. This makes the scalar `wp` act as a consistent exchange rate between conservative value and relative support.  
**Core assumption:** The behavior model `µ_φ` is mis-specified w.r.t. the true data density, but its relative ranking within a candidate set remains meaningful for support control.  
**Evidence anchors:** The abstract and Section 2.4 define candidate-set standardization; Section 4.6 shows raw likelihood leads to unstable `wp` semantics while normalization stabilizes the trade-off.  
**Break condition:** If the candidate set has near-zero variance in `log µ_φ`, the `z_score` becomes numerically unstable despite clamping, potentially making `wp` overly sensitive.

### Mechanism 2
**Claim:** Guided EM-style updates shift mixture mass toward high-advantage regions while preserving multiple distinct components, avoiding collapse to a unimodal “in-between” policy.  
**Mechanism:** The actor GMM is trained by maximizing a responsibility-weighted surrogate where dataset samples are reweighted by `ω(s,a) ∝ exp(βA(s,a))` (critic-derived advantage). Responsibilities are computed from current mixture log-joints and detached during backprop; an entropy bonus on the gating distribution discourages early collapse.  
**Core assumption:** The critic provides a meaningful advantage ordering over dataset actions, and the dataset contains multiple separated modes in the conditional action distribution.  
**Evidence anchors:** Abstract and Section 3.2 (Phase I actor update) describe the procedure; Section 4.5 ablation “unimodal actor” shows large score drops on multimodal tasks (Table 6).  
**Break condition:** If guidance weight `β` is too high or entropy bonus `α` too low, components collapse prematurely; if `β` is too low, improvement stalls.

### Mechanism 3
**Claim:** Ensemble lower-confidence bound (LCB) counters extreme-value amplification when candidate budget `N` increases, preventing selection of high-uncertainty outliers.  
**Mechanism:** For each candidate, compute `LCB_λ(s,a) = Q̄(s,a) - λ·Std(Q_i(s,a))` from the critic ensemble. Maximizing this score penalizes actions with high ensemble disagreement, reducing their chance of being selected among many candidates.  
**Core assumption:** Ensemble disagreement correlates with estimation uncertainty or overestimation bias in the fixed offline dataset.  
**Evidence anchors:** Abstract and Section 2.4 define LCB; Section 4.5 ablation “no lcb” shows increased collapse distance and score degradation when `N` is large (Table 6, Figure 17).  
**Break condition:** If ensemble diversity is low (e.g., critics converge), the LCB penalty weakens; if `λ` is too high, the agent becomes overly pessimistic and selects only low-reward safe actions.

## Foundational Learning
- **Expectile Regression**  
  **Why needed here:** Trains the value baseline `V(s)` to be robust to outliers and aligned with pessimistic control, unlike Bellman residual loss. The expectile parameter `τ` shapes `V(s)` to be more conservative.  
  **Quick check:** How does increasing `τ` (e.g., from 0.5 to 0.9) affect `V(s)` estimates and why does this benefit advantage-weighted actor updates?

- **Gaussian Mixture Model (GMM) parameterization**  
  **Why needed here:** Both actor and behavior models use GMMs to maintain multimodal hypotheses and provide tractable likelihoods for support scoring, with diagonal covariances for efficiency.  
  **Quick check:** What role does the gating entropy bonus play in preventing component collapse during GMM training, and how does it interact with the responsibility weighting?

- **Candidate-based action selection**  
  **Why needed here:** Replaces single-shot policy execution with a propose–select paradigm, exposing an inference-time compute knob (`N`) that can be tuned without retraining to manage distributional shift.  
  **Quick check:** Why does increasing `N` amplify extreme-value effects, and which two signals in the score function specifically mitigate this amplification?

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map:**  
  Training Phase: Critic ensemble `{Q_i}` → Value network `V` (via expectile regression) → Actor GMM `π_θ` (guided EM updates using `A = Q_min - V`) and Behavior GMM `µ_φ` (MLE on actions).  
  Inference Phase: For state `s`, generate candidate set: anchor `a0` (mean of top actor component) + `N` samples from one source (actor or behavior) → compute LCB and normalized support → score → select action (top-1 or top-`k` smoothed mean).

- **Critical path:** Offline dataset is used to train all three models (critic ensemble, actor, behavior) first. During deployment, the fixed models generate and score candidates per state; no further learning occurs. The actor and behavior must be trained before inference; the critic ensemble and value baseline are used both for computing advantage during actor training and for LCB during scoring.

- **Design tradeoffs:**  
  - **Parallel vs sequential compute:** Candidate generation and scoring are parallel (batch operations), so latency grows slowly with `N` (mostly memory), unlike diffusion models where sequential denoising steps increase latency linearly.  
  - **Expressivity vs tractability:** GMMs are less expressive than diffusion policies but allow exact likelihoods for support scoring and efficient sampling, trading potential performance for interpretable, auditable control.  
  - **Single-source vs mixed candidates:** Using a single source (actor or behavior) avoids mixing inconsistent hypotheses; anchoring reduces tail risk from unlucky draws but may limit exploration of the candidate space.

- **Failure signatures:**  
  - High **Support Violation Rate** (fraction of selected actions with `z(log µ_φ) < -2`) indicates drift off-support even with large `N`.  
  - High **Collapse Distance** (distance from selected action to nearest behavior component mean) suggests policy collapse to in-between regions or overfitting to critic noise.  
  - Score degradation when increasing `N` without proper `λ` or `wp` indicates failure to control extreme-value amplification.

- **First 3 experiments:**  
  1. **Benchmark anchoring (Table 1):** Compare GEM to offline RL baselines on D4RL suites after sweeping inference-time hyperparameters; isolate the interface’s contribution by comparing to IQL (shared training backbone).  
  2. **Candidate-budget scaling (Figure 2):** Sweep `N` from 0 to 2048 and record return, violation, collapse distance; show returns increase with `N` while audits stay controlled, demonstrating the compute knob.  
  3. **Support normalization ablation (Figure 18, Section 4.6):** Compare candidate-set `z`-scoring vs raw log-likelihood in the score; show raw likelihood makes `wp` unstable across states, while normalization stabilizes the risk–return tradeoff.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- How can GEM automatically adapt inference-time knobs (N, λ, wp) based on per-state uncertainty or support, removing the need for manual sweeps?
- Can candidate generation use more expressive models (e.g., diffusion) while retaining an independent, calibrated support interface for risk control?
- How can behavior support modeling (µφ) be improved for rare high-reward actions, and can candidate budgeting be made state-dependent rather than fixed globally?

## Limitations
- **Hyperparameter sensitivity:** Critical settings (guidance weight β, entropy α, LCB λ, wp schedule) are not fully detailed and may require extensive tuning per environment.
- **Behavior model mis-specification risk:** The assumption that relative `log µ_φ` rankings within candidate sets remain meaningful can break under candidate-set degeneracy (e.g., all candidates near one mode), leading to unstable `wp`.
- **Task scope limited:** Evaluation is restricted to D4RL locomotion and 2D mazes; performance on high-dimensional, image-based, or real-world tasks is unknown.

## Confidence
- **Support normalization stability:** Medium (ablation shows raw likelihood instability, but theoretical justification for z-scoring is light; break condition near zero variance not fully probed).
- **EM-guided multimodal actor preservation:** High (ablation “unimodal actor” yields large drops; guided EM mechanism aligns with EM theory and empirical results).
- **Ensemble LCB for OOD control:** High (ablation “no lcb” increases collapse distance; ensemble disagreement is a standard uncertainty proxy).
- **Compute-quality trade-off (returns ↑ with N):** Medium (consistent trend on D4RL, but may not generalize to richer action landscapes or non-stationary dynamics).

## Next Checks
- **Normalization quantitative ablation:** Measure per-state variance of `wp` when using raw vs. normalized `log µ_φ`; correlate `wp` variance with candidate-set support entropy to confirm scaling stability.
- **Candidate-set degeneracy stress test:** Construct candidate sets with extremely low support variance (e.g., samples tightly clustered around one mode) and verify `z`-score normalization does not yield uncontrolled `wp` spikes or instability.
- **Hyperparameter sensitivity portraits:** Systematically sweep λ and `wp_end` on a held-out D4RL suite; map regions achieving low support violation and high returns to validate robustness of the compute-quality knob.
