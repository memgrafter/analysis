# Effort-Based Criticality Metrics for Evaluating 3D Perception Errors in Autonomous Driving
## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2603.28029
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.28029
- Reference count: 28
- Primary result: Introduces FSR, MDR, LEA to separate FP/FN errors; BEVFusion reduces critical FPs by ~88% with stable FN severity.

## Executive Summary
The paper tackles a fundamental flaw in current criticality metrics for autonomous driving perception: they conflate false positives and false negatives, obscuring the true safety impact of perception errors. The authors introduce three effort‑based metrics—False Speed Reduction (FSR), Maximum Deceleration Rate (MDR), and Lateral Evasion Acceleration (LEA)—which directly quantify the collision‑avoidance effort required for each error type. A reachability‑based ellipsoidal collision filter gates scoring to only dynamically plausible threats. Extensive evaluation on nuScenes and Argoverse 2 demonstrates that 65–93% of perception errors are non‑critical, and the new metrics are orthogonal to established time‑based and deceleration‑based measures. When comparing perception pipelines, BEVFusion reduces critical false positives by approximately 88% while false negative severity (mean MDR ~2.0–2.5 m/s²) remains stable, suggesting a geometry‑driven safety floor.

## Method Summary
The evaluation pipeline proceeds as follows: (1) Per‑frame Hungarian matching with a 2.0 m center‑distance threshold labels each detection as a false positive (FP) or false negative (FN) relative to ground truth. (2) Consecutive per‑frame labels are grouped into tracks using the tracker’s object IDs. (3) For each track, a reachability filter computes ellipsoidal reachable sets for both ego and the object, using vehicle dimensions and acceleration bounds, to check if a collision is dynamically possible within a 5 s horizon. Only tracks for which a plausible collision exists are scored. (4) FP tracks are evaluated by averaging the per‑frame braking acceleration needed to avoid the phantom detection (assuming the phantom moves at constant velocity) and multiplying by track duration, yielding cumulative FSR (m/s). (5) FN tracks are evaluated by taking the maximum braking acceleration over the track as MDR (m/s²). (6) Lateral evasion effort (LEA) is derived by finding the earliest time of ellipsoidal overlap and computing the minimum lateral acceleration required to avoid collision, capped at 5 m/s². (7) Pipeline performance is compared via aggregated track counts, mean/ cumulative effort, worst‑case values, and Spearman correlations against traditional metrics.

## Key Results
- The reachability filter classifies 65–93% of perception errors as non‑critical.
- The proposed metrics are orthogonal to TTC and DRAC (Spearman correlation analysis).
- BEVFusion reduces critical false positives by ~88%, while mean MDR (~2.0–2.5 m/s²) stays constant across pipelines, indicating a geometry‑driven safety floor.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The approach works by directly measuring the physical effort (deceleration or lateral acceleration) an autonomous vehicle would need to avoid a collision, rather than relying on surrogate time‑based indicators. Separating false positives and false negatives prevents mixing distinct error modes. The reachability filter, based on ellipsoidal reachable sets, ensures that only collisions that are dynamically feasible within the vehicle’s physical limits contribute to the scores. This combination yields a safety‑relevant, disentangled assessment of perception errors, as validated by orthogonality to existing metrics and the observed pipeline improvements.

## Foundational Learning
- **Hungarian algorithm**: Optimal bipartite matching for per‑frame detection‑to‑ground‑truth association. *Why needed*: To label FPs and FNs. *Quick check*: On a toy example with two detections and one ground‑truth box, verify the matching minimizes total distance.
- **Reachable sets**: The set of all positions an agent can reach under given dynamics within a time horizon. *Why needed*: To determine whether a collision between ego and an object is dynamically possible. *Quick check*: For constant velocity, reachable set is a circle; compute radius = distance + speed * time.
- **Ellipsoidal intersection**: Algebraic test for overlap of two ellipsoids, used as an efficient proxy for reachable set collision. *Why needed*: Avoids computational complexity of exact set operations. *Quick check*: Test two identical ellipsoids with known centers and semi‑axes; confirm condition yields true when centers are within sum of radii.
- **Constant‑acceleration braking model**: Computes required deceleration to avoid collision given relative distance and velocity. *Why needed*: Core of FSR and MDR calculations. *Quick check*: With relative distance 20 m and closing speed 10 m/s, required deceleration is a = v²/(2d) = 2.5 m/s².
- **Spearman rank correlation**: Non‑parametric measure of monotonic association between two variables. *Why needed*: To evaluate orthogonality of new metrics to established ones. *Quick check*: Compute manually on two small ranked lists.

## Architecture Onboarding
Component map:  
Detection/Tracking (3D boxes + velocities) -> Per‑frame Hungarian Matching -> Track Aggregation (by tracker ID) -> Reachability Filter (ellipsoidal overlap) -> Effort Computation (FSR, MDR, LEA) -> Aggregation across tracks -> Pipeline metrics.

Critical path: The ellipsoidal reachability filter is the computational bottleneck; it must evaluate overlap over a 5 s horizon for each track. Effort computation scales linearly with track duration and is gated by the filter.

Design tradeoffs:  
- Ellipsoidal reachable sets balance conservatism and intersection complexity; more accurate sets (e.g., polytopes) would be costly.  
- Assuming constant velocity for FPs simplifies but may underestimate effort if the phantom accelerates.  
- Capping accelerations (10 m/s² braking, 5 m/s² lateral) ensures metrics stay within physical bounds but may clip extreme but valid maneuvers.  
- Matching threshold (2.0 m) trades off false positives/negatives in error labeling; sensitivity analysis recommended.

Failure signatures:  
- Over‑scoring: high percentage of tracks pass filter (>95% FN/FP) indicates overly permissive bounds; adjust acceleration limits downward.  
- Under‑scoring: very few tracks score; filter may be too strict.  
- Fragmented tracks: many single‑frame tracks suggest poor ID continuity; examine tracker’s ID switch handling.  
- Unphysical LEA ( > 5 m/s² or negative for diverging objects): indicates sign errors in lateral kinematics or ellipsoid overlap logic.

First experiments to validate reproduction:  
1. **Ellipsoidal intersection test**: Implement with synthetic pairs to ensure overlap condition matches expected geometric results.  
2. **Replicate Table II**: For a single pipeline (e.g., CenterPoint on nuScenes), compute track‑level metrics and verify aggregate statistics (mean FSR, MDR, LEA, and scored track percentages) closely match the paper’s numbers.  
3. **Orthogonality check**: Compute Spearman correlation between proposed metrics and TTC/DRAC over a common set of errors; confirm absolute correlations are modest (<0.5), as reported.
