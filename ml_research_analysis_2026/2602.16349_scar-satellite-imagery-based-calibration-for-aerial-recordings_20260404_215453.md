# SCAR: Satellite Imagery-Based Calibration for Aerial Recordings

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2602.16349
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.16349
- **Reference count:** 31
- **Primary result:** SCAR reduces median reprojection error from ~45-47 px to ~5-7 px and visual localization rotation error from ~2° to ~0.3-1.3° on large-scale aerial campaigns, achieving higher pose accuracy thresholds without manual intervention.

## Executive Summary
SCAR tackles long-term calibration drift in aerial visual-inertial systems by using georeferenced satellite imagery as a persistent global reference. It constructs 2D–3D correspondences by matching on-board aerial images to orthophotos and lifting them to 3D via a digital elevation model. These "automatic GCPs" are fused with GNSS/INS pose priors in a factor graph that jointly optimizes camera intrinsics and extrinsics using a staged optimization schedule to avoid poor local minima. Evaluated on six diverse aerial campaigns spanning two years, SCAR consistently outperforms state-of-the-art auto-calibration methods (Kalibr, COLMAP, VINS-Mono), reducing reprojection errors by an order of magnitude on temporally distant sequences and improving visual localization accuracy, all without requiring manual intervention.

## Method Summary
Given aerial images and synchronized GNSS/INS poses, SCAR first renders orthophoto cutouts aligned using the current calibration. It detects robust features (SuperPoint) and matches them to the orthophotos (LightGlue), filtering with a homography and a slope constraint based on a DEM. Matched pixels are lifted to 3D world points, and multi-view anchor observations are created via KLT tracking. The resulting anchor set, together with GNSS/INS pose priors and reprojection residuals, forms a factor graph where variables include camera poses, landmark positions, and camera intrinsics. Each factor is weighted by an uncertainty model that accounts for sensor noise, map resolution, and matching quality. Optimization proceeds in a fixed staged schedule: (1) rotations, (2) translations, (3) XY-landmarks, (4) intrinsics, and finally (5) all jointly. After convergence, the refined extrinsic transformation from the INS to the camera frame is recovered by aligning the optimized camera poses with the GNSS/INS poses using robust least-squares. The final calibration is evaluated via anchor reprojection error and a visual localization benchmark that uses RoMa for matching and PnP for pose estimation.

## Key Results
- Median reprojection error on temporally distant sequences reduced from ~45-47 px (Kalibr, COLMAP, VINS-Mono) to ~5-7 px.
- Visual localization rotation error decreased from ~2° to ~0.3-1.3°.
- Pose accuracy at 2 m/2° and 5 m/5° thresholds consistently higher than competing methods.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1
- **Claim:** SCAR reduces long-term calibration drift by using georeferenced satellite imagery as a persistent, global reference anchor.
- **Mechanism:** It generates 2D–3D correspondences by matching on-board aerial images to orthophotos and lifting them to 3D world points using a DEM. These "automatic GCPs" provide absolute constraints that are integrated with GNSS/INS pose priors in a factor graph, enabling the system to detect and correct gradual extrinsic and intrinsic drift against a stable world frame.
- **Core assumption:** The orthophoto and DEM accurately represent the static, geodetic ground truth at the time of flight, and aerial-to-satellite matching yields sufficiently correct correspondences to serve as anchor points.
- **Evidence anchors:** 
  - [abstract] "exploits georeferenced satellite imagery as a persistent global reference" and "aligning aerial images with 2D–3D correspondences derived from publicly available orthophotos and elevation models."
  - [section III-A] "SCAR operates on existing intrinsic and extrinsic calibration parameters... and refines them during long-term operation. Our approach uniquely integrates georeferenced 6-DoF poses and image-based anchors into a unified optimization pipeline."
- **Break condition:** If orthophotos are outdated (e.g., post-construction changes) or DEM vertical accuracy is poor, generated anchors become biased, leading to corrected parameters that reflect map error rather than sensor drift. Severe seasonal changes (e.g., foliage) can also degrade matching quality.

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim:** Joint factor graph optimization of camera poses, landmarks, and intrinsics, weighted by appropriate uncertainty models, converges to a calibration that balances absolute geospatial anchors with relative motion constraints.
- **Mechanism:** The factor graph incorporates three residual terms with covariances: (i) pose priors from GNSS/INS (with lumped uncertainty), (ii) anchor priors from satellite/DEM resolution and matching noise, and (iii) reprojection residuals linking optimized camera intrinsics/extrinsics to landmark observations. Weighting by inverse covariance ensures no single noisy source dominates.
- **Core assumption:** The uncertainty models accurately represent the true noise statistics of each measurement stream, and the Huber kernel effectively mitigates gross outliers from failed matches.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [section III-D] "The optimization goal is to minimize the weighted sum... Each term is weighted by its covariance, ensuring consistent integration of heterogeneous information sources."
  - [section III-D] "Large outliers in image-based anchors... can dominate the solution. To mitigate this effect, all residuals are wrapped in a robust kernel."
- **Break condition:** If uncertainty is significantly underestimated for a source (e.g., GNSS/INS multipath errors not captured by Σlump_t), that source will overconstrain the solution. If the Huber kernel threshold is poorly set, either outliers persist or inliers are down-weighted excessively.

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim:** The staged optimization schedule (rotations → translations → XY-landmarks → intrinsics → joint) prevents poor local minima and parameter entanglement, enabling stable refinement from coarse initial calibrations.
- **Mechanism:** By freezing sensitive parameters (e.g., intrinsics) while first aligning rotations and translations to the global anchors, the method avoids the optimizer absorbing systematic extrinsic errors into intrinsics. The final joint refinement then fine-tunes all parameters within the basin of attraction found by the staged steps.
- **Core assumption:** The initial calibration is close enough to the global optimum that staged monotonic improvement is possible, and the sequence of block optimizations does not trap the solution in a suboptimal region inaccessible from the initial guess via joint optimization.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [section III-D] "Direct joint optimization... is prone to poor convergence... To improve stability, we adopt a staged, block-coordinate schedule... This strategy prevents systematic errors... from being absorbed into sensitive parameters such as intrinsics."
  - [section IV-B, ablation] "Joint optimization of camera poses in rotation and translation yields errors similar to the core method on average, yet often produces physically implausible extrinsics, confirming the benefit of the staged optimization."
- **Break condition:** If the initial extrinsic error is very large (e.g., >10° roll/pitch), the early rotation optimization may converge to a geometrically inconsistent state because anchor generation assumes near-nadir views. The schedule provides no recovery from such early missteps.

## Foundational Learning
### Visual-Inertial Odometry (VIO)
- **Why needed:** SCAR refines the extrinsic transformation between the camera and the INS frame. Misalignment affects fused pose estimates and reprojection errors, so understanding VIO fundamentals explains what is being corrected.
- **Quick check:** If the camera-INS extrinsic has a 5° yaw error, how would that manifest in the reprojection error of georeferenced anchors over a straight flight line?

### Factor Graph Optimization for Sensor Fusion
- **Why needed:** SCAR’s core is a nonlinear factor graph combining absolute (GNSS/INS, satellite anchors) and relative (feature tracking) constraints. Understanding variables, factors, and uncertainty weighting is key to seeing how the calibration emerges.
- **Quick check:** Why might modeling the anchor prior covariance as isotropic (diag(σ²Xj I3)) be insufficient for anchors derived from building rooftops in a city?

### Bundle Adjustment and Reprojection Error
- **Why needed:** The reprojection residual r_rep = u - π(K, C_t, X_j) links optimized calibration to image data. The pinhole+distortion model explains why intrinsic errors cause systematic reprojection patterns.
- **Quick check:** Given a set of anchors with known world coordinates, what pattern of reprojection errors would indicate a focal length error versus a principal point error?

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component map:** Input Preprocessor → Anchor Generator → Uncertainty Modeler → Factor Graph Builder (GTSAM) → Staged Optimizer → Extrinsic Recoverer → Evaluation Module

**Critical path:** Data preparation → Anchor generation (most computationally heavy) → Build graph → Run 5-stage optimization → Recover extrinsics → Evaluate on validation segment. Anchor quality directly determines final accuracy.

**Design tradeoffs:**
- Nadir assumption vs. generality: Near-nadir views simplify anchor lifting (single elevation per (x,y)). Oblique views require multi-scale DEM raycasting, increasing complexity and ambiguity.
- Global isotropic anchor uncertainty vs. per-correspondence confidence: A single σ_X per scene is simple but ignores that matching is more reliable on textured rooftops than on homogeneous fields. Per-match confidence from feature matching scores could improve weighting.
- Staged schedule vs. adaptive damping: Fixed stage order is simple but may not be optimal for all initial error regimes. An adaptive Levenberg-Marquardt damping strategy might reduce stage count at the cost of more hyperparameters.

**Failure signatures:**
- High reprojection error after optimization: Likely poor anchor generation (check matching inliers, DEM slope filter thresholds) or incorrect initial extrinsics (beyond nadir assumption limits).
- Physically implausible extrinsics (e.g., gross scale): Could indicate stage order failure—check if joint optimization was accidentally run first, or if anchor uncertainty was set too low.
- VL performance not improving despite lower reprojection error: Might indicate overfitting to training segment; check if anchors are spatially clustered and not representative of validation area.

**First 3 experiments:**
1. **Anchor quality sanity check:** On a held-out frame, manually verify a random sample of satellite-aerial matches post-filtering. Compute reprojection error using ground-truth pose to ensure anchors are approximately correct (<20 px error).
2. **Factor graph convergence test:** With synthetic priors (e.g., known slight extrinsic error), run only the first three stages (rot, trans, XY-landmarks) to verify that reprojection error decreases monotonically before intrinsics are touched.
3. **Ablation replication:** Reproduce the "no nadir" and "joint opt" ablations on a single sequence to observe the specific failure modes (e.g., extrinsic roll estimates diverging when rotations/translations are optimized jointly).

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
### Open Question 1: Extending to Oblique Cameras
The paper explicitly states that the method assumes near-nadir views, and oblique viewpoints or significant roll/pitch reduce geometric robustness and increase ambiguity. Extending SCAR to handle such configurations would require modeling off-nadir projection geometry, but the current pipeline does not address this.

### Open Question 2: Per-Correspondence Uncertainty and Temporal Correlations
The paper acknowledges that uncertainties in aerial-to-satellite correspondences are modeled with global isotropic covariances rather than per-correspondence uncertainty or correlations along KLT tracks. Incorporating local matching quality and temporal consistency could improve weighting in the factor graph.

### Open Question 3: Sensitivity to Temporal Mismatches
The method's accuracy depends on the quality and recency of orthophotos and DEMs. Real-world changes (construction, vegetation updates) can introduce systematic biases in anchor positions, which are not accounted for in the current uncertainty model.

### Open Question 4: Mitigating Overfitting and Improving Generalization
Mild overfitting can occur when optimizing on specific training segments. The paper suggests that stronger regularization and cross-region validation could help, but concrete strategies remain to be explored.

## Limitations
- The method relies on accurate, up-to-date orthophotos and DEMs; outdated maps or rapid terrain changes degrade anchor quality.
- Key uncertainty parameters (pose prior covariances, anchor covariances, Huber thresholds) are not disclosed, hindering exact reproducibility.
- The near‑nadir assumption and static terrain requirement limit applicability to oblique or dynamic environments.
- Anchor generation details (slope filter threshold, min pixel spacing) are unspecified, making quality control difficult.

## Confidence
- **Error reduction:** Medium – Strong evaluation across six campaigns but missing hyperparameters limits reproducibility.
- **Staged optimization:** Medium – Ablations support the claim, yet exact stage parameters are unspecified.
- **Anchor generation:** Low – No reported anchor inlier rates or failure statistics; success heavily depends on external map quality.

2. **Uncertainty Sensitivity:** Perturb anchor and pose prior covariances (±50%) and measure resulting changes in reprojection error and extrinsic parameters to assess robustness.
