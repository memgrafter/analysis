# The Scaffold Effect: How Prompt Framing Drives Apparent Multimodal Gains in Clinical VLM Evaluation

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2603.28387  
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.28387  
- **Reference count:** 40  
- **Primary result:** Merely mentioning MRI availability in prompts drives 70–80% of performance gains in clinical VLMs, independent of actual image presence—a textual "scaffold effect" that creates an illusion of multimodal reasoning.

## Executive Summary
This study investigates whether performance gains in clinical vision-language models (VLMs) reflect genuine multimodal reasoning or stem from prompt artifacts. Evaluating 12 open-weight VLMs on two neuroimaging cohorts (FOR2107, OASIS-3) where structural MRI carries no reliable diagnostic signal, the authors introduce a controlled five-condition evaluation framework that progressively adds: tabular text only, a textual prompt mentioning MRI availability, the actual MRI plot, parcellation text, and an irrelevant swap image. Key findings show that smaller VLMs exhibit dramatic F1 gains up to 58% upon introducing the MRI prompt alone, with distilled models matching much larger ones. Contrastive confidence analysis reveals that the prompt accounts for 70–80% of the shift, irrespective of image presence—a domain-specific modality collapse termed the scaffold effect. Expert evaluation uncovers consistent fabrication of neuroimaging-grounded justifications across all conditions. Preference alignment (MPO) that suppresses MRI-referencing collapses both multimodal and text-only performance toward the random baseline (~0.52 F1), demonstrating an irreversible "symmetry-without-recovery" effect. The work cautions that surface evaluations can exaggerate diagnostic ability, as gains are driven by textual scaffolding rather than evidence integration.

## Method Summary
The study evaluates 12 open-weight VLMs on binary classification tasks (MDD vs Control; Cognitive Decline vs Normal) using the FOR2107 and OASIS-3 datasets, which provide paired tabular clinical records and sMRI scans but where imaging is individually uninformative. Tabular data is serialized to natural language; sMRI is processed into three orthogonal slice plots and Harvard-Oxford parcellation text. Five controlled conditions are defined: C1 (text only), C2 (+MRI prompt mention), C3 (+real MRI plot), C4 (+parcellation text), C5 (+swap image). Inference uses deterministic decoding (`do_sample=False`) with first-token probability extraction to estimate confidence (ˆP(MDD)). Primary metrics are macro F1 scores. The scaffold effect is quantified via contrastive confidence analysis (δ = ˆP(MDD)condition − ˆP(MDD)TEXT), phrase probes (substituting the MRI preamble with other phrases), false-modality ablations (e.g., fake fMRI preamble), and expert evaluation of model justifications. A Mixed Preference Optimization (MPO) alignment fine-tunes models to suppress MRI referencing, testing whether the scaffold can be removed without degrading performance.

## Key Results
- Smaller VLMs show dramatic F1 gains (up to 58%) when the MRI prompt is added (C2), even without actual images.
- Contrastive confidence analysis attributes 70–80% of the total performance shift to the MRI prompt alone, independent of image presence.
- Expert evaluation finds consistent fabrication of neuroimaging-grounded justifications across all conditions, including when irrelevant or no images are provided.
- MPO alignment that suppresses MRI referencing collapses both multimodal and text-only performance to near-random (~0.52 F1), indicating irreversible entanglement ("symmetry-without-recovery").

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

**Mechanism 1: Textual scaffolding triggers latent associations**  
Claim: Performance gains are primarily driven by textual scaffolding (mention of MRI availability) rather than genuine evidence integration.  
Mechanism: Pretraining creates spurious correlations between neuroimaging context and diagnostic outcomes. When the prompt mentions MRI, it activates a latent pathway that shifts predictions toward the positive class, irrespective of visual input.  
Core assumption: Models learn domain-specific terminology triggers during pretraining that can be activated by surface-level cues.  
Evidence anchors: Abstract statement that MRI prompt accounts for 70–80% of shift; Section 5 contrastive confidence (e.g., δ(TEXT+PROMPT←TEXT) = +0.458 for Qwen2.5-VL-3B, ~72% of total shift); Section 7 phrase probes (MRI/neuroimaging phrases cluster in high-δ quadrant); weak direct corpus, supported by prompt sensitivity literature.

**Mechanism 2: Modality collapse with text dominance**  
Claim: VLMs exhibit modality collapse where visual input fails to meaningfully influence predictions even when nominally required.  
Mechanism: The architecture and pretraining objectives make the text modality dominant; the visual pathway is underutilized, so apparent multimodal performance actually reflects text-only processing with superficial priming.  
Core assumption: Modern VLMs are fundamentally text-first; visual inputs are secondary context.  
Evidence anchors: Abstract conclusion; Section G.1 swap equivalence (e.g., Qwen2.5-VL-72B F1: 0.849 real MRI vs 0.849 swap image; GLM-4.6V-Flash: 0.788 vs 0.791); Section 6 expert evaluation showing consistent fabrication; corroborated by general modality collapse literature.

**Mechanism 3: Irreducible entanglement of scaffold and reasoning**  
Claim: The scaffold trigger is entangled with the model's core inferential capacity, making selective suppression impossible without degrading overall performance.  
Mechanism: The latent pathway activated by the MRI preamble shares computational resources with genuine reasoning mechanisms. Lightweight alignment (MPO) cannot disentangle them, causing both multimodal and text-only performance to collapse toward random.  
Core assumption: Scaffold operates through the same residual-stream modifications that support authentic multimodal reasoning.  
Evidence anchors: Abstract and Section 8 (MPO results: Qwen2.5-VL-3B FOR2107 F1 C1 0.153→0.459; C2 0.728→0.496; both converge near 0.52); statement that aligned model no longer references MRI and both modes converge; appears novel to this work.

## Foundational Learning

**Concept: Modality collapse**  
*Why needed:* Central finding that apparent multimodal performance can be spurious; explains why swap images don't degrade performance.  
*Quick check:* If a VLM shows no performance drop when you replace the medical image with a dog photo, what does that suggest about its reasoning? (Answer: The model ignores the visual input and relies solely on text; no true evidence integration.)

**Concept: Prompt sensitivity & priming**  
*Why needed:* Scaffold effect is a specific instance of prompt sensitivity amplified by domain-specific priming; must distinguish direct instruction from priming.  
*Quick check:* How would you design an experiment to separate the effect of mentioning a modality from the effect of actually providing it? (Answer: Use controlled conditions: same image with/without modality mention, and same text with different images.)

**Concept: Contrastive confidence analysis**  
*Why needed:* Core method for quantifying how much of the performance shift is attributable to prompt framing versus image content.  
*Quick check:* Why extract the probability at the first label token rather than using full sequence likelihood or sampling-based uncertainty? (Answer: Isolates the model's initial decision bias; avoids confounding from subsequent reasoning; aligns with a classification setup where the first token often encodes the prediction.)

## Architecture Onboarding

**Component map:**  
Data ingestion -> Prompt constructor -> Model runner -> Confidence analyzer -> Evaluation suite

**Critical path:**  
The insight emerged from noticing that condition C2 (MRI prompt only) matched or exceeded C3/C4 (with real MRI). Critical experiments were: (1) contrastive confidence analysis to quantify ~70–80% prompt attribution, (2) false-modality ablation (fake fMRI preamble) to generalize beyond exact wording, (3) MPO fine-tuning to test recoverability.

**Design tradeoffs:**  
- Breadth vs depth: 12 models × 2 datasets × 5 conditions provides generalization but limits per-model mechanistic analysis.  
- Determinism: `do_sample=False` ensures reproducibility but discards calibration information from sampling distributions.  
- Dataset selection: Using cohorts with no true MRI signal sacrifices ecological validity for clean causal inference about artifact detection.  
- Expert evaluation: Single-psychologist case study provides depth but limits inter-rater reliability.

**Failure signatures:**  
1. Swap condition (C5) significantly outperforms or underperforms C4 (real MRI).  
2. Models refuse JSON format or generate malformed outputs.  
3. First-token extraction fails because label tokens are not at the generation start.  
4. Phrase probe shows no clustering of MRI-related phrases.  
5. MPO improves multimodal F1 while suppressing MRI references (opposite of reported entanglement).

**First 3 experiments:**  
1. Replicate scaffold effect: Run Qwen2.5-VL-3B on 10 FOR2107 patients in C1 vs C2. Expect ≥0.3 F1 gain from adding MRI prompt alone.  
2. Verify swap equivalence: For same 10 patients, compare C4 (real MRI) vs C5 (dog photo). Expect <0.05 F1 difference.  
3. Implement confidence extraction: Confirm that first-token "Major" vs "Control" probabilities sum to 1 and correlate with binary predictions.

## Limitations
- **Ecological validity:** Evaluation uses cohorts where structural MRI carries no diagnostic signal; findings may not generalize to clinically relevant scenarios with informative imaging.  
- **Expert evaluation scope:** Single-psychologist assessment limits inter-rater reliability and may introduce bias in judging justifications.  
- **Dataset access barriers:** FOR2107 and OASIS-3 are controlled-access, hindering independent replication on the exact cohorts.

## Confidence
- **Scaffold effect (textual framing drives gains): High** — Robust across 12 models and two datasets; quantified via contrastive confidence (70–80% attribution) and false-modality ablations.  
- **Modality collapse (visual input ignored): High** — Swap-image condition yields near-identical performance to real MRI; expert-annotated justifications show fabrication.  
- **Irreversible entanglement (MPO collapses both modes): Medium** — One alignment method shows symmetry-without-recovery; alternative techniques might disentangle scaffold from reasoning.

## Next Checks
1. **Generalization to informative imaging:** Apply the five-condition framework to a dataset with established MRI biomarkers (e.g., ADNI). Expect scaffold effect attenuation if imaging contributes genuine signal.  
2. **Alternative alignment strategies:** Replace MPO with adversarial losses or contrastive preferences that specifically penalize MRI-referencing while preserving text-only performance, to test if selective suppression is possible.  
3. **Inter-rater reliability for expert evaluation:** Recruit ≥3 clinicians to rate model justifications on the same sample; compute Cohen’s κ to quantify agreement on fabrication detection.
