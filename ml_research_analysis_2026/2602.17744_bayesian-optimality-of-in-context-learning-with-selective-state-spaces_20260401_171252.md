# Bayesian Optimality of In-Context Learning with Selective State Spaces

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2602.17744
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.17744
- **Reference Count:** 40
- **Primary Result:** Meta-trained selective SSMs asymptotically implement the Bayes-optimal predictor for Linear Gaussian SSM tasks, achieving consistency and superior statistical efficiency compared to ERM-based predictors like Transformers, especially under temporally correlated observation noise.

## Executive Summary
This paper theoretically and empirically establishes that in-context learning (ICL) in Selective State Space Models (SSMs) corresponds to Bayesian optimal sequential prediction. The authors frame ICL as meta-learning over a distribution of Linear Gaussian State Space Model (LG-SSM) tasks. They prove that a meta-trained selective SSM converges to the Bayes-optimal predictor (posterior predictive mean), effectively implementing an adaptive Kalman filter. A key theoretical separation shows that for tasks with temporally correlated observation noise, any empirical risk minimization (ERM)-based predictor (including Transformers) suffers an excess risk that scales with a constant that diverges as noise correlation increases, while the SSM's constant remains bounded. Experiments on synthetic LG-SSMs and a character-level Hidden Markov Model benchmark confirm the SSM's robustness in long-context latent state tracking, in contrast to a linear Transformer's performance decay.

## Method Summary
The study combines theoretical analysis with controlled experiments. Theoretically, it formalizes ICL as minimizing expected next-token prediction error over a task distribution from the LG-SSM family. The selective SSM architecture uses input-dependent linear recurrences (`ht = Atht-1 + Btxt`) where `At` and `Bt` are generated from the current token. Meta-training via gradient descent on next-token MSE across many LG-SSM tasks is shown to asymptotically produce parameters that implement the Kalman filter. For experiments, synthetic LG-SSM tasks (with and without AR(1) noise) and a discrete HMM text benchmark are used. Models compared include a simplified selective SSM (single layer, hidden size 16) and a linear Transformer (embedding size 16). The Bayes-optimal oracle is approximated via Hamiltonian Monte Carlo combined with the Kalman filter. Performance metrics are excess risk (MSE above Bayes error) for synthetic tasks and next-character accuracy for the HMM.

## Key Results
- **Theorem 1 (Bayesian Optimality):** A meta-trained selective SSM with sufficient hidden dimension asymptotically implements the Bayes-optimal predictor, achieving consistency and asymptotic efficiency.
- **Theorem 2 (Statistical Separation):** For LG-SSMs with temporally correlated observation noise, the SSM's excess risk scales as `c₁/k`, while any ERM predictor (including linear Transformers) scales as `c₂/k` with `c₂/c₁ → ∞` as noise correlation approaches 1.
- **Empirical Validation:** On synthetic LG-SSMs, the SSM's excess risk converges near zero while a linear Transformer's remains positive. On a character-level HMM, the SSM maintains high accuracy for long contexts (`k > 200`), whereas the Transformer's accuracy peaks and then decays.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1
- **Claim:** Meta-training a selective SSM on next-token prediction over many LG-SSM tasks drives its forward pass to approximate the Bayesian posterior predictive mean, implementing an adaptive Kalman filter.
- **Mechanism:** (1) The selective SSM's state update `ht = Atht-1 + Btxt` can exactly match the Kalman filter in innovations form when `At = A−KtC` and `Bt = Kt`. (2) The meta-training objective `min E||fϕ(Ck) − xk+1||²` forces the model's predictions to converge to the conditional expectation `E[xk+1|Ck]`. (3) Gradient dynamics with respect to `At, Bt` implicitly perform a stochastic approximation of an EM algorithm, learning task-appropriate Kalman gains that approximate the posterior `p(θ|Ck)`.
- **Core assumption:** The task distribution `π(τ)` is covered by the LG-SSM family; the selective network class is expressive enough; meta-training reaches a global optimum.
- **Evidence anchors:** Abstract states the SSM "asymptotically implements the Bayes-optimal predictor, converging to the posterior predictive mean." Section 4.1 shows the Kalman update matches the SSM dynamics under the parameterization.
- **Break condition:** If meta-training fails to converge or the true task distribution includes dynamics not approximable by LG-SSMs, optimality is lost.

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim:** The inductive bias of selective SSMs (stateful, input-dependent recurrences) provides strictly superior statistical efficiency to the ERM bias of Transformers (stateless, pooled loss minimization) for tasks with temporally correlated observation noise.
- **Mechanism:** (1) Transformers, interpreted as performing implicit gradient descent on an empirical squared loss, inherently assume conditionally independent (i.i.d.) observations. (2) When observation noise is temporally correlated (e.g., AR(1)), this assumption is misspecified. (3) Under misspecification, the asymptotic Fisher information is reduced by a factor `1/(1−ρ²)`, inflating the minimum achievable excess risk for any ERM-based estimator. (4) The Bayesian optimal predictor (and the SSM approximating it) correctly models the noise correlation by augmenting the state, preserving Fisher information and achieving a lower excess risk constant.
- **Core assumption:** The task family includes temporally correlated noise; the Transformer's forward pass approximates ERM under an i.i.d. noise model; the SSM learns the correct state augmentation.
- **Evidence anchors:** Abstract mentions constructing tasks with correlated noise where the Bayes-optimal predictor strictly outperforms any ERM estimator. Section 4.3 details the state augmentation strategy for the correlated noise system.
- **Break condition:** If observation noise is truly i.i.d., the separation vanishes. The advantage also diminishes if the SSM cannot learn the correct state augmentation.

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim:** The internal state of a selective SSM functions as an asymptotically sufficient statistic for the latent process, enabling robust long-context prediction by optimally filtering and forgetting irrelevant latent states.
- **Mechanism:** (1) The selective SSM maintains a belief state `ht` that approximates the Kalman filter's posterior mean `ˆzt|t(θ)`. (2) As context length `k` grows, `ht` aggregates sufficient information for an asymptotically efficient estimate of the latent state `zk`. (3) The selective, input-dependent transitions (`At, Bt`) allow dynamic adjustment of the forgetting rate, tracking slowly evolving latent variables while discarding outdated evidence. (4) This contrasts with Transformer attention, which computes a static weighted average over all context tokens, leading to confusion when the latent state shifts.
- **Core assumption:** Latent state dynamics are slow relative to observation noise; selective networks can map observations to appropriate gains; SSM state dimension `n ≥ d`.
- **Evidence anchors:** Abstract states SSMs "track latent states more robustly than linear Transformers." Section 5.2 shows Mamba maintains high accuracy for `k > 200` while Transformer accuracy decays.
- **Break condition:** If latent states change very rapidly or `n < d`, the belief state becomes insufficient and performance degrades.

## Foundational Learning
- **Concept: Bayesian Sequential Prediction (Filtering)**
  - **Why needed:** The paper's core thesis is that ICL in selective SSMs is Bayesian filtering. Understanding the Kalman filter recursion and posterior predictive mean is essential to grasp why the SSM's state update is optimal.
  - **Quick check:** Given an LG-SSM with known parameters, what is the formula for the one-step-ahead optimal predictor `E[xt+1|xt,...,x1]`, and how does it differ from a simple moving average?

- **Concept: Meta-Learning as Nonparametric Regression**
  - **Why needed:** The paper frames ICL as meta-learning over a task distribution. Recognizing that minimizing expected prediction error across tasks is equivalent to learning the conditional expectation `E[xk+1|Ck]` links the training objective to the Bayes-optimal predictor.
  - **Quick check:** If you minimize `E[||f(Ck) - xk+1||²]` over all measurable functions `f` with respect to the joint distribution of `(Ck, xk+1)`, what is the optimal `f`?

- **Concept: Statistical Efficiency & Model Misspecification**
  - **Why needed:** Theorem 2's separation hinges on asymptotic relative efficiency under misspecification. Understanding how ignoring temporal correlations in noise reduces Fisher information and inflates risk constants is key to the "why SSMs beat Transformers" argument.
  - **Quick check:** In a simple linear regression `y = βx + ε`, if you mistakenly assume `ε` is i.i.d. N(0,σ²) but it actually follows an AR(1) process, what happens to the asymptotic variance of the OLS estimator `β̂`?

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map:**
  Input Projection (`st = σ(Uxt + b)`) -> Selective Linear Layers (`At, Bt`) -> Recurrent State (`ht = Atht-1 + Btxt`) -> Output Head (`x̂ = Wout ht`)

- **Critical path:**
  1. **Meta-Training:** Sample many tasks `τ ~ π(θ)`, generate sequences from the LG-SSM.
  2. **Forward Pass per Sequence:** For each token `xt`, compute `st`, then `At, Bt` from `st`, update state `ht = Atht-1 + Btxt`.
  3. **Prediction & Loss:** Output `x̂t+1` from `ht`, compute MSE loss against true `xt+1`.
  4. **Gradient Flow:** Loss gradients flow back through output head to `ht`, and through `At, Bt` to the selective networks, shaping them to output gains minimizing prediction error.
  5. **Convergence:** After sufficient training, `At, Bt` functions approximate the Bayes-optimal adaptive gains.

- **Design tradeoffs:**
  - **State Dim (`n`) vs. Latent Dim (`d`):** `n < d` leads to insufficient statistics and suboptimal risk; `n >> d` adds parameters but provides robustness (Theorem 1 requires `n` sufficiently large).
  - **Selective Network Capacity:** The MLP generating `At, Bt` must be expressive enough to map context history to the correct gain sequence; limited capacity caps performance.
  - **Training Task Prior `π(θ)`:** Must cover the test task distribution; mismatch leads to poor generalization.
  - **Interpretability vs. Performance:** Theory assumes `At, Bt` implement exact Kalman gains; in practice, nonlinearities and finite capacity mean the SSM approximates the filter.

- **Failure signatures:**
  - **Excess Risk Plateau:** Training loss decreases but test excess risk stops improving significantly above Bayes error, indicating convergence to an ERM-like solution.
  - **Sensitivity to Noise Correlation:** On tasks with high `ρ`, the SSM's advantage over a Transformer should grow; if not, the SSM may not be learning correlations.
  - **State Collapse or Explosion:** During training, `ht` norms grow without bound (unstable filter) or collapse to zero (no information).
  - **Poor Long-Context Performance:** On HMM-like tasks with topic shifts, accuracy should not decay after `k > 200`; if it decays similarly to a Transformer, the state is not effectively forgetting.

- **First 3 experiments:**
  1. **Convergence to Bayes Risk:** Meta-train a small selective SSM and a linear Transformer on synthetic LG-SSMs; plot excess risk vs. number of meta-training tasks. Expected: SSM risk → 0; Transformer risk → positive constant.
  2. **Noise Correlation Stress Test:** Train both on LG-SSMs with AR(1) noise (`ρ` varying). Evaluate excess risk for fixed large `k` (e.g., 256) as a function of `ρ`. Expected: SSM risk flat; Transformer risk increases sharply as `ρ → 1`.
  3. **Latent State Tracking in Discrete HMM:** Meta-train on character-level HMM text. Plot next-character accuracy vs. context length `k`. Expected: SSM accuracy remains high for `k > 200`; Transformer accuracy peaks and decays.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- Can the Bayesian optimality and statistical separation theorems be validated on full-scale Mamba-2 and Transformer models in natural language tasks?
- How can the Bayesian sequential prediction framework be extended to nonlinear state space models, which better capture real-world dynamics?
- What architectural hybrid of selective SSM recurrences and attention mechanisms optimally balances long-range reasoning and latent state tracking for diverse ICL tasks?
- Do selective SSMs follow different scaling laws than Transformers with respect to model and data size on tasks involving latent dynamics?

## Limitations
- Theoretical results are asymptotic (infinite meta-training tasks, perfect optimization); finite-sample convergence rates are unknown.
- Performance relies on the task distribution being well-covered by the LG-SSM prior; degradation is expected on out-of-distribution or non-LG-SSM tasks.
- The architectural comparison uses a simplified linear Transformer; scaling to real Transformers (with softmax, multiple layers) may alter the separation.
- The HMM experiment's implementation details for "Mamba" are not fully specified, affecting reproducibility of the long-context robustness claim.

## Confidence
- **Bayesian optimality of meta-trained selective SSM:** High (theorem proof solid, but asymptotic).
- **Statistical separation due to correlated noise:** Medium (theory clean, but depends on precise ERM interpretation and extends to real Transformers).
- **Superior long-context tracking in HMM:** Low (empirical only; alternative architectures/commercial LLMs not tested).

## Next Checks
1. **Finite-sample convergence test:** Meta-train selective SSMs with varying numbers of tasks (1k, 10k, 100k) on LG-SSMs; measure excess risk decay rate vs. theoretical `1/k` prediction.
2. **Noise correlation ablation:** On the correlated-noise LG-SSM family, systematically vary `ρ` from 0.0 to 0.99; verify SSM excess risk remains flat while Transformer’s scales as `1/(1−ρ²)`.
3. **State sufficiency test:** Train selective SSMs with hidden dimension `n < latent dimension d`; check if excess risk plateaus above Bayes error, confirming `n ≥ d` is necessary for Theorem 1.
