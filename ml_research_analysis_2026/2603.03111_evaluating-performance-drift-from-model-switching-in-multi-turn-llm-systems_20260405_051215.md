# Evaluating Performance Drift from Model Switching in Multi-Turn LLM Systems

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2603.03111
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.03111
- **Reference count:** 5
- **Primary result:** A single-turn handoff between models causes statistically significant performance drift (Multi-IF strict success: -8 to +13 percentage points; CoQA F1: ±4 absolute points), an effect comparable to gaps between model tiers, and can be factorized into prefix influence and suffix susceptibility explaining ~70% of variance.

## Executive Summary
Multi-turn LLM systems often switch models mid-dialogue, but the performance implications of such handoffs are poorly understood. This paper introduces a switch-matrix protocol to measure drift: for every ordered pair of models (A,B), run A for the first L−1 turns and B for the final turn, then compare B’s performance to a no-switch baseline where B runs from the start. Using paired bootstrap confidence intervals on two multi-turn benchmarks (CoQA and Multi-IF), the authors find that even a single-turn handoff induces large, directional drift—changing final-turn scores by up to ±4 points on CoQA and ±13 points on Multi-IF, comparable to gaps between model tiers. The drift is not simply due to model quality; some suffixes improve under certain prefixes while others degrade. Decomposing drift into per-model prefix influence and suffix susceptibility accounts for roughly 70% of the observed variance, suggesting a practical way to monitor and mitigate handoff effects in production systems.

## Method Summary
The study evaluates K=9 LLMs on two benchmarks (CoQA with 10 QA turns, Multi-IF with 3 constraint-accumulating turns) using 200 episodes each. Its core method is a switch-matrix: for each ordered pair (A,B), the protocol runs model A for the first L−1 turns, then model B for the final turn L. Suffix performance `s_A→B(e)` is compared to a baseline `s_B→B(e)` (where B runs the entire dialogue). The difference `Δ_A→B = s_A→B - s_B→B` isolates the handoff effect. To control for episode difficulty, paired per-episode differences `δ_A→B(e)` are bootstrapped (1000 BCa resamples) to obtain confidence intervals. Prefix generations are cached and reused across all suffixes for efficiency and exact pairing. The matrix of off-diagonal drifts is then fit to an additive model `Δ_A→B = μ + α_A + β_B` with sum-to-zero constraints, where `α_A` estimates the prefix influence of A, and `β_B` estimates the suffix susceptibility of B. The model explains ~70% of variance (LOO-CV R² ≈ 0.70), enabling a compressed representation for handoff-aware monitoring.

## Key Results
- A single-turn handoff produces statistically significant directional drift; Multi-IF strict success ranges from -8 to +13 percentage points, CoQA F1 from -4 to +4 absolute points.
- Drift magnitudes are comparable to gaps between model tiers (e.g., between GPT-4 and GPT-3.5).
- Some suffix models consistently degrade under foreign prefixes (e.g., DeepSeek-v3.2 on CoQA), while others often improve (e.g., Qwen-2.5-72B on CoQA).
- The additive factorization `Δ = μ + α_A + β_B` explains ~70% of the variance across off-diagonal cells, offering a parsimonious model for operational monitoring.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1: Context Mismatch
Swapping models mid-dialogue creates a context mismatch because the suffix model must process a dialogue history generated by a different model with potentially different conventions, formatting, and grounding styles. The switch-matrix’s paired design (`A→B` vs `B→B`) isolates this effect by holding the episode, turn position, and scoring constant; only the prefix author differs. The measured `Δ_A→B` thus quantifies the performance change due solely to the handoff.
- *Break condition:* If prefix caching introduces artifacts or benchmarks are not deterministic, the paired comparison may conflate handoff effects with other noise.

### Mechanism 2: Factorizable Structure
Most drift patterns can be approximated as additive contributions from the prefix model (α_A) and the suffix model (β_B). This low-rank structure is captured by fitting `Δ = μ + α_A + β_B` to the off-diagonal matrix. The model explains ~70% of variance, implying that the handoff effect is largely decomposable into two per-model tendencies: how much a prefix helps or harms any suffix, and how robust a suffix is to foreign prefixes. This allows monitoring via two scalar scores instead of the full K×K matrix.
- *Break condition:* If task-specific pairwise interactions dominate, the additive model’s R² would be low, limiting its generalizability.

### Mechanism 3: Regime Anchoring (Directionality)
Drift is not always negative and correlates weakly with absolute model quality. Positive drifts occur when a stronger prefix establishes a compliant output regime (e.g., consistent formatting, constraint tracking) that a weaker suffix can leverage. Within-family switches (e.g., GPT-5 variants) show near-zero drift, suggesting shared conventions reduce mismatch. The asymmetry—some suffixes degrade under most prefixes, others improve—indicates that behavioral compatibility, not just capability, drives drift.
- *Break condition:* If all drifts were negative and scaled with model capability gaps, the regime-anchoring effect would not hold.

## Foundational Learning
### Multi-turn LLM Evaluation
- **Why needed:** The paper’s entire setup relies on multi-turn benchmarks where context accumulates and the final turn’s outcome is sensitive to the history. Single-turn benchmarks would not expose handoff-induced protocol shifts.
- **Quick check:** *How does Multi-IF strict success differ from per-turn scoring, and why might it be vulnerable to formatting changes caused by a handoff?*

### Paired Experimental Design & Bootstrapping
- **Why needed:** To isolate the handoff effect, each `A→B` run is paired with a `B→B` run on the same episode. Paired bootstrap CIs on per-episode differences control for episode difficulty and randomness.
- **Quick check:** *Why can’t we simply compare the mean scores of `A→B` and `B→B` across different episodes? What does pairing control for?*

### Factorization of Effects (ANOVA-style)
- **Why needed:** The additive model compresses the K×K drift matrix into two per-model vectors (α, β). This low-rank approximation enables practical monitoring and identifies which models tend to help or suffer from handoffs.
- **Quick check:** *If α_A is positive, what does that imply for any suffix B that follows A? How would you compute α_A from the observed Δ matrix?*

## Architecture Onboarding
### Component Map
Switch Matrix Harness -> Prefix Caching Layer -> Scoring Subsystem -> Bootstrap Analyzer. The harness coordinates runs; the prefix cache stores early-turn generations; the scoring subsystem verifies final-turn outputs; the bootstrap analyzer computes paired differences and fits the additive model.

### Critical Path
1. Precompute and cache all prefix-model generations for the first L−1 turns across episodes.
2. For each suffix model B: run baseline (B all turns) and store per-episode scores.
3. For each (A,B,e): load A’s cached prefix, append user turn L, generate B’s response, score, and store `s_A→B(e)`.
4. Compute per-episode differences `δ_A→B(e) = s_A→B(e) - s_B→B(e)`; aggregate into mean Δ and bootstrap CI.
5. Fit additive model `Δ = μ + α_A + β_B` (sum-to-zero) to off-diagonal entries; report R² and LOO-CV R².

### Design Tradeoffs
- **Cache key design:** Must include prompt template, sampling parameters (temperature=0), and early-turn count; mismatches cause invalidation.
- **Bootstrap resampling:** Paired bootstrap requires storing `(s_A→B, s_B→B)` per episode, increasing memory to O(K×E) vs. O(E) for unpaired.
- **Final-turn only vs. multi-turn handoffs:** The paper focuses on a single final-turn handoff for tractability; exploring earlier or multiple switches would increase combinatorial complexity.

### Failure Signatures
- **High variance on diagonal:** If `B→B` cells exhibit large bootstrap noise, detecting small Δ becomes difficult; more episodes may be needed.
- **Large additive residuals:** Substantial `|ε_A,B|` (Tables 5-6) indicate pair-specific interactions not captured by α/β, requiring manual investigation.
- **Cache mismatch:** Score discrepancies between cached and fresh runs point to cache invalidation (e.g., prompt template drift).

### First 3 Experiments
1. **Cache integrity check:** For a random (A,B) pair, regenerate prefixes without using the cache and compare `s_A→B` against the cached run; the difference should lie within bootstrap noise.
2. **Single-pair validation:** Pick one (A,B) pair; manually run 1000 bootstrap replicates for `Δ_A→B` using a trusted statistical package and verify the harness’s CI matches.
3. **Additive model sanity:** Fit the additive model on CoQA; confirm α and β sum to zero, that residuals for diagonal (excluded) are zero, and that LOO-CV R² aligns with reported ~0.70; inspect residuals for heteroscedasticity.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
1. **How do handoff effects generalize to earlier-turn or multiple model switches within a dialogue?** The study isolates final-turn handoffs; earlier switches may involve different context dependencies and accumulation effects.
2. **What drives the residual ~30% of variance in drift not explained by prefix influence and suffix susceptibility?** Unmodeled factors like pairwise model-specific interactions or task-context dependencies may contribute.
3. **How can handoff-aware monitoring be operationally implemented and validated in production multi-turn LLM systems?** The paper proposes monitoring α and β but lacks concrete metrics, thresholds, or integration with logging/tracing systems.
4. **Are the observed drift patterns consistent across a broader range of multi-turn tasks beyond conversational QA and instruction following?** The study only uses CoQA and Multi-IF; other tasks (e.g., creative generation, tool use) may exhibit different sensitivities.

## Limitations
- Prompt templates and formatting instructions are not fully disclosed, risking output mismatches and reproducibility gaps.
- Exact episode samples (200 per benchmark) are unreleased; different random seeds may alter drift magnitudes and significance.
- Model API specifics (reasoning effort, verbosity, precise identifiers) are ambiguous, hindering faithful replication.
- Scoring details (Multi-IF strict success verifier, CoQA F1 normalization) may diverge from the original implementation.
- The additive factorization assumes independent prefix/suffix effects; substantial residuals indicate unmodeled pairwise interactions.

## Confidence
- **Switching causes significant directional drift:** Medium (effects are large but depend on unreleased implementation details).
- **Factorization explains ~70% of variance:** Medium (validated on specific model set and tasks; residuals remain non‑trivial).
- **Drift is directional and not solely a function of model quality:** Medium (observational support; causal mechanisms not definitively proven).
