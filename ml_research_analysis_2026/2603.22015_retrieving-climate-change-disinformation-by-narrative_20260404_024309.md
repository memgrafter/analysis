# Retrieving Climate Change Disinformation by Narrative

## Quick Facts
- **ArXiv ID:** 2603.22015
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.22015
- **Reference count:** 0
- **Primary result:** SpecFi achieves a Mean Average Precision (MAP) of 0.494 on the CARDS dataset without any access to narrative labels, and shows substantially greater robustness to narrative variance than BM25.

## Executive Summary
The paper addresses the inflexibility of fixed taxonomies in detecting evolving climate disinformation narratives. It redefines the detection task as a retrieval problem: given a narrative’s core message as a query, rank texts from a corpus by semantic alignment. The proposed SpecFi framework bridges abstract narratives and concrete texts by generating hypothetical documents via HyDE, using graph‑based community summaries as few‑shot examples. Evaluated on three climate disinformation datasets, SpecFi reaches MAP 0.494 on CARDS without label supervision. The study also introduces an embedding‑based narrative variance metric, finding that standard retrieval (BM25) loses 63.4% of its MAP on high‑variance narratives, whereas SpecFi‑CS loses only 32.7%, highlighting its robustness. Additionally, unsupervised community summaries often converge qualitatively with expert‑crafted taxonomies.

## Method Summary
SpecFi operates in two phases. Offline, it constructs a heterogeneous knowledge graph (NodeRAG) from the reference corpus, discovers thematic clusters via Leiden community detection, and generates an abstract summary for each community using an LLM. Online, for a given narrative query, either (a) retrieves the nearest reference text (SpecFi‑DR) or (b) searches the graph to retrieve the most relevant community summary (SpecFi‑CS). That summary serves as a few‑shot example to prompt the LLM to generate multiple hypothetical documents exemplifying the narrative. The hypotheticals are encoded with an embedding model (Qwen3‑E‑4B), averaged, and used to rank test corpus texts by cosine similarity. This pipeline eliminates the need for a fixed label set and adapts to emerging narratives by directly matching narrative semantics.

## Key Results
- SpecFi‑CS achieves **MAP 0.494** on CARDS without using any narrative labels during retrieval.
- On high‑variance narratives, BM25’s MAP drops by **63.4%** relative to low‑variance narratives, while SpecFi‑CS’s drop is only **32.7%**, demonstrating greater robustness.
- Unsupervised community summaries often align with expert‑crafted taxonomy descriptions, though some suffer from collapse (merging distinct sub‑narratives) or drift (inverting stance).

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1: Reframing narrative detection as retrieval
- **Claim:** Framing the task as retrieval removes dependence on a predefined label set and adapts to emerging narratives.
- **Mechanism:** The narrative’s core message is embedded and used to query the corpus; texts are ranked by semantic similarity, not by classification into fixed categories.
- **Core assumption:** The core message sufficiently captures the narrative’s essence for retrieval.
- **Evidence anchors:** Abstract states the formulation requires no predefined label set; Section 1 argues classification limits adaptability.
- **Break condition:** Vague queries or absence of relevant texts in the corpus cause failure; standard retrievers degrade on high‑variance narratives (Section 5).

### Mechanism 2: Hypothetical document generation (HyDE)
- **Claim:** Generating hypothetical documents bridges the gap between abstract narrative descriptions and concrete textual instances.
- **Mechanism:** An LLM produces *n* example texts based on the query and few‑shot context; these are embedded and used to retrieve similar real texts.
- **Core assumption:** Generated hypotheticals occupy the same embedding region as real aligned texts.
- **Evidence anchors:** Abstract describes SpecFi’s hypothetical document generation; Section 2.2 references HyDE.
- **Break condition:** Generated documents may be generic, refuse disinformation content, or invert the narrative’s stance (Section 6 reports drift). Model calibration (e.g., abliteration) affects style match.

### Mechanism 3: Graph‑based community summaries as robust few‑shot examples
- **Claim:** Community summaries provide abstract yet thematically comprehensive few‑shot cues, improving performance on high‑variance narratives compared to retrieving single texts.
- **Mechanism:** NodeRAG search returns a top community summary, which guides HyDE generation. Summaries capture the thematic essence without being tied to specific lexical instantiations.
- **Core assumption:** Summaries reflect the narrative’s core while covering its diverse manifestations.
- **Evidence anchors:** Abstract reports MAP 0.494; Section 6 shows convergence of summaries to expert taxonomies; Table 9 illustrates patterns.
- **Break condition:** Coarse community detection can collapse distinct sub‑narratives into one summary (e.g., CARDS 4_1/4_2) or produce summaries with inverted stance (drift, e.g., CARDS 4_4), reducing discriminative power.

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component map:**  
Offline: Reference corpus → NodeRAG graph builder (entity/relation extraction) → Leiden community detection → LLM‑generated community summaries (added as high‑level nodes).  
Online: Narrative query → (SpecFi‑DR: nearest‑text lookup from reference corpus) or (SpecFi‑CS: NodeRAG graph search for top community summary) → LLM generates *n* hypothetical documents (HyDE) → Encoder (Qwen3‑E‑4B) averages embeddings → Dense retriever ranks test corpus by cosine similarity.

**Critical path (SpecFi‑CS):**  
1. NodeRAG search retrieves top community summary.  
2. Generate 10 hypotheticals using summary as few‑shot.  
3. Encode hypotheticals with Qwen3‑E‑4B and compute mean embedding.  
4. Retrieve top‑*k* test texts by similarity.  
For SpecFi‑DR, step 1 uses nearest‑text retrieval instead of graph search.

**Design tradeoffs:**  
- SpecFi‑DR uses a concrete retrieved text as few‑shot, which may be lexically aligned but might not cover the narrative’s full variability; SpecFi‑CS uses an abstract summary that generalizes better but risks collapse or drift.  
- Model abliteration improves disinformation‑style generation but may increase toxicity.  
- Graph granularity: finer Leiden resolution avoids merging distinct sub‑narratives but may fragment communities; coarser resolution risks collapse.  
- Number of hypotheticals (*n*): larger *n* improves performance up to a plateau (*n*=10) but increases latency.

**Failure signatures:**  
- Sharp MAP decline on high‑variance narratives (Figure 3).  
- For SpecFi‑CS: community summaries that are identical across related narratives (collapse) or express opposite stance (drift).  
- High refusal or over‑cautious generation in HyDE (not observed here but possible with other models).  
- Performance drop from low‑ to high‑variance narratives is much smaller for SpecFi‑CS than for BM25/dense baselines.

**First 3 experiments:**  
1. **Baseline retrieval:** Run BM25 and Qwen3‑E‑4B dense retrieval on CARDS (no generation). Record MAP; expect ~0.326 and ~0.499 respectively.  
2. **SpecFi‑DR replication:** For each narrative, retrieve the nearest reference corpus text (cosine), use it as few‑shot to generate 10 hypotheticals with Gemma‑3‑27b‑it (abliterated), then retrieve from test corpus. Expected MAP ~0.457.  
3. **SpecFi‑CS replication:** Build NodeRAG graph on CARDS training (OpenAI models). For each narrative, retrieve top community summary via NodeRAG search, generate 10 hypotheticals, and retrieve. Expected MAP ~0.494. Also inspect retrieved summaries for convergence/collapse/drift patterns (Table 9).

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
1. **Generalizability to other disinformation domains** – The framework has only been tested on climate change datasets; its effectiveness on other domains (e.g., COVID‑19 conspiracies, political disinformation) remains unknown.
2. **Reliability of the narrative variance metric** – The metric’s correlation with retrieval difficulty was consistent only on CARDS; it behaves unpredictably on sparse or multi‑label datasets like PolyNarrative.
3. **Stance‑aware community detection** – Current graph clustering ignores argument direction, causing “drift” where summaries invert a narrative’s core position; a stance‑aware variant could mitigate this.
4. **Optimal alignment of unsupervised summaries to expert taxonomies** – Unsupervised summaries sometimes merge distinct sub‑narratives; the graph parameters (e.g., Leiden resolution) that best preserve narrative distinctions across corpora are not established.
5. **Open‑source models for community summary generation** – The pipeline relies on OpenAI models for structured output; whether state‑of‑the‑art open‑source models can match this performance without compromising reproducibility is open.

## Limitations
- Exact NodeRAG hyperparameters (Leiden resolution, community size thresholds) are not specified, hindering faithful replication of community summarization.
- Handling of multi‑label relevance judgments in the Climate Obstruction and PolyNarrative datasets is unclear, which may affect MAP calculations.
- Prompt details (temperature, max tokens) and potential version drift for HyDE generation are insufficiently documented, risking reproducibility gaps.

## Confidence
- **SpecFi’s retrieval performance (MAP 0.494 on CARDS):** High  
- **Robustness to narrative variance (reduced MAP drop vs. BM25):** High  
- **Generalization to other datasets (CO, PN):** Medium
