# Learning Invariant Visual Representations for Planning with Joint-Embedding Predictive World Models

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2602.18639  
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.18639  
- **Reference count:** 26  
- **Primary result:** Achieves 0.76–0.82 success rate on PointMaze with aggressive backgrounds, outperforming DINO-WM (0.48) and domain randomization.

## Executive Summary
This paper tackles the brittleness of joint-embedding predictive architectures (JEPAs) like DINO-WM when faced with slow, task-irrelevant visual features (e.g., background changes, distractors). The authors introduce a bisimulation encoder that enforces control-relevant state equivalence: states with similar transition dynamics are mapped close together in latent space, while slow features are discarded. To avoid representational collapse, they propose PCA-VICReg, a variant of VICReg that enforces minimum variance only on the tail principal components. The method is evaluated on a PointMaze navigation task with varied test-time backgrounds and a moving distractor, showing substantial robustness gains over domain randomization and ablations. A theoretical analysis provides a horizon‑dependent generalization bound for the reward‑free bisimulation objective.

## Method Summary
Starting from a frozen pretrained visual encoder (e.g., DINOv2), the approach maps patch tokens to a lower‑dimensional latent space via a trainable bisimulation encoder. A transition model (decoder‑only transformer) predicts the next latent state from a history of latents and actions. The loss combines: (i) a predictive loss from the JEPA objective, (ii) a bisimulation loss that aligns pairwise latent distances with on‑policy one‑step bisimulation targets (without reward), and (iii) a PCA‑VICReg regularization that prevents collapse by applying a variance floor only to non‑dominant principal components. At test time, planning is performed with Cross‑Entropy Method (CEM) in latent space, minimizing a terminal cost between predicted and goal latents.

## Key Results
- Success rate of 0.76–0.82 on PointMaze with aggressive backgrounds (LCG), while DINO-WM drops to 0.48.
- Latent space size reduced by 10× compared to DINO-WM (196×32 vs. 196×384).
- Outperforms domain randomization and remains robust across DINOv2, SimDINOv2, and iBOT backbones.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1
- **Claim:** The bisimulation encoder learns to map visually distinct but behaviorally equivalent states to nearby latent representations, collapsing task‑irrelevant variation while preserving control‑relevant information.
- **Mechanism:** By minimizing the discrepancy between pairwise distances in the latent space `w` and the on‑policy one‑step bisimulation target `γ‖T(w,a)−T(w',a')‖²`, the encoder ignores visual features that do not affect transition dynamics. States that evolve similarly under the same actions become equidistant according to `dπ`.
- **Core assumption:** Task‑relevant information (agent position, velocity) strongly influences transition dynamics, while slow features (background, lighting) do not.
- **Evidence anchors:**  
  – [abstract] “augments the predictive objective with a bisimulation encoder that enforces control‑relevant state equivalence, mapping states with similar transition dynamics to nearby latent states while discarding task‑irrelevant visual information”  
  – [section 3.2] Formal definition of on‑policy bisimulation metric and explicit dropping of the reward term  
  – [corpus] Weak: Neighboring papers explore JEPA robustness broadly but do not test this specific bisimulation integration.
- **Break condition:** If many distinct states share identical transition distributions under the policy (e.g., many terminal states), the bisimulation objective risks trivial collapse; VICReg/PCA‑VICReg is required to maintain sufficient representation diversity.

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim:** PCA‑based VICReg selectively enforces variance only on the tail principal components, allowing the encoder to collapse dominant slow‑feature directions while still maintaining necessary diversity for control.
- **Mechanism:** Standard VICReg enforces a minimum variance threshold uniformly across all latent dimensions. Pretrained encoders (DINOv2, etc.) concentrate variance in the first PC(s), which correlate with slow features. PCA‑VICReg detects this dominant direction via one‑time PCA after warm‑up and applies the variance floor only to components beyond a cutoff `i₀` (e.g., `i₀=2`). This permits collapsing slow‑feature variance while the covariance term still spreads information.
- **Core assumption:** In the pretrained feature space, the slow features (backgrounds, distractors) are aligned with the leading principal components.
- **Evidence anchors:**  
  – [section 4.3] “by not enforcing a minimum variance on the leading PCA components i < i₀, the bisimulation encoder can collapse the directions that are dominated by slow features”  
  – [section 4.3] Figure 3 showing PC1 captures background information for DINOv2, SimDINOv2, and iBOT  
  – [corpus] Not directly addressed in neighbors; this is a novel adaptation of VICReg to pretrained features.
- **Break condition:** If slow features are not concentrated in the top PCs or task‑relevant information has large variance along those components, PCA‑VICReg may collapse critical features, harming performance.

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim:** Dropping the reward term from the bisimulation metric maintains sufficient invariance for JEPA‑based planning while keeping the representation learning reward‑free and modular.
- **Mechanism:** Standard bisimulation includes a reward term to ensure states with different immediate values are not collapsed. For JEPAs, which do not predict rewards during training, the transition term alone provides a useful notion of equivalence: states with similar on‑policy future distribution should be close. This simplifies training and aligns with the reward‑free nature of JEPAs. Experiments show the reward predictor converges to zero quickly and adds little signal.
- **Core assumption:** For goal‑reaching tasks planned via latent rollout similarity, transition behavior similarity is more important than per‑step reward equivalence; the cost function `c(w_T)=‖w_T−w_goal‖²` handles goal states directly.
- **Evidence anchors:**  
  – [section 3.2] “dropping the reward term is well aligned with JEPAs, which are inherently reward‑free and focus on predictive consistency in latent space”  
  – [section 5.6] Reward prediction loss goes to zero early and provides negligible additional signal; Figure 11 shows it is not active throughout training.  
  – [corpus] Neighbors like VJEPA explore probabilistic JEPAs but do not evaluate reward‑free bisimulation.
- **Break condition:** For tasks where small reward differences demarcate critical state distinctions (e.g., safety vs. danger, success vs. failure), dropping the reward term may collapse states that should be separated, leading to unsafe plans.

## Foundational Learning
- **Concept: Joint‑Embedding Predictive Architecture (JEPA)**  
  *Why needed:* The paper builds directly on JEPAs (e.g., DINO‑WM) as the base world model. Understanding that JEPAs learn by predicting future latent states (not reconstructing pixels) and prevent collapse via stop‑gradient and predictor networks is essential to grasp why they still suffer from slow features.  
  *Quick check:* If a JEPA’s predictive loss is minimized by encoding slowly varying background, what is the underlying failure mode?  
  → The JEPA exploits slow‑varying background features to minimize temporal prediction error, ignoring task‑relevant dynamics.

- **Concept: Bisimulation Metric**  
  *Why needed:* The core technical contribution is a bisimulation encoder that enforces state equivalence. This requires the formal definition: `dπ(s,s') = |rπ(s)−rπ(s')| + γW₁(Pπ(·|s),Pπ(·|s'))`. Recognizing that it captures behavioral similarity (reward and transition distributions) explains why it is robust to nuisance variation.  
  *Quick check:* Why does the Wasserstein distance term in bisimulation encourage invariance to visual distractors?  
  → Because it measures the distance between transition distributions; if two states lead to similar distributions under the same action, they are considered equivalent regardless of visual appearance.

- **Concept: Slow Features Principle**  
  *Why needed:* The problem statement is that JEPAs are sensitive to slow, task‑irrelevant features (backgrounds, lighting). This concept identifies those features and explains why they dominate prediction loss: they change slowly, minimizing temporal difference.  
  *Quick check:* Would a fast‑moving distractor be a “slow feature”?  
  → No; slow features are defined by low temporal variance. A fast‑moving distractor changes quickly, so it is not a slow feature.

- **Concept: VICReg (Variance‑Invariance‑Covariance Regularization)**  
  *Why needed:* Bisimulation loss alone can cause representation collapse (all states map to same point). VICReg prevents this by enforcing variance, invariance, and covariance regularization. The paper adapts it to PCA space.  
  *Quick check:* What happens if the variance term in VICReg is omitted?  
  → The encoder may collapse all states to a single point or a low‑dimensional subspace, losing information needed for control.

- **Concept: Domain Randomization**  
  *Why needed:* Serves as a primary baseline: augment training with varied backgrounds. The paper shows domain randomization generalizes only to similar backgrounds, not aggressive unseen shifts, highlighting the bisimulation advantage.  
  *Quick check:* Why might domain randomization fail under an aggressive background change at test time?  
  → Because the randomization during training may not cover the distribution of aggressive backgrounds, so the learned representation remains biased toward the training distribution.

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map:**  
  Pretrained visual encoder (DINOv2 ViT‑S/14, frozen) → Bisimulation encoder `h_η` (per‑patch MLP + LN + pos) → latent `w_t` (196×32) → Transition model `T_ϕ` (6‑layer decoder‑only transformer) → predicts next `w_{t+1}`; Bisimulation memory buffer (size 1000) stores past `w` states for pairwise loss; Planner (CEM) uses `T_ϕ` to roll out actions and minimize terminal cost `c(w_T)=‖w_T−w_goal‖²`.

- **Critical path:**  
  1. Observation → extract patches.  
  2. Encode each patch with pretrained encoder → `z_t`.  
  3. Encode `z_t` with `h_η` → `w_t`.  
  4. For planning: encode start and goal images to `w_0`, `w_goal`.  
  5. CEM samples action sequences, simulates latent rollouts via `T_ϕ`.  
  6. Evaluate cost for each rollout; update CEM distribution.  
  7. Execute first action of best rollout, repeat from step 4.

- **Design tradeoffs:**  
  - *Latent dimension (32 vs. 384):* Smaller reduces slow‑feature capacity but risks losing control signals.  
  - *Loss weighting `λ_bisim`:* Higher weight increases invariance but may weaken predictive accuracy.  
  - *PCA cutoff `i₀` (usually 2):* Too low → not enough tail variance, collapse; too high → slow features not suppressed.  
  - *Patch‑based design:* Aligns with ViT output, reduces parameters vs. flattening, but assumes spatial correspondence.

- **Failure signatures:**  
  - **Representation collapse:** All `w_t` nearly constant → check batch variance; increase VICReg strength or `σ_min`.  
  - **Background‑sensitive planning:** Success rate drops on new backgrounds → inspect top PCs of `w`; if PC1 still encodes background, lower `i₀` or strengthen PCA‑VICReg.  
  - **Training instability:** Bisimulation loss diverges → reduce `λ_bisim`, keep `h_η` learning rate low (5e‑7), clip gradients.  
  - **Poor planning despite low L_dyn:** Model inaccuracy → increase data or transition model capacity.  
  - **“No Encoder” baseline failure:** Confirms random features lack structure; bisim cannot compensate.

- **First 3 experiments:**  
  1. Reproduce PointMaze baseline: Train DINO‑Bisim on default background, evaluate on all 6 test scenarios (NC, SC, C, LC, LCG, D). Expect SR ~0.76–0.86, especially on LCG where DINO‑WM fails.  
  2. PCA‑VICReg ablation: Train with standard VICReg vs. PCA‑VICReg (switch at epoch 50). Visualize PC1 of `w` embeddings for same maze under different backgrounds. PCA version should cluster same state across backgrounds.  
  3. Backbone transfer: Using same bisimulation encoder and hyperparameters, train with SimDINOv2 and iBOT backbones. Success rates should remain consistent across background changes, demonstrating backbone agnosticism.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
1. **Linear dependence on horizon:** Is the O(T) scaling in the reward‑free bisimulation generalization bound fundamental, or can a tighter, horizon‑independent bound be established?
2. **Reward term incorporation:** Does adding an explicit reward prediction term improve robustness and planning on tasks with dense rewards, and what are the theoretical implications?
3. **Gradient‑based planning integration:** How does the bisimulation encoder perform with gradient‑based planners (e.g., analytical gradients), and does it improve sample efficiency or robustness compared to CEM?
4. **Online adaptation:** Can the encoder be adapted online to handle unseen visual shifts without forgetting previous invariances, enabling robustness in non‑stationary environments?
5. **Joint training of visual encoder:** Can the approach work without pretrained features by jointly training the visual encoder or using alternative self‑supervised objectives while still achieving robustness to slow features?

## Limitations
- Hyperparameters not fully disclosed; reproduction relies on assumptions.
- Evaluation limited to one navigation task; broader applicability unknown.
- PCA‑VICReg assumes slow features align with top PCs; this may not generalize to other domains.
