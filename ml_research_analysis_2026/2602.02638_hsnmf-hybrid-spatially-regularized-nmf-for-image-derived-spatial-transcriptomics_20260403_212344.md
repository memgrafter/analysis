# hSNMF: Hybrid Spatially Regularized NMF for Image-Derived Spatial Transcriptomics
## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2602.02638
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.02638
- Reference count: 0
- Primary result: hSNMF achieves superior spatial compactness (CHAOS < 0.004), strong spatial autocorrelation (Moran's I > 0.96), improved cluster separability (Silhouette > 0.12, DBI < 1.8), and higher biological coherence compared to spatial baselines on a cholangiocarcinoma Xenium dataset.

## Executive Summary
Spatial transcriptomics data often suffers from spatially fragmented clustering, where cells of the same type are split across non-contiguous regions. To address this, the authors propose two spatially regularized Nonnegative Matrix Factorization (NMF) extensions: Spatial NMF (SNMF) and Hybrid Spatial NMF (hSNMF). SNMF smooths NMF factors over local spatial neighborhoods, while hSNMF iteratively diffuses factors over a hybrid graph that combines spatial proximity and transcriptomic similarity before clustering. Evaluated on a high-resolution cholangiocarcinoma Xenium dataset, hSNMF demonstrates superior spatial compactness (CHAOS < 0.004), strong spatial autocorrelation (Moran's I > 0.96), and improved cluster separability (Silhouette > 0.12, DBI < 1.8) compared to existing spatial baselines like RASP and NSF, along with higher biological coherence.

## Method Summary
hSNMF processes a cell-by-gene expression matrix and spatial coordinates from high-resolution spatial transcriptomics. It first applies standard NMF to obtain a low-dimensional factor matrix W. Then it constructs a hybrid spatial adjacency A_s from a contact graph (20µm radius) and a radius graph (80µm). A diffusion operator P is derived from A_s and applied iteratively (β=0.8, two steps) to smooth W, producing W^(2). Separately, a transcriptional k-NN graph A_f (k=15) is built in the smoothed latent space. These are mixed into A_mix = αA_s + (1-α)A_f (α=0.5 default). Leiden clustering on A_mix yields spatially contiguous and biologically separable clusters. The method is evaluated using spatial metrics (CHAOS, Moran's I) and clustering metrics (Silhouette, DBI).

## Key Results
- Spatial compactness and autocorrelation: CHAOS < 0.004, Moran's I > 0.96.
- Cluster separability: Silhouette > 0.12, DBI < 1.8.
- Biological coherence: Improved marker specificity (CMC, MER) and enrichment over RASP and NSF.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1
- **Claim:** Post-hoc neighborhood averaging of NMF factors enforces local spatial smoothness.
- **Mechanism:** After standard NMF yields factor matrix **W**, each cell's factor vector **w**ᵢ is replaced by the mean of its *k* nearest spatial neighbors (k=15). This low-pass filter smooths the latent representation, reducing salt-and-pepper noise.
- **Core assumption:** Spatially adjacent cells are more likely to share similar cell states or types.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract] SNMF "enforces local spatial smoothness by diffusing each cell’s NMF factor vector over its spatial neighborhood."
  - [section 2.3] Explicitly defines the averaging operation: `w'_i = (1/|NS(i)|) Σ_{j∈NS(i)} w_j`.
  - [corpus] Limited direct evidence; neighboring papers benchmark spatial DR but do not detail SNMF's specific averaging mechanism.
- **Break condition:** Abrupt biological boundaries (e.g., tumor-stroma interface) may be blurred, mixing transcriptionally distinct populations.

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim:** Iterative diffusion over a hybrid contact-radius graph integrates spatial proximity with transcriptomic coherence.
- **Mechanism:** A hybrid spatial adjacency **A**ₛ merges short-range contact edges (20µm radius) and broader radius edges (80µm) via max-weight selection. A diffusion operator **P** = **D**⁻¹(**A**ₛ + **I**) is applied iteratively (`W^(t+1) = (1-β)W^(t) + βPW^(t)`) to the NMF factors, blending representations across multi-scale spatial neighborhoods.
- **Core assumption:** Spatial proximity is a reliable proxy for biological similarity, but multi-scale graphs capture both immediate contacts and broader tissue context.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract] hSNMF "performs spatially regularized NMF followed by Leiden clustering on a hybrid adjacency that integrates spatial proximity... and transcriptomic similarity."
  - [section 2.3] Details construction of **A**ₛ (contact + radius graphs) and diffusion steps (β=0.8, two iterations).
  - [corpus] Weak; SemanticST [52336] uses semantic graphs but not hybrid contact-radius; SPHENIC [81183] uses multi-view clustering but different graph design.
- **Break condition:** Invasive or heterogeneous tumors where spatial neighbors are transcriptionally dissimilar may cause over-smoothing and loss of biologically distinct microdomains.

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim:** Leiden clustering on a mixed adjacency (spatial + transcriptomic) yields spatially contiguous yet biologically separable clusters.
- **Mechanism:** After spatial diffusion, a transcriptomic *k*-NN graph **A**ᶠ (k=15) is built in the smoothed latent space. A hybrid adjacency **A**ₘᵢₓ = α**A**ₛ + (1-α)**A**ᶠ (α tunable, default 0.5) balances spatial and molecular relationships. Leiden clustering on **A**ₘᵢₓ directly optimizes for communities that are both geometrically cohesive and transcriptionally coherent.
- **Core assumption:** An optimal trade-off between spatial contiguity (α high) and molecular separability (α low) exists for cluster quality.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract] hSNMF "iteratively diffuses factors over a hybrid graph... before clustering."
  - [section 2.3] Explicitly defines **A**ₘᵢₓ and states Leiden is applied "directly to **A**ₘᵢₓ."
  - [corpus] Weak; SAGE-FM [80122] uses GCNs for spatial conditioning but not hybrid graph clustering; others focus on different integration strategies.
- **Break condition:** Inappropriate α (e.g., α=1 ignores expression, α=0 ignores space) degrades either spatial compactness or cluster separability, as seen in Pareto analysis where α=0.4–0.8 performed best.

## Foundational Learning
- **Concept:** Nonnegative Matrix Factorization (NMF)
  - **Why needed here:** hSNMF builds on NMF for initial low-dimensional representation; nonnegativity constraints ensure factors are additive and interpretable as gene expression contributions.
  - **Quick check question:** "Why must factors in NMF be nonnegative?" Answer: To ensure parts-based representation and align with count data's nonnegative nature.
- **Concept:** Spatial Graph Construction
  - **Why needed here:** The method relies on graphs (contact-radius, *k*-NN) to encode spatial relationships; graph topology directly controls the scale and nature of spatial smoothing.
  - **Quick check question:** "How does a contact-radius graph differ from a *k*-NN graph in spatial context?" Answer: Contact-radius connects all cells within a fixed distance; *k*-NN connects each cell to its *k* nearest neighbors regardless of distance, which may include farther cells in sparse regions.
- **Concept:** Spatial Clustering Metrics (CHAOS, Moran’s *I*)
  - **Why needed here:** These quantify spatial coherence of clusters and factors, which is the primary evaluation goal beyond standard transcriptomic clustering metrics.
  - **Quick check question:** "What does a low CHAOS score indicate?" Answer: High spatial compactness (cells within a cluster are close to each other).

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map:**
  1. **Input:** Xenium cell-by-gene matrix **X** (log-normalized) and spatial coordinates.
  2. **Spatial graph construction:** Build hybrid adjacency **A**ₛ by merging contact (20µm) and radius (80µm) graphs.
  3. **NMF factorization:** Compute **W**, **H** from **X** with *k* components.
  4. **Spatial diffusion (hSNMF only):** Iteratively smooth **W** using diffusion operator **P** from **A**ₛ.
  5. **Transcriptomic graph:** Build *k*-NN graph **A**ᶠ (k=15) on smoothed **W**.
  6. **Graph mixing:** Form **A**ₘᵢₓ = α**A**ₛ + (1-α)**A**ᶠ.
  7. **Clustering:** Apply Leiden algorithm to **A**ₘᵢₓ.
  8. **Evaluation:** Compute CHAOS, Moran’s *I*, Silhouette, DBI, CMC, MER, Enrichment.
- **Critical path:** Preprocessing → NMF → (for hSNMF) diffusion → **A**ᶠ construction → **A**ₘᵢₓ → Leiden → metrics. SNMF diverges after NMF: directly average **W** over spatial neighbors (no diffusion) and cluster on the smoothed factors (likely via a graph, but unspecified).
- **Design tradeoffs:**
  - **SNMF vs. hSNMF:** SNMF is simpler (single-step smoothing) but less flexible; hSNMF’s iterative diffusion and hybrid graph better balance spatial and transcriptomic signals at the cost of tuning α.
  - **α parameter:** Controls spatial vs. molecular emphasis; requires validation on a subset or Pareto optimization.
  - **Graph parameters:** Radius and *k* control locality; larger values increase connectivity but risk over-smoothing across tissue boundaries.
  - **Computational cost:** NMF and diffusion are scalable; graph operations and Leiden scale with cell count (O(*n* log *n*) typically).
- **Failure signatures:**
  - **High CHAOS (>0.005) or low Moran’s *I* (<0.9):** Poor spatial coherence; likely insufficient smoothing (low α, small radius) or graph construction issues.
  - **Negative Silhouette or high DBI:** Clusters overlap; likely α too high (over-spatialized) or *k* mismatched to biological complexity.
  - **High MER or low CMC:** Markers not cluster-specific; indicates transcriptomic coherence lost, possibly from over-smoothing or α too low.
  - **Known biology ignored:** E.g., tumor and stromal cells mixed; suggests α or graph parameters not capturing true tissue architecture.
- **First 3 experiments:**
  1. **Baseline NMF:** Run standard NMF (*k*=10) and cluster on *k*-NN graph of **W**. Expect high CHAOS, low Moran’s *I*, fragmented clusters.
  2. **SNMF evaluation:** Apply SNMF (k=15 neighbors) and cluster. Expect improved spatial metrics but possibly lower Silhouette than hSNMF due to simple smoothing.
  3. **α tuning for hSNMF:** Fix *k*=10, vary α ∈ {0, 0.5, 1}. α=0 should yield poor spatial compactness; α=1 poor separability; α=0.5 should balance, validating the hybrid approach.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
### Open Question 1
- **Question:** How to integrate multi‑scale spatial graphs into hSNMF?
- **Basis in paper:** [explicit] Future work says "incorporate multi‑scale spatial graphs".
- **Why unresolved:** Only two radii are used; multi‑scale may capture hierarchy but is not explored.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Build graphs at several scales (varying radii), modify diffusion, compare performance (CHAOS, Moran’s I, Silhouette) on multiple datasets.

### Open Question 2
- **Question:** How does hSNMF compare to deep generative spatial models?
- **Basis in paper:** [explicit] Future work includes "compare against deep generative spatial models".
- **Why unresolved:** Only RASP and NSF were evaluated; deep methods like SpaGCN are missing.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Benchmark hSNMF against leading deep models (SpaGCN, STGI) on common datasets using identical spatial and biological metrics; include runtime.

### Open Question 3
- **Question:** Does hSNMF generalize across platforms and tissue types?
- **Basis in paper:** [inferred] Evaluation used only one Xenium cholangiocarcinoma dataset; no cross‑platform/tissue tests.
- **Why unresolved:** Platform differences (resolution, gene panel) and tissue variation may affect performance.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Apply hSNMF to public datasets from Visium, Slide‑seq, and various tissues; report metric consistency and identify limitations.

## Limitations
- **Dataset accessibility:** The Xenium cholangiocarcinoma data from MDACC may not be publicly available, hindering exact replication.
- **Hybrid graph construction:** Ambiguity in whether the contact and radius graphs are binary or weighted, and how “maximum‑weight selection” is performed.
- **Clustering parameters:** The Leiden resolution parameter and the distance metric (Euclidean vs. cosine) for the transcriptional *k*-NN graph are not specified.
