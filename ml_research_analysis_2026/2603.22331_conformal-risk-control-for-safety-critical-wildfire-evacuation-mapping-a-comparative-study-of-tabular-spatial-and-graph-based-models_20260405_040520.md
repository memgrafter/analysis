# Conformal Risk Control for Safety-Critical Wildfire Evacuation Mapping: A Comparative Study of Tabular, Spatial, and Graph-Based Models

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2603.22331  
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.22331  
- **Reference Count:** 37  
- **Primary Result:** Conformal risk control uniformly achieved ≥94% fire coverage across tabular, convolutional, and graph models, but spatial architectures reduced flagged evacuation area by ~4× versus tabular; a shift-aware three-way framework collapsed the SAFE zone under extreme class imbalance.

## Executive Summary
This study addresses the lack of formal safety guarantees in wildfire evacuation mapping by applying conformal risk control (CRC) to enforce a finite-sample false negative rate (FNR ≤ 0.05). Using the NDWS dataset, three model families—tabular LightGBM, convolutional Tiny U-Net, and graph-based Hybrid ResGNN-UNet—were trained and calibrated. While standard thresholds (p ≥ 0.5) captured only 7–72% of true fire pixels, CRC consistently achieved ≥94% coverage. However, evacuation efficiency varied substantially: spatial models flagged ~15% of pixels for evacuation, a 4.2× reduction compared to LightGBM’s 62.6%. The graph model offered no meaningful efficiency gain over the U-Net. A key limitation emerged: the shift-aware three-zone CRC framework (SAFE/MONITOR/EVACUATE) collapsed the SAFE zone under the dataset’s extreme class imbalance (~5% fire pixels), exposing a brittleness in prevalence-weighted bounds.

## Method Summary
The authors trained three binary segmentation models on the NDWS dataset to predict fire pixels from satellite imagery. LightGBM used tabular pixel features; Tiny U-Net was a shallow convolutional encoder-decoder; Hybrid ResGNN-UNet combined graph neural networks (ResGNN) with a U-Net decoder. Standard model thresholds (p ≥ 0.5) were compared against a conformal risk control post-processing step that adjusted prediction thresholds to guarantee FNR ≤ 0.05 with high confidence. A shift-aware three-zone framework assigned each pixel to SAFE, MONITOR, or EVACUATE based on conformal intervals and a target false negative risk. Performance was evaluated via AUROC, fire coverage (sensitivity), and the proportion of pixels flagged for evacuation (efficiency).

## Key Results
- **Model AUROCs:** LightGBM 0.854, Tiny U-Net 0.969, Hybrid ResGNN-UNet 0.964.
- **CRC Coverage:** All models achieved ≥94% fire coverage under FNR ≤ 0.05 guarantee, versus 7–72% with standard thresholding.
- **Efficiency Gap:** Spatial models (U-Net, ResGNN-UNet) flagged ~15% of pixels for evacuation; LightGBM flagged 62.6% (4.2× higher). The graph model showed no significant efficiency improvement over the U-Net.
- **SAFE-Zone Collapse:** Under ~5% fire prevalence, the prevalence-weighted conformal bounds yielded a vanishingly small SAFE zone, limiting practical utility.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
Conformal risk control provides finite-sample guarantees on user-specified risks (here, false negative rate) by using a held-out calibration set to learn data-dependent thresholds. For each model, CRC constructs conformal intervals for fire probability; pixels with upper interval bounds above a risk-adjusted threshold are flagged for evacuation. This ensures that, regardless of model miscalibration, the true FNR on new data will not exceed the target with high probability. The three-zone extension adds a SAFE zone (lower bound below a safe threshold) and MONITOR zone (in-between), enabling graduated responses. The mechanism works because it makes no distributional assumptions, instead relying on exchangeability to transfer the calibration set’s empirical risk bound to the test domain.

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component Map:**  
NDWS Dataset (satellite images + masks) → Model Training (LightGBM / Tiny U-Net / Hybrid ResGNN-UNet) → Calibration Set → CRC Threshold Adjustment → Three-Zone Assignment (SAFE/MONITOR/EVACUATE) → Evacuation Map

**Critical Path:**  
1. Train base fire segmentation model on training set.  
2. Evaluate on held-out calibration set to collect nonconformity scores (e.g., 1 - predicted probability for fire pixels).  
3. Compute quantile of scores to achieve target FNR risk.  
4. Apply quantile to test set: for each pixel, if upper conformal bound > risk-adjusted threshold → EVACUATE; if lower bound < safe threshold → SAFE; else MONITOR.

**Design Tradeoffs:**  
- *Tabular vs. Spatial:* Tabular models (LightGBM) treat pixels independently, losing spatial context → lower AUROC and higher flagged area. Spatial models capture local patterns → higher AUROC and efficiency but require more computation.  
- *Convolutional vs. Graph:* Both spatial; graph models aim to capture long-range dependencies but here showed no efficiency gain over U-Net, suggesting local context may dominate for wildfire fronts.  
- *Three-Zone vs. Binary:* Three-zone provides granularity but relies on two thresholds; under extreme imbalance, SAFE zone may disappear due to wide intervals for the majority class.

**Failure Signatures:**  
- SAFE zone size → 0 when fire prevalence is very low (as in NDWS), indicating prevalence-weighted bounds are too conservative for rare events.  
- High flagged area from tabular model suggests failure to leverage spatial smoothness, leading to over-cautious evacuation.  
- No efficiency gain from graph model implies added complexity may not justify cost unless long-range interactions are demonstrably present.

**First Three Experiments:**  
1. **Reproduce core result:** Train all three models on NDWS, apply CRC with FNR ≤ 0.05, and verify ≥94% coverage and relative flagged-area reduction (spatial vs. tabular).  
2. **Boundary stress test:** Artificially vary fire prevalence in calibration set (1%, 5%, 10%, 20%) to measure SAFE zone collapse and test alternative prevalence-weighting schemes.  
3. **Model swap:** Replace Hybrid ResGNN-UNet with a standard Graph U-Net and compare efficiency; if still no gain, hypothesize that wildfire expansion is primarily local and graph edges add noise.

## Limitations
- **Dataset specificity:** Findings rely solely on the NDWS dataset, which may not generalize to global wildfire regimes with different vegetation, topography, or climate conditions.  
- **SAFE-zone collapse:** The three-zone CRC framework fails to maintain a non-zero SAFE area under extreme class imbalance (~5% fire), a common scenario in wildfire mapping, revealing a brittleness in prevalence-weighted conformal bounds.  
- **Real-world cost translation:** While spatial models reduce flagged area, the paper does not link this to concrete evacuation metrics (time, congestion, safety), leaving practical impact uncertain.

## Confidence
- **High:** CRC achieving ≥94% coverage; empirical observation of SAFE-zone collapse under imbalance. Both are direct, reproducible results.  
- **Medium:** Claim that spatial models improve evacuation efficiency over tabular; claim that the graph model offers no meaningful gain over U-Net. These are based on a single dataset and specific implementations; external validation is needed.  
- **Low:** None explicitly stated; confidence is generally bounded by single-dataset evaluation.

## Next Checks
1. **External validation:** Evaluate CRC on at least two additional wildfire datasets (e.g., from California and Australia) to test generalizability of coverage guarantees and efficiency comparisons across diverse regimes.
2. **Imbalance stress test:** Systematically vary fire prevalence (1%, 5%, 10%, 20%) in calibration to characterize the SAFE-zone collapse threshold and develop modified conformal bounds that preserve non-zero SAFE areas under extreme imbalance.
3. **End-to-end simulation:** Integrate generated evacuation maps with a traffic simulator (e.g., SUMO) to measure evacuation time, congestion, and safety outcomes, validating whether flagged-area reduction translates to practical benefits.
