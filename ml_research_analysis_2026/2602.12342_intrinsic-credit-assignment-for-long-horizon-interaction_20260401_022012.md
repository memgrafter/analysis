# Intrinsic Credit Assignment for Long Horizon Interaction
## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2602.12342
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.12342
- Reference count: 40
- Primary result: ΔBelief-RL (CIA) achieves mean success rate 33.72% on 20 Questions (Qwen3-4B), a 9.36% absolute improvement over STARPO, with strong out-of-distribution transfer (e.g., +11.13% on Customer Service, +28.5% on Murder Mystery) and scaling beyond the 20-turn training horizon.

## Executive Summary
The paper introduces ΔBelief-RL, a reinforcement learning approach for training language agents to perform long-horizon, open-ended interactions with sparse outcome rewards. The key innovation is using the agent's own belief shift—measured as the change in log-probability assigned to the correct target—as a dense, turn-level intrinsic reward. Combined with turn-wise group-relative policy optimization (GRPO), this enables granular credit assignment without an external critic. Evaluated on the 20 Questions task, ΔBelief-RL with Qwen3-4B outperforms state-of-the-art baselines, and gains transfer to out-of-distribution tasks and longer interaction budgets.

## Method Summary
ΔBelief-RL augments the final outcome reward with a per-turn intrinsic reward derived from the agent's belief update. Given a target concept y, the belief at turn t, b_t = p_θ(y | h_t, e_i), is elicited via a fixed prompt ("Is the secret {target}"). The ΔBelief reward for turn t is the clipped positive difference Δb_t = max(log b_t - log b_{t-1}, 0) scaled by λ. The total reward r_t combines λ·Δb_t with efficiency penalties (turn, repeat, invalid) and the terminal outcome reward. Training uses a variant of GRPO that computes advantages per turn by normalizing rewards within a group of G sampled trajectories, and updates the policy via LoRA. This design provides dense feedback for intermediate actions, addressing the credit assignment problem in sparse-reward, multi-turn settings.

## Key Results
- On 20 Questions (Qwen3-4B), ΔBelief-RL (CIA) achieves a mean success rate of 33.72% vs. 24.36% for STARPO (9.36% absolute gain).
- Out-of-distribution transfer: CIA-4B exceeds STARPO by 11.13% absolute Pass@1 on Customer Service and by 28.5% on Murder Mystery.
- Generalization to longer interactions: performance improves when test-time budgets extend beyond the 20-turn training horizon, demonstrating learned information-seeking strategies that scale.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1
- **Claim**: ΔBelief provides a dense, turn-level reward signal by measuring the agent’s belief shift toward the target concept, enabling granular credit assignment without an external critic.
- **Mechanism**: At each turn t, the agent’s belief b_t is the log-probability assigned to the target concept y given the history h_t (using a fixed elicitation prompt). The ΔBelief reward is the log-ratio ΔBelief_t = log(b_t/b_{t-1}), which is positive if the action increased confidence in the correct solution. This intrinsic reward is combined with the final outcome reward for RL training.
- **Core assumption**: The agent’s internal belief updates (log-probability changes) meaningfully correlate with progress toward the ground-truth target in the task.
- **Evidence anchors**:
  - [abstract] The paper uses change in belief as a dense, turn-level intrinsic reward.
  - [section 3.1] Empirical validation shows that belief trajectories from off-policy data (DeepSeek-v3.2) correlate strongly with final success: “successful episodes exhibit significantly higher cumulative belief increases after few turns.”
  - [corpus] Limited direct evidence; neighboring papers like “Hindsight Credit Assignment for Long-Horizon LLM Agents” address similar credit assignment but use future hindsight relabeling. Assumption: Belief calibration varies across model scales and tasks.
- **Break condition**: If the agent’s belief distribution is poorly calibrated (e.g., overconfident on wrong answers or insensitive to evidence), ΔBelief becomes a misleading reward, potentially reinforcing incorrect reasoning paths.

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim**: Turn-wise Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO) distributes the final outcome credit across turns using per-turn advantages, allowing intermediate actions to receive differentiated feedback.
- **Mechanism**: Instead of assigning a uniform trajectory-level advantage, for each turn t across G sampled trajectories, the reward r_t^i (composed of ΔBelief and outcome components) is normalized group-relative: Â_t^i = (r_t^i – mean({r_t^i})) / std({r_t^i}). This advantage is assigned to tokens generated at turn t.
- **Core assumption**: The per-turn reward signal (r_t) accurately reflects the contribution of the action at turn t to the final outcome, and group-relative normalization stabilizes advantage estimation across diverse trajectories.
- **Evidence anchors**:
  - [section 3.2] Explicitly defines turn-wise GRPO: “By replacing a uniform trajectory-level advantage with the sequence (Â_1^i, …, Â_N^i), we can provide the policy with granular feedback on each specific interaction.”
  - [abstract] Enables credit assignment to intermediate actions via intrinsic ΔBelief rewards.
  - [corpus] “MAPO: Mixed Advantage Policy Optimization for Long-Horizon Multi-Turn Dialogue” uses a mixture of advantages for subjective dialogue tasks, supporting feasibility of per-turn credit assignment in a different context.
- **Break condition**: If turn boundaries are ambiguous (e.g., multi-sentence responses) or the reward signal is extremely noisy, per-turn advantages may misattribute credit between turns, leading to policy oscillation or degenerate policies (e.g., repeating questions).

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim**: Training with ΔBelief-RL induces generalizable information-seeking strategies that scale beyond the training interaction horizon (e.g., 20→50 turns), because the intrinsic reward encourages sustained belief updates rather than memorizing trajectory patterns.
- **Mechanism**: The ΔBelief reward incentivizes actions that reduce uncertainty at every step, not just actions leading to success within the training horizon. Therefore, the learned policy adapts to longer interactions by continuing to ask informative questions as new evidence accumulates.
- **Core assumption**: The belief-seeking behavior learned on 20-turn episodes transfers to longer horizons because the underlying objective (maximizing belief shift toward the target) remains consistent.
- **Evidence anchors**:
  - [abstract] “performance continues to improve as we scale test-time interactions beyond the 20-turn training horizon, with interaction-efficiency increasing even on Pass@k metrics.”
  - [section 6.1] Figure 8 shows CIA’s success rate continues to rise from 20 to 50 turns, while STARPO plateaus.
  - [corpus] Limited direct evidence; “Intrinsic Stability Limits of Autoregressive Reasoning” discusses scaling breakdowns in long-horizon reasoning, but ΔBelief-RL may mitigate this by providing dense feedback.
- **Break condition**: If the task structure changes fundamentally beyond the training horizon (e.g., new question types appear or evidence dynamics differ), the belief-seeking heuristic may become suboptimal, and performance gains could diminish or reverse.

## Foundational Learning
- **Concept: Belief Tracking in Language Models**
  - *Why needed here*: ΔBelief requires extracting the model’s internal probability distribution over the target concept given the interaction history. The engineer must implement a reliable elicitation prompt and compute log-probabilities from token outputs.
  - *Quick check*: Given a history h_t and target y, how would you compute p_θ(y|h_t, e_i) from Qwen3’s logits? What issues might arise with tokenization or phrasing variations of y?

- **Concept: Multi-Turn Reinforcement Learning with Sparse Outcomes**
  - *Why needed here*: The environment provides only a binary outcome reward at the end of up to 20 turns. Understanding the credit assignment problem in such sparse-reward settings is essential to appreciate why ΔBelief and turn-wise GRPO are needed.
  - *Quick check*: Why does standard trajectory-level GRPO (like STARPO) struggle with sparse outcomes in long-horizon tasks? How does turn-wise advantage estimation reduce variance?

- **Concept: Reward Normalization and Clipping for Stability**
  - *Why needed here*: ΔBelief values are unbounded and can be negative. The paper uses ReLU clipping and a scaling weight λ. Engineers must tune these to avoid training collapse or degenerate rewards.
  - *Quick check*: What happens if λ is set too high (e.g., 0.5) or too low (e.g., 0.01)? Why is ReLU clipping (non-negative shaping) preferred over tanh or EMA normalization in this setting?

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map**: Policy (Qwen3) -> Action -> Environment (20Q simulator) -> Observation -> Belief Estimator (same Qwen3 with elicitation prompt) -> ΔBelief -> Reward Module (compose r_t with λ, penalties, outcome) -> GRPO Trainer (sample G=16, compute turn-wise group-relative advantages, asymmetric clipping) -> Policy Update (LoRA) -> (back to Policy)
- **Critical path**:
  1. Initialize from SFT checkpoint (trained on 341 successful Gemini 2.0 Flash trajectories).
  2. For each training step: sample batch of target words y; for each y, rollout G=16 trajectories using current policy.
  3. At each turn t in each trajectory: compute belief b_t before action using elicitation prompt; execute action a_t; get observation o_t; compute belief b_{t+1}; compute ΔBelief_t and r_t (including outcome at terminal turn, efficiency penalties).
  4. After trajectory completion, compute per-turn advantages via turn-wise group-relative normalization across the G trajectories.
  5. Compute policy loss: -mean(Â_t^i * log π(a_t^i|h_t)) with asymmetric clipping; optimize via LoRA (rank 64, α=64, LR=3e-5) using AdamW.
- **Design tradeoffs**:
  - λ scaling: Too high → over-optimizes ΔBelief, ignoring outcome; too low → outcome dominates, sparse credit persists. Optimal around 0.1.
  - Clipping ΔBelief at zero: Prevents penalizing temporary dips (plausible), but may allow wasteful actions if decreases are informative. Alternatives like tanh or EMA were less effective.
  - Turn-wise vs. trajectory-wise: Turn-wise reduces variance and provides granular feedback, but requires stable per-turn reward design; noisy ΔBelief can misattribute credit.
  - Simulator choice: Training with Qwen3-14B may cause overfitting; generalization to stronger simulators (DeepSeek, Qwen3-235B) is claimed but could be brittle.
- **Failure signatures**:
  - Training collapse when λ ≥ 0.5 (see ablation Figure 7).
  - No improvement over STARPO if belief updates are not correlated with success (observed for 1.7B model).
  - Overfitting to training simulator: performance drop on stronger simulators or OOD tasks.
  - Policy oscillation or degenerate repeats if turn-wise advantages become noisy or unnormalized.
- **First 3 experiments**:
  1. **Belief validation**: Use off-policy trajectories (DeepSeek-v3.2) to plot belief trajectories split by success; confirm successful episodes show steeper belief increases.
  2. **ΔBelief heuristic test**: Oracle best-of-8 at each turn selecting action with max ΔBelief; shows significant boost over SFT baseline, validating ΔBelief as a useful intrinsic signal.
  3. **Ablations**: Vary λ (0.0 to 1.0) and normalization schemes (ReLU, tanh, EMA, PACR); find optimal λ=0.1, ReLU clipping best. Also test reward composition variants and KL penalty omission.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
1. Can ΔBelief-RL generalize effectively to a wide range of long-horizon tasks beyond information-seeking, such as robotic control or complex strategic games? (Conclusion notes it remains to be seen how broadly it can be applied.)
2. Does optimizing a single-reference belief hinder performance or reduce output diversity on tasks with multiple viable solutions? (Limitations mention this for multi-solution scenarios.)
3. Do log-probabilities over a fixed reference accurately capture an agent's belief state in subjective or preference-based tasks? (Future work needed on subjective tasks.)
4. How does the effectiveness of ΔBelief-RL correlate with the agent's inherent belief calibration? (Inferred from size comparisons and introduction's claim about world models.)

## Limitations
- Belief calibration: ΔBelief assumes model log-probability shifts toward the target are meaningful; miscalibrated models may produce misleading intrinsic rewards.
- Simulator dependence: Training relies on a specific user simulator (Qwen3-14B); policies may overfit to its response patterns, limiting real-world transfer.
- Reward specification gaps: Ambiguity about whether terminal outcome reward is added to every turn or only the final, and interaction with per-turn penalties, could affect stability and performance.

## Confidence
- ΔBelief as dense reward signal: Medium
- Turn-wise GRPO design: Medium
- Generalization to new domains and simulators: Low

## Next Checks
1. **Belief-progress audit**: Compute correlation between ΔBelief and a ground-truth progress measure (e.g., reduction in candidate words) across diverse simulators and model scales; a weak correlation (<0.3) would challenge the reward's validity.
2. **Outcome reward routing**: Ablate two conditions: (a) r_outcome only at terminal turn, (b) uniformly added to all turns; compare success rates, stability, and advantage entropy.
3. **Cross-simulator stress test**: Train on one simulator (e.g., Qwen3-14B) and evaluate on three others (rule-based, DeepSeek-v3.2, human data); a drop >10% absolute indicates overfitting and calls for domain randomization.
