# Benchmarking Unlearning for Vision Transformers

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID**: 2602.20114
- **Source URL**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.20114
- **Reference count**: 40
- **Primary result**: Vision Transformers exhibit long-tailed memorization distributions similar to CNNs; CNN-derived memorization proxies remain effective, and architecture-specific compatibility (ViT: fine-tuning; Swin-T: NegGrad+) is key for robust unlearning within the RUM framework.

## Executive Summary
This paper establishes the first benchmark for machine unlearning in Vision Transformers (VTs), evaluating Fine-tune, NegGrad+, and SalUn under the RUM framework across ViT and Swin-T models on CIFAR-10/100, SVHN, and ImageNet-1K. It finds that VTs share the long-tailed memorization pattern of CNNs, allowing effective use of CNN-derived memorization proxies (Confidence, Holdout Retraining). Unlearning performance is architecture-dependent: ViT responds best to fine-tuning, while Swin-T favors gradient-based NegGrad+. The benchmark provides baseline ToW/ToW-MIA scores and demonstrates that continual unlearning incurs minimal degradation, offering a foundation for future VT unlearning research.

## Method Summary
The work evaluates unlearning algorithms within the RUM (Refinement, Matching, Unlearning) framework. Original models are obtained by fine-tuning ImageNet-1K pretrained ViT/Swin-T on target datasets. A forget set of 3,000 examples is partitioned into low/medium/high memorization bins using proxies (Confidence or Holdout Retraining). For each bin, the base unlearning algorithm (Fine-tune on retain, NegGrad+ gradient ascent on forget plus descent on retain, or SalUn's saliency-based selective update) is applied sequentially. The unlearned model is compared against a model retrained from scratch on the retain set using ToW (train-on-holdout) and ToW-MIA (membership inference attack resistance) metrics. Hyperparameters for each algorithm and architecture are fixed across datasets, with specific saliency thresholds (γ=0.1 for ViT, 0.3 for Swin-T) for SalUn.

## Key Results
- VTs (ViT, Swin-T) exhibit heavy-tailed memorization distributions akin to CNNs, validated via Feldman memorization scores.
- CNN-derived proxies (Confidence, Holdout Retraining) correlate strongly with ground-truth memorization for VTs (Spearman |ρ| up to 0.91), confirming their transferability.
- Unlearning algorithm efficacy is architecture-specific: ViT favors fine-tuning; Swin-T favors NegGrad+, especially when using Holdout Retraining on complex datasets (CIFAR-100, ImageNet-1K).
- SalUn achieves high ToW but low ToW-MIA for VTs, indicating vulnerability to membership inference attacks.
- Pretraining benefits unlearning on simple datasets (CIFAR-10) but diminish on complex ones (ImageNet-1K).
- Continual unlearning (sequential 600-example forget steps) shows minimal performance decay with NegGrad+.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1: Architectural Inductive Biases Mediate Memorization Distribution
- **Claim**: Vision Transformers exhibit a long-tailed memorization distribution similar to CNNs, despite differences in architecture and pretraining.
- **Mechanism**: Overparameterization and optimization dynamics in deep networks cause a small subset of training examples to have high influence (head of the tail) while most have minimal influence (long tail). This pattern emerges consistently, overriding architectural differences like global vs. local attention.
- **Evidence anchors**: [section 4.1] Figures 3 & 4 show memorization histograms for ViT-Small, Swin-Tiny, and ResNets on CIFAR-10/100, all exhibiting heavy tails. [abstract] "VTs exhibit similar long-tailed memorization as CNNs".
- **Break condition**: A VT variant with extreme pretraining or architectural change showing a uniform memorization distribution would invalidate this for that variant.

### Mechanism 2: Memorization Proxies Transfer from CNNs to VTs
- **Claim**: Computationally efficient memorization proxies developed for CNNs (Confidence, Holdout Retraining) remain effective for example-level memorization estimation in VTs.
- **Mechanism**: Overfitted examples in both architectures tend to have high prediction confidence. Holdout Retraining works because retraining on a subset reveals model reliance on removed examples, an architecture-agnostic property.
- **Evidence anchors**: [section 4.1] Table 1 shows Spearman correlations (|ρ| up to 0.91) between Confidence/Holdout Retraining and true memorization for ViT/Swin-T, comparable to ResNets. [abstract] "CNN-derived proxies remain effective."
- **Break condition**: A new proxy or architecture breaks the correlation pattern (e.g., a proxy works for CNNs but shows near-zero correlation for all VTs tested).

### Mechanism 3: Architecture-Specific Parameter Saliency and Gradient Dynamics Shape Unlearning Algorithm Compatibility
- **Claim**: ViT's global attention vs. Swin-T's local/hierarchical attention leads to different parameter influence patterns, making fine-tuning more effective for ViT and gradient-based methods (NegGrad+) more effective for Swin-T.
- **Mechanism**: Swin-T's windowed attention creates localized, sparse parameter updates during training; gradient ascent on forget examples (NegGrad+) precisely counteracts these. ViT's global attention distributes influence across many parameters, making indiscriminate fine-tuning on retain data more effective at overwriting forget-example influences.
- **Evidence anchors**: [section 4.3] ViT favors fine-tuning, Swin-T favors NegGrad+, supported by ToW/ToW-MIA results in Figure 1 and Table 13. [section 4.3] Swin-T requires higher saliency threshold γ=0.3 vs. ViT γ=0.1.
- **Break condition**: If a ViT variant with highly localized attention or a Swin-T variant with globally connected attention reversed the performance trends, the mechanism would be invalidated for those designs.

## Foundational Learning
- **Concept: Memorization Score (Feldman Score)**
  - **Why needed**: The paper partitions the forget set into memorization bins for RUM. This ground-truth metric (expected risk difference when removing an example) validates proxies and interprets unlearning results.
  - **Quick check**: If a model’s prediction on an example changes significantly when that example is excluded from training, what does that indicate about its memorization score? → It indicates a high memorization score; the example was influential and stored.

- **Concept: Memorization Proxies (Confidence, Holdout Retraining)**
  - **Why needed**: True memorization scores require training many models, infeasible for large VTs. Proxies enable scalable unlearning. The paper validates these proxies for VTs.
  - **Quick check**: How does Holdout Retraining (HR) proxy estimate memorization without retraining on many subsets? → Train a model on a random holdout subset; compute symmetric KL divergence between the original model’s and holdout model’s predictions per example; higher divergence indicates greater memorization.

- **Concept: RUM Framework (Refinement, Matching, Unlearning)**
  - **Why needed**: All evaluated algorithms (FT, NegGrad+, SalUn) are instantiated within RUM, which partitions forget sets by memorization and applies methods sequentially. This is central to reported performance.
  - **Quick check**: What are the three stages of RUM and why does sequentially applying partitions improve unlearning? → 1) Refinement: bin forget set by memorization proxy; 2) Matching: for each bin, apply base unlearning algorithm; 3) Unlearning: iterate bins sequentially. Sequentially targeting high-memorization examples first prevents catastrophic forgetting of retain data and improves overall unlearning efficacy.

- **Concept: ToW and ToW-MIA Metrics**
  - **Why needed**: These metrics quantify unlearning quality by comparing unlearned model to retrained-from-scratch model. ToW uses accuracy differences; ToW-MIA replaces forget accuracy with MIA resistance.
  - **Quick check**: What does a high ToW score indicate? → The unlearned model’s performance on forget, retain, and test sets is similar to a model retrained without the forget set, indicating successful unlearning.

- **Concept: Parameter Saliency (for SalUn)**
  - **Why needed**: SalUn perturbs only parameters with high saliency (gradient magnitude) on forget examples to prevent over-unlearning. The saliency threshold γ is architecture-specific.
  - **Quick check**: Why might SalUn’s saliency calculation differ in effectiveness between ViT and Swin-T? → Because parameter influence distribution differs: Swin-T’s local attention yields more concentrated saliency, requiring a higher γ to capture meaningful updates.

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map**: Dataset (CIFAR-10/100, SVHN, ImageNet-1K) -> Pretrained VT (ViT-Small, Swin-Tiny) -> Fine-tuned original model θₒ -> Memorization proxy computation (Confidence/Holdout Retraining) -> Forget set partitioning (low/medium/high) -> RUM (apply FT/NegGrad+/SalUn sequentially) -> Unlearned model θᵤ -> Evaluation vs. Retrained model θᵣ using ToW/ToW-MIA
- **Critical path**:
  1. Fine-tune ImageNet-pretrained VT on target dataset to obtain θₒ.
  2. Compute memorization proxies for all training examples (using held-out models or HR).
  3. Partition forget set Df into three equal bins by proxy value.
  4. Apply chosen unlearning algorithm sequentially to each bin (RUM) to obtain θᵤ.
  5. Train retrained model θᵣ from scratch on retain set.
  6. Evaluate θᵤ and θᵣ on forget, retain, test sets; compute ToW/ToW-MIA.
  7. For continual unlearning, repeat with smaller sequential forget sets.
- **Design tradeoffs**:
  - **ViT vs. Swin-T**: Swin-T’s locality favors NegGrad+ but requires higher saliency threshold (γ=0.3). ViT’s global context makes fine-tuning more stable.
  - **Proxy choice**: Confidence is high-fidelity but computes 10 models; Holdout Retraining is cheaper and performs better on complex datasets but needs an extra retraining step.
  - **Capacity**: Under-capacity (ViT-Tiny) limits unlearning; over-capacity (Swin-Small on CIFAR-10) may reduce ToW-MIA due to overfitting.
  - **Pretraining**: Helps on simple datasets by reducing initial memorization; advantage diminishes on complex datasets.
- **Failure signatures**:
  - **SalUn on ViT**: Very low ToW-MIA (e.g., 0.582 on CIFAR-10 with Confidence) indicates failure to protect against MIA, due to diffuse parameter influence.
  - **High Original baseline**: If θᵣ’s accuracy on Df is already very high (>90% on CIFAR-10 for VTs), ToW/ToW-MIA gains are marginal, obscuring algorithm efficacy.
  - **Proxy mismatch**: Using Confidence on a dataset where HR is superior (e.g., CIFAR-100) yields suboptimal unlearning.
- **First 3 experiments**:
  1. Train ViT-Small and Swin-Tiny on CIFAR-10/100, compute Feldman memorization scores, plot histograms. Expect long-tailed distribution similar to ResNet baselines.
  2. For each VT, compute all five proxies (Conf, MaxConf, Ent, BA, HR) and correlate with ground-truth memorization. Expect Confidence and HR to show strong correlations (|ρ| > 0.6).
  3. Run RUM with NegGrad+ and Fine-tune on ViT-Small and Swin-Tiny for CIFAR-100 using both proxies. Expect:
     - ViT-Small: Fine-tune + Confidence/HR performs well.
     - Swin-Tiny: NegGrad+ + HR significantly outperforms others in ToW/ToW-MIA.
     - SalUn: High ToW but low ToW-MIA, especially on ViT.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
### Open Question 1
- **Question**: Why do ViT and Swin-T exhibit different optimal unlearning methods (fine-tuning vs. NegGrad+), and can this be predicted from architectural properties?
- **Basis in paper**: [inferred] From Section 4.3, the authors link Swin-T's local windowed attention to better compatibility with gradient-based methods like NegGrad+, and ViT's global attention to fine-tuning, but without deeper analysis.
- **Why unresolved**: The paper observes and speculates on the role of inductive biases but does not provide empirical evidence or a theoretical framework to explain or predict architecture-method compatibility for VTs.
- **What evidence would resolve it**: Controlled experiments ablating architectural components (e.g., window size in Swin-T) and correlating them with unlearning performance; or developing a metric based on attention pattern dispersion to forecast method efficacy.

### Open Question 2
- **Question**: Are CNN-derived memorization proxies (e.g., Confidence, Holdout Retraining) sufficient for VTs, or do VTs require new proxies that better capture their memorization patterns?
- **Basis in paper**: [inferred] Section 4.1 validates existing proxies but notes varying correlation strengths (e.g., Swin-T closer to CNNs), implying potential for VTs-specific improvements.
- **Why unresolved**: The paper evaluates only CNN-originated proxies and does not explore whether transformer-specific proxies (e.g., leveraging attention entropy) could yield higher fidelity or better unlearning outcomes.
- **What evidence would resolve it**: Design and evaluation of novel proxies tailored to VTs (e.g., using attention head importance or patch memorization), measuring their Spearman correlation against true memorization scores and impact on ToW/ToW-MIA.

### Open Question 3
- **Question**: What is the mechanism behind the decline in pretraining benefits for unlearning as dataset complexity increases?
- **Basis in paper**: [explicit] Sections 4.2 and 4.4 explicitly state that pretraining aids unlearning on simpler datasets (CIFAR-10) but not on complex ones (ImageNet-1K), without explaining why.
- **Why unresolved**: The paper documents the trend but does not investigate underlying causes such as increased feature entanglement, representation quality degradation, or overfitting in pretrained models on complex data.
- **What evidence would resolve it**: Comparative analysis of feature spaces (e.g., via CKA or linear probing) between pretrained and retrained models across dataset complexities; or measuring memorization scores in pretrained vs. randomly initialized VTs on diverse datasets.

### Open Question 4
- **Question**: Why does SalUn perform poorly on MIA metrics for VTs, particularly ViT, and can its performance be improved for transformer architectures?
- **Basis in paper**: [inferred] Consistent results in Tables 13 and 14 show SalUn achieving high ToW but low ToW-MIA for VTs, especially with Confidence proxy, yet the paper offers no analysis.
- **Why unresolved**: The paper does not examine why SalUn's saliency-based parameter perturbation fails to mitigate MIA in global attention models like ViT, leaving the root cause unclear.
- **What evidence would resolve it**: Visualization of saliency distributions across transformer layers; modifying SalUn to incorporate attention-aware saliency or adaptive thresholds; or systematic study of how different saliency criteria affect MIA resistance in VTs.

## Limitations
- Generalizability beyond studied architectures (ViT, Swin-T) is uncertain; corpus support for core claims relies primarily on internal experiments.
- Mechanistic explanations for architecture-algorithm compatibility (e.g., "global vs. local attention") remain speculative without parameter-level analysis.
- The role of ImageNet-1K pretraining in shaping memorization distributions is not isolated; findings may not hold for randomly initialized or differently pretrained VTs.
- Evaluation uses a fixed 3k forget set; sensitivity to forget set size/selection is unexplored.
- RUM framework's sequential partitioning is central to reported results, but its contribution versus base algorithms alone is unclear.

## Confidence
- **Memorization Distribution Similarity**: Medium (empirical histograms show pattern, but weak theoretical/corpus grounding; break condition untested on extreme variants).
- **Proxy Transfer Effectiveness**: High (strong |ρ| correlations in Table 1 for studied models; break condition would require new architecture/proxy combo to fail).
- **Architecture-Algorithm Compatibility**: Medium (robust empirical trends in Figure 1/Table 13, but mechanistic saliency/gradient explanation is post-hoc; break condition plausible).

## Next Checks
1. **Test distribution generalizability**: Train additional VT architectures (e.g., ConViT, PVT, CrossViT) on CIFAR-10/100, compute Feldman memorization scores, and compare tail heaviness against ResNet baselines. Validates: whether long-tailed memorization is truly architecture-agnostic or specific to hierarchical/non-hierarchical designs.
2. **Analyze parameter saliency gradients**: For ViT-Small vs. Swin-Tiny trained on CIFAR-100, compute example-wise parameter influence (e.g., using gradient inner products) and compare spatial distribution (global vs. localized). Validates: mechanistic claim about Swin-T's concentrated updates favoring NegGrad+.
3. **Isolate pretraining effects**: Compare memorization distributions and proxy correlations for (a) ImageNet-pretrained ViT/Swin-T, (b) supervised-trained from scratch on CIFAR-10, and (c) self-supervised pretrained (e.g., MAE) then fine-tuned. Validates: whether pretraining is a confounding factor in reported similarity to CNNs.
