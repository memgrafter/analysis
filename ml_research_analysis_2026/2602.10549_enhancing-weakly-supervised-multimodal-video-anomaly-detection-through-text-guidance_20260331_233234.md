# Enhancing Weakly Supervised Multimodal Video Anomaly Detection through Text Guidance

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID**: 2602.10549
- **Source URL**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.10549
- **Reference count**: 40
- **Primary result**: State-of-the-art AUC 89.67% on UCF-Crime (Text+RGB+Flow) and AP 85.92% on XD-Violence (all modalities).

## Executive Summary
This paper tackles weakly supervised multimodal video anomaly detection (WS-MVAD) by introducing text as an underexplored modality to boost performance and explainability. The authors propose a multi-stage text augmentation (MSTA) mechanism that leverages in-context learning with an LLM to generate high-quality anomalous text samples, addressing data scarcity and imbalance. For multimodal fusion, a multi-scale bottleneck Transformer (MSBT) progressively integrates modalities through compressed bottleneck tokens, reducing redundancy and imbalance. Evaluations on UCF-Crime and XD-Violence achieve new state-of-the-art results, demonstrating the effectiveness of the text-guided approach.

## Method Summary
The method consists of three main parts. First, MSTA generates a balanced set of anomalous text samples using LLaMA-3-8B over three stages: summarizing video captions, assigning pseudo-anomaly scores via in-context learning, and generating new anomalous sentences from high-scoring captions. This augmented set fine-tunes a BERT-base text encoder with binary cross-entropy loss, producing robust text anomaly features. Second, unimodal features are extracted: RGB and optical flow via I3D, audio via VGGish, and text via the fine-tuned BERT. Each feature vector is projected to a common dimension (DE=128) and passed through a unimodal Transformer. Third, the MSBT fusion module processes each pair of modalities with a multi-scale bottleneck Transformer that halves the number of bottleneck tokens across five layers, progressively distilling cross-modal information. Pairwise fused outputs are weighted using the final bottleneck tokens via an additional Transformer and MLP, then aggregated by a global encoder and regressed to anomaly scores. Training uses a top-K multiple instance learning (MIL) loss, with separate optimizers for the text encoder (AdamW, LR=5e-5) and fusion model (SGD, LR=4e-4 for UCF, 5e-3 for XD). At inference, the final snippet score blends the fused anomaly score and the text anomaly probability (α=0.5).

## Key Results
- Achieves state-of-the-art performance: 89.67% AUC on UCF-Crime (Text+RGB+Flow) and 85.92% AP on XD-Violence (all modalities).
- Ablation studies demonstrate that MSTA contributes significantly to the performance gains.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
MSTA effectively mitigates the scarcity and class imbalance of anomalous textual descriptions by generating diverse, high-quality samples through LLM-based in-context learning. This enables fine-tuning a text encoder that reliably captures anomalous semantics, as evidenced by strong text-only detection performance. Meanwhile, MSBT addresses modality redundancy and imbalance by compressing cross-modal interactions into progressively fewer bottleneck tokens, forcing the model to retain only the most informative representations. The weighting of pairwise fused features using the final bottleneck tokens allows adaptive emphasis on the most relevant modality combinations, leading to robust multimodal integration.

## Foundational Learning
- **Weakly Supervised Learning**: Only video-level labels are available; frame-level anomaly must be inferred. *Why needed*: WS-MVAD avoids costly frame-level annotation. *Quick check*: Explain how top-K MIL loss uses video-level labels to supervise snippet scores.
- **Multimodal Fusion**: Combining RGB, flow, audio, and text modalities. *Why needed*: Different modalities capture complementary aspects of anomalies. *Quick check*: Describe how MSBT differs from naive concatenation or average pooling of modality features.
- **Transformer Architecture**: Core building block for unimodal and cross-modal processing. *Why needed*: Enables modeling long-range dependencies and complex interactions. *Quick check*: What are the roles of self-attention and feed-forward layers in the unimodal Transformers?
- **In-Context Learning (ICL)**: LLM technique to perform tasks without gradient updates by conditioning on examples. *Why needed*: Generates pseudo-labels and new text samples for augmentation without additional training data. *Quick check*: How does Stage-II use ICL to assign anomaly scores to captions?
- **Multiple Instance Learning (MIL)**: Framework where bags (videos) of instances (snippets) have a single label. *Why needed*: Matches the weakly supervised setting. *Quick check*: How does selecting top-K snippets for loss computation encourage anomaly localization?
- **Bottleneck Token Reduction**: Progressive halving of token count in MSBT layers. *Why needed*: Reduces redundancy and computational cost while preserving essential cross-modal information. *Quick check*: If the initial bottleneck token count is 16 and L_M=5, what are the token counts after each layer?

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component Map**  
MSTA → Text Encoder (BERT) → Unimodal Transformer → (for each modality) → MSBT Fusion (pairwise) → Bottleneck Token Weighting (Transformer+MLP) → Global Encoder → Anomaly Regressor. Additionally, text anomaly probability feeds into final score blending at inference.

**Critical Path**  
1. Video input is split into snippets.  
2. For each modality, pre-trained extractors (I3D for RGB/Flow, VGGish for Audio, fine-tuned BERT for text) produce feature vectors.  
3. Features are projected to DE=128 and processed by unimodal Transformers.  
4. For every pair of modalities, the MSBT applies a series of bottleneck Transformer layers (L_M=5) that halve the token count, producing a fused representation.  
5. The fused outputs from all pairs are combined by weighting their bottleneck tokens via a Transformer and MLP.  
6. The globally encoded representation is passed to an MLP regressor to predict a snippet-level anomaly score.  
7. During inference, the final score `ŝ_i = α * s_i + (1-α) * p_i` mixes the fused score `s_i` and the text-only probability `p_i`.

**Design Tradeoffs**  
- **LLM-based MSTA** vs. lightweight augmentation: LLM yields high-quality texts but introduces significant latency and non-determinism.  
- **Bottleneck token reduction** vs. information loss: Aggressive halving reduces parameters and redundancy but may discard subtle cues; L_M=5 and N_bt1=16 provide a balance.  
- **Unimodal Transformers** vs. simple linear projections: Transformers add capacity to model intra-modality dynamics but risk overfitting with limited data.  
- **Top-K MIL** vs. mean/max pooling: Top-K focuses on outliers but requires setting K; the paper uses K=9 without extensive tuning discussion.

**Failure Signatures**  
- Text-only anomaly detection performance far below reported baselines indicates poor MSTA generation or fine-tuning.  
- Bottleneck token counts do not halve across MSBT layers (e.g., 16→16→…) suggest incorrect implementation of the multi-scale reduction.  
- Training loss diverges or video-level predictions saturate at 0/1 during MIL training, suggesting K selection or loss weighting issues.  
- High variance in final AUC across different LLM runs points to instability in pseudo-label generation; setting temperature=0 may be necessary.

**First Experiments**  
1. **Unimodal Baseline Reproduction**: Extract I3D and VGGish features using standard protocols (16-frame snippets, 224×224 resize). Train a simple MIL classifier (e.g., mean pooling + linear) and verify unimodal AUC/AP matches reported baselines (~84-87% on UCF-Crime).  
2. **MSTA Quality Audit**: Sample ≥100 Stage-III generated anomalous texts. Manually assess semantic coherence by comparing to ground-truth anomalous captions. Optionally compute BERTScore against human-annotated references to quantify relevance.  
3. **MSBT Bottleneck Verification**: Feed dummy data through the MSBT and log the shape of bottleneck tokens after each of the L_M=5 layers. Confirm that the token count reduces by half each time (e.g., 16→8→4→2→1). Also check that the weighting module correctly aggregates pairwise outputs.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
1. **Efficiency of MSTA**: How can the computational overhead of the MSTA text augmentation be reduced while preserving its effectiveness? The pipeline requires ~7 seconds per video using LLaMA-3-8B, making it expensive for large datasets.  
2. **Scalability of MSBT**: Does the proposed MSBT fusion module scale effectively to scenarios with more than four modalities, and does it maintain advantages in reducing redundancy and imbalance? The paper only evaluates up to text, RGB, flow, audio.  
3. **Accuracy of ICL Pseudo-Labels**: How accurate are the Stage-II ICL-generated pseudo-labels for video captions, and how does label noise affect the fine-tuned text feature extractor and overall anomaly detection? The paper notes ambiguous captions receive scores around 0.5 but does not evaluate label quality.  
4. **Explainability in Practice**: Does the integration of text modality truly improve explainability for human operators in video anomaly detection, beyond quantitative performance gains? No user study or interpretability evaluation is conducted.

## Limitations
- **Reproducibility gaps**: The exact prompts for MSTA stages, LLM generation parameters (temperature, top-p), and fusion module hyperparameters (layer norms, dropout) are not specified.  
- **Feature extraction ambiguity**: The pre-trained I3D and VGGish versions, frame sampling rates, and snippet lengths are unreported, risking mismatched features.  
- **Training details omission**: The number of epochs, learning rate decay schedule, and early stopping criteria for the MSBT fusion model are missing, potentially affecting convergence and final performance.

## Confidence
- **SOTA performance claims (89.67% AUC, 85.92% AP)**: Medium – results depend on undisclosed implementation choices and LLM output variance.  
- **MSTA contribution (ablation significance)**: Medium-High – ablation shows benefit, but text augmentation quality is sensitive to prompts and LLM randomness.  
- **MSBT fusion efficacy**: Medium-High – core mechanism is described, but exact Transformer configuration and token reduction implementation are critical and not fully detailed.
