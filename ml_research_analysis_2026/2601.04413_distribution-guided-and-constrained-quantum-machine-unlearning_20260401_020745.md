# Distribution-Guided and Constrained Quantum Machine Unlearning

## Quick Facts
- **ArXiv ID:** 2601.04413  
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.04413  
- **Reference count:** 24  
- **Primary result:** Distribution-guided quantum unlearning achieves sharp forgotten-class suppression (e.g., Covertype recall: 0.633→0.067), minimal retained-class degradation, and low KL divergence (mean 0.047) to gold retrained models.

## Executive Summary
Quantum machine unlearning aims to remove the influence of specific data from a trained model without full retraining. Existing approaches often use uniform target distributions for the forgotten class, leading to ineffective suppression and uncontrolled trade-offs with retained-class performance. This work introduces a distribution-guided framework that constructs a tunable, similarity-based forgetting target from the original model’s outputs on a calibration subset and employs anchor-based constraints to preserve retained-class behavior. Experiments on Iris and Covertype datasets with variational quantum classifiers show near-complete forgetting of the target class while retaining accuracy on other classes and aligning closely with models retrained from scratch. The method significantly outperforms uniform-target unlearning across all metrics.

## Method Summary
The approach operates in two phases. First, a variational quantum classifier (VQC) is trained on the full dataset using Adam with parameter-shift gradients for 300 iterations. Second, for a chosen forgotten class, unlearning optimizes a new parameter set \( w \) via gradient ascent on the objective  
\[
J(w) = \mathbb{E}_{x\in F}\Big[\sum_k q_k \log p_w(k|x)\Big] + \alpha \mathbb{E}_{x\in A}\Big[\sum_k p_{\text{orig}}(k|x) \log p_w(k|x)\Big] - \lambda \|w - w_{\text{orig}}\|^2.
\]  
The first term drives predictions on the forget set \( F \) toward a target distribution \( q \), which is derived from the original model’s softmax outputs on a calibration subset \( S \subseteq F \) and sets the forgotten class probability to zero. The second term (anchor) preserves the original behavior on an anchor set \( A \) of retained samples, and the third (L2 regularization) keeps \( w \) close to \( w_{\text{orig}} \). The target includes a tunable exponent \( \beta \) (default 1.0). Hyperparameters \( \alpha=1.0 \) and \( \lambda=0.01 \) are used. Optimization starts from \( w_{\text{orig}} \) and uses the same parameter-shift technique.

## Key Results
- **Forgetting efficacy:** On Covertype, recall for the forgotten class drops from 0.633 to 0.067; on Iris, similarly sharp suppression is achieved.
- **Retention:** Class-wise recalls on retained classes remain unchanged relative to the original model, indicating minimal degradation.
- **Alignment:** Mean KL divergence (renormalized) between unlearned and gold retrained models on retained test data is 0.047, substantially lower than for uniform-target unlearning.
- **Overall superiority:** The framework outperforms uniform-target baselines on all examined metrics.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The distribution-guided target is constructed from the original model’s outputs on a subset of the forget set, capturing the inherent similarity structure among classes. By zeroing the forgotten class probability and scaling the remaining class probabilities proportionally (via exponent \( \beta \)), the target provides a nuanced direction for unlearning that respects the relative importance of retained classes—unlike a uniform target, which would collapse all retained classes to equal probability and distort decision boundaries. The anchor set of retained samples explicitly constrains the model to preserve its original behavior on data that should remain unaffected. The L2 regularization prevents excessive parameter drift, maintaining overall model stability. Together, these elements balance strong forgetting with retention, yielding a model nearly identical to one retrained solely on retained data.

## Foundational Learning
- **Variational Quantum Classifier (VQC):** Quantum circuit with trainable parameters mapping classical data to class probabilities. *Why needed:* It is the underlying model being unlearned. *Quick check:* Confirm circuit has feature map + ansatz, 72 parameters, and outputs from three measurement qubits.
- **Parameter-shift rule:** Gradient estimation for quantum circuits via finite differences at shifted parameters. *Why needed:* Enables gradient-based training on quantum hardware. *Quick check:* Verify gradients are computed by evaluating the circuit at \(\pm \pi/2\) shifts per parameter.
- **KL divergence:** Distributional difference measure. *Why needed:* Quantifies alignment between unlearned and gold retrained models. *Quick check:* Compute \( \text{KL}(p_{\text{unlearn}} \| p_{\text{retrained}}) \) on retained test samples, renormalized to sum to 1.
- **Class-level unlearning:** Removing influence of an entire class rather than individual samples. *Why needed:* Privacy regulations may require forgetting all data of a certain type. *Quick check:* Ensure forget set \( F \) contains only one class label.
- **Distribution-guided target:** Target distribution derived from original model’s outputs, not uniform. *Why needed:* Provides a similarity-informed forgetting direction that respects retained class relationships. *Quick check:* Compute \( q_k \propto (\mathbb{E}_{S}[p_{\text{orig}}(k|x)])^\beta \) and set \( q_f = 0 \).
- **Anchor-based constraints:** Using retained samples to preserve original predictions. *Why needed:* Prevents catastrophic forgetting of useful patterns. *Quick check:* Verify loss term includes \( \mathbb{E}_{A}[p_{\text{orig}} \log p_w] \).

## Architecture Onboarding

**Component map (plain text):**  
Data → Min-max scaling to \([0, \pi]\) → Quantum feature map (Ry rotations + CX‑Rz‑CX entangling blocks, reuploaded twice) → Variational ansatz (Ry, Rz rotations + ring CNOT, 3 repetitions) → Pauli‑Z measurements on qubits 3–5 → Softmax → Class probabilities  
Unlearning components: \( w_{\text{orig}} \), forget set \( F \), anchor set \( A \), calibration subset \( S \subseteq F \) → target \( q \) construction → loss \( J(w) \) → gradient ascent via parameter‑shift starting from \( w_{\text{orig}} \) → unlearned parameters \( w \).

**Critical path:**  
1. Train VQC on full dataset to obtain \( w_{\text{orig}} \).  
2. Partition data: define forget set \( F \) (class \( f \)), retained set \( R \); select anchor \( A \subseteq R \) and calibration \( S \subseteq F \).  
3. Cache original predictions \( p_{\text{orig}}(\cdot|x) \) on \( A \) and \( S \).  
4. Build target distribution \( q \) from \( S \).  
5. Optimize \( w \) to maximize \( J(w) \) using gradient ascent.  
6. Evaluate forgetting (recall on \( F \)), retention (recall on \( R \)), and KL divergence vs. gold retrained model.

**Design tradeoffs:**  
- Exponent \( \beta \) smooths the target distribution; higher values emphasize high‑probability classes.  
- Anchor weight \( \alpha \) balances preservation (↑α) vs. forgetting (↓α).  
- Regularization strength \( \lambda \) controls deviation from \( w_{\text{orig}} \); low \( \lambda \) risks instability, high \( \lambda \) hinders forgetting.

**Failure signatures:**  
- High mean predicted probability \( p_f \) on \( F \) indicates incomplete forgetting.  
- Drop in retained‑class recall or overall accuracy indicates over‑forgetting.  
- KL divergence substantially above 0.047 signals misalignment with retrained model.  
- Non‑convergence or oscillation of \( J(w) \) suggests inappropriate learning rate or iterations.

**First experiments:**  
1. Train VQC on Iris; verify baseline accuracy >90% and record confusion matrix.  
2. Apply uniform‑target unlearning to a class (e.g., class 0); measure forgetting and retention.  
3. Apply proposed distribution‑guided unlearning with default \( \beta=1.0 \), \( \alpha=1.0 \), \( \lambda=0.01 \); compare metrics to uniform baseline and gold retrained model.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- **Instance-level extension:** How can the framework be extended to forget individual data points rather than whole classes? The paper notes this as future work; instance‑level forgetting poses challenges like very small forget sets and over‑fitting to anchors. Resolution would require a working implementation validated by membership inference attacks and preserved accuracy.
- **Robustness to noise:** How does the method perform on noisy intermediate‑scale quantum (NISQ) devices? The authors assumed no hardware noise; noise could distort gradient estimates and circuit dynamics. Evidence would come from systematic experiments on noisy simulators or real hardware, measuring forgetting efficacy and retention across noise levels, possibly with mitigation.
- **Scalability to high dimensions and many classes:** Does the approach generalize beyond three classes and low‑dimensional (4‑feature) inputs? All experiments used PCA‑reduced features; high‑dimensional data may require different circuit architectures and affect the target construction. Validation would involve comprehensive tests on benchmark datasets with original features and >5 classes, reporting unlearning quality and computational cost.

## Limitations
- Hyperparameters for unlearning (iterations, learning rate, batch size) are unspecified; optimal values likely depend on the dataset and circuit.
- Sizes and selection criteria for anchor set \( A \) and calibration subset \( S \) are not defined, potentially affecting reproducibility and performance.
- Evaluation is limited to Iris and Covertype (both low‑dimensional after PCA); generalizability to complex, high‑dimensional data remains unproven.

## Confidence
- **Forgotten‑class suppression:** Medium (strong results on two datasets but hyperparameter‑dependent).  
- **Retained‑class preservation:** Medium (minimal degradation observed, yet robustness unclear).  
- **Low KL divergence:** High (consistently low divergence directly measured).  
- **Overall superiority over uniform‑target:** Medium (limited datasets; baseline may not be fully tuned).

## Next Checks
1. **Hyperparameter resolution:** Systematically vary unlearning learning rate (e.g., 1e‑3, 1e‑4), iterations (50–200), and batch size; monitor convergence of \( J(w) \) and mean \( p_f \) to establish stable defaults.
2. **Set size evaluation:** Test anchor fractions (10%, 25%, 50% of retained data) and calibration fractions (5%, 20%, 50% of forget set); analyze impact on forgetting efficacy and retention.
3. **Dataset generalization:** Apply the method to an additional dataset (e.g., MNIST binary or a high‑dimensional tabular set) with appropriate embedding; verify replication of results and superiority over uniform baseline.
