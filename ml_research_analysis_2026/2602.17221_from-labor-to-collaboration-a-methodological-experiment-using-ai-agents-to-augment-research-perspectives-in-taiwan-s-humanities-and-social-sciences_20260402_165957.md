# From Labor to Collaboration: A Methodological Experiment Using AI Agents to Augment Research Perspectives in Taiwan's Humanities and Social Sciences

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2602.17221
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.17221
- **Reference count:** 40
- **Primary result:** Proposed and validated a seven‑stage Agentic Workflow that boosts research efficiency by 89% while keeping human judgment indispensable for Theory, ethics, and contextual reasoning.

## Executive Summary
This study fills a methodological gap by designing and testing an AI‑augmented workflow for secondary data research in humanities and social sciences (HSS). The workflow decomposes research into seven sequential stages, assigning execution‑intensive tasks to AI agents while reserving judgment‑intensive decisions for humans. Applied to Taiwan’s Claude.ai usage data (7,729 conversations), it demonstrates clear operability and reveals three operational modes: direct execution, iterative refinement, and human‑led. Human judgment remains irreplaceable for research questions, theoretical interpretation, contextual reasoning, and ethics. Empirical results show 25.8% of AI use is academic‑related, median task time drops from 1.75 hours to 12 minutes (89% saving), and 82.9% of tasks are human‑completable—indicating AI accelerates rather than replaces core research capabilities.

## Method Summary
The authors used Claude Code (Claude Opus 4.6) linked to a Git repository as the execution environment. They filtered the publicly released Anthropic Economic Index (AEI) dataset to Taiwan (7,729 conversations), then applied a seven‑stage Agentic Workflow: (0) Planning, (1) Literature Collection, (2) Literature Analysis, (3) Data Exploration, (4) Data Analysis, (5) Manuscript Writing, (6) Reference Management. Each stage defines a specialized AI agent role with explicit “must‑not‑do” constraints and a human review checkpoint, enforcing a verifiability principle. Human researchers provided direction, verified every AI output, and made all theoretical and ethical decisions. The workflow was run on the AEI data to compute academic task share, time savings, and human‑completion rate, and to diagnose an error case (hallucinated reference) to illustrate recovery.

## Key Results
- Designed a seven‑stage Agentic Workflow based on task modularization, human‑AI division of labor, and verifiability.
- Identified three operational modes: direct execution, iterative refinement, and human‑led.
- Empirical findings: 25.8% of AI use pertains to academic tasks; median task time saved 89% (1.75 hrs → 12 min); 82.9% of tasks completable by humans alone, proving acceleration rather than capability replacement.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1: Task Modularization
- **Claim:** Breaking research into discrete, sequential stages with clear inputs/outputs lets AI Agents execute reliably and humans intervene at quality gates.
- **Mechanism:** The workflow decomposes the entire process into seven modules (e.g., literature collection, data analysis). Each module has a dedicated AI role and a human review point, reducing complexity and making the process teachable, reproducible, and manageable.
- **Core assumption:** Research tasks can be sufficiently structured to enable clean decomposition without losing contextual coherence.
- **Evidence anchors:** Abstract mentions seven‑stage Agentic Workflow with clear roles; Table 1 defines stages, human/AI roles, outputs; Section IV shows operational modes vary by stage, supporting module‑specific tailoring.
- **Break condition:** Tightly coupled, iterative tasks (e.g., concurrent theory building and data coding) may lose nuance if forced into a sequential modular structure, causing back‑tracking and inefficiency.

### Mechanism 2: Human‑AI Division of Labor by Judgment vs. Execution
- **Claim:** Assigning judgment‑intensive tasks (research questions, interpretation, ethics) to humans and execution‑intensive tasks (search, coding, drafting) to AI leverages complementary strengths, preserving research integrity while accelerating productivity.
- **Mechanism:** The workflow embodies a hybrid intelligence model: humans provide direction, critical evaluation, and final quality assurance; AI retrieves information, performs computations, and generates drafts. This division is explicitly encoded in each stage’s role definition.
- **Core assumption:** Humans retain irreplaceable capabilities in ambiguous, contextual, and value‑laden reasoning; AI excels at high‑volume, rule‑based, pattern‑consistent operations.
- **Evidence anchors:** Abstract delineates clear roles for humans (research judgment and ethical decisions) and AI (information retrieval and text generation); Section V.1 identifies four irreplaceable human functions; empirical 82.9% human‑completable rate indicates AI accelerates rather than replaces.
- **Break condition:** Over‑delegation of judgment (e.g., letting AI formulate research questions or interpret data without scrutiny) may yield mechanized output, loss of theoretical depth, or embedded biases.

### Mechanism 3: Verifiability and Iterative Refinement Loop
- **Claim:** Mandating human review of every AI output and enabling prompt‑driven iteration catches errors, aligns results with intent, and progressively improves quality.
- **Mechanism:** The “verifiability principle” requires all AI outputs be verified by humans, with full version history (Git) retained. When errors are found, humans issue refined prompts; AI revises. This iterative dialogue mimics pair‑programming or collaborative writing.
- **Core assumption:** AI outputs are inherently fallible (hallucinations, mis‑classifications) and can be steadily improved through targeted human guidance.
- **Evidence anchors:** Abstract grounds the workflow in verifiability; Section IV details a four‑step recovery process for a hallucinated reference; Table 4 shows iterative refinement appears across multiple stages, confirming iteration as a common mode.
- **Break condition:** Skilled verification is essential; complacency or omission in review propagates errors. Poor prompts may cause non‑convergent iteration or degradation.

## Foundational Learning
To operate within this architecture, new engineers must internalize:

- **Seven‑Stage Workflow** – The entire collaboration is orchestrated through these stages; misunderstanding module boundaries or dependencies breaks the process.  
  *Quick check:* What is the primary output of Stage 3 (Data Understanding & Exploration), and how does it feed into Stage 4?

- **Operational Modes** – Each stage may operate in direct execution, iterative refinement, or human‑led; recognizing the appropriate mode helps allocate effort and set expectations.  
  *Quick check:* For “generate a bar chart from a cleaned dataset,” which mode is most likely and why?

- **Human‑in‑the‑Loop Verification** – AI is fallible; verification is the safety net preventing error accumulation. Engineers must design prompts and checkpoints that facilitate effective review.  
  *Quick check:* Name two specific verification techniques used in the workflow (e.g., requiring DOIs, cross‑checking statistics).

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map:** The system consists of:  
  • Seven sequential stages (Planning → Literature Collection → Literature Analysis → Data Exploration → Data Analysis → Manuscript Writing → Reference Management)  
  • For each stage: a specialized AI Agent role (e.g., Literature Collection Agent) with a “must‑not‑do” constraint list  
  • Human roles: define research questions, interpret results, verify outputs, make ethical decisions  
  • Supporting infrastructure: Git version control, data folder structure, prompt templates, verification checklists.

- **Critical path:** The minimal viable path is linear Stage 0 → 1 → 2 → 3 → 4 → 5 → 6. In practice, feedback loops are common; e.g., after seeing analysis results (Stage 4) one may return to literature search (Stage 1) to fill gaps.

- **Design tradeoffs:**  
  • Modularity vs. flexibility: Strict modules enable clear handoffs but may hinder holistic thinking; back‑tracking compensates.  
  • AI autonomy vs. control: Autonomy median 4.0/5.0; higher autonomy speeds execution but risks drift; managed via explicit constraints and mandatory human review.  
  • Efficiency vs. depth: Routine tasks see huge time savings (89%) but may tempt skipping deep reading; human‑led stages protect against this.

- **Failure signatures:**  
  • High volume of hallucinated references or factual errors → inadequate verification at Stage 1 or 6.  
  • Frequent revisiting of early stages (e.g., repeatedly resetting literature search) → poor initial scoping or unclear module outputs.  
  • A stage consistently operated in direct execution when human‑led is recommended (e.g., planning) → over‑reliance on AI.  
  • No Git commits or prompt iterations → workflow not followed transparently.

- **First 3 experiments:**  
  1. **Replicate the literature collection pipeline** using the provided prompts on a known topic. Count hallucinated references and measure human verification time. Goal: calibrate verification rigor for your domain.  
  2. **Map operational modes** on a small AEI sample (e.g., 100 conversations). For each stage, decide which mode (direct, iterative, human‑led) naturally fits and record justification. Goal: internalize how task characteristics drive mode selection.  
  3. **Stress‑test the division of labor**: assign a judgment‑heavy task (e.g., “interpret whether the AI autonomy score reflects user trust”) to the AI Agent. Document failures and human corrections. Goal: solidify understanding of irreplaceable human roles.

## Limitations
- Unknown data specifics: exact AEI dataset version (column names, precomputed variables) and aggregation logic for metrics (e.g., 25.8% academic share) are not disclosed.
- Undocumented implementation: complete prompt library, full agent role constraints, and verification checklists are absent; findings rely on representative examples.
- Generalizability limits: corpus restricted to Taiwan’s Claude.ai usage; unclear transferability to other regions, LLMs, or qualitative HSS methods.

## Confidence
- **High:** Task modularization mechanism (explicit seven‑stage design) and verifiability loop (documented error recovery).
- **Medium:** Human‑AI division claim (82.9% human‑completable rate supports acceleration), overall workflow operability (case study demonstrates but prompt/role opacity reduces replication certainty).
- **Low:** Cross‑context applicability (findings may not hold for non‑English corpora, different AI systems, or research with heavy iterative theorizing).
