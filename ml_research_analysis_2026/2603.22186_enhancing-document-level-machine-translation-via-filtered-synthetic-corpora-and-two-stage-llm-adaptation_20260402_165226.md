# Enhancing Document-Level Machine Translation via Filtered Synthetic Corpora and Two-Stage LLM Adaptation

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2603.22186
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.22186
- **Reference count:** 0
- **Primary result:** Optimal multi-metric filtering (sacreBLEU≥35, COMET≥0.75, LaBSE-CosSim≥0.85) combined with a two-stage fine-tuning curriculum yields document-level En-De translation quality of sacreBLEU 15.96, COMET 0.701, and LaBSE-CosSim 0.860.

## Executive Summary
The paper addresses the challenge of underperforming large language models (LLMs) in document-level machine translation, which stems from a scarcity of high-quality parallel documents and the propensity for LLMs to hallucinate or omit content. The proposed solution creates a large synthetic document-level corpus by translating summarization data (CNN/Daily Mail) with a powerful LLM (Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct). This synthetic data is rigorously filtered using a combination of lexical, learned, and semantic metrics (sacreBLEU, COMET, LaBSE-CosSim) to mitigate noise. A smaller target model (Llama-3.2-1B-Instruct) is then fine-tuned in two stages: first on abundant sentence-level data to learn foundational translation, and second on the filtered document-level corpus to adapt to context. This pipeline demonstrably outperforms single-stage training and establishes optimal filtering thresholds.

## Method Summary
The method begins by augmenting 20K English documents from the CNN/Daily Mail summarization corpus into German pseudo-parallel documents using Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct. For each document, a pseudo-reference is generated by applying Google Translate at the sentence level. Each generated document is then evaluated against this pseudo-reference using three metrics: sacreBLEU (lexical precision), COMET (learned quality), and LaBSE-CosSim (cross-lingual semantic similarity). Only document pairs meeting all three thresholds (sacreBLEU≥35, COMET≥0.75, LaBSE-CosSim≥0.85) are retained. For fine-tuning, Llama-3.2-1B-Instruct undergoes Stage 1 training on the News Commentary v16 sentence-level corpus, followed by Stage 2 continuation on the filtered document-level synthetic data. Performance is evaluated on a held-out document-level test set using the same three metrics.

## Key Results
- Two-stage fine-tuning significantly outperforms single-stage training on the same (unfiltered) augmented data (e.g., sacreBLEU 15.07 vs. 11.24).
- Multi-metric filtering yields the best results; the optimal three-way filter (≥35 BLEU, ≥0.75 COMET, ≥0.85 LaBSE-CosSim) achieves the top scores: sacreBLEU 15.96, COMET 0.701, LaBSE-CosSim 0.860.
- The filtering process retains approximately 30% of the initial 20K augmented documents, indicating aggressive but effective noise reduction.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
**Claim:** LLM-augmented document-level data can be made viable for fine-tuning by mitigating the hallucination/omission noise introduced during the augmentation process.
**Mechanism:** A larger, more capable LLM (Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct) is prompted to translate source documents from a summarization corpus (CNN/Daily Mail) into a target language (German), creating pseudo-parallel document-level pairs. The inherent noise (hallucinations/omissions) in these synthetic pairs is reduced by filtering with multiple automatic metrics before the data is used for fine-tuning a smaller target model. The core assumption is that noise patterns in LLM-generated translations are detectable by a combination of reference-based (sacreBLEU, COMET) and reference-free (LaBSE-CosSim) metrics when using a high-quality pseudo-reference (Google Translate).
**Break condition:** If the filtering metrics fail to correlate well with human judgment for LLM-generated *document-level* hallucinations, or if the pseudo-reference itself contains significant errors, the filtered corpus may remain too noisy, degrading fine-tuning performance.

### Mechanism 2
**Claim:** Multi-metric filtering using complementary signals (lexical, learned, semantic) is more effective at improving synthetic corpus quality than any single metric.
**Mechanism:** sacreBLEU (n-gram precision), COMET (learned quality prediction), and LaBSE-CosSim (cross-lingual semantic similarity) are applied with thresholds. Their combination leverages orthogonal information: sacreBLEU penalizes lexical deviation, COMET captures fluency and adequacy, and LaBSE-CosSim guards against semantic divergence. Jointly filtering requires a candidate translation to pass all criteria, leading to a higher precision corpus. The core assumption is that the failure modes of the three metrics are largely non-overlapping.
**Break condition:** If the metrics' decision boundaries for "acceptable" translation become misaligned for document-context outputs, strict joint filtering could over-filter, discarding useful data and reducing corpus diversity needed for generalizable learning.

### Mechanism 3
**Claim:** A two-stage fine-tuning curriculum (sentence-level → document-level) successfully transfers foundational translation skill from abundant sentence-level data to the challenging document-level task, outperforming direct document-level fine-tuning.
**Mechanism:** Stage 1 trains the base LLM (Llama-3.2-1B-Instruct) on high-quality, abundant sentence-level parallel corpora (News Commentary). This establishes robust lexical and syntactic mapping skills in a simpler context. Stage 2 then fine-tunes the already competent model on the filtered, context-rich document-level synthetic corpus. The model adapts to cross-sentence coherence and context usage without having to simultaneously learn basic translation, avoiding catastrophic interference or confusion from noisy document-level data. The core assumption is that sentence-level translation competence is a necessary prerequisite for learning effective document-level translation.
**Break condition:** If the sentence-level and document-level data distributions are too divergent (e.g., different domains, styles), Stage 1 training may induce strong biases that hinder adaptation to the document-level corpus in Stage 2, a form of negative transfer.

## Foundational Learning
- **Concept: Data Augmentation for Low-Resource MT**  
  *Why needed here:* The core problem is the scarcity of genuine document-level parallel corpora. The paper's solution hinges on creating a large synthetic corpus from an existing, abundant monolingual/summarization resource (CNN/Daily Mail).  
  *Quick check question:* What are the risks of using an LLM to generate parallel data for a task it itself will later be fine-tuned on, and how does the paper address them?
- **Concept: Evaluation Metrics for Hallucination & Omission**  
  *Why needed here:* Standard metrics like sacreBLEU do not directly measure faithfulness. The paper's filtering strategy explicitly targets these errors using COMET (quality) and LaBSE-CosSim (semantic similarity) as proxies.  
  *Quick check question:* Why is a reference-free metric like LaBSE-CosSim necessary in the filtering pipeline alongside reference-based metrics?
- **Concept: Curriculum Learning / Staged Fine-Tuning**  
  *Why needed here:* The two-stage training process is the central architectural innovation. It embodies a curriculum from simpler (sentence) to more complex (document) tasks.  
  *Quick check question:* Why might training directly on the (noisy) document-level data from the start be less effective than the proposed two-stage approach?

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component map:**  
Source Data (CNN/Daily Mail) -> Augmentation Engine (Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct) -> Pseudo-Reference Generator (Google Translate) -> Filtering Module (sacreBLEU, COMET, LaBSE-CosSim thresholds) -> Stage 1 Trainer (News Commentary) -> Stage 2 Trainer (Filtered Doc-Level Corpus) -> Evaluation Suite.

**Critical path:** Source Data is translated by the Augmentation Engine. Pseudo-references are generated for each document. The Filtering Module applies thresholds to retain only high-quality pairs. The Stage 1 Trainer produces a base model on sentence-level data. The Stage 2 Trainer adapts this model on the filtered corpus. Finally, the Evaluation Suite measures performance.

**Design tradeoffs:**  
- **Augmentation Model Size:** Using a larger model (8B) for augmentation yields higher-quality initial pairs but increases computational cost versus using the same small model (1B).
- **Filtering Thresholds:** Higher thresholds (e.g., 40/0.8/0.9) produce ultra-clean but very small datasets (~30% retention), risking underfitting. Lower thresholds retain more data but introduce more noise.
- **Two-Stage vs. Single-Stage:** Two-stage adds complexity and time but is shown more effective. A single-stage on filtered data alone may be insufficient if the model hasn't learned basic translation.

**Failure signatures:**  
- **Filtering failure:** Performance degrades despite filtering, indicating metrics are poor proxies for document-level hallucination or over-filtering removed valid data.
- **Stage 1 failure:** If Stage 1 model overfits to News Commentary domain, Stage 2 adaptation to CNN/Daily Mail style may fail. Check for domain mismatch in evaluation.
- **Out-of-distribution:** Poor performance on a new document-level test set from a different genre (e.g., Europarl) indicates the augmented corpus domain (news summarization) is too narrow.

**First 3 experiments:**
1. **Baseline replication:** Train *only* on unfiltered augmented data (2k docs) for the 1B model. Expect ~11.2 BLEU.
2. **Two-stage validation:** Train via two-stage pipeline on 20k News Commentary + 2k unfiltered docs. Expect ~15.1 BLEU.
3. **Filtering efficacy test:** Using the two-stage pipeline, train on 2k docs filtered *only* by sacreBLEU≥35. Compare to unfiltered two-stage and full three-metric filter.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- **Open Question 1:** Does the proposed two-stage fine-tuning and filtering approach generalize effectively to language pairs beyond English-German and domains beyond news?  
  *Basis in paper:* Conclusion states "suggest that our approach can generalize to other domains and language pairs."  
  *Why unresolved:* The study only evaluates En→De on news data; cross-lingual and cross-domain transferability remains untested.  
  *What evidence would resolve it:* Replicate experiments on diverse language pairs (e.g., typologically varied, low-resource) and domains (e.g., technical, conversational).
- **Open Question 2:** How does the reliance on Google Translate as a pseudo-reference for filtering impact data quality and final model performance, especially for language pairs where conventional MT systems may exhibit hallucinations?  
  *Basis in paper:* Filtering uses pseudo-references from Google Translate, assuming its reliability, but no analysis of its error propagation.  
  *Why unresolved:* Google Translate's own hallucinations could corrupt the synthetic corpus, yet the paper doesn't assess reference quality or alternative reference sources.  
  *What evidence would resolve it:* Compare models trained on data filtered using different reference systems (e.g., human translations, other MT systems, or reference-free methods) and measure performance variance.
- **Open Question 3:** Are the empirically determined filtering thresholds (sacreBLEU≥35, COMET≥0.75, LaBSE-CosSim≥0.85) optimal across diverse LLMs and data augmentation strategies?  
  *Basis in paper:* Thresholds are chosen based on 20K augmented samples without justification for generalizability.  
  *Why unresolved:* Thresholds may depend on model capacity, augmentation method, or language pair; the study doesn't explore sensitivity or alternative thresholds.  
  *What evidence would resolve it:* Systematic evaluation varying thresholds across multiple LLMs (different sizes, architectures) and augmentation techniques (e.g., different prompt strategies), analyzing performance trade-offs.
- **Open Question 4:** Can document-level evaluation metrics that don't require sentence-level alignment (unlike Doc-COMET) be effectively integrated into the filtering pipeline to improve data quality?  
  *Basis in paper:* The paper excludes Doc-COMET due to lack of sentence alignment, and xCOMET doesn't support document inputs.  
  *Why unresolved:* The filtering relies on sentence-level metrics applied per sentence, which may not capture document-level coherence; the suitability of alternative metrics is unexplored.  
  *What evidence would resolve it:* Implement and test document-level metrics (e.g., doc-level versions of COMET, BERTScore) in filtering, comparing resulting model performance on document-level coherence tasks.

## Limitations
- **Hyperparameters & training details** (Medium impact): Exact learning rates, schedulers, and epochs for both stages are unreported, risking inconsistent replication.
- **Evaluation set provenance** (High impact): The 1,500-document test set's source and construction are unspecified, hindering comparability and external validation.
- **Pseudo-reference quality** (High risk): Using Google Translate as a "gold" reference assumes its output is error-free, but it may contain hallucinations itself, contaminating filtering.

## Next Checks
1. **Threshold sensitivity sweep:** Train models with relaxed filters (e.g., sacreBLEU≥30/COMET≥0.7/LaBSE≥0.8) to quantify the precision/recall trade-off and identify over/under-filtering.
2. **Stagewise error analysis:** Evaluate the Stage 1 checkpoint on both sentence-level and document-level test sets to confirm skill transfer and diagnose catastrophic forgetting or domain bias.
3. **Human correlation study:** Have annotators judge a sample of filtered vs. unfiltered document pairs for hallucinations/omissions; compute correlation with the three metrics to validate their proxy effectiveness.
