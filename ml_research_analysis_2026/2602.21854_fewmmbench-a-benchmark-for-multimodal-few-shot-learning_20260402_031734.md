# FewMMBench: A Benchmark for Multimodal Few-Shot Learning

## Quick Facts
- ArXiv ID: 2602.21854
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.21854
- Reference count: 40
- Primary result: Instruction-tuned multimodal LLMs show strong zero-shot performance but minimal or negative gains from few-shot or chain-of-thought prompting; retrieval-based example selection fails to consistently beat random, and increasing shot count often degrades accuracy.

## Executive Summary
FewMMBench is a new benchmark designed to evaluate multimodal few-shot in-context learning (ICL) and chain-of-thought (CoT) prompting in large language models. It comprises nine diverse vision-language tasks, using a graph-cut algorithm to curate demonstration sets that balance relevance and diversity. The paper evaluates 26 open-weight models under various settings (zero-shot, random few-shot, retrieved few-shot, and few-shot with CoT). Key findings reveal that instruction-tuned models already perform well without demonstrations, and adding examples offers little improvement or even harms accuracy. Retrieval-based selection of demonstrations does not reliably outperform random selection. Moreover, CoT prompting uniformly decreases performance across all model families, suggesting a mismatch between current multimodal reasoning strategies and the underlying models.

## Method Summary
The benchmark defines nine multimodal tasks (e.g., attribute recognition, temporal reasoning) with fixed query sets of 250 samples each, sourced from existing datasets like MMBench and SeedBench. Demonstration sets are curated using a submodular graph-cut selection on CLIP embeddings to ensure both relevance to the query and diversity among examples. For each query, the system retrieves 8 similar demonstrations (or random if specified). Chain-of-thought rationales are generated using Qwen2.5-VL-7B-Instruct and verified for correctness. Models are evaluated in four configurations: 0-shot, 4-shot random, 4-shot similar (retrieved), and 4/8-shot similar with CoT. Performance is measured via multiple-choice accuracy and a secondary perplexity-based pairwise ranking to mitigate answer formatting biases. Uncertainty is analyzed by examining probability distributions over answer choices.

## Key Results
- Instruction-tuned multimodal LLMs achieve strong zero-shot performance but gain minimal or even negative benefits from few-shot or CoT demonstrations.
- Retrieval-based example selection using graph-cut fails to consistently outperform random example selection, indicating models do not effectively leverage fine-grained semantic similarity.
- CoT prompting uniformly reduces performance across all tested model families, often introducing hallucinations or irrelevant reasoning that distracts from visual grounding.
- Increasing the number of shots beyond four frequently degrades accuracy, suggesting context dilution.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1: Instruction tuning already provides direct task-solving mappings
- **Claim:** Instruction-tuned models exhibit minimal few-shot gains because their supervised training already provides direct task-solving mappings, reducing reliance on context-based pattern induction.
- **Mechanism:** Instruction tuning uses curated task-question-answer triples to teach models to parse queries and generate correct responses without needing to infer patterns from in-context demonstrations. This creates a strong prior for zero-shot performance that is hard to improve upon with additional examples.
- **Core assumption:** The model's internal representations are already aligned to the task structure via instruction data, making contextual priming redundant or even disruptive.
- **Evidence anchors:** The abstract notes "instruction-tuned models exhibit strong zero-shot performance but benefit minimally, or even regress, with additional demonstrations"; Section 5, Finding 1 states that instruction tuning already equips models with effective strategies, leaving little room for benefit; neighboring work reports similar limited ICL gains.

### Mechanism 2: Models lack sensitivity to fine-grained semantic similarity for retrieval
- **Claim:** Retrieval-based example selection fails because current MLLMs lack the sensitivity to leverage fine-grained semantic similarity between demonstrations and queries within their forward pass.
- **Mechanism:** Models process each token in the context window largely independently without explicit mechanisms to compare and integrate similarity metrics across examples. Retrieved examples, while semantically close to the query, may not align with the model's internal feature space used for prediction.
- **Core assumption:** MLLMs do not have a dedicated "similarity matching" module; they rely on surface-level pattern matching, which is not reliably enhanced by retrieval.
- **Evidence anchors:** The abstract reports "retrieval-based example selection fails to consistently outperform random selection"; Section 5, Finding 2 says retrieval provides no clear advantage and models may fail to leverage fine-grained distributional cues; Section 3.2 notes the graph-cut selection ensures high CLIP similarity but this does not translate to gains.

### Mechanism 3: CoT distracts from multimodal reasoning
- **Claim:** Chain-of-thought (CoT) prompting degrades multimodal performance because step-by-step textual reasoning distracts from the core visual grounding required for the tasks.
- **Mechanism:** CoT prompts force models to generate intermediate textual rationales, which can introduce hallucination, verbosity, or misalignment with visual evidence. The model's computation is split between maintaining visual context and producing coherent text, weakening the final decision.
- **Core assumption:** The model's multimodal fusion layer is optimized for direct answer prediction, not for maintaining a consistent reasoning trace across modalities.
- **Evidence anchors:** The abstract states CoT uniformly reduces performance; Section 6 discusses CoT approaches vs. multimodal reasoning needs; Appendix D details failure modes: hallucinated reasoning, missing CoT, malformed outputs, infinite loops.

## Foundational Learning
- **In-Context Learning (ICL)**: The ability of a model to adapt to a new task by processing a few examples provided in the prompt. *Why needed*: FewMMBench evaluates whether demonstrations improve performance, so ICL is central. *Quick check*: If zero-shot accuracy is high due to instruction tuning, what is the theoretical maximum gain from one correct demonstration? Why might it be small? Because the model already has a strong prior; the demonstration provides minimal new information.
- **Chain-of-Thought (CoT) Prompting**: A technique to elicit step-by-step reasoning from a model before final answer. *Why needed*: The benchmark tests CoT as a reasoning enhancement in multimodal settings. *Quick check*: What are two specific ways CoT could conflict with visual evidence? (1) The model might hallucinate details not present in the image; (2) The textual reasoning might ignore contradictory visual cues, leading to incorrect premises.
- **Submodular Optimization (Graph-Cut) for Example Selection**: A method to select a diverse and relevant subset of demonstrations by maximizing a submodular function. *Why needed*: The benchmark uses this to curate example sets; understanding it prevents misattributing results to similarity alone. *Quick check*: If λ (relevance weight) is set too high, the trade-off is likely over-prioritizing relevance at the cost of diversity, leading to redundant examples.

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map**: Task Definition Module -> Example Selector -> CoT Generator -> Evaluation Engine -> Uncertainty Analyzer
- **Critical path**: Task curation → feature extraction (CLIP) → graph-cut selection → similar-shot retrieval → CoT annotation (if needed) → model inference (multi-setting) → accuracy/perplexity calculation → statistical analysis.
- **Design tradeoffs**:
  - Graph-cut λ: Higher λ (e.g., 200) for large pools increases diversity but may reduce query-relevance; lower λ (e.g., 50) prioritizes cohesion but risks redundancy.
  - CoT generator bias: Using a single model (Qwen2.5-VL) to create rationales may introduce its own style/errors; alternative generators could change results.
  - Evaluation metrics: Regex-based answer extraction is scalable but brittle; perplexity-based ranking mitigates format issues but is computationally heavier.
- **Failure signatures**:
  - Shot count decay: Accuracy drops when increasing from 4-shot to 8-shot, indicating context dilution.
  - CoT collapse: Performance decrease in CoT settings across models, often with hallucinated or irrelevant rationales.
  - Retrieval parity: No gap between random and similar-shot performance, suggesting models ignore semantic cues.
- **First 3 experiments**:
  1. **Baseline Reproduction**: Run zero-shot evaluation on all 26 models; confirm strong performance for instruction-tuned variants and weak for base models.
  2. **Shot Sensitivity Test**: For a top instruction-tuned model (e.g., Qwen2.5-VL-7B-I), compare 0-shot, 4-shot random, 4-shot similar, and 8-shot similar; verify minimal gains or regression with more shots.
  3. **CoT Stress Test**: Take a model that degrades with CoT (e.g., InternVL3-8B) and analyze its CoT outputs for hallucinations, missing steps, or incorrect final answers despite correct reasoning.

## Limitations
- Exact graph-cut hyperparameter λ per task is not specified, limiting precise replication of diversity-relevance trade-offs.
- CoT generation prompt, correction loop details, and temperature settings are undefined, affecting rationale quality.
- Evaluation relies on regex answer extraction, which is brittle especially with verbose CoT outputs; handling of malformed outputs is undocumented.
- Hardware and inference settings (quantization, precision) per model are incompletely specified, hindering performance comparability.
- Findings are restricted to open-weight models; trends may not hold for proprietary or larger-scale models.

## Confidence
- Mechanism 1 (Instruction tuning suppresses ICL gains): High
- Mechanism 2 (Retrieval fails due to model insensitivity): Medium
- Mechanism 3 (CoT degrades multimodal performance): High

## Next Checks
1. **Hyperparameter Sensitivity Test**: Re-run graph-cut selection with λ=50 vs. λ=200 on a few tasks; measure impact on similar-shot vs. random-shot accuracy gaps.
2. **CoT Generation Audit**: Manually inspect 50 CoT outputs per model family; quantify hallucination rates, step compliance, and visual grounding errors to correlate with performance drops.
3. **Retrieval Method Swap**: Replace CLIP-graph-cut with alternative retrieval (e.g., BM25 or FAISS on image embeddings); test if any retrieval strategy yields consistent gains over random.
