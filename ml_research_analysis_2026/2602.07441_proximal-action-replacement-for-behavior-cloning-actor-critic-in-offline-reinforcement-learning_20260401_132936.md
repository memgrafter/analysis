# Proximal Action Replacement for Behavior Cloning Actor-Critic in Offline Reinforcement Learning
## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2602.07441
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.07441
- Reference count: 28
- Primary result: PAR consistently improves BC-regularized offline RL on D4RL; e.g., TD3+BC average normalized score on MuJoCo rises from 75.3 to 83.2; combined with SSAR reaches SOTA average 90.9.

## Executive Summary
Offline reinforcement learning learns policies from fixed datasets without environment interaction. A common design combines actor‑critic training with behavior cloning (BC) regularization to avoid distribution shift. However, when the dataset contains suboptimal actions, BC forces the policy to imitate all actions, imposing a structural performance ceiling. This paper proposes Proximal Action Replacement (PAR), a plug‑and‑play module that progressively replaces low‑value dataset actions in the actor’s mini‑batch with high‑value actions generated by a stable actor, using proximal constraints to maintain training stability. Across D4RL benchmarks, PAR boosts multiple BC‑regularized algorithms. For TD3+BC, average normalized MuJoCo scores increase from 75.3 to 83.2; with SSAR, PAR achieves a new state‑of‑the‑art average of 90.9, with especially large gains on challenging Medium‑Replay and sparse‑reward navigation tasks. PAR effectively expands the action exploration space beyond the dataset distribution while preserving stability.

## Method Summary
PAR operates within a BC‑regularized actor‑critic framework. The critic is trained normally on the original offline data. Before each actor update, PAR checks critic stability via a moving average of the critic loss. If the current loss is sufficiently low (below a threshold β times the moving average) and a warm‑up period has passed, PAR activates: it generates synthetic (state, action) pairs using a slowly updated actor (πθ_sma) and stores them in a buffer (Dsyn). The actor’s mini‑batch is then partially replaced—the lowest‑Q‑value samples from the real dataset are discarded and substituted with high‑value samples from Dsyn. The replacement ratio grows linearly throughout training. This gradual injection of higher‑value actions lets the actor explore beyond the dataset’s action manifold without causing excessive out‑of‑distribution error for the critic, thereby breaking the BC performance ceiling. The approach is hyperparameter‑driven but agnostic to the specific BC or critic implementation, making it widely applicable.

## Key Results
- On D4RL MuJoCo, PAR increases TD3+BC average normalized score from 75.3 to 83.2 (+7.9 absolute).
- Combined with SSAR, PAR achieves SOTA average performance of 90.9 on MuJoCo, matching or exceeding prior SOTA methods like QIPO and FQL.
- PAR shows particularly large gains on challenging datasets: e.g., Hopper Medium‑Replay (+34.9), and on sparse‑reward navigation tasks like AntMaze.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1: BC regularization imposes a performance ceiling when dataset actions are suboptimal
The behavior cloning loss (MSE, KL, or MLE) pulls the policy toward every dataset action, including suboptimal ones. Even when the critic identifies higher‑value actions, gradient updates still incorporate pulls from low‑value actions, anchoring the policy to a suboptimal manifold. Theorem 3.1 proves that for any non‑zero BC weight, the resulting policy is suboptimal if the dataset contains suboptimal actions, creating a structural ceiling.

### Mechanism 2: Naive action replacement destabilizes critic learning due to distribution shift
Directly replacing dataset actions with generated ones without constraints increases policy divergence from the behavior policy. The critic’s target values then involve actions that are out‑of‑distribution relative to its training data, leading to high extrapolation error and training instability. Theorem 3.2 formalizes this: the critic loss lower bound includes a term proportional to the squared divergence between current policy and behavior policy. Empirically, non‑proximal replacement triggers spikes in critic loss.

### Mechanism 3: Proximal Action Replacement expands the effective action space while maintaining stability
PAR mitigates both issues by replacing low‑value actions with high‑value ones from a stable actor, while using proximal constraints to bound policy divergence. It only replaces when the critic loss is low (indicating reliable value estimates) and uses a slowly updated actor to generate synthetic actions, reducing variance. The replacement ratio ramps up gradually, allowing the actor to safely explore beyond the dataset’s action manifold, thus breaking the BC ceiling without sacrificing stability.

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component map**  
Synthetic buffer Dsyn: FIFO queue (capacity 2000) storing (state, action from πθ_sma) pairs.  
Stability gate: condition β·L_sma > L_critic, where L_sma is moving average of critic loss and L_critic is current batch loss.  
Replacement scheduler: linearly increasing replacement ratio p from p_min to p_max after warm‑up T_start.  
Value‑based filtering: sort batch by Q‑values (descending), keep top n_real real samples, replace remaining with n_syn samples from Dsyn.  
Slow‑moving targets: πθ_sma and ϕ_sma (exponential moving averages of actor and critic parameters) used for synthetic action generation and stability monitoring.

**Critical path**
1. Sample batch B from offline dataset D.  
2. Update critic using B (no modification).  
3. Compute current critic loss L_critic; update moving average L_sma.  
4. If β·L_sma > L_critic and training step t > T_start:  
   a. For each state in B, compute action using πθ_sma and enqueue (s, action) into Dsyn.  
   b. Update replacement ratio p = p_min + (t - T_start)/(T_total - T_start)·(p_max - p_min).  
   c. n_syn = ⌊p·|B|⌋, n_real = |B| - n_syn.  
   d. Sort B by Q‑values descending; extract top n_real into B_real.  
   e. Sample n_syn entries from Dsyn into B_syn.  
   f. Form hybrid batch B' = B_real ∪ B_syn.  
5. Update actor using B' (or B if PAR inactive).  
6. Update moving averages: θ_sma ← α·θ_sma + (1‑α)·θ, ϕ_sma similarly; L_sma similarly.

**Design tradeoffs**
- Dsyn capacity: Too small limits diversity; too large may store outdated actions. Paper uses 2000.  
- Replacement ratio schedule: Aggressive high p_max expands action space quickly but risks instability; conservative schedule slows progress.  
- Warm‑up T_start: Must wait until critic is reliable; too early causes instability, too late delays benefits.  
- Stability threshold β: Lower β permits more frequent replacement but may be less stable.  
- Using πθ_sma reduces variance in synthetic actions but may lag behind current policy, trading stability for relevance.

**Failure signatures**
- Critic loss spikes or diverges after PAR activation: indicates excessive distribution shift from synthetic actions.  
- Actor performance plateaus or degrades compared to baseline: replacement either too infrequent (p too low) or harmful (p too high).  
- Dsyn fills with low‑quality actions if the actor hasn’t improved, causing detrimental replacements.  
- Replacement ratio increases too quickly due to mismatched T_start/T_total, causing early instability.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
### Open Question 1
- **Question:** How can we design more sophisticated stability mechanisms or alternative optimization paradigms to enable offline RL policies to explore beyond the dataset distribution safely and effectively?
- **Basis in paper:** [explicit] The authors state in the Discussion and Conclusion: “future work could further explore how to stably and effectively extend the exploration space to broader and more optimal manifolds, potentially through more sophisticated stability mechanisms or alternative optimization paradigms that allow for safer exploration beyond the data distribution.”
- **Why unresolved:** PAR provides an initial solution but remains limited to self‑optimization near behavior data due to alternating optimization and Bellman stability constraints. Extending exploration further while maintaining stability is an open challenge.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Theoretical stability analysis for exploration beyond the dataset distribution and consistent empirical gains on benchmarks without increased variance or divergence.

### Open Question 2
- **Question:** Under what conditions does PAR converge to a policy that is closer to optimal than standard BC regularization, and how do the synthetic action quality and stability threshold affect this convergence?
- **Basis in paper:** [inferred] The paper proves BC regularization inherently yields suboptimal policies (Theorem 3.1) and that policy divergence harms critic stability (Theorem 3.2). However, PAR’s convergence properties are not formally analyzed.
- **Why unresolved:** Without theoretical guarantees, the limits and reliability of PAR remain unclear, making hyperparameter tuning and application to new domains heuristic.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** A convergence proof or finite‑time bounds for PAR under assumptions about synthetic action accuracy and replacement schedule, validated on tasks with known optimal policies.

### Open Question 3
- **Question:** What dataset characteristics (e.g., action suboptimality, diversity, reward density) most influence the magnitude of PAR’s performance improvement?
- **Basis in paper:** [inferred] Results show large gains on some datasets (e.g., Hopper‑Medium‑Replay: +34.9 for TD3+BC) but modest gains on others (e.g., Walker2d‑Medium‑Replay: +5.5). The paper does not analyze why.
- **Why unresolved:** Understanding these factors would guide when to use PAR and how to curate or augment datasets for maximum benefit.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Systematic correlation studies between dataset metrics (e.g., coverage, optimality gap) and PAR’s improvement across many tasks, including controlled synthetic datasets.

### Open Question 4
- **Question:** How does the design of the synthetic buffer (capacity, sampling strategy, replacement policy) impact PAR’s stability and performance?
- **Basis in paper:** [inferred] PAR uses a fixed FIFO buffer of size 2000, but the paper does not explore alternatives. The ablation varies replacement ratio but not buffer management.
- **Why unresolved:** Buffer design may affect the diversity and quality of synthetic actions, potentially offering further gains or improved stability.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Empirical comparisons of buffer variants (e.g., priority sampling, larger capacity, rolling windows) across tasks, measuring both final performance and training stability metrics like critic loss variance.

## Limitations
- Missing critical hyperparameters (moving average decay α, total training steps T_total, L_sma initialization) makes exact replication difficult.
- Theoretical assumptions (presence of suboptimal actions, critic reliability) may not hold uniformly across all datasets, potentially limiting applicability.
- Evaluation confined to D4RL MuJoCo and navigation tasks; generalizability to image observations or discrete action spaces remains unknown.

## Confidence
- **BC regularization ceiling**: High (Theorem 3.1 proves sub‑optimality; toy experiment visualizes escape)
- **Naive replacement destabilizes critic**: Medium (Theorem 3.2 gives bound; empirical validation on simple setting only)
- **PAR expands action space stably**: High (consistent improvements across tasks; Figure 3 shows stable critic loss)
- **SOTA performance (90.9 avg)**: Medium (depends on hyperparameters and base algorithms; partial reproduction possible but details missing)
