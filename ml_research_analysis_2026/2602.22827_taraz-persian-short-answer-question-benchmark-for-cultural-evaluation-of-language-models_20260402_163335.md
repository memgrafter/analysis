# TARAZ: Persian Short-Answer Question Benchmark for Cultural Evaluation of Language Models

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2602.22827
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.22827
- **Reference count:** 0
- **Primary result:** TARAZ introduces a Persian-specific short-answer evaluation framework that improves scoring consistency by +10% over exact-match baselines and achieves 95.1% human agreement via a hybrid semantic metric.

## Executive Summary
TARAZ addresses the lack of culturally-aware evaluation for Persian large language models. By converting three existing Persian cultural datasets into short-answer questions and applying Persian-specific postprocessing alongside a hybrid semantic similarity metric, the framework yields more reliable assessment of cultural understanding. Evaluations on 15 LLMs show a 10% improvement in scoring consistency over exact-match baselines and a 95.1% agreement with human judgments, outperforming LLM-as-judge approaches. The dataset and code are publicly released to support future research.

## Method Summary
The framework transforms BLEnD, PerCul, and ISN datasets into short-answer format. Model responses undergo a rule-based Persian normalizer (digit/character conversion, diacritic removal, suffix stemming, conjunction splitting). The cleaned sentences are embedded with `maux-gte-persian-v3`. The maximum cosine similarity between any response sentence and any ground-truth answer is thresholded at 0.85 to determine correctness. This hybrid metric (Maux+Post) reduces errors from Persian orthographic variability and captures paraphrases beyond lexical overlap.

## Key Results
- Hybrid evaluation (Maux+Post) improves scoring consistency by +10% compared to exact-match baselines.
- Human evaluation confirms the metric achieves 95.1% agreement with human majority, outperforming an LLM-judge (82.4%).
- The TARAZ framework and datasets are publicly released.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1
**Claim:** Short-answer questions (SAQs) provide a more valid probe of cultural competence than multiple-choice questions (MCQs).  
**Mechanism:** The authors manually rewrite existing Persian cultural resources into open-ended prompts, forcing models to generate culturally appropriate responses rather than select from predefined options. This tests generative understanding of social norms and contexts.  
**Core assumption:** Constructing a free-form response requires deeper cultural knowledge than recognition-based tasks.  
**Evidence anchors:** Abstract and Section 3 describe the conversion; neighboring benchmarks (FarsEval-PKBETS, TaarofBench) highlight MCQ limitations.  
**Break condition:** Nonsensical or repetitive responses cannot be distinguished from lack of cultural knowledge.

### Mechanism 2
**Claim:** Persian-specific response postprocessing is necessary due to the language's orthographic variability.  
**Mechanism:** A rule-based pipeline normalizes digits (Arabic/English → Persian), characters (Arabic variants → Persian), removes diacritics, strips suffixes, and splits on conjunctions. This reduces surface variation that would cause false negatives in lexical matching.  
**Core assumption:** Multiple valid surface strings exist for the same semantic answer; normalization aligns them for consistent scoring.  
**Evidence anchors:** Section 5.2 lists normalization steps; Section 6 shows Maux+Post outperforms Maux without postprocessing by up to ~10%.  
**Break condition:** Over-normalization (e.g., stemming alters meaning) or failure on novel forms may inflate/deflate scores.

### Mechanism 3
**Claim:** A hybrid semantic metric (cosine similarity with Persian embeddings) aligns better with human judgment than exact match or LLM-as-judge.  
**Mechanism:** Responses are postprocessed, then embedded using `maux-gte-persian-v3`. The maximum cosine similarity between any sentence in the response and any ground-truth answer is computed against a threshold (0.85) to yield a binary score.  
**Core assumption:** A dedicated Persian embedding model fine-tuned on formal Persian better captures semantic equivalence than multilingual embeddings or LLM judges prone to bias.  
**Evidence anchors:** Abstract reports +10% improvement over exact match; Section 6 shows Maux+Post generally yields higher scores; human evaluation (Table 5) shows Maux achieves 95.1% agreement vs. LLM-judge's 82.4% and higher Cohen’s κ.  
**Break condition:** The embedding model’s bias toward formal Persian may misjudge colloquial or culturally embedded responses; the fixed threshold may not generalize.

## Foundational Learning
### Persian Morphology & Orthography
- **Why needed:** The postprocessing pipeline targets Persian-specific issues (whitespace, diacritics, Arabic/Persian letter variants, numeral systems, suffixation). Understanding these is essential to interpret normalization steps.
- **Quick check:** Why might "خوردن" (to eat) and "می‌خورم" (I eat) require different normalization than English verb conjugations?

### Short-Answer vs. Multiple-Choice Evaluation
- **Why needed:** The paper’s premise is that SAQs are superior for cultural probing. Understanding the trade-offs (generation freedom vs. scoring difficulty) is crucial to grasp TARAZ’s scoring innovations.
- **Quick check:** How might a model "game" a multiple-choice cultural question versus a short-answer one?

### Semantic Text Similarity Metrics
- **Why needed:** The evaluation moves beyond Exact Match/ROUGE to cosine similarity with embeddings. One must understand why lexical overlap fails for Persian (e.g., "چای" vs. "cha-ye") and how embeddings capture meaning.
- **Quick check:** Why would Exact Match score two semantically identical Persian sentences differently if one uses informal spelling?

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map:** Data Ingestion -> Model Inference -> Postprocessing Module -> Embedding & Scoring -> Metrics Aggregation
- **Critical path:** Raw Response -> Postprocessing -> Sentence Splitting -> Embed all sentences -> Compute max cosine similarity with ground-truth answers -> Apply threshold (0.85) -> Binary score
- **Design tradeoffs:**  
  - Rule-based vs. ML normalization: rules are transparent but may miss edge cases; an ML normalizer needs annotated data.  
  - Single threshold (0.85) vs. per-dataset calibration: fixed threshold simplifies but may not account for dataset-specific answer distributions.  
  - Embedding model choice: `maux-gte-persian-v3` is Persian-specific but a black box; multilingual models reduce specificity.
- **Failure signatures:**  
  - High EM but low Maux+Post: model memorizes exact strings but fails on paraphrasing.  
  - Persian fine-tuned models scoring near-zero on ROUGE: indicates degenerate output; check generation parameters or data quality.  
  - Consistent LLM-judge vs. Maux+Post discrepancy: LLM-judge is stricter; if Maux+Post is higher, it may be over-lenient.
- **First 3 experiments:**  
  1. **Baseline replication:** Run GPT-5 on BLEnD with EM only; expect ~73% vs. paper's 85.8%.  
  2. **Postprocessing ablation:** Run same model with/without postprocessing; measure Maux delta.  
  3. **Embedding sensitivity:** Vary threshold (0.80-0.90) on a subset; plot agreement with human eval to validate 0.85.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
### 1. Multimodal cultural understanding for Persian
- **Question:** How can multimodal (vision-language) cultural understanding be evaluated for Persian, and what gaps exist in current text-only benchmarks like TARAZ?
- **Basis in paper:** The Limitations state the work focuses only on text-based evaluation and does not cover multimodal or vision-language understanding.
- **Why unresolved:** Multimodal cultural cues (e.g., imagery in Nowruz, architecture, dress) are central but not captured by text-only SAQs. Existing Persian benchmarks lack paired image-text data.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Development of a Persian multimodal cultural benchmark with image-based questions and protocols for cross-modal reasoning, validated by human studies on essential visual-cultural knowledge.

### 2. Factors behind Persian fine-tuned model underperformance
- **Question:** Why do Persian fine-tuned models consistently underperform compared to multilingual open-weight models (e.g., Gemma-2-27B) on Persian cultural SAQs, and what factors—training data quality, model scale, or fine-tuning objectives—are most responsible?
- **Basis in paper:** The conclusion and limitations note that Persian fine-tuned models lag far behind, suspecting translationese artifacts, small model scale, and instruction tuning optimizing format over cultural knowledge.
- **Why unresolved:** The paper identifies correlational factors but does not disentangle causality, hindering design of effective future Persian LLMs.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Controlled ablation studies comparing Persian fine-tuned models trained on native vs. translated data, different scales, and objectives mixing cultural tasks with instruction following, evaluated on TARAZ.

### 3. Generalization of the semantic evaluation framework to other morphologically rich languages
- **Question:** Does the proposed semantic evaluation metric (Maux with Persian-specific postprocessing) generalize to other morphologically rich languages (e.g., Arabic, Turkish), and what modifications are necessary for languages with different morphological and orthographic systems?
- **Basis in paper:** The authors acknowledge the framework is language-specific and tailored to Persian’s morphology; the general method could be applied but normalization steps would need redesign.
- **Why unresolved:** It is unclear whether the hybrid semantic approach or the specific postprocessing rules drive the gains; without adaptation studies for other languages, portability is unknown.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Apply the TARAZ methodology (with language-specific postprocessing) to short-answer benchmarks for other morphologically complex languages, comparing gains of "hybrid + native postprocessing" against "hybrid without postprocessing" and "exact match".

### 4. Biases in the maux-gte-persian-v3 embedding model
- **Question:** What biases does the maux-gte-persian-v3 embedding model introduced in the evaluation framework have regarding regional dialects, formality levels, or demographic groups within Persian-speaking cultures, and how do these biases affect cultural competence scores?
- **Basis in paper:** The Limitations note that Maux depends on maux-gte-persian-v3, which is fine-tuned on formal Persian text and may not favor colloquial text; bias analysis remains future work.
- **Why unresolved:** If the embedding is biased toward formal Persian, it may unduly penalize valid colloquial or regional responses, leading to underestimation of cultural competence. The datasets include context-dependent norms that might have informal realizations.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Conduct a bias probe of maux-gte-persian-v3 using contrastive sets of formal vs. colloquial, standard vs. dialectal Persian sentences that are semantically equivalent; correlate similarity scores with human judgments on such pairs within TARAZ.

### 5. Missing dimensions of Persian cultural understanding
- **Question:** Beyond the TARAZ datasets, what other dimensions of Persian cultural understanding (e.g., historical context, poetic conventions, religious nuance, generational shifts) are missing from current evaluation, and how can benchmark design better capture the dynamism and diversity of Persian culture?
- **Basis in paper:** The Limitations mention that annotator bias may fail to capture the full range of cultural interpretations across Persian-speaking regions and demographics.
- **Why unresolved:** Evaluating "cultural competence" requires defining scope. TARAZ may oversample certain contexts (e.g., Iranian urban norms) and underrepresent others (e.g., Afghan Dari, diaspora practices, youth culture). It also focuses on normative understanding, missing interpretative or creative knowledge (e.g., metaphors in Hafez poetry).
- **What evidence would resolve it:** A meta-analysis of TARAZ error cases to identify blind spots, combined with qualitative studies with diverse Persian speakers to enumerate missing cultural domains, followed by design and validation of new SAQ categories probing these dimensions.

## Limitations
- **Threshold selection:** The 0.85 cosine threshold is empirically set but not validated across datasets; may not generalize.
- **Normalization completeness:** The suffix list and stemming aggressiveness are incompletely specified; risk of over/under-normalization.
- **Embedding bias:** `maux-gte-persian-v3` is a black box fine-tuned on formal Persian; may penalize colloquial or culturally embedded responses.

## Confidence

- **Postprocessing gains:** High
