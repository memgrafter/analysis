# Few-Shot Continual Learning for 3D Brain MRI with Frozen Foundation Models

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID**: 2602.23533  
- **Source URL**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.23533  
- **Reference count**: 18  
- **Primary result**: Using frozen backbone and per-task LoRA adapters yields zero forgetting (BWT=0) in 32-shot continual learning, but segmentation performance (T1 Dice 0.60) remains below full fine‑tuning (0.80).

## Executive Summary
This paper addresses catastrophic forgetting in few-shot task‑incremental continual learning for 3D brain MRI. The core idea is to freeze a large pretrained 3D UNet backbone and, for each new task, inject a dedicated Low‑Rank Adaptation (LoRA) adapter and a task‑specific head. Only the adapter and head are trained on the current task; all previous task parameters and the backbone remain untouched. This guarantees zero backward transfer (BWT=0) by design and uses less than 0.1 % trainable parameters per task. On BraTS tumor segmentation (T1) and IXI brain age regression (T2) with 32‑shot samples, the approach maintains balanced performance (T1 Dice 0.62±0.07, T2 MAE 0.16±0.05) while avoiding severe forgetting suffered by sequential fine‑tuning. However, a performance gap remains compared to full fine‑tuning on segmentation.

## Method Summary
The method leverages a pretrained 3D UNet (FOMO `unet_b`) as a frozen feature extractor. For each task, a LoRA module is introduced into selected convolutional layers (encoder + decoder for segmentation; encoder‑only for regression), and a task‑specific head is attached (full decoder for segmentation, global pooling + linear for regression). During training on a task, only the LoRA adapter and head are updated; the backbone and all previously trained adapters stay frozen. After training, the adapter and head are saved for that task. At inference, the backbone and the appropriate adapter/head are loaded. LoRA’s low‑rank matrices (rank = 8) keep trainable parameters per task below 0.1 % of the backbone, ensuring parameter efficiency while adapting to new tasks.

## Key Results
- The LoRA approach achieves zero backward transfer (BWT=0) with balanced performance: T1 Dice 0.62 ± 0.07, T2 MAE 0.16 ± 0.05 (32‑shot).
- Sequential full fine‑tuning catastrophically forgets T1 (Dice drops from 0.80 to 0.16), while sequential linear probing fails at T2 (MAE 1.45).
- Ablations show decoder‑only LoRA adaptation is necessary for segmentation; encoder‑only LoRA yields Dice ~0.19. Performance improves with more few‑shot samples (e.g., 64 shots).

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1: Forgetting Prevention via Parameter Isolation
Freezing the backbone and all previously trained adapters ensures no gradients from new tasks reach prior task parameters. When training task \(k\), only the new adapter \(\phi_k\) and head \(h_k\) are updated; all \(\{\phi_j\}_{j<k}\) and \(f_\theta\) remain static. This isolation guarantees zero backward transfer (BWT=0) because prior weights are never modified. The assumption is that the frozen backbone retains sufficient general features from its large‑scale pretraining, and task‑specific adaptations can be captured entirely by the small LoRA modules. Evidence: [abstract] and [section 2.3] explicitly describe the freezing procedure. Break condition: if the backbone lacks features for a new task (e.g., severe domain shift), performance may suffer despite zero forgetting, as seen in the T1 Dice gap vs. full fine‑tuning.

### Mechanism 2: Parameter Efficiency via Low‑Rank Adaptation
LoRA approximates the task‑specific weight update \(\Delta W = B \cdot A\) with low‑rank matrices (\(r \ll \min(d,k)\)), injecting them as 1×1×1 convolutions into target layers. This creates a tiny, tunable subspace that adapts the backbone without overfitting scarce data. The underlying assumption is that the optimal update has low intrinsic rank and the pretrained weights have enough spectral capacity. Evidence: [abstract] and [section 2.2] define the formulation and note rank \(r\in\{4,8,16\}\) yields <0.1 % trainable parameters. Ablations confirm LoRA’s efficacy in 3D medical imaging. Break condition: a high‑rank required update would underfit; the encoder‑only LoRA failure for segmentation suggests rank/location constraints.

### Mechanism 3: Heterogeneous Tasks via Dedicated Heads & Adapters
Each task receives its own LoRA adapter and head, allowing diverse output spaces (segmentation vs. regression) to coexist. The frozen backbone acts as a shared generic feature extractor; the lightweight modules specialize these features. The head shapes the output: a full decoder for segmentation produces spatial masks, while a global pooling + linear head yields a scalar for regression. Evidence: [abstract] and sections 2.1/2.4 define different heads. The assumption is that backbone features are generic and spatially aligned for both tasks. Break condition: if tasks need drastically different input modalities or resolutions, the shared backbone becomes a bottleneck; the paper uses different channel sets (BraTS vs. IXI), showing some flexibility but not unlimited.

## Foundational Learning
- **Catastrophic Forgetting**: In sequential learning, updating shared weights for a new task degrades performance on earlier tasks. Why needed: It is the core problem this method solves. - **Parameter‑Efficient Fine‑Tuning (PEFT) / LoRA**: Adds small trainable components to a frozen model. ## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map**: Pretrained 3D UNet backbone (fθ) → Task‑specific LoRA adapter (ϕk) injected into encoder/decoder layers → Task‑specific head (hk) (full decoder for segmentation, regression head for age). Inputs: modality‑specific channels (BraTS: T2/FLAIR/T1c; IXI: T1/T2) → Z‑score norm → 64³ patches.
- **Critical path**:
  1. Load pretrained backbone weights, freeze all parameters.
  2. For new task \(k\): initialize new LoRA adapter \(\phi_k\) and head \(h_k\).
  3. Train only \(\phi_k\) and \(h_k\) on the few‑shot samples of task \(k\) (Adam, LR = 1e‑3, 100 epochs, batch = 2).
  4. Save checkpoint for task \(k\) containing \(\phi_k\), \(h_k\), and reference to frozen \(f_\theta\).
  5. Repeat for next task. At inference for task \(k\): load \(f_\theta\), \(\phi_k\), \(h_k\).
- **Design tradeoffs**:
  - Forgetting vs. Performance: Freezing backbone guarantees zero forgetting but may limit peak performance on later tasks (e.g., T1 Dice gap vs. full fine‑tuning).
  - Adapter scope: Encoder‑only LoRA fails for segmentation; decoder adaptation is required, slightly increasing parameters but necessary for good Dice.
  - Shot sensitivity: Performance scales with \(n_{shot}\); with 16 shots segmentation degrades (~0.45 Dice).
- **Failure signatures**:
  - Segmentation (T1): Under‑segmentation, missing tumor boundaries, fragmented masks, especially in low‑contrast regions.
  - Regression (T2): Systematic age underestimation (Wilcoxon p < 0.001). Implausibly low MAE (<0.1) suggests overfitting to the tiny few‑shot validation set.
- **First 3 experiments**:
  1. Replicate main LoRA (enc+dec) result with \(n_{shot}=32\): expect T1 Dice ~0.60 ± 0.08, T2 MAE ~0.012 ± 0.003, BWT ≈ 0.
  2. Ablation: Train T1 with encoder‑only LoRA (no decoder). Expect severe drop (Dice ~0.19), confirming decoder adaptation necessary.
  3. Shot count sweep: Train LoRA with \(n_{shot}\) ∈ {16, 32, 64}. Expect monotonic improvement; at 16 shots segmentation degrades markedly (Dice ~0.45).

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
1. **Can the framework handle severe class imbalance?** Infarct detection (ISLES 2022) was excluded due to lack of negative samples; future work could supplement with external controls.
2. **What modifications close the segmentation gap?** The T1 Dice gap (0.60 vs. 0.79) suggests LoRA capacity issues; systematic hyperparameter search over rank, dropout, and decoder blocks is needed.
3. **Why the age underestimation bias?** Systematic underestimation in T2 (Wilcoxon p < 0.001) may stem from few‑shot training, dataset demographics, or imputed ages; no correction yet.
4. **How does performance scale with more shots?** Only 16 and 32‑shot results are reported; evaluating at 64 (or more) shots could reveal whether the gap with full fine‑tuning narrows.

## Limitations
- Frozen backbone may limit peak performance on later tasks (e.g., T1 Dice gap vs. full fine‑tuning).
- LoRA’s low‑rank bottleneck might underfit tasks requiring high‑rank adaptations.
- Imputation of missing IXI ages to 50.0 years may bias regression and contribute to age underestimation.
- Exact source and preprocessing of the FOMO pretrained checkpoint remain unspecified, hindering faithful reproduction.
- Few‑shot sampling strategy (e.g., foreground ratio for BraTS) is not detailed, affecting performance variance.

## Confidence
- Zero forgetting via frozen backbone: **High** (design explicitly prevents gradient flow; BWT=0 reported).
- LoRA parameter efficiency: **High** (explicit <0.1 % per task).
- LoRA efficacy in 3D medical MRI: **Medium** (novel application; ablations validate decoder LoRA but gap persists).
- Handling heterogeneous tasks (segmentation + regression): **Medium** (dedicated heads/adapters separate tasks logically but limited external validation).
- Generalizability to other modalities/tasks: **Low** (only two brain MRI tasks evaluated).
