# Learning-guided Prioritized Planning for Lifelong Multi-Agent Path Finding in Warehouse Automation
## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2603.23838
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.23838
- **Reference count:** 40
- **Primary result:** RL-RH-PP improves throughput per agent by 25% over RH-PP with random priority sampling and achieves the highest total throughput against several strong baselines in high-density warehouse layouts.

## Executive Summary
This paper tackles lifelong multi-agent path finding (MAPF) for warehouse robots, where new tasks arrive continuously and congestion must be managed dynamically to maximize throughput. The proposed RL-RH-PP framework combines reinforcement learning (RL) with rolling-horizon prioritized planning (RH-PP). An attention-based transformer encodes agents' shortest paths to model spatial-temporal dependencies and decodes effective priority orders. These orders sequence agents in the RH-PP planner, which executes paths over short horizons and repairs conflicts. The learned policy generalizes zero-shot across agent counts, planning horizons, and layout variations, consistently outperforming baselines like RH-CBS, RH-PBS, PIBT, and WPPL, especially in constrained, high-density environments like the Symbotic warehouse.

## Method Summary
RL-RH-PP operates in rolling planning cycles. At each replanning step, it observes each agent's shortest path to its current goal. A transformer encoder with alternating temporal and spatial attention processes these paths to produce agent embeddings. An autoregressive decoder then samples *K* total priority orders (permutations of agents). For each order, the RH-PP backbone—a prioritized planning (PP) planner using SIPP for single-agent planning—computes conflict-free paths within the planning horizon, applying a repair mechanism to ensure safety during execution. The path set with the lowest cost (balancing travel distance and congestion) is selected. Robots execute the first *h* steps of their planned paths before the next replanning. The RL policy (trained with PPO) is updated from execution rollouts using a reward that penalizes average distance to goals, congestion, and infeasibility.

## Key Results
- RL-RH-PP achieves a **25% average improvement in throughput per agent (TPA)** over RH-PP with randomly sampled priority orders.
- It **attains the highest total throughput** compared to RH-CBS, RH-PBS, PIBT, and WPPL, with particularly large gains in the dense, constrained Symbotic warehouse layout.
- The learned policy **generalizes zero-shot** to different agent counts, planning horizons, and map layout variants without retraining.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1: RL-guided priority assignment
- **Claim:** Learning to assign priority orders via RL improves throughput in lifelong MAPF by adaptively addressing congestion.
- **Mechanism:** The RL policy encodes agents’ shortest paths with a transformer, captures spatial-temporal dependencies, and autoregressively decodes priority orders. These orders guide RH-PP’s sequential planning, reducing conflict and deadlock.
- **Core assumption:** The priority order in PP significantly impacts solution quality; a learned order can better handle dynamic congestion than random or heuristic orders.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract]: “An attention-based neural network encodes agents’ shortest paths to capture spatial-temporal dependencies and autoregressively decodes effective total priority orders.”
  - [section]: Section 4.3 formulates priority assignment as a POMDP and Section 4.4 details the transformer architecture; Section 5.2 shows consistent TPA improvement over RH-PP.
  - [corpus]: Corpus works (e.g., PRIMAL2, Followers) explore RL for lifelong MAPF but note inconsistent superiority over search-based methods; RL-RH-PP bridges this by hybridizing with PP.
- **Break condition:** If the training distribution (warehouse layout, agent density) differs significantly from test conditions, the learned policy may fail to generalize, especially if the map size changes beyond the fixed dictionary.

### Mechanism 2: Rolling-horizon prioritized planning
- **Claim:** Rolling-horizon planning with RH-PP enables continual adaptation to new tasks while maintaining computational efficiency.
- **Mechanism:** RH-PP operates in cycles: within each planning horizon *w*, it samples priority orders, plans paths using PP, and executes for *h* steps. A repair mechanism ensures conflict-free execution within the window by inserting waits.
- **Core assumption:** Short-horizon planning with frequent re-planning can effectively manage the lifelong setting by reacting to new tasks and evolving congestion.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract]: “rolling horizon mechanism enables continual re-planning as new tasks arrive.”
  - [section]: Section 4.2 defines RH-PP and its parameters *w* and *h*; Section 5.3 compares RH-PP baselines.
  - [corpus]: RHCR (Li et al., 2020b) uses rolling horizon with CBS/PBS; RH-PP extends to PP for better scalability.
- **Break condition:** If the planning horizon *w* is too short relative to task arrival rate or congestion propagation speed, the system may become myopic and accumulate deadlocks. The repair mechanism only handles local conflicts and may not resolve cyclic deadlocks.

### Mechanism 3: Attention-based spatial-temporal encoding
- **Claim:** Capturing both temporal (along paths) and spatial (among agents) dependencies via attention leads to better priority orders.
- **Mechanism:** The encoder uses alternating temporal and spatial attention across stacked layers. Temporal attention aggregates information across time steps for each agent, while spatial attention models interactions between agents at each time step.
- **Core assumption:** Effective priority assignment requires understanding both how an agent’s path evolves over time and how it interacts with others in space.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract]: “captures both temporal and spatial dependencies in multi-agent interactions.”
  - [section]: Section 4.4.1 details the encoder; ablation in Section 5.6.3 shows that removing either attention degrades performance, especially on the Symbotic map.
  - [corpus]: Corpus learning-based MAPF works use CNNs or GNNs; the alternating attention design is novel and not directly supported.
- **Break condition:** With very long paths (large *r*) or many agents (large *N*), attention complexity may become prohibitive, and the model might fail to capture long-range dependencies due to training limitations.

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map:**  
  Shortest paths → Encoder (dictionary embeddings + temporal/spatial attention) → Agent embeddings → Decoder (autoregressive) → *K* priority orders → RH-PP Planner (PP with SIPP + repair) → Cost evaluation → Best path set → Execution (*h* steps) → Reward → PPO update.
- **Critical path:**  
  At each replanning trigger (every *h* steps): (1) observe agents' current shortest paths, (2) encode to embeddings, (3) decode *K* priority orders, (4) run RH-PP for each order (plan within *w*, repair), (5) select lowest-cost path set, (6) execute first *h* steps. Training collects these execution trajectories to update the RL policy.
- **Design tradeoffs:**  
  - PP backbone vs. CBS/PBS: PP scales linearly (O(N)) but is incomplete; repair ensures safety but not global deadlock freedom.
  - Learning vs. heuristics: RL policy adapts to congestion but requires training; heuristic orders (e.g., distance query) are static but fast.
  - Top-*K* sampling: Larger *K* improves solution quality by exploring more orderings but linearly increases CPU evaluation time.
  - Encoder: Absolute position embeddings enable zero-shot on same-sized maps but preclude different sizes; alternating attention adds complexity vs. simpler aggregation.
- **Failure signatures:**  
  - Persistently low throughput: Check if *K* is too small or policy under-trained; visualize priority heatmaps for congestion patterns.
  - High infeasibility rate (many agents forced onto shortest paths): Inspect cost function β and reward infeasibility weight σ; policy may generate poor orders.
  - Runtime spikes: Likely due to large *K* or long *w*; parallelize order evaluation or reduce *K*.
  - Training instability: Monitor reward scaling and entropy coefficient; gradient clipping may help.
- **First 3 experiments:**  
  1. **Visualization run:** Small map (e.g., 10×10), N=5. Output priority orders and agent traces; confirm RL assigns high priorities to congested agents and observe backtracking behavior.
  2. **Ablation of RL:** Compare RL-RH-PP (K=5) vs. RH-PP (random, K=5) on Symbotic map, N=100. Measure TPA gain; runtime should be comparable.
  3. **Zero-shot test:** Evaluate policy trained at N=120, w=20 on N=80, w=30 and on Shorter Aisle layout. Check if TPA degrades gracefully (<15% drop).

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- **Question 1:** Can RL-RH-PP scale to thousands of robots in real-time, and what architectural or algorithmic optimizations are needed to maintain throughput while reducing the computational overhead of Top-K priority order evaluation?
  - **Basis:** Conclusion identifies scaling as future work; Section 5.5 shows K=200 takes ~9 seconds.
  - **Why unresolved:** Current evaluation is serial CPU; runtime grows linearly with K.
  - **Needed evidence:** Parallelized Top-K evaluation (multi-threaded/GPU) on large-scale scenarios; report solve times and throughput vs. real-time budget.
- **Question 2:** How can the learned policy achieve full map-agnostic generalization (zero-shot to entirely new layouts and sizes)?
  - **Basis:** Encoder uses absolute position embeddings tying it to training map size; conclusion proposes "full map-agnostic state representations."
  - **Why unresolved:** Absolute embeddings prevent transfer to different map dimensions.
  - **Needed evidence:** Train with relative/GNN-based encoder on diverse maps; evaluate zero-shot on unseen larger/smaller maps with comparable in-distribution performance.
- **Question 3:** Does jointly optimizing task assignment and priority ordering within the RL framework lead to further throughput gains?
  - **Basis:** Conclusion proposes extending decoder to emit (agent,task) pairs with feasibility masks.
  - **Why unresolved:** Current framework assumes external task assignment; interaction between assignment and ordering is unmodeled.
  - **Needed evidence:** Implement joint decoder with masking; compare total throughput against separate assignment + RL-RH-PP on same lifelong simulation.
- **Question 4:** How robust is RL-RH-PP to realistic uncertainties like stochastic movement delays or partial observability?
  - **Basis:** Simulation is deterministic and fully observable; conclusion mentions "robustness to model uncertainties" as extension.
  - **Why unresolved:** Policy may overfit to clean simulator dynamics; no noise experiments.
  - **Needed evidence:** Introduce stochasticity (random move failures) or observation noise; measure throughput degradation vs. baselines under increasing noise.
- **Question 5:** Can the learned priority ordering policy be interpreted to derive explainable heuristics for non-learning-based planners?
  - **Basis:** Section 6 provides qualitative analysis of policy behavior (e.g., backtracking), but remains a black box.
  - **Why unresolved:** No systematic extraction of simple deterministic rules from the neural policy.
  - **Needed evidence:** Feature importance/decision boundary analysis to derive low-dimensional rules; implement these in RH-PP and show performance closing most of the gap to RL.

## Limitations
- Missing precise map specifications (dimensions, exact obstacle distributions) hinders exact replication.
- RH-PP repair mechanism and SIPP integration details are incompletely specified.
- Task assignment and goal selection procedures are not described, assuming an external system.
- Baseline implementations (RH-CBS, RH-PBS, WPPL) are not provided, raising fairness concerns.
- Attention complexity O(N²r²) may not scale to very large N or long paths; no scalability analysis provided.
- Training distribution limited to agent counts {80,100,120} and w=20; generalization to extreme densities or layouts untested.

## Confidence
- **Performance superiority (25% TPA gain, highest throughput):** Medium. Evidence based on reported experiments; baseline reimplementations might differ.
- **Generalization (zero-shot across N, w, layouts):** Medium. Tests cover limited variations; out-of-distribution robustness unknown.
- **Mechanistic understanding (attention captures dependencies, proactive prioritization):** Medium. Ablations support attention importance but causal attribution not fully isolated.
