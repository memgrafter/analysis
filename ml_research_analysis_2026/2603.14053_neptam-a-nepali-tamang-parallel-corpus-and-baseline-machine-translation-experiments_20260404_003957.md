# NepTam: A Nepali-Tamang Parallel Corpus and Baseline Machine Translation Experiments

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2603.14053
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.14053
- **Reference count:** 22
- **Primary result:** Created NepTam20K (20K gold) and NepTam80K (80K synthetic) corpora; NLLB-200 fine-tuned on the combined data achieved sacreBLEU scores of 41.79 (Nepali→Tamang) and 48.28 (Tamang→Nepali).

## Executive Summary
To address the scarcity of parallel data for Nepali-Tamang translation, the authors constructed **NepTam20K**, a gold-standard, sentence-aligned corpus of 20,000 pairs across five domains, and **NepTam80K**, an 80,000-pair synthetic corpus generated via model translation. Baseline experiments fine-tuned several multilingual pre-trained models (mBART, M2M-100, NLLB-200) and a from-scratch Transformer on NepTam20K. NLLB-200 performed best, and leveraging the synthetic data further improved its results. The work demonstrates that transfer learning from large multilingual models is highly effective for low-resource language pairs, even when the target language is absent from pre-training, and that synthetic data augmentation can yield additional gains.

## Method Summary
The research pipeline involved collecting and cleaning monolingual Nepali text from the web, then creating a high-quality parallel corpus through human translation and rigorous filtering (semantic deduplication, rule-based classification by length/tense/polarity). This yielded the **NepTam20K** corpus (15K train, 5K test). Baseline machine translation models were fine-tuned on this gold data. The best-performing model (NLLB-200) was then used to translate a large set of additional Nepali sentences, creating the **NepTam80K** synthetic corpus. All models were subsequently re-fine-tuned on the combined dataset and evaluated using sacreBLEU, chrF++, METEOR, and COMET. A crucial practical detail was using the Hindi language tag (`>>hin<<`) to represent Tamang during training for all multilingual models, as Tamang was not in their original vocabularies.

## Key Results
- Multilingual pre-trained models (NLLB-200, M2M-100, mBART) significantly outperformed a vanilla Transformer trained from scratch, confirming the power of transfer learning.
- NLLB-200 achieved the highest baseline scores: **40.92** (Nepali→Tamang) and **45.26** (Tamang→Nepali) sacreBLEU on the test set.
- Incorporating synthetic data from **NepTam80K** further improved NLLB-200's performance to **41.79** and **48.28** sacreBLEU, respectively.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1
- **Claim:** Multilingual pre-trained models transfer cross-lingual knowledge to adapt effectively to the low-resource Nepali–Tamang pair with limited parallel data.
- **Mechanism:** The models leverage subword vocabularies and shared representations learned from hundreds of languages during pre-training. Fine-tuning on a small, high-quality parallel corpus adjusts these representations for the specific linguistic structures of Nepali and Tamang.
- **Evidence:** All multilingual models outperformed the Transformer (Table 6). NepTam20K provides the fine-tuning data. The use of a Hindi tag for Tamang is a key practical workaround.

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim:** Synthetic data augmentation (NepTam80K) improves translation quality by expanding training data size and diversity.
- **Mechanism:** The best model (NLLB-200) translates a large set of monolingual Nepali sentences to create synthetic parallel data. Re-fine-tuning on this combined set exposes the model to a wider variety of structures, reducing overfitting on the small gold set.
- **Evidence:** Synthetic data use improved NLLB-200's BLEU scores (Table 7). The Transformer showed no improvement, indicating benefit is model-dependent.

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim:** A curated, linguistically diverse corpus across multiple domains provides a robust foundation, mitigating domain bias.
- **Mechanism:** The corpus pipeline (scraping, semantic filtering, rule-based classification, expert translation) explicitly targets diversity in sentence length, tense, polarity, and domain (5 categories). This teaches broader linguistic patterns.
- **Evidence:** NepTam20K's design goals and distributional targets are documented (Section 3, Table 4).

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map:** Data Ingestion Pipeline (Common Crawl, HTML parsing, Indic NLP) -> Filtering & Classification Engine (FAISS, rule-based classifier) -> Translation Layer (Human expert & verification) -> Synthetic Layer (NLLB-200 translation) -> Model Zoo (mBART, M2M-100, NLLB-200, Transformer) -> Training/Evaluation (Hugging Face, fairseq, sacreBLEU).
- **Critical path:**
  1. Source text collection & cleaning
  2. Sentence segmentation & initial filtering
  3. Semantic deduplication & rule-based classification
  4. Human translation & verification (creates NepTam20K)
  5. Fine-tune models on NepTam20K train set
  6. Identify best model (NLLB-200)
  7. Generate synthetic translations (creates NepTam80K)
  8. Re-fine-tune best model on combined data
  9. Final evaluation on held-out test set
- **Design tradeoffs:**
  - **Script vs. Model Vocabulary:** Using Devanagari for Tamang reuses Nepali/Hindi tokenizers but may not capture Tamang-specific orthography.
  - **Hindi Tag for Tamang:** Necessary for pretrained models but risks conflating Tamang with Hindi semantics.
  - **Gold vs. Synthetic Data Scale:** 20K gold is high-quality but small; 80K synthetic adds scale but introduces potential noise.
  - **Metric Selection:** Automatic metrics (BLEU) are efficient but may miss semantic/fluency errors.
- **Failure signatures:**
  - **Abysmal BLEU (<10):** Check data loading, tokenization, language tags, training instability.
  - **High BLEU but obvious semantic errors:** Indicates metric limitations; requires human evaluation.
  - **Strong asymmetry (one direction good, reverse poor):** Check data imbalance or morphological complexity differences.
  - **Devanagari garbled output:** Tokenizer/vocabulary mismatch, especially for Tamang-specific characters.
- **First 3 experiments:**
  1. **Baseline sanity check:** Fine-tune NLLB-200 on 1K sentences to confirm pipeline.
  2. **Replicate main result:** Fine-tune all models on full 15K train, evaluate on 5K test to confirm NLLB-200 superiority.
  3. **Synthetic data impact test:** Generate 80K synthetic pairs, re-fine-tune best NLLB-200 on combined 95K, evaluate for gain.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- **Q1:** What is the impact of using the Hindi language tag as a proxy for Tamang on translation quality and linguistic representation?
- **Q2:** How does the quality of synthetically generated NepTam80K compare to gold-standard NepTam20K in linguistic accuracy and cultural nuance?
- **Q3:** To what extent do the models generalize to domains or linguistic phenomena outside the five covered categories?
- **Q4:** Do automatic evaluation metrics reliably correlate with human judgments for this language pair?

## Limitations
- The Hindi tag strategy for Tamang is a workaround with unverified linguistic and bias implications.
- The synthetic data generation process (source pool, parameters, filtering) is underspecified, introducing unknown noise risks.
- Evaluation relies solely on automatic metrics; no human evaluation of output quality or synthetic data was conducted.

## Confidence
- **Multilingual pre-training advantage:** High Confidence (clear empirical gap over from-scratch Transformer).
- **Synthetic data benefit for NLLB-200:** Medium-High Confidence (BLEU improved, but reliability conditional on synthetic quality; Transformer did not benefit).
- **Corpus diversity leading to robust performance:** Medium Confidence (pipeline is sound, but imperfect balancing and no out-of-domain tests limit claims).

## Next Checks
1. **Tag Strategy Efficacy Test:** Fine-tune NLLB-200 with three tagging schemes (`>>hin<<`, novel `<tam>` tag, no tag) to quantify the impact of the Hindi surrogate.
2. **Synthetic Data Quality Audit:** Manually evaluate a stratified sample of NepTam80K for grammatical correctness, semantic fidelity, and cultural accuracy; correlate error rates with performance gain.
3. **Tokenization Sensitivity Analysis:** Re-evaluate the best model using multiple sacreBLEU tokenizers (`flores200`, `none`) to confirm metric robustness and standardize reporting.
