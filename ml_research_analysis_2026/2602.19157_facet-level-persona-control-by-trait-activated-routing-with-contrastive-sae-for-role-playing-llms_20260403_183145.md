# Facet-Level Persona Control by Trait-Activated Routing with Contrastive SAE for Role-Playing LLMs

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2602.19157
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.19157
- **Reference count:** 35
- **Primary result:** The combined SAE+Prompt method achieved state-of-the-art persona fidelity, with full-accuracy up to 88.5% on contextual questions (Qwen3-4B), reducing MSE to 2.4 and MAE to 12.1 while maintaining multi-turn stability (MTR ≤0.1).

## Executive Summary
This paper introduces a method for precise, facet-level personality control in role-playing LLMs that avoids prompt dilution in long dialogues and eliminates costly retraining for new personas. The approach learns disentangled control vectors from a newly curated 15,000-sample Big Five facet dataset using a contrastive sparse autoencoder (SAE). These vectors are dynamically routed via a trait-activated agent and injected into a mid-residual layer. The combined SAE+Prompt configuration sets a new state-of-the-art in persona fidelity and stability across two model families, demonstrating that contrastive training in a sparse latent space yields pure, robust steering directions.

## Method Summary
The method operates in three stages: first, a sparse autoencoder (SAE) is pre-trained on general text (WikiText-103) to learn a sparse, interpretable latent representation of the target model’s mid-layer activations. Second, for each of the 30 Big Five facets, a control vector (CV) is computed as the difference between mean latent codes of positive and negative facet samples from the curated dataset, then refined with a contrastive loss that pulls the vector toward the positive centroid and pushes it away from the negative one while maintaining sparsity. Third, at inference, the base LLM acts as a routing agent to analyze the current dialogue turn and dynamically select the most relevant facet CV(s) for injection. The selected vector is decoded into the activation space and added to the model’s residual stream at a predetermined middle layer, amplifying the desired trait without interfering with the prompt’s semantic guidance.

## Key Results
- **State-of-the-art fidelity:** CV-SAE+Prompt achieves 88.5% full-accuracy on contextual Big Five questions (Qwen3-4B), outperforming all baselines.
- **Low error and high stability:** Reduces MSE to 2.4 and MAE to 12.1 while keeping multi-turn rate (MTR) ≤0.1, indicating stable persona maintenance across long dialogues.
- **Contrastive benefit:** SAE vectors are more stable and purer than CAA baselines; contrastive training improves vector separation and alignment with facet semantics.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- **Claim:** Contrastive Sparse AutoEncoder (SAE) training learns disentangled, facet-pure control vectors that are more stable and effective than non-contrastive baselines.
- **Mechanism:** An SAE is first trained on general language activations (WikiText-103) to form a sparse, interpretable latent basis. Control vectors (CVs) are then initialized as the difference between mean latent codes of positive/negative facet samples from the curated dataset. A contrastive loss (angular/cosine margins) is applied in this latent space to pull the injected representation toward the positive centroid and push it away from the negative one, while a distance loss enforces separation. This yields a direction that more purely represents the target facet.
- **Core assumption:** The curated dataset's minimal cross-trait leakage (validated by a 30-way classifier with 6.2% cross-dimension errors) provides samples where the intended facet is the dominant variance source, allowing the SAE's latent dimensions to align with single facets.
- **Evidence anchors:** 
  - Abstract: "contrastive Sparse AutoEncoder (SAE) framework that learns facet-level personality control vectors...SAE vectors proved more stable than CAA baselines."
  - Section 4.1: Details the CL loss (Eq. 5-6) and initialization from class-centroid difference (Eq. 2).
- **Break condition:** If dataset leakage exceeds reported levels (e.g., >15% cross-dimension misclassifications), learned vectors would capture mixed trait signals, degrading purity and steering effectiveness.

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim:** A trait-activated, agent-based decision module dynamically selects which facet-level CVs to inject per dialogue turn, avoiding interference from irrelevant traits and maintaining stability in long contexts.
- **Mechanism:** Before generating a response, the base LLM acts as a routing agent. It analyzes the user query and context to infer which of the six facets within a dimension (e.g., Assertiveness within Extraversion) are cued. Only the CV corresponding to the most relevant facet(s) is then injected (at a mid-residual layer). This activates only the trait-relevant direction, preventing cumulative noise from injecting all 30 facet vectors simultaneously.
- **Core assumption:** Trait activation theory holds for LLM prompts: a situational cue in the dialogue turn selectively primes the corresponding facet, and steering should follow this activation to be context-appropriate.
- **Evidence anchors:** 
  - Abstract: "dynamically selected by a trait-activated routing module..."
  - Section 4.2: Describes using the role-play model’s base LLM as a routing agent to select CVs based on the current prompt.
  - Section 5.3: CV-SAE+Prompt's low MTR (≤0.1) vs. CV-CAA's high MTR (25.6-29.1) suggests dynamic, sparse injection prevents instability.
- **Break condition:** If the routing agent's inference about relevant facets is consistently inaccurate (e.g., due to ambiguous queries), it would inject the wrong CVs, leading to persona drift or incorrect facet expression.

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim:** The synergistic combination of SAE-based CV injection (CV-SAE) with standard persona prompting (CV-SAE+Prompt) yields superior, stable persona fidelity because CVs provide a robust, noise-resistant amplification layer that complements the semantic guidance from prompts.
- **Mechanism:** The prompt provides high-level, descriptive persona knowledge ("You are a highly agreeable person"). The SAE-derived CV injects a low-dimensional, disentangled activation shift that directly biases the model's internal representations toward the target facet distribution. This mid-layer injection happens after prompt processing, reinforcing the desired trait direction without adding competing textual noise, thus combating prompt dilution in long dialogues.
- **Core assumption:** The CV's effect is additive and does not destructively interfere with the prompt's semantic content when applied at a middle layer; instead, it amplifies the latent trait signal.
- **Evidence anchors:** 
  - Abstract: "The combined SAE+Prompt configuration achieves the best overall performance..."
  - Section 5.3: Table 1 shows CV-SAE+Prompt achieves highest FA and lowest errors while maintaining low MTR; Prompt-Label alone fails on contextual questions.
  - Section 5.4: Fig. 3 shows CV-SAE maintains low MTR across α, while CV-CAA's MTR rises sharply.
- **Break condition:** If the CV's injected direction is misaligned with the prompt's implied persona, the combination could cause conflicting signals, degrading coherence or reducing fidelity.

## Foundational Learning
- **Concept: Sparse Autoencoder (SAE)**
  - **Why needed here:** SAEs discover a disentangled, interpretable latent space over LLM activations. The paper assumes that applying control (a vector offset) in this sparse space, where dimensions correspond more cleanly to human-understandable concepts like personality facets, leads to purer steering than operating in the dense residual space.
  - **Quick check question:** What is the key difference between training an SAE on general text (WikiText) versus on persona-specific activations, and why might the former be preferable for learning *transferable* control directions?
- **Concept: Contrastive Learning (CL) in Latent Space**
  - **Why needed here:** CL is applied to the SAE latent codes to explicitly shape the control vector's geometry: make the "positive" (facet-expressing) representations tight and separated from the "negative" ones. This directly combats the "noisy or brittle" CVs from prior work that used simple mean differences without an explicit separation objective.
  - **Quick check question:** How does adding an angular margin loss (ArcFace-style) to the contrastive objective change the optimization goal compared to a standard InfoNCE loss?
- **Concept: Trait Activation Theory**
  - **Why needed here:** This psychological theory posits that personality traits are expressed situationally. The paper operationalizes this via the routing agent: only facets cued by the current dialogue turn should be activated via CV injection. This is central to avoiding universal trait injection which causes interference.
  - **Quick check question:** If trait activation theory holds, what would you expect to happen to a CV's effectiveness if you injected it during a dialogue turn where the situational cue is *opposite* to the facet (e.g., injecting an Assertiveness CV during a "receiving gentle feedback" scenario)?

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map:**
  Dataset Module -> SAE Training Module -> CV Computation Module -> Routing Agent Module -> Injection Module -> Evaluation Harness

- **Critical path:** Curated Dataset → SAE Pre-training → CV Initialization & CL Training → (At Inference) Routing Agent Selection → CV Decoding & Injection → Generation → Evaluation.

- **Design tradeoffs:**
  - **Layer choice:** Mid-layer (e.g., 15th) injection balances between early (too semantic, may disrupt flow) and late (too close to logits, may cause incoherence). Empirical tuning required per model.
  - **α (injection strength):** Must be tuned per method/backbone. Too low: under-steering (low FA). Too high: semantic corruption (high MTR). SAE vectors allow higher α than CAA before degradation.
  - **Routing fidelity vs. speed:** Using the base LLM as an agent adds latency. A smaller, fine-tuned classifier could be faster but may lose nuance.

- **Failure signatures:**
  - **High MTR (>0.5):** Likely CV injection too strong (α too high) or CVs impure (no contrastive training). Also possible if routing is wrong and injects conflicting CVs.
  - **Low contextual FA vs. Abstract FA:** Prompt dilution not mitigated. CV injection might be absent or ineffective (wrong layer, zeroed CVs).
  - **Low Abstract FA:** CVs misaligned with facet. Check dataset leakage or CL training failure.
  - **CV-CAA+Prompt collapse (like Mistral-7B):** Suggests CAA vectors capture non-facet noise that conflicts with prompt semantics; SAE's sparsity/contrast prevents this.

- **First 3 experiments:**
  1. **Ablation: Contrastive Learning Loss.** Train CVs with and without the CL loss (only L_dist). Expect: Without CL, ⟨z, µ+⟩ decreases and FA drops sharply. Validates CL term's necessity.
  2. **Ablation: Routing vs. Universal Injection.** Compare CV-SAE (trait-activated routing) against a variant that injects all 30 facet CVs every turn. Expect: Universal injection shows higher MSE/MAE and MTR due to interference, confirming routing module's role.
  3. **Hyperparameter Sweep: Injection Strength α.** Sweep α ∈ {0.1, 0.5, 1.0, 2.0, 5.0} for both CV-SAE and CV-CAA on both models. Expect: CV-SAE's FA-MTR curve peaks at higher α and is flatter; CV-CAA's MTR rises steeply with α. Identifies stable operating regime.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- **Open Question 1:** How accurate and impartial is the base LLM-based routing agent in selecting relevant facets for injection, and does its self-reflection introduce model-specific biases?
  - **Basis:** Section 4.2 describes using the base model itself as a trait-activated routing agent, but provides no evaluation of its selection accuracy or potential biases in interpreting situational cues.
  - **Why unresolved:** The paper assumes the agent correctly activates only the cued traits, yet no metrics assess routing fidelity or compare against ground-truth facet relevance. This could lead to misrouting and ineffective steering.
  - **What evidence would resolve it:** Human evaluation of routing decisions on a held-out set of context-trait pairs, or comparison with a supervised facet classifier trained on the dataset. Ablating the agent (e.g., random or rule-based routing) would also quantify its impact.

- **Open Question 2:** What specific mechanism in the contrastive SAE latent space leads to improved vector purity and stability compared to CAA—is it due to the angular margin, sparsity constraints, or latent disentanglement?
  - **Basis:** Section 5.5 demonstrates that contrastive learning (CL) boosts full-accuracy, but does not isolate whether the margin parameters, the sparse autoencoder's structure, or the contrastive objective itself is primarily responsible for separating facet directions.
  - **Why unresolved:** The ablation study shows CL's overall benefit but lacks mechanistic analysis. Without probing the latent geometry (e.g., feature overlap, cluster compactness), it remains unclear why CL enhances stability.
  - **What evidence would resolve it:** Visualizing SAE latent clusters (e.g., t-SNE) with and without CL, measuring intra-facet cohesion and inter-facet separation metrics, or systematically ablating margin values and sparsity penalties to correlate with purity metrics.

- **Open Question 3:** Is there a theoretical relationship between SAE sparsity (i.e., number of active features) and the optimal injection strength α, given that SAE requires higher α than CAA for effective steering without instability?
  - **Basis:** Section 5.4 reports that CV-SAE performs best at mid-range α, while CV-CAA peaks at lower α. The paper hypothesizes SAE’s "purer" directions allow stronger injection but offers no quantitative link between sparsity and α.
  - **Why unresolved:** α tuning is purely empirical; SAE’s sparse latent codes might compress facet information into fewer dimensions, requiring larger perturbations to influence activations. However, this is not formalized or tested across sparsity levels.
  - **What evidence would resolve it:** Train SAEs with varying sparsity constraints and measure the optimal α for each. A theoretical analysis could derive the expected perturbation magnitude based on the decoder’s conditioning on active features.

- **Open Question 4:** Do the learned control vectors generalize to authentic human personality expressions, or are they overfitted to the GPT-5-generated dataset’s stylistic and lexical artifacts?
  - **Basis:** Section 3 details dataset construction using GPT-5 and LLM-based validation. No experiments test vectors on human-written role-play dialogues or cross-model datasets, leaving open whether vectors capture universal psychological traits or model-specific quirks.
  - **Why unanswered:** The paper uses a leakage-controlled, synthetic corpus for training and evaluation. If vectors encode GPT-5’s generation patterns (e.g., phrasing, scenarios), they may fail on real human role-play or texts from other models.
  - **What evidence would resolve it:** Evaluate the vectors on human-authored personality datasets (e.g., essays, dialogues) or role-play corpora from diverse sources. Measuring performance drop compared to the in-domain test set would indicate overfitting. Additionally, training on human data instead of GPT-5 data and comparing vector efficacy would clarify generalization.

## Limitations
- Dataset curation details are unspecified; cross-trait leakage may be underreported, risking mixed CV signals.
- SAE and contrastive training hyperparameters (latent size, margins, sparsity) are omitted, hindering replication.
- Routing agent prompt and parsing logic are vague; its accuracy directly affects stability.
- Evaluation suite (44 contextual questions) is not released, limiting independent verification.
- Results are shown on two models only; generalizability to other architectures is unknown.

## Confidence
- **Contrastive SAE yields pure facet vectors:** Medium – depends on unverified dataset purity and hyperparameters.
- **Trait-activated routing ensures stability:** Medium – routing accuracy not measured; could degrade with ambiguous cues.
- **SAE+Prompt synergy combats dilution:** Medium – α tuning critical; lack of ablation on prompt-only baseline in long dialogues.
