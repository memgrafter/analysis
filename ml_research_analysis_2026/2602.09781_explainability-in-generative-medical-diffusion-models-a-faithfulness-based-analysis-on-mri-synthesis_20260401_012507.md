# Explainability in Generative Medical Diffusion Models: A Faithfulness-Based Analysis on MRI Synthesis

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2602.09781
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.09781
- **Reference Count:** 22
- **Primary Result:** EPPNet achieves the highest faithfulness score (0.1534) on the DUKE Breast MRI dataset, outperforming PPNet (0.0965) and ProtoPool (0.1420), indicating superior alignment between prototype activations and generative dynamics for enhanced transparency.

## Executive Summary
This paper tackles the critical lack of explainability in diffusion models for medical image synthesis. It introduces a faithfulness-based framework that analyzes the denoising trajectory of a conditional diffusion model and integrates prototype-based explainability methods (PPNet, EPPNet, ProtoPool) to link generated MRI images with specific, representative patches from the training data. Evaluated on the DUKE Breast MRI dataset, the Enhanced Prototypical Part Network (EPPNet) yields the highest faithfulness score, suggesting its explanations best align with the model's internal generative process. The work aims to increase trust and transparency in AI-generated medical imagery.

## Method Summary
The proposed framework has three integrated components. First, it leverages the denoising trajectory of a conditional diffusion model, visually tracing how anatomical structures emerge from noise across timesteps. Second, it applies a prototype-based explanation layer (PPNet, EPPNet, or ProtoPool) to the feature maps of generated images. Each prototype corresponds to a real training patch; the model's activations indicate which prototypes most influence the generated output. Third, it defines a novel Faithfulness Score (Equation 14) that quantifies how well the spatial activation pattern of a "important" prototype (by Normalized Influence Score) correlates with the actual image content in the region it supposedly explains. A higher score indicates a more reliable, spatially consistent explanation.

## Key Results
-   On the DUKE Breast MRI dataset, EPPNet achieves a faithfulness score of **0.1534**, significantly higher than PPNet (0.0965) and ProtoPool (0.1420).
-   This ranking (EPPNet > ProtoPool > PPNet) suggests that EPPNet's design, including normalized influence scores and a diversity loss, produces explanations that are more faithful to the generative dynamics.
-   The framework maintains high image quality (PSNR, SSIM) alongside improved explainability, indicating the two objectives are not necessarily antagonistic in this single-class generation setup.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1: The Denoising Trajectory as an Interpretable Canvas
The claim is that the iterative reverse diffusion process provides an intrinsically visual and interpretable account of image formation. The mechanism involves starting from Gaussian noise and applying a trained U-Net at each timestep to predict and remove noise. The spatial pattern of these noise predictions reveals where anatomical structures are being actively reconstructed, allowing a direct audit of feature emergence. The core assumption is that coherent, anatomically plausible noise reduction patterns correlate with meaningful structure acquisition. Evidence is drawn from visualizations in the paper (Figure 3) showing progressive outline formation, though quantitative validation against anatomical landmarks is limited. The mechanism fails if noise predictions are spatially incoherent or fail to align with known boundaries across samples.

### Mechanism 2: Prototypes for Data Attribution
The claim is that prototype layers attribute generated image features to specific, representative training patches. The mechanism operates post-hoc: after image generation, the feature map is compared against a learned set of prototype vectors (each from a real MRI patch). Similarity scores (e.g., max-pooled for PPNet, weighted for ProtoPool) indicate which training-derived patterns activated. The core assumption is that the feature space (from a backbone network) is aligned such that prototype matching meaningfully reflects influence on the generated pixel space. Evidence is the paper's description of architecture and equations (2.2), but direct validation that high-activation prototypes are visually similar to the explained region is absent. The mechanism fails if prototype activations for generated images show no systematic correspondence to actual similar training patches.

### Mechanism 3: Faithfulness Score for Explanation Reliability
The claim is that the proposed Faithfulness Score (Equation 14) quantifies how reliable a prototype-based explanation is by measuring spatial correlation between the activated prototype and the image region. The mechanism multiplies a prototype's Normalized Influence Score (NIS) by a spatial correlation measure (`corr(p_j, x)`), then averages. The core assumption is that high spatial correlation between a prototype's stored patch and the region it activates for is a valid proxy for genuine influence, not spurious activation. Evidence is the reported score ranking (0.1534 for EPPNet) interpreted as superior alignment. The metric's validity is established only within the paper's framework; if random prototypes or shuffled NIS yield similar scores, the metric fails to discriminate faithful explanations.

## Architecture Onboarding

### Component map
Diffusion U-Net (Generator) -> Feature Extractor/Backbone -> Prototype Layer (PPNet/EPPNet/ProtoPool) -> Influence Aggregator -> Faithfulness Calculator
Conditioning Input (Segmentation map) guides Diffusion U-Net.

### Critical path
1. Train conditional diffusion model on real MRI + segmentation pairs (200 epochs, LR 2e-5).
2. Generate synthetic MRI by sampling noise and iteratively applying the trained reverse diffusion process conditioned on a segmentation.
3. For each generated image, pass its feature map through the *trained* prototype layer to obtain Normalized Influence Scores (NIS).
4. Compute the Faithfulness Score for the image using its NIS and the spatial correlation between each prototype's patch and the generated image.
5. Aggregate scores over a test set to compare PPNet, EPPNet, and ProtoPool.

### Design tradeoffs
-   **EPPNet vs. PPNet:** EPPNet adds output normalization (eq. 7) and a diversity loss (eq. 8) to encourage balanced, distinct prototype contributions. This likely boosts faithfulness (0.1534 vs. 0.0965) but may reduce the precision of spatial localization.
-   **ProtoPool vs. others:** ProtoPool's soft assignment (eq. 9) allows prototype sharing, improving generalization to similar textures but potentially blurring the "which exact training patch" attribution, possibly explaining its intermediate score (0.1420).
-   **Fidelity vs. Interpretability:** The paper shows high SSIM/LPIPS alongside faithfulness, suggesting these goals are not inherently conflicting here. However, adding prototype constraints could theoretically limit the diffusion model's capacity if not carefully integrated.

### Failure signatures
-   **Prototype Collapse:** Many prototypes become near-identical (high similarity, near-zero `L_div`), making explanations redundant.
-   **Low Faithfulness Score:** Consistently near-zero across methods, indicating prototype activations do not spatially correlate with relevant image regions, violating the feature space alignment assumption.
-   **Degraded Image Quality:** If prototype supervision is joint, a significant drop in PSNR/SSIM would indicate the explainability mechanism harms generative performance.
-   **Unstable NIS Distribution:** For ProtoPool, if assignment weights (`α_ij`) become extremely peaked (one prototype dominates), the flexibility of soft pooling is lost.

### First 3 experiments
1.  **Reproduce denoising trajectory:** Generate an image from fixed noise and segmentation. Save intermediate `x_t` and noise predictions at spaced timesteps (e.g., t=1000, 750, 500, 250, 0). Visualize to confirm coherent anatomy emerges.
2.  **Implement and compare prototype layers:** Using a fixed, trained diffusion model (or its encoder), train separate PPNet, EPPNet, and ProtoPool heads on the *same* set of real MRI feature patches. For a set of *generated* images, extract NIS distributions from each method.
3.  **Compute and compare faithfulness scores:** For each generated image and method, calculate `corr(p_j, x)` (e.g., via normalized cross-correlation on aligned patches or features) and compute the final Faithfulness Score (eq. 14). Report mean/std across a test set to validate the ranking (EPPNet > ProtoPool > PPNet).

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
-   **Generalization to Multi-Class/Multimodal Data:** The framework is tested only on single-class (breast) MRI. Its performance on diverse anatomies, pathologies, or modalities like CT remains unknown. Resolution requires systematic experiments on multi-class (e.g., brain tumors) and multimodal datasets, tracking changes in faithfulness, quality, and prototype stability.
-   **Ablation of EPPNet Components:** While EPPNet outperforms others, the specific contribution of its normalization and diversity loss versus other factors is unclear. Resolution requires an ablation study isolating these components within the diffusion explainability pipeline.
-   **Correlation with Human Expert Assessment:** The proposed Faithfulness Score is a statistical measure. Its correlation with radiologists' judgments of explanation quality (realism, diagnostic utility) is unestablished. Resolution requires a user study comparing prototype-based explanations, with rankings correlated to automated scores.
-   **Performance Across Subpopulations:** Analysis on the aggregate DUKE dataset hides potential variability. The framework's faithfulness and image quality might differ across breast density levels or lesion types. Resolution requires stratified experiments on DUKE subsets (e.g., high/low density, benign/malignant) to identify biases or instability.

## Limitations
-   **Faithfulness Metric Undefined:** The implementation of `corr(p_j, x)` in Equation 14 (e.g., patch size, feature layer) is unspecified, making scores potentially arbitrary and hard to reproduce.
-   **Feature Space Alignment Assumption Untested:** The backbone providing `f(x)` for the prototype layer is not detailed. If its latent representations do not preserve spatial correspondence, the attribution mechanism lacks a reliable foundation.
-   **Narrow Dataset Scope:** All results are on a single dataset (DUKE Breast MRI). Generalizability to other organs, pathologies, or imaging protocols is not demonstrated.
