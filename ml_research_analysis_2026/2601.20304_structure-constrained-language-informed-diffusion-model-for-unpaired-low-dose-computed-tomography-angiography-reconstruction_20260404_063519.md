# Structure-constrained Language-informed Diffusion Model for Unpaired Low-dose Computed Tomography Angiography Reconstruction

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2601.20304  
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.20304  
- **Reference count:** 40  
- **Primary result:** The proposed SLDM achieves superior performance on unpaired low-dose CT angiography reconstruction with PSNR 24.44 dB, SSIM 0.8050, and significantly higher radiologist scores.

## Executive Summary
Unpaired low-dose CT angiography (CTA) reconstruction aims to synthesize normal-dose contrast-enhanced CT (NDCT) from low-dose contrast-enhanced CT (LDCT) without requiring paired training data. Existing methods often suffer from structural distortions and false enhancements. The paper introduces the Structure-constrained Language-informed Diffusion Model (SLDM), which integrates three key components: a topologically constrained diffusion process that preserves LDCT structures via dedicated structural channels, a CTA-CLIP module that provides semantic text supervision for accurate anatomical enhancement, and a subtraction angiography enhancement module for adaptive contrast adjustment. Evaluated on a clinical dataset, SLDM outperforms GAN and diffusion baselines, achieving PSNR 24.44 dB and SSIM 0.8050, and receives notably higher scores from radiologists. Ablation studies confirm the individual contribution of each module, demonstrating the effectiveness of the combined approach in maintaining structural fidelity and contrast accuracy in unpaired settings.

## Method Summary
SLDM builds on an Image Restoration Stochastic Differential Equation (IR-SDE) backbone that models the LDCT-to-NDCT transformation as a continuous diffusion process. The model's state is extended to include a topological structure channel derived from Canny edges; during training the channel uses NDCT edges and during testing it uses LDCT edges. This channel has a analytically derived drift term that forces it to converge toward the test-time LDCT topology, thereby preserving structural boundaries throughout the reverse diffusion. Semantic guidance is supplied by CTA-CLIP, a CLIP model fine-tuned on text descriptions generated by LLaVA-Med from NDCT images. These text embeddings are injected into the U-Net score network via cross-attention, steering the generation toward anatomically plausible enhancements and suppressing false positives (e.g., bone). Finally, a Subtraction Angiography Enhancement Module (SAEM) adaptively adjusts contrast by computing a filtered subtraction of the diffusion output from an approximate NDCT background and adding a weighted component back, effectively tuning vessel HU toward diagnostic ranges. Training proceeds in two stages: first pre-train CTA-CLIP with a contrastive loss, then train the IR-SDE with both image reconstruction and structural constraint losses. Inference runs reverse SDE sampling with the combined constraints, followed by SAEM post-processing.

## Key Results
- **Quantitative superiority:** On a clinical CTA dataset, SLDM attains PSNR 24.44 dB and SSIM 0.8050, outperforming both GAN-based and diffusion-based baselines.
- **Radiologist endorsement:** Blind evaluation by radiologists yields significantly higher scores (1–5 scale) for sharpness, noise, contrast, and detail compared to competing methods.
- **Ablation confirmation:** Removing any of the three core modules (topological constraint, CTA-CLIP guidance, SAEM) leads to measurable performance drops, validating each component's necessity.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1: Topological Structure Preservation via Constrained State Space
- **Claim:** Injecting LDCT's Canny-derived topology as a dedicated channel in the IR-SDE state space forces the reverse diffusion process to preserve structural boundaries, mitigating false distortions from unpaired data misalignment.
- **Mechanism:** The model's state is extended to $\tilde{x}(t) = [x(t), s(t)]$, where $s(t)$ is a 3‑channel topological map (from NDCT during training, from LDCT during testing). The IR‑SDE's drift term for $s(t)$ is analytically derived to converge toward $s_{\text{lq}}$ (the test‑time LDCT topology) as diffusion steps $T\to\infty$ (Appendix A proof). This anchoring ensures vascular/bone edges in the generated image align with LDCT's topology.
- **Core assumption:** LDCT's structural edges, while noisy, are sufficiently accurate to serve as geometric anchors; NDCT and LDCT share underlying anatomy except for contrast agent distribution.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract]: "topologically constrained diffusion process that preserves LDCT structures via dedicated structural channels"
  - [section III.B]: Formal definition of extended state $\tilde{x}(t)$ and Eq. (8) for optimal structural reversal; Theorem proving $\lim \mathbb{E}[\|s_0 - s_{\text{lq}}\|^2]=0$.
  - [corpus]: Neighbor papers focus on low-dose CT denoising but lack explicit structural channel constraints.
- **Break condition:** If LDCT topology is severely corrupted (e.g., extreme noise, motion blur), $s_{\text{lq}}$ becomes a poor anchor, leading to structural drift despite the constraint.

### Mechanism 2: Semantic Spatial Guidance via CTA‑CLIP Cross‑Attention
- **Claim:** Fine‑tuning a CLIP model on LLM‑generated anatomical text descriptions (organ types, locations, HU values) provides semantically‑aware spatial guidance that prevents false positives (e.g., bone enhancement) and false negatives (missed vessels) by aligning image features with text queries during diffusion.
- **Mechanism:** LLaVA‑Med generates structured text prompts (e.g., "ascending aorta located left side, 100 HU"). CTA‑CLIP pre‑trains image‑text contrastive alignment between LDCT images and NDCT text descriptions. During diffusion, the text encoder's output is fed into a cross‑attention module within the U‑Net, modulating feature maps to enhance regions matching the text and suppress mismatched ones.
- **Core assumption:** Anatomical structures have recognizable semantic patterns (location, HU range) that can be captured by a VLM and used to steer a diffusion model; LLM descriptions are accurate enough for coarse guidance.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract]: "CTA‑CLIP module providing semantic text supervision for accurate anatomical enhancement"
  - [section III.C]: "semantic supervision strategy with spatial intelligence... integrates functions of visual perception and spatial reasoning"; cross‑attention equation (11).
  - [corpus]: Neighbor papers show VLM use in chest X‑ray analysis, but none apply LLM‑guided prompts to CTA enhancement.
- **Break condition:** If LLM descriptions are factually wrong (hallucinated locations/HU), or if the visual‑semantic embedding fails to capture subtle contrast differences, guidance becomes misleading.

### Mechanism 3: Adaptive Contrast Calibration via Subtraction Angiography
- **Claim:** The SAEM leverages the principle of digital subtraction angiography to locally adjust the HU range of enhanced vessels by fusing the diffusion output with a filtered (bilateral) subtraction of LDCT from the generated image, enabling dose‑like tuning without re‑acquisition.
- **Mechanism:** After obtaining the diffusion result $x_{\text{mask}}$, compute $x_{\text{sub}} = x_{\text{mask}} - x_{\text{fill}}$ (where $x_{\text{fill}} \approx$ NDCT). This subtraction isolates regions with added contrast agent. Denoise $x_{\text{sub}}$ with a bilateral filter to $x_{\text{B\_sub}}$, then output $x_{\text{out}} = x_{\text{mask}} + \lambda x_{\text{B\_sub}}$. $\lambda$ controls enhancement strength, effectively shifting vessel HU toward a diagnostically optimal window.
- **Core assumption:** The diffusion output's non‑vascular regions are structurally correct (due to Mechanisms 1–2) but may have suboptimal HU scaling; subtraction cleanly isolates the agent's distribution.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract]: "subtraction angiography enhancement module for adaptive contrast adjustment"
  - [section III.D]: Eqs. (12)–(14); "SAEM confers... ability to achieve controllable grayscale adjustment targeting the enhancement region."
  - [section IV.E.3]: Ablation showing $\lambda$ tunes histogram and visual contrast.
  - [corpus]: Neighbors lack this specific post‑hoc subtraction module for diffusion‑based CTA.
- **Break condition:** If diffusion output contains residual false positives (bone/enhanced tissue), subtraction will erroneously boost those regions; bilateral filtering may oversmooth small vessels.

## Foundational Learning

### IR‑SDE (Image Restoration Stochastic Differential Equation)
- **Why needed:** SLDM's backbone is a mean‑reverting SDE that models the degradation (low‑dose→normal‑dose) as a continuous diffusion process. Understanding Eq. (1)–(2) and the training objective (Eq. 5) is essential to grasp how structural and text scores are integrated.
- **Quick check:** How does the optimal reverse state $x^*_{t-1}$ (Eq. 4) depend on the conditioning mean $\mu$? What happens if $\mu$ is incorrect?

### CLIP‑style Contrastive Learning
- **Why needed:** CTA‑CLIP aligns LDCT images with NDCT text descriptions via a cosine‑similarity‑based loss (Eq. 10). This cross‑modal grounding enables semantic supervision. An engineer must understand how positive/negative pairs are constructed and how the text encoder's features are later used in cross‑attention.
- **Quick check:** In the contrastive loss $L(a,b)$, what does $b^*$ represent and why is it crucial for learning fine‑grained anatomical distinctions?

### Topological Constraints as Per‑Channel Regularization
- **Why needed:** The core innovation is augmenting the image with extra channels that are forced to follow a constrained SDE (Eq. 7). This is not a simple concatenation; the structure channels have their own drift term designed to converge to the test‑time LDCT topology.
- **Quick check:** Why is the NDCT topology used as $s_{\text{gt}}$ during training but LDCT topology $s_{\text{lq}}$ used at test time? What would happen if both used LDCT topology?

## Architecture Onboarding

### Component Map
Input preprocessing → Topologically‑constrained IR‑SDE backbone → CTA‑CLIP module → SAEM post‑processor

### Critical Path
Topology extraction → Diffusion sampling with text‑guided score → SAEM contrast adjustment

### Design Tradeoffs
- **Structural channel fidelity vs. noise:** Using Canny edges is lightweight but sensitive to LDCT noise. Alternative: train a segmentation network to produce cleaner topology, but that adds complexity and may fail out‑of‑distribution.
- **LLM prompt engineering vs. automation:** Current manual two‑prompt strategy (structure/location + HU) works but requires domain knowledge. A fully automated prompt generator could reduce effort but might lose specificity.
- **SAEM simplicity vs. robustness:** The linear fusion $x_{\text{out}} = x_{\text{mask}} + \lambda x_{\text{B\_sub}}$ is fast and interpretable, but assumes clean separation of "added contrast" signal, which may not hold if diffusion introduced false positives.

### Failure Signatures
- **Structural warping:** Vessel boundaries deviate from LDCT edges. Check: visualize topology channels during sampling; likely cause: LDCT Canny too broken or $\theta_t/\sigma_t$ schedule mis‑tuned.
- **False enhancement (bone/vessel mix):** Bones appear enhanced. Check: disable CTA‑CLIP; likely cause: text embeddings not attending correctly, or LLM prompt missing "bone" suppression.
- **Contrast under/over‑shoot:** Vessels too dim or saturated. Check: vary $\lambda$ in SAEM; likely cause: diffusion output HU far from target, or subtraction artifacts from imperfect non‑vessel regions.

### First 3 Experiments
1. **Ablate structure channels:** Train without topology input. Expect: PSNR drop, radiologist scores ↓, visible edge distortion in small vessels.
2. **Ablate text guidance:** Remove CTA‑CLIP cross‑attention. Expect: false positives in bone, false negatives in faint vessels; radiologists note "unexplained enhancements."
3. **SAEM parameter sweep:** Vary $\lambda \in [1.5, 4.0]$ on a held‑out slice. Measure: (a) vessel HU relative to NDCT, (b) SNR. Expected: $\lambda\approx3.0$ balances contrast and noise; too high amplifies subtraction artifacts.

## Limitations
- Exact implementation details (LLM prompts, IR‑SDE hyperparameters, Canny thresholds, cross‑attention injection) are not provided, hindering full reproduction.
- Core assumptions: LDCT topology may be too noisy to serve as a reliable geometric anchor; LLM‑generated descriptions may contain hallucinations or inaccuracies.
- SAEM's fixed $\lambda$ may not generalize across patients with varying baseline contrast; the selection criterion is unclear.

## Confidence
- Topological constraint mechanism: *Medium* (theoretical convergence but real‑world noise may degrade anchor quality)
- CTA‑CLIP semantic guidance: *Low* (novel LLM‑VLM integration, no verification of prompt accuracy)
- SAEM contrast calibration: *Medium* (simple arithmetic assumes clean separation, may fail with diffusion artifacts)

## Next Checks
