# CoPeP: Benchmarking Continual Pretraining for Protein Language Models

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2603.00253
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.00253
- Reference count: 40
- Primary result: CoPeP introduces a benchmark and shows that temporal replay reduces validation perplexity by up to 7% and that various continual learning methods outperform naive sequential pretraining across 31 protein understanding tasks.

## Executive Summary
Protein language models must adapt as biological databases like UniProt evolve. The CoPeP benchmark addresses this by treating ten years of UniRef100 releases as sequential tasks and evaluating continual learning methods on 31 protein understanding tasks. The core innovation is using temporal meta-information—such as sequence persistence across years—to guide learning. Results demonstrate that specialized continual learning strategies, including temporal replay and plasticity-preserving methods, enhance model performance and generalization in large-scale, real-world settings.

## Method Summary
CoPeP uses UniRef100 releases from 2015 to 2024, yielding 580M unique sequences after deduplication. The base model is AMPLIFY (120M parameters). Each year’s data is trained for 100k steps with an AdamW optimizer and Warmup-Stable-Decay (WSD) learning rate schedule; at each year transition, the model resets to the checkpoint from step 90k of the previous year and restarts WSD. Continual methods include Temporal Replay (sampling past data proportional to sequence multiplicity), Gradient Ascent (using a forget set), Hare and Tortoise (EMA slow weights), and Shrink and Perturb (parameter perturbation). Evaluation uses a held-out UniProt validation set, ProteinGym (217 fitness assays), and multi-task benchmarks PEER (14 tasks) and DGEB (16 tasks).

## Key Results
- Temporal Replay reduces validation perplexity by up to 7% compared to joint training on all data.
- Gradient Ascent, Hare and Tortoise, and Shrink and Perturb consistently outperform naive sequential pretraining on ProteinGym fitness prediction (Spearman correlation) and PEER/DGEB win rates.
- Method effectiveness varies by benchmark: replay excels at natural distribution modeling, Gradient Ascent/Hare & Tortoise at fitness prediction, and Shrink & Perturb/Random Labels at multi-task transfer.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The mechanism hinges on leveraging temporal meta-information—specifically, sequence persistence across years—to curate replay data and guide updates. Proteins that persist over many years are likely biologically stable and well-studied; replaying these helps the model retain core knowledge while adapting to new sequences. This reduces catastrophic forgetting and aligns the model with the persistent core of protein space, improving generalization to held-out distributions and downstream tasks.

## Foundational Learning
- **Continual Learning**: Incrementally updating models on new data without forgetting past knowledge. *Why needed*: Protein databases grow continuously; models must adapt without full retraining. *Quick check*: After training on year t, evaluate performance on year 1 data to quantify forgetting.
- **Catastrophic Forgetting**: Neural networks’ propensity to lose previously learned information when trained on new data. *Why needed*: A central challenge that continual methods aim to mitigate. *Quick check*: Compare sequential fine-tuning performance against a model trained jointly on all years.
- **Replay Buffers**: Storing subsets of past data to replay during training, reducing forgetting. *Why needed*: Provides a computationally feasible way to revisit historical data. *Quick check*: Verify replay sampling distribution matches intended strategy (e.g., persistence weights).
- **Protein Sequence Databases (UniRef100)**: Clustered protein sequence resources updated regularly. *Why needed*: Source of pretraining data reflecting biological knowledge growth. *Quick check*: Confirm deduplication and versioning across yearly releases.
- **Evaluation Benchmarks (ProteinGym, PEER, DGEB)**: Standard suites for measuring protein understanding. *Why needed*: Enable assessment across diverse tasks like fitness prediction and multi-task transfer. *Quick check*: Run zero-shot or fine-tuning evaluations following original protocols to ensure comparability.
- **Learning Rate Schedules (Warmup-Stable-Decay)**: Training schedule with initial warmup, stable period, and decay. *Why needed*: Stabilizes training across multiple years; resets at task boundaries help adapt to new distributions. *Quick check*: Plot learning rate over steps for each year to confirm correct reset and decay.

## Architecture Onboarding
Component map: UniRef100 Yearly Releases -> MMSeqs2 Deduplication -> AMPLIFY Tokenization -> AMPLIFY 120M Model -> Training Loop (AdamW, WSD schedule, continual method modifications) -> Checkpoint Saving -> Evaluation (Perplexity, Sequence Recovery, ProteinGym, PEER/DGEB)

Critical path: Data acquisition and preprocessing must complete before training; yearly training depends on previous checkpoints; evaluation requires saved checkpoints after each year. Any bottleneck in deduplication or tokenization delays the pipeline.

Design tradeoffs: Replay buffer size vs. memory and compute; learning rate reset frequency vs. stability and adaptation; method hyperparameters (e.g., EMA decay, shrink factor) require careful tuning for each year’s data distribution.

Failure signatures: Validation perplexity significantly higher than paper trends (e.g., >7.5 in early years) indicates training instability; large drops in ProteinGym Spearman correlation suggest poor generalization; inconsistent win rates across PEER/DGEB tasks may signal evaluation mismatches.

First three experiments:
1. Pilot run: Train on 2015 data only for 100k steps to verify data pipeline, tokenization, and basic training loop produce expected loss curves.
2. Replay validation: Implement Temporal Replay for years 1–2 and compare validation perplexity against non-replay sequential training; expect replay to show lower perplexity on year 2 validation.
3. Downstream smoke test: Take the year-10 checkpoint from a quick run and evaluate on ProteinGym; ensure Spearman correlation is within reasonable range (e.g., >0.2) to confirm model has learned useful representations.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- **Open Question 1**: Can combining different continual learning methods (e.g., replay with plasticity preservation or unlearning) yield synergistic benefits that overcome individual trade-offs across protein understanding tasks?  
  *Basis*: The authors state in the conclusion: "Future work could explore how to combine these orthogonal methods to develop more effective approaches."  
  *Why unresolved*: Different methods excel on different benchmarks; no hybrid approaches were tested.  
  *Resolving evidence*: Systematically construct hybrid models and evaluate across CoPeP suite; a hybrid that significantly outperforms single-method baselines on multiple metrics, supported by statistical tests.

- **Open Question 2**: Why does Temporal Replay, which leverages sequence persistence, underperform on zero-shot fitness prediction (ProteinGym) despite superior performance on natural distribution modeling (UniProt validation)?  
  *Basis*: Section 6 notes Temporal Replay lags on ProteinGym; authors speculate about bias or overfitting but do not test.  
  *Why unresolved*: The exact mechanism linking replay buffer curation to poor generalization on mutation tasks remains conjectural.  
  *Resolving evidence*: Controlled experiments varying replay strategies and measuring memorization, validation overlap, and ProteinGym correlation; analyze wild-type sequence distribution relative to replay data.

- **Open Question 3**: What characteristics of protein understanding tasks determine which continual learning method (e.g., replay, plasticity-preserving, unlearning) is most effective?  
  *Basis*: Results show distinct method优势 across benchmarks: replay best for natural distribution, Gradient Ascent/Hare & Tortoise for fitness, Shrink & Perturb/Random Labels for multi-task transfer.  
  *Why unresolved*: No analysis connects task attributes (distribution shift, task type, data size, noise) to method performance.  
  *Resolving evidence*: Extract features from each of the 31 tasks and correlate with relative method performance; develop a predictive model or find significant correlations.

- **Open Question 4**: Do the CoPeP findings generalize to larger protein language models (e.g., >1B parameters) and different architectures (e.g., autoregressive vs. bidirectional)?  
  *Basis*: The study uses AMPLIFY-120M; scalability to larger models (e.g., ESM-2-650M) is untested.  
  *Why unresolved*: Plasticity, forgetting, and unlearning behaviors may change with model capacity, altering method rankings.  
  *Resolving evidence*: Replicate key experiments with larger models (350M, 1B); if performance ordering remains consistent, results are robust; if not, scaling laws are needed.

## Limitations
- Implementation details for continual methods (e.g., forget set update frequency, EMA decay) are partially unspecified, relying on code release which may hide implicit choices.
- Evaluation protocols for PEER and DGEB (fine-tuning hyperparameters, number of seeds) are not standardized, making win-rate comparisons potentially noisy.
- Baseline comparisons (Joint, Single Year Incremental, Single Year Matched) may not be perfectly matched in total compute or data exposure, affecting the validity of relative performance claims.
