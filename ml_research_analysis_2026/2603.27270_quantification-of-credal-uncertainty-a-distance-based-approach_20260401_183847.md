# Quantification of Credal Uncertainty: A Distance-Based Approach
## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2603.27270
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.27270
- **Reference count:** 40
- **Primary result:** Introduces an IPM-based framework for quantifying total, aleatoric, and epistemic uncertainty from credal sets; with total variation, yields closed-form expressions matching entropy/Hartley baselines in accuracy–rejection while being computationally cheaper.

## Executive Summary
The paper presents a novel distance-based approach to quantify three types of uncertainty—total, aleatoric, and epistemic—from credal sets, which are convex sets of probability distributions. Using Integral Probability Metrics (IPMs), the authors define measures that capture the worst-case distance to certainty (total), the range of aleatoric uncertainty across member distributions (set-valued aleatoric), and the maximal disagreement among plausible distributions (epistemic). When specialized to total variation, these measures simplify to closed-form formulas: total = 1 − max_y p_Q(y), aleatoric = 1 − max_y p(y) per member (with interval summary), and epistemic = ¼ max_{p,q} Σ_y |p(y)−q(y)|. The approach recovers known binary uncertainty measures, offers clear semantics, and empirically matches entropy and Hartley baselines in accuracy–rejection experiments, while running significantly faster (linear time for total/aleatoric, quadratic for epistemic). The work establishes a principled foundation for multiclass credal uncertainty quantification.

## Method Summary
Given an ensemble of M probabilistic classifiers producing probability vectors p^(1),…,p^(M) for K classes, the credal set Q is defined as the convex hull of these vectors. The framework uses an Integral Probability Metric d_F to measure distances between probability distributions. For total variation (TV), the measures are computed as follows:
- Total uncertainty: TUF(Q) = 1 - max_y min_j p^(j)(y) (i.e., 1 minus the maximum lower probability).
- Aleatoric uncertainty: For each member j, AU_j = 1 - max_y p^(j)(y); the set-valued AU consists of all AU_j, typically summarized by its endpoints [min_j AU_j, max_j AU_j].
- Epistemic uncertainty: EUF(Q) = ¼ * max_{j,i} Σ_y |p^(j)(y) - p^(i)(y)| (half the maximum ℓ1 distance between any two ensemble members).
These formulas are derived from general IPM definitions and rely on the convexity of Q and the fact that TV distance equals ½ ℓ1 distance between probabilities.

## Key Results
- Closed-form uncertainty measures for credal sets under total variation, recovering binary measures as special cases.
- Empirical accuracy–rejection experiments on CIFAR-10/100 and 64 KEEL datasets show comparable or better AUC/MR than entropy and Hartley baselines.
- Substantial computational savings: TV total/aleatoric are O(MK), TV epistemic is O(M²K) vs. iterative solvers for entropy and exponential scaling of Hartley.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1: Total Uncertainty
- **Claim:** Total uncertainty is the worst-case distance between the credal set and the nearest certainty point (a Dirac measure).
- **Mechanism:** TUF(Q) = inf_{y} sup_{p∈Q} d_F(p, δ_y). The inner supremum measures how far every distribution in Q is from a specific Dirac δ_y; the infimum selects the closest Dirac.
- **Core assumption:** Dirac measures represent full certainty; the IPM d_F is a metric on probability measures (separating and uniformly continuous).
- **Evidence anchors:** Abstract: “total uncertainty—worst-case distance to the nearest Dirac vertex”; Section 4, Equation (1); builds on Müller (1997).
- **Break condition:** If Q is empty or d_F is degenerate, the measure is undefined or trivial.

### Mechanism 2: Aleatoric Uncertainty
- **Claim:** Aleatoric uncertainty for a credal set is set-valued; it is the collection of aleatoric uncertainties of its member distributions.
- **Mechanism:** For each p∈Q, AU(p)=inf_y d_F(p, δ_y). Then AUF(Q) = {AU(p) : p∈Q}. A summary operator Φ (e.g., endpoints) maps this set to an interval or scalar.
- **Core assumption:** Aleatoric uncertainty is intrinsic to each distribution; aggregating before summarization would discard the ambiguity encoded by the credal set.
- **Evidence anchors:** Abstract: “aleatoric uncertainty—set-valued (all member distances to nearest Dirac)”; Section 4, Equation (3).
- **Break condition:** If Q empty, AUF(Q)=∅; choice of Φ affects information retention.

### Mechanism 3: Epistemic Uncertainty
- **Claim:** Epistemic uncertainty equals half the maximal pairwise distance within the credal set, capturing the extent of disagreement among plausible distributions.
- **Mechanism:** EUF(Q) = ½ sup_{p,q∈Q} d_F(p,q). For TV, d_F(p,q)=½ Σ_y|p(y)-q(y)|, so EU = ¼ max_{p,q} Σ_y|p(y)-q(y)|.
- **Core assumption:** The diameter of Q directly measures epistemic uncertainty; the IPM appropriately reflects distributional differences.
- **Evidence anchors:** Abstract: “epistemic uncertainty—half the maximal pairwise distance within the set.”; Section 4, Equation (4); Proposition 4.6.
- **Break condition:** If Q is a singleton, EUF=0; if d_F is insensitive to certain differences, epistemic may understate ignorance.

## Foundational Learning
- **Concept: Credal Sets**
  - **Why needed here:** They generalize precise probabilities to sets, enabling simultaneous representation of aleatoric (within-set randomness) and epistemic (set-size) uncertainty.
  - **Quick check:** Given a credal set Q defined by extreme points {p^(j)}, how do you compute its lower probability p_Q(y) for a class y?  
    **Answer:** p_Q(y) = min_j p^(j)(y).

- **Concept: Integral Probability Metrics (IPMs)**
  - **Why needed here:** They provide a flexible family of distance measures d_F(p,q) used to quantify how far distributions are from certainty and from each other.
  - **Quick check:** What condition on the function class F guarantees that d_F is a metric (i.e., non-negative, symmetric, triangle inequality, and d_F(p,q)=0 iff p=q)?  
    **Answer:** F must be a class of bounded continuous functions that is saturated (contains all functions of the form f_y = sign(p(y)-q(y)) for each y) to ensure identity of indiscernibles.

- **Concept: Extreme Point (Finite) Representation**
  - **Why needed here:** In practice, credal sets are often formed as convex hulls of ensemble predictions; Assumption A7 ensures uncertainty measures depend only on extreme points, enabling efficient computation.
  - **Quick check:** Why is it sufficient to consider only the ensemble members when computing TUF and the AU endpoints under the TV instantiation?  
    **Answer:** Because TUF depends on lower probabilities, which are minima over Q, attained at extreme points; similarly, AU endpoints (min and max of 1 - max p(y) over p∈Q) are attained at extreme points due to linearity of p(y) on the simplex and the max function being convex; thus extremizing over the convex hull reduces to extremizing over its extreme points.

## Architecture Onboarding
Input (M × K matrix) → IPM chooser (TV) → [Total module, Aleatoric module, Epistemic module] → Output (TU scalar, AU interval/scalar, EU scalar)

### Critical Path
1. Obtain M probability vectors (samples from models) for a given input.
2. Total uncertainty: compute per-class minima across M vectors; take max of those minima; TU = 1 minus that max.
3. Aleatoric uncertainty: for each vector, compute 1 - max(probability); take elementwise min and max across M to get lower and upper endpoints of AU interval.
4. Epistemic uncertainty: compute pairwise ℓ1 distances between all M vectors (sum of absolute differences across K classes); find the maximum distance D_max; EU = D_max / 4.
5. Optionally summarize AU interval to a scalar (e.g., midpoint) if needed.

### Design Tradeoffs
- **IPM selection:** TV offers O(MK) for TU/AU and O(M²K) for EU, but ignores label semantics (e.g., ordinality). Wasserstein with ground metric would add O(K) per distance but capture label similarity at higher cost.
- **Aleatoric summary:** Endpoint interval is lossless under continuity and extreme-point attainment; scalar summaries (e.g., mean) may lose information about the spread.
- **Scalability:** Epistemic pairwise O(M²) becomes bottleneck for large ensembles; can use approximate max via random subsets or exploit extreme-point reduction (only extreme points matter).
- **Memory:** Storing full ensemble may be heavy; can stream elementwise for TU and AU, but EU requires all pairwise comparisons (unless using approximate methods).

### Failure Signatures
- **Dirac contamination:** Including a deterministic model (max probability = 1) can cause TU and EU to become anchored to that class and produce inverted accuracy–rejection curves. **Detection:** Check if any ensemble member has max probability exactly 1.
- **Degenerate IPM:** If the function class F is not rich enough (e.g., only constant functions), all distances become zero, yielding zero uncertainties. **Detection:** Verify d_F(p,q) > 0 for distinct p,q.
- **Non-convex credal set:** If Q is not convex, the extreme-point reduction may fail. **Detection:** Ensure Q is convex hull of given points; if not, compute convex hull first.
- **Numerical underflow:** Probabilities near uniform or near degenerate can cause AU ≈ 1 - 1/K or near 0, but are stable. **Check:** Ensure probability vectors sum to 1 after any preprocessing to avoid NaNs.

### First 3 Experiments
1. **CIFAR-10/100 accuracy–rejection curves:** Compare TV measures against entropy and Hartley baselines; show matching or better AUC/MR with lower runtime.
2. **KEEL datasets benchmark:** Evaluate on 64 tabular datasets using random forests; TV measures achieve comparable AUC/MR but with dramatically lower runtime, especially aleatoric which is linear in M.
3. **Computational complexity analysis:** Theoretically and empirically confirm O(MK) for total/aleatoric vs. iterative solvers for entropy; confirm O(M²K) for epistemic vs. exponential (2^K) for Hartley.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- **Alternative IPMs:** What are the semantic and computational trade-offs when using Wasserstein or kernel-based IPMs instead of total variation?
- **Structured prediction:** How can the framework be extended to tasks with combinatorial label spaces (e.g., sequence labeling, segmentation) without intractable enumerations?
- **Dirac robustness:** Under what conditions does including a Dirac measure in the credal set lead to pathological behavior, and how can credal set construction be made robust to such extreme points?
- **Continuous credal sets:** For credal sets arising from Bayesian inference (e.g., Bayesian neural networks) that are not finitely generated, how can the measures be computed or approximated efficiently?

## Limitations
- Aleatoric uncertainty is set-valued; choosing a scalar summary (e.g., interval endpoint or mean) may discard information about the spread.
- Deterministic ensemble members (Dirac measures) cause pathological spikes in total and epistemic uncertainty, potentially inverting accuracy–rejection curves.
- Epistemic uncertainty computation scales quadratically with ensemble size, limiting practicality for large M.
