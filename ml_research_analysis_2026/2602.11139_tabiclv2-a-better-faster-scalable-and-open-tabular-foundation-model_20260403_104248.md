# TabICLv2: A better, faster, scalable, and open tabular foundation model
## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2602.11139
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.11139
- **Reference count:** 40
- **Primary result:** Untuned TabICLv2 surpasses tuned, ensembled, and fine-tuned RealTabPFN-2.5 on TabArena and TALENT benchmarks while scaling to million-sample datasets and being 10.6× faster.

## Executive Summary
TabICLv2 is a tabular foundation model advancing the Prior-data Fitted Network (PFN) paradigm for in-context learning on structured data. It introduces three core innovations: a highly diverse synthetic data generation engine, a Query-Aware Scalable Softmax (QASSMax) to address attention fading in long contexts, and the adoption of the Muon optimizer for faster, more stable pretraining. The model achieves state-of-the-art performance on major tabular benchmarks (TabArena, TALENT) without any task-specific tuning. It demonstrates superior scalability, handling datasets with over a million samples within 50GB GPU memory, and offers significant speed improvements. The full training and inference codebase is open-sourced.

## Method Summary
TabICLv2 is pretrained entirely on synthetic data generated by a novel prior designed for maximal diversity. This prior samples random graph structures, applies eight distinct function types (e.g., neural networks, tree ensembles, Gaussian processes), uses correlated scalar sampling to mimic real-world feature distributions, and includes extensive post-processing and filtering. The architecture processes tabular data through three transformer stages: 1) a column-wise Set Transformer (`TFcol`) with induced self-attention using QASSMax, 2) a row-wise interaction transformer (`TFrow`) with `[CLS]` tokens, and 3) a dataset-wise in-context learning transformer (`TFicl`) where test positions causally attend to the training context, also using QASSMax. Inputs undergo repeated feature grouping (circular shifts) to preserve all features and include target-aware embeddings. For regression, the model predicts 999 quantiles; for many-class classification, it uses a mixed-radix ensembling strategy. Pretraining follows a three-stage protocol with increasing dataset sizes, utilizing the Muon optimizer and memory-efficient techniques like FlashAttention-3 and selective QKV projections.

## Key Results
- Surpasses RealTabPFN-2.5 (tuned, ensembled, fine-tuned) in improvability (4.66 vs. 5.11) and Elo on TALENT.
- Scales to datasets with over one million samples under 50GB GPU memory.
- Inference is 10.6× faster than TabPFN-2.5.
- Ablations confirm the synthetic data prior and QASSMax provide the largest performance gains.
- Full model and inference code are openly released.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1: Diverse Synthetic Data Prior
- **Claim:** A highly diverse synthetic data generation engine is critical for state-of-the-art generalization to real-world tabular data.
- **Mechanism:** The prior samples random Cauchy-connected graphs, applies eight random function types, uses correlated beta-distributed scalars, and includes multiple filtering steps. This broad inductive bias prevents overfitting to narrow patterns.
- **Core assumption:** The prior's distribution sufficiently covers the manifold of real tabular datasets.
- **Evidence anchors:** [abstract] mentions "novel synthetic data generation engine designed for high pretraining diversity"; [section 5] details prior components; [section 7] Figure 10 shows validation loss degrades with lower-diversity prior.
- **Break condition:** Excessive diversity including non-transferable patterns could harm performance; current evidence shows a monotonic improvement up to the proposed design.

### Mechanism 2: Query-Aware Scalable Softmax (QASSMax)
- **Claim:** QASSMax mitigates attention fading, enabling generalization to longer contexts without expensive long-sequence pretraining.
- **Mechanism:** QASSMax rescales each query's attention logits by `MLPbase(log n) * (1 + tanh(MLPgate(qh)))`. The `log n` factor counteracts the linear denominator growth of softmax, while the query-aware gating allows per-head and content-specific modulation.
- **Core assumption:** The `log n` scaling is necessary and sufficient, and query-aware modulation improves calibration beyond static per-head scalars.
- **Evidence anchors:** [abstract] cites "new scalable softmax... improving generalization to larger datasets"; [section 3] defines QASSMax; [section 3] Figure 3 shows 100% accuracy on a needle-in-haystack task with 15k negatives where standard softmax fails; [section 7] ablation shows ~100 Elo gain from QASSMax.
- **Break condition:** Incorrectly learned `MLPgate` biases or extreme extrapolation beyond `log n` assumptions could degrade performance.

### Mechanism 3: Muon Optimizer
- **Claim:** Replacing AdamW with the Muon optimizer accelerates pretraining convergence and improves final performance for large transformers.
- **Mechanism:** Muon scales the learning rate per parameter by `0.2 * sqrt(max{n, m})` (matrix dimensions) and uses cautious weight decay (applied only when update and parameter share sign). This adapts optimization to the geometry of large weight matrices.
- **Core assumption:** Per-parameter scaling based on matrix shape is more effective than isotropic updates for large networks, especially in early high-learning-rate phases.
- **Evidence anchors:** [abstract] notes "optimized pretraining protocols, notably replacing AdamW with the Muon optimizer"; [section 4.1] details Muon settings; [section 7] Figure 10 ablation shows a performance drop when switching from Muon to AdamW, particularly early in pretraining.
- **Break condition:** Gains may be specific to large transformers; inappropriate weight decay values (default 0.01) or scaling could hamper convergence for other architectures.

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map:**
  Input -> Repeated Feature Grouping -> E1
  Training Rows: E2 = E1 + EmbedTAE(y_train)
  E1, E2 -> TFcol (Set Transformer with QASSMax) -> Feature embeddings per row/column -> Pool/Reshape -> Row embeddings
  Row embeddings -> TFrow (3-layer Transformer with CLS tokens) -> Fixed-size row vectors
  Training row vectors + EmbedICL(y_train) + Test row vectors -> TFicl (12-layer Transformer with QASSMax, causal mask)
  Test position outputs -> MLP head -> Predictions (logits for classification, 999 quantiles for regression)
  For C>10: Mixed-radix decomposition -> Multiple TFcol/TFrow/TFicl runs -> Averaged outputs

- **Critical path (forward pass):**
  1. Apply circular shifts to columns, pass triples through a linear layer to produce initial embeddings E1.
  2. For training rows, add target-aware embeddings (TAE) to create E2.
  3. Process E1 and E2 through `TFcol`, which uses induced set attention with QASSMax to produce feature-wise representations, then pool/reshape into row embeddings.
  4. Feed row embeddings into `TFrow`, which uses CLS tokens to aggregate row-wise interactions into fixed-length vectors.
  5. Concatenate training row vectors (with ICL-style target embeddings) and test row vectors into a single sequence; feed into `TFicl` (causal mask). QASSMax is applied here.
  6. Extract outputs corresponding to test positions, pass through the final MLP head to generate predictions.

- **Design tradeoffs:**
  - **QASSMax vs. speed:** Two small MLPs per attention head add parameters and minor compute overhead (<5%) but enable million-sample contexts.
  - **Repeated grouping vs. TabPFNv2:** Circular shifts preserve all features (no dimension loss) but require multiple linear layer applications.
  - **Mixed-radix vs. ECOC:** Native many-class handling avoids 3× slowdown of ECOC wrappers but may sacrifice slight accuracy; ensemble averaging keeps complexity manageable.
  - **999 quantiles vs. bin-based:** Provides smooth predictive distributions but increases head size (1024 hidden units) compared to cross-entropy on fixed bins.

- **Failure signatures:**
  - **Insufficient prior diversity:** Validation loss plateaus/decreases slowly; TALENT accuracy significantly below reported figures.
  - **QASSMax mis-calibration:** Attention entropy grows with context length; synthetic needle-in-haystack accuracy collapses beyond ~1k negatives.
  - **Muon misconfiguration:** Training instability (loss spikes) with too-high LR; slow convergence with too-low LR relative to AdamW.
  - **Quantile crossing:** Non-monotonic predicted quantiles; requires robust monotonicity correction (sorting/isotonic regression).
  - **Memory blow-up:** OOM on large datasets; verify selective QKV projections, layer norm reuse, and dynamic offloading are active.

- **First 3 experiments (validation):**
  1. **Prior diversity ablation:** Train two models for 100k steps—one with the full TabICLv2 prior, one with the lower-diversity TabICL prior. Expect the full prior to achieve lower validation loss and higher accuracy on a held binary classification dataset.
  2. **QASSMax needle test:** Evaluate the model with QASSMax on the 2D synthetic needle-in-haystack task with 10k, 15k, and 20k negatives. Record accuracy and average attention entropy. Expect 100% accuracy and low entropy up to 15k negatives; a QASSMax-disabled variant should fail.
  3. **Optimizer comparison:** Run a short pretraining (20k steps, Stage 1) using Muon (official hyperparams) and AdamW (lr 1e-4). Plot training loss vs. steps and final normalized accuracy on a small validation set. Expect Muon to converge faster and reach lower loss.
