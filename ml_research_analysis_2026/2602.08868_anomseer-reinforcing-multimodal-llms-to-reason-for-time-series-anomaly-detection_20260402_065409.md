# AnomSeer: Reinforcing Multimodal LLMs to Reason for Time-Series Anomaly Detection

## Quick Facts
- **ArXiv ID**: 2602.08868
- **Source URL**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.08868
- **Reference Count**: 40
- **Primary Result**: AnomSeer (Qwen2.5-VL-3B/7B) outperforms GPT-4o with 62.8–65.0% classification accuracy and up to 87.8% Affinity-F1 on TSB-UAD, while generating verifiable chain-of-thought reasoning.

## Executive Summary
Time‑series anomaly detection (TSAD) with multimodal LLMs often yields coarse, numerically imprecise reasoning, limiting trust and effectiveness for complex patterns. AnomSeer introduces two key innovations: (1) Expert Chain‑of‑Thought (ExpCoT) traces automatically derived from classical TSAD methods (e.g., statistical measures, FFT) to provide stepwise, numerically grounded analysis; (2) TimerPO, a reinforcement learning algorithm that aligns the MLLM’s reasoning with ExpCoT via optimal transport while using orthogonal projection to preserve detection performance. Experiments show that AnomSeer, built on Qwen2.5‑VL (3B/7B), significantly surpasses larger models like GPT‑4o, achieving 62.8–65.0% classification accuracy and up to 87.8% Affinity‑F1 in anomaly localization. The approach generalizes to real‑world benchmarks such as TSB‑UAD and yields interpretable reasoning traces, advancing both detection efficacy and explainability.

## Method Summary
AnomSeer first trains an expert chain‑of‑thought (ExpCoT) generator by applying classical time‑series anomaly detection techniques (including statistical thresholds, FFT‑based spectral analysis) to labeled data. Each ExpCoT trace details a verifiable sequence of numeric computations and logical deductions leading to an anomaly decision. The multimodal LLM (Qwen2.5‑VL) is then fine‑tuned with TimerPO, a reinforcement learning objective that computes a time‑series grounded advantage by aligning the model’s token‑level output distribution with the ExpCoT distribution using optimal transport. An orthogonal projection step decouples the reasoning alignment from the detection task, ensuring that improvements in interpretability do not degrade anomaly detection accuracy. At inference, the model processes a time‑series input and produces both an anomaly label and a chain‑of‑thought that mirrors the analytical rigor of classical methods.

## Key Results
- AnomSeer (3B/7B) surpasses GPT‑4o on TSB‑UAD with 62.8–65.0% classification accuracy and up to 87.8% Affinity‑F1 for localization.
- Notable gains on point anomalies and frequency‑based anomalies demonstrate the benefit of numerically grounded reasoning.
- The generated chain‑of‑thought traces are interpretable and align with classical TSAD analyses, enabling verification.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The core insight is that classical TSAD methods provide a reliable, stepwise analytical template that can be distilled into chain‑of‑thought traces. By exposing the MLLM to these ExpCoT traces during training, the model learns to perform fine‑grained numeric reasoning that is often missing from standard prompting. TimerPO further refines this by directly optimizing the model’s token distribution to match the expert reasoning, using optimal transport to measure distributional divergence. The orthogonal projection ensures that the reasoning objective does not conflict with the primary detection loss, thus maintaining or even improving anomaly detection accuracy. This two‑stage alignment strategy bridges the gap between high‑performance anomaly detection and faithful, verifiable explanation generation.

## Foundational Learning
The paper builds upon several key concepts:
- **Expert Chain‑of‑Thought (ExpCoT)**: ExpCoT provides a structured, numerically precise reasoning template derived from classical TSAD methods. *Why needed?* To overcome the coarse, qualitative reasoning of vanilla MLLMs and enforce stepwise, verifiable analysis. *Quick check:* Does the ExpCoT include explicit calculations (e.g., z‑scores, FFT bins) and logical deductions from the time‑series?
- **Classical Time‑Series Anomaly Detection**: Techniques such as statistical measures (e.g., z‑score, IQR) and Fourier transform are used to generate ExpCoT. *Why needed?* They offer mathematically sound, interpretable ways to detect anomalies of various types (point, contextual, frequency). *Quick check:* Are both point‑wise and frequency‑domain features incorporated to cover diverse anomaly patterns?
- **TimerPO (Reinforcement Learning with Optimal Transport)**: A policy gradient method that aligns the model’s output distribution with the ExpCoT distribution via an optimal transport‑based reward. *Why needed?* To fine‑tune the MLLM at the token level, ensuring its reasoning matches the expert’s both in content and structure. *Quick check:* Is the advantage function computed as a time‑series grounded metric (e.g., alignment cost) and combined with detection performance?
- **Orthogonal Projection for Multi‑Objective Optimization**: The reasoning alignment objective is projected orthogonally to the detection gradient. *Why needed?* To prevent interference; improvements in reasoning should not degrade detection accuracy. *Quick check:* Does the training show that detection performance is preserved or improved while reasoning quality increases?

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component Map**:  
Input time series → Classical TSAD Analysis (statistical tests, FFT) → ExpCoT Generator → TimerPO Training (Advantage via optimal transport + orthogonal projection) → MLLM (Qwen2.5‑VL) → Inference: Anomaly label + reasoning trace.

**Critical Path**:  
Training: Labeled data → ExpCoT extraction → compute alignment reward → TimerPO gradient update on MLLM.  
Inference: Time‑series → MLLM forward pass → generate detection decision and CoT.

**Design Tradeoffs**:  
- Relying on classical TSAD for ExpCoT ensures numerical correctness but may limit coverage of anomalies not captured by those methods.  
- TimerPO’s orthogonal projection protects detection goals but adds computational overhead and may slow convergence.  
- The current framework is univariate; extending to multivariate data requires new ExpCoT templates that handle cross‑variable interactions.

**Failure Signatures**:  
- High detection accuracy but low reasoning alignment (e.g., ExpCoT mismatch) indicating the model is ignoring alignment.  
- Reasoning traces with arithmetic errors (e.g., wrong z‑score computation) that are not penalized.  
- Poor performance on multivariate anomalies, exposing the univariate design bias.

**First Experiments**:  
1. Ablation study: Compare full AnomSeer against ablations (ExpCoT only SFT, TimerPO without orthogonal projection, vanilla MLLM).  
2. Reasoning validation: Parse generated reasoning, recompute numeric steps, and measure fidelity to ExpCoT; supplement with human evaluation.  
3. Generalization test: Evaluate on additional TSAD benchmarks (e.g., UCR, Yahoo) to check robustness beyond TSB‑UAD.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
1. **Multivariate TSAD**: The current univariate design does not address cross‑variable interactions; extending to multivariate data remains an open challenge.
2. **External knowledge integration**: Real‑world anomalies often involve external events (e.g., logs); the model lacks such context and needs to incorporate external knowledge.
3. **Unsupervised ExpCoT generation**: ExpCoT currently requires labeled anomalies; a method to generate it from unsupervised detectors is needed for broader applicability.
4. **Numerical accuracy**: Reasoning traces may contain incorrect numeric values (e.g., mis‑computed z‑scores), undermining trust; stronger mechanisms are required.

## Limitations
- Evaluation protocol lacks crucial details (dataset splits, baseline hyperparameters, statistical tests), hindering result verification.
- The interpretability claim is unsupported by any user study or faithfulness metric for the generated chain‑of‑thought traces.
- Generalization evidence is limited to TSB‑UAD; performance on other TSAD benchmarks or under covariate shift remains unknown.
