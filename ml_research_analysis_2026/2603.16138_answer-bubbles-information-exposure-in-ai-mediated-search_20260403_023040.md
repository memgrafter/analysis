# Answer Bubbles: Information Exposure in AI-Mediated Search

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2603.16138
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.16138
- **Reference count:** 9
- **Primary result:** Generative search systems create divergent "answer bubbles" via biased source selection and synthesis; search grounding reduces hedging by up to 60% but compounds source disparities (e.g., Wikipedia over-covered, social media under-covered).

## Executive Summary
Generative search systems—such as Search GPT and Google AI Overviews—are transforming how users access information by directly synthesizing answers from retrieved web sources. This paper investigates how these systems differ from traditional search and from each other in terms of source selection, linguistic framing, and synthesis fidelity. Through a large-scale audit of 11,000 real queries across four systems (Vanilla GPT, Search GPT, Google AI Overviews, Google Search), the authors find that generative systems exhibit sharp source-selection biases: they rely heavily on encyclopedic over social media sources, and the overlap between Search GPT and Google Search in top cited domains is only 24–25%. Grounding responses in search results reduces hedging language by up to 60% while preserving confidence markers. However, the synthesis process further amplifies source biases: Wikipedia is both the most cited source and the most overrepresented in summary content, while negatively framed and social media sources are substantially undercovered. These results reveal that generative search systems create distinct "answer bubbles"—systematic variations in the information and epistemic framing presented to users—raising important exposure and fairness concerns.

## Method Summary
The study conducted a comprehensive audit of four search systems using 11,000 queries sampled from the Google Natural Questions corpus and balanced across 11 topical categories. Queries were classified by topic using GPT-4o-mini with a few-shot prompt. System responses were collected via the OpenAI API (for Vanilla GPT and Search GPT) and SerpAPI (for Google Organic results and AI Overviews) with a fixed geographic location. The analysis pipeline included: (1) source diversity assessment—extracting cited URLs, mapping domains to categories (e.g., encyclopedia, news, social media), computing Jaccard overlap of top-100 domains, and comparing category distributions; (2) linguistic analysis—computing LIWC psycholinguistic categories (tentative, certainty, affect, etc.), readability (Coleman-Liau), and other style metrics, with statistical comparisons using Kruskal-Wallis and Mann-Whitney U tests; (3) fidelity analysis—for a stratified sample of 1,100 queries (100 per topic per system), decomposing summaries into Atomic Content Units (ACUs) using GPT-4o-mini, scraping and chunking cited sources, computing entailment probabilities with RoBERTa-large-MNLI, and deriving Equal Coverage (EC) and Coverage Parity (CP) metrics to quantify over/under-representation of source attributes. Bootstrapping (1,000 iterations) assessed significance.

## Key Results
- **Source divergence:** Search GPT shares only 24–25% of its top-100 cited domains with Google Search, heavily favoring encyclopedic sources (0.1% social) versus Google AIO (8.5%) and Organic (13.4%).
- **Epistemic language shift:** Incorporating search grounding reduces hedging (tentative language) by 40% (Search GPT) to 60% (Google AIO) relative to Vanilla GPT, while certainty markers decrease more slowly, raising the certainty-to-hedging ratio.
- **Compounded synthesis bias:** Wikipedia is significantly over-covered (CP +2.6 pp for Search GPT, +5.4 pp for Google AIO), whereas social media and negatively framed sources are under-covered (e.g., social forums under-represented by 22.1 pp in Google AIO).

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1
- **Claim:** Different generative search systems (e.g., Search GPT vs. Google AIO) exhibit fundamentally different source-selection philosophies, leading to divergent "answer bubbles."
- **Mechanism:** Systems employ distinct retrieval-augmentation pipelines and source-ranking heuristics (e.g., favoring encyclopedic vs. user-generated content), resulting in low overlap in cited domains (e.g., 24–25% Jaccard similarity between Search GPT and Google Organic).
- **Core assumption:** Source selection is a non-neutral design choice shaped by retrieval models, ranking signals, and post-processing filters, not just a reflection of web content.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract] "generative search systems exhibit sharp source-selection biases... Search GPT cites 24–25% overlapping top domains with Google Search, heavily favoring encyclopedic over social media sources."
  - [section 4.1] "Search GPT draws almost none of its citations from social platforms (0.1%), while Google AIO (8.5%) and Organic (13.4%) rely on them substantially more."
  - [corpus] Related work (e.g., "Search Engines in an AI Era") corroborates source-citation divergence but does not directly provide the quantitative overlap metrics reported here; primary evidence is from the paper's own audit.
- **Break condition:** If multiple systems show >80% overlap in top-100 cited domains across diverse topics, the claim of "fundamentally different source pools" would be falsified.

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim:** Search grounding (retrieval-augmentation) selectively attenuates epistemic markers in generated summaries, specifically reducing hedging language while preserving or slightly reducing certainty markers.
- **Mechanism:** The synthesis process prioritizes concise, assertive prose likely because retrieved source content (especially from encyclopedic sources) contains less tentative language, or because post-training/instruction-tuning for search systems deprioritizes hedging to project confidence.
- **Core assumption:** Linguistic style is an emergent property of the combined retrieval-generation pipeline, not solely inherited from source texts; the model actively re-phrases information.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract] "Incorporating search also selectively attenuates epistemic markers, reducing hedging by up to 60% while preserving confidence language."
  - [section 5.1] "Tentative language drops by 40% from Vanilla GPT to Search GPT... and by 60% from Vanilla GPT to Google AIO... certainty drops slower than hedging, where the ratio of certainty-to-tentative language rises."
  - [corpus] Corpus shows related work on epistemic language in search (e.g., "AI summaries influence attitudes"), but the specific differential impact on hedging vs. certainty is uniquely evidenced in this paper's RQ2 analysis.
- **Break condition:** If search-grounded systems showed no significant difference in hedging/certainty ratios compared to vanilla parametric models (d < 0.2), the selective attenuation claim would be undermined.

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim:** Biases in source selection compound during the synthesis stage, where certain source types (e.g., Wikipedia, long documents) are not only cited frequently but also disproportionately weight in summary content.
- **Mechanism:** The summarization/entailment model exhibits a "length bias" (favoring longer source chunks) and an "assertiveness bias" (favoring sources with causal/certain language), which amplifies initial retrieval preferences for authoritative, verbose sources.
- **Core assumption:** Fidelity metrics (Coverage Parity) capture systematic over/under-representation beyond mere citation counts, indicating that synthesis is an active filtering/weighting process.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract] "AI summaries further compound the citation biases: Wikipedia and longer sources are disproportionately overrepresented, whereas cited social media content and negatively framed sources are substantially underrepresented."
  - [section 6.2] "The largest CP effect for both systems is for source document length... Wikipedia specifically... is significantly over-covered... social media and forum sources are under-covered by 22.1pp in Google AIO."
  - [section 7] "This creates a citation-synthesis gap: the model lists user-driven sources... while relying on a narrower set of encyclopedic or authoritative texts."
  - [corpus] "Coverage-based fairness in multi-document summarization" (Li et al., 2025) provides the methodological framework, but the compounding finding is an original application to generative search.
- **Break condition:** If Coverage Parity (CP) values for key attributes (e.g., Wikipedia, length) were near zero or showed inverse patterns (under-representation), the compounding effect claim would fail.

## Foundational Learning
- **Concept: Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG) Pipeline**
  - *Why needed here:* The paper compares systems with and without explicit web retrieval (Vanilla GPT vs. Search GPT/Google AIO). Understanding the RAG architecture—query → retrieval → synthesis → citation—is essential to grasp where divergences arise (retrieval vs. generation).
  - *Quick check question:* What are the three sequential stages in the RAG pipeline as operationalized in this study (per sections 3 & 6.1)?

- **Concept: Psycholinguistic Markers (LIWC)**
  - *Why needed here:* RQ2 quantifies epistemic and affective language using LIWC categories (tentative, certainty, posemo, etc.). These operationalize abstract concepts like "hedging" and "confidence" into measurable features to compare system outputs.
  - *Quick check question:* Which LIWC category directly measures epistemic caution/hedging, and what was its proportional change from Vanilla GPT to Google AIO?

- **Concept: Coverage Parity (CP) & Equal Coverage (EC)**
  - *Why needed here:* RQ3's core metrics assess fidelity. EC measures per-query evenness of source usage; CP measures systematic bias across source attributes. These move beyond citation counts to analyze *how much* each cited source contributes to the summary.
  - *Quick check question:* What does a higher EC value indicate about a summary's use of its cited sources?

## Architecture Onboarding
### Component map
1. **Query Input:** Real-user search queries (from NQ corpus).
2. **Retrieval Module:** (a) Search GPT's `web_search` tool; (b) Google Search via SerpAPI (returning both organic links and AI Overviews); (c) Vanilla GPT: *no retrieval*.
3. **Synthesis Module:** LLM (GPT-4o-mini for Search GPT; Gemini for Google AIO) that conditions on retrieved sources to generate a summary with inline citations.
4. **Citation Extraction:** Post-processor to parse cited URLs/domains from generated text.
5. **Analysis Pipeline:** (a) Source aggregation & diversity metrics (Jaccard, domain categories); (b) LIWC & readability scoring of summaries; (c) ACU decomposition → entailment scoring → EC/CP computation.

### Critical path
Query -> Retrieval -> Synthesis -> Citation Parse -> **Source Diversity Analysis (RQ1)** & **Linguistic Analysis (RQ2)** -> **Fidelity Analysis (RQ3: ACU → Entailment → EC/CP)**.

### Design tradeoffs
- **Verbosity vs. Conciseness:** Search GPT produces longer, formal summaries; Google AIO produces shorter, compressed ones. Trade-off between detail and scanability.
- **Source Diversity vs. Editorial Authority:** Systems citing diverse user-generated content (Google) vs. curated encyclopedic/news (Search GPT) trade breadth for perceived authority.
- **Fidelity vs. Compression:** More sources cited (Google AIO) correlates with lower per-source coverage (EC↑), suggesting a trade-off between breadth of attribution and depth of synthesis.

### Failure signatures
- **Low Citation Overlap:** Jaccard similarity < 30% between top-100 domains of any two systems.
- **High EC Values:** EC > 0.2 indicates a summary relies heavily on a subset of its sources.
- **Topic-dependent AIO Suppression:** If a topic (e.g., Sports) shows <40% AIO generation rate, comparative analysis for that topic is underpowered.
- **Scraping Failure Rate:** >20% of cited URLs unscrapable (paywalls, blocks) biases CP calculations if non-scrapable sources differ systematically.

### First 3 experiments
1. **Replicate RQ1 Source Overlap:** Compute Jaccard similarity of top-100 cited domains for Search GPT vs. Google AIO vs. Organic on a 1,000-query subset. Expect ~24–25% overlap for Search GPT vs. Google systems.
2. **Replicate RQ2 Hedging Attenuation:** Run LIWC on 100 summaries per system. Compute Cohen’s *d* for `tentat` between Vanilla GPT and Google AIO; expect *d* ≈ 0.6 (60% reduction).
3. **Replicate RQ3 Compounding for Wikipedia:** For queries where Wikipedia is cited, compute CP for the `Wiki vs. Non-Wiki` attribute. Expect positive difference (+2.6pp for Search GPT, +5.4pp for Google AIO) indicating over-coverage.

## Limitations
- Proprietary LLM checkpoints, retrieval heuristics, and undisclosed prompts for topic classification and ACU decomposition hinder exact replication.
- Domain-to-category mapping is heuristic and incomplete; LIWC2015 dictionary is proprietary, forcing approximate substitutes that may not align perfectly.
- Geographic/temporal variation in Google AI Overviews and potential scraping failures (paywalls, blocks) could bias coverage metrics if non-random.

## Confidence
- **Mechanism 1** (divergent source pools): Medium
- **Mechanism 2** (selective hedging attenuation): Medium
- **Mechanism 3** (compounding biases): Medium
