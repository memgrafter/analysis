# Controllable Evidence Selection in Retrieval-Augmented Question Answering via Deterministic Utility Gating
## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2603.18011  
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.18011  
- **Reference count:** 0  
- **Primary result:** A deterministic evidence-first framework with MUE and DUE produces compact, auditable evidence sets or abstains, prioritizing traceable justification over response coverage.

## Executive Summary
Similarity-based retrieval in retrieval-augmented question answering (RAG) often yields topically relevant but not directly supportive text, obscuring evidentiary justification. This paper proposes a deterministic evidence-first framework that decouples evidence selection from answer generation. It introduces Meaning-Utility Estimation (MUE) to score retrieved sentences using fixed signals—semantic similarity, explicit term coverage, and dataset-based conceptual distinctiveness—followed by Diversity-Utility Estimation (DUE) to iteratively assemble a non-redundant evidence set. An evidence gate requires at least one selected unit to independently satisfy the query’s evidentiary need; otherwise, the system abstains. Prototype evaluations on legal and historical corpora show that definition-style queries with explicit statements reliably pass the gate, while condition- or claim-based queries frequently fail despite moderate similarity scores. The system thus outputs either a concise, auditable evidence set or no answer, explicitly trading broader coverage for traceable justification.

## Method Summary
The method comprises three core stages. First, MUE assigns a utility score to each retrieved sentence based on three fixed signals: semantic similarity to the query, explicit term coverage (presence of query keywords), and conceptual distinctiveness derived from the dataset’s global statistics. Second, DUE iteratively selects sentences to maximize diversity while maintaining a minimum utility threshold, yielding a compact, non-redundant evidence set. Third, an evidence gate checks whether at least one sentence in the set independently supports the query’s information need; if none do, the system abstains from providing an answer. This deterministic pipeline prioritizes evidential sufficiency over answer completeness, with all signals predefined and no learned parameters.

## Key Results
- Definition-style queries with explicit statements consistently satisfied the evidence gate.
- Condition- or claim-based queries often failed the gate despite achieving moderate similarity scores.
- The framework produces compact, auditable evidence sets or abstains, emphasizing traceable justification.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The approach works because it replaces opaque similarity matching with explicit, multi-faceted utility signals that directly measure evidential support. MUE’s combination of semantic similarity, term coverage, and conceptual distinctiveness captures different dimensions of relevance beyond topicality, while DUE ensures the selected set covers distinct aspects without redundancy. The evidence gate enforces a high bar: only queries for which at least one sentence independently answers the question are answered, preventing the aggregation of partially relevant fragments. By making the scoring and selection deterministic, the system provides full transparency and auditability, and by abstaining when evidence is insufficient, it avoids generating unsupported answers. The observed failure on condition/claim queries suggests that such queries often require synthesizing multiple pieces of evidence, which the gate’s independence requirement prohibits, highlighting a deliberate design trade-off.

## Foundational Learning
- **Meaning-Utility Estimation (MUE)**: Why needed? To quantify how well an individual retrieved sentence supports the query using fixed, interpretable signals. Quick check: Does the sentence share key terms and concepts with the query while being semantically similar?  
- **Diversity-Utility Estimation (DUE)**: Why needed? To assemble a non-redundant evidence set that maximizes coverage of distinct relevant information. Quick check: Does adding a new sentence increase the diversity of the set without dropping below the utility threshold?  
- **Evidence Gate**: Why needed? To ensure the final evidence set contains at least one sentence that independently satisfies the query, guaranteeing a traceable justification. Quick check: Is there any selected sentence that, on its own, provides a complete answer to the query?  
- **Deterministic Utility Scoring**: Why needed? To avoid black-box learned models and provide stable, auditable scores across runs. Quick check: Are the scores computed solely from the query, sentence, and dataset statistics without model training?

## Architecture Onboarding
Component map:  
Retrieved Sentences -> MUE Scoring -> Utility-Weighted Candidates -> DUE Iterative Selection -> Evidence Set -> Evidence Gate -> [Answer if pass, Abstain if fail]

Critical path: Retrieval provides initial candidates; MUE assigns scores; DUE picks a diverse subset; the gate validates sufficiency; output is either an answer derived from the evidence set or an abstention.

Design tradeoffs: Deterministic signals vs. learned flexibility; compact evidence vs. comprehensive coverage; strict independence requirement vs. ability to handle complex multi-hop queries. The system favors interpretability and reliability over recall.

Failure signatures: Condition- or claim-based queries that require combining multiple pieces of evidence fail the gate; queries with high similarity but low term coverage or distinctiveness may also be rejected.

First experiments:
1. Apply the prototype to open-domain QA datasets (e.g., SQuAD) to test generalization beyond legal/historical texts.
2. Conduct a human evaluation comparing the traceability of the generated evidence sets against a standard similarity-based RAG baseline.
3. Perform an error analysis on failing condition/claim queries to determine whether evidence is absent or simply not scored highly by MUE.

## Limitations
- Domain generalization is untested beyond legal/historical corpora.
- Fixed MUE signals may not capture nuanced evidentiary relationships in complex queries.
- The evidence gate threshold and its independence requirement are not validated against human judgment or alternative gating strategies.

## Confidence
- **High:** The prototype produces compact, auditable evidence sets and abstains appropriately on the tested corpora.
- **Medium:** The observed pattern (definition queries pass, condition/claim queries fail) is consistent in evaluation but may be domain-specific.
- **Low:** The claim that this approach broadly prioritizes traceable justification over coverage as a superior trade-off lacks comparative evaluation against state-of-the-art RAG systems.

## Next Checks
1. Cross-domain replication on at least two diverse QA datasets (e.g., SQuAD, PubmedQA).
2. Comparative utility study with human evaluation of evidence sufficiency and traceability versus a similarity-based RAG baseline.
3. Failure mode analysis on condition/claim queries to distinguish between missing evidence and scoring deficiencies.
