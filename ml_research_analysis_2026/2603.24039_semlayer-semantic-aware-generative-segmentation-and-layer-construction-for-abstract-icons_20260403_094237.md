# SemLayer: Semantic-aware Generative Segmentation and Layer Construction for Abstract Icons
## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2603.24039
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.24039
- Reference count: 40
- Primary result: State-of-the-art segmentation (84.3% mIoU, 76.1% PQ) and completion (85.2% mIoU, 46.6 CD) on 48 real-world icons.

## Executive Summary
SemLayer addresses the problem of recovering editable, semantically meaningful layer structures from flattened vector icons where original layering is lost. The proposed three-stage pipeline employs a generative segmentation model (diffusion transformer) to colorize parts, an amodal completion module to reconstruct occluded shapes, and an integer linear programming optimization to infer occlusion hierarchy. On a test set of 48 real-world icons, the method significantly outperforms baselines and enables part-level editing, restyling, and animation for static assets.

## Method Summary
The method comprises three core stages: (1) A diffusion transformer (fine-tuned EasyControl) conditions on the monochrome silhouette and a text prompt to produce a colorized output where each semantic part is assigned a distinct color; masks are extracted via thresholding. (2) For each visible fragment, an amodal completion model (pix2gestalt) fine-tuned on synthetic icon overlaps predicts the full shape, with merging of fragments having high IoU. (3) Layer ordering is determined by solving an integer linear program that maximizes occlusion consistency of extra pixels while penalizing misocclusion of visible parts. The final vector layers are generated using Potrace vectorization combined with a curve-reuse technique that preserves original Bézier segments.

## Key Results
- Segmentation mIoU 84.3%, PQ 76.1%; completion mIoU 85.2%, Chamfer Distance 46.6, all state-of-the-art on 48 icons.
- Outperforms baselines including SAM2 and gpt-image-1 by significant margins.
- Enables practical design workflows: part-level editing, restyling, and animation.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1: Generative Segmentation via Colorization
- **Claim:** Semantic segmentation of abstract icons is reformulated as a chromatically differentiated colorization task using a diffusion transformer, producing visually separable semantic parts.
- **Mechanism:** The segmentation model (fine-tuned EasyControl) conditions a diffusion transformer on the input monochrome silhouette and a text prompt. It generates a colorized output where each distinct color corresponds to a semantic component. Masks are then extracted by thresholding color channels. The generative prior provides shape and semantic associations that classical segmentation (e.g., SAM2) lacks for highly abstract, minimally textured icons.
- **Core assumption:** A diffusion model trained on icon data can associate structural patterns with semantic parts and reliably colorize them distinctly, creating separable masks.
- **Evidence:** [abstract] "first, a generative segmentation model reformulates part separation as a colorization task using a diffusion transformer to produce chromatically differentiated outputs"; [section 3.2] "We formulate segmentation as a colorization task... Our model generates a colorized segmentation where each color represents a distinct semantic part." Corpus signals indicate novelty in this specific application; related works focus on medical or general segmentation, not abstract icons. Weak direct corpus support for this exact mechanism.
- **Break condition:** If icons contain highly ambiguous, tangled structures where semantic parts cannot be distinguished by color without realistic cues, the colorization may merge or fragment parts incorrectly (see failure cases Fig. 9 left).

### Mechanism 2: Amodal Completion with Icon-Specific Training
- **Claim:** Amodal completion recovers full shapes of occluded semantic parts by fine-tuning a diffusion-based amodal inpainting model (pix2gestalt) on icon-specific synthetic overlaps.
- **Mechanism:** For each visible fragment `V_k` from segmentation, the completion model takes the occluded patch and a visible mask. Conditioned on a CLIP image embedding, it denoises to hallucinate the complete amodal mask `A_k`. Training with fragmented visibility (many fragments → same full shape) teaches robustness to heavy occlusion. A merging step (IoU > 0.7) consolidates multiple completions of the same object.
- **Core assumption:** Icon-specific amodal patterns (e.g., how limbs or tools extend behind objects) can be learned from synthetic icon overlaps, and the model can generalize to unseen icons with similar stylistic conventions.
- **Evidence:** [abstract] "an amodal completion module fine-tuned on icon-specific data reconstructs full shapes of occluded parts"; [section 3.3] "To address this, we curate a custom amodal-overlap dataset... and finetune the model on the icon domain... training with fragmented visibility... enforces a many-to-one completion behavior."; [section 5.3.2] Quantitative results show our completion model achieves 85.2 mIoU and CD 46.6, significantly outperforming MP3D and gpt-image-1.
- **Break condition:** If an occluded structure is entirely unseen in training (e.g., sun rays fully hidden behind a cloud, Fig. 9 right), the model may fail to hallucinate geometrically coherent or semantically expected shapes.

### Mechanism 3: ILP-Based Layer Ordering
- **Claim:** The correct occlusion hierarchy is inferred by formulating layer ordering as an integer linear programming (ILP) problem that maximizes occlusion consistency of extra pixels (`E_k = A_k \ I`) while penalizing incorrect occlusion of visible pixels (`V_k`).
- **Mechanism:** For ordered pairs `(i,j)`, binary variable `x_ij` indicates if `i` is above `j`. Precomputed indicators `c_ij` (=1 if `E_i` overlaps `F_j`) and `d_ij` (=1 if `V_i` overlaps `F_j`) link order to coverage. Auxiliary variables `y_i` (extra region covered) and `z_i` (visible region incorrectly covered) are constrained by `x_ji`. The objective maximizes `∑ y_i - λ ∑ z_i`, ensuring each extra pixel is covered by at least one upper layer and each visible pixel is not covered. Transitivity constraints enforce a valid layer stack.
- **Core assumption:** The fill region `F_k` (outermost contour of vectorized `A_k`) is a sufficient geometric proxy for occlusion, and pairwise overlap tests between `E_i`/`V_i` and `F_j` can reliably infer a globally consistent stacking order without cyclic interleaving.
- **Evidence:** [abstract] "an integer linear programming optimization infers the correct occlusion hierarchy"; [section 3.4] Full ILP formulation with variables, constraints, and objective; [section 5] Layer ordering results are not separately reported, but the final layered vector output enables downstream editing, implying ordering is correct enough for practical use.
- **Break condition:** If multiple layers have complex interlocking holes (non-nested occlusion) or the `F_k` fill-region assumption fails (e.g., internal cut-outs should not occlude), the ILP may produce an ordering that violates perceptual plausibility.

## Foundational Learning
### Diffusion Models (Latent & Flow Matching)
- **Why needed:** The core segmentation and completion stages rely on diffusion transformers trained with flow-matching objectives to generate colorized segmentations and amodal completions. Understanding noise scheduling, conditioning (via LoRA, cross-attention), and latent space operations is crucial for grasping how generative priors are applied.
- **Quick check:** How does conditioning on the input silhouette and text prompt steer the diffusion process to produce semantically meaningful color regions instead of arbitrary textures?

### Amodal Completion
- **Why needed:** The task requires inferring the *complete* shape of parts, not just their visible regions. This involves hallucinating invisible geometry consistent with the part's semantics, which differs from standard inpainting (which fills holes *within* visible regions).
- **Quick check:** Why is training on synthetic icon overlaps with fragmented visibility (many fragments → same completion) necessary for robustness, and how does it differ from training on natural-image amodal datasets?

### Vector Graphics Parametrization (Bézier Curves)
- **Why needed:** The final output must be an editable vector layer. The "curve reuse" strategy (Section A.3) surgically replaces only missing segments while preserving original high-quality curves. This requires understanding curve splitting, G1 continuity, and parametric operations.
- **Quick check:** What is the advantage of reusing original Bézier segments versus re-vectorizing the entire rasterized completion, and how are bridge curves constructed?

## Architecture Onboarding
### Component Map
Segmentation Module -> Completion Module -> Ordering Module -> Vectorization

### Critical Path
The segmentation quality directly impacts the inputs to completion. Completion must produce accurate amodal masks for each part; merging reduces fragment count. Ordering depends on accurate amodal masks and their fill regions. Vectorization finally transforms rasters into editable vectors with curve reuse. Failures at any stage propagate downstream.

### Design Tradeoffs
- *Generative segmentation vs. deterministic:* Diffusion provides strong priors but is stochastic and slower (14.56 sec/icon) than deterministic networks. Tradeoff: robustness on abstract icons vs. runtime.
- *ILP ordering vs. heuristic:* ILP ensures global optimality under constraints but scales as O(K³) with parts K (typical icons K=4-10, manageable). Heuristics would be faster but potentially less consistent.
- *Curve reuse vs. full re-vectorization:* Reuse preserves original curve quality but requires precise contour alignment and Bézier surgery. Full re-vectorization is simpler but discards original high-quality paths.

### Failure Signatures
- **Segmentation failure:** Over-fragmentation (too many visible fragments) or under-fragmentation (missing semantic boundaries). Visual check: are parts cleanly separated by distinct colors?
- **Completion failure:** Missing limbs/features or unnatural shape hallucinations. Check amodal mask `A_k` against visible fragment `V_k`: does completion extend plausibly?
- **Ordering failure:** Visible parts occluded by wrong layers (e.g., a handle appearing under a pot). Visually inspect final layered stack: do occlusion relationships match the input silhouette?

### First 3 Experiments
1. **Segmentation sanity check:** Run the trained segmentation model on a known training icon. Verify it produces the expected number of color regions and that extracted masks align with semantic parts. Measure mIoU vs. training annotation.
2. **Completion edge case:** Take a simple icon (e.g., a circle partially occluded by a rectangle). Feed the circle's visible fragment to the completion model. Does it recover the full circle? Introduce heavy occlusion (90% coverage) and check robustness.
3. **ILP constraint validation:** Construct a synthetic 3-layer example (A over B, B over C) with known amodal masks, visible fragments, and extra regions. Manually compute pairwise indicators. Solve ILP and verify returned order matches ground truth. Test transitivity constraint on a 4-layer cycle.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- **Multi-color and stylistically diverse icons:** The current pipeline focuses on black-and-white line drawings. Extending to multi-color icons would require architectural changes and larger datasets. The authors expect the framework to generalize given appropriate training data.
- **Tangled icons and fully occluded parts:** Highly tangled or heavily occluded icons challenge both segmentation and completion. For tangled structures, color-based segmentation may merge or fragment parts. Fully occluded parts provide no visible guidance for amodal completion, leading to failures.
- **Scalability and robustness of ILP ordering:** The ILP formulation's performance on icons with many layers (scalability) and its sensitivity to errors in amodal masks are not studied. For complex interlocking holes, the fill region assumption may break.

## Limitations
- Diffusion-based segmentation is stochastic and slow (~14.6s/icon), and may fail on highly tangled abstract structures.
- Completion model trained on synthetic overlaps may not generalize to real-world occlusions with unseen part shapes or stylistic conventions.
- ILP ordering relies on the fill region as an occlusion proxy; it fails for complex interlocking holes or internal cut-outs.
- Evaluation on only 48 icons raises concerns about statistical significance and domain coverage.
