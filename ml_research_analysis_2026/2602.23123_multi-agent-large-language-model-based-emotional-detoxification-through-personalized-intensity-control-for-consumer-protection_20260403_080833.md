# Multi-Agent Large Language Model Based Emotional Detoxification Through Personalized Intensity Control for Consumer Protection

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2602.23123
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.23123
- Reference count: 18
- Primary result: MALLET reduces emotional stimulus scores by up to 19.3% while preserving semantics.

## Executive Summary
This paper introduces MALLET, a multi-agent LLM system that reduces excessive emotional stimulation in online text to support calmer consumer decision-making. The system comprises four agents: Emotion Analysis (scores stimulus), Emotion Adjustment (rewrites high-stimulus content), Balance Monitoring (weekly aggregation and advice), and Personal Guide (sensitivity-based mode selection). Evaluated on 800 AG News articles, MALLET achieves up to 19.3% reduction in stimulus scores (COOL mode) and 13.1% (BALANCED), while maintaining semantic similarity (SBERT 0.827-0.846) and low contradiction rates (<4%). Personalization is demonstrated through simulated user histories and mode recommendations. However, the study lacks human validation and shows limited effectiveness for World news.

## Method Summary
MALLET processes text through a pipeline: (1) Emotion Analysis uses a 6-emotion BERT classifier to compute a stimulus score \(S\) (sum of anger, fear, surprise probabilities), High-Impact Rate (HIR, \(S>0.6\)), and Emotion Balance Index (EBI). (2) For texts with \(S>0.6\), Emotion Adjustment employs GPT-4.1-mini to rewrite them in BALANCED (single-sentence neutralization) or COOL (neutralization plus supplementary sentence) mode, respecting a ±20% length constraint. (3) Balance Monitoring aggregates weekly metrics (mean S, HIR, EBI) for simulated users and generates advice via GPT-4.1-mini. (4) Personal Guide recommends a content mode (RAW/BALANCED/COOL) based on a user persona describing stimulus sensitivity. Evaluation uses 800 AG News articles (200 per category) and simulates 5 pseudo-consumers with 100-text browsing histories. Metrics include stimulus reduction, semantic preservation (SBERT similarity, NLI contradiction), and readability.

## Key Results
- Up to 19.3% stimulus score reduction in COOL mode and 13.1% in BALANCED mode.
- Semantic preservation: SBERT cosine similarity 0.827-0.846, NLI contradiction rate <4%, with near-zero correlation between reduction and preservation.
- Category analysis: 17.8–33.8% reduction in Sports, Business, Sci/Tech; minimal effect in World news.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The system works by first identifying emotionally charged texts via a BERT classifier. High-stimulus texts are rewritten by GPT-4.1-mini to reduce anger, fear, and surprise probabilities, with COOL mode adding calming context. The rewriting prompt emphasizes factual preservation and enforces length constraints, which helps maintain semantic integrity. Personalization through the Personal Guide ensures users receive content balanced according to their sensitivity, while Balance Monitoring provides weekly feedback to promote long-term emotional equilibrium. The near-zero correlation between stimulus reduction and semantic preservation indicates that the two objectives are largely independent, allowing effective detoxification without sacrificing meaning.

## Foundational Learning
- **Multi-Agent LLM Systems**: Enables modular design where each agent performs a specialized function (analysis, rewriting, monitoring, guidance). Quick check: Agents interact via structured data (e.g., stimulus scores, rewritten text) without tight coupling.
- **Emotion Classification with BERT**: Provides a scalable, automated way to quantify emotional intensity in text. Quick check: The 6-emotion model outputs probabilities; stimulus score sums anger, fear, surprise.
- **Semantic Preservation Metrics**: SBERT similarity and NLI contradiction ensure detoxified text remains factually consistent with the original. Quick check: SBERT >0.8 and NLI contradiction <5% are considered acceptable.
- **Personalization via User Sensitivity Profiles**: Tailors content mode to individual tolerance, improving acceptance and effectiveness. Quick check: The Personal Guide maps persona descriptors (high/mod/low sensitivity) to mode recommendations.
- **Stimulus Score and High-Impact Rate**: Define the primary reduction targets. Quick check: S = P(anger)+P(fear)+P(surprise); HIR = fraction of texts with S>0.6.
- **Balance Monitoring and Feedback Loops**: Aims to foster healthy information consumption habits over time. Quick check: Weekly aggregation of S, HIR, EBI across user history, with GPT-generated advice.

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component Map**:
Personal Guide -> (user mode) -> for each text: Emotion Analysis -> (if S>0.6 and mode != RAW) -> Emotion Adjustment -> output (BALANCED/COOL); else raw.
Balance Monitoring <- aggregates all user texts over week -> generates advice.

**Critical Path**: The real-time processing of a text depends on Emotion Analysis and, conditionally, Emotion Adjustment. The mode from Personal Guide must be known before Adjustment can proceed.

**Design Tradeoffs**:
- Threshold-based rewriting (S>0.6) vs. universal adjustment: balances reduction with natural expression.
- BALANCED (concise) vs. COOL (adds context) modes: tradeoff between brevity and additional calming effect.
- Length constraint (±20%): preserves readability but may limit rewriting freedom.
- GPT-4.1-mini vs. smaller models: better quality but higher cost and latency.
- Persona-based personalization: adds complexity but may improve user experience.

**Failure Signatures**:
- Emotion Analysis yields low average S (<0.4): likely implementation error in classifier or probability extraction.
- COOL outputs exceed length limit (>120% of original): prompt may not enforce sentence count or token limit.
- NLI contradiction rate >5%: rewriting introduces factual inconsistencies; prompt needs stronger fact preservation clause.
- No reduction in World category: expected due to inherently high-stimulus facts; indicates classifier is working but rewriting ineffective for such content.

**First Three Experiments**:
1. Validate Emotion Analysis by scoring a sample of AG News texts and confirming average S ~0.482 and HIR ~0.3 (as per paper).
2. Test Emotion Adjustment: generate BALANCED and COOL outputs for 50 high-stimulus texts; check sentence count, token ratio, and manually verify fact preservation in a subset.
3. Run end-to-end on 50 articles: compute reduction (S, HIR), SBERT similarity, and NLI contradiction; ensure they match reported ranges (19.3%/13.1% reduction, SBERT >0.82, contradiction <4%).

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
1. **Human Validation of Stimulus Metrics**: How do classifier-based stimulus scores correlate with human subjective assessments of emotional intensity and cognitive load? The paper acknowledges the need for a user study.
2. **Alternative Interventions for Inherently High-Stimulus Content**: What non-lexical strategies (e.g., staged disclosure, contextual framing) can reduce impact for categories like World news where facts themselves are alarming? The current approach shows minimal effect.
3. **Longitudinal Impact of Personalized Advice**: Does weekly advice from Balance Monitoring lead to measurable, sustained improvements in real users' information consumption diversity and emotional balance? The study uses simulated browsing histories.
4. **Cross-Lingual Generalization**: Can MALLET be effectively transferred to languages with different emotional expression norms? The paper targets only English.
5. **Editorial Bias in COOL Mode's Supplementary Text**: Does the added calming context inadvertently introduce new biases or framing effects not captured by contradiction metrics? The NLI neutral rate is high, but selective emphasis may nudge interpretation.

## Limitations
- Prompt sensitivity: Exact GPT-4.1-mini prompts are unreported; minor wording changes may alter rewriting behavior and metrics.
- Personalization simulation: Details of pseudo-consumer construction (sampling without replacement, category distribution) are unspecified, affecting confidence in personalization results.
- Domain specificity: Evaluation on formal AG News articles may not generalize to social media or user-generated content, where emotional language and factual density differ.

## Next Checks
1. **Prompt Variation Test**: Systematically vary the Emotion Adjustment agent's instruction phrasing (e.g., "neutralize" vs. "reduce emotional intensity") and measure changes in stimulus reduction and NLI contradiction to quantify sensitivity.
2. **Sampling Bias Audit**: Re-run the pseudo-consumer simulation with controlled category proportions (e.g., uniform random vs. mirroring the 200/category pool) and report variance in weekly S aggregation and advice diversity.
3. **Cross-Domain Robustness**: Apply MALLET to a social media corpus (e.g., Twitter posts) and compare reduction/preservation trade-offs against AG News results to test generalization.
