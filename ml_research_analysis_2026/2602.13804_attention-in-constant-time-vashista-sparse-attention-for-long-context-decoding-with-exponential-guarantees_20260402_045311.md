# Attention in Constant Time: Vashista Sparse Attention for Long-Context Decoding with Exponential Guarantees

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2602.13804  
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.13804  
- **Reference count:** 9  
- **Primary result:** Vashista Sparse Attention achieves near-constant decode latency (~24 ms) up to 128k tokens on Llama-3-8B with O(PD+KD) per-token complexity by exploiting exponential concentration via a support gap Δ.

## Executive Summary
Long-context attention in large language models (LLMs) has a per-token computational cost that scales linearly with context length, making it expensive for long sequences. This paper introduces Vashista Sparse Attention, which under a positive "support gap" Δ—measuring strict separation of the active key set—shows that entropic attention concentrates exponentially on a constant-size active face. The method uses paged KV routing and convex optimization to select a small candidate set per query, yielding theoretical guarantees: off-face attention mass decays as exp(−Ω(Δ/ε)) while on-face error scales linearly with temperature ε. Empirically on Llama-3-8B with H100 GPUs, decode latency becomes near-constant (~24 ms) up to 128k context, reducing the dense cost by a factor of >50× at large T, with quality governed by the Δ diagnostic.

## Method Summary
Vashista Sparse Attention replaces dense attention with a pipeline: for each query, page-level routing selects P candidate pages based on coarse summaries; within those pages, token-level routing selects K_c candidate tokens. On this candidate set, an entropically-regularized convex optimization (e.g., Frank-Wolfe) computes attention weights. The theoretical foundation—exponential concentration of attention on a constant-size active face when the support gap Δ>0—ensures that a constant number of pages and tokens suffice with high probability. The system integrates with paged KV caches and uses a fused CUDA kernel to gather and weight value vectors, achieving O(PD+K_cD) per-token complexity independent of context length T.

## Key Results
- Theoretical: Off-face attention mass decays as exp(−Ω(Δ/ε)); on-face error is O(ε) under Δ>0 and well-conditioned active face.
- Empirical: On Llama-3-8B/H100, decode latency is near-constant (~24 ms) up to 128k tokens, reducing dense cost by >50× at large T.
- Quality: The Δ diagnostic (or proxy bΔ) predicts sparsification safety and governs output quality.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

**Mechanism 1:** Entropic attention concentrates exponentially on a constant-size active face when the support gap Δ > 0. The paper models attention as Euclidean projection onto the convex hull of key vectors. The unregularized projection yields KKT multipliers; the support gap Δ is the minimum multiplier for inactive keys. Under positive Δ and tangential nondegeneracy (σ_min(B)>0), the entropically-regularized solution's off-face weights decay as exp(−Ω(Δ/ε)), while on-face error is O(ε). Proof uses strong metric regularity of the KKT system. Core assumption: Δ(q) > 0 and active face well-conditioned. Evidence: Section 6 (Theorem 1, Corollary 1), abstract. Break condition: Δ(q) ≈ 0 destroys exponential decay.

**Mechanism 2:** Paged KV routing and convex optimization bound the candidate set size independently of context length. The system first selects P candidate pages via coarse summaries, then K_c tokens within those pages. Convex solver (e.g., Frank-Wolfe) computes weights on this candidate set. Because the active face has constant size, bounded pages/tokens suffice with high probability. Core assumption: active face lies within small number of pages/tokens; support gap ensures off-face keys irrelevant. Evidence: Section 7.1 (page/token routing), Section 7.2 (Corollary 3). Break condition: active face spans many pages/tokens due to large face or poor summaries.

**Mechanism 3:** The combination yields near-constant decode latency and quality control via the Δ diagnostic. With constant P and K_c, per-token cost is independent of T. Empirically, decode time stays ~24ms up to 128k tokens on Llama-3-8B/H100. The Δ statistic (gap between top two scores) predicts when sparsification is safe; bΔ scales as 1/√log M. Evidence: abstract, Section 9.1 (Table 2, Figure 1), Section 8. Break condition: small Δ forces fallback to dense or larger candidate sets.

## Foundational Learning

- **Concept: Convex hull projection.**  
  Why needed: Attention weights are barycentric coordinates over keys; geometric view enables analysis of active sets/faces.  
  Quick check: "If the query is projected onto the convex hull of keys, what does the active set represent in terms of attention?"

- **Concept: Support gap (strict complementarity).**  
  Why needed: Δ quantifies margin between active face and rest; controls exponential leakage from KKT multipliers.  
  Quick check: "How is the support gap Δ defined in terms of the dual variables of the Euclidean projection problem?"

- **Concept: Paged KV cache.**  
  Why needed: Modern serving stores KV in fixed-size pages; routing at page granularity reduces IO overhead and enables constant-time selection.

## Architecture Onboarding

**Component map:**  
Query scorer -> Page router -> Token router -> Convex solver -> Fused gather kernel

**Critical path:**  
1. Compute query vector from model.  
2. Compute page scores, select top P pages.  
3. For tokens in selected pages, compute token scores, select top K_c.  
4. Run convex solver on candidate tokens to obtain attention weights.  
5. Fused kernel reads K_c value vectors, multiplies by weights, accumulates output.  
6. Return output to model.

**Design tradeoffs:**  
- P and K_c: Larger improves quality but increases latency linearly.  
- Temperature ε: Smaller ε increases sparsity but may cause numerical instability; tightens on-face error bound.  
- Routing heuristic: Coarse page summaries trade accuracy for speed; more sophisticated summaries may improve recall.

**Failure signatures:**  
- High Δ diagnostic (small bΔ) indicates unstable active face; expect quality drop or need larger candidate set.  
- Latency spikes if routing selects too many pages/tokens due to poor summaries.  
- Solver not converged (large duality gap gt); monitor convergence, increase candidate set or iterations.

**First 3 experiments:**  
1. Synthetic test: Generate keys with known active face and Δ; verify off-face mass decays as exp(−Δ/ε) and candidate recall for constant P, K_c.  
2. Scaling test: Measure decode latency vs context length on Llama-3-8B with fixed P,K_c; confirm near-constant time up to large T.  
3. Diagnostic correlation: Compute bΔ per query and correlate with quality metrics (perplexity) and with whether sparse matches dense output.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- The paper does not specify the exact mechanism for page summary computation; future work could explore efficient summary representations that maintain high recall while minimizing overhead.
- Hyperparameter choices (temperature ε, number of solver iterations, candidate set sizes P and K_c) are not thoroughly analyzed; understanding their impact on quality-latency trade-offs remains open.
- The theoretical guarantees rely on the support gap Δ>0 and a well-conditioned active face; how often these conditions hold in practice for diverse models and datasets is an open question.
- Extending the exponential concentration results to more general attention formulations beyond Euclidean projection onto the convex hull is an open theoretical direction.
- The scalability of Vashista Sparse Attention to larger models (e.g., 70B parameters) and longer contexts (beyond 128k tokens) is not evaluated and could reveal new challenges.

## Limitations
- Page routing scoring and page summary details are not specified, affecting recall and latency.
- Hyperparameters (temperature ε, solver iterations) and system configuration missing; they impact results.
- Theoretical assumptions (Δ>0, well-conditioned active face) may not hold uniformly in practice, necessitating fallback to dense attention.
