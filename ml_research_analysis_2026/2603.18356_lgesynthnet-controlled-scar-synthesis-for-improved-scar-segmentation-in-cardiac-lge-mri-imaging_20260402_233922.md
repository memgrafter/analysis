# LGESynthNet: Controlled Scar Synthesis for Improved Scar Segmentation in Cardiac LGE-MRI Imaging

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2603.18356
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.18356
- **Reference count:** 0
- **Primary result:** Augmenting scar segmentation models with LGESynthNet's high-fidelity synthetic LGE-MRI images improves Dice similarity coefficient by up to 6 points and patient-level detection accuracy by up to 20 points.

## Executive Summary
LGESynthNet tackles the severe scarcity of pixel-level annotations for myocardial scar segmentation in late gadolinium enhancement (LGE) MRI by generating synthetic images with precisely controllable scar morphology. It uses a latent diffusion inpainting model conditioned on a scar edge map, a masked negative LGE image, and an anatomically descriptive caption. The model integrates reward-based supervision from a pre-trained segmentation model and a biomedical text encoder (BiomedBERT), all trained on just 429 images from 79 patients. By augmenting segmentation training data with these synthetics, downstream model performance improves significantly, demonstrating a viable path for data augmentation in domains with extremely limited pixel-level labels.

## Method Summary
LGESynthNet synthesizes scar tissue in LGE-MRI via a ControlNet-augmented Latent Diffusion Model (LDM) formulated as an inpainting task. Given a "negative" LGE image (scar region masked with mean intensity) and a corresponding scar edge map (from Canny detection of the ground-truth mask), the model generates only the high-intensity scar texture within the masked region, using the surrounding native image as anatomical context. This spatial conditioning is complemented by text captions describing scar location and transmurality (e.g., "Transmural enhancement in the posteroseptal wall"), encoded by BiomedBERT. A pre-trained scar segmentation "reward model" provides a fixed supervisory signal during early diffusion steps, aligning the generated scar with the conditioning mask. Generated images are filtered by the reward model (Dice > 0.6) before being used to augment segmentation model training.

## Key Results
- Downstream scar segmentation Dice coefficient improved by up to **6 points** when models were trained with LGESynthNet synthetics versus real data only.
- Patient-level scar detection accuracy improved by up to **20 points** (balanced accuracy) with synthetic augmentation.
- The quality control filter retained only **~18%** of generated samples (Dice > 0.6), indicating that precise conditioning remains challenging even with the proposed mechanisms.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1: Reward-based conditioning supervision
- **Claim:** A frozen pre-trained scar segmentation model (reward model) provides a segmentation loss during early diffusion steps to improve fidelity of generated scar morphology to the specified size, location, and transmural extent.
- **Mechanism:** For a generated image, the reward model predicts a scar mask, which is compared to the ground-truth conditioning mask via cross-entropy. This loss is combined with the standard diffusion loss but applied only for diffusion timesteps *t* < *t*<sub>thresh</sub> (when structure is malleable). This injects task-specific supervision without expensive multi-step backpropagation through the generative process.
- **Core assumption:** The reward model, despite limited training data, provides a meaningful supervisory signal correlating with clinically valid scar morphology.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [section 3.1]: Explicit loss formulation combining diffusion and reward losses.
  - [section 4.3]: Reward model reused as quality filter (Dice > 0.6) confirms its utility for post-hoc validation.
- **Break condition:** If the reward model is poorly calibrated or biased, its signal could misguide generation, harming realism or causing mode collapse.

### Mechanism 2: BiomedBERT with anatomically descriptive captions
- **Claim:** Captions generated from clinical anatomical knowledge (AHA segments, radial layers) and encoded with a biomedical language model (BiomedBERT) enhance the model's semantic understanding and spatial coherence of scar placement.
- **Mechanism:** For each real [LGE image, scar mask] pair, a rule-based caption (e.g., "Subendocardial enhancement in the inferior wall") is generated by analyzing mask overlap with predefined anatomical maps. BiomedBERT encodes this text, and its embedding is fused into the diffusion U-Net, providing high-level semantic guidance that complements the low-level edge map.
- **Core assumption:** Domain-specific text embeddings grounded in anatomical templates effectively steer generation toward clinically plausible patterns.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [section 3.1]: Caption generation module using AHA/radial templates.
  - [section 4.2, Table 3b]: Ablation shows removing captions or using OpenCLIP instead of BiomedBERT degrades image quality metrics (SSIM/RMSE).
- **Break condition:** Systematic errors in automatic anatomical labeling (e.g., misassigned AHA segments) could poison the text conditioning, leading to implausible scar locations.

### Mechanism 3: Inpainting with ControlNet for data efficiency
- **Claim:** Framing generation as inpainting (synthesizing scar only within a masked region of a negative LGE image) using a ControlNet architecture enables high-fidelity controlled generation from a very small dataset (429 images).
- **Mechanism:** The pre-trained Stable Diffusion backbone is frozen. A trainable ControlNet module injects the conditioning: a scar edge map (spatial location) and the masked negative LGE image (background context). This adapter learns to modulate the backbone's features while the backbone's prior knowledge is preserved, drastically reducing required training data.
- **Core assumption:** The inpainting formulation strongly constrains the problem—the background is known and realistic—so the model only needs to learn plausible scar texture insertion.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract]: "Formulated as inpainting using a ControlNet-based architecture."
  - [section 2, 3.1]: Explicit description of inpainting setup and ControlNet usage.
- **Break condition:** Assumes the masked negative image is artifact-free and coherent. Existing pathology or noise in the background would lead to blending artifacts. The approach is also inherently limited to adding scar, not removing or altering existing scar.

## Foundational Learning
- **Concept: Latent Diffusion Models (LDMs)**
  - *Why needed:* LGESynthNet operates in a compressed latent space (via VQ-VAE), enabling data-efficient training and faster inference compared to pixel-space diffusion.
  - *Quick check:* Why might latent space allow training with fewer images than pixel-space diffusion, and what is a common trade-off?
- **Concept: ControlNet for Conditional Generation**
  - *Why needed:* The spatial conditioning (edge map, masked image) is injected via ControlNet, which locks the large pre-trained LDM backbone and trains only a small adapter, making fine-tuning on 429 images feasible.
  - *Quick check:* What is the purpose of "zero convolution" layers in ControlNet, and how do they prevent catastrophic forgetting?
- **Concept: Biomedical Language Models (BiomedBERT)**
  - *Why needed:* BiomedBERT, pre-trained on biomedical literature, is expected to better represent and relate cardiac anatomical terms (e.g., "posteroseptal", "transmural") than general CLIP, improving text conditioning fidelity.
  - *Quick check:* How might a general-domain text encoder fail to capture the spatial relationships implied by cardiac descriptors, and why would BiomedBERT be better?

## Architecture Onboarding
Pre-trained LDM Backbone <- ControlNet Encoder
BiomedBERT Text Encoder -> LDM
Caption Generator -> BiomedBERT
Reward Model -> (Training Loss & Quality Filter)

- **Critical path:**
  - *Training:* Real [LGE+, mask] -> Caption Generator + (Mask -> Canny Edge + Masked Negative LGE) -> ControlNet+LDM+Text generate image -> Reward model predicts mask -> Compute diffusion loss + reward loss (t<200) for ControlNet update.
  - *Inference:* Desired scar mask -> Edge + Masked Negative LGE + Generated caption -> ControlNet+LDM+Text generate synthetic -> Quality filter (Reward model Dice>0.6) -> Use for segmentation augmentation.
- **Design tradeoffs:**
  - *Data Efficiency vs. Fidelity:* Inpainting + ControlNet enables training on 429 images but latent compression causes lower SSIM vs. pixel-space GANs.
  - *Conditioning Strength vs. Diversity:* Strong conditioning (edge map, reward loss) improves alignment but may reduce texture diversity.
  - *Automatic Captioning vs. Ground Truth:* Scalable but assumes perfect anatomical parsing; errors propagate to text conditioning.
- **Failure signatures:**
  - Low conditioning Dice (scar doesn't match edge map): likely insufficient reward supervision or ControlNet failure.
  - Unrealistic scar texture or incoherent background: latent artifacts or overfitting to limited data.
  - Very low quality filter pass rate (~18%): reward model too strict or generation fundamentally misaligned.
  - Training instability or mode collapse: reward loss dominance or lack of caption dropout.
- **First 3 experiments:**
  1. **Baseline Validation of Synthetic Quality:** Generate from 149 real positive images (using real masks as condition). Compare SSIM, RMSE, conditioning Dice to baselines (SPADE, vanilla ControlNet, template-guided DM) to confirm improved balance of realism and conditioning.
  2. **Quality Control Filter Tuning:** Generate a large batch from negative images. Analyze Dice distribution of reward model predictions and pass rate. Visualize passed vs. failed samples to establish selection reliability.
  3. **Downstream Segmentation Augmentation:** Train a DenseNet121 scar segmentor on (a) real data only, (b) real + 300 synthetics from each generator (SPADE-LC, SPADE-FC, LGESynthNet). Evaluate Dice, TP-Dice, and patient-level accuracy on held-out test set (53 patients) to measure clinical utility gain.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
### Open Question 1
- **Question:** How well does LGESynthNet generalize to unseen multi-center, multi-vendor LGE-MRI datasets with varying acquisition protocols and patient demographics?
- **Basis in paper:** The authors state in the Discussion: "Future work includes evaluation of the generalization of the model and expanding the data to multi-center, multi-vendor cohorts." The study uses single-center data from 1.5T scanners.
- **Why unresolved:** Model trained and evaluated on a single-center dataset; robustness to scanner differences, field strengths, and diverse populations remains untested.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** External validation on multi-center LGE-MRI datasets (e.g., EMIDEC challenge), measuring drops in conditioning fidelity, synthetic quality, and downstream segmentation performance.

### Open Question 2
- **Question:** Does the reward model's supervision effectiveness depend on its training data size and architecture, and can it reliably guide generation for subtle scar features when trained on limited annotations?
- **Basis in paper:** Authors note: "selecting an effective reward model is challenging, particularly in the domain of LGE segmentation, where large-scale, generalizable models are not readily available." They used a segmentation model trained on their limited real data (429 images) as a fixed reward.
- **Why unresolved:** No exploration of how reward model capacity or training scale affects LGESynthNet's performance; limitations of the small-data reward model may constrain conditioning fidelity.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Systematic ablations varying reward model architecture (e.g., larger U-Nets, transformers) and training set size (e.g., 100, 200, 429 images), measuring changes in conditioning Dice and downstream utility.

### Open Question 3
- **Question:** Can the framework generate clinically realistic, non-ellipsoidal scar patterns (e.g., irregular borders, branching, subendocardial vs. transmural variations) and does this improve segmentation model robustness?
- **Basis in paper:** Authors state: "in this work, we have prompted the DM with simple, elliptical scar shapes. Future work involves exploring more realistic clinical patterns of LGE occurrence." Synthetic masks are generated from random ellipsoids.
- **Why unresolved:** Ellipsoidal masks are a simplification; real scars exhibit complex morphologies. Impact of shape realism on downstream performance is unknown.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Replace parametric ellipsoid generation with shape models derived from real scar annotations (e.g., statistical shape models), compare downstream Dice/detection accuracy against current ellipsoid-based synthetics.

### Open Question 4
- **Question:** What alternative metrics beyond Dice-based quality control best quantify conditioning fidelity for sparse, small structures like myocardial scars, considering spatial precision and texture plausibility?
- **Basis in paper:** The Discussion notes: "alignment is difficult to quantify (location, texture etc.), making downstream performance a critical benchmark." Their quality filter uses Dice > 0.6, which assesses rough shape overlap but not fine details.
- **Why unresolved:** Dice may not capture misalignments in scar location, border sharpness, or texture heterogeneity; no alternatives are validated.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Develop/validate new metrics: boundary-based distances (Hausdorff), texture dissimilarity (GLCM, LPIPS) within scar regions, or clinician assessments correlated with downstream utility.

## Limitations
- **Reward model specification unknown:** Exact architecture and training details (beyond "DenseNet121") are missing, hindering replication of its supervisory signal.
- **Inference input sources unclear:** Provenance of "negative LGE images" for generation (real scar-negative patients vs. synthetic) is unspecified, affecting clinical applicability.
- **Captioning implementation inaccessible:** Details of AHA/radial layer parsing and template population (from cited [9]) are unavailable, risking anatomical mislabeling.
- **Hyperparameter transparency missing:** Key training details (reward loss weight λ, exact ControlNet hyperparameters) are not provided, impeding faithful reproduction.

## Confidence
- **High:** Ablation results (Table 3b) demonstrating contribution of descriptive captions and BiomedBERT encoder.
- **Medium:** Logical soundness of core mechanisms (reward supervision, inpainting formulation) based on paper's own evaluations.
- **Low:** Generalizability beyond the single-center 79-patient dataset; no external validation reported.
