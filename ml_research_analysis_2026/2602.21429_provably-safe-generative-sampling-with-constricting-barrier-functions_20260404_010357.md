# Provably Safe Generative Sampling with Constricting Barrier Functions

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2602.21429
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.21429
- **Reference count:** 19
- **Primary result:** A modular safety filter for flow-based generative models that provably guarantees final samples lie within a user-specified safe set via a constricting Control Barrier Function tube, while minimally perturbing the learned distribution and achieving 100% constraint satisfaction in experiments.

## Executive Summary
This paper tackles the absence of formal safety guarantees in flow-based generative models (e.g., diffusion, flow matching) by introducing a plug-in safety filtering framework. The method defines a time-varying "safety tube" that starts relaxed at the initial noise distribution and progressively tightens to the target safe set, mirroring the coarse-to-fine structure of the generative process. At each sampling step, a convex Quadratic Program synthesizes a minimal-norm control input that enforces a Control Barrier Function condition, ensuring the state remains within the tube. Theoretical results prove that the final sample is guaranteed to lie in the safe set and that the greedy control minimizes the per-step Kullback–Leibler divergence between the safe and original distributions. Empirical evaluations on physics-consistent trajectories, pixel-constrained images, and robotic policy generation show perfect constraint satisfaction while preserving semantic fidelity and incurring only ~34% inference overhead.

## Method Summary
The approach augments any pre-trained flow-based model (specified by drift $f_\theta(x,t)$ and noise scale $g(t)$) with a safety shield. A constricting barrier function $\tilde{h}(x,t) = h(x) + \epsilon(t)$ is defined, where $h(x)$ characterizes the safe set $C = \{x \mid h(x) \geq 0\}$ and $\epsilon(t)$ linearly decreases from $\epsilon_0 = \max(0, -h(x_T)) + c$ (ensuring initial sample is inside the tube) to zero. During reverse-time sampling, for each step $t_k$, the current state $x_k$, a noise sample $\xi_k \sim \mathcal{N}(0,I)$, and the model's drift are used to solve a Quadratic Program: $\min \frac{1}{2} \|u\|^2$ subject to $\tilde{h}_k + \nabla\tilde{h}_k \cdot (-[f_\theta + u]\Delta t + g(t_k)\sqrt{\Delta t} \cdot \xi_k) - \dot{\epsilon}\Delta t \geq (1 - \gamma(\tilde{h}_k)\Delta t)\tilde{h}_k$. The optimal $u^*$ is applied, and the state is updated via an Euler–Maruyama step. This greedy intervention concentrates control when noise is high (intervention cheap) and the safety tube is loose, aligning with the generative model’s coarse-to-fine progression.

## Key Results
- **Theoretical guarantees:** Theorem 4.1 ensures the final sample lies in the safe set $C$ under continuous dynamics; Theorem 4.2 shows the minimum-norm control minimizes the instantaneous contribution to the KL divergence between the safe and learned distributions.
- **Empirical performance:** 100% constraint satisfaction across all tested domains (Lorenz trajectories, pixel-constrained images, smooth robotic policies), matching the task performance of unconstrained models (e.g., 0.92 mean reward on Push-T).
- **Efficiency:** Requires no retraining; adds approximately 34% overhead to inference time due to the QP solve.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1: Constricting Tube Aligns with Coarse-to-Fine Generation
The safety tube $\tilde{C}(t) = \{x \mid \tilde{h}(x,t) \geq 0\}$ starts wide (large $\epsilon$) at high noise and shrinks to $C$ at $t=0$. This exploits the inherent coarse-to-fine structure of flow-based models, where global structure forms under high noise and fine details under low noise. Interventions are therefore most frequent when the generative process is less sensitive to perturbations, minimizing disruption to semantic content. Evidence is drawn from the paper’s abstraction and Section 3.1. The mechanism breaks down if the generative model determines fine details early, mistiming interventions.

### Mechanism 2: Minimum-Norm QP Provides Provable Safety with Minimal Effort
At each step, the convex QP (single linear constraint) computes the smallest $u$ that enforces the discrete-time CBF condition. For any finite noise realization $\xi$, the QP is feasible unless $\nabla\tilde{h} \approx 0$ on the boundary (violating regularity). The closed-form solution applies control only when the unconstrained drift would violate the barrier, pointing opposite $\nabla\tilde{h}$. This yields exact safety for each trajectory in continuous time, with minimal per-step norm. Evidence is in Section 4 and 4.2. Infeasibility may arise from unbounded $\xi$ (probability zero under Gaussian) or non-smooth barriers.

### Mechanism 3: Greedy Control Minimizes Instantaneous KL Divergence
The KL divergence between the safe and original distributions is given by $D_{KL} = \frac{1}{2} \mathbb{E}\left[\int_0^T \frac{\|u\|^2}{g(t)^2} dt\right]$ (Theorem 4.2). The greedy $u^*$ minimizes the integrand pointwise. Since $g(t)$ is large early in the reverse process, the cost of control in KL terms is cheapest then, and the loose tube allows cheap interventions. This aligns minimal distributional shift with maximal tolerance. Evidence is in Section 4 and Theorem 4.2. The greedy approach may not achieve global KL optimality, and if $g(t)$ is small or zero (e.g., deterministic ODEs), the advantage diminishes.

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component map:** Pre-trained flow-based model ($f_\theta$, $g(t)$) -> Constricting CBF wrapper (computes $\tilde{h}(x,t)$) -> QP solver (CVXPY/OSQP) -> Discrete-time integrator (Euler–Maruyama).  

**Critical path:**  
1. Sample terminal state $x_K$ from initial noise distribution.  
2. For $k = K, \dots, 1$:  
   a. Sample noise $\xi_k \sim \mathcal{N}(0,I)$.  
   b. Compute $\tilde{h}_k = h(x_k) + \epsilon(x_T, t_k)$ and its gradient $\nabla\tilde{h}_k$.  
   c. Solve the QP for control $u_k^*$.  
   d. Update $x_{k-1} = x_k - f_\theta(x_k,t_k)\Delta t - u_k^*\Delta t + g(t_k)\sqrt{\Delta t} \cdot \xi_k$.  
   e. Decrement $t_k \to t_{k-1}$.  
3. Return $x_0$ as the final sample.

**Design tradeoffs:**  
- *Constriction schedule:* Linear $\epsilon(t)=\epsilon_0 \cdot t/T$ is simplest; exponential front-loads intervention; polynomial back-loads. Choice affects control effort distribution.  
- *Class-$\mathcal{K}$ function $\gamma$:* Linear $\gamma(h)=\alpha h$ (e.g., $\alpha=0.5$) is used; larger $\alpha$ tightens enforcement but may increase control magnitude.  
- *Barrier $h(x)$:* Must be continuously differentiable ($C^1$). A poorly designed $h$ (e.g., non-smooth, misaligned with model support) increases control effort or causes QP infeasibility.  
- *Step size $\Delta t$:* Larger $\Delta t$ increases linearization error; the paper uses $\Delta t=0.01$ or $0.005$ and adds margin $c>0$ to $\epsilon_0$ to compensate.

**Failure signatures:**  
- Final sample $x_0 \notin C$: indicates discrete-time linearization error or insufficient $\epsilon_0$ margin. Check barrier curvature vs. $\Delta t$.  
- Excessive $\|u\|$ throughout: suggests $h(x)$ is mis-specified or disjoint from the model's data distribution, or constriction is too aggressive.  
- Solver convergence issues: may arise if $\nabla\tilde{h} \approx 0$ on the boundary (violates regularity) or if multi-pixel QPs are poorly conditioned.  
- Latent diffusion breakdown: constraint satisfaction in pixel space fails because VAE decoder is non-diffeomorphic; $\nabla_z h(D(z))$ may not align with image-space constraint boundary.

**First 3 experiments:**  
1. **Lorenz trajectory generation** (section 5.1): Validates physics adherence by defining $h(x)$ as a barrier on integration error vs. the true ODE. Shows control is front-loaded and $\tilde{h}$ remains non-negative.  
2. **Pixel-level image constraint** (section 5.2.1): Enforces an exact reference patch location in an off-the-shelf DDPM (Bedroom dataset). Demonstrates modular application to a pre-trained model; spatial mask enables seamless blending.  
3. **Robotic policy smoothness** (section 5.3): Applies to Diffusion Policy for the Push-T task. Barrier on action jerk yields 100% satisfaction, matches unconstrained reward (0.92), and incurs 34% inference overhead. Shows constraint enforcement in a structured, high-dimensional action space.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
1. **Automatic construction of CBFs for high-level semantic safety.**  
   *Basis:* Section 6 notes that when safety is ambiguous (e.g., filtering inappropriate content), constructing a suitable barrier is nontrivial; attempts using CLIP or classifiers failed due to misalignment with true safety boundaries.  
   *Why unresolved:* Semantic classifiers do not provide differentiable, formal guarantees on the constraint boundary.  
   *Evidence needed:* A general methodology to derive provably safe barrier functions from high-level intents, validated on real-world constrained generation tasks.

2. **Exact constraint satisfaction for latent diffusion models.**  
   *Basis:* Section 6 explicitly states that extending the approach to latent diffusion yields approximate constraints because the VAE decoder is non-diffeomorphic; achieving exact satisfaction requires either decoder fine-tuning or a hybrid latent-pixel approach.  
   *Why unresolved:* The decoder distorts the constraint boundary, so latent-space CBF enforcement does not transfer exactly to pixel space.  
   *Evidence needed:* An approach (e.g., constraint-aware decoder training or two-stage filtering) with a formal proof that latent barriers imply image-space guarantees, tested on high-resolution generation.

3. **Receding-horizon MPC for globally optimal KL minimization.**  
   *Basis:* Section 6 proposes that using a Model Predictive Control framework over multiple steps could yield tighter KL bounds and globally optimal policies rather than the greedy per-step minimization.  
   *Why unresolved:* The current per-step QP ignores future constriction and noise, potentially leading to suboptimal distributional shift.  
   *Evidence needed:* Implementation of an MPC-guided sampler with theoretical analysis proving a lower bound on KL divergence and empirical comparisons showing reduced shift while maintaining safety.

## Limitations
- **Discretization sensitivity:** Theoretical guarantees assume continuous-time SDE with exact $f_\theta$, $g(t)$ and full noise observability. Large step size $\Delta t$ or high barrier curvature can cause discrete-time violations.
- **CBF regularity requirement:** The condition $\nabla\tilde{h} \neq 0$ near the boundary may fail for non-smooth barriers, leading to QP infeasibility or poor conditioning.
- **Coarse-to-fine assumption mismatch:** The method relies on the generative process establishing global structure early; if fine details are determined early, interventions may be mistimed and more disruptive.
- **Latent-space models:** For VAE-based latent diffusion, the non-diffeomorphic decoder breaks the geometric correspondence needed for exact pixel-space constraint satisfaction.

## Confidence
- **Safety guarantee (Theorem 4.1):** Medium – Provable under ideal continuous dynamics; empirical success depends on discretization choice and barrier design.
- **KL optimality (Theorem 4.2):** Medium – Continuous-time pointwise optimality is established; greedy discrete implementation may not achieve the global KL bound.
- **Empirical results:** High – Paper reports 100% constraint satisfaction across three diverse tasks; reproducibility hinges on missing implementation details (e.g., exact drift/noise conversion).

## Next Checks
1. **Discretization study:** Systematically vary the number of diffusion steps $K$ (i.e., $\Delta t$) on the Lorenz and image tasks. Measure the rate of final-sample constraint violations; larger $\Delta t$ should degrade safety, confirming sensitivity to linearization error.
2. **QP feasibility under extreme noise:** Sample noise $\xi$ from a heavy-tailed distribution (e.g., Cauchy) to stress-test the QP. Track infeasibility instances against $\|\nabla\tilde{h}\|$; if failures concentrate near zero gradient, test mitigation via jitter or adjusting $\gamma$.
3. **Latent diffusion transfer:** Implement the method on a VAE-based latent diffusion model. Approximate the pixel-space barrier gradient via finite differences in latent space (i.e., $\nabla_z h(D(z))$). Compare constraint success rate and sample fidelity against a pixel-space application of the same barrier to quantify the distortion introduced by the decoder.
