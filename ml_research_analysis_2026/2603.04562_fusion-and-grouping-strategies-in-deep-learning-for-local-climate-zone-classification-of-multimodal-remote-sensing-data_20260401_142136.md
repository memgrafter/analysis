# Fusion and Grouping Strategies in Deep Learning for Local Climate Zone Classification of Multimodal Remote Sensing Data
## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2603.04562
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.04562
- **Reference count:** 40
- **Primary result:** A hybrid CNN fusion model (FM1) combined with spectral band grouping (BG) and label merging (LM) achieves 76.6% overall accuracy on the So2Sat LCZ42 benchmark, outperforming state-of-the-art methods.

## Executive Summary
This study systematically analyzes fusion and grouping strategies for deep learning-based Local Climate Zone (LCZ) classification using multimodal Sentinel-1 SAR and Sentinel-2 multispectral data. The authors compare four CNN-based fusion architectures operating at pixel, feature, and decision levels, and evaluate two supplementary strategies: spectral band grouping (BG) to reduce input redundancy and label merging (LM) to mitigate class imbalance. The best-performing configuration, FM1 with BG and LM (FM1BL), reaches 76.6% overall accuracy on the So2Sat LCZ42 test set. The results demonstrate that a hybrid fusion approach, which combines raw data concatenation with high-level feature interaction, benefits significantly from preprocessing strategies that enhance feature discrimination and reduce intra-class confusion, particularly for underrepresented LCZ classes.

## Method Summary
The core method involves comparing four multimodal fusion models: a baseline hybrid fusion model (FM1) that merges pixel-level concatenation with feature-level multiplicative interaction; an attention-enhanced variant (FM2); a multi-scale Gaussian-smoothed variant (FM3); and a weighted decision-level fusion model (FM4). Alongside these, the study applies two strategies: spectral band grouping (SBG), which reorganizes SAR bands by polarization (VH, VV, CMOE) and MSI bands by spectral range (RGB, VRE, SWIR, NIR) into separate processing branches; and label merging (LM), which consolidates the original 17 LCZ classes into 8 broader categories based on surface albedo and semantic similarity. Ablation experiments isolate the contributions of each fusion level and the supplementary strategies. Models are trained on the So2Sat LCZ42 dataset (32x32 pixel patches) using Adam (lr=0.0001), categorical cross-entropy, and evaluated with overall accuracy, F1-score, Kappa, and Matthews Correlation Coefficient.

## Key Results
- The FM1BL configuration (hybrid fusion + band grouping + label merging) achieves 76.6% overall accuracy, setting a new state-of-the-art on the So2Sat LCZ42 benchmark.
- Band grouping (BG) and label merging (LM) both contribute to improved accuracy, with LM being particularly effective at reducing misclassifications among spectrally similar LCZ classes.
- The hybrid fusion model (FM1) outperforms purely pixel-level, feature-level, or decision-level fusion, demonstrating the benefit of multi-abstraction-level information integration.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1: Hybrid Fusion
Hybrid fusion (pixel-level concatenation + feature-level multiplication) improves accuracy by capturing both low-level raw sensor data and high-level modality-specific features, exploiting complementary information from SAR and MSI. Evidence shows FM1 consistently outperforms other fusion levels in ablation studies.
### Mechanism 2: Spectral Band Grouping
Grouping bands by polarization (SAR) and spectral range (MSI) reduces redundancy and noise, leading to more discriminative feature extraction. This is evidenced by performance gains when BG is applied to FM1, particularly for minority classes.
### Mechanism 3: Label Merging
Merging 17 LCZ classes into 8 broader categories reduces intra-class confusion by absorbing errors between semantically adjacent classes (e.g., compact built types 1–3), directly addressing class imbalance and improving metrics like MCC. Confusion matrices show clearer class separation post-merging.

## Architecture Onboarding
Data -> Band grouping -> Parallel branches: Pixel-level path (concatenated groups -> conv -> ReLU -> batch norm -> dropout -> GAP) and Feature-level paths (per-group CNN blocks -> modality concatenation -> element-wise multiplication -> conv -> pooling -> batch norm -> ReLU -> GAP) -> Hybrid concatenation -> Dense (64) -> Softmax -> Loss.
- **Critical path:** Input bands are first grouped (SBG). Then, two parallel processing streams run: one for pixel-level fused data, another for separately extracted SAR and MSI features that are multiplied. Their pooled outputs are concatenated and classified.
- **Design tradeoffs:** Hybrid fusion balances raw data integration (pixel-level) with denoised feature interaction (feature-level). Band grouping reduces parameters but risks losing subtle spectral differences. Label merging boosts minority-class performance at the cost of fine-grained distinctions. Attention (FM2) and multi-scale (FM3) increase capacity but showed overfitting.
- **Failure signatures:** Overfitting (train acc >> val acc) suggests insufficient regularization. Poor minority-class recall indicates unresolved imbalance. Band grouping hurting performance may imply suboptimal groups. If fine classes are needed, LM is inappropriate.
- **First 3 experiments:** 1) Train FM1 without BG/LM (17 classes) to establish hybrid baseline. 2) Train FM1 with BG only to isolate grouping effect. 3) Train FM1 with both BG and LM (FM1BL) to replicate the top result.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
1. Can a mixture of experts (MoE) mechanism enhance hybrid fusion and multi-branch models for LCZ classification, especially for underrepresented classes? (Conclusion explicitly states this as future work.)
2. What architectural or training adjustments are needed to optimize attention-based (FM2) and decision-level fusion (FM4) models, given their theoretical strengths but empirical underperformance? (Authors note these require special considerations.)
3. Does replacing static Gaussian kernel sizes in FM3 with adaptive or learnable multi-scale representations improve generalization and accuracy? (Authors suggest adaptive scales could improve FM3.)
4. To what extent do the proposed strategies generalize to other multimodal remote sensing datasets or LCZ benchmarks beyond So2Sat LCZ42? (The study is exclusively on So2Sat LCZ42.)

## Limitations
- Architectural details (exact layer depths, FM4’s U-Net/CNN specifications) are unspecified, hindering exact replication.
- Key hyperparameters (FM4’s α weight, Gaussian kernel σ interpretation) are ambiguously reported.
- Results are confined to the So2Sat LCZ42 dataset; cross-dataset generalizability is unverified.
- Label merging reduces class granularity (17→8), which may be unacceptable for applications requiring fine distinctions.

## Confidence
- **Hybrid fusion (FM1) outperforms other fusion levels:** Medium. Supported by ablation comparisons, but exact contribution magnitudes are not fully quantified and may be dataset-dependent.
- **Spectral band grouping (BG) improves performance:** Medium. Plausible mechanism with reported gains, but not compared against alternative grouping schemes; risk of discarding useful spectral nuances.
- **Label merging (LM) reduces intra-class misclassification:** High. Confusion matrices directly show improved separation of merged classes, though at the cost of resolution.
