# LongCat-Next: Lexicalizing Modalities as Discrete Tokens

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID**: 2603.27538
- **Source URL**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.27538
- **Reference count**: 40
- **Primary result**: LongCat-Next unifies language, vision, and audio under a single next-token prediction objective via the Discrete Native Autoregressive (DiNA) framework, achieving competitive performance across understanding (MMMU 70.6), generation (GenEval 84.44), and audio (MMAU 76.4) benchmarks.

## Executive Summary
LongCat-Next introduces the Discrete Native Autoregressive (DiNA) paradigm to address fragmentation in multimodal systems. It treats vision, audio, and text as discrete token sequences processed by a modality-agnostic Mixture-of-Experts (MoE) backbone under a unified next-token prediction loss. Key to its design is the dNaViT tokenizer, which uses Residual Vector Quantization (RVQ) and semantically aligned encoders to convert images at any resolution into hierarchical tokens without sacrificing information. Trained on over 2T tokens, the model reconciles understanding and generation within one framework, matching or exceeding specialized models on diverse benchmarks and demonstrating that discrete modeling can overcome prior performance ceilings.

## Method Summary
LongCat-Next employs DiNA: modality-specific tokenizer/detokenizer pairs convert continuous signals (images, audio) into discrete IDs and back. Vision uses dNaViT (SAE + hierarchical RVQ) for any-resolution tokenization; audio uses a Whisper encoder plus RVQ; text uses standard subword tokenization. All IDs are embedded into a shared space and processed autoregressively by a modality-agnostic MoE decoder-only backbone (LongCat-Flash-Lite A3B, ~68.5B total, ~3B active). Training occurs in four stages: (1) pre-align to warm up codebooks and the DepthTransformer with a frozen LLM; (2) full multimodal pre-training on 2T tokens of interleaved data; (3) mid-training cluster rebalancing for the generation branch; (4) instruction tuning. This eliminates modality-specific branches and enables both discriminative and generative tasks via the same objective.

## Key Results
- Vision understanding: MMMU 70.6, MathVista 83.1, OCRBench 86.5, surpassing Qwen3-VL-A3B.
- Generation: GenEval 84.44, TIFF 82.85/84.38, competitive with Flux.
- Audio: MMAU 76.4, TTS WER <2%.
- Demonstrates discrete modeling can achieve state-of-the-art results in both understanding and generation within a unified framework.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1
- **Claim**: The Discrete Native Autoregressive (DiNA) paradigm unifies language, vision, and audio under a single next-token prediction objective by representing all modalities as discrete token sequences, eliminating fragmented architectures and enabling architectural synergy.
- **Mechanism**: Modality-specific tokenizer/detokenizer pairs convert continuous signals into hierarchical discrete IDs and back. These IDs are embedded into a shared space and processed by a modality-agnostic MoE decoder-only backbone, which predicts the next token across any modality. Understanding and generation become inverse sequence modeling within the same discrete space.
- **Core assumption**: Discrete tokens can preserve sufficient semantic information (semantic completeness) for both discriminative tasks (e.g., VQA) and generative tasks (e.g., image synthesis) without modality-specific branches.
- **Evidence anchors**: [abstract] "DiNA… represents multimodal information within a shared discrete token space, enabling consistent autoregressive modeling across modalities… minimal modality-specific design." [section 2.1] "We adopt a structural decomposition: modality-specific tokenizer and detokenizer pairs… the decoder-only backbone remains modality-agnostic." [corpus] Neighbor work "NextFlow" and "Orthus" also explore unified discrete autoregressive modeling, but LongCat-Next emphasizes industrial-scale training and any-resolution capability.
- **Break condition**: Irrecoverable information loss from quantization causes catastrophic forgetting or performance ceilings in either domain.

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim**: The Discrete Native Any-resolution Vision Transformer (dNaViT) overcomes the discrete vision performance ceiling by ensuring semantic completeness through Semantic-and-Aligned Encoders (SAE) and hierarchical Residual Vector Quantization (RVQ), preserving both high-level semantics and fine-grained details at arbitrary resolutions.
- **Mechanism**: SAE (e.g., Qwen2.5-ViT) extracts semantically aligned features. RVQ applies L cascaded codebooks to quantize residuals instead of a single step, minimizing discretization error. Multi-level tokens are summed for embedding and decoded in parallel by a DepthTransformer, enabling any-resolution tokenization/de-tokenization with up to 28× compression.
- **Core assumption**: Semantic completeness—the discrete representation must approximate the posterior P(answer|image) for any vision task—is achievable if the pre-quantization encoder retains mutual information with the original image, and RVQ approximates the continuous manifold with low distortion.
- **Evidence anchors**: [section 2.2.1] "Semantic completeness… P(A|z,Q)≈ P(A|I,Q)… Discriminative Invariance [and] Generative Sufficiency." [section 2.2.3] "We employ RVQ… decomposes quantization into L cascaded codebook levels… hierarchical refinement… preserving information for both understanding and generation." [section 3.2.2] Empirical reconstruction analysis shows even randomly initialized ViTs retain low-level information via residual pathways, supporting the architectural information recovery claim.
- **Break condition**: Insufficient RVQ codebook size or depth accumulates quantization error, degrading either OCR-level detail (generation) or logical reasoning (understanding); any-resolution flexibility breaks if tokenizer cannot generalize beyond training resolutions.

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim**: A modality-agnostic Mixture-of-Experts (MoE) backbone naturally evolves functional specialization and efficient capacity utilization during native multimodal training, enabling a single model to handle diverse modalities without performance compromise.
- **Mechanism**: The MoE router dynamically allocates tokens to experts. Initially modality-agnostic, experts develop soft preferences for vision, audio, or text as training progresses, while overall expert activation count increases, indicating better capacity usage. This occurs because the discrete token space aligns modalities, allowing the same routing policy to specialize based on token statistics.
- **Core assumption**: Modality tokens, when learned in a unified space, form an interlaced distribution rather than separate clusters, enabling the router to learn cross-modal patterns without explicit modality tags.
- **Evidence anchors**: [section 3.2.5] "After introducing multimodal training, the average number of routed tokens per expert increases from 507.1 to 584.6… a subset of experts gradually becomes functionally specialized." [section 3.2.6] t-SNE visualization shows LongCat-Next tokens are interwoven vs. separated clusters in non-native Qwen2.5-VL, supporting the Platonic representation hypothesis. [corpus] "Orthus" uses modality-specific heads, while LongCat-Next’s agnostic MoE achieves competitive performance with fewer inductive biases.
- **Break condition**: Modality tokens remain in separate embedding subspaces, router overfits to modality-specific patterns, causing expert collapse or interference; training instability arises if data mixtures are highly imbalanced.

## Foundational Learning
- **Autoregressive Next-Token Prediction (NTP)**: The core paradigm extending language modeling to multimodality. *Why needed*: DiNA extends NTP to non-text modalities, using a single loss for both understanding and generation. *Quick check*: How does predicting the next token in a mixed modality sequence implicitly train both comprehension (e.g., answering from an image) and generation (e.g., synthesizing an image from text)?
- **Residual Vector Quantization (RVQ)**: Hierarchical quantization of residuals across multiple codebooks. *Why needed*: RVQ is the core discretization technique that minimizes information loss in vision tokenization. Without understanding its hierarchical nature, the advantage over single-stage VQ and the design of multi-level tokens is unclear. *Quick check*: Why does quantizing residuals across multiple codebook levels preserve more information than a single quantization step, and how does this support semantic completeness?
- **Mixture-of-Experts (MoE) routing**: Dynamic allocation of tokens to experts. *Why needed*: The backbone’s capacity allocation and emergent specialization depend on MoE dynamics. Engineers must understand how sparsity and routing affect multimodal learning. *Quick check*: In a modality-agnostic MoE, what signals drive expert specialization toward vision vs. audio, and why does activation count increase after multimodal training?

## Architecture Onboarding
Component map: Input (text/image/audio) -> Tokenizers (text: subword; vision: dNaViT; audio: Whisper+RVQ) -> Discrete IDs -> Embedding (summed multi-level vectors) -> Modality-agnostic MoE backbone -> Heads (text: MLP; vision/audio: DepthTransformer) -> De-tokenizers (pixel decoder/flow refiner for vision, vocoder for audio) -> Output modality.

Critical path:
1. Tokenization: Modality input → discrete IDs (any resolution for vision).
2. Embedding lookup → summed multi-level vector.
3. MoE backbone processes cross-modal sequence autoregressively.
4. For generation: at each step, model outputs logits over combined vocabulary; sample token; if visual/audio token, DepthTransformer decodes multi-level IDs; de-tokenizer renders pixels/waveform.
5. For understanding: generate text tokens until EOS.

Design tradeoffs:
- Discrete vs continuous: Discrete enables unification and RL compatibility but risks information loss; mitigated by SAE+RVQ and extended training.
- Any-resolution vs efficiency: Native resolution avoids resizing artifacts but requires variable-length FlashAttention and careful batching.
- MoE capacity: Modality-agnostic design reduces engineering overhead but may need careful data mixing to avoid expert collapse.

Failure signatures:
- Modality boundary errors in input sequences: Missing special tokens (e.g., `<vision>`) cause misalignment and garbled outputs.
- OOM from arbitrary-resolution images: dNaViT handles any resolution but sequence length scales with pixels; high-resolution images (>1.7k) exceed max sequence length (8192 in training).
- Poor generation quality from misaligned embeddings: Using mismatched tokenizer/model checkpoints (e.g., from different training runs) causes semantic misalignment.
- Training instability in RL fine-tuning: Entropy explosion from training-inference mismatch in GRPO; requires sequence-level filtering (Appendix 9.1).

First 3 experiments:
1. Pre-align stage: Freeze LLM backbone, warm up codebook embeddings and DepthTransformer with mixed modality data to align discrete tokens to language space.
2. Full multimodal pre-training: Unfreeze entire model, train on 2T tokens of interleaved text-image-audio data (including OCR, STEM, GUI, speech) with single NTP loss.
3. Mid-training cluster rebalancing: For generation branch, reweight semantic clusters to flatten long-tail distribution, improving concept coverage and text rendering.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- How can non-linguistic modalities be effectively represented within a discrete token space? DiNA demonstrates a solution for vision and audio, but extending this approach to all modalities (e.g., video, tactile) while preserving performance and scalability remains untested.
- Does multimodality introduce capabilities beyond those already captured by language-only models? Current evaluations rely on standard benchmarks; it is unclear whether multimodal training yields genuinely novel abilities (e.g., physical intuition, affordance reasoning) that cannot be acquired by scaling language models alone.
- How can a visual tokenizer and de-tokenizer be designed to achieve both semantic completeness for understanding and high-fidelity pixel reconstruction? The high compression ratio (28×) and frozen semantic encoder limit fine-detail preservation; reconstruction quality is sufficient for recognition but may fall short for generation tasks demanding photographic realism.
- Can the DiNA paradigm be extended to support any‑to‑any multimodal generation and interleaved reasoning across arbitrary input‑output modality combinations? The current system is limited to text–image and text–audio directions; the single next‑token objective may not seamlessly handle many‑to‑many mappings (e.g., audio‑to‑image, image + text‑to‑audio) without architectural adjustments.

## Limitations
- Information loss in discrete vision: Quantization may degrade OCR detail or reasoning at certain RVQ depths, not fully quantified.
- Training data specifics: Corpus composition (percentages of image-text, interleaved, GUI, speech) undisclosed, blocking replication and affecting generalization claims.
- Architecture hyperparameters: Full specs for MoE backbone (layer count, expert dimensions) absent, requiring inference from related models.
- RL fine-tuning instability: Entropy explosion issue acknowledged; sensitive implementation details not fully open-sourced.

## Confidence
- DiNA unification claim: Medium. Strong theoretical framing and architectural separation are described, but break condition (irrecoverable information loss) not experimentally disproven; performance could be due to scale rather than inherent superiority of discrete modeling.
- dNaViT semantic completeness claim: Medium. Evidence includes reconstruction analysis and benchmark results, but lacks ablation of RVQ depth/codebook size on understanding vs. generation tasks. The "any-resolution" generality is asserted but not stress-tested on extreme resolutions.
- Modality-agnostic MoE specialization claim: Medium. Supported by activation statistics and t-SNE visuals, yet risk of expert collapse under data imbalance is noted as break condition without mitigation details beyond mid-training rebalancing.

## Next Checks
1. Test semantic completeness boundary: Using the released dNaViT tokenizer, run reconstruction on ImageNet with a randomly initialized SAE (as in section 3.2.2) and measure rFID/detail preservation at increasing RVQ levels. Expect incremental RVQ to outperform single-stage VQ; failure occurs if rFID plateaus while understanding scores drop on fine-grained VQA subsets.
2. Probe MoE modality separation: Extract expert activation distributions on mixed-modality batches (text+image+audio) from the open-sourced model. Compute the modality bias per expert (fraction of tokens from each modality). Validate that experts show soft preferences but remain interlaced (per section 3.2.6); failure if >80% of experts are >90% modality-specific.
3. Replicate RL entropy filter: Implement a small-scale GRPO run on a multimodal QA task using the provided RL pipeline. Compare sequence entropy distributions with and without the max-probability/sequence-entropy filters (Appendix 9.1). Expect filtered rollouts to reduce policy variance and stabilize training; failure if filters discard >50% of samples or degrade performance.
