# Improved Bounds for Reward-Agnostic and Reward-Free Exploration

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2602.16363
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.16363
- **Reference count:** 40
- **Primary result:** Achieves minimax-optimal high-order sample complexity for both reward-agnostic and reward-free exploration, with improved low-order terms and a tight lower bound for reward-free.

## Executive Summary
This paper studies reward-free and reward-agnostic exploration in finite-horizon tabular Markov Decision Processes (MDPs). The authors propose a meta-algorithm comprising three stages: first, an exploration policy is generated using a single online MDP procedure with adaptively shaped rewards; second, the transition dynamics are estimated from trajectories collected by this policy; third, a pessimistic planning algorithm computes an near-optimal policy for any revealed reward (from a small class for reward-agnostic, arbitrary for reward-free). This unified approach reduces low-order sample complexity terms compared to prior multi-procedure methods. For reward-agnostic exploration, they achieve the minimax-optimal high-order complexity $\mathcal{O}(|S||A|H^3/\varepsilon^2)$ with a smaller low-order term, relaxing constraints on $\varepsilon$. For reward-free exploration, they prove a tight lower bound $\Omega(|S|^2|A|H^3/\varepsilon^2)$, matching existing upper bounds and resolving an open conjecture. The results enhance both practical applicability and theoretical understanding.

## Method Summary
The algorithm operates in three clear stages. Stage 1 (Algorithm 2) constructs an exploration policy $\pi_{\text{exp}}$ by repeatedly solving an online MDP with rewards $\lambda_t(s,a)=1/(c+N_t(s,a))$, where $N_t$ counts past visits. This is done via a single call to an online mirror descent subroutine (Algorithm 5) that maintains a confidence set over transitions and optimizes occupancy measures. Stage 2 (Algorithm 3) collects $N$ additional trajectories using $\pi_{\text{exp}}$ and estimates empirical transitions $\hat{P}_h$. Stage 3 (Algorithm 4) performs pessimistic value iteration with Bernstein-style penalties that depend on visit counts and empirical variance. The sample complexities are $\tilde{\mathcal{O}}(|S||A|H^3/\varepsilon^2)$ for reward-agnostic and $\tilde{\mathcal{O}}(|S|^2|A|H^3/\varepsilon^2)$ for reward-free, where logarithmic factors are absorbed into $\ell_0 = \ln(|S||A|H/\delta)$ (subject to a minor ambiguity). The key innovation is the single online MDP procedure that yields near-uniform coverage with reduced low-order overhead.

## Key Results
- Minimax-optimal high-order sample complexity $\mathcal{O}(|S||A|H^3/\varepsilon^2)$ for reward-agnostic exploration, with a smaller low-order term than prior work.
- Tight lower bound $\Omega(|S|^2|A|H^3/\varepsilon^2)$ for reward-free exploration, matching upper bounds and settling an open conjecture.
- A unified exploration algorithm that replaces multiple independent no-regret calls, leading to simpler analysis and improved practical efficiency.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1
- **Claim:** A single online MDP procedure with adaptive reward shaping replaces multiple independent no-regret subroutine calls, reducing low-order sample complexity while maintaining near-uniform exploration coverage.
- **Mechanism:** Algorithm 2 constructs a sequence of reward functions $\lambda_t(s,a) = 1/(c + N_t(s,a))$ that monotonically increase visitation incentives for under-explored state-action-horizon triples. These rewards are fed to a unified online MDP solver (Algorithm 5, based on Rosenberg & Mansour) using occupancy-measure mirror descent. The average of policies $(1/T)\sum \pi_t$ produced serves as the exploration policy $\pi_{\text{exp}}$.
- **Core assumption:** The online algorithm’s regret $\text{Reg}_{\text{EPA}}(T)$ scales with $\mathcal{O}(|S||A|H \ell^2)$ and the reward sequence $\lambda_t$ sufficiently approximates gradients $-1/\mu_h(s,a)$ of the negative-log-barrier objective $f(\mu) = -\sum \log \mu_h(s,a)$.
- **Evidence anchors:**  
  - Abstract: "This approach significantly reduces low-order sample complexity terms... Our algorithm employs a single online MDP procedure with carefully designed reward functions."  
  - Section 3.2 & 4: Algorithm 2 and Theorem 4.2 explicitly prove that the returned $\pi_{\text{exp}}$ satisfies $\sum_{\omega\text{-significant}} \mu^{\pi}/\mu^{\pi_{\text{exp}}} \le \mathcal{O}(|S||A|H \ell^2)$ and $\sum_{\text{non-significant}} \mu^{\pi} \le \mathcal{O}(\omega|S||A|H \ell^2)$.  
  - Corpus: Neighbor papers (e.g., Li et al. 2024b) use multiple EULER calls, while Zhang et al. 2020 use model-free methods with higher complexity; this paper’s novelty is the single-procedure design.
- **Break condition:** If the online algorithm fails to achieve low regret (e.g., due to poor confidence set construction or non-convex optimization), the coverage guarantee (Theorem 4.2) collapses, leading to insufficient data for dynamics estimation.

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim:** The exploration policy $\pi_{\text{exp}}$ generates data with near-uniform coverage over all $\omega$-significant $(s,a,h)$ triples, enabling high-fidelity transition estimation with $N = \mathcal{O}(|S||A|H^3 \ell^3/\varepsilon^2)$ samples.
- **Mechanism:** Algorithm 3 collects $N$ trajectories using $\pi_{\text{exp}}$. The occupancy guarantee (Theorem 4.2) ensures $N_h(s,a) \ge (1/2) N \cdot \mu^{\pi_{\text{exp}}}_h(s,a)$ for significant triples (Lemma B.1/B.2), which translates into a Bernstein-type uncertainty term in the downstream pessimistic planning (Eq. (15)/(16)) that scales correctly with $1/\sqrt{N_h(s,a)}$.
- **Core assumption:** $\pi_{\text{exp}}$'s coverage property holds with high probability (good event $E_{\text{RAE}}$/$E_{\text{RFE}}$), and the Bernstein penalty constants ($c_a \ge 48$, $c_f \ge 64$) are sufficient to dominate the estimation error from Lemma B.7/B.9.
- **Evidence anchors:**  
  - Section 3.3: Derives that $N = \Theta(H^2|S||A|/\varepsilon^2)$ suffices if $\pi_{\text{exp}}$ satisfies $\sum \mu^{\pi}/\mu^{\pi_{\text{exp}}} \le \mathcal{O}(|S||A|H)$ (Eq. (5) discussion).  
  - Section 5: Theorem 5.1 (RAE) and Theorem 5.2 (RFE) directly tie $N = \mathcal{O}(|S||A|H^3 \ell^3/\varepsilon^2)$ or $\mathcal{O}(|S|^2|A|H^3 \ell^3/\varepsilon^2)$ to the coverage guarantee via Lemma B.8/B.10.  
  - Corpus: Prior work (Jin et al. 2020b; Ménard et al. 2021) also uses policy-cover type exploration, but with higher-order terms; this paper’s improvement stems from a tighter coverage analysis (Lemma 3.3).
- **Break condition:** If $\pi_{\text{exp}}$ fails to visit a significant $(s,a,h)$ with probability $\ge \omega$, the variance term in the pessimistic Bellman error (Eq. (18)/(19)) may be underestimated, causing the policy $\hat{\pi}$ to be suboptimal in those regions.

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim:** Pessimistic policy computation (Algorithm 4) with Bernstein penalties transforms the dynamics estimation error (from Mechanism 2) into a suboptimality bound $\le \varepsilon$ for any reward in the class (RAE) or arbitrary reward (RFE).
- **Mechanism:** Given $\hat{P}_h$, Algorithm 4 performs backward value iteration with $\hat{Q}_h(s,a) = \max\{ r_h(s,a) + \hat{P}_{s,a} \hat{V}_{h+1} - b_h(s,a), 0 \}$. The penalty $b_h$ (Eq. (15)/(16)) uses empirical variance and visit counts to form a high-probability lower confidence bound on $Q^*_h$. This pessimism ensures $V^* - \hat{V} \le 2\sum \mu^{\pi^*}_h b_h(s,a)$ (Lemma B.4).
- **Core assumption:** The Bernstein concentration (Lemma B.7/B.9) holds uniformly over all $(s,a,h)$ on the good event, and the penalty term exactly cancels the dominant error term from the value difference lemma.
- **Evidence anchors:**  
  - Section 3.3: Discusses standard pessimism "discouraging poorly covered state-actions."  
  - Section B: Lemmas B.4 and B.8/B.10 explicitly bound $V^* - \hat{V}$ by terms involving $\sum \mu^{\pi^*}_h / N_h(s,a)$ and constants, which Theorem 4.2 controls.  
  - Corpus: The pessimistic principle is common in offline RL (e.g., Jin et al. 2020a); this paper adapts it to the reward-free/agnostic setting with careful dependency on $|S|$ (RAE: no $|S|$ factor; RFE: tight lower bound matches).
- **Break condition:** If the Bernstein concentration fails (e.g., due to insufficient samples in a "significant" region despite coverage guarantee), the penalty $b_h$ may be too small, allowing $\hat{\pi}$ to overestimate values of poorly estimated transitions and become suboptimal.

## Foundational Learning
- **Concept: Occupancy measures ($\mu^\pi_h(s,a)$)**  
  - Why needed here: The entire analysis is built on occupancy measures (Section 2) as a convex space for optimization. The exploration policy’s quality is defined via ratios $\mu^\pi/\mu^{\pi_{\text{exp}}}$ (Theorem 4.2), and the value difference lemmas (Lemmas B.4, B.8) decompose error in terms of $\mu^{\pi^*}_h$.  
  - Quick check question: How does the linearity of value in occupancy measures ($\mathbb{E}[\sum r] = \sum \mu^\pi_h r_h$) enable reduction to a coverage problem?

- **Concept: Online Mirror Descent (OMD) for occupancy optimization**  
  - Why needed here: Algorithm 5 uses OMD over the convex set $\Lambda(P^t)$ (Eq. (14)) to solve the online MDP problem driving the exploration rewards. The regret decomposition $\text{Reg} = \hat{R}_{\text{Dynamics}} + \hat{R}_{\text{Policy}}$ (Section A.2) isolates the effect of unknown transitions.  
  - Quick check question: What is the intuition behind using the negative entropy regularizer $R(\mu) = \sum \mu \log \mu$ in OMD for this problem, and how does it relate to the log-barrier objective in Eq. (4)?

- **Concept: Bernstein-type concentration for MDPs**  
  - Why needed here: The penalty terms $b_h(s,a)$ (Eqs. (15)/(16)) rely on empirical Bernstein inequalities (Maurer & Pontil 2009; Lemma B.7/B.9) to get tight, variance-dependent confidence intervals. This is critical for avoiding the $|S|$ blowup in RAE vs. RFE.  
  - Quick check question: Why does the RFE penalty include an extra $|S|$ factor (Eq. (16)) compared to RAE (Eq. (15)), and how does this match the lower bound?

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map:**  
  1. `CREATE_EXPLORATION_POLICY` (Alg. 2): Takes $(S,A,H,\varepsilon,\delta)$, returns $\pi_{\text{exp}}$. Internally runs `EXPLORATION-POLICY-ALGO` (Alg. 5) on $\lambda_t$ rewards.  
  2. `ESTIMATE_DYNAMICS` (Alg. 3): Takes $\pi_{\text{exp}}, N$, returns dataset $D$ and $\{\hat{P}_h\}$.  
  3. `PESSIMISTIC_MODEL-BASED_OFFLINE_RL` (Alg. 4): Takes $r, D, \hat{P}$, returns $\hat{\pi}$.

- **Critical path:** Step 1 is foundational—its coverage guarantee (Theorem 4.2) directly determines $N$ in Step 2 and the penalty scale in Step 3. Errors in Step 1 propagate irrecoverably.

- **Design tradeoffs:**  
  - *Single vs. multiple online procedures:* Prior work (Li et al. 2024b) uses multiple EULER instances, incurring $|S|^4$ low-order terms. This design sacrifices some per-iteration optimality for data reuse, gaining $\mathcal{O}(|S|^2)$ reduction.  
  - *Reward design:* $\lambda_t(s,a) \propto 1/N_t(s,a)$ is simple but requires constant $c = 4|S|H^2 \ell_0$ to bound rewards by $1/c$ for Algorithm 5. Too small $c$ violates assumptions; too large $c$ slows exploration.  
  - *Penalty choice:* RAE uses $\mathcal{O}(\ell_0)$ Bernstein term (Eq. (15)), RFE uses $\mathcal{O}(|S|\ell_0)$ (Eq. (16)). The $|S|$ gap is inherent (tight lower bound).

- **Failure signatures:**  
  - *Step 1 failure:* If $\text{Reg}_{\text{EPA}}(T)$ exceeds $\mathcal{O}(|S||A|H \ell^2)$ (e.g., due to poor OMD step size or confidence set), Theorem 4.2 fails → $\pi_{\text{exp}}$ may leave some significant $(s,a,h)$ unexplored.  
  - *Step 2 failure:* Despite $\pi_{\text{exp}}$ coverage, finite $N$ may yield $N_h(s,a)=0$ for some significant triples (probability $\le \delta$ via Lemma D.1). This causes $b_h = H$ (Eq. (15)/(16)) → over-pessimism.  
  - *Step 3 failure:* If the reward class is misrepresented (e.g., RFE with arbitrary rewards but using RAE penalties), the bound $V^* - \hat{V} \le \varepsilon$ may not hold.

- **First 3 experiments:**  
  1. *Coverage validation:* Run `CREATE_EXPLORATION_POLICY` on a small tabular MDP (e.g., $|S|=10, |A|=4, H=5$). Compute empirical ratios $\mu^\pi/\mu^{\pi_{\text{exp}}}$ for random policies $\pi$. Verify they approach the $\mathcal{O}(|S||A|H \ell^2)$ bound from Theorem 4.2.  
  2. *Concentration check:* After Step 2, for each $(s,a,h)$, compute $|\hat{P}_h(s'|s,a) - P_h(s'|s,a)|$ (if true $P$ known). Compare to empirical Bernstein radius $\epsilon_t$ (Eq. (12)). Should be $\le$ radius with $\ge 1-\delta$ probability.  
  3. *End-to-end regret:* For a fixed reward $r$ (RAE from small class, RFE arbitrary), compute $V^*_1(s_1) - V^{\hat{\pi}}_1(s_1)$ over multiple random MDP instances. Should be $\le \varepsilon$ when $N$ matches Theorem 5.1/5.2.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- **Open Question 1:** Is the $\mathcal{O}(|S|^2|A|H^4/\varepsilon)$ term in the reward-agnostic sample complexity necessary, or can it be removed to achieve $\mathcal{O}(|S||A|H^3/\varepsilon^2)$ for all $\varepsilon$?  
  *Basis in paper:* The authors state a gap in low-accuracy regimes (Conclusion) and their upper bound includes that term, while the lower bound is only $\Omega(|S||A|H^3/\varepsilon^2)$.  
  *Why unresolved:* It is unclear whether the term is an artifact of the algorithmic analysis or a fundamental limitation of the problem.  
  *What evidence would resolve it:* A lower bound proving the necessity of a $1/\varepsilon$ contribution, or a new algorithm that eliminates the extra term, would close the gap.

- **Open Question 2:** Does the sample complexity of reward-agnostic exploration depend on the size of the reward class, and if so, what is the precise dependence?  
  *Basis in paper:* The reward class is assumed $\text{poly}(|S|,|A|,H)$ but the final bounds contain no explicit factor of the class size, suggesting any dependence is at most logarithmic.  
  *Why unresolved:* The analysis treats the reward class size as a constant absorbed in log terms, so the impact of a larger reward class on sample complexity remains unknown.  
  *What evidence would resolve it:* A lower bound that scales with $|R|$ (e.g., $\Omega(\log|R|)$ or stronger) or an upper bound that explicitly includes a dependence on $|R|$ (and matches the lower bound) would clarify the role of the reward class size.

- **Open Question 3:** Can the information-theoretic results for reward-free and reward-agnostic exploration be extended beyond the tabular finite-horizon setting, e.g., to infinite-horizon MDPs or function approximation (linear, low-rank)?  
  *Basis in paper:* The paper exclusively considers finite-horizon tabular MDPs; all definitions, algorithms, and proofs rely on this structure and do not address more general models.  
  *Why unresolved:* Real-world problems often involve large or continuous state spaces and infinite horizons; it is unknown whether the tabular rates survive under approximation errors or require new techniques.  
  *What evidence would resolve it:* Developing algorithms with matching upper and lower bounds for specific function-approximation classes (e.g., linear MDPs) would extend the theory, as would impossibility results delineating inherent limitations.

## Limitations
- Ambiguous definition of $\ell_0$: Whether $\varepsilon$ appears inside the logarithm $\ell_0 = \ln(|S||A|H/(\delta \varepsilon))$ is unclear, affecting the logarithmic factor in sample complexity.
- Allocation of total confidence $\delta$ across all high-probability events is not explicitly enumerated; potential union-bound violation could inflate failure probability.
- The constants 9440, 28320, and 885 are taken from proofs without derivation; their correctness is critical for the claimed low-order improvements.

## Confidence
- **Single online MDP approach:** High (Theorem 4.2 provides rigorous coverage guarantee).
- **RAE sample complexity:** High for the $\mathcal{O}(|S||A|H^3/\varepsilon^2)$ high-order term; Medium for the assertion of a smaller low-order term (exact constants not fully disclosed).
- **RFE lower bound:** Medium (proof not presented; independent verification needed to confirm tightness and resolution of the conjecture).
