# The Implicit Bias of Steepest Descent with Mini-batch Stochastic Gradient

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2602.11557
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.11557
- Reference count: 40
- Primary result: Mini-batch stochastic steepest descent converges to an approximate norm-induced max-margin solution only in the large-batch regime without momentum; momentum enables small-batch convergence via a trade-off but with slower rates; variance reduction restores exact full-batch bias for any batch size.

## Executive Summary
This paper investigates the implicit bias of mini-batch stochastic steepest descent methods (including SignSGD, Normalized-SGD, and Muon) in multi-class linear classification. It analyzes how batch size, momentum, and variance reduction shape the limiting max-margin solution and convergence rates under general entry-wise and Schatten-p norms. Without momentum, convergence to an approximate norm-induced max-margin solution occurs only in the large-batch regime (b > 4Rn/γ+4R), with a batch-dependent margin gap but identical convergence rates to full-batch methods. Momentum enables small-batch convergence via a batch-size–momentum trade-off, albeit with slower, dimension-free rates. Variance reduction eliminates batch-size dependence, recovering the exact full-batch max-margin solution for any batch size, though at a more conservative rate. For batch size one without momentum, the algorithm converges to a distinct bias derived from sample averaging rather than max-margin geometry, as shown by an explicit data construction. The analysis provides explicit, dimension-free convergence rates and unifies understanding of when stochastic optimization aligns with full-batch implicit bias. Experiments across ℓ2, ℓ∞, and spectral norms validate theoretical predictions.

## Method Summary
The paper studies a unified steepest descent framework (Algorithm 1) encompassing SignSGD, Normalized-SGD, and Muon. The update uses a mini-batch stochastic gradient G_t and performs a steepest step within a norm-induced unit ball: Δ_t = argmax_{‖Δ‖≤1} ⟨G_t, Δ⟩, which corresponds to an appropriate operator (e.g., sign for ℓ∞, normalized gradient for ℓ₂, full SVD-based map for spectral norm). The algorithm optionally incorporates momentum (exponential moving average of gradients) and variance reduction (SVRG). Training follows random reshuffling (without replacement) and uses a decaying learning rate η_t = c t^{-a}. The analysis assumes linear separability, bounded data norms, and that the steepest subproblem is solved exactly. The method is evaluated on synthetic multi-class linear classification tasks with varying batch sizes, momentum coefficients, and variance-reduction settings, comparing against full-batch steepest descent baselines.

## Key Results
- **Large‑batch without momentum:** Convergence to an approximate norm‑induced max‑margin solution occurs only if the batch size satisfies b > 4Rn/(γ + 4R). The effective margin is γ’ = γ – 4(m‑1)R, and the convergence rate matches the full‑batch rate (dimension‑dependent).
- **Momentum:** Enables convergence even for small batches via a trade‑off. The effective margin becomes γ’ = γ – 2(1‑β₁)m(m²‑1)R > 0. The rate becomes slower and dimension‑free.
- **Variance reduction (SVRG):** Restores the exact full‑batch max‑margin solution (margin γ) for any batch size, with or without momentum, but at a more conservative rate that includes an extra factor of m².
- **Batch size one without momentum:** The algorithm converges to a distinct bias derived from sample averaging, not the max‑margin solution, as shown by an explicit construction.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1
- **Claim:** Mini-batch stochastic steepest descent without momentum converges to an approximate norm-induced max‑margin solution **only** in the large‑batch regime.
- **Mechanism:** The batch‑size‑dependent term $4(m-1)R$ in the descent inequality acts as an effective margin penalty. When $b$ is large enough ($4(m-1)R < γ$), the stochastic gradient aligns sufficiently with the full gradient, enabling the usual geometric decay $⟨∇L(W_t), Δ_t⟩ ≈ ∥∇L(W_t)∥^*$. This yields monotonic loss reduction and margin growth toward the $ρ = γ - 4(m-1)R$ max‑margin solution.
- **Core assumption:** Linear separability (Assumption 3.1) and bounded data norms (Assumption 3.2); the analysis also relies on Random Reshuffling causing epoch‑wise gradient cancellations.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - *Abstract*: “Without momentum, convergence only occurs with large batches, yielding a batch‑dependent margin gap but the full‑batch convergence rate.”
  - *Theorem 4.1*: Formal statement and proof in §4.1 and Appendix D.
  - *Corpus*: The corpus includes Fan et al. [2025] on full‑batch spectral/implicit bias, providing the baseline from which stochastic effects deviate.
- **Break condition:** Batch size falls below $4Rn/(γ + 4R)$; the effective margin $ρ ≤ 0$, loss may increase, and the iterates fail to align with any max‑margin solution.

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim:** Momentum (β₁ > 0) enables convergence to an approximate max‑margin solution **even with small batches**, at cost of slower rates.
- **Mechanism:** Exponential momentum averaging creates a temporal smoothing effect. The accumulated buffer $M_t$ integrates gradients over an epoch, leveraging the zero‑sum property of Random Reshuffling to cancel batch‑level noise. This reduces the variance in the descent direction, but introduces a bias term $2(1-β₁)m(m²-1)R$ in the effective margin. When $ρ = γ - 2(1-β₁)m(m²-1)R > 0$, descent is restored.
- **Core assumption:** Random Reshuffling; β₁ sufficiently high to overcome the batch‑size‑dependent penalty. Requires learning‑rate schedule satisfying Assumption 3.4.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - *Abstract*: “Momentum enables small‑batch convergence through a batch‑size–momentum trade‑off, albeit at a slower, dimension‑free rate.”
  - *Theorem 4.3*: Formal rate and effective margin bound in §4.2 and Appendix E.
  - *Corpus*: Cutkosky & Mehta [2020] showed momentum relaxes large‑batch needs; this work quantifies the trade‑off for multi‑class steepest descent.
- **Break condition:** Momentum β₁ too small for given batch‑size; $ρ ≤ 0$ and the descent inequality may not hold, leading to divergence or convergence to a non‑max‑margin solution.

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim:** Variance reduction (SVRG‑style) restores **exact** full‑batch implicit bias (margin γ) for **any** batch size, with or without momentum.
- **Mechanism:** The control variate $V_t = ∇L_{B_t}(W_t) - ∇L_{B_t}(W_t') + ∇L(W_t')$ corrects the stochastic gradient bias by adding the full‑gradient difference at a snapshot $W_t'$. This forces the descent direction’s alignment error $⟨∇L(W_t), Δ_t⟩$ to match the full‑batch case asymptotically, eliminating the $m$‑dependent penalty. The cost is an extra $O(m)$ factor in the convergence rate due to gradient variance on the snapshot difference.
- **Core assumption:** Accurate snapshot full‑gradient ∇L(ʼW̃) is periodically recomputed; data separability and boundedness hold. Assumption 3.4 governs learning‑rate interactions with momentum.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - *Abstract*: “Variance recovery can recover the exact full‑batch implicit bias for any batch size, albeit at a slower convergence rate.”
  - *Theorem 4.6*: Statement and proof in §4.3, Appendices F and G.
  - *Theorem 4.6*: The $γ$ margin is recovered irrespective of $b$ or β₁.
- **Break condition:** Stale snapshot ($‖W_t - W_t'‖$ large) leads to large variance terms; the bound on parameter difference must be controlled, which typically restricts the snapshot refresh frequency relative to learning‑rate decay.

## Foundational Learning
- **Concept:** Implicit Bias of Optimization Algorithms  
  - **Why needed here:** The paper’s central thesis is that the batch size/momentum/VR settings determine *which* solution is reached, not just convergence speed. Understanding full‑batch steepest descent bias (Fan et al. [2025]) is prerequisite.  
  - **Quick check question:** In an overparameterized linear classifier, what solution does full‑batch gradient descent converge to under exponential loss? (Answer: The ℓ₂ max‑margin solution.)
- **Concept:** Norm‑Induced Geometries (Entry‑wise and Schatten $p$‑norms)  
  - **Why needed here:** The update rule $Δ_t = argmax_{‖Δ‖≤1} ⟨G_t, Δ⟩$ defines a geometry. SignSGD corresponds to ℓ∞, Normalized‑SGD to ℓ₂, Muon to spectral norm. Each norm yields a different max‑margin solution.  
  - **Quick check question:** For a matrix $A$, what is the dual norm of the entrywise ℓ₁ norm? (Answer: The entrywise ℓ∞ norm.)
- **Concept:** Random Reshuffling vs. With‑Replacement Sampling  
  - **Why needed here:** The analysis crucially uses the property that within an epoch, the sum of mini‑batch gradients equals the full gradient (Equation for Ξ). This zero‑sum property is what allows momentum to cancel noise.  
  - **Quick check question:** If you sample with replacement, does the epoch‑wise sum of gradients equal $m∇L(W)$ in expectation only or almost surely? (Answer: Only in expectation.)

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map:**  
  - **Hyperparameter switches:** `(batch_size b)`, `(momentum β₁)`, `(variance_reduction ν)`. Each affects the “effective margin” ρ in Theorem 4.1–4.3 and the noise terms α₁.  
  - **Norm selector:** `∥·∥` (ℓ₂, ℓ∞, Schatten‑p) determines the max‑margin definition and the steepest descent operator `ϕ`.  
  - **Learning‑rate schedule:** `η_t = c t⁻ᵃ` satisfying Assumption 3.3/3.4, controlling the trade‑off between convergence and noise suppression.  
- **Critical path:**  
  1. Start from full‑batch steepest descent (Fan et al. [2025] base case).  
  2. Introduce mini‑batching → effective margin penalty $ρ < γ$ (Theorem 4.1).  
  3. Add momentum → new penalty $2(1-β₁)m(m²-1)R$ (Theorem 4.3).  
  4. Add variance reduction → penalty removed, exact γ recovered (Theorem 4.6).  
  5. Extreme case b=1 without momentum → different bias altogether (Theorem 4.9).  
- **Design tradeoffs:**  
  - **Batch size vs. noise:** Larger `b` reduces gradient noise but increases per‑iteration cost. Momentum can substitute for larger batches but slows asymptotic rate by $Θ(m/(1-β₁))$.  
  - **Exactness vs. rate:** Variance reduction gives exact full‑batch bias but has a more conservative rate (e.g., $m^{2+a}/γ$ versus $m/γ$ without VR).  
  - **Norm choice:** ℓ∞ (SignSGD) may be more communication‑efficient but the bound on $‖x‖_∞$ is tighter, affecting the large‑batch condition.  
- **Failure signatures:**  
  1. **Vanishing descent:** If $ρ ≤ 0$, $L(W_{t+1})$ may increase; check if $4(m-1)R < γ$ (no momentum) or $2(1-β₁)m(m²-1)R < γ$ (with momentum).  
  2. **Oscillatory loss:** Often indicates learning rate too large relative to the effective margin; violation of Assumption 3.4.  
  3. **Direction not max‑margin:** Measure $cos(W_t, W^*) →$ constant $< 1$. Could be due to small `b` and low `β₁`, or because VR snapshot is stale.  
- **First 3 experiments:**  
  1. **No momentum, vary batch size:** Fix β₁=0, run for $b \in \{20, 200\}$. Expect convergence only for $b=200$ (large‑batch regime).  
  2. **Fixed large batch, vary momentum:** Fix $b=200$, vary β₁ ∈ {0, 0.5, 0.99}. Observe that even β₁=0.5 may suffice; plot normalized margin error vs. β₁.  
  3. **Enable variance reduction:** Set ν=1, test $b=20$ with/without momentum. Expect recovery of the ℓ₂ max‑margin solution ($cos → 1$) regardless of β₁, but confirm slower rate (longer tail in loss curve).

## Limitations
- Core assumptions (linear separability, exact random reshuffling, exact steepest step, decaying η_t) may not hold in practice.
- Results are limited to linear models; applicability to deep networks is unknown.
- Batch-size-one distinct bias is established under a specific data construction; its generality is uncertain.

## Confidence
- Large-batch convergence (no momentum): High
- Momentum enabling small-batch: High
- VR exact recovery: High
- Batch-size-one bias: Medium
- Extension to deep learning: Low
