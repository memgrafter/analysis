# DeepGen 1.0: A Lightweight Unified Multimodal Model for Advancing Image Generation and Editing

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2602.12205
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.12205
- Reference count: 40
- Primary result: A 5B‑parameter model (3B VLM + 2B DiT) using Stacked Channel Bridging and a three‑stage training pipeline achieves state‑of‑the‑art results among open‑source models, scoring 0.73 on WISE (28% above 80B HunyuanImage) and 77.5 on UniREditBench (37% above 27B Qwen‑Image‑Edit).

## Executive Summary
DeepGen 1.0 demonstrates that a compact 5B‑parameter unified multimodal system can match or exceed much larger (>10B) counterparts on diverse image generation and editing tasks. The model integrates a 3B VLM with a 2B DiT through a novel Stacked Channel Bridging (SCB) module that fuses hierarchical VLM features and learnable “think tokens” to provide rich conditioning. Training follows a data‑centric three‑stage pipeline (alignment pre‑training, joint SFT, RL with MR‑GRPO and an auxiliary SFT loss) that prevents catastrophic forgetting and aligns with human preferences. Evaluated on WISE, UniREditBench, DPGBench, and UniGenBench, DeepGen 1.0 sets new open‑source benchmarks, proving the viability of lightweight unified multimodal generation.

## Method Summary
The method employs a three‑stage training pipeline on ~50M samples. Stage 1 freezes the pretrained VLM (Qwen‑2.5‑VL 3B) and DiT (SD3.5‑Medium 2B) and trains only the SCB connector (SigLIP encoder + six transformer layers) and 128 think tokens to align representations. Stage 2 unfreezes the DiT and applies LoRA (rank 64) to the VLM, jointly fine‑tuning on a mixed dataset covering general generation, reasoning, and text rendering. Stage 3 uses the proposed MR‑GRPO reinforcement learning with three reward functions (VLM preference, OCR, CLIP) and an auxiliary supervised diffusion loss to optimize for human preferences while preventing capability drift. Key innovations include SCB for hierarchical VLM‑to‑DiT feature fusion and reward‑wise advantage normalization in MR‑GRPO for stable multi‑objective optimization.

## Key Results
- **Reasoning:** WISE score 0.73, outperforming 80B HunyuanImage (0.57) by 28%.
- **Editing:** UniREditBench 77.5, beating 27B Qwen‑Image‑Edit (56.5) by 37%.
- **Omni‑capability:** DPGBench 87.90, UniGenBench 75.74, demonstrating strong performance across generation, editing, and text rendering with only ~50M training samples.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1: Stacked Channel Bridging (SCB) Prevents Fine‑Grained Detail Loss
SCB extracts hidden states from six uniformly distributed VLM layers (low/mid/high) and concatenates them channel‑wise with injected “think tokens”, fusing via a lightweight connector. This preserves multi‑granularity features (edges, textures, semantics) and enriches them with implicit Chain‑of‑Thought representations. The core assumption is that single VLM‑layer outputs discard low/mid‑level details critical for DiT’s pixel synthesis, while uniform multi‑layer fusion provides robust conditioning without prohibitive parameters. Evidence: abstract and Section 2 confirm the design; corpus analysis shows neighbors explore multi‑layer fusion but without SCB’s specific channel‑wise concatenation + think token design. **Break condition:** if VLM layers are too shallow or connector capacity insufficient, fused features remain semantically biased or noisy, degrading fidelity.

### Mechanism 2: Three‑Stage Training Prevents Catastrophic Forgetting
The pipeline (Alignment Pre‑training → Joint SFT → RL with auxiliary loss) avoids misalignment and drift. Stage 1 synchronizes representation spaces by training only the connector. Stage 2 jointly fine‑tunes DiT and LoRA‑adapted VLM on mixed data, fostering omni‑capabilities. Stage 3 applies MR‑GRPO RL with mixture rewards and an auxiliary SFT loss to anchor the model to its high‑quality SFT distribution while optimizing preferences. Core assumption: direct joint training from random initialization causes misalignment; progressive staged training and auxiliary regularization allow stable optimization in low‑parameter regimes. Evidence: Section 3.3 and 5.3.2 ablations show removing auxiliary SFT loss causes performance collapse after ~300 steps. **Break condition:** poor RL hyperparameter choices (step count, KL coefficient) without auxiliary loss cause drift from reasoning tasks.

### Mechanism 3: MR‑GRPO Stabilizes Multi‑Objective RL
MR‑GRPO normalizes each reward independently within groups before aggregation and uses noise‑preserving sampling. This prevents high‑variance rewards (e.g., OCR) from dominating updates and ensures granular learning signals. The auxiliary SFT loss complements velocity‑space KL regularization to maintain fidelity. Core assumption: joint normalization across heterogeneous rewards obscures task‑specific gradients; decoupled normalization preserves them. Evidence: Section 5.3.2 shows removing reward‑wise normalization drops text generation scores; Appendix B details noise‑preserving sampling. **Break condition:** mis‑tuned reward weights or omitted normalization leads to imbalanced capability gains (e.g., OCR improves while general generation stagnates).

## Architecture Onboarding
### Component map
Text+Image → VLM (Qwen‑2.5‑VL 3B) → Extracted features from 6 layers + 128 think tokens → SCB connector (SigLIP Encoder + 6 Transformer layers) → Conditioning tokens → DiT (SD3.5‑Medium 2B) → Noise prediction → Image.

### Critical path
Data flow: tokenization → VLM forward pass (multi‑layer extraction) → SCB fusion → DiT conditioning → noise prediction. The SCB fusion step is the architectural pivot; errors in feature integration propagate to all generation and editing results.

### Design tradeoffs
- **SCB depth:** more layers capture finer hierarchy but add connector compute; too few risk losing detail.
- **Parameter split (3B VLM + 2B DiT):** allocating more to VLM improves reasoning but reduces DiT capacity for fine details; this split was chosen empirically.
- **LoRA rank (64):** balances VLM adaptation with preserving pretrained knowledge; higher rank risks overfitting SFT data.

### Failure signatures
- **Collapsed reasoning** (low WISE/RISE): likely due to insufficient think tokens or VLM LoRA overwriting world knowledge.
- **Poor text rendering** (low CVTG‑2K Word Accuracy): indicates inadequate OCR reward weight or insufficient text‑rendering data in RL/SFT.
- **Visual artifacts/instability** during RL: often caused by missing noise‑preserving sampling or too low KL coefficient.

### First 3 experiments
1. **SCB ablation:** Train without SCB (use only final VLM layer). Expect drops in DPGBench/GenEval and reasoning benchmarks, confirming multi‑layer fusion necessity.
2. **Stage isolation:** Train only Stage 1 (connector). Expect poor generation quality but no catastrophic forgetting; validates alignment pre‑training step.
3. **RL auxiliary loss test:** Run RL for 1500 steps with/without ℒSFT. Monitor UniGenBench text sub‑score; without ℒSFT, expect initial gains then collapse after ~300 steps, demonstrating auxiliary loss’s role in preventing drift.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
1. **Optimal VLM layer selection for SCB:** Is uniform distribution of six layers optimal? The paper does not compare alternative strategies (e.g., learned weights, top‑k).
2. **Adaptive think token count:** Could dynamic token counts improve reasoning performance without increasing overhead? Fixed 128 tokens were used without exploration.
3. **Data efficiency lower bound:** What is the minimal number of training samples required? The paper only contrasts with larger models but does not systematically study scaling with reduced data.
4. **Generalization beyond static images:** Does the architecture and training pipeline transfer to video, 3D, or audio generation? The evaluation is limited to images, leaving cross‑modality applicability unresolved.

## Limitations
- Connector architecture details (hidden dimensions, layer sizes) are unreported, blocking precise replication.
- Exact dataset mixing ratios and sampling criteria for SFT and RL stages are undisclosed.
- Implementation specifics of the reward models (VLM preference, OCR) and their scoring logic are not provided.
