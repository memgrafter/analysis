# Hardware-Aware Tensor Networks for Real-Time Quantum-Inspired Anomaly Detection at Particle Colliders

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2603.26604
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.26604
- **Reference count:** 40
- **Primary result:** Developed quantum-inspired tensor networks (SMPO/CSMPO) for unsupervised anomaly detection on LHC trigger hardware, achieving ROC-AUCs of 0.80–0.90 on simulated new physics signals with sub-microsecond FPGA latency and zero DSP usage.

## Executive Summary
This work tackles the challenge of real-time anomaly detection for particle collider triggers by employing quantum-inspired tensor networks deployable on classical edge hardware. The authors introduce Spaced Matrix Product Operators (SMPOs), a tensor network architecture trained unsupervised on background collision events to score anomalies via norm deviation. A cascaded variant (CSMPO) reduces parameters by ~50% and computational cost by ~17% while preserving detection performance. On simulated LHC data covering four new physics signals, the SMPO achieves strong ROC-AUCs (0.80–0.90) and a high true positive rate (6.35%) at an extremely low false positive rate (10⁻⁵) for the most distinct signal. Both models are successfully synthesized to an FPGA (Xilinx Kintex UltraScale), achieving latency below 0.4 µs without using any DSP blocks, demonstrating a feasible path for deployment in real-time trigger systems.

## Method Summary
The method processes high-energy collision events by first embedding 57 kinematic features (from 19 particles) into a 19-site Matrix Product State (MPS) with physical dimension 3, applying particle-type-specific scaling and zero-padding. A spectral ordering algorithm based on Quantum Mutual Information reorders the MPS sites to cluster highly correlated particles, enabling low bond dimensions. The core model is an SMPO, a tensor network that contracts the input MPS to a final vector; its squared L2 norm serves as an anomaly score after calibration on background data. Training is unsupervised: the SMPO is optimized (using Pseudo-Huber loss) to produce a target norm on background events. The cascaded SMPO (CSMPO) factorizes the SMPO into two consecutive layers with smaller bond dimensions and intermediate site spacing, reducing parameters and MACs. For hardware deployment, models are quantized to 16-bit fixed-point and implemented on an FPGA via High-Level Synthesis, with a dedicated contraction engine that performs nearest-neighbor tensor operations in parallel.

## Key Results
- SMPO achieves ROC-AUCs between 0.80 and 0.90 across four benchmark new physics signals, with a 6.35% true positive rate at a false positive rate of 10⁻⁵ for the A→4ℓ signal.
- Cascaded SMPO (19→7→1) reduces trainable parameters by ~50% and MAC operations by ~17% relative to a standard SMPO, with comparable anomaly detection performance (AUC within 0.01–0.02 for three signals).
- FPGA implementation of the SMPO attains sub-microsecond inference latency (<0.4 µs) on a Xilinx Kintex UltraScale (XCKU115) with zero DSP block usage, confirming feasibility for real-time trigger deployment.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1: Tensor Network Factorization Enables Efficient Hardware Mapping
- **Claim:** Tensor networks efficiently capture complex correlations in high-dimensional particle event data while enabling low-latency hardware implementation.
- **Mechanism:** The event is represented as a Matrix Product State (MPS), factorizing the joint feature distribution into a chain of low-rank tensors. This factorization exploits area-law-like correlations, requiring only a small bond dimension. Inference reduces to linear tensor contractions with strictly nearest-neighbor connectivity, mapping efficiently to FPGAs via parallel compute units and predictable data flow.
- **Core assumption:** Particle event data exhibits limited long-range correlations, so mutual information between distant features decays sufficiently fast to be captured by a low bond dimension TN.
- **Evidence anchors:** [abstract] notes inference reduces to sequences of bilinear contractions with no nonlinearities, and nearest-neighbor connectivity yields sparse graphs conducive to FPGA deployment. [section 2.1.3] uses Quantum Mutual Information ordering to cluster correlated sites, enabling smaller bond dimensions. Corpus support is indirect.

### Mechanism 2: Unsupervised Norm Deviation for Anomaly Scoring
- **Claim:** Unsupervised training on background events enables anomaly detection because the TN learns a compressed representation of the background distribution; anomalies yield large deviations in the output norm.
- **Mechanism:** The SMPO is trained to map the input MPS to a smaller output MPS (ultimately a vector) with a target squared norm μ (using Pseudo-Huber loss). Background events produce norms near μ, while anomalous events produce norms that deviate. The anomaly score is the absolute deviation from the median background norm.
- **Core assumption:** The background data manifold is well-represented by the TN model, and anomalies are statistically rare and produce norm values outside the typical background range.
- **Evidence anchors:** [abstract] states SMPOs are trained unsupervised on background events. [section 2.3] describes the loss and scoring scheme. [section 3.1] ROC curves show separation between background and signals.

### Mechanism 3: Cascaded Structure Reduces Cost with Minimal Expressivity Loss
- **Claim:** Cascaded SMPO (CSMPO) reduces trainable parameters and MAC operations by factorizing weight tensors across layers and exploiting sparsity from output site spacing, while maintaining comparable learning capacity.
- **Mechanism:** A single SMPO with bond dimension b = b1 × b2 decomposes into two consecutive SMPOs with smaller bond dimensions b1 and b2. The first layer reduces MPS length from N to M with spacing, producing an intermediate MPS; the second layer reduces from M to 1. Weight tensors in each layer have smaller bond dimensions (b1² vs b²), and the first layer’s horizontal contractions exploit sparsity, reducing overall MACs.
- **Core assumption:** The factorization constraint (effective weight tensor is a product of two separate tensors) does not significantly reduce expressive power for the anomaly detection task.
- **Evidence anchors:** [abstract] reports ~50% parameter and ~17% MAC reductions with comparable performance. [section 2.2.2] describes the factorization. [section 3.1] Table 1 shows CSMPO19→7→1 achieves similar AUC to SMPO, though CSMPO19→2→1 degrades.

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map:** Input Preprocessor -> Site Ordering Module -> Tensor Network Model (SMPO/CSMPO) -> Contraction Engine -> Norm and Scoring Unit -> Quantizer -> FPGA Wrapper
- **Critical path:**
  1. Receive event's 57 scaled features (pre-ordered by particle type).
  2. Embed into 19-site MPS (physical dim 3).
  3. Reorder MPS sites via spectral ordering permutation.
  4. Perform vertical contractions (input MPS × SMPO physical legs).
  5. Execute horizontal contraction sweep:
     - SMPO: bidirectional sweep until three sites remain, contract all.
     - CSMPO: after first layer vertical, contract non-output sites in groups to form intermediate MPS; second layer vertical; bidirectional sweep.
  6. Compute squared L2 norm of final vector.
  7. Subtract median_background_norm (constant) and take absolute value → anomaly score.
  8. Optionally compare to threshold for trigger decision.
- **Design tradeoffs:**
  - Bond dimension: larger captures more correlations but increases MACs quadratically.
  - CSMPO layers/spacing: more layers/smaller spacing allow smaller bond dimensions but increase total parameters and may hurt expressivity.
  - Quantization: lower bits save LUTs/FFs but may distort norm distribution; 16-bit chosen as minimal without performance drop.
  - Contraction algorithm: bidirectional sweep minimizes latency but uses more LUTs; serial saves resources but increases latency.
  - Loss hyperparameters (μ, δ): control model capacity and sensitivity; tuned for high TPR at low FPR.
- **Failure signatures:**
  - Performance drop: ROC-AUC ≪ 0.80 or TPR @ 10⁻⁵ FPR much lower than paper.
  - Resource overrun: FPGA synthesis fails (exceeds LUT/FF limits); DSP usage appears (should be zero).
  - Latency violation: inferred latency > 1 µs.
  - Norm distribution shift: after quantization, background norm median changes substantially.
  - Numerical instability: saturation/underflow causing many events to have zero/max norm.
- **First 3 experiments:**
  1. *Software correctness:* Implement SMPO/CSMPO in Python/tensor library; train on small background subset; verify signal norms higher and ROC-AUC positive vs. paper.
  2. *Quantization impact:* Quantize trained model to 16-bit fixed-point; evaluate on full test set; check ROC-AUC/TPR degradation and norm distribution shift.
  3. *FPGA prototype synthesis:* Write contraction engine in C++ for HLS; synthesize for Kintex UltraScale; check resource usage (LUTs, FFs), latency, and zero DSP usage; tune if needed.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- **Does introducing non-linear activation functions between cascaded SMPO layers improve anomaly detection performance or model expressivity without significantly increasing computational cost?**
  - *Basis:* Conclusion suggests future work on non-linearity between layers.
  - *Why unresolved:* Non-linearity could break the TN's linear structure but might capture more complex features; impact on performance-latency trade-off unknown.
  - *Needed evidence:* Train/evaluate CSMPO variants with element-wise non-linearities (e.g., ReLU) between layers; compare ROC metrics and FPGA resources.
- **How well do SMPO/CSMPO models generalize to beyond-Standard-Model signals with kinematic signatures substantially different from the four benchmarks?**
  - *Basis:* Models trained only on QCD background and evaluated on four specific signals; no test on unseen anomaly classes.
  - *Why unresolved:* Robustness to unknown, diverse new physics signatures is unproven; may overfit to training background.
  - *Needed evidence:* Test on additional synthetic BSM signals or real data-aggregated background variations; measure AUC/TPR degradation.
- **What is the optimal CSMPO architecture (spacing at each layer, intermediate MPS length) for minimizing FPGA resources while maintaining target performance across diverse signal topologies?**
  - *Basis:* Paper explores only two CSMPO configurations (19→7→1, 19→2→1) and notes a flexible hyperparameter space.
  - *Why unresolved:* Continuous space of intermediate lengths and spacing distributions unexplored; optimal may be signal-dependent.
  - *Needed evidence:* Systematic hyperparameter search over first-layer spacing (1–18) and second-layer bond dimensions; evaluate Pareto frontier of AUC vs. LUT usage/latency.
- **How do quantization bit-widths and target FPGA architectures affect the resource-latency-performance trade-off?**
  - *Basis:* Quantized to 16-bit on Xilinx Kintex UltraScale; performance holds until 16-bit for TPR.
  - *Why unresolved:* Results tied to one FPGA family; lower precisions (e.g., 8-bit) or other architectures (Intel, ASIC) may further reduce resources but harm performance; scaling laws unquantified.
  - *Needed evidence:* Synthesize on multiple FPGA families (Xilinx Zynq, Intel Stratix) with bit-widths 8–24; measure LUT/FF/DSP usage, latency, and performance degradation.
- **How sensitive is SMPO performance to the input particle ordering and feature scaling choices during MPS embedding?**
  - *Basis:* Uses spectral ordering based on QMI and piecewise scaling by particle type, claiming reduced bond dimensions; no ablation.
  - *Why unresolved:* Ordering and scaling directly affect correlation capture; suboptimal choices might inflate bond dimensions or hurt discriminative power.
  - *Needed evidence:* Train with alternative orderings (random, by pT) and scaling schemes (min-max, uniform); compare required bond dimension for equivalent AUC and final ROC.

## Limitations
- **Site ordering dependency:** The exact spectral site ordering used is not fully documented; performance is sensitive to this preprocessing step, and the provided partial ordering is insufficient for faithful reproduction without code.
- **Generalizability uncertainty:** Models were validated on only four simulated BSM signals; robustness to truly unknown anomalies or real detector conditions remains unproven.
- **Simulated data context:** Performance metrics come from simulated LHC data, which may not fully capture the complexity, noise, and systematic effects of real collider data.

## Confidence
- **Hardware feasibility claim (sub-µs FPGA latency, zero DSPs):** High (concrete synthesis results on specific FPGA model).
- **Anomaly detection performance (ROC-AUC 0.80–0.90 on 4 signals):** Medium (validated on simulation; real-world transferability uncertain).
- **Core mechanism (area-law correlations in event data):** Medium/Low (QMI ordering supports it, but no proof area law holds for full 57-feature representation).
- **Unsupervised training sufficiency:** Medium (validated on benchmarks; sensitivity to diverse anomalies unknown).
- **CSMPO parameter reduction without expressivity loss:** Medium (similar AUC for 3/4 signals; degradation seen with aggressive compression).
