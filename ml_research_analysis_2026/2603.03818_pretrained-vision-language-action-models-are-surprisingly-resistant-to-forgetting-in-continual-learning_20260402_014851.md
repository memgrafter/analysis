# Pretrained Vision-Language-Action Models are Surprisingly Resistant to Forgetting in Continual Learning

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2603.03818
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.03818
- **Reference count:** 40
- **Primary result:** Large pretrained Vision-Language-Action models (VLAs) exhibit near-zero catastrophic forgetting during continual robot learning when using minimal Experience Replay, contrasting sharply with severe forgetting in smaller non-pretrained policies.

## Executive Summary
This paper investigates catastrophic forgetting in continual imitation learning for robotic manipulation. Using the LIBERO benchmark, the authors compare large pretrained Vision-Language-Action models (e.g., Pi0, GR00T N1.5) against smaller non-pretrained behavior cloning policies under a standard Experience Replay (ER) protocol. The key finding is that pretrained VLAs demonstrate remarkable resistance to forgetting—achieving near-zero Negative Backward Transfer even with a tiny 2% replay buffer—while smaller models forget severely. Furthermore, when forgetting appears to occur, VLAs recover performance rapidly via minimal finetuning, unlike non-pretrained models that require near-full retraining. The study concludes that large-scale pretraining fundamentally changes continual learning dynamics, making simple ER highly effective for VLAs.

## Method Summary
The evaluation uses the LIBERO benchmark, where a policy sequentially learns 10 manipulation tasks per suite (e.g., Spatial, Object, Goal) without revisiting old tasks. Each task provides multi-view RGB images, proprioception, and a language instruction. The continual learning protocol employs fixed-size Experience Replay: after completing a task, a fixed number of its demonstration transitions (e.g., 100 for 2% buffer) are stored and frozen. During training on a new task, each minibatch is an equal mix (1:1) of current-task samples and replay samples from all previously stored tasks. Pretrained VLAs (Pi0, GR00T) are adapted using parameter-efficient finetuning (e.g., LoRA) with a fixed budget per task. Non-pretrained BC baselines are fully finetuned with a larger budget. After each task, the policy is evaluated on all seen tasks to compute average success rate (SR) and Negative Backward Transfer (NBT), which quantifies forgetting.

## Key Results
- Pretrained VLAs achieve near-zero NBT (≈0) with as little as 2% replay data, while smaller BC models suffer high NBT (>0.2).
- VLAs sometimes show positive backward transfer, indicating that learning new tasks can improve performance on old ones.
- When performance degrades, VLAs recover lost skills rapidly (<10% of original training steps) via brief finetuning, whereas non-pretrained models require retraining on nearly all original data.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
Pretraining on vast and diverse vision-language-action data instills robust, generalizable representations in the VLA's backbone that are resilient to interference from new tasks. This reduces the gradient conflict between old and new tasks that typically causes catastrophic forgetting. The model’s prior knowledge acts as a stable foundation; even if task-specific heads drift, the core VL encoder retains sufficient information to recover performance quickly with minimal replay or finetuning. In contrast, non-pretrained models lack this foundation and must overwrite parameters to learn new tasks, leading to severe forgetting.

## Foundational Learning
- **Vision-Language-Action Models (VLAs):** Large models that map visual input and language instructions to robot actions. *Why needed:* They are the subject of the study; their pretrained nature is hypothesized to confer forgetting resistance. *Quick check:* Can the model follow novel language instructions for visual scenes without task-specific finetuning?
- **Continual/Lifelong Learning:** Setting where a model learns a sequence of tasks without catastrophic forgetting. *Why needed:* Defines the problem scope. *Quick check:* Measure performance on all previous tasks after learning a new one.
- **Catastrophic Forgetting:** The tendency of neural networks to lose previously acquired knowledge upon learning new information. *Why needed:* Core challenge being addressed. *Quick check:* Sharp drop in old-task performance after new-task training.
- **Experience Replay (ER):** A continual learning technique that stores samples from previous tasks and mixes them into current training batches. *Why needed:* Primary method used to mitigate forgetting. *Quick check:* Does replay buffer size correlate with reduced forgetting?
- **Negative Backward Transfer (NBT):** Metric quantifying forgetting: average performance decrease on old tasks after learning new ones. *Why needed:* Key evaluation metric. *Quick check:* Compute (old-task performance before new task) minus (after), average over all old tasks.
- **Parameter-Efficient Finetuning (e.g., LoRA):** Updating only a small subset of model parameters during adaptation. *Why needed:* Used to adapt large VLAs efficiently. *Quick check:* Verify only low-rank adapters and specific heads are trainable.

## Architecture Onboarding
Component map for a typical VLA (e.g., Pi0):
Vision Encoder (frozen/partially updated) -> Vision-Language Fusion (frozen/partially updated) -> Language Encoder (frozen/partially updated) -> Action Decoder/Head (finetuned/LoRA)

Critical path: Input RGB images and proprioception are encoded by vision backbone; language instruction is encoded; cross-modal fusion produces a representation; action head decodes robot actions. During continual learning, only a subset of parameters (e.g., LoRA adapters in fusion and action head) are updated per task.

Design tradeoffs: Using a frozen, pretrained VL backbone preserves general knowledge but limits adaptation; partial updates (LoRA) balance plasticity and stability. Full finetuning risks catastrophic forgetting but may allow better task specialization.

Failure signatures: 1) VLA fails to learn new task (current-task SR stays low). 2) VLA exhibits high NBT even with replay—likely incorrect replay mixture or buffer size. 3) Non-pretrained baseline shows >0.5 NBT—expected, but if near zero, suggests replay implementation error.

First three experiments to run:
1. **Buffer size ablation:** Train Pi0 on LIBERO-Spatial with replay buffer sizes {0%, 2%, 10%, 20%} and plot NBT vs buffer size. Expected: sharp drop in NBT from 0% to 2%, then flat.
2. **LoRA rank sensitivity:** Vary LoRA rank (e.g., 4, 8, 16, 32) on Pi0’s language and action heads with 2% buffer. Expected: forgetting resistance robust to rank; too low rank may hurt current-task learning.
3. **Task order sensitivity:** Shuffle the order of the 10 tasks in one suite and recompute NBT and recovery efficiency. Expected: low forgetting across orders, but positive backward transfer may depend on order.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- How does the scale and diversity of pretraining data affect forgetting resistance in VLAs during continual learning?
- Do the findings on VLA forgetting resistance generalize to real-world robotic systems with noise and non-stationarity?
- What specific internal mechanisms in the VLA's vision-language backbone enable rapid recovery of seemingly forgotten tasks?
- How sensitive is VLA forgetting resistance to the order and similarity of tasks in the sequence?

## Limitations
- Exact model configuration details (LoRA ranks, frozen layers, Qwen variant) are missing, hindering precise replication.
- Statistical reporting is unclear (number of random seeds, error bars), limiting confidence in result significance.
- The LIBERO filtering process is undocumented; using raw data may alter baseline performance.
- Evaluation protocol specifics (rollouts per task, success threshold) are omitted, risking metric drift.
- Fixed task sequence may mask order effects that could influence forgetting.

## Confidence
- Near-zero forgetting with minimal ER: **Medium** (strong trend but reproducibility issues).
- Pretraining reduces forgetting in low-data regimes: **Medium** (buffer-size trend clear but limited to one suite).
- Rapid recovery via finetuning: **Medium** (recovery experiment details sparse).
