# SortScrews: A Dataset and Baseline for Real-time Screw Classification

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2603.13027
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.13027
- **Reference Count:** 2
- **Primary Result:** ResNet-18 achieves 96.4% validation accuracy vs. EfficientNet-B0's 86.2% on a 560-image screw classification dataset.

## Executive Summary
This paper introduces SortScrews, a new dataset aimed at addressing the scarcity of publicly available datasets for industrial screw classification, particularly in controlled single-object scenarios. The dataset consists of 560 standardized RGB images (512×512 pixels) depicting six screw types and a background class, captured under mild variations in lighting and perspective. The authors provide a reusable data collection script to facilitate similar hardware dataset creation and establish baseline performance using transfer learning with EfficientNet-B0 and ResNet-18. Key findings show that ResNet-18 significantly outperforms EfficientNet-B0 (96.4% vs. 86.2% validation accuracy), suggesting that under tightly controlled acquisition conditions, effective learning is possible even with very small datasets. A failure analysis highlights persistent confusion between visually similar screw categories, underscoring the fine-grained recognition challenges inherent to the task.

## Method Summary
The authors constructed the SortScrews dataset using a reproducible script that captures images of individual screws against a consistent background under mild lighting and perspective variations. The dataset contains 560 images evenly distributed across seven classes (six screw types plus background). For baselines, they employed transfer learning: two standard convolutional architectures (ResNet-18 and EfficientNet-B0) were initialized with ImageNet pre-trained weights and fine-tuned on the SortScrews training set. Model performance was evaluated using validation accuracy, with no explicit test set separation mentioned. The training procedure involved standard hyperparameters (e.g., SGD optimizer, cross-entropy loss) without extensive augmentation, reflecting the controlled data collection premise.

## Key Results
- ResNet-18 achieves 96.4% validation accuracy, while EfficientNet-B0 reaches 86.2% under identical training conditions.
- Systematic confusion occurs primarily between visually similar screw categories (e.g., different head types or drive styles), indicating fine-grained discrimination remains challenging.
- The results suggest that highly controlled image acquisition can enable effective model training with very small datasets (560 images) via transfer learning.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The paper's core claim is that meticulously controlled data collection—minimizing variability in lighting, viewpoint, and background—reduces the complexity of the visual recognition task. This controlled environment compress the necessary feature space, allowing transfer learning from large-scale datasets (ImageNet) to adapt quickly to a small target dataset. The mild variations present are sufficient to teach the model essential invariances (e.g., slight rotation, illumination change) without requiring massive data volumes. Essentially, the dataset's design reduces the domain gap between pre-training and fine-tuning, making the small scale adequate for learning discriminative features.

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component Map (ResNet-18):**  
Input -> Conv7x7/stride2 -> BatchNorm -> ReLU -> MaxPool -> [BasicBlock(64) x2] -> [BasicBlock(128) x2] -> [BasicBlock(256) x2] -> [BasicBlock(512) x2] -> AvgPool -> FC(7) -> Output  

**Component Map (EfficientNet-B0):**  
Input -> StemConv3x3 -> [MBConv1(k3x3,s1) x1] -> [MBConv6(k3x3,s2) x2] -> [MBConv6(k5x5,s2) x2] -> [MBConv6(k3x3,s2) x1] -> [MBConv6(k3x3,s1) x1] -> [MBConv6(k5x5,s1) x1] -> HeadConv1x1 -> AvgPool -> FC(7) -> Output  

**Critical Path:** Forward pass sequentially through all convolutional blocks, ending with global average pooling and a final fully-connected layer for 7-way classification.  

**Design Tradeoffs:**  
- *ResNet-18:* Simpler, deeper residual design; less prone to vanishing gradients; potentially more robust to small datasets due to fewer specialized operations (e.g., no depthwise convolutions).  
- *EfficientNet-B0:* Uses compound scaling and MBConv blocks for parameter efficiency; but its squeeze-and-excitation and depthwise convolutions may introduce biases that require more data to learn effectively, possibly explaining its underperformance on the tiny, uniform dataset.  

**Failure Signatures:**  
- EfficientNet-B0's 10% lower accuracy suggests its inductive biases (e.g., mobile-friendly depthwise convolutions) are mismatched to the low-variance, fine-grained dataset. It may overfit to subtle dataset artifacts or fail to capture the necessary texture/edge features due to aggressive downsampling.  
- Both models likely fail on screws with occlusion, debris, or extreme angles not present in training. Confusion matrix would show errors between classes with similar profiles (e.g., flat-head vs. round-head screws).  

**Exactly 3 First Experiments:**  
1. **Augmentation Sensitivity Test:** Train both models with mild augmentations (rotation ±15°, slight brightness jitter) to see if EfficientNet-B0's accuracy improves, indicating it benefits from more "effective" data diversity.  
2. **Feature Visualization:** Compare first-layer filter activations and Grad-CAM heatmaps for both models to see if ResNet-18 learns more discriminative low-level features (edges, corners) critical for screw parts.  
3. **Latency Measurement:** Benchmark inference time (ms/image) on a representative industrial edge device (e.g., Jetson Nano) to validate "real-time" claims; ResNet-18 may actually be faster despite similar parameter count due to simpler operations.

## Limitations
- Dataset scale is extremely small (560 images) and lacks real-world diversity (e.g., glare, occlusion, varied backgrounds, wear), limiting claims about generalizability.
- The large performance gap between ResNet-18 and EfficientNet-B0 is unexplained and may reflect overfitting to the narrow dataset or architectural mismatches not investigated.
- The "real-time" assertion is unsupported by any latency or throughput benchmarks; the background class may artificially simplify the task.

## Confidence
- **High Confidence:** The SortScrews dataset exists as described (560 images, 7 classes, 512×512 RGB); the reported validation accuracies (96.4% for ResNet-18, 86.2% for EfficientNet-B0) are reproducible from the given training setup.
- **Medium Confidence:** The conclusion that controlled acquisition enables learning with small datasets is plausible but not rigorously evidenced—no ablation on dataset size or variation degree is provided.
- **Low Confidence:** Broader claims about applicability to industrial automation and the inherent suitability of the tested architectures. The failure analysis hints at fine-grained challenges, but the dataset's constraints may prevent meaningful progress on them.

## Next Checks
1. **External Robustness Test:** Evaluate the published ResNet-18 and EfficientNet-B0 checkpoints on an independently collected, more diverse screw image set (varying lighting, angles, backgrounds, contaminants) to quantify performance degradation.
2. **Architectural Ablation with Augmentation:** Re-train both models with controlled augmentations (simulated glare, rotation, blur) to diagnose whether EfficientNet-B0's lower accuracy stems from its inductive biases or the original training's lack of augmentation.
3. **Real-Time Benchmark:** Measure and report actual inference latency (ms) and throughput (FPS) for both models on standardized industrial hardware (e.g., a common edge PC or embedded device) to validate the "real-time" claim.
