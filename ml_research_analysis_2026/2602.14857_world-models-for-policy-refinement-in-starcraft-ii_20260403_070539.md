# World Models for Policy Refinement in StarCraft II

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID**: 2602.14857
- **Source URL**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.14857
- **Reference count**: 40
- **Primary result**: Integrating a learnable world model into LLM decision loops yields up to 30% win-rate gains and significant macro-management improvements in StarCraft II.

## Executive Summary
This paper introduces StarWM, a world model for StarCraft II that predicts future game observations conditioned on actions. Trained via supervised fine‑tuning on a dataset of replay‑derived instruction‑tuning pairs, StarWM factorizes observations into five semantic textual modules to capture hybrid dynamics. Integrated into an LLM‑based decision loop (Generate–Simulate–Refine), StarWM enables foresight‑driven policy refinement. Offline evaluation shows substantial reductions in resource prediction error (SMAPE 0.19/0.09) and accurate queue and unit trajectory forecasting. Online testing against Blizzard’s built‑in AI yields consistent win‑rate gains of 15–30% and notable macro‑management improvements, including reduced supply blocks and better resource conversion.

## Method Summary
The authors construct SC2‑Dynamics‑50k, a dataset of 50,000 samples pairing current observations and action sequences (τ=5 s) with subsequent observations, extracted from replays of a rule‑based bot playing against built‑in AI. Observations are represented as five textual modules (game info, production queue, owned units, owned structures, visible hostiles). A Qwen3‑8B language model is fine‑tuned with LoRA on this dataset to produce StarWM, an action‑conditioned world model. The trained StarWM is embedded into a Generate–Simulate–Refine loop: an LLM policy first proposes an action, StarWM simulates the resulting future observation, and the policy then revises its action based on this predicted outcome. This integration extends the SC2Arena framework, enabling online evaluation against StarCraft II’s built‑in AI at various difficulty levels.

## Key Results
- Offline prediction: 60–65% reduction in resource SMAPE (to 0.19/0.09), Queue F1 0.92, Progress MAE 0.43%, Self‑unit HP MAE 4.15%, Self‑side Augmented Wasserstein Distance 3.46 (~60% improvement over baselines).
- Online performance: Win‑rate gains of 30% (Hard LV5), 15% (Harder LV6), and 30% (VeryHard LV7) using a 32B policy; Supply Block Rate reduced by 15–53%; Resource Conversion Rate increased by 23–49%; Kill‑Loss Ratio improved by 21%.
- Ablation: Gains stem from accurate simulation rather than additional inference compute; zero‑shot world model provides no benefit.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1: Accurate Short‑Horizon Prediction
**Claim**: StarWM accurately predicts short‑horizon future observations by learning deterministic SC2 dynamics.  
**Mechanism**: It is trained via supervised fine‑tuning on the SC2‑Dynamics‑50k dataset, which pairs current observations and action sequences with ground‑truth future observations. The structured textual representation (five semantic modules) decomposes the hybrid dynamics into manageable sub‑tasks, allowing the LLM to internalize rules such as resource accumulation, queue progression, and unit movement.  
**Evidence**: Nearly 60% improvements in resource prediction accuracy; Queue F1 0.92; detailed metrics in Section 4.2.  
**Break condition**: If game mechanics are patched or balance changes occur, the learned dynamics may become stale, requiring retraining.

### Mechanism 2: Foresight‑Driven Policy Refinement
**Claim**: The Generate–Simulate–Refine loop uses StarWM’s predictions to revise policy outputs, improving macro‑management and tactical risk assessment.  
**Mechanism**: The policy first generates an initial action. StarWM simulates the resulting future observation (e.g., resource levels, supply, combat outcomes). The policy then re‑evaluates its action conditioned on this predicted future, enabling preemptive adjustments (e.g., avoid building a Supply Depot that would starve minerals, retreat from a bad trade).  
**Evidence**: Online win‑rate gains (30%/15%/30%); macro improvements (supply block rate reduction up to ~53%); ablation (Table 3) shows trained world model yields extra gains beyond self‑reflection.  
**Break condition**: If StarWM’s predictions are systematically biased or too inaccurate, they could mislead the policy and degrade performance.

### Mechanism 3: Conservative Tactical Risk Assessment under Partial Observability
**Claim**: Under partial observability, StarWM learns data‑driven patterns of opponent presence and behavior, enabling more cautious tactical decisions.  
**Mechanism**: Trained on human/bot replays, the model captures statistical regularities (e.g., enemy units often occupy certain map regions when the player scouts). When the player’s units move into unobserved areas, StarWM may predict enemy entities there, implicitly signaling potential threats.  
**Evidence**: Case study (Figure 5) shows StarWM predicting enemies in fogged areas; offline results show self‑side AWD improvement (3.46 vs 9.79) while enemy‑side AWD worsens slightly, indicating the model prioritizes self‑consistency and learns to anticipate hidden opponents.  
**Break condition**: In match‑ups with novel opponent strategies outside the training distribution, predictions may become unreliable and cause over‑cautious or erroneous revisions.

## Architecture Onboarding
### Component Map
Replay collection -> Observation parsing -> SC2‑Dynamics‑50k dataset -> StarWM training (Qwen3‑8B + LoRA) -> Generate–Simulate–Refine loop -> SC2Arena online integration.

### Critical Path
1. Collect 100 TvT replays at Harder (LV6) and VeryHard (LV7).
2. Parse replays into tick‑level structured text observations.
3. Generate `(current_obs, action_seq, future_obs)` samples with τ=5 s sliding window.
4. Split dataset (train/val/test = 8:1:1) and perform SFT on Qwen3‑8B via LoRA.
5. Evaluate StarWM offline using multi‑dimensional metrics.
6. Integrate trained StarWM into SC2Arena: add simulation and refinement steps to policy inference.
7. Run online matches against built‑in AI at LV5/LV6/LV7; record primary and secondary metrics.
8. Conduct ablations (e.g., zero‑shot WM, self‑reflection only).

### Design Tradeoffs
- *Text vs vector*: Textual format is LLM‑compatible but may lose fine‑grained numeric precision; mitigated by supervised tuning.
- *Prediction horizon*: Fixed 5‑second window balances computational cost and foresight; longer horizons risk error accumulation.
- *Single‑frame*: No temporal history simplifies training but limits opponent intent modeling.
- *LoRA*: Reduces compute and memory but may cap model capacity for complex dynamics.
- *Partial observability*: Model attempts to predict hidden enemies; hallucinations can be beneficial (caution) or harmful (false alarms).

### Failure Signatures
- High enemy‑side AWD (>> baseline) → poor opponent modeling.
- Resource SMAPE > 0.5 → unreliable economic forecasts.
- Action revision rate near 0% → world model not influencing policy.
- Online win‑rate drop vs baseline → simulation misleading the policy.
- Degradation on new race/map → limited generalization.

### First 3 Experiments
1. *Offline evaluation*: Train StarWM and compare prediction errors (SMAPE, F1, AWD) against zero‑shot Qwen3 and static bias on test set.
2. *Online main test*: Deploy StarWM‑Agent (8B/32B) against LV5/LV6/LV7 built‑in AI; report win rates, supply block rate, resource conversion rate, kill‑loss ratio, valid action rate.
3. *Ablation study*: Sequentially remove simulation (self‑reflection only) and replace StarWM with zero‑shot WM; measure impact on win‑rate and action revision.

## Limitations
- **Dataset & Generalization**: Trained on limited race/difficulty replays; performance may degrade on patches, new maps, or novel strategies.
- **Textual Representation Trade‑off**: Factorization into semantic modules may sacrifice fine‑grained numeric precision, potentially harming edge‑case predictions.
- **Evaluation Scope**: Online tests only against Blizzard’s built‑in AI, not human players, limiting claims about real‑world adaptability.

## Confidence
- Offline prediction accuracy: High
- Online macro‑management improvements: Medium
- Tactical risk assessment via hidden‑unit prediction: Low

## Next Checks
1. Human‑player evaluation: Deploy StarWM‑Agent in competitive ladder matches or against a diverse pool of human players to measure real‑world transfer of win‑rate and macro improvements.
2. Distribution shift robustness: Test StarWM on (a) newer SC2 patches/balance changes, (b) unseen race matchups (e.g., ZvT), and (c) custom maps to quantify prediction error spikes and online performance decay.
3. Hallucination analysis: Ablate the model’s ability to predict hidden units (e.g., by masking fog‑of‑war data during training) and measure the impact on tactical metrics (e.g., kill‑loss ratio, supply block rate) to isolate the benefit/cost of conservative foresight.
