# Finetuning Large Language Models for Automated Depression Screening in Nigerian Pidgin English: GENSCORE Pilot Study

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2601.00004
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.00004
- **Reference Count:** 3
- **Primary Result:** Fine-tuned GPT-4.1 achieved 94.5% accuracy in PHQ-9 severity scoring from Nigerian Pidgin text, with 100% correct escalation for self-harm risk and top ratings for cultural appropriateness.

## Executive Summary
This pilot study addresses the critical shortage of depression screening tools in Nigerian Pidgin English, a low-resource language hindered by clinician shortages and stigma. Researchers created a dataset of 432 annotated audio responses to PHQ-9 prompts, capturing culturally specific idioms and slang. Three large language models (Phi-3, Gemma, GPT-4.1) were fine-tuned on this data with explicit safety protocols. GPT-4.1 demonstrated exceptional performance in accurately scoring depression severity, maintaining semantic alignment, and safely handling high-risk content. The results suggest that culturally and linguistically adapted LLMs can provide accurate, accessible, and safe automated mental health screening in resource-constrained settings.

## Method Summary
The study involved collecting 432 audio responses from Nigerian adults (aged 18–40) to prompts derived from the PHQ-9 questionnaire. Responses were transcribed, annotated with PHQ-9 severity scores (0–3), and interpreted for local slang and idiomatic expressions. Three models—Phi-3-mini-4k-instruct, Gemma-3-4B-it, and GPT-4.1—underwent supervised fine-tuning (SFT) on this instruction-formatted dataset. Models were constrained by system prompts specifying an empathetic Pidgin tone, strict non-diagnostic behavior, and crisis escalation protocols. Evaluation occurred on a held-out test set using quantitative metrics (PHQ-9 accuracy, BERTScore) and qualitative human assessment for clarity, relevance, and cultural appropriateness.

## Key Results
- GPT-4.1 achieved 94.5% PHQ-9 severity classification accuracy on the test set.
- Semantic alignment with human interpretations was high (BERTScore F1 = 0.95).
- The model attained 100% correct escalation for self-harm risk (PHQ-9 Item 9) and top ratings for clarity, relevance, and cultural appropriateness.
- Smaller models (Phi-3: 71.8%, Gemma) showed significantly lower performance, highlighting a scale effect.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1: Cultural-Linguistic Adaptation via Fine-Tuning
- **Claim:** Fine-tuning on a culturally and linguistically specific dataset enables accurate interpretation of Nigerian Pidgin expressions for depression symptoms.
- **Mechanism:** The model learns direct mappings between Pidgin idioms/metaphors (e.g., "my head dey heavy") and PHQ-9 symptom categories (e.g., fatigue) through supervised learning on annotated examples.
- **Core Assumption:** A model's pre-trained linguistic knowledge can be effectively adapted to a low-resource dialect with sufficient in-domain paired examples (responses + clinical labels).
- **Evidence Anchors:** Abstract reports fine-tuned models achieved 94.5% accuracy and 0.95 semantic alignment. Sections describe annotation of slang/idioms and mapping to clinical meanings. Broader NLP literature on Nigerian Pidgin challenges supports the need for specific adaptation.
- **Break Condition:** If annotated data fails to capture the full diversity of regional Pidgin idioms, model performance degrades on unseen expressions.

### Mechanism 2: Safety via Structured Prompting and Fine-Tuning
- **Claim:** Structured safety prompting combined with fine-tuning enforces safe, non-diagnostic behavior even when interpreting high-risk content.
- **Mechanism:** System prompts embed rules ("do not diagnose; provide supportive guidance") and crisis escalation protocols, which are reinforced during fine-tuning to override the model's default generative tendencies.
- **Core Assumption:** LLMs can reliably adhere to domain-specific safety constraints when these constraints are explicitly encoded in both training data (annotations) and inference-time prompts.
- **Evidence Anchors:** Abstract notes 100% correct escalation for self-harm risk. Sections describe prompt templates with explicit safety instructions and rules constraining models from diagnostic claims.
- **Break Condition:** If a user employs novel self-harm phrasing not covered in training data or prompts, the model may fail to escalate correctly.

### Mechanism 3: Model Scale Determines Performance on Nuanced Low-Resource Tasks
- **Claim:** Model scale (parameter size, multilingual pretraining) is a primary determinant of performance on this culturally nuanced, low-resource task.
- **Mechanism:** Larger models possess greater capacity to absorb fine-grained mappings between colloquial Pidgin, clinical constructs, and safety rules from a limited dataset, and have broader multilingual priors that aid generalization.
- **Core Assumption:** Performance gaps between models (Phi-3: 71.8% vs. GPT-4.1: 94.5%) are primarily attributable to inherent model capacity rather than just fine-tuning hyperparameters.
- **Evidence Anchors:** Abstract states GPT-4.1 outperformed smaller models. Sections contrast Phi-3's compact architecture with GPT-4.1's advanced generalization, noting the comparison was designed to isolate capacity effects.
- **Break Condition:** If a smaller model were fine-tuned on a significantly larger, more diverse dataset, the performance gap might narrow, challenging the primacy of scale.

## Foundational Learning
- **Concept: Low-Resource Language Adaptation**
  - **Why needed:** Nigerian Pidgin lacks standardized text corpora and NLP tools; models must overcome orthographic variability and scarcity of training data.
  - **Quick Check:** What are two specific linguistic features of Nigerian Pidgin that complicate standard English-trained LLMs' comprehension?
- **Concept: Cultural-Linguistic Nuance in Mental Health Expression**
  - **Why needed:** Emotional distress is expressed via culturally-specific idioms and metaphors; literal translation to PHQ-9 items fails.
  - **Quick Check:** Why is annotating slang/idioms (e.g., "no get ginger") a necessary step before model training?
- **Concept: Safety-Aligned LLM Deployment in Sensitive Domains**
  - **Why needed:** Incorrect scoring or unsafe responses in mental health contexts can cause harm; models must avoid diagnosis and handle crisis signals.
  - **Quick Check:** What is the difference between a model's *diagnostic* output and its *screening* output, and why is this distinction critical?

## Architecture Onboarding
1) Data Collection (GENCURATE platform → audio responses) -> 2) Preprocessing/Annotation (transcription → semantic labeling, idiom mapping) -> 3) Model Finetuning (SFT on annotated Q&A pairs) -> 4) Safety Layer (system prompts + rules) -> 5) Evaluation (quantitative metrics + human review)
- **Critical Path:** Data Annotation → Model SFT → Prompt Integration → Safety Evaluation. The quality of idiom-to-symptom annotation directly determines the upper bound of model learning.
- **Design Tradeoffs:** Model size vs. deployability (GPT-4.1 best but API-dependent; Gemma as open-source midpoint); dataset diversity vs. annotation cost (432 samples small, expansion risks inconsistency); strict safety prompts vs. conversational fluency.
- **Failure Signatures:** High BERTScore but low PHQ-9 accuracy (model paraphrases but misassigns severity); low cultural appropriateness despite high accuracy (outputs clinically correct but unnatural Pidgin); 100% escalation on training but <90% on test (overfitting to crisis phrases).
- **First 3 Experiments:**
  1. **Ablation: No fine-tuning.** Test if base models can score PHQ-9; expect very low accuracy/semantic alignment.
  2. **Idiom stress test.** Create a test set of pure idiomatic expressions; evaluate if semantic alignment drops.
  3. **Safety red-team.** Generate adversarial self-harm statements using novel metaphors; measure escalation failure rate.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- How does the limited size (432 samples) and single-language focus impact generalization across diverse Nigerian Pidgin dialects and evolving slang in real-world usage?
- What is the trade-off between model capacity (e.g., parameter count) and practical deployability (e.g., latency, cost) for achieving clinically acceptable PHQ-9 scoring accuracy in low-resource settings?
- Do end-users (Nigerian young adults) find the AI-mediated screening culturally appropriate, trustworthy, and preferable to traditional clinician-led screening, and does it affect help-seeking behavior?
- How robust are the safety protocols against adversarial or ambiguous inputs that could lead to missed self-harm escalations or inappropriate responses?
- Can the fine-tuning approach and safety framework be effectively adapted to other low-resource Nigerian languages (e.g., Yoruba, Hausa) without extensive re-engineering?

## Limitations
- The primary 432-sample dataset is not public, limiting assessment of its representativeness and annotation consistency.
- Exact OpenAI fine-tuning configuration for GPT-4.1 (API parameters, epoch count) is undisclosed, making reported 94.5% accuracy and 100% safety compliance non-replicable.
- Performance on unseen Pidgin idioms or regional variations is unevaluated, risking overfitting to the training distribution.
- Safety compliance (100% escalation) was tested on a fixed test set without adversarial construction, risking selection bias.

## Confidence
- **Core Task Performance (Accuracy/Semantic Alignment):** High Confidence. Directly reported metrics from a held-out test set are strong evidence, though dependent on dataset representativeness.
- **Safety & Ethics (Escalation, Non-Diagnostic Adherence):** Medium Confidence. Results are stated as perfect, but evaluation design (test set size, construction) is unclear, creating selection bias risk.
- **Generalizability & Deployment (Beyond Study Sample, Model Choice):** Low Confidence. Claims about broader applicability to other dialects or settings are speculative, based on a single narrow dataset and unverified model comparisons.

## Next Checks
1.  **Dataset Replication & Blind Test:** Independently collect a new Pidgin response dataset (n≥100) using the same PHQ-9 prompts from a demographically distinct Nigerian cohort. Have experts blind-annotate it. Use this as an external validation set to test the published model's generalization.
2.  **Adversarial Safety Stress-Test:** Generate 50+ novel, culturally-grounded self-harm statements using rare Pidgin metaphors. Systematically evaluate the fine-tuned model's escalation rate on this out-of-distribution set to probe safety robustness.
3.  **Cross-Dialect Baseline Comparison:** Test the best-performing fine-tuned model (GPT-4.1) without further tuning on a comparable dataset from a distinct Nigerian language (e.g., Yoruba). A significant accuracy drop would confirm high specificity of cultural-linguistic tuning.
