# CitiLink-Minutes: A Multilayer Annotated Dataset of Municipal Meeting Minutes

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2602.12137
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.12137
- **Reference count:** 40
- **Primary result:** Introduces a manually annotated, de-identified dataset of 120 European Portuguese municipal meeting minutes with four annotation layers and establishes strong baselines (BERTimbau F1 >0.81 across key tasks).

## Executive Summary
CitiLink-Minutes is a novel resource addressing the scarcity of structured data for NLP on local governance. It provides 120 European Portuguese municipal meeting minutes with dense, double-annotated, multi-layer annotations covering metadata, discussion subjects, voting procedures, and personal information (de-identified). The dataset, spanning 1M+ tokens and six municipalities, enables structured information extraction from bureaucratic text. Fine-tuned domain models (BERTimbau) achieve high performance on three core tasks: Metadata Extraction (micro-F1 0.959), Vote Identification (macro-F1 0.814), and Topic Classification (macro-F1 0.642), demonstrating the dataset's utility and establishing robust benchmarks.

## Method Summary
The core methodological innovation is a four-layer annotation schema designed to capture the heterogeneous structure of meeting minutes. Documents were annotated in INCEpTION with linked entities and relations across layers: personal information (L1, de-identified), metadata (L2), subjects of discussion (L3), and voting events (L4). A temporal data split (train on older meetings, test on newer) simulates real-world deployment. Baselines compare a fine-tuned, domain-adapted Portuguese encoder (BERTimbau Large) against a few-shot generative model (Gemini-2.5-Pro) for span extraction and classification tasks, using standard BERT fine-tuning hyperparameters.

## Key Results
- Dataset contains 120 minutes, 20,375 entities, and 11,162 relations across four semantic layers.
- BERTimbau fine-tuning achieves micro-F1 0.959 on Metadata Extraction and macro-F1 0.814 on Vote Identification.
- For the 22-label Topic Classification, BERTimbau reaches macro-F1 0.642 and Hamming Loss 0.047.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1
**Claim:** The multilayer annotation schema enables structured information extraction from heterogeneous municipal minutes.
**Mechanism:** Manual annotation transforms unstructured bureaucratic narratives into a machine-readable format where key decision-making elements (e.g., who voted on what, when, where) are explicitly linked across complementary layers. This provides a clear supervised signal for models to learn domain-specific patterns without parsing from scratch.
**Break condition:** If critical information (e.g., causal links between subjects) is not captured, or if inter-annotator agreement for key layers is too low (<0.6), the structured signal becomes noisy or incomplete.

### Mechanism 2
**Claim:** A fine-tuned, domain-adapted BERT model (BERTimbau) establishes a strong baseline by leveraging in-domain linguistic patterns.
**Mechanism:** Continued pre-training or fine-tuning on this specific domain allows the encoder to internalize the vocabulary, syntax, and formulistic phrases (e.g., "deliberou por unanimidade") unique to Portuguese council minutes. This addresses the distributional shift from general Portuguese text.
**Break condition:** If the domain shift is minimal (highly templated minutes), non-fine-tuned models might suffice, reducing the annotation effort's value. Performance collapse on unseen municipalities would indicate overfitting to local style.

### Mechanism 3
**Claim:** A temporally ordered data split creates a realistic evaluation for ongoing civic monitoring.
**Mechanism:** Chronologically reserving the most recent minutes for testing measures a model's ability to generalize to future meetings, testing robustness to linguistic and topical drift over time, rather than just static stylistic variance.
**Break condition:** If minutes are highly templated with minimal temporal evolution, the split's difficulty is artificial and performance gaps may not reflect true generalization challenges.

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map:** Raw minute document (PDF/HTML) -> Preprocessed plain text -> INCEpTION annotation backend (four-layer schema) -> Structured JSON (entities/links) -> Chronological data split (72 train, 24 val, 24 test) -> Preprocessing for task (span-to-BIO or text-to-labels) -> BERTimbau model head (token or sequence classification) -> Evaluation metrics (F1, HL, AP).
- **Critical path:** 1. Load JSON for a minute and extract `full_text` with target layer annotations. 2. For extraction tasks: align character offsets to tokenizer and convert spans to BIO tags. 3. For classification: extract subject `text` and multi-hot `topics` vector. 4. Train BERTimbau with AdamW (lr=5e-5, batch=16) for 10 epochs, validate, and evaluate on test set using provided metrics.
- **Design tradeoffs:**
  - **Encoder vs. Generative:** Encoder (BERTimbau) needs fine-tuning and span setup but yields higher extraction F1. Generative (Gemini) requires prompt engineering and has zero-shot potential but lower performance. Tradeoff: development cost vs. performance.
  - **Layer Independence:** Treating layers as separate tasks simplifies modeling but ignores cross-layer dependencies (e.g., Subject linked to Vote). Joint modeling could improve results at complexity cost.
  - **De-identification:** Replacing PI spans with `***` protects privacy but destroys PI entity type info, preventing PI recognition as a task.
- **Failure signatures:**
  - Low IAA (<0.6) on `theme` attribute indicates ambiguous guidelines → model will struggle with this sub-task.
  - Severe class imbalance (e.g., rare `non-voter` entity) and its low IAA (0.41) predicts poor recall for minority classes.
  - Significant performance drop when testing on a municipality not seen in training indicates overfitting to municipal style.
- **First 3 experiments:**
  1. Reproduce Metadata Extraction: Fine-tune BERTimbau on L2 `metadata` entities using provided splits; verify micro-F1 ~0.959.
  2. Vote Identification Ablation: Remove `voting_evidence` entity from training to measure its impact on `Voter-*` entity performance.
  3. Topic Classification Error Analysis: Compute per-topic F1 on the 22-topic task; identify worst-performing topics and inspect their lexical ambiguity/co-occurrence.

## Limitations
- Dataset scope limited to six municipalities, risking overfitting to local stylistic quirks and limiting generalization.
- Low inter-annotator agreement for `theme` (0.48) and `non-voter` (0.41) indicates ambiguous guidelines, injecting noise into training signal.
- Temporal split assumes significant linguistic/topic drift (2021–2024); if minutes are highly templated, the evaluation difficulty may be artificial.

## Confidence
- **Multilayer schema efficacy:** Medium (novelty claimed, but causal evidence is internal; no ablation removing layers).
- **BERTimbau baseline strength:** High (consistent high metrics and solid IAA for most layers support reliability).
- **Temporal split validity:** Medium (split clearly described, but drift magnitude assumed, not measured).
