# Predicting Time Pressure of Powered Two-Wheeler Riders for Proactive Safety Interventions

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2601.03173
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.03173
- **Reference Count:** 40
- **Primary Result:** MTPS achieves 91.53% accuracy and 98.93% ROC AUC in classifying rider time pressure states from multivariate telemetry.

## Executive Summary
This research tackles the critical gap in predicting cognitive time pressure (TP) among powered two-wheeler (PTW) riders to enable proactive safety systems. The authors introduce a novel, large-scale simulator dataset comprising over 129,000 multivariate time-series sequences from 153 rides under controlled No, Low, and High TP conditions. They propose MotoTimePressure (MTPS), a compact deep learning model that fuses convolutional layers, dual-stage temporal attention, and Squeeze-and-Excitation blocks. MTPS significantly outperforms eight baseline models in TP classification. Furthermore, integrating its probabilistic TP predictions into an Informer-based collision risk predictor boosts accuracy, demonstrating TP's value as a leading risk indicator. The study paves the way for graded, context-aware intelligent transportation system (ITS) interventions like adaptive alerts and speed guidance.

## Method Summary
The core methodological contribution is the creation of a comprehensive simulator dataset to capture rider behavior under experimentally induced time pressure conditions. Telemetry data includes 63 features spanning kinematics, control inputs, traffic violations, and contextual variables, sampled at high frequency. The proposed MTPS architecture processes this sequential data through a hierarchical pipeline: initial 1D convolutional layers extract local maneuver patterns, followed by two multi-head attention blocks to model long-range temporal dependencies, and a Squeeze-and-Excitation block for dynamic feature recalibration. The final classification layer maps the processed representation to one of three TP states. The model is trained with standard stochastic optimization (Adam) and cross-entropy loss, using an 80/20 stratified train-test split.

## Key Results
- MTPS attains 91.53% accuracy and 98.93% ROC AUC, surpassing eight comparative baselines.
- Fusing MTPS’s predicted TP into a collision risk predictor improves accuracy from 91.25% to 93.51%, nearing oracle performance (93.72%) with ground-truth TP.
- The model is highly compact (172K parameters, 2.16 MB), making it viable for on-vehicle edge deployment.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
**Claim:** Induced time pressure produces a consistent, measurable behavioral signature in rider telemetry, creating a discriminative signal for classification.
**Mechanism:** Cognitive time pressure narrows attention and reduces situational awareness, leading to quantifiable degradations in vehicle kinematics (e.g., 48% higher speeds, 36.4% greater speed variability), control inputs (e.g., 36% more sudden braking), and violation rates (e.g., 58% more risky turns). These changes are systematically captured by the 63-dimensional feature set.
**Evidence Anchors:** Statistical evidence from controlled experiments (Table V) confirms significant differences across No, Low, and High TP conditions for key metrics. The success of MTPS on the held-out test set implies the signal is learnable and robust within the simulator domain.

### Mechanism 2
**Claim:** The MTPS architecture is specifically designed to decode the latent TP state from high-dimensional, multi-scale temporal patterns in the telemetry sequence.
**Mechanism:** Stacked Conv1D layers capture localized micro-patterns (e.g., throttle impulses). Dual-stage multi-head attention identifies and weights globally salient temporal segments (e.g., critical intersection approaches). The Squeeze-and-Excitation block recalibrates channel-wise feature importance, suppressing noise and amplifying TP-indicative signals (e.g., speed variability).
**Evidence Anchors:** The architectural design is detailed in Section IV.B. Ablation studies (Table IX) show that removing any key component (SE block, attention layers, residual normalization) degrades accuracy by 0.43%–1.11%, confirming their integrated necessity for optimal performance on the dataset.

### Mechanism 3
**Claim:** Thresholded probabilistic TP outputs serve as a semantically meaningful, actionable risk index that enhances downstream prediction and enables graded interventions.
**Mechanism:** The calibrated probability scores map onto a cognitive stress continuum. When concatenated to the original feature vector, this latent variable provides orthogonal, leading-indicator information to models like Informer that rely solely on kinematics. Discrete states (NTP/LTP/HTP) allow for risk-tiered ITS responses (e.g., gentle speed guidance vs. urgent haptic alerts).
**Evidence Anchors:** Integrating predicted TP into Informer improves collision risk prediction accuracy (Section VII.A). Thresholds are derived from precision-recall trade-offs and calibration curves (Figs. 7b, 8) to balance sensitivity and specificity for intervention safety.

## Foundational Learning

- **Multi-Scale Temporal Modeling:** Rider TP manifests across timescales—from momentary micro-maneuvers to extended route segments. A single-layer recurrent model often fails to jointly capture short-term impulses and long-term trends. *Quick check:* Why might a single LSTM layer struggle to learn both a sharp brake event and a gradual acceleration pattern correlated with building time pressure?

- **Feature Recalibration via Squeeze-and-Excitation (SE):** Not all 63 input features are equally relevant for TP detection (e.g., steady fuel economy vs. dynamic speed variance). SE blocks learn to suppress uninformative channels and amplify discriminative ones, effectively performing a learned feature selection. *Quick check:* If the SE block were removed, which type of features would become more noisy in the representation, and how would that likely affect class separation?

- **Calibration for Safety-Critical Thresholding:** ITS interventions require reliable confidence estimates. A model outputting P(HTP)=0.9 must be correct ~90% of the time to avoid alert fatigue (false positives) or missed warnings (false negatives). Proper calibration ensures threshold-based triggers meet operational safety requirements. *Quick check:* How does using the Wilson score interval for precision/recall confidence bounds provide more conservative estimates than a normal approximation, especially with rare class events?

## Architecture Onboarding

**Component map:**  
Input (Batch, Timesteps, 63) -> 2× Conv1D (64,128 filters, kernel=3, ReLU) -> Multi-Head Attention Block 1 (4 heads, dim=32) + Residual & LayerNorm -> Multi-Head Attention Block 2 + Residual & LayerNorm -> Squeeze-and-Excitation Block (reduction r=16) -> Global Average Pooling -> Linear (128->3) -> Softmax

**Critical path:** Input → Conv1D → MHA1 → MHA2 → SE → Global Pool → Classifier. Residual connections around the attention blocks are critical for stable gradient flow during training.

**Design tradeoffs:**
- **Complexity vs. Edge Deployment:** Dual MHA blocks increase computational load versus a simpler LSTM, but the tradeoff is justified by the SOTA accuracy. The final model size (2.16 MB) remains suitable for embedded systems.
- **Interpretability vs. Performance:** SE provides channel-level feature importance, but per-timestep attention weights are not directly used for intervention logic, limiting fine-grained explainability.
- **Sequence Length:** The model uses a fixed input window length (T unspecified). Longer windows capture more context but increase memory use; the chosen T balances pattern capture with efficiency.

**Failure signatures:**
- **Calibration drift:** Probability outputs become over/under-confident on new data. Monitor calibration curves (like Fig. 7b) for systematic deviation from the diagonal.
- **Class imbalance shift:** Real-world HTP events may be rarer than in simulation. Watch for drops in per-class F1-score, especially for the minority HTP class.
- **Feature distribution shift:** Changes in sensor suite or riding environment alter feature covariances. A significant accuracy drop when using the full 63-feature set indicates a shift; feature importance analysis (like Table XI) may need updating.

**First 3 experiments:**
1. **Baseline Reproduction:** Train MTPS on the provided 80/20 stratified split. Verify test accuracy >91% and ROC AUC >98.5%. Confirm confusion matrix aligns with Fig. 6.
2. **Ablation Validation:** Sequentially remove the SE block, one MHA block, and residual connections. Expect accuracy degradation of ~0.6%, ~0.8%, and ~0.4% respectively (per Table IX), confirming each component's contribution.
3. **Threshold Calibration Test:** On the test set, apply proposed thresholds (e.g., P(HTP)≥0.70 for "Critical"). Validate that precision for HTP is ~99% with a tight Wilson confidence interval. If precision falls below 95%, the threshold is likely too low for safe deployment.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- **Open Question 1:** Does MTPS generalize to naturalistic riding conditions outside the simulator? The study acknowledges future validation on large-scale naturalistic data is needed, as the simulator lacks real-world unpredictability.
- **Open Question 2:** Can integrating multimodal signals (e.g., physiological, visual) enhance TP prediction robustness? Section VIII-A explicitly states this as future work to strengthen robustness beyond vehicle telematics.
- **Open Question 3:** Are the proposed TP thresholds effective for safe and timely ITS interventions? Thresholds are simulation-calibrated; their real-world efficacy in balancing false alarms and misses remains unevaluated.
- **Open Question 4:** How does MTPS perform across diverse rider demographics, vehicle types, and cultures? The study sampled only male Indian riders; domain adaptation techniques are suggested to address potential generalizability gaps.

## Limitations
- **Simulator-to-reality gap:** The behavioral TP signature is established in a controlled simulator; performance may drop significantly in naturalistic riding with unmodeled confounders (traffic, weather, varied infrastructure).
- **Label fidelity:** TP states were induced via experimental instructions, which may not perfectly mirror the internally generated, context-specific cognitive pressure of real riders, introducing potential label noise.
- **Individual variability:** The model assumes a consistent behavioral signature across riders, but coping strategies, skill levels, and risk tolerance could create high-variance subgroups with degraded performance.

## Confidence
- **Behavioral Signature ( measurable degradations in kinematics/controls under TP):** High Confidence. Strong statistical evidence from controlled experiments (Table V) supports the claim.
- **MTPS Architecture Efficacy (hierarchical design captures TP signal):** Medium Confidence. Ablation studies validate components *on the simulator test set*, but confidence is lower regarding necessity for real-world, out-of-distribution sequences.
- **TP as Actionable Risk Index (thresholded TP improves collision prediction and enables graded interventions):** Medium-Low Confidence. Integration results show correlation, but causal proof of orthogonal informative value and safe real-world intervention efficacy are limited.

## Next Checks
1. **Naturalistic Data Transfer Test:** evaluate MTPS on a small, real-world PTW dataset with TP proxies (e.g., post-hoc surveys). A >5% accuracy drop would confirm the simulator-to-reality gap as a primary blocker.
2. **Subpopulation Performance Audit:** stratify test performance by rider attributes (experience, age). Identify subgroups with F1-score <0.85 to pinpoint where personalization or cohort-specific training is needed.
3. **Intervention Threshold Stress Test:** simulate deployment using test-set predictions and proposed thresholds. Calculate intervention density (interventions/km) and its correlation with actual risky events. Density significantly higher than the risky event base rate suggests poor specificity and alert fatigue risk.
