# Probing neural audio codecs for distinctions among English nuclear tunes

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID**: 2603.14035
- **Source URL**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.14035
- **Reference count**: 0
- **Primary result**: Linear probes classify English nuclear tunes with above-chance accuracy; binary tasks achieve up to 90% TATA on unquantized latents.

## Executive Summary
This paper investigates whether the neural audio codec Mimi encodes English nuclear intonation tunes—pitch trajectories that convey pragmatic meaning—in its latent representations. Using linear and nonlinear probes trained on a labeled corpus of utterances, the authors classify tunes based on both unquantized latents and quantized codewords from Mimi’s hierarchical codebooks. Results show above-chance accuracy across several tasks, with binary distinctions reaching 0.74–0.89 TATA (90% for the hhh-vs-lll contrast on unquantized latents). Tune information is distributed across all codebooks, and the audio-reconstruction-focused Codebook1 often outperforms the WavLM-aligned Codebook0. However, multi-class accuracy remains far below human levels, indicating current codecs have limited capacity for fine-grained intonational distinctions.

## Method Summary
The study uses a labeled dataset of 4,656 English utterances (including pitch-resynthesized and imitated recordings) with final accented words annotated via forced alignment. Audio is encoded with the Mimi codec, yielding either continuous 512‑dimensional latents at 12.5 Hz or discrete 256‑dimensional codewords from eight hierarchical codebooks. For each sample, vectors corresponding to the accented word are aggregated using a weighted average that emphasizes specific temporal regions (governed by forward/backward decay parameters). PCA reduces the aggregated vector to a lower dimension before training a linear classifier (softmax) or a nonlinear two‑layer MLP with LayerNorm and leaky ReLU. Hyperparameters are optimized via Optuna on unquantized latents and then reused across all codebooks. The data is split 70/15/15 (train/dev/test), and performance is measured by average test accuracy (TATA) and compared to a majority‑class baseline.

## Key Results
- Linear probes achieve above-chance multi‑class accuracy: 8‑class TATA 0.31, 5‑class TATA 0.45.
- Binary contrasts reach 0.74–0.89 TATA, with the hhh‑vs‑lll distinction at 90% on unquantized latents.
- Tune information is spread across all codebooks; Codebook 1 (audio‑reconstruction‑focused) often outperforms Codebook 0 (WavLM‑aligned “semantic”).
- Nonlinear probes improve performance but 5‑class accuracy remains far below human levels, highlighting limitations of current codecs for encoding fine‑grained intonation.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1
- **Claim**: Weighted averaging over the accented word’s latent vectors emphasizes time regions relevant to tune components (pitch accent vs. edge tones), improving probe accuracy.
- **Mechanism**: Compute weights as \(w_i = \exp(-\delta_F d(0,i) - \delta_B d(i,N))\) where \(\delta_F\) and \(\delta_B\) control forward/backward decay; larger \(\delta_B\) emphasizes final frames (edge tones), larger \(\delta_F\) emphasizes initial frames (pitch accent).
- **Core assumption**: Tune information is temporally localized within the accented word, with different phonological components occupying distinct intervals.
- **Evidence anchors**:
  - [abstract] “Probes aggregate vectors over the accented word”
  - [section 2.3.1] Detailed formula and rationale for weighted averaging.
  - [corpus] Weak: Neighboring papers on neural audio codecs do not address aggregation for intonation probing.
- **Break condition**: If optimal weights do not align with expected temporal patterns (e.g., high \(\delta_F\) improves edge‑tune classification) or if no improvement over simple averaging is observed.

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim**: PCA dimensionality reduction on aggregated vectors removes noise and irrelevant variance, enabling linear probes to better separate tune classes.
- **Mechanism**: PCA is fitted on training aggregated vectors; then all vectors are projected onto the top \(d_{PCA}\) principal components before classification.
- **Core assumption**: Tune‑discriminative information lies in a low‑dimensional subspace of the high‑dimensional latent space; PCA preserves this subspace while discarding nuisance variation.
- **Evidence anchors**:
  - [abstract] “apply PCA, and classify”
  - [section 2.3.2] Explicit justification: reduce parameters and overfitting risk.
  - [corpus] Weak: PCA is standard in probing literature but not specifically validated for audio codec latent spaces in the provided neighbors.
- **Break condition**: If test accuracy decreases with increasing \(d_{PCA}\) beyond a point, or if nonlinear probes vastly outperform linear ones even after PCA, suggesting nonlinear manifolds.

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim**: Information about nuclear tunes is distributed across all hierarchical codebooks, with Codebook1 (audio‑reconstruction‑focused) often more informative than Codebook0 (WavLM‑aligned “semantic”).
- **Mechanism**: Train identical probes on codewords from each codebook; compare accuracies. The RVQ hierarchy means Codebook1 captures the largest residual error from the “semantic” codebook, which may include prosodic detail.
- **Core assumption**: The “semantic” codebook (distilled from WavLM) does not prioritize speaker‑independent prosody because WavLM was not evaluated on prosody‑dependent tasks.
- **Evidence anchors**:
  - [abstract] “Information about tunes is spread among all codebooks… Codebook1 often outperforming Codebook0”
  - [section 4] Analysis and discussion linking WavLM’s training to lack of prosodic benchmarks.
  - [corpus] Weak: Neighboring papers focus on compression efficiency, not tune encoding distribution.
- **Break condition**: If future codecs explicitly train for prosody, concentration in Codebook0 might emerge; or if tune information is found exclusively in later codebooks (contradicting current spread).

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map**: Input audio with forced alignment → Mimi encoder → (A) unquantized latents or (B) codewords per codebook → extraction of accented word vectors → weighted aggregation → PCA transformation → linear/nonlinear classifier → tune prediction.
- **Critical path**:
  1. Align audio to locate final accented word.
  2. Encode audio through Mimi; collect unquantized latent sequence or codeword indices for each codebook.
  3. For each sample, slice latent/codeword vectors corresponding to accented word frames.
  4. Compute weighted average using chosen δF, δB.
  5. Apply pre‑fitted PCA to reduce to d_PCA dimensions.
  6. Train classifier (logistic regression or 2‑layer MLP) on the reduced vectors.
  7. Evaluate accuracy on held‑out test set.
- **Design tradeoffs**:
  - *Unquantized vs. quantized*: Unquantized likely preserves more information but is not directly used by downstream LM; quantized is discrete and practical but may lose nuance.
  - *Linear vs. nonlinear probe*: Linear is interpretable and less prone to overfitting; nonlinear may capture complex relationships but risks learning artifacts.
  - *Aggregation hyperparameters*: Allowing separate δF, δB adds flexibility but requires hyperparameter search; simple average may be sufficient for some contrasts.
  - *PCA dimension*: Balances overfitting vs. information retention; too low discards discriminative signals, too high invites noise.
  - *Codebook selection*: Using all codebooks may yield best performance but increases computational cost; focusing on one (e.g., Codebook1) could be efficient.
- **Failure signatures**:
  - Test accuracy near majority‑class baseline → probe cannot detect tune info; check Mimi version, alignment, or hyperparameters.
  - No improvement from nonlinear probes → representation may lack nonlinear tune structure or linear probe already saturated.
  - Systematic confusion between perceptually distinct tunes (e.g., lhh vs. hhh) → representation missing key pitch trajectory details.
  - Codebook0 consistently worst → possible mismatch between WavLM’s training and prosody tasks.
  - Large gap between train and test accuracy → overfitting; reduce PCA dimension, add regularization, or gather more data.
- **First 3 experiments**:
  1. *Baseline replication*: Train a linear probe on unquantized latents for binary hhh-vs-lll using simple averaging (δF=δB=0) and d_PCA=50. Expect >0.85 accuracy to confirm pipeline.
  2. *Codebook comparison*: For 5‑class task, train identical linear probes on each codebook (0–7) with optimal hyperparameters. Verify Codebook1 accuracy > Codebook0 and that all codebooks exceed chance.
  3. *Aggregation ablation*: For binary xll‑vs‑xhh, sweep δB (0–5) with δF=0 and vice versa. Expect best performance with high δB (emphasizing final boundary tones) because this contrast hinges on edge tones.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- **Open Question 1**: Why is information about nuclear tunes more robustly encoded in Codebook 1 (focused on audio reconstruction) rather than in Codebook 0 (aligned with WavLM's 'semantic' representations), and does this challenge the assumed semantic‑acoustic dichotomy in neural codecs?
- **Open Question 2**: To what extent does the distribution of nuclear tunes in Mimi's training corpus account for the discrepancy between human‑perceived robust tune clusters and the codec's lower multi‑class accuracy?
- **Open Question 3**: Can architectural changes or training objectives in neural audio codecs significantly improve the encoding of fine‑grained intonational distinctions (e.g., pitch accent differences) to approach human‑level discrimination?
- **Open Question 4**: How should speech processing benchmarks (e.g., SUPERB) be revised to include tasks that evaluate speaker‑independent, pragmatic intonational representations in neural codecs?

## Limitations
- **Dataset access**: Cole et al. (2023) audio and alignments are not public, hindering independent replication.
- **Missing hyperparameter details**: Optuna search ranges and optimal values not reported; nonlinear probe architecture specifics unknown.
- **Performance gap**: Multi‑class accuracy ≤0.45 TATA is far below human levels, suggesting limited encoding or suboptimal probing.
