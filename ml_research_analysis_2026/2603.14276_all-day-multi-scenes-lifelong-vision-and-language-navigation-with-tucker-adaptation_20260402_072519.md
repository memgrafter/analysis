# All-day Multi-scenes Lifelong Vision-and-Language Navigation with Tucker Adaptation

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2603.14276  
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.14276  
- Reference count: 40  
- Primary result: AlldayWalker achieves 65% success rate on Allday-Habitat with only 0.3M trainable parameters, substantially outperforming LoRA-based baselines and reducing catastrophic forgetting.

## Executive Summary
The paper tackles all-day multi-scenes lifelong vision-and-language navigation (AML-VLN), where an agent must continuously learn across diverse scenes and lighting conditions without forgetting previously acquired knowledge. Existing LoRA-based adapters use two-dimensional matrices, which cannot capture the multi-hierarchical structure of navigation knowledge (e.g., scene-level and environment-level variations). The proposed solution, Tucker Adaptation (TuKA), lifts adaptation to a high-order tensor decomposed via Tucker decomposition into a shared core tensor and scenario-specific expert factors (scene and environment). Coupled with decoupled knowledge incremental learning (DKIL), the method consolidates shared subspaces during lifelong training while preserving specialized experts, thereby mitigating catastrophic forgetting. AlldayWalker, built on TuKA, is evaluated on a new Allday-Habitat benchmark covering 24 sequential tasks across five simulated scenes, four lighting conditions, and two real-world scenes. Results show state-of-the-art performance: 65% success, 58% SPL, 68% OSR, and a forgetting rate of only 11%, all with just 0.3M trainable parameters.

## Method Summary
Tucker Adaptation (TuKA) replaces the low-rank matrices of LoRA with a high-order tensor that is factorized using Tucker decomposition. This yields a shared core tensor that captures universal navigation patterns and separate factor matrices (experts) that specialize for different scenarios (e.g., scene type or lighting condition). During lifelong learning, Decoupled Knowledge Incremental Learning (DKIL) updates only the core tensor across tasks while keeping the expert factors fixed, thereby consolidating common knowledge without overwriting scenario-specific adaptations. AlldayWalker integrates TuKA into a pre-trained VLN backbone, applying the adapted weights (core × experts) to linear layers within the network. At inference, the appropriate experts are selected based on the current scene/environment, and the core is combined with them to produce task-specific weight updates.

## Key Results
- 65% success rate on Allday-Habitat (vs. 38% HydraLoRA, 44% BranchLoRA) with only 0.3M trainable parameters.  
- SPL of 58% and OSR of 68%, demonstrating efficient and accurate navigation.  
- Catastrophic forgetting reduced to 11% compared to 46% (HydraLoRA) and 36% (BranchLoRA).

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
TuKA works because it lifts parameter adaptation from a 2D matrix to a high-order tensor, enabling explicit modeling of multi-hierarchical knowledge (e.g., scene and environment as separate modes). Tucker decomposition cleanly separates a shared core from specialized experts, allowing common navigation strategies to be captured centrally while variations are handled by lightweight factors. DKIL further ensures that during lifelong learning the shared core is incrementally updated and consolidated, while experts remain untouched, thus preventing interference between tasks. This design yields both high expressivity and strong resistance to catastrophic forgetting, while remaining parameter-efficient.

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component map** (plain text):  
Observation & Instruction -> Feature Extraction -> Base VLN Network -> TuKA Adapter (Core + Scene Expert + Env Expert) -> DKIL Consolidation -> Policy Decoder -> Action

**Critical path**: During training on a new task, data flows through the base network and TuKA-adapted layers to produce predictions; loss is computed and backpropagation updates only the core tensor via DKIL while expert factors remain fixed. The updated core is then retained for subsequent tasks. At inference, the appropriate experts (chosen by scene/environment metadata) are combined with the core to compute effective weights for navigation.

**Design tradeoffs**: TuKA’s high-order tensor provides richer adaptation than LoRA but introduces more complex decomposition and rank selection. DKIL reduces forgetting but risks underfitting if the core becomes too generic; balancing core size vs. expert capacity is key. Parameter efficiency is maintained by keeping experts narrow and core shared.

**Failure signatures**: 
- Forgetting rate rises sharply on previous tasks (core consolidation insufficient).  
- Success on current task plateaus at a low level (experts not specialized or ranks too low).  
- Parameter count grows unexpectedly (chosen tensor ranks too high).

**First three experiments to try**:

3. Run a side-by-side comparison with HydraLoRA or BranchLoRA on the same two-task sequence to confirm the performance and forgetting advantage.

## Limitations
- Allday-Habitat benchmark is novel and lacks external validation; real-world testing is confined to only two scenes.  
- Baselines are restricted to LoRA-based adapters; broader lifelong VLN methods (e.g., EWC) are not considered.  
- No statistical significance tests are provided for observed performance gaps (e.g., 65% vs. 38% success rate).

## Confidence
- Parameter efficiency (0.3M parameters): High  
- Performance on Allday-Habitat (65% success, SPL 58%, OSR 68%): Medium  
- Catastrophic forgetting reduction (11%): Low  
- Effectiveness of high-order tensor adaptation: Medium
