# PRISM: Differentially Private Synthetic Data with Structure-Aware Budget Allocation for Prediction
## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2602.10228
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.10228
- Reference count: 40
- Primary result: PRISM improves DP synthetic data for prediction via structure-aware budget allocation, achieving AUC ≈0.73 under spurious shift (vs ≈0.49 for correlation selection) and ≈0.875 on Adult (outperforming MST 0.792 and PrivBayes 0.831 at ε=1.0)

## Executive Summary
PRISM is a framework for generating differentially private synthetic data tailored to a prediction task. It focuses privacy budget on features relevant to the target Y, using structural knowledge from three regimes: causal parents for shift robustness, Markov blanket for prediction optimality, or DP feature selection when no structure exists. Budget allocation follows a closed-form solution to minimize an upper bound on prediction error. Empirically, PRISM-Causal maintains robustness under distribution shift where naive correlation-based selection fails, and PRISM-Predictive matches or outperforms existing synthesizers on the Adult dataset.

## Method Summary
Given private data D (features X, target Y), privacy parameters (ε, δ), and optionally causal parents Pa(Y) or DAG G, PRISM first selects a predictive subset S according to one of three regimes. It then splits the budget into pools for feature selection (if needed), mutual information estimation, task-related marginals (over S ∪ {Y}), and background marginals (1-way for non-S features plus DP-MST 2-way among them). Using Theorem 6.3, it allocates per-query (ε_q, δ_q) within each pool, proportional to √(w_q |Ω_q|), where w_q are task weights and |Ω_q| is domain size. Each marginal is measured with Gaussian noise and projected to the simplex. A max-entropy distribution is fitted via Private-PGM to satisfy all noisy marginals, and synthetic records are sampled.

## Key Results
- Under spurious distribution shift, PRISM-Causal achieves AUC ≈0.73, while correlation-based selection collapses to ≈0.49.
- On Adult dataset at ε=1.0, PRISM-Predictive reaches AUC ≈0.875, outperforming MST (0.792) and PrivBayes (0.831) and matching strong internal baselines.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1 (Causal Regime)
- **Claim**: Prioritizing privacy budget on the causal parents of Y preserves robustness under spurious distribution shift.
- **Mechanism**: S is set to Pa(Y). The workload focuses on marginals involving (X_{Pa(Y)}, Y). Budget allocation directs more noise-sensitive measurements to these task-critical marginals, while coarse/no measurement on spurious children/irrelevant features.
- **Core assumption**: Pa(Y) are known a priori; deployment distribution may change spurious correlations while preserving P(Y|Pa(Y)).
- **Evidence anchors**: Abstract and Section 7.1 show PRISM-Causal robust under shift; correlation-based top-k picks spurious correlates and collapses to ≈0.49.
- **Break condition**: Incorrect Pa(Y) or change in causal mechanism P(Y|Pa(Y)); no benefit if shift does not affect spurious correlations.

### Mechanism 2 (Graphical Regime)
- **Claim**: Targeting the Markov blanket of Y yields prediction-optimal synthetic data under a fixed, stable distribution.
- **Mechanism**: S = MB(Y). Workload includes marginals over S ∪ {Y}. Allocation ensures accurate reconstruction of P(X_S, Y). Under the Markov assumption, this preserved joint is sufficient for Bayes-optimal predictor.
- **Core assumption**: Known Bayesian network DAG G; training and deployment distributions identical.
- **Evidence anchors**: Abstract, Proposition 6.1 (Markov blanket sufficiency), Section 7.2 showing near-perfect AUC when children predictive.
- **Break condition**: Shift where children become unreliable; incorrect DAG.

### Mechanism 3 (Predictive Regime)
- **Claim**: When no structural knowledge exists, private feature selection followed by task-aware budget allocation concentrates fidelity on empirically predictive features.
- **Mechanism**: S is selected via DP methods (e.g., exponential mechanism on clipped χ² or DP-regularized logistic regression). Workload targets marginals over S ∪ {Y}, with allocation weights tied to DP-estimated feature importance.
- **Core assumption**: Exists subset S supporting good prediction; DP feature selection can identify it without causal/graphical guarantees.
- **Evidence anchors**: Abstract, Section 5.2/7.3 showing gains over MST/PrivBayes on Adult.
- **Break condition**: DP selection suboptimal under tight budgets/small n; no robustness under shift.

## Foundational Learning
- **Differential Privacy (DP) Composition**: Needed to reason about total privacy when combining DP steps (selection, MI estimation, marginal measurement). Quick check: If selection uses ε_sel and m marginals each (ε_q, δ_q), overall (ε, δ) = (ε_sel + Σ ε_q, Σ δ_q) via basic composition.
- **Markov Blanket & Graphical Sufficiency**: Justifies why preserving P(X_MB(Y), Y) suffices for optimal prediction under stability. Quick check: MB(Y) = parents, children, co-parents of children. If distribution Markov to DAG G, then Y ⊥ ⊥ X \ MB(Y) | X_MB(Y).
- **Total Variation (TV) Distance as Risk Proxy**: Lemma 6.2 shows TV(PS,Y, QS,Y) bounds risk difference for any predictor using only S. Quick check: TV directly bounds difference in expected loss for any bounded loss and any predictor, without assumptions on P or h, unlike KL.

## Architecture Onboarding
Component map: Input Handler -> Regime Router -> Budget Splitter -> Workload Builder -> Allocation Engine -> DP Measurement Layer -> PGM Synthesis Backend -> Sampler.

Critical path: Input → Regime Router → Budget Splitter → Workload Builder → Allocation Engine → DP Measurement (all queries) → PGM Fitting → Sampler → Output. Budget split and allocation occur before measurement; PGM fitting is post-processing.

Design tradeoffs:
- Budget split vs utility: Allocating too much to feature selection starves task measurement; default 10% heuristic.
- Workload granularity: Including full joint (X_S, Y) gives optimal reconstruction but domain size explodes; default limits 3-way.
- Task weights: Uniform weights simpler but suboptimal; oracle weights can yield gains.
- Backend choice: Uses Private-PGM; swapping synthesizers may break allocation theorem.

Failure signatures:
- High-dimensional S: Domain size blows up → TV error → utility collapse.
- DP selection error: In Regime 3, selecting suboptimal S due to noise.
- Mis-specified regime: Using Causal when parents incorrect, or Graphical under shift, leads to loss.
- PGM fitting failure: For large cliques or low n_syn, may not converge or overfit.

First 3 experiments:
1. Privacy accounting validation: For fixed (ε, δ), compute per-query ε_q via Theorem 6.3, verify with DP accountant, ensure PGM output is (ε, δ)-DP.
2. Marginal fidelity check: On held-out real data, compute 1-way/2-way L1 errors; confirm task marginals have lower error with Optimal vs Unif allocation.
3. TSTR utility smoke test: Train logistic regression on synthetic, evaluate ROC-AUC on held-out real test; for correct regime, PRISM should exceed MST/PrivBayes. Also run spurious shift benchmark to confirm Regime 1 robustness.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
1. How to incorporate DP causal discovery or Markov blanket estimation when structural knowledge is not provided a priori, and what is the combined privacy-utility trade-off of jointly learning structure and synthesizing data?
2. How should the privacy budget be allocated when the synthetic data is intended for multiple prediction tasks, and can the regime-based framework be generalized to multi-target settings where different tasks may have conflicting feature importance?
3. In PRISM-Predictive, how does noise in the DP weight estimates affect the optimality of the closed-form allocation and the final prediction accuracy, and what portion of the privacy budget should be allocated to weight estimation to minimize overall risk?
4. How sensitive is PRISM's performance to inaccuracies in the assumed structural knowledge—such as including irrelevant features or omitting true parents/children—and can the framework be made robust to moderate structural misspecifications?

## Limitations
- Omission of 3-way marginals and DP-MST background edges may under-represent the full workload, altering allocation gains.
- DP feature selection sensitivity bound likely loose; actual ε_sel impact on selection quality unknown.
- Requires accurate structural knowledge (Pa(Y) or DAG); incorrect assumptions harm utility.

## Confidence
- Causal regime (shift robustness): High
- Graphical regime (optimal prediction under stability): Medium
- Predictive regime (DP feature selection): Low-Medium
