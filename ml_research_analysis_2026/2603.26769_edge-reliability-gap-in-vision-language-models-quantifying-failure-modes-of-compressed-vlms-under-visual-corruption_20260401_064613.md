# Edge Reliability Gap in Vision-Language Models: Quantifying Failure Modes of Compressed VLMs Under Visual Corruption

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2603.26769
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.26769
- **Reference count:** 40
- **Primary result:** Compressed VLMs exhibit qualitatively distinct failure signatures (e.g., catastrophic negation collapse, asymmetric calibration) beyond simple accuracy drops, with critical implications for edge deployment.

## Executive Summary
This study investigates whether compressing vision-language models (VLMs) for edge deployment alters the fundamental nature of their failures, not just their frequency. Comparing a 500M-parameter FP16 model (SmolVLM2) against a 4-bit quantised 7B model (Qwen2.5-VL) on VQAv2 and COCO Captions reveals divergent failure profiles: the compact model suffers a severe negation collapse on logical inversion templates, while the quantised large model exhibits degenerate saturated confidence on closed-ended tasks. Calibration errors are asymmetric and dataset-dependent, and no significant differential sensitivity to Gaussian blur is observed. The findings indicate that model compression can reshape failure modes, challenging the safety of confidence-based gating at the edge.

## Method Summary
The authors evaluate two VLMs—SmolVLM2-500M (FP16) and Qwen2.5-VL-7B (4-bit NF4)—on VQAv2 (2k samples) and COCO Captions (2k samples). Correctness is measured via soft string match (VQAv2) and keyword overlap (COCO). For robustness and failure analysis, they: (1) construct a *both-correct* subset (n=100 per dataset) where both models succeed on clean images; (2) apply a three-category error taxonomy (A–E) via a text-only GPT-4o judge on 100 failures per model per dataset; (3) compute Expected Calibration Error (ECE) using geometric mean token probability; (4) run negation probes on the both-correct set using four templates; and (5) measure accuracy drop under Gaussian blur (σ=2.0) on the both-correct set. All experiments use deterministic decoding and a single RTX 5090 GPU.

## Key Results
- **Negation collapse:** SmolVLM2 fails the `false_yn` template catastrophically (0% success on COCO vs. Qwen’s 86%), driving a 12.5pp larger overall negation gap.
- **Calibration asymmetry:** Qwen-7B NF4 shows saturated confidence on VQAv2 (ECE 0.443, all predictions ≥0.9 confidence); SmolVLM2 is severely underconfident on COCO (ECE 0.431).
- **Divergent error profiles:** SmolVLM2 shows more Object Blindness on COCO; Qwen-7B shows higher Prior Bias on VQAv2 and more Semantic Drift on COCO.
- **Blur robustness:** Both models drop ~3.0pp on blur (p=0.68), with no significant differential sensitivity at n=100.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1: Quantisation-Induced Confidence Saturation
- **Claim:** 4-bit NF4 quantisation in Qwen2.5-VL-7B flattens the softmax output distribution, pushing token probabilities toward a uniform high-confidence peak (~0.999), destroying discriminative calibration on VQAv2.
- **Core assumption:** Geometric mean of token log-probabilities reliably reflects sequence-level certainty.
- **Evidence anchors:** ECE of 0.443 with all 2,000 predictions in the top confidence bin (≥0.9).
- **Break condition:** If a 4-bit VLM maintains a distributed confidence profile (multiple bins) on VQAv2, this mechanism is falsified for that architecture/task.

### Mechanism 2: Scale-Dependent Negation Collapse
- **Claim:** SmolVLM2’s 135M LLM backbone lacks capacity to parse logical inversion in the `false_yn` template, defaulting to affirming false statements (“Yes”) due to language prior dominance.
- **Core assumption:** `false_yn` isolates negation reasoning from general instruction-following; 0% success is not solely an instruction deficit.
- **Evidence anchors:** 0% success on COCO `false_yn` vs. Qwen’s 86%; SmolVLM2 responds “Yes” on 100% of those trials.
- **Break condition:** A 500M-parameter VLM achieving >70% success on `false_yn` falsifies this scale-dependent collapse for this task.

### Mechanism 3: Dataset-Asymmetric Calibration
- **Claim:** Calibration quality depends on task output distribution: closed-ended VQAv2 induces peaked token distributions (overconfidence), while open-ended COCO accumulates low-probability tokens (illusion of underconfidence).
- **Core assumption:** Geometric mean token probability is a valid, comparable confidence proxy across tasks.
- **Evidence anchors:** Qwen-7B clusters all VQAv2 predictions in the top bin; SmolVLM2 clusters COCO predictions in bins 3–6 (confidence 0.37–0.63) despite high accuracy.
- **Break condition:** A model showing consistent ECE (<0.1) across VQAv2 and COCO with similar output-length distributions falsifies this asymmetry.

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map:** Vision Encoder (SigLIP-400M / Qwen dynamic tiling) -> LLM Backbone (SmolLM2-135M / Qwen2.5-7B) -> Output. Precision paths: FP16 (SmolVLM2) vs. 4-bit NF4 (Qwen). Judge: GPT-4o text-only.
- **Critical path:**
  1. Load both models on a single RTX 5090 (32 GiB); verify VRAM footprints.
  2. Stream VQAv2 and COCO Captions datasets; skip corrupted images.
  3. Run baseline inference with greedy decoding; capture token log-probabilities.
  4. Identify both-correct subsets (n=100 per dataset); apply negation probes and blur.
  5. Run GPT-4o judge on 100 failures per model per dataset.
  6. Compute ECE, error taxonomy distributions, and accuracy drops; aggregate.
- **Design tradeoffs:**
  - Accuracy vs. calibration: Qwen-7B NF4 is more accurate on VQAv2 but has zero confidence signal; SmolVLM2 is underconfident on COCO.
  - Coarse COCO metric (keyword overlap) inflates both-correct set, risking false positives in negation probes.
  - Text-only judge cannot verify visual grounding; category A/B on VQAv2 yes/no questions is unreliable.
  - Small n=100 for negation/blur reduces power; both-correct sampling may over-represent simple images.
- **Failure signatures:**
  - **SmolVLM2-500M:** 0% `false_yn` success (COCO), underconfident on COCO (ECE 0.431), Object Blindness predominant on COCO.
  - **Qwen2.5-VL-7B (NF4):** Saturated confidence on VQAv2 (ECE 0.443), Prior Bias on VQAv2 (20.4%), Semantic Drift on COCO.
  - **Shared:** No significant differential blur sensitivity (ρ≈1.0) at n=100.
- **First 3 experiments:**
  1. **Confidence distribution sanity check:** Run 100 random VQAv2 samples; plot histogram of geometric mean token probabilities. Expect Qwen-7B NF4 to show a spike at ~0.999.
  2. **False_yn probe on COCO:** Apply template to 20 both-correct COCO images. Expect SmolVLM2 to say “Yes” on ≥90%; Qwen-7B to say “No” on ≥80%.
  3. **Blur ablation on a single image:** Pick one clearly correct VQAv2 image; test clean vs. Gaussian blur (σ=2). Expect ~3pp drop for both; larger deviation suggests sample variance.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
1. **Generalisability:** To what extent do the observed failure signature differences (negation collapse, calibration asymmetry) generalise beyond the specific model pair studied? The study acknowledges its results characterise only this pairing due to confounding (scale, precision, architecture).
2. **Causal mechanism of `false_yn` collapse:** Is the 0% success due to limited language capacity, inadequate negation data, instruction-tuning misalignment, or a vision-language fusion bottleneck? The template conflates negation reasoning with instruction-following.
3. **Calibration fixability:** Can confidence scores from compressed VLMs be reliably calibrated for deployment gating, or is miscalibration inherent to the compression-technique/scale combination? The observed asymmetry is entangled with model factors.
4. **Blur robustness validity:** Does the lack of differential blur sensitivity hold under diverse corruptions (noise, weather) and larger samples? The `both-correct` subset may over-represent simple images.
5. **Metric influence:** How does the coarse COCO keyword-overlap metric distort failure profiles and the negation gap? Standard captioning metrics (CIDEr) might reveal a larger quality gap and alter taxonomy distribution.

## Limitations
- **Confounding:** Comparison confounds model scale, architecture, and precision (FP16 vs. 4-bit).
- **Judge limitations:** Text-only GPT-4o may misclassify errors requiring visual verification.
- **Statistical power:** Small n=100 for negation/blur probes limits generalisability.
- **Confidence metric:** Geometric mean token probability may not align with true uncertainty; dataset-dependent binning effects.
- **Task scope:** Results limited to VQAv2 and COCO Captions; other tasks (e.g., counting, fine-grained recognition) may differ.
- **Reproducibility gaps:** Missing model checkpoints, GPT-4o API details, and dataset preprocessing specifications.

## Next Checks
1. **Quantisation saturation test:** Run VQAv2 inference with other 4-bit VLMs (e.g., LLaVA-7B); check if confidence distribution collapses to a single high bin.
2. **False_yn replication:** Replicate negation probes on 500 COCO samples with human verification; control instruction-following by using a simpler fixed-output template.
3. **Calibration robustness:** Recompute ECE using alternative confidence elicitation (e.g., entropy, verbalized uncertainty) across a matrix of models (FP16/NF4/INT8, multiple scales) to isolate factor effects.
