# Uncertainty-aware Language Guidance for Concept Bottleneck Models

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2602.23495  
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.23495  
- **Reference count:** 32  
- **Primary result:** ULCBM with augmentation achieves higher overall accuracy (e.g., 75.5% vs. 74.4% on CUB) and notably better worst-class accuracy (25.0% vs. 20.0% on CUB), with Concept Compliance Accuracy consistently surpassing baselines.

## Executive Summary
Concept Bottleneck Models (CBMs) rely on human-understandable concepts for interpretable predictions, but when concepts are generated from large language models (LLMs), hallucinations and imprecision lead to unreliable annotations. This paper introduces ULCBM, which uses conformal prediction to provide distribution-free uncertainty quantification for LLM-generated concepts across three criteria—discriminability, coverage, and diversity—calibrating a global threshold that provably controls empirical risk. A targeted data augmentation pipeline then uses this threshold to synthesize rare but reliable visual concepts, mitigating training data sparsity. Experiments on CIFAR-10, CIFAR-100, and CUB show that all empirical loss constraints are satisfied, and ULCBM with augmentation improves both overall and worst-class accuracy while achieving higher Concept Compliance Accuracy than prior methods.

## Method Summary
ULCBM operates in two main phases. First, it calibrates a global threshold λ̂ using conformal prediction on a held-out set to ensure that concept sets constructed from LLM outputs satisfy user-specified risk levels for discriminability, coverage, and diversity, with finite-sample guarantees under exchangeability. Second, it identifies rare concepts (those selected infrequently under λ̂) and performs targeted data augmentation: for each rare concept, it crops a reliable visual patch from a source image and inserts it into a target image at a location that avoids overlapping with other reliably selected concept bounding boxes, thereby augmenting the training set with plausible rare-concept examples. The CBM is then trained on this augmented, uncertainty-aware dataset using a joint loss that combines concept and task objectives.

## Key Results
- ULCBM satisfies all conformal risk constraints (discriminability, coverage, diversity) on held-out data across CIFAR-10, CIFAR-100, and CUB.
- With augmentation, ULCBM achieves higher overall accuracy (e.g., 75.5% vs. 74.4% on CUB) and substantially better worst-class accuracy (25.0% vs. 20.0% on CUB) compared to baselines.
- Concept Compliance Accuracy (CCA) consistently surpasses prior LLM-guided CBM methods (e.g., LaBo, VLG-CBM), indicating more reliable and useful concept sets.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- **Claim:** Conformal prediction calibrates a global threshold λ̂ to construct concept sets that provably satisfy user-specified risk levels for discriminability, coverage, and diversity, with distribution-free guarantees under exchangeability.
- **Mechanism:** For each loss criterion (ℓ_dis, ℓ_cov, ℓ_div), empirical risk is computed on a calibration set for varying thresholds λ. The smallest threshold λ̂_k is found such that bR_k(λ) ≤ α_k - (1-α_k)/N_cal. The final λ̂ = max(λ̂_dis, λ̂_cov, λ̂_div) ensures all constraints hold simultaneously.
- **Core assumption:** The calibration set and any target sample are exchangeable. The loss functions are proper, bounded, and exchangeable random variables.
- **Evidence anchors:** Abstract mentions distribution-free guarantees for three criteria; Section 3.1 details the conformal approach and Theorem 1; neighboring papers lack such formal guarantees.
- **Break condition:** Violation of exchangeability (e.g., dataset shift in LLM annotations or image distributions) may cause risk control to fail on unseen samples.

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim:** A targeted data augmentation pipeline, guided by the calibrated uncertainty threshold, synthesizes reliable visual patches for sparse but high-reliability concepts, mitigating training data scarcity.
- **Mechanism:** For a rare concept s_r (frequency below a threshold), a source patch from an image where s_r is reliably selected (t_j ≥ 1-λ̂) is cropped and inserted into a target image. The insertion location b̂ is constrained to avoid overlapping with any reliably selected concept box B_i' in the target image, and the concept label is updated to include s_r.
- **Core assumption:** The spatial layout of reliably selected concepts is semantically coherent and non-overlapping; inserting patches in space avoided by these boxes does not corrupt existing reliable concepts.
- **Evidence anchors:** Abstract mentions augmentation guided by uncertainty; Section 3.2 describes the non-overlap insertion constraint; prior work does not address calibrated uncertainty sparsity.
- **Break condition:** If reliable concept boxes B_i' are erroneous (e.g., poor grounding), the non-overlap constraint may place patches in semantically irrelevant regions, introducing noise.

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim:** Jointly training the concept predictor g and label predictor h on the augmented, uncertainty-aware dataset D̂_tr_aug allows the model to effectively learn from concepts of varying reliability, improving both overall and worst-class accuracy.
- **Mechanism:** Minimize a combined loss: L = L_C + γ₁ L_Y + γ₂ R_β, where L_C is BCE between predicted concepts g∘ϕ(x_i) and the augmented one-hot label o_i^aug (including inserted concepts), and L_Y is CE for final classification.
- **Core assumption:** The uncertainty-thresholded and augmentation-augmented concept labels o_i^aug are sufficiently correct as training targets, and the model capacity is adequate to learn the mixed-reliability mapping.
- **Evidence anchors:** Abstract states incorporation of quantified uncertainty into training; Section 3.2 details the joint optimization; compared to VLG-CBM which lacks uncertainty-guided augmentation.
- **Break condition:** If λ̂ is too conservative, augmented labels may still lack important concepts, or inserted patches may be contextually incoherent, harming g's learning and subsequently h's performance.

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map:**  
  LLM + Grounding Detector → Calibration Module → Uncertainty Set Constructor → Rare Concept Identifier & Augmentation Engine → CBM Trainer

- **Critical path:** Raw LLM/Detector output → Calibration (find λ̂) → Build uncertainty-annotated D̂_tr → Augmentation (fix sparsity) → Train CBM on D̂_tr_aug. Calibration is the gatekeeper for downstream reliability.

- **Design tradeoffs:**  
  - Risk level (α_dis, α_cov, α_div) selection: Lower α yields smaller, more reliable concept sets but may increase sparsity and require more augmentation; higher α allows more concepts but risks noise.  
  - Augmentation threshold: Trades off between mitigating sparsity and creating unnatural samples if overused.  
  - γ₁ (task loss weight): Higher γ₁ prioritizes task accuracy over concept fidelity; lower γ₁ might yield uncertainty-aware but less useful concepts.

- **Failure signatures:**  
  - Calibration failure: Empirical losses on calibration set exceed α values for chosen λ̂, indicating exchangeability violation or poor loss specification.  
  - Augmentation artifacts: Visual inspection shows patches inserted in semantically implausible locations (e.g., "beak" on a wing) or blurry after resizing.  
  - Training divergence: BCE concept loss remains very high, suggesting λ̂ was too strict or augmentation introduced incoherent labels.

- **First 3 experiments:**  
  1. **Validity Check:** Train ULCBM without augmentation; verify on held-out data that empirical ℓ_dis, ℓ_cov, ℓ_div are at or below preset α levels, confirming conformal calibration.  
  2. **Concept Compliance Accuracy:** For a fixed number of effective concepts (NEC), compute CCA for ULCBM vs. baselines (LaBo, VLG-CBM); higher CCA indicates concept sets are simultaneously high-quality and lead to correct predictions.  
  3. **Worst-Class Accuracy Impact:** Compare ULCBM (w/ augmentation) against its ablated version (w/o augmentation) and baselines on worst-class accuracy; significant improvement (e.g., CUB: 25.0% vs. 20.0%) validates targeted augmentation alleviates failure on sparse-concept classes.

## Limitations
- Exchangeability fragility: LLM annotation or image distribution shifts between calibration and test may break risk control guarantees.
- Augmentation artifacts: Non-overlap constraint may place patches in irrelevant regions if reliable concept boxes are erroneous.
- Over-conservative calibration: Tight risk thresholds could exclude valid concepts, limiting vocabulary and increasing reliance on augmentation.

## Confidence
- **Conformal risk control:** High for theoretical guarantee; Medium for empirical validity under distribution shift (exchangeability not fully validated).
- **Augmentation guided by uncertainty:** Medium, as it assumes reliable concept boxes are correct; errors could degrade gains.
- **Overall accuracy and worst-class improvement:** High, supported by experiments on three datasets with consistent gains.

## Next Checks
1. Stress-test exchangeability: Artificially shift calibration set (e.g., different LLM prompts) and measure empirical risk violation on original-distribution test samples.
2. Augmentation quality audit: Human evaluation of augmented images for semantic plausibility; compare against random placement ablation to isolate guided insertion effect.
3. Per-class failure analysis: Break down accuracy by class, correlate worst-class improvement with concept sparsity and augmentation coverage to verify targeted effect.
