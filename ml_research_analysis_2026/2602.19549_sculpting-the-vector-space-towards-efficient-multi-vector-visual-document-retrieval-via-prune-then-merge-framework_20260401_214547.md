# Sculpting the Vector Space: Towards Efficient Multi-Vector Visual Document Retrieval via Prune-then-Merge Framework

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID**: 2602.19549
- **Source URL**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.19549
- **Reference count**: 40
- **Primary result**: The PRUNE-THEN-MERGE framework extends near‑lossless compression by ~10 percentage points (from 50‑60% to 60‑70%) and reduces storage by >54% with only a 0.45% relative drop in nDCG@5, while remaining robust even at 80%+ compression where pruning‑only collapses.

## Executive Summary
Multi‑vector visual document retrieval (VDR) provides high accuracy but incurs high storage and computational costs due to numerous patch embeddings. Existing methods address compression either by pruning or merging, each with trade‑offs. This paper introduces **PRUNE‑THEN‑MERGE**, a two‑stage framework that first adaptively prunes low‑information patches using attention scores, then hierarchically merges the remaining embeddings via clustering. Evaluated on 29 datasets with three state‑of‑the‑art multi‑vector models, the approach consistently outperforms single‑stage baselines. It extends the near‑lossless compression range by an average of 10 percentage points (from 50‑60% to 60‑70% compression) and maintains robust performance even at 80%+ compression, where pruning‑only collapses. Across models, storage is reduced by >54% with a negligible relative drop in nDCG@5 (0.45%). The framework offers an effective “refine‑then‑compress” paradigm for practical large‑scale VDR.

## Method Summary
Given a document image, the pipeline proceeds offline in two stages. First, the LVLM encoder produces patch embeddings \(D\) and the attention matrix from its final layer. The importance of each patch \(d_j\) is the average attention from the global [EOS] token to that patch. An adaptive threshold \(\tau_d = \mu_d + k \cdot \sigma_d\) (mean plus \(k\) standard deviations of importance scores) determines pruning; patches below \(\tau_d\) are discarded, yielding a pruned set \(D'\). If all patches are pruned, the highest‑scoring patch is retained. In the second stage, the pruned embeddings are L2‑normalized, a cosine distance matrix is computed, and hierarchical agglomerative clustering (Ward linkage) partitions \(D'\) into \(N''_p = \max(1, \lfloor |D'| / m \rfloor)\) clusters. The centroid of each cluster becomes a compressed embedding, and the collection of centroids \(D''\) is stored. At retrieval time, queries are encoded normally and the late‑interaction MaxSim score is computed between query patch embeddings and the compressed set \(D''\). Compression is controlled by the pruning sensitivity \(k\) (typically negative) and the merging factor \(m\). This decomposition removes noise (pruning) before redundancy reduction (merging), approximating an information‑bottleneck objective.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
**Adaptive Pruning via Intra‑Document Attention**: The framework prunes patches with low attention scores from the [EOS] token, effectively removing non‑informative visual tokens (e.g., whitespace). This relies on the assumption that [EOS] attention reflects a patch’s contribution to the document’s overall semantics. A per‑document threshold \(\tau_d = \mu_d + k \cdot \sigma_d\) makes pruning adapt to the document’s attention distribution. Evidence: Inspired by DocPruner; details in Section 3.2.1. Over‑pruning beyond ~70% discards critical content, motivating the merge stage.

**Hierarchical Merging to Summarize Semantic Redundancy**: After pruning, the remaining embeddings are merged via hierarchical agglomerative clustering (Ward linkage). Semantically related patches fall into the same cluster; centroids optimally compress the representation (minimizing MSE). This reduces redundancy among informative patches, which is more effective than merging the full noisy set. Evidence: Section 3.2.2 and Appendix D.2 formalize the rate‑distortion justification; comparisons to Sem‑Cluster show gains from pruning first. Aggressive merging (\(m>25\)) risks concept collapse.

**Synergistic Decomposition of the Information Bottleneck**: The two‑stage process approximates the Information Bottleneck objective \(\max I(D''; s(q,D)) - \beta I(D''; D)\). Stage 1 isolates high‑signal patches \(D_{\text{sig}}\) by maximizing \(I(D'; h_{\text{eos}})\). Stage 2 applies rate‑distortion quantization on \(D'\). Theoretically, this yields lower distortion than naive merging because noise vectors are excluded. Evidence: Theoretical derivation in Section 3.3.4 and Corollary 1; experiments confirm hybrid superiority over single stages.

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component map** (plain‑text flow):  
LVLM encoder → (Attention + Patch embeddings) → Pruning module → Merging module → Indexer (stores compressed vectors)  
Online: Query encoder → Retrieval scorer (MaxSim over compressed vectors).

**Critical path**: The offline pipeline processes each document: forward pass through the LVLM, extract attention scores, apply adaptive pruning, perform hierarchical clustering, and store centroids. The query‑time path remains unchanged from base multi‑vector retrieval.

**Design tradeoffs**:
- Pruning sensitivity \(k\): More negative \(k\) retains more patches (less pruning); higher \(k\) increases pruning aggressiveness and risk of signal loss.
- Merging factor \(m\): Larger \(m\) yields higher compression but may merge distinct concepts, degrading discrimination.
The combination (e.g., \(k=-0.75, m=4\)) must be tuned per model/dataset.

**Failure signatures**:
- **Pruning‑only**: Sharp nDCG drop beyond 60‑70% compression (“cliff”).
- **Merging‑only**: Gradual, sometimes unstable degradation, particularly at moderate compression.
- **Framework**: If \(k\) too high, \(D'\) too small, merging centroids become meaningless; if \(k\) too low, the framework reduces to merging‑only, losing pruning benefits.

**First 3 experiments**:
1. **Baseline replication**: Re‑run all base models (ColQwen2.5, ColNomic, Jina‑v4) and single‑stage baselines (DocPruner, Sem‑Cluster) on ViDoRe‑V1 to verify reported curves.
2. **Ablation – Stage removal**: Run prune‑only (skip merging) and merge‑only (merge full set) for each model; compare nDCG@5 vs. compression to quantify each stage’s contribution.
3. **Hyperparameter sensitivity**: Sweep \(k \in \{-1, -0.75, -0.5, 0\}\) with fixed \(m=4\) on a dense document subset (e.g., Financial Report from REAL‑MM‑RAG) to identify the best trade‑off and observe its effect on \(|D'|\).

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
1. **Reliability of attention as a universal proxy** for low‑information patches across diverse layouts, languages, and LVLM architectures.
2. **Automatic hyperparameter selection** for \(k\) and \(m\) without manual tuning; currently the framework relies on predefined values.
3. **Validity of the clean signal/noise partition** assumption in the theoretical analysis; real documents often contain mixed‑signal patches.
4. **Transferability to non‑late‑interaction paradigms** (e.g., single‑vector or dual‑encoder models) and whether principles can be adapted.
5. **System‑level scalability** of increased offline encoding latency (∼50% slower) and whether storage savings offset compute costs in petabyte‑scale deployments.

## Limitations
- **Attention reliability**: Pruning quality depends on the LVLM’s internal attention as an importance proxy; biases may limit generalization.
- **Static hyperparameters**: Fixed \(k\) and \(m\) require manual per‑dataset tuning; no automated adaptation.
- **Simplified theoretical assumption**: The IB decomposition assumes a clean separation between informative and non‑informative patches, an approximation that may fail for dense, mixed‑content documents. Evaluation is also predominantly English‑centric, leaving cross‑lingual performance unclear.

## Confidence
- **Adaptive Pruning via Intra‑Document Attention**: Medium
- **Hierarchical Merging (Ward Clustering)**: Medium
- **Synergistic Decomposition / IB Approximation**: Low
- **Overall retrieval gains (storage reduction with minimal accuracy drop)**: High

## Next Checks
1. **Ablation of stages**: For each base model, run prune‑only, merge‑only, and full framework across compression rates; quantify each stage’s contribution to nDCG.
2. **Attention saliency correlation**: Collect human‑annotated saliency masks for important regions on a sample of documents; compute correlation (e.g., ROC‑AUC) with [EOS] attention scores.
3. **Cross‑architecture consistency**: Apply the same \(k,m\) to diverse LVLM backbones (e.g., CLIP, SigLIP, Pix2Struct, mPLUG‑Owl) and analyze failure modes to test generality of the adaptive threshold.
