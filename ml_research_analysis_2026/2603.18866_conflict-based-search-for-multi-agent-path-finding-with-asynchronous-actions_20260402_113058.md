# Conflict-Based Search for Multi Agent Path Finding with Asynchronous Actions

## Quick Facts
- **ArXiv ID:** 2603.18866
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.18866
- **Reference Count:** 22
- **Primary Result:** CBS-AA introduces Constraint on Single Action (CSA) and Constraint on Multiple Actions (CMA) for asynchronous MAPF, combined with SIPPS-WC low-level planning. CMAS variant reduces high-level node expansions by up to 90% vs CCBS, scales to 50 agents with high success rates, maintains optimality identical to LS-M*, and solves faster.

## Executive Summary
The paper tackles the problem of Multi-Agent Path Finding with Asynchronous Actions (MAPF-AA), where agents have heterogeneous speeds and actions have continuous durations. Existing solvers like CCBS suffer from incomplete behavior due to infinite branching of wait actions. The authors propose Conflict-Based Search with Asynchronous Actions (CBS-AA), introducing two key conflict resolution techniques: Constraint on Single Action (CSA) that handles wait conflicts via time-interval occupancy constraints, and Constraint on Multiple Actions (CMA) that propagates constraints using duration occupancy bounds to prune many branches at once. The low-level planner is enhanced with SIPPS-WC, which accounts for soft conflicts during waiting. Experimental results show that CBS-AA, particularly the CMAS variant (CMA + SIPPS-WC), dramatically reduces high-level node expansions (up to 90% less than CCBS), achieves higher success rates, scales to 50 agents on large maps where CCBS fails, and remains optimal while outperforming LS-M* in runtime.

## Method Summary
CBS-AA extends the two-level CBS framework to continuous-time MAPF-AA. At the high level, a tree search maintains sets of constraints per agent. The conflict detector identifies the earliest duration-conflict (based on Duration Occupancy) between any two agents and classifies it as IN-IN, OUT-IN, or WAIT-IN. The constraint generator then creates new constraints either via CSA (adding a single occupancy constraint at a critical time point, effective for all wait durations) or via CMA (computing an interval using minimum IN/OUT occupancy times and adding a motion/ wait constraint over that interval, thus pruning many related conflicts). The low-level planner solves each agent’s individual pathfinding problem under the current constraint set using a modified SIPP algorithm (SIPPS-WC), which tracks safe intervals and a counter for potential soft conflicts during waits to prefer paths that cause fewer high-level conflicts. The algorithm is optimal and complete under Assumption 1 (all edges for an agent have equal travel time). The synergy between stronger constraints and soft-conflict guidance yields significant performance gains.

## Key Results
- CMAS reduces high-level node expansions by up to 90% compared to CCBS.
- Achieves substantially higher success rates and scales to 50 agents on large maps where CCBS fails.
- Maintains optimality identical to LS-M* while solving faster.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1: Constraint on Single Action (CSA)
- **Claim:** CSA resolves the infinite branching problem of CCBS by constraining a vertex over a time interval rather than a specific wait-action duration.
- **Mechanism:** For WAIT-IN conflicts, CSA adds an Occupancy Constraint (OC) on the conflict vertex `v` at the critical time point `t_r` (`min(t_i2, t_j2)`), forbidding *all* actions (IN, OUT, WAIT) that occupy `v` at `t_r`. This implicitly forbids all possible wait-action durations whose time interval overlaps `t_r` because any such wait action would occupy `v` at `t_r`.
- **Core assumption:** The set of all actions occupying a vertex `v` at a single time point `t_r` includes every possible wait-action duration whose interval overlaps `t_r`. Forbidding occupancy at `t_r` is sufficient to cover the uncountably infinite set.
- **Evidence anchors:** Abstract mentions CSA; Section 4.3 details Eq. (3) and states that CSA may affect all IN, OUT, WAIT actions, contrasting with CCBS’s specific WAIT constraint.
- **Break condition:** If the computed time interval for the OC is invalidated by other constraints or if the conflict model’s duration occupancy definition changes such that a single time point is insufficient to block overlapping wait intervals.

### Mechanism 2: Constraint on Multiple Actions (CMA)
- **Claim:** CMA propagates conflict constraints across multiple actions and extended time intervals using Duration Occupancy (DO) properties, drastically reducing high-level node expansions.
- **Mechanism:** For each conflict type (IN-IN, OUT-IN, WAIT-IN), CMA calculates a time interval `T` based on the minimum IN/OUT durations (`τ_in`, `τ_out`) for the involved agents. It then adds Motion Constraints (MC) on `*→v` moves or Wait Constraints (WC) on `v` for `T`, forbidding entire classes of actions over that range. This single branching eliminates many potential future conflicts.
- **Core assumption:** The minimum travel times `τ_in(v)`, `τ_out(v)` for an agent at a vertex `v` are well-defined and provide a lower bound on occupancy duration. Propagating constraints using these bounds is safe (does not eliminate optimal solutions) and effective.
- **Evidence anchors:** Abstract mentions CMA and up to 90% reduction; Section 4.4 provides equations (4)-(8) computing `T`; Section 4.5 introduces Assumption 1 to guarantee valid intervals.
- **Break condition:** If Assumption 1 (uniform edge duration per agent) does not hold and `τ_in(v)`, `τ_out(v)` differ significantly, the computed interval `T` may become invalid (`r < l`) or overly restrictive, potentially eliminating feasible solutions and harming completeness/optimality.

### Mechanism 3: SIPPS-WC
- **Claim:** SIPPS-WC improves low-level path quality by tracking soft conflicts (potential future conflicts with other agents' paths) that occur during safe-interval waiting, guiding the search toward paths with fewer high-level conflicts.
- **Mechanism:** In SIPP, a state is `(v, t, t_h)` (vertex, arrival time, safe interval end). SIPPS-WC adds a counter `c_w_v` counting the number of soft conflicts (overlaps with other agents' planned actions) that would occur if the agent waited from `t` to `t_h` at `v`. States are dominated by `(v, t_h, c_w_v)`. This allows the low-level planner to prefer paths that delay moves to avoid accumulating soft wait-conflicts.
- **Core assumption:** A low-level path that incurs fewer soft conflicts during its safe-interval waits is more likely to remain conflict-free at the high level, reducing costly constraint propagation.
- **Evidence anchors:** Abstract mentions SIPPS-WC; Section 4.2 describes the state and domination rule; Section 5.1 shows CMAS success rates for 30+ agents.
- **Break condition:** If soft conflict counting is inaccurate or the overhead outweighs benefits, overall solver performance may degrade, especially on small maps.

- **Concept: Duration Occupancy (DO)**
  - *Why needed here:* DO is the core conflict model for MAPF-AA. It defines that an agent occupies both endpoints of its traversed edge during the entire move duration. This changes conflict detection (interval overlap at vertices) and enables CMA’s propagation.

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map:**
  1. **High-Level Controller (Tree Search):** Manages OPEN list, selects node with min `g` (sum of costs), calls `DetectConflict`.
  2. **Conflict Detector:** For each vertex `v`, collects time intervals where agents occupy `v` (via DO). Detects earliest conflict `c_ft` between any agent pair, classified as IN-IN, OUT-IN, or WAIT-IN.
  3. **Constraint Generator (CSA/CMA):** Takes `c_ft`, produces a set `Ω` of constraints (1 or 2 branches). CSA uses simple OC/MC. CMA computes extended intervals `T` using `τ_in/τ_out`.
  4. **Low-Level Planner (SIPPS-WC):** For each agent with new constraint set `Ω'`, runs continuous-time SIPP. State includes `(v, t, t_h, c_w_v)`. Generates optimal individual path satisfying all `Ω'` and minimizing soft conflicts (`c_w_v`).
- **Critical path:** Root node → LowLevelPlan (individual paths) → DetectConflict (find earliest `c_ft`) → GenerateConstraints (CSA or CMA, produce 2 child constraints) → For each constraint: new `Ω'` → LowLevelPlan → if conflict-free, return solution; else recurse.
- **Design tradeoffs:**
  - *Completeness/Optimality vs. Scalability:* CSA is simpler and preserves MD constraints but may need many branches. CMA uses stronger propagation (better scaling) but relies on Assumption 1 for guarantee of valid intervals.
  - *Low-level Optimality vs. Speed:* SIPPS-WC considers soft conflicts (better high-level guidance) but increases per-call runtime.
  - *Constraint Strength:* Stronger constraints (CMA) prune more but risk invalid intervals if `τ_in(v) ≠ τ_out(v)` or edges have heterogeneous durations without vertex splitting.
- **Failure signatures:**
  1. **Infinite branching (CCBS legacy):** High-level tree keeps expanding on wait conflicts without resolution. Check if WAIT-IN constraints are OCs (CSA) or improper MCs on specific wait durations.
  2. **Invalid constraint interval:** CMA generates constraint `<agent, *→v, [l, r)>` where `r < l`. This occurs if `t_j1 + τ_j_out < t_i1` for OUT-IN, etc. Check Assumption 1 adherence; if violated, edge-uniformization or vertex splitting may be needed.
  3. **Low-level failure:** `LowLevelPlan` returns `failure` for a constraint set `Ω'`. Means constraints are unsatisfiable (e.g., an OC blocks all safe intervals at start). Indicates over-constrained branch; should be pruned.
- **First 3 experiments:**
  1. **Reproduce CCBS failure case:** Run CCBS on Example 1 (Fig. 2) with 3 agents and heterogeneous speeds. Observe it fails to find optimal solution due to wait-action constraint issue.
  2. **Compare CSA vs. CCBS:** On small instances (e.g., 10 agents, `empty-32-32`), measure success rate and high-level node expansions. CSA should match or slightly exceed CCBS due to proper WAIT-IN handling.
  3. **Validate CMA propagation:** On medium instances (e.g., 20 agents), compare number of high-level expansions between CSA and CMA. Expect ~90% reduction per paper. Also test with/without Assumption 1 (non-uniform edges) to observe potential failure from invalid intervals.

## Limitations
- The paper’s comparison with CCBS relies on an inconsistent description of CCBS’s wait constraint handling, which may confound performance claims.
- CMA’s propagation depends on Assumption 1 (uniform edge duration per agent); on graphs with heterogeneous edge lengths it may generate invalid constraint intervals (`r < l`) or incorrectly prune optimal solutions.
- SIPPS-WC adds soft-conflict tracking overhead, and its net benefit on scaling, especially on small instances, is not conclusively proven.
