# Persona Vectors in Games: Measuring and Steering Strategies via Activation Vectors

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2603.21398
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.21398
- **Reference count:** 22
- **Primary result:** Persona vectors extracted via contrastive activation addition enable causal steering of high-level behavioral traits (e.g., altruism, forgiveness) in LLM agents, shifting both strategic choices and rhetorical expression in canonical games.

## Executive Summary
The paper introduces *persona vectors*—directions in activation space that correspond to abstract behavioral traits such as altruism and forgiveness. Using contrastive activation addition on Qwen 2.5-7B, the authors compute these vectors from mean activation differences between trait-positive and trait-negative responses. When applied to six canonical strategic games, steering along persona vectors causally influences quantitative decisions (e.g., donations in the Dictator Game) and LLM-rated trait expression. However, notable divergences emerge: rhetoric and strategy are not always aligned, especially for forgiveness where higher trait ratings coincide with fewer forgiving actions. The study also finds that vectors for an agent’s own behavior and its expectations of others are partially distinct, allowing independent modulation. Positive steering is reliable; negative steering is weaker and coherence degrades at high magnitudes.

## Method Summary
For each target trait (altruism, forgiveness, expectations of others), the authors first generate 50 moral dilemma questions, each paired with 5 positive and 5 negative prompt prefixes designed to elicit opposing trait expressions. They run Qwen 2.5-7B in teacher-forcing mode, recording mean hidden-state activations at layer 20 for each generated response. Using GPT-4.1-mini to rate responses on a 0–100 scale, they retain only prefix pairs where the positive response scores ≥50 and the negative response scores <50. The persona vector *x* at layer 20 is then computed as the mean difference between positive and negative activations across all retained pairs: *x*(20) = mean(*P*ᵢ – *N*ᵢ). To steer behavior, they modify the model’s activations at generation time: *ã*(20) = *a*(20) + β·*x*(20), varying β. The model is evaluated on six one-shot games; outcomes are measured via extracted numeric decisions, GPT-4.1-mini trait ratings, and projection of activations onto the persona vector. Coherence is also rated to monitor degradation.

## Key Results
- Altruism steering (β=3) raised mean donations in the Dictator Game from $15 (baseline) to $55 while increasing LLM-rated altruism.
- Steering forgiveness increased trait ratings but paradoxically reduced forgiving choices in several games, revealing a rhetoric‑strategy gap.
- Vectors representing self‑behavior and expectations of others are partially distinct; steering the “expected altruism” vector shifts predicted others’ generosity more strongly than steering the original altruism vector.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1: Linearity of trait representation
- **Claim:** Persona vectors capture linearly separable directions in activation space corresponding to high-level traits.
- **Mechanism:** Contrastive activation addition computes the mean difference in hidden states between trait‑positive and trait‑negative responses at a chosen layer (ℓ=20). This mean difference is hypothesized to point toward the abstract representation of the trait.
- **Core assumption:** Consistent trait expression differences correspond to a systematic, approximately linear shift in intermediate activations that can be isolated by averaging across many contrastive examples.
- **Evidence anchors:** [abstract] “constructing persona vectors … by contrastive activation addition.”; [section 3.2] formula *x*(ℓ) = mean(*P*ᵢ – *N*ᵢ) and focus on layer 20; background on activation steering (e.g., Rimsky et al., 2024).
- **Break condition:** If trait‑positive and trait‑negative behaviors activate overlapping or non‑opposed circuits, the mean difference may not isolate a clean direction, yielding noisy or ineffective steering.

### Mechanism 2: Single‑layer intervention affects both decision and justification
- **Claim:** Adding the persona vector at a key layer causally influences both the model’s strategic choices and its rhetorical justifications.
- **Mechanism:** The intervention shifts the forward pass toward the activation pattern associated with the target trait, which then biases both the final decision token and the reasoning chain.
- **Core assumption:** Modifying activations at an intermediate layer propagates to the output distribution in a way that systematically changes both the “what” (choice) and the “how” (justification) of the response.
- **Evidence anchors:** [section 4.2] β‑dependent increases in donations (Dictator Game: $15 → $55 at β=3) and GPT‑rated altruism; [section 4.3] qualitative shifts in word choice and moral framing.
- **Break condition:** If the trait’s representation is decoupled such that intervening at a single layer affects justification generation but not the final decision head (or vice versa), steering may produce rhetoric‑strategy divergence as seen with forgiveness.

### Mechanism 3: Separability of self versus expectation representations
- **Claim:** Vectors for an agent’s own behavioral tendencies and its expectations about others are partially distinct, allowing independent tuning.
- **Mechanism:** Separate vectors are constructed by contrasting scenarios where the model is the *decider* (self‑behavior) versus where it *predicts* another’s behavior (expectation of others). These directions, while not orthogonal, activate sufficiently different patterns to yield divergent steering effects.
- **Core assumption:** The model maintains separable internal representations for “I will do X” and “others will do X,” which can be teased apart by changing the actor in the prompt.
- **Evidence anchors:** [section 5.2] Steering the “expected altruism” vector shifts predicted others’ generosity more strongly than the original altruism vector; differential effects across games shown in Fig. 6.
- **Break condition:** If the model’s theory of mind entirely mirrors its own policy (i.e., “I am altruistic, therefore others are altruistic”), the two vectors would be nearly identical, and steering one would equally affect both self and expectation measures.

## Foundational Learning
- **Concept: Contrastive Activation Addition (CAA)**
  - **Why needed:** Core method for extracting and applying persona vectors; understanding how mean activation differences from prompt prefixes define a trait direction is essential to replicate the approach.
  - **Quick check:** What is the mathematical definition of a persona vector at layer ℓ, and why are positive/negative prefixes applied to the same set of questions?
- **Concept: Game‑theoretic action spaces as behavioral metrics**
  - **Why needed:** The paper evaluates steering effects on concrete, quantifiable choices (e.g., dollars given, cooperate/defect). Interpreting these actions requires knowing what they signal about traits like altruism.
  - **Quick check:** In an Ultimatum Game, what does a low offer by Agent 1 indicate about altruism or fairness, and how might rejection by Agent 2 complicate this interpretation?
- **Concept: LLM‑as‑judge for rhetorical trait measurement**
  - **Why needed:** The study uses GPT‑4.1‑mini to rate how strongly responses express a trait, providing a second, language‑based measure of steering beyond numeric choices; this introduces dependency and potential bias.
  - **Quick check:** What are two limitations of using an LLM (like GPT‑4.1‑mini) to rate the “altruism” of another LLM’s responses, and how might these affect the validity of the rating as a trait measure?

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component map:**  
Base Model -> Prompt Template System -> Activation Recorder -> Vector Computation Module -> Steering Interceptor -> Game Suite Runner -> Measurement Pipeline -> Coherence Evaluator

**Critical path:**  
Data Collection (prefix‑question pairs) → Activation Extraction → Vector Definition → Steering Intervention → Multi‑Metric Evaluation (strategy, rhetoric, coherence)

**Design tradeoffs:**  
- *Steering magnitude vs. coherence:* Stronger steering (higher |β|) yields larger behavioral shifts but degrades linguistic coherence; optimal β is context‑dependent (typically |β| ≤ 3).  
- *Layer selection:* Later layers are chosen (ℓ=20) under the hypothesis they contain more abstract representations; this is a heuristic and may not generalize.  
- *Rater dependency:* All trait ratings depend on GPT‑4.1‑mini; any biases in this judge directly shape measured “trait expression.”

**Failure signatures:**  
- *Coherence collapse:* At |β| > 3–4, responses become rambling, contradictory, or switch languages.  
- *Rhetoric‑strategy divergence:* High β increases trait‑rated rhetoric but decreases trait‑consistent actions (e.g., forgiveness steering).  
- *Weak negative steering:* β < 0 produces smaller, less reliable changes in both ratings and strategy compared to β > 0.  
- *Vector non‑transferability:* Effects are demonstrated only on Qwen 2.5‑7B; the same vector may fail on other architectures.

**First 3 experiments:**  
1. *Replicate altruism vector construction:* Use positive/negative prefixes from Appendix A on 50 moral dilemmas with Qwen 2.5‑7B; filter responses to GPT rating contrast (≥50 vs. <50); compute x(20) and verify projection distinguishes prefix conditions on a held‑out set.  
2. *Baseline steering in Dictator Game:* For β ∈ {0, 1, 2, 3, -1, -2, -3}, run 50 trials each; measure mean dollars given, GPT altruism rating, and coherence. Expect monotonic increase in giving and rating for β>0.  
3. *Compare self vs. expectation vectors:* Construct “expected altruism” vector using prefixes where the model predicts Agent 2’s giving. In the Dictator Game, compare steering effects of the altruism vector (on model’s own giving) versus the expected altruism vector (on model’s prediction of Agent 2’s giving); the latter should shift predicted generosity more strongly.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- **Are self‑behavior and expectation representations orthogonal in activation space, and does this separability generalize across traits and models?**  
  The paper reports they are “partially distinct” for Qwen 2.5‑7B but does not quantify linear independence or test across other models. Resolution would require computing cosine similarities and subspace analyses across multiple model families and traits.

- **What mechanism causes the divergence between LLM‑judged trait expression and actual strategic choices under steering (e.g., forgiveness ratings rise while forgiving actions fall)?**  
  The root cause is not investigated; possible factors include judge bias, separate neural pathways for decision vs. language, or steering artifacts. Evidence could come from comparing internal activations during divergent vs. aligned responses, using multiple judges (human and diverse LLMs), or analyzing attention distributions.

- **How robust are persona vector effects when applied to diverse model architectures and repeated strategic environments with memory and reputation?**  
  The study is limited to Qwen 2.5‑7B and one‑shot anonymous games; transferability to other LLMs and stability in iterative interactions remain unknown. Resolution would involve replicating the pipeline on at least three additional model families and implementing iterated versions of key games (e.g., repeated Prisoner’s Dilemma, sequential Dictator with reputation tracking).

## Limitations
- Single‑model scope: only Qwen 2.5‑7B tested; transferability unknown.
- Unstable negative steering: β < 0 produces inconsistent shifts in strategy and ratings.
- Rhetoric‑strategy divergence: forgiveness steering increased ratings but decreased forgiving choices.

## Confidence
- Trait separability (linearly distinguishable positive/negative prefixes): **High**
- Steering causality (positive β reliably shifts behavior and ratings): **Medium**
- Self/expectation distinctness (partial independence of vectors): **Medium**
