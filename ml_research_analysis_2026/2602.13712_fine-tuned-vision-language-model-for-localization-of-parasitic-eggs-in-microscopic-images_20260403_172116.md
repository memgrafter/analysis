# Fine-tuned Vision Language Model for Localization of Parasitic Eggs in Microscopic Images

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2602.13712
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.13712
- Reference count: 17
- Primary result: Mean Intersection over Union (mIoU) of 0.94 on the test set, significantly outperforming both the base Florence-2 model and EfficientDet.

## Executive Summary
Soil-transmitted helminth infections rely on manual microscopic examination of stool samples to detect parasitic eggs, a process that is time-consuming and susceptible to human error. This study develops an automated system for localizing parasitic eggs by fine-tuning the Microsoft Florence-2 vision-language model on the Chula-ParasiteEgg-11 dataset. The fine-tuned model achieves a mean Intersection over Union (mIoU) of 0.94 on the test set, far surpassing the base Florence-2 (which fails without adaptation) and the EfficientDet detector. The high accuracy and consistency across varied imaging conditions demonstrate the viability of prompt-driven VLMs for parasitological diagnostics.

The method converts the detection task into a text prompt (“detect parasitic eggs”) and trains the model to output bounding box coordinates as a structured token sequence. Using LoRA for parameter-efficient adaptation, the model is trained for three epochs on over 6,600 images with preprocessing to a fixed resolution. The resulting IoU distribution peaks near perfect overlap, indicating precise and reliable localization. This work highlights the potential of VLMs to reduce diagnostic burden in endemic regions.

## Method Summary
The authors fine-tune Florence-2-large (0.77B parameters), a vision-language model pretrained on the FLD-5B dataset, for parasitic egg detection. The Chula-ParasiteEgg-11 dataset contains 11,000 microscopic images across 11 egg species, deliberately capturing diverse resolutions, lighting, focus, and noise to reflect real-world variability. Images are resized to 768×768 and normalized. The model processes a text prompt (“detect parasitic eggs”) and learns to generate a token sequence encoding bounding box coordinates. Training employs a sequence-to-sequence cross-entropy loss, LoRA (rank 8) applied to attention layers, a learning rate of 5×10⁻⁵, batch size 1, gradient accumulation steps 8, and runs for three epochs on an NVIDIA RTX2000 Ada GPU. The dataset is split 60/20/20 into training, validation, and test sets. Performance is evaluated using mean Intersection over Union (mIoU).

## Key Results
- Mean Intersection over Union (mIoU) of **0.94** on the test set.
- The base Florence-2 model fails without fine-tuning, producing near-zero IoU scores.
- The fine-tuned model outperforms EfficientDet in both accuracy and consistency (IoU distribution peaks closer to 1.0 with fewer low-IoU outliers).

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- **Claim**: The Florence-2 model's prompt-driven, sequence-to-sequence architecture allows it to be adapted for the specific object detection task of parasitic egg localization through fine-tuning.
- **Mechanism**: Florence-2 is pretrained as a unified vision-language model that interprets a text prompt (e.g., "detect parasitic eggs") to generate structured output (bounding box coordinates). Fine-tuning adjusts the model's parameters on the domain-specific Chula-ParasiteEgg-11 dataset, aligning its text-to-output mapping to the visual patterns and coordinate format of microscopic egg images.
- **Core assumption**: The underlying visual representations learned on massive general data (FLD-5B) contain sufficient low-level features (edges, textures, shapes) that are transferable to the parasitic egg domain with targeted adaptation.
- **Evidence anchors**:
  - [abstract] "fine-tunes the Microsoft Florence-2 vision-language model on the Chula-ParasiteEgg-11 dataset"
  - [section III.B] "Florence-2 model is built in a sequence-to-sequence design structure... pretrained on the FLD-5B dataset... The model has been pretrained... in terms of fine-tuning capability, the model can compete with larger specialist models after the model is fine-tuned with public human-annotated data."
  - [corpus] Corpus neighbors show VLMs being fine-tuned for other medical imaging tasks (TB diagnosis, skin lesions), providing indirect evidence that prompt-based VLM architectures are amenable to medical domain fine-tuning. Direct evidence for this specific parasitic task is absent from the provided corpus.
- **Break condition**: If the base model's representations are too dissimilar to the target domain (evidenced by complete failure, e.g., the reported spike at 0 IoU for base Florence-2), or if the domain shift involves novel visual primitives not present in pretraining data, fine-tuning may fail to converge or generalize.

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim**: The significant performance gain from fine-tuning (mIoU 0.94 vs. base failure) is primarily driven by the model learning to overcome the severe domain shift between natural images in pretraining data and the specialized, noisy, low-contrast microscopic images.
- **Mechanism**: The Chula-ParasiteEgg-11 dataset intentionally includes challenging real-world variations (out-of-focus, noise, motion blur, varied resolutions/lighting from different microscopy setups). Fine-tuning forces the model to adjust its internal feature extraction and attention mechanisms to become robust to these degradations and to discriminate the subtle morphological features of 11 visually similar egg species against complex backgrounds.
- **Core assumption**: The dataset's diversity in acquisition conditions is representative of real-world diagnostic variability, and learning these invariances is critical for a deployable system.
- **Evidence anchors**:
  - [section III.A] "Image acquisition is carried out using a variety of devices... This approach introduces differences in resolutions, lighting and setting conditions to the images... The broad spectrum of image quality and visual characteristic is included intentionally to improve the robustness and generation capability of the detection models."
  - [section IV.B] "In contrast, after fine-tuning with parasitic egg images, the model’s localization performance improved substantially, achieving a high mean IoU of 0.94." (Implies robustness gained).
  - [corpus] Corpus evidence is weak; neighbors discuss general medical imaging challenges but not the specific noise/artifact profile of parasitic microscopy. The mechanism of robustness to acquisition variability is inferred from the dataset description.
- **Break condition**: If the deployment environment's image quality distribution is not covered by the training set's "broad spectrum" (e.g., a new microscope type with a unique artifact), model performance could degrade significantly. The paper does not test out-of-distribution robustness explicitly.

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim**: The VLM's unified architecture may implicitly perform a form of simultaneous classification and localization, where the text-generation process is conditioned on spatial regions, leading to more precise bounding box regression than a pure detection head like EfficientDet's.
- **Mechanism**: In Florence-2's seq2seq framework, generating the output token sequence representing box coordinates may be more tightly coupled with the visual tokens representing object regions than in a standard CNN-based detector where classification and box regression heads are separate. Fine-tuning may optimize this coupling for the precise geometry of eggs.
- **Core assumption**: The task of "localizing all parasitic eggs" expressed as a language instruction engages the model's grounding capabilities in a way that yields more accurate spatial predictions than a task-specific detection loss alone.
- **Core assumption**: The comparative advantage over EfficientDet stems from this architectural coupling, not just from using a larger pretrained model.
- **Evidence anchors**:
  - [section III.B] Describes Florence-2 as supporting "grounding, object detection to segmentation" via "prompt-driven representation."
  - [section IV.B] Shows fine-tuned Florence-2 has a "higher frequency of near-perfect overlaps and fewer low-IoU outliers" compared to EfficientDet. The distribution shift suggests more consistent, precise regression.
  - [corpus] Corpus neighbors use varied architectures (SIGLIP+Gemma, CoAtNet, DETR). No direct comparison isolates the "unified VLM vs. pure detector" variable for parasitic eggs. Evidence is comparative result only.
- **Break condition**: If the task required extreme efficiency or real-time speeds, the generative seq2seq process might be a liability. Also, if egg shapes were extremely irregular or non-rectangular, the bounding box paradigm itself (used by both models) would be a limiting factor, not the architecture.

## Architecture Onboarding

**Component map:**  
Input (image) -> Preprocessing (resize to 768×768, normalize, pad) -> Core Model (Florence-2: DaViT image encoder -> multimodal encoder-decoder -> text decoder) with prompt (“detect parasitic eggs”) -> Output Head (linear projection to structured tokens) -> Post-processing (parse tokens to bounding box coordinates)

**Critical path:**  
Data Loading (11k images) -> Preprocessing (resize/normalize) -> Forward Pass (Image + Prompt -> Token Seq) -> Loss Calc (vs. Target Token Seq) -> Backward Pass (with LoRA) -> Parameter Update -> Validation mIoU Check -> Repeat Epochs (3) -> Final Test Evaluation

**Design tradeoffs:**  
- Batch Size (1) vs. Gradient Accumulation (8): Chosen due to GPU memory limits (RTX2000 Ada) with a 0.77B model. Accumulation simulates a larger batch (8) for stable gradient estimation but slows iteration.
- LoRA (rank 8) vs. Full Fine-Tuning: Saves memory/compute, reduces overfitting risk on a 11k dataset, but capacity for adaptation is limited by low rank.
- Resolution (768x768) vs. Native Resolution: Balances preserving egg-scale details (eggs ~15-100μm) with memory constraints. Downsampling may lose fine details.
- Seq2Seq Loss vs. Detection-Specific Loss: Uses the model's native objective. Advantage: unified framework. Disadvantage: may be less sample-efficient than a dedicated detection loss (like EfficientDet's).

**Failure signatures:**  
1. **Training Instability:** Loss NaN or spikes, likely due to too-high LR or poor gradient accumulation.
2. **Base Model Failure Pattern:** As seen in Fig 3, a massive spike at IoU=0 indicates the model generates no valid boxes or boxes with zero overlap—a sign of complete task misalignment.
3. **Overfitting:** Training mIoU continues rising while validation mIoU plateaus or drops. Watch for this with only 3 epochs (may be underfit) but possible with higher LR or insufficient data augment.
4. **Poor Generalization:** High validation mIoU but low test mIoU suggests dataset split issues or lack of robustness to a specific condition (e.g., a certain microscope type only in test).

**First 3 experiments:**  
1. **Baseline Verification:** Run the *base* (untuned) Florence-2-large on a small, fixed subset (e.g., 100 images) of the test set with the prompt “detect parasitic eggs”. Confirm the failure pattern (massive zero-IoU detections) matches Figure 3. This validates the need for fine-tuning.
2. **Sanity Fine-Tune:** Perform a minimal fine-tune on a tiny subset (e.g., 100 training images) for 1 epoch. Goal is to see if *any* learning occurs—check that validation IoU moves meaningfully from 0. Expect low but non-zero IoU. Checks data pipeline, loss function, and backpropagation.
3. **Full Replication & Ablation:** Run the full training protocol as described (3 epochs, LoRA rank 8, 60/20/20 split). Primary metric: test mIoU. Must reproduce ~0.94. A key ablation: compare to a *full fine-tune* (no LoRA) if memory allows to assess if LoRA bottlenecked performance. Also, visualize 5-10 predictions to manually verify box quality matches Figure 6.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- **Question:** How does the fine-tuned Florence-2 model generalize to externally collected microscopic images from diverse clinical settings?
- **Basis in paper:** [inferred] The study uses the Chula-ParasiteEgg-11 dataset from a single source (ICIP challenge) and does not evaluate on independent datasets, despite acknowledging variations in imaging devices and conditions within that dataset.
- **Why unresolved:** Real-world deployment requires robustness across different laboratories, microscope types, and sample preparation methods. Without external validation, the model's generalizability remains uncertain.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Performance evaluation on multiple external datasets from various geographic locations and labs, with statistical comparison of mIoU and detection consistency across domains.

### Open Question 2
- **Question:** What is the per-species classification accuracy of the model, and does it reliably distinguish between morphologically similar parasitic eggs?
- **Basis in paper:** [inferred] The paper reports only localization mIoU (0.94) and does not provide classification metrics (e.g., precision, recall per class), even though the dataset contains 11 distinct egg species and classification is a key diagnostic need.
- **Why unresolved:** Localization alone is insufficient for diagnosis; correct species identification determines treatment. High IoU does not imply correct classification, especially for similar species like hookworm vs. trichuris.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Reporting classification accuracy, confusion matrices, and failure analysis for each species on the test set, possibly by extending the VLM to output class labels and evaluating jointly.

### Open Question 3
- **Question:** What are the inference speed and resource requirements of the fine-tuned Florence-2 model, and can it be deployed on low-cost hardware suitable for endemic regions?
- **Basis in paper:** [inferred] The training hardware is specified (NVIDIA RTX2000 Ada), but no inference metrics (e.g., latency, FPS) or model size details are provided. The claim of a "scalable engineering solution" lacks evidence of computational efficiency.
- **Why unresolved:** STH-endemic areas often have limited computational resources. A model requiring high-end GPUs is impractical. Without efficiency data, deployment feasibility is unknown.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Measuring inference time, memory usage, and power consumption on a range of devices (e.g., Raspberry Pi, low-end laptops) and comparing with lighter detectors; exploring quantization or distillation for optimization.

## Limitations
- **Reproducibility gaps**: Exact prompt template, LoRA target modules, annotation file format, and normalization values are unspecified, preventing exact replication.
- **Generalization scope**: Dataset’s "broad spectrum" of acquisition conditions is claimed to aid robustness, but out-of-distribution tests (e.g., unseen microscope types) are absent.
- **Efficiency & clinical utility**: No reporting of inference speed, memory footprint, or integration cost challenges for real-world deployment.
