# When Should a Model Change Its Mind? An Energy-Based Theory and Regularizer for Concept Drift in Electrocardiogram (ECG) Signals

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2602.22294  
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.22294  
- **Reference count:** 21  
- **Primary result:** ECRL reduces fused representation drift by >45% and lifts perturbed accuracy from 72.6% to 85.5% in a trimodal hybrid model, while clean accuracy stays above 94%.

## Executive Summary
Deep ECG classifiers mistake benign physiological variations (e.g., amplitude or heart‑rate changes) as concept drift, leading to unstable predictions—especially in multimodal fusion. This paper introduces the Physiologic Energy Conservation Theory (PECT), which asserts that under label‑preserving perturbations, latent representation drift should scale proportionally with the change in signal energy. The authors operationalize PECT via Energy‑Constrained Representation Learning (ECRL), a regularizer that penalizes energy‑inconsistent latent movement during training. Across seven unimodal and multimodal ECG models, ECRL dramatically improves robustness (e.g., +12.9% absolute perturbed accuracy in the strongest trimodal model) while preserving clean performance, demonstrating that aligning latent dynamics with physiologic energy stabilizes predictions under virtual drift.

## Method Summary
The study uses the Khan and Hussain ECG dataset, preprocessing signals into three aligned modalities: 1‑D temporal (length 1000), 2‑D time‑frequency scalograms (128×128), and 1‑D frequency vectors (128 bins). Each modality is processed by a dedicated encoder (1D‑CNN, 2D‑CNN, Transformer). Late fusion concatenates the embeddings and projects them to a 256‑D fused representation, which is classified by a linear layer. PECT posits that for any benign perturbation, the squared latent displacement is bounded by a constant times the absolute change in physiologic energy (computed as the sum of squared amplitudes). ECRL enforces this during fine‑tuning by adding two losses: a Basic Invariance Term (BIT) that discourages any drift, and a PECT term that penalizes deviation from a batch‑level energy‑coupling coefficient. Models are trained on clean‑perturbed pairs with Adam (lr=3e‑4) and specific regularization weights (λ_BIT=1e‑4, λ_PECT=1e‑2) for five epochs. Robustness is evaluated on held‑out clean and perturbed test sets using accuracy, robustness gap, and representation drift metrics.

## Key Results
- Trimodal hybrid: perturbed accuracy increases from 72.6% to 85.5%; clean accuracy remains high (96.0% → 94.1%).  
- Fused representation drift decreases by over 45% across all multimodal models.  
- All seven models show substantial robustness gains (up to +266% relative improvement in perturbed accuracy) with negligible clean accuracy loss (<2%), confirming ECRL’s effectiveness without compromising performance.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
**Claim:** Latent representation drift in ECG models should scale proportionally with the underlying physiologic energy change under label‑preserving perturbations.  
**Mechanism:** PECT establishes an energy–drift consistency principle: the normalized squared displacement in latent space (∥δz∥²) is linearly bounded by the absolute change in signal energy (|ΔE_phys|). This acts as a physical law governing representation stability during benign variations.  
**Core assumption:** Physiologic energy (E(x)=∑x(t)²) is a meaningful scalar that correlates with acceptable, label‑preserving signal changes; the relationship is approximately linear with a modality‑specific constant L_m (energy‑Lipschitz condition).  
**Evidence anchors:** Abstract and Section III.B (Eqs. 5 & 6) formalize the proportionality and Lipschitz bound; internal experiments show strong correlation, while no prior work anchors drift to a measured energy quantity.  
**Break condition:** Persistent violations of ∥δz_fused∥² ≤ κ|ΔE_phys| across samples of the same class indicate real concept change rather than benign virtual drift. A break occurs if, for a fixed |ΔE_phys|, the variance of ∥δz_fused∥² is high or the mean far exceeds the bound.

### Mechanism 2
**Claim:** Energy‑Constrained Representation Learning (ECRL) operationalizes PECT by regularizing latent movement during training, forcing encoders and fusion to respect energy‑drift consistency.  
**Mechanism:** ECRL adds two penalty terms to the classification loss: (1) the Basic Invariance Term (BIT = ∥δz_fused∥²₂) encourages overall stability, and (2) the PECT term penalizes deviation of the batch‑average energy‑coupling coefficient Γ = ∥δz∥²/(|ΔE_phys|+ϵ) from its mean, enforcing proportionality. This reshapes latent geometry without altering inference.  
**Core assumption:** Minimizing these losses on clean‑perturbed pairs aligns the encoder/fusion responses with the energy‑drift law for all future unseen perturbations; the small ϵ prevents gradient explosions near zero energy change.  
**Evidence anchors:** Abstract and Section IV.I (Algorithm 1) describe the loss; Section V.D & Table IV show large reductions in robustness degradation (e.g., M1: 0.5430→0.0591) and drift (40–65%) with clean accuracy within ±2%.  
**Break condition:** If clean accuracy degrades >3–5% during fine‑tuning, over‑regularization is harming discriminative features; reducing κ (via λ_PECT) restores balance.

### Mechanism 3
**Claim:** Multimodal fusion amplifies representation drift under virtual drift because different modality encoders have mismatched intrinsic sensitivities to physiologic energy changes.  
**Mechanism:** Unimodal encoders exhibit distinct energy‑response functions g_m(·) with different Lipschitz constants L_m. Late fusion (linear concatenation + projection) combines latent vectors, so the fused displacement is a weighted sum of modality drifts. If one modality has a high L_m while another is stable, the fused drift can become disproportionally large (κ = Σ α_m L_m), violating the energy‑drift bound even for small perturbations.  
**Core assumption:** Late fusion is locally linear on concatenated embeddings; fused drift accumulates additively based on modality‑specific sensitivities.  
**Evidence anchors:** Abstract notes “fused representation drift decreases by over 45%” and that fusion amplifies instability; Section V.C & Table III show baseline multimodal models suffer much higher robustness degradation than unimodal ones, which ECRL dramatically reduces.  
**Break condition:** If ECRL fails to reduce fused drift for a multimodal model, either the fusion operator is highly nonlinear (violating the linearity assumption) or the perturbation suite does not cover the dominant virtual‑drift modes relevant to that fusion topology.

## Foundational Learning

- **Physiologic Energy of an ECG Signal**  
  *Why needed:* It is the anchor variable for PECT; the entire theory hinges on computing E(x)=∑x(t)² as a physically interpretable measure of signal strength that changes under benign perturbations.  
  *Quick check:* Given a normalized ECG segment x[n], its discrete energy is ∑ x[n]². A 10% amplitude scaling (0.9→1.1×) increases energy by approximately 21% because energy scales with the square of the amplitude factor (1.1²=1.21 vs. 0.9²=0.81, a net increase relative to original 1.0).

- **Virtual Drift vs. Real Concept Drift**  
  *Why needed:* PECT’s decision criterion depends on distinguishing these: virtual drift preserves labels (should not flip predictions), while real drift changes P(Y|X) and should trigger a change. Misclassifying one as the other causes instability.  
  *Quick check:* Examples: (i) Virtual – respiration‑induced amplitude modulation; (ii) Real – onset of myocardial infarction altering waveform morphology. PECT’s energy‑drift test classifies each by checking whether the latent drift magnitude ∥δz∥² is consistent with the signal energy change |ΔE_phys|. Consistent → virtual; inconsistent → real.

- **Multimodal Fusion Geometry and Drift Accumulation**  
  *Why needed:* PECT explains why fusion, while improving clean accuracy, can catastrophically worsen robustness. Understanding that fusing embeddings linearly combines modality‑specific drifts (δz_m) and that mismatched energy sensitivities (L_m) lead to amplified fused drift (∥δz_fused∥) is key to diagnosing failure.  
  *Quick check:* If encoder 1 has a high energy‑coupling coefficient C₁ and encoder 2 has a low C₂, the fused coefficient after late fusion is approximately C_fused ≈ α₁C₁+α₂C₂ (weighted by projection scales). If C₁ is large, C_fused can become large as well, causing excessive drift under virtual drift and violating the energy–drift bound.

## Architecture Onboarding

**Component map:**  
Raw ECG → Preprocessing (split into 1D temporal, 2D scalogram, 1D frequency) → Modality encoders (f_time: 1D‑CNN, f_tf: 2D‑CNN, f_freq: Transformer) → Concatenation + projection → Fused embedding z_fused → Classifier (linear layer).  
Additionally: Drift & Energy Measurement Module (computes E(x), ΔE_phys, δz, Γ) and ECRL Regularizer (adds BIT and PECT losses) integrated during training.

**Critical path:**  
Clean batch (x_i, y_i) → synthesize perturbed (˜x_i, y_i) → compute E_clean, E_pert, ΔE_phys → obtain embeddings z_clean, z_pert from all encoders → fuse to z_fused_clean, z_fused_pert → compute δz_fused and Γ_i → total loss L = L_cls + λ_BIT·BIT + λ_PECT·PECT → backward pass updates all encoder and fusion parameters.

**Design tradeoffs:**  
- Regularization strength (λ_PECT): higher values enforce stricter energy‑consistency but may over‑smooth representations, harming clean accuracy; lower values preserve clean performance but leave drift unchecked.  
- Fusion topology: ECRL as formulated assumes late fusion; early or intermediate fusion changes drift accumulation dynamics and may require reformulating the PECT constant κ.  
- Perturbation suite: must cover physiologically plausible, label‑preserving transformations (amplitude, rate, spectral). Inadequate coverage yields a poorly estimated energy‑drift relationship.

**Failure signatures:**  
- High robustness degradation (Acc_clean − Acc_pert > 0.2) in unimodal models indicates an intrinsically high L_m encoder.  
- Multimodal robustness degradation far exceeding the worst unimodal degradation signals fusion‑level drift amplification.  
- Clean accuracy drop >2% during ECRL fine‑tuning indicates over‑regularization (λ_PECT too high).  
- Post‑ECRL energy‑drift correlation r near 0 suggests the model has not learned the proportionality.

**First 3 experiments:**  
1. Unimodal Baseline Sensitivity: Train M1 (1D‑CNN), M2 (2D‑CNN), M3 (Transformer) without ECRL. Compute per‑model energy‑coupling coefficient C_m and robustness gap; expect M1 to show highest C_m and degradation.  
2. Multimodal Drift Amplification: Train baseline M7 (trimodal late fusion). Compare its fused drift magnitude and robustness gap to the best unimodal (M1); expect higher clean accuracy but severe robustness degradation, confirming fusion‑level amplification.  
3. ECRL Stabilization: Fine‑tune all seven models with ECRL (λ_BIT=1e‑4, λ_PECT=1e‑2). Verify: (a) fused drift decreases >40% in multimodal models, (b) perturbed accuracy improves substantially with clean accuracy loss <2%, (c) correlation between ∥δz_fused∥² and |ΔE_phys| increases toward 1.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

1. **Generalizability to other physiological signals** – Does PECT hold for PPG, EMG, EEG, or non‑biomedical time series? The paper only validated on one ECG dataset; cross‑modality replication is needed.  
2. **Robustness to real‑world artifacts** – How does ECRL perform under chaotic, non‑physiologic noise (e.g., electrode motion)? The perturbation suite is idealized; evaluation on raw recordings with artifacts is required.  
3. **Interaction with fusion topology** – Does ECRL benefit early or intermediate fusion? The current study uses late fusion; earlier mixing of features may alter energy dynamics and regularizer placement.  
4. **Hyperparameter sensitivity and tuning** – What are reliable ranges for λ_BIT, λ_PECT, and ϵ across architectures and datasets? The paper used fixed values from cross‑validation on one dataset; systematic studies are needed for practical deployment.

## Limitations
- Critical hyperparameters (ϵ, exact perturbation details, fusion architecture specifics) are missing, hindering exact reproduction.  
- Empirical validation is limited to a single ECG dataset; generalizability across populations and databases is uncertain.  
- The core assumption of energy‑Lipschitz continuity lacks independent theoretical or experimental justification beyond the presented correlations.

## Confidence
- **PECT theory (energy‑drift proportionality):** Medium – internal evidence strong but no external validation or mechanistic proof.  
- **ECRL regularizer and robustness gains:** High – consistent, large improvements across seven models with negligible clean accuracy loss.  
- **Fusion amplification of drift:** Medium – convincing within the study, but reliance on linear fusion may not generalize to other topologies.

## Next Checks
1. **Parameter reconstruction and sensitivity analysis:** Use grid search/Bayesian optimization to infer plausible values for missing hyperparameters (ϵ, perturbation parameters) and verify that ECRL’s benefits remain stable across reasonable ranges.  
2. **Cross‑dataset replication:** Apply the same pipeline to at least two other ECG datasets (e.g., PTB‑XL, CPSC) and assess whether energy‑drift correlation and robustness improvements hold.  
3. **Direct measurement of energy‑Lipschitz constants:** For each unimodal encoder, vary only amplitude scaling (e.g., 0.8–1.2×), compute empirical ratio ∥δz∥²/|ΔE_phys|, test linearity, and compare estimated L_m to fused κ values reported.
