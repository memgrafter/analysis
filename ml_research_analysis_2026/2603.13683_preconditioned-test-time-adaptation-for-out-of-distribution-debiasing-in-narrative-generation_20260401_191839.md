# Preconditioned Test-Time Adaptation for Out-of-Distribution Debiasing in Narrative Generation

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2603.13683
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.13683
- **Reference count:** 40
- **Primary result:** CAP-TTA reduces bias (BB Bias from 0.452 to 0.443 on toxic prompts) while improving fluency (0.307 vs. 0.298) and achieving 6× lower update latency (0.839s vs. ~5.3s) compared to AdamW/SGD baselines; human bias rate drops to 10% from 15%.

## Executive Summary
The paper addresses the failure of static debiased large language models (LLMs) on out-of-distribution (OOD) high-bias prompts by framing such prompts as a distribution shift. It introduces CAP-TTA, a test-time adaptation framework that performs context-aware LoRA updates only when a real-time bias-risk trigger exceeds a threshold. Updates leverage a precomputed diagonal Fisher preconditioner for fast, stable optimization under a KL trust region, mitigating catastrophic forgetting. Empirical results on narrative generation show CAP-TTA reduces bias scores while improving fluency and drastically cutting adaptation latency versus standard optimizers, with human evaluations confirming reduced bias judgments.

## Method Summary
CAP-TTA operates in an episodic test-time adaptation setting where the model generates narratives segment by segment. A committee of toxicity detectors computes a real-time bias score for each segment; if this score exceeds a threshold τ_trig, the system triggers an update. The update adapts a LoRA module attached to a frozen base LLM using a small batch of safe texts aligned with the current narrative history. Optimization uses a precomputed diagonal preconditioner derived from Fisher information on a generic safe corpus, which scales gradients stably and avoids the high latency of adaptive optimizers like AdamW. A KL trust-region constraint bounds each update, preserving prior language capabilities while steering the output distribution toward lower risk.

## Key Results
- **Bias reduction with maintained fluency:** On toxic prompts, BB Bias decreases from 0.452 to 0.443 while fluency (inverse perplexity-based) increases from 0.298 to 0.307 versus the base debiased model.
- **Efficiency breakthrough:** Per-update latency is 0.839s for CAP-TTA vs. ~5.3s for SGD/AdamW baselines, enabling practical test-time adaptation.
- **Human-validated safety:** Human evaluation shows a 10% bias rate for CAP-TTA versus 15% for the base model and self-correction baseline.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1: Framing High-Bias Prompts as Out-of-Distribution Shift
**Claim:** Static debiased LLMs fail on high-bias prompts because these prompts constitute an out-of-distribution (OOD) shift relative to the in-distribution safe prompt distribution, causing degradation in safety behavior.
**Mechanism:** The paper validates this by applying standard OOD detectors (kNN, Mahalanobis) to embeddings from a base LLM (Qwen3-4B). Results show toxic prompts (RealToxicityPrompts) are far-OOD compared to in-distribution writing prompts (WritingPrompts). Under this shift, the conditional output distribution moves toward regions of higher expected bias risk.
**Core assumption:** Bias can be operationalized as a distributional shift where the model assigns higher probability to unsafe outputs under OOD prompts.
**Evidence anchors:**
- [abstract] "We first validate that such high-bias prompts constitute a *distribution shift* via OOD detection, and show static models degrade under this shift."
- [section 3.1] OOD detection setup with WritingPrompts as ID and RealToxicityPrompts as OOD; AUROC results in Table 2.
**Break condition:** If OOD detection fails to identify high-bias prompts as OOD (e.g., due to detector limitations), the shift-driven degradation premise is undermined.

### Mechanism 2: Threshold-Triggered, Context-Aware Updates
**Claim:** Performing test-time adaptation only when a real-time bias-risk trigger exceeds a threshold controls overhead and prevents unnecessary parameter drift, mitigating catastrophic forgetting while addressing emergent bias.
**Mechanism:** During generation, a toxicity ensemble computes a score \(\bar{b}(y^{(k)})\). If \(\bar{b}(y^{(k)}) > \tau_{\text{trig}}\), a LoRA update is triggered using a small batch of safe texts aligned with the current context (e.g., via history concatenation). Selective updating bounds cumulative drift \(\|\phi_K - \phi_0\|^2\) by limiting the expected number of updates.
**Core assumption:** High bias can be detected online reliably, and small, targeted updates on safe data can steer outputs toward lower risk without harming general fluency.
**Evidence anchors:**
- [abstract] "performs context-aware LoRA updates only when the bias-risk *trigger* exceeds a threshold."
- [section 3.2.5] Formal trigger indicator \(u_k = \mathbb{I}[\bar{b}(y^{(k)}) > \tau_{\text{trig}}]\) linked to parameter drift (Eq. 14).
- [section 5.1] Ablation in Table 4 shows varying \(\epsilon\) (threshold) changes trigger rate and bias/fluency trade-offs.
**Break condition:** If the trigger is miscalibrated (many false positives/negatives) or safe data is misaligned with context, updates may be unnecessary or misdirected, causing overhead without safety gains or introducing new biases.

### Mechanism 3: Preconditioned Updates with Offline Diagonal Fisher
**Claim:** Using a precomputed diagonal preconditioner \(P_0\) (based on Fisher information from a generic safe corpus) enables fast, stable few-step LoRA updates by providing well-scaled gradients from the first step, avoiding cold-start instability of AdamW/SGD.
**Mechanism:** The update rule \(\phi_{t+1} = \phi_t - \alpha P_0 g_t\) preconditioned by the inverse diagonal Fisher (Eq. 9) approximates a natural gradient step, normalizing by parameter curvature. This avoids per-step online curvature estimation, reducing latency significantly vs. AdamW/SGD (Table 3: 0.839s vs. ~5.3s).
**Core assumption:** The Fisher matrix computed offline on a generic safe corpus \(D_{\text{ref}}\) remains a stable, approximately valid preconditioner for slight online updates on OOD prompts (Assumption 2 in section K).
**Evidence anchors:**
- [abstract] "using a precomputed diagonal *preconditioner* for fast and stable updates."
- [section 3.2.3] Derivation from KL trust-region objective and offline Fisher estimation details.
- [section 4.6] Figure 7 (ECDF of update latency) shows preconditioning reduces tail latency vs. SGD.
- [section 5.1] Table 4 shows consistent quality/bias for preconditioned variants; non-preconditioned baselines are slower and sometimes unstable.
**Break condition:** If \(D_{\text{ref}}\) is too dissimilar from the online OOD context, \(P_0\) may mis-scale updates, leading to ineffective or destabilizing steps.

## Foundational Learning
- **Test-Time Adaptation (TTA)**  
  *Why needed:* CAP-TTA operates under an episodic test-time adaptation setting, where the model adapts its parameters during inference in response to each new narrative segment. This is crucial for handling out-of-distribution high-bias prompts because static debiasing fails under such distribution shifts. TTA allows the model to correct bias dynamically without full retraining, balancing safety and fluency.  
  *Quick check:* What is the key difference between offline fine-tuning and test-time adaptation in terms of data access and update timing? (Answer: Offline fine-tuning uses a static dataset before deployment; TTA uses streaming data during inference and updates parameters in real-time, often with limited compute per step.)

- **KL Divergence Trust Region**  
  *Why needed:* The optimization objective in CAP-TTA includes a KL divergence constraint (Eq. 5) that limits each update to a small neighborhood around the current policy. This trust region prevents catastrophic forgetting by ensuring the adapted distribution does not drift too far from the original, preserving general language capabilities while reducing bias risk.  
  *Quick check:* How does a KL trust region constraint in Eq. 5 prevent the adapter from overwriting the model's general language skills during debiasing updates? (Answer: It penalizes large deviations from the current policy, forcing updates to be conservative and thus maintaining proficiency on prior tasks.)

- **Fisher Information Matrix & Preconditioning**  
  *Why needed:* The Fisher information matrix captures the curvature of the loss landscape. Preconditioning gradients by its inverse (natural gradient) normalizes step sizes across parameters, leading to stable and efficient optimization especially in ill-conditioned LLM adaptation. CAP-TTA uses a precomputed diagonal Fisher to avoid per-step curvature estimation, drastically reducing latency.  
  *Quick check:* Why is the diagonal approximation of the Fisher matrix used instead of the full matrix, and what is the trade-off? (Answer: The full Fisher is computationally prohibitive for large models; diagonal approximation is cheap but may ignore parameter interactions, potentially reducing preconditioning effectiveness.)

## Architecture Onboarding
Base LLM (θ) -> LoRA adapter (ϕ) -> Bias detector ensemble -> Trigger controller -> Safe data sources (Csafe, SafeBank) -> Offline preconditioner (P₀) -> Context-aligned update loop  
**Critical path:** For each narrative segment: generate \(y^{(k)}\) -> compute \(\bar{b}(y^{(k)})\) -> if \(> \tau_{\text{trig}}\), sample safe batch \(D_{\text{safe}}(h_k)\) -> form context-aligned \(\tilde{s}_j = \text{concat}(h_k, s_j)\) -> compute gradient \(g = \nabla_\phi \frac{1}{m} \sum -\log p_\theta(\tilde{s}_j | h_k)\) -> update \(\phi \leftarrow \phi - \alpha P_0 g\) -> continue generation.  
**Design tradeoffs:**  
- Trigger threshold τ_trig: Lower values increase updates (better bias mitigation) but raise latency and drift risk; higher values reduce overhead but may miss emergent bias.  
- Segment count/length: More/longer segments provide more correction opportunities but increase test-time and may fragment coherence.  
- Safe data diversity: Typed SafeBank improves type-specific debiasing but risks overfitting to its distribution, affecting fluency or introducing new biases.  
**Failure signatures:**  
- High trigger rate (>0.8): Over-sensitive detector or τ too low; leads to excessive updates, latency spikes, potential catastrophic forgetting (check PPL/fluency).  
- Low trigger rate (~0): Under-sensitive detector or τ too high; adaptation never activates, bias unmitigated.  
- Quality drop post-update: Safe batch misaligned with context or P₀ inappropriate for current gradient region.  
- Stable bias despite triggering: Safe data lacks coverage of bias type, updates too small (learning rate low), or P₀ mis-scales directions.  
**First 3 experiments:**  
1. Reproduce OOD detection: Compute AUROC/AUPR for kNN and Mahalanobis between WritingPrompts and RealToxicityPrompts embeddings using base model; expect >95% AUROC (Table 2).  
2. Benchmark update latency: Run CAP-TTA (triggered) vs. TTA-SGD/AdamW on fixed toxic prompts; measure per-update and total time; expect ~0.8s vs. ~5s (Table 3).  
3. Ablate trigger threshold: Sweep τ_trig (e.g., 0.2, 0.25, 0.3); record trigger rate, BB Bias, PPL, update time; expect U-shaped bias vs. threshold and monotonic latency decrease (Table 4, Fig 6).

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- **Robustness to detector and threshold selection across contexts:** How robust is CAP-TTA to the choice of bias detector ensemble and trigger threshold τ_trig across diverse cultural and linguistic contexts? The paper uses a fixed committee of three English toxicity classifiers and a single threshold. Detector biases (e.g., varying false positive/negative rates across dialects) may mislead adaptation; the threshold's generalizability is untested. Systematic experiments varying detector types (multilingual/culturally-aware) and thresholds on diverse prompt sets, with disaggregated bias metrics, are needed.
- **Generalization of preconditioner to domain-specific shifts:** Does CAP-TTA's preconditioner, precomputed on a generic safe corpus (WikiText-2), generalize to domain-specific or stylistic shifts without catastrophic forgetting? The safe corpus may be stylistically narrow (e.g., encyclopedic), causing P₀ to mis-scale updates for creative narratives. Ablations with alternative corpora (fiction, dialogue) measuring update stability, narrative quality, and parameter drift are required.
- **Extension to multi-objective safety constraints:** Can CAP-TTA's thresholded adaptation be extended to multi-objective safety (e.g., balancing bias, toxicity, factuality) without manual tuning of separate triggers and safe data sources? The current unidimensional trigger and typed SafeBank may not scale to nuanced, conflicting objectives. Developing a multi-objective formulation (constrained optimization or Pareto-based triggers) and evaluating holistic safety coverage versus latency would test this.
- **Theoretical limits of test-time adaptation for debiasing:** What are the theoretical limits of test-time adaptation for debiasing under severe distribution shift, and does CAP-TTA's KL trust-region approach achieve optimal worst-case guarantees? The method provides no formal safety guarantees; adversarial prompts could increase trigger frequency and drift. Deriving sample complexity/regret bounds and stress-testing with adversarial prompts would characterize limits.
- **Interaction of narrative segmentation with adaptation dynamics:** To what extent does narrative length and segmentation strategy interact with CAP-TTA's adaptation dynamics, and can adaptation be made segment-agnostic to reduce hyperparameter sensitivity? Fixed segmentation (K segments, 128 tokens) strongly affects bias/quality trade-offs (e.g., 8 segments reduce bias but increase PPL). The interaction between segment granularity and trigger mechanism is unanalyzed; adaptive segmentation or dynamic triggering could improve robustness.

## Limitations
- **OOD detection reliability:** The framing relies on OOD detectors (e.g., kNN, Mahalanobis) to flag high-bias prompts; however, these detectors themselves may have biases or limitations (e.g., sensitivity to embedding choices, false positives on certain dialects) that could lead to erroneous adaptation decisions, either triggering updates unnecessarily or missing harmful prompts.
- **Trigger calibration:** The threshold τ_trig is set heuristically and not systematically derived from safety-fluency trade-offs; mis-calibration (too low or too high) risks either excessive update overhead and potential drift, or missed adaptation opportunities leaving bias unmitigated.
- **Safe data retrieval policy:** The policy for selecting \(D_{\text{safe}}(history)\) (e.g., from Csafe or SafeBank) is unspecified; poor context alignment (e.g., retrieving irrelevant safe texts) may harm narrative fluency or introduce new biases by pulling from inappropriate sources.
- **Preconditioner validity:** The offline preconditioner \(P_0\) computed on a generic safe corpus (e.g., WikiText-2) may not accurately reflect the curvature of the loss landscape in OOD gradient regions, potentially causing unstable or ineffective updates when adapting to high-bias prompts.

## Confidence
- OOD shift explanation for static model failure: **High** (empirical AUROC >95% in Table 2).
- Threshold-triggered adaptation controlling drift: **Medium** (ablation shows trade-offs, but optimal threshold not determined).
- Preconditioned update efficiency (latency reduction): **High** (6× reduction, Table 3/Fig 7).
- Bias reduction without catastrophic forgetting: **Medium** (human bias ↓10%→15%, but long-term language quality not fully established).
