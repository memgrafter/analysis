# LAMB: LLM-based Audio Captioning with Modality Gap Bridging via Cauchy-Schwarz Divergence

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2601.04658
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.04658
- Reference count: 40
- Key outcome: LAMB achieves SOTA performance on AudioCaps (METEOR 27.1, CIDEr 91.1, SPICE 19.7) and competitive results on Clotho

## Executive Summary
LAMB is an LLM-based audio captioning framework that addresses the modality gap between audio and text embeddings through a novel combination of Cauchy-Schwarz divergence and mutual information maximization. The framework employs a Two-Stream Adapter to extract semantically and temporally enriched audio features, a Cross-Modal Aligner for fine-grained alignment, and a Token Guide that steers output logits within the LLM's text embedding space. LAMB achieves state-of-the-art performance on AudioCaps with METEOR 27.1, CIDEr 91.1, and SPICE 19.7, outperforming prior methods in both semantic coverage and structural fidelity.

## Method Summary
LAMB bridges the modality gap in audio captioning by processing audio embeddings through a Two-Stream Adapter that separately captures semantic and temporal features before fusing them. A Cross-Modal Aligner then minimizes Cauchy-Schwarz divergence while maximizing mutual information to achieve tight alignment between audio and text embeddings at both global and token levels. Finally, a Token Guide steers the LLM decoder logits using squared L2 distances computed in the text embedding space, enabling semantically coherent caption generation without requiring external modules or corpora.

## Key Results
- LAMB achieves state-of-the-art METEOR score of 27.1 on AudioCaps benchmark
- CIDEr score of 91.1 and SPICE score of 19.7 on AudioCaps demonstrate superior semantic coverage
- Competitive performance on Clotho dataset with FENSE score of 52.6
- Ablation studies confirm the effectiveness of each proposed component

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: Cross-Modal Aligner bridges the modality gap by minimizing Cauchy-Schwarz divergence while maximizing mutual information
- Mechanism: The Cross-Modal Aligner uses Cauchy-Schwarz divergence to measure symmetric distance between audio and text distributions, while InfoNCE loss maximizes mutual information. This combination creates a robust alignment objective that captures both global distribution structures and fine-grained token-level correspondences.
- Core assumption: Audio and text embeddings can be meaningfully compared using distributional distances when projected to a common space
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract]: "Cross-Modal Aligner that minimizes Cauchy-Schwarz divergence while maximizing mutual information, yielding tighter alignment between audio and text at both global and token levels"
  - [section 2.2]: "The CS divergence, DCS, provides a symmetric metric...combined with mutual information, provides a robust distance estimation that effectively captures both global structures and pairwise semantic correspondences"
  - [corpus]: Weak - no direct corpus evidence found for this specific divergence-based alignment approach
- Break condition: If the audio and text distributions are too dissimilar in their learned representations, the CS divergence may become unstable or the InfoNCE loss may fail to provide meaningful gradients

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: Two-Stream Adapter extracts semantically and temporally enriched audio features that provide richer information to the alignment module
- Mechanism: The Two-Stream Adapter processes audio embeddings through parallel semantic and temporal modules, each extracting different aspects of audio content. Semantic features capture meaning through cross-attention with learnable queries, while temporal features capture temporal patterns through convolution and GRU layers. These streams are then fused and projected to the LLM embedding space.
- Core assumption: Audio content contains both semantic meaning and temporal structure that can be separately modeled and then combined
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract]: "We further design a Two-Stream Adapter that extracts semantically enriched audio embeddings, thereby delivering richer information to the Cross-Modal Aligner"
  - [section 2.1]: "We then introduce the Two-Stream Adapter to capture semantic and temporal context of ha through two parallel modules"
  - [section 2.1]: "The outputs of the two parallel modules hs and ht are concatenated along the token axis, and then processed with Ng global queries"
- Break condition: If the audio content is too short or lacks clear temporal patterns, the temporal module may not provide meaningful features, or if the semantic content is ambiguous, the semantic module may fail to extract useful representations

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: Token Guide steers decoder logits using squared L2 distance between audio embeddings and token embeddings within the LLM's text space
- Mechanism: At each decoding step, the Token Guide computes Guide Scores as the squared L2 distance between the mean audio embedding and each token embedding in the LLM's dictionary. These scores are used to adjust the original logits, effectively biasing the decoder toward tokens semantically related to the audio content without requiring external modules or corpora.
- Core assumption: The LLM's token embedding space contains meaningful semantic relationships that can be leveraged for cross-modal guidance
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract]: "Finally, leveraging the aligned audio embeddings, a proposed Token Guide directly computes scores within the LLM text embedding space to steer the output logits of generated captions"
  - [section 2.3]: "Guide Scores, squared L2 distance between global mean za and token embeddings, scaled by a learnable coefficient β"
  - [section 2.3]: "Since the Guide Scores are computed as the squared L2 distance between aligned audio embeddings and LLM token embeddings, semantically related tokens receive higher weights"
- Break condition: If the LLM's token embedding space is not semantically meaningful (e.g., if trained with different objectives) or if the audio-text alignment is poor, the guidance may be ineffective or misleading

## Foundational Learning

- Concept: Cauchy-Schwarz divergence as a symmetric distance metric
  - Why needed here: Provides a symmetric, robust measure of distance between audio and text distributions that captures both global structures and pairwise correspondences
  - Quick check question: How does Cauchy-Schwarz divergence differ from KL divergence in measuring distribution distances?

- Concept: Mutual information maximization via InfoNCE loss
  - Why needed here: Ensures that the aligned audio and text embeddings preserve the semantic relationships between modalities by maximizing their mutual dependence
  - Quick check question: What is the relationship between InfoNCE loss and the lower bound on mutual information?

- Concept: Two-stream architecture for multimodal feature extraction
  - Why needed here: Separately captures semantic meaning and temporal structure from audio before combining them, allowing the model to better leverage both types of information
  - Quick check question: Why might processing semantic and temporal features separately be beneficial compared to a single unified approach?

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map: Audio encoder → Two-Stream Adapter (Semantic + Temporal streams) → Cross-Modal Aligner (CS divergence + InfoNCE) → Token Guide → LLM decoder
- Critical path: Audio features must flow through Two-Stream Adapter to produce enriched embeddings, then through Cross-Modal Aligner to achieve proper alignment, and finally through Token Guide to ensure coherent text generation
- Design tradeoffs: The Two-Stream Adapter adds computational complexity but improves feature quality; Token Guide requires additional computation per decoding step but provides better control over generation; CS divergence is more computationally intensive than simpler alignment methods but provides better alignment quality
- Failure signatures: Poor alignment manifests as degraded CIDEr and METEOR scores; ineffective guidance shows up as less detailed or semantically inconsistent captions; temporal features may be missing if audio is too short or lacks temporal patterns
- First 3 experiments:
  1. Test Cross-Modal Aligner alone with simple linear projection to verify CS divergence alignment works
  2. Evaluate Two-Stream Adapter contribution by comparing with single-stream processing
  3. Assess Token Guide effectiveness by comparing with baseline LLM generation without guidance

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- Question: How does the performance of LAMB vary when using different kernel widths (σ) in the Gaussian kernel for the Cauchy-Schwarz divergence calculation?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper mentions using a Gaussian kernel with kernel width σ for KDE in the Cross-Modal Aligner, but does not explore the sensitivity of the method to this parameter.
- Why unresolved: The paper does not provide an analysis of how varying σ affects the alignment quality or downstream performance metrics.
- What evidence would resolve it: A systematic ablation study varying σ and reporting changes in alignment quality (e.g., L2 distance, cosine similarity) and final captioning performance metrics.

### Open Question 2
- Question: Can the Cross-Modal Aligner be extended to work with other modalities beyond audio, such as video or tactile data?
- Basis in paper: [inferred] The Cross-Modal Aligner is designed to minimize modality gaps between audio and text embeddings, suggesting it could potentially be applied to other modality pairs.
- Why unresolved: The paper focuses exclusively on audio captioning and does not explore the generalizability of the CMA to other multimodal tasks.
- What evidence would resolve it: Experiments applying the CMA framework to other modality pairs (e.g., video-text, audio-vision) and comparing performance to existing alignment methods.

### Open Question 3
- Question: How does the Two-Stream Adapter compare to alternative audio feature extraction methods like wav2vec 2.0 or HuBERT when used with the Cross-Modal Aligner?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper introduces the Two-Stream Adapter as a way to enrich audio features, but does not compare it to other pre-trained audio encoders.
- Why unresolved: The paper does not benchmark the TSA against other audio feature extraction approaches to quantify its contribution.
- What evidence would resolve it: Replacing the TSA with other audio encoders (wav2vec 2.0, HuBERT) while keeping the CMA and Token Guide components fixed, then comparing performance metrics.

## Limitations

- The Two-Stream Adapter's contribution is not fully quantified against other audio feature extraction methods
- The kernel bandwidth sensitivity for Cauchy-Schwarz divergence is not explored, potentially affecting robustness
- The Token Guide's stability on out-of-distribution audio content is not evaluated
