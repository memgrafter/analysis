# On the Role of Encoder Depth: Pruning Whisper and LoRA Fine-Tuning in SLAM-ASR

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2603.27981  
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.27981  
- **Reference count:** 0  
- **Primary result:** Pruning top two Whisper encoder layers causes only 2–4% absolute WER degradation; combining with LoRA outperforms the unpruned baseline while reducing total parameters by 7–14%.

## Executive Summary
This paper explores the interplay between encoder depth and adaptation efficiency in SLAM-ASR, specifically examining whether top-down pruning of Whisper encoder layers harms accuracy and if LoRA fine-tuning of the frozen LLM can recover any losses. Experiments span three Whisper scales (Small, Medium, Large‑v2) and three languages from Common Voice 22—Danish (4.2h), Dutch (54h), and English (100h)—covering a range of data resource conditions. The findings reveal that removing the top two encoder layers leads to only a marginal 2–4% absolute increase in WER across all scales and languages. Notably, when LoRA is applied to the LLM in conjunction with this shallow pruning, the combined model not only matches but often surpasses the unpruned baseline, achieving a net parameter reduction of 7–14%. The benefit of LoRA is most pronounced for medium/high‑resource languages (11–21% error reduction) by correcting substitutions and deletions, while for low‑resource Danish gains are modest (4–7%) and come with increased insertion errors. These results suggest that the upper encoder layers are somewhat redundant when a powerful LLM decoder is present, and that parameter‑efficient LoRA adaptation can effectively leverage the LLM’s linguistic knowledge to compensate for reduced acoustic abstraction, though the magnitude of compensation strongly depends on training data availability and the LLM’s language proficiency.

## Method Summary
The study employs a SLAM‑ASR pipeline consisting of a frozen Whisper encoder, a trainable two‑layer MLP projector, and a frozen Qwen2.5‑3B LLM decoder. The projector maps variable‑length encoder outputs (downsampled by concatenating 5 consecutive frames) to fixed‑dimensional vectors that match the LLM’s input embedding size. The encoder is pruned by sequentially removing its top N layers (down to as few as 2 layers), and LoRA adapters are injected into all attention projection matrices (Q, K, V, O) of the LLM. Only the projector parameters (and LoRA parameters when used) are trained via cross‑entropy loss on the Common Voice 22 dataset for Danish (4.2h), Dutch (54h), and English (100h). Training uses AdamW (learning rate 1e‑4, weight decay 0.01, gradient clipping 1.0), a cosine learning‑rate schedule with 5% warm‑up, mixed‑precision (bfloat16), and a single NVIDIA A6000 GPU. Models are evaluated via beam search (size 2) and WER after lower‑casing and removing punctuation. Over 200 runs systematically vary Whisper scale, language, pruning depth, and LoRA usage.

## Key Results
- Pruning the top two encoder layers across all Whisper scales and languages results in only a 2–4% absolute WER increase relative to the unpruned baseline.
- Combining 2‑layer pruning with LoRA consistently outperforms the unpruned baseline while reducing total model parameters by 7–14%.
- LoRA reduces total word errors by 11–21% for Dutch and English, primarily by decreasing substitutions and deletions; for Danish, gains are smaller (4–7%) and insertion errors increase, highlighting resource‑dependent compensation.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1
**Claim:** Removing top encoder layers causes minimal WER degradation because the upper layers encode abstract linguistic features that are redundant given the LLM's pre‑existing linguistic processing capabilities.  
**Mechanism:** In SLAM‑ASR, the frozen LLM handles high‑level linguistic decoding, so the fine‑grained acoustic‑linguistic embeddings from the top encoder layers provide diminishing returns. The projector can map shallower encoder outputs directly into the LLM’s embedding space without substantial loss of critical information for transcription.  
**Core assumption:** The LLM’s decoder is sufficiently powerful to compensate for the loss of encoder‑side abstraction.  
**Evidence anchors:** The abstract and Section 5.1 report only 2–4% absolute WER increase after top‑2 pruning; Table 2 shows non‑monotonic degradation for Danish when pruning deeper, supporting the redundancy hypothesis.  
**Break condition:** Pruning more than two layers, especially for low‑resource languages, leads to erratic and substantial WER increases.

### Mechanism 2
**Claim:** LoRA applied to the LLM compensates for information lost due to encoder pruning by enabling the LLM to leverage its internal linguistic priors to correct errors.  
**Mechanism:** The pruned encoder produces noisier acoustic embeddings. LoRA fine‑tunes a low‑rank adaptation of the LLM’s attention projections (Q, K, V, O), allowing the LLM to re‑weight its contextual processing toward more linguistically plausible sequences, thereby reducing substitution and deletion errors caused by ambiguous acoustic evidence.  
**Core assumption:** The LLM’s base linguistic knowledge is sufficient to infer correct transcriptions from imperfect acoustic cues when its attention is slightly adapted.  
**Evidence anchors:** LoRA reduces total word errors by 11–21% for Dutch and English, mainly by decreasing substitutions and deletions (abstract, Section 5.3.3). For Danish, LoRA increases insertions (Table 7), indicating compensation via linguistic priors rather than acoustic repair.  
**Break condition:** Low‑resource settings where the LLM’s pre‑existing proficiency is limited, leading to increased insertions and inconsistent recovery.

### Mechanism 3
**Claim:** The effectiveness of both pruning and LoRA compensation is modulated by training data resource availability, with low‑resource scenarios exhibiting higher sensitivity and instability.  
**Mechanism:** With limited data, the projector learns a noisier mapping from encoder outputs to LLM embeddings, making the system more vulnerable to encoder pruning. For LoRA, low‑resource fine‑tuning data may not provide enough signal to adapt the LLM effectively, and a lower LoRA rank (used for Danish) mitigates overfitting. The LLM’s inherent knowledge of the language also correlates with data availability in its pre‑training.  
**Core assumption:** Data scarcity affects the stability of the learned projection function and the generalizability of adaptation.  
**Evidence anchors:** Danish exhibits erratic, non‑monotonic WER behavior versus smooth degradation for Dutch/English (Section 5.1). The paper uses a lower LoRA rank for Danish (Section 4.2). LoRA gains are smaller and insertion errors increase for Danish (abstract, Table 7).  
**Break condition:** Extremely low‑resource settings (<4 h) where both pruning degradation and LoRA recovery become unpredictable.

  Why needed: Understanding the fixed components (frozen Whisper encoder, frozen LLM) and the sole trainable component (projector) is essential to interpret why pruning the encoder and adapting the LLM via LoRA are non‑trivial and decoupled interventions.  
  Quick check: If the projector is the only component updated during training, what happens to the error profile when the encoder is pruned and the LLM is adapted with LoRA?

- **Concept: Low‑Rank Adaptation (LoRA)**  
  Why needed: LoRA is the primary mechanism for LLM‑side compensation. Knowing it decomposes weight updates into low‑rank matrices (ΔW = BA) explains the parameter efficiency and why it can be applied without full fine‑tuning of the 3B‑parameter LLM.  
  Quick check: Why does applying LoRA to the LLM’s attention matrices (Q, K, V, O) allow it to adapt to new input modalities (speech embeddings) while keeping base weights frozen?

- **Concept: Encoder Layer Function in Whisper**

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map:** Whisper Encoder (frozen, prunable) → Temporal Projector (trainable) → LLM (Qwen2.5‑3B, frozen, LoRA‑adaptable).
- **Critical path:** Audio input → [Whisper Encoder (pruned configuration)] → [Projector] → [LLM with/without LoRA] → Transcription. Training updates Projector (+LoRA). Inference uses frozen components.
- **Design tradeoffs:**  
  - Pruning depth vs. accuracy: Shallow pruning (2 layers) offers net parameter reduction with potential accuracy gain when combined with LoRA; deep pruning risks catastrophic degradation, especially for low‑resource languages.  
  - LoRA rank (r) vs. overfitting: Higher rank (r = 16) for medium/high‑resource; lower rank (r = 8) for low‑resource.  
  - Parameter budget: Trade encoder parameters (millions per layer) for LLM LoRA parameters (~0.8M‑1.5M). Net reduction only achieved with sufficient pruning.
- **Failure signatures:**  
  - Non‑monotonic WER vs. layers (low‑resource): Spiking and dropping WER as layers are removed (Danish).  
  - Increased insertion errors with LoRA (low‑resource): +10‑18% insertions for Danish.  
  - Poor recovery on named entities/idioms: Without sufficient data or LLM knowledge, pruned+LoRA models may still fail.
- **First 3 experiments:**  
  1. Baseline replication: Train projector‑only (no pruning, no LoRA) on English (100h). Expect WER ~21.84%.  
  2. Top‑2 pruning test: Prune top 2 layers from each Whisper variant. Train projector‑only. Expect WER increase within 2–4% absolute.  
  3. Pruning + LoRA integration: Take best 2‑layer pruned configuration (e.g., Small 10L). Add LoRA (r = 16 for EN/NL, r = 8 for DA). Retrain projector+LoRA. Expect WER to drop below baseline and net parameter reduction.

## Limitations
- Missing hyperparameter specifics (exact epochs/steps, batch size, gradient accumulation, dev set selection) hinders exact replication.
- Limited experimental scope: only three languages from Common Voice 22, only top‑down pruning tested, no multi‑seed statistics, raising robustness concerns especially for low‑resource Danish.
- The observed non‑monotonic WER behavior for Danish suggests high variance tied to data splits, but the analysis of this instability is insufficient.

## Confidence
- Top‑2 pruning causes minimal WER degradation (2–4% absolute) for medium/high‑resource languages: **Medium–High**.  
- LoRA adaptation of the frozen LLM recovers (or exceeds) unpruned performance: **Medium** for Dutch/English, **Low** for Danish.  
- Effectiveness strongly depends on training data resource level: **High**.  
- Combined pruning + LoRA yields net parameter reduction while matching or beating baseline: **Medium**.

## Next Checks
1. Baseline replication with full hyperparameter disclosure: Infer or obtain exact epochs, batch size, dev split; retrain unpruned projector‑only models to verify reported WERs within ±0.5% absolute.
2. Multi‑seed stability analysis: Run ≥5 random seeds per language for the 2‑layer pruned + LoRA configuration; compute mean/variance of WER and error‑type distributions to confirm substitution/deletion reduction and insertion increase in low‑resource settings.
3. Generalization to additional languages and LLM scales: Test on a diverse low‑resource language (e.g., Swahili, Nepali) and with a larger LLM (Qwen2.5‑7B) to examine whether pruning tolerance and LoRA compensation extend beyond the original setup.
