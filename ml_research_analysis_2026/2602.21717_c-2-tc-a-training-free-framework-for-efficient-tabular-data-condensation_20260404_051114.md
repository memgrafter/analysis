# C$^{2}$TC: A Training-Free Framework for Efficient Tabular Data Condensation

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2602.21717
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.21717
- **Reference count:** 40
- **Primary result:** A training-free framework achieving >100× speedup over gradient-based condensation with superior accuracy and Macro-F1, especially on imbalanced tabular data.

## Executive Summary
C²TC introduces a training-free framework for tabular data condensation that eliminates costly gradient-based optimization. It reformulates condensation as a class-adaptive cluster allocation problem (CCAP), solved via a heuristic local search (HFILS) that dynamically optimizes per-class sample counts to handle imbalance. A hybrid categorical feature encoding (HCFE) preserves semantics for heterogeneous data. Evaluated on 10 real-world datasets, C²TC provides at least two orders of magnitude speedup over state-of-the-art baselines while delivering higher accuracy and Macro-F1, with particularly strong gains on imbalanced datasets.

## Method Summary
C²TC proceeds in three stages: (1) **Hybrid Categorical Feature Encoding (HCFE)** transforms heterogeneous features into a unified numerical space—string features use n-gram similarity and an autoencoder, integer categoricals use smoothed target encoding with noise—followed by min-max scaling. (2) **Heuristic First-Improvement Local Search (HFILS)** solves the NP-hard CCAP by iteratively reallocating cluster counts among classes via a soft allocation step, guided by a weighted within-cluster sum of squares (WCSS) loss that reweights classes by $1/n_i^\gamma$ to favor minorities. A cache avoids redundant K-means runs. (3) Final **class-wise K-means** with the best allocation produces cluster centroids as the condensed dataset.

## Key Results
- At least 2 orders of magnitude faster condensation than gradient-based methods (DM, GM, MTT).
- Superior test Accuracy and Macro-F1 across 10 real-world datasets at multiple reduction ratios.
- Strong improvements on imbalanced datasets, e.g., +9.6% Macro-F1 on Adult at 1% ratio.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- **Claim:** C²TC eliminates expensive gradient-based optimization by reformulating condensation as a class-wise partition problem in the raw feature space.
- **Mechanism:** Unifies gradient-based objectives into a class-level alignment framework. By linearizing the feature encoder and applying EM-style clustering (K-means) per class, the synthetic dataset is the cluster centroids, avoiding backpropagation.
- **Core assumption:** A linear encoder sufficiently preserves semantic relationships among tabular features, making raw-space clustering an adequate proxy for model-space distribution matching.
- **Evidence anchors:** Abstract states CCAP "eliminates costly training"; Section IV-B shows the objective reduces to class partition; corpus identifies high computational cost of existing DC methods as the bottleneck C²TC addresses.
- **Break condition:** If tabular data contain highly nonlinear feature interactions beyond linear projection capacity, raw-space partition may fail to preserve downstream performance, especially for complex architectures.

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim:** C²TC dynamically allocates condensed samples per class via CCAP, mitigating performance drops on imbalanced datasets.
- **Mechanism:** CCAP jointly optimizes the number of clusters per class and intra-class clustering quality. The objective reweights clustering loss by $1/n_i^\gamma$ ($\gamma \in [0,1]$) to counterbalance majority-class dominance. The NP-hard problem is solved by HFILS, which alternates soft reallocation and class-wise clustering.
- **Core assumption:** The elbow-shaped decay of WCSS allows heuristic adjustments favoring minority classes, balancing global accuracy and per-class fairness.
- **Evidence anchors:** Abstract notes "adaptive label allocation to handle class imbalance"; Section IV-C explains the weighting scheme; experiments (Table II, IV) show significant Macro-F1 gains on imbalanced datasets vs. static Ratio/FIPC.
- **Break condition:** Under extreme imbalance (e.g., one class <0.1% samples), the elbow heuristic may still allocate too few clusters to minorities if marginal WCSS gain is insufficient, harming minority recall.

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim:** The hybrid categorical feature encoding (HCFE) preserves semantic information of heterogeneous categorical attributes, enabling effective clustering-based condensation.
- **Mechanism:** For string-type features, HCFE uses n-gram similarity encoding followed by an autoencoder to obtain a compact, semantically meaningful representation. For integer-coded categoricals, it applies smoothed target encoding with noise injection. All features are normalized to [0,1] for distance-based clustering.
- **Core assumption:** Semantic relationships among categorical values (lexical similarity or predictive correlation with target) are critical for meaningful clustering and can be captured without one-hot expansion.
- **Evidence anchors:** Abstract states HCFE is "for semantics-preserving clustering"; Section V-A describes the unified low-dimensional space; ablation in Table VII shows HCFE outperforms label, one-hot+PCA, and target encoding on accuracy and Macro-F1.
- **Break condition:** For categorical features with extremely high cardinality and sparse co-occurrence, the autoencoder may fail to learn a stable latent space, and similarity encoding could become computationally prohibitive, leading to information loss.

## Foundational Learning
- **Concept:** Dataset Condensation  
  **Why needed:** Core problem C²TC solves—synthesizing a small dataset that preserves training utility.  
  **Quick check:** What is the primary goal of dataset condensation? *To generate a compact synthetic dataset that enables model training with comparable performance to training on the full dataset.*
- **Concept:** Class Imbalance in Tabular Data  
  **Why needed:** C²TC’s adaptive allocation specifically addresses the imbalance issue that plagues existing DC methods.  
  **Quick check:** Why do predefined label allocation strategies (Ratio, FIPC) fail on imbalanced tabular datasets? *Ratio preserves original distribution, favoring majority classes; FIPC forces equal allocation, overrepresenting minorities and hurting overall accuracy; neither balances global vs. per-class performance.*
- **Concept:** Clustering-Based Data Reduction  
  **Why needed:** C²TC reduces condensation to class-wise clustering, where condensed samples are cluster centroids.  
  **Quick check:** How does clustering relate to dataset condensation in C²TC? *The synthetic dataset is obtained by partitioning each class into $n_i'$ clusters and using the centroids as condensed samples; this approximates the distribution matching objective.*

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map:** HCFE Preprocessor -> HFILS Solver -> K-means Subroutine
- **Critical path:** Raw tabular data → HCFE encoding → HFILS initialization (ratio-preserving allocation) → repeat: SoftAllocate → ClassWiseClustering (with loss cache) → until convergence → final K-means per class → outputs condensed dataset (centroids and labels).
- **Design tradeoffs:**
  - *Training-free vs. quality:* Removing gradient-based optimization drastically speeds up condensation but relies on linear encoder approximation and clustering heuristics, which may not capture complex nonlinear patterns.
  - *Adaptive allocation vs. cost:* HFILS’s local search improves class balance but introduces combinatorial overhead; the loss cache mitigates redundant K-means runs.
  - *HCFE complexity vs. semantics:* Autoencoder for string features adds preprocessing cost but preserves semantic similarity; alternative encodings (one-hot) are simpler but lose semantics.
- **Failure signatures:**
  - excessive OOM/OOT on very large datasets (though C²TC is designed to scale, extreme dimensionality may still strain K-means)
  - HFILS stagnates early (loss improvement < tolerance) indicating poor local optimum; check elbow heuristic and step decay parameters
  - HCFE reconstruction loss remains high, suggesting encoding failure—inspect categorical cardinality and autoencoder capacity
  - condensed size rounding: sum $n_i'$ may slightly differ from intended $N'$ due to integer constraints; algorithm adjusts by modifying one class's allocation (expected but verify $N' \approx r \cdot N_{train}$)
- **First 3 experiments:**
  1. **Overall performance comparison** (Table II): Train downstream models on condensed data from C²TC vs. baselines (DM, GM, MTT, coreset) across 10 datasets and 3 reduction ratios; report Accuracy and Macro-F1.
  2. **Cross-architecture generalization** (Table III): Evaluate condensed datasets on 6 tabular architectures (MLP, MLP PLR, RealMLP, FT Transformer, TabNet, TabR) to test transferability.
  3. **Efficiency evaluation** (Figures 3–5): Measure condensation time (vs. baselines), scaling with N/F, and training-time acceleration on condensed vs. full data.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- **What are the approximation bounds and convergence guarantee for HFILS?**  
  *Basis:* HFILS is proposed as a heuristic for NP‑hard CCAP, but no theoretical analysis is provided.  
  *Why unresolved:* Without bounds, solution quality and behavior on large instances cannot be assessed.  
  *What evidence would resolve it:* Derive an approximation ratio or empirically compare to optimal solutions on small instances; analyze convergence under worst-case conditions.
- **How does HCFE handle extremely high‑cardinality categorical features (e.g., >10⁵ distinct values)?**  
  *Basis:* HCFE uses an autoencoder for string features; latent dimension equals number of string features, which may be infeasible for very high cardinalities, and this regime is unevaluated.  
  *Why unresolved:* Real‑world tabular data often include high‑cardinality attributes; scalability and semantic preservation remain unknown.  
  *What evidence would resolve it:* Benchmark HCFE on synthetic datasets with varying cardinalities, measuring runtime, memory, and downstream accuracy; investigate alternative scalable encodings.
- **Can the C²TC framework be extended to streaming or concept‑drift scenarios?**  
  *Basis:* C²TC condenses a static dataset offline and does not consider incremental updates or adaptation to non‑stationary data.  
  *Why unresolved:* Many industrial applications involve continuously arriving data; a one‑time condensed set may become stale.  
  *What evidence would resolve it:* Design an online version of C²TC that incrementally updates the condensed set and evaluate on synthetic and real streams with shifting distributions.

## Limitations
- Linear encoder assumption may fail for data with strong nonlinear interactions, harming fidelity.
- HFILS elbow‑based allocation can be suboptimal for extreme class imbalance (e.g., minority <0.1%), hurting minority recall.
- HCFE autoencoder encoding of high‑cardinality strings is costly and may not scale to massive datasets.
- Reproducibility gaps: missing details (autoencoder design, hyperparameters, seeds) hinder exact replication.

## Confidence
- **Mechanism 1 (CCAP reformulation):** High — elimination of gradient‑based optimization is inherent and consistently yields >100× speedups.
- **Mechanism 2 (adaptive allocation):** Medium — gains on imbalanced data are evident, but heuristic stability under extreme imbalance and γ‑tuning need validation.
- **Mechanism 3 (HCFE):** Medium — ablations confirm advantage over simple encodings, yet autoencoder robustness and generalization to high‑cardinality categories remain to be proven.

## Next Checks
