# NNiT: Width-Agnostic Neural Network Generation with Structurally Aligned Weight Spaces

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2603.00180
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.00180
- **Reference count:** 27
- **Primary result:** NNiT achieves over 85% zero‑shot success on unseen MLP architectures for robotic manipulation, while prior baselines fall to 0–59%.

## Executive Summary
NNiT introduces a unified framework for generating functional neural network weights that is agnostic to architecture width. It overcomes permutation symmetries by first producing structurally aligned weights via Graph HyperNetworks (GHNs). These aligned weights are tokenized into spatial patches, enabling a single Diffusion Transformer to model both discrete architecture tokens and continuous weight patches. This decoupling allows zero‑shot transfer to entirely new architectures, as demonstrated on ManiSkill3 robotic tasks with success rates above 85%.

## Method Summary
The method builds a dataset of expert MLP policies using GHNs. GHNs take an architecture graph (layer dimensions) and, after GNN propagation, generate weight tensors through a CNN decoder, producing weights with consistent structural alignment (e.g., banded patterns) across different widths. The aligned weights are globally normalized and tokenized: each matrix is padded to a maximal grid and split into p×p patches; the architecture is encoded as a sequence of width integers. These tokens form a unified sequence fed to a Diffusion Transformer (NNiT). Training uses a Dual‑modality Mixture of Noise Levels (MoNL), applying either joint (architecture+weight) or conditional (weight only) noising to learn the joint distribution. Sampling with DDIM yields weight patches for a given architecture, which are reassembled into a functional MLP evaluated on robotic tasks.

## Key Results
- Zero‑shot generation for entirely unseen MLP architectures: >85% success on ManiSkill3 tasks.
- Conditional weight synthesis (p(w|a)) on unseen widths: 86–99% success.
- Joint architecture‑weight generation (p(a,w)): 90–100% success.
- Prior baselines (SANE, D2NWG) degrade sharply to 0–59% under the same zero‑shot conditions.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
NNiT eliminates the permutation symmetry barrier that normally ties weight representations to specific matrix dimensions. GHNs create structurally aligned weights—tensors that share consistent local patterns regardless of width. Tokenizing these aligned tensors into patches preserves their structure while abstracting away exact dimensions. The Diffusion Transformer then learns a unified latent space where architecture tokens and weight patches coexist, enabling generation conditioned on any architecture sequence. MoNL training forces the model to master both the marginal distribution over architectures and the conditional mapping p(w|a). As a result, weight generation becomes decoupled from fixed sizes, allowing seamless zero‑shot transfer.

## Foundational Learning
- **Permutation Symmetry**: Neural networks are invariant to neuron reordering, causing weight spaces to fragment. *Why needed*: Explains why naive weight generation fails; alignment resolves it. *Quick check*: Permuting the weight columns of a trained MLP yields identical policy behavior.
- **Graph HyperNetworks (GHNs)**: A meta‑network that maps an architecture graph to weight tensors. *Why needed*: Generates aligned weights at scale; alignment is the cornerstone of width‑agnosticism. *Quick check*: Train a GHN on a few architectures and inspect weight heatmaps for regular vertical bands.
- **Structural Alignment via CNN Decoder**: The CNN shapes GNN embeddings into weight matrices with consistent local correlations. *Why needed*: Alignment ensures patch tokenization is width‑invariant. *Quick check*: Compute cosine similarity between corresponding patches of different widths; values >0.3 indicate alignment.
- **Tokenization of Weight Tensors**: Convert matrices into fixed‑size patches after padding. *Why needed*: Allows a Transformer to treat variable‑dimensional tensors as a sequence of tokens. *Quick check*: Patches should perfectly reconstruct the padded tensor and be croppable to the original size.
- **Diffusion Transformers (DiT)**: Transformer‑based denoising diffusion models. *Why needed*: DiTs can generate high‑dimensional continuous data conditioned on discrete tokens. *Quick check*: Train a DiT on a toy image dataset and verify sample diversity.
- **Mixture of Noise Levels (MoNL)**: Training scheme mixing joint and conditional noising. *Why needed*: Enforces learning of both p(a,w) and p(w|a), improving conditional generation. *Quick check*: Monitor both joint and conditional loss components; both should decrease over training.

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component map:**  
Architecture graph → GHN (GNN + CNN decoder) → Aligned weight tensors → Tokenizer (padding + patch extraction) → Unified token sequence → NNiT (Diffusion Transformer) → Sampled patches → Detokenizer (reassembly + cropping) → MLP policy.

**Critical path:**  
The pipeline’s critical path consists of GHN generation → alignment verification → tokenization → NNiT training → conditional sampling → policy evaluation. Any flaw early (e.g., misalignment) cascades downstream.

**Design tradeoffs:**  
- Patch size (p): Smaller patches retain fine details but increase sequence length; larger patches reduce compute but may blur local structure.  
- Maximal grid (H×W): Must cover the widest architecture; larger grids raise memory usage and produce many empty patches.  
- MoNL ratio: The balance between joint and conditional noising affects tradeoffs between unconditional diversity and conditional accuracy.  
- GHN alignment vs diversity: Strong alignment might reduce diversity of candidate policies, potentially harming the generative model’s coverage.

**Failure signatures:**  
- Misaligned weights: Heatmaps lack banding; nearest‑neighbor cosine similarity <0.3.  
- NNiT fails to generalize: Success rate on unseen widths drops >10% relative to reported numbers.  
- Tokenization mismatch: Shape errors when reassembling tensors; information loss during padding/cropping.  
- Training instability: NNiT loss diverges or MoNL components remain high throughout training.

**Three first experiments:**  
1. Replicate GHN generation (3 message‑passing layers, embed dim 128; CNN decoder with 4 transposed convs). Train with HyperPPO, generate 100 candidates per architecture, filter by success/return thresholds. Visualize weight banding to confirm alignment.  
2. Implement tokenization for patch size 4 and maximal width 128. Verify that patches reconstruct padded tensors exactly and that architecture token sequences are [in, h₁, h₂, h₃, h₄, out].  
3. Train a small NNiT (e.g., 4 layers, hidden 192) on the tokenized dataset for several hundred epochs. MoNL losses should decrease; then scale to full architecture.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
1. **Stabilization for deeper MLPs**: How can stabilization techniques from pixel‑space diffusion models be adapted to handle the high dynamic range of neural weights for training deeper MLPs with NNiT? Current experiments are limited to ≤4 hidden layers, and deeper architectures remain unevaluated.
2. **Scaling to billion‑parameter models**: Can NNiT be scaled to synthesize large foundation models by integrating linear attention mechanisms from video diffusion transformers to reduce quadratic complexity? This potential scalability has not been demonstrated.
3. **Conditioning beyond architecture**: Could NNiT’s conditional generation be extended to include environment or task tokens for meta‑learning and rapid adaptation? This idea is proposed but lacks empirical validation.

## Limitations
- GHN training details (message‑passing functions, CNN architecture, HyperPPO specifics) are omitted, hindering exact replication of the aligned dataset.
- Tokenization parameters (patch size, maximal grid, normalization factor) are not fully specified, making width‑agnostic behavior uncertain.
- Evaluation is restricted to shallow MLPs; scalability to deeper or larger models is unproven.
- No reference implementation exists, so reliance on textual interpretation risks subtle mismatches.

## Confidence
- **Width‑agnostic generation**: Medium  
- **Reported success rates** (≥85% zero‑shot, 86–99% conditional, 90–100% joint): Low  
- **Superiority over baselines** (SANE, D2NWG): Low
