# Generative Data Transformation: From Mixed to Unified Data
## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2602.22743
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.22743
- Reference count: 40
- Primary result: Taesar, a data-centric framework, improves next-item prediction metrics (e.g., HR@10 from 0.0350 to 0.0441 on Electronics vs. SASRec) by regenerating mixed-domain sequences into unified target-domain sequences.

## Executive Summary
Cross-domain sequential recommendation suffers from negative transfer due to domain gaps when naively merging data. Existing model-centric solutions are complex and struggle to capture subtle cross-domain sequence dependencies. This paper introduces Taesar, a data-centric framework that transforms mixed-domain interaction sequences into unified target-domain sequences using adaptive contrastive decoding. Leveraging a base model pre-trained on mixed data and domain-specific experts, Taesar scores and replaces source items with appropriate target items, thereby creating richer and more coherent training sequences. Extensive experiments across Amazon datasets and various sequential recommenders demonstrate consistent improvements over state-of-the-art model-centric methods. Ablation studies confirm the necessity of each component, and analysis reveals that regenerated data increases item diversity and sequence length, providing more contextual signals for recommendation.

## Method Summary
Taesar operates in two phases: regeneration and training. First, a base model is pre-trained on mixed-domain sequences (chronologically interleaved interactions from all domains for users with at least one interaction in the target domain). Then, domain-specific decoders are fine-tuned from the base model using a Domain-Specific Prediction (DSP) loss, which only considers consecutive pairs belonging to the same domain with no intervening same-domain items. For regeneration, adaptive contrastive decoding iterates over each position in a mixed sequence. For source items (non-target), it computes global scores α·p_t − β·p_s where p_t and p_s are target and source domain distributions from the respective decoders, weighted by α (target entropy) and β (JSD between base and source). If the global argmax falls within the target item set, the source item is replaced by the local argmax (computed on the target-only distribution); otherwise it is discarded. Target items are kept unchanged. The regenerated sequences are combined with original target-domain sequences to form a unified training set. Finally, any sequential recommender (e.g., SASRec) is trained on this dataset. At inference, a user’s mixed history is regenerated on-the-fly and fed to the trained model.

## Key Results
- On the Electronics dataset, SASRec+Taesar improves HR@10 from 0.0350 to 0.0441 and NG@10 from 0.0237 to 0.0291 compared to the vanilla SASRec.
- Taesar consistently outperforms state-of-the-art model-centric cross-domain methods across multiple datasets and model architectures.
- Ablation studies show that both the domain-specific prediction adaptation and adaptive contrastive decoding are essential; removing either degrades performance significantly, and analysis of regenerated sequences shows increased average length and item diversity.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The core insight is that cross-domain negative transfer arises from domain gaps in raw mixed sequences. By regenerating these sequences into coherent target-domain trajectories, Taesar provides the recommender with data that respects the target domain’s distribution while preserving cross-domain contextual signals. Adaptive contrastive decoding uses the base model’s global knowledge and the target/source experts’ specialization to make fine-grained replacement decisions. The entropy weight (α) increases confidence when the target distribution is peaked, and the JSD weight (β) suppresses source items that are too dissimilar to the target. This selective replacement and discarding filters out noise while injecting useful cross-domain information, leading to longer and more diverse training sequences that enhance the model’s ability to capture long-range dependencies and generalize.

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component Map**: Base Model (θₘ) → Domain-Specific Decoders (θₜ, θₛ) → Adaptive Contrastive Decoding → Regenerated Sequences (𝓧ₙ) → Final Recommender (any sequential model)

**Critical Path**: 1) Pre-train base model on mixed sequences. 2) Fine-tune target and source experts with DSP. 3) For each mixed training sequence, run adaptive contrastive decoding to produce regenerated sequences. 4) Merge regenerated sequences with original target sequences. 5) Train final recommender on the merged set. 6) At inference, regenerate user history on-the-fly and apply final model.

**Design Tradeoffs**: 
- *Data-centric* approach decouples transformation from recommendation model, allowing flexibility but adding a preprocessing step and requiring storage of regenerated sequences.
- Adaptive contrastive decoding introduces hyperparameters (α, β weights) which may need tuning; deterministic decoding vs. stochastic sampling affects reproducibility.
- Using a base model and experts increases computational overhead during regeneration (three forward passes per position) but is done offline once.
- Regeneration may discard many source items, potentially reducing context length; balancing discarding vs. replacement is critical.

**Failure Signatures**:
- Regenerated sequences become too short (average length drops significantly), indicating excessive discarding.
- Selected replacement items have low semantic similarity to source items (check embedding cosine similarity).
- Final model overfits the regenerated training set (high training accuracy, low validation performance).
- Metrics fall far below reported numbers, often due to misaligned evaluation (e.g., using sampled negatives instead of full item set, or incorrect user splits).

**First Three Experiments**:
1) **Base Model Verification**: Pre-train θₘ on mixed data and evaluate its next-item prediction on target-only validation set to establish baseline performance.
2) **Decoding Sanity Check**: On a small fixed set of 10 mixed sequences, manually compute α, β and the global/local scores for a few positions; verify that replacements and discards follow expected patterns (e.g., target argmax chosen when global argmax is target).

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- **Open Question 1**: What are the theoretical limits of information preservation during the regeneration of mixed-domain sequences into target-domain sequences, and how does the proposed adaptive contrastive decoding balance between preserving cross-domain context and enforcing target-domain coherence?
- **Open Question 2**: Under what conditions (e.g., domain similarity, base model capacity, data sparsity levels) does Taesar's data-centric transformation provide maximal benefit compared to model-centric approaches, and are there diminishing returns or failure modes?
- **Open Question 3**: Can the regeneration process be optimized or approximated to reduce computational overhead during offline preprocessing, and what is the trade-off between regeneration quality and efficiency?

## Limitations
- Unspecified hyperparameters and training configuration (epochs, learning rates, DSP loss weighting) require assumptions that may impact convergence and regeneration quality.
- Ambiguous algorithmic details (α/β formulation, entropy normalization, JSD computation, and decoding determinism) create reproducibility risks.
- Incomplete description of data split strategy and user filtering may lead to misaligned experimental setups compared to the paper.

## Confidence
- **Primary performance claim (Taesar > SOTA)**: High. Results are quantitatively reported across datasets and models; ablations support component necessity.
- **Component necessity (DSP, Adaptive Decoding)**: High. Ablation studies show performance drops when either is removed.
- **Data-centric superiority over model-centric methods**: Medium. The paper contrasts with model-centric solutions but lacks direct ablation against a naive data-merging baseline with identical models.
- **Analysis of regenerated data (diversity/length)**: Medium. Correlative observations are presented but not causally linked to gains.
- **Reproducibility of exact numbers**: Low due to unspecified hyperparameters and algorithmic details.
