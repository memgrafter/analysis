# Huntington Disease Automatic Speech Recognition with Biomarker Supervision
## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2603.11168
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.11168
- Reference count: 0
- Primary result: HD-specific adaptation of Parakeet-TDT achieves 4.95% WER; biomarker supervision reshapes error patterns (↓substitutions/insertions, ↑deletions in severe cases) without overall WER improvement.

## Executive Summary
Huntington’s Disease (HD) speech exhibits hyperkinetic dysarthria, characterized by irregular timing and articulation, which challenges standard automatic speech recognition (ASR). This study introduces a novel clinical HD corpus (4.5 hours, 130 speakers across severity cohorts) and conducts a systematic evaluation of ASR architectures. A transducer model (Parakeet-TDT) significantly outperforms encoder-decoder (Whisper) and CTC baselines in zero-shot evaluation. Parameter-efficient HD-specific adaptation further reduces word error rate (WER) to 4.95%. While auxiliary supervision from clinically-derived biomarkers does not lower overall WER, it selectively alters error patterns: substitutions and insertions decrease, but deletions increase, particularly in more severe speech. Thus, architectural robustness and domain adaptation yield substantial gains, whereas biomarker-informed training introduces non-uniform clinical trade-offs rather than uniform transcription improvements.

## Method Summary
The study employs a three-stage framework on a stratified HD corpus (controls, pre-HD, prodromal, manifest). Stage I compares zero-shot performance of Whisper (encoder-decoder), Parakeet-TDT (transducer), and Omnilingual (CTC). Stage II applies parameter-efficient adaptation to Parakeet-TDT 0.6B using encoder-side adapters, trained with AdamW (LR 3e-4, weight decay 1e-3) for 8 epochs on the 70% training split. Stage III initializes from the Stage II checkpoint, adds three linear heads predicting discretized biomarker families (prosody, phonation, articulation) from masked mean-pooled encoder states, and jointly trains with loss L_total = L_ASR + 0.1 * L_bio for 10 epochs, using fixed schedules due to validation instability. Evaluation uses WER and error decomposition (substitution, deletion, insertion) on a 20% speaker-independent test set, with transcript normalization applied consistently.

## Key Results
- Parakeet-TDT transducer achieves the lowest zero-shot WER (6.99%) among Whisper and CTC baselines.
- HD-specific adaptation via encoder adapters reduces WER to 4.95%.
- Auxiliary biomarker supervision does not improve overall WER but decreases substitutions and insertions while increasing deletions, with the deletion effect pronounced in manifest (severe) HD.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The transducer architecture (Parakeet-TDT) inherently handles irregular timing and articulation in dysarthric speech through joint ASR and alignment modeling, outperforming sequence-to-sequence and CTC approaches. HD-specific adaptation aligns the encoder to domain-specific acoustic patterns using parameter-efficient adapters, avoiding overfitting on the small dataset. Biomarker supervision forces the encoder to learn representations aligned with clinical features (prosody, phonation, articulation), which indirectly influences decoding bias: the model becomes more conservative, leading to fewer insertions/substitutions but more omissions (deletions), especially in severe cases where biomarker signals are strongest.

## Foundational Learning
- **Hyperkinetic dysarthria**: Motor disorder causing irregular speech timing/articulation in HD. *Why needed*: Explains why standard ASR fails; requires models robust to temporal variations. *Quick check*: Compute coefficient of variation for speech rate and pause duration across utterances; expect >20% in manifest HD.
- **Transducer models (RNN-T/TDT)**: Jointly predict tokens and alignments, non-autoregressive in training. *Why needed*: Handle variable-length outputs without strict monotonic alignment, suiting dysarthric timing. *Quick check*: Visualize alignments on dysarthric vs. normal speech; transducers should show fewer forced alignments.
- **Parameter-efficient adaptation (adapters)**: Small trainable modules inserted into pretrained model. *Why needed*: Adapt to low-resource HD data without catastrophic forgetting. *Quick check*: Count adapter parameters; ensure <1% of total parameters for efficiency.
- **Auxiliary biomarker supervision**: Multi-task learning with clinical features. *Why needed*: Inject domain knowledge to shape encoder representations toward clinically relevant attributes. *Quick check*: Verify auxiliary head accuracy on held-out biomarker labels; expect >70% for discretized classes.
- **Error decomposition analysis**: Breaking WER into substitutions, deletions, insertions. *Why needed*: Reveal trade-offs not captured by aggregate WER. *Quick check*: Compare per-cohort error type percentages; biomarker supervision should show ↑deletions in manifest HD.

## Architecture Onboarding
Component map: Audio -> Encoder (with adapters) -> Masked mean pooling -> Linear heads (biomarker prediction) & Decoder (ASR output). Critical path: Encoder processes features; decoder generates transcripts; auxiliary heads branch from encoder for biomarker prediction. Design tradeoffs: Adapter bottleneck dimension trades capacity vs. overfitting; fixed auxiliary loss weight (λ=0.1) balances ASR and biomarker objectives; fixed epochs avoid validation instability but risk suboptimal checkpoints. Failure signatures: Significant WER increase on manifest HD (>+3%) dominated by deletion rate rise; divergent training loss during joint optimization; large performance gap between control and manifest cohorts (>10% WER difference). First experiments: 1) Zero-shot WER comparison across Whisper, Parakeet-TDT, Omnilingual on test set. 2) Train adapters only; validate WER reduction vs. baseline. 3) Add biomarker heads; compare per-cohort error type shifts to detect deletion bias.

## Limitations
- Corpus accessibility: The HD corpus is cited but not directly downloadable; exact speaker splits and transcript normalization rules are undocumented.
- Implementation under-specification: Adapter architecture (e.g., bottleneck size), biomarker discretization bins, and encoder masking strategy are missing, hindering exact replication.
- Small dataset size (4.5 hours, 130 speakers) may limit robustness and generalization across diverse HD speech profiles.
