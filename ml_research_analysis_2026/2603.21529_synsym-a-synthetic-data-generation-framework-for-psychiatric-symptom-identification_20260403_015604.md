# SynSym: A Synthetic Data Generation Framework for Psychiatric Symptom Identification

## Quick Facts
*   **arXiv ID:** 2603.21529
*   **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.21529
*   **Reference Count:** 40
*   **Primary Result:** Models trained solely on SynSym's 18k synthetic samples matched real-data model performance on three benchmarks (0.778 Macro-F1 on PsySym) and exceeded all baselines after real-data fine-tuning (up to 0.830 Macro-F1).

## Executive Summary
SynSym addresses the scarcity of large-scale, labeled data for psychiatric symptom identification by generating synthetic text expressions using large language models (LLMs). Starting only from a list of DSM-5 symptom classes and their descriptions, the framework systematically expands each symptom into sub-concepts, generates both clinical and colloquial expressions, creates multi-symptom posts based on literature-derived co-occurrence patterns, and validates the output. The resulting dataset of 18,254 samples for 14 depressive symptoms enables models to achieve performance competitive with models trained on real, annotated data. After fine-tuning on a small amount of real data, SynSym-trained models surpass all baselines, demonstrating the potential of high-quality synthetic data as a scalable alternative for this sensitive, data-scarce domain.

## Method Summary
The pipeline is a four-stage synthesis process requiring only symptom keywords and brief clinical descriptions. **Stage 1** uses GPT-4o (temp 0.0) to decompose each symptom into 10+ fine-grained sub-concepts. **Stage 2** generates 50 first-person expressions per sub-concept (25 clinical, 25 colloquial) using GPT-4o (temp 0.8). **Stage 3** synthesizes multi-symptom expressions by prompting the LLM with extracted clinical comorbidity patterns to create 2-5 symptom combinations, then generating expressions for each combination in both styles. **Stage 4** employs an LLM (temp 0.0) to filter out semantically misaligned expressions (score ≤2/5). The final filtered dataset is used to fine-tune MentalBERT. For evaluation, models are tested on benchmark datasets (PsySym, PRIMATE, D2S) via cross-validation or official splits, with an optional LLM-based rewriting step for the figurative D2S test set.

## Key Results
*   Synthetic-only models trained on SynSym data matched the performance of real-data-trained models across three benchmarks (e.g., 0.778 Macro-F1 on PsySym).
*   After fine-tuning on real benchmark data, SynSym models exceeded all prior baselines, reaching up to 0.830 Macro-F1.
*   The framework generalized across datasets and disorders (tested on PTSD, ED, ADHD), though with performance variability indicating domain-specific tuning may be needed.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
SynSym works by injecting two forms of structured knowledge into LLM generation to overcome typical synthetic data pitfalls: **lack of clinical grounding** and **incoherent multi-label samples**. First, the symptom sub-concept expansion forces the LLM to produce a diverse yet ontologically constrained vocabulary for each symptom, preventing semantic drift. Second, enforcing clinically documented co-occurrence patterns for multi-symptom generation ensures the label combinations are epidemiologically plausible, creating realistic multi-label training instances. The dual-style generation (clinical/colloquial) and semantic filtering further enhance diversity while maintaining label integrity, resulting in a dataset that teaches models the *linguistic manifestations* of symptoms rather than just their surface keywords.

## Foundational Learning
*Concept 1: Clinical Ontology-Guided Generation*
    *   **Why needed:** Prevents LLM hallucination and ensures all generated text is semantically tied to a defined symptom concept.
    *   **Quick check:** Verify every synthetic sample contains at least one phrase from its assigned symptom's sub-concept list.
*Concept 2: Comorbidity Pattern Integration*
    *   **Why needed:** Real-world symptom expressions often mention multiple concurrent symptoms; modeling this is essential for multi-label learning.
    *   **Quick check:** Compare distribution of symptom co-occurrences in synthetic data against known epidemiological rates from cited literature.
*Concept 3: Stylistic Dualism (Clinical vs. Colloquial)*
    *   **Why needed:** Captures the spectrum from formal diagnostic language to layperson social media expression, improving out-of-distribution robustness.
    *   **Quick check:** Classify a sample of generated texts; each (symptom, sub-concept) pair should yield expressions in both styles.

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component Map:**
Symptom Keywords + Descriptions -> Sub-Concept Expansion -> Single-Symptom Expression Gen -> Multi-Symptom Combination Rules -> Multi-Symptom Expression Gen -> Semantic Validation -> Filtered Synthetic Dataset -> MentalBERT Fine-Tuning -> Evaluation on Benchmark Test Sets

**Critical Path:** Symptom Definition -> Sub-Concept Expansion -> Multi-Symptom Combination -> Expression Generation. Failures in early stages propagate.

**Design Tradeoffs:** 
*   **Scale vs. Authenticity:** Using LLMs enables massive scale but risks generating "textbook" rather than "lived experience" language.
*   **Style Diversity vs. Label Noise:** Explicit dual-style prompts increase diversity but may produce ambiguous expressions that fail validation.
*   **Co-occurrence Fidelity vs. Flexibility:** Hard-coded comorbidity patterns ensure valid combinations but may miss atypical or culturally specific presentations.

**Failure Signatures:**
*   Overly generic or repetitive expressions (failed sub-concept diversity).
*   Multi-symptom samples that list symptoms disjointly without narrative coherence.
*   Systematic avoidance of sensitive terms (e.g., "suicide") leading to missing critical indicators.
*   Severe class imbalance mirroring over-sampled co-occurrence patterns.

**First Three Experiments:**
1.  **Single-Stage Quality Audit:** Manually evaluate 100 samples from Stage 2 (single-symptom) for clinical accuracy and style adherence per symptom.
2.  **Co-occurrence Distribution Analysis:** Compute and visualize the symptom co-occurrence matrix in the final synthetic dataset; compare against target patterns.
3.  **Baseline Model Sanity Check:** Train a shallow classifier (e.g., SVM on TF-IDF) on a small synthetic subset; it should achieve non-random performance, confirming signal exists before full fine-tuning.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
*   **Generalization Beyond Depression:** How effectively does SynSym generalize to psychiatric conditions with different core symptom structures (e.g., anxiety, psychosis)? Initial tests on PTSD, ED, ADHD are insufficient for broad claims.
*   **Figurative Language Inclusion:** Can SynSym be extended to generate clinically valid metaphorical expressions without sacrificing label reliability, and would this improve performance on datasets like D2S?
*   **Cultural & Platform Robustness:** How robust is the reliance on Western clinical literature for co-occurrence patterns across diverse cultural contexts and social media platforms?
*   **Symptom Ontology Gaps:** Does the exclusion of symptoms during cross-dataset remapping (e.g., Anger/Irritability) introduce systematic biases, and can a unified ontology improve cross-dataset training?

## Limitations
*   The exact clinical descriptions for the 14 depressive symptoms and the precise co-occurrence rules extracted from literature are unreported, hindering faithful reproduction and assessment of epidemiological validity.
*   The prompt template for single-symptom expression generation (required for the 5 clinical + 5 colloquial output) is missing from the appendix.
*   SynSym explicitly excludes figurative or metaphorical language, which may limit linguistic diversity and performance on datasets that rely on such expression (e.g., D2S).
