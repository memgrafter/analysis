# Coverage Guarantees for Pseudo-Calibrated Conformal Prediction under Distribution Shift

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2602.14913
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.14913
- Reference count: 38
- Primary result: The paper derives a lower bound on target coverage for pseudo-calibrated conformal prediction under bounded label-conditional covariate shift and proposes a source-tuned algorithm that restores coverage empirically on MNIST and CIFAR datasets.

## Executive Summary
Conformal prediction (CP) is a powerful tool for constructing prediction sets with guaranteed marginal coverage when data are exchangeable. However, under distribution shift and when target labels are unavailable—as is common in deployment—CP's coverage can severely deteriorate. This paper investigates pseudo-calibration, a variant that uses pseudo-labels from a source model on the target data, within the framework of bounded label-conditional covariate shift. The authors employ domain adaptation tools to derive a theoretical lower bound on the target coverage: at least 1-α minus a term involving the source ramp loss, a margin Lipschitz constant, and a Wasserstein distance that measures the shift magnitude. To enforce the guarantee, they propose inflating the conformal quantile by a slack parameter that accounts for these shift contributions. For practical robustness, they introduce a source-tuned pseudo-calibration algorithm that adaptively mixes hard pseudo-labels (from the classifier) with random pseudo-labels based on the classifier's uncertainty. Empirical evaluations on MNIST, CIFAR-10, and CIFAR-100—using controlled synthetic shifts—show that the theoretical bound tracks empirical behavior and that the source-tuned method successfully restores coverage near nominal levels (e.g., from 0.437 to 0.879 on CIFAR-10 at shift σ=1.05) while preserving meaningful set sizes.

## Method Summary
The paper addresses conformal prediction (CP) under distribution shift when target labels are absent. They assume bounded label-conditional covariate shift: the marginal label distribution is invariant between source and target, but the feature distribution given a label may change. A source-trained classifier f is used to generate pseudo-labels for target instances. Conformal scores (e.g., 1 - maximum predicted class probability) are computed using these pseudo-labels. A quantile of the scores on a calibration mixture (source and target pseudo-labeled) determines the conformal threshold. To compensate for the shift, the threshold is inflated by a slack term δ which is derived from domain adaptation bounds: δ = L_r(f,P) + L_γ ρ_mix, where L_r is the ramp loss of f on source data, L_γ is a Lipschitz constant of the margin function, and ρ_mix is a Wasserstein distance between source and target feature distributions (mixture). The slack ensures a coverage lower bound ≥ 1-α - δ. For improved robustness, the authors propose a source-tuned pseudo-calibration: instead of always using hard pseudo-labels, they mix in random labels with probability p that depends on the classifier's uncertainty (e.g., high entropy → higher p). This mixing reduces the risk of overconfident pseudo-labels and helps maintain effective exchangeability, leading to better empirical coverage.

## Key Results
- Theoretical guarantee: target coverage is lower-bounded by 1-α minus a slack dependent on source ramp loss, margin Lipschitz constant, and Wasserstein shift distance.
- Source-tuned pseudo-calibration algorithm: adaptively combines hard and random pseudo-labels based on classifier uncertainty to mitigate pseudo-label noise.
- Empirical validation: on MNIST, CIFAR-10, and CIFAR-100 under synthetic shifts, the bound predicts coverage trends. On CIFAR-10 with shift σ=1.05, naive hard pseudo-calibration yields coverage 0.437 (effective sample size 0.80), while source-tuned achieves 0.879 (ESS 4.32) compared to nominal 0.8, with reasonable set sizes.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The coverage guarantee stems from bounding the distributional discrepancy between source and target using domain adaptation metrics. The slack term δ aggregates two quantities: (1) the ramp loss L_r, which measures the classifier’s margin distribution on source data, and (2) the product of the margin Lipschitz constant L_γ and the Wasserstein distance ρ_mix, which captures how much the feature distributions have drifted. By inflating the conformal quantile by δ, the method compensates for the potential increase in non-exchangeability caused by pseudo-label errors and shift, ensuring that even in the worst-case alignment of quantiles, coverage does not fall below 1-α - δ. The source-tuned mixing further reduces the effective shift by substituting uncertain pseudo-labels with random ones, which preserves the marginal label distribution and limits the influence of classifier miscalibration. This combination yields robust coverage under the assumed shift model.

## Foundational Learning
- **Conformal Prediction (CP)**: A distribution-free framework for constructing prediction sets with guaranteed marginal coverage under exchangeability. *Why needed*: Provides the baseline approach. *Quick check*: Does CP guarantee coverage if data are i.i.d.? Yes.
- **Label-Conditional Covariate Shift**: A distribution shift assumption where P(Y) remains constant but P(X|Y) may change between source and target. *Why needed*: Enables theoretical analysis without requiring target labels. *Quick check*: Does this imply the label distributions are identical? Yes.
- **Pseudo-Labeling**: Using a model trained on source data to infer labels for unlabeled target instances. *Why needed*: Target labels are unavailable for CP calibration. *Quick check*: What is a major risk of pseudo-labels? Errors can break exchangeability.
- **Domain Adaptation Metrics**: Quantities like Wasserstein distance that measure discrepancy between source and target feature distributions. *Why needed*: The coverage bound depends on such a metric. *Quick check*: Is Wasserstein a probability metric? Yes, it measures cost of transporting one distribution to another.
- **Ramp Loss**: A smooth loss that is constant for correctly classified points beyond a margin. *Why needed*: Its value on source data appears in the coverage bound. *Quick check*: Does ramp loss penalize points far from the decision boundary? No.
- **Margin Lipschitz Constant**: An upper bound on how much the classifier's margin (difference between top and runner-up scores) changes with small input perturbations. *Why needed*: Translates feature shift (Wasserstein) into margin shift affecting coverage. *Quick check*: If margin is L-Lipschitz, does a small input change guarantee small margin change? Yes.

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component Map**: Source labeled data → Train classifier f → Compute pseudo-labels on target → Estimate classifier uncertainty (e.g., softmax entropy) → Mixer (if uncertainty high, replace with random label; else keep hard pseudo-label) → Compute conformity scores based on pseudo-labels → Quantile estimation on calibration mixture (source + target pseudo-labeled) → Inflate quantile by slack δ → Construct prediction sets for target.
- **Critical Path**: The pipeline’s reliability hinges on accurate estimation of the slack parameters (L_r, L_γ, ρ_mix) and the uncertainty estimate; errors propagate to coverage violation or oversized sets.
- **Design Tradeoffs**: Hard pseudo-labels yield smaller sets but risk coverage loss; random pseudo-labels preserve coverage but produce trivially large sets; adaptive mixing balances informativeness and robustness. Slack inflation directly trades off coverage guarantee versus set size; a larger δ guarantees tighter coverage but may produce less useful sets.
- **Failure Signatures**: 
  - Coverage significantly below 1-α despite inflation → shift larger than estimated or inaccurate L_r/L_γ.
  - Prediction sets approaching universal coverage → over-inflated slack or excessive random label mixing.
  - High variance in coverage across random seeds → unstable estimation of domain adaptation metrics or uncertainty quantification.
- **First Experiments** (exactly 3):
  1. **Synthetic shift validation**: Apply controlled label-conditional covariate shift (varying σ) on MNIST/CIFAR-10; compare coverage of hard pseudo-calibration, source-tuned, and naive CP (no inflation) against theoretical bound.
  2. **Source model quality ablation**: Train source classifiers with varying accuracy and calibration (e.g., different architectures, amounts of labeled data); evaluate impact on coverage and set size for the source-tuned method, and test sensitivity to mis-specified L_r.
  3. **Comparison to OT-based CP**: Implement a recent optimal transport-based conformal prediction method under identical shift scenarios; compare coverage, set size, and computational overhead.

## Limitations
- The theoretical bound’s tightness in practice relies on accurate estimation of L_r (source ramp loss) and L_γ (margin Lipschitz constant); the paper provides no practical guidance on selecting γ or handling misspecified source models.
- The required slack inflation parameter needs a validation set drawn from a mixture of source and target distributions, which may be hard to obtain in purely unsupervised target settings.
- Experimental evaluation uses synthetic shifts with a single control parameter σ; performance under more realistic, correlated, multi-source, or gradual shifts remains unclear.
