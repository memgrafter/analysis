# AILS-NTUA at SemEval-2026 Task 12: Graph-Based Retrieval and Reflective Prompting for Abductive Event Reasoning

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2603.04319
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.04319
- **Reference count:** 40
- **Primary result:** A three-stage system ranks first on SemEval-2026 Task 12 with 95% accuracy by combining graph-based retrieval, reflective prompting, and post-hoc consistency heuristics.

## Executive Summary
This paper tackles abductive event reasoning—selecting the most direct, plausible cause of a real-world event from textual context and candidate options. The proposed solution uses a hybrid document-graph retrieval to filter distractors while preserving multi-hop evidence, an LLM reasoner with a prompt optimized via reflective evolution (GEPA), and deterministic post-hoc consistency heuristics. The system achieved first place on the SemEval-2026 Task 12 leaderboard with 95% accuracy. Cross-model analysis across 14 models reveals three shared inductive biases (causal chain incompleteness, proximate cause preference, salience bias) that drive conservative under-selection, especially on multi-answer questions. Post-hoc consistency enforcement yields the largest performance gain (+5.6 percentage points on dev, +4.8 on test).

## Method Summary
The pipeline operates in three stages. First, a hybrid retrieval constructs a similarity graph over topic documents using dense embeddings (α=0.7) and BM25+ with entity boosting. Entry points are identified via top-3 dense and top-2 sparse results, then a BFS traverses the full connected component to collect reachable documents, filtering disconnected nodes as distractors. Second, a structured XML prompt (informed by GEPA-discovered heuristics) guides the LLM to provide analysis before selecting answer options, with self-consistency sampling (k=3, τ=1.0). Third, eight deterministic post-hoc rules enforce logical invariants (e.g., "None" exclusivity, duplicate consistency, cross-sibling propagation) iteratively until convergence. The final answer set is produced after this consistency refinement.

## Key Results
- System achieves **0.95 accuracy** on SemEval-2026 Task 12 test set, ranking first.
- Analysis reveals three systematic inductive biases across 14 models that reduce selected cause count by **51%**, causing under-selection in 83% of no-exact-match failures.
- **Post-hoc consistency heuristics** provide the single largest gain (+5.6 pp on dev, +4.8 pp on test), with 85.4% of corrections being genuine improvements.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Hybrid document-graph retrieval filters distractors while preserving multi-hop causal evidence
- **Mechanism:** Build a graph where edge weight `w(di,dj)=0.7·cos(emb_i,emb_j)+0.3·BM25_score(i,j)` (threshold 0.4). At query time, seed with top-3 dense + top-2 sparse results, then collect the full connected component via BFS. Disconnected nodes are treated as distractors.
- **Core assumption:** True causal evidence forms a connected subgraph; missing nodes break chains, while extra connected nodes add tolerable noise.
- **Evidence:** [abstract] notes "filters distractors and preserves multi-hop evidence"; [section 3.1] details hybrid weighting and BFS traversal, reporting 73% document selection rate and 91% cache hit.

### Reflective prompt evolution (GEPA) surfaces effective structured prompting heuristics
- **Mechanism:** Use GEPA (via DSPy) to automatically evolve prompts on a validation set. The optimizer discovers heuristics (e.g., single-step preference, explicit causal language) and dataset invariants (duplicate consistency, "None" exclusivity). These insights are manually distilled into a final structured XML prompt requiring analysis before answers.
- **Core assumption:** Automatic optimization can identify transferable reasoning strategies and deterministic invariants that improve zero-shot generalization when manually curated.
- **Evidence:** [section 3.2 & C.2] describes GEPA setup, five discovered heuristics, and how they informed the final prompt (Figure 15); dataset artifacts were moved to post-hoc rules.

### Post-hoc consistency heuristics enforcing logical invariants yield the largest gain
- **Mechanism:** Apply eight deterministic rules (mutual exclusivity of "None", duplicate option consistency, cross-sibling propagation among questions sharing topic/event) iteratively until convergence. These operate on predictions without additional LLM calls.
- **Core assumption:** Task-specific logical constraints are consistently violated by LLMs and can be repaired programmatically with high precision.
- **Evidence:** [abstract] cites +5.6 pp gain (largest); [section 3.3 & A.9] lists the eight heuristics, shows cumulative improvement (Figure 13), and notes 85.4% correction precision.

## Foundational Learning

### Concept: Abductive Reasoning
- **Why needed:** The task requires inferring the most direct cause from incomplete context, not just retrieving. LLMs default to associative or multi-hop reasoning; the system must constrain to single-step sufficient causes.
- **Quick check:** Given an event and context, can you distinguish a proximate trigger from an enabling condition, and decide which qualifies as a "direct cause" under the task's definition?

### Concept: Multi-Label Classification with Partial Credit
- **Why needed:** Questions may have 1–4 correct causes; scoring gives 1.0 for exact match, 0.5 for partial (non-empty subset). This penalizes over-selection (extra wrong = 0) and under-selection (missing any correct = 0.5).
- **Quick check:** If gold={A,C} and model predicts {A,B,C}, what score? What about {A} or {A,D}?

### Concept: Inductive Biases in LLM Causal Reasoning
- **Why needed:** Error analysis reveals three systematic biases (causal chain incompleteness, proximate cause preference, salience bias) causing conservative under-selection. Understanding these is key to diagnosing failures.
- **Quick check:** For "X resigned after scandal Y", would an LLM more likely select "scandal Y broke" (proximate) or "long-term corruption enabled scandal" (enabling), and why?

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map:** Retrieval -> LLM reasoner -> Post-hoc heuristics
- **Critical path:** Event → Retrieval (filtered context) → LLM reasoner (initial predictions) → Post-hoc heuristics (final predictions) → Evaluation.
- **Design tradeoffs:** 
  - Retrieval: Recall-heavy traversal vs. noise tolerance; graph construction cost amortized via topic-wide caching.
  - Prompting: Structured scratchpad vs. prompt complexity; manual curation from GEPA risks dev overfitting.
  - Heuristics: Deterministic corrections vs. generalizability; task-specific invariants may not transfer.
- **Failure signatures:** Under-selection bias (predicting 1 option when gold has >1); proximate cause preference; logical inconsistency ("None" co-selected with others).
- **First 3 experiments:**
  1. Retrieve with/without graph traversal on small vs. large models to quantify distractor filtering impact.
  2. Ablate the final prompt's GEPA-derived heuristics (e.g., remove "single-step" criterion) to measure contribution.
  3. Disable each of the eight post-hoc rules iteratively; expect cross-question propagation rules to dominate gains.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- **Question:** How can post-hoc consistency heuristics, which exploit task-specific structural properties (topic groupings, duplicate options, "None" exclusivity), be generalized to other causal reasoning benchmarks lacking these features?
- **Basis:** Limitations state heuristics require adaptation for other benchmarks.
- **Why unresolved:** No methodology provided for transferring deterministic invariants to tasks with different logical structures.
- **What evidence would resolve:** Apply heuristics to 3+ diverse causal benchmarks, quantify degradation, and analyze which fail and why to derive adaptation principles.

### Open Question 2
- **Question:** Does the graph-based retrieval's recall-oriented strategy (selecting 73% of documents) systematically miss critical causal evidence, and can targeted precision improvements be made without breaking multi-hop chains?
- **Basis:** Retrieval prioritizes recall because "a missing document can break a multi-hop causal chain," but excluded 27% are unvalidated "likely distractors."
- **Why unresolved:** Unknown if excluded docs contain evidence that would resolve multi-answer under-selection failures; improving precision might increase recall errors.
- **What evidence would resolve:** Manual annotation of excluded docs for no-exact-match questions to see if they contain valid causal evidence; then experiment with adjusted similarity thresholds.

### Open Question 3
- **Question:** Are the three identified inductive biases independent failure modes, or do they stem from a single underlying bias in how LLMs process causal information?
- **Basis:** Biases are correlated across families (error overlap 2× above independence) but treated as distinct; paper does not investigate shared origins.
- **Why unresolved:** If biases are manifestations of a "single-cause default" tendency, a common intervention could improve multi-answer performance more efficiently.
- **What evidence would resolve:** Construct adversarial test cases isolating each bias while controlling others; measure failure correlation across models using hierarchical modeling.

### Open Question 4
- **Question:** Can targeted ensemble or multi-agent architectures that combine predictions from diverse model families close the 6.7 pp gap to the oracle upper bound (0.895), and what fusion strategies are most effective?
- **Basis:** Oracle upper bound shows complementarity, but only simple majority voting across three models was tested (0.811).
- **Why unresolved:** Sophisticated ensembles (confidence-weighted, stacked generalization, multi-agent debate) remain unexplored, especially on hardest multi-answer questions.
- **What evidence would resolve:** Implement and compare ensemble techniques on dev/test sets, with detailed ablation on multi-answer vs. single-answer subsets.

## Limitations
- Data access uncertain: SemEval-2026 test set may not be publicly released; reproduction relies on official data that may require permission.
- GEPA optimization details opaque: Exact optimization signature, validation split, and evolved prompts unspecified, making prompt replication from scratch difficult.
- Persistent under-selection bias: LLMs reduce selected cause count by 51%, indicating fundamental limitations in multi-label abductive reasoning not fully resolved.

## Confidence
- **Hybrid retrieval:** Medium – Parameters untested; no direct ablation of graph construction.
- **GEPA-inspired prompting:** Medium – Opaque optimization; manual curation risks dev overfit.
- **Post-hoc consistency heuristics:** High – Strong ablation (+5.6 pp) and high correction precision (85.4%).
- **Inductive biases analysis:** High – Cross-model κ substantial; explains 83% of no-exact-match failures.
- **Overall system claim (0.95 accuracy):** High – Verified on official test leaderboard.
