# MAEB: Massive Audio Embedding Benchmark

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2602.16008  
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.16008  
- **Reference count:** 40  
- **Primary result:** A comprehensive 30-task audio embedding benchmark integrated into MTEB reveals fundamental trade-offs between acoustic and linguistic understanding across 53 models, with no single model dominating all domains.

## Executive Summary
The Massive Audio Embedding Benchmark (MAEB) introduces a unified, large-scale evaluation framework for audio embedding models, addressing the previous lack of standardized, comprehensive assessment. By curating 30 diverse tasks—spanning speech, music, environmental sounds, bioacoustics, and cross-modal audio-text reasoning across 100+ languages—from an initial pool of 98, MAEB balances coverage with computational efficiency. Integrated into the MTEB ecosystem, it enables consistent evaluation via classification, zero-shot classification, clustering, pair classification, retrieval, and reranking protocols. Evaluations on 53 models show that contrastive audio-text models (e.g., CLAP) excel on environmental sounds but fail on multilingual speech, while speech-pretrained models (e.g., Whisper) show the opposite pattern, highlighting a persistent acoustic–linguistic trade-off. Clustering remains universally challenging, and overall rankings are led by LCO-Embedding-Omni-7B and Qwen2-Audio-7B. High correlation (R²=0.86) with downstream Audio LLM performance on MMAU validates MAEB’s relevance. The benchmark, code, and leaderboard are publicly released to drive community progress.

## Method Summary
MAEB constructs a representative subset of 30 tasks from an initial 98 (MAEB+) using five criteria: data validity, unique coverage per acoustic domain, linguistic diversity, redundancy removal (Spearman ρ > 0.8 between task rankings), and runtime efficiency. Tasks span audio-only and audio-text modalities, with inputs including raw waveforms (resampled to model-specific rates, typically 16–48 kHz, truncated to 30 seconds) and text prompts or labels. Metrics vary by task type: accuracy for classification (8-shot linear probing for audio-only, direct label matching for zero-shot), V-measure for clustering, average precision for pair classification, recall@5 for retrieval, and MAP@1000 for reranking. Models from four categories—audio encoders, sequence-to-sequence models, contrastive alignment models, and large audio-language models—are evaluated via a standardized MTEB-integrated pipeline, with embedding extraction tailored to architecture (e.g., mean pooling for transformers, L2-normalization for contrastive models). The framework ensures reproducibility through versioned datasets and a unified `encode` interface.

## Key Results
- **No universal model:** Contrastive audio-text models (e.g., CLAP) dominate environmental sound classification but perform near random on multilingual speech tasks (e.g., SIB-FLEURS), while speech-pretrained models (e.g., Whisper) show the inverse pattern.
- **Clustering bottleneck:** Even the best model achieves only 22.7% V-measure, indicating substantial room for improvement in unsupervised semantic organization.
- **Top performers:** LCO-Embedding-Omni-7B and Qwen2-Audio-7B rank highest overall, balancing acoustic and linguistic capabilities.
- **Predictive validity:** MAEB scores correlate strongly (R²=0.86, n=4) with downstream Audio LLM performance on the MMAU benchmark, suggesting embeddings capture key aspects of audio understanding.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
**Mechanism 1: Curated subset sufficiency**  
A carefully selected 30-task subset can represent the full diversity of audio embedding evaluation while drastically reducing cost. By filtering the initial 98 tasks for validity, coverage, redundancy (Spearman ρ > 0.8), and efficiency, MAEB maintains very high correlation with the full set (Pearson r=0.981, Spearman ρ=0.912), providing a 2.2–3.3× speedup. Core assumption: model rankings on the subset are stable proxies for full-set rankings. Evidence: empirical correlations across 53 models; potential break condition if future architectures or domains shift the ranking relationship.

**Mechanism 2: Trade-off revelation via diversity**  
Evaluating a wide spectrum of models across diverse acoustic and linguistic tasks exposes inherent representational limitations. The observed pattern—specialization in either acoustic (e.g., CLAP on environmental sounds) or linguistic (e.g., Whisper on speech) domains—suggests a trade-off that current architectures cannot reconcile. Core assumption: performance gaps reflect fundamental model constraints, not just data bias. Evidence: consistent underperformance of CLAP on multilingual speech (e.g., <3% on FLEURS) and Whisper on environmental sounds; support from related work noting domain gaps in audio-text pretraining.

**Mechanism 3: MTEB ecosystem integration**  
Leveraging the established MTEB framework provides standardized protocols, modularity, reproducibility via versioning, and long-term community maintenance. This reduces duplication, ensures fair comparison across modalities, and encourages uptake. Core assumption: a shared evaluation backbone accelerates progress in embedding research. Evidence: success of MTEB for text and images; independent benchmarks like MMAU exist, highlighting MAEB’s unique integration. Break condition: if MTEB fails to adapt to new paradigms or lacks audio-specific contributions, integration could hinder rather than help.

- **Benchmark curation:** Strategic selection of tasks to balance diversity, coverage, and efficiency. *Why needed:* MAEB’s value lies in its principled subset that approximates the full pool. *Quick check:* If two tasks have highly correlated model rankings (Spearman ρ > 0.8), why is removing one justified? ## Architecture Onboarding
**Component map:**  
Dataset registry -> Model interface -> Task evaluator -> Aggregator -> Leaderboard

**Critical path:**  
1. Implement `encode` method for the model, adhering to preprocessing standards (resampling, truncation to 30s).  
2. Register the model in MTEB configuration (name, parameters, modality support).  
3. Select MAEB tasks and run the evaluation pipeline.  
4. Verify embedding dimensionality and normalization (e.g., L2-normalize for retrieval).  
5. Submit results to the public leaderboard.

**Design tradeoffs:**  
- Generality vs. specialization: Supporting both audio-only and audio-text models complicates the interface but maximizes coverage.  
- Efficiency vs. thoroughness: The 30-task subset offers practicality; full MAEB+ is costly.  
- Standardization vs. flexibility: Strict embedding formatting ensures comparability but may require adaptation for some architectures.

**Failure signatures:**  
- Dimension mismatch (e.g., unexpected embedding size).  
- Modality mismatch (e.g., evaluating audio-only model on cross-modal retrieval without skipping).  
- Preprocessing drift (e.g., incorrect resampling rate relative to protocol).

**First 3 experiments:**  
1. **Smoke test:** Run on 2–3 samples from a classification task (e.g., ESC50) to verify embeddings yield reasonable accuracy (>10% random, >50% for strong models).  
2. **Full MAEB run:** Execute all 30 tasks; ensure completion and correct aggregation of Borda rank and category scores.  
3. **Baseline comparison:** Check scores against reported top models (e.g., LCO-Embedding-Omni-7B); deviations in expected strengths/weaknesses may indicate implementation errors.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- **Universal cross-domain performance:** Can models achieve high accuracy across speech, music, and environmental sounds without trade-offs? *Basis:* Observed specialization gap; *Why unresolved:* Current architectures show inverse patterns (e.g., CLAP vs. Whisper); *Resolving evidence:* A model scoring >80% across all MAEB domains or effective unified training objectives.
- **Multilingual audio-text alignment:** How to lift cross-modal retrieval performance above random for 100+ languages, especially low-resource ones? *Basis:* CLAP models score <3% on most language pairs in FLEURS; *Why unresolved:* English-centric pretraining; *Resolving evidence:* Multilingual pretraining or transfer methods achieving >20% recall@5 on diverse low-resource languages.
- **Joint acoustic-linguistic representations:** Can embeddings capture both acoustic (e.g., speaker gender) and linguistic (e.g., language) features simultaneously? *Basis:* Trade-off on VoxPopuli tasks (CLAP: 94.4% gender ID vs. 30.0% language ID; Whisper: 99.4% vs. 59.2%); *Why unresolved:* Representational entanglement; *Resolving evidence:* Model achieving >90% on both acoustic and linguistic benchmarks via disentangled representations.
- **Unsupervised semantic clustering:** Can embeddings naturally cluster by semantic categories without supervision? *Basis:* Best V-measure is 22.7%; top overall models score 1.7–12.7%; *Why unresolved:* Lack of clustering-aware objectives; *Resolving evidence:* New objectives yielding >50% V-measure on MAEB clustering tasks.

## Limitations
- **Subset generalizability:** The 30-task subset correlates highly with the full 98-task set for current models but may not represent future architectures or novel domains.
- **Dataset versioning drift:** Exact splits, versions, and preprocessing details are not fixed to a specific commit, risking performance comparability over time.
- **Fragile predictive correlation:** The high R² (0.86) between MAEB and MMAU is based on only n=4 models, requiring more validation.

## Confidence
- **Curated subset sufficiency:** High for present model families (empirical correlations), Medium for future/unseen architectures due to potential distribution shift.
- **Acoustic–linguistic trade-off:** Medium–High (consistent across 53 models), but could be confounded by training data biases.
- **MTEB integration benefits:** Medium (proven for other modalities, but audio-specific upkeep and adoption uncertain).
- **MAEB–MMAU predictive relationship:** Low (only four data points; needs expansion).

## Next Checks
