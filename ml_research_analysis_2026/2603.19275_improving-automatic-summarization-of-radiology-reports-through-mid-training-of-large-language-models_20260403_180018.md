# Improving Automatic Summarization of Radiology Reports through Mid-Training of Large Language Models

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2603.19275
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.19275
- Reference count: 0
- Primary result: Mid-training on MIMIC-CXR yields GatorTronT5-Radio, which consistently outperforms models without mid-training; for the 3B model, ROUGE-L improves from 0.6157 to 0.6362 and RadGraph-F1 from 0.5428 to 0.5655 on the OpenI test set.

## Executive Summary
This study addresses the suboptimal performance of standard pre-train, fine-tune LLM strategies in radiology report summarization, which fail to capture radiology-specific language patterns. The authors introduce an intermediate "mid-training" stage using a large corpus of radiology reports (MIMIC-CXR) after general clinical pre-training but before supervised fine-tuning on the target dataset (OpenI). The resulting mid-trained model, GatorTronT5-Radio, shows consistent performance gains across model scales (0.2B, 0.7B, 3B). Notably, mid-training dramatically improves few-shot learning, achieving ROUGE-L 0.4716 with only 5 training samples compared to 0.0992 without mid-training, thereby mitigating the cold start problem.

## Method Summary
The proposed pipeline consists of three stages: (1) Pre-training: Train a T5-based model (GatorTronT5) from scratch on a broad clinical corpus (UF Health notes, PubMed, Wikipedia, MIMIC-III) using the span-corruption objective. (2) Mid-training: Initialize from the pre-trained checkpoint and continue unsupervised training on MIMIC-CXR reports (concatenated Findings and Impression sections) with the same objective for 2 epochs. (3) Fine-tuning: Supervised sequence-to-sequence training on the OpenI dataset (Findings → Impression generation). Evaluation uses ROUGE-L, RadGraph-F1, and other metrics, including few-shot scenarios.

## Key Results
- For the 3B model, mid-training yields ROUGE-L 0.6362 vs 0.6157 (clinical pre-training only) and RadGraph-F1 0.5655 vs 0.5428 on the OpenI test set.
- In few-shot learning with only 5 samples, the mid-trained base model achieves ROUGE-L 0.4716, a nearly 5× improvement over the non-mid-trained counterpart (0.0992).
- Performance gains are consistent across all model scales (0.2B, 0.7B, 3B), demonstrating the scalability of the approach.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The paper attributes the effectiveness of mid-training to three mechanisms:

**1. Capturing radiology-specific linguistic patterns:** Mid-training on a large corpus of radiology reports adjusts the model’s internal representations to better model the subdomain’s syntax (e.g., dense anatomical jargon, omission of function words, frequent negation). The distinct data distribution of radiology compared to general clinical notes makes a dedicated adaptation phase beneficial. Evidence includes the observed improvements across scales and the literature on radiology-specific language. A break condition would be minimal benefit if target reports are stylistically similar to the pre-training corpus.

**2. Mitigating the cold start in few-shot learning:** Mid-training provides a warm-start that pre-aligns the model to the radiology subdomain, reducing the fine-tuning burden. The model already understands radiology syntax, so few-shot learning focuses on mapping findings to impressions rather than learning fundamentals. Evidence is the dramatic few-shot gains (e.g., ROUGE-L 0.4716 vs 0.0992 with 5 samples). A break condition is misalignment if few-shot data diverges significantly from mid-training data (e.g., different imaging modality).

**3. Regularization against catastrophic forgetting:** Mid-training on a diverse radiology corpus, starting from a general clinical checkpoint, exposes the model to subdomain specifics without overwriting broadly useful knowledge. The unsupervised objective on large data promotes stability. Evidence includes consistent performance improvements from pre-training to mid-training to fine-tuning. A break condition is excessive mid-training duration or learning rate causing catastrophic interference.

## Foundational Learning
**Concept: Transfer Learning & Domain Adaptation in NLP**  
Why needed: The study uses a staged transfer (general → clinical → radiology), highlighting the importance of intermediate domain adaptation to avoid negative transfer and improve specialization.  
Quick check: If fine-tuning directly on a small specialized dataset, what is the primary risk compared to first adapting on a larger same-domain corpus? (Answer: Risk of overfitting or failing to capture domain-specific patterns due to insufficient data.)

**Concept: Sequence-to-Sequence Pre-training Objectives (e.g., Span Corruption)**  
Why needed: Both pre-training and mid-training use T5-style denoising. This objective enables unsupervised learning of language structure and content patterns without labeled data.  
Quick check: How does span corruption encourage learning of both syntax and potential content structures? (Answer: By masking spans and requiring reconstruction, the model learns to predict missing tokens based on context, capturing syntactic rules and semantic relationships.)

**Concept: Evaluation Metrics for Summarization (ROUGE, BERTScore, RadGraph-F1)**  
Why needed: The study reports multiple metrics to assess different aspects: lexical overlap (ROUGE), semantic similarity (BERTScore), and clinical factual consistency (RadGraph-F1). Interpreting improvements requires understanding their distinct focuses.  
Quick check: Why might high ROUGE but low RadGraph-F1 indicate limited clinical utility? (Answer: High ROUGE suggests surface-level similarity, but low RadGraph-F1 indicates errors in clinical entities/relations, making the summary potentially misleading.)

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component map:** Clinical Pre-training (broad corpus) -> GatorTronT5 base -> Mid-training (MIMIC-CXR) -> GatorTronT5-Radio checkpoint -> Fine-tuning (OpenI) -> Final model -> Evaluation (ROUGE-L, RadGraph-F1, etc.)

**Critical path:** Load Clinical Pre-trained Checkpoint -> Mid-train on MIMIC-CXR -> Fine-tune on OpenI -> Evaluate. The mid-trained checkpoint is the critical artifact; its quality determines downstream performance.

**Design tradeoffs:** 
- Mid-training Data Size vs. Duration: Large corpus allows brief training (2 epochs) to avoid catastrophic forgetting but may not be optimal.
- Objective Consistency: Using the same span-corruption objective for all stages simplifies implementation but may not optimally task-specify for summarization.
- Compute Cost: Adding mid-training increases total compute (~13M tokens) but may reduce fine-tuning data needs.

**Failure signatures:**
1. Degraded OOD performance: Mid-trained model performs worse on OpenI than the clinical baseline, indicating overfitting to MIMIC-CXR.
2. No few-shot benefit: Performance gap between mid-trained and non-mid-trained models does not narrow in low-resource settings (k < 200).
3. Factuality drop: RadGraph-F1 decreases while ROUGE increases, showing the model learns style but corrupts clinical facts.

**First 3 experiments:**
1. **Baseline establishment:** Fine-tune three base models (T5, GatorTronT5, GatorTronT5-XL) on OpenI without mid-training to establish scale-up and clinical pre-training benefits.
2. **Mid-training ablation:** For each model scale, introduce mid-training and compare performance on the full OpenI test set to isolate the marginal gain.
3. **Few-shot threshold analysis:** For k ∈ {5,10,20,50,100,200}, fine-tune all six model variants (with/without mid-training) and plot performance vs. sample size to visualize cold start alleviation.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
**Open Question 1:** Why does mid-training alleviate the cold start problem in few-shot learning?  
Basis: The paper shows dramatic few-shot gains but does not investigate the mechanism (whether better initialization, enhanced pattern recognition, or reduced forgetting).  
What would resolve it: Controlled experiments comparing training dynamics (loss curves, gradient norms) during few-shot fine-tuning and probing internal representations on radiology-specific features.

**Open Question 2:** What are optimal mid-training hyperparameters (data volume, epochs, objective)?  
Basis: The study uses fixed settings (2 epochs, full MIMIC-CXR, span-corruption) without justification or comparison.  
What would resolve it: Systematic hyperparameter search varying mid-training duration, data size, and objectives, evaluating downstream performance (full and few-shot) to find Pareto-optimal trade-offs.

**Open Question 3:** Does mid-training generalize to radiology subdomains beyond chest X-ray?  
Basis: The study only uses chest X-ray reports from MIMIC-CXR and OpenI.  
What would resolve it: Replicate the pipeline using datasets from other modalities (e.g., MIMIC-MRI, CT reports) and compare performance of models mid-trained on chest X-ray vs. domain-specific data.

**Open Question 4:** How do internal representations differ after mid-training?  
Basis: The hypothesis claims mid-training captures radiology-specific syntax, but no representational analysis is provided.  
What would resolve it: Apply probing tasks (e.g., predicting presence of radiology terms, negation cues) on frozen models; visualize attention patterns; use representational similarity analysis (RSA) to quantify divergence.

## Limitations
- Replication is hindered by undisclosed GatorTronT5 pre-training details and the non-public UF Health corpus; RadGraph-F1 scoring may vary across implementations.
- Generalizability beyond MIMIC-CXR and OpenI is unknown; benefits likely depend on similarity between mid-training and target data, which remains untested across diverse radiology styles.
- The study does not analyze the internal mechanisms of mid-training in depth (e.g., which linguistic features are captured), leaving the theoretical foundation under-explored.

## Confidence
- Mid-training captures radiology-specific patterns: Medium (supported by performance gains but lacking direct mechanistic evidence).
- Mid-training mitigates cold start in few-shot: High (robustly validated by empirical results).
- Mid-training acts as a regularizer preventing catastrophic forgetting: Medium (inferred from consistent improvements, but no explicit forgetting measurement).

## Next Checks
1. **Data Distribution Sensitivity Test:** Train mid-models on subsets of MIMIC-CXR with controlled stylistic variations (e.g., anonymized vs. original templates) and fine-tune on OpenI to quantify the impact of corpus similarity.
2. **Cross-Domain Robustness:** Apply the same mid-training pipeline to a structurally different radiology corpus (e.g., historical reports from another hospital) and evaluate on OpenI to detect potential misalignment failures.
3. **Probing for Syntax Transfer:** Introduce a radiology-specific syntax probe (e.g., anatomical entity identification in unlabeled reports) to compare mid-trained vs. non-mid-trained models, directly assessing the claim about jargon and negation pattern acquisition.
