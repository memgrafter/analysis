# Exploring the Performance of ML/DL Architectures on the MNIST-1D Dataset

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID**: 2602.13348
- **Source URL**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.13348
- **Reference count**: 19
- **Primary result**: TCN and DCNN achieve near-human test accuracy (~96%) on MNIST-1D, significantly outperforming logistic regression (32%), MLP (68%), CNN (94%), and GRU (91%).

## Executive Summary
The paper investigates whether modern neural architectures can achieve human-level performance on a challenging variant of the MNIST dataset, MNIST-1D, which embeds sequential complexity and variability. By adapting Residual Networks (ResNet), Temporal Convolutional Networks (TCN), and Dilated Convolutional Neural Networks (DCNN) to the 1D setting and comparing them against prior benchmarks (logistic regression, MLP, CNN, GRU), the study finds that TCN and DCNN reach accuracies approaching the human benchmark of 96%, while simpler models lag significantly. The results underscore the importance of architectural inductive biases—such as hierarchical feature extraction and large receptive fields—for small but structured datasets.

## Method Summary
The authors evaluate three architectures on the MNIST-1D dataset, which consists of 1D sequences of length 40 derived from digit templates with added transformations (padding, circular shift, Gaussian noise, smoothing). The models—ResNet, TCN, and DCNN—are implemented as 1D adaptations: ResNet uses initial convolution and residual blocks with skip connections; TCN employs stacked residual blocks with dilated convolutions and dropout; DCNN uses three dilated convolutional layers with increasing dilation rates. All models are trained with the Adam optimizer, and test accuracy is recorded at 1,000, 2,000, and 10,000 steps. The results are compared against previously reported benchmarks (logistic regression, MLP, CNN, GRU) and a human performance baseline of 96%. However, the paper does not specify exact hyperparameters (learning rate, batch size, weight decay) nor provide statistical aggregates across multiple random seeds.

## Key Results
- TCN and DCNN achieve the highest test accuracies, reaching ~96% at 10k steps, comparable to human performance.
- ResNet shows improvement over shallower models but remains below TCN/DCNN, highlighting the benefit of hierarchical feature extraction.
- Simpler models (logistic regression, MLP) perform poorly (<70%), while CNN and GRU achieve 94% and 91% respectively, demonstrating MNIST-1D's discriminative power.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
TCN and DCNN excel because their dilated convolutional layers provide exponentially growing receptive fields, allowing them to integrate information across the entire 40-step sequence without excessive pooling. This global context is crucial for distinguishing digits that have been transformed by shifts and noise. Moreover, TCN's residual blocks mitigate vanishing gradients and enable deep networks, while DCNN's stacked dilated layers offer a lightweight way to capture multi-scale patterns. ResNet, though deeper, uses strided convolutions early which may reduce resolution and limit fine-grained temporal modeling; nevertheless, its skip connections support hierarchical feature learning, leading to strong but slightly lower performance. The inductive biases of these architectures—locality, hierarchical abstraction, and parameter sharing—are well-aligned with the structured sequential nature of MNIST-1D.

## Foundational Learning
- **MNIST-1D dataset**: A synthetic 1D sequence dataset derived from MNIST digits, transformed via padding, circular shift, Gaussian noise, and downsampling to length 40.  
  *Why needed*: Provides a more discriminative benchmark than standard MNIST by introducing sequential variability that favors architectures with appropriate inductive biases.  
  *Quick check*: Regenerate a few samples using the official Greydanus implementation; confirm sequence length=40 and visual resemblance to Figure 4 in the paper.

- **Residual Networks (ResNet)**: Deep convolutional networks with skip connections that add the input of a residual block to its output, alleviating vanishing gradients.  
  *Why needed*: Enables training of very deep models for hierarchical feature extraction, which improves performance on structured data.  
  *Quick check*: Inspect model summary to ensure residual blocks implement identity addition (sum of skip and main path) and include batch normalization.

- **Temporal Convolutional Networks (TCN)**: Stacked 1D dilated convolutional layers with residual connections, designed for sequence modeling.  
  *Why needed*: Dilations expand receptive field exponentially while preserving resolution, allowing the network to capture long-range dependencies in sequences like MNIST-1D.  
  *Quick check*: Verify dilation rates double per layer (e.g., 1,2,4,8) and that the total receptive field covers the entire 40-step input.

- **Dilated Convolutional Neural Networks (DCNN)**: A simpler architecture using only dilated convolutions without residual connections, followed by ReLU activations.  
  *Why needed*: Demonstrates the impact of dilation alone; serves as a lightweight baseline for sequential modeling.  
  *Quick check*: Ensure dilation rates are set (e.g., 2,4,8) and that padding is adjusted to maintain input length.

- **Inductive biases**: Built-in assumptions in model architectures (e.g., locality, stationarity, hierarchical processing) that match data structure.  
  *Why needed*: Explains why certain architectures generalize better on MNIST-1D; the dataset's transformations favor models that respect temporal continuity and scale invariance.  
  *Quick check*: Compare performance of models with different biases (e.g., strided vs dilated convs) to see which align with the data's generative factors.

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component map**  
ResNet: Input -> 1D Conv (kernel=7, stride=2, pad=3) -> [ResBlock(Conv3x3, Conv3x3) x N] -> Global Avg Pool -> Linear  
TCN: Input -> 1D Conv (kernel=7, stride=2, pad=3) -> [TCN Residual Block(dilated Conv3x3, Conv3x3, dropout) x N] -> Global Avg Pool -> Linear  
DCNN: Input -> Dilated Conv (kernel=3, dilation=2) -> ReLU -> Dilated Conv (kernel=3, dilation=4) -> ReLU -> Dilated Conv (kernel=3, dilation=8) -> ReLU -> Global Avg Pool -> Linear

**Critical path**  
All architectures share a common macro-path: initial strided convolution reduces sequence length, deep feature extraction (via residual or dilated blocks) aggregates contextual information, global average pooling collapses temporal dimension, and a final linear layer produces class logits. The critical transformations occur in the feature extraction blocks where inductive biases (residual connections, dilation) are applied.

**Design tradeoffs**  
- ResNet: Skip connections ease gradient flow and enable depth, but early strided convolution may discard fine temporal details; may require more parameters.  
- TCN: Dilations provide large receptive field with few layers, but at the cost of sparse activations and potential loss of high-frequency information; dropout in residual blocks adds regularization but slows convergence.  
- DCNN: Simpler and parameter-efficient due to absence of residual connections; however, without skip connections, deeper stacks might suffer from vanishing gradients and limited representational capacity.  
- Common: All use 1D convolutions to respect temporal locality, which is essential for sequence data; using 2D convolutions would break this bias.

**Failure signatures**  
- ResNet: Forgetting batch normalization in blocks leads to unstable training; mismatched channel dimensions in skip addition without projection convs cause shape errors.  
- TCN: Using dilation=1 (no dilation) reduces receptive field to local context; incorrect padding (e.g., no padding) shrinks sequence length; omitting dropout may cause overfitting.  
- DCNN: Using stride instead of dilation diminishes context aggregation; insufficient dilation growth (e.g., linear instead of exponential) fails to cover full sequence; missing ReLUs kills nonlinearity.  
- General: Mis-specifying kernel sizes or padding values that alter expected sequence length between blocks; implementing with 2D convs by setting input shape incorrectly.

**First experiments**  
1. **Architecture sanity check**: Print a model summary for each network; verify number of layers, parameter counts, and that output shapes after each block match expectations (e.g., after initial conv, length halved for stride=2).  
2. **Baseline reproduction**: Train the standard CNN benchmark reported at ~94% accuracy on MNIST-1D; if test accuracy deviates significantly, suspect dataset generation errors.  
3. **Overfitting test**: For each architecture, train on a tiny subset (e.g., 10 samples) for many steps; it should achieve 100% training accuracy. Failure indicates implementation bugs (e.g., broken residual connections).
