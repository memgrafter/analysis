# Amortising Inference and Meta-Learning Priors in Neural Networks
## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2602.08782
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.08782
- Reference count: 40
- Primary result: BNNP meta-learns an informative weight prior that improves predictive accuracy and uncertainty quantification across Bayesian inference methods and scales to data-scarce meta-learning tasks.

## Executive Summary
Bayesian deep learning relies on weight priors, but common isotropic priors often reduce BNNs to Gaussian processes, losing hierarchical representation learning. Neural processes meta-learn function priors but lack explicit weight-level uncertainty. This paper introduces the Bayesian Neural Network Process (BNNP) to address both: it learns a BNN weight prior from multiple tasks via amortized variational inference. Each layer uses an inference network to map data to pseudo-observations and precisions, yielding closed-form Gaussian layerwise posteriors. This design enables exact sequential updates, minibatching within tasks, and adjustable prior flexibility. BNNP demonstrates high-quality approximate inference, replicates complex synthetic function generators, generates realistic ECG and MNIST samples, and its learned prior boosts predictive performance across several inference methods (SWAG, SGLD/SGHMC, MFVI, GIVI). Moreover, partially trainable priors (e.g., 0.5–0.8 proportion) significantly outperform both fully flexible neural processes and conventional BNNs in data-scarce meta-learning benchmarks (Abalone age prediction, Paul15 cell data), highlighting the benefit of disentangling amortization capacity from prior flexibility.

## Method Summary
The BNNP framework treats a Bayesian neural network as a latent-variable model where the weights are sampled from a prior that is itself conditioned on a collection of tasks. For a given task, an amortized inference network per layer processes the dataset to produce pseudo-observations (η) and their precisions (λ). These define a Gaussian posterior over the layer's weight matrix with mean η/λ and variance 1/λ, enabling closed-form sampling. During meta-training, the model maximizes an ELBO that combines the expected log-likelihood under these posteriors with a KL divergence to a shared hyperprior over (η, λ). The hyperprior's parameters are learned across tasks, and its flexibility can be controlled by fixing a subset of parameters, giving a spectrum from fully flexible neural-process-like priors to static priors. The resulting approach supports exact Bayesian updates when new data arrives, efficient minibatching, and scales to deep networks because all posteriors are Gaussian and sampling is straightforward.

## Key Results
- BNNP achieves high-quality approximate inference (low KL divergence) and learns functional priors that replicate complex synthetic generators (sawtooth, Heaviside, standard BNN), generate realistic ECG curves and MNIST digit completions.
- The BNNP-learned prior consistently improves predictive performance (higher log posterior predictive density, lower mean absolute error) across multiple Bayesian inference methods (SWAG, SGLD/SGHMC, MFVI, GIVI) compared to isotropic Gaussian priors.
- In data-scarce meta-learning tasks (Abalone age prediction, Paul15 cell data), partially trainable priors (e.g., 0.5–0.8 proportion) substantially outperform both fully flexible neural processes and conventional BNNs, demonstrating the advantage of separating amortization capacity from prior flexibility.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
BNNP succeeds because it combines amortized variational inference with explicit weight-level Bayesian modeling. The per-layer inference networks efficiently map datasets to Gaussian posterior parameters, avoiding costly per-task optimization while maintaining a proper treatment of weight uncertainty. The meta-learned prior captures statistical strength across many tasks, leading to more informed initializations than handcrafted isotropic priors. The closed-form Gaussian posteriors enable exact sequential updates and cheap sampling, making the approach practical for deep networks. Moreover, the ability to freeze portions of the prior parameters provides a knob to trade flexibility against overfitting, which is particularly beneficial when meta-training tasks are limited or when generalization to new tasks is desired. The latent-variable formulation as a neural process also connects to rich function-space priors while keeping weight-space uncertainty explicit.

## Foundational Learning
- **Amortized Variational Inference**: Why needed: to perform fast adaptation on new tasks by learning an inference network that directly outputs posterior approximations, eliminating per-task optimization. Quick check: train the inference network on a set of tasks and verify that the learned ELBO closely matches that of a standard variational inference run on a held-out task.
- **Bayesian Neural Networks**: Why needed: to quantify epistemic uncertainty in neural network predictions, crucial for decision-making under limited data. Quick check: compare predictive log-likelihood of a BNN with an isotropic prior against a deterministic network on a regression benchmark.
- **Meta-Learning**: Why needed: to extract common structure from a distribution of tasks and learn a prior that generalizes to unseen tasks, improving data efficiency. Quick check: evaluate the meta-learned prior on a new task and measure improvement over a non-adapted prior.
- **Neural Processes**: Why needed: to model distributions over functions conditioned on context sets, providing a flexible prior over functions that can be adapted with few observations. Quick check: sample functions from the model given a context set and check they match the training distribution statistics.
- **Closed-form Gaussian Posteriors**: Why needed: to enable exact, computationally efficient posterior updates per layer without requiring gradient-based inference. Quick check: for a linear-Gaussian model, compare the analytical posterior mean and variance to those obtained by sampling.

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component map**: Input Data -> Inference Network (per layer) -> (η, λ) -> Gaussian Posterior (μ = η/λ, σ² = 1/λ) -> Sample Weights -> BNN Forward Pass -> Output Predictions.

**Critical path**: During meta-training, for each task in the batch: (1) pass the dataset through the per-layer inference networks to obtain (η, λ); (2) sample weight matrices from the resulting Gaussian posteriors; (3) forward pass through the BNN to compute log-likelihood; (4) compute ELBO by adding KL divergence between (η, λ) and the hyperprior; (5) backpropagate gradients to update inference network and hyperprior parameters. During adaptation to a new task, steps (1)-(3) are applied with fixed hyperprior, and optionally the hyperprior can be updated sequentially via exact Bayesian updates using the pseudo-observations.

**Design tradeoffs**:
- *Prior flexibility vs overfitting*: More trainable prior parameters increase capacity but may overfit to the meta-training task distribution; partial trainability (freezing some hyperprior parameters) mitigates this.
- *Gaussian posterior assumption*: While tractable, it may be a poor approximation for weights in deep networks with multi-modal posteriors; this could limit inference quality in complex models.
- *Amortization cost*: The inference networks add computational overhead and memory, which may limit scalability to very large architectures like transformers.
- *Sequential update exactness*: The closed-form update relies on conjugacy; violations (e.g., non-Gaussian likelihoods) require approximations.

**Failure signatures**:
- Inference networks output degenerate precisions (λ → 0 or ∞) leading to numerical instability or posteriors that collapse to the prior.
- KL divergence between meta-learned prior and task posteriors becomes very high, indicating poor prior generalization.
- On held-out tasks from a different distribution, predictive performance degrades sharply, suggesting overfitting to the meta-training tasks.
- Memory usage grows linearly with the number of layers, making deep networks prohibitively expensive.

**First three experiments to run**:
1. **Synthetic replication**: Train BNNP on tasks generated from a known complex function (e.g., sawtooth). Check that the learned prior can produce functions matching the generator by computing KL divergence between samples or visual inspection.
2. **MNIST digit completion**: Use BNNP as a prior for inpainting on MNIST. Compare log posterior predictive density and reconstruction quality against isotropic BNNs and neural processes.
3. **Partial prior trainability on Abalone**: On the Abalone age regression dataset with limited data, vary the proportion of trainable prior parameters (e.g., fix 0.2, 0.5, 0.8). Measure MAE and LPPD against fully flexible NP and standard BNN baselines to confirm the benefit of disentangling amortization capacity from prior flexibility.

## Limitations
- No statistical reporting (confidence intervals, significance tests) for key metrics (LPPD, MAE), making it hard to gauge reliability.
- Meta-learning tasks: number and diversity of datasets used to learn the prior are unspecified; risk of overfitting to a narrow task distribution.
- Computational cost and scalability to modern architectures (e.g., ResNets, transformers) are not addressed, leaving practical deployment uncertain.
