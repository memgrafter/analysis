# Logit-Level Uncertainty Quantification in Vision-Language Models for Histopathology Image Analysis

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2603.03527  
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.03527  
- **Reference count:** 20  
- **Primary result:** A logit-level uncertainty quantification framework reveals a critical separation: pathology-specific VLM (PRISM) exhibits near-deterministic behavior, while general-purpose and biomedical VLMs show high stochastic sensitivity and abrupt uncertainty transitions for complex diagnostic prompts.

## Executive Summary
The trustworthiness of Vision-Language Models (VLMs) in histopathology image analysis is undermined by inherent stochasticity in autoregressive decoding. This study proposes a logit-level uncertainty quantification framework that evaluates temperature-scaled output logits across multiple repetitions using complementary metrics (cosine similarity, Jensen-Shannon/Kullback-Leibler divergences, mean absolute error). Testing on three VLMs demonstrates that the pathology-specific PRISM model maintains near-deterministic generation, whereas general-purpose (VILA-M3-8B) and biomedical (LLaVA-Med v1.5) models exhibit substantial variability, strong temperature sensitivity, and abrupt uncertainty shifts for advanced diagnostic prompts. These findings underscore the necessity of rigorous uncertainty assessment before deploying VLMs in high-stakes medical contexts.

## Method Summary
The pipeline processes 100 histopathology patches from the ARCH dataset with three diagnostic prompts of increasing complexity (basic morphology, intermediate diagnosis, advanced quantitative). For each VLM (VILA-M3-8B, LLaVA-Med v1.5, PRISM), image embeddings are extracted and combined with prompt tokens. Autoregressive generation runs at temperatures T∈{0.0,…,1.0} with nucleus sampling (p=0.9) for N=30 repetitions, storing all decode-step logits until EOS or maximum length. Logit sequences are aligned to the minimum shared length per (image, prompt, temperature) configuration. Pairwise metrics (normalized Cosine Similarity, Jensen-Shannon divergence, Kullback-Leibler divergence, Mean Absolute Error) are computed across the 30 repetitions, then aggregated and normalized for cross-model comparison.

## Key Results
- General-purpose and biomedical VLMs display high stochastic sensitivity: mean Cosine Similarity <0.71 (VILA) / <0.84 (LLaVA), Jensen-Shannon <0.57 / <0.38, Kullback-Leibler <0.55 / <0.35.
- Temperature has near-maximal impact (ΔT ≈1.00) and causes abrupt uncertainty transitions for advanced diagnostic prompts.
- Pathology-specific PRISM shows near-deterministic behavior: mean Cosine Similarity >0.90, Jensen-Shannon/Kullback-Leibler <0.10, with minimal temperature effects (ΔT <0.10).

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
Logit-level uncertainty quantification isolates the stochasticity inherent in VLM decoding by examining raw output distributions before final probability conversion. Temperature scaling directly modulates the softmax entropy: low temperatures sharpen logits (more deterministic), high temperatures flatten them (more random). By repeating generation with identical inputs but stochastic sampling, the framework captures the dispersion of logit sequences. Metrics like cosine similarity measure directional consistency, Jensen-Shannon and Kullback-Leibler quantify distributional divergence, and mean absolute error gauges pointwise volatility. Large variations across repetitions indicate that the model's internal representations are unstable under sampling, undermining reliability for medical diagnosis where consistency is paramount. PRISM's architecture likely incorporates explicit deterministic mechanisms (e.g., constrained decoding or noise injection), yielding robust logits independent of temperature.

## Foundational Learning
- **Vision-Language Models (VLMs):** Models that fuse visual and textual modalities for joint understanding. *Why needed:* Enable automated analysis of histopathology images via natural language queries. *Quick check:* Can the model generate coherent descriptions from tissue patches?
- **Temperature Scaling:** A post-softmax divisor that controls output distribution sharpness. *Why needed:* Adjusts randomness during decoding; essential for probing model sensitivity. *Quick check:* Does increasing T yield more diverse sampled tokens?
- **Uncertainty Quantification (UQ):** Techniques to estimate prediction confidence. *Why needed:* Critical for high-stakes medical decisions; identifies unreliable outputs. *Quick check:* Are model outputs consistent under repeated identical inputs?
- **Logits vs. Probabilities:** Logits are pre-softmax values; probabilities are normalized via softmax. *Why needed:* Logits preserve richer information about model confidence and inter-class relations. *Quick check:* Does softmax(logits) produce a valid probability distribution (sum=1)?
- **Stochastic Sampling (Nucleus):** Top-p sampling selects from the smallest set of tokens whose cumulative probability exceeds p. *Why needed:* Balances diversity and coherence in autoregressive generation. *Quick check:* Is the sampled token subset dynamic and probability-weighted?
- **Histopathology Image Analysis:** Interpretation of microscopic tissue images for disease diagnosis. *Why needed:* Domain-specific visual patterns require tailored model capabilities. *Quick check:* Can the model distinguish between benign and malignant morphological features?

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component map:**  
Image Patch -> Visual Encoder -> Visual Embedding -> Text Decoder (with temperature-scaled logits) -> Token Sequence (logits stored per step)

**Critical path:**  
1. Load image and prompt; preprocess to model-specific format.  
2. Encode image via visual backbone; extract embedding.  
3. Initialize text decoder with visual context and prompt tokens.  
4. For each temperature T, perform N=30 autoregressive loops: at each step, apply temperature to decoder logits, sample next token via nucleus (p=0.9), append, repeat until EOS or max length.  
5. Collect all logits; align sequences to shortest length per (I,p,T) group.  
6. Compute pairwise metrics across repetitions; aggregate by temperature and model.

**Design tradeoffs:**  
- Temperature range [0,1] spans deterministic to highly stochastic regimes but may include numerically unstable extremes.  
- N=30 repetitions offers reasonable statistical power without excessive compute; higher N would improve metric stability.  
- Metric suite (CS, JS, KL, MAE) captures both linear and distributional variability, but introduces complexity in interpretation.  
- Fixed nucleus p=0.9 is standard but could interact with temperature effects; varying p might alter sensitivity.

**Failure signatures:**  
- Significant sequence length variance within a (I,p,T) group: indicates inconsistent EOS handling or max-length truncation.  
- Saturation of JS/KL at maximum values for high T: suggests degenerate sampling (e.g., uniform random tokens) or numerical overflow.  
- PRISM showing zero temperature sensitivity: may reflect true determinism or a missed implementation detail (e.g., required explicit noise injection).

**First experiments:**  
1. Determinism check: Run single (image,prompt) at T=0.0; all logits should be identical across repetitions.  
2. Metric sanity: Create synthetic logit sequences with controlled differences (e.g., constant offset, random noise); verify that metrics respond as expected.  
3. Temperature sweep validation: For one model, compute metrics across T; confirm that Cosine Similarity monotonically decreases and divergences increase with T.

## Limitations
- Exact prompts and ARCH patch selection criteria are undisclosed, preventing precise replication.
- PRISM's near-deterministic behavior may depend on undocumented modifications; standard temperature scaling implementations might not reproduce the effect.
- Evaluation covers only three VLMs on a single dataset; findings may not generalize to other histopathology tasks or model architectures.

## Confidence
- **Proposed logit-level UQ pipeline:** High confidence. The methodology is clearly described and aligns with established practices for uncertainty analysis.
- **Comparative model findings (VILA/LLaVA vs PRISM):** Medium confidence. The observed patterns are plausible given model specifications, but missing prompt and dataset details introduce uncertainty.
- **Claim that logit-level UQ is essential for trustworthiness:** Low confidence. This conclusion extends beyond the evidence; no linkage to clinical outcomes or pathologist trust is demonstrated.

## Next Checks
1. **Material release:** Authors should publish the exact list of 100 ARCH patch identifiers and the full text of the three diagnostic prompts to enable precise replication.
2. **Cross-dataset validation:** Apply the framework on at least two external histopathology datasets (e.g., TCGA, Camelyon) and with additional VLMs to test robustness and generalizability.
3. **Clinical relevance test:** Correlate logit-level uncertainty metrics with downstream diagnostic errors (e.g., disagreement with gold-standard labels or pathologist consensus) to assess practical utility in medical decision-making.
