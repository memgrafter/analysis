# On the Complexity of Offline Reinforcement Learning with $Q^\star$-Approximation and Partial Coverage

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2602.12107
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.12107
- Reference count: 40
- Primary result: Q*-realizability and Bellman completeness alone are insufficient for sample-efficient offline RL under partial coverage; the proposed Offline Robust DEC framework achieves ε⁻² sample complexity, improving over prior ε⁻⁴ bounds.

## Executive Summary
This paper studies offline reinforcement learning under Q*-approximation and partial coverage. The central question is whether the standard assumptions of Q*-realizability (the optimal action-value function lies in a known function class) and Bellman completeness (the class is closed under Bellman optimality backups) suffice for sample efficiency when the offline dataset only partially covers the state-action space. The authors answer negatively by proving an information-theoretic lower bound: any algorithm must use at least Ω(1/ε·min{√|S|, 1/Δ²}) samples, where Δ is the suboptimality gap. This rules out polynomial sample complexity under these two assumptions alone.

To overcome this barrier, the paper introduces the Offline Robust Estimation-to-Decision (E2D.OR) framework. Unlike prior value‑centric pessimistic methods (which minimize estimated Q at the initial state), E2D.OR employs a policy‑centric robust optimization: it finds a mixture over policies that minimizes the worst‑case sub‑optimality across a confidence set of models. This decouples the problem into decision complexity (optimizing over policy mixtures) and estimation error (controlled via Bellman error). For regularized MDPs (e.g., with KL penalties), a novel second‑order performance difference lemma yields an ε⁻² sample complexity—the first to break the ε⁻⁴ barrier. The framework further extends to low‑Bellman‑rank MDPs without Bellman completeness (requiring double sampling and policy feature coverage) and provides the first theoretical analysis of Conservative Q‑Learning beyond the tabular case.

## Method Summary
The core algorithm, E2D.OR, first constructs a confidence set F_conf that with high probability contains the true optimal Q*-function. This set is built using either Bellman completeness (by minimizing a penalized squared Bellman error) or weight realizability (in linear settings), with statistical tolerance ε_stat = O(H√log|F|/√n). The algorithm then solves a min‑max problem: min_ρ ∈ Δ(Π) max_{M ∈ M_conf} [J_M(π_M) - E_{π∼ρ}[J_M(π)] - γ·max_{f∈F} D^{π^M}(f||M)]. Here, M_conf is the set of MDPs consistent with F_conf, π_M is the greedy policy in M, and D is a divergence (e.g., average Bellman error). The offset γ regularizes the trade‑off; a carefully chosen γ (or the ratio version) yields optimal dependence on the coverage coefficient C^{π*}. The output is either the mixture policy ρ or a greedy policy derived from the minimizer f ∈ F_conf. This policy‑centric robust formulation hedges against model uncertainty across all states, avoiding pitfalls of value‑centric pessimism, especially under partial coverage.

## Key Results
- **Negative result:** Q*-realizability and Bellman completeness alone cannot ensure sample‑efficient offline RL; an information‑theoretic lower bound of Ω(1/ε·min{√|S|, 1/Δ²}) is necessary.
- **Improved sample complexity:** For soft Q‑learning with a strictly convex regularizer, E2D.OR achieves ε⁻² sample complexity, improving the prior ε⁻⁴ dependence.
- **Broader applicability:** The framework eliminates the need for on‑policy interaction when the value gap is unknown, characterizes offline learnability for low‑Bellman‑rank MDPs without Bellman completeness (via double sampling and policy feature coverage), and provides the first theoretical analysis of Conservative Q‑Learning beyond tabular settings.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1: The DEC Framework for Robust Decision-Making under Partial Coverage
- **Claim:** Q*-realizability and Bellman completeness alone are insufficient for sample‑efficient offline RL under partial coverage; a robust optimization framework (DEC) that jointly optimizes policy mixture and model discrepancy is needed.
- **Mechanism:** The Offline Robust DEC decomposes sub‑optimality into (1) decision complexity (minimizing worst‑case gap over a model confidence set using a policy mixture ρ) and (2) estimation error (controlled via Bellman error under the optimal policy). This replaces value‑centric pessimism (minimizing estimated Q at s₁) with a min‑max over policies and models, hedging against coverage gaps.
- **Core assumption:** Q*-realizability, Bellman completeness, and existence of a function class F_conf containing Q* with a well‑defined discrepancy (e.g., average Bellman error).
- **Evidence anchors:** Abstract and Section 4.2 define Ordec and Algorithm 2; Section 4.3 compares with value‑centric pessimism (Example 1).
- **Break condition:** When C^{π*} is very small (partial coverage), decision complexity Ordec scales as 1/Δ² for small value gaps; DEC's policy mixture optimization reduces this sensitivity but cannot eliminate it entirely.

### Mechanism 2: Second‑Order Performance Difference via Regularization
- **Claim:** For regularized MDPs (e.g., KL penalty), a second‑order performance difference lemma yields ε⁻² sample complexity under partial coverage, improving over ε⁻⁴.
- **Mechanism:** Lemma 20 shows that for strictly convex regularizers ψ satisfying Assumption 2, J(π_M) - J(π_f) ≤ O(E_{s∼d^{π^M}}[Breg_ψ(π_f, π_M)]). The Bregman divergence scales quadratically with policy difference, turning first‑order terms (Δ_f⁻¹) into second‑order ones (Δ_f⁻²) in the final bound.
- **Core assumption:** Regularizer ψ is Legendre and satisfies biconditional bounding (Assumption 2), e.g., KL, Tsallis, or log‑barrier regularizers.
- **Evidence anchors:** Abstract and Section 5.2: Lemma 20 and Theorem 6; Lemma 7 for specific regularizers.
- **Break condition:** Requires ψ to be strictly convex and satisfy relative smoothness conditions; fails for unregularized MDPs where only first‑order TV divergence is available.

### Mechanism 3: Low Bellman Rank without Bellman Completeness Requires Double Sampling and Policy Feature Coverage
- **Claim:** Low‑Bellman‑rank MDPs become learnable offline only with double policy sampling and policy feature coverage conditions, not just realizability.
- **Mechanism:** For linear structure with mappings X: Π→ℝ^d and W: F→ℝ^d satisfying ⟨X(π), W(f)⟩ = E^{d^π}[f(s,a)-R(s,a)-E_{s'}f(s')], the offline data must consist of pairs of independent samples drawn from the same policy (Assumption 5). Coverage is defined via C^{π*} = X(π*)^T Σ_μ^† X(π*), where Σ_μ = E_{π∼μ}[X(π)X(π)^T]. Both are necessary; dropping either yields exponential lower bounds (Lemmas 23, 24).
- **Core assumption:** Q‑Bellman rank structure (Assumption 4), double policy sampling (Assumption 5), and policy feature coverage (Definition 4).
- **Evidence anchors:** Abstract and Section 6.3; Lemmas 23/24 show necessity; Lemma 11 gives sample complexity O(C^{π*}·ε_stat).
- **Break condition:** Without double sampling, polynomial sample complexity is impossible (Lemma 24); without policy feature coverage, bound degenerates to O(√|S|) instead of O(C^{π*}).

## Foundational Learning

### Concept: Q*-approximation
- **Why needed here:** The setting assumes the optimal Q* lies in a known function class F; this dictates the function approximation scheme and influences the design of pessimism and confidence sets.
- **Quick check:** How does Q*-approximation differ from Q^π-approximation in terms of representability and pessimism principles?  
  *Answer: Q*-approximation focuses on representing the optimal value alone, enabling pessimism via min‑max over models. Q^π-approximation represents values of many policies, often leading to importance‑weighted or fitted Q‑iteration methods.*

### Concept: Bellman completeness
- **Why needed here:** Ensures the function class is closed under Bellman optimality backups; this property allows constructing confidence sets via squared Bellman error minimization (rather than density ratio realizability) and is crucial for the Bellman error‑based confidence sets in the DEC framework.
- **Quick check:** What goes wrong if the function class is not Bellman complete, and how does low Bellman rank try to fix it?  
  *Answer: Incompleteness means the Bellman error of functions in F may not be approximable by other functions in F, making it hard to construct valid confidence sets. Low Bellman rank introduces a linear structure that allows estimating Bellman errors via dual features X(π).*

### Concept: Partial coverage
- **Why needed here:** Real‑world offline data rarely covers all state‑actions; the coverage coefficient C^{π*} = max_{(s,a)} d^{π*}(s,a)/(H μ(s,a)) adapts guarantees to the distribution mismatch between the optimal policy and the dataset distribution μ.
- **Quick check:** Why does the definition use H μ(s,a) instead of just μ(s,a), and how does it affect algorithm design?  
  *Answer: The H factor (horizon) corrects for multiple visits to the same state‑action within a trajectory; without it, coverage could be overestimated. C^{π*} appears as a multiplicative factor in sample complexity and in the DEC objective via the Ordec term.*

### Concept: Regularized MDPs
- **Why needed here:** For soft Q‑learning, the policy is derived from Q via a strictly convex regularizer (e.g., KL). This structure enables the second‑order performance difference lemma, which is key to achieving ε⁻² sample complexity.
- **Quick check:** What property of the regularizer yields the second‑order bound, and can we use any convex function?  
  *Answer: The regularizer must be Legendre and satisfy biconditional bounding (Assumption 2), which ensures the Bregman divergence scales quadratically with policy differences. Not all convex functions satisfy this.*

## Architecture Onboarding

**Component map:**  
Dataset D, function classes F/G/ψ, horizons H -> ε_stat computation -> Confidence set construction (F_conf via Bellman error or weight realizability) -> Model confidence set M_conf -> DEC optimizer (solve min_ρ max_M robust loss) -> Policy extraction (output mixture ρ or greedy from min_f f(s₁)) -> Learned policy.

**Critical path:**
1. Build F_conf with high probability containing Q* using either Bellman completeness or weight realizability, setting ε_stat = O(H√log|F|/√n).
2. Compute the DEC objective: minimize over ρ ∈ Δ(Π) the maximum over M ∈ M_conf of [J_M(π_M) - E_{π∼ρ}[J_M(π)] - γ·max_f D^{π^M}(f||M)].
3. Output the resulting mixture policy ˆρ (or a greedy policy derived from the minimizer f ∈ F_conf). For soft Q‑learning with second‑order analysis, the second step may simplify to picking the f that minimizes f(s₁) within F_conf.

**Design tradeoffs:**
- **Offset vs. ratio DEC:** The offset version (Equation 3) adapts to unknown value gap Δ but requires tuning γ; the ratio version (Equation 4) gives optimal dependence on C^{π*} but needs C^{π*} known or estimable.
- **Decision complexity vs. estimation error:** Ordec captures the worst‑case gap over M_conf; tighter Ordec (via curvature or regularization) reduces sample complexity but may demand stronger structural assumptions (e.g., low Bellman rank).
- **Pessimism type:** Policy‑centric (DEC’s min‑max over policies and models) vs. value‑centric (minimizing estimated Q at s₁). DEC avoids the failure modes of value‑centric pessimism under partial coverage (Example 1).

**Failure signatures:**
- **Near-zero value gap Δ:** Ordec term blows up as 1/Δ²; DEC mitigates but still suffers from inability to distinguish near‑optimal policies.
- **Large state space |S| without structure:** Lower bound √|S| forces polynomial blowup; function approximation (linear, low rank) is essential.
- **Incorrect coverage estimate C^{π*}:** Overestimation causes over‑pessimism (excessive conservatism); underestimation causes under‑pessimism (suboptimality).
- **Violations of assumptions:** If Bellman completeness fails and low Bellman rank is not used, confidence sets may not control true Bellman error, breaking theoretical guarantees.

**First three experiments:**
1. **Lower bound verification:** Implement the MDP construction from Theorem 1 (e.g., with m states and gap Δ). Vary dataset size n and plot empirical suboptimality; error should not drop below the Ω(min{√m, 1/Δ²}) lower bound for a fixed ε.
2. **DEC vs. value‑centric pessimism:** Simulate Example 1 with the four model families. Run DEC (E2D.OR) and the value‑centric GDE algorithm; observe that DEC consistently picks the safe action z while GDE may choose the suboptimal w due to model error at s₁.
3. **Low‑Bellman‑rank offline RL:** In a linear Q* setting (features φ(s,a)), generate data from behavior policy μ. Vary C^{π*} (by controlling μ) and verify that sample complexity scales proportionally when double policy sampling is enforced; removing double sampling should cause the algorithm to fail.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- **Question:** How can the DEC framework be extended to density‑ratio approximation settings in offline RL?
- **Basis in paper:** The authors note that their framework "provides a path to identifying a corresponding complexity notion" for density‑ratio approximation (Section 5 and conclusion).
- **Why unresolved:** The paper treats only Q*-approximation and average Bellman error; the required DEC‑type complexity for density‑ratio remains unformulated and unanalyzed.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** A proper DEC definition for density‑ratio realizability, together with algorithms and upper/lower bounds matching it.

### Open Question 2
- **Question:** What minimal structural assumptions beyond Q*-realizability and Bellman completeness enable polynomial sample complexity with partial coverage?
- **Basis in paper:** The negative lower bound rules out those two assumptions alone. The authors propose several additional conditions (low Bellman rank with double sampling and policy feature coverage, weight realizability, regularization) and prove they suffice, but necessity is not shown.
- **Why unresolved:** It is unknown whether the introduced conditions are essentially the weakest possible, or if alternative/weaker assumptions exist.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** A theorem classifying all sufficient conditions or proving that the known ones are necessary.

### Open Question 3
- **Question:** Can the linear and non‑linear results for offline RL under Q*-approximation and partial coverage be unified under a single framework?
- **Basis in paper:** In Section 3 the authors remark, "It is an interesting direction to unify the linear and non‑linear results."
- **Why unresolved:** Their analysis provides a separate linear result (Theorem 6) and a general DEC framework for non‑linear classes, but no single complexity measure captures both regimes.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** A unified DEC‑based guarantee that recovers both the linear and general rates simultaneously.

### Open Question 4
- **Question:** Can the E2D.OR algorithm be implemented efficiently without solving the intractable min‑max optimization over policy mixtures and model confidence sets?
- **Basis in paper:** Algorithm 2 requires optimizing over Δ(Π) and M_conf, which may be infinite and unparameterized. The paper focuses on statistical guarantees and does not address computational tractability.
- **Why unresolved:** The outer min‑max seems computationally challenging for large state/action spaces; no practical implementation or approximation scheme is proposed.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Efficient approximate solvers (e.g., based on convex relaxations or iterative methods) that preserve the statistical guarantees, or a hardness result showing the exact problem is NP‑hard.

## Limitations
- Theoretical guarantees depend on strong structural assumptions (Bellman completeness, double policy sampling, low Bellman rank) that may not hold in practice; the full necessity of these conditions remains unclear.
- The DEC min‑max optimization (Equations 3/4) is combinatorially hard; the paper does not propose scalable approximate solvers or analyze their robustness.
- Hyperparameters such as the offset γ and confidence set size require problem‑dependent tuning; while theory suggests γ ∝ √n, the optimal finite‑sample choice is unknown.
- The coverage constant C^{π*} is typically unknown and difficult to estimate from offline data alone, potentially leading to over‑ or under‑pessimism in practice.

## Confidence
- **Lower bound Ω(1/ε·min{√|S|, 1/Δ²})**: **High** — rigorous information‑theoretic proof, aligns with existing bounds.
- **DEC framework achieving ε⁻² sample complexity**: **Medium** — novel second‑order lemma, but relies on idealized assumptions (strictly convex regularizer, well‑specified confidence sets).
- **Offline learnability for low‑Bellman‑rank MDPs**: **Medium‑Low** — correctly identifies need for double sampling and feature coverage, but assumptions are strong and lack empirical validation.
