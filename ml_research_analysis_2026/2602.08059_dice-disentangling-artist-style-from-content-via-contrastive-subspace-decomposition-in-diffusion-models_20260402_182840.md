# DICE: Disentangling Artist Style from Content via Contrastive Subspace Decomposition in Diffusion Models

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2602.08059
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.08059
- **Reference count:** 40
- **Primary result:** A training-free, inference-time framework for artistic style erasure that balances thorough purification with content preservation, adding only ~3 seconds overhead.

## Executive Summary
DICE addresses the copyright risk of artistic style mimicry in diffusion models by enabling style purification without requiring a replacement style or model weight edits. It operates at inference time by first constructing contrastive triplets (anchor, positive, negative) to disentangle style and content representations in a shallow feature space via a generalized eigenvalue problem. This yields a style subspace `U_style`. During denoising, DICE differentially edits the Key/Value (style suppression) and Query (content enhancement) projections in self-attention layers. An adaptive controller modulates the erasure strength per token based on its local style concentration. Evaluations show DICE achieves superior trade-offs between style erasure (high DLPIPS `Cstyle`) and content integrity (high CLIP `cscontent`, low DLPIPS `Ccontent`) compared to baselines.

## Method Summary
DICE is a two-stage, training-free process. **Stage 1 (Subspace Capture):** Given a target artist and base content prompt, it generates three images: an *anchor* (target style + content), a *positive* (target style + different content), and a *negative* (different style + same content). Shallow feature maps from these are extracted and semantically aligned via patch-wise cosine similarity. A contrastive CCA objective, reformulated as a generalized eigenvalue problem, is solved on these aligned features to compute the style subspace `U_style`. A swapped objective yields the content subspace `U_content`. **Stage 2 (Guided Erasure):** During the denoising steps (e.g., 100–400), for each attention layer, the token projections onto `U_style` are subtracted from the Key and Value matrices, while projections onto `U_content` are added to the Query matrix. The suppression strength `γ` is modulated per token by an Adaptive Erasure Controller that fuses style scores from Q, K, V and applies a sigmoid threshold.

## Key Results
- Outperforms baseline style erasure methods on CLIP Score (high `cscontent`, low `csstyle`) and DLPIPS metrics (high `Cstyle`, low `Ccontent`).
- Adds minimal computational overhead (~3 seconds per image).
- Achieves a more favorable balance between thorough style removal and structural/content preservation than existing approaches.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
**Claim:** Contrastive triplet construction with token alignment disentangles style and content in latent space via a generalized eigenvalue problem, enabling precise style subspace identification.  
**Mechanism:** Three samples—anchor (target style+content), positive (same style, different content), negative (different style, same content)—are passed through the diffusion model to extract shallow feature maps. Patches are semantically aligned using cosine similarity to correct spatial misalignment. A contrastive CCA objective maximizes projected variance between anchor and positive (rewarding shared style) while minimizing that between anchor and negative (penalizing shared content). This Rayleigh quotient optimization is reformulated as a generalized eigenvalue problem; its top eigenvectors define the style subspace `U_style`.  
**Core assumption:** Artistic style is an abstract concept that cannot be learned from a single sample; explicit comparison across samples that isolate style from content is necessary.  
**Evidence anchors:** Abstract states the method "compel[s] the model to distinguish between style and non-style features" via a "solvable generalized eigenvalue problem." Section 4.1 details triplet construction and alignment; Section 4.2 derives the GEP.  
**Break condition:** If token alignment fails due to extreme spatial misalignment or ambiguous semantics, covariance estimates become noisy, misidentifying `U_style`. If triplet samples poorly isolate style/content, the optimization targets incorrect directions.

### Mechanism 2
**Claim:** Differential editing of the Query (Q), Key (K), and Value (V) matrices in self-attention suppresses style while preserving content by exploiting their distinct roles: K/V encode texture/style, Q encodes structure/content.  
**Mechanism:** During inference, for each token feature from shallow layers, its projection onto `U_style` is computed and subtracted from K and V (`K' = K - γ·proj_Ustyle(K)`, `V' = V - γ·proj_Ustyle(V)`), removing style components. Separately, a content subspace `U_content` is used to enhance Q (`Q' = Q + γ_q·(Q·U_content)·U_content^T`), reinforcing structural information. The edited Q', K', V' are used in attention.  
**Core assumption:** In diffusion U-Nets, K/V vectors predominantly carry low-level style features (brushstrokes, color) while Q vectors carry high-level structural/layout information.  
**Evidence anchors:** Abstract mentions "Adaptive Attention Decoupling Editing strategy dynamically assesses style concentration... performs differential suppression and content enhancement." Section 4.3 explicitly states the role split and provides the editing equations.  
**Break condition:** If the Q/K/V role assumption is inaccurate for a given architecture or layer, decoupling may suppress content in K/V or reintroduce style via Q enhancement. Overly strong `γ_q` can hallucinate content; too weak leaves blurring.

### Mechanism 3
**Claim:** An adaptive per-token erasure controller modulates suppression strength based on local style concentration, balancing thorough erasure with content preservation.  
**Mechanism:** For each token in Q, K, V, its coordinate in `U_style` is computed (L2 norm = style score). Scores are normalized across tokens and fused with weights `w_q < w_k < w_v`. A sigmoid function (steepness `k`, threshold `τ`) converts the fused score into a modulation factor `m_i`. Erasure strength `γ` is interpolated between `α_min` and `α_max` using `m_i`, giving high strength for style-dense tokens and low strength elsewhere.  
**Core assumption:** Artistic style intensity is spatially non-uniform across an image (e.g., strong in textured areas, weak in smooth regions), requiring variable erasure strength.  
**Evidence anchors:** Abstract describes "Adaptive Erasure Controller dynamically calculates the style concentration for each token." Section 4.4 defines the per-token style score, fusion weights, and sigmoid-based `γ` control. Empirical results (Figures 4,5, Table 3) show uniform strength fails.  
**Break condition:** If style score calculation is poorly calibrated (`U_style` noisy, fusion weights off), high-style tokens may be under-suppressed or low-style tokens over-erased. Sigmoid parameters (`k`, `τ`) must be tuned; poor choices cause binary-like erasure or minimal effect.

## Foundational Learning
- **Concept: Contrastive Learning (Triplet Loss Framework)**  
  **Why needed:** DICE uses (anchor, positive, negative) triplets to teach the model what constitutes "style" (shared between anchor/positive) vs. "content" (shared between anchor/negative). This comparative framework is essential for the subspace learning objective.  
  **Quick check:** Why can't a single image define the style subspace, and how do the triplets resolve this?

- **Concept: Generalised Eigenvalue Problem (GEP)**  
  **Why needed:** The contrastive CCA objective (maximize style correlation, minimize content correlation) is a Rayleigh quotient reducible to a GEP. Solving the GEP yields eigenvectors that span the style subspace `U_style`.  
  **Quick check:** Given covariance matrices Σ_AP, Σ_AA, Σ_AN, what does the top eigenvector of `(Σ_AP Σ_PA) u = ρ (Σ_AA + λ Σ_AN Σ_NA) u` represent?

- **Concept: Self-Attention Mechanism in Diffusion U-Nets**  
  **Why needed:** DICE edits the Q, K, V projections inside attention blocks. Understanding that these are linear transformations of token features and that their dot-product attention governs feature mixing helps interpret the asymmetric decoupling logic.  
  **Quick check:** In `Attn(Q,K,V) = softmax(QK^T/√d) V`, if you project K and V onto a style subspace and subtract that projection, what effect might that have on the attention output?

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map:**  
  Triplet Constructor & Token Aligner -> Subspace Solver (GEP for U_style, U_content) -> Attention Decoupling Editor + Adaptive Erasure Controller  
- **Critical path:** During each denoising step (in a chosen interval), for each attention layer: (a) compute per-token style scores from Q/K/V; (b) fuse scores and apply sigmoid to derive per-token `γ`; (c) suppress K/V via projection onto `U_style` using `γ`; (d) enhance Q via projection onto `U_content` using `γ_q`; (e) recompute attention with edited Q',K',V'.  
- **Design tradeoffs:**  
  - Subspace dimension `r`: Low `r` incompletely captures style; high `r` introduces noise/artifacts.  
  - Feature extraction interval: Early steps yield separable features; later steps blend style/content. More steps increase cost but may improve alignment.  
  - Adaptive vs. uniform erasure: Adaptive handles non-uniform style but adds complexity; uniform risks over/under-erasure.  
- **Failure signatures:**  
  - Residual style: Swirling brushstrokes or color patterns remain, especially in high-frequency areas → `U_style` incomplete or `γ` too low.  
  - Content blurring/distortion: Objects lose edges, layout warps → over-suppression of K/V or insufficient Q enhancement.  
  - Artifacts/chaos: Grainy textures, color bands → large `r` or excessive `γ_q`.  
- **First 3 experiments:**  
  1. **Token alignment ablation:** Compare erasure quality with vs. without cosine-similarity patch alignment. Expect severe degradation (blurry patches, distortion) without alignment.  
  2. **Attention decoupling variants:** Test (a) KV-only suppression, (b) QKV-before-attention suppression, (c) full DICE decoupling. Measure DLPIPS `C_content`; expect (c) lowest.  
  3. **Hyperparameter sweep on `r`:** Test `r` ∈ [8,32]. Plot `C_style` and `C_content` vs. `r`. Expect peak performance in mid-range (e.g., 12–21); low `r` → low `C_style`, high `r` → low `C_content`.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- **Generalization beyond artistic style:** Can DICE disentangle other abstract concepts (e.g., lighting, material properties) using the same contrastive triplet and GEP framework? The paper claims potential but only validates on artist styles.  
  - *Basis:* Conclusion states core technology can edit "highly entangled and abstract visual concepts."  
  - *Why unresolved:* No experiments on non-style concepts.  
  - *Needed evidence:* Systematic tests on erasure/edit tasks for concepts like "time of day" with metrics showing successful disentanglement without content damage.
- **Automatic subspace dimension selection:** How to choose the subspace dimension `r` automatically per style? The paper manually tunes `r` (e.g., 12–21) with no theoretical criterion.  
  - *Basis:* Section discusses trade-off but provides no automatic method.  
  - *Why unresolved:* Manual tuning is impractical; a principled approach (e.g., eigenvalue decay threshold) is needed.  
  - *Needed evidence:* An algorithm (e.g., elbow method on eigenvalue spectrum) that consistently matches or exceeds manually chosen `r` across unseen artists.
- **Token alignment robustness:** When does cosine-similarity patch matching fail (e.g., occlusions, multiple instances), and how does misalignment affect `U_style`?  
  - *Basis:* Section 4.1 describes alignment; ablation (Figure 7) shows severe drop without it, but boundary cases unanalyzed.  
  - *Why unresolved:* The pipeline's reliability hinges on alignment; failure modes are uncharacterized.  
  - *Needed evidence:* Controlled tests with synthetic data/known correspondences to correlate alignment error with erasure metrics.
- **Adaptive controller weighting:** Can the fixed weights `w_q, w_k, w_v` in the style score fusion be learned or adapted per style/step?  
  - *Basis:* Equation (26) uses fixed weights with no sensitivity analysis.  
  - *Why unresolved:* Style information distribution in Q/K/V may vary across artists (e.g., color palette vs. brushstroke).  
  - *Needed evidence:* Optimizing weights on a validation set per style, showing improved metrics over fixed weights.

## Limitations
- **Triplet construction ambiguity:** Automated prompt selection for positive/negative samples is unspecified; scalability to arbitrary artists is questionable.
- **Token alignment fragility:** Robustness to semantic ambiguity (e.g., cluttered scenes) and implementation details (one-to-one matching) are assumptions not fully validated.
- **Hyperparameter sensitivity:** Subspace dimension `r`, erasure strengths, and feature extraction interval require per-artist tuning; sensitivity not fully explored.

## Confidence
- **Contrastive triplet & GEP mechanism:** Medium – derivation is sound, but triplet quality and alignment are critical and not guaranteed.
- **Attention decoupling mechanism:** Medium – the Q/K/V role split is plausible but lacks validation across architectures/layers; may break if assumptions fail.
- **Adaptive erasure controller:** Low – heuristic design; style score computation and sigmoid parameters are hand-tuned and may not generalize.
- **Overall claim (superior balance):** Medium – metrics show improvement on limited artists/prompts; no large-scale study.

## Next Checks
1. **Token alignment ablation:** Replace cosine-similarity matching with random patch pairing or no alignment. Compare `C_style` and `C_content`; a large drop confirms alignment necessity.
2. **Q/K/V projection analysis:** For each attention layer, compute mean L2 norm of token projections onto `U_style` and `U_content` separately for Q, K, V. Expect style norm higher in K/V and content norm higher in Q across layers.
3. **Cross-artist and cross-model robustness:** Run DICE on 20 artists across SD v1.4, v1.5, and SDXL. Report percentage of cases where `C_style` > 0.8 and `C_content` < 0.2. Low success rate indicates narrow applicability.
