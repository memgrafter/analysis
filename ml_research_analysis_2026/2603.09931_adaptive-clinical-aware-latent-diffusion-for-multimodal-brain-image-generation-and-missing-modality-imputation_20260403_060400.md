# Adaptive Clinical-Aware Latent Diffusion for Multimodal Brain Image Generation and Missing Modality Imputation

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2603.09931
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.09931
- **Reference count:** 0
- **Primary result:** ACADiff synthesizes missing brain imaging modalities with high fidelity (PSNR 27.9, SSIM 0.911) and maintains 77.5% AD vs. HC classification accuracy even with 80% missing data.

## Executive Summary
Missing neuroimaging modalities (sMRI, FDG-PET, AV45-PET) are a common challenge in Alzheimer's disease datasets, compromising diagnostic robustness. The paper proposes ACADiff, an adaptive latent diffusion framework that generates missing modalities by denoising latent representations conditioned on available imaging data and clinical metadata (MMSE, ADAS13, CDR-SOB). The model dynamically selects a fusion mechanism (cross‑attention or projection) based on the number of available inputs and uses GPT‑4o‑encoded prompts for semantic clinical guidance. Evaluated on 1,028 ADNI subjects, ACADiff achieves superior generation quality (PSNR 27.9, SSIM 0.911) and robust downstream diagnosis, maintaining 77.5% accuracy for AD vs. HC classification under 80% missing data, outperforming all baselines. An ablation confirms that GPT‑4o encoding significantly improves performance over learnable embeddings.

## Method Summary
ACADiff uses three modality‑specific 3D VAEs to compress each imaging type into a compact latent (20×22×20). A denoising U‑Net operates in this latent space, receiving the noisy latent of the target modality, a fused representation of the available modalities, an embedding of the diffusion timestep, and a clinical text embedding. The fusion module adapts: if two modalities are available, it employs multi‑head cross‑attention; if only one is available, it uses a 3D convolution projection. Clinical metadata are transformed into structured prompts, which are encoded via GPT‑4o to produce semantic embeddings that guide generation. Training incorporates random modality dropout (simulating 1→1 or 2→1 missingness) and a consistency loss that aligns the diffusion output with the VAE reconstruction when all modalities are present. The model is optimized with AdamW for 1000 timesteps. At inference, a 10‑step Monte Carlo sampler generates the missing latent, which is decoded by the corresponding VAE.

## Key Results
- **Generation quality:** PSNR 27.9, SSIM 0.911 on the test set, indicating high‑fidelity synthesis.
- **Downstream robustness:** AD vs. HC classification accuracy of 77.5% under 80% missing modality rate, significantly outperforming all baseline methods.
- **Ablation:** GPT‑4o‑encoded prompts yield a substantial performance gain over learnable embeddings, validating the value of explicit semantic guidance.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
ACADiff succeeds by combining several synergistic mechanisms. First, latent diffusion reduces computational burden while modeling complex distributions. Second, dynamic fusion preserves complementary information from whatever modalities are present, using cross‑attention for richer integration when two are available and a simpler projection when only one exists. Third, GPT‑4o‑encoded clinical prompts provide high‑level context that steers generation toward disease‑relevant features, bridging numeric scores and semantic concepts. Fourth, consistency regularization ensures the diffusion model’s output remains aligned with the VAE manifold when data is complete, preventing collapse. Together, these elements generate anatomically plausible, clinically meaningful images that support robust diagnosis even under severe missingness.

## Foundational Learning
- **Latent Diffusion Models:** Operate in a compressed space to make diffusion feasible for high‑resolution 3D data. *Why needed:* Direct diffusion on full volumes is computationally intractable. *Quick check:* Review DDPMs and VAEs.
- **Adaptive Fusion (Cross‑Attention/Projection):** Adjusts fusion strategy based on the number of available modalities. *Why needed:* Missing data patterns are variable; a fixed fusion would be sub‑optimal. *Quick check:* Study multi‑head attention and conditional computation.
- **LLM‑Based Clinical Encoding:** Uses GPT‑4o to convert structured scores into semantic embeddings. *Why needed:* Raw numeric metadata lack the nuance of clinical interpretation; LLM embeddings capture relationships among scores and disease concepts. *Quick check:* Explore text encoders (e.g., CLIP) for conditioning.
- **Consistency Regularization:** Enforces that the diffusion output matches the VAE reconstruction when all modalities are present. *Why needed:* Prevents the model from ignoring conditioning signals and collapsing to the prior. *Quick check:* Understand regularization in generative models (e.g., reconstruction losses).
- **Modality Dropout Training:** Randomly omits modalities during training to simulate missingness. *Why needed:* Teaches the model to generate from partial inputs, improving real‑world robustness. *Quick check:* View dropout as a data‑augmentation technique.

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component map:**  
Input modality (sMRI/FDG/AV45) → Modality‑specific VAE encoder → Latent Z_available  
Dynamic fusion (cross‑attention for two inputs, 3D‑conv projection for one) → Fused latent Z_fused  
Clinical data → Structured prompt → GPT‑4o → Text embedding z_text  
Diffusion timestep t → Sinusoidal embedding z_t  
Denoiser U‑Net takes concatenated [noisy target latent z_t, Z_fused, z_text, z_t] → predicted noise  
Sampler (DDPM, 10‑step MC) → denoised latent Z0  
Target modality VAE decoder → Generated volume  

**Critical path:** Encode available modalities → Fuse → Encode clinical → Denoise (with t) → Decode.

**Design tradeoffs:**  
- Separate VAEs per modality vs. shared VAE: separate preserve modality‑specific details but increase parameters; shared risks cross‑modal interference.  
- Adaptive fusion choice: cross‑attention (two inputs) is expressive but heavier; projection (one input) is efficient but may lose nuance.  
- GPT‑4o vs. learnable embeddings: GPT‑4o offers rich semantics but introduces API dependency; learnable embeddings are controllable but need more data.  
- Consistency loss weight λ: high λ improves alignment but may overfit; low λ risks drift.

**Failure signatures:**  
- Degraded VAE reconstructions → blurry generated images.  
- Removal of clinical guidance → downstream accuracy collapses to baseline.  
- Mode collapse → low diversity, low PSNR/SSIM across samples.  
- Mismatched fusion type (e.g., projection with two inputs) → quality drop.

**First three experiments to onboard:**  
1. Train each VAE to convergence; evaluate reconstruction loss on a hold‑out set; visualize reconstructions to ensure latent fidelity.  
2. With all modalities present, run the diffusion model (no dropout) to reproduce ground‑truth latent; measure L2 distance between generated Z0 and true Z0 to verify denoiser training.  
3. Perform modality dropout (randomly drop 1 or 2 modalities), generate missing volumes, compute PSNR/SSIM; confirm adaptive fusion selection aligns with expected input count.

## Limitations
- Exact 3D VAE architecture (depth, channels, KL weight) and training regime are unspecified, blocking faithful replication.
- GPT‑4o prompt templates and dimensional alignment with U‑Net features are not detailed, compromising reproducibility of clinical guidance.
- Consistency regularization coefficient λ and its scheduling are omitted, making it difficult to balance reconstruction and generation fidelity.

## Confidence
- **Framework design (ACADiff structure):** High – Core latent diffusion and adaptive fusion logic are clearly described.

- **Ablation (GPT‑4o > learnable embeddings):** Low – Prompt engineering details are absent; the comparison may be unfair without controlled prompt testing.
