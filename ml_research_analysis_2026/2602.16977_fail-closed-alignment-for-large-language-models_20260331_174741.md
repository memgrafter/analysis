# Fail-Closed Alignment for Large Language Models

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2602.16977
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.16977
- **Reference Count:** 35
- **Primary Result:** Reduces attack success rates by 92–97% across models and jailbreaks, achieves highest average compliance rate (86.4%), and preserves utility with 61.6% average accuracy.

## Executive Summary
The paper addresses the fail-open weakness in LLM alignment, where refusal relies on a single dominant directional feature that prompt-based jailbreaks can suppress. It proposes a progressive fail-closed alignment framework: iteratively identify and ablate previously learned refusal directions, forcing the model to reconstruct safety along new, independent subspaces. Evaluated on four models and multiple jailbreak attacks, the method reduces attack success rates by 92–97%, achieves the highest average compliance rate (86.4%), and preserves utility with 61.6% average accuracy, outperforming baselines. The approach works with LoRA fine-tuning (~5% parameters), and mechanistic analysis confirms models learn multiple causally independent refusal directions that jailbreaks cannot suppress all at once, validating the fail-closed principle.

## Method Summary
The method is an iterative training framework. Starting from a base model, each iteration: (1) identifies a new dominant refusal direction using gradient-based optimization (RDO), ensuring linear independence from previously found directions; (2) constructs a Multi-Feature Ablation (MFA) operator that projects out all identified refusal directions from activations; (3) retrains the model on safety data under MFA (to force new refusal mechanisms) and utility data with KL regularization (to preserve benign behavior); (4) selects the checkpoint with lowest attack success rate on validation. This process repeats for a fixed number of iterations, building a set of independent refusal directions that collectively provide robust safety.

## Key Results
- Attack success rates reduced by 92–97% across four models and four jailbreak attacks.
- Highest average compliance rate on benign prompts (86.4%).
- Preserves utility with 61.6% average accuracy on NLP benchmarks.
- Outperforms baselines (e.g., SFT, DeepRefusal) in robustness with minimal utility loss.
- Works with LoRA fine-tuning (~5% parameters), making it efficient.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1: Iterative Direction Identification
Claim: Each iteration finds a new, causally effective refusal direction that is linearly independent. Mechanism: Uses gradient-based RDO to optimize a direction that induces refusal when added to benign activations and prevents refusal when ablated from harmful ones; projects onto orthogonal complement of previous directions. Core assumption: Refusal is mediated by linear features. Evidence: Abstract and section 3.1; Figure 3 shows ASR increases only 1% after ablating all 10 directions, indicating independent contribution. Break condition: Direction residual norm after projection too small (<1e-5) indicates dependence.

### Mechanism 2: Multi-Feature Ablation Forcing
Claim: MFA operator prevents reuse of any old direction, forcing novel refusal pathways. Mechanism: At iteration k, builds orthonormal basis Q_k from all previous directions; applies MF_A^k(h) = h - Q_k Q_k^T h to all activations on harmful prompts during training. Core assumption: Suppressing the entire subspace of known directions creates pressure for new mechanisms. Evidence: Section 3.2, 4.4; Figure 3 shows MFA reduces ASR to 1% vs. 97% for single-feature ablation. Break condition: Imperfect orthogonality in Q_k could leave residual exploitable components.

### Mechanism 3: Distributed Robustness via Utility-Regularized Training
Claim: Training under MFA with KL regularization yields models with refusal mechanisms distributed across multiple independent features, robust to suppression of any single direction. Mechanism: Objective L_train = L_safe (NLL under MFA) + λ·L_util (KL to base model). Balances new refusal learning with utility preservation. Core assumption: Model can learn orthogonal refusal mechanisms while maintaining capability. Evidence: Section 3.3, 4.2; Table 1 shows low ASR with high CR/Acc; Figure 4 shows no single direction uniformly suppressed by all jailbreaks. Break condition: λ mis-calibration causes over/under-refusal or weak new mechanisms.

## Foundational Learning
- **Directional Ablation in Activation Space:** Needed to causally test if a linear feature mediates refusal; ablating it should prevent refusal on harmful prompts. Quick check: What distinguishes DIM from RDO for estimating refusal directions? DIM uses mean difference; RDO uses gradient optimization for stronger causal effect.
- **Multi-Feature Redundancy (Fail-Closed Principle):** Ensures safety persists even if some components fail. Quick check: Why doesn't having multiple refusal directions automatically confer fail-closed robustness? Because jailbreaks might suppress all known directions if they are not truly independent; redundancy requires independent subspaces.
- **KL Regularization for Utility Preservation:** Prevents ablation training from destroying benign capabilities by keeping output distributions close to the base model. Quick check: Why use base-model-generated responses for XSTest when training Llama2-7B? To match the distribution of the model being fine-tuned and avoid style artifacts from an external model.

## Architecture Onboarding
### Component Map
Direction Identifier (RDO) -> MFA Operator Builder -> Training Loop -> Model Selector

### Critical Path
Initialize model θ and empty set R. For k=1 to K: (1) Run RDO to find new direction r_k; (2) Add r_k to R; (3) Build MFA operator MF_A^k from R via QR; (4) Fine-tune θ on safety data with MF_A^k and utility data without, using objective L_safe + λ·L_util; (5) Evaluate checkpoint on validation harmful prompts; (6) Select checkpoint with lowest average ASR.

### Design Tradeoffs
- Efficacy vs. Compute: MFA requires separate safety/utility batches (2x passes) but avoids costly latent optimization of methods like CAT/LAT.
- Safety vs. Over-refusal: λ balances new refusal learning (lower λ) and utility preservation (higher λ); λ=1 found optimal.
- Direction Quality: Gradient-based RDO vs. activation-based DIM: RDO yields better ASR reduction but needs careful setup.

### Failure Signatures
- High ASR despite MFA: Indicates MFA operator incomplete (check Q_k orthogonality) or r_k not independent.
- High Over-refusal (low CR): Suggests λ too low or model learned trivial refusal; check L_util implementation and data.
- Low Feature Activations: Later directions (e.g., r6–r10) show near-zero similarity with harmful prompts, hinting at non-linear mechanisms not captured.

### First 3 Experiments
1. Causal Effect Validation: For a trained checkpoint, ablate all {r1,...,r_i} and measure ASR; add each r_i to benign prompts and measure CR. Expect: ASR low even after full ablation, and each r_i reduces CR when added.
2. Jailbreak Transfer Test: Apply GCG, AutoDAN, PAIR, HumanJailbreaks (generated on base model) to fail-closed model. Expect: ASR <5% across attacks; no single r_i suppressed by all attacks (>50% suppression).
3. Neuron-Level Robustness Check: Identify safety neurons via Wanda scores; ablate them and measure ASR/Acc. Expect: Fine-tuned model shows steeper trade-off (higher ASR for same Acc. drop) than base model.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
1. **Non-linear refusal mechanisms:** Can methods capture non-linear pathways that appear in later iterations but aren't found by linear direction extraction?
2. **Impact on internal representations:** Does MFA alter computations outside refusal circuits? Could it disrupt correlated utility representations?
3. **Scaling to larger models:** How many independent directions needed for >70B models? Does the redundancy principle hold uniformly?
4. **Multiplicity vs. ablation specificity:** Is improvement due to multiple directions or the MFA operator's structure? Could a single robust direction suffice?
5. **Dataset dependence:** How does data quality/diversity affect results? Can dynamic adversarial data synthesis improve robustness?
6. **Unified mechanistic map:** How do multiple directions interact with safety neurons? Can we construct a unified theory of fail-closed safety?

## Limitations
- Linear direction assumption may fail after several iterations; later mechanisms may be non-linear.
- Hyperparameter λ requires careful per-model tuning; fixed values may not generalize.
- Evaluation limited to 2–9B parameter models and four jailbreak attacks; robustness to unseen threats unproven.

## Confidence
- **Mechanism 1 (Iterative Direction ID):** Medium. RDO works initially but later directions may capture non-linear mechanisms.
- **Mechanism 2 (MFA Forcing):** High. Ablation comparisons strongly validate MFA's role.
- **Mechanism 3 (Distributed Robustness):** Medium. No single direction uniformly suppressed, but simultaneous suppression of >10 directions not tested.

## Next Checks
1. **Non-linear probing:** Challenge later iterations with non-linear activation transformations (e.g., layer-wise jitter) to test linearity assumptions.
2. **Hyperparameter transfer:** Apply fixed λ and LoRA settings from Llama3/Gemma2 to a new architecture (e.g., Mistral) and measure performance drop.
3. **Multi-direction attack:** Optimize a prompt to suppress the span of {r₁,…,r₅} simultaneously; if ASR rises above 10%, fail-closed guarantee weakens.
