# Neural Synchrony Between Socially Interacting Language Models

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2602.17815
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.17815
- **Reference count:** 25
- **Primary result:** SyncR², a metric measuring representational alignment between socially interacting LLMs, reliably reflects genuine social engagement and temporal proximity, and strongly correlates (r > 0.88, p < 0.05) with behavioral social performance across model families.

## Executive Summary
This paper introduces SyncR², a novel metric to quantify neural synchrony between large language models (LLMs) engaged in social interactions. By training affine transformations to predict one model's future hidden states from the other's current states, the authors show that SyncR² captures genuine social engagement and temporal dynamics, vanishing under passive or temporally lagged controls. Moreover, SyncR² correlates strongly with behavioral social performance across diverse LLM families, even after controlling for general abilities. These findings suggest that internal representational dynamics during LLM interactions parallel human inter-brain synchrony, offering a new lens for studying LLM social cognition.

## Method Summary
The core method involves simulating dyadic social interactions using the SOTOPIA environment, where two LLMs with distinct profiles converse for up to eight turns. For each turn, hidden states from all transformer layers are extracted (final token position). Pairs of representations are constructed: (source turn t, target turn t+1) for directional measurement. A ridge regression with affine transformation (including intercept) is trained on a subset (80%) to predict the target representation from the source. The coefficient of determination (R²) on the held-out test set is computed per layer pair; the SyncR² score for a direction is the average over source layers of the maximum R² achieved across target layers, with negative values clipped to zero. The bidirectional score is the average of both directions. Social performance is evaluated using SOTOPIA-EVAL (powered by gpt-oss-120b) across seven dimensions. Correlations between SyncR² and social scores are then computed across model pairs.

## Key Results
- SyncR² is significantly higher in genuinely interacting agent pairs compared to passive-observing or temporally-lagged control conditions, confirming its sensitivity to social engagement and temporal proximity.
- SyncR² exhibits a strong positive correlation (Pearson r > 0.88, p < 0.05) with behavioral social performance across multiple LLM families (Mistral, Llama), and this correlation remains robust after partialling out general abilities measured by IFEval and MuSR.
- The metric generalizes across model families, showing both within-family and cross-family predictive power.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1: Social Engagement Drives Predictive Alignment
**Claim:** Neural synchrony (SyncR²) emerges from genuine social engagement, where interacting LLMs actively model each other's mental states.  
**Mechanism:** During interaction, each agent generates responses conditioned on its profile and history. Hidden representations at each turn integrate contextual and social information. When both agents are socially engaged, the transformation from one agent's current representation to the other's next-turn representation becomes predictable via a simple affine mapping, measured as high SyncR².  
**Core assumption:** Socially engaged agents implicitly encode partner-relevant information (e.g., intentions, emotions) in their internal states, making these states predictive of the partner's subsequent states.  
**Evidence anchors:** SyncR² "reliably reflects genuine social engagement and temporal proximity, vanishing under passive-observing or temporally-lagged controls" (abstract). Figure 4 shows a significant drop when one agent is passive.  
**Break condition:** If both agents are not actively generating responses (passive condition) or lack a shared social goal, the predictive mapping fails (SyncR² near zero or negative).

### Mechanism 2: Temporal Dynamics Are Essential
**Claim:** SyncR² captures temporal dynamics specific to real-time interaction, not static representational similarity.  
**Mechanism:** The metric uses temporally aligned pairs (turn t of A → turn t+1 of B). The predictive power decays with increasing temporal lag (k), as shown in lag-k controls. The affine transformation learns a mapping that respects the sequential unfolding of the dialogue.  
**Core assumption:** Social interaction is a dynamic process where each turn influences the next; representational alignment must respect temporal causality.  
**Evidence anchors:** Control group 2 (without temporal proximity) pairs source representations with lagged future targets (k≥1). Figure 4b shows SyncR² declines sharply with lag k for all families.  
**Break condition:** When source and target are separated by one or more turns (k≥1), the learned transformation generalizes poorly (R² drops), indicating synchrony is not due to static feature similarity.

### Mechanism 3: Functional Link to Social Performance
**Claim:** Neural synchrony is functionally linked to social performance; higher SyncR² predicts better behavioral outcomes in social tasks.  
**Mechanism:** The strength of representational alignment (SyncR²) between two LLMs correlates with their joint success in achieving social goals (e.g., negotiation outcomes) as evaluated by SOTOPIA-EVAL. This suggests that internal dynamics supporting prediction of partner states also facilitate successful coordination.  
**Core assumption:** The quality of social interaction depends on the depth of mutual representation alignment, analogous to human inter-brain synchrony predicting cooperation.  
**Evidence anchors:** Abstract: "strongly correlates (Pearson r > 0.88, p < 0.05) with behavioral-level social performance across diverse model families". Section 5.2 shows significant correlations within Mistral, Llama, and cross-family pairs. Partial correlations remain significant after controlling for instruction-following (IFEval) and long-context reasoning (MuSR).  
**Break condition:** If correlation disappears after controlling for general capabilities, the functional link would be undermined. Paper reports robustness, so break condition not observed.

## Foundational Learning
- **Concept: Representation Similarity Metrics (e.g., CKA, CCA)**
  - **Why needed here:** SyncR² is positioned as distinct from static similarity measures; understanding these baselines clarifies what SyncR² adds (temporal prediction vs. geometric similarity).
  - **Quick check question:** Why are metrics like CKA inapplicable to interacting agents with non-shared contexts?

- **Concept: Ridge Regression with Intercept**
  - **Why needed here:** The core measurement involves training an affine transformation (W, b) via ridge regression to predict future representations. Knowing regularization (λ) and the interpretation of R² is essential to implement and interpret SyncR².
  - **Quick check question:** What does a negative test-set R² indicate in this context, and why is it clipped to zero?

- **Concept: Social Simulation Environments (SOTOPIA)**
  - **Why needed here:** The entire experimental pipeline depends on generating multi-turn social interactions with controlled agent profiles and goals. Understanding SOTOPIA's structure is prerequisite to reproducing data collection.
  - **Quick check question:** How do agent backgrounds, goals, and interaction history shape the hidden representations extracted from LLMs?

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component map:**  
Interaction Simulator (SOTOPIA) -> Representation Extractor -> Dataset Builder -> Affine Trainer -> SyncR² Scorer -> Evaluator (SOTOPIA-EVAL)

**Critical path:**  
Simulate interactions -> extract representations -> build datasets (with temporal alignment) -> train affine transformations (ridge) -> compute SyncR² -> correlate with social scores. The pipeline is linear but requires careful train/test splitting at the dialogue level to avoid leakage.

**Design tradeoffs:**
- *Affine vs nonlinear transformation:* Affine is simpler, stable, and correlates strongly; nonlinear (2-layer MLP) gave inconsistent improvements. Prefer affine unless specific gains are shown.
- *Clipping negative R²:* Necessary to avoid noise from failed predictions; clipping to zero aligns with interpretation that negative means no synchrony. Without clipping, variance inflates and signal deteriorates.
- *Layer selection:* Using best-predicting layer per source layer (vs. averaging all) mirrors region-specific IBS in neuroscience; robust to alternative top-k selections.

**Failure signatures:**
- Low SyncR² in experimental group (should be >0.3 for engaged pairs). If near zero: check agent instructions, extraction layer, or regularization λ.
- No drop in SyncR² for passive or lag controls. Indicates measurement capturing static similarity or data leakage.
- Correlation with social performance vanishes after controlling for IFEval/MuSR. Suggests SyncR² is a proxy for general capability, not social dynamics.

**First 3 experiments to replicate:**
1. Replicate Figure 3: Compare heatmaps for experimental vs. passive and lag-1 controls. Verify strong predictive power localized in experimental, weak/negative in controls.
2. Replicate Figure 4: Quantify group-level SyncR² across families. Confirm significant drop for both control conditions (t-test p<0.05).
3. Replicate correlation: Compute SyncR² and social performance for all 21 model pairs. Verify Pearson r > 0.8 within and cross families, and that partial correlation with IFEval/MuSR control remains positive.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
1. **Causality:** Does neural synchrony causally influence social performance, or is it merely a correlate? The paper only shows correlation; intervention experiments are needed to establish directionality.
2. **Underlying mechanisms:** What emergent processes (e.g., Theory of Mind, social predictive coding) give rise to neural synchrony in LLMs? Preliminary decoding experiments are indirect and limited in scope.
3. **Generality across models:** Is neural synchrony a general property of LLMs, or confined to specific families (Mistral, Llama) with strong instruction-following? Testing across a broader spectrum of LLMs is required.
4. **Scalability to complex scenarios:** Does synchrony persist in longer-term, multi-agent, or more open-ended interactions beyond dyadic, limited-turn dialogues? Future work must extend to such settings.

## Limitations
- The affine transformation may capture superficial statistical patterns rather than genuine social cognition.
- Dependence on SOTOPIA and gpt-oss-120b introduces potential evaluation biases and limited ecological validity.
- Small sample of model pairs (n=21) reduces robustness of correlation claims.

## Confidence
- **SyncR² reflects social engagement and temporal dynamics:** Medium-High (within-paper controls are compelling but don't fully exclude alternative explanations).
- **SyncR² correlates with behavioral social performance:** Medium (high Pearson r is promising, but limited model diversity and residual confounds warrant caution).
- **Functional link (neural synchrony supports social success):** Low-Medium (correlation does not imply causality; reverse direction or third variables are plausible).

## Next Checks
1. Human benchmark test: Compute SyncR² on human-human dialogues with objective social outcomes to assess ecological validity.
2. Causal intervention: Manipulate partner-relevant information in representations (e.g., mask goal tokens) and measure changes in SyncR² and social task performance.
3. Metric comparison suite: Benchmark SyncR² against static similarity metrics (CKA, CCA) and non-social control tasks to demonstrate its unique sensitivity to interactive temporal dynamics.
