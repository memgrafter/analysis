# Uncertainty-Aware Vision-Language Segmentation for Medical Imaging

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2602.14498
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.14498
- Reference count: 40
- Primary result: Proposes an uncertainty-aware vision-language segmentation model that achieves state-of-the-art Dice scores (up to 93.83%) on three medical datasets with high computational efficiency (39.9M params).

## Executive Summary
This paper introduces a novel vision-language segmentation framework for medical imaging that explicitly models uncertainty to improve reliability in ambiguous scenarios. The approach combines a Modality Decoding Attention Block (MoDAB) for efficient cross-modal fusion with a Spectral-Entropic Uncertainty (SEU) loss that integrates spectral consistency and entropy regularization. Evaluated on QaTa-COV19, MosMed++, and Kvasir-SEG, the method achieves state-of-the-art segmentation performance while being the most computationally efficient among compared models. However, external validation and clinical assessment remain limited.

## Method Summary
The method addresses the challenge of reliable segmentation when medical images are ambiguous (e.g., low contrast, noisy) by incorporating uncertainty into a vision-language framework. It proposes two key components: (1) MoDAB, which integrates self-attention, cross-attention, and a State Space Mixer (SSMix) to fuse image and text features efficiently and capture long-range dependencies; and (2) SEU loss, which combines Dice overlap, Fourier spectral consistency, and entropy-based uncertainty regularization to produce segmentations that are both accurate and uncertainty-aware. Ablations confirm the contribution of each component.

## Key Results
- SOTA segmentation: Dice 92.24% (QaTa-COV19), 79.67% (MosMed++), 93.83% (Kvasir-SEG); mIoU 84.9%, 66.38%, 87.62%.
- High efficiency: 39.9 million parameters and 17.87 GFLOPs, the most efficient among SOTA comparators.
- Improved robustness in poor image quality scenarios, as demonstrated by ablation studies.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
MoDAB enables efficient cross-modal fusion by leveraging self-attention for intra-modal features, cross-attention for inter-modal alignment, and SSMix for capturing long-range dependencies with linear complexity, thus balancing performance and efficiency. SEU loss regularizes the segmentation by enforcing spectral consistency (via Fourier transforms) to preserve structural patterns and using entropy to identify ambiguous regions, guiding the model to focus on high-certainty areas. This dual regularization makes the model more reliable under ambiguity.

## Architecture Onboarding
Component map: Image Encoder -> MoDAB -> Decoder -> Segmentation Output; Text Encoder -> MoDAB.
Critical path: Image and text features extracted by encoders; MoDAB fuses features via self/cross-attention and SSMix; decoder predicts mask; SEU loss computed from prediction and Fourier spectrum of mask.
Design tradeoffs: MoDAB balances fusion richness vs. complexity by using lightweight SSMix; SEU loss adds spectral computation overhead but improves robustness. Efficiency prioritized via parameter count (39.9M) and FLOPs (17.87).
Failure signatures: 1) High entropy in predictions indicating ambiguity; 2) Spectral divergence between predicted and expected patterns; 3) Inconsistent cross-modal alignment in attention maps.
First experiments: 1) Ablate MoDAB components (remove SSMix, use only cross-attention) on QaTa-COV19; 2) Replace SEU loss with standard Dice loss and compare uncertainty calibration; 3) Visualize attention maps to verify cross-modal alignment on ambiguous samples.

## Limitations
- Validation limited to three datasets; generalizability to other medical modalities and imaging protocols is unclear.
- No clinical evaluation of reliability in real-world ambiguous cases (e.g., radiologist assessment).
- SEU loss’s spectral regularization may be sensitive to noise characteristics not represented in the test datasets.

## Confidence
- Performance (Dice/mIoU): Medium (strong results but unreplicated and limited datasets).
- Efficiency (params/FLOPs): High (concrete, measurable improvements).
- Reliability under ambiguity: Low (no out-of-distribution or clinical validation).
