# DeepFact: Co-Evolving Benchmarks and Agents for Deep Research Factuality

## Quick Facts
- **ArXiv ID**: 2603.05912  
- **Source URL**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.05912  
- **Reference count**: 40  
- **Primary result**: DeepFact-Eval achieves 83.4% accuracy on DeepFact‑Bench (+14.3 to +27.5 points over baselines) and Audit‑then‑Score raises expert micro‑gold accuracy to 90.9%.

## Executive Summary
Deep Research Reports (DRRs) demand nuanced factuality verification beyond atomic claims. Existing static benchmarks are brittle: PhD‑level experts hit only 60.8% accuracy on a hidden micro‑gold set when labeling independently. This paper introduces Audit‑then‑Score (AtS), a protocol that continuously refines ground truth through human–AI auditing—a challenger agent submits evidence for disagreements, an auditor adjudicates, and accepted updates revise the benchmark before final scoring. Over four AtS rounds, expert micro‑gold accuracy climbs to 90.9%, proving experts are far more reliable as auditors than as one‑shot labelers. Instantiating AtS yields DeepFact‑Bench (versioned, auditable) and DeepFact‑Eval, a multi‑step verification agent. DeepFact‑Eval closes the gap between snippet‑based checkers and deep research verification, beating baselines by up to 27.5 points and transferring well externally. Co‑evolving benchmarks and agents is essential as AI approaches expert‑level performance, and many residual disagreements on static benchmarks stem from annotation noise rather than model error.

## Method Summary
The task is claim‑level factuality verification for DRRs, where each claim is a verbatim sentence and the context is the full report. Labels are Supported, Inconclusive, or Contradictory (often merged into Supported vs. Unsupported). The primary metric is accuracy on a test set. DeepFact‑Eval is a zero‑shot agentic pipeline: (1) extract claim context from the report; (2) breadth‑oriented query planning; (3) document search and summarization (using Google Search and GPT‑4.1 mini); (4) depth‑oriented detail questioning to extract claim‑critical nuances; (5) iterate until evidence suffices, then output verdict and rationale. AtS orchestrates benchmark evolution: a challenger model runs on the current benchmark, disagreements trigger auditor adjudication (human or trusted agent), accepted revisions update the versioned dataset, and the challenger is scored on the refined benchmark. This co‑evolutionary loop progressively improves ground truth and verifier capabilities.

## Key Results
- AtS raises expert micro‑gold accuracy from 60.8% to 90.9% across four rounds, showing that experts are more reliable when auditing rationales than when labeling independently.
- DeepFact‑Eval achieves 83.4% accuracy on DeepFact‑Bench, gaining +14.3 to +27.5 points over existing fact‑checkers, and transfers effectively to external datasets.
- The co‑evolution of benchmarks and agents reveals that many persistent disagreements on static benchmarks are due to annotation noise rather than model error.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1: Audit‑then‑Score (AtS) Protocol
- **Claim**: AtS overcomes static benchmark brittleness by evolving ground truth through human–AI auditing, raising expert micro‑gold accuracy from 60.8% to 90.9% across rounds.
- **Mechanism**: A challenger model that disagrees with the current benchmark label submits an evidence‑backed proposal; an auditor (human or trusted agent) adjudicates by comparing rationales; accepted updates revise the benchmark; the challenger is then scored against the refined consensus.
- **Core assumption**: Experts are more reliable when auditing evidence and rationales than when labeling independently, because they can identify superior arguments even if their initial one‑shot labels are noisy.
- **Evidence anchors**: Section 5.1 provides a formal definition, workflow (Figure 1), and algorithm (Algorithm 1); Section 6.2 and Figure 3 show monotonic micro‑gold improvement across four rounds.
- **Break condition**: If auditors lack domain depth, are swayed by challenger rhetoric, or if auditing cost explodes (e.g., conflict set does not shrink), evolution may stall or degrade label quality.

### Mechanism 2: DeepFact‑Eval’s Breadth‑Depth Verification
- **Claim**: DeepFact‑Eval closes the performance gap between snippet‑based checkers and deep research verification by combining breadth‑oriented query planning (coverage) with depth‑oriented detail questioning (precision).
- **Mechanism**: Given a claim and report, it first formulates diverse search queries to retrieve a broad document set, summarizes each, then generates targeted follow‑up questions to extract claim‑critical details; this iterative process continues until evidence suffices, then yields a verdict and rationale.
- **Core assumption**: DRR evidence is distributed across documents; verification requires full‑document reasoning and nuanced detail extraction, not snippet‑level entailment.
- **Evidence anchors**: The abstract highlights the design and gains; Section 5.2 and Figure 2 detail the workflow and contrast with traditional pipelines; experiments show large improvements over snippet‑based methods.
- **Break condition**: Poor retrieval (missing key documents) or weak depth questioning (failing to surface decisive details) leads to low recall/precision; computational cost also limits scalability.

### Mechanism 3: Co‑evolutionary Benchmark‑Agent Loop
- **Claim**: Co‑evolving benchmarks and agents yields progressively more reliable ground truth; as verifiers improve, they expose benchmark flaws, and audits incorporate stronger rationales, raising micro‑gold accuracy to 90.9%.
- **Mechanism**: Each AtS round: a new challenger audits current consensus; accepted updates refine the benchmark; scoring occurs on the refined version. Over rounds, both benchmark and agents improve in a feedback loop.
- **Core assumption**: Auditors can recognize superior evidence even from fallible challengers, and consensus converges toward a mutually reinforcing high‑quality standard.
- **Evidence anchors**: Abstract emphasizes co‑evolution’s importance; Section 6.2 and Figure 3 show monotonic micro‑gold improvement with human auditors; Section 6.3 demonstrates that agent‑only auditing also yields gains, consolidating evidence.
- **Break condition**: Evolution may drift toward verifier‑specific biases without periodic human recalibration; stagnation occurs if challengers fail to introduce novel evidence.

## Architecture Onboarding

### Component Map
DeepFact‑Bench → AtS Engine → DeepFact‑Eval → Predictions; AtS Engine ↔ Auditor (human or agent); Utilities: micro‑gold generator, importance‑risk sampler, annotation UI.

### Critical Path
1. Load current benchmark version \( B_t \).
2. Run challenger \( M \) on all claims; record outputs and identify disagreements \( (\hat{y}_i \neq y_i^{(t)}) \).
3. For each disagreement, call auditor \( A \) to compare rationales; if accepted, add update to \( \Delta B \).
4. Update benchmark: \( B_{t+1} = B_t \oplus \Delta B \).
5. Compute score\( (M) = \text{accuracy}(\hat{Y}, Y^{(t+1)}) \).

### Design Tradeoffs
- **Audit frequency**: Full auditing maximizes accuracy but costs more; sampling (p=0.75) gives near‑optimal gains with lower cost.
- **Revision strictness**: Requiring dual (human+agent) approval filters noise but may reject beneficial updates.
- **Grouped verification**: Batches semantically related claims to cut cost (DeepFact‑Eval‑lite) with minor accuracy drop.
- **Versioning**: Frozen snapshots ensure reproducibility but require managing multiple benchmark states.

### Failure Signatures
- Micro‑gold accuracy plateaus or drops across rounds → auditors not improving consensus.
- High auditor rejection rate of challenger proposals → either challenger is poor or auditors are overly conservative.
- Cost per round does not decline as conflicts shrink → inefficient auditing or conflict set not reducing.
- Label distribution drifts dramatically (>5% verdict changes) without micro‑gold gain → requires human re‑calibration.
- DeepFact‑Eval recall low → breadth queries failing to retrieve key documents.
- DeepFact‑Eval precision low → depth questions missing nuances or misinterpreting evidence.

### First 3 Experiments
1. Baseline comparison: Run a snippet‑based checker (SAFE) on DeepFact‑Bench to reproduce the >25‑point gap.
2. AtS microsimulation: Use a weak challenger (e.g., random) and human auditors to verify micro‑gold improvement across two rounds.
3. Ablate DeepFact‑Eval: Disable breadth planning (single query) and/or depth questioning to isolate their contributions on a subset.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
1. **Handling literature gaps**: How can verifiers for DRRs handle claims where scientific literature is silent, contradictory, or non‑existent, requiring empirical validation beyond existing sources?  
   *Basis*: Explicitly stated in Limitations.  
   *Why unresolved*: DeepFact‑Eval operates solely on retrieved existing literature; no mechanism for generating new evidence.  
   *Resolving evidence*: Develop and evaluate an extended verifier that can propose and interpret synthetic experiments or simulations when evidence is lacking.

2. **Agent auditors replacing humans**: Can agent auditors fully replace human experts in AtS without degrading benchmark reliability, especially as verifier capabilities grow?  
   *Basis*: Inferred from Section 6.3; protocol still relies on human auditors in experiments, though agent auditors show non‑regressive consolidation.  
   *Why unresolved*: No full AtS cycle with agent‑only adjudication; risk of accumulating biases (e.g., retrieval preferences, reasoning patterns).  
   *Resolving evidence*: Run complete AtS with agent‑only auditors across many rounds; compare micro‑gold accuracy, stability, evidence diversity, and sensitivity to agent architecture.

3. **Long‑term effects of continuous benchmark evolution**: What are the long‑term effects of AtS on model generalizability and evaluation fairness, particularly regarding overfitting to audit patterns or version‑specific artifacts?  
   *Basis*: Inferred from Section H (policies to manage drift) but no analysis of model performance across versions.  
   *Why unresolved*: No tracking of model accuracy across benchmark versions; risk that models adapt to audit‑specific shortcuts rather than true factuality.  
   *Resolving evidence*: Evaluate a model (e.g., DeepFact‑Eval) across multiple historical versions; analyze stability and version‑specific error patterns; test training on outputs from specific audit rounds and test on later versions.

4. **Scaling AtS to high‑specialization domains**: How does AtS scale to domains with even higher specialization or lower expert availability than academic DRRs (e.g., legal or medical fact‑checking)?  
   *Basis*: Inferred from paper noting domain drift and fragmentation; AtS reduces workload but still requires scarce hyper‑specialists.  
   *Why unresolved*: AtS tested only in specific academic domains; untested in high‑stakes, ambiguous, or ethically constrained settings.  
   *Resolving evidence*: Pilot AtS in a new high‑stakes domain (e.g., clinical guidelines verification); measure micro‑gold evolution, expert time, and cost‑effectiveness; compare to static labeling baselines.

## Limitations
- Exact prompts, search parameters, and hyperparameters for DeepFact‑Eval are undisclosed, hindering exact replication.
- AtS protocol details (adjudication thresholds, sampling rates, cost scaling) are vague; break conditions (auditor bias, conflict set stagnation) are not empirically evaluated.
- Micro‑gold set construction and adversarial modification methods are insufficiently documented, raising concerns about gold standard integrity.

## Confidence
- **AtS improves micro‑gold accuracy (60.8% → 90.9%)**: **High** confidence (monotonic improvement across four rounds with human auditors).
- **DeepFact‑Eval outperforms baselines (+14.3–27.5 points)**: **Medium** confidence (internal evaluation on evolving benchmark; key implementation details missing).
- **Co‑evolution of benchmarks and agents is essential**: **Medium** confidence (supported by AtS results, but long‑term dynamics and alternative designs not explored).
