# Tiny Inference-Time Scaling with Latent Verifiers

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2603.22492
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.22492
- **Reference count:** 40
- **Primary result:** VHS reduces joint generation-and-verification time by 63.3%, FLOPs by 51%, and VRAM by 14.5% versus a standard MLLM verifier under a matched wall-clock budget, while improving GenEval overall accuracy by +2.7%.

## Executive Summary
This paper introduces Verifier on Hidden States (VHS), a method to drastically reduce the computational overhead of using Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs) as verifiers in inference-time scaling for image generation. VHS bypasses the expensive decode-to-pixels and re-encode steps by directly tapping into intermediate hidden representations of a Diffusion Transformer (DiT) generator. A lightweight connector projects these latent features into the LLM's input space, enabling verification with far lower latency, compute, and memory. Under equal time budgets, VHS not only slashes resource use but also yields a measurable quality gain on the GenEval benchmark.

## Method Summary
VHS employs a two-stage training pipeline. First, a randomly initialized MLP connector is trained to align DiT hidden states (extracted from a specific intermediate layer ℓ*) with the input space of a frozen LLM verifier, using image-caption pairs from the LLaVA dataset (recaptioned by Gemma3-4B). Second, the connector and the LLM are fine-tuned jointly with a weighted cross-entropy loss on a synthetic binary classification dataset generated by the Reflect-DiT pipeline. During inference, for each candidate, the DiT forward pass is truncated at ℓ* to obtain hidden states, which are normalized, projected by the connector, and fed to the LLM along with the prompt to produce a verification score. Only the top-scoring candidate undergoes the full DiT forward pass and final decoding to pixels.

## Key Results
- Cuts joint generation-and-verification latency by 63.3%, FLOPs by 51%, and VRAM usage by 14.5% compared to an MLLM verifier using a pixel-space visual encoder, under matched wall-clock budgets.
- Improves overall GenEval accuracy by +2.7% relative to the MLLM baseline at the same inference-time budget.
- Demonstrates consistent gains across GenEval categories, with notable improvements in counting and position tasks.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- **Claim:** VHS reduces computational cost by eliminating the decode-encode overhead inherent in standard MLLM verifiers.
- **Mechanism:** The standard pipeline decodes generator latent \( z_0 \) to pixels \( x_0 = D(z_0) \) and re-encodes with a visual encoder \( V(x_0) \). VHS instead directly uses intermediate DiT hidden states \( h_{\ell^*} \), projecting them into LLM space via a connector \( C \). This removes the need for the autoencoder decoder \( D \) and external visual encoder \( V \) during verification.
- **Core assumption:** The semantic information required for verification is already sufficiently encoded in the DiT's intermediate hidden states, making explicit pixel reconstruction and separate visual encoding redundant.
- **Evidence anchors:** Abstract and Sec. 3.2 formally define the pipeline change; Table 1 provides direct latency measurements supporting the claim.

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim:** Selecting an appropriate intermediate DiT layer \( \ell^* \) balances semantic richness against computational savings, enabling more candidate exploration within a fixed time budget.
- **Mechanism:** Deeper layers (\( \ell^* \) near output) contain more mature semantics but are costlier to extract and offer less truncation benefit. Shallower layers are cheaper but noisier. VHS truncates the DiT forward pass at \( \ell^* \) for non-selected candidates, skipping remaining layers.
- **Core assumption:** There exists an optimal "sweet spot" in DiT depth where features are semantically expressive enough for verification yet early enough to yield significant computation savings from layer truncation.
- **Evidence anchors:** Sec. 4.5 (Table 3) shows \( h_7 \) (mid-depth) outperforms both shallower and deeper layers under a fixed 1100 ms budget, confirming a non-monotonic trade-off.

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim:** A weighted cross-entropy loss during verifier fine-tuning mitigates label imbalance in the synthetic training data, improving calibration on underrepresented "incorrect" samples.
- **Mechanism:** The synthetic verifier training set is skewed toward "correct" (Yes) samples (~63%). Standard cross-entropy under-weights the minority "incorrect" (No) class, causing bias. Weighted XE assigns higher loss weight to the minority class, forcing the model to learn discriminative features for both classes.
- **Core assumption:** The imbalance in synthetic verifier data harms verifier performance, and cost-sensitive learning via loss weighting can counteract this without data collection changes.
- **Evidence anchors:** Sec. 3.3 describes the imbalance and weighting; Sec. 4.5 (Table 3) shows weighted XE yields a +4.2% GenEval improvement over standard XE.

## Foundational Learning
- **Latent Space Alignment in Generative Models**
  - *Why needed here:* VHS relies on the premise that the generator's latent space (e.g., Stable Diffusion VAE latents) is a semantically meaningful, compressed representation. Understanding this space is key to seeing why decoding to pixels and re-encoding may be redundant.
  - *Quick check question:* In a latent diffusion model, what is the relationship between the autoencoder's latent space and the pixel space it reconstructs? (Answer: The encoder maps pixels to a lower-dimensional latent; the decoder maps latents back to pixels. The latent is learned to be a semantically rich, compressed representation.)

- **Inference-Time Scaling (Test-Time Compute)**
  - *Why needed here:* VHS exists within the Best-of-N scaling paradigm, where additional test-time compute (generating multiple candidates and verifying them) trades off for improved output quality. Understanding the two primary costs (generation and verification) is essential to evaluate VHS's efficiency gains.
  - *Quick check question:* In Best-of-N scaling, what are the two primary costs that determine the feasibility of a verifier? (Answer: The cost of generating N candidates and the per-candidate cost of running the verifier.)

- **Diffusion Transformer (DiT) Architecture**
  - *Why needed here:* VHS is designed for DiT-based single-step generators. Knowing that DiTs process noisy latents through transformer layers, with intermediate activations (\( h_\ell \)) representing progressively refined features, explains where and why VHS taps into the network.
  - *Quick check question:* In a DiT, what does the sequence of hidden states \( \{h_\ell\} \) represent as \( \ell \) increases from 0 to L-1? (Answer: progressively more refined, semantically coherent representations of the image, moving from noisy latent toward the final denoised latent \( z_0 \).)

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map:**  
  DiT Generator (SANA-Sprint/PixArt-α-DMD) -> Connector (two-layer MLP with GELU) -> LLM Verifier (Qwen2.5-0.5B)  
  Text prompt is concatenated with projected visual tokens before LLM.
- **Critical path:**  
  1. Receive prompt \( p \).  
  2. For each candidate: sample noise \( z_T \), run DiT forward **only up to layer \( \ell^* \)** to get \( h_{\ell^*} \).  
  3. Normalize \( h_{\ell^*} \), project via connector \( C \).  
  4. Concatenate projected visual tokens with prompt tokens.  
  5. Run LLM forward pass, extract probability for "yes" token as score.  
  6. Select candidate with highest score.  
  7. For selected candidate: run full DiT forward (remaining layers), decode \( z_0 \to x_0 \) via AE decoder.
- **Design tradeoffs:**  
  - *Layer depth \( \ell^* \):* Shallower → faster extraction & truncation but weaker semantics; deeper → better semantics but higher cost and less truncation benefit. Optimal is typically mid-depth (e.g., \( h_7 \) for 20-layer SANA-Sprint).  
  - *LLM size:* Larger LLMs offer stronger reasoning but higher latency; under fixed wall-clock budget, visual feature quality often becomes the bottleneck, so smaller LLMs can be preferable.  
  - *Loss function:* Standard cross-entropy underperforms due to synthetic data imbalance; weighted or focal loss is critical for good calibration.
- **Failure signatures:**  
  - Poor GenEval scores in complex categories (counting, position, attribute binding) despite time savings, indicating \( h_{\ell^*} \) lacks required semantic granularity.  
  - Latency reduction not realized due to inefficient implementation (e.g., failing to skip remaining DiT layers for non-selected candidates).  
  - Verifier overfitting to synthetic data style, causing poor generalization to real prompts if training distribution diverges.
- **First 3 experiments:**  
  1. **Baseline Latency Benchmark:** Measure end-to-end time for Best-of-N (N=2,4,6) using MLLM w/ CLIP, MLLM w/ AE, and VHS (various \( h_\ell \)). Verify the ~63% time saving claim.  
  2. **GenEval Performance under Matched Budget:** For fixed time budgets (550ms, 1100ms, 1650ms), compare overall and category-wise GenEval scores of VHS vs. baselines. Confirm the +2.7% overall gain.  
  3. **Ablation on DiT Layer & Loss:** Systematically vary \( \ell^* \) (e.g., \( h_1, h_5, h_7, h_9, h_{19} \)) and loss function (XE, weighted XE, focal loss) under a fixed 1100ms budget. Validate that \( h_7 \) + weighted XE is optimal and understand degradation at extremes.

## Limitations
- **Layer selection heuristic:** Optimal \( \ell^* \) (e.g., \( h_7 \) for SANA-Sprint) is derived from limited ablations; generalizability to other DiT architectures or resolutions is unverified.
- **Baseline implementation details:** Exact configuration for MLLM+AE (e.g., which latent, flattening method) is underspecified; reproduction may yield different efficiency baselines.
- **GenEval labeling fidelity:** The automatic labeling pipeline using Gemma3-4B is not fully detailed (inclusion/exclusion list generation); potential distribution shift from human judgments exists.
