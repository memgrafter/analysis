# Do Foundation Models Know Geometry? Probing Frozen Features for Continuous Physical Measurement
## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2603.06459
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.06459
- **Reference count:** 40
- **Primary result:** Frozen linear probes on VLMs achieve 6.1° MAE on hand joint angles, while text output is 20.0° MAE, revealing a 3.3× bottleneck in the autoregressive pathway.

## Executive Summary
This paper explores whether large vision-language models (VLMs) encode fine-grained geometric information—such as joint angles, head pose, and object orientation—and why their textual descriptions often lack such precision. The authors employ a probing methodology: they extract frozen features from a diverse set of vision backbones (self-supervised, contrastive, hybrid) and apply lightweight linear probes (reduced-rank regression) to predict continuous geometric quantities. This allows them to isolate the information retained in the visual representation from the ability of the text-generation pathway to express it.

The results reveal a substantial gap: frozen probes achieve an average error of 6.1° on hand joint angles (FreiHAND), while direct text generation from the same VLMs yields 20.0°—a 3.3× deficit. Low-rank adaptation (LoRA) applied to the language model layers narrows the gap to 6.5°, suggesting the visual backbone already encodes the geometry but the autoregressive decoder fails to route it. Additional experiments show that pretraining objectives, not architectural choices, dictate how well geometry is encoded: five different encoders reach statistically similar accuracy (R²≈0.55) despite highly dissimilar internal representations (CKA as low as 0.41). The spatial distribution of geometric signals is task-dependent, peaking in mid-network layers and concentrated in specific regions only for loosely framed subjects. Overall, the work demonstrates that frozen backbones act as effective geometric sensors via simple linear readouts, and that the generation pathway imposes a bottleneck for continuous physical measurement.

## Method Summary
The evaluation covers four datasets with precise ground-truth geometry: FreiHAND (hand joint angles), BIWI (head pose), YCB-Video (rigid object pose), and MPIIFaceGaze (gaze direction). For each vision or vision-language model (e.g., SigLIP2, DINOv3, CLIP, InternViT, Gemma 3), the authors extract token embeddings from every transformer layer. They then apply model-specific pooling: typically mean-pooling across all spatial tokens, excluding special and register tokens. The core analysis uses a reduced-rank ridge regression (RRR) probe: ridge regression (with regularization α) is trained, and the resulting weight matrix is truncated via singular value decomposition to a low rank r (selected from {3,4,5,6,8}). This probe predicts the continuous target values (e.g., 5 joint angles per finger). Hyperparameters (layer, r, α) are chosen via nested cross-validation (e.g., 10 folds) to maximize validation R². Final test performance is reported as mean absolute error (MAE) and uniform-mean R² across targets.

The probe’s effectiveness is contrasted with two baselines: (1) direct text output from the VLM when prompted to output numeric angles (without tools), and (2) text output after applying LoRA adapters (rank 16, trained on 2000 images) to the language model layers. Additional comparative analyses include: probing every layer to map the trajectory of geometric signal; ablating high-activation patches to assess spatial concentration; and computing centered kernel alignment (CKA) between different backbones to measure representational similarity. This comprehensive pipeline enables disentangling the visual encoding of geometry from the expressivity of the text generation head.

## Key Results
- **Probe-text gap:** Frozen linear probes achieve 6.1° MAE on FreiHAND, while the best text output from the same VLM is 20.0° MAE—a 3.3× bottleneck. LoRA fine-tuning (r=16, 2k images) reduces MAE to 6.5°, recovering 69–79% of the probe’s R², indicating a pathway-training deficit.
- **Functional convergence despite representational divergence:** Five diverse encoders (self-supervised, contrastive, hybrid) yield statistically equivalent R²≈0.55 on hand pose prediction even though pairwise CKA similarities can be as low as 0.41. This shows that different pretraining objectives can lead to equivalent functional readouts without aligning internal representations.
- **Mid-network peak and task-dependent spatial concentration:** Geometric accuracy peaks in layers 16–20 for ViT-L models and declines thereafter. Patch ablation experiments reveal that removing high-activation patches dramatically hurts head pose (loosely-framed faces) with ΔR² = -0.126, but barely affects object pose (tightly-cropped objects) with ΔR² = -0.003, explaining variable benefits of attention pooling across datasets.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
#### Pathway-Training Deficit in Autoregressive Decoding
The 3.3× gap between frozen probe performance and text output arises because the vision backbone already encodes continuous geometry in its activations, but the VLM’s autoregressive decoder—trained on next-token prediction over discrete text—fails to route these fine-grained continuous signals to the output. A linear probe accesses the raw geometric signal directly, bypassing the bottleneck. LoRA fine-tuning partially teaches the decoder to utilize the existing representation, narrowing the MAE gap and recovering 69–79% of the probe’s R², but not fully closing it. This indicates a deficit in the generation pathway rather than a lack of representational capacity.

#### Pretraining Objective Determines Encoding, Not Architecture
The equivalence in geometric probe accuracy (R²≈0.55) across encoders with vastly different pretraining objectives—self-supervised distillation (DINOv3), contrastive alignment (CLIP), hybrid (SigLIP)—despite low representational similarity (CKA as low as 0.41) demonstrates that the training objective, not architectural differences, is the primary factor shaping the backbone’s suitability for geometric readouts. The geometric signal resides in a low-dimensional manifold (5–6 DoF) within a high-dimensional feature space (~1024D). Different sculpting processes produce distinct yet equally decodable embeddings, consistent with a “weak” platonic representation: functional equivalence for a specific task without representational alignment.

#### Spatial Task-Dependence of Geometric Signal
The distribution of geometric information across feature maps is task-dependent. For tasks with loosely framed subjects (e.g., head pose in BIWI), the signal concentrates in a small subset of high-activation patches (the face region). Removing these patches causes a large drop in probe R² (Δ≈-0.13), and dedicated attention pooling yields a significant boost (+0.23–0.36 R²). In contrast, for tightly-cropped, centered objects (e.g., YCB-Video), the signal is spread evenly across patches; patch ablation has negligible effect and attention pooling provides no benefit. This explains why attention pooling improves performance on some geometry datasets but not others.

## Foundational Learning
**Linear Probing & Reduced-Rank Regression (RRR)**  
*Why needed:* To test whether frozen features contain a linear readout of continuous geometry without overfitting; RRR controls probe complexity via rank truncation, addressing concerns that a full linear probe might memorize rather than reveal true separability.  
*Quick check:* Why not simply use a nonlinear probe (e.g., MLP) to measure geometry? A high score with a nonlinear probe would indicate that the geometry is present but requires nonlinear decoding, whereas a linear probe gives a stricter test of what is directly accessible in the feature space.

**Functional Convergence vs. Representational Convergence (Platonic Hypothesis)**  
*Why needed:* The paper extends the platonic representation hypothesis to continuous targets: different pretraining objectives can yield equivalent functional performance (same probe accuracy) despite dissimilar internal representations (low CKA). Distinguishing functional from representational similarity is crucial for claims about what models “know” about geometry.  
*Quick check:* If two models have identical probe accuracy but CKA=0.2, does it mean they encode geometry in the same way? No—the identical accuracy shows they both can decode the geometry via a linear readout, but the low CKA indicates they use different internal feature arrangements to do so.

**Autoregressive Pathway Degradation**  
*Why needed:* Explains the text bottleneck: the shift from image features to discrete token prediction in the LLM layers actively discards or distorts fine-grained continuous information, as shown by layer-wise probing (geometric signal peaks early in LLM layers then declines).  
*Quick check:* If LoRA recovers most of the probe’s accuracy, does that prove the LLM initially destroyed the signal or merely failed to route it? The narrowing gap suggests the backbone retained the signal but the pretrained LLM routing was inadequate; LoRA teaches a better routing, though the probe’s advantage may also stem from its simplicity.

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component Map:**  
Image -> Frozen Backbone -> Token Embeddings -> Feature Pooling -> {Linear Probe (RRR) OR Text Generation Pathway (with optional LoRA adapters) -> Output}

**Critical Path:**  
1. Split datasets (train/validation/test) as specified (e.g., FreiHAND fixed 8k subset, BIWI subject-stratified).  
2. Load a frozen pretrained backbone and extract token embeddings from all layers for each image.  
3. Apply model-specific pooling (mean across spatial tokens, excluding special/register tokens) to obtain pooled features.  
4. For each model-layer combination, train a reduced-rank ridge regression (RRR): fit ridge(α) then truncate weight matrix to rank r via SVD.  
5. Select the best layer, r, and α via nested cross-validation (e.g., 10-fold) maximizing validation R².  
6. Retrain with selected hyperparameters on full train set, evaluate on test, record MAE and uniform-mean R².  
7. Compare probe performance against: (a) direct text output from the VLM prompted for numeric angles; (b) LoRA fine-tuned text output (r=16, 2k images); (c) other backbones.  
8. Conduct supplementary analyses: layer-wise probing, patch ablation, CKA similarity.

**Design Tradeoffs:**  
- **Frozen probes vs. LoRA:** Probes are extremely cheap (∼6k parameters) and measure pure encoding; LoRA (∼1M) enables human-readable output but only partially recovers geometry and is model-specific.  
- **Probe complexity:** Reduced-rank regression (rank 3–8) avoids overfitting and provides a stricter test of linear separability than full ridge regression.  
- **Layer selection:** Geometric signal consistently peaks in mid-network layers (16–20 for ViT-L); using only the final layer may underperform, so empirical selection across layers is necessary.

**Failure Signatures:**  
- Probes yielding near-zero or negative R²: indicates pretraining objective is misaligned with geometry or features are corrupted.  
- LoRA fine-tuning does not narrow the probe-text gap: suggests geometric signal is lost in later layers or the generation objective is catastrophically incompatible.  
- High variance across cross-validation folds: signals unstable geometric encoding, possibly due to dataset bias.

**First 3 Experiments:**  
1. **Core gap replication:** On FreiHAND, extract features from SigLIP2 ViT-L16, train RRR (rank 5, α=100). Expected MAE ≈ 6.1°. Prompt Gemma 3 directly for joint angles; expected MAE ≈ 20°. Confirm ~3× gap.  
2. **Layer-wise trajectory:** For DINOv3 and Gemma 3, probe every layer. Expect vision encoder accuracy to rise to a peak around layer 20 then decline slightly. Expect LLM layers (Gemma) to peak early (0–4) and decline monotonically. Quantify per-layer LoRA delta (see Tab 17).  
3. **Task-dependent spatial concentration:** On DINOv3, compute per-patch activation norms. Remove top-100 norm patches from BIWI and YCB-Video features. Expect large R² drop on BIWI (~-0.1) but minimal on YCB-Video; contrast with random patch removal.

## Limitations
- Dataset splits (e.g., exact FreiHAND indices, BIWI subject assignments) are not fully specified, preventing exact replication.  
- Model checkpoint versions, layer indexing conventions, and feature preprocessing details (normalization, register token handling) are missing.  
- Hyperparameter selection may have been performed on the test set (acknowledged limitation M1), risking optimistic bias in reported numbers.

## Confidence
- Pathway-training deficit: Medium  
- Training objective over architecture: High  
- Spatial task-dependence: High

## Next Checks
1. Replicate core gap with exact splits: Use the paper’s unreleased FreiHAND/BIWI splits to confirm the frozen probe vs. text output MAE gap (~3.3×) and LoRA recovery (~69–79% R²).
