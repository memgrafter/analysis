# Geometry-Aware Probabilistic Circuits via Voronoi Tessellations
## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2603.11946
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.11946
- **Reference count:** 40
- **Primary result:** Incorporates Voronoi tessellations into probabilistic circuits to capture local manifold structure while restoring tractability via certified bounds or hierarchical factorization.

## Executive Summary
Probabilistic circuits (PCs) provide tractable inference but use data-independent mixture weights, limiting geometric awareness of the data manifold. This paper integrates Voronoi tessellations (VT) into PC sum nodes, replacing constant weights with data-dependent gates derived from nearest-centroid assignments. A naïve implementation breaks exact tractability because Voronoi cells are oblique polyhedra whose integrals do not factor along variable scopes. To address this, the authors propose two remedies: (1) a certified approximate inference framework that replaces Voronoi cells with axis-aligned boxes, yielding provable lower and upper bounds on partition functions and marginals; (2) a hierarchical factorized Voronoi (HFV) construction that aligns geometric partitioning with the PC’s variable decomposition (e.g., vtree), restoring exact tractability. A soft gating mechanism with annealing enables gradient-based learning. Experiments on eight synthetic 2D/3D density estimation tasks show VT models achieving higher certified lower bounds than baseline PCs, while HFV matches baseline exact performance but with interpretable geometry and guaranteed exact inference.

## Method Summary
The method replaces constant weights in PC sum nodes with data-dependent gates computed via Voronoi tessellations. For each sum node, centroids define cells; the gate assigns an input to the nearest centroid. Naïve Voronoi gating breaks tractability because cell integrals over oblique polyhedra do not factor along variable scopes, violating decomposability. To recover tractability, two solutions are offered: (1) For general Voronoi tessellations (VT), each cell is conservatively approximated by inner and outer axis-aligned boxes; integration over boxes is decomposable, so propagating bounds through the circuit yields certified intervals. Adaptive refinement tightens bounds at exponential cost. (2) For hierarchical factorized Voronoi (HFV), the circuit’s variable partition (e.g., from a vtree) is respected: independent Voronoi tessellations are created per block, making joint cells Cartesian products. This aligns gating with the product structure, restoring exact tractability via Fubini’s theorem. Training uses soft gating (softmax over distances) with an annealing schedule to transition to hard assignments.

## Key Results
- VT models achieve higher certified lower bounds on partition functions compared to baseline PCs on synthetic density estimation tasks.
- HFV recovers exact inference tractability while providing interpretable geometric partitioning and matching baseline PC likelihood performance.
- Theoretical analysis confirms tractability restoration for HFV and convergence guarantees for the bounding scheme.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1: Incompatibility of Voronoi Gating with Tractable Inference
Naïve Voronoi-tessellated sum nodes break exact tractability because Voronoi cells are convex polytopes with oblique boundaries. Integrating a factorized expert density over such a cell couples variables across the scope, preventing recursive marginalization via Fubini’s theorem. Probabilistic circuits rely on decomposability (disjoint scopes at product nodes) and smoothness for efficient bottom-up evaluation; Voronoi geometry violates the decomposability of integration domains. This is formally proven for single-layer circuits (Proposition 3.2) and accumulates in deeper circuits. Tractable inference is restored only if Voronoi cells align exactly with the variable decomposition (e.g., axis-aligned boxes).

### Mechanism 2: Certified Approximate Inference via Box Approximation
Replacing each Voronoi cell with conservative axis-aligned inner and outer boxes resolves the tractability issue. Outer boxes are computed via linear programming to contain the cell; inner boxes are derived from centroid and half-space distances. Integration over boxes factorizes along variable scopes, enabling decomposable bound propagation through the circuit (Theorem 3.10). This yields provable lower and upper bounds on partition functions and marginals. Anytime refinement subdivides boxes to tighten bounds, though cost grows exponentially with dimension (Theorem 3.9). The framework provides certified approximate inference for arbitrary Voronoi tessellations.

### Mechanism 3: Hierarchical Factorized Voronoi (HFV) for Exact Tractable Inference
HFV constructs Voronoi tessellations that respect the circuit’s variable partition (e.g., defined by a vtree). At each sum node, the scope is split into blocks consistent with product children. Independent Voronoi tessellations are induced per block, so the joint cell is a Cartesian product of block-local cells. The gating function factors accordingly, aligning with the factorization of expert densities. This ensures that integration domains are products, satisfying Fubini’s theorem and restoring exact tractability (Theorem 3.14). HFV thus retains geometric awareness while enabling exact bottom-up evaluation, though it restricts geometry to factorized forms and may incur higher complexity with many blocks.

## Foundational Learning
- **Smoothness and decomposability in probabilistic circuits:** These structural properties ensure that marginalization and normalization can be computed in linear time via recursive bottom-up evaluation. *Why needed:* The paper demonstrates how Voronoi gating violates decomposability of integration domains, breaking tractability. *Quick check:* Why does a sum node require all children to share the same scope for tractable marginalization?
- **Voronoi tessellations:** A partition of space into convex cells where each cell contains points closest to a particular centroid. *Why needed:* It is the core geometric primitive for data-dependent gating; its polyhedral (often oblique) boundaries cause integration difficulties. *Quick check:* In 2D, what is the shape of a Voronoi cell given two centroids? How do additional centroids change the boundary?
- **Fubini’s theorem and product integration domains:** The theorem permits iterated integration when the domain is a Cartesian product of sets. *Why needed:* HFV relies on factorizing integration by ensuring Voronoi cells align with variable blocks, making the domain a product. *Quick check:* When integrating \(f(x_1, x_2)\) over a region \(R \subseteq \mathbb{R}^2\), what condition on \(R\) makes \(\iint_R f = \left(\int f \, dx_1\right)\left(\int f \, dx_2\right)\) possible?

## Architecture Onboarding
Component map: Voronoi-gated sum layer → (Box approximator for VT / HFV constructor for HFV) → Circuit evaluator

Critical path:
1. Initialize centroids: for HFV, run k-means on projected data per block; for VT, random or k-means globally.
2. Train with soft gating: use softmax over distances to compute gate values; optimize PC parameters via MLE while annealing softness parameter α.
3. At test time, switch to hard gating: for VT, compute inner/outer boxes per cell and propagate bounds; for HFV, evaluate exactly with hard assignments.

Design tradeoffs:
- VT vs HFV: VT is more expressive (arbitrary polyhedral cells) but only yields approximate certified bounds; HFV is exact but restricts geometry to factorized partitions, limiting cross-variable correlations.
- Box tightness: Inner boxes provide valid lower bounds but can be very small in high dimensions; outer boxes are conservative. Adaptive refinement tightens bounds but cost grows exponentially with dimension.
- Soft annealing schedule: Too fast (large initial α decay) causes centroid collapse; too slow slows convergence. Schedule is heuristic and sensitive.

Failure signatures:
- VT: Certified lower bound well below validation likelihood indicates boxes are too loose; refinement may be insufficient or cell shape too complex.
- HFV: Validation likelihood drop relative to baseline PC suggests factorization is overly restrictive for the data manifold.
- Soft gating: Loss oscillates or diverges early in training indicates annealing starts too aggressively; gradients may vanish or explode.

First 3 experiments:
1. Density estimation on eight synthetic 2D/3D benchmarks: compare test log-likelihood of baseline PCs, VT (certified lower bound), and HFV (exact).
2. Visualization of learned Voronoi tessellations on 2D pinwheel dataset: inspect inner boxes (VT) and factorized regions (HFV).
3. Learning curves on spiral dataset: track validation bound tightness (VT) or likelihood (HFV) over training epochs.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
1. **Incorporating non-linear learned embeddings:** How to extend the framework to embeddings \(\phi\) (e.g., neural networks) while preserving tractability (exact or certified) and enabling end-to-end training? Non-linear warping creates oblique Voronoi boundaries that break decomposability and certified bounds; it is unclear how to design embeddings compatible with HFV’s factorization or tight certification.
2. **Tighter high-dimensional approximations:** How to design less conservative approximations for Voronoi cells that scale to high-dimensional data, tightening certified bounds without exponential cost? Current inner-box methods yield extremely small volumes in high dimensions, and adaptive refinement is \(O(2^d)\); alternatives like convex relaxations or structured covers are needed.
3. **Continual learning via geometric awareness:** Can the locality and interpretability of Voronoi tessellations enable continual (lifelong) learning in probabilistic circuits, minimizing catastrophic forgetting? Current work is static; it is unknown whether HFV’s deterministic routing or VT’s certification can naturally accommodate new data regions or tasks incrementally.
4. **Controlled generation and anomaly detection:** How to exploit explicit geometric partitioning for controlled generation (e.g., sampling from specific cells) and interpretable anomaly detection (e.g., points in low-density or unassigned regions)? Voronoi cells suggest natural mechanisms, but no concrete methods have been developed or evaluated.

## Limitations
- VT certified bounds may become vacuous in high dimensions due to loose box approximations and exponential refinement complexity.
- HFV’s factorized geometry restricts representational power, inability to capture cross-variable correlations that oblique Voronoi cells could model.
- Experiments are limited to low-dimensional (2D/3D) synthetic data; scalability and performance on real-world high-dimensional datasets are unvalidated.
- Soft gating annealing schedule is heuristic; no theoretical convergence guarantees and sensitivity to hyperparameters is unknown.
