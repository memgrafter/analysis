# When Learning Rates Go Wrong: Early Structural Signals in PPO Actor-Critic

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2603.09950
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.09950
- **Reference count:** 12
- **Primary result:** Early OUI measurement (10% training) robustly discriminates learning rate regimes and, combined with early return, can prune 97.2% of runs while retaining 81.8% successful ones.

## Executive Summary
This paper investigates the sensitivity of learning rate (LR) in Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO) actor-critic methods. It introduces the Overfitting-Underfitting Indicator (OUI), a batch-based metric that quantifies the balance of binary neuron activation patterns on a fixed probe batch. The authors establish a theoretical connection between LR, activation sign changes, and OUI dynamics. Experiments across three discrete-control environments show that OUI measured early in training identifies optimal LR regimes: the critic performs best at intermediate OUI (avoiding saturation) while the actor benefits from high OUI. In early screening tests with matched recall, OUI-based selection outperforms metrics like KL divergence, clipping, and activation flips. A simple combination of early return and OUI achieves high precision-recall trade-offs, offering a practical structural signal for efficient hyperparameter tuning.

## Method Summary
The core method extends the Overfitting-Underfitting Indicator (OUI) to a batch-based formulation suitable for PPO’s actor-critic architecture. OUI measures the entropy of binary activation patterns (signs of neuron pre-activations) across a fixed probe batch of states. By keeping the probe batch constant throughout training, changes in OUI reflect how the network’s internal representations evolve under different learning rates. The authors derive a theoretical relationship: LR influences the frequency of activation sign flips; extreme LRs cause rapid saturation (low OUI) or insufficient adaptation (low OUI), while an intermediate LR yields a balanced pattern (intermediate OUI for critic, high OUI for actor). Empirically, OUI is computed at 10% of total training steps and correlated with final returns. Early screening protocols compare OUI against baseline metrics (KL divergence, clipping, activation flips) under matched recall to assess pruning efficiency. The combined rule—retain runs with both early return above a threshold and OUI within a target range—achieves strong precision-recall trade-offs.

## Key Results
- OUI measured at 10% training clearly discriminates LR regimes across three discrete environments.
- Highest final returns occur when the critic operates at intermediate OUI and the actor maintains high OUI, avoiding saturation.
- In early screening with matched recall, OUI alone provides the strongest precision across broader recall ranges; combined OUI+early return yields precision 0.818 at recall 0.111, pruning 97.2% of runs while 81.8% of retained runs are successful.
- OUI-based rules outperform alternatives (KL divergence, clipping, activation flips) in identifying promising hyperparameter configurations.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The OUI captures the diversity and balance of neuron activations on a fixed data distribution. A suitable learning rate steers weight updates so that neurons toggle between active/inactive states, maintaining a healthy level of representational capacity. Too high an LR causes weights to jump erratically, driving many neurons into a saturated regime (all signs identical) → low OUI → underfitting or instability. Too low an LR yields minimal changes, leading to premature convergence to a narrow set of patterns → also low OUI. An intermediate LR allows gradual exploration of weight space, preserving a mix of activation signs (intermediate OUI for the critic) while keeping the actor’s policy flexible (high OUI). The probe batch provides a consistent reference, turning OUI into a stable early diagnostic of the optimization trajectory. Thus, monitoring OUI early signals whether the LR is in a productive range before expensive full training.

## Foundational Learning
- **Learning rate (LR):** Step size for gradient updates; directly influences weight change magnitude. *Why needed:* Controls optimization speed and stability; mis-tuning leads to divergence or slow convergence. *Quick check:* Is LR too high if loss spikes? Too low if loss decreases very slowly?
- **Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO):** On-policy actor-critic RL algorithm with clipped objective to bound updates. *Why needed:* Maintains stable training while allowing policy improvement. *Quick check:* Does the policy update stay within a trust region (e.g., KL divergence small)?
- **Overfitting-Underfitting Indicator (OUI):** Metric based on entropy of binary activation signs over a probe batch. *Why needed:* Quantifies model’s representational balance; signals overfitting (saturation) or underfitting (lack of adaptation). *Quick check:* Compute activation signs on a fixed batch; if nearly all signs identical → OUI low.
- **Binary neuron activation patterns:** Represent neuron behavior by sign of pre-activation; reduces continuous activations to discrete patterns. *Why needed:* Simplifies monitoring of network internal state; robust to scaling. *Quick check:* Threshold activations at zero to get +1/-1.
- **Probe batch:** A fixed, representative subset of states kept constant across training. *Why needed:* Provides a stable reference to track OUI changes without influence from data distribution drift. *Quick check:* Does OUI change mainly due to weight updates rather than new data?
- **Actor-Critic architecture:** Two networks: actor (policy) and critic (value function). *Why needed:* Enables both action selection and advantage estimation. *Quick check:* Does critic loss decrease? Does policy improve (higher returns)?

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component map:** Probe Batch -> Actor Network & Critic Network -> Activation Signs -> OUI Calculator -> (Diagnostic Output)

**Critical path:** During training, a fixed probe batch is selected. At the designated screening point (e.g., 10% of total steps), the current actor and critic networks process this batch. Their pre-activations signs are extracted, aggregated into binary patterns per layer, and fed to the OUI calculator which outputs an OUI value (e.g., entropy). This OUI is then compared against empirically determined thresholds to judge LR quality.

**Design tradeoffs:** 
- Fixed vs. dynamic probe batch: fixed ensures consistency but may become unrepresentative; dynamic adapts but introduces variability.
- Binary threshold: zero is standard, but choice influences sensitivity; could use learned quantization.
- Layer selection: OUI can be computed per layer or averaged; trade-off between granularity and noise.
- Timing: Early screening (10%) vs. later; earlier saves compute but may be less reliable.

**Failure signatures:**
- OUI near 0 for both actor and critic → saturation, likely due to LR too high or too low.
- Critic OUI too high? The paper finds optimal intermediate for critic; high might indicate insufficient learning.
- Actor OUI too low → policy collapse; high is desirable.
- OUI unstable across seeds → probe batch too small or not representative.

**Three first experiments:**
1. Baseline OUI dynamics: Train PPO with a moderate LR, compute OUI for actor/critic at multiple checkpoints to observe typical trajectories and variability.
2. LR sweep and correlation: Sample a range of LRs (e.g., 1e-5 to 1e-1), train briefly, record OUI at 10% and final return. Plot OUI vs return to confirm empirical relationship for your tasks.
3. Early screening comparison: For a set of random LRs, apply early screening using OUI thresholds versus KL divergence or return baselines. Compare pruning efficiency (fraction of runs discarded) and success rate of retained runs.

## Limitations
- **Environment scope:** OUI validation limited to three discrete-control tasks; applicability to continuous control (e.g., MuJoCo) and other RL algorithms remains unknown.
- **Theoretical assumptions:** The derivation linking LR to activation sign changes relies on simplified dynamics; real-world networks with diverse architectures and non-stationary data may deviate.
- **Probe batch dependency:** Sensitivity of OUI to batch size, distribution, and random seed is not assessed, raising concerns about reliability across different choices.

## Confidence
- **Empirical OUI discrimination across LR regimes:** High
- **Theoretical link between LR, activation signs, and OUI:** Medium
- **OUI-based early screening outperforming alternatives (KL, clipping):** High
- **Combined OUI+return precision/recall metrics:** High
