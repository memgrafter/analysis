# Consensus-Aligned Neuron Efficient Fine-Tuning Large Language Models for Multi-Domain Machine Translation
## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2602.05694
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.05694
- **Reference count:** 10
- **Primary result:** CANEFT achieves state-of-the-art multi-domain machine translation by updating only 1% of model parameters, with average improvements of +1.3 BLEU (De→En) and +1.4 BLEU (Zh→En).

## Executive Summary
Multi-domain machine translation (MDMT) aims to train a single model that performs well across diverse domains, but parameter-efficient fine-tuning (PEFT) methods often suffer from domain shift and interference. This paper introduces Consensus-Aligned Neuron Efficient Fine-Tuning (CANEFT), which identifies neurons that are consistently important across all training domains using mutual information and selectively updates only those neurons. Evaluated on 10 German-English and Chinese-English domains with three large language models, CANEFT outperforms strong PEFT baselines by up to +1.4 BLEU while updating merely 1% of parameters, demonstrating superior generalization to both seen and unseen domains.

## Method Summary
CANEFT proceeds in two phases: (1) Neuron selection and (2) selective fine-tuning. For each domain, neuron importance is computed as the product of activation and gradient (I = A * G) for all neurons in feed-forward network (FFN) layers over a fixed sample (10k sentences). These continuous importance scores are binned, and mutual information (MI) between the binned scores and domain labels is calculated per neuron. Neurons are then ranked by the minimum MI across all training domains; the top 1% are selected as consensus-aligned neurons (N_MDCA). A binary mask is constructed to zero out gradients for all other parameters. During fine-tuning, the model is trained on the full multi-domain parallel data with standard language modeling loss, but only the masked FFN parameters receive updates. This approach ensures that parameter updates are confined to neurons that encode domain-general features, minimizing interference.

## Key Results
- CANEFT consistently outperforms strong PEFT baselines (Full FT, LoRA, IA3, etc.) on all 10 MDMT datasets.
- Average improvements: +1.3 BLEU for De→En and +1.4 BLEU for Zh→En over the best baseline.
- The method attains state-of-the-art performance on both seen and unseen domains while updating only ~1% of model parameters.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
CANEFT’s effectiveness stems from its neuron selection criterion: by identifying neurons that exhibit high mutual information with domain labels across *all* training domains, it focuses updates on representations that are shared and beneficial for every domain. This avoids the parameter interference that arises when updating neurons that are only important for a subset of domains. Freezing the vast majority of parameters also mitigates catastrophic forgetting, as the core language modeling capabilities remain untouched. The minimal set of consensus-aligned neurons acts as a pivot that can steer the model toward domain-specific adaptation without destabilizing its general knowledge, thereby preserving performance on unseen domains.

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component map:**  
Training Data (multi-domain) → Neuron Importance Computation (per domain) → Mutual Information Estimation → Consensus Neuron Selection → Gradient Mask Construction → Parameter-Efficient Fine-Tuning → Evaluation

**Critical path:**  
1. On a fixed sample per domain, compute I = A × G for all FFN neurons.  
2. Bin I scores and compute MI(I, domain) for each neuron.  
3. For each neuron, take min_d(MI); select top 1% as N_MDCA.  
4. Build binary mask M for FFN projection matrices.  
5. During fine-tuning, apply M to gradients so only N_MDCA parameters update.  
6. Train on full multi-domain data and evaluate.

**Design tradeoffs:**  
- MI-based selection ensures cross-domain consistency but may be computationally heavier than average importance; it also yields a more conservative neuron set.  
- Updating only 1% of parameters maximizes efficiency but relies heavily on the quality of the selected set; a poor selection could underfit.  
- Using a small, fixed sample for importance estimation introduces sampling variance but keeps the selection cost low.

**Failure signatures:**  
- Drop in BLEU/COMET across all domains, indicating over-specialization or bad mask.  
- Non-zero gradients for frozen neurons, suggesting mask misapplication.  
- Selected neurons concentrated in a few layers (unexpected distribution).  
- Inability to replicate base model scores, pointing to data preprocessing mismatches.

**First experiments:**  
1. Replicate zero-shot base inference scores for all 10 domains to verify data and inference setup.  
2. Confirm that the constructed mask covers approximately 1% of FFN parameters.  
3. Log gradient norms during the first fine-tuning step; ensure frozen neurons have zero gradient.

## Limitations
- MI computation details (binning strategy, number of bins) are not specified, affecting reproducibility and stability.
- The dynamic threshold γ (top 1% selection) is ambiguous: it is unclear whether ranking is global or per layer.
- Exact fine-tuning hyperparameters (learning rate, batch size, epochs) are omitted, making fair comparison with baselines difficult.
