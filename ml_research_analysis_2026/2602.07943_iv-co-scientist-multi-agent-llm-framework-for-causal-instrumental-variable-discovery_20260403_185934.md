# IV Co-Scientist: Multi-Agent LLM Framework for Causal Instrumental Variable Discovery
## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2602.07943
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.07943
- **Reference count:** 40
- **Primary result:** LLM-based multi-agent framework proposes, critiques, and grounds IVs; recovers canonical IVs (Exact Match up to 0.74, Conceptual Match up to 1.00) and discovers novel IVs with strong first-stage relevance and high internal consistency.

## Executive Summary
We introduce IV Co-Scientist, a multi-agent LLM framework for discovering valid instrumental variables (IVs) for causal inference. The system orchestrates agents that generate candidate IVs and confounders, critique them against exclusion and independence assumptions, and anchor them to observational data. Evaluated on canonical IV examples and the Gapminder dataset, the framework achieves high recovery rates for known IVs and yields statistically plausible novel instruments with strong first-stage F-statistics and improved internal consistency compared to random proxies. This work demonstrates the potential of LLMs to automate key aspects of causal hypothesis generation and validation.

## Method Summary
The pipeline consists of six sequential agents: (1) PreSelector filters variable pairs by high Pearson correlation; (2) HumanProxy selects policy-relevant pairs; (3) CausalOracle infers causal direction by combining LLM reasoning with Granger causality; (4) HypothesisGenerator proposes five candidate IVs and five confounders via persona-based prompts; (5) two CriticAgents evaluate each IV for exclusion restriction and independence; (6) Grounder maps validated conceptual IVs to actual dataset variables. The system uses pre-trained LLMs (GPT-4o, o3-mini, QwQ, Llama3.1) without training. Evaluation metrics include Exact/Conceptual Match for canonical recovery, first-stage F-statistic for relevance, and a normalized consistency score (C_norm) comparing 2SLS ATE estimates across multiple IVs. Experiments use the Gapminder socio‑economic dataset.

## Key Results
- LLMs recover canonical literature IVs with Exact Match up to 0.74 and Conceptual Match up to 1.00.
- Critic agents successfully reject historically invalidated instruments (e.g., rainfall for GDP→conflict).
- For novel treatment‑outcome pairs in Gapminder, suggested IVs achieve strong first‑stage relevance (F-stat > 10) and significantly higher internal consistency (normalized consistency score < 1) versus random proxies.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1: Hypothesis Generation via Structured Agent Prompting
- **Claim:** The system proposes candidate instrumental variables (IVs) and confounders by prompting LLMs with explicit causal roles.
- **Mechanism:** A `HypothesisGenerator` agent uses few-shot, persona-based prompts (e.g., "You are an economist...") to generate a fixed-size list (`i=5`) of potential instruments and confounders for a given treatment-outcome pair `(T, Y)`. The prompts explicitly encode the IV conditions (relevance, exclusion, independence) to steer generation.
- **Core assumption:** LLMs, trained on vast scientific and domain-specific text, can retrieve and creatively recombine implicit knowledge about plausible sources of exogenous variation.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract] "multi-agent framework that proposes, critiques, and refines IVs"
  - [section 4.1, 4.4] Describes the `HypothesisGenerator` prompts and its role in the pipeline.
  - [corpus] Neighbors focus on statistical IV methods; the corpus lacks direct evidence for prompt-based generation, indicating novelty.
- **Break condition:** The generator produces irrelevant, tautological, or highly contested instruments that fail downstream statistical checks (F-statistic <10).

### Mechanism 2: Dual-Critic Validation for Causal Assumptions
- **Claim:** Separate critic agents evaluate each candidate IV against the non-testable exclusion and independence assumptions using argumentative reasoning.
- **Mechanism:** For each proposed `Z`, two `CriticAgent`s independently assess: (1) *Exclusion Restriction*: Does `Z` affect `Y` only via `T`? (2) *Independence*: Is `Z` independent of unobserved confounders `U` (list also generated)? Only instruments passing both binary critiques are kept (`Z_valid`).
- **Core assumption:** LLMs can simulate expert judgment to reason about potential indirect pathways and hidden confounding based on textual context and the listed `U`.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [section 4] "Critic Agents... evaluate the validity of the IVs proposed... One agent reasons that the exclusion restriction holds... the second agent assesses independence."
  - [section 4.3.2] Table 2 shows critic agents successfully reject historically invalidated IVs (e.g., rainfall).
- **Break condition:** Critics exhibit high false-negative rates (rejecting valid canonical IVs) or false-positive rates (passing clearly invalid instruments like `rainfall` for `GDP→Conflict`).

### Mechanism 3: Data Grounding and Consistency-Based Validation
- **Claim:** Proposed IVs are anchored to concrete dataset variables, and their collective validity is assessed via internal consistency of 2SLS estimates.
- **Mechanism:** A `Grounder` agent matches conceptual IVs (e.g., "distance to port") to actual variables in the Gapminder dataset. Valid grounded instruments are used in 2SLS. The key evaluation metric is *normalized consistency* `C_norm = |Δ_LLM / Δ_Rand|`, where Δ is the absolute difference in ATE estimates from two different instruments. Lower `C_norm` (vs. random proxy baseline) indicates the instrument set isolates similar exogenous variation.
- **Core assumption:** Instruments that yield mutually consistent causal effect estimates are more likely to be jointly valid (i.e., satisfy IV assumptions).
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [section 4.6] Reports `C_norm < 1` for LLM-suggested IVs vs. random proxies.
  - Figure 2 shows narrower ATE distribution for LLM IVs vs. random.
- **Break condition:** `C_norm` approaches 1 (no consistency gain over random), or all grounded instruments have weak first-stage (F-statistic <10).

## Foundational Learning
- **Concept: Instrumental Variable Validity Conditions**  
  Why needed here: The entire pipeline (`HypothesisGenerator`, `CriticAgent`s) is built around operationalizing relevance, exclusion, and independence. Understanding these is necessary to design prompts, interpret critic decisions, and evaluate results.  
  Quick check: For `Z = "quarter of birth"` as an IV for `education → earnings`, what is the most plausible pathway that could violate the exclusion restriction?

- **Concept: Two-Stage Least Squares (2SLS) & LATE**  
  Why needed here: The framework uses 2SLS as the estimation engine; understanding its mechanics (first-stage prediction, second-stage outcome regression) is necessary to grasp why `F-statistic` (first-stage strength) and `ATE` consistency (from second-stage) are the chosen metrics.  
  Quick check: If an instrument is weak (first-stage F-statistic ≈ 2), what is the likely behavior of the 2SLS estimator in finite samples?

- **Concept: Multi-Agent System Design**  
  Why needed here: The architecture separates creative generation (`HypothesisGenerator`), critical evaluation (`CriticAgent`s), and empirical grounding (`Grounder`). This modularity is central to the proposed framework.  
  Quick check: Why might using separate agents for *exclusion* and *independence* critique be preferable to a single agent performing both checks?

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component map**: PreSelector → HumanProxy → CausalOracle → HypothesisGenerator → CriticAgents → Grounder  
- PreSelector: Filters variable pairs by Pearson correlation |ρ| > τ_ρ.  
- HumanProxy (LLM): Filters pairs for socio‑economic meaningfulness → Set S.  
- CausalOracle: Combines LLM reasoning + Granger causality to assign direction (T→Y).  
- HypothesisGenerator: Proposes i IVs and j confounders.  
- CriticAgents: Two LLMs score each IV on exclusion & independence.  
- Grounder: Maps conceptual IVs to concrete dataset variables.

**Critical path**: Correlation filter → Meaningful pair selection → Direction inference → IV proposal → Dual critique → Proxy grounding. Feedback loop: if no grounded IVs survive, return to IV proposal.

**Design tradeoffs**:
- Creativity vs. Validity: Higher i (more instruments) may increase valid IV chance but raises token cost and noise.
- Critic Strictness: Higher j (more confounders) makes independence checks more conservative, possibly rejecting good IVs.
- Dataset Scope: Gapminder provides breadth but limits depth; domain‑specific datasets need tailored prompts.

**Failure signatures**:
- Stage 1 (PreSelector): Exhausts all pairs (τ_ρ too low) or yields none (τ_ρ too high).
- Stage 3 (CausalOracle): LLM‑Granger disagreement >50% → ambiguous direction.
- Stage 6 (Grounder): <20% of valid IVs map to dataset → overly abstract proposals.
- Final: C_norm ≈ 1 and/or mean first‑stage F < 5 → instruments no better than random.

**First 3 experiments**:
1. Canonical Recovery Validation: Run pipeline on 5 known (T,Y) pairs; verify Exact/Conceptual Match align with Table 1.
2. Invalid IV Rejection Test: Inject known invalid IV (e.g., `rainfall` for `GDP→conflict`) into candidate list; verify `CriticAgent` rejects it.
3. Gapminder Discovery Smoke Test: Run on novel pair (e.g., `sanitation→child mortality`); check ≥2 grounded IVs with F > 10 and C_norm < 0.7.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
#### Open Question 1
**Question:** Does the IV Co-Scientist framework generalize to domains beyond socio-economic variables (e.g., biomedical, legal) or datasets with different structures and noise characteristics than Gapminder?  
- **Basis in paper:** "our framework is tested in relatively well-understood domains and may not generalize to noisier or less-documented fields" (Conclusion, Limitations).  
- **Why unresolved:** Evaluation is confined to one curated dataset (Gapminder) with predominantly country‑level, time‑series indicators. Performance in other domains remains unknown.  
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Replicate the pipeline on diverse, domain‑specific datasets (e.g., electronic health records, legal case data) and compare the statistical strength and consistency of discovered IVs against domain expert validation or known standards.

#### Open Question 2
**Question:** Is the proposed "consistency" metric (comparing ATE estimates from multiple candidate IVs) a reliable indirect validator of IV validity in the absence of ground truth, or can it be gamed by sets of invalid IVs that happen to produce similar biased estimates?  
- **Basis in paper:** The metric is introduced as a novel proxy for validity (§4.5.2), inspired by self‑compatibility in causal discovery, but the paper acknowledges that "it does not confirm the validity of any single instrument" and serves only as "indirect evidence."  
- **Why unresolved:** The metric assumes that valid IVs converge to the true ATE while invalid ones diverge. However, multiple invalid instruments sharing the same bias could yield low Δ_LLM, falsely appearing consistent. The paper provides no formal conditions or simulations establishing the metric's false‑positive/false‑negative rates.  
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Simulation studies manipulating specific IV violations (weakness, direct effects, confounding) to measure how often the consistency score distinguishes valid from invalid IV sets. Theoretical analysis linking the variance of Δ_LLM to instrument strength and bias structure.

#### Open Question 3
**Question:** What is the optimal division of labor between LLM‑based critics (for exclusion/independence) and human expert oversight, and can the critic agents reliably approximate expert judgment across diverse contexts?  
- **Basis in paper:** The framework uses two separate LLM critic agents (§4.1), and a human evaluation was conducted with one economist (§Appendix B), who found "reasoning generally sound" but noted accepted vs. rejected IVs often differ in "generality." The paper does not explore systematic human‑AI disagreement or a scalable human‑in‑the‑loop integration.  
- **Why unresolved:** The human evaluation is anecdotal (single expert, small set of examples). It is unclear whether critic agents consistently replicate expert vetoes/acceptances, especially on novel or borderline cases. The framework also lacks a mechanism for incorporating nuanced human feedback back into the generation loop.  
- **What evidence would resolve it:** A larger‑scale study with multiple domain experts rating a broad sample of LLM‑proposed and LLM‑criticized IVs, measuring inter‑rater reliability between experts and the critic agents. Experimenting with interactive frameworks where human critiques are used to fine‑tune or prompt the critic agents.

#### Open Question 4
**Question:** How sensitive is the multi‑agent pipeline to the prompts and personas used for the HypothesisGenerator and CriticAgents, and could suboptimal prompting lead to systematically biased or narrow IV proposals?  
- **Basis in paper:** The authors use fixed prompts (e.g., "economist persona") and hyperparameters (i=5 instruments, j=5 confounders) determined by an ablation on a single pair. The framework's creativity and critique quality depend entirely on prompt design and LLM reasoning, but no sensitivity analysis across prompt variations or personas is reported.  
- **Why unresolved:** The ablation (§4.6.1) only varies the number of instruments/confounders, not prompt wording, persona specificity, or chain‑of‑thought instructions. Different prompts could yield vastly different IV sets. The paper does not assess whether the default "economist" persona is sufficiently broad or if specialized personas (e.g., "epidemiologist") would work better for non‑economic outcomes.  
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Systematic experimentation with prompt variants (e.g., differing levels of detail, alternative personas, inclusion of examples) across a diverse set of (T,Y) pairs, measuring impacts on IV diversity, relevance, and critique agreement. Qualitative analysis of failure modes where prompts lead to invalid or repetitive suggestions.

#### Open Question 5
**Question:** For novel treatment‑outcome pairs, do LLM‑suggested IVs that achieve statistical first‑stage strength (F‑stat > 10) and high internal consistency also satisfy the exclusion and independence assumptions when subjected to more rigorous, domain‑specific validation beyond the LLM's own critique?  
- **Basis in paper:** The primary results for novel Gapminder pairs rely on the F‑statistic for relevance and the novel consistency metric (§4.6). However, the authors state that exclusion and independence "cannot be verified from observational data alone and must ultimately be justified using domain knowledge" (§3.1), and their critic agents are LLM‑based proxies.  
- **Why unresolved:** The paper presents no external validation (e.g., expert review of the novel IVs' exclusion/independence plausibility) beyond the internal consistency check. An IV could be strong and consistent yet still be invalid due to a subtle direct effect or confounding not captured by the LLM's reasoning or the chosen confounder set.  
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Subjecting the top‑ranked novel IVs from the pipeline to independent evaluation by domain experts (not involved in the study) who are blinded to the LLM's rationale. Experts would assess each IV against the exclusion and independence criteria using their knowledge of the specific domain (e.g., economics, public health). Correlations between expert judgments and the LLM critic's decisions, as well as between expert judgments and the consistency score, would be measured.

## Limitations
- Exact specifications for several pipeline components (Granger causality lag selection, effective sample size threshold, random proxy sampling) are missing, hindering faithful replication.
- The proposed consistency metric (C_norm) lacks external validation; its relationship to true IV validity remains unclear.
- Reliance on pre‑trained LLMs may introduce training data biases, and the broad Gapminder dataset may obscure domain‑specific nuances.

## Confidence
- Canonical IV recovery (Exact/Conceptual Match): **Medium**
- Critic agents rejecting invalid IVs: **Medium**
- Novel discovery (F>10, C_norm<1): **Low**
