# StatLLaMA: A multi-stage training framework for building a domain-optimized statistical language model

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2601.09718
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.09718
- **Reference Count:** 40
- **Primary Result:** A 3B parameter model (StatLLaMA) achieves strong, balanced performance on domain (AP Statistics, 41.46%) and general reasoning (GSM8K, 58.98%; ARC, 41.81%) via a multi-stage pipeline starting from an instruction-tuned base.

## Executive Summary
This study presents StatLLaMA, a 3B parameter statistical language model built through a multi-stage training pipeline. The core finding is that effective domain specialization requires starting from an instruction-tuned foundation model (LLaMA-3.2-3B-Instruct), not a base model. The optimal pipeline combines a carefully balanced supervised fine-tuning (SFT) stage that trades off domain expertise against general reasoning, a Direct Preference Optimization (DPO) alignment stage that restores general abilities degraded by SFT, and a minimally intensive downstream task fine-tuning (DTFT) stage to avoid catastrophic forgetting. The resulting model outperforms pipelines starting from a base model and demonstrates that parameter-efficient methods can yield a capable, dual-competence specialist.

## Method Summary
The construction follows a three-stage process after loading `LLaMA-3.2-3B-Instruct`. First, **Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT)** uses LoRA (rank/alpha unspecified in reproduction notes) on a curated mixture: statistical term definitions (3x repetition), statistical chain-of-thought (2x), statistical GRPO data (2x), and GSM8K training data (1x), with loss computed only on assistant responses. Second, **Preference Alignment** applies DPO for 2 epochs on a merged dataset of statistical and mathematical preference pairs, re-balancing the model. Third, **Downstream Task Fine-Tuning (DTFT)** applies a very low-intensity LoRA update (rank=8, alpha=16) for approximately 150 steps on Cross Validated and Knowledge Graph data. All stages use 4-bit quantization and the LLaMA-3.2 chat template.

## Key Results
- Pipelines beginning with a non-instruct base model failed to achieve meaningful statistical reasoning (<36% GSM8K, <26% AP Statistics), regardless of extensive continual pretraining or SFT.
- An instruct-tuned starting point enabled effective specialization, with the best SFT configuration (SFT-v3.4) reaching ~41% on AP Statistics while preserving ~55% on GSM8K.
- DPO alignment reliably restored general reasoning (GSM8K +3-4%, ARC +4-5%) without sacrificing domain gains, whereas GRPO proved unstable.
- DTFT must be applied with extreme caution; even a full epoch causes catastrophic forgetting, while ~150 steps yields net benefit.
- Final **StatLLaMA** scores: **58.98%** GSM8K, **41.46%** AP Statistics, **41.81%** ARC.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1: Instruction-Tuned Base as Prerequisite
- **Claim:** Effective domain specialization requires starting from an instruction-tuned foundation model.
- **Mechanism:** Instruction tuning establishes a stable "policy" for interpreting prompts and generating structured responses. Without this interface, subsequent stages (CoP, SFT, RLHF) cannot reliably map inputs to desired output formats, leading to noisy or ineffective learning.
- **Core Assumption:** General instruction-following is a separable and necessary foundation that must precede domain-specific fine-tuning for efficient adaptation.
- **Evidence:** Pipelines from Base never exceeded ~36% GSM8K / ~26% AP Stats; Pipeline 3 (Instruct start) reached ~59% / ~41% (abstract, Tables 2–3).

### Mechanism 2: SFT Mixture Controls Domain-General Trade-off
- **Claim:** SFT directly trades domain expertise for general reasoning; data mixture controls the balance.
- **Mechanism:** SFT updates the model’s response distribution toward statistical QA. Over-representing domain data (e.g., repeating term definitions) steeply increases domain performance but causes "overfitting" to domain patterns, degrading general reasoning that relies on broader schemas.
- **Core Assumption:** Domain and general reasoning compete for representational capacity in the limited 3B parameter space during PEFT.
- **Evidence:** SFT-v3.1 (3x term defs) achieved 41.11% AP Stats but 51.18% GSM8K; SFT-v3.6 (no term defs) preserved 58.15% GSM8K but only 38.68% AP Stats (Table 5).

### Mechanism 3: DPO Re-Balances After SFT
- **Claim:** DPO effectively restores general abilities degraded by SFT while preserving domain gains.
- **Mechanism:** DPO’s binary cross-entropy loss on preference pairs adjusts the policy away from the SFT model’s narrow distribution toward a region satisfying the preference model. This shift recovers fluent, general reasoning patterns lost during aggressive SFT.
- **Core Assumption:** Preference data contains implicit signals about both general response quality and domain accuracy.
- **Evidence:** After SFT-v3.3, GSM8K dropped to 53.60% and ARC to 36.77%. Following DPO, GSM8K recovered to 57.24% and ARC to 41.13%, while AP Statistics held at 41.46% (Table 8).

## Foundational Learning
- **Concept: Instruction Tuning as a Base Policy**
  - **Why needed:** Without it, the model cannot consistently format outputs, making domain SFT ineffective.
  - **Quick check:** What happens if you apply SFT on statistical QA to a raw base model vs. an instruct model? (Base fails to format outputs; instruct adapts.)
- **Concept: Catastrophic Forgetting in Fine-Tuning**
  - **Why needed:** Explains why aggressive or prolonged DTFT destroys the balanced model.
  - **Quick check:** Why does 150 steps of DTFT help while 300 steps harms GSM8K? (Short updates tweak without overwriting critical weights.)
- **Concept: Preference Alignment (DPO vs. GRPO)**
  - **Why needed:** DPO provided stable re-balancing; GRPO was unstable for small models/data.
  - **Quick check:** Which alignment method preserved or improved ARC after a domain-focused SFT? (DPO.)
- **Concept: Data Mixture & Repetition as a Control Knob**
  - **Why needed:** Repetition factors (e.g., 3x for terms) directly bias the SFT toward domain or general skills.
  - **Quick check:** SFT-v3.2 vs. v3.4 (both 3x terms, 2x CoT/GRPO) differ in inclusion of FineTome-100k. Primary difference? (v3.4 has higher AP Stats and GSM8K, suggesting FineTome introduces noise.)
- **Concept: Parameter-Efficient Fine-Tuning (LoRA)**
  - **Why needed:** Enables multi-stage training on a 3B model within resource limits; capacity management via dynamic rank.
  - **Quick check:** What LoRA advantage suits the brief DTFT stage? (Small trainable set limits forgetting risk.)

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component map:** LLaMA-3.2-3B-Instruct -> LoRA adapters (q_proj, k_proj, v_proj, o_proj, gate_proj, up_proj, down_proj) -> Training Orchestrator (Hugging Face Trainer + Unsloth/DeepSpeed) -> Data Pipeline (preprocessing -> chat template -> weighted mixing) -> Alignment Modules (DPO/GRPO loss)

**Critical path:**
1. Start from `LLaMA-3.2-3B-Instruct` (not Base).
2. Apply SFT (config like SFT-v3.4) with LoRA; train 1 epoch; loss on responses only.
3. Apply DPO (2 epochs) on SFT model using combined Statistical + Math preference pairs.
4. Apply DTFT (150 steps) with low-rank LoRA (r=8, α=16) on DPO model using CV+KG data.
5. Merge LoRA weights into base for deployment.

**Design tradeoffs:**
- **LoRA Rank:** Higher in SFT (e.g., 16/32) allows more domain injection but risks overwriting general skills; lower in DTFT (8) constrains updates.
- **Data Repetition:** Repeating domain datasets (3x) boosts domain metrics but harms general reasoning; balance is critical (e.g., 2x for CoT/GRPO).
- **Loss Masking:** Computing SFT loss only on assistant responses focuses capacity on task learning, not prompt formatting.
- **Alignment Choice:** GRPO unstable with small data; DPO robust but sensitive to SFT model quality.

**Failure signatures:**
- After SFT: Large GSM8K drop (>10%) or ARC drop (>5%) indicates over-specialization.
- After RLHF: Performance crash across all metrics indicates unstable alignment (e.g., GRPO issues).
- After DTFT: Any benchmark drop from the SFT+DPO peak signals too-intense fine-tuning.

**First 3 experiments:**
1. Establish baselines: Evaluate Base vs. Instruct on GSM8K, AP Statistics, ARC. Confirm Instruct has strong general reasoning (GSM8K ~64%) but weak domain (AP Stats ~37%).
2. Pipeline comparison: Run Base-based pipelines (1 & 2) vs. Instruct-based (3). Verify only Pipeline 3 achieves meaningful AP Stats (>40%).
3. SFT sweep: Implement SFT-v3.x variants to map trade-off surface between AP Statistics and GSM8K/ARC. Identify candidates for DPO (e.g., v3.3, v3.4).

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
1. Does the multi-stage strategy generalize to larger LLaMA scales (7B, 70B) while preserving the domain-general balance?
2. What comprehensive evaluation benchmarks are needed for statistical LLMs on end-to-end applied tasks (ambiguity resolution, error diagnosis, critique)?
3. How can high-quality statistical CoT datasets be constructed to reduce reliance on teacher-model distillation (e.g., Gemini) and avoid bias?
4. Which PEFT techniques (LoRA variants, AdapterFusion, partial freezing) offer the best stability-adaptability trade-off for domain specialization?

## Limitations
- Incomplete hyperparameter reporting (SFT LoRA rank/alpha, learning rates, DPO β) limits faithful replication.
- Evaluation prompt templates and chat formatting details are omitted, risking metric drift.
- Experiments restricted to a single 3B model; scalability to larger architectures or different base LLMs is unknown.
- Preference data exclusively from Gemini; potential bias in alignment signal not examined.
- 4-bit quantization used throughout; its impact on stability was not ablated.

## Confidence
- **Instruction-tuned prerequisite:** High (pipeline comparisons show overwhelming advantage)
- **SFT mixture trade-off:** Medium (ablation supports claim but may not generalize across scales/datasets)
- **DPO restoration:** Medium (consistent recovery observed, tied to SFT quality and preference content)
- **DTFT intensity limit:** Medium (150-step threshold clear here but could shift with other data mixes)

## Next Checks
1. **Hyperparameter sweep:** Exhaustively vary LoRA rank (8, 16, 32), learning rates, and repetition factors to reconstruct the SFT trade-off surface and verify optimal SFT-v3.4 settings.
2. **Base model instruction-tuning:** Apply a comprehensive instruction-tuning phase to LLaMA-3.2-3B-Base before domain SFT, to test if the "instruction prerequisite" can be overcome.
3. **Scale and preference diversity:** Replicate the full pipeline on a larger model (e.g., LLaMA-3.2-7B-Instruct) and replace Gemini preferences with GPT-4–generated pairs to assess robustness at increased scale and with varied alignment sources.
