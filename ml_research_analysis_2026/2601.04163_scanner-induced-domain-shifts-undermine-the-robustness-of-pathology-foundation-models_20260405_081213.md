# Scanner-Induced Domain Shifts Undermine the Robustness of Pathology Foundation Models

## Quick Facts
- **ArXiv ID:** 2601.04163
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.04163
- **Reference count:** 37
- **Primary result:** Pathology foundation models are not robust to scanner-induced domain shifts; embedding geometry and calibration are systematically distorted, though some vision-language models (CONCH) show improved stability.

## Executive Summary
This paper evaluates 14 pathology foundation models (PFMs) on a controlled multiscanner breast cancer dataset. Scanner variability is found to systematically distort both tile- and slide-level embedding geometry, as measured by increased pairwise cosine distance, reduced 1-NN match rate, and lower Mantel correlation. While discriminative performance (AUC) often remains stable across scanners, prediction consistency (Fleiss’ κ) varies substantially and predicted probabilities exhibit systematic calibration shifts, revealing hidden scanner-dependent bias. No model achieves fully reliable robustness; vision-language models (CONCH, CONCHv1.5) show the best embedding stability and calibration consistency, but robustness does not scale with model size, recency, or pretraining data volume. These results underscore the necessity of evaluating PFMs beyond task accuracy and addressing scanner-induced domain shifts before clinical deployment.

## Method Summary
The study employs the CHIME Multiscanner dataset (384 breast cancer WSIs scanned on five devices) and the TCGA-BRCA cohort. For each of 14 PFMs, frozen tile-level embeddings are extracted from preprocessed 256×256 tissue tiles. Slide-level embeddings are obtained via mean pooling. Unsupervised robustness is quantified using geometric metrics: average pairwise cosine distance (Dcos), 1-NN match rate, Mantel correlation, and Intersection-over-K. For supervised evaluation, ABMIL classifiers are trained on TCGA-BRCA for tasks (ER, PR, HER2, NHG) and evaluated on each scanner of CHIME. Performance is measured by AUC, Fleiss’ κ (prediction consistency across scanners), and calibration stability via LOWESS curves comparing predicted probabilities between scanner pairs.

## Key Results
- Scanner variability systematically distorted tile- and slide-level embedding geometry across most PFMs, with increased pairwise cosine distance, reduced 1-NN match rate, and lower Mantel correlation.
- Although discriminative AUC often remained stable, prediction consistency (Fleiss’ κ) varied substantially and predicted probabilities exhibited systematic calibration shifts, revealing scanner-dependent bias.
- No model provided fully reliable robustness; vision-language models (CONCH, CONCHv1.5) showed the best embedding stability and calibration consistency, but robustness did not scale with model size, recency, or training data volume.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
**Mechanism 1: Embedding geometry distortion.** Different whole-slide scanners introduce systematic variations in color, resolution, and post-processing. These low-level artifacts propagate through the frozen PFM encoder into the high-dimensional embedding space, altering both local (neighbourhood) and global (pairwise distance) relationships. Metrics such as increased Dcos, reduced MR1NN, and lower Mantel confirm this distortion. A robust feature extractor would produce consistent representations for identical tissue across devices; the break condition is near-zero pairwise cosine distance and perfect 1-NN match across scanners.

**Mechanism 2: Calibration bias despite stable AUC.** Downstream ABMIL classifiers trained on embeddings from one scanner learn decision boundaries tied to that scanner’s feature distribution. When applied to embeddings from another scanner (with shifted geometry), predicted probabilities drift non-uniformly, breaking calibration consistency. Clinical reliability requires stable probabilities across acquisition contexts. The break condition is LOWESS curves aligning with the diagonal and Fleiss’ κ approaching 1.

**Mechanism 3: Robustness is not a simple function of scale.** Vision-language models (CONCH) trained on image-caption pairs may force abstraction away from scanner-specific cues; distillation (H0-mini) can explicitly optimize for feature space stability. Evidence: CONCH/CONCHv1.5 outperform larger vision-only models; H0-mini beats its larger teacher. The break condition would be if larger models consistently outperformed all robustness metrics, indicating scale as primary driver.

## Foundational Learning
- **Domain shift (scanner-induced):** Understanding scanner variability as a primary, clinically realistic source of distribution shift, separate from biological confounders. *Why needed:* To evaluate PFM generalisation to real-world acquisition diversity. *Quick check:* If two WSIs are from the same patient but scanned on different devices, should a robust PFM produce nearly identical slide-level embeddings? (Answer: They should be highly similar, not necessarily identical, because scanner noise should not dominate the biological signal.)
- **Embedding geometry consistency:** Using geometric metrics (pairwise cosine, Mantel, IoK) to quantify representation stability beyond task accuracy. *Why needed:* To capture whether the feature space preserves morphological relationships across scanners. *Quick check:* What does a high 1-NN match rate but low Mantel correlation suggest? (Answer: Local neighbourhoods are preserved but global pairwise relationships are distorted.)
- **Calibration stability vs. discriminative performance:** AUC can remain high even if predicted probabilities drift across scanners. *Why needed:* Calibration stability is critical for trustworthy clinical thresholds; this paper reveals it as a hidden failure mode. *Quick check:* Why is a model with stable AUC but unstable calibration probabilities unsuitable for deployment across multiple scanner sites? (Answer: Fixed decision thresholds would yield variable sensitivity/specificity across sites; probability estimates are unreliable for clinical decision-making.)

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component map:**  
WSI -> tile extraction (256x256) -> frozen PFM encoder -> tile embeddings -> mean-pooling -> slide embeddings (unsupervised) OR ABMIL aggregator -> slide-level prediction -> classifier head -> output probability.

**Critical path:** For unsupervised analysis: WSI → tile extraction → PFM encoder → mean-pool → slide embedding. For supervised evaluation: WSI → tile extraction → PFM encoder → ABMIL → classifier → prediction.

**Design tradeoffs:**
- Frozen vs fine-tuned encoder: Frozen isolates intrinsic robustness; fine-tuning could mitigate shifts but obscures encoder properties.
- Tile-level vs slide-level analysis: Tile-level reveals fine-grained scanner signatures; slide-level aggregates but may dilute local shifts.
- Geometric metrics vs AUC: Geometric metrics expose embedding distortions; AUC may hide them. Both are needed.
- Memory vs completeness: Using all tissue tiles is ideal but memory-intensive; sampling may affect metrics.

**Failure signatures:**
- High Dcos + low MR1NN + low Mantel → embedding space distortion and neighbourhoods not preserved.
- High AUC but low Fleiss’ κ + non-diagonal LOWESS → predictions discriminative but inconsistent/biased across scanners.
- ResNet-IN pattern: Low Dcos but also low MR1NN/Mantel → collapsed feature space (high density) masquerading as robustness.

**First 3 experiments:**
1. UMAP sanity check: Visualize tile- and slide-level embeddings coloured by scanner. Expect clusters for sensitive models; overlap for robust ones (CONCH/CONCHv1.5).
2. Quantitative embedding metrics: Compute Dcos, MR1NN, Mantel, IoK for all scanner pairs across all PFMs. Expect CONCH/v1.5 at top, Virchow/Phikon-v2 at bottom.
3. Supervised calibration test: Train ABMIL on TCGA-BRCA, evaluate on CHIME multiscanner. Plot LOWESS curves for scanner pairs; check deviation from diagonal and Fleiss’ κ on hard cases (e.g., NHG 1 vs 3).

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
1. Do scanner-induced domain shifts generalize to other tissue types and staining protocols beyond breast cancer H&E? (Only breast cancer evaluated; robustness in other contexts unknown.)
2. Which specific mitigation strategies (e.g., stain normalization, scanner-aware augmentations, contrastive losses) effectively improve embedding stability and calibration consistency of PFMs across scanners? (No systematic assessment done.)
3. What is the relative contribution of pretraining data diversity versus multimodal objectives in conferring improved scanner robustness as seen in vision-language models? (Hypothesized but not isolated.)

## Limitations
- The CHIME Multiscanner dataset is not publicly available, hindering independent verification.
- Implementation specifics (UMAP hyperparameters, LOWESS smoothing span, exact ABMIL splits) are not fully documented.
- Findings are limited to breast cancer WSIs; generalizability to other tissue types and scanners is unknown.

## Confidence
- PFMs exhibit systematic embedding distortion across scanners: **High**
- Discriminative AUC remains stable but calibration shifts occur: **High**
- Vision-language models (CONCH) show superior robustness: **Medium**
- Robustness does not scale with model size/recency: **Medium**
- Distillation improves robustness (H0-mini): **Medium**

## Next Checks
1. Replicate the study on an independent multiscanner dataset (e.g., SCORPION or MIDOG 2025 Track2) to verify embedding geometry and calibration results.
2. Evaluate whether standard preprocessing (e.g., Macenko color normalization) mitigates embedding shifts, using the same metrics (Dcos, MR1NN, Mantel).
3. Conduct a fine-tuning experiment where the PFM encoder is adapted on mixed-scanner data (TCGA+CHIME), then reassess embedding consistency and calibration stability.
