# Physics Informed Viscous Value Representations

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2602.23280
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.23280
- Reference count: 40
- Primary result: Improves success rates on 13 OGBench tasks, with dual representations achieving 96% on pointmaze-medium (+20), 84% on scene-play (+12), and 99% on cube-single-noisy where Eikonal fails; average success increases from 41% to 48%.

## Executive Summary
Offline goal-conditioned reinforcement learning (GCRL) suffers from inaccurate value estimation due to limited data coverage. Existing physics-informed regularizers based on first-order PDEs like Eikonal become ill‑posed in complex, high‑dimensional environments. This paper introduces a viscosity‑based regularization derived from the Hamilton–Jacobi–Bellman (HJB) equation, recast via the Feynman–Kac theorem into a tractable Monte Carlo objective that avoids unstable higher‑order gradients. The method provides a physics prior that bounds value updates during learning.

Evaluated on 13 tasks from the OGBench benchmark, the approach improves geometric consistency and success rates compared to Eikonal and other baselines. Combined with dual‑goal representations, it achieves 96% on pointmaze‑medium (+20 absolute), 84% on scene‑play (+12), and 99% on cube‑single‑noisy where Eikonal completely fails. Across tasks, average success increases from 41% to 48%, demonstrating robustness and scalability to high‑dimensional manipulation and stochastic domains.

## Method Summary
The method augments any offline GCRL algorithm (e.g., GCIVL, IQL, dual‑goal representations) with a viscous regularization term $\mathcal{L}_{\text{phy}}$. Inspired by the vanishing viscosity solution to HJB, the regularizer enforces a bound on the value function:
$$V(s,g) \leq \mathbb{E}_{s'}[V(s',g)] + q(s)\Delta t/\nu,$$
where $\nu$ is the viscosity parameter, $q(s)$ is a running cost (often constant), and $\Delta t=1$.  
To avoid explicitly computing the Laplacian (which would require second‑order gradients), the authors use the Feynman–Kac theorem. This reformulates the expectation as a Monte Carlo average over states perturbed by Gaussian noise: $s' = s + \min(\nu, d_{\partial X})\cdot\epsilon$ with $\epsilon\sim\mathcal{N}(0,I)$, clipped to stay within environment bounds. The bound is evaluated using a target network $\bar{V}$ for stability. The total loss combines the standard TD loss and $\mathcal{L}_{\text{phy}}$ (typically squared violation) with weight 1.0. The approach is representation‑agnostic but shows best results with dual‑goal representations that pre‑compute distances to anchor states.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
**Claim:** Viscosity regularization bounds value updates, preventing uncontrolled growth and improving geometric consistency.  
**Mechanism:** The vanishing viscosity solution adds a Laplacian term $ν ∆V$ to the HJB equation, ensuring uniqueness and smoothing. The resulting Hamiltonian $H = q(s) - \frac{1}{2} \|∇V\|^2 + ν∆V$ (equation 3) regularizes updates. Via Cole‑Hopft and Feynman‑Kac, this yields the bound $V(s,g) ≤ \mathbb{E}_{s'}[V(s',g)] + q(s)Δt/ν$ (equation 6). The loss $\mathcal{L}_{phy}$ (equation 7) enforces it, stabilizing value iteration.  
**Core assumption:** The optimal value satisfies the viscosity HJB, and the random walk with step $ν$ approximates the diffusion process.  
**Evidence anchors:** [abstract]; [section 4.1] (Equation 3); [section 4.3] (Equations 6,7); corpus limited.  
**Break condition:** If $ν$ is too small (e.g., ≤ 1e‑3), contours align with obstacles rather than geodesics (Figure 7d). Teleport gaps larger than $ν$ remain disconnected.

### Mechanism 2
**Claim:** Feynman‑Kac reformulation avoids unstable higher‑order gradients by replacing Laplacian computation with Monte Carlo sampling.  
**Mechanism:** The linearized PDE (after Cole‑Hopft) is solved via Feynman‑Kac as an expectation over trajectories. Instead of computing $∆V$, we sample perturbed states $s' = s + \mathcal{N}(0, ν I)$ (clipped) and evaluate $V(s')$. This estimator requires only first‑order gradients.  
**Core assumption:** The value function $Ψ$ is sufficiently smooth, and the discrete random walk approximates the continuous diffusion.  
**Evidence anchors:** [abstract]; [section 4.2]; [section 4.3] (Equation 5 and perturbation description); corpus: theoretical foundations exist in stochastic control literature but are absent from the provided neighbor corpus.  
**Break condition:** In non‑Euclidean state spaces (e.g., image latents), Gaussian noise violates geometry, reducing effectiveness (Powderworld results). Large $Δt$ also introduces discretization error.

### Mechanism 3
**Claim:** Viscous regularization enforces orthogonal value contours aligned with geodesics, unlike Eikonal which distorts in complex geometries.  
**Mechanism:** The Laplacian term promotes harmonic behavior away from sources, causing level sets to intersect boundaries orthogonally. Eikonal’s constant‑gradient constraint ($|∇V|=1$) forces contours to wrap around corners. Viscosity allows gradient magnitude to vary, preserving orthogonality.  
**Core assumption:** Isotropic dynamics and smooth running cost; continuous state space.  
**Evidence anchors:** [abstract] ("improves geometric consistency"); [section 5.1] (Figure 2 and text: "Our viscous formulation strictly preserves orthogonal, geodesic‑aligned contours."); [section 5.1] (Figure 4 shows Eikonal pushes policies into walls, ours aligns parallel); corpus: the Eik‑HIQL baseline [1] discusses geometry, but viscous correction is novel.  
**Break condition:** Anisotropic dynamics or non‑Euclidean metrics break orthogonality. Constant speed assumption ($S(s)=1$) may not hold universally.

## Foundational Learning
- **Viscosity solution**  
  *Why needed:* Provides a well‑posed HJB solution despite non‑smoothities (obstacles, contacts), forming the theoretical basis for the physics prior.  
  *Quick check:* Why are classical derivatives insufficient for HJB in environments with obstacles?
- **Feynman‑Kac theorem**  
  *Why needed:* Enables sampling‑based computation instead of unstable second‑order gradient calculation.  
  *Quick check:* How does the theorem convert a PDE into a stochastic process expectation?
- **Offline goal‑conditioned RL**  
  *Why needed:* The target setting where limited data coverage causes value estimation errors; the physics prior compensates.  
  *Quick check:* What makes offline GCRL value learning particularly prone to bias from dataset coverage?

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component map:** Replay buffer -> Sample batch -> TD loss computation -> Perturbation sampling (K walks) -> Expectation (target network) -> L_phy -> Total loss -> Online network update -> Target network polyak update.  

**Critical path:**  
1. Sample batch $(s, g, a, s', ...)$ from replay.  
2. Compute TD loss on $V(s,g)$.  
3. For each $s$, sample $K$ perturbed $s'$, compute target values using $θ̄$, compute $\mathcal{L}_{\text{phy}}$.  
4. Total loss = TD loss + λ·$\mathcal{L}_{\text{phy}}$.  
5. Update $θ$, then update $θ̄$ via Polyak ($τ=0.005$).  

**Design tradeoffs:**  
- $ν$: larger improves global connectivity but may oversmooth; tuned ≥ 1e‑2.  
- $K$: more walks reduce variance; saturates ~ 10.  
- Clipping to boundaries ensures valid states but may limit diffusion.  
- $q(s)$: should match speed profile; constant assumption may limit applicability.  
- Representation‑agnostic: works with various backbones, best with dual.  

**Failure signatures:**  
- Contours parallel to walls → $ν$ too small.  
- Teleport/stitch failure → $ν$ smaller than gap size.  
- Powderworld latent space → non‑Euclidean geometry breaks assumption.  
- Instability → imbalance between TD and physics loss weights.  

**First 3 experiments:**  
1. **Backbone ablation:** Apply FK to Original, VIB, Dual (Table 1). Consistent improvement; Dual‑FK averages 48% vs Dual’s 41%.  
2. **Eikonal comparison:** Dual‑FK vs Dual+EIK (Table 2). FK excels in complex manipulation (scene‑play 84% vs 13%) and noisy tasks.  
3. **Hyperparameters:** Vary $K$ and $ν$ (Table 3b). $K≥5$ stabilizes; $ν≥1e‑2$ needed for good geometry.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
1. **How can viscosity‑based physics‑informed regularization be effectively adapted for offline model‑free goal‑conditioned reinforcement learning?**  
   The authors note that this remains an open question. Their method partially addresses it but still shows limitations in massive long‑horizon tasks and requires careful hyperparameter tuning ($ν$, $K$). Evidence needed includes theoretical convergence guarantees under function approximation, systematic empirical evaluation on diverse offline datasets, and analysis of estimator variance impact.

2. **Can physics‑informed regularization eliminate the need for hierarchical representations in solving complex, long‑horizon goal‑conditioned tasks?**  
   The introduction questions whether physics‑informed strategies can solve such tasks without hierarchies. The method improves performance on mid‑horizon tasks but still struggles on extremely long horizons unless combined with hierarchical policies. Evidence: experiments combining viscous regularization with hierarchical architectures on standardized long‑horizon benchmarks.

3. **How can viscous regularization be effectively imposed in high‑dimensional visual domains where the latent space of deep networks is non‑Euclidean?**  
   The conclusion identifies extending to pixel domains via latent‑space regularization as a key challenge and proposes "latent manifold‑aware regularization" as future work. Direct application in the Impala encoder’s latent space yields only superficial gains due to violations of Euclidean geometric assumptions. Evidence: development of regularization techniques that respect manifold structure, empirical improvements on stochastic visual tasks like Powderworld, theoretical analysis of geometric priors transfer.

4. **What are the theoretical properties (well‑posedness, uniqueness) of the viscous HJB regularization when applied to high‑dimensional continuous spaces with unknown dynamics?**  
   The paper uses viscosity solutions to avoid ill‑posedness of first‑order PDEs but does not formally analyze the regularized objective’s properties in offline RL. The derivation relies on Cole‑Hopft transformation and Feynman‑Kac without proofs regarding convergence stability or generalization bounds under function approximation and limited data. Evidence: mathematical analysis linking viscosity parameter $ν$ to optimization landscape conditioning; experiments correlating $ν$ with sample efficiency and robustness; connections to existing RL theory for regularized value iteration.

## Limitations
- Exact dual‑goal representation construction details (BFS on offline data, handling stochastic transitions) are missing, blocking exact replication of DUAL baseline results.
- Exact scaling and form of cost function $q(s)$ relative to $ν$ are unspecified, affecting regularization strength.
- Implementation for pixel‑based tasks (Powderworld) in latent space is vague regarding perturbation clipping, validity checks, and geometry assumptions.

## Confidence
- Viscosity regularization bounds value updates: Medium. Empirical evidence supports improved stability and success rates, but theoretical guarantees under function approximation are not established.
- Feynman‑Kac avoids higher‑order gradients: Medium–High. The reformulation is theoretically sound and empirically effective, but the Monte Carlo estimator introduces variance that requires careful tuning.
- Viscous regularization improves geometric consistency vs. Eikonal: Medium. Visual and qualitative results show superiority, but quantitative orthogonality metrics are limited.
