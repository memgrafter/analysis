# DeMa: Dual-Path Delay-Aware Mamba for Efficient Multivariate Time Series Analysis

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2601.05527
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.05527
- Reference count: 40
- Primary result: State-of-the-art performance across four MTS tasks; up to 25.8% lower MSE on PEMS04; top-1 ranks in 34/36 long-term forecasting settings; linear O(NLd²) complexity.

## Executive Summary
DeMa tackles key challenges in applying state-space models like Mamba to multivariate time series (MTS) data: insufficient modeling of cross-variate relationships, entanglement of temporal and variate contexts, and poor handling of time-lag effects. It introduces a dual-path backbone that first applies an Adaptive Fourier Filter to split the input into Cross-Time (low-frequency, intra-series) and Cross-Variate (high-frequency, inter-series) components. Each component is processed by a specialized Mamba module: the temporal path employs Mamba-SSD for parallel, series-independent long-range temporal modeling, while the variate path uses Mamba-DALA with delay-aware linear attention to capture lagged cross-variate dependencies. The outputs are fused and transformed by a feed-forward network to produce the final task-specific prediction.

DeMa achieves state-of-the-art performance across a diverse set of MTS tasks including long-term and short-term forecasting, imputation, classification, and anomaly detection. Notably, it reduces prediction error by up to 25.8% on the PEMS04 dataset compared to the strongest baseline and secures top-1 rankings in 34 out of 36 long-term forecasting benchmarks. The design maintains linear computational complexity O(NLd²), yielding superior scalability to Transformer-based models as sequence length and number of variates grow, while offering an excellent accuracy-efficiency trade-off.

## Method Summary
DeMa's architecture comprises three stages: decomposition, dual-path processing, and task-specific output. First, an Adaptive Fourier Filter (AFF) applies a Fast Fourier Transform along the temporal dimension, selects the top θ fraction of frequency bands by amplitude (low-frequency), and inverse transforms to obtain the Cross-Time component; the remaining high-frequency part forms the Cross-Variate component. Next, each component undergoes RevIN normalization, patchification with learnable patch size P, and linear projection to embeddings of dimension D. The embeddings are rearranged via two scans: Cross-Time Scan stacks the variates dimension first (shape N×L×D) to treat each series independently, while Cross-Variate Scan stacks the time steps first (shape L×N×D) to emphasize interactions across series.

Both representations are fed into B identical DuoMNet blocks. In the temporal path, Mamba-SSD applies a selective state-space model with a semi-separable diagonal (SSD) parallel scan, enforcing independence across variates by zeroing hidden states at series boundaries, thus modeling long-range intra-series dynamics. The variate path employs Mamba-DALA, which estimates a global correlation delay τ between each pair of variates via cross-correlation, maps it to a token shift Δ, and combines it with rotary position embeddings (RoPE) to enable delay-aware linear attention with a causal mask. The outputs of both paths are normalized, weighted by fixed scalars α and β, summed, and passed through a feed-forward network with a residual connection. The block outputs are aggregated across depths, and a final MLP (or softmax) head produces the prediction for forecasting, imputation, anomaly detection, or classification. The overall complexity scales as O(NLd²) per block, allowing efficient training on long, high-dimensional MTS.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechananism 1: Frequency-domain decomposition disentangles dynamics
- Claim: Decomposing MTS into Cross-Time (low-frequency) and Cross-Variate (residual) components reduces entanglement of intra- and inter-series dependencies, enabling dedicated modeling.
- Mechanism: Adaptive Fourier Filter applies FFT along time, ranks amplitudes, selects top θ fraction as low-frequency bands (global structures), and inverse FFT to obtain Cross-Time component; remainder forms Cross-Variate component.
- Core assumption: Intra-series temporal dynamics (trends, cycles) concentrate in low frequencies, while inter-series interactions (time-varying cross-variate effects) appear in higher frequencies.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract]: "decomposes MTS into Cross-Time (intra-series dynamics) and Cross-Variate (inter-series interactions) components."
  - [section 3.1.1]: Defines AFF via FFT filtering (Eq. 8) and motivates spectral separation.
  - [corpus]: Corpus neighbors on MTS/Mamba do not specifically address frequency decomposition; evidence is weak.
- Break condition: If components are non-orthogonal (Theorem 1), they share basis directions and separation is incomplete. Contamination occurs when inter-series interactions have strong low-frequency content.

### Mechanism 2: Dual-path Mamba with specialized modules
- Claim: Separate temporal and variate paths with Mamba-SSD and Mamba-DALA capture cross-time and delay-aware cross-variate dependencies effectively while maintaining linear complexity.
- Mechanism:
  - Temporal path: Cross-Time Scan stacks variates into (N, L, D); Mamba-SSD applies selective SSM with SSD parallel scan, zeroing hidden states across variates to enforce independence, modeling intra-series dynamics.
  - Variate path: Cross-Variate Scan stacks tokens by position into (L, N, D); Mamba-DALA estimates global correlation delay (τ_ab) via cross-correlation, maps to token shift Δ_ab, and uses RoPE for token-level relative delay; then computes kernelized linear attention with causal constraint.
- Core assumption: Intra-series dynamics are largely independent across variates; cross-variate dependencies can be modeled by linear attention with explicit delay awareness.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract]: "temporal path uses Mamba-SSD for parallel, series-independent long-range temporal modeling; variate path uses Mamba-DALA with delay-aware linear attention."
  - [section 3.2.2–3.2.3]: Detailed algorithms (Eqs. 13–26) for Mamba-SSD and Mamba-DALA.
  - [corpus]: Related Mamba-MTS works (e.g., CMamba, S-Mamba) propose cross-variate modeling but without dual-path delay-aware design; evidence is moderate.
- Break condition: If intra-series dynamics exhibit strong cross-variate temporal patterns, temporal path misses them. If delay estimation (τ_ab) is inaccurate or dependencies non-linear, variate path underperforms.

### Mechanism 3: Linear complexity via SSD and linear attention
- Claim: DeMa achieves O(NL d²) complexity, enabling scalability to long sequences and many variates while retaining accuracy.
- Mechanism: Mamba-SSD uses SSD to reformulate selective SSM as semi-separable matrix multiplication, parallel over N variates (O(NL d²)). Mamba-DALA uses kernelized linear attention (O(LN d²)). Both paths compute in parallel, maintaining per-block O(NL d²).
- Core assumption: In long-horizon MTS, N and L dominate d (i.e., N, L ≫ d), so linear in NL outweighs quadratic in d.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract]: "linear computational complexity O(NLd²)" and "superior scalability—lower runtime and memory usage than Transformer baselines."
  - [section 3.4]: Formal complexity analysis and comparison with Transformers.
  - [section 4.6]: Figure 5 empirically shows linear vs. quadratic scaling with series length.
  - [corpus]: Mamba-2 introduced SSD for linear complexity; linear attention is established. Corpus supports efficiency claims indirectly.
- Break condition: If d becomes large (comparable to N or L), d² term dominates. Constant factors may still make Transformers competitive for moderate lengths.

## Architecture Onboarding

Component map:
- Input: Raw MTS X ∈ ℝ^{N×T}.
- Adaptive Fourier Filter: Splits into Cross-Time eX and Cross-Variate ˆX via FFT filtering (Eq. 8).
- Tokenization: RevIN + patching (length P) + linear embedding → embeddings for each component.
- Scan: Cross-Time Scan (stack variates) → eX ∈ ℝ^{N×L×D}; Cross-Variate Scan (stack tokens) → ˆX ∈ ℝ^{L×N×D}.
- DuoMNet Block (B blocks):
  * Temporal path: eX_b → LayerNorm → Mamba-SSD → eY_b; residual: eX_{b+1} = eX_b - eY_b.
  * Variate path: ˆX_b → LayerNorm → Mamba-DALA → ˆY_b; residual: ˆX_{b+1} = ˆX_b - ˆY_b.
  * Fusion: U_b = α·LN(eY_b) + β·LN(ˆY_b).
  * FFN: Z_b = LN(U_b + FFN(U_b)).
- Aggregation: Z = Σ_{b=1}^B Z_b.
- Task head: MLP for regression (forecasting/imputation/anomaly) or softmax for classification.

Critical path:
1. Compute AFF on raw input.
2. Tokenize and scan each component to get eX₁, ˆX₁.
3. For each DuoMNet block:
   a. Temporal: eY_b = Mamba-SSD(eX_b); eX_{b+1} = eX_b - eY_b.
   b. Variate: ˆY_b = Mamba-DALA(ˆX_b); ˆX_{b+1} = ˆX_b - ˆY_b.
   c. Fuse: U_b = α LN(eY_b) + β LN(ˆY_b).
   d. Z_b = LN(U_b + FFN(U_b)).
4. Aggregate Z = Σ Z_b.
5. Apply task head.

Design tradeoffs:
- AFF: θ controls split; extreme θ may lose information. FFT adds overhead but disentangles.
- Dual-path: ~2× parameters/compute vs. single-path; fusion weights (α,β) require task-specific tuning.
- Mamba-SSD: Assumes variate independence; if cross-variate temporal patterns exist, consider hybrid.
- Mamba-DALA: Delay estimation via cross-correlation is heuristic; may fail for non-linear/non-stationary lags.
- Patch size P: Smaller P → longer L, finer details but higher compute; larger P → coarser, faster.

Failure signatures:
- Ablation: Removing AFF, Mamba-SSD, or Mamba-DALA causes large performance drop (Table 5). If not, components may be incorrectly wired.
- Scalability: Runtime/memory should scale ~linearly with L (Fig. 5). Quadratic growth indicates SSD/linear attention not properly implemented.
- Task sensitivity: Imputation/anomaly detection sensitive to α,β (Fig. 6). Poor choice leads to underfitting one path.
- Delay modeling: On lag-critical datasets (e.g., traffic), Mamba-DALA must contribute. Verify delay estimation (τ_ab) and DALA's causal masking.

First 3 experiments:
1. **Ablation study**: On Traffic (forecasting) or PEMS04 (short-term), remove AFF or replace Mamba-SSD/DALA. Expect significant MSE increase when any key component is missing (Table 5 rows 5–7).
2. **Scalability test**: On ETTh1, vary lookback length (384–3072). Measure GPU memory and runtime per iteration. Expect DeMa to scale linearly while Transformers (e.g., PatchTST) scale quadratically (Fig. 5).
3. **Cross-task validation**: Evaluate on at least three tasks (e.g., long-term forecasting on Electricity, imputation on ETTh1, classification on Heartbeat). Compare against strong baselines (iTransformer, ModernTCN). Expect DeMa to achieve top-2 accuracy with better efficiency.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

Open Question 1
- Question: Can the spectral decomposition in the Adaptive Fourier Filter be made adaptive or learned to better disentangle cross-time and cross-variate factors across diverse MTS datasets?
- Basis in paper: [inferred] Section 3.1.1 describes using FFT to split frequencies by amplitude (top θ fraction), relying on the heuristic that low frequencies capture intra-series dynamics and high frequencies capture inter-series interactions.
- Why unresolved: The fixed θ and amplitude-based split are dataset-dependent and may not generalize; a learnable decomposition could adapt to varying spectral characteristics.
- What evidence would resolve it: Introduce a parameterized decomposition module (e.g., a small neural network or attention-based weighting of frequency components) and compare its performance and robustness against the fixed Fourier approach on a suite of benchmark datasets.

Open Question 2
- Question: Does modeling only a single global correlation delay per variate pair suffice, or should cross-variate dependencies incorporate time-varying delays to reflect dynamic propagation effects in real-world MTS?
- Basis in paper: [inferred] In Section 3.2.3, Mamba-DALA computes a global τab via cross-correlation and uses a static shift ∆ab for all tokens, while token-level relative delays are captured via RoPE.
- Why unresolved: Real systems often exhibit non-stationary delay patterns (e.g., traffic congestion propagation speeds vary with time-of-day); a static offset may oversimplify.
- What evidence would resolve it: Extend DALA to allow τ to be predicted per token (e.g., via a lightweight network) and evaluate improvement on tasks sensitive to delay accuracy like short-term forecasting or anomaly detection.

Open Question 3
- Question: Is a fixed weighted sum (α, β) for fusing temporal and variate paths optimal, or can we learn the fusion dynamically based on the input or task?
- Basis in paper: [inferred] Section 3.2.4 uses fixed hyperparameters α and β (selected via grid search) to combine normalized path outputs (Eq. 28).
- Why unresolved: The relative importance of temporal versus cross-variate cues likely varies across datasets and even within a series; static weights may not fully exploit their complementary information.
- What evidence would resolve it: Replace fixed weights with a learned gating mechanism (e.g., attention over path representations) or a meta-network that predicts α, β from the input; assess performance gains across tasks.

Open Question 4
- Question: How does DeMa's computational efficiency scale with extremely high numbers of variates (N) and long token sequences (L) in practice, and what are the memory bottlenecks of Mamba-SSD and Mamba-DALA?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper states computational complexity O(2NLd^2) (Section 3.4) and demonstrates linear scaling up to N=862 and L≈100 (Fig 5). However, constant factors and state memory usage are not deeply analyzed.
- Why unresolved: For massive multivariate systems (e.g., sensor networks with thousands of variates), the per-block memory for storing state matrices (e.g., in SSD) may become prohibitive; the theoretical linear scaling may not translate to wall-clock efficiency due to memory bandwidth.
- What evidence would resolve it: Conduct systematic experiments varying N from 100 to >5000 and L from 100 to >1000 on synthetic data, measuring GPU memory, throughput, and scaling exponent; compare with theoretical predictions and identify bottlenecks.

## Limitations
- Spectral separation assumption may fail when cross-variate effects have strong low-frequency content, causing contamination.
- Dual-path design requires careful tuning of fusion weights and doubles parameters.
- Delay estimation via cross-correlation may be inaccurate for non-linear or non-stationary dependencies.
- Independent validation is limited; related work has low citations.

## Confidence
- Frequency decomposition disentangles dynamics: Medium
- Dual-path Mamba with Mamba-SSD/DALA: Medium
- Linear complexity O(NLd²): High
- SOTA across forecasting/imputation/classification/anomaly: High
- Scalability vs Transformers: High

## Next Checks
1. Frequency separation analysis: On datasets with known mixed-frequency dynamics (e.g., synthetic MTS with controlled low-frequency cross-variate links), compute component orthogonality and compare with ground truth. Measure performance when AFF is replaced by PCA-based separation.
2. Delay estimation robustness: Replace cross-correlation τ estimation with a learnable lag module (e.g., via differentiable soft-DTW) or use ground-truth lags on synthetic data. Evaluate impact on accuracy, especially under non-linear lag structures.
3. Hyperparameter sensitivity: Systematically vary α,β and patch size P across multiple tasks (forecasting, imputation, classification). Plot performance heatmaps to assess tuning difficulty and identify robust regions.
