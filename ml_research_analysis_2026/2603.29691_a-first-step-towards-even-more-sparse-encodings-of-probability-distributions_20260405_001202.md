# A First Step Towards Even More Sparse Encodings of Probability Distributions

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2603.29691
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.29691
- **Reference count:** 17
- **Primary result:** CoFE reduces parfactor exponential formula counts (e.g., 8→2) while keeping low query error under low noise and halving Hellinger distance under high noise, via Hellinger-bounded potential merging and formula minimization.

## Executive Summary
CoFE (Combinatorial Formula Extraction) addresses the exponential number of formulas required to encode lifted probability distributions (parfactors) as Markov Logic Networks. It merges similar potential values within a Hellinger distance ε using either DBSCAN clustering or quantile binning, then extracts Boolean formulas from the reduced potentials and minimizes them with Quine-McCluskey.

Evaluation on benchmark (smokers) and artificial datasets shows significant compression: formula counts drop exponentially (e.g., from 8 to 2), with short formula lengths (1–7 atoms). Under low noise, mean absolute query errors are low (0.004–0.021). Even with high noise (σ=1), CoFE halves the Hellinger distance to the original model compared to the noised input, effectively filtering noise while preserving core structure.

## Method Summary
CoFE tackles the exponential blow-up in formula count when transforming parfactors (lifted probability distributions) into Markov Logic Networks. Its three-step pipeline first reduces the number of distinct potential values by clustering or quantizing them within a Hellinger distance ε; this keeps the overall distribution close to the original while drastically cutting the number of different numbers. Next, for each remaining potential, a Boolean conjunction over the predicate’s random variables is extracted, with weight set to the natural logarithm of the potential. Finally, formulas with identical weights are fed to Quine-McCluskey to derive a minimal disjunctive normal form, merging many conjunctions into one compact formula.

The authors evaluate CoFE on the standard smokers dataset and on artificial parfactors, adding Gaussian noise of varying standard deviations. They report exponential reductions in formula counts (e.g., from 8 to 2), lengths of 1–7 atoms per formula, and low mean absolute query errors under low noise (0.004–0.021). Even under high noise (σ=1), the reduction still halves the Hellinger distance to the original noiseless model, demonstrating effective noise filtering.

## Key Results
- Exponential reduction in formula count (example: from 8 to 2 for a 3-Boolean-PRV parfactor).
- Short formula lengths (1–7 atoms) and low mean absolute query error under low noise (0.004–0.021).
- Under higher noise (σ=1), CoFE still halves the Hellinger distance to the original model relative to the noised input, effectively filtering noise.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1
- **Claim:** CoFE reduces the number of distinct potentials in a parfactor by merging similar values within a Hellinger distance ε, enabling fewer formulas.
- **Mechanism:** Potentials are grouped via DBSCAN clustering or quantile binning; each group is mapped to its mean, ensuring the Hellinger distance between original and reduced parfactor ≤ ε.
- **Core assumption:** Potentials close under Hellinger distance can be approximated by a single value without significant semantic loss in the distribution.

- **Break condition:** If no grouping satisfies ε (e.g., all potentials too dispersed), reduction fails; or if noise is high, distinct clusters may be erroneously merged.

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim:** Formula extraction followed by Quine-McCluskey minimization yields compact MLN representations from reduced potentials.
- **Mechanism:** Each reduced potential generates an initial logical formula (e.g., conjunction of PRV truth assignments); formulas with identical weights are bucketed and minimized into a single equivalent formula via Quine-McCluskey.
- **Core assumption:** Multiple formulas sharing a weight can be combined into a minimal formula without changing the joint distribution semantics.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract]: "then extracting and minimizing first-order formulas using Quine-McCluskey."
  - [section 3]: "the formulas of each bucket are set to be minimized into one minimal formula, for which CoFE uses the Quine-McCluskey algorithm."
  - [corpus]: Corpus includes work on Boolean minimization, but application to probabilistic logic formulas is specific to this paper; evidence is primarily from the paper.
- **Break condition:** Minimization may not reduce formula length if formulas have no shared structure, or computational limits may prevent exact minimization for large buckets.

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim:** CoFE filters noise from perturbed parfactors while preserving core information by reconstructing original cluster structures under bounded ε.
- **Mechanism:** With ε set smaller than noise amplitude, reduction maps noisy potentials back to original cluster means, denoising the distribution and recovering latent formulas.
- **Core assumption:** Noise introduces small perturbations; underlying potential clusters remain separable via density or quantile methods when ε is appropriately chosen.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract]: "effectively filtering noise while preserving core information."
  - [section 4.2]: "For Smokers1 and Art1, CoFE can effectively filter out the noise and reconstruct the original distribution."
  - [corpus]: Weak; corpus neighbors address noise in sampling or Bayesian networks, but not this specific clustering-based denoising for lifted distributions.
- **Break condition:** When noise standard deviation is too high (e.g., σ=1 in Smokers2), clusters overlap, and reduction mixes distinct values, failing reconstruction.

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component map:** Input (parfactor with PRVs and potentials) -> Reduction module (DBSCAN clustering/quantile binning) -> Extraction module (formula generation) -> Minimization module (Quine-McCluskey) -> Output (set of (minimized formula, weight) pairs representing an MLN).

**Critical path:**
1. Extract potentials from input parfactor.
2. Run reduction strategy; if Hellinger distance > ε, revert to no reduction.
3. For each reduced potential, create formula based on PRV assignments.
4. Group formulas by weight (log potential).
5. Minimize each group to one formula using Quine-McCluskey.
6. Output final formula set.

**Design tradeoffs:**
- ε vs. accuracy: Larger ε increases formula reduction but raises query error; requires user calibration.
- Clustering vs. quantiles: DBSCAN adapts to density (parameters θ_d, θ_n) but may fail on uniform data; quantiles are simpler but may not capture natural groupings.
- Minimization: Quine-McCluskey guarantees minimality but is exponential; for large buckets, consider heuristic methods.
- Noise handling: ε must be set relative to expected noise level; too small prevents denoising, too large causes over-merging.

**Failure signatures:**
- No formula count reduction: ε too small or potentials inherently distinct.
- High query error: ε too large or noise exceeding tolerance.
- DBSCAN output: Many tiny clusters (θ_n too low) or one large cluster (θ_d too high); indicate parameter misconfiguration.
- Minimization output: Formulas unchanged from extracted; suggests no shared structure across weights.

**First 3 experiments:**
1. **Reconstruction test:** Apply CoFE to a known parfactor (e.g., smokers example) with low noise; verify extracted formulas match latent structure (Eqs. (1)-(2)).
2. **Parameter sweep:** Systematically vary ε on a dataset; plot formula count reduction vs. mean absolute query error to identify elbow point.
3. **Strategy comparison:** On artificial datasets with controlled cluster separations, compare DBSCAN and quantile strategies for reduction efficiency and formula compactness.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
### Open Question 1
- **Question:** What alternative reduction methods can improve the trade-off between sparsity and fidelity compared to the current quantile and DBSCAN clustering strategies?
- **Basis in paper:** The conclusion explicitly states future work includes "testing out further reduction strategies."
- **Why unresolved:** The evaluation only compares two straightforward reduction strategies (quantiles and DBSCAN) without exploring a broader range of approximation or clustering techniques that might offer better compression for the same ε-bound.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Implement and empirically compare additional reduction methods (e.g., k-means, hierarchical clustering, or learned approximations) across diverse datasets, measuring formula count reduction and query error under controlled ε.

### Open Question 2
- **Question:** How do modifications to parfactor potentials within the ε-bound quantitatively affect the resulting formula count reduction and the mean absolute error in query answers?
- **Basis in paper:** The conclusion identifies "analyzing the relationship between the changes in the distribution and resulting reduction as well as error" as future work.
- **Why unresolved:** The paper uses fixed ε parameters in evaluation and does not systematically analyze how varying ε influences the dual objectives of compression and accuracy across different noise levels and dataset structures.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Conduct a controlled study varying ε over a fine-grained grid for multiple datasets, plotting reduction ratios against query errors to characterize the trade-off curve and identify optimal ε ranges.

### Open Question 3
- **Question:** Does the compact formula set produced by CoFE actually improve human interpretability compared to the canonical exponential MLN encoding?
- **Basis in paper:** The paper hypothesizes in Section 4.3 that fewer and shorter formulas enhance readability and states that investigating "the potential for increased readability and human understanding" is future work.
- **Why unresolved:** The claim is based on intuition; no user study or formal metric for interpretability is provided to validate that reduced formulas are easier for humans to understand.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Design and execute a controlled user experiment with domain experts, comparing comprehension tasks (e.g., explanation extraction, error detection) between canonical MLN encodings and CoFE’s output, using standardized readability metrics.

## Limitations
- **Noise threshold for reliable denoising:** The exact noise level (σ) at which CoFE's clustering fails to reconstruct original structures is not systematically mapped; only two σ values are tested, leaving the tolerance boundary unclear.
- **Minimization implementation fidelity:** The paper does not specify whether Quine-McCluskey handles "don't care" conditions or how it scales for large buckets; implementations may diverge, affecting formula lengths.
- **Query evaluation protocol:** The "representative queries" for MAE are vaguely defined (arbitrary grounding per PRV), risking non-comparable error metrics without a fixed random seed or query set.
