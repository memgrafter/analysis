# It's TIME: Towards the Next Generation of Time Series Forecasting Benchmarks

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2602.12147
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.12147
- **Reference count:** 40
- **Primary result:** TIME introduces 50 fresh datasets and 98 zero-shot tasks, eliminates data leakage, identifies top TSFMs (Chronos-2, TimesFM-2.5, TiRex), and demonstrates pattern-level evaluation for generalizable insights.

## Executive Summary
Time series forecasting (TSF) benchmarks suffer from legacy data contamination, misaligned tasks, and coarse analysis. TIME addresses these by introducing a next-generation benchmark built on 50 entirely fresh datasets, ensuring zero-shot evaluation without data leakage. A human-in-the-loop pipeline guarantees data integrity and aligns forecasting horizons with real-world operational needs. The benchmark features 98 zero-shot tasks and a novel pattern-level evaluation that uses structural time series features to group variates by temporal dynamics, enabling cross-dataset generalizable insights. Evaluation of 12 TSFMs reveals top performers and highlights the importance of multi-granular leaderboards. TIME provides a more rigorous, transparent, and practically relevant foundation for TSF research.

## Method Summary
TIME constructs its benchmark by curating 50 never-before-used datasets from diverse sources, explicitly avoiding any overlap with prior benchmarks to prevent data leakage. The pipeline combines automated screening—timestamp rectification, Ljung-Box test for white noise, IQR outlier filtering, correlation checks—with expert human review to finalize data quality and define forecasting horizons that match real-world operational contexts. For evaluation, 12 TSFMs are run zero-shot across rolling windows on test sets, producing point forecasts (MASE) and probabilistic forecasts (CRPS) normalized against a Seasonal Naive baseline. Pattern-level analysis extracts seven STL-based structural features per variate, thresholds them at the dataset median to create binary pattern codes, and aggregates normalized scores across variates sharing the same code. This reveals model performance conditioned on temporal dynamics such as trend strength, seasonality, and stationarity.

## Key Results
- **Top TSFMs:** Chronos-2, TimesFM-2.5, and TiRex achieve the best overall performance; newer model versions consistently improve over predecessors.
- **Pattern-specific strengths:** Models excel on variates with strong trends, seasonality, and non-stationarity. Rankings shift significantly with aggregation granularity (e.g., task vs. pattern level), underscoring the need for multi-granular leaderboards to capture complementary capabilities.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1
- **Claim:** Using exclusively fresh, never-before-benchmarked datasets eliminates data leakage, enabling a fair zero-shot evaluation of TSFMs.
- **Mechanism:** The authors curate datasets from government portals, industry partners, open-access sites, and competitions that have not been used in prior benchmarks. Manual curation ensures no overlap with legacy sources.
- **Core assumption:** Legacy datasets have already been ingested into large pre-training corpora of TSFMs, leading to inadvertent or malicious contamination that inflates performance.
- **Evidence anchors:** [abstract] "comprising 50 fresh datasets... free from data leakage"; [section] "Data Sources... We prioritize data novelty by curating fresh datasets... Legacy datasets... are likely to have been partially ingested into large-scale pre-training corpora."
- **Break condition:** If future work discovers that these "fresh" datasets were actually used in model pre-training (e.g., via data provenance audits) or if models trained on them leak into future pre-training corpora.

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim:** A human-in-the-loop pipeline combining automated screening with expert review ensures high data integrity and context-aligned task formulation.
- **Mechanism:** Automated checks (timestamp rectification, rule-based validation, Ljung-Box test, IQR outlier filtering, correlation checks) generate a Quality Summary. Human experts then make final decisions on variate/series pruning and define forecasting horizons aligned with real-world operational needs.
- **Core assumption:** Pure automation cannot fully capture domain-specific data quality nuances and practical forecasting constraints; human judgment is essential for final validation.
- **Evidence anchors:** [abstract] "integrating large language models and human expertise... ensure high data integrity and redefine task formulation by aligning forecasting configurations with real-world operational requirements"; [section] "Human Decision Making... dataset finalization via review" and "Context-Aligned Task Formulation".
- **Break condition:** If human decisions become inconsistent across datasets or if the process does not scale to larger benchmark construction, leading to bottlenecks or subjective biases.

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim:** Pattern-level evaluation using structural time series features reveals generalizable, diagnostic model capabilities across heterogeneous datasets, unlike coarse dataset-level rankings.
- **Mechanism:** STL decomposition extracts seven interpretable features (trend strength/linearity, seasonality strength/correlation, residual ACF1, complexity, stationarity). Variates are binarized (above/below median) into pattern codes. Retrieval of variates sharing identical codes allows aggregation of performance for a specific temporal pattern.
- **Core assumption:** Model performance is better explained by intrinsic temporal dynamics (e.g., non-stationarity, strong seasonality) than by superficial dataset metadata (e.g., domain, frequency).
- **Evidence anchors:** [abstract] "novel pattern-level evaluation uses structural time series features to enable cross-dataset, generalizable insights"; [section] "Pattern-Driven Benchmarking... By leveraging a curated set of interpretable temporal features... enables effective pattern-based stratification and retrieval".
- **Break condition:** If the selected features fail to capture key forecasting-relevant variations, or if median-based binarization proves too coarse, leading to spurious groupings that mask true model differences.

## Foundational Learning
- **STL Decomposition:** *Why needed:* The core of pattern feature extraction relies on decomposing series into trend, seasonality, and remainder to compute normalized strengths and residuals. *Quick check:* What are the three additive components from STL, and how does each contribute to the feature set?
- **Ljung-Box Test:** *Why needed:* Used in the automatic screening pipeline to detect white noise; variates failing this test are deemed unpredictable and removed. *Quick check:* What is the null hypothesis of the Ljung-Box test, and why would rejecting it be necessary for forecastability?
- **Normalized Metrics (MASE/CRPS):** *Why needed:* Allows comparison across datasets with different scales by scaling errors against a Seasonal Naive baseline; geometric mean aggregation handles multiplicative relationships robustly. *Quick check:* How is the normalized MASE computed, and what does a value less than 1 indicate about model performance relative to the baseline?

## Architecture Onboarding
### Component Map
Data Curation → Automated Screening (Quality Summary) → Human Review → Task Formulation → STL Feature Extraction → Pattern Encoding → Model Evaluation (rolling windows, MASE/CRPS) → Pattern Retrieval → Leaderboard Aggregation

### Critical Path
Fresh data acquisition → quality checks → expert finalization → context-aware horizon definition → feature computation → per-variate scoring → pattern-based grouping → geometric mean aggregation.

### Design Tradeoffs
- Freshness vs. dataset scale
- Automation throughput vs. human review cost
- Pattern granularity (binary codes) vs. data sparsity in intersectional groups; cross-dataset generalizability vs. loss of domain-specific nuance

### Failure Signatures
- High pairwise correlations between fresh TIME datasets and legacy ones (contamination)
- Inconsistent human decisions across similar series
- Pattern groups with <50 variates yielding unstable aggregates
- Visualization showing models produce overly conservative forecasts despite good metric scores

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
### Open Question 1
- **Question:** What is the most appropriate aggregation level (e.g., task-level, dataset-level, variate-level, or pattern-level) for constructing leaderboards in time series forecasting benchmarks to best reflect model utility?
- **Basis in paper:** In Section 6.3, the authors state: "Determining the most appropriate aggregation level remains an open question, highlighting that a single leaderboard ranking is not the sole metric for success and requires more granular quantitative analysis." This follows from their finding that model rankings vary significantly with aggregation granularity.
- **Why unresolved:** The paper demonstrates that rankings differ across aggregation levels (e.g., Chronos-2 dominates stationary data while TimesFM-2.5 leads on non-stationary), but offers no principled guidance on which level should be prioritized for standard evaluation or how it relates to real-world performance.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Studies correlating aggregation-level outcomes with actual deployment success across diverse applications (e.g., inventory management, energy forecasting) could identify which aggregated metrics best predict practical utility. Alternatively, theoretical work linking aggregation schemes to robustness or generalizability properties would provide a foundation.

### Open Question 2
- **Question:** How can pattern-level evaluation be extended to capture fine-grained temporal pattern combinations (e.g., high trend *and* high seasonality) without encountering data sparsity that undermines statistical reliability?
- **Basis in paper:** In Section G (Limitations), the authors explain: "Pattern Granularity and Data Sparsity... We explored finer-grained quantization (e.g., dividing features into 5 quintiles) but encountered a combinatorial explosion challenge... Future work will address this by expanding the scale of variates in the benchmark and developing more sophisticated pattern retrieval mechanisms."
- **Why unresolved:** The current binary encoding scheme (high/low per feature) may be too coarse to distinguish nuanced behaviors, yet increasing feature discretization multiplies subgroups, leaving many with too few variates for meaningful evaluation.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** The development of new retrieval or weighting methods—such as hierarchical pattern clustering or density-based grouping—that can combine sparse groups without losing statistical power, or the release of a substantially larger benchmark dataset ensuring sufficient coverage of all intersections.

### Open Question 3
- **Question:** What evaluation metrics should be adopted to assess the operational utility of forecasts in specific downstream tasks, moving beyond generic error measures like MASE and CRPS?
- **Basis in paper:** In Section G, the authors identify: "Task-Aligned Evaluation Metrics. Time series forecasting fundamentally serves downstream decision-making. However, standard general-purpose metrics like MASE and CRPS often fail to provide actionable insights specific to operational contexts." They note that different applications (e.g., finance, supply chain) require metrics aligned with their objectives (e.g., directional accuracy, stockout avoidance).
- **Why unresolved:** The benchmark currently relies on scale-independent error metrics that may not correlate with real-world decision quality; a low MASE does not guarantee that predictions are fit-for-purpose (e.g., capturing spikes or trends critical for operations).
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Empirical research linking forecast errors to downstream performance in representative domains, or the proposal of a standardized suite of task-specific metrics (e.g., profit-driven metrics for trading, service-level metrics for inventory) that can be integrated into future benchmark iterations.

## Limitations
- **Data and parameter opacity:** The curated datasets, exact task configurations (horizons, test splits), and pattern threshold medians are not publicly released, preventing exact replication and independent verification.
- **Human review scalability:** The human-in-the-loop pipeline lacks documented decision criteria and inter-rater reliability metrics, raising concerns about consistency, scalability, and potential subjective biases.
- **Pattern-level oversimplification:** Binary encoding of structural features may miss important interactions between temporal dynamics, and fine-grained grouping risks data sparsity without a larger variate pool.

## Confidence
- **Dataset Freshness/Leakage Elimination:** Medium
- **Zero-Shot Fairness via Fresh Data:** Medium
- **Pattern-Level Generalizability:** Low
