# PACED: Distillation and Self-Distillation at the Frontier of Student Competence

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2603.11178
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.11178
- **Reference count:** 40
- **Primary result:** PACED achieves substantial reasoning gains (+7.5 to +16.7 on MATH/AIME) with near-zero forgetting (~0.2% on MMLU) by focusing distillation on the student's zone of proximal development via pass-rate weighting.

## Executive Summary
PACED addresses a fundamental inefficiency in large language model distillation: training compute is wasted on problems the student has already mastered (yielding near-zero gradients) and those far beyond its reach (producing incoherent gradients that erode capabilities). The authors prove this waste is structurally inevitable, as the gradient signal-to-noise ratio in distillation provably vanishes at both the high and low extremes of the student's pass rate. To remedy this, PACED introduces a principled pass-rate weighting function, w(p) = p^α(1-p)^β, which concentrates learning on the "zone of proximal development"—the frontier of student competence. Derived from the boundary-vanishing structure of distillation gradients, this Beta kernel automatically suppresses trivial and intractable problems, requires only student rollouts to estimate pass rates, and is compatible with any KL divergence direction without architectural changes. Empirical results on reasoning benchmarks (MATH-500, AIME) demonstrate large gains while maintaining negligible catastrophic forgetting on MMLU.

## Method Summary
PACED computes a per-problem weight based on the student's estimated pass rate p on that problem. For each training prompt, the student generates K rollouts (default K=8) from its current model to estimate p as the fraction of correct solutions. Each problem's distillation loss is then scaled by w(p)=p^α(1-p)^β, typically with α=β=1 (symmetric Beta(2,2) kernel). The weights are normalized to unit mean over the training set. The core training loop applies these weighted losses using either forward KL (for cross-model distillation, where the teacher's distribution is broader) or reverse KL (for self-distillation, to consolidate high-quality modes). A two-stage schedule alternates between forward KL (to expand mode coverage) and reverse KL (to sharpen and stabilize), with pass rates recomputed once at the stage boundary. The method is agnostic to the specific distillation objective and model architecture, requiring only student-side rollouts and a frozen teacher.

## Key Results
- In forward KL distillation (Qwen3-14B → Qwen3-8B), PACED yields +7.5 on MATH-500 and +14.8 on AIME 2025 over the base student, with only 0.2% forgetting on MMLU.
- In reverse KL self-distillation (Qwen2.5-Math-7B-Instruct), gains are +9.8 on MATH-500 and +13.6 on AIME 2025.
- A two-stage forward-KL-then-reverse-KL schedule achieves the strongest results: +9.1 on MATH-500, +15.2 on AIME 2024, and +16.7 on AIME 2025.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1: Gradient SNR Vanishing at Competence Extremes
**Claim:** Standard LLM distillation wastes compute on problems where the student's gradient signal-to-noise ratio (SNR) is near zero, either because the problem is already mastered (p≈1) or far beyond reach (p≈0). This waste is structurally inevitable.
**Mechanism:** Theoretical analysis shows the gradient SNR in distillation vanishes at both pass-rate boundaries. As p→1, the student's logits align with the teacher's, so the expected gradient norm ∥E[g]∥→0. As p→0, gradient directions become incoherent across diverse intractable prompts, so the ratio of gradient variance to squared mean norm tr(Cov(g))/∥E[g]∥²→∞, driving SNR→0.
**Core assumption:** The student's pass rate p reflects its current competence, and the teacher's responses are learnable by the student (ensured by using an in-family teacher conditioned on expert solutions).
**Evidence anchors:** Abstract and Section 2.1 (Proposition 2) formally establish these boundary conditions. Empirical Figure 3 confirms the bell-shaped SNR profile.
**Break condition:** If the teacher's distribution is fundamentally unlearnable (e.g., cross-architecture with a large capacity gap), pass rate may not correlate with gradient SNR, and the boundary vanishing could fail.

### Mechanism 2: Beta Kernel as Leading-Order, Minimax-Robust Solution
**Claim:** The Beta-kernel weight w(p)=p^α(1-p)^β is the leading-order, minimax-robust solution for concentrating distillation on the "zone of proximal development" where learning signal is maximal.
**Mechanism:** Under power-law boundary regularity (SNR²(p)∝p^{a'}(1-p)^{b'}), any SNR profile decomposes as SNR²(p)=p^{a'}(1-p)^{b'}·e^{r(p)} with bounded r(p). Setting r≡0 yields the Beta kernel as the leading-order term. Minimax robustness analysis shows that if the true SNR deviates by a factor e^{±δ}, the worst-case efficiency loss is only O(δ²).
**Core assumption:** The gradient SNR exhibits asymptotic power-law decay at both boundaries (Assumption 3(b)), and the pass-rate distribution has support across (0,1) with finite rollouts.
**Evidence anchors:** Abstract and Section 3.3 (Theorem 6) provide the theoretical guarantee; Section 4.2 shows consistent empirical gains over baselines with minimal forgetting.
**Break condition:** If the true SNR profile is highly non-monotonic in the interior (e.g., multiple peaks), the unimodal Beta kernel may misallocate weight. The minimax guarantee only covers bounded multiplicative misspecification.

### Mechanism 3: Two-Stage KL Schedule for Mode Coverage then Consolidation
**Claim:** A two-stage schedule using forward KL then reverse KL synergizes with pass-rate weighting: forward KL first expands the student's mode coverage (exploration), then reverse KL sharpens and consolidates high-quality modes (exploitation).
**Mechanism:** Forward KL (KL(p_T∥p_S)) encourages the student to cover the teacher's broader distribution, useful when many problems are initially too hard (p≈0). As pass rates rise and the zone of proximal development contracts, reverse KL (KL(p_S∥p_T)) focuses on consolidating confident modes, stabilizing performance and reducing forgetting. Recomputing pass rates between stages adapts the Beta kernel to the shifted competence frontier.
**Core assumption:** The pass-rate distribution evolves predictably during training—problems migrate from the low-p tail through the ZPD to the high-p tail—so a schedule change aligns with the student's developmental stage.
**Evidence anchors:** Section 4.3.3 (Table 8) shows KL→RevKL outperforms other orders and single-stage variants; Section 4.4.1 (Table 10) documents pass-rate migration, confirming ZPD contraction.
**Break condition:** If the student's competence does not shift monotonically (e.g., oscillates due to poor optimization), recomputing pass rates may misalign the schedule. The two-stage benefit assumes the initial ZPD mean is low; for datasets where students start near mastery, the order may not help.

## Foundational Learning
- **Gradient Signal-to-Noise Ratio (SNR):** The ratio of the squared norm of the expected gradient to the variance (or trace of covariance). It measures the informativeness of a gradient signal for learning. Needed because PACED's core insight is that SNR vanishes at pass-rate extremes, explaining why uniform distillation is inefficient.
  - *Quick check:* If for a given problem the student's gradient has high variance across rollouts but a small mean direction, what does that imply about its SNR and learning utility?
- **Pass-rate as a competence proxy:** The fraction of correct student rollouts on a problem, used to estimate the student's current ability. Needed because PACED weights each problem by a function of this estimated p.
  - *Quick check:* Why is it crucial that the teacher's responses are generated within the same model family as the student (e.g., Qwen3-14B → Qwen3-8B) rather than from a black-box API directly?
- **KL divergence directionality (forward vs. reverse):** Forward KL KL(p_T∥p_S) encourages the student to cover the teacher's distribution; reverse KL KL(p_S∥p_T) encourages the student to consolidate its own high-probability modes. Needed because the choice interacts with the pass-rate curriculum and affects mode coverage vs. consolidation.
  - *Quick check:* In the forward KL objective KL(p_T∥p_S), which model's distribution is being "covered"? How does this relate to mode coverage vs. consolidation?

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map:** Expert Solution Generation → Teacher Regeneration (frozen, conditioned on expert solution) → Pass-Rate Estimation (student rollouts) → Weighted Distillation Training (forward or reverse KL, with weights w(p))
- **Critical path:** Generate expert solutions y_E for each prompt x. Condition the frozen teacher T on (x, y_E) to produce teacher responses y_T. Estimate initial student pass rates p using K rollouts per prompt. Compute fixed weight ˜w_i = w(ˆp_i)/¯w. Train student S_θ by minimizing the weighted distillation loss (forward KL on y_T or reverse KL on a student rollout y_S). For two-stage schedules, recompute pass rates once at the stage boundary and switch KL direction.
- **Design tradeoffs:**
  - K rollouts: Larger K gives more accurate p estimates but higher upfront inference cost (default K=8).
  - Exponents (α,β): Default symmetric w(p)=p(1-p) balances gains and forgetting; asymmetric kernels shift the ZPD peak but risk higher forgetting.
  - Single-pass vs. recompute: Single-pass is simpler; periodic recomputation adapts to shifting p distributions but adds overhead.
  - KL direction: Forward KL for cross-model distillation; reverse KL for self-distillation; two-stage (forward→reverse) leverages both.
- **Failure signatures:**
  - High forgetting (>2% on MMLU): Likely due to too aggressive weighting toward hard problems (e.g., α=1,β=3) or noisy pass-rate estimates (K too small).
  - No reasoning gain: Could be unlearnable teacher responses, flat weighting (α=β=0), or insufficient training steps. Validate that base student pass rate on teacher responses is intermediate (20-80%) and check empirical SNR vs. p profile.
  - Gradient variance explosion: If pass-rate distribution is extremely bimodal and w(p) suppresses many samples, effective batch size drops. Check normalized weight distribution.
- **First 3 experiments:**
  1. Ablate K ∈ {4,8,16} to verify robustness of Beta weighting to pass-rate estimation noise (expect small drop for K=4).
  2. Sweep exponents (α,β) pairs (1,1), (1,2), (2,1) on a held-out slice to observe the plasticity-stability tradeoff (expect (1,2) for highest reasoning but slightly higher forgetting).
  3. Test two-stage order: compare KL→RevKL vs RevKL→KL vs single-stage on a small subset (expect KL→RevKL to outperform).

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- **Open Question 1:** How can we dynamically interpolate between forward and reverse KL during distillation based on real-time measurements of the student's zone of proximal development? Basis: Future work suggests continuous loss interpolation L= (1−λ_t)KL_fwd + λ_t KL_rev with λ_t driven by ZPD statistics. Unresolved because the current two-stage schedule uses a fixed switch point, which may not adapt to evolving competence.
- **Open Question 2:** Does PACED maintain its benefits when applied to cross-architecture distillation (different model families) and to multi-teacher ensembles? Basis: Future work notes pass-rate weighting may transfer naturally to cross-architecture settings and multi-teacher ensembles. Unresolved because all experiments used same-family teachers or self-distillation; cross-architecture and multi-teacher scenarios may alter pass-rate distributions and gradient structures.
- **Open Question 3:** Can the Beta kernel exponents (α, β) be adapted online during training using gradient SNR estimates rather than fixed or one-time data-driven selection? Basis: Limitations note that the closed-form exponent selection is a heuristic; fully adaptive online estimation via gradient SNR tracking remains open. Unresolved because optimal weighting may shift as the student's competence evolves.
- **Open Question 4:** What is the optimal frequency for recomputing pass rates in PACED when using a dynamic curriculum? Basis: The method section mentions optional periodic recomputation; only a two-stage schedule recomputed once. Unresolved because the trade-off between compute overhead and alignment with shifting ZPD is unknown.

## Limitations
- The theory assumes the teacher's distribution is learnable by the student and relies on in-family teacher generation; cross-architecture distillation may violate pass-rate as a competence proxy.
- Pass-rate estimation is sensitive to the number of rollouts K; small K introduces noise that can misallocate weights and increase forgetting or degrade gains.
- The Beta kernel's unimodal shape assumes a single-peaked SNR profile; highly non-monotonic or multimodal SNR structures could lead to suboptimal weighting.

## Confidence
- **Mechanism 1 (Gradient SNR Vanishing):** High. Formal proof (Proposition 2) is provided and aligns with gradient theory.
- **Mechanism 2 (Beta Kernel Optimality):** Medium. Theoretical derivation (Theorem 3) is strong, but minimax robustness assumes bounded multiplicative misspecification; real-world SNR may have non-monotonic interior deviations.
- **Mechanism 3 (Two-Stage Schedule):** Low-Medium. Empirical support exists (Table 8), but lacks formal analysis; success depends on ZPD contraction dynamics that may not generalize.
