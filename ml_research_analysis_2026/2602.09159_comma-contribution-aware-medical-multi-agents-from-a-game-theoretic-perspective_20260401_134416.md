# CoMMa: Contribution-Aware Medical Multi-Agents From A Game-Theoretic Perspective

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID**: 2602.09159
- **Source URL**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.09159
- **Reference count**: 13
- **Primary result**: On HCC Tumorboard, CoMMa achieves 0.831±0.010 accuracy with superior stability and interpretable agent contributions compared to baselines.

## Executive Summary
Existing LLM-based medical multi-agent systems rely on stochastic role-play dialogues over centralized data, leading to instability and a lack of explicit evidence attribution. CoMMa introduces a decentralized framework that partitions clinical evidence into separate streams processed by specialist agents via deterministic embedding projections. These agents’ logits are aggregated through a learnable matrix regularized by Shapley values, enabling fair, contribution-aware credit assignment. Evaluated on HCC Tumorboard and MTBBench, CoMMa demonstrates higher predictive accuracy (e.g., 0.831±0.010 on HCC) and markedly more stable performance across random seeds than both data-centralized and role-based baselines, while providing quantitative agent-level attributions grounded in cooperative game theory.

## Method Summary
CoMMa processes multimodal clinical text by first partitioning it into N distinct evidence streams (e.g., imaging reports, laboratory results) and a global anchor. Each stream is encoded by a frozen LLM to extract the <CLS> token embedding, which is then passed through a learnable MLP to produce class-specific logits (the agent’s output). These per-agent logits are aggregated via a learnable decision matrix W (size N×C), where each entry W_{i,k} represents the weight of agent i for class k. A global agent processes the concatenated full input, and its output is linearly mixed with the aggregated agent output. The loss combines binary cross-entropy with two regularizers: L_pg (a policy gradient term using an advantage estimator based on marginal loss reduction) and L_shap (a KL divergence that aligns W with Shapley values Φ). Φ is approximated offline by Monte Carlo sampling of coalitions and averaging marginal contributions in terms of BCE loss. Training optimizes all MLP parameters and W while keeping the LLM frozen, thus enabling stable gradient-based learning and interpretable credit assignment.

## Key Results
- **Accuracy**: HCC Tumorboard accuracy of 0.831±0.010, surpassing centralized and role-play baselines; MTBBench also shows improvement.
- **Stability**: Performance variance across random seeds is significantly lower than stochastic multi-agent approaches, indicating robust training dynamics.
- **Attribution**: The Shapley-regularized matrix W yields quantitative, fair contributions per agent per class, offering clinical interpretability.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1
- **Claim:** Replacing stochastic, narrative-based agent communication with deterministic embedding projections stabilizes multi-agent collaboration by eliminating generative variance and enabling gradient-based optimization.
- **Mechanism:** Each partitioned clinical evidence stream `x_i` is passed through a frozen LLM encoder to extract a fixed `<CLS>` token embedding, which is then projected via a learnable MLP (`f_θi`) into a task-specific logit vector `h_i`. This removes open-ended text generation from the coordination channel.
- **Core assumption:** The frozen LLM's hidden state for the `<CLS>` token suffices to capture the salient clinical signals from each partition without requiring full-sequence generation.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract]: "CoMMa utilizes deterministic embedding projections to approximate contribution-aware credit assignment... yielding interpretable and mathematically grounded decision pathways with improved stability."
  - [section 3.1]: Formal definition of `F_θ(x_i) = MLP_θ(LLM_frozen(x_i)[<CLS>])` and explicit contrast with "stochastic narrative-based reasoning."
  - [corpus]: Weak direct support; neighboring papers like "Tree-of-Reasoning" and "UCAgents" also address instability from generative dialogue but use different mitigation strategies.
- **Break condition:** If the frozen LLM's `<CLS>` embedding fails to preserve modality-specific decision-critical information (e.g., due to catastrophic forgetting during pre-training), the downstream agent projections would operate on insufficient representations.

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim:** Data-decentralized agent specialization (each agent processes only its assigned evidence partition) structurally enforces modality-specific expertise and enables explicit per-agent attribution, unlike centralized models where evidence mixing is implicit.
- **Mechanism:** The clinical context `x` is partitioned into `x_1, ..., x_N`. Agent `i` computes `h_i = f_θi(x_i)`. These specialized logits are aggregated via a learnable agent-decision matrix `W` (Eq. 4), making each agent's contribution to each output class explicitly parameterized.
- **Core assumption:** Isolating evidence streams at the input level forces agents to develop representations that are conditionally independent in their raw inputs, preventing one agent from dominating by simply accessing another's data.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract]: "In contrast to most agent architectures... CoMMa partitions the clinical context into distinct information streams and assigns each to a dedicated agent, enforcing specialization by construction."
  - [section 3.2]: Contrasts "Data-Centralized" (Eq. 2) and "Data-Decentralized" (Eq. 3) formulations, highlighting that decentralization "enables explicit measurement of each agent’s utility before final aggregation."
  - [corpus]: Strong support from "UCAgents" and "MedMMV," which also use evidence partitioning to combat reasoning detachment, though they differ in aggregation mechanics.
- **Break condition:** If the predefined data partitions are highly correlated (e.g., imaging report `x_i` and corresponding findings `x_j`), decentralization may force agents to redundantly learn similar features, harming efficiency without improving attribution.

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim:** Regularizing the learnable agent-decision matrix `W` toward Shapley values (estimated via Monte Carlo) aligns the model's implicit credit assignment with a cooperative game-theoretic notion of fair marginal contribution, stabilizing training and providing interpretable attribution.
- **Mechanism:** For each agent `i` and class `k`, the Shapley value `Φ_{i,k}` is approximated by averaging the marginal reduction in BCE loss (`L_BCE_k`) when adding agent `i` to random coalitions `S`. `W` is regularized to match `Φ` via KL divergence (`L_shap`), preventing any agent's weight from growing unless its inclusion demonstrably improves the loss across multiple coalition configurations.
- **Core assumption:** The Shapley value is the unique fair allocation rule for cooperative games (efficiency, symmetry, dummy, additivity axioms), and approximating it via finite coalitions yields a stable target for `W`.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract]: "We adopt the principle of Shapley value... to regularize contribution learning, aligning each agent’s learned contribution weight with its estimated marginal utility."
  - [section 3.3.3]: Full derivation of `Φ` via Monte Carlo and the KL loss `L_shap = E[∑ W log(W/Φ)]`. Explicitly states that Shapley captures "inter-agent interactions and dependencies" unlike the advantage `A_i` which only uses the full coalition.
  - [section A.2]: Notes the ReLU operator on Shapley estimates to ensure non-negativity for KL divergence.
  - [corpus]: "Beechey et al." cited for Shapley in RL explainability; "Mendonça et al." and "Lau et al." cited for game theory in clinical decisions, showing domain precedent.
- **Break condition:** If the Monte Carlo sampling of coalitions (`M = 2^{N/2}`) is insufficient for `N` agents (e.g., `N=5` gives `M≈5.6`), `Φ` becomes a high-variance estimate, causing `L_shap` to impose noisy constraints that destabilize `W`'s convergence.

## Foundational Learning
- **Concept:** Deterministic Embedding Projection
  - **Why needed here:** The system must replace stochastic LLM dialogue with a fixed, optimizable representation to enable gradient-based training of the multi-agent aggregator and avoid mode collapse from generative variance.
  - **Quick check question:** If you replace the frozen LLM encoder with a random projection matrix, what happens to downstream agent performance and why? (Answer: Performance collapses; the frozen LLM provides semantically meaningful, clinical-domain pretrained features that a random projection lacks.)

- **Concept:** Data-Decentralization vs. Role-Play
  - **Why needed here:** Role-play (e.g., "act as an oncologist") relies on the LLM's prompt-engineered persona, which is a semantic rather than structural constraint. Decentralization by data partition guarantees that each agent's knowledge is bounded to a specific evidence source, enabling unambiguous attribution.
  - **Quick check question:** In a role-play system, how would you definitively determine whether a wrong recommendation stemmed from poor radiology interpretation or flawed oncology reasoning? (Answer: You couldn't, because both agents see all data; evidence attribution is ambiguous.)

- **Concept:** Shapley Value Regularization
  - **Why needed here:** A naive learnable weight matrix `W` may assign high weight to an agent that coincidentally correlates with the label in training but does not provide robust marginal utility (overfitting). Shapley regularization forces `W` to reflect each agent's *average* marginal contribution across coalition subsets.
  - **Quick check question:** Why is the advantage vector `A_i` (based on removing agent `i` from the full coalition only) insufficient for stable credit assignment compared to Shapley? (Answer: `A_i` captures only one coalition (the full set), which may be unrepresentative; Shapley averages over all subsets, accounting for interactions and preventing dominance by a single full-coalition configuration.)

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map:**
  1. **Input Partitioning:** Raw clinical text (reports, labs, imaging) is split into `N` streams `x_1..x_N` + a global anchor `x_global`.
  2. **Deterministic Encoder:** Frozen LLM (Llama3.1-70B) extracts `<CLS>` embedding for each `x_i`.
  3. **Agent Projection Heads:** `N` separate MLPs (`f_θi`) map each embedding to class logits `h_i`.
  4. **Agent-Decision Matrix `W`:** `N x C` matrix; `W_{i,k}` = weight of agent `i` for class `k`.
  5. **Aggregation:** `z_A,k = Σ_i W_{i,k} * h_{i,k}` (per-class weighted sum).
  6. **Global Mixture:** `ŷ = MLP_ϕ( ˜w_A * z_A + ˜w_G * f_θglobal(x_global))`.
  7. **Loss Components:** `L_BCE` (task), `L_pg` (policy gradient on `W` using advantage `A_i`), `L_shap` (KL between `W` and Shapley matrix `Φ`).
  8. **Shapley Estimator:** Offline module that, for each batch, samples `M` coalitions `S` and computes `Φ_{i,k}` via marginal loss differences.

- **Critical path:** Input → Frozen Encoder → Agent MLPs → `W` Aggregation → Global MLP → `ŷ`. The `L_shap` regularizer runs in parallel, requiring periodic coalition sampling and loss recomputation for `Φ` updates.
- **Design tradeoffs:**
  - *Determinism vs. Expressivity:* Fixed embeddings sacrifice some generative nuance but gain stability and trainability.
  - *Decentralization vs. Integration:* Strict partition limits cross-modal reasoning within agents but makes attribution clean; the global anchor `x_global` partially re-integrates context.
  - *Shapley Accuracy vs. Cost:* More coalition samples (`M`) yields a more accurate `Φ` but slows training; `M=2^{N/2}` is a heuristic compromise.
- **Failure signatures:**
  - **Collapsed `W`:** One agent dominates nearly all `W_{i,k}` → indicates either data partitions are redundant or `L_shap` failed to suppress overfit.
  - **Noisy `Φ` estimates:** High variance in `Φ` across training steps → `M` too small or partitions too correlated, making marginal utility unstable.
  - **Global mixture weight `˜w_G → 1`:** Agents contribute nothing → `L_shap`/`L_pg` may be mis-scaled (`λ` values) or agents individually underperform.
- **First 3 experiments:**
  1. **Ablation 1 (Determinism):** Replace deterministic projections with full LLM generation (like MDAgents). Expect: higher variance in results, inability to compute AUC (only binary accuracy), loss of gradient signal to `W`.
  2. **Ablation 2 (Decentralization):** Feed concatenated `[x_1;...;x_N]` to a single agent (bypass `W`). Expect: performance drop relative to CoMMa (per Table 3, (b) vs. (c)), and **zero** attribution (only one agent exists).
  3. **Ablation 3 (Shapley Reg.):** Train with `W` but without `L_shap` (only `L_pg`). Expect: initial gains from `L_pg` but possible collapse of `W` to a few agents (per Figure 4, "without module" case); less stable validation scores.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
### Open Question 1
- **Question:** How can CoMMa be adapted for open-ended clinical decisions with uncertain outcomes and free-form queries?
- **Basis in paper:** [explicit] The authors identify "extending contribution-aware multi-agent modeling to settings involving free-form queries, partially observed or uncertain outcomes, and inherently ambiguous decision spaces" as a key future direction in the conclusion.
- **Why unresolved:** The current CoMMa implementation is limited to discrete classification tasks with clear ground-truth labels, which does not reflect the open-ended and ambiguous nature of real-world multidisciplinary decision-making.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Develop and evaluate CoMMa variants on datasets containing free-form clinical questions, partial evidence, and probabilistic outcomes, measuring performance against expert judgments and stability under uncertainty.

### Open Question 2
- **Question:** What methods can disentangle multimodal clinical data to enable finer-grained agent reasoning and contribution attribution?
- **Basis in paper:** [explicit] The authors state that "achieving granular reasoning remains challenging when processing highly clustered information across modalities" and plan to "prioritize increasing data scale and disentangling data representations to enhance the resolution and minimize information entanglement."
- **Why unresolved:** CoMMa partitions data by modality but does not address potential feature entanglement within each stream, which could limit the precision of agent contribution attribution.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Incorporate disentanglement techniques (e.g., contrastive learning, independent component analysis) into the embedding projection module and evaluate improvements in interpretability and accuracy on multimodal benchmarks.

### Open Question 3
- **Question:** Can credit assignment in CoMMa be improved beyond Shapley values to model synergistic agent interactions in oncology decisions?
- **Basis in paper:** [explicit] The authors note they will "continue to address the limitations of the role-based multi-agents by further strengthening credit assignment principles to better capture the complex dynamics of oncology decision-making."
- **Why unresolved:** The current Shapley-based regularization approximates marginal contributions but may not fully capture non-additive synergies or redundancies among agents when evidence streams overlap.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Replace or augment the Shapley regularizer with alternative cooperative game solutions (e.g., Banzhaf index, core stability) and compare model stability, clinical fidelity, and predictive performance on oncology benchmarks.

## Limitations
- **Partitioning ambiguity:** The mapping of the 24 HCC variables into 5 data streams is not fully specified, hindering exact replication.
- **Shapley sample size:** The exact Monte Carlo sample count `M` is ambiguous (possibly `2^{N/2}` or `2^{N}/2`), affecting approximation accuracy and stability.
- **Evaluation scope:** Results are limited to two oncology benchmarks; generalizability to other clinical domains or open-ended decision-making remains untested.

## Confidence
- **Deterministic projections for stability**: Medium.
- **Data decentralization enabling specialization**: High.
- **Shapley regularization for fair attribution**: Medium.
- **Overall performance claims**: Medium-High.

## Next Checks
1. **Determinism ablation**: Replace deterministic projections with full LLM dialogue (e.g., MDAgents). Expect increased performance variance and loss of gradient signal to `W`.
2. **Partition correlation stress test**: Create data streams with high vs. low correlation. Expect redundancy, weight collapse, and degraded attribution when partitions overlap.
3. **Monte Carlo sample sensitivity**: Vary `M` (e.g., {8,16,32}). Expect an optimal range where Shapley estimates stabilize without excessive compute; too small `M` increases noise in `L_shap` and hurts convergence.
