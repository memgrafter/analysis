# SleepMaMi: A Universal Sleep Foundation Model for Integrating Macro- and Micro-structures

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2602.07628  
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.07628  
- **Reference count:** 40  
- **Primary result:** State-of-the-art sleep staging (81.9% accuracy, 70.0 Kappa on SHHS1) and strong few-shot learning (>70% F1 with 30 samples), with robust performance across apnea segmentation and disease prediction.

## Executive Summary
SleepMaMi is a universal sleep foundation model that integrates micro-structural (fine-grained) and macro-structural (full-night) representations from polysomnography (PSG) via a hierarchical dual-encoder. The Micro-Encoder uses a hybrid masked autoencoder and multi-modal contrastive objective to capture local signal features across EEG, EOG, EMG, ECG, respiratory, and SpO₂ modalities. The Macro-Encoder, a bi-directional Mamba, models long-range temporal dependencies and is trained with Demographic-Guided Contrastive Learning (DGCL) that aligns overnight sleep patterns with metadata (age, sex, BMI). Pre-trained on 20,964 PSG recordings (158K hours), SleepMaMi achieves state-of-the-art results on multiple downstream tasks—including sleep staging, apnea segmentation, and disease prediction—while demonstrating superior label efficiency in few-shot settings.

## Method Summary
The method employs a two-stage hierarchical architecture. First, a Micro-Encoder processes multi-modal PSG signals into 30-second epoch embeddings. Its hybrid objective combines masked autoencoding (reconstructing randomly masked 500ms/2s patches) and multi-modal contrastive learning (aligning shared representations across modalities within the same timeslot, using private embeddings as negatives). Second, a Macro-Encoder ingests the sequence of epoch embeddings and is trained via DGCL, which pulls together latent representations of subjects with similar demographic distances (age, sex, BMI) while pushing apart dissimilar ones. Downstream tasks are addressed by attaching task-specific heads (linear probe or Cox PH) to either encoder and fine-tuning with minimal labeled data.

## Key Results
- Sleep staging on SHHS1: 81.9% accuracy, 70.0 Kappa.
- Apnea segmentation: 60.6 Macro-F1.
- Disease prediction (e.g., angina): C-Index 0.778, comparable to baselines.
- Few-shot sleep staging: >70% F1 with only 30 labeled samples.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1
- **Claim:** The Micro-Encoder's hybrid objective (masked autoencoding + multi-modal contrastive) learns both fine-grained signal morphologies and modality-invariant features.
- **Mechanism:** MAE reconstructs masked temporal patches, forcing capture of local textures; contrastive loss aligns cross-modality embeddings within timeslots while using private embeddings as negatives to disentangle modality-specific features.
- **Break condition:** If contrastive weighting fails or reconstruction fidelity is low, the model may capture noise instead of physiological patterns, degrading fine-grained tasks like apnea segmentation.

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim:** Demographic-Guided Contrastive Learning (DGCL) aligns sleep macro-structure across subjects by using objective metadata (age, sex, BMI) as a supervisory signal, capturing global temporal dependencies without manual labels.
- **Mechanism:** The Macro-Encoder processes epoch embeddings; contrastive loss weights positive/negative pairs by demographic distance, implicitly modeling normative sleep architecture trajectories.
- **Break condition:** Skewed or unrepresentative demographic distributions in pretraining yield macro-representations that poorly generalize to diverse populations, harming disease prediction or cross-dataset transfer.

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim:** The hierarchical dual-encoder design (Micro→Macro) seamlessly integrates micro-events (e.g., spindles, apneas) and full-night macro-architecture (e.g., sleep cycles), supporting both fine-grained and subject-level tasks.
- **Mechanism:** Micro produces compact epoch embeddings; Macro contextualizes each epoch via bi-directional state-space modeling over the full sequence, avoiding the trade-offs of a single monolithic encoder.
- **Break condition:** If Micro embeddings lose critical information (e.g., due to aggressive patch merging), Macro cannot recover macro-patterns, leading to poor long-horizon task performance.

## Foundational Learning
- **Masked Autoencoding (MAE)**  
  *Why needed:* Forces reconstruction of raw biosignal patches to learn local temporal patterns (e.g., spindle waveforms, apnea-related flow drops) without labels.  
  *Quick check:* How does masking patch size (500ms for high-frequency vs. 2s for low-frequency signals) affect learning across modalities with different dominant frequencies?

- **Contrastive Learning (CL)**  
  *Why needed:* Aligns representations across modalities and time without manual annotation, ensuring the shared encoder captures physiology rather than sensor noise, and the Macro-Encoder learns demographic-sensitive macro-patterns.  
  *Quick check:* What defines a valid positive pair in the multi-modal CL objective (same timeslot, different modalities) versus temporal negatives (same modality, different times)?

- **Selective State Space Models (Mamba)**  
  *Why needed:* Enables efficient long-sequence modeling of full-night PSG (~800 epochs) with linear complexity, avoiding Transformer's quadratic cost while preserving bi-directional context.  
  *Quick check:* Why use bi-directional Mamba instead of uni-directional for full-night sequences where sleep onset/offset vary?

## Architecture Onboarding
Component map: Raw multi-modal PSG -> Modality-specific patchify (500ms patches) -> Micro-Encoder (private encoders -> shared MoE Transformer -> cross-attention fusion -> MAE decoder) -> Epoch-level embeddings -> Macro-Encoder (bi-directional Mamba) -> Downstream heads (linear probe/Cox PH).

Critical path:  
1. Pre-train Micro-Encoder on all datasets (except PHY for Macro due to missing BMI) with hybrid loss (MAE + CL + KoLeo).  
2. Freeze Micro-Encoder; extract epoch embeddings for all recordings.  
3. Pre-train Macro-Encoder on epoch embeddings using DGCL with stratified demographic batch sampling.  
4. For downstream tasks, attach task-specific heads and fine-tune (or linear probe) using small labeled data.

Design tradeoffs:  
- MAE masking: Higher mask ratio (50%) improves robustness but may lose rare micro-events; patch size balances frequency resolution across modalities.  
- Mamba vs. Transformer: Mamba reduces memory for long sequences but may be less expressive for very complex temporal dynamics; bi-directionality adds cost versus causal inference needs.  
- DGCL signal: Using demographics is scalable but assumes demographics fully capture macro-variation; may miss pathology-specific macro-alterations not correlated with age/BMI/sex.

Failure signatures:  
- **Micro-Encoder:** High MAE reconstruction loss; contrastive loss collapses to zero; embeddings show modality misalignment in visualization (e.g., t-SNE).  
- **Macro-Encoder:** U-MAP (Figure 6 analog) shows no demographic clustering; downstream disease prediction C-Index drops significantly.  
- **Integration:** Minimal performance gap between Micro-only and full SleepMaMi (e.g., sleep staging accuracy difference <1%) indicates Macro-Encoder adds negligible value—possibly due to information loss in epoch embeddings or unstable DGCL.

First 3 experiments:  
1. **Ablate MAE:** Train Micro-Encoder with CL only; expect drop in apnea segmentation (fine-grained) performance (see Table 2 baseline).  
2. **Replace Mamba with Transformer:** Train Macro-Encoder with a small Transformer (handling 800+ epochs via windowing); expect increased memory usage and potentially worse long-range coherence or training instability.  
3. **Ablate DGCL demographics:** Train Macro-Encoder without one demographic factor (e.g., remove age); expect sleep staging accuracy drop, especially if age is removed (strongest macro-influencer per Table 12).

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
1. **Hardware generalizability:** Performance drops on Nox/Embla systems versus Compumedics (KISS dataset); future work needs multi-hardware pretraining or explicit domain adaptation.  
2. **Metadata expansion:** Beyond age, sex, BMI, what other objective metadata (e.g., ethnicity, medication, clinical history) could most effectively guide DGCL to capture richer macro-structural representations?  
3. **Hybrid objective scope:** Does MAE+CL provide tangible benefits for all downstream tasks, or is its advantage specific to high-resolution segmentation (e.g., apnea) at the expense of tasks where macro-structure dominates (e.g., disease prediction)?  
4. **Implicit clinical constructs:** Does DGCL implicitly encode unstated, complex clinical constructs (e.g., sleep resilience, aging trajectories) beyond the explicit demographic variables used for supervision?

## Limitations
- Channel selection is unspecified across datasets, risking inconsistent feature extraction and hindering reproduction.
- Contrastive learning parameters (timeslot definition, negative sampling, KoLeo radius) are underspecified, making convergence unstable.
- Demographic batch sampling strategy for DGCL is vague ("stratified" without criteria), potentially causing unstable training and poor demographic alignment.
- DGCL assumes demographics fully capture macro-sleep variation; this may not hold for pathology-specific alterations not correlated with age/BMI/sex.
- Patch merging in Micro-Encoder could discard rare micro-events if hyperparameters are not carefully tuned.

## Confidence
- **Micro-Encoder hybrid objective (MAE+CL):** *Medium* – Novel combination shows promise but sensitive to masking and contrastive setup; risk of feature entanglement if weighting is off.  
- **Demographic-Guided CL:** *Medium* – Leverages known demographic effects but relies on metadata quality and distribution; skew risk in pretraining data.  
- **Hierarchical dual-encoder architecture:** *High* – Ablation (Table 4) confirms Macro-Encoder benefits; design aligns with multi-granularity need; failure only if Micro embeddings lose essential information.

## Next Checks
1. **Channel ablation:** Re-train Micro-Encoder with a fixed channel set (e.g., EEG+EOG+EMG) across all datasets; compare downstream performance to full multi-modal input to identify essential modalities and ensure consistency.  
2. **CL hyperparameter sweep:** Systematically vary timeslot duration (15/30/60 sec) and negative sampling ratios; measure impact on sleep staging accuracy and apnea segmentation to find robust configuration.  
3. **Cross-population DGCL test:** Apply pretrained Macro-Encoder to an external cohort with distinct age/BMI distributions (e.g., non-Western populations); evaluate demographic clustering (silhouette score) and disease prediction C-Index to assess generalizability.
