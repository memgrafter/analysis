# Evaluating Cross-Lingual Unlearning in Multilingual Language Models

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2601.06675
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.06675
- **Reference count:** 5
- **Primary result:** Standard unlearning methods fail across languages, while the subspace-projection method UNLEARN achieves significant forgetting in all evaluated languages without harming model utility.

## Executive Summary
This paper investigates whether unlearning a factual association in one language causes a multilingual language model to forget that same fact in other languages. The authors find that common unlearning techniques—such as gradient ascent on forget data, KL minimization, and preference optimization—are ineffective for cross-lingual transfer. These methods only suppress the specific surface forms of the target language, leaving the fact intact in other languages. In contrast, the proposed subspace-projection method UNLEARN identifies a low-dimensional weight subspace that encodes the forgotten fact and removes it entirely. This operation induces statistically significant forgetting across multiple languages while preserving overall model capability. Analysis reveals that multilingual models store facts in a shared, language-agnostic "interlingua" subspace plus language-specific residuals; removing the shared component causes universal forgetting. The work demonstrates that effective multilingual unlearning must target this geometric structure rather than language-specific loss surfaces.

## Method Summary
The study uses translated versions of the TOFU benchmark (fictional author facts) across seven languages/scripts (English, Spanish, Italian, Hindi-Devanagari, Hindi-romanized, Chinese-hanzi, Chinese-pinyin). Base models include Llama 2/3/4, Mistral/Mixtral/Magistral, and Aya-23. Unlearning is performed with several algorithms: gradient ascent, KL minimization, gradient difference, NPO, FLAT, and the novel subspace-projection method UNLEARN. For UNLEARN, weight updates from fine-tuning on the forget set are contrasted with updates from a control task; singular value decomposition extracts the top-k discriminative directions, which are then removed via orthogonal projection. Evaluation uses Forget Quality (p-value > 0.1 indicates forgetting) and Model Utility (harmonic mean of nine capabilities, relative to the base model). Key configurations include one-to-one (unlearn in L1, evaluate all) and many-to-one (unlearn in multiple L, evaluate held-out L) settings, plus transliteration controls to isolate script effects.

## Key Results
- Standard unlearning methods (gradient ascent, KL, preference optimization) fail to transfer forgetting across languages, showing near-zero Forget Quality in held-out languages while often harming utility.
- The subspace-projection method UNLEARN achieves statistically significant forgetting (p-value > 0.1) across all evaluated languages while maintaining high utility (~1.0), as shown in cross-lingual transfer experiments.
- Causal evidence reveals a shared interlingua subspace: removing it causes uniform forgetting across languages, whereas removing language-specific residuals affects only the target language. Many-to-one unlearning improves transfer over one-to-one, and script transliteration influences but does not eliminate cross-lingual forgetting.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1: Subspace Projection Identifies Task-Discriminative Directions
- **Claim:** Effective multilingual unlearning requires operating directly in weight space by identifying and removing the low-dimensional subspace that encodes the forgotten facts, rather than relying on loss-based updates that remain tethered to surface language forms.
- **Mechanism:** UNLEARN first computes weight updates from the forget set and control tasks, then performs singular value decomposition to extract the top-k directions that best discriminate the forget task. During unlearning, it projects the model weights onto the orthogonal complement of this subspace, removing the task-specific components while preserving other functionality.
- **Core assumption:** Factual knowledge occupies a low-dimensional, disentanglable subspace in model weights that can be isolated by contrasting task-specific updates against control task updates.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract] "subspace-projection method UNLEARN achieves statistically significant forgetting across languages while preserving model utility"
  - [section 4.3] "Removing the Shared Interlingua Subspace... produces a nearly uniform effect across languages"
  - [corpus] The corpus is strong for this mechanism, showing multiple papers engaging with subspace-based unlearning approaches (e.g., "Shared Path: Unraveling Memorization in Multilingual LLMs through Language Similarities" suggests shared representational analysis)
- **Break condition:** If the forget set does not produce a sufficiently discriminative subspace (e.g., when facts are encoded across high-dimensional, overlapping subspaces), projection fails to isolate the target knowledge.

### Mechanism 2: Shared Interlingua Subspace Enables Cross-Lingual Forgetting Transfer
- **Claim:** Multilingual LLMs encode factual knowledge across a shared, language-independent semantic subspace (the interlingua) plus language-specific residuals; removing the shared component induces forgetting in all languages.
- **Mechanism:** The interlingua captures abstract semantic content common to equivalent facts across languages, while residuals absorb script, tokenization, and morphological variation. Causal ablation shows that projecting out the shared subspace causes statistically significant forgetting across all evaluated languages while maintaining utility, confirming its role as the primary carrier of cross-lingual factual structure.
- **Core assumption:** Semantic equivalence across languages maps to overlapping weight directions in the model, creating a common subspace that stores language-agnostic factual associations.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [section 4.1] "Three empirical observations support this conclusion... forgetting often transfers from the source language to others"
  - [section 4.3] "Removing the Shared Interlingua Subspace... all languages exhibit statistically significant forget quality"
  - [section 3.3] "Many-to-One unlearning... yields both higher forget quality and a more compact distribution of results across held-out languages"
- **Break condition:** If the model has not developed a robust interlingua (e.g., in early multilingual training or with typologically distant languages), shared subspace removal may fail to induce cross-lingual forgetting.

### Mechanism 3: Gradient-Based Methods Fail Due to Language-Specific Loss Surfaces
- **Claim:** Standard unlearning methods (gradient ascent, KL divergence, preference optimization) fail at cross-lingual unlearning because they optimize loss on the forget set, which remains anchored to language-specific surface forms and token patterns rather than the shared semantic substrate.
- **Mechanism:** These methods update weights to reduce probability on the specific token sequences of the forget set. Since multilingual models represent equivalent facts using different tokens and scripts across languages, optimizing against one language's surface form does not substantially affect the shared semantic subspace that activates for other languages.
- **Core assumption:** Loss-based optimization operates primarily in the tangent space of the language-specific residual subspaces rather than penetrating the shared interlingua where semantic content is stored.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [section 3.3] "Most methods completely degrade performance across any language pairs. UNLEARN is the only algorithm that still attains statistically significant forgetting"
  - [section 2.1] "These methods can be effective in monolingual settings but rely heavily on surface form cues, which as we later show limits their generalization across languages"
  - [corpus] "What Causes Knowledge Loss in Multilingual Language Models?" discusses challenges of cross-lingual transfer that likely relate to this surface/semantic split
- **Break condition:** If the forget set includes diverse linguistic realizations that collectively span both shared and residual subspaces, gradient-based methods might achieve partial transfer, though this remains empirically unsupported in the paper.

## Foundational Learning
- **Concept: Cross-lingual representation alignment**
  - Why needed here: Understanding that multilingual models develop partially shared semantic spaces (interlingua) is essential to predict why unlearning might transfer across languages. Without this, one might incorrectly assume each language's knowledge is isolated.
  - Quick check question: If you remove a fact from a multilingual model using only English examples, in which other languages would you expect forgetting to occur, and why?

- **Concept: Subspace geometry in neural networks**
  - Why needed here: The subspace-projection method treats model weights as points in a high-dimensional space where task-relevant directions can be identified via SVD. This geometric perspective is fundamental to understanding why UNLEARN works where gradient methods fail.
  - Quick check question: How does extracting task-discriminative directions via contrastive updates differ from simply following the forget gradient?

- **Concept: Unlearning as orthogonal projection vs. loss minimization**
  - Why needed here: The paper contrasts two families: projection methods that remove entire subspaces versus loss-based methods that minimize on forget data. This conceptual split explains the performance divide. The projection approach removes the *entire* capability to represent the fact, while loss minimization only suppresses specific outputs.
  - Quick check question: Why might unlearning via gradient ascent on forget data leave the model's underlying representation of a fact intact in other languages?

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map:** 
  Forget dataloader -> Fine-tune model on forget set -> Compute weight delta -> Control task dataloader -> Fine-tune on control -> Compute control delta -> Stack deltas -> SVD -> Extract top-k singular vectors (basis B) -> Model weights -> Orthogonal projection: W <- (I - BBᵀ)W -> Unlearned checkpoint -> Evaluation suite (Forget Quality, Utility)

- **Critical path:** The process begins with generating forget and control weight updates via fine-tuning. These updates are stacked and decomposed by SVD to find directions that discriminate the forget task. The model's weights are then orthogonally projected against these directions. Finally, the modified model is evaluated across all language variants. The most delicate step is ensuring the control task produces updates orthogonal to the true forget subspace; contaminated control data leads to incomplete removal.

- **Design tradeoffs:** 
  - *Subspace rank k*: Too small misses task dimensions → incomplete forgetting; too large risks removing useful functionality → utility loss. The paper uses validation to select k.
  - *Control task selection*: Must be semantically unrelated but similar in distribution to avoid overlapping subspaces. Using random data may produce uninformative deltas.
  - *Projection exactness*: Full projection removes all components in the span; approximate methods (e.g., gradient step along negative direction) may leave residual traces.

- **Failure signatures:**
  - High forget quality in source language but near-zero forgetting in target languages → indicates gradient-based method failing to penetrate shared subspace
  - Utility drop below 0.8 after projection → suggests subspace rank too large or contaminated control data
  - Inconsistent forgetting across languages when using many-to-one → fails to capture stable interlingua component
  - Forget quality improves with transliteration → points to script-specific residual dominance, meaning the shared subspace was not targeted

- **First 3 experiments:**
  1. **Reproduce Table 1:** Run UNLEARN on Llama 3 English TOFU with 10% forget set. Confirm forget quality > 0.1 and utility ≈ 0.6. This establishes baseline monolingual success.
  2. **Cross-lingual transfer test:** Using the same unlearned model from experiment 1, evaluate on Spanish, Italian, and Hindi TOFU. Expect statistically significant forgetting (p > 0.1) in all languages per Table 4, demonstrating cross-lingual propagation.
  3. **Causal ablation validation:** For Aya-23, manually construct the shared interlingua subspace by intersecting bases from all languages (e.g., via common singular vectors). Project it out and measure forgetting across *all* languages. Expect uniform forgetting signal, confirming the interlingua mechanism from section 4.3.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- **Open Question 1:** Do subspace-based unlearning methods like UNLEARN generalize to non-factual knowledge types (procedural knowledge, conversational norms, reasoning) across languages, or is their success specific to factual associations tested with TOFU?
- **Open Question 2:** How does cross-lingual unlearning performance vary for low-resource languages and morphologically complex families (agglutinative, polysynthetic) compared to the languages evaluated (English, Spanish, Italian, Hindi, Chinese)?
- **Open Question 3:** What is the optimal many-to-one unlearning strategy when the target set of languages to forget is smaller than available? Does adding more source languages always improve transfer, or does performance saturate?

## Limitations
- Implementation details for UNLEARN (subspace rank selection, control task design, projection strength) are not fully disclosed, hindering faithful replication.
- Language coverage is narrow (7 languages) and lacks low-resource or typologically distant cases, limiting generalizability of the shared interlingua claim.
- The utility metric (harmonic mean of 9 tasks) may obscure task-specific degradation; individual task performance is not reported.

## Confidence
- **Standard gradient-based unlearning fails for cross-lingual transfer:** High confidence, due to consistent experimental evidence across models and language pairs.
- **UNLEARN's subspace projection achieves statistically significant forgetting while preserving utility:** Medium confidence, as the paper lacks full implementation details and sensitivity analyses, which could affect reproducibility and robustness.
- **Existence of a shared interlingua subspace that drives cross-lingual forgetting:** Medium confidence, supported by causal ablation experiments but not fully validated across diverse language families or with alternative subspace identification methods.

## Next Checks
1. **Replication with full hyperparameter sweep:** Implement UNLEARN for Llama 3 and Aya-23, varying SVD rank k and control task choices, to confirm reported FQ/utility trade-offs.
2. **Stress-test shared subspace across language families:** Extend the interlingua ablation to at least 10 languages spanning Indo-European, Sino-Tibetan, Afro-Asiatic, etc., to verify uniformity of forgetting.
3. **Fine-grained utility audit:** Evaluate the unlearned model on each of the nine utility tasks separately (e.g., world knowledge, reasoning, coding) to detect any subtle but significant drops hidden by the aggregate harmonic mean.
