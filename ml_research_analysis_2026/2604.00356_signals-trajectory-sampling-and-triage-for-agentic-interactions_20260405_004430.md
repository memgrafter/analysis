# Signals: Trajectory Sampling and Triage for Agentic Interactions
## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID**: 2604.00356  
- **Source URL**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.00356  
- **Reference count**: 22  
- **Primary result**: Signal-based trajectory triage achieves 82% informativeness and a 1.52× efficiency gain over random/heuristic baselines on τ-bench.

## Executive Summary
This paper introduces a lightweight, model-free signal framework to triage the vast number of trajectories generated by agentic systems post-deployment. Signals are descriptive markers drawn from interaction (misalignment, stagnation, disengagement, satisfaction), execution (failure, loop), and environment (exhaustion) layers, and can be computed without additional LLM calls, reducing review costs. On the τ-bench benchmark, signal-based sampling attains an 82% informativeness rate, significantly outperforming heuristic filtering (74%) and random sampling (54%), and provides a 1.52× efficiency gain per informative trajectory. The improvement holds across success/failure strata and domains, demonstrating robust per-trajectory gains.

## Method Summary
The method defines three layers of signals: interaction (e.g., misalignment, stagnation, disengagement, satisfaction), execution (e.g., failure, infinite loop), and environment (e.g., exhaustion). These are computed directly from interaction logs and system states without invoking any external model, making the approach lightweight and scalable. The signals are aggregated into a triage score that ranks trajectories for developer review; selecting the top-ranked ones ensures the reviewed subset is highly informative for understanding system behavior and failures.

## Key Results
- Signal-based triage attains 82% informativeness rate on τ-bench, vs 74% for heuristic filtering and 54% for random sampling.  
- Efficiency gain of 1.52× per informative trajectory compared to baselines.  
- The advantage is robust across success/failure strata and domains within τ-bench.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The signal framework works because it directly encodes known failure modes and qualitative states (e.g., misalignment, stagnation) through inexpensive checks, avoiding the need for costly LLM-based evaluation. By aggregating such indicators, it reliably flags trajectories that are most informative for developer review. The model-free design ensures low overhead and adaptability across agent implementations.

## Foundational Learning
- **Agentic Systems**: Autonomous agents that interact with environments; need monitoring.  
  *Why needed*: The paper focuses on triaging their interaction trajectories.  
  *Quick check*: Does the system generate open-ended, non-deterministic outputs?  

- **Trajectory**: Sequence of steps/actions taken by an agent during a task.  
  *Why needed*: Trajectories are the unit of review; understanding their properties is central.  
  *Quick check*: Can each interaction be logged as a timestamped sequence?  

- **Signal**: A lightweight, descriptive marker computed from interaction, execution, or environment data.  
  *Why needed*: Signals serve as proxies for informativeness without costly evaluation.  
  *Quick check*: Is the signal computable from available logs without LLM calls?  

- **Triage**: The process of prioritizing which trajectories to review.  
  *Why needed*: Reduces human review burden while maximizing information gain.  
  *Quick check*: Does the triage metric correlate with manual judgments of informativeness?  

- **Informativeness**: Measure of how much a trajectory reveals about agent behavior or failures.  
  *Why needed*: Ultimate goal is to select trajectories that are most useful for developers.  
  *Quick check*: Are informative trajectories those with clear failure modes or insights?

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component Map**:  
Trajectory -> Interaction Signals -> Execution Signals -> Environment Signals -> Aggregator -> Triage Score -> Selection for Review

**Critical Path**:  
Trajectory ingestion → computation of all three signal layers (potential parallelism) → aggregation → scoring → final selection. The longest chain runs through the aggregation step that depends on all signal outputs.

**Design Tradeoffs**:  
- Lightweight vs. coverage: simple signals are cheap but may miss subtle failures.  
- Model-free vs. expressivity: avoiding LLMs reduces cost but may limit nuanced detection.  
- Instrumentation vs. adoption: requiring environment hooks improves signal accuracy but complicates integration.

**Failure Signatures**:  
- High false negative rate for informative trajectories that lack explicit signal triggers.  
- Overwhelming number of false positives due to noisy or overly generic signals.  
- Degraded performance when environment instrumentation is incomplete.

**First Three Experiments**:  
1. Independent replication on τ-bench with multiple seeds, including confidence intervals and significance tests.  
2. Ablation study removing each signal layer individually to quantify contribution to informativeness.  
3. Evaluation on out-of-domain trajectories from a different benchmark to test generalization.

## Limitations
- Evaluation confined to τ-bench; generalizability to other agentic systems and real-world deployments unknown.  
- Baselines limited to heuristic filtering and random sampling; missing comparisons to advanced triage methods (e.g., learned models, active learning).  
- No statistical reporting (confidence intervals, significance tests) for performance metrics; effect size uncertainty unclear.

## Confidence
- Model-free, lightweight design: Medium  
- Signal coverage (interaction, execution, environment): Medium  
- Informativeness rate superiority (82% vs 74%/54% on τ-bench): Medium  
- Efficiency gain (1.52× per informative trajectory): Medium  
- Robustness across strata/domains: Low
