# Instruction Anchors: Dissecting the Causal Dynamics of Modality Arbitration

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2602.03677
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.03677
- **Reference Count:** 29
- **Primary Result:** A sparse subset of attention heads acts as "instruction anchors"; manipulating ~5% of these heads causally shifts modality-following behavior by ~60%.

## Executive Summary
Multimodal large language models (MLLMs) can selectively follow user instructions to use vision or text, but the internal decision-making process is opaque. This paper uses causal attention knockout and signal tracking to reveal that instruction tokens function as structural anchors. Shallow attention layers non-selectively route multimodal cues to these anchors as a latent buffer. Deep attention layers then resolve modality competition guided by the instruction's intent, while MLP layers exhibit semantic inertia opposing this arbitration. A small set of specialized attention heads drives the final decision, and directly intervening on them can drastically alter model behavior.

## Method Summary
The authors apply causal mediation analysis to a MLLM. They perform targeted attention head knockouts (ablation) and amplification (boosting) to isolate the influence of specific components. Signal tracking traces the flow of multimodal information through the network, identifying where and how instruction intent modulates modality competition. This allows them to pinpoint "instruction anchors"—latent representations that strongly align with the final modality choice—and attribute causal responsibility to a sparse set of attention heads.

## Key Results
- **Sparse Causal Heads:** Blocking a specific subset (~5%) of attention heads reduces the model's ability to follow modality instructions by 60%. Amplifying them restores compliance on failing samples by 60%.
- **Layered Dynamics:** Shallow layers route cues non-selectively to instruction tokens; deep layers perform competitive arbitration guided by instruction intent; MLP layers introduce semantic inertia.
- **Anchor Finalization:** Instruction anchor activations show >95% alignment with the model's final modality choice, confirming their role as the decision finalization point.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The mechanism hinges on a hierarchical, two-phase process. First, shallow attention layers act as a non-selective aggregator, routing all incoming multimodal signals (visual and text) toward the embedded instruction tokens. This creates a "latent buffer" at the anchor points. Second, deep attention layers inspect this buffered information in light of the instruction's semantic intent, effectively arbitrating between competing modality signals. A small number of specialized attention heads are the primary drivers of this competitive selection. Meanwhile, later MLP layers process the selected representation but exhibit "semantic inertia"—they tend to preserve the chosen modality path and resist further switching, stabilizing the decision.

## Foundational Learning
*   **Causal Mediation Analysis:** A technique to isolate the causal effect of a specific model component (e.g., a neuron or attention head) on the output by intervening on it.
    *   *Why needed:* To move beyond correlation and prove which parts of the network *cause* modality arbitration.
    *   *Quick check:* Can you intervene on a component and get a predictable, targeted change in behavior?
*   **Attention Heads & Sparsity:** Individual attention heads within a layer often specialize; important functions are frequently carried out by a small, sparse subset of heads.
    *   *Why needed:* Identifies the minimal set of components responsible for a behavior, enabling efficient steering.
    *   *Quick check:* Does ablating a small, identified subset cause a large effect, while ablating others does not?
*   **Modality Arbitration:** The internal process of selecting which input modality (e.g., vision vs. text) to prioritize or follow based on an instruction.
    *   *Why needed:* Core capability for reliable, instruction-following MLLMs; failure leads to ignoring user intent.
    *   *Quick check:* Does the model correctly use image input when instructed "describe the image" but ignore it for "continue the text"?
*   **Instruction Tokens as Latent Representations:** The embedded tokens representing the user's instruction serve as a central, structured locus for information routing and decision-making.
    *   *Why needed:* Explains how a distributed system achieves a coherent, goal-directed decision.
    *   *Quick check:* Do activations at instruction token positions strongly predict the final output modality?
*   **Signal Tracking:** Tracing the propagation and transformation of activations from input layers to output layers.
    *   *Why needed:* To map the flow of modality-specific information and identify where competition occurs.
    *   *Quick check:* Can you visualize when visual vs. textual information peaks and diminishes across layers?
*   **Semantic Inertia (MLP Layers):** The tendency of downstream MLP layers to stabilize and reinforce a previously chosen processing path (e.g., a modality), making late-stage reversals difficult.
    *   *Why needed:* Explains why the decision is "finalized" early and resists perturbation.
    *   *Quick check:* Does intervening on MLP layers after deep attention have less effect on the modality choice than intervening on deep attention itself?

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component Map:**
Input Tokens (Text + Image Patches) -> Token Embedding -> Shallow Transformer Layers (Non-selective routing) -> Instruction Token Embeddings (Structural Anchors) -> Deep Transformer Layers (Competitive Arbitration via Sparse Heads) -> MLP Layers (Semantic Inertia) -> Output Logits

**Critical Path:** The causal influence flows from multimodal input -> shallow layer aggregation -> instruction anchor buffer -> deep layer sparse-head arbitration -> MLP stabilization -> output. The instruction anchors are the central hub.

**Design Tradeoffs:** The architecture trades off *flexibility* (non-selective early routing gathers all information) for *efficiency* (sparse heads in deep layers allow rapid, specialized arbitration) and *stability* (MLP inertia prevents vacillation). A potential tradeoff is brittleness: reliance on a small set of heads may make the system sensitive to their dysfunction.

**Failure Signatures:**
1.  **Anchor Misalignment:** Instruction token activations do not strongly align (>95%) with the final modality prediction.
2.  **Head Sparsity Failure:** The identified sparse head set shows weak or inconsistent causal impact across samples.
3.  **Inertia Override:** The MLP layer's stabilizing effect is too strong, preventing the model from switching modalities even when the instruction clearly changes.

**First Three Experiments:**
1.  **Head Knockout Battery:** Systematically ablate each attention head (or small groups) across deep layers on a modality-conflict benchmark and measure change in instruction-following accuracy.
2.  **Signal Flow Visualization:** Track the norm or entropy of modality-specific signal components (e.g., image patch vs. text token contributions) at each layer for correct vs. incorrect arbitration cases.
3.  **Cross-Task Anchor Test:** Test if the same identified instruction anchors and sparse heads are causal for modality arbitration on a different task type (e.g., video-audio disagreement vs. image-text disagreement).

## Limitations
- Analysis is conducted on a single or small set of MLLMs; the discovered anchor mechanism and head sparsity may not generalize to all architectures.
- Interventions (knockout/amplification) may have unintended side effects on unrelated model capabilities not measured in the study.
- The robustness of instruction anchors across diverse instruction phrasings and task formats is not established.
- Effect sizes and the specific identified heads may be dataset-specific.

## Confidence
- **Instruction anchors as finalization points:** Medium (strong correlation, model-specific).
- **Layered dynamic (shallow buffer, deep arbitration, MLP inertia):** Medium (observational evidence from signal tracking).
- **Existence of a sparse set of causal attention heads:** High (supported by strong causal intervention results).
- **Magnitude of intervention impact (~60% shift):** High (replicable experimental finding).
- **Anchor alignment (>95%) with final prediction:** Medium (correlational, depends on measurement).

## Next Checks
1.  **Cross-Architecture Replication:** Test the anchor mechanism and identify sparse causal heads in at least three distinct MLLM families (e.g., LLaVA, InstructBLIP, a proprietary model like GPT-4V).
2.  **Broad Task Validation:** Evaluate the influence of the identified sparse heads on modality arbitration across a diverse suite of conflict tasks (e.g., VQA with contradictory text, video-audio alignment, text-image disambiguation).
3.  **Holistic Performance Profiling:** Before and after manipulating the sparse heads, evaluate the model on standard multimodal benchmarks (MME, MMBench, SEED-Bench) to quantify any degradation in general capabilities.
