# Drive-Through 3D Vehicle Exterior Reconstruction via Dynamic-Scene SfM and Distortion-Aware Gaussian Splatting

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2603.26638  
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.26638  
- **Reference Count:** 25  
- **Primary Result:** Achieves PSNR 28.66 dB, SSIM 0.89, LPIPS 0.21 on held-out views, a 3.85 dB improvement over standard 3D-GS, enabling inspection-grade interactive models from drive-through data.

## Executive Summary
This paper presents an end-to-end pipeline for high-fidelity 3D reconstruction of vehicle exteriors from drive-through videos in cluttered dealership environments. It addresses key challenges including moving vehicles against static backgrounds, wide-angle fisheye distortion, specular paint reflections, and rotating wheels. The system integrates motion-gated semantic masking to isolate the rigid vehicle body (excluding wheels), a distortion-native learned matcher guided by semantic confidence, rig-aware Structure-from-Motion (SfM) with CAD-derived pose priors, and a distortion-aware Gaussian Splatting variant with stochastic densification. The full method produces photorealistic, interactive 3D models from raw 4K footage without controlled studio conditions.

## Method Summary
The pipeline begins with one-time calibration of a 14-camera dual-pillar rig to obtain fisheye intrinsics and CAD-derived relative extrinsics. During video capture, motion-gated SAM-3 tracks the target vehicle and subtracts wheel masks to produce a rigid-body mask. Distortion-native correspondences are then extracted directly on raw images using RoMA v2, with the mask injected as a confidence prior during feature sampling. A global bundle adjustment optimizes camera poses (split into rig trajectory and static camera-from-rig extrinsics) and sparse points, with a quadratic penalty enforcing the CAD extrinsics priors to prevent scale drift. Finally, 3DGUT (an Unscented Transform-based fisheye projection) renders the scene, and an MCMC-based densification strategy samples new Gaussians along high-gradient boundaries to handle specular surfaces. Training uses 10% held-out frames with full-vehicle masks and runs in ~20–30 minutes on an RTX A6000.

## Key Results
- On 25 real vehicles across 10 dealerships, the full pipeline achieves PSNR 28.66 dB, SSIM 0.89, LPIPS 0.21 on held-out views.
- This represents a 3.85 dB PSNR improvement over standard 3D-GS.
- Ablations show catastrophic failure without rigid-body masking (background reconstruction), significant degradation without rig-aware SfM (scale drift), and increased floaters without MCMC densification.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- **Claim:** Dynamic-scene disambiguation via rigid-body masking resolves the moving-object/static-background ambiguity and enforces epipolar geometry compliance.
- **Mechanism:** Motion-gated SAM-3 tracks the target vehicle across frames. Wheel masks are explicitly subtracted from the vehicle mask to construct a rigid-body mask that excludes rotating wheels. This mask is used as a spatial confidence prior during matching, ensuring downstream geometric constraints (epipolar geometry, bundle adjustment) apply only to the rigid car body.
- **Core Assumption:** Wheels are the primary source of non-rigid motion (rotation) that violates the single rigid-body assumption fundamental to multi-view SfM. Masking them is necessary for stable reconstruction.
- **Evidence Anchors:** Abstract: "...explicitly masking out non-rigid wheels to enforce strict epipolar geometry." Section III-C: "Because this local rotation R_w(t) is time-varying and distinct, integrating wheel features introduces severe non-rigid reprojection errors... Therefore, we explicitly segment and subtract the wheel masks..." Corpus: Weak/absent; neighbor papers address dynamic reconstruction but not the wheel-rotation sub-problem.
- **Break Condition:** Poor segmentation or motion-gating failure leaves rotating pixels in matches, leading to undefined reprojection error and collapse onto the static background.

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim:** Distortion-native dense correspondence on raw imagery using a learned matcher guided by semantic confidence preserves high-frequency detail and overcomes wide-angle lens distortion and specular surfaces.
- **Mechanism:** RoMA v2 is run directly on raw fisheye images (no pre-undistortion). The rigid-body mask is injected as a confidence prior into the matcher’s internal overlap tensors during keypoint sampling, forcing feature extraction to focus on the rigid body while retaining global context for symmetry and reflections.
- **Core Assumption:** A learned matcher can establish robust correspondences in distorted image space if guided to ignore invalid regions, and global context is essential for matching symmetric panels and specular highlights.
- **Evidence Anchors:** Abstract: "...extract robust correspondences directly on raw, distorted 4K imagery using the RoMa v2 learned matcher guided by semantic confidence masks." Section III-D: "We pass the full, un-undistorted image pairs... We then apply our semantic masks as confidence priors directly to the network’s internal overlap tensors." Corpus: Strong support from neighbors on learned matching for distorted scenes and Gaussian Splatting with distortion.
- **Break Condition:** Inaccurate masks, unresolved symmetry on specular surfaces, or mis-specified distortion models lead to sparse, fragmented matches and poor SfM registration.

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim:** Hardware-informed geometric optimization (rig-aware SfM) and stochastic densification for Gaussian Splatting jointly eliminate scale drift and render reflective surfaces without floaters.
- **Mechanism:**
  - **Part A (SfM):** Global bundle adjustment optimizes camera poses split into a dynamic rig trajectory and static camera-from-rig extrinsics. CAD-derived priors for static extrinsics are enforced with a quadratic penalty, preventing inter-camera scale drift while allowing minor adjustments for installation tolerances.
  - **Part B (GS):** 3DGUT replaces the standard pinhole Jacobian with an Unscented Transform to accurately project Gaussians through the fisheye distortion model. An MCMC-based densification strategy stochastically samples new splats along high-gradient boundaries (e.g., specular reflections), escaping local minima that cause floaters.
- **Core Assumption:** The physical rig is a rigid, known structure (CAD model provides accurate relative poses), and specular surfaces trap deterministic densification in local minima, requiring stochastic exploration.
- **Evidence Anchors:** Abstract: "...rig-aware SfM with CAD-derived pose priors to eliminate scale drift... distortion-aware 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGUT) coupled with a stochastic Markov Chain Monte Carlo (MCMC) densification strategy..." Section III-E: "We enforce structural rigidity by defining... and injecting CAD-derived camera-from-rig pose priors..." Section III-F: "To retain the uncompressed 4K quality, we use 3DGUT... To mitigate... floaters, we use the Markov Chain Monte Carlo (MCMC) strategy..."
- **Break Condition:**
  - **SfM:** Without CAD priors or overly relaxed constraints, wide-baseline optimization suffers inter-camera drift (fragmented reconstructions).
  - **GS:** Without distortion modeling, rendering quality degrades; without MCMC, specular regions develop floating artifacts.

## Foundational Learning
- **Concept:** Structure-from-Motion (SfM) assumptions and failure modes.
  - **Why needed:** The pipeline inverts the classic SfM assumption (static scene, moving camera). Understanding why a moving object against a static background breaks standard SfM is foundational.
  - **Quick Check:** Why does a moving vehicle in a static drive-through rig cause standard incremental SfM (like COLMAP) to reconstruct the background instead of the vehicle?

- **Concept:** Gaussian Splatting (3D-GS) and its differentiable rendering pipeline.
  - **Why needed:** The final output is a 3D Gaussian representation. Understanding the role of covariance, splatting, and optimization (densification, pruning) is key to modifying the architecture.
  - **Quick Check:** In standard 3D-GS, what does the "2D covariance" computed via the Jacobian of the pinhole projection represent, and how does it fail for fisheye lenses?

- **Concept:** Camera distortion models (e.g., fisheye) and their mathematical representation.
  - **Why needed:** The system operates on raw, distorted 4K imagery. All subsequent components (matching, SfM, GS) must incorporate the exact distortion parameters to avoid information loss.
  - **Quick Check:** What is the key difference between undistorting an image *before* processing versus propagating a calibrated distortion model *through* the geometry/rendering equations?

- **Concept:** Semantic segmentation and tracking in dynamic scenes.
  - **Why needed:** The rigid-body masking step relies on accurately isolating the target vehicle and subtracting wheels across frames.
  - **Quick Check:** How does motion-gating (e.g., background subtraction) improve robustness of SAM-based vehicle tracking in a drive-through where the vehicle is the primary moving object?

- **Concept:** Bundle adjustment with structural priors.
  - **Why needed:** Rig-aware SfM optimizes poses under a rigidity constraint and extrinsics priors to prevent scale drift across a multi-camera rig.
  - **Quick Check:** What is the effect of a quadratic penalty on camera extrinsics in a global bundle adjustment, and how does it balance structural stability with photometric fit?

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component Map:** Camera Calibration -> Rigid Masking (SAM 3 + motion-gating + wheel subtraction) -> Distortion-Native Matching (RoMA v2 + mask prior) -> Rig-Aware Global SfM (BA with CAD priors) -> Distortion-Aware Gaussian Splatting (3DGUT + MCMC densification)
- **Critical Path:** Calibration -> Rigid Masking -> Matching -> SfM. Errors in early steps are irreversible. SfM failure (no meaningful sparse cloud) stops the pipeline. GS is downstream and depends entirely on SfM output.
- **Design Tradeoffs:**
  - **Raw vs. Undistorted:** Retaining raw frames preserves resolution but requires distortion-aware algorithms (matching, SfM, GS). Pre-undistorting simplifies integration but loses peripheral detail and adds interpolation artifacts.
  - **Semantic vs. Geometric Masking:** Semantic masks (SAM 3) are robust to texture but can drift; motion-gating and temporal locking add robustness at the cost of potential failures on slow/stop motion.
  - **Global vs. Incremental SfM:** Global BA with rig priors prevents scale drift but is computationally heavier than incremental. The bounded extrinsics relaxation is a tradeoff between structural stability and photometric fit.
- **Failure Signatures:**
  - **SfM:** Low registration rate (<60%), undefined reprojection error, point cloud collapses onto background structure ("cube-like" or "room-like" as in Fig 6d).
  - **GS:** High LPIPS (>0.3), visible "floaters" (unattached splats) in specular regions, blurry or distorted rendering at image periphery.
  - **Masking:** Mask drift onto background vehicles when target is occluded/stalled; wheels not fully subtracted (leading to "spoked" artifact in sparse cloud).
- **First 3 Experiments:**
  1. Validate the rigid-body mask: Run the masking pipeline on a sequence with clearly rotating wheels. Visually inspect `M_rigid` masks and confirm wheels are subtracted while body remains. Quantify wheel pixel exclusion rate.
  2. Ablate the mask prior in RoMA v2: Run matching with and without the `M_rigid` confidence prior on the same image pair. Compare match count, inlier ratio after RANSAC, and spatial distribution (should be concentrated on body, not background).
  3. Test rig prior regularization: Run rig-aware SfM with different λ values for the extrinsics penalty (e.g., λ=0, λ=paper's value, λ=very high). Check for scale drift (measure distance between known rig points in point cloud vs. CAD) and final reprojection error. λ=0 should show drift; λ=too high may prevent convergence to accurate poses.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- **Question 1:** How can automated anomaly detection (e.g., scratches, dents) be directly integrated into the 3D Gaussian Splatting volume to enable efficient exterior defect identification?  
  **Basis:** Conclusion states future work will focus on integrating automated anomaly detection directly into the 3DGS volume.  
  **Why Unresolved:** No method, implementation, or evaluation provided; open direction with questions about representing anomalies within Gaussians and training without defect annotations.  
  **Evidence to Resolve:** A working system embedding an anomaly scoring function (e.g., reconstruction error, reflectance inconsistency) into Gaussian parameters, validated on a dataset with annotated defects; quantitative metrics like detection rate and false positive rate.

- **Question 2:** How does the presence of multiple moving vehicles in the dealership environment affect the robustness of the motion-gated semantic isolation step?  
  **Basis:** Inferred from Section III-C: motion-gating assumes the target is the sole significant moving object; experiments only use single-vehicle captures.  
  **Why Unresolved:** Isolation failures would propagate erroneous matches into SfM, causing collapse. Robustness to realistic distractor motion is critical for deployment.  
  **Evidence to Resolve:** Sequences with controlled moving distractors; measure final vehicle mask IoU vs. ground truth and downstream SfM metrics (registration rate, reprojection error). Ablations varying distractor speed/density.

- **Question 3:** What is the sensitivity of the rig-aware SfM reconstruction to errors in the wheel masks used for rigid-body masking?  
  **Basis:** Inferred: Pipeline subtracts wheel masks to construct rigid-body mask (Eq. 7); paper does not analyze wheel segmentation quality or impact of imperfect masks.  
  **Why Unresolved:** Inaccurate masks could leave rotating pixels violating rigidity, corrupting bundle adjustment. Success hinges on near-perfect subtraction, but real-world wheel segmentation may suffer from occlusion, similar paint, motion blur.  
  **Evidence to Resolve:** Systematically degrade wheel masks (erode/dilate, random pixel removal) on a held-out set; measure mean reprojection error, registration rate, and body point cloud completeness vs. mask IoU.

- **Question 4:** Does the MCMC-driven densification strategy improve the distribution of Gaussians on specular versus diffuse surfaces, and does it reduce floating artifacts more effectively than standard densification?  
  **Basis:** Inferred: Section III-F adopts MCMC for reflective surfaces, citing prior work, but lacks quantitative comparison of Gaussian density or artifact count.  
  **Why Unresolved:** Effectiveness is inferred from overall PSNR/SSIM gains; no direct evidence it specifically addresses "floater" problem or alters spatial density distribution across material types.  
  **Evidence to Resolve:** Count detached floaters per view (alpha threshold). Segment vehicle into specular/diffuse regions; compare Gaussian density (splats/area) and rendering variance for MCMC vs. standard densification. Correlate with PSNR.

- **Question 5:** How does the performance of the RoMA v2 feature matcher vary with different vehicle paint finishes (e.g., mirror-finish, metallic, matte), and does this variation directly explain differences in final reconstruction quality?  
  **Basis:** Inferred: Pipeline relies on RoMA v2 for robust correspondences under distortion and specularities; results section does not break down metrics by paint type.  
  **Why Unresolved:** Specular paints degrade matching due to reflections and lack of texture. Without per-finish analysis, unclear whether overall performance is limited by a subset of vehicles.  
  **Evidence to Resolve:** Manually label paint finish for each test vehicle. For each category, compute match density, inlier ratio after geometric verification, and SfM metrics (reprojection error, registration rate). Correlate with Gaussian Splatting quality (PSNR/SSIM).

## Limitations
- Four critical components are poorly specified: SAM 3 configuration/weights, RoMA v2 integration details and hyperparameters, CAD extrinsics prior generation and regularization bounds, and 3DGUT/MCMC implementation specifics.
- The pipeline is tightly coupled to a single pre-calibrated rig (14-camera dual-pillar), limiting generalizability to other capture setups.
- The assumption that wheels are the sole major non-rigid element may fail for other moving parts (e.g., mirrors, suspended components) or in scenes with multiple moving vehicles.

## Confidence
- **Mechanism 1 (Rigid-body masking):** Medium. Strongly supported by ablations (catastrophic failure without masks), but confidence capped by unspecified SAM 3/tracking and the assumption that wheels are the only major non-rigid element.
- **Mechanism 2 (Distortion-native matching):** Medium. Convincing gains over pre-undistorted baselines, but weak direct evidence that the mask prior is the key factor versus RoMA v2's inherent robustness; corpus support is indirect.
- **Mechanism 3 (Rig-aware SfM & MCMC GS):** Medium. Rig-prior necessity shown in ablations, MCMC's role plausible, but both hinge on unstated CAD-prior generation and MCMC hyperparameters.

## Next Checks
1. **Rigid-Body Mask Validation:** On a sequence with clearly rotating wheels, quantify IoU between full vehicle mask and `M_rigid`. Wheel exclusion rate must be >95% while body retention remains >90%. A failure here invalidates the core geometric assumption.
2. **RoMA v2 Mask Prior Ablation:** Run matching with identical weights and image pairs, with and without multiplying the overlap tensor by `M_rigid`. Report change in (a) inlier match count, (b) spatial distribution heatmap (should concentrate on body, not wheels/background), and (c) SfM registration rate. The prior must improve inlier ratio by >15%.
3. **Rig Prior Bounded Relaxation Test:** Perform rig-aware SfM with three λ settings: (a) λ=0 (no prior), (b) λ=paper's value, (c) λ=10x paper's value. For each, compute mean Euclidean error between optimized `T_rig_c` and CAD prior for cross-pillar camera pairs. (b) should show bounded residual (<5cm), (a) should show scale drift (>50cm), and (c) should show high final reprojection error, proving the necessity of the bounded relaxation.
