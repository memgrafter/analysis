# Reject, Resample, Repeat: Understanding Parallel Reasoning in Language Model Inference

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2603.07887
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.07887
- Reference count: 40
- Primary result: The paper introduces a theoretical framework based on Sequential Monte Carlo to analyze accuracy-cost tradeoffs in inference-time aggregation methods for LLMs, leading to new algorithms (SMC-RS, outer rejection) and fundamental limits.

## Executive Summary
This paper addresses the lack of theoretical understanding for inference-time methods that aggregate and prune parallel LLM samples (e.g., Best-of-N). It frames these methods as Sequential Monte Carlo (SMC) particle filtering, where a base language model and a process reward model (PRM) are used to sample from a tilted target distribution with limited PRM evaluations. Theoretical analysis identifies two key criteria—bounded action-level coverage (Cact) and bounded χ²-divergence (Cχ²)—that guarantee a non-asymptotic total variation error bound: DTV ≤ sqrt(H·2 Cact (Cχ²+1)/N). The paper also proposes algorithmic improvements: SMC-RS and an outer rejection loop that achieve exponential convergence under strong L∞ PRM accuracy. A fundamental lower bound shows any myopic particle method requires Ω(log H / log log H) particles even with constant PRM errors. Empirical studies on a prompt-switching task confirm that the theoretical criteria predict sampling error, while on Math500 and AIME, SMC with N=32 consistently outperforms Best-of-N. However, measured χ² divergence does not correlate with final accuracy, indicating a gap between current theory and practice and a need for refined metrics.

## Method Summary
The paper considers sampling from a tilted distribution π*_H ∝ π_ref·r*, where π_ref is a base autoregressive language model and r* is an unknown reward (approximated by a process reward model bV). The core algorithm is Sequential Monte Carlo (SMC): particles are advanced stepwise by sampling from π_ref, weighted by the ratio bV(x_i^h)/bV(parent_i), and resampled to maintain diversity. The weighted empirical distribution at the end approximates π*_H. Two enhancements are introduced: (1) SMC-RS, which replaces resampling with per-step rejection sampling conditioned on bV, and (2) an outer rejection loop that accepts the SMC output with probability proportional to an estimate of the normalizing constant, yielding exponential convergence in N when bV is L∞-accurate relative to the true value function. Theoretical guarantees are derived under two criteria: bounded action-level coverage (Cact) and bounded χ²-divergence (Cχ²) between the intermediate target and bV-induced distributions, giving the bound DTV ≤ sqrt(H·2 Cact (Cχ²+1)/N). A lower bound demonstrates that myopic particle methods (those not looking ahead) require at least Ω(log H / log log H) particles even under constant-factor PRM errors, and SMC itself needs Ω(√H) particles even with a perfect PRM, whereas SMC-RS achieves constant particle complexity only with a perfect PRM.

## Key Results
- Bounded action-level coverage (Cact) and bounded χ²-divergence (Cχ²) guarantee that SMC with N particles achieves total variation error ≤ sqrt(H·2 Cact (Cχ²+1)/N).
- An outer rejection loop, when combined with an L∞-accurate PRM, yields exponential convergence: DTV ≤ δ with N = O(H log(H/δ)) particles and expected O(C∞² log(1/δ)) restarts.
- Any myopic particle filtering method requires Ω(log H / log log H) particles under constant PRM errors; SMC requires Ω(√H) even with a perfect PRM, while SMC-RS reduces to O(1) only when the PRM is perfect.
- Empirically, on a controlled prompt-switching task the theoretical criteria predict SMC sampling error; on Math500 and AIME, SMC with N=32 consistently outperforms Best-of-N, though χ²-divergence does not correlate with final accuracy, revealing a limitation of the current theory.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1: SMC Guarantees via Bounded Coverage and Divergence
- **Claim:** Under bounded action-level coverage ($C_{\text{act}}$) and bounded $\chi^2$-divergence ($C_{\chi^2}$) between intermediate target and PRM distributions, SMC with $N$ particles achieves total variation error $\leq \sqrt{H \cdot 2 C_{\text{act}} (C_{\chi^2}+1) / N}$.
- **Mechanism:** SMC samples particles from the base LM, weights each by the ratio $\tilde{V}(x)/\tilde{V}(\text{parent})$ from the PRM, resamples to mitigate degeneracy, and outputs a weighted empirical measure. The error bound emerges from a martingale analysis of the normalizing constant product, where the two criteria control the variance of the weights and the deviation between reference and target.
- **Core assumption:** The PRM provides a (average) $\chi^2$-close approximation to the true value function, and the density ratios $\pi^*(a_{h+1}\mid a_{1:h})/\pi_{\text{ref}}(a_{h+1}\mid a_{1:h})$ are uniformly bounded by $C_{\text{act}}$.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [Abstract] explicitly lists the two criteria and the non-asymptotic bound.
  - [Theorem 3.2] presents the formal guarantee DTV $\leq \sqrt{H\cdot 2 C_{\text{act}} (C_{\chi^2}+1)/N}$.
  - [Corpus] indicates active work on SMC for LLMs (e.g., Power-SMC, Self-Rewarding SMC) but does not directly anchor this specific bound.
- **Break condition:** If either $C_{\text{act}}$ or $C_{\chi^2}$ grows with $H$, the bound becomes vacuous unless $N$ is increased superlinearly; $\chi^2$ may be infinite under heavy-tailed PRM errors, necessitating Mechanism 2.

### Mechanism 2: Tail-Robust SMC via Coverage Probability
- **Claim:** For any $M \geq 1$, SMC with $N$ particles satisfies DTV $\leq H\sqrt{M C_{\text{act}}/N} + \sum_{h} \text{Cov}_M(\pi^*_h \parallel \tilde{\pi}_h)$, where $\text{Cov}_M$ is the probability that the density ratio exceeds $M$. This bound remains meaningful even when $\chi^2$-divergence is infinite.
- **Mechanism:** The analysis replaces second-moment control with a first-moment (truncation) approach. By bounding the probability of large importance weight ratios—captured by $\text{Cov}_M$—the martingale increments are bounded in expectation without relying on $\chi^2$ moments.
- **Core assumption:** Action-level coverage $C_{\text{act}}$ is finite; the tail probabilities $\text{Cov}_M(\pi^*_h \parallel \tilde{\pi}_h)$ are small for some fixed $M$. This holds even if the PRM has heavy tails relative to the target.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [Theorem 3.5] states the bound in full generality.
  - [Section 3.1.1] introduces $\text{Cov}_M$ and explains its tail-robustness.
  - [Corpus] lacks direct treatment of such coverage probabilities; neighbor papers focus on algorithmic variants, not tail analysis.
- **Break condition:** If $\text{Cov}_M$ decays too slowly with $M$, the additive term dominates; large $C_{\text{act}}$ inflates the $\sqrt{M C_{\text{act}}/N}$ term, requiring more particles.

### Mechanism 3: Exponential Convergence via Outer Rejection
- **Claim:** If the PRM is $L_\infty$-accurate (i.e., $C_\infty^{-1} \leq \tilde{V}(x)/V^*(x) \leq C_\infty$ for all $x$) and action-level coverage is bounded, then wrapping SMC in an outer rejection sampling loop yields DTV $\leq \delta$ with $N = O(H \log(H/\delta))$ particles and expected $O(C_\infty^2 \log(1/\delta))$ restarts.
- **Mechanism:** The outer loop accepts the SMC output with probability $\min(c_{W_H} / (C_\infty \tilde{Z}_1), 1)$. Under $L_\infty$ accuracy, the acceptance probability is bounded away from zero, and the accepted distribution is close to the target. The rejection sampler’s geometric tail gives exponential convergence in $N$.
- **Core assumption:** The PRM satisfies a worst-case relative error bound ($C_\infty$ constant) and action-level coverage $C_{\text{act}}$ is finite. No tail assumptions needed.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [Algorithm 1 Output Option 2] and [Theorem 3.6] describe and analyze the outer rejection mechanism.
  - [Section 3.2] contrasts this with the polynomial rates of standard SMC.
  - [Corpus] includes prior rejection sampling for LLMs (Yang & Klein, 2021) but the combination with SMC for exponential rates is novel to this work.
- **Break condition:** If the $L_\infty$ assumption fails (PRM can be arbitrarily wrong on some inputs), the acceptance probability may vanish and exponential convergence is lost; large $C_\infty$ also increases the constant overhead.

## Foundational Learning
- **Concept:** Sequential Monte Carlo (Particle Filtering)  
  *Why needed here:* It is the core algorithmic paradigm; understanding resampling and weighting is essential to grasp the error analysis and improvements like SMC-RS.  
  *Quick check question:* "Why does SMC resample particles at each step, and how does resampling affect the particle diversity?"

- **Concept:** Total Variation Distance  
  *Why needed here:* The theoretical guarantees are expressed in terms of TV error, which directly measures the distinguishability of the output distribution from the target.  
  *Quick check question:* "What is the relationship between total variation distance and KL divergence? When might TV be preferred in analysis?"

- **Concept:** Action-Level Coverage  
  *Why needed here:* It is one of the two key criteria; it quantifies how much the target conditional distribution deviates from the reference LM’s conditional.  
  *Quick check question:* "If action-level coverage is large, what does that imply about the optimal proposal distribution relative to $\pi_{\text{ref}}$?"

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map:**
  Base LM ($\pi_{\text{ref}}$) -> SMC Core (sample, weight, resample) -> Output Selector (uniform or max-$\tilde{V}$).  
  Optional: Outer Rejection Loop wraps SMC Core; SMC-RS replaces resampling with stepwise rejection.

- **Critical path:**
  1. Initialize $N$ particles at start token $\bot$.
  2. For $h = 1$ to $H$:
     - For each particle, generate a child token via $\pi_{\text{ref}}(\cdot\mid \text{parent})$.
     - Compute weight $w_i = \tilde{V}(x_i^h)/\tilde{V}(\tilde{x}_i^{h-1})$.
     - Normalize weights; resample $N$ particles with replacement from weighted empirical distribution.
  3. If using outer rejection: compute $c_{W_H}$; accept output with probability $\min(c_{W_H}/(C_\infty \tilde{Z}_1), 1)$ else restart from step 1.
  4. Without rejection, output a particle from $\hat{\nu}_H$ uniformly at random or take the max-$\tilde{V}$ particle.

- **Design tradeoffs:**
  - **Particle count ($N$):** Larger $N$ improves accuracy (error $\sim 1/\sqrt{N}$) but linearly increases compute and PRM queries. SMC-RS may reduce required $N$ for perfect PRM but adds rejection overhead.
  - **PRM quality:** Bounded coverage and $\chi^2$ are essential; inaccurate PRMs cause weight degeneracy. Achieving $L_\infty$ accuracy enables exponential rates but may be harder in practice.
  - **Granularity of actions:** Using token blocks (instead of single tokens) reduces PRM calls but may miss fine-grained reward signals; requires truncation at natural boundaries to avoid invalid inputs to the PRM.
  - **Resampling strategy:** Systematic vs. multinomial resampling affects variance; systematic resampling reduces variance but is less parallelizable.

- **Failure signatures:**
  - **Weight collapse:** Most weights become extremely small (high empirical coefficient of variation); indicates large $C_{\text{act}}$ or $C_{\chi^2}$.
  - **Mode collapse:** All particles converge to identical sequences (low particle diversity); check distinct sequences count at final step.
  - **Excessive restarts:** Outer rejection loop repeats many times; suggests $c_{W_H}$ often far from $Z$ due to poor PRM accuracy.
  - **No convergence with $N$:** TV error (e.g., via partition function estimator) plateaus; suspect coverage or divergence criteria violation.

- **First 3 experiments:**
  1. **Prompt-switching with controlled PRM accuracy:** Follow Section 4.2: fix $\pi_{\text{ref}} = \pi^*$ (so coverage constant), vary PRM via Eq. (2) with $\alpha$, measure KL divergence between $\hat{\pi}_h$ and $\pi^*_h$ and sampling error (P‑logprob discrepancy). Expect correlation as in Fig 2b.
  2. **Prompt-switching with varying coverage:** Follow Section 4.3: keep PRM perfect ($\tilde{V}=V^*$), vary reference prompt to change action-level coverage (estimated via DKL$(\pi^*\parallel\pi_{\text{ref}})$), measure sampling error. Expect correlation as in Fig 2a.
  3. **Particle scaling and Best-of-N comparison:** Follow Section 4.4: on prompt-switching task, run SMC with $N \in \{2,4,\dots,128\}$, plot sampling error vs. wall‑clock time and compare to sequential importance sampling and Best-of-N baselines; expect SMC to dominate (Fig 3).

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- **Open Question 1**  
  *Question:* Why does the chi-square divergence between the approximate intermediate target distribution $b\pi_h$ and the true target $\pi^\star_h$ fail to correlate with final accuracy on challenging math reasoning benchmarks, despite theory predicting a strong correlation between this divergence and sampling error?  
  *Basis in paper:* In Section 4.5, the authors observe that on Math500 and AIME, SMC accuracy often increases with $\chi^2$ divergence (measured via inverse temperature scaling of the PRM), contrary to theoretical expectations. They conclude: "the quality of the PRM $bV$... does not seem to positively correlate with performance, as often larger divergence leads to higher accuracy. We leave as an intriguing open question the development of a more refined framework that captures performance on such benchmarks."  
  *Why unresolved:* The theoretical criteria ($C_{\text{act}}$, $C_{\chi^2}$) are designed to bound total variation distance of SMC's output, but empirical accuracy on real tasks appears to depend on other factors not captured by distributional closeness (e.g., mode coverage, diversity, or interactions with Best-of-N selection).  
  *What evidence would resolve it:* New theoretical metrics that correlate with downstream accuracy in practice (e.g., coverage of correct solutions rather than total variation), or controlled experiments disentangling the effects of sampling error, diversity loss, and search bias.

- **Open Question 2**  
  *Question:* What is the optimal particle complexity for myopic particle filtering methods under constant-factor PRM error? Can the gap between the upper bound $\text{poly}(H)$ (e.g., $\Omega(\sqrt{H})$ for SMC) and the lower bound $\Omega(\log H / \log \log H)$ for myopic methods be closed?  
  *Basis in paper:* Theorem 3.9 shows any myopic particle filtering algorithm (including SMC variants) needs $\Omega(\log H / \log \log H)$ particles to achieve non-trivial coverage under mild PRM imperfections. In contrast, SMC requires $\Omega(\sqrt{H})$ even with a perfect PRM (Proposition C.8), and SMC-RS reduces to $O(1)$ only when $bV = V^\star$. The authors state: "Closing this gap is open." (Section 3.4 and Discussion).  
  *Why unresolved:* The lower bound demonstrates that some horizon dependence is necessary, but it is unclear whether $\log H / \log \log H$ is tight or if a super-logarithmic lower bound exists. The upper bounds for myopic methods are polynomial, and reducing them would likely require non-myopic strategies (lookahead).  
  *What evidence would resolve it:* A matching $\Omega(\text{poly}(H))$ lower bound for myopic methods under constant-factor errors, or a new algorithm (possibly with lookahead) achieving $O(\log H)$ particles while maintaining robustness to PRM errors.

- **Open Question 3**  
  *Question:* What weaker notions of distributional proximity (than total variation) should be used to analyze SMC's effectiveness when the goal is not to approximate the entire target distribution but to capture a subset of high-reward solutions?  
  *Basis in paper:* In the Discussion (Section 5), the authors ask: "Can we develop metrics for the performance of SMC... which are weaker than total variation distance but still meaningfully capture ‘usefulness’ of outputs? For instance, for math problems, typically one only desires a correct answer..." They also note that the current framework’s focus on distributional closeness fails to explain SMC gains on Math500 (Section 4.5).  
  *Why unresolved:* Total variation requires uniform closeness over all events, but for tasks like math problem-solving, success only requires that the output set contains at least one correct solution (partial coverage). Existing bounds either require uniform closeness (TV) or suffer from vacuous constants.  
  *What evidence would resolve it:* Theoretical guarantees in terms of coverage probabilities (e.g., $\mathbb{P}(x \in E)$ for high-reward events $E$) instead of TV, validated by empirical correlation with accuracy on benchmark tasks.

## Limitations
- Theoretical guarantees (Cact, Cχ²) may not hold in complex reasoning tasks: on Math500/AIME, Cact is extremely large and empirical χ² shows no correlation with final accuracy, indicating a mismatch between assumptions and reality.
- The integration of the PRM (how step-level scores are aggregated into bV(a₁:ₕ)) is underspecified, hindering reproducibility and accurate computation of Cact/Cχ².
- Estimating Cact and Cχ² in realistic LLM settings is computationally challenging; the paper’s reliance on a KL-divergence proxy may not fully capture weight variance or degeneracy.
- The outer rejection loop’s exponential guarantee depends on an unlikely L∞ accuracy assumption; if violated, the acceptance probability can become negligible, rendering the method inefficient.
