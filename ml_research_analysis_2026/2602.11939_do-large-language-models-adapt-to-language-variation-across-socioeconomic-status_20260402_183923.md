# Do Large Language Models Adapt to Language Variation across Socioeconomic Status?

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2602.11939
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.11939
- **Reference Count:** 40
- **Primary Result:** Large language models minimally adapt their language style to socioeconomic status (SES) communities, often producing caricatured rather than accurate emulations, with a latent bias toward upper-SES patterns.

## Executive Summary
This study investigates whether large language models (LLMs) can adapt their linguistic style to match that of distinct socioeconomic status (SES) communities. Using SES-stratified datasets from Reddit and YouTube, the researchers prompted four LLMs to complete texts with three levels of SES instruction (implicit, explicit style, explicit SES). Generated outputs were compared to human-written continuations across 94 sociolinguistic features. The findings indicate that LLMs fail to accurately replicate community-specific registers; instead, they produce approximations or caricatures, exaggerating differences and showing a latent tendency to align more closely with upper-SES language. This raises concerns about the validity of LLM-based simulations that rely on authentic social language variation.

## Method Summary
The researchers built two SES-stratified corpora (Reddit and YouTube) using keywords and hobbies associated with lower- and upper-SES groups, validated via seven readability metrics. For each human-authored text, the first 25 words served as a prompt. Four LLMs (Gemma-3-27B-it, Mistral-Small-3.2-24B-Instruct-2506, Qwen3-30B-A3B-Instruct-2507, GPT-5) were prompted to generate a single continuation using three strategies: implicit (IMP), explicit style-matching (ELS), and explicit SES labeling (ELS-SES). For all human and model completions, 94 sociolinguistic features (Biber dimensions, part-of-speech ratios, length metrics, lexical diversity) were extracted. Statistical comparisons between lower- and upper-SES human text and between model outputs and the human baseline were performed using Mann-Whitney U tests with Holm-Bonferroni correction. An ablation study varied the prompt context length (25 vs. 80 words).

## Key Results
- LLMs show only minor modulation of linguistic style by SES, frequently resulting in inaccurate approximations or exaggerated caricatures of human community patterns.
- Explicit SES labeling (ELS-SES) does not improve alignment and often worsens inaccuracies, amplifying stylistic differences beyond human norms.
- A latent bias toward upper-SES language is observed, particularly with longer context, where models integrate more seamlessly with upper-SES text than lower-SES text.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1
- **Claim:** LLMs fail to accurately replicate SES-specific linguistic styles, often producing caricatured outputs.
- **Mechanism:** Standard next-token prediction objectives prioritize semantic coherence and statistical likelihood over fine-grained, socially-situated stylistic adaptation. Models latch onto surface-level cues (e.g., word length, function words) but cannot capture integrated community registers.
- **Evidence anchors:** Abstract states models modulate style "to only a minor extent, often resulting in approximation or caricature." Section 6.1 notes struggles with matching register formality and concreteness. The 94-feature analysis quantifies discrepancy but does not specify architectural cause.
- **Break condition:** Introduction of a dedicated stylistic conditioning module trained on SES-stratified data might reduce caricature; current evidence suggests standard objectives are insufficient.

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim:** Explicitly instructing the LLM about the target SES (ELS-SES) does not improve alignment and frequently exacerbates inaccuracies.
- **Mechanism:** The explicit SES label is a high-level semantic directive that the model cannot robustly map onto the low-level, distributed stylistic features learned during training. The model's concept of SES-linked writing is a crude stereotype, not a nuanced template.
- **Evidence anchors:** Section 4 contrasts prompt strategies, finding little difference between IMP and ELS, but ELS-SES "exacerbates the differences." Section 6.1 confirms all four models exaggerate differences under ELS-SES.
- **Break condition:** Discovery of a prompt structure or context that reliably elicits accurate SES style matching across a broad feature set would falsify this.

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim:** There is a latent bias in LLM generation toward linguistic patterns associated with upper-SES communities, more evident with longer contexts.
- **Mechanism:** Training data distributions likely overrepresent "standard" or prestige language varieties aligned with upper-middle-class norms, creating a model prior closer to upper-SES patterns. Longer input contexts provide more signal for the model to default to this prior, while lacking a strong coherent prior for lower-SES non-standard patterns.
- **Evidence anchors:** Section 7's context-length ablation shows Gemini integrates better with upper-SES language at 80 words. Section 8 notes "latent tendency for models to better emulate the style of upper SES communities." Convergent evidence from a neighboring paper on systemic SES bias in language technologies supports the data skew inference, though the main paper does not analyze its own training corpus.
- **Break condition:** A model trained on a perfectly SES-balanced corpus that showed no upper-SES preference in the context ablation would challenge this.

## Foundational Learning
- **Concept: Sociolinguistic Register & SES Variation**
  - **Why needed:** The entire study hinges on the premise that language style (lexical choice, syntax, formality) correlates with socioeconomic status. This foundational knowledge validates the chosen metrics and the interpretation of human vs. model differences.
  - **Quick check:** What are three linguistically validated markers (e.g., specific pronouns, tense usage, vocabulary diversity) that typically distinguish higher-SES from lower-SES speech/writing in Western contexts?
- **Concept: Readability Metrics as SES Proxy**
  - **Why needed:** Standardized readability scores (ARI, Flesch-Kincaid) externally validate that the dataset collection strategy successfully stratified language by SES. This is a core validation step.
  - **Quick check:** Why would a higher grade-level readability score generally be associated with upper-SES text in this study's context?
- **Concept: LLM Prompting Limitations for Non-Semantic Attributes**
  - **Why needed:** The experimental design tests how instruction explicitness affects a model's ability to perform style adaptation. Understanding the risks of using high-level labels (like "lower SES") versus implicit cues is key.
  - **Quick check:** What is a key risk of using an explicit SES label in a prompt for a style transfer task, as opposed to an implicit style cue?

## Architecture Onboarding
Data Pipeline -> LLM Inference Engine -> Linguistic Feature Extractor -> Statistical Analysis Module
- **Critical path:** Data validation (readability metrics) -> Prompt definition & instance splitting -> Batch inference -> Feature extraction -> Statistical testing -> Visualization.
- **Design tradeoffs:**
  - **Breadth vs. Depth:** 94 features provide breadth but may miss nuanced, higher-order stylistic patterns (e.g., discourse structure). Deeper analysis might use fewer, more theoretically grounded features.
  - **Platform Representativeness:** Using Reddit & YouTube provides scale but limits findings to these registers. Text from formal writing or spoken transcripts would yield different feature distributions.
  - **SES Proxy:** Keyword/hobby-based community sourcing is pragmatic but imperfect. True SES is multi-dimensional (income, education, occupation).
- **Failure signatures:**
  1. Non-significant readability difference between "lower" and "upper" dataset splits → Data stratification failed.
  2. Model output length >> human output across all conditions → Indicates verbosity bias.
  3. No significant feature differences between model prompts (P1 vs P3) → Prompts are ineffective.
  4. Model token type ratio consistently lower than human → Model is less lexically diverse.
- **First 3 experiments:**
  1. **Dataset Sanity Check:** Compute and compare all 7 readability metrics for your "lower" and "upper" SES data splits. Expect statistically significant differences in the predicted direction.
  2. **Single-Model Baseline:** Run P1 (IMP) for one model on a small sample (n=100 per SES). Extract the 94 features. Plot the ratio of model/human for each feature.
  3. **Prompt Efficacy Test:** For one model, compare P1 (IMP) and P3 (ELS-SES) on the same 100-instance sample. Calculate the average absolute log ratio across all features.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
### Open Question 1
- **Question:** Why do LLMs exhibit a latent upper SES bias in style adaptation, as suggested by the better integration with upper SES language under longer contexts?
- **Basis in paper:** Inferred from the context-length ablation (Section 7). The authors note the tendency but do not investigate root causes.
- **Why unresolved:** The paper identifies the bias but does not explore whether it stems from training data distribution, architectural preferences, or other factors.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Controlled experiments varying the socioeconomic distribution of training data, analyzing model attention patterns on SES markers, or comparing adaptation across models with different pretraining corpora.

### Open Question 2
- **Question:** To what extent do the findings generalize to other social media platforms and to middle-SES communities, which were excluded from the study?
- **Basis in paper:** Explicitly stated in Limitations; dataset limited to Reddit and YouTube and lacks a middle-SES group.
- **Why unresolved:** The study focuses on binary SES extremes on two platforms; it is unclear whether the poor adaptation and upper-SES bias hold in other contexts or across the full SES spectrum.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Replicating the study on additional platforms (e.g., Twitter/X, Facebook) with a tripartite SES stratification using consistent methodologies.

### Open Question 3
- **Question:** Can more nuanced or deeper linguistic analyses (e.g., pragmatic markers, discourse structure, semantic features) reveal latent SES adaptation not captured by the 94 surface-level sociolinguistic metrics used?
- **Basis in paper:** Inferred; authors acknowledge their set of 94 metrics is "not exhaustive" and that LLMs often resulted in "approximation or caricature."
- **Why unresolved:** The study relies on predefined, primarily surface-level features; it is possible LLMs adapt in ways not measured by these metrics but were not tested.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Incorporating qualitative analysis, human evaluation of "naturalness" for SES groups, or extending the feature set to include more complex discourse and semantic measures.

### Open Question 4
- **Question:** How does the observed failure to accurately emulate SES-specific styles affect the validity and outcomes of LLM-based agent simulations in social science research?
- **Basis in paper:** Explicitly stated in abstract/conclusion; calls into question validity for agent-based social simulation, survey experiments, and research relying on language style as a social signal.
- **Why unresolved:** The paper demonstrates stylistic mismatch but does not test how this translates to specific simulation tasks.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Running comparative agent-based simulations using LLM agents versus human data in SES-diverse scenarios, measuring divergence in emergent phenomena attributable to stylistic inaccuracies.

## Limitations
- **SES Proxy Validity:** Using Reddit/YouTube keywords/hobbies as SES markers is pragmatic but indirect; it assumes community affiliation correlates reliably with multidimensional SES (income, education, occupation) without validation against ground-truth demographic data.
- **Training Data Skew Assumption:** The claim of a latent upper-SES bias rests on inferring that the models’ pretraining corpora overrepresent "prestige" varieties. The paper does not analyze its training data composition, leaving this as an untested conjecture.
- **Feature Set Scope:** The 94 sociolinguistic features capture many surface and syntactic patterns but may miss higher-order stylistic registers (e.g., narrative structure, audience design). Results could differ with a theoretically narrower or discourse-focused feature set.

## Confidence
- **"LLMs minimally modulate style by SES and often caricature"** → **Medium-High** (Directly supported by main analysis across 94 features, but "caricature" is an interpretive label for observed exaggeration).
- **"Explicit SES instruction (ELS-SES) exacerbates inaccuracies"** → **High** (Consistently observed across models in Section 6.1 and Figure 4).
- **"Latent upper-SES bias evident with longer context"** → **Medium** (Supported by context-length ablation, but effect size is "minor" and mechanism unverified).

## Next Checks

2. **Measure output diversity vs. human baseline:** Calculate the Token Type Ratio (TTR) and hapax legomena rate for all model outputs and human completions. Verify the paper’s finding that models have systematically lower lexical diversity.
3. **Test a few-shot style prompt:** For one model (e.g., GPT-5), design a prompt that includes 2–3 actual human continuations (with SES labels) as exemplars before the target cue. Compare feature ratios to the paper’s ELS-SES prompt. A significant improvement would challenge the claim that prompts cannot elicit accurate style transfer.
