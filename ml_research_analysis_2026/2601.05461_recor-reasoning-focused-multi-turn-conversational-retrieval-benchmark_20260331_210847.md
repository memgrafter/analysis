# RECOR: Reasoning-focused Multi-turn Conversational Retrieval Benchmark

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2601.05461  
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.05461  
- **Reference count:** 40  
- **Primary result:** Incorporating explicit retrieval reasoning into multi-turn conversations doubles retrieval performance (nDCG@10 from 0.236 to 0.479).

## Executive Summary
This paper introduces RECOR, a benchmark designed to bridge the gap between multi-turn conversational search and reasoning-intensive retrieval. Using a Decomposition-and-Verification framework, the authors construct 707 conversations (2,971 turns) across 11 domains by transforming complex queries into dialogues with fact-grounded, atomic reasoning steps. The key finding is that explicitly modeling retrieval reasoning—beyond just conversation history—substantially improves performance, with reasoning-specialized models like DIVER achieving state-of-the-art results. However, a persistent 6.3% failure rate on queries requiring implicit logical connections highlights remaining challenges.

## Method Summary
RECOR is built via a Decomposition-and-Verification pipeline. Complex information-seeking queries are first decomposed into a sequence of simpler, fact-based sub-questions. Each sub-question is then verified against a knowledge source to ensure factuality, and the verified facts are woven into a multi-turn dialogue where each turn corresponds to a reasoning step that requires retrieval. This process yields structured conversations with explicit retrieval rationales, enabling evaluation of models’ ability to perform stepwise reasoning within a conversational context.

## Key Results
- Adding explicit retrieval reasoning to conversation history doubles nDCG@10 (0.236 → 0.479).
- Reasoning-specialized models (e.g., DIVER at 0.584) significantly outperform dense retrievers (best at 0.464).
- 6.3% of queries fail due to implicit reasoning needs, regardless of model configuration.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The explicit decomposition and verification process forces models to engage in stepwise, fact-checked reasoning. By grounding each dialogue turn in a verified atomic fact and a clear retrieval goal, the benchmark makes reasoning interpretable and reduces hallucination. This structure allows retrieval systems to leverage intermediate reasoning states, improving document ranking by aligning retrieval with the logical flow of the conversation rather than just surface-level query matching.

## Foundational Learning
- **Multi-turn conversational retrieval:** Need to model context dependencies across dialogue turns. Quick check: Does history truncation degrade performance on later turns?
- **Reasoning-intensive queries:** Queries requiring logical inference beyond keyword matching. Quick check: Can models identify when a query needs decomposition?
- **Query decomposition:** Breaking complex questions into atomic sub-questions. Quick check: What decomposition depth yields optimal retrieval?
- **Fact verification:** Ensuring sub-questions are answerable from the corpus. Quick check: How does verification accuracy affect final retrieval?
- **Explicit reasoning traces:** Providing intermediate rationales for retrieval decisions. Quick check: Do traces correlate with human judgment of reasoning quality?
- **Retrieval-augmented reasoning:** Using retrieved documents to inform next-step reasoning. Quick check: When does retrieval introduce noise vs. helpful context?

## Architecture Onboarding
Component map: User Query -> Decomposer -> Fact Verifier -> Dialogue Constructor -> Retriever/Reasoner -> Ranked Results. Critical path: Decompose → Verify → Retrieve per turn. Design tradeoffs: Structured dialogues vs. natural conversational flow; explicit reasoning overhead vs. interpretability. Failure signatures: Inability to infer unstated logical links (6.3% failure); over-reliance on surface form without deep reasoning. First experiments: 1) Evaluate DIVER on TREC CAsT zero-shot; 2) Human evaluation of reasoning trace validity; 3) Apply framework to noisy customer service logs and measure performance drop.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
The paper does not explicitly articulate open questions beyond highlighting the challenge of implicit reasoning. The primary unresolved issue is handling queries where necessary logical connections are not stated but must be inferred—a failure mode affecting 6.3% of the benchmark.

## Limitations
- The Decomposition-and-Verification construction may not capture the ambiguity and noise of real user conversations.
- Performance gains demonstrated on RECOR may not generalize to other benchmarks or production environments.
- The 6.3% implicit reasoning failure suggests a fundamental capability gap not addressed by current explicit reasoning approaches.

## Confidence
- **Benchmark creation & dataset release:** High (detailed methodology, clear metrics).
- **Relative performance of reasoning vs. dense models:** High (consistent, substantial quantitative gap).
- **Real-world impact & solvability of implicit reasoning:** Low (artificial dataset, persistent failures indicate unsolved challenges).

## Next Checks
1. Conduct zero-shot evaluation of top RECOR models (e.g., DIVER) on TREC CAsT to test cross-benchmark generalization.
2. Perform a human study annotating the necessity and validity of model-generated reasoning traces versus human implicit reasoning.
3. Stress-test the Decomposition-and-Verification framework on noisy conversation logs (e.g., customer service chats) to assess robustness and resulting data quality.
