# Sinkhorn-Drifting Generative Models

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2603.12366
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.12366
- **Reference count:** 40
- **Primary result:** Sinkhorn drifting eliminates mode collapse at low temperatures and reduces mean FID from 187.7 to 37.1 on FFHQ-ALAE at τ=0.1.

## Executive Summary
This paper resolves theoretical identifiability gaps and practical instability in drifting generative models by replacing heuristic one-sided normalization with full two-sided Sinkhorn scaling. The drift field is derived from the Wasserstein gradient flow of the Sinkhorn divergence, ensuring that zero drift implies exact match between model and target distributions. This change prevents mode collapse at low temperatures, as demonstrated by substantial improvements in FID and latent EMD on FFHQ-ALAE and consistent class coverage on MNIST across a wide temperature range, while adding no overhead at inference time.

## Method Summary
Sinkhorn drifting computes the negative drift term using a doubly stochastic coupling obtained via the Sinkhorn algorithm, rather than a row-normalized kernel. Given model samples X and reference samples Y_pos (positives) and Y_neg (negatives, often self-sampled X), the drift is V = P_pos @ Y_pos - P_neg @ Y_neg, where P_pos and P_neg are Sinkhorn-scaled Gibbs kernels (marginals enforced by alternating row/column scaling for T iterations). Training minimizes the squared error between X and one-step displaced points (X+V) via stop-gradient, learning the generator to mimic this drift. Inference requires only a single forward pass through the generator, identical in cost to baseline drifting.

## Key Results
- On FFHQ-ALAE at τ=0.1, mean FID drops from 187.7 to 37.1 and mean latent EMD from 453.3 to 144.4 versus one-sided drifting.
- On MNIST, Sinkhorn drifting maintains perfect class coverage (≥99.97% accuracy) across all temperatures τ∈[0.005,0.1], while the baseline collapses to a single mode for τ≤0.05.
- The method eliminates mode collapse at low temperatures, with Figure 1 showing diverse trajectories on 2D mixtures where baseline collapses.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1: Identifiability via Sinkhorn Definiteness
The drift field is the gradient flow of the Sinkhorn divergence, which is definite (zero iff distributions equal). Under smooth densities on a compact domain, a zero drift (stationary point) forces model and target to match because the Sinkhorn divergence is strictly convex in its second argument. This resolves the identifiability gap of prior drifting, where one-sided normalization allowed zero drift without distributional equality. Propositions 3.7 and 3.8 formalize this under regularity conditions (compact support, Lipschitz cost, absolutely continuous measures); full Sinkhorn convergence is required, and violations (e.g., non-smooth densities) may weaken the guarantee.

### Mechanism 2: Mitigating Low-Temperature Mode Collapse
At low τ, the Gibbs kernel becomes sharply peaked. With self-negatives (Y_neg = X), row-normalization concentrates weight on self-interactions because k(x_i,x_i)=1 while off-diagonals →0. This makes the negative drift term vanish (since subtracting self yields zero), leaving only attraction and causing collapse. Two-sided Sinkhorn scaling enforces column sums too, forcing off-diagonal mass to remain non-zero even when self-weights dominate, thereby preserving repulsion and diversity. This is detailed in section 5.1 and visually confirmed in Figure 1, relying on distinct points and self-sampling; separate negative batches avoid the specific degeneracy but doubly stochastic constraints still help.

### Mechanism 3: Improved One-Step Generative Quality
By aligning the drift with the Wasserstein gradient flow of the Sinkhorn divergence—especially at low τ where it approximates the 2-Wasserstein flow—the learned generator produces better one-step samples. The drift combines cross-attraction to positives and repulsion from negatives with balanced weights from Sinkhorn scaling. Empirical results on FFHQ-ALAE and MNIST show large FID/EMD improvements at low τ, confirming that the principled drift yields a generator closer to the target after one step. Gains narrow at higher τ (Sinkhorn approaches MMD) and depend on sufficient Sinkhorn iterations.

## Architecture Onboarding
Component map: Noise sampler -> Generator f_θ -> Sinkhorn drift module (with Y_pos, Y_neg) -> Drift V -> Loss (squared error) -> Optimizer -> Parameter update.

Critical path: The Sinkhorn scaling loop (alternating row/column normalization of the Gibbs kernel) is the essential added computation, taking O(T·B²) per batch.

Design tradeoffs:
- **Sinkhorn iterations T**: More iterations improve coupling accuracy but increase time linearly; T≈30 near convergence, T=1 recovers baseline.
- **Temperature τ**: Lower τ sharpens kernel and often improves results but risks numerical issues and requires more iterations; typical range 0.01–10.
- **Negative samples**: Self-sampling (Y_neg = X) is simplest; separate batches add data-loading overhead but avoid specific degeneracies.
- **Batch size B**: Larger B better approximates uniform marginals but increases O(B²) cost; constrained by memory.

Failure signatures:
- **Mode collapse** (e.g., MNIST accuracy ~10%): likely T too small or τ too low; verify Sinkhorn iterations applied and Y_neg set correctly.
- **NaNs/Infs in Sinkhorn**: caused by extreme τ or duplicate points; mitigate with distance clipping or jitter.
- **Exploding drift magnitude ‖V‖**: may indicate τ too large relative to distance scale; monitor and reduce τ.
- **No improvement over baseline**: check T≥5 and that row-normalization occurs after Sinkhorn; ensure doubly stochastic matrices used.
- **Slow training**: profile Sinkhorn loop; consider reducing T or B, or using early stopping.

First 3 experiments:
1. **Toy 2-D multimodal coverage**: Train on 8-Gaussians with B=500, τ=0.1, T=30. Plot final samples and W₂ curve. Expect Sinkhorn to cover all 8 modes (W₂<1), baseline collapse.
2. **MNIST class-conditional sweep**: Train with τ∈{0.005,0.01,0.02,0.1}. Compute class accuracy on 1000 generated samples using pretrained classifier. Baseline should collapse (~10%) for τ≤0.05, Sinkhorn >99% across all τ.
3. **Ablation on Sinkhorn iterations T**: On a simple dataset (e.g., 2-Moons) with fixed τ=0.1, vary T∈{1,5,10,30}. Measure final W₂ after fixed training steps. Expect monotonic improvement with T, plateauing around T=30.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
1. For empirical measures with uniform weights on a finite set, does the Sinkhorn divergence remain strictly convex? If not, under what conditions does zero drift imply p=q for any n? The paper shows stationarity but not necessarily identity for n≥3 with τ>0; resolving this would complete the identifiability proof.
2. Does Sinkhorn drifting retain its advantages when scaled to large-scale benchmarks like ImageNet-1K? Current experiments are smaller (MNIST, FFHQ-ALAE) due to compute constraints; scalability and stability at massive scale remain open.
3. How does truncating Sinkhorn iterations (finite T) affect the identifiability guarantee and dynamics? The theory assumes converged couplings; finite-T drift may introduce bias and alter equilibria, potentially undermining the gradient flow interpretation.

## Limitations
- The identifiability proof relies on smooth densities on compact domains, which may not hold for real-world high-dimensional data, weakening theoretical guarantees.
- Training incurs O(T·B²) overhead from Sinkhorn scaling, which could be prohibitive for large batch sizes or high-resolution images, though inference is unaffected.
- Results are demonstrated only on MNIST and latent-space FFHQ; generalization to more diverse, large-scale datasets (e.g., raw pixels, ImageNet) is unvalidated.
