# Unintended Memorization of Sensitive Information in Fine-Tuned Language Models

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2601.17480
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.17480
- **Reference count:** 40
- **Primary result:** Fine-tuning language models on data with input-only PII leads to measurable memorization; differential privacy reduces leakage most effectively but is unstable, while post-training methods offer more consistent privacy-utility trade-offs.

## Executive Summary
This study investigates the privacy vulnerability wherein language models memorize personally identifiable information (PII) that appears solely in the input during supervised fine-tuning, even when the downstream task does not require predicting it. Using a True-Prefix Attack (TPA) to probe for exact PII completion across synthetic financial and real German medical datasets, the authors quantify memorization and evaluate four mitigation strategies: differential privacy (DP) fine-tuning, machine unlearning (UnDial), regularization, and direct preference optimization (DPO). Key findings indicate that fine-tuning increases model confidence in predicting PII tokens, that raw PII frequency is a weak predictor of extraction, and that DP offers the strongest leakage reduction but with high training instability. Post-training methods (unlearning, DPO) provide more stable trade-offs, though no approach eliminates memorization entirely.

## Method Summary
The study employs parameter-efficient fine-tuning (QLoRA) on Llama 3.2 1B across three datasets: a synthetic multilingual financial corpus (GretelAI), German pathology reports, and German discharge summaries with manually or semi-automatically annotated PII. The central evaluation metric is the True-Prefix Attack (TPA), which provides the model with the exact preceding context of a PII span and checks whether greedy or sampled decoding reproduces the PII. Memori zation is also assessed via "cross-memorization" (comparing generations to all same-type PII in the corpus). Mitigations compared include: (1) DP-SGD via Opacus with varying ε; (2) UnDial, a self-distillation unlearning method applied to a subset of PII; (3) a focused regularization loss on PII tokens; and (4) DPO, using masked PII as preferred responses. Privacy-utility trade-offs are evaluated by comparing TPA extraction rates against standard task performance metrics (accuracy, F1, BERTScore).

## Key Results
- Fine-tuning significantly increases model confidence (lower negative log-likelihood) for PII tokens that appear only in inputs, confirming memorization.
- PII frequency in the training set is a poor predictor of extractability (R² = 0.237); contextual utility matters more.
- Differential privacy (ε=6) reduces cross-memorization by over 60% on discharge summaries but exhibits high variance across random seeds and requires careful hyperparameter tuning.
- Post-training methods (UnDial, DPO) yield more consistent privacy-utility trade-offs across datasets, though they do not match DP's peak leakage reduction.
- Scaling model size increases absolute PII extraction but does not uniformly worsen relative risk; no mitigation entirely eliminates memorization.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1: Fine-Tuning Induces Confidence Shifts in PII Tokens
- **Claim:** Supervised fine-tuning on sequences containing input-only PII increases the model's predicted likelihood for those specific PII tokens.
- **Mechanism:** The loss gradient updates the model's parameters to maximize the probability of the target output. Since the input sequence (including irrelevant PII) is processed entirely during the forward pass, its tokens influence the internal representations and output logits. The model implicitly internalizes high-probability continuations for observed contexts, including verbatim PII spans, even if they are not part of the training label.
- **Core assumption:** The fine-tuning objective does not selectively exclude gradient attribution to irrelevant input tokens; all tokens contribute to the conditional distributions learned.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract]: "fine-tuning increases model confidence in predicting PII tokens."
  - [section 4.1, Figure 2]: The distribution of per-token negative log-likelihood for PII completions shifts left (closer to zero) and becomes less variable after fine-tuning.
  - [corpus]: Prior work on membership inference and extraction attacks (e.g., PrivacyScalpel) supports that models can memorize training data, but this paper isolates the input-only memorization dynamic.

### Mechanism 2: Contextual Utility Over Frequency Drives Memorization
- **Claim:** The raw frequency of a PII token in the fine-tuning set is a weak predictor of whether it will be successfully extracted via TPA.
- **Mechanism:** Memorization is driven more by the contextual uniqueness and utility of a PII instance for the downstream task. PII appearing in task-relevant contexts (e.g., in a section the model must generate) or in highly distinctive surrounding text receives stronger gradient signals. High-frequency PII in boilerplate headers or repetitive irrelevant contexts may be downweighted by the optimization process.
- **Core assumption:** The fine-tuning loss prioritizes information that reduces task error; PII in low-utility contexts contributes minimally to gradient updates relative to its count.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract]: "PII frequency is a weak predictor (R²=0.237)."
  - [section 4.1, Figure 3]: Scatter plot shows negligible linear correlation between true PII count and extraction count in discharge summaries.
  - [corpus]: Contrasts with some privacy literature that assumes frequency directly correlates with risk.

### Mechanism 3: Mitigation Strategies Exhibit Distinct Stability and Efficacy Profiles
- **Claim:** Differential privacy fine-tuning achieves the greatest leakage reduction but suffers from instability, while post-training methods (unlearning, DPO) offer more consistent privacy-utility trade-offs.
- **Mechanism:** DP-SGD adds calibrated noise to gradients during training, bounding the influence of any single sample and thus suppressing verbatim memorization. However, the noise magnitude (controlled by ε) interacts unfavorably with LLM fine-tuning dynamics, causing training instability (loss spikes, convergence issues). Post-training methods like UnDial and DPO adjust model behavior *after* standard fine-tuning by explicitly suppressing PII logits or learning to avoid them, avoiding the noisy gradient updates but relying on the quality of constructed unlearning/preference data.
- **Core assumption:** Privacy can be enforced either prophylactically during weight updates (DP) or rectively (unlearning/DPO), leading to different optimization landscapes and robustness.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract]: "Differential privacy achieves dramatic leakage reduction ... but suffers from training instability. Post‑training methods provide more consistent privacy‑utility trade‑offs."
  - [section 4.2, Table 2 & Figure 6]: DP (ε=6) shows high variance in extraction rates across seeds; UnDial and DPO show lower variance.
  - [corpus]: Other works (e.g., R.R.: Unveiling LLM Training Privacy) examine extraction but not this comparative stability analysis.

## Foundational Learning
- **Concept: Token-level likelihood & True-Prefix Attack (TPA)**
  - *Why needed:* TPA is the core evaluation method for quantifying memorization; it tests whether the model assigns the highest probability to the exact PII token given its preceding context.
  - *Quick check:* If the model's top-1 prediction for a PII span under the true prefix matches ground truth, does this definitively prove memorization, or could it reflect general linguistic patterns (e.g., common names following "My name is")?
- **Concept: Differential Privacy (DP) in LLM fine-tuning**
  - *Why needed:* DP is a primary mitigation strategy; understanding ε-δ privacy, gradient clipping, and noise addition is essential to interpret its trade-offs.
  - *Quick check:* Why does a smaller ε (tighter privacy) typically degrade task performance more severely in DP fine-tuning?
- **Concept: Parameter-Efficient Fine-Tuning (LoRA/QLoRA)**
  - *Why needed:* All experiments use QLoRA; memorization and mitigation occur within the low-rank adapter parameters, not the full model.
  - *Quick check:* If LoRA rank is increased eight-fold, why does total PII extraction remain constant while distinct PII increases (per Table 5)? What does this imply about where memorization is stored?
- **Concept: Machine Unlearning (UnDial)**
  - *Why needed:* UnDial is a key post-training method; it uses self-distillation to forget a selected subset of PII.
  - *Quick check:* How does unlearning a *subset* of PII instances lead to reduced extraction of *unseen* PII of the same type? Is the effect due to suppressing a general PII representation?
- **Concept: Direct Preference Optimization (DPO) for Privacy**
  - *Why needed:* DPO is adapted to align the model away from generating PII by constructing preferences where masked outputs are favored.
  - *Quick check:* Why might DPO be less sensitive to the specific seed PII set used for preference construction compared to UnDial?
- **Concept: Cross-memorization**
  - *Why needed:* An attack variant that checks if the model generates any PII of the same *type* (e.g., any name) seen in training, not necessarily the exact instance. This probes broader leakage.
  - *Quick check:* Why is cross-memorization typically easier than exact TPA, and what does its reduction imply about a mitigation's mechanism?

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map:**
  Base LLM (Llama 3.2 1B) -> QLoRA adapters (trainable low-rank matrices) -> Fine-tuned model with adapters -> True-Prefix Attack evaluator (prefix construction + decoding) -> Mitigation modules (DP noise injector / UnDial loss wrapper / DPO trainer) applied during/after fine-tuning.
- **Critical path:**
  Data preprocessing (PII annotation + masking) -> SFT baseline training (QLoRA) -> TPA evaluation (establish leakage baseline) -> Apply chosen mitigation (DP/UnDial/Reg/DPO) -> Re-train/re-tune -> Re-evaluate TPA & task metrics -> Compare privacy-utility trade-offs.
- **Design tradeoffs:**
  - *DP*: Strongest leakage reduction but unstable; requires large batches (1024+), high LR (1e-3), and careful ε/δ tuning; may harm generation coherence.
  - *UnDial/DPO*: More stable, less resource-intensive; efficacy depends on quality/size of seed PII set (UnDial) or preference pairs (DPO); may cause over-masking if β/strength too high.
  - *Regularization*: Simplest to integrate but least effective; can conflict with main task loss if not weighted properly.
- **Failure signatures:**
  - DP: High variance in extraction rates across random seeds (>10% relative change); training loss divergence or slow convergence.
  - DPO: Output becomes overly generic or contains excessive placeholder tokens (e.g., [MASK]); task performance drop >5% without corresponding privacy gain.
  - UnDial: No reduction in distinct PII extraction after increasing unlearning strength; unlearning loss dominates training loss early on.
  - TPA: Low extraction rates on real PII due to overly strict fuzzy matching (90% Levenshtein) or tokenization misalignment across model families.
- **First 3 experiments:**
  1. **Baseline memorization quantification:** Fine-tune Llama 3.2 1B via QLoRA on the Discharge Summary dataset; run TPA (greedy & sampling) to measure PII extraction rates.
  2. **Prefix length sensitivity:** Run TPA with varying prefix token lengths (10, 25, 50, 100) to confirm plateau; adopt 50-token prefix for standardized comparison.
  3. **Mitigation comparison and robustness:** Train models with DP (ε=6), UnDial (20% seed), and DPO (β=0.01); evaluate with standard TPA and an enhanced variant (exact first PII character) to test attack robustness.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- **What are the precise mechanistic reasons why current privacy-preserving fine-tuning methods fail to completely eliminate input-only PII memorization?** The paper states that even effective methods leave ample room for improvement, and a deeper investigation into these mechanisms is needed for more robust defenses.
- **How does the downstream task's output format influence memorization and mitigation effectiveness?** Observations suggest structured outputs (JSON) do not universally reduce memorization; a controlled study varying output format while holding data constant is lacking.
- **Why does differential privacy fine-tuning exhibit such high variance and instability?** The causes in the context of QLoRA on small sensitive datasets are not diagnosed; principled stabilization methods without sacrificing privacy are an open challenge.
- **To what extent do PII frequency and contextual diversity predict memorization risk?** The weak correlation (R²=0.237) suggests other factors dominate; a predictive risk score based on features like contextual uniqueness and positional importance remains to be formalized.
- **Do combined mitigation strategies (e.g., DP followed by DPO) offer synergistic benefits?** The tested hybrid (DP+DPO) did not consistently combine advantages and showed optimization interference; principles for effective composition are unknown.

## Limitations
- The PII annotation pipeline for discharge summaries (regex, LLM, manual) is underspecified; coverage and accuracy are unknown, affecting baseline counts.
- DPO preference construction details (system prompt, sliding window parameters, pair filtering) are missing, limiting reproducibility.
- UnDial's seed set selection strategy (random vs. stratified) is not described, potentially influencing reported consistency.
- Findings are based primarily on QLoRA (rank 8) with Llama 3.2 1B; generalization to full fine-tuning, larger models, or other architectures is uncertain.
- TPA's fuzzy matching threshold (90% Levenshtein) may inflate extraction rates; its impact is not quantified.

## Confidence
- **Fine-tuning increases confidence in predicting PII tokens** – High. Directly measured via token-level NLL shifts; corroborated by adjacent literature.
- **PII frequency is a weak predictor of memorization** – Medium. Based on a single dataset (R²=0.237); requires validation across more corpora.
- **DP fine-tuning dramatically reduces leakage but is unstable** – High. Strong effect size with high variance across seeds is consistently reported.
- **Post-training methods provide more consistent trade-offs** – Medium. Limited to specific datasets/hyperparameters; broader testing needed.
- **No mitigation eliminates memorization entirely** – High. Negative result is robust across all tested approaches.

## Next Checks
1. **DP stability across seeds:** Train DP-fine-tuned models with ≥5 random seeds; report mean ± std of extraction rates and task metrics to quantify claimed instability.
2. **Frequency-memorization correlation on new datasets:** Replicate the R² analysis on at least two additional real-PII corpora (e.g., PANORAMA) to test generalizability of the weak predictor claim.
3. **LoRA rank scaling study:** Systematically vary LoRA rank (r=4, 8, 16, 32) on synthetic (GretelAI) and real (discharge summary) data; verify that total extracted PII remains constant while distinct PII increases, confirming the scaling observation.
