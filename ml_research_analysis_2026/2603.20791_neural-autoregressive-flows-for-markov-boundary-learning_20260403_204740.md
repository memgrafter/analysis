# Neural Autoregressive Flows for Markov Boundary Learning

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2603.20791
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.20791
- **Reference count:** 40
- **Primary result:** Achieves state-of-the-art Markov boundary discovery (e.g., 99.44% F1 on linear d100-1) and improves causal DAG recovery via warm-starting.

## Executive Summary
This paper introduces FANS, a masked autoregressive flow model for efficient amortized estimation of conditional entropy, enabling scalable Markov boundary (MB) discovery under faithfulness. The proposed FANSITEMB algorithm leverages FANS within a parallelizable greedy search with polynomial time complexity and theoretical error bounds. Comprehensive evaluations on linear and nonlinear synthetic graphs, as well as real gene regulatory networks, demonstrate superior MB identification compared to constraint-based and score-based baselines. Integration into causal discovery (FANSITE-DCD) by warm-starting from the induced moral graph yields improved DAG recovery, highlighting the practical utility of accurate MB learning.

## Method Summary
FANS extends masked autoencoders (MADE) with a dynamic weight masking mechanism to model the density \(p(\mathbf{x}_S)\) for arbitrary subsets \(\mathbf{S}\) from a single trained network. This allows efficient computation of conditional entropy \(H(T|\mathbf{S})\) via the change-of-variables formula. FANSITEMB then performs a greedy, parallelizable search for each target variable: it alternately grows the candidate MB by adding the variable that most reduces conditional entropy, then shrinks by removing variables with minimal entropy increase. Theoretical guarantees tie excess entropy to measurement noise and MB size. For causal discovery, FANSITE-DCD constructs a moral graph from all pairwise MBs and uses it as a structural mask in the SDCD differentiable causal discovery framework.

## Key Results
- FANSITEMB achieves **99.44% F1** on linear sparse graph d100-1 and **91.2% F1** on nonlinear graph d30-G, significantly outperforming baselines like PCMCI and GES.
- In causal discovery, FANSITE-DCD reduces **SHD to 12.8** (vs. 13 for SDCD) and increases **AUC-PR to 0.592** (vs. 0.570) on the nonlinear d30-G dataset.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1: Conditional Entropy Minimization Identifies Markov Boundaries
- **Claim:** Under faithfulness, the Markov boundary of a target \(T\) is the minimal subset \(\mathbf{S}\) that minimizes \(H(T|\mathbf{S})\).
- **Mechanism:** The MB maximizes mutual information \(I(T;\mathbf{S}) = H(T) - H(T|\mathbf{S})\). Uniqueness of MB follows from faithfulness (no conditional independence cancellations).
- **Core assumption:** Causal sufficiency and faithfulness between the distribution and the true causal DAG.
- **Break condition:** Violation of faithfulness (e.g., exact cancellations) or presence of hidden confounders.

### Mechanism 2: FANS Amortizes Conditional Entropy Estimation for Any Subset
- **Claim:** FANS (Any-subset Masked Autoregressive Flows) estimates \(H(T|\mathbf{S})\) for any \(\mathbf{S}\) using a single trained model.
- **Mechanism:** FANS modifies MADE with dynamic weight masking that allocates neural capacity to any subset \(\mathbf{S}\). For a given \(\mathbf{S}\), the model computes \(H(T|\mathbf{S})\) by masking non-\(\mathbf{S}\) inputs and summing log Jacobians from the flow transformations (Eq. 6).
- **Core assumption:** FANS acts as a universal conditional density approximator (can fit any smooth distribution arbitrarily well).
- **Break condition:** Insufficient model capacity for highly complex, multimodal distributions; masking inefficiency when \(|\mathbf{S}|\) approaches network width.

### Mechanism 3: Greedy Search with Parallel Entropy Estimation is Polynomial and Bounded
- **Claim:** FANSITEMB finds MBs in polynomial time with an error bound that scales with measurement noise and MB size.
- **Mechanism:** The algorithm alternates a growing phase (add variable minimizing \(H(T|\mathbf{S}\cup\{X_j\})\)) and a shrinking phase (remove variable with minimal \(\delta H\) increase). GPU parallelism batches entropy queries for all candidates. Theorem 4 bounds excess entropy by terms involving measurement noise \(\gamma\) and MB size.
- **Core assumption:** Conditional entropy estimates are accurate enough; greedy search is near-optimal for weakly submodular objectives (the \(R^2\) term) under noise.
- **Break condition:** High measurement noise (\(\gamma\)) degrades the bound; very large MBs (\(|\text{MB}| \approx d\)) may cause combinatorial mask blowup despite compact FANS.

## Foundational Learning
- **Conditional Entropy (\(H(T|\mathbf{S})\))**
  - *Why needed:* It is the scoring criterion; MB minimizes this quantity.
  - *Quick check:* Why does \(H(T|\mathbf{S}) = H(T,\mathbf{S}) - H(\mathbf{S})\) enable estimation via neural density models? (It separates joint and marginal densities, both tractable with autoregressive flows.)
- **Masked Autoregressive Flows (MAFs)**
  - *Why needed:* Provide tractable density estimation and exact Jacobian determinant computation for autoregressive models.
  - *Quick check:* How does the triangular Jacobian from autoregressive masking make determinant computation efficient? (It reduces to product of diagonal entries.)
- **Greedy Sequential Information Maximization**
  - *Why needed:* Ensures polynomial-time MB discovery with theoretical guarantees, avoiding NP-hard combinatorial search.
  - *Quick check:* What property of the \(R^2\) objective (derived from \(H(T|\mathbf{S})\) in linear Gaussian case) makes greedy near-optimal? (Weak submodularity.)

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component map:**  
FANS model (masked MAF with dynamic weight allocation) → FANSITEMB algorithm (greedy grow-shrink with batched entropy queries) → FANSITE-DCD pipeline (moral graph construction + SDCD warm-start)

**Critical path:**  
Input data → Train FANS (stochastic masking on subsets) → For each target \(X_i\): run FANSITEMB (grow/shrink using parallel \(H(X_i|\cdot)\) estimates) → Aggregate all MBs → Build moral graph → Use as adjacency mask in SDCD → Output DAG.

**Design tradeoffs:**  
- *Compact FANS (M ≪ d) vs non-compact:* Fewer parameters and memory vs. potential underfitting for large MBs.  
- *Greedy search vs continuous relaxation:* Simpler, parallelizable, but may get stuck in local minima; continuous methods (e.g., gradient-based) are harder to scale.  
- *Amortized (one FANS per dataset) vs per-variable models:* Amortization efficient but may underfit special target distributions; per-variable models are more flexible but costly.

**Failure signatures:**  
- Entropy estimates do not decrease as candidate MB grows → model underfitting or poor training.  
- Growing phase stalls with very small \(|\tilde{MB}_+|\) on dense graphs → thresholds \(\epsilon_g\) too large or poor initialization.  
- Predicted MB sizes wildly inconsistent with expected sparsity (e.g., all targets have MB size near \(d\)) → masking mechanism broken or \(\epsilon\) thresholds misconfigured.

**First 3 experiments:**  
1. **Entropy validation:** Train FANS on small linear Gaussian data (d=20); compare estimated \(H(T|\mathbf{S})\) to closed-form analytic values for 100 random subsets. Expect \(R^2 > 0.95\).  
2. **FANSITEMB sanity:** Run on linear d30-1 graph; check F1 >95% and that the growing phase recovers a subset of the true MB before shrinking.  
3. **Causal integration:** Use FANSITEMB output to warm-start SDCD on nonlinear d30-G; verify moral graph reduces SHD vs. vanilla SDCD and improves AUC-PR.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
1. **Discrete/mixed data extension:** How can FANS estimate conditional entropy for discrete or mixed data types? The current continuous normalizing flow approach does not address this.
2. **Order-agnostic masking:** Can order-agnostic masked autoregressive flows accurately estimate conditional entropy for arbitrary variable orders without prohibitive cost? The paper restricts to a single order for efficiency, potentially biasing estimates.
3. **Sample complexity and finite-capacity error:** What sample complexity and network size are needed for FANS to approach the universal approximator ideal assumed in Theorem 4? The bound does not account for parametric limitations.
4. **Adaptive maximum subset size \(M\):** How does the heuristic choice of \(M\) (max subset size for masking) affect accuracy when true Markov boundaries exceed \(M\)? An adaptive strategy is unexplored.

## Limitations
- **Faithfulness dependency:** The equivalence between MB and conditional entropy minimization relies on faithfulness, which is often violated in real-world data (e.g., cancellations, hidden confounders).
- **FANS capacity uncertainty:** The redesigned FANS architecture with dynamic masking for arbitrary subsets lacks independent verification of its universal approximation capability in practice, especially for highly nonlinear, high-dimensional distributions.
- **Greedy search optimality assumptions:** The polynomial-time guarantee and error bound depend on weak submodularity and a Gaussian noise model; performance may degrade for strongly non-submodular objectives or heavy-tailed noise.

## Confidence
- Conditional entropy criterion as MB scoring: **High** (well-established information-theoretic equivalence).
- FANS as amortized conditional entropy estimator: **Medium** (relies on correct masking implementation and MAF universality; novel redesign not externally validated).
- Greedy search polynomial time & error bounds: **Medium** (proof provided but assumptions may not hold broadly).
- Superior empirical performance on benchmarks: **Medium** (results strong but depend on undisclosed implementation details and thresholding choices).

## Next Checks
1. **Entropy estimation sanity check:** Train FANS on linear Gaussian data (d=20) and compute \(R^2\) between estimated and analytic \(H(T|\mathbf{S})\) over 100 random subsets; expect \(R^2 > 0.95\).
2. **Greedy search sensitivity analysis:** On nonlinear d30-G, sweep FANSITEMB thresholds (\(\epsilon_g, \epsilon_s \in [0.0005, 0.01]\), patience \(\rho \in [5,50]\)). Measure stability of F1-score and average MB size; low variance (<5% F1 change) indicates robustness.
3. **Moral graph fidelity test:** For FANSITE-DCD on d30-G, extract the moral graph induced by all FANSITEMB results. Compute precision/recall against the true moral graph; a drop >10% from MB-level F1 would indicate error propagation.
