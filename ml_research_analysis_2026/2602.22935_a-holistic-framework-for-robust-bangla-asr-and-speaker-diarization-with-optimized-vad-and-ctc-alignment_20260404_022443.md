# A Holistic Framework for Robust Bangla ASR and Speaker Diarization with Optimized VAD and CTC Alignment

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2602.22935
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.22935
- **Reference Count:** 15
- **Primary Result:** Fine-tuned ASR achieves 21.988% public WER / 23.585% private WER; diarization best DER 21.460% public / 32.663% private with ~2-hour inference on dual T4 GPUs.

## Executive Summary
This work tackles the degradation of standard Bangla ASR and speaker diarization on long-form audio (30+ seconds), where fixed-window processing causes context loss, hallucinations, and overlapping-speech errors. The proposed two-part pipeline addresses ASR by optimizing Voice Activity Detection (VAD) and using CTC forced alignment to segment audio at precise word boundaries into sub-30-second chunks, followed by fine-tuning a Whisper-medium model. For diarization, a three-stage curriculum learning process fine-tunes a pyannote model using raw audio, Demucs source-separated vocals, and dynamic gain augmentation. Evaluated on the DL Sprint 4.0 benchmark, the system significantly outperforms zero-shot and other fine-tuned baselines in both tasks while maintaining feasible inference times.

## Method Summary
The ASR pipeline begins with transcript normalization and a refined VAD to filter non-speech regions. A pretrained multilingual CTC aligner (MMS-300M) then generates frame-level emissions and, after romanizing the transcript for phonetic mapping, computes Viterbi alignments to extract word-level timestamps. Audio is chunked strictly at these word boundaries into segments under 30 seconds to avoid truncation. These chunks fine-tune the `tugstugi_bengaliai-asr_whisper-medium` model. The diarization pipeline uses Demucs to isolate vocal stems from raw audio. A curriculum fine-tunes `pyannote/segmentation-3.0` in three phases: Phase 1 on raw 5-second chunks to learn real-world acoustics, Phase 2 on Demucs-cleaned vocals to refine speaker signatures, and Phase 3 on 10-second augmented vocals with dynamic gain (±6dB) to harden against volume variance. The final ASR transcriptions and diarization RTTMs are merged via timestamp alignment.

## Key Results
- Fine-tuned ASR model achieves public WER 21.988% and private WER 23.585%, substantially beating zero-shot Whisper (public WER ~40%) and other fine-tuned baselines.
- Best diarization configuration (fine-tuning with augmentation) yields public DER 21.460% and private DER 32.663%, with Stage 3 augmentation improving generalization.
- Total inference time is reduced to approximately 2 hours using dual T4 GPUs for the benchmark dataset.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1: CTC forced-alignment segmentation
- **Claim:** CTC forced word alignment enables context-preserving segmentation for long-form audio, mitigating hallucinations and context loss in Transformer ASR models with fixed input windows.
- **Mechanism:** A pretrained multilingual CTC aligner processes audio-transcript pairs to produce frame-level emissions; the transcript is romanized for phonetic mapping, and Viterbi alignment extracts precise word start/end timestamps. Audio is then chunked strictly at word boundaries into sub-30-second segments, ensuring no word is truncated.
- **Core assumption:** Standard fixed-length chunking severs linguistic units, confusing the decoder and causing hallucination or forgetting. Word-boundary-respecting segmentation maintains semantic continuity.
- **Evidence anchors:** [abstract] "utilizing VAD optimization and CTC segmentation via forced word-alignment to maintain temporal accuracy and transcription integrity over long durations." [section III.A] Details the pipeline: "precise start and end timestamps for each localized word," "intelligently segmented into discrete chunks of strictly under 30 seconds... word boundaries are preserved."

### Mechanism 2: Optimized VAD pre-processing
- **Claim:** A refined VAD layer reduces non-speech interference in both ASR and diarization pipelines, improving model focus on vocal segments.
- **Mechanism:** VAD filters out non-speech regions before ASR chunking and as a preliminary step in diarization, reducing input length and minimizing distraction from noise/silence.
- **Core assumption:** Standard VAD is brittle in low-resource, noisy real-world Bangla audio (e.g., broadcasts, conversations), producing false positives/negatives that degrade downstream performance.
- **Evidence anchors:** [abstract] Lists "VAD Optimization" as a core contribution. [section I] Highlights challenges including "background noise, overlapping speech." Corpus signal shows high neighbor FMR (~0.54) for robust processing papers, but direct VAD optimization evidence is weak.

### Mechanism 3: Curriculum learning for diarization
- **Claim:** Curriculum learning—progressively training the diarization model from raw noisy audio to clean vocal tracks and finally augmented data—builds robust speaker embeddings and boundary detection adaptable to diverse real-world conditions.
- **Mechanism:** Stage 1: Fine-tune on raw audio to learn general Bengali acoustic patterns in noise. Stage 2: Refine on Demucs source-separated vocals to focus on pure speaker signatures. Stage 3: Apply dynamic gain augmentation (±6dB) on cleaned audio to simulate volume variance and prevent overfitting to Demucs's normalized output.
- **Core assumption:** Training on only clean audio yields a brittle model that fails on real-world volume fluctuations and residual noise. Staged training grounds the model in reality, sharpens discrimination, then hardens it.
- **Evidence anchors:** [section III.B] Explicitly names "three-stage Curriculum Learning pipeline" with phases: "Base Adaptation (Real-World Acoustics)", "Clean Refinement (Source Separation)", "Robustness and Dynamic Augmentation." [Table V] Shows "Fine-tuning + Demucs Refinement" lowers public DER but raises private DER vs. "Fine-tuning + Data Augmentation," suggesting augmentation's role in generalization.

## Foundational Learning
- **CTC Forced Alignment**
  - **Why needed:** Whisper's 30-second input limit necessitates chunking, but naive chunking severs words. CTC alignment provides word-level timestamps to chunk at natural boundaries, preserving linguistic coherence.
  - **Quick check:** If you randomly split a 60-second audio into two 30-second chunks, what specific errors (e.g., word fragments, context loss) would a Transformer decoder like Whisper likely introduce?
- **Source Separation for Diarization**
  - **Why needed:** Bangla long-form audio often contains background noise, music, or overlapping speech. Demucs isolates vocal stems, providing cleaner input for speaker embedding extraction and boundary detection.
  - **Quick check:** How might imperfect source separation (e.g.,残留音乐 in vocals) affect speaker embedding clustering?
- **Curriculum Learning**
  - **Why needed:** Training a diarization model directly on a mix of raw and augmented data is unstable. Staging from simple (raw) to complex (clean+augmented) provides a stable learning trajectory.
  - **Quick check:** Why would training *only* on Demucs-cleaned audio yield worse private DER than training on augmented cleaned audio, as shown in Table V?

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map:** Input (Raw .wav + transcript/RTTM) -> ASR Path: VAD Filter -> CTC Aligner (MMS-300M) -> Word-timestamped Chunks -> Fine-tune `tugstugi_bengaliai-asr_whisper-medium`; Diarization Path: Demucs (htdemucs) -> Vocal Stems -> Curriculum Fine-tune `pyannote/segmentation-3.0` (Stage1 raw -> Stage2 clean -> Stage3 augmented) -> Speaker Embeddings & Clustering -> RTTM Output; Integration: Merge chunk-level ASR outputs and diarization RTTMs via timestamps.
- **Critical path:** Upstream, the CTC forced aligner's timestamp accuracy directly dictates ASR chunking quality; failure propagates downstream. For diarization, Demucs separation quality is critical for Stage 2/3 training stability.
- **Design tradeoffs:** Chunk duration: smaller chunks (<30s) fit model limits but lose long-range context; the paper uses strict <30s, sacrificing some context for reliability. Clean vs. real: Stage 2 (clean) improves boundary precision but risks overfitting; Stage 3 (augmented) improves robustness but may add noise. Compute vs. performance: CTC alignment adds offline cost but enables faster online inference.
- **Failure signatures:** ASR: sudden WER spike on files with certain background noises (VAD/alignment failure); hallucinations persist (poor chunk boundaries). Diarization: high DER on segments with volume fluctuations (Stage 3 insufficiency) or overlapping speech (Stage 1 failure); private DER >> public DER indicates overfitting.
- **First 3 experiments:** 1) Validate CTC alignment sanity: run aligner on a curated 60s audio with known transcript; inspect 5 random word timestamps for phonetic plausibility; compute failure rate on a noisy subset. 2) Isolate VAD impact: run ASR pipeline with/without VAD on a noisy validation set; compare WER, inference time, and speech duration retained (detect false negatives). 3) Ablate diarization curriculum: train three models: (a) Stage1 only, (b) Stage1+2, (c) Stage1+2+3; evaluate on a held-out set with mixed clean/noisy/volume conditions; expect (c) to have lowest DER variance.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
1. **VAD refinement specifics:** What modifications constitute the "refined VAD layer" and how do they improve speech/non-speech detection for noisy Bangla audio compared to standard VAD? The paper lacks architectural details, implementation specifics, or comparative evaluation.
2. **CTC alignment failures:** What are the causes and frequency of CTC forced alignment failures on Bangla speech with code-switching, dialects, or severe noise, and how effective is the fallback mechanism? The paper does not quantify failure rates or characterize common modes.
3. **Curriculum learning necessity:** Does the three-stage curriculum yield statistically significant improvements over a single-stage fine-tuning approach that combines all data sources and augmentations from the start? No ablation compares staged vs. unified training.
4. **Long-form continuity:** How does fixed sub-30-second chunking affect performance on extremely long-form audio (e.g., multi-hour recordings), and could adaptive chunking reduce context fragmentation? The paper evaluates only on 30-60 second clips.

## Limitations
- CTC forced alignment configuration (romanization mapping, fallback criteria) is not fully described.
- Diarization training specifics (RTTM conversion method, exact epochs per phase, checkpoint selection) are missing.
- VAD optimization implementation details (thresholds, handling of low-volume speech) are sparse.
- Break conditions (alignment failures, Demucs artifacts, over-augmentation) are not quantified.
- Generalizability to other Bengali datasets beyond DL Sprint 4.0 is uncertain.
