# GRAZE: Grounded Refinement and Motion-Aware Zero-Shot Event Localization

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID**: 2604.01383
- **Source URL**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.01383
- **Reference count**: 25
- **Primary result**: 97.4% coverage on 738 videos; 77.5% within ±10 frames, 82.7% within ±20 frames.

## Executive Summary
GRAZE is a zero-shot pipeline for frame-accurate First Point of Contact (FPOC) localization in unconstrained American football practice videos. Without any task-specific training, it identifies the exact frame where a player first touches a tackle dummy. The method combines open-vocabulary grounding (Grounding DINO) for candidate detection, motion-aware scoring to filter irrelevant athletes, a two-phase backward refinement to correct temporal bias, and SAM2 mask propagation as an independent contact verifier. Evaluated on 738 videos, GRAZE achieves 97.4% coverage, with 77.5% of clips localized within ±10 frames and 82.7% within ±20 frames, demonstrating robustness even in cluttered scenes via a multi-candidate fallback strategy.

## Method Summary
GRAZE processes untrimmed monocular videos in four main stages: (1) Candidate generation using hierarchical text prompts with Grounding DINO across six temporal positions and progressively relaxed confidence thresholds, detecting player and dummy bounding boxes. (2) Temporal validation and motion scoring: candidates are matched across a 14‑frame window; a directional approach score (cosine similarity between player motion vector and vector to dummy) and a displacement score are computed; candidates below thresholds are discarded and the rest ranked by a weighted confidence. (3) Two‑phase backward refinement: from the grounding frame, the pipeline steps backward frame‑by‑frame (Phase 1) and then probes exponential offsets with binary search (Phase 2) to locate the earliest frame where both objects are consistently visible (`t_FFBO`). (4) Contact verification: SAM2 propagates separate masks for the player and dummy from `t_FFBO` forward; the earliest frame with at least one pixel of mask overlap is declared `t_FPOC`. If no overlap occurs, the candidate is rejected and the next‑ranked candidate is evaluated, ensuring high coverage.

## Key Results
- Coverage reached **97.4%** across 738 videos, with only 2.6% of clips requiring manual review.
- Accuracy: **77.5%** of evaluable clips had FPOC within ±10 frames; **82.7%** within ±20 frames.
- The multi‑candidate fallback mechanism maintained robustness in cluttered scenes where ground truth was missed on the first candidate.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
**Mechanism 1: SAM2 mask overlap as independent contact verifier**  
- *Claim*: SAM2 mask propagation serves as an independent pixel‑level contact verifier, decoupling physical contact evidence from detection confidence [Abstract, Sec 2.6].  
- *Mechanism*: After candidate player‑dummy pairs are proposed, SAM2 propagates separate binary masks for each object forward from the earliest co‑visible frame (`t_FFBO`). The pipeline computes the pixel‑wise logical intersection of these masks per frame; the first frame with non‑zero intersection is declared `t_FPOC`.  
- *Core assumption*: Pixel‑level mask intersection is a reliable proxy for physical contact onset, even when 2D bounding boxes overlap prematurely or fail to overlap at true contact due to occlusion.  
- *Evidence*: The paper explicitly states this approach [Sec 2.6] and shows that ablated versions without SAM2 verification lose coverage.  
- *Break condition*: If SAM2 mask propagation fails (e.g., identity drift, segmentation collapse) before contact occurs, the candidate is rejected and the next ranked candidate is tried.

**Mechanism 2: Directional motion scoring filters irrelevant athletes**  
- *Claim*: The directional approach score (cosine similarity between player motion vector and dummy approach vector) filters out temporally consistent but spatially irrelevant background athletes [Sec 2.4].  
- *Mechanism*: For each candidate, a motion vector from past detections to the current frame and an approach vector from the player to the dummy are computed; their cosine scaled to [0,1] yields `m_dir`. Candidates with `m_dir < 0.30` are discarded.  
- *Core assumption*: The true tackler’s image‑plane motion direction points toward the dummy’s center in the final stride, while bystanders or lateral movers do not.  
- *Evidence*: The TRACE ablation (removing motion scoring) collapses coverage to 46.9% [Sec 4.1], demonstrating the necessity of a directional gate.  
- *Break condition*: Fails for approach‑from‑behind trajectories where the player’s silhouette overlaps the dummy before physical contact [Sec 4.2] or for concurrent players with similarly directed approaches.

**Mechanism 3: Two‑phase backward refinement corrects temporal bias**  
- *Claim*: The two‑phase backward refinement corrects the bias where initial Grounding DINO anchors occur later than the true `t_FFBO` [Sec 2.5].  
- *Mechanism*: Phase 1 steps backward frame‑by‑frame from the grounding frame `t_g`, re‑detecting with reference boxes and allowing at most one consecutive miss. Phase 2 probes exponential offsets (5, 10, 20, 50 frames) and uses binary search to locate the earliest frame where both objects are consistently detectable. This recovered frame becomes `t_FFBO`.  
- *Core assumption*: Both player and dummy remain visible in the moments preceding mid‑contact grounding, and their detectability decreases gradually backward rather than disappearing abruptly.  
- *Evidence*: The SOLE/MARS comparison shows that without backward refinement, catastrophic errors (|err| ≥ 20 frames) increase [Sec 4.2].  
- *Break condition*: Fails if one object becomes completely invisible before `t_FFBO` (e.g., off‑camera approach) or if abrupt camera motion removes both from view.

## Foundational Learning
**Concept: Open‑vocabulary grounding (e.g., Grounding DINO)**  
- *Why needed*: Replaces task‑specific detectors; handles diverse player attire/equipment via text prompts without fine‑tuning.  
- *Quick check*: Why must the grounding system accept free‑form text prompts describing player gear/posture, and how does hierarchical prompt scheduling improve coverage?

**Concept: Video object segmentation with memory (SAM2)**  
- *Why needed*: Propagates object masks across frames to maintain identity and compute intersection; its memory bank handles occlusion during contact.  
- *Quick check*: How does SAM2’s streaming memory architecture differ from per‑frame segmentation, and why is mask propagation essential for verifying contact onset?

**Concept: Motion‑based trajectory analysis**  
- *Why needed*: Distinguishes active tackler from stationary bystanders using directional motion cues; no tracking required.  
- *Quick check*: Why does temporal persistence alone (detection in consecutive frames) fail in cluttered scenes, and how does cosine similarity between motion and approach vectors resolve this?

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component map**  
- **Candidate Generator**: Grounding DINO with hierarchical prompts (`P_gear`, `P_nogear`, `P_generic`) and progressive threshold relaxation; probes 6 normalized time positions.  
- **Validator/Ranker**: Temporal consistency matcher (14‑frame window), displacement score (`m_disp`), directional approach score (`m_dir`), composite `conf_overall`.  
- **Refinement Engine**: Two‑phase backward search to find `t_FFBO` (frame‑by‑frame backtracking → exponential probes → binary search).  
- **Contact Verifier**: SAM2 mask propagation from `t_FFBO` using refined boxes; pixel‑wise intersection to detect first contact.  
- **Fallback Manager**: Multi‑candidate retry loop; if SAM2 overlap fails, proceeds to next‑ranked candidate.

**Critical path**  
Grounding DINO → Candidate Filtering (temporal + motion) → Backward Refinement → SAM2 Verification → Overlap Detection → (on failure) next candidate.

**Design tradeoffs**  
- *Zero‑shot vs. accuracy*: No domain fine‑tuning enables deployment but limits precision; motion scoring and backward refinement mitigate at computational cost.  
- *Mask overlap vs. physical contact*: 2D intersection is efficient but susceptible to approach‑from‑behind false positives; depth‑aware verification is proposed future work.  
- *Coverage vs. speed*: Multi‑prompt progressive search and multi‑candidate retry maximize coverage (97.4%) but increase inference time; thresholds could be tightened if coverage relaxes.

**Failure signatures**  
1. **No co‑visible frames**: Grounding fails across all prompts/thresholds → coverage drop (off‑camera approaches).  
2. **Directional scoring collapse**: All `m_dir` scores below threshold → TRACE‑like coverage collapse in concurrent‑player scenes.  
3. **SAM2 propagation failure**: Mask drift or identity swap before contact → candidate rejection, fallback to next.  
4. **Refinement overshoot**: Backward refinement steps past true `t_FFBO` → `t_FPOC` shifts into adjacent error band (≤ 2 frames penalty).

**First 3 experiments** (ablations)  
1. Remove motion scoring: Expect coverage collapse (~47%) as temporal validation becomes overly restrictive (TRACE baseline).  
2. Remove backward refinement: Expect increased catastrophic errors (|err| ≥ 20 frames) as grounding bias goes uncorrected (SOLE/MARS comparison).  
3. Use a single prompt only: Expect coverage drop (from 97.4% to ~92%) due to missed players with atypical attire/pose; tests prompt hierarchy effectiveness.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
1. **Temporal multi‑object tracker integration**: How to maintain identity continuity across frames to improve robustness in concurrent‑player scenes? *Basis*: Authors state future work; current per‑frame grounding disambiguation fails with multiple athletes. *Resolution*: Implement a tracker (e.g., based on SAM2 memory) and evaluate on multi‑player clips.  
2. **Depth‑aware contact verification**: Can depth replace image‑plane mask overlap to reduce false positives from approach‑from‑behind? *Basis*: Authors suggest extension; 2D overlap triggers early on behind approaches. *Resolution*: Integrate depth estimation/sensors and compare FPOC accuracy on such cases.  
3. **Prompt design principles**: What prompt hierarchy and relaxation strategy maximize grounding success? *Basis*: The current prompt set is heuristic; not systematically studied. *Resolution*: Systematically ablate prompt variations and thresholds to derive guidelines.  
4. **Annotation uncertainty impact**: Annotations have ±3 frame uncertainty; should evaluation metrics incorporate this? *Basis*: Mentioned; evaluation uses fixed tolerances. *Resolution*: Re‑evaluate with adjusted tolerances or use inter‑annotator agreement to set realistic thresholds.

## Limitations
- Reproducibility is hampered by incomplete specification of model variants, input resolution, SAM2 parameters, and exact prompt strings/thresholds.  
- The 2D mask overlap contact verifier is fundamentally limited; it cannot distinguish when a player is behind the dummy in 3D, causing early detections.  
- Multi‑candidate fallback helps but cannot recover scenes with no co‑visible frames (off‑camera approaches) or with overwhelming concurrent‑player interference.

## Confidence
- **Claim**: SAM2 mask propagation serves as an independent pixel‑level contact verifier. **Confidence**: High.  
- **Claim**: Directional motion scoring (`m_dir`) filters out temporally consistent but spatially irrelevant background athletes. **Confidence**: Medium.  
- **Claim**: Two‑phase backward refinement corrects the temporal bias where initial Grounding DINO anchors occur later than true `t_FFBO`. **Confidence**: Medium.

## Next Checks
1. **Prompt Hierarchy Stress Test**: Ablate the three‑level prompt scheduling on a held‑out subset; compare coverage with only `P_gear` vs. full hierarchy to quantify marginal gain from relaxed prompts.  
2. **SAM2 Propagation Stability Audit**: Measure mask identity drift (e.g., mask IoU with initial frame) over time under varying motion/occlusion; correlate drift metrics with FPOC error to quantify failure modes.  
3. **Directional Score Threshold Sweep**: Vary the `m_dir` threshold (e.g., [0.2, 0.4]) on a challenging multi‑player subset; plot coverage vs. precision trade‑offs to validate the 0.30 cutoff and identify where directional assumption breaks.
