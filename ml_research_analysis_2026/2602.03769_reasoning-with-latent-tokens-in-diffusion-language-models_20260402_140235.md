# Reasoning with Latent Tokens in Diffusion Language Models

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2602.03769
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.03769
- Reference count: 40
- Primary result: Diffusion models achieve strong reasoning by jointly predicting latent tokens, and this mechanism transfers to autoregressive models, yielding substantial gains.

## Executive Summary
This paper identifies latent tokens—masked positions predicted but not decoded—as a key factor behind diffusion models' superior reasoning performance. Ablating joint prediction degrades accuracy, confirming the importance of latent tokens. The authors propose latent token modulation, a training-free inference method that varies the number of latent tokens, revealing a smooth speed-quality trade-off (e.g., Sudoku accuracy rises from 68.1% to 80.2%). Moreover, they introduce latent tokens into autoregressive models via auxiliary multi-token prediction (AR-MTP), drastically improving reasoning accuracy (Zebra: 54.7%→68.8%) and even surpassing uniform-decoding diffusion on multiple tasks. These findings establish latent tokens as a general mechanism for global coherence across modeling paradigms.

## Method Summary
The paper explores masked diffusion models (MDM, SCDM, SIDM) and autoregressive models (ARM, AR-MTP). Diffusion variants differ in attention patterns among masked tokens: MDM uses full bidirectional attention, SCDM uses causal attention, and SIDM uses independent attention. The core insight is that predicting a set of tokens jointly—including those not decoded (latent tokens)—provides intermediate computational states that enhance global reasoning. For SCDM, latent token modulation enables inference-time control over the number of latent tokens by reordering the masked positions. AR-MTP modifies training to predict a suffix of future tokens, introducing latent tokens into autoregressive models. Evaluation covers constraint satisfaction (Sudoku, Zebra, Countdown) and language modeling (OpenWebText), with metrics including accuracy and perplexity.

## Key Results
- Diffusion models' joint prediction of latent tokens improves reasoning accuracy; ablating it (SIDM) degrades performance across tasks.
- Latent token modulation yields a training-free speed-quality tradeoff: on Sudoku, accuracy increases from 68.1% (no latent tokens) to 80.2% (all latent tokens); on OpenWebText, perplexity improves from 48.86 to ~40.
- AR-MTP introduces latent tokens into autoregressive models, achieving large gains (e.g., Zebra +14.1%, Sudoku-Gen +50.9%) and outperforming uniform-decoding diffusion on all three reasoning tasks tested.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1
- **Claim:** Latent tokens (jointly predicted but not decoded masked positions) provide intermediate computational states that improve accuracy on tasks requiring global coherence.
- **Mechanism:** During diffusion inference, the model makes a joint forward pass over all masked positions (`Pt = π≥t`). The target token (`πt`) attends to the hidden states of other masked positions (`π>t`), which are trained to predict their own future values. This allows information about constraints across unknown variables to flow between positions before decoding.
- **Core assumption:** Accurate prediction of a single target token conditioned on partial context fundamentally depends on reasoning about other simultaneously unknown tokens whose values are mutually constrained.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract] "Diffusion models are trained to jointly predict a distribution over all unknown tokens, including those that will not actually be decoded... ablating this joint prediction yields faster inference but degrades performance."
  - [section 3.3] Ablation showing MDM outperforms SIDM (no joint prediction) on Sudoku, Zebra, Countdown.
  - [section 4.3] Performance of SCDM improves monotonically with more latent tokens on reasoning tasks.
  - [corpus] Neighbors like "LaDiR: Latent Diffusion Enhances LLMs for Text Reasoning" and "Latent Thought Models" explore related latent space reasoning, but direct empirical linkage to token-level joint prediction is unique to this paper.
- **Break condition:** If task performance does not degrade when ablating joint prediction (i.e., `Pt = {πt}`), the mechanism is not necessary for that task.

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim:** The latent token mechanism is generalizable beyond diffusion and can be injected into autoregressive models via auxiliary multi-token prediction.
- **Mechanism:** AR-MTP removes causal masking and the output shift, training the model to predict a suffix of future tokens (`pθ(x[≥t]|x[<t])`) from a masked right-side. At inference, only the next token (`πt`) is decoded, but hidden states from other predicted positions (`t+1...`) act as latent tokens.
- **Core assumption:** The benefit of joint prediction arises from the training objective and architecture enabling information flow across future positions, not from the specific noise schedule or non-autoregressive decoding of diffusion.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract] "We demonstrate that latent tokens can be introduced into autoregressive models through an auxiliary multi-token prediction objective... yielding substantial improvements."
  - [section 5.2] Table 3: AR-MTP dramatically outperforms standard AR on all reasoning tasks (e.g., Zebra: 54.7%→68.8%).
  - [section 5.3] Figure 7: AR-MTP performance scales with number of latent tokens on Sudoku-Large, while AR-NTP (same arch, no multi-token loss) degrades.
  - [corpus] "Planner and Executor: Collaboration between Discrete Diffusion And Autoregressive Models" discusses hybrid approaches but does not demonstrate latent token transfer via objective modification.
- **Break condition:** If AR-MTP fails to improve over standard AR when controlling for model capacity (as shown by AR-NTP ablation), the mechanism is not due to auxiliary supervision alone.

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim:** Latent token modulation provides a post-hoc, training-free knob to trade inference speed for quality.
- **Mechanism:** For SCDMs (which support causal attention among masked tokens), the inference algorithm can deliberately reorder the masked input to place `n` future masked positions before the target (`πt`). These positions now participate in the target's computation via causal attention, varying the number of latent tokens without retraining.
- **Core assumption:** The positional order of masked tokens in the input determines how many other masked positions a target can attend to under the SCDM attention mask, and this ordering is free to vary at inference.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [section 4.2] "By deliberately repositioning other masked tokens before the target position... we can experiment with a controllable number of latent tokens."
  - [section 4.3] Figure 4 & 5 show smooth trade-offs: more latent tokens → higher accuracy/perplexity, at the cost of processing more tokens per step.
  - [section E.2] Adaptation of top-prob decoding to work with latent token modulation.
  - [corpus] "Deferred Commitment Decoding for Diffusion Language Models" addresses similar efficiency-quality tradeoffs but does not show explicit latent token count modulation.
- **Break condition:** If performance plateaus or degrades beyond a certain `n`, or if the speed-quality curve is not smooth, modulation does not provide a useful knob.

## Foundational Learning
- **Concept: Masked Diffusion Reverse Process**
  - *Why needed here:* The paper's core contrast is between diffusion's joint prediction over masked positions and autoregressive next-token prediction. Understanding the forward/reverse process and the role of the masking schedule is essential.
  - *Quick check question:* In masked diffusion, what does the model predict at a partially masked state `xt`, and how is the next state `xt+1` sampled?
- **Concept: Transformer Attention Patterns (Full, Causal, Independent)**
  - *Why needed here:* The definitions of MDM, SCDM, and SIDM hinge on specific attention masks among masked tokens. These patterns define what positions can participate in the forward pass.
  - *Quick check question:* For a reordered sequence `[clean, MASK, MASK, MASK]`, which attention mask (Equation 1 or 2) allows the third `MASK` to attend to the second `MASK`?
- **Concept: Training vs. Inference Objectives in Sequence Models**
  - *Why needed here:* The paper manipulates prediction sets (`Pt`) at inference while keeping training fixed (for modulation) or changes the training objective (for AR-MTP). Distinguishing these is key.
  - *Quick check question:* How does the training loss for AR-MTP (Sec B.3) differ from standard AR training in terms of which positions receive loss?

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map:**
  - `Schedule π`: Permutation defining which token is decoded at each step.
  - `Prediction set Pt`: Set of positions the model jointly predicts at step `t`.
  - `Attentionmask (Eq 1/2)`: Determines which positions in `Pt` can attend to each other.
  - `Latent tokens Zt = Pt \ {πt}`: The predicted-but-not-decoded subset.
  - `Latent token modulation`: Inference-time algorithm to control `|Zt|` by reordering `Pt`.
  - `AR-MTP`: Architecture that removes causal mask and shift to enable multi-token training.
- **Critical path:**
  1. Train a base model (MDM, SCDM, or ARM) with a standard objective.
  2. For diffusion: Ablate to SIDM (independent attention) to confirm joint prediction necessity.
  3. For SCDM: Implement latent token modulation and vary `n` to observe speed-quality trade-off.
  4. For ARM: Switch to AR-MTP training and compare to standard AR on downstream tasks.
- **Design tradeoffs:**
  - `|Zt|` (number of latent tokens): ↑ improves accuracy/perplexity but ↑ inference tokens processed.
  - SCDM attention: Causal among masks enables modulation but may limit expressivity vs. full bidirectional (MDM).
  - AR-MTP: Adds auxiliary loss and removes causal masking at train time; at inference, remains AR but with richer hidden states.
- **Failure signatures:**
  - No performance gain when increasing `n` beyond a small value on a reasoning task.
  - AR-MTP performs similarly to AR-NTP (same arch, no multi-token loss), indicating benefits come from capacity not latent training.
  - Modulation fails to interpolate between SIDM and MDM performance, suggesting architectural differences (not just `n`) matter.
- **First 3 experiments:**
  1. **Ablation:** Train SIDM vs. MDM on a reasoning task (e.g., Sudoku) and compare accuracy with same inference budget. Expect MDM > SIDM if latent tokens help.
  2. **Modulation sweep:** For an SCDM, evaluate across `n ∈ {0, 1, 2, 4, 8, 16, L}` on a validation set. Expect monotonic improvement (or plateau) in accuracy/perplexity as `n` increases.
  3. **AR-MTP transfer:** Train ARM and AR-MTP on a reasoning task. Compare accuracy. Then train AR-MTP-Windowed with varying window sizes (controlling `n`) and plot performance vs. `n`. Expect AR-MTP > ARM and scaling with `n`.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
### Open Question 1
- Question: Are latent tokens the primary factor explaining diffusion models' superiority on reasoning tasks, or do other mechanisms like adaptive decoding orders contribute independently?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The introduction questions whether "subgoal decomposition and flexible generation order fully account" for diffusion's advantages, proposing latent tokens as central. Ablations in Section 3.3 show joint prediction (latent tokens) improves performance, but adaptive decoding (Section E) also helps.
- Why unresolved: The paper does not isolate latent tokens from adaptive decoding; both co-occur in standard MDMs. It remains unclear if latent tokens alone suffice or if their interaction with adaptive orders is key.
- What evidence would resolve it: Train models with matched latent token counts (via modulation) under uniform vs. adaptive decoding, or ablate latent tokens while fixing decoding order. Compare to autoregressive models with added latent tokens but no adaptive order.

### Open Question 2
- Question: Under what data characteristics does latent token scaling emerge or fail, as seen in differing trends between Sudoku-Gen and Sudoku-Puzzle?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] Section 4.3.2 notes that "the model trained directly on Sudoku Puzzle data does not exhibit a strong scaling trend with latent tokens, while the model trained on unconditional generation does," hypothesizing differences in training data distribution.
- Why unresolved: No analysis of what specific properties (e.g., constraint density, solution space structure) cause this discrepancy. The hypothesis is untested and may generalize to other tasks.
- What evidence would resolve it: Analyze datasets for features like constraint tightness or diversity; train models on interpolated or synthetic datasets varying these properties; measure scaling curves to identify predictive factors.

### Open Question 3
- Question: Why does a performance gap persist between SCDM (with full latent tokens) and standard MDM in language modeling, despite similar latent token counts?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] Figure 5 shows SCDM with n=1024 still worse than MDM (36.48 vs. ~40). Authors attribute this to "differences in expressivity and training dynamics arising from the modified attention structure" (Section 4.3.2).
- Why unresolved: The gap likely stems from two confounding factors: SCDM's causal attention among masked tokens vs. MDM's bidirectional attention, and SCDM's training without time-step normalization (Eq. A vs. B.3). Their isolated effects are unknown.
- What evidence would resolve it: Cross-ablations: train MDM with causal attention among masks, or SCDM with bidirectional attention and matched training objective. Compare representations and performance to disentangle attention vs. training effects.

### Open Question 4
- Question: Do latent tokens contribute to diffusion models' empirical advantages in data-constrained settings, as suggested by prior work?
- Basis in paper: [inferred] Related work (Prabhudesai et al., 2025) reports diffusion models outperform autoregressive models in low-data regimes, but the paper's experiments use full datasets (Section G.3). Latent tokens' role in data efficiency is unexplored.
- Why unresolved: The paper does not test varying data scales; it remains open whether latent tokens provide sample efficiency gains or if other factors (e.g., training objectives) dominate in constrained settings.
- What evidence would resolve it: Train diffusion and autoregressive models (with/without latent tokens) on subsets of varying sizes for reasoning tasks; measure sample efficiency and correlate with latent token usage (e.g., via modulation).
