# CSEval: A Framework for Evaluating Clinical Semantics in Text-to-Image Generation

## Quick Facts
- arXiv: 2602.12004
- Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.12004
- References: 0
- Primary result: CSEval correlates with radiologist ratings (Kendall τ=0.375) and identifies semantic inconsistencies missed by existing metrics.

## Executive Summary
Medical text-to-image generation often produces images that are visually realistic but may lack clinical accuracy. Current evaluation metrics focus on image quality or generic text-image alignment but do not assess anatomical or pathological correctness. CSEval is a new framework that evaluates clinical semantic fidelity by converting generated images into radiology reports using a pretrained model (MAIRA-2), extracting clinical entities with RadGraph-XL, and computing RadGraph-F1 against the original prompt. Experiments on 310 chest X-ray images across four thoracic diseases show that CSEval scores correlate with radiologist assessments and correctly rank disease severity, whereas traditional metrics fail. This demonstrates CSEval's potential as a scalable, clinically aware evaluation tool.

## Method Summary
CSEval operates in three steps: First, a synthetic medical image is fed into MAIRA-2, a radiology report generator, to produce a corresponding radiology report. Second, RadGraph-XL, an information extraction model, parses the generated report to identify clinical entities (e.g., findings, anatomy, diseases). Third, the set of extracted entities is compared to the clinical entities present in the original text prompt using RadGraph-F1, a metric that measures exact match of entity types and text spans. This pipeline transforms the image generation evaluation problem into a semantic comparison between prompt and generated report, thereby assessing whether the image contains the clinically relevant content described in the prompt. The approach leverages existing robust models in radiology report generation and entity extraction, avoiding the need for manual annotation.

## Key Results
- CSEval scores show a moderate but statistically significant correlation with radiologist ratings (Kendall τ = 0.375).
- CSEval correctly ranks images by disease pathology severity, while FID, MS-SSIM, and CLIP-based metrics do not.
- The framework identifies semantic inconsistencies in generated images that are overlooked by traditional metrics, highlighting its sensitivity to clinical details.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
CSEval works because it bridges the gap between image generation and clinical semantics through an indirect but effective evaluation chain. By using MAIRA-2 to generate a radiology report from the synthetic image, it encodes the image's perceived clinical content in a structured textual form. RadGraph-XL then extracts a set of standardized clinical entities, capturing key findings and their attributes. Comparing these entities to those in the original prompt via RadGraph-F1 directly measures whether the image conveys the intended clinical semantics. This approach avoids the need for explicit anatomical segmentation or pathology detection models, instead leveraging the knowledge embedded in pretrained generative and extraction models. The moderate correlation with radiologists suggests that the pipeline captures clinically relevant aspects, though errors in report generation or extraction limit perfect agreement.

## Foundational Learning
*Clinical Report Generation (MAIRA-2)*
- Why needed: To translate a synthetic image into a textual description of its clinical findings, enabling semantic comparison with the prompt.
- Quick check: MAIRA-2 is pretrained on MIMIC-CXR and yields accurate reports for real chest X-rays; its performance on synthetic images is a known variable.

*Clinical Entity Extraction (RadGraph-XL)*
- Why needed: To systematically identify and categorize medical entities (e.g., diseases, findings, anatomy) from the generated report.
- Quick check: RadGraph-XL achieves state-of-the-art on the RadGraph benchmark for entity extraction from radiology reports.

*RadGraph-F1 Metric*
- Why needed: To provide a quantifiable measure of overlap between the extracted entity set and the prompt's entity set.
- Quick check: RadGraph-F1 computes exact match of entity spans and types, sensitive to missing or spurious entities.

*Chest X-ray Domain Specificity*
- Why needed: Both MAIRA-2 and RadGraph-XL are trained on chest X-ray data; using them outside this domain may yield unreliable results.
- Quick check: Training datasets (MIMIC-CXR, RadGraph) are exclusively chest X-ray; thus CSEval is currently limited to this modality.

## Architecture Onboarding
Component map: Prompt -> Text-to-Image Generator -> Synthetic Image -> MAIRA-2 -> Generated Report -> RadGraph-XL -> Extracted Entities -> RadGraph-F1 vs. Prompt Entities -> CSEval Score

Critical path: The evaluation hinges on the accuracy of MAIRA-2 (report generation) and RadGraph-XL (entity extraction); errors in either component propagate to the final score.

Design tradeoffs: Utilizing off-the-shelf models avoids costly training but ties CSEval to their limitations (e.g., MAIRA-2 may hallucinate findings). RadGraph-F1 offers simplicity but ignores relationships between entities, potentially missing nuanced clinical errors.

Failure signatures: (1) MAIRA-2 produces a report with clinically inaccurate entities (e.g., wrong laterality), (2) RadGraph-XL fails to extract entities due to unconventional phrasing in the generated report, (3) RadGraph-F1 yields a high score despite missing critical relations (e.g., finding location).

First experiments:
1. Apply CSEval to real chest X-ray images with ground-truth reports to establish an upper bound on achievable RadGraph-F1.
2. Compare CSEval scores between synthetic images from a state-of-the-art generator and a baseline generator to confirm discriminative capability.
3. Correlate CSEval with radiologist severity ratings across a diverse set of disease presentations to validate ranking accuracy.

## Limitations
- Moderate correlation with radiologist ratings (Kendall τ=0.375) suggests unaccounted factors such as entity relations or image quality subtleties.
- Dependence on MAIRA-2 and RadGraph-XL, which may produce inaccurate reports or extractions on synthetic images.
- Limited to chest X-rays and four thoracic diseases; generalizability to other modalities and diseases remains untested.
- Using prompts as ground truth may omit clinically relevant findings that appear in real reports.

## Confidence
- Gap addressed: High – CSEval directly targets clinical semantic evaluation.
- Correlation/ranking: Medium – correlation is modest; ranking utility needs broader validation.
- Identifies overlooked issues: Medium – experiments show it catches issues missed by other metrics, but scope is limited.
- Clinically aware: High; Scalable: Medium (requires running MAIRA-2 and RadGraph-XL, more compute than FID).

## Next Checks
1. Evaluate CSEval on additional imaging modalities (CT, MRI, ultrasound) and a broader set of diseases (>50) to test generalizability.
2. Conduct human evaluation of MAIRA-2 reports and RadGraph-XL extractions on synthetic images to quantify error sources and their impact on CSEval scores.
3. Test CSEval with alternative image generators (e.g., Med-VLP) and entity extractors (e.g., QuickUMLS) to assess robustness to model choices.
