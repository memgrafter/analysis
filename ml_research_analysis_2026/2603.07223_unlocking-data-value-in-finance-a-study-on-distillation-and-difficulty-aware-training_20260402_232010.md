# Unlocking Data Value in Finance: A Study on Distillation and Difficulty-Aware Training

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2603.07223  
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.07223  
- Reference count: 40  
- Primary result: ODA-Fin-RL-8B achieves state-of-the-art performance among 8B-scale models, averaging 74.6% across nine financial benchmarks and matching the larger Qwen3-32B.

## Executive Summary
This study addresses the challenge of deploying large language models in finance, where domain-specific knowledge, numerical reasoning, and factual accuracy are critical. The authors propose a data-centric approach: constructing ODA-Fin-SFT-318k via multi-stage distillation and verification to provide high-quality chain-of-thought supervision, and curating ODA-Fin-RL-12k for difficulty- and verifiability-aware reinforcement learning. The resulting 8B model (ODA-Fin-RL-8B) reaches SOTA among 8B models, with dominant gains in numerical reasoning (e.g., 89.3% on TaTQA) and matches the performance of a 32B-parameter model.

## Method Summary
The method consists of two stages. First, supervised fine-tuning (SFT) uses a distilled chain-of-thought (CoT) dataset. From an initial pool of 697K financial QA pairs, semantic deduplication is applied, then missing reasoning steps are generated by a large teacher model (Qwen3-235B-A22B-Thinking). Short answers are verified by CompassVerifier-7B; long answers by Qwen3-235B-Instruct. This yields 318K high-quality CoT samples (ODA-Fin-SFT-318k). Second, reinforcement learning (RL) with GRPO is applied to the SFT model. The RL data is filtered to retain only samples the SFT model fails on (≥50% failure rate over 4 rollouts) and whose ground-truth answers are shorter than 16 tokens, producing 12K samples (ODA-Fin-RL-12k). Rewards combine format score and correctness multiplier (0.5 for wrong, 1.0 for correct). Both stages use standard hyperparameters (full fine-tuning, cosine LR decay; GRPO with KL penalty).

## Key Results
- SOTA among 8B models: 74.6% average across nine financial benchmarks.  
- Matches Qwen3-32B performance despite being four times smaller.  
- Exceptional numerical reasoning: 89.3% on TaTQA, surpassing prior financial LLMs.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1  
**Claim:** High-quality, verified CoT supervision during SFT establishes a robust foundation for domain-specific reasoning, outperforming raw or partially distilled data.  
**Mechanism:** A multi-stage distillation and verification pipeline (semantic deduplication, CoT synthesis via Qwen3-235B-A22B-Thinking, and length-adaptive verification) transforms noisy QA pairs into consistent, step-by-step reasoning traces. This provides explicit demonstrations of logical and computational processes, reducing learning from spurious correlations.  
**Core assumption:** The fidelity of the CoT traces is sufficiently high that a smaller student model can learn correct reasoning patterns without inheriting the teacher’s hallucinations or noise.  
**Evidence anchors:**  
- Abstract: "We show that high-quality CoT distillation establishes a robust foundation during SFT."  
- Section 4.3 (Table 2): Training on the full 318K distilled CoT set yields the highest average performance; raw or partially augmented data degrades results, especially on strong base models.  
- Break condition: Raw data inclusion causes performance collapse on capable base models (e.g., Qwen3-8B drops from 71.5% to 66.1%).

### Mechanism 2  
**Claim:** Difficulty- and verifiability-aware sampling for RL training, using hard-but-verifiable samples and a constrained answer format, optimizes the reward-precision/task-diversity trade-off to drive systematic improvement beyond the SFT plateau.  
**Mechanism:** RL filters the SFT dataset to retain only samples the SFT model frequently fails on (≥50% failure rate) and with concise ground-truth answers (<16 tokens). This focuses optimization on challenging yet reliably scorable tasks, using CompassVerifier-7B for precise reward signals. The short answer constraint reduces verifier noise; the difficulty filter prevents reinforcement of superficial heuristics.  
**Core assumption:** A reliable, low-latency verifier exists for a meaningful subset of hard financial tasks, and optimizing on this subset leads to generalized reasoning gains rather than overfitting.  
**Evidence anchors:**  
- Abstract: "difficulty- and verifiability-aware sampling improves RL generalization."  
- Section 4.4 (Table 3, Figure 4): The 16-token answer length constraint yields the best average performance (74.6%), balancing data size (12.2K) and verification reliability; stricter or looser constraints reduce results.  
- Break condition: Overly lenient length (>128 tokens) introduces verifier noise, lowering performance (73.1% vs. 74.6%).

### Mechanism 3  
**Claim:** Indiscriminate data augmentation (e.g., mixing general math or broad CoT datasets) with domain-specific financial data induces negative transfer due to distribution shifts, harming specialized performance.  
**Mechanism:** Financial reasoning relies on domain-specific conventions (accounting standards, contextual interpretations of earnings reports) that differ from pure mathematical logic or general knowledge. Augmenting with out-of-domain data dilutes the task distribution, weakening alignment with financial nuances.  
**Core assumption:** The optimal training distribution for financial LLMs is a narrow, high-quality domain-specific manifold; deviation harms performance on core benchmarks.  
**Evidence anchors:**  
- Section 4.3: Adding general math (DeepMath) or table-calculation (MultiHiertt) data to the distilled CoT set degrades average scores, especially for Qwen3 variants (e.g., Finova drops to 41.7% from 47.8%).  
- Discussion: "Contrary to the intuition that general mathematical skills transfer directly to finance, we observed that mixing general mathematical data... harmed performance. This negative transfer suggests that financial reasoning relies on specific conventions... that are distinct from pure mathematical logic."

## Foundational Learning
- **Concept: Chain-of-Thought (CoT) Distillation**  
  - Why needed here: Financial tasks require multi-step numerical and logical reasoning. Explicit CoT traces provide a supervised signal for intermediate processes, not just the final answer. The paper’s premise is that CoT quality is the primary determinant of SFT success.  
  - Quick check question: If you remove the reasoning steps from a financial QA pair (e.g., "Calculate EBITDA from given income statement items"), how does the model's ability to generalize to new, complex calculations change?

- **Concept: Difficulty-Aware Data Curation for RL**  
  - Why needed here: RL on easy samples leads to shallow learning and reward hacking. Filtering for samples the SFT model fails on (≥50% failure rate) focuses RL on hard-but-verifiable examples that push reasoning boundaries. The verifier’s precision is tied to answer length, creating a key trade-off.  
  - Quick check question: What happens to RL performance if you include samples the SFT model gets 100% correct on? What if you include samples where the verifier cannot reliably judge the answer?

- **Concept: Vertical Domain Distribution Shift**  
  - Why needed here: Adding general-domain high-quality data (math, broad CoT) harms financial performance, indicating a sharp domain boundary. Financial reasoning has unique patterns (accounting standards, jargon) not transferred from general skills.  
  - Quick check question: If you train a model on 1M general math problems and then 10k financial CoT problems, will it perform better or worse on a financial reasoning benchmark than if trained only on the 10k financial problems?

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map:** Raw Data Pool (697K) -> SFT Pipeline (semantic deduplication, CoT synthesis, length-adaptive verification) -> ODA-Fin-SFT-318k -> SFT Training (Qwen3-8B) -> ODA-Fin-SFT-8B -> RL Pipeline (difficulty filter ≥50% failure, answer length <16 tokens) -> ODA-Fin-RL-12k -> GRPO RL -> ODA-Fin-RL-8B.  
- **Critical path:** Raw Data → Multi-stage Distillation → ODA-Fin-SFT-318k → SFT Training → ODA-Fin-SFT-8B → Difficulty/Verifiability Filtering → ODA-Fin-RL-12k → GRPO RL → ODA-Fin-RL-8B.  
- **Design tradeoffs:**  
  - SFT: Purity vs. Coverage – full replacement with distilled CoT beats mixing raw or general data, but risks losing broad financial knowledge if distillation is too narrow.  
  - RL: Reward Precision vs. Task Diversity – short answers (<16 tokens) ensure verifier reliability but exclude complex analysis tasks; the 16-token constraint is empirically optimal.  
  - Verifier: Speed vs. Accuracy – rule-based matching is fast but brittle; model-based verifier (CompassVerifier-7B) is more robust but adds latency.  
- **Failure signatures:**  
  - SFT failure: Raw data inclusion causes collapse on capable base models (Qwen3-8B average: 71.5% → 66.1%).  
  - RL failure: Overly lenient answer-length filtering (>128 tokens) introduces verifier noise, reducing average (73.1% vs. 74.6%).  
  - Generalization failure: Augmentation with out-of-domain data degrades financial scores (e.g., Finova 47.8% → 41.7%).  
- **First 3 experiments:**  
  1. **SFT Data Composition Ablation:** Train Qwen3-8B on (a) raw 697K, (b) partial CoT (192K), (c) full distilled CoT (318K), (d) augmented sets; validate that full distilled CoT maximizes average performance.  
  2. **RL Answer Length Constraint Sweep:** Using ODA-Fin-SFT-8B, train RL on filtered sets with max GT answer lengths: 1 token (6.2K), 16 tokens (12.2K), 128 tokens (24.9K); measure average to find precision-diversity optimum.  
  3. **Verifier Type Comparison:** For RL with 16-token constraint, compare rule-based vs. model-based (CompassVerifier-7B) verification, especially on sentiment analysis and Finova where model-based verifier shows gains.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- **Open Question 1:** What is the optimal failure-rate threshold for defining "hard" RL samples, and does it vary across financial task types? The paper uses a fixed >50% failure rate but does not explore alternatives; thresholds might need task-specific tuning.
- **Open Question 2:** How can RL reward design be extended to reliably verify long-form financial reasoning (e.g., compliance reports) rather than short answers? Current pipeline filters out long answers due to verification noise; real-world tasks often require detailed explanations.
- **Open Question 3:** Does the data-centric approach (CoT distillation + difficulty‑aware RL) transfer to other financial subdomains (e.g., risk assessment, quantitative trading, forecasting) beyond QA, sentiment, and numerical reasoning? Those subdomains have distinct data structures and reasoning patterns.
- **Open Question 4:** Is the overall approach computationally cost‑effective compared to simply scaling model size, considering the use of a 235B‑parameter teacher for distillation? The teacher inference cost may far outweigh any savings from the smaller student.

## Limitations
- Data construction pipeline details (similarity thresholds, generation/verification prompts) are underspecified, hindering exact replication.  
- Training hyperparameters (optimizer, weight decay, gradient clipping) are omitted, leaving configuration gaps.  
- Evaluation is restricted to short-answer benchmarks; long-form reasoning and real-world deployment remain unaddressed.  
- Claims rely heavily on Qwen3-8B; generalizability to other architectures or weaker models is uncertain.

## Confidence
- Mechanism 1 (high-quality CoT distillation): High  
- Mechanism 2 (difficulty/verifiability-aware RL): High  
- Mechanism 3 (negative transfer from general data): Medium  

## Next Checks
1. **Pipeline ablation:** Re-run SFT with sequential removal of deduplication, synthesis, and verification steps; measure CoT correctness and downstream performance to isolate each component’s contribution.  
2. **RL hyperparameter sweep:** Vary failure-rate thresholds (e.g., 30%, 50%, 70%) and max answer lengths (including longer answers with a more accurate verifier) to map the reward-precision/diversity trade-off and test robustness.  
3. **Cross-model transfer:** Apply the same data and RL pipeline to weaker base models (e.g., Qwen2.5-7B) and evaluate whether negative transfer still occurs and if RL gains persist.
