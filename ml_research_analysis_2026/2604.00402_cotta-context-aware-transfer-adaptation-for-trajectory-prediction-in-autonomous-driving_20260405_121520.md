# COTTA: Context-Aware Transfer Adaptation for Trajectory Prediction in Autonomous Driving

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2604.00402  
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.00402  
- Reference count: 10  
- Primary result: Selectively fine-tuning only the decoder while freezing the pretrained encoder reduces minADE by 66.3% and minFDE by 83.7% compared to training from scratch on the Korean ETRI dataset.

## Executive Summary
Trajectory prediction models trained on large-scale driving datasets (e.g., Argoverse 2) often suffer from significant performance drops when deployed in new geographic domains due to environmental and behavioral shifts. This paper investigates transfer learning strategies to adapt the query-centric model QCNet from a US dataset to the Korean ETRI dataset. Four strategies are compared: zero-shot, training from scratch, full fine-tuning, and encoder freezing (fine-tuning only the decoder). The key finding is that freezing the pretrained encoder (map and agent networks) while adapting the decoder yields the best trade-off in accuracy and data efficiency, dramatically improving long-horizon metrics. This demonstrates that general motion priors captured during large-scale pretraining are broadly transferable, while high-level behavioral policies remain domain-specific and require targeted adaptation.

## Method Summary
The authors build upon QCNet, a query-centric trajectory predictor pretrained on Argoverse 2 (US). To adapt to the Korean ETRI dataset, they implement four transfer regimes: (1) zero-shot evaluation with pretrained weights; (2) training from random initialization; (3) full fine-tuning of all parameters; and (4) encoder freezing, where only the decoder (query refinement module) is fine-tuned while the map and agent encoders are kept fixed. All fine-tuning runs use identical hyperparameters: 150 epochs, AdamW optimizer (learning rate 1e-4, weight decay 1e-4), batch size 16, and 10% dropout. Performance is measured via minADE and minFDE (K=6 predicted trajectories) on the ETRI test set. The encoder freezing approach leverages the pretrained encoder’s robust, geographically invariant features while allowing the decoder to specialize to local driving behaviors.

## Key Results
- Encoder freezing achieves the best accuracy-efficiency trade-off, reducing minADE by 66.3% and minFDE by 83.7% relative to training from scratch.
- Freezing the encoder while fine-tuning the decoder outperforms full fine-tuning, particularly on long-horizon minFDE, indicating better preservation of stable future predictions.
- The pretrained encoder provides essential general motion representations that transfer positively across the US–Korea domain gap, even without adaptation.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The superior performance of encoder freezing stems from three mechanisms:

1. **Pretrained encoder captures universal motion features**: The encoder (map and agent networks) learns fundamental scene context (lane topology, relative agent positioning) from diverse US data. These low-level primitives are invariant across most road environments. Freezing preserves this robust feature extraction backbone. Evidence: Zero-shot transfer already beats training from scratch, and the authors state that large-scale pretraining yields strong general motion priors (abstract).  
   *Break condition*: If the target domain has radically different infrastructure (e.g., non-lane-based traffic), the encoder may need adaptation.

2. **Decoder encodes domain-specific behavioral policies**: The query-centric decoder generates and refines candidate trajectories, implicitly learning the mapping from scene context to behavioral outcomes. This mapping is sensitive to regional norms (e.g., Korean intersection dynamics). Fine-tuning the decoder allows it to re-weight queries according to ETRI data while relying on the frozen encoder’s generic context. Evidence: Encoder freezing (decoder tuned) outperforms full fine-tuning (both tuned) in minADE/minFDE, suggesting that over-adapting the encoder harms performance.

3. **Catastrophic interference in full fine-tuning**: Full fine-tuning updates the encoder on the small ETRI dataset (~20k vs. Argoverse 2’s 250k scenarios), introducing noisy, domain-specific signals that corrupt the encoder’s general features. This is most detrimental to long-term prediction (minFDE). Evidence: Full fine-tuning’s minFDE (0.721) is far worse than encoder freezing’s (0.444), despite lower minADE.  
   *Break condition*: If the target dataset is comparable in size to the source, full fine-tuning may not suffer catastrophic forgetting.

## Foundational Learning
- **Query-Centric Trajectory Prediction (QCNet Architecture)**: QCNet uses learnable queries in the decoder to iteratively refine trajectory proposals. *Why needed*: Understanding that the decoder is the query refinement module is key to interpreting the freezing strategy. *Quick check*: In a query-centric model, what is the primary function of the decoder, and how does it differ from a classic encoder-decoder trajectory predictor?
- **Transfer Learning Regimes**: The paper compares zero-shot, scratch training, full fine-tuning, and selective freezing. *Why needed*: The core contribution is showing that freezing the encoder while fine-tuning the decoder is optimal. *Quick check*: What is the key risk of full fine-tuning on a small target dataset compared to training from scratch, and how does parameter freezing mitigate it?
- **Domain Gap in Autonomous Driving Datasets**: The shift between US (Argoverse) and Korean (ETRI) driving environments includes differences in map topology, traffic density, and driving aggressiveness. *Why needed*: The entire problem arises from this domain shift. *Quick check*: List three specific characteristics of Korean driving environments that differ from US environments and could cause a domain gap.

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map**: Input Encoder Network (Map & Agent) → Scene Context Embedding → Query-Centric Decoder → Multimodal Trajectory Outputs (via loss functions).  
- **Critical path**: The frozen encoder processes historical trajectories and lane maps into a scene context, which is consumed by the decoder to produce final trajectory samples and confidence scores.  
- **Design tradeoffs**:
  - *Freezing Encoder*: Maximizes preservation of general priors, reduces training time and overfitting risk on small ETRI data. Cost: Limits adaptation of scene parsing to new map styles/traffic patterns.
  - *Full Fine-tuning*: Allows full adaptation but risks catastrophic forgetting of encoder features and poor long-term accuracy on limited data.
  - *From Scratch*: No transfer; model learns only from ETRI data, requiring more data/time to acquire basic motion concepts and struggling with long-horizon predictions.
- **Failure signatures**:
  - Zero-shot: High minADE & minFDE, especially minFDE, indicating poor long-horizon generalization.
  - Scratch training: Moderate minADE but very high minFDE, showing short-term pattern learning but inability to model consistent long-term intent.
  - Full fine-tuning: Lower minADE than encoder freezing but higher minFDE, suggesting decoder adaptation helps short-term accuracy while encoder updates harm long-term stability.
- **First 3 experiments**:
  1. Zero-shot Evaluation: Load Argoverse 2 pretrained QCNet and evaluate on ETRI test set.
  2. Scratch Training: Initialize random QCNet weights and train on ETRI train set.
  3. Full Fine-tuning: Load pretrained QCNet and train all parameters on ETRI train set.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
1. **Expanding data diversity and augmentation**: Does enlarging the Korean dataset to cover more geographic areas and complex scenarios, and applying region-specific data augmentation, improve robustness and accuracy?
2. **Unsupervised domain adaptation (UDA) on the encoder**: Can adversarial learning, feature alignment, or style transfer applied to the encoder reduce structural and contextual feature discrepancies without full fine-tuning?
3. **Multi-domain learning framework**: How can a single trajectory prediction model generalize across multiple countries' environments while maintaining high per-domain performance (e.g., via domain discriminators, domain-specific encoders, or mixture-of-experts)?

## Limitations
- The raw ETRI-to-QCNet LMDB conversion pipeline is not described, risking implementation errors and subtle biases.
- The exact modules/layers that constitute the "encoder" to freeze are unspecified (e.g., whether all transformer blocks in map and agent encoders are included).
- There is a temporal horizon mismatch: Argoverse 2 uses 5 s history/6 s prediction, while ETRI uses 2 s/4 s. It is unclear whether the model is modified or data resampled, potentially affecting transferability.

## Confidence
- Encoder captures general motion priors: Medium confidence
- Decoder must be adapted to the domain: Medium confidence
- Full fine-tuning causes catastrophic interference: Low confidence
