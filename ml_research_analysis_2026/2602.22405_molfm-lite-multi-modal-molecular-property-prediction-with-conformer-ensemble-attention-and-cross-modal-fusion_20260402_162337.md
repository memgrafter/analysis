# MolFM-Lite: Multi-Modal Molecular Property Prediction with Conformer Ensemble Attention and Cross-Modal Fusion

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2602.22405
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.22405
- **Reference count:** 7
- **Primary result:** Tri-modal fusion yields 7–11% ROC-AUC improvement over single-modality baselines on MoleculeNet, achieving 0.956 AUC on BBBP, 0.902 on BACE, 0.848 on Tox21, and 0.570 RMSE on Lipophilicity; conformer ensembles contribute an additional ~2% gain over single-conformer variants.

## Executive Summary
MolFM-Lite is a multi-modal molecular property prediction model that jointly encodes molecules via SELFIES sequences (1D), molecular graphs (2D), and conformer ensembles (3D). Its core innovations are a Boltzmann-weighted conformer ensemble attention mechanism that aggregates multiple 3D structures based on thermodynamic priors, and a cross-modal fusion layer that uses cross-attention to enable mutual information sharing between modalities. Pre-trained on ZINC250K with contrastive and masked-atom objectives, the model fine-tunes on four MoleculeNet scaffold-split benchmarks. Results demonstrate significant gains: tri-modal fusion improves ROC-AUC by 7–11% over single-modality baselines, setting new state-of-the-art on BBBP (0.956), BACE (0.902), Tox21 (0.848), and Lipophilicity (0.570 RMSE). The architecture also includes a FiLM-based context conditioning module, intended for future adaptation to assay metadata, though its impact on standard benchmarks is minimal due to lack of context.

## Method Summary
MolFM-Lite processes three molecular representations with separate encoders: a 4-layer Transformer for SELFIES strings, a 4-layer Graph Isomorphism Network (GIN) for molecular graphs (38 atom features), and a 3-layer SchNet-Lite for 3D conformer coordinates. For each molecule, *K*=5 conformers are generated using RDKit's ETKDG algorithm and MMFF94 minimization. The 3D encoder yields *K* representations; a conformer ensemble attention aggregates them via a weighted sum where weights combine a learned query-key score with the log Boltzmann probability derived from MMFF94 energies, producing a single *h*<sub>3D</sub>. The 1D and 2D vectors are then enhanced through cross-attention: each attends to the other two modalities (e.g., *h̃*<sub>1D</sub> = *h*<sub>1D</sub> + CrossAttn(*h*<sub>1D</sub>, *h*<sub>2D</sub>) + CrossAttn(*h*<sub>1D</sub>, *h*<sub>3D</sub>)), while the 3D vector remains unchanged. The three vectors are concatenated and passed through an MLP to produce a fused embedding *h*<sub>fused</sub>. An optional FiLM layer modulates *h*<sub>fused</sub> using a context vector (e.g., assay conditions). Pre-training on ZINC250K (~250k molecules) uses two objectives: a symmetric InfoNCE contrastive loss between each pair of modalities and a masked atom prediction task on the graph. Downstream fine-tuning on MoleculeNet tasks uses a two-layer MLP head with dropout and MC Dropout for uncertainty estimation. Training employs AdamW with a 30-epoch pre-training phase (batch 64, lr 1e-4) and a 100-epoch fine-tuning phase (batch 16, lr 5e-5, early stopping).

## Key Results
- Tri-modal fusion (1D+2D+3D) yields 7–11% ROC-AUC improvement over single-modality baselines on MoleculeNet scaffold splits.
- State-of-the-art results: BBBP 0.956 AUC, BACE 0.902 AUC, Tox21 0.848 AUC, Lipophilicity 0.570 RMSE.
- Conformer ensemble attention contributes an additional ~2% performance gain compared to using a single conformer, under controlled evaluation.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1: Conformer Ensemble Attention
- **Claim:** Capturing the thermodynamic distribution of molecular shapes via a learned attention mechanism over multiple conformers improves representation over a single static 3D structure.
- **Mechanism:** For each molecule, *K* conformers are generated; the 3D encoder produces a representation *h*<sub>k</sub> for each. Attention weights are computed as a softmax over a sum of a learnable query-key score and the log Boltzmann probability *p*<sub>k</sub><sup>Boltz</sup> derived from MMFF94 energies. The final 3D representation is *h*<sub>3D</sub> = Σ α<sub>k</sub> *h*<sub>k</sub>.
- **Core assumption:** The biologically relevant conformation may not be the global minimum; Boltzmann weighting provides a useful prior, but the model must learn to re-weight for the specific task.
- **Evidence anchors:** Abstract states "conformer ensemble attention mechanism that combines learnable attention with Boltzmann-weighted priors". Section 3.3 gives equations (3)-(4). Neighboring papers on ensemble representations provide contextual support but not specific validation.
- **Break condition:** If MMFF94 systematically mis-calibrates relative energies for a chemotype, or if the task's bioactive conformation is consistently high-energy and the learned attention cannot override the strong prior due to limited data.

### Mechanism 2: Cross-Modal Fusion
- **Claim:** Cross-attention enables each modality to selectively integrate complementary information from the others, leading to synergistic gains.
- **Mechanism:** After projecting encodings to a common dimension (*R*<sup>256</sup>), the 1D and 2D vectors are enhanced by adding cross-attention outputs where they serve as Query and another modality as Key-Value. The final representation concatenates the enhanced vectors and projects via an MLP.
- **Core assumption:** The three modalities encode partially orthogonal information (e.g., 1D: functional group syntax; 2D: bonding topology; 3D: spatial arrangement), and the model can learn to query relevant features across modalities.
- **Evidence anchors:** Abstract mentions "cross-modal fusion layer enabling mutual information sharing". Section 3.4 defines equations (5)-(7). Section 4.5 ablation shows cross-attention outperforms concatenation by 2.0–2.7%.
- **Break condition:** If modalities are highly redundant for a given task (e.g., property determined mostly by 2D connectivity), cross-attention's added parameters may yield no benefit or cause overfitting on small datasets.

### Mechanism 3: Contrastive Pre-Training
- **Claim:** Modest-scale, multi-modal contrastive pre-training on ZINC250K aligns the three encoders' representation spaces, stabilizing fine-tuning on small downstream datasets.
- **Mechanism:** Simultaneous training on unlabeled molecules uses (1) symmetric InfoNCE contrastive loss for each modality pair to pull same-molecule representations together across modalities, and (2) masked atom prediction on the 2D graph to enforce chemical semantics.
- **Core assumption:** Aligning latent spaces via contrastive learning on a large generic pool creates a coherent multi-modal starting point, preventing one encoder from dominating or misalignment during early fine-tuning.
- **Evidence anchors:** Abstract notes pre-training on ZINC250K enables effective weight initialization at modest compute. Section 3.7 lists the two objectives. Section 4.5 ablation shows "No pre-training" degrades performance by 3.3% on BBBP consistently.
- **Break condition:** If the pre-training distribution (ZINC250K) is mismatched to downstream tasks (different chemical space or property regime), aligned representations may be suboptimal or detrimental.

## Foundational Learning
### Contrastive Learning (InfoNCE)
- **Why needed here:** The cross-modal contrastive pre-training objective (Section 3.7) aligns three separate encoders without explicit supervision, essential for multi-modal integration.
- **Quick check question:** What does minimizing the InfoNCE loss for a positive pair (same molecule, different modalities) incentivize about the relationship between their representation vectors *z*<sup>(a)</sup> and *z*<sup>(b)</sup>?

### Cross-Attention Mechanism
- **Why needed here:** The cross-modal fusion module (Section 3.4) replaces simple concatenation with cross-attention, requiring understanding of how Query, Key, Value roles allow dynamic feature selection across modalities.
- **Quick check question:** In the call `CrossAttn(Q=h<sup>1D</sup>, KV=h<sup>2D</sup>)`, what does each attention head's output vector represent in terms of information source?

### Graph Isomorphism Network (GIN)
- **Why needed here:** The 2D encoder is a GIN (Section 3.2), chosen for its theoretical guarantee of maximal discriminative power for graph structures, underpinning distinct bonding topology capture.
- **Quick check question:** How does the GIN update rule `h<sub>v</sub><sup>(l+1)</sup> = MLP( (1+ϵ)·h<sub>v</sub><sup>(l)</sup> + Σ h<sub>u</sub><sup>(l)</sup> )` differ from a standard sum-based message passing layer, and why is the ϵ term important?

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component map:**  
SELFIES → Transformer (4L,256d) → h1D; Graph → GIN (4L,256d) → h2D; K conformers → SchNet-Lite (3L,128d) → [h3D^1...h3D^K]; [h3D^k] → Conformer Attention (Boltzmann-weighted) → h3D (projected to 256d); (h1D, h2D, h3D) → Cross-Fusion (1D & 2D enhanced via cross-attention to other modalities) → h_fused (concatenation + MLP); h_fused → FiLM (γ,β) → MLP Head → output.

**Critical path:** The data flow is strictly sequential: Encoders → Conformer Attention (3D only) → Cross-Fusion (enhances 1D/2D) → Concatenation/MLP → FiLM → Prediction Head. No feedback loops; notably asymmetric as 3D does not receive cross-attention from others.

**Design tradeoffs:**
- *Conformer count (K) vs. Cost:* K=5 balances ensemble sampling vs. linear 3D encoder cost; attention+Boltzmann enables small K.
- *Cross-Attention vs. Concatenation:* cross-attention adds O(d²) complexity but yields 2-2.7% AUC gains over concatenation.
- *Pre-training Scale:* Using ZINC250K (250K) instead of large-scale (e.g., Uni-Mol 209M) reduces compute; architectural design compensates on standard benchmarks.

**Failure signatures:**
- High uncertainty on novel scaffolds: MC Dropout variance spikes for out-of-distribution molecules.
- Ablation collapse: Removing cross-attention or Boltzmann prior causes significant performance drops across tasks.
- Context conditioning silence: On MoleculeNet (no context), FiLM parameters may not receive meaningful gradients; expected but must be validated before contextual deployment.
- Split leakage: Using non-scaffold splits invalidates comparisons; must use author-provided splits.

**First 3 experiments:**
1. **Modality Ablation:** Fine-tune with only 1D, only 2D, only 3D, and all two-modality combos on BBBP; expect full tri-modal to outperform all, with 1D+2D as strongest dual-modality pair.
2. **Conformer Ensemble Analysis:** On BBBP test set, compute correlation between learned attention weights αₖ and log Boltzmann probabilities; expect positive correlation (~0.6-0.8) but deviations for high-uncertainty predictions.
3. **Context Conditioning Sanity Check:** Introduce a synthetic binary context correlated with label on a held-out BBBP subset; verify FiLM's γ/β receive gradient signal and performance improves only on that subset.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
1. Does FiLM-based context conditioning improve performance on datasets with rich experimental metadata (e.g., assay type, cell line)? The paper notes minimal impact on MoleculeNet due to lack of such metadata and plans evaluation on ChEMBL assay data.
2. Does scaling pre-training from ZINC250K to a significantly larger corpus (e.g., ZINC20 with ~1.5B molecules) yield substantial transfer gains, or is the benefit primarily from multimodal alignment? The authors propose this as future work.
3. Does replacing RDKit's ETKDG conformer generator with a learned, task-specific conformer generation module (e.g., a diffusion model) improve property prediction beyond the current Boltzmann-weighted ensemble attention? The paper suggests investigating learned conformer generation.
4. Does tri-modal fusion provide consistent gains on a broader suite of ADMET and bioactivity tasks beyond the four MoleculeNet benchmarks, particularly on endpoints where 3D shape is critical? The authors call for evaluation on ADMET suites, protein-ligand binding, and in vivo endpoints.

## Limitations
- Conformer generation specifics (RDKit ETKDG/MMFF94 parameters like maxAttempts, pruneRmsThresh) are not detailed; small variations may alter ensemble quality and affect reproducibility.
- Pre-training negative sampling strategy (in-batch vs. offline) is unspecified; this could impact contrastive learning stability and final model performance.
- Scaffold split algorithm (Murcko scaffold function, tie-breaking) is not provided; using non-author splits risks invalid performance comparisons.

## Confidence
- **Conformer Ensemble Attention mechanism:** Medium (internal evidence supports design but relies on MMFF94 calibration; external validation is indirect).
- **Cross-Modal Fusion mechanism:** High (clear ablation attributing 2-2.7% gains to cross-attention).
- **Contrastive Pre-Training mechanism:** Medium (ablation shows benefit but distribution mismatch risks unprobed; limited external corroboration).

## Next Checks
1. **Attention-Boltzmann correlation:** Compute Pearson correlation between learned αₖ and log Boltzmann probabilities on BBBP test set; expect positive (~0.6-0.8) but with deviations for high-uncertainty predictions.
2. **Cross-attention ablation with matched capacity:** Replace cross-attention with concatenation but increase fused MLP hidden size to match parameter count; if gains vanish, improvement is due to attention's dynamic routing rather than extra capacity.
3. **Context conditioning with synthetic signal:** Add a synthetic binary context (e.g., "has_chlorine") correlated with BBBP labels on a held-out subset; verify FiLM parameters receive gradient signal and that performance improves only on that subset.
