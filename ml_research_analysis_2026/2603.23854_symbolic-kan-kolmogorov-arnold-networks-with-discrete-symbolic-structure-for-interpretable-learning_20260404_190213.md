# Symbolic--KAN: Kolmogorov-Arnold Networks with Discrete Symbolic Structure for Interpretable Learning

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2603.23854
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.23854
- **Reference count:** 40
- **Primary result:** Symbolic-KAN achieves high predictive accuracy with relative errors as low as 1.04×10⁻⁵ in regression and below 9.37×10⁻³ in PDEs, while recovering ground-truth symbolic primitives and reducing solution errors by up to 95% compared to baselines like cPIKAN and PINN.

## Executive Summary
Symbolic-KAN introduces a neural architecture that embeds discrete symbolic structure within trainable networks to resolve the interpretability–scalability trade-off in scientific machine learning. It leverages the Kolmogorov-Arnold Representation Theorem by composing learned univariate primitives with scalar projections, and uses hierarchical gating with Gumbel-Softmax to sharpen continuous mixtures into one-hot selections. This yields compact closed-form expressions without post-hoc fitting. Evaluated on data-driven regression, inverse dynamics, and physics-informed PDEs, it demonstrates high accuracy and faithful primitive recovery, while significantly outperforming baselines.

## Method Summary
Symbolic-KAN models multivariate functions via a two-layer decomposition inspired by the Kolmogorov-Arnold Representation Theorem. Each network unit first computes a learned scalar projection from all upstream activations. A library of analytic primitives (e.g., polynomials, trigonometric functions) is then applied to this projection, with continuous coefficients for each primitive. Hierarchical gating—using Gumbel-Softmax with temperature annealing and entropy regularization—progressively sharpens these coefficients into one-hot selections at the primitive, edge, and unit levels. This produces a sparse symbolic mask that isolates a single primitive and projection per active unit. Training occurs in two stages: first optimizing continuous parameters and relaxed gates, then hardening the masks and optionally fine-tuning. A regularization suite encourages sparsity and diversity, ensuring the final expression is compact and human-readable.

## Key Results
- Achieves relative errors as low as 1.04×10⁻⁵ on 1D regression and below 9.37×10⁻³ on PDE inverse problems.
- Reduces solution errors by up to 95% compared to cPIKAN and PINN baselines.
- Faithfully recovers ground-truth symbolic primitives and unknown parameters (e.g., Van der Pol dynamics) without post-hoc fitting.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1
- **Claim:** A single univariate primitive applied to a learned scalar projection can represent a multivariate function component, per Kolmogorov-Arnold.
- **Mechanism:** Each unit learns a scalar projection `s = w^T·h + b` from upstream activations `h`. A single analytic primitive `fp(γs+β)` from library `P` is applied.
- **Core assumption:** The Kolmogorov-Arnold Representation Theorem guarantees that compositions of such univariate projections and outer sums can represent any continuous multivariate function.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract] "...models multivariate functions as compositions of learned univariate primitives applied to learned scalar projections..."
  - [section] "Symbolic–KAN follows the Kolmogorov–Arnold representation theorem... Eq. (1)" and reformulation into unit-level `ψk(sk)` (Eq. 4).
- **Break condition:** Fails if primitive library `P` lacks a basis for true univariate functions, or if learned projection `s` cannot isolate relevant input combinations.

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim:** Differentiable gating (Gumbel-Softmax) can be trained to produce discrete one-hot primitive selections from continuous mixtures.
- **Mechanism:** Primitive coefficients `α kep` are sampled via Gumbel-Softmax (Eq. 12) with annealed temperature `τ(t)`. Entropy regularization (Eq. 22) encourages sharpness. After training, hardening (Eq. 13) replaces `α kep` with one-hot vectors.
- **Core assumption:** Continuous relaxation allows gradient flow while annealing and entropy penalty reliably drive the distribution to a discrete limit.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract] "...hierarchical gating... progressively sharpens continuous mixtures into one-hot selections."
  - [section] Detailed in Section 2.3, including Eq. 12 (Gumbel-Softmax), Eq. 22 (entropy), and hardening (Eq. 13).
- **Break condition:** Breaks if temperature annealing is too fast/slow, entropy weight insufficient, or multiple primitives remain competitive at low temperature.

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim:** Hierarchical gating (primitive, edge, unit) isolates a single primitive-per-edge and a single active edge-per-unit, yielding a compact symbolic expression.
- **Mechanism:** Edge-selection mask `ηk` derived from primitive gate sharpness `ck = max_p α kep` via softmax and Top-1 (Eq. 14-15). Unit gates `ζk` (Eq. 16-18) prune inactive units. Final expression uses one primitive and one projection per surviving unit (Eq. 11).
- **Core assumption:** `ck` is a valid proxy for edge importance, and Top-1 selection yields a meaningful projection. Unit pruning removes redundancy without losing capacity.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract] "...hierarchical gating... each active unit selects a single primitive and projection direction..."
  - [section] Section 2.3 describes edge selection (Eq. 14-15) and unit gating (Eq. 16-18), including NMS penalty (Eq. 23).
- **Break condition:** Fails if NMS penalty fails to prevent duplicate primitives across edges, or if unit pruning removes necessary components.

## Foundational Learning
- **Kolmogorov-Arnold Representation Theorem (KART)**  
  - *Why needed:* Provides theoretical foundation that a multivariate function can be decomposed into compositions of univariate functions and sums, justifying the architectural blueprint.  
  - *Quick check:* Does the network's two-layer decomposition (inner projections + outer univariate functions) align with a constructive interpretation of KART, or is it a looser analogy?
- **Differentiable Discrete Sampling (Gumbel-Softmax)**  
  - *Why needed:* Enables training of discrete symbolic selections (which primitive, which edge) via gradient descent by relaxing the one-hot constraint.  
  - *Quick check:* Is the temperature annealing schedule (`τ(t)`) and entropy regularization sufficient to avoid suboptimal local minima in the discrete space?
- **Sparse Regularization (Entropy & Non-Maximum Suppression)**  
  - *Why needed:* Drives the continuous gates toward sparsity (one-hot) and diversity (different primitives per edge), ensuring a compact and non-redundant final symbolic form.  
  - *Quick check:* Do the regularization weights (`λ ent`, `λ nms`) require problem-specific tuning to balance sparsity and accuracy?

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map:** Input ξ -> Layer ℓ: (1) Scalar Projections `ske` (Eq.5), (2) Primitive Application `Eϕ kep(ske)` (Eq.6), (3) Edge Mask `ηk` (Eq.9-10), (4) Unit Gate `ζk` (Eq.16-17) -> Linear Readout.
- **Critical path:** Projection weights (`wke, bke`) -> Primitive parameters (`γ kep, β kep, A kep, B kep`) -> Gate logits (`gkep, ck, dk`) -> Hard masks (`α kep, ηk, ζk`) -> Symbolic expression (Eq.11).
- **Design tradeoffs:**
  - Library size `P` vs. overfitting/selection complexity: Larger libraries increase coverage but make one-hot selection harder.
  - Gating sharpness vs. training stability: Fast annealing risks gradient starvation; slow annealing risks non-discrete final states.
  - Unit pruning (`ζk`) vs. representational capacity: Aggressive pruning yields simpler expressions but may underfit.
- **Failure signatures:**
  - High final entropy `H(αke)` → gates didn't sharpen → no discrete symbolic form.
  - Poor parameter recovery in inverse dynamics (e.g., Van der Pol `µ` error >5%) → incorrect primitive selection or projection.
  - Large PDE validation error (`E(uθ) > 10⁻²`) → network failed to capture solution structure, possibly due to inadequate library or unstable gating.
- **First 3 experiments:**
  1. **Data-driven 1D regression:** Learn `F(x)=x²` from 250 samples in `[0,5]` with `[L=2, Kℓ=2, E=3]`. Verifies primitive selection (linear, quadratic) and low error (`~10⁻⁵`).
  2. **Van der Pol inverse dynamics:** Identify parameters `a, µ, c` from trajectory data with `[L=4, Kℓ=6, E=3]`. Tests robustness to non-integer powers (`x^2.15`) and long-term stability (`T=50`). Validates symbolic discovery of `sin, cos, x`.
  3. **Reaction-Diffusion PDE:** Inverse problem on `[-4,4]` with `[L=4, Kℓ=6, E=3]`. Tests scalability to larger domains and nonlinear `tanh(u)` reaction. Compares error reduction vs. cPIKAN/PINN (target >95%) and checks primitive library alignment.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

2. What are the fundamental limitations of Symbolic-KAN in extrapolating PDE solutions beyond the training domain, and under what conditions does it outperform or fail compared to PINNs?
3. How does the choice of predefined analytic primitive library affect Symbolic-KAN's ability to recover true governing equations, and can the library be adaptively expanded during training?
4. What are the optimal hyperparameter settings (temperature annealing, regularization weights, gating thresholds) for symbolic regularization, and how sensitive is the method across problem classes?
5. For systems not adhering to KART (e.g., non-smooth compositions, high-dimensional interactions), how does Symbolic-KAN compare to standard KANs, and can architectural modifications improve applicability?

## Limitations
- Exact hyperparameter values (λ_ent, λ_nms, λ_bias), temperature schedule, and training epochs are omitted, hindering reproducibility.
- Primitive library composition (including constants 0,1) and ordering are unspecified, which may affect symbolic discovery.
- Unit-gating details (sparsity penalty form, threshold) are vague, leaving implementation ambiguity.
