# A Controlled Study of Double DQN and Dueling DQN Under Cross-Environment Transfer

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2602.09810
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.09810
- **Reference count:** 20
- **Primary result:** Under a fixed layer-wise transfer from CartPole to LunarLander, Double DQN avoids negative transfer (validation reward 182.60 ± 31.63), while Dueling DQN suffers consistent negative transfer (-156.87 ± 15.03), with a significant performance gap (Mann-Whitney U, p=0.0079).

## Executive Summary
This study compares the robustness of Double DQN (DDQN) and Dueling DQN to cross-environment transfer learning. Agents are trained on CartPole, and two hidden layers are transferred to LunarLander with an initial freezing period. DDQN achieves validation rewards comparable to its from-scratch baseline, demonstrating stable adaptation. In contrast, Dueling DQN exhibits severe negative transfer, yielding highly negative rewards and unstable optimization. The findings suggest that DDQN's bias-reduction mechanism supports robust knowledge reuse under substantial domain shift, while Dueling DQN's value decomposition introduces representational constraints that hinder transfer.

## Method Summary
The experiment uses a fixed layer-wise transfer protocol. First, source agents (DDQN and Dueling DQN) are trained to convergence on CartPole-v2. The weights of the two hidden fully connected layers (128 units, ReLU) are extracted and inserted into new target models for LunarLander-v3. The input and output layers are randomly initialized. For the first 100 episodes on LunarLander, the transferred hidden layers are frozen while only the output layer is trained. After 100 episodes, all layers are unfrozen and training continues to 500 total episodes. Both architectures use identical hyperparameters during transfer: learning rate 0.0005, discount factor 0.99, ε-greedy decay from 1.0 to 0.01, replay buffer size 100k, batch size 64, and soft target updates (τ=0.001). Baseline agents trained from scratch on LunarLander with the same settings provide performance references.

## Key Results
- DDQN transfer validation reward (182.60 ± 31.63) matches its from-scratch baseline (160.61 ± 50.61), indicating no negative transfer.
- Dueling DQN transfer validation reward (-156.87 ± 15.03) is drastically worse than its from-scratch baseline and DDQN transfer.
- The performance difference is statistically significant (Mann-Whitney U test, p=0.0079).
- Training loss curves show DDQN transfer decreases smoothly below 2, while Dueling DQN transfer plateaus between 8 and 12 with high oscillations.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- **Claim:** DDQN’s overestimation bias reduction enables stable Q-value updates during transfer, maintaining learning progress after representation reuse.
- **Mechanism:** Double DQN decouples action selection (online network) from action evaluation (target network), reducing positive bias in TD targets. Smoother targets lead to less volatile gradient updates, allowing the transferred network to fine-tune without catastrophic divergence.
- **Core assumption:** The hidden layers transferred from CartPole contain reusable features; with stable TD errors, the output layer can adapt to LunarLander’s dynamics.
- **Evidence anchors:** Abstract states DDQN avoids negative transfer due to bias-reduction; Section IV shows Transfer DDQN loss stays below 2 and decreases smoothly, while Dueling loss oscillates at 8–12.
- **Break condition:** If domain shift is extreme (e.g., dense to extremely sparse rewards) such that TD targets become highly unreliable even with bias reduction, the mechanism may fail.

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim:** Dueling DQN’s value-advantage decomposition creates representational constraints that become maladaptive when transferred across environments with different action semantics.
- **Mechanism:** Separation into V(s) and A(s,a) forces early features to encode both state value and per-action preferences learned in CartPole (e.g., symmetric left/right actions). When these frozen layers are reused in LunarLander, whose thrust actions are asymmetric, the decomposition resists adaptation, causing unstable Q-estimates and poor performance.
- **Core assumption:** The advantage stream captures fine-grained, task-specific action biases that are not generalizable.
- **Evidence anchors:** Abstract notes Dueling DQN’s negative transfer and degraded performance; Section IV shows Transfer Dueling DQN remains around -370, far below baseline; significant gap in Section V (p=0.0079).
- **Break condition:** If all layers (including those feeding the decomposition heads) are unfrozen from the start, Dueling DQN might avoid the initial constraint and adapt successfully.

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim:** The interaction between the layer-wise freezing protocol and the architecture’s update dynamics determines whether transferred knowledge is preserved; DDQN’s decoupled updates tolerate freezing, while Dueling DQN’s coupled decomposition does not.
- **Mechanism:** The protocol freezes the two hidden layers for the first 100 episodes to protect source representations. DDQN’s target-network separation allows the output layer to learn meaningful Q-values despite frozen features. In Dueling DQN, the value and advantage heads depend on those frozen features; any misalignment between the frozen representation and the target task’s required decomposition cannot be corrected until after the freeze period, leading to persistent error.
- **Core assumption:** The freezing period is long enough to solidify source knowledge but not so long that target learning is permanently hindered; DDQN’s bias-reduction makes it robust, while Dueling DQN is sensitive.
- **Evidence anchors:** Section III.C describes the freezing protocol; Section IV shows DDQN thrives while Dueling fails under identical conditions.
- **Break condition:** Omitting the freezing period entirely might allow Dueling DQN to adapt its decomposition heads concurrently with feature adjustments, potentially avoiding negative transfer.

Quick check: In what type of environments does separating V and A improve data efficiency, and why might that same separation hinder transfer?

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map:** Input (state dim) -> FC Layer 1 (128 units, ReLU) -> FC Layer 2 (128 units, ReLU) -> [Dueling: split into V(s) stream (scalar) and A(s,a) stream (action_dim); recombine Q = V + A - mean(A); DDQN: direct Q output (action_dim)] -> Output (Q-values for each action)
- **Critical path:** 1. Train source agent (CartPole) to convergence (~150-200 episodes, reward ≥195). 2. Extract weights of the two hidden FC layers; discard input/output layers. 3. Build target network for LunarLander: new input (8-dim), same hidden layers with transferred weights, new output (4-actions) random. 4. Freeze hidden layers for first 100 episodes of LunarLander training; train only output with α=0.0005, ε-greedy decay, etc. 5. Unfreeze all layers after 100 episodes and continue training to 500 total episodes. 6. Evaluate greedy policy every 10 episodes to compute validation reward.
- **Design tradeoffs:** Freezing duration protects source knowledge but may delay adaptation (100 episodes chosen arbitrarily). Layer selection assumes abstract mid-level features; shallower layers may be too low-level, deeper layers too task-specific. Identical learning rates for both architectures may not account for Dueling DQN’s higher variance; tuning could improve transfer but would break the controlled comparison.
- **Failure signatures:** Persistent high TD loss (≥8) with oscillations (as in Dueling transfer). Validation reward stuck below -200 or decreasing over time. Large seed-to-seed variance (σ>100) contrasting with DDQN’s stable spread (σ≈30).
- **First 3 experiments:** 1. Vary frozen period (0, 50, 100, 200 episodes) to test whether shorter freezing helps Dueling DQN recover. 2. Transfer only the first hidden layer (leaving second layer random) to isolate which abstraction level is most transferable. 3. Combine Double and Dueling (Rainbow-style) to evaluate whether bias-reduction can offset decomposition instability under transfer.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- Does extending training duration in LunarLander allow Dueling DQN to recover from negative transfer and approach DDQN's performance? Agents were trained for only 500 episodes, insufficient for full convergence in the sparse-reward LunarLander; long-term trajectories remain unknown. Evidence: Continue training transferred Dueling DQN agents beyond 500 episodes (e.g., up to 2000) and assess whether validation reward increases significantly and variance decreases to levels comparable to DDQN transfer.
- How do hybrid architectures like Rainbow, combining DDQN and Dueling DQN innovations, perform under the same cross-environment transfer protocol? The study evaluated only canonical DDQN and Dueling DQN separately; combining their components could yield novel transfer behaviors. Evidence: Train Rainbow agents with identical layer-wise transfer from CartPole to LunarLander and compare validation reward, stability, and learning dynamics.
- Can alternative transfer mechanisms—such as policy distillation, meta-learned initialization, or task-specific adapter layers—mitigate the negative transfer observed in Dueling DQN? The study used a single, naive direct weight transfer with early layer freezing; more sophisticated methods might better align representations. Evidence: Implement each alternative transfer method for Dueling DQN under the same setup and evaluate whether they achieve validation rewards comparable to DDQN transfer or the from-scratch baseline.
- Does the choice of transferred layers (early vs. middle vs. late) differentially affect transfer robustness in DDQN versus Dueling DQN? The fixed middle-layer transfer may interact uniquely with each architecture's inductive bias; other layers might transfer more or less effectively. Evidence: Conduct layer-wise ablation studies where different subsets of layers are transferred and frozen, then measure validation reward and optimization stability for both architectures.
- Are the observed performance gaps generalizable across environment pairs with varying domain similarity? Testing only one extreme pair (CartPole → LunarLander) limits conclusions about generalizability. Evidence: Replicate with multiple source-target pairs spanning a spectrum of similarity (e.g., similar control tasks, different state dimensionalities, varying reward densities) and analyze whether the DDQN advantage persists or diminishes with milder domain shifts.

## Limitations
- Only one source-target pair tested (CartPole → LunarLander), limiting generalizability.
- Freezing duration (100 episodes) arbitrarily chosen; sensitivity unexplored.
- Hyperparameters identical across architectures; Dueling DQN may require different tuning.
- Unknowns: exact Gym version (v2 vs v3), layer mapping, and advantage normalization details could affect replication.

## Confidence
- **DDQN avoids negative transfer:** High (significant p=0.0079, consistent loss curves).
- **Dueling DQN suffers negative transfer:** High (consistent poor rewards and high variance across seeds).
- **Mechanistic explanations (bias-reduction, decomposition constraints):** Medium (plausible but lack direct ablation).
- **Protocol interaction (freezing exacerbates Dueling instability):** Medium (inferred from comparative design, not explicitly varied).

## Next Checks
1. Ablate freezing period: test 0, 50, 100, 200 frozen episodes for both architectures; expect Dueling performance to improve with shorter freeze, DDQN to remain stable.
2. No-freeze transfer: unfreeze all layers from episode 0; if Dueling still underperforms, freezing is not the sole cause.
3. Cross-task replication: repeat experiment with a different source (e.g., Acrobot-v1) and same target (LunarLander-v3) to test generalizability.
