# Cross-Modal Taxonomic Generalization in (Vision-) Language Models

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2603.07474
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.07474
- Reference count: 40
- Primary result: Frozen pretrained LMs in VLMs achieve above-chance hypernym prediction (macro F1 ~80% for Qwen3-1.7B) without hypernym label exposure during training.

## Executive Summary
This paper examines whether language models' taxonomic knowledge (hypernyms) generalizes to vision in vision-language models (VLMs). Using a controlled setup with frozen image encoder (DINOv2) and language model (Qwen3), only a projector is trained, and hypernym labels are removed via random or systematic ablation during training. Testing on hypernym prediction for unseen images using the THINGS database, they find that pretrained LMs achieve above-chance performance even with zero hypernym exposure, demonstrating cross-modal transfer. Generalization relies on visual coherence within leaf categories, and the image encoder's pretraining objective (self-supervised vs. text-supervised) does not affect results. This indicates that hypernym knowledge resides in the LM's weights and can be activated for visual inputs when visual features are coherently structured.

## Method Summary
The study evaluates cross-modal taxonomic generalization by training VLMs on a yes-no question answering task using the THINGS dataset. Image encoders (DINOv2 or SigLIP) and language models (Qwen3 series) are frozen; only a two-layer MLP projector is trained to align image patches with the LM's embedding space. Training data consists of image-question pairs where questions ask about leaf categories, while hypernym labels are ablated either randomly or systematically. Generalization is tested on held-out hypernym categories using macro F1. Visual coherence is manipulated via within-category (preserves coherence) and across-category (destroys coherence) shuffles of leaf concepts. Negative sampling is applied at the image level to form yes-no pairs.

## Key Results
- Pretrained LMs achieve ~80% macro F1 on unseen hypernyms with 0% hypernym exposure, far above the ~40% majority baseline; random LMs perform at chance.
- Within-category shuffle (preserving visual coherence) yields performance similar to original training, while across-category shuffle (destroying coherence) reduces performance to chance.
- A positive correlation (r=0.43) exists between category visual coherence (measured by DINOv2 embedding similarity) and generalization performance.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
Mechanism 1: The pretrained language model backbone encodes taxonomic hypernym relations from text-only training (e.g., distributional patterns like "X such as Y"). When visual features are aligned to the LM's embedding space via a trained projector, the LM's latent hypernym knowledge can be accessed for visual inputs without any hypernym exposure during VLM training. Evidence: Above-chance performance at 100% hypernym ablation and high text-only hypernym QA F1.

Mechanism 2: Cross-modal generalization depends on visual coherence within leaf categories. The projector learns to map coherent visual clusters to lexical members, creating a scaffold for the LM's hypernym knowledge. When visual coherence is destroyed (across-category shuffle), the scaffold collapses and generalization fails. Evidence: Performance preserved under within-category shuffle but not across-category shuffle; positive correlation between coherence and F1.

Mechanism 3: The image encoder's pretraining objective (self-supervised vs. text-supervised) does not contribute to hypernym knowledge; the LM backbone is the sole source. Evidence: No significant performance difference between DINOv2 (no text) and SigLIP (text-supervised) encoders.

## Foundational Learning
- **Distributional semantic knowledge in LMs**: LMs capture hypernymy from text (e.g., "bird" appears in contexts of "sparrow"). This is needed because cross-modal transfer relies on the LM's internal hypernym structure. Quick check: Does your LM backbone score >70% F1 on a text-only hypernym QA task using the dataset's categories?
- **Visual coherence of categories**: Leaf categories must cluster in visual feature space to provide a stable scaffold for LM generalization. Quick check: For a given hypernym, is the average pairwise cosine similarity of DINOv2 embeddings within its leaf categories >0.25?
- **Frozen component training**: Freezing the image encoder and LM isolates the projector's learning and ensures hypernym knowledge can only originate from the LM. Quick check: Did you verify that the image encoder's weights remained unchanged during training?

## Architecture Onboarding
Component map: DINOv2 Image Encoder (frozen) -> 2-layer MLP Projector (trained) -> Qwen3 LM (frozen)

Critical path: Train only the projector while keeping encoder and LM frozen. Input: image patches + question tokens; output: next-token prediction for "yes"/"no". The projector maps image tokens to LM embedding dimension.

Design tradeoffs: Freezing components preserves pretrained knowledge and reduces compute, but prevents adaptation to the task. Using a simple QA task isolates hypernym prediction but may not reflect natural VLM training on multimodal corpora.

Failure signatures: Random LM backbone → chance-level performance on held-out hypernyms. Across-category shuffle → chance-level F1. Low leaf-level F1 (<90%) → projector failed to learn basic mapping.

First 3 experiments:
1. LM hypernym knowledge check: Run text-only QA on frozen LM to verify F1 >70%.
2. Baseline VLM training: Train projector on all hypernym labels; expect >90% leaf F1 and >75% seen-hypernym F1.
3. Ablation & shuffle validation: At 100% hypernym ablation, ensure pretrained LM yields >50% F1 on held-out hypernyms; within-category shuffle should match original, across-category should drop to ~40%.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
1. How does scaling the size of the language model backbone and image encoder influence cross-modal taxonomic generalization?
2. Does cross-modal taxonomic generalization extend to other semantic relations (e.g., meronymy, spatial relations) and other input modalities (e.g., audio, tactile)?
3. Can cross-modal taxonomic generalization occur across languages when leaf-level supervision is provided in one language and hypernymy testing in another?
4. What specific visual properties determine the "visual coherence" that enables generalization, and is there a minimum coherence threshold?
5. Does cross-modal taxonomic generalization persist under more naturalistic VLM training regimes, such as end-to-end training on multimodal corpora or training on tasks like image captioning?

## Limitations
- Incomplete description of negative sampling and train/test splits hinders exact replication.
- THINGS hypernym set is shallow (53 categories); results may not generalize to deeper taxonomies.
- Potential data leakage: LM pretraining corpora may contain THINGS leaf-hypernym pairs, inflating performance.

## Confidence
- Pretrained LM transfers hypernym knowledge cross-modally: Medium
- Visual coherence required for generalization: High
- Image encoder supervision irrelevant: Medium

## Next Checks
1. Test with additional image encoders (e.g., MAE, CLIP, supervised ResNet) to confirm the lack of encoder effect.
2. Audit LM pretraining data for overlap with THINGS leaf-hypernym pairs; if found, train a fresh LM on a filtered corpus and repeat experiments.
3. Compute visual coherence at multiple feature depths (early/late layers) and correlate with per-hypernym F1 to identify relevant representational level.
