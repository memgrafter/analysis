# Exposing Vulnerabilities in Explanation for Time Series Classifiers via Dual-Target Attacks

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID**: 2602.02763
- **Source URL**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.02763
- **Reference count**: 40
- **Primary result**: A dual-target attack can decouple predictions from explanations in time series classifiers, achieving misclassification while keeping the explanation plausibly aligned with a target rationale.

## Executive Summary
This paper challenges the common assumption that stable explanations imply robust decisions in interpretable time series systems. The authors demonstrate that predictions and their corresponding explanations can be adversarially decoupled, enabling attacks that change the model's output while maintaining a plausible explanation. They propose TSEF (Time Series Explanation Fooler), a method that uses a temporal vulnerability mask to select sensitive time windows and a frequency perturbation filter to apply coherent spectral edits. Experiments across multiple datasets and explainers (TIMEX++, TIMEX, Integrated Gradients) show TSEF achieves high attack success rates (up to ~0.95) and significantly better explanation alignment compared to baselines, exposing a critical vulnerability where explanation stability misleads robustness assessment.

## Method Summary
TSEF executes a two-stage adversarial manipulation. First, a temporal vulnerability mask identifies time windows where small perturbations maximally influence both the classification decision and the explanation gradient. Second, a frequency perturbation filter applies additive edits in the spectral domain to these windows, ensuring the perturbations are coherent (e.g., preserving periodic structures) to remain stealthy. The attack is guided by a reference explanation (e.g., from a correctly classified example) and optimizes for two concurrent goals: targeted misclassification of the input and alignment of the resulting explanation with the reference rationale.

## Key Results
- TSEF achieves attack success rates (ASR) up to ~0.95 across multiple time series datasets and explainers.
- Explanation alignment, measured by AUPRC against a reference rationale, improves by 0.3–0.5 over existing adversarial attack baselines.
- The attack reveals that explanation stability is not a reliable indicator of decision robustness in time series classifiers.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
TSEF succeeds because it exploits the gradient-based nature of common explainers (e.g., Integrated Gradients) and the spectral characteristics of time series data. By operating in the frequency domain, perturbations can be made coherent—preserving natural signal structures—while still altering the model's decision boundary in targeted ways. The temporal vulnerability mask ensures edits are confined to maximally sensitive windows, minimizing overall distortion. This dual targeting forces the model to misclassify while the explainer, relying on similar gradient information, produces an attribution map that aligns with a benign reference, thus maintaining plausibility.

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map**: Temporal Vulnerability Mask -> Frequency Perturbation Filter -> Explanation Alignment Optimizer
- **Critical path**: Input time series → identify vulnerable windows via gradient analysis → transform to frequency domain → apply spectral edits → inverse transform → verify dual objectives (misclassification + explanation alignment).
- **Design tradeoffs**: Perturbation magnitude (stealth vs. effectiveness), computational overhead of frequency transforms, generalizability across model architectures, and dependency on white-box access.
- **Failure signatures**: Low attack success rate, poor explanation alignment (low AUPRC), high perturbation norms detectable by adversarial defenses, or inability to find vulnerable windows.

## Limitations
- Evaluation restricted to CNN/ResNet models and gradient-based explainers (TIMEX++, TIMEX, Integrated Gradients); other architectures (Transformers, RNNs) and explainers (LIME, SHAP) are not tested.
- Assumes white-box access with full gradient information; effectiveness in black-box or query-limited settings remains unknown.
- Alignment metric (AUPRC against a reference rationale) may not correlate with human judgment of explanation plausibility.
- Experiments limited to standard benchmarks; real-world noisy or high-dimensional time series (e.g., physiological signals) are not considered.
- No analysis of whether frequency-based perturbations evade detection by adversarial detection or robustness methods.

## Confidence
- **High attack success & explanation alignment**: High – Strong empirical evidence across multiple datasets and explainers.
- **Explanation stability ≠ robust decisions**: Medium – Supported by experiments but restricted to specific settings; needs broader validation.
- **Exposed critical vulnerability**: Medium – Convincing within scope, but real-world impact on robustness assessment pipelines remains to be tested.
