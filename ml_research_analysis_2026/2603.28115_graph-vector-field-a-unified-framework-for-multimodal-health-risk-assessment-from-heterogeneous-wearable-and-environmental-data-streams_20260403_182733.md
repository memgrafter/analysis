# Graph Vector Field: A Unified Framework for Multimodal Health Risk Assessment from Heterogeneous Wearable and Environmental Data Streams

## Quick Facts
*   **arXiv ID:** 2603.28115
*   **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.28115
*   **Reference Count:** 40
*   **Primary Result:** Proposes a geometric deep learning framework that models health risk as a vector field on time-varying simplicial complexes, using discrete exterior calculus and a bundle MoE to achieve mechanistic interpretability via Helmholtz-Hodge decomposition into directional, cyclic, and topologically-locked risk components.

## Executive Summary
Multimodal health risk assessment suffers from a lack of interpretability and structural fusion. The Graph Vector Field (GVF) framework addresses this by representing risk as a vector-valued field on a simplicial complex built from heterogeneous data streams (wearable, environmental, clinical). It employs discrete exterior calculus operators and a bundle-structured mixture-of-experts to learn the risk flow. A key innovation is the Helmholtz-Hodge decomposition, which separates the flow into interpretable components corresponding to distinct intervention strategies: gradient (sources/sinks), curl (cyclic dynamics), and harmonic (topology-locked). The paper provides theoretical guarantees (universal approximation, modality identifiability, stability) but has not yet been empirically validated on real-world data.

## Method Summary
GVF constructs a time-varying simplicial complex from synchronized multimodal streams using thresholds for proximity, synchronization, and dwell time, closing triangles only when all edges exist and involve multiple node types. Four modality-specific experts (e.g., simplicial GCNs) produce node-level risk potentials. An antisymmetric flow constructor combines node risks and edge features into an edge flow. The model is trained end-to-end with a classification/regression loss, a geometric regularizer to encourage non-zero curl, and an orthogonality penalty to enforce modality separation via a direct-sum bundle structure. At inference, the Helmholtz-Hodge decomposition is solved to extract components for deriving the Disease Progression Score (from gradient) and Cyclic Risk Index (from curl).

## Key Results
*   Introduces a vector-valued risk field on simplicial complexes, enabling a Helmholtz-Hodge decomposition that yields three theoretically grounded, interpretable risk components.
*   Provides formal guarantees: universal approximation for simplicial experts, modality identifiability under block-orthogonalization and dropout, and stability under simplicial perturbations.
*   Uniquely combines vector field outputs, curvature (curl) detection, topological risk locking, and inherent modality attribution within a single unified framework.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
**Mechanism 1** The gradient component (∇φ) identifies directional risk propagation sources and sinks. The discrete gradient operator converts node potentials into edge flows; non-zero divergence at nodes indicates net outflow/inflow, mapping to intervention targets (source reduction). This assumes dominant linear or tree-like influence pathways. *Break condition:* If cyclic or harmonic components dominate, gradient-based interventions may be misleading.

**Mechanism 2** The curl component (curlψ) detects self-sustaining, cyclic risk dynamics (e.g., sleep-stress-recovery loops). The discrete curl, computed via the edge-face incidence matrix, quantifies circulation around 2-simplices. Non-zero curl indicates closed-loop flow orthogonal to gradient flow, requiring cycle-breaking interventions. *Break condition:* Requires active 2-simplices (β₁>0); if the complex lacks triangles or the model collapses to exact flow, cyclic detection fails.

**Mechanism 3** The harmonic component (h) represents topology-locked risk that persists despite local interventions. Harmonic forms live in the kernel of the Hodge Laplacian, with dimension equal to the first Betti number β₁. Risk trapped here is insensitive to potential or solenoidal modifications, instead being entrained by global topological "holes" (e.g., HVAC loops). *Break condition:* If the complex is contractible (β₁=0) or the learned flow has no harmonic part, the topological signal is lost.

## Foundational Learning
*   **Simplicial Complexes & Discrete Exterior Calculus (DEC):** Provides the geometric substrate (nodes, edges, triangles) and differential operators (∇, div, curl). Needed to define the risk vector field and perform HHD. *Quick check:* For a triangle (A,B,C), write signed incidence matrices B₁, B₂ and compute curl for F(AB)=1, F(BC)=1, F(CA)=1.
*   **Helmholtz-Hodge Decomposition (HHD):** Orthogonally decomposes any edge flow into gradient (exact), curl (coexact), and harmonic components. Core interpretability mechanism. *Quick check:* On a square, what conditions on F make it (a) exact, (b) coexact, (c) harmonic?
*   **Bundle-Structured Mixture-of-Experts (MoE):** Enforces modality separation. Each expert writes exclusively to its dedicated fibre in a direct-sum bundle, enabling structural attribution and identifiability by construction. *Quick check:* If environmental expert outputs in ℝ² and physiological in ℝ³, what is the bundle dimension? How does gating respect this structure?
*   **Hodge Laplacian & Betti Numbers:** Δ = div∇ + curl*curl; its kernel dimensions (Betti numbers) count topological holes. β₁ counts independent 1-cycles, governing the harmonic subspace size. Needed to interpret topology-locked risk.

## Architecture Onboarding
Component map: Raw multimodal streams → Simplicial Constructor (Algorithm 1) → K(t) → Expert Networks (per modality) → rᵢ(t) → Flow Constructor (Ψω) → Fᵢⱼ(t) → HHD Engine → (∇φ, curlψ, h) → DPS/CRI. Supervision: L_cls on rᵢ, L_geo on F, L_orth on expert outputs.

Critical path: Data → Complex construction → Node risk potentials → Edge flow → Decomposition → Interpretable scores. Training losses act on intermediate representations.

Design tradeoffs:
*   Simplicial order: Including 2-simplices enables curl detection but increases cost/complexity. Omitting them reduces to a scalar GNN with no cyclic capacity.
*   Bundle vs standard MoE: Direct-sum bundle ensures attribution but requires block-orthogonalization; standard MoE is simpler but loses identifiability.
*   L_geo strength (λ₁): Higher λ₁ prevents collapse to exact flow but can over-penalize legitimate gradients.
*   Complex update frequency (Δt): Balances temporal resolution against topological stability.

Failure signatures:
*   Curl collapse: ρ = ∥curlKF∥²/(∥F∥²+ε) ≈ 0 → check λ₁, β₁(K(t)).
*   Modality leakage: High L_orth or off-fibre projections ≠ 0 → check input whitening (C1), architectural blocking.
*   Topological instability: Spectral shift between successive K(t) spikes → revisit thresholds τ.
*   Degenerate harmonic: h ≈ 0 despite β₁>0 → check solver convergence or flow exactness.

First 3 experiments:
1.  **Simplicial construction validation:** Apply Algorithm 1 to a real multimodal wearable cohort. Report |V|, |K₁|, |K₂|, β₁(t). Verify Δt=5min yields non-trivial, stable topology.
2.  **HHD component separation on synthetic data:** Generate a synthetic risk field with known exact (gradient), coexact (curl on triangle), and harmonic (cycle) components. Train GVF and compute component correlations with ground truth.
3.  **Modality identifiability under dropout:** Train with p_drop=0.2. For held-out agents with only Physio+Env, compare gating weights for missing modality to zero and off-fibre flow norm.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
*   **OQ1:** Does GVF improve prediction and interpretability on real multimodal wearable health data? Basis: Section 10.3 lists empirical validation as primary future work. Unresolved due to lack of experiments.
*   **OQ2:** What is the incremental value of modeling 2-simplices (higher-order interactions) beyond pairwise graphs? Basis: Section 10.3 calls for an ablation on simplicial complex order. Unresolved; current implementation relies on 1-skeleton.
*   **OQ3:** Can expert architectures be upgraded to full simplicial message-passing networks to meet the universal approximation guarantee? Basis: Section 10.3 notes the gap between UAT assumptions and the proposed Table 1 experts that operate mainly on 1-skeleton.

## Limitations
*   No empirical validation; unknown suitable datasets and missing implementation details (expert architectures, hyperparameters, threshold selection procedure).
*   Theoretical guarantees depend on strong practical conditions (block-orthogonalized inputs, expert specialization, presence of all modality subsets during training) that may be hard to satisfy.
*   HHD interpretability assumes clean separation into exact, coexact, harmonic components; real risk flows may be noisy or mixed.
*   Bundle-MoE modality attribution requires careful orthogonalization and dropout; otherwise leakage occurs.
*   Simplicial constructor thresholds are vague; poor choices yield β₁=0 or unstable topology.
*   Computational cost of solving HHD on large time-varying complexes is unaddressed.
