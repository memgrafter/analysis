# FP-THD: Full page transcription of historical documents
## Quick Facts
*   **arXiv ID:** 2601.17040
*   **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.17040
*   **Reference Count:** 40
*   **Primary Result:** A two-stage pipeline (layout analysis + OCR) achieves near-perfect transcription (CER 0.0178%, WER 0.0450%) on a new printed medieval Latin dataset and competitive results on handwritten benchmarks, preserving archaic characters without post-processing.

## Executive Summary
Transcribing 15th-16th century historical documents is challenging due to archaic characters, ligatures, and scribal abbreviations that standard OCR modernizes or loses. This paper introduces FP-THD, a pipeline that first uses a layout analysis model (ParseNet) to detect and extract normalized text lines from full-page images. A modified Masked Autoencoder with Vision Transformer (MAE-ViT), trained with span masking, then recognizes the original character forms. Evaluated on handwritten (Rodrigo, Bentham) and a new printed medieval Latin dataset (Molino), the system outperforms commercial baselines on Molino and demonstrates effective full-page transcription for both printed and handwritten texts, outputting results in structured, editable, and plain text formats.

## Method Summary
FP-THD decomposes full-page transcription into two sequential stages. First, a layout analysis model (ParseNet) processes a full-page image to detect text lines and regions, outputting their coordinates in XML PAGE format. Second, each detected line is cropped, geometrically rectified, and resized to a normalized height (50px). These normalized line images are fed to an extended MAE-ViT OCR model. This model uses a CNN (ResNet-18) for feature extraction, applies span masking (masking contiguous token groups up to length 8) as regularization, and employs a transformer encoder trained with CTC loss to predict character sequences. The OCR output is injected back into the PAGE XML, from which Markdown and TXT formats are derived. The pipeline is trained per dataset (Rodrigo, Bentham, Molino) and operates without any post-processing or language model.

## Key Results
*   On the new printed Molino dataset, FP-THD achieves 0.0178% CER and 0.0450% WER, significantly outperforming baselines Pero-OCR and ABBY.
*   On handwritten Rodrigo and Bentham benchmarks, it achieves competitive results (Rodrigo: CER 1.30%, WER 6.97%; Bentham: CER 4.46%, WER 7.68%).
*   The system qualitatively improves preservation of tildes and sensitive diacritics (e.g., distinguishing 's' from 'ſ') compared to systems that modernize output.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1
*   **Claim:** Decomposing full-page transcription into sequential layout analysis followed by line-level OCR improves accuracy on complex historical layouts.
*   **Mechanism:** ParseNet detects baselines and polygons for text lines, producing normalized line images with standardized height (50px). This geometric rectification mitigates variations in skew, curvature, and font size, providing consistent inputs to the OCR model and simplifying its sequential prediction task.
*   **Core assumption:** Isolating text lines is easier for the OCR model than processing an entire page at once.
*   **Evidence anchors:** [abstract] describes the two-stage approach; [section 3.1] details ParseNet's baseline and polygon detection.
*   **Break condition:** Failure by ParseNet to detect a text line (e.g., a marginal note) results in permanent loss of that content, as the pipeline has no recovery mechanism.

### Mechanism 2
*   **Claim:** Extending MAE-ViT with span masking and a CNN feature extractor effectively preserves archaic characters and ligatures by learning robust representations from limited training data.
*   **Mechanism:** A ResNet-18 extracts spatial features from a line image. Span masking replaces contiguous groups of tokens (up to 8) with learned mask tokens. The transformer encoder predicts missing features, trained with CTC loss. This forces the model to learn contextual relationships between glyph parts, crucial for recognizing unified ligatures (e.g., 'æ') or abbreviations.
*   **Core assumption:** Self-supervised masking and transformer-based sequential modeling can capture visual and positional nuances of historical glyphs without a language model.
*   **Evidence anchors:** [abstract] mentions preserving tildes/diacritics; [section 3.3] details span masking and CTC loss usage.
*   **Break condition:** Performance degrades on severely degraded text (ink stains, fading) where glyph features are obscured, or on single-word images due to training on long lines.

### Mechanism 3
*   **Claim:** Outputting structured (XML PAGE), editable (Markdown), and plain text (TXT) formats maximizes utility for downstream scholarly workflows.
*   **Mechanism:** PAGE XML binds text to coordinates for visualization/alignment. Markdown offers human-editable plain text with basic markup for collaborative tools. TXT provides a clean stream for NLP/indexing. The pipeline populates XML with OCR results, then derives the other formats.
*   **Core assumption:** Different analysis phases require different representations: computational tasks need structure (XML/TXT), human review benefits from simplicity (Markdown).
*   **Evidence anchors:** [abstract] lists the three output formats; [section 3.4] justifies Markdown over TEI for readability and publication ease.
*   **Break condition:** Corruption in XML (e.g., line ordering errors) propagates to all downstream formats. Markdown lacks semantic richness for complex scholarly tagging compared to TEI.

## Foundational Learning
*   **Concept: Document Layout Analysis**
    *   **Why needed here:** Historical pages have non-uniform layouts (multi-column, marginalia). Incorrect line detection dooms subsequent OCR. Understanding ParseNet's baseline/polygon output vs. simple bounding boxes is key.
    *   **Quick check question:** How does predicting both a text baseline *and* a polygon (for line extent) help handle curved or skewed text lines compared to a rectangular bounding box detector?

*   **Concept: Sequence-to-Sequence OCR with CTC Loss**
    *   **Why needed here:** The OCR model predicts a variable-length character sequence from a fixed-size line image. CTC learns alignments without pre-segmentation, essential for cursive/connected handwriting.
    *   **Quick check question:** Why is CTC loss necessary for the MAE-ViT OCR model instead of standard cross-entropy loss per token?

*   **Concept: Span Masking as Regularization**
    *   **Why needed here:** Historical datasets are small. Random patch masking (like original MAE) would break character shapes. Span masking removes contiguous regions, forcing reconstruction of coherent segments and learning longer dependencies.
    *   **Quick check question:** How does masking contiguous spans (up to 8 tokens) better respect the sequential nature of text than masking randomly distributed individual patches?

## Architecture Onboarding
*   **Component map:** Input -> ParseNet (Layout) -> Line Cropper -> MAE-ViT (OCR) -> Result Mux
*   **Critical path:** Page image -> ParseNet -> line crops -> MAE-ViT -> text strings -> XML injection -> Markdown/TXT. Failure at any stage stops the pipeline.
*   **Design tradeoffs:**
    *   *No post-processing / LM:* Simpler, faster, but cannot correct plausible-but-wrong sequences (e.g., 's' vs. 'ſ').
    *   *Fixed line height (50px):* Normalizes input but may distort extreme glyphs; requires careful resizing.
    *   *TEI rejected:* Semantic richness sacrificed for editor accessibility (Markdown).
*   **Failure signatures:**
    *   **Layout failure:** Missing `<TextLine>` elements in PAGE XML (check detection threshold).
    *   **OCR failure:** High CER/WER on validation; often due to unseen glyph variants or severe degradation.
    *   **Mapping failure:** Line image filenames don't match XML line IDs, causing empty text fields.
    *   **Formatting failure:** Markdown/TXT misorder lines (reading order error in region sorter).
*   **First 3 experiments:**
    1.  **Isolate Layout:** Run only ParseNet on a Molino page. Visually inspect overlaid predicted baselines/polygons. Are all main-column lines detected? Are marginalia lines missed?
    2.  **Single-component OCR:** Train MAE-ViT *only* on the Rodrigo/Bentham line images. Report CER/WER. Does it match Table 3/4? Validates the OCR model independently of layout.
    3.  **End-to-end vs. Oracle:** For 5 Molino test pages: (a) Run full pipeline. (b) "Oracle" experiment: feed *ground-truth* cropped line images (from GT baselines) to MAE-ViT. Compare CER. The gap isolates error from layout analysis.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
*   **Open Question 1:** How can the FP-THD pipeline be adapted to accurately transcribe text lines consisting of only a single word?
*   **Open Question 2:** How can large language models (LLMs) be effectively integrated into the FP-THD pipeline to automatically expand medieval Latin abbreviations without degrading the fidelity of the initial transcription?
*   **Open Question 3:** What automated methods can reduce the manual effort required to curate ground-truth transcriptions for historical datasets containing complex scribal abbreviations and archaic characters?
*   **Open Question 4:** How well does the FP-THD pipeline generalize to diverse historical document types (e.g., different scripts, languages, degradation levels) beyond the three datasets used in the paper?

## Limitations
*   Layout analysis failures cause permanent loss of undetected lines (e.g., marginalia, very short lines).
*   OCR performance degrades on severely degraded text and on single-word lines, as noted in the conclusions but not fully quantified.
*   Replication is hindered by unspecified implementation details of the extended MAE-ViT and ambiguity around the completeness of the manually corrected Molino dataset.

## Confidence
*   **Decomposition (ParseNet + OCR):** Medium. Full-page results are strong, but neighbors don't directly validate this specific pipeline; layout error contribution is not isolated in main metrics.
*   **Span-masking MAE-ViT:** Low-Medium. Claimed to preserve archaic glyphs, but no ablation shows its impact vs. alternatives; corpus lacks direct comparisons.
*   **Multi-format output utility:** Medium. Utility for workflows is asserted but not empirically validated with end-users; trade-offs with TEI remain theoretical.
