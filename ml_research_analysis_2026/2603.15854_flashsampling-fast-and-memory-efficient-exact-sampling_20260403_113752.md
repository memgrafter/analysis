# FlashSampling: Fast and Memory-Efficient Exact Sampling
## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2603.15854
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.15854
- Reference count: 40
- Primary result: FlashSampling speeds up decode kernels by up to 1.84× vs multinomial and 2.52× vs top‑k/p, and reduces time‑per‑output‑token by up to 19% in vLLM serving.

## Executive Summary
FlashSampling tackles the memory and kernel overhead of categorical sampling in large‑vocabulary LLM decoding. By fusing exact Gumbel‑Max sampling into the LM‑head matrix multiply, it computes logits tile‑by‑tile on‑chip, adds noise, keeps only the tile‑local maximizer per row, and performs a small reduction—never materializing the full `[B, V]` logits tensor in HBM. This turns a post‑processing step into a lightweight epilogue, eliminating separate sampling kernels and their HBM traffic.

On four NVIDIA GPU architectures, FlashSampling achieves up to 1.84× speedup versus a multinomial baseline and 2.52× versus top‑k/p in the memory‑bandwidth‑bound regime (small batch sizes). In end‑to‑end vLLM serving, it reduces time‑per‑output‑token by up to 19% on Qwen3‑1.7B, with consistent gains across tested models. The approach is exact (preserves the target categorical distribution) and extends to tensor‑parallel and streaming settings via hierarchical factorization.

## Method Summary
FlashSampling’s core idea is to fuse Gumbel‑Max sampling into the LM‑head matrix multiplication. Instead of computing the full `[B, V]` logits tensor in HBM, the kernel iterates over vocabulary tiles that fit in on‑chip memory. For each tile, it loads the weight tile, computes partial dot products with the hidden state, adds Gumbel noise drawn from a counter‑based RNG, and updates a per‑row record of the current maximum perturbed score and its vocabulary index. After all tiles are processed, a small reduction aggregates the per‑tile maximizers to produce the global argmax per row. The Gumbel‑Max theorem guarantees that `argmax_i (ℓ_i + g_i)` yields an exact sample from `softmax(ℓ)` without ever forming probabilities. The argmax decomposition `max_i x_i = max_t (max_{i∈T_t} x_i)` justifies the tiling.

For tensor‑parallel or online streaming regimes, FlashSampling uses hierarchical factorization (Group‑Gumbel‑Max). Each shard computes its log‑mass (logsumexp) and a local sample; an all‑gather of these scalar pairs allows a second Gumbel‑Max to select the winning shard, with the corresponding local sample mapped to the global token ID. This communicates only `O(n_ranks)` scalars instead of `O(V)` logits and remains exact.

## Key Results
- Kernel‑level speedups: up to **1.84×** vs multinomial and up to **2.52×** vs top‑k/p on small batch sizes (memory‑bandwidth‑bound) across H100, H200, B200, B300.
- End‑to‑end serving: reduces time‑per‑output‑token by up to **19%** on Qwen3‑1.7B in vLLM, with measurable gains on larger models.
- Exactness: Statistical tests (χ², KS) confirm samples are indistinguishable from reference Gumbel‑Max and multinomial baselines.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1 (Tile-wise argmax decomposition)
- Claim: FlashSampling avoids materializing the full `[B, V]` logits tensor by computing the global argmax as the reduction of tile‑local argmaxes over a partition of the vocabulary.
- Mechanism: The global argmax `max_i (ℓ_i + g_i)` equals `max_t (max_{i∈T_t} (ℓ_i + g_i))` for any partition `{T_t}` of the vocabulary indices. By computing logits and adding Gumbel noise tile‑by‑tile, storing only the tile‑local maximizer (score and index) per row, and then reducing across tiles, the algorithm eliminates the need to write full logits to HBM.
- Core assumption: The vocabulary can be partitioned into contiguous tiles that fit in on‑chip memory (SRAM/shared memory), and the argmax operation is fully decomposable over such a partition.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "computing logits tile‑by‑tile on chip, adding noise, keeping only the tile‑local maximizer per row, and performing a small reduction—never materializing the full logits tensor in HBM."
  - [section 4.5] Lemma 4.5 proves the max decomposition identity for any partition.
  - [corpus] The provided corpus lacks prior work that applies argmax decomposition to avoid logits materialization in LLM decoding; the closest related work (FlashInfer) still materializes logits before sampling.
- Break condition: If tile size exceeds on‑chip memory capacity, the computation spills to HBM, eroding the benefit. In extreme cases, a tile might contain all finite logits for a row, making the tile‑local reduction equivalent to full materialization.

### Mechanism 2 (Gumbel‑Max elimination of softmax)
- Claim: Exact categorical sampling from `softmax(ℓ)` can be performed without ever forming probabilities or computing the normalizing constant, by instead computing `argmax_i (ℓ_i + g_i)` with i.i.d. Gumbel noise.
- Mechanism: The Gumbel‑Max theorem states that adding i.i.d. `Gumbel(0,1)` variables to the logits and taking the argmax yields an exact sample from the softmax distribution. This bypasses the need for explicit softmax, normalization, or prefix sums, reducing sampling to a single reduction over perturbed logits.
- Core assumption: The transformed logits `ℓ` must have at least one finite entry; the Gumbel noise must be i.i.d. from the standard Gumbel distribution. Ties occur with probability zero due to continuity.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "exact sampling does not require forming probabilities at all."
  - [section 2.3] Theorem 2.1 states the Gumbel‑Max trick.
  - [section 3.1] Describes the online streaming algorithm that maintains only the current best perturbed score and index.
  - [corpus] The corpus includes work on exact discrete stochastic simulation (paper 2) but not the Gumbel‑Max trick specifically for LLM sampling; the trick is standard in machine learning but not explicitly covered in the provided abstracts.
- Break condition: Incorrect Gumbel generation (e.g., not i.i.d., wrong support) biases the distribution. All `-∞` logits (or ties) break the assumption of a well‑defined categorical distribution.

### Mechanism 3 (Hierarchical factorization for distributed/online settings)
- Claim: For tensor‑parallel or online streaming regimes, exact sampling can be achieved by hierarchically factorizing the categorical distribution over groups (e.g., vocabulary shards), communicating only `O(1)` scalars per group (log‑mass and local sample) instead of full logits.
- Mechanism: Partitioning the vocabulary into disjoint groups `G_k`, the target distribution factorizes: `P(K=k) ∝ exp(L_k)` where `L_k = logsumexp(ℓ_{G_k})`, and `P(I=i|K=k) ∝ exp(ℓ_i)` for `i∈G_k`. Exact sampling proceeds by first sampling a group `K` (using Gumbel‑Max on `{L_k}`), then sampling within the selected group. In the online variant, a running log‑mass and sample are updated with each new group via a binary merge rule (`p_replace ∝ exp(L_k)`).
- Core assumption: Groups are disjoint and cover the vocabulary; each group with nonzero mass is processed correctly. The binary merge rule (Lemma 4.3) preserves exactness when combining independent samples from two groups.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "grouped variants for online and tensor‑parallel settings are exact by hierarchical factorization of the categorical distribution."
  - [section 4.1] Introduces Group‑Gumbel‑Max and defines the factorization.
  - [section 4.4] Theorem 4.4 proves exactness; Lemma 4.3 proves the binary merge rule.
  - [section 4.2] Applies factorization to tensor‑parallel vocabularies.
  - [corpus] The corpus does not contain prior hierarchical factorization for LLM sampling; paper 2 (Exact Discrete Stochastic Simulation) uses different factorization for CTMCs.
- Break condition: Zero‑mass groups (all logits `-∞`) must be skipped; including them causes `logsumexp(-∞)=-∞` and zero probability, but if accidentally treated as finite, they may corrupt the group selection. In online mode, the first nonzero group must initialize the running state correctly.

## Architecture Onboarding
### Component map
- **Fused matmul‑sample kernel**: For each batch row and vocabulary tile, loads weight tile, computes partial dot products with hidden state, accumulates in registers, applies transforms (temperature/bias/mask), generates Gumbel noise, computes perturbed score, and reduces to tile‑local `(max_score, max_index)`.
- **Tile reduction kernel**: Takes per‑tile `(max_score, max_index)` for all rows, reduces across tiles (typically via a small GEMM or parallel reduction) to produce final sample indices.
- **Distributed coordinator**: In tensor‑parallel mode, each rank runs the fused kernel on its shard, computes local `log‑mass` and local sample, then all‑gathers these scalars; each rank samples a winning shard via Gumbel‑Max on gathered log‑masses and maps the local sample to global index.
- **RNG subsystem**: Counter‑based RNG (e.g., Philox) ensures deterministic, reproducible Gumbel noise per position.

### Critical path
1. Hidden state loaded from cache/HBM.
2. Weight matrix streamed tile‑by‑tile; for each tile, compute partial logits, add Gumbel noise, update tile‑local maximizer.
3. Write tile‑local maximizers to a small buffer in HBM or shared memory.
4. Reduce buffer across tiles to get global sample per row.
   *In distributed*: After step 3, exchange `(log‑mass, local_sample)` with other ranks (all‑gather), then perform group selection (Gumbel‑Max on log‑masses) and index mapping.

### Design tradeoffs
- Tile size: Larger tiles improve GEMM efficiency but require more on‑chip memory for accumulator and for storing tile‑local maximizers. Too small tiles increase kernel launch overhead.
- On‑chip memory vs. reduction strategy: If the number of tiles (`ceil(V / tile_vocab)`) is small enough, the reduction can be fused into the same kernel using shared memory/warp‑level primitives; otherwise, a separate small kernel is needed.
- Precision: FP32 accumulation and Gumbel generation are used for stability even with FP16/BF16 inputs, costing minor extra arithmetic vs. HBM traffic savings.
- Distributed communication: Communicating `(log‑mass, local_sample)` (two scalars per rank) instead of full logits reduces bandwidth from `O(V)` to `O(n_ranks)`, but adds a small all‑gather and an extra Gumbel sample for group selection.

### Failure signatures
- Statistical bias: Samples deviate from the target distribution (detected via chi‑squared test against reference). Causes: incorrect Gumbel generation, overflow/underflow in perturbed scores, or mishandling of zero‑mass groups.
- Incorrect argmax: Tile‑local maximizer not properly updated (e.g., accidentally keeping max of logits without Gumbel noise) leads to wrong samples.
- Distributed mismatch: A rank with nonzero mass omitted from group selection (e.g., due to `logsumexp` returning `-∞` for a group with some finite logits because of numerical issues) biases distribution toward other groups.
- Memory overflow: Tile size too large for shared memory/SRAM causes spills to L2/HBM, erasing performance gains.

### First 3 experiments
1. **Unit test: tile decomposition correctness** – For a random `[B, V]` logits matrix (with known global argmax), run the tile‑wise argmax reduction and verify that every row's final index matches the true global argmax.
2. **Statistical equivalence test** – Generate 10,000 samples from FlashSampling (fused) and from reference multinomial (softmax + `torch.multinomial`) using identical logits and Gumbel seeds. Run a chi‑squared goodness‑of‑fit test; expect p‑value > 0.05.
3. **Distributed exactness test** – Simulate 2‑way tensor parallelism by sharding a weight matrix. Compare the marginal distribution of samples from FlashSampling (distributed) with samples from the reference (all‑gather logits then sample) using a two‑sample Kolmogorov‑Smirnov test on the token frequencies.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
1. **Integration of top‑p and constrained decoding** – How can top‑p (nucleus) sampling and direct token masking be efficiently incorporated into FlashSampling while preserving exactness? The paper notes that top‑p does not naturally decompose and leaves the implementation to future work.
2. **Matching cuBLAS performance at large batch sizes** – Can the Triton kernel be optimized to achieve parity with cuBLAS in compute‑bound regimes (large batches), thereby extending speedup benefits across all practical batch sizes? Current implementation prioritizes portability over absolute peak performance.
3. **Portability beyond NVIDIA and vLLM** – Does FlashSampling yield similar benefits on non‑NVIDIA hardware (AMD, Intel GPUs) and within other LLM serving engines (TensorRT‑LLM, TGI)? The paper only evaluates on NVIDIA GPUs and in vLLM, so portability claims require empirical validation.

## Limitations
- Uncertainties in vLLM integration details (commit version, request‑rate settings) may affect reproducibility of end‑to‑end gains.
- The method is incompatible with nucleus (top‑p) sampling due to lack of decomposition, limiting applicability to decoding strategies that rely on top‑p.
- Tile size and on‑chip memory thresholds are hardware‑specific; on memory‑constrained devices the benefits may diminish or require careful manual tuning.
