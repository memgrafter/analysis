# Evaluating Interactive 2D Visualization as a Sample Selection Strategy for Biomedical Time-Series Data Annotation
## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2603.26592
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.26592
- Reference count: 40
- Primary result: Interactive 2D visualization (2DV) outperformed random and farthest-first sampling for aggregating multiple annotator labels and capturing rare classes in biomedical time-series annotation.

## Executive Summary
This study examined how different sample selection strategies affect the annotation of biomedical time-series data by human annotators. The authors compared random sampling (RND), farthest-first traversal (FAFT), and interactive 2D visualization (2DV) via the TSExplorer interface across four classification tasks in infant motility assessment and speech emotion recognition. Results showed that 2DV yielded better model performance when labels from multiple annotators were aggregated and it captured rare classes more effectively. However, 2DV also introduced higher label distribution variability, which made FAFT superior for individual annotators in infant motility tasks and increased failure risk. Random sampling remained the safest under uncertainty. Annotators reported that 2DV made the task more engaging and interesting. The findings highlight the potential of interactive visualization but also point to its nuanced trade-offs and the need for further research.

## Method Summary
The authors conducted a user study with human annotators to evaluate three sample selection methods on two biomedical time-series domains: infant motility assessment (IMA) and speech emotion recognition (SER). For each domain, two binary classification tasks were used, yielding four tasks total. Annotators labeled samples selected by RND, FAFT, or 2DV through the TSExplorer graphical user interface, which displayed a 2D projection of the time-series feature space. After labeling, models were trained on either individual annotator labels or aggregated labels (across annotators) and evaluated on a held-out test set. The study measured classification performance, label distribution variability, rare class coverage, and failure risk. Post-experiment interviews gathered qualitative feedback on annotator experience.

## Key Results
- For aggregated labels across annotators, 2DV yielded higher model performance and better rare class coverage than RND and FAFT.
- For models trained on single-annotator labels, FAFT outperformed 2DV in infant motility tasks (because 2DV introduced higher label variability), whereas 2DV was superior for expert annotators in speech emotion recognition.
- Random sampling was the safest under uncertainty, with 2DV showing higher risk of outlier label distributions; however, annotators reported greater engagement with the 2DV interface.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The paper suggests that interactive 2D visualization improves annotation by enabling annotators to explore the data distribution holistically. This leads to higher variability in the sets of samples chosen across annotators, which when aggregated yields comprehensive coverage of the feature space, including rare classes. The increased variability can be a double-edged sword: it benefits aggregation but harms individual annotator consistency, as seen in the IMA tasks where FAFT (which selects uniformly) yielded more homogeneous labels. The engaging nature of the interface may also sustain annotator focus, further enhancing label quality.

## Foundational Learning
The study rests on several key concepts:
- **Active Learning / Sample Selection:** The core of the work compares strategies (random, farthest-first, interactive) for choosing which samples to annotate. *Why needed:* To determine which method yields the most informative labels for model training. *Quick check:* How does farthest-first traversal aim to maximize coverage of the feature space?
- **Dimensionality Reduction:** 2DV relies on projecting high-dimensional time-series into a 2D space for visual exploration. *Why needed:* To make the data structure interpretable by human annotators. *Quick check:* What are common techniques (e.g., t-SNE, UMAP) for such projections?
- **Label Aggregation:** Combining multiple noisy annotations into a single target (e.g., majority vote, Dawid-Skene). *Why needed:* To obtain a consensus label that reflects the collective judgment and reduces individual bias. *Quick check:* What is the difference between majority voting and probabilistic aggregation?
- **Class Imbalance / Rare Classes:** The ability to identify and label minority classes is crucial in biomedical contexts. *Why needed:* Ensuring models can detect rare but critical conditions. *Quick check:* Why might random sampling fail to capture rare classes adequately?
- **Human Annotation and Cognitive Load:** The annotation process involves human annotators whose performance can be affected by task difficulty and interface design. *Why needed:* To understand how interactive tools influence annotator behavior and label quality. *Quick check:* What factors contribute to cognitive fatigue during repetitive labeling?

## Architecture Onboarding
The TSExplorer system for interactive annotation comprises:
- **Component map:**  
  Raw time-series data → Feature extraction → Dimensionality reduction (precomputed) → 2D scatter plot (frontend) ↔ Annotator interaction (point selection, label assignment) → Label storage.  
  Optional: Feedback loop to recompute projection with new labels (future extension).
- **Critical path:**  
  1. Preprocess data and compute fixed 2D embedding.  
  2. Load embedding into GUI; annotator selects points in the scatter plot.  
  3. Annotator assigns categorical label via interface controls.  
  4. Record label and time stamp; update visual highlighting of labeled points.  
  5. After annotation session, export labels for model training.
- **Design tradeoffs:**  
  - *Static vs. adaptive projections:* Precomputed embeddings are fast but may not reflect evolving data understanding; adaptive updates could incorporate early labels but risk introducing bias.  
  - *Categorical-only labeling:* Simplifies implementation but cannot capture intensity or uncertainty.  
  - *Exploration freedom:* Annotators can freely browse the space, but this may lead to uneven coverage if annotators have different strategies.
- **Failure signatures:**  
  - *Coverage gaps:* Rare or outlier regions remain unlabeled if annotators do not venture there.  
  - *High label variance:* Excessive disagreement among annotators, leading to unstable models.  
  - *Cognitive overload:* Complex interface might confuse annotators, reducing efficiency.  
  - *Projection artifacts:* Poor dimensionality reduction may misrepresent clusters, misleading annotators.
- **First three experiments:**  
  1. **Usability pilot:** Small-scale test with a few annotators to ensure the interface is intuitive and the 2D projection accurately reflects data structure.  
  2. **Comparative study:** Main experiment comparing RND, FAFT, and 2DV on annotation quality (model performance, rare class recall) and annotator experience.  
  3. **Qualitative assessment:** Post-study interviews or surveys to gather insights on engagement, perceived difficulty, and suggestions for improvement.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
### Open Question 1
- **Question:** How does the annotation budget (number of samples labeled) modulate the trade-off between label variability and rare class coverage for 2DV compared to RND and FAFT?
- **Basis in paper:** [explicit] "Future studies should thus primarily target higher annotation budgets across diverse annotation tasks to better determine the specific applications of 2DV‑based annotation."
- **Why unresolved:** The current experiments used a moderate fixed budget and did not systematically vary it; the point at which 2DV becomes advantageous or risky remains unknown.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Controlled studies that vary the number of annotations per annotor across a wide range, measuring label distribution consistency, rare class representation, and downstream model performance for each method.

### Open Question 2
- **Question:** How does an annotator’s prior machine learning experience influence their exploration behavior (e.g., use of multiple projections, coverage of feature space) and the resulting label distribution and model performance when using 2DV?
- **Basis in paper:** [explicit] "further research is needed to better analyze this phenomenon" regarding observed differences between annotators with and without ML experience.
- **Why unresolved:** The study was not designed to systematically assess ML experience; the observed differences are based on a small, unbalanced sample and their impact on classification quality is unclear.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** A dedicated experiment recruiting annotators with varying ML expertise, tracking their interaction patterns with the 2DV interface, and comparing label distributions and model training outcomes across groups.

### Open Question 3
- **Question:** How can TSExplorer be extended to support continuous‑valued labels (e.g., intensity ratings) and what impact would this have on its applicability to biomedical annotation tasks?
- **Basis in paper:** [explicit] "TSExplorer currently supports only categorical labels, whereas extending the framework to handle continuous‑valued labels would increase its applicability."
- **Why unresolved:** The current implementation is limited to categorical labels; it is unknown how continuous labels could be visualized, selected, and integrated into downstream models.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Development and user evaluation of a prototype that allows continuous labeling (e.g., via sliders or direct marking) and assessment of its usability and effect on annotation efficiency and label quality.

### Open Question 4
- **Question:** How should the 2D visualizations be updated as annotations accumulate to maintain a meaningful representation of the data space without introducing bias from early labeling decisions?
- **Basis in paper:** [explicit] "A further direction for potential future work is to iteratively update the 2DVs based on the accumulating annotations."
- **Why unresolved:** The study used static precomputed projections; the feasibility, timing, and potential benefits or drawbacks of adaptive updates have not been explored.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Implement a system that periodically recomputes embeddings using all available labels, compare annotation efficiency and model performance against static projections, and analyze whether adaptive updates reduce coverage gaps or introduce unwanted drift.

### Open Question 5
- **Question:** To what extent do motivational and cognitive benefits (e.g., increased engagement, reduced fatigue) associated with 2DV annotation influence long‑term annotator consistency and label quality?
- **Basis in paper:** [explicit] "these aspects should be examined more systematically in future work" regarding the observation that 2DV made the task more interesting and enjoyable and may mitigate cognitive fatigue.
- **Why unresolved:** The current evidence is qualitative (post‑experiment interviews); no quantitative measures of fatigue or sustained performance were collected.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Longitudinal studies that record annotator engagement, fatigue (e.g., via surveys or physiological sensors), and label consistency over multiple sessions, correlating these with objective quality metrics.

## Limitations
- **Generalizability:** Findings are based on only four classification tasks across two specific domains (IMA, SER). It is unclear if results hold for other biomedical time-series types (e.g., EEG, ECG) or annotation budgets.
- **Annotator Variability Mechanism:** The explanation that higher label distribution variability in 2DV leads to FAFT’s advantage in IMA is inferred but not directly tested; psychological factors remain speculative.
- **Risk Quantification:** The identified higher risk for 2DV lacks a formal probabilistic model or decision framework for when to avoid it.

## Confidence
- *2DV outperforms with aggregated labels & captures rare classes:* **Medium** (consistent across tasks but limited domain diversity).
- *FAFT excels in IMA due to 2DV label variability:* **Medium** (correlation observed, causation unproven).
- *RND is safest under uncertainty:* **Medium** (based on risk analysis, but "safest" context-dependent).
- *2DV increases engagement:* **Low** (subjective, self-reported data).

## Next Checks
1. **Replicate across expanded biomedical contexts:** Test 2DV vs. FAFT vs. RND on at least three new time-series annotation tasks (e.g., Parkinson’s gait cycles, cardiac arrhythmia segments) with varied class imbalances and ≥10 annotators per task to assess generalizability and annotator-population effects.
2. **Measure annotator cognitive processes:** During annotation, capture metrics like decision time, backtrack frequency, and confidence ratings for each method; conduct think-aloud protocols to identify *why* 2DV reduces (or increases) perceived variability and engagement.
3. **Stress-test 2DV’s failure modes:** Artificially inject annotator noise or simulate low-budget scenarios to quantify the breakpoint where 2DV’s performance degrades vs. RND/FAFT; develop a risk metric (e.g., label entropy per sample batch) to predict when 2DV should be avoided.
