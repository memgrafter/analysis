# Flow Matching with Uncertainty Quantification and Guidance

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2602.10326
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.10326
- Reference count: 40
- Primary result: UA-Flow augments flow matching with heteroscedastic velocity uncertainty, propagates it to per-sample reliability scores, and uses them for improved guidance, yielding stronger uncertainty-fidelity correlation and better generation quality on CIFAR-10 and ImageNet.

## Executive Summary
Flow matching (FM) generates samples by integrating a learned velocity field along an ODE trajectory. While effective, standard FM provides no reliability estimates for individual samples, limiting its use in safety-critical applications. This paper introduces Uncertainty-Aware Flow Matching (UA-Flow), which models the velocity field as a heteroscedastic Gaussian, predicting both a mean and an element-wise variance. The predicted uncertainty is propagated through the deterministic ODE dynamics using a linearized mean update and an Euler variance update that accounts for state-velocity covariance (estimated via Hutchinson’s method). This yields a per-sample reliability score (e.g., mean of the top-10% variance values). Two guidance mechanisms leverage this score: Uncertainty-aware Classifier Guidance (U-CG) steers the generation toward low-uncertainty regions by descending the gradient of an uncertainty-based pseudo-likelihood; Uncertainty-aware Classifier-Free Guidance (U-CFG) adaptively selects the CFG scale at each step to minimize the predicted velocity variance under linear extrapolation. Experiments on CIFAR-10 and ImageNet show that UA-Flow’s uncertainty correlates more strongly with sample fidelity than prior diffusion-based uncertainty baselines (AU, BayesDiff). Filtering high-uncertainty samples improves FID and precision. Both U-CG and U-CFG enhance generation quality: U-CG trades recall for higher precision, while U-CFG mitigates the typical recall degradation caused by large fixed CFG scales. UA-Flow incurs lower computational overhead than existing uncertainty methods and is modality-agnostic, promising for reliability-sensitive applications.

## Method Summary
UA-Flow extends a pretrained flow matching model (e.g., ADM for CIFAR-10, DiT-B/2 for ImageNet) by adding a small variance head that predicts element-wise log variances for the velocity field. Training uses a conditional uncertainty-aware flow matching loss (LCUFM) based on the β-negative log-likelihood (β=1.0), which jointly optimizes the mean and variance predictions. During sampling, the state mean follows the standard ODE (linearized velocity), while the state variance evolves via an Euler step that incorporates the velocity variance and an estimate of the state-velocity covariance. The covariance is approximated using a single Hutchinson probe (S=1) for efficiency. The final per-sample reliability score is computed as the mean of the top-10% highest variance elements in the final state variance. Two guidance schemes are integrated: U-CG adds a gradient term w·∇f(σ²) to the velocity, where f(σ²) = -mean(σ²)²; U-CFG computes both conditional and unconditional velocities and variances, then solves for an adaptive guidance scale λ* that minimizes the predicted velocity variance after extrapolating the conditional and unconditional velocities (clamped by a user-specified λ_max). The sampling ODE solver (Heun, 50 steps) alternates these updates, with velocity uncertainties recomputed every four steps to reduce overhead.

## Key Results
- **Uncertainty-fidelity correlation**: UA-Flow’s uncertainty scores correlate more strongly with sample fidelity than prior diffusion-based uncertainty methods (AU, BayesDiff). Filtering the top-k% highest-uncertainty samples yields better FID and precision on CIFAR-10 and ImageNet.
- **U-CG**: Steers generation toward low-uncertainty regions, increasing precision at the cost of recall; a precision-recall trade-off is observed across guidance strengths.
- **U-CFG**: Adaptively choosing the guidance scale mitigates the sharp drop in recall that typically occurs with large fixed CFG scales, while maintaining or improving FID.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
**Mechanism 1: Heteroscedastic velocity modeling**  
UA-Flow models the velocity field as a heteroscedastic Gaussian with learned mean and diagonal variance. Training minimizes a β-NLL loss, which jointly optimizes both predictions and naturally captures input-dependent noise. The core assumption is that the true velocity field possesses element-wise independent noise whose variance depends on the input state. If the predicted variances are miscalibrated, the subsequent reliability scores become unreliable.

**Mechanism 2: Uncertainty propagation via ODE dynamics**  
The propagated uncertainty is the state variance Var[x_t] that evolves alongside the mean trajectory. Under a linearization assumption, the mean follows dx̄/dt = ū(x̄) (Equation 4). The variance update (Equation 5) approximates Var[x_{t+Δt}] ≈ Var[x_t] + (σ(x̄)Δt)² + 2Δt·Cov(x_t, u(x_t)). The covariance is approximated via a first-order Taylor expansion and estimated using Hutchinson’s diagonal estimator with a single probe (Algorithm 1). Euler integration and the diagonal covariance assumption keep the computation tractable. The validity depends on local linearity and small step sizes; large steps or highly nonlinear flows may cause the approximation to break down, leading to under- or over-estimated variance.

**Mechanism 3: Guidance driven by reliability scores**  
Two guidance mechanisms use the propagated reliability:  
- *U-CG* adds a gradient step that decreases an uncertainty-based pseudo-likelihood (e.g., f(σ²) = -mean(σ²)²) to the velocity, pushing samples toward regions of lower predicted velocity variance.  
- *U-CFG* selects a guidance scale λ* at each step that minimizes the predicted velocity variance after extrapolating the conditional and unconditional velocities, assuming their uncertainties are strongly correlated. λ* is clamped by λ_max. If the assumption fails or λ_max is too high, the adaptive guidance may become ineffective or unstable.

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component map**  
Velocity backbone (pre-trained FM network, e.g., DiT/ADM) → predicts mean velocity ū_θ.  
Variance head (small MLP/conv) → predicts element-wise logσ² (per timestep).  
Training: LCUFM loss (β‑NLL) with reweighted mini‑batch estimator.  
Sampling: ODE solver (Heun, 50 steps, EDM/uniform schedule) → integrates mean via dx̄/dt = ū_θ; uncertainty propagation module updates Var[x_t] using Eq. (5) with covariance from Hutchinson (S=1); guidance modules (U‑CFG, U‑CG) modify velocity; final reliability score computed from Var[x₁].  
Uncertainty update frequency: every 4 steps.

**Critical path**  
1. Obtain a pretrained flow matching model (conditional).  
2. Add a variance head and fine‑tune using the β‑NLL loss for ~100 epochs with lower learning rates for the backbone.  
3. During sampling, at each step:  
   a. Evaluate conditional (and unconditional if using CFG) mean and variance.  
   b. Propagate state mean and variance (Equations 4 and 5).  
   c. If U‑CFG, compute adaptive λ* and extrapolate mean/variance.  
   d. If U‑CG, add gradient correction to the mean.  
   e. Perform ODE step.  
4. After integration, output the final sample and its reliability score (mean of top‑10% variance values).

**Design tradeoffs**  
- Covariance estimation: diagonal assumption vs. full covariance; Hutchinson with S=1 trades accuracy for speed.  
- Variance integration: Euler vs. higher‑order solvers; Euler is simple but may accumulate error over many steps.  
- Uncertainty update frequency: updating every step is more accurate but costlier; every 4 steps balances overhead.  
- Guidance frequency: applying U‑CG every two steps vs. every step; trade‑off between quality and compute.  
- β‑NLL weighting: β=1.0 used; lowering β may improve uncertainty calibration at the cost of mean accuracy.

**Failure signatures**  
- Poor uncertainty–fidelity correlation: filtering does not improve FID; inspect uncertainty maps—they may be noisy or uninformative.  
- Numerical instability: predicted variances explode; may need clipping or regularization.  
- U‑CFG assumption violation: conditional and unconditional uncertainties not correlated, making λ* ineffective; monitor λ* distribution.  
- Memory/time pressure: Jacobian‑vector products increase memory use; reducing S or using mixed precision helps but may reduce accuracy.  
- Over‑guidance: too large U‑CG scale w or λ_max in U‑CFG oversimplifies samples and reduces diversity.

**First 3 experiments**  
1. **Uncertainty filtering evaluation**: Generate 100k samples, compute per-sample uncertainty (mean of top‑10% final variance), filter top‑k% (k up to 50), evaluate FID/precision/recall against baselines (AU, BayesDiff, GenUnc).  
2. **U‑CG sweep**: For fixed CFG scales λ ∈ {0, 0.25, 0.5}, sweep U‑CG scale w ∈ [0,50]; record FID, precision, recall to observe trade‑off.  
3. **U‑CFG sweep**: Compare fixed CFG (sweep λ) against U‑CFG (sweep λ_max); measure metrics and visualize λ* distribution across timesteps to confirm adaptation.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
1. **Generalization to other modalities and safety‑critical applications**: The paper claims modality‑agnosticism but only validates on image datasets. How well do the uncertainty estimates and guidance transfer to audio, video, 3D, or real‑world control tasks?  
2. **Why prior element‑wise uncertainty methods fail under flow matching**: AU and BayesDiff show poor fidelity correlation and noisy uncertainty maps in this context; the underlying causes remain unexplained.  
3. **Accuracy of the proposed uncertainty propagation**: The Euler+linear approximation may accumulate errors; how does it compare to exact (but intractable) probability dynamics?  
4. **Optimality and choice of λ_max in U‑CFG**: The adaptive λ* is derived to minimize predicted variance; does this truly minimize sample fidelity? Could learned schedules improve performance?

## Limitations
- **Uncertainty calibration**: No direct validation that the predicted variances match empirical errors; mis‑calibration could invalidate reliability scores.  
- **Propagation approximations**: Euler integration and diagonal covariance assumption may break down for highly nonlinear flows or large step sizes, leading to biased reliability estimates.  
- **Guidance assumptions**: U‑CFG relies on a strong correlation between conditional and unconditional uncertainties, which might not hold universally; U‑CG may push toward oversimplified outputs.

## Confidence
- Heteroscedastic velocity modeling (Mechanism 1): **Medium** – β‑NLL is standard, but alternative UQ heads are unexplored.  
- Uncertainty propagation (Mechanism 2): **Low** – theoretical justification based on local linearity; empirical validation only via fidelity correlation, no ground‑truth uncertainty.  
- Guidance mechanisms (Mechanism 3): **Medium** for U‑CG (precision gains shown); **Lower** for U‑CFG (adaptive λ heuristic not thoroughly analyzed).  
- Overall claim (uncertainty ↔ fidelity): **Medium‑High** – strong filtering results on two image datasets, but no out‑of‑distribution or failure‑case analysis.
