# Don't Ignore the Tail: Decoupling top-K Probabilities for Efficient Language Model Distillation

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2602.20816
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.20816
- Reference count: 40
- Primary result: Tail-Aware Distillation (TAD) improves language model distillation by explicitly modeling the tail of the teacher's probability distribution, yielding +2.2% average accuracy gain on a Qwen 1.2B student over vanilla KD.

## Executive Summary
Standard knowledge distillation for language models relies on KL divergence, which overly focuses on the teacher's top-predicted tokens and neglects the often informative lower-probability tail. This paper introduces Tail-Aware Distillation (TAD), which mathematically decouples the KL loss into two terms: one for the top‑K probabilities and another for the normalized tail. The tail term is amplified by a factor β that is normalized across the sequence to ensure training stability. Across pretraining and supervised distillation tasks spanning model sizes from 0.5 B to 9 B parameters, TAD consistently outperforms strong baselines such as vanilla KD and MiniPLM, achieves similar computational cost to vanilla KD, yields better‑calibrated models, and operates without extra teacher generation.

## Method Summary
TAD modifies the usual KL divergence objective by splitting the teacher’s probability distribution for each token into two disjoint sets: the top‑K highest‑probability tokens and the remaining tail. The KL divergence is rewritten as D_KL = D_KL1 + α_T^K * D_KL2, where α_T^K is the tail mass (1 minus the sum of top‑K probabilities). D_KL1 covers the top‑K tokens and includes a composite residual term, while D_KL2 is the KL divergence between the normalized tail distributions of teacher and student. A scalar amplification factor β is applied to the tail contribution but is normalized by the mean tail mass across the sequence to prevent instability. The final loss combines this decoupled divergence with the student’s standard causal language modeling loss. The method adds minimal overhead (finding top‑K) and does not require the teacher to generate additional data.

## Key Results
- TAD yields consistent average accuracy improvements across diverse model sizes and tasks, e.g., +2.2% relative gain for Qwen 1.2B student over vanilla KD.
- The approach maintains similar FLOPs to vanilla KD and does not require extra teacher‑generated data.
- Distilled models exhibit better calibration, as evidenced by lower Full‑ECE values.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1: Decoupling the KL divergence
TAD mathematically decouples D_KL(p_T||p_S) into two components: one covering the teacher’s top‑K tokens (including a composite residual) and one covering the normalized tail. Explicitly, D_KL = D_KL1 + α_T^K * D_KL2, with α_T^K the tail mass. This separation allows independent scaling of the tail term without distorting the teacher’s distribution shape.

### Mechanism 2: Sequence‑level normalization of amplification factor
To avoid instability caused by varying tail masses across sequences, TAD normalizes the amplification factor β by the mean tail mass of the sequence: β_seq = β / ( (1/N) Σ_t α_T^K(t) ). This ensures consistent gradient magnitudes for nominal β values (e.g., 1–2) regardless of the sequence’s average tail mass.

### Mechanism 3: Gradient amplification for tail tokens
The gradient of the TAD loss with respect to a tail logit contains an extra term proportional to (β_seq−1) · [ p_S · (1−Σ_top‑K p_T)/(1−Σ_top‑K p_S) − p_T ]. When the student’s top‑K mass is high, this term increases the gradient magnitude for tail tokens, actively encouraging the student to allocate probability to the tail. Standard KL provides negligible tail gradients because p_T is tiny.

## Architecture Onboarding
### Component map
Teacher model → Student model → TAD loss module → Optimizer

### Critical path
1. For each token in a batch, compute teacher and student probabilities via softmax.
2. Identify the top‑K teacher probabilities and their indices; compute tail mass α_T^K = 1 − Σ(top‑K probabilities).
3. Compute D_KL1: KL for the top‑K tokens plus a composite residual term.
4. Normalize the teacher’s tail probabilities by dividing by α_T^K to form a proper distribution.
5. Compute D_KL2: KL between the normalized tail distributions of teacher and student.
6. Compute sequence‑level scaling factor: β_seq = β / ( (1/N) Σ_t α_T^K(t) ).
7. Combine losses: L_DIV = D_KL1 + β_seq * α_T^K * D_KL2.
8. Add the student’s causal language modeling loss; backpropagate.

### Design tradeoffs
- K: Smaller K focuses on very top tokens’ immediate tail; larger K includes more tail but may admit noise. Empirically optimal K ∈ {5, 10}.
- β: Higher β amplifies tail more aggressively; sequence normalization allows robust β ∈ [1, 2].
- Compute: Finding top‑K is cheap relative to softmax; FLOPs similar to vanilla KD.

### Failure signatures
- No improvement over vanilla KD on validation tasks.
- Full‑ECE (calibration error) increases rather than decreases.
- Training loss diverges if β is too high without normalization.
- Student’s top‑K probability mass fails to decrease (tail does not rise).
