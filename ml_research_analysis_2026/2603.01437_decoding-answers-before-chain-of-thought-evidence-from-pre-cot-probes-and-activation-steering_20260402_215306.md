# Decoding Answers Before Chain-of-Thought: Evidence from Pre-CoT Probes and Activation Steering

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2603.01437
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.01437
- Reference count: 34
- Primary result: Linear probes on pre-CoT activations predict final answers with >0.9 AUC; steering along probe directions flips answers in >50% of cases.

## Executive Summary
This paper investigates whether large language models (LLMs) commit to answers before generating chain-of-thought (CoT) reasoning. Using linear probes on activations at the first token of CoT generation, the authors find that the final answer is highly predictable (AUC >0.9 on most tasks). Activation steering along the probe direction flips the model's answer in over half of cases, far exceeding orthogonal baselines. When steering produces incorrect answers, the model exhibits two failure modes: confabulation (fabricating false premises) and non-entailment (stating true premises but drawing unsupported conclusions). These results suggest that LLMs often determine answers early in the forward pass, and that this early representation is causally involved in answer selection.

## Method Summary
The study employs two core techniques: linear probing and activation steering. Linear probes are trained to predict the final answer from the model's internal activations at the very start of CoT generation (i.e., pre-CoT). High probe AUC indicates that answer information is linearly separable in these early representations. For activation steering, the authors add a scaled version of the learned probe weight vector to activations at a chosen layer. This rotates the representation along the answer-related direction, biasing the model toward the opposite answer. Flip rates (the proportion of answers that change) are measured and compared against control conditions like random or orthogonal steering. When steering causes errors, the authors manually classify the resulting CoT outputs into confabulation or non-entailment.

## Key Results
- Pre-CoT activations encode the final answer with high linear separability (AUC >0.9 on most reported tasks).
- Steering activations along the probe direction flips the model's answer in >50% of cases, significantly above orthogonal and random baselines.
- When steering induces incorrect answers, the model typically either confabulates (invents false premises) or exhibits non-entailment (states true premises but draws an unsupported conclusion).

## Why This Works
The high AUC of linear probes implies that the model's internal representation of the answer exists in a linearly decodable subspace before any CoT is generated. This suggests the model "commits" to an answer early, likely during initial question processing. The steering results demonstrate causal involvement: adding the probe direction biases the representation toward the opposite answer, causing the output to flip. The emergence of failure modes (confabulation and non-entailment) when steering forces incorrect answers indicates that the model's CoT generation adapts to justify the new answer, but sometimes fails to maintain logical consistency or factual accuracy.

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component map**: Input tokens -> Token embeddings -> Transformer layers (attention + MLP) -> Output logits. The experiment extracts activations from a specific layer (often early-mid) at the first generated token after the question and prompt (pre-CoT). A linear probe is trained on these activations to predict the final answer. Steering modifies those activations by adding α·(probe direction).

**Critical path**: The model processes the input and initial prompt, then begins CoT generation. The pre-CoT activation at the first generated token is taken. The final answer token is produced later, but the probe suggests the answer is already encoded in the pre-CoT state.

**Design tradeoffs**:
- Probe linearity: Linear probes are simpler and less prone to overfitting on small probe datasets, but may miss nonlinear answer encodings.
- Steering strength (α): Too small yields no flip; too large may degrade output coherence or cause drastic changes.
- Target layer: Early layers might lack abstract answer information; later layers may already be too committed to flip.
- Task specificity: Answer encodability and steering efficacy likely vary across reasoning benchmarks.

**Failure signatures**:
- Confabulation: The model's CoT includes statements contradicted by the question or common knowledge.
- Non-entailment: The model's reasoning steps are individually plausible but the conclusion does not follow logically from them.

**First experiments**:
1. Reproduce probe AUC: Train linear probes on pre-CoT activations for a selected task and measure AUC against the final answer.
2. Reproduce steering flip rate: Apply steering using the learned probe direction and measure the percentage of answers that flip, comparing to random and orthogonal baseline directions.
3. Failure mode analysis: Manually inspect a sample of steering-induced incorrect answers to classify them as confabulation or non-entailment and compute proportions.

## Limitations
- Task/model generalizability: Findings may be confined to specific reasoning benchmarks (e.g., math, commonsense) and model families; unclear if they hold for open-ended or domain-specific tasks.
- Probe linearity assumption: Linear probes may inadequately capture nonlinear answer representations in later layers or larger models, potentially overestimating early answer encoding.
- Steering context dependence: Flip rates likely vary with prompt phrasing, steering magnitude, and target layer; reported >50% may not generalize without careful tuning.

## Confidence
- Pre-CoT activations predict final answer (>0.9 AUC): High (robust empirical result across most reported tasks).
- Models determine answers before CoT: Medium (probe correlation implies early encoding but does not prove temporal precedence or causality).
- Steering flips answers >50%: Medium (result contingent on specific steering setup; baseline comparisons may not control for all confounders).
- Identified failure modes: Medium (based on illustrative examples; needs systematic quantification across diverse cases).

## Next Checks
1. Cross-dataset replication: Apply identical probing and steering pipelines to at least 3 new task categories (e.g., logical deduction, code generation) across 2 model architectures (e.g., Llama, Claude), measuring AUC and flip-rate variance.
2. Nonlinear probe robustness: Replace linear probes with 2-layer MLP probes per layer; compare performance to linear baselines to test if answer information is truly linearly separable or an artifact of probe simplicity.
3. Steering parameter sweep: Systematically vary steering strength (α ∈ [0.1, 2.0]) and layers (early/mid/late) while measuring (a) flip rate, (b) post-steering answer correctness, and (c) output coherence; report Pareto frontiers to characterize trade-offs.
