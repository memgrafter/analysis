# A Behavioural and Representational Evaluation of Goal-Directedness in Language Model Agents

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2602.08964  
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.08964  
- **Reference count:** 40  
- **Primary result:** An LLM agent navigating grid worlds exhibits goal-directed behavior tied to internal spatial representations, but is biased by irrelevant goal-like artifacts and shows representational shifts between pre- and post-reasoning phases.

## Executive Summary
This paper evaluates whether a language model agent acts goal-directedly by combining behavioral evaluation with representation probing in grid-world navigation tasks. Behaviorally, the agent’s accuracy declines with larger grids and more obstacles but remains robust to difficulty-preserving transformations, indicating some planning capability. However, it is systematically biased by irrelevant objects associated with goals (e.g., keys). Via linear probing, the agent non-linearly encodes a coarse cognitive map of its position and goal location, with pre-reasoning representations better predicting multi-step plans and post-reasoning representations better supporting immediate next actions. The authors conclude the agent pursues goals based on internal representations rather than simple stimulus-response, though biases and uncertainties in the cognitive map limit reliability.

## Method Summary
The study uses a GPT-OSS-20B model agent interacting with variants of the MiniGrid environment. Behavioral tests measure action accuracy across grid sizes, obstacle densities, and transformations that preserve task difficulty. Representation analyses apply linear probes to the model’s residual stream activations at two points: before and after a “reasoning” prompt. Probes decode spatial variables (agent position, goal location) and planned action sequences. Performance is compared between pre- and post-reasoning states and correlated with behavioral outcomes.

## Key Results
- Behavioral accuracy decreases with grid size and obstacle density but is invariant to difficulty-preserving transformations, suggesting planning beyond simple pattern matching.
- The agent is biased by irrelevant goal-like artifacts (e.g., keys), leading to suboptimal actions even when the artifact is non-functional.
- Linear probes recover coarse cognitive maps (agent and goal positions) with ~75% accuracy pre-reasoning and ~60% post-reasoning; decoded maps show blurred spatial certainty.
- Pre-reasoning activations better decode full multi-step plans, while post-reasoning activations better predict the immediate next action, indicating a representational shift during reasoning.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The paper does not provide an explicit mechanistic account. It hypothesizes that the agent builds an internal spatial representation (a cognitive map) that guides planning, and that the reasoning prompt triggers a reorganization of this representation from broad spatial cues to concrete action information. However, causal evidence is lacking; results are correlational.

## Foundational Learning
- **Cognitive maps:** Internal representations of spatial relationships; needed to explain how the agent encodes position and goal; quick check: probe for spatial variables.
- **Goal-directedness:** Behavior aimed at achieving a hidden objective; needed to distinguish planning from reactive behavior; quick check: test robustness to transformations.
- **Representation probing:** Using external classifiers to read model internal states; needed to infer encoded information; quick check: train linear probes on held-out activation/label pairs.
- **Linear vs. nonlinear encoding:** Probes assume linear decodability; needed to assess simplicity of internal representations; quick check: compare linear probe accuracy to a nonlinear baseline.
- **Pre-/post-reasoning comparison:** Isolating reasoning effects; needed to study planning dynamics; quick check: align activations at corresponding token positions.

## Architecture Onboarding
Component map: GPT-OSS-20B -> MiniGrid environment -> Behavioral logits / Residual activations -> Linear probes -> Decoded spatial/action outputs  
Critical path: Agent observes grid -> generates reasoning text -> produces action token; probes intervene on activations after observation and after reasoning.  
Design tradeoffs: MiniGrid simplifies perception (textual grid) and action (discrete moves) at the cost of ecological validity; probing uses linear models for interpretability but may undercapture nonlinearities.  
Failure signatures: (1) Performance drop with grid size/obstacles; (2) Bias toward goal-associated artifacts; (3) Blurred decoded cognitive maps.  
First experiments: (1) Replicate behavioral accuracy curves on unseen grid configurations; (2) Probe for artifact representations and correlate with susceptible trials; (3) Replace reasoning prompt with neutral text to test dependency on explicit reasoning.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
### Open Question 1
- Question: What causes the LLM agent's systematic bias toward irrelevant goal-like artifacts (e.g., keys), and can this bias be mitigated through architectural changes, training data, or prompting?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper explicitly states the agent is biased by goal-like artifacts in KeyNoDoorEnv and 2PathKeyEnv (Section 4.3), conjecturing it could be due to "semantic associations between entities and goals that the LLM acquired during training" that are not suppressed.
- Why unresolved: The hypothesis is speculative; the paper does not test *why* the bias occurs or explore interventions.
- What evidence would resolve it: Ablation studies across model sizes, training datasets, or prompt designs; causal analysis of internal activations linked to artifact representations.

### Open Question 2
- How do internal representations and planning dynamics change when moving from fully observable to partially observable grid worlds, and can current LLM agents maintain goal-directedness under memory and belief-updating constraints?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper explicitly abandoned a partially observable study because even GPT-5.1 showed "pathological reasoning" and "tunnel vision" (Appendix A), noting the difficulty in decoupling capability from goal-directedness.
- Why unresolved: The issue is raised as a critical limitation but not investigated; the paper only presents results for fully observable settings.
- What evidence would resolve it: Developing robust partially observable tasks; measuring decoded beliefs and policy consistency across hidden state histories; comparing model architectures with explicit memory modules.

### Open Question 3
- Does the reasoning process induce a causal reorganization of internal representations (from broad spatial cues to immediate action information), or is the observed shift merely correlational?
- Basis in paper: [inferred] The paper finds pre-reasoning activations better decode longer-horizon plans while post-reasoning activations better support next actions (Section 5.3), interpreting this as a "shift in representational emphasis." However, causal mechanisms are not tested.
- Why unresolved: Probing shows correlation, not causation; interventions (e.g., steering representations) are needed to confirm if reasoning *causes* the reorganization.
- What evidence would resolve it: Causal mediation analysis; manipulating pre-reasoning representations and measuring post-reasoning changes; training ablations that remove reasoning steps.

### Open Question 4
- To what extent do the findings generalize beyond GPT-OSS-20B and the specific MiniGrid navigation task to other LLM architectures, scales, and agentic domains (e.g., multi-step tool use, language-based decision making)?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The conclusion explicitly states the need to extend the framework to "a broader range of architectures, scales, and training regimes" and more complex environments (Section 6).
- Why unresolved: The case study is narrow; generalizability is an open empirical question.
- What evidence would resolve it: Replicating the behavioural and probing pipeline on multiple models (e.g., Llama, Claude) across diverse goal-directed tasks, checking for consistent patterns in representation shifts and robustness.

### Open Question 5
- How can we formally characterize the *uncertainty* in the agent's decoded cognitive maps (e.g., blurred agent/goal positions), and does this fuzzy representation explain deviations from optimal behaviour more reliably than alternative hypotheses (e.g., exploration, stochasticity)?
- Basis in paper: [inferred] The paper notes the decoded cognitive maps show "blurred agent and goal spatial representations" (Section 5.2) and uses a single-location selection, which may miss representational uncertainty. The recovery metric (Rec.) suggests some failures are due to faulty world models.
- Why unresolved: The link between fuzzy representations and suboptimal actions is suggestive but not quantified; uncertainty in the probe itself vs. the model's internal uncertainty needs disentanglement.
- What evidence would resolve it: Probing with uncertainty-aware methods (e.g., Bayesian probes); correlating per-step map uncertainty with action optimality; comparing against behavioural metrics like policy entropy.

## Limitations
- Grid-world environments are highly simplified; scalability to realistic, high-dimensional tasks is unknown.
- Probing results lack baselines (e.g., untrained model, shuffled labels), making it hard to judge whether observed accuracies reflect meaningful encoding.
- Bias by irrelevant goal-like artifacts may produce behavior that superficially resembles goal-directedness without genuine planning.
- Linear probes may not fully capture nonlinear representational structure; causal roles of decoded features remain unverified.

## Confidence
- Behavioral patterns (accuracy decline with size/obstacles, robustness to transformations): **High**
- Existence of a coarse cognitive map for position and goal: **Medium**
- Pre/post reasoning representational differences in plan decodability: **Medium**
- Overall conclusion that the agent pursues goals based on internal representations: **Low**

## Next Checks
1. Test transfer to continuous or 3D environments and substantially larger state spaces to evaluate scalability beyond grid worlds.
2. Introduce controlled ablations where irrelevant goal artifacts are removed or replaced to disentangle artifact bias from true goal-directed behavior.
3. Perform causal interventions (e.g., activation patching) on the putative cognitive map representations to test whether disruption impairs planning performance.
