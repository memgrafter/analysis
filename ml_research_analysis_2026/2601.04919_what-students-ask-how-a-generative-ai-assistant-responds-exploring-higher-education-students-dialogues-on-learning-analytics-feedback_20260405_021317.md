# What Students Ask, How a Generative AI Assistant Responds: Exploring Higher Education Students' Dialogues on Learning Analytics Feedback

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2601.04919
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.04919
- **Reference Count:** 0
- **Primary Result:** A GenAI assistant integrated into a Learning Analytics Dashboard provides real-time, dialogue-based scaffolding that particularly benefits students with lower self-regulated learning (SRL) competence by helping them interpret feedback, though its efficacy varies with query complexity and lacks conversation memory.

## Executive Summary
This study investigates how higher education students with varying levels of self-regulated learning (SRL) competence interact with a Generative AI (GenAI) assistant embedded within a Learning Analytics Dashboard (LAD). Over a 10-week semester, authentic dialogues from 22 students were analyzed to compare query patterns and assess response quality. Findings reveal a stark divergence: students with lower SRL primarily sought clarification (17.7%) and emotional reassurance (5.9%), while those with higher SRL asked about technical aspects (8.6%) and requested personalized strategic insights (34.5%). The assistant's responses were rated highly for clarity and reliability but fell short on personalization and handling complex or emotionally charged queries. The work concludes that GenAI scaffolding can narrow engagement gaps for low-SRL learners but requires enhanced adaptivity, context-awareness, and emotional responsiveness to reach its full potential.

## Method Summary
The study deployed an LAD with an integrated GenAI assistant (OpenAI GPT-4) in a postgraduate course. Twenty-two students interacted with the system over 10 weeks, generating 168 chat logs. Students' SRL competence was measured via a pre-semester questionnaire and clustered into high/low groups. Researchers conducted a thematic analysis of all queries, identifying seven distinct themes. They applied Epistemic Network Analysis (ENA) to compare discourse network structures between SRL groups. Each GenAI response was manually rated on eight quality criteria (e.g., clarity, personalization) using a 5-point Likert scale. Student perceptions were gathered through post-semester reflection texts. The analysis linked SRL competence to help-seeking behavior and evaluated the assistant's performance across query types.

## Key Results
- Low-SRL students' queries were dominated by **Clarification (17.7%)** and **Emotional Expression (5.9%)**, while high-SRL students predominantly asked for **Personalised Insight (34.5%)**.
- GenAI responses scored highest on **Clarity (M=4.63/5)** and **Reliability (M=4.12/5)** but were notably weaker on **Personalisation (M=3.56)** and **Error Handling (M=3.29)**, especially for technical and strategic queries.
- Students valued the data-driven, motivational support but criticized generic outputs, slow response times, and the assistant's inability to recall prior conversation context.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1: Real-Time Scaffolding for Interpretation
- **Claim:** GenAI integration into LADs provides real-time, dialogue-based scaffolding that particularly benefits students with lower SRL competence by reducing the cognitive load of interpreting analytics feedback.
- **Mechanism:** The assistant acts as an on-demand interpreter, converting complex visualizations and metrics into accessible language, thereby lowering the threshold for initial sense-making. This allows low-SRL students to move past confusion and engage with the feedback's implications.
- **Core Assumption:** Lower-SRL students' primary barrier is the initial interpretation of raw data, not a lack of desire to improve. Conversational support bridges this gap more effectively than static dashboards.
- **Evidence Anchors:** [abstract] "GenAI scaffolding notably benefits low SRL students, helping narrow engagement gaps." [section 3.1] Low-SRL students' queries were dominated by "Clarification (17.7%)" and "Emotional Expression (5.9%)."
- **Break Condition:** If the assistant's responses are generic or fail to connect data to concrete examples, the scaffolding collapses, leaving low-SRL students with the same interpretive burden as the original dashboard.

### Mechanism 2: SRL Competence Predicts Help-Seeking Framing
- **Claim:** Students' SRL competence fundamentally shapes the type of help-seeking behavior exhibited with the GenAI assistant, reflecting deeper cognitive and metacognitive strategies.
- **Mechanism:** High-SRL students, possessing stronger goal-setting and monitoring skills, use the assistant as a strategic tool for optimization ("Personalised Insight" 34.5%, "Technical Aspects" 8.6%). Low-SRL students, requiring more foundational support, use it for comprehension and reassurance ("Clarification" 17.7%, "Emotional Expression" 5.9%).
- **Core Assumption:** Query patterns are a reliable proxy for underlying regulatory processes. High-SRL queries target "feed forward" and system mechanics; low-SRL queries target "feed back" and basic understanding.
- **Evidence Anchors:** [section 3.1] ENA analysis shows a significant difference (p=.02, r=-.62) in query network structures between high and low SRL students along the primary dimension. [section 4] "Low SRL students were primarily seeking clarification and reassurance, while high SRL students instead focused on technical queries and requested personalised strategies."
- **Break Condition:** If a student's SRL level does not predict their dominant query cluster (e.g., a low-SRL student consistently asks only for strategic plans), the assumed causal link between regulatory competence and help-seeking framing is weakened.

### Mechanism 3: Response Quality Varies Systematically with Query Complexity
- **Claim:** The assistant's response quality is not uniform but varies systematically with the cognitive complexity and affective load of the student's query type, revealing architectural strengths and blind spots.
- **Mechanism:** The system excels at clarity and reliability for queries involving data explanation (e.g., "Clarification," "Justification") because these align with its prompt's role to "offer real-time, context-aware responses." It underperforms on personalisation and actionability for queries requiring synthesis ("Personalised Insight") because its prompt lacks deep integration of multi-source student history and strategic pedagogical reasoning.
- **Core Assumption:** Response quality is a function of prompt engineering and available contextual data slots, not just the base LLM's capability. The system's design prioritizes accurate data explanation over generative strategy formulation.
- **Evidence Anchors:** [section 3.2] Clarity (M=4.63) and Reliability (M=4.12) scored highest; Personalisation (M=3.56) and Actionability (M=3.72) were moderate; Error Handling (M=3.29) was lowest. [section 3.2.1] Personalisation scores dropped to M=2.43 for "Technical Aspects" queries and were low (M=2.86) for "Personalised Insight" queries.
- **Break Condition:** If response quality scores do not vary meaningfully across the seven query themes, it would indicate the assessment framework is flawed or the system is exceptionally robust, contradicting the paper's core finding of differential efficacy.

## Foundational Learning
- **Concept: Self-Regulated Learning (SRL) Theory (Winne & Hadwin)**
  - **Why Needed Here:** The entire study's framing, grouping, and interpretation depend on SRL as the lens for understanding differential student engagement. It defines the "phases" (task definition, goal setting, etc.) that map onto query types.
  - **Quick Check:** How would you categorise a student's query "How can I improve my debate participation?" in terms of SRL phases (e.g., is it primarily about goal setting, strategy enactment, or monitoring)?

- **Concept: Feedback Literacy & Dialogic Feedback**
  - **Why Needed Here:** The paper posits that the GenAI assistant's value lies in making feedback "dialogic." Understanding principles of effective feedback (Hattie & Timperley: feed up/back/forward) and dialogic co-construction (Carless & Boud) is essential to evaluate the assistant's role beyond simple Q&A.
  - **Quick Check:** What makes a feedback interaction "dialogic" versus "transactional," and which query types from the paper (e.g., "Justification") best exemplify a dialogic move?

- **Concept: Prompt Engineering & Context Window Design**
  - **Why Needed Here:** The assistant's performance is directly engineered via the system prompt, few-shot examples, and the structured injection of visual descriptions, data, and written feedback. This is the core technical mechanism for attempting personalisation and context awareness.
  - **Quick Check:** Looking at the described system prompt ("As a chat-based assistant, your role is to offer real-time, context-aware responses..."), what is one specific instruction you would add to improve handling of "Emotional Expression" queries?

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component Map:**
  Frontend LAD -> Context Aggregator -> LLM Gateway -> Response Renderer. (Missing: Conversation Memory)
- **Critical Path:** Student asks question -> Frontend sends query + current page context ID -> Context Aggregator fetches student's latest data & relevant visual descriptions -> Prompt assembled -> LLM Gateway (OpenAI API call) -> Response returned -> Renderer displays. No retrieval of chat history.
- **Design Tradeoffs:**
  - Token Economy vs. Richness: The 150-word limit and need to fit visual descriptions/data force tradeoffs between comprehensive data synthesis and response conciseness.
  - Safety vs. Depth: The prompt's emphasis on "clear, context-aware responses" and high "Motivation" scores suggest tuning for supportive, non-confrontational tone, potentially at the cost of critical, challenging feedback.
  - Static vs. Dynamic Context: Using predefined visual descriptions is robust but inflexible; it cannot explain novel visual elements or user-generated artefacts.
- **Failure Signatures:**
  - Log Pattern: Repeated queries on the same topic from the same user within a short time window.
  - Response Content: High frequency of phrases like "Based on your data..." without specific numbers, or generic advice ("try to engage more") when data shows a clear, specific trend.
  - User Behaviour: Session ends immediately after a "Personalised Insight" query, or user switches to "greeting/other" queries after a technical question.
- **First 3 Experiments:**
  1. Prompt Sensitivity Test: A/B test two prompt variations for "Personalised Insight" queries, measuring change in Actionability and Personalisation scores.
  2. Memory Injection Prototype: Instrument the Context Aggregator to append the last 3 Q/A pairs to the prompt. Analyze changes in coherence and affective response for "Justification" and "Emotional Expression" queries.
  3. Query Intent Classifier Pilot: Build a classifier to tag incoming queries in real-time and trigger different few-shot sets or system prompts, testing if per-intent tuning improves response quality.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
1. How does the effectiveness of a GenAI-scaffolded LAD generalize across diverse academic disciplines, course designs, and student demographics?
2. To what extent does incorporating persistent conversational memory into the GenAI assistant improve dialogue coherence, personalization, and long-term support for self-regulated learning?
3. Can explicit training in prompt engineering enhance students' ability to elicit more personalized and actionable insights from GenAI assistants, thereby improving feedback literacy and learning outcomes?
4. How can GenAI assistants be designed to detect and adaptively respond to students' emotional states during feedback dialogues to enhance motivation, trust, and self-regulation?

## Limitations
- The exact GenAI system prompt, few-shot examples, and visual description logic are undisclosed (appendix missing), blocking precise replication.
- The specific SRL questionnaire instrument and k-means clustering parameters are ambiguous, risking group definition inconsistencies.
- Generalizability is limited: 22 postgraduate students, single institution, short 10-week duration; effects on diverse cohorts are unknown.

## Confidence
- **Mechanism 1 (GenAI scaffolding for low-SRL students):** Medium. Strong internal evidence but weak external anchoring.
- **Mechanism 2 (SRL predicts query-type divergence):** High. Supported by significant ENA results (p=.02, r=-.62) and clear thematic separation.
- **Mechanism 3 (Response quality varies by query complexity):** Medium. Ratings data is robust, but findings are tightly coupled to the unreported prompt design.
