# ALTER: Asymmetric LoRA for Token-Entropy-Guided Unlearning of LLMs

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2603.01792
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.01792
- **Reference count:** 13
- **Primary result:** Asymmetric LoRA unlearning reduces WMDP accuracy to ~25% while preserving MMLU, achieving >95% forget quality and >90% utility retention.

## Executive Summary
ALTER introduces a parameter-efficient unlearning framework for large language models that leverages an asymmetric LoRA architecture guided by token-level entropy. The method isolates forgetting parameters by routing high-entropy structural tokens to a shared matrix (preserving language capability) and low-entropy knowledge tokens to domain-specific matrices (enabling targeted removal). Evaluated on Llama3-8B and Zephyr-7B across TOFU, WMDP, and MUSE benchmarks, ALTER achieves near-random performance on hazardous knowledge (WMDP ~25% accuracy) while maintaining general task proficiency (MMLU >90%). It significantly outperforms prior methods in the forget-retain trade-off, improves generation fluency, and reduces training time by ~86% compared to full fine-tuning.

## Method Summary
ALTER builds on LoRA’s low-rank update paradigm (ΔW = BA) with an asymmetric design: a single shared matrix **A** and multiple **B** experts—one retention expert **B^r** for general knowledge and separate forgetting experts **B_d^f** per unlearning subdomain. Token-wise Tsallis entropy **S_q** determines routing: high-entropy tokens (structural/functional) use **A** (preserved), while low-entropy tokens (knowledge-intensive) are routed to a specific **B_d^f** for forgetting. Training employs a hierarchical loss: an importance-weighted hinge loss on forgetting tokens applied to **B_d^f**, a retention loss on **B^r**, and a gradient penalty on **A** to constrain updates from high-entropy tokens, thereby protecting structural integrity. Inference dynamically selects experts based on entropy.

## Key Results
- On WMDP (hazardous knowledge), ALTER reduces multiple-choice accuracy to ~25% (near-random) while preserving MMLU scores, surpassing baselines (47.8–83.6%).
- Achieves >95% forget quality and >90% utility retention on TOFU, WMDP, and MUSE benchmarks.
- Training time cut by ~86% versus full fine-tuning, with high generation fluency (Flu-mean 3.46).

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1: Asymmetric LoRA Parameter Isolation
**Claim:** Asymmetric LoRA architecture isolates forgetting parameters from retention parameters at the expert level.  
**Mechanism:** Shared matrix **A** is used for all tokens, while separate matrices **B_d^f** (forgetting subdomains) and **B^r** (retention) are independent. Gradient updates for each **B** expert are strictly confined to its designated loss, preventing interference between unlearning different data subsets and between forgetting/retention tasks.  
**Core assumption:** Knowledge relevant to different subdomains or tasks can be decoupled into distinct low-rank subspaces.  
**Evidence:** Abstract states architecture “achieves a specified forgetting objective by parameter isolation.” Section (Page 2) notes it “transforms the complex problem of forgetting heterogeneous data into localized optimization tasks.” Corpus context on parameter collision in multi-LoRA provides indirect support.

### Mechanism 2: Token-Level Entropy Routing
**Claim:** Tsallis entropy provides a robust signal to route tokens between shared **A** (high-entropy, structural) and specific **B** experts (low-entropy, knowledge-intensive), enabling precise removal.  
**Mechanism:** High-entropy tokens (e.g., function words) are routed to **A** to preserve generation quality. Low-entropy tokens (e.g., domain entities) are routed to **B_d^f** experts for targeted unlearning. The Tsallis parameter **q** adjusts for non-extensive properties of LLM distributions.  
**Core assumption:** Token entropy bimodally correlates with role: high entropy indicates structural elements (to preserve), low entropy indicates factual content (to forget), and this pattern remains stable during PEFT.  
**Evidence:** Abstract describes routing “via Tsallis entropy.” Section (Page 3) observes “high entropy tokens are mostly structural… low entropy tokens contain knowledge-intensive content” with >87% stability during PEFT. Neighboring work on selective unlearning supports token-granularity benefits.

### Mechanism 3: Hierarchical Loss with Structural Invariance
**Claim:** A hierarchical loss, including an entropy-protected gradient term on **A**, enforces structural invariance while allowing precise forgetting in **B** experts.  
**Mechanism:** Loss combines: (1) entropy-weighted forgetting loss on **B_d^f**, (2) retention loss on **B^r**, and (3) regularization λE[‖∇_A S_q(x_t)‖²] that penalizes large gradient updates on **A** for high-entropy tokens, preserving their representations.  
**Core assumption:** Structural knowledge resides primarily in **A** updates; constraining **A**’s gradients prevents over-forgetting and maintains fluency.  
**Evidence:** Abstract mentions “isolating forgetting parameters.” Section (Page 4) details “gradient constraints on A’s high entropy tokens preserve structural integrity” and “strict gradient isolation” for **B** experts. Related entropy regularization for unlearning (e.g., EntUn) provides contextual support.

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map**: Pretrained Model (**W₀**) -> Shared Matrix (**A**) -> Forgetting Experts (**B_d^f**) & Retention Expert (**B^r**) with Entropy Router (**W_g**) controlling routing. Forward pass: ΔW = (**B^r** + Σ **ω_d^f**·**B_d^f**)**·A**.
- **Critical path**: For each batch: (1) Compute token-wise Tsallis entropy **S_q**. (2) Router outputs expert weights **g_d**. (3) Forward: high-entropy tokens fuse **A** and top-3 **B** experts; low-entropy tokens use single **B** expert. (4) Compute hierarchical loss (Eq. 8). (5) Backprop with strict gradient isolation: **B_d^f** updated via forgetting loss, **B^r** via retention loss, **A** updated only via high-entropy tokens, **W_g** via all.
- **Design tradeoffs**:
  - Rank **r**: Balances capacity vs. efficiency (paper uses **r=8**).
  - Tsallis **q**: Controls entropy bimodality; **q<1** preserves structure, **q>1** breaks associations (paper uses **q=1.5** for forgetting).
  - Loss weights (**β,γ,λ**): **β** (forget strength), **γ** (retain strength), **λ** (structural preservation); set to **β=γ=1.0, λ=0.01**.
  - Routing temp **τ**: **0.8** for high-entropy (soft multi-expert), **0.01** for low-entropy (hard single-expert).
- **Failure signatures**:
  - Poor forgetting (WMDP accuracy high): router misclassifies low-entropy tokens as high-entropy, or **β** too low/**λ** too high.
  - Utility collapse (MMLU drops): **B^r** undertrained, **γ** too low, or structural invariance loss failed.
  - Low fluency: **A** corrupted due to broken gradient isolation or incorrect entropy threshold.
  - No improvement over single-LoRA: asymmetric initialization or router failed to separate.
- **First 3 experiments**:
  1. **TOFU (Entity Unlearning)**: Synthetic benchmark; tests forgetting efficacy (KS-test p-value) and utility retention at 1%/5%/10% forgetting ratios vs. gradient-based, NPO, single-LoRA baselines via trade-off curves.
  2. **WMDP (Hazardous Knowledge)**: Real-world sensitive domains (Biosafety, Cybersecurity). Measures MC accuracy drop on WMDP vs. MMLU retention; fluency via GPT-4o. Key result: ~25% WMDP accuracy with preserved MMLU.
  3. **HarryPotter (Copyrighted Unlearning)**: Unlearning copyrighted text. Uses BLEU/ROUGE-L against Retain model for forget quality, MMLU for utility, fluency scoring. Validates on a different task type.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
1. **How can PEFT achieve efficient unlearning while preserving overall performance in multi-domain coupled parameter spaces?**  
   Basis: Page 2 raises this explicitly.  
   Why unresolved: Only tested on ≤8B models and three English benchmarks; larger models, multiple languages, diverse tasks unverified.
2. **How should the Tsallis entropy deformation parameter q be chosen to balance structural preservation and forgetting?**  
   Basis: Tsallis entropy used without guidance on setting **q**; value omitted from experiments.  
   Why unresolved: Impact on forget-retain trade-off unexplored, limiting reliable application.
3. **Does the high-entropy/low-entropy token dichotomy hold universally across languages and knowledge types?**  
   Basis: Paper relies on this dichotomy (Page 3) but validates only on English LLMs and few domains.  
   Why unresolved: Other languages/domains may have different entropy patterns, potentially breaking routing logic.

## Limitations
- **B-matrix initialization clustering method** (automatic subdomain discovery via feature clustering) is unspecified, a critical step for asymmetric setup without prior knowledge.
- **Exact LIHL loss formulation and gradient isolation mechanics** (e.g., custom backprop hooks) are not detailed; implementation relies on “strict” isolation claims.
- **Generalizability of entropy routing**: Bimodal distribution and optimal **q=1.5** validated only on specific datasets; may fail on diverse domains or model architectures.

## Confidence
- **Asymmetric LoRA parameter isolation**: High (architectural design logically enforces separation; experimental results show preserved MMLU while dropping WMDP).
- **Tsallis entropy as robust routing signal**: Medium (empirical evidence of bimodal distribution on benchmarks, but dataset-dependent assumption).
- **Hierarchical loss with gradient constraint preserves structure**: Medium (loss described, results indicate high fluency, but validation depends on correct implementation of penalty and router).
