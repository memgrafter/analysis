# Koopman Operator Identification of Model Parameter Trajectories for Temporal Domain Generalization (KOMET)

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2603.26923
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.26923
- **Reference count:** 30
- **Primary result:** Achieves mean autonomous rollout accuracies between 0.981 and 1.000 over 100 held-out timesteps on six synthetic temporal drift datasets without retraining, matching or exceeding fully retrained upper bounds.

## Executive Summary
KOMET addresses temporal domain drift, where deployed machine learning models degrade as data distributions evolve over time. It treats sequences of trained model parameters as trajectories of a nonlinear dynamical system and identifies a linear Koopman operator governing their evolution via Extended Dynamic Mode Decomposition (EDMD). A warm-start sequential training protocol—carrying over full Adam optimizer state (weights, first and second moments) and adding a smoothness penalty—produces bounded, smooth weight trajectories. A Fourier-augmented observable dictionary exploits the periodic structure of the drift. On six synthetic datasets with diverse drift geometries (rotating, oscillating, expanding), KOMET’s autonomous rollout (predicting future parameters without retraining) matches the performance of models retrained at every timestep. Spectral analysis confirms the Koopman operator captures interpretable dynamics aligned with the drift, and parameter coupling analyses reveal strong layer-wise interactions that enable effective dimensionality reduction via PCA.

## Method Summary
KOMET operates in two phases. Phase 1 (training): Models are trained sequentially on drifting data (t=0 to T_train). For each timestep t, training starts from the previous timestep’s converged weights and full Adam state (mₜ₋₁, vₜ₋₁), with an added smoothness penalty λₛ∥θₜ − θ̂ₜ₋₁∥² and weight decay λᵂᴰ to prevent unbounded growth. This yields a smooth parameter trajectory W = [θ₀,…,θ_T_train]. Phase 2 (Koopman identification): The trajectory is standardized per parameter, then PCA-whitened to a low-dimensional subspace (retaining ≥99.5% variance). A lifted observable vector ψ(θₜ, t) = [1, zₜ, sin(kωt), cos(kωt)]ₖ₌₁⁴ is constructed, where zₜ are the PCA-reduced weights, ω = 2π/T, and T is the known drift period. EDMD solves for a linear matrix A such that ψ(θₜ₊₁) ≈ Aψ(θₜ). Eigenvalues of A are clipped to enforce spectral radius ρ(A) < 1 for stable autonomous rollout: ˆθₜ₊ₖ = inverse_pca(Aᵏψ(θ̂ₜ)). For datasets with monotonic trends (e.g., expanding distributions), a detrending step is applied before PCA and reversed after rollout.

## Key Results
- Autonomous rollout accuracies over 100 held-out timesteps range from 0.981 to 1.000, matching or exceeding the fully retrained upper bound on all six synthetic datasets.
- Spectral analysis of the Koopman matrix A reveals dominant eigenvalues clustered near the unit circle at angles corresponding to the drift frequency ω and its harmonics, confirming the operator captures the periodic driving force.
- Strong inter-parameter coupling (measured by distance correlation) is observed: 26–57% of parameter pairs co-vary strongly (|R| > 0.5). This coupling allows PCA to compress the high-dimensional weight space (n_θ ∈ {17,27}) to just 9–10 principal components while retaining ≥99.5% of trajectory variance.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1: Warm-Start Training with Adam State Carry-Over and Smoothness Penalty Produces Bounded, Smooth Trajectories
- **Claim:** Carrying the full Adam state (θₜ, mₜ, vₜ) across timesteps, combined with an explicit smoothness penalty λₛ∥θₜ − θ̂ₜ₋₁∥² and weight decay λᵂᴰ, ensures the weight trajectory is smooth and bounded, satisfying key conditions for Koopman identification.
- **Mechanism:** The Adam first and second moments (mₜ, vₜ) are exponential moving averages of gradients and squared gradients, acting as a low-pass filter that reduces transient oscillations when warm-starting. The smoothness penalty enforces continuity between consecutive minima. Weight decay counteracts scale-invariance in softmax networks, preventing linear trends that would violate periodicity assumptions.
- **Core assumption:** The underlying weight dynamics are sufficiently smooth and bounded such that a finite-dimensional linear Koopman operator can approximate their evolution.
- **Evidence anchors:** Abstract mentions "warm-start sequential training protocol with Adam moment continuity enforces trajectory smoothness." Section III-B details the threefold effect: faster convergence, smoother trajectories, and curvature encoding via moments. For softmax networks, ℓ₂ weight decay reduces the linear-trend fraction.
- **Break condition:** If drift is highly discontinuous or hyperparameters (λₛ, λᵂᴰ) are mis-specified, trajectories may remain jagged or divergent, leading to a Koopman matrix with spectral radius ρ(A) ≥ 1 and unstable rollouts.

### Mechanism 2: Strong Parameter Coupling Enables Low-Dimensional PCA Subspace for Accurate Koopman Identification
- **Claim:** High inter-parameter coupling (measured by distance correlation and transfer entropy) means the effective degrees of freedom in the weight trajectory are far fewer than n_θ, making PCA compression to p ≪ n_θ components both viable and effective for EDMD.
- **Mechanism:** PCA whitening projects the high-dimensional trajectory onto leading principal components, retaining ≥99.5% of variance. The coupling analyses show parameters within and across layers co-move in a coordinated fashion (e.g., orbital symmetry), indicating a few latent modes govern the collective dynamics.
- **Core assumption:** The principal components aligned with maximum variance are also the modes most relevant to the drifting decision boundary; the coupling structure is stable across the training window.
- **Evidence anchors:** Section II-C reports 26–57% of parameter pairs co-vary strongly (R>0.5), motivating PCA compression. Section IV-D2 shows 9–10 components suffice to retain ≥99.5% of weight variance across all binary networks.
- **Break condition:** If drift induces decorrelated weight movements (e.g., independent layer-wise adaptation), PCA may require many components to preserve decision-boundary dynamics, potentially causing numerical instability in EDMD or requiring an impractically large dictionary.

### Mechanism 3: Fourier-Augmented Observable Dictionary Exploits Periodic Drift Structure
- **Claim:** A dictionary ψ(zₜ, t) that includes Fourier harmonics at the known drift period ω allows EDMD to identify a Koopman operator whose eigenvalues directly encode the drift frequency and damping, enabling accurate autonomous rollout.
- **Mechanism:** After PCA, the observable vector explicitly includes time harmonics sin(kωt), cos(kωt). Since the true weight dynamics are (quasi-)periodic, the Fourier basis spans the dominant Koopman-invariant subspace, making the fitted matrix A an accurate finite-dimensional approximant. Spectral clipping ensures ρ(A) < 1 for stability.
- **Core assumption:** The weight trajectory exhibits dominant periodicity at a known frequency T (or is detrended to achieve periodicity), and the Fourier harmonics capture the essential nonlinearities.
- **Evidence anchors:** Section III-C states the harmonic terms encode periodic forcing and reflect physical insight. Section IV-C2 shows that for the expanding-monotonic Dataset F, standard Fourier fails but detrending recovers stable prediction; detrended residuals account for ≤0.2% of total weight variation, confirming quasi-periodicity.
- **Break condition:** If drift contains aperiodic or chaotic components not captured by the harmonic set (or detrending fails), the Koopman model will have poor predictive fidelity, with ρ(A) ≥ 1 or rapid rollout divergence.

## Foundational Learning

- **Concept: Koopman Operator & EDMD**
  - **Why needed:** KOMET’s core claim is that weight dynamics are linearizable via a Koopman operator. Understanding that observables ψ(θ) evolve linearly even under nonlinear dynamics F explains why EDMD on ψ(θₜ) yields a matrix A for autonomous rollout.
  - **Quick check:** Given a nonlinear system θₜ₊₁ = F(θₜ), if ψ(θ) = [θ, θ²] and you observe {θₜ}, what linear relation would EDMD aim to find between ψ(θₜ₊₁) and ψ(θₜ)?

- **Concept: Adam Optimizer State (m, v)**
  - **Why needed:** Warm-starting requires carrying the full Adam state (not just weights) to smooth trajectories. The first and second moments are exponential moving averages that act as a low-pass filter on gradient information, reducing cold-start transients.
  - **Quick check:** How do the first (m) and second (v) moment estimates in Adam influence the effective step direction and adaptive learning rate, and why would carrying them over reduce "cold start" oscillations?

- **Concept: PCA Whitening & Dimensionality Reduction**
  - **Why needed:** Before EDMD, the n_θ-dimensional weight trajectory is compressed to p ≪ n_θ principal components. Whitening decorrelates and scales components to unit variance, simplifying the Koopman fit and avoiding ill-conditioning.
  - **Quick check:** If two weight parameters are strongly coupled (high dCor), what does that imply about the eigenvalues of their covariance matrix, and why might PCA yield a low effective dimensionality?

## Architecture Onboarding
Data sampler → Model f_θ → Loss (task + λₛ smoothness + λᵂᴰ) → Adam (with state carry) → converged θₜ → Trajectory W → Per-parameter standardization → PCA whitening (retain ≥99.5% variance) → Dictionary ψ(zₜ,t) with Fourier harmonics → EDMD solve A = Ψ⁺Ψ⁻ → Spectral reprojection (clip |λ|≥1 to 1-ε) → Autonomous rollout ˆθₜ₊ₖ = inverse_pca(Aᵏ ψ(θ̂ₜ)); for monotonic drifts, detrend before PCA and re-add post-rollout.

**Critical path:** Phase 1 must produce a smooth, bounded trajectory (check: min/max accuracy >90%, no linear trends). Phase 2 requires PCA variance retention ≥99.5% and Fourier dictionary to yield ρ(A) < 1.

**Design tradeoffs:**
- Dictionary complexity vs. interpretability: More harmonics capture nonlinearities but risk overfitting; Fourier is interpretable if period T is known.
- PCA variance threshold: Lower threshold (e.g., 99%) keeps more dimensions, possibly improving accuracy but increasing A’s size and risk of spectral pollution.
- Weight decay (λᵂᴰ): Essential for softmax to bound weights but must be tuned; too high may underfit the drift.

**Failure signatures:**
- Training: Any timestep with accuracy <90% indicates trajectory corruption.
- EDMD fit: ρ(A) ≥ 1 (or very close to 1) after spectral clipping suggests dictionary mismatch or non-periodic trend.
- Rollout: Accuracy drops sharply within 10–20 steps implies poor Koopman approximation; check PCA variance retention and Fourier harmonic count.

**First 3 experiments:**
1. Warm-start sanity check: Train sequentially on a simple periodic dataset (e.g., Dataset C). Plot ‖θₜ − θₜ₋₁‖₂ over t; should show smooth oscillations, not spikes. Compare epochs-to-converge with/without Adam state carry.
2. PCA coupling validation: Compute dCor matrix on W. Verify that >40% of off-diagonal entries >0.5 and that PCA with 99.5% threshold reduces n_θ by >50%. Plot cumulative explained variance vs. components.
3. Koopman rollout benchmark: On a held-out cycle (t=300–399), compute autonomous rollout accuracy every 10 steps. Should stay within 2% of retrained upper bound for all 100 steps. If not, inspect eigenvalue spectrum of A for near-unit eigenvalues beyond the expected harmonic cluster.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
1. Can KOMET's two-phase pipeline be successfully applied to time-varying regression tasks, and what are the performance characteristics compared to classification? Although experiments instantiate fθ as a classifier, the pipeline is agnostic to the loss function and applies equally to regression; validating this on time-varying regression benchmarks is a natural next step.
2. Does incorporating Adam optimizer state vectors (first and second moments) into the Koopman observable dictionary enhance prediction accuracy and robustness of parameter trajectories? Augmenting the dictionary with [θt, mt, vt] may improve fidelity by incorporating curvature information currently discarded, though this requires principled noise filtering.
3. Can KOMET be modified with online or sliding-window EDMD to handle non-periodic and slowly non-stationary distribution drifts, such as those in real-world benchmarks like Wild-Time? Online or sliding-window EDMD would allow the operator to adapt continuously, broadening applicability to slowly drifting, non-stationary settings.
4. What are the convergence rates, sample complexity, and generalization guarantees for EDMD with a Fourier dictionary in KOMET's setting? Formal convergence guarantees, sample complexity bounds, uncertainty bounds, and generalization bounds for autonomous rollout accuracy remain open theoretical questions.

## Limitations
- Evaluation restricted to six small synthetic datasets with clean, known drift geometries; real-world temporal drift often includes noise, non-stationarity, and unknown or drifting periods.
- Requires the drift period T as input; in practice this must be estimated and may be inaccurate.
- Hyperparameter choices (λₛ, λᵂᴰ, PCA variance threshold, Fourier harmonics) are not systematically studied, raising robustness questions.
- Scalability to larger, deeper networks is unproven; parameter coupling observed in small MLPs may weaken in deeper architectures, making PCA compression insufficient.
- Adam state carry-over implementation details are underspecified (e.g., bias-corrected vs. raw moments), potentially affecting trajectory smoothness and reproducibility.
