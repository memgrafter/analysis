# When Numbers Tell Half the Story: Human-Metric Alignment in Topic Model Evaluation

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID**: 2603.01945
- **Source URL**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.01945
- **Reference count**: 0
- **Primary result**: TWM aligns with diversity metrics and captures human-perceived distinctness, while coherence metrics often diverge from human judgments (e.g., Top2Vec high coherence, low TWI accuracy).

## Executive Summary
This paper investigates the misalignment between automated evaluation metrics (topic coherence and diversity) and human judgments for topic models, particularly on domain-specific corpora. The authors introduce a novel human evaluation task called Topic Word Mixing (TWM), in which annotators decide whether a set of eight words originates from a single topic or a mixture of two similar topics, thereby probing inter-topic distinctness. Using approximately 4,000 annotations on a philosophy of science corpus, they compare six topic models (LDA, NMF, Top2Vec, BERTopic, CFMF, CFMF-emb) across multiple topic granularities. Results show that word intrusion (TWI) accuracy and coherence metrics frequently disagree; for instance, Top2Vec yields the highest coherence but the poorest TWI performance, while BERTopic shows the opposite pattern. In contrast, TWM scores correlate with diversity metrics and capture human-perceived topic boundaries. The embedding-enhanced CFMF-emb model achieves the best overall balance on both human tasks. The study highlights the limitations of relying solely on automated metrics and underscores the need for human-grounded evaluation, especially in specialized domains.

## Method Summary
The study evaluates topic models on a philosophy of science corpus comprising 16,917 full-text journal articles (~65 million words). After preprocessing (tokenization, lemmatization, content-word filtering, and machine translation of non-English content), six topic modeling algorithms are trained at various granularities (K ≈ 10, 25, 50). For each model, two automated metrics are computed: CoherenceCV (sliding-window coherence) and Diversity (ratio of unique top words). Two human evaluation tasks are constructed from the model outputs:
- **Word Intrusion (TWI)**: participants see four top words from a topic plus one "intruder" word (highly ranked in another topic) and identify the intruder.
- **Topic Word Mixing (TWM)**: participants see eight words either from a single topic or evenly split between the two most similar topics (measured by embedding cosine similarity) and judge whether the set is "mixed" or "pure".
Approximately 4,000 annotations are collected from 10 trained philosophy undergraduates via LabelStudio, with randomized task order and control questions. Inter-annotator agreement (Fleiss' Kappa) is computed, and per-model macro-averaged accuracy (TWI) and F1 (TWM) are derived. The analysis correlates human and automated scores, adjusts TWI for intruder-topic semantic similarity to account for difficulty variations, and synthesizes overall model performance.

## Key Results
- Coherence metrics and TWI accuracy diverge on the domain-specific corpus; e.g., Top2Vec attains high CoherenceCV (0.72–0.80) but low TWI accuracy (0.693), while BERTopic exhibits lower coherence (0.52–0.62) but the highest TWI accuracy (0.900).
- TWM aligns with diversity metrics: Top2Vec achieves near-perfect diversity (0.98–1.00) and the best TWM performance, whereas models with lower diversity (e.g., LDA) perform poorly on TWM.
- The embedding-enhanced CFMF-emb model yields the best overall balance across both human evaluation tasks, suggesting its topics are both coherent and distinct as perceived by humans.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- **Claim**: In domain-specific corpora, automated coherence metrics (e.g., CoherenceCV) may not align with human-perceived topic coherence as measured by Word Intrusion (TWI).
- **Mechanism**: Coherence metrics quantify semantic relatedness via word co-occurrence patterns within the target corpus or a general reference. Human judgment in TWI, however, relies on broader semantic and domain knowledge to identify an intruder. In specialized domains with technical jargon and abstract concepts, corpus-internal co-occurrence may not capture the conceptual groupings that domain experts recognize.
- **Core assumption**: Human annotators leverage external, domain-specific knowledge and broader semantic understanding beyond local corpus statistics.
- **Evidence anchors**: 
  - Abstract states "...word intrusion and coherence metrics do not always align, particularly in specialized domains..."
  - Section 5: "Top2Vec achieved the highest coherence (CV=0.72–0.80)... yet performs poorest on TWI (0.693). Conversely, BERTopic excels at TWI (0.900) despite having the second-lowest automated coherence (0.52–0.62)."
  - The corpus comprises philosophy of science texts with domain-specific terminology; divergence is demonstrated on this corpus.

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim**: Topic Word Mixing (TWM) provides a human-grounded assessment of inter-topic distinctness that aligns with, but is not identical to, automated diversity metrics.
- **Mechanism**: TWM tasks ask annotators to judge if a word set comes from one or two topics. If a model produces semantically distinct topics, mixed sets should be easily identifiable, leading to high accuracy. Diversity metrics measure lexical overlap among top words; TWM captures the perceptual consequence of that overlap (or lack thereof) for human judges.
- **Core assumption**: Human perception of topic boundaries is a valid proxy for inter-topic distinctness, complementing lexical overlap measures.
- **Evidence anchors**: 
  - Abstract: "TWM captures human-perceived distinctness while appearing to align with diversity metrics."
  - Section 5: "Compared with automated measures of diversity, the TWM results show a consistent pattern. Top2Vec has near perfect diversity scores (0.98–1.00) and outperforms all other models on TWM."
  - Alignment observed in the philosophy of science corpus; generalizability beyond this domain is not tested.

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim**: The difficulty of TWI tasks, driven by the semantic similarity between intruder words and topic heads, confounds the measurement of model quality in human evaluations.
- **Mechanism**: Annotator success in TWI depends on the intruder's semantic distance from the topic's top words. If an intruder is semantically close (e.g., similar embedding cosine), the task is harder regardless of the topic's intrinsic coherence. Without controlling for this, model comparisons can be biased by task difficulty variations introduced during sampling.
- **Core assumption**: Task difficulty is a significant nuisance variable in human evaluation that must be controlled to isolate topic quality.
- **Evidence anchors**: 
  - Section 5: "a posteriori examination... reveals clear discrepancies... Average task accuracy is inversely correlated with the mean similarity between top-words and intruder (r=−0.145, p=0.0079)."
  - Section 5: "Adjustment... alleviates the impact of intruder to topic head similarity" and changes scores, confirming the confound.
  - Evidence comes from the paper’s annotation data; intruder selection criteria aimed to control similarity but post-hoc analysis shows residual variation.

## Architecture Onboarding
### Component map
Corpus → Preprocessing → Topic Models → Automated Metric Engine → Analysis Pipeline; and Topic Models → Task Generator → Annotation Platform → Analysis Pipeline.

### Critical path
Corpus Preprocessing → Model Training & Top-Word Extraction → Automated Metric Calculation → Task Generation (TWI & TWM) → Human Annotation → Inter-Annotator Agreement Check → Metric Correlation & Difficulty Adjustment → Result Synthesis.

### Design tradeoffs
- **Human Cost vs. Insight**: ~4,000 annotations provide ground truth but are expensive; automated metrics are cheap but potentially misleading.
- **Task Difficulty Control**: Initial intruder criteria (frequency, ranking) aimed for consistency, but post-hoc embedding similarity analysis revealed uncontrolled variation, necessitating statistical adjustment.
- **Generality vs. Depth**: Evaluating K=10,25,50 explores granularity but dilutes annotation effort per configuration; full coverage for small K only.

### Failure signatures
- Low Fleiss' Kappa (<0.4) indicating ambiguous tasks or poor annotator training.
- Large confidence intervals on model scores, suggesting insufficient annotation volume per model.
- Persistent divergence between a model’s automated scores and human performance (e.g., Top2Vec) indicating a metric failure mode.

### First three experiments
1. Replicate TWI on a general-domain corpus (e.g., 20 Newsgroups) to test if the observed coherence-human misalignment is specific to the philosophy domain.
2. Run TWM with controlled semantic similarity between mixed-topic pairs to validate the alignment with diversity metrics without the confound of varying topic distinctness.
3. Compare the paper’s intruder selection heuristic against a purely similarity-based (embedding cosine) selection to see which yields more balanced TWI difficulty across models.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
1. Why do automated coherence metrics (e.g., CV) diverge from human word intrusion (TWI) accuracy in domain-specific corpora, as seen with Top2Vec (high CV, low TWI) and BERTopic (low CV, high TWI)?
2. Does Topic Word Mixing (TWM) primarily measure inter-topic distinctness or indirectly reflect intra-topic coherence, given its correlation with both diversity metrics and coherence-sensitive models?
3. How can embedding-based topic coherence metrics be designed to better align with human judgments in specialized domains, as suggested by the authors’ call for such metrics?
4. What is the systematic effect of topic model granularity (number of topics K) on human-perceived coherence and distinctness in domain-specific corpora, given inconsistent findings (e.g., LDA_54 outperforming LDA_10)?
5. Can preemptively controlling for semantic similarity between intruder words and topic top-words eliminate task difficulty bias in word intrusion evaluations, and how does this affect model rankings?

## Limitations
- **Domain-Generalizability (Low Confidence)**: Findings are limited to a philosophy of science corpus; may not hold for general or other specialized domains.
- **Task Generation Specificity (Medium Confidence)**: Exact algorithmic details for selecting topic pairs in TWM and intruder selection are not fully specified, risking replication variability.
- **Annotation Protocol (Low Confidence)**: Full annotator instructions, interface logic, and training regimen are not exhaustively documented, limiting interpretability of agreement statistics.

## Confidence
- **Coherence-Human Divergence in This Domain**: Medium
- **TWM as Distinctness Proxy**: Medium
- **TWI Difficulty Confound**: High

## Next Checks
1. Cross-Domain Replication: Re-run the full evaluation pipeline on a standard general-domain corpus (e.g., 20 Newsgroups) to test if the coherence-TWI misalignment persists.
2. TWM Pair Selection Audit: Implement the paper’s TWM generation procedure to verify that the distribution of cosine similarities between selected topic pairs matches the paper’s implied range and that resulting TWM scores correlate as reported with diversity metrics.
3. Intruder Similarity Ablation: For the existing TWI tasks, recompute model accuracy after stratifying and comparing performance on “easy” (low intruder-topic similarity) vs. “hard” (high similarity) task subsets to test the claimed confound.
