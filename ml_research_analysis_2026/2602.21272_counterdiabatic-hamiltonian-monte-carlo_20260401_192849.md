# Counterdiabatic Hamiltonian Monte Carlo

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2602.21272
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.21272
- **Reference count:** 40
- **Primary result:** CHMC reduces standardized squared error on multimodal benchmarks by orders of magnitude vs. naive HMC (e.g., 86.5 → 0.65 on annealing Gaussian).

## Executive Summary
This paper introduces Counterdiabatic Hamiltonian Monte Carlo (CHMC), which adapts quantum counterdiabatic driving to classical sampling. CHMC adds a learned time-dependent potential to the HMC Hamiltonian, enabling perfect transport of probability mass during fast annealing schedules and mitigating lag in multimodal targets. Evaluated on three 1D benchmarks, CHMC achieves dramatic error reductions compared to naive HMC. The method is framed as a Hamiltonian SMC sampler with an efficient learned kernel and uses particle weighting derived from the nonequilibrium fluctuation theorem to correct residual bias.

## Method Summary
CHMC modifies standard HMC by incorporating a counterdiabatic term \(A_\lambda\) that depends on an annealing parameter \(\lambda\). At each discrete step of a schedule \(\lambda_k\), \(A_\lambda\) is learned online via variational minimization of a Poisson bracket residual using the current weighted particle population. The modified Hamiltonian \(H_\lambda + \dot{\lambda} A_\lambda\) guides particles along the path while preserving the target distribution. After leapfrog integration, weights are updated as \(w \propto \exp(-\Delta H)\), where \(\Delta H\) is the change in the original Hamiltonian \(H_\lambda\) along the trajectory. This yields an SMC sampler where the transition kernel is learned to efficiently transport mass across changing targets.

## Key Results
- Naive HMC suffers high error on multimodal benchmarks due to lag (e.g., \(b^2 = 86.5\) on annealing Gaussian).
- CHMC reduces error drastically (e.g., \(b^2 = 0.65\) on annealing Gaussian).
- Learned \(A_\lambda\) approximates analytic counterdiabatic potentials in simple cases, enabling fast transport.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1: Perfect Transport via Time-Dependent Potential
- **Claim:** Adding \(\dot{\lambda} A_\lambda\) to the Hamiltonian enables perfect mass transport during fast schedule changes.
- **Mechanism:** The term satisfies \(\{A_\lambda, H_\lambda\} = \partial_\lambda H_\lambda - \mathbb{E}[\partial_\lambda H_\lambda]\), making \(\rho_{H_\lambda}(t)\) stationary under the flow [section 2.1].
- **Core assumption:** Learned \(A_\lambda\) approximates the true counterdiabatic potential well enough to suppress non-adiabatic transitions.
- **Evidence:** Abstract and [section 2.1] derive the condition; benchmarks show large error reduction vs. naive HMC.

### Mechanism 2: Variational Learning of \(A_\lambda\)
- **Claim:** \(A_\lambda\) can be learned online from particles by minimizing the Poisson bracket residual.
- **Mechanism:** Minimize \(L_H(A) = \mathbb{E}_z[ |\{A, H\} - \partial_\lambda H + \mathbb{E}[\partial_\lambda H]|^2 ]\) using weighted particle estimates at each step (Algorithm 1).
- **Core assumption:** The current particle distribution approximates \(\rho_{H_\lambda}(t)\) sufficiently for gradient estimation.
- **Evidence:** [Section 2.2] presents Lemma 2.1 and Algorithm 1; Appendix C details implementation (neural net or polynomial).

### Mechanism 3: SMC Weighting for Bias Correction
- **Claim:** Weighting \(w \propto \exp(-\Delta H)\) corrects residual bias from imperfect transport and discretization.
- **Mechanism:** Derived from the nonequilibrium fluctuation theorem; uses change in original \(H_\lambda\) along trajectories, not the modified Hamiltonian [section 4].
- **Core assumption:** The weight formula holds even with approximate \(A_\lambda\) and discrete integrator (shadow work accounted for) [section 4.2].
- **Evidence:** [Section 4] extends the fluctuation theorem to Hamiltonian kernels; [section 4.2] argues discretization does not alter the form.

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map:** Initialization → Schedule definition → \(A_\phi\) learning per step → Dynamics integration (leapfrog with modified Hamiltonian) → Weighting with \(\exp(-\Delta H)\) → Optional resampling → Output final weighted particles.
- **Critical path:** Sequential loop: for each \(\lambda\) step, first learn \(A_\phi\) using current population, then integrate particles, then update weights. Learning must precede integration.
- **Design tradeoffs:** 
  - \(A_\phi\) parametrization: neural net (expressive, costly) vs. polynomial (cheap, limited capacity).
  - Integrator: non-symplectic due to non-separable \(A_\phi\); requires small step size \(\epsilon\).
  - Schedule: fast \(\dot{\lambda}\) demands more accurate \(A_\phi\); slow \(\dot{\lambda}\) increases total steps.
- **Failure signatures:** 
  - High weight variance / low ESS: poor \(A_\phi\) or too fast schedule.
  - Particles stuck in initial mode: insufficient force from \(A_\phi\).
  - Integrator instability: large \(\epsilon\) causing energy drift.
- **First 3 experiments:**
  1. **Moving mean Gaussian:** Validate tracking; learned \(A_\phi \approx p\) analytically.
  2. **Annealing Gaussian:** Stress test; expect error reduction from ~86.5 to ~0.65.
  3. **Double well:** Multimodal test; measure \(b^2\) improvement and visualize barrier crossing.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- **Ideal gauge choice:** The question of the ideal gauge (e.g., \(H_\lambda\) vs. alternatives) for CHMC is open. Different gauges affect dynamics and counterdiabatic term effectiveness.
- **Scalability to higher dimensions:** Experiments are limited to 1D; performance in high dimensions (learning \(A_\lambda\), computational overhead) is unproven.
- **Automatic hyperparameter tuning:** How to tune \(\epsilon\), \(n_l\), mass matrix automatically? Manual tuning is difficult; interactions with counterdiabatic term unknown.
- **Weight variance analysis:** Derivation assumes exact dynamics and perfect \(A_\lambda\); approximations may increase variance. Need empirical effective sample size measurements.
- **Optimal schedule and parametrization:** Choice of \(\lambda(t)\) and \(A_\lambda\) parametrization (neural vs. polynomial) may affect bias; optimal paths are unclear.

## Limitations
- Critical hyperparameters (\(T_{\text{learn}}\), Adam learning rate, resampling threshold) are not fully specified, risking inconsistent implementations.
- Online learning of \(A_\lambda\) from evolving weighted particles may be unstable, especially early in the schedule.
- The leapfrog variant is non-symplectic, forcing small step sizes and higher computational cost.
- Evaluation restricted to 1D benchmarks; scalability to higher dimensions remains unproven.
- High weight variance in multimodal targets could collapse effective sample size.
- Comparisons only against naive HMC; performance relative to advanced HMC variants (e.g., NUTS, tempering) is unknown.

## Confidence
- **Perfect transport via counterdiabatic term:** Medium (derivation sound but relies on accurate learning and exact integration; real-world approximations likely prevent perfection).
- **Variational learning of \(A_\lambda\):** Medium (loss well-motivated but depends on hyperparameters, model capacity, and particle representativeness; limited to low-D).
- **SMC weighting for bias correction:** High (standard fluctuation theorem extension; should correct bias though variance may be high with poor transport).
- **Overall empirical results:** Medium (substantial error reductions on 1D tests, but limited scope and no strong comparators).
