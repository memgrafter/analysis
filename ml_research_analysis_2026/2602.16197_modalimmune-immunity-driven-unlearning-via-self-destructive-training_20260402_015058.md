# ModalImmune: Immunity Driven Unlearning via Self Destructive Training

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2602.16197  
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.16197  
- **Reference count:** 40  
- **Primary result:** Improves robustness to missing/corrupted modalities via self‑destructive training, achieving state‑of‑the‑art accuracy on CMU‑MOSI (92.1%), CMU‑MOSEI (91.7%), and IEMOCAP (85.7% WA/UA) with modest overhead (~5%).

## Executive Summary
ModalImmune introduces a self‑destructive training framework to harden multimodal models against missing or corrupted modalities. It intentionally collapses the spectral representation of selected modalities during training while preserving reconstruction through property vectors and conditional generators. The system automatically selects which modality to impair using an EXP3.P bandit controller, stabilizes training via curvature‑aware gradient masking, and tunes meta‑parameters via a Neumann‑truncated hyper‑gradient. On three benchmark datasets, it significantly outperforms prior methods in robustness scenarios and shows strong zero‑shot cross‑modality transfer, with limited computational cost.

## Method Summary
ModalImmune interleaves standard reconstruction‑driven batches with Self‑Destructive Learning (SDL) batches. During SDL, an EXP3.P bandit picks a modality *m*⁠∗ to collapse based on an information‑gain surrogate. A spectrum‑adaptive regularizer *L*₍ₗₒₗ₎ irreversibly flattens *m*⁠∗’s covariance by injecting noise scaled to its spectral radius and penalizing stable rank. The collapsed modality is excluded from fusion; instead, a property vector *p*ₘ and generator *G*ₘ synthesize its embedding from the remaining modalities. Curvature‑aware masking (using empirical Fisher eigenvalues) prevents destabilizing gradient updates on the attacked encoder. A bi‑level hyper‑gradient descent (BHGD) with Neumann series truncation automatically adapts meta‑parameters {λ, η, κ}. Training alternates between minimizing a reconstruction loss *L*ᵣₑc (task + reconstruction + property + contrastive terms) on normal batches and *L*ₛDL on SDL batches, while the bandit updates arm rewards from squared task‑loss differences.

## Key Results
- **MOSI/MOSEI:** Full‑modality Acc2 ↑~5% (MOSI 92.1%, MOSEI 91.7%), MAE ↓~16% (MOSI 0.590, MOSEI 0.448).  
- **IEMOCAP:** WA/UA both 85.7%, exceeding prior ~78%.  
- **Robustness:** Under simulated missing modalities, average Acc2/F1/Acc7 ≈ 81.5/81.5/39.7, ~4–7 points above baselines; corruption tests show <1% accuracy drop.  
- **Efficiency:** BHGD matches grid‑search quality with ~38% less tuning time; overhead +4.9% parameters, +5.8% memory/latency.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
Self‑destructive training forces the model to develop redundancy by deliberately degrading one modality’s spectral information during training. The collapse regularizer ensures the degradation is irreversible enough to compel reliance on other modalities, yet the property‑vector pathway retains just enough invariant structure for reconstruction. The bandit controller allocates interventions to modalities whose absence most harms performance, maximizing robustness per training step. Curvature‑aware masking stabilizes updates to the attacked encoder, preventing collapse from destabilizing the joint system. Hyper‑parameter automation via BHGD maintains optimal collapse intensity without manual tuning. Together, these components create an “immune response” where the model learns to function despite impaired inputs.

## Foundational Learning
- **Self‑Destructive Training**  
  *Why needed:* To simulate missing modalities and force learning of complementary representations.  
  *Quick check:* Monitor stable rank of the attacked modality’s embeddings during SDL; it should drop significantly.

- **Spectral Collapse via Noise Injection**  
  *Why needed:* To irreversibly flatten a modality’s covariance, preventing trivial reuse of its spectrum.  
  *Quick check:* Verify noise scale σ = ρ/√d (ρ = spectral radius) and that stable‑rank penalty decreases post‑collapse.

- **EXP3.P Bandit for Intervention Selection**  
  *Why needed:* To dynamically focus collapse on the most harmful modality, improving sample efficiency.  
  *Quick check:* Track per‑modality selection frequencies; they should stabilize and correlate with information‑gain ℓₘ.

- **Curvature‑Aware Gradient Masking**  
  *Why needed:* To avoid destabilizing updates when the attacked modality’s Fisher has negative eigenvalues.  
  *Quick check:* Compute λₘᵢₙ of empirical Fisher; if λₘᵢₙ < −τ (τ = 0.01/lr), gradients should be frozen or masked.

- **Neumann‑Truncated Hyper‑Gradient (BHGD)**  
  *Why needed:* To automatically tune meta‑parameters {λ, η, κ} without costly grid search.  
  *Quick check:* Validate that the truncated series (K ≤ 10) approximates true hyper‑gradient within tolerance; check that β (Hessian spectral norm) remains bounded.

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component Map:**  
Data → Encoders fₘ (per modality) → [Optional SDL: collapse m* via Lcoll] → Fusion F → Task head C  
+ Property vectors pₘ and generators Gₘ for reconstruction → Lrec (MSE, contrastive, property reg.)  
+ EXP3.P controller (observes ℓₘ, selects m*) ↔ Training loop  

**Critical Path:**  
Batch → (with prob pₛₗ) EXP3.P chooses m* → apply Lcoll to m* embeddings, exclude m* from fusion, generate ŷₘ* from pₘ and other modalities → compute LSDL → backprop with gradient masking on fₘ* → else (normal) compute Lrec → BHGD update {λ,η,κ} periodically → repeat.

**Design Tradeoffs:**  
- *Collapse intensity (λ, η)*: Higher forces robustness but risks unrecoverable generators; lower yields weaker immunity.  
- *Bandit exploration*: More exploration stabilizes selection but slows convergence; EXP3.P balances via γ.  
- *Neumann truncation (K)*: Deeper series improves hyper‑gradient accuracy but increases compute; K capped at 10.

**Failure Signatures:**  
- Stable rank of collapsed embeddings does not decrease → collapse ineffective.  
- Generator reconstruction loss rises over time → property vectors/generators failing to capture invariant traits.  
- Bandit selection frequencies remain uniform → ℓₘ too noisy or bandit misconfigured.  
- Hyper‑parameters diverge → BHGD step size too large or series truncation insufficient.

**First 3 Experiments:**  
1. **Fixed‑modality collapse:** Train with always collapsing audio; evaluate robustness when audio is missing at test.  
2. **Ablate property pathway:** Remove pₘ and generators; measure drop in reconstruction quality and robustness under missing modalities.  
3. **BHGD vs. grid search:** Tune {λ,η,κ} via BHGD and via grid search on validation; compare final robustness scores and total tuning GPU‑hours.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
1. **Simultaneous deprivation & diffusion priors:** How does ModalImmune degrade when two or more modalities are missing? Can language‑guided diffusion priors hallucinate absent context? (Current experiments only cover ≤2 missing modalities; triple‑deprivation untested.)
2. **Causal guarantees:** Can we formalize causal guarantees that self‑destructive interventions selectively remove destructive information while preserving task‑relevant signals? (Method framed as causal intervention but no proof.)
3. **Property vector theory:** What is the theoretical principle behind property vector design (e.g., “average spectral slope”)? Can it be replaced by a principled, adaptive prior? (Ablation shows property pathway dominant but design is ad‑hoc.)
4. **Information‑gain surrogate:** Is ℓₘ = (Lₘᵢₛₛᵢₙɡ − Lₘᵢₛₛᵢₙɡ\{ₘ})² justified as an estimate of conditional mutual information? Could other surrogates improve bandit efficiency? (No comparison to entropy‑based measures.)
5. **Scaling of Neumann truncation:** How does Hessian spectral norm β scale with model size, modality count, and dataset complexity? Does it impact practicality for larger models? (β≈6.1 on MOSI; scaling properties unexplored.)

## Limitations
- **Missing implementation details:** Exact weights α,β,γ for reconstruction losses; encoder specifications (e.g., BERT, openSMILE dimensions); CovB computation scope (batch vs. dataset).  
- **Potential collapse instability:** If λ or η are mis‑set, collapsed modality may become irrecoverable; no analysis of sensitivity.  
- **Bandit variance:** ℓₘ based on batch loss differences may be noisy; no study of convergence or regret.  
- **Evaluation scope:** Limited to three datasets; real‑world modality corruptions (e.g., sensor noise) not tested.  
- **BHGD transparency:** Neumann truncation rule and validation usage details scarce; overhead breakdown not provided.

## Confidence
