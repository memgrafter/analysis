# Enabling Option Learning in Sparse Rewards with Hindsight Experience Replay

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2602.13865  
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.13865  
- **Reference count:** 40  
- **Primary result:** MOC-2HER achieves up to 90% success on Fetch manipulation tasks (Push, Slide, PickAndPlace), while MOC and MOC-HER remain below 11%.

## Executive Summary
Hierarchical Reinforcement Learning (HRL) methods like Multi-updates Option Critic (MOC) fail in sparse-reward, multi-goal environments such as object manipulation tasks because the reward depends solely on the object's final state, creating a credit assignment problem where the agent cannot learn necessary interactions. The paper first integrates Hindsight Experience Replay (HER) with MOC (MOC-HER) to densify rewards by relabeling goals from achieved outcomes. However, MOC-HER remains insufficient for manipulation because it only relabels object positions, not explicitly incentivizing agent-object contact. The core solution is Dual Objectives HER (2HER), which also generates hindsight goals from the agent's effector positions and combines rewards for interaction and task completion. On Fetch Robotics benchmarks, MOC-2HER achieves up to 90% success on Push, Slide, and PickAndPlace tasks, whereas MOC and MOC-HER obtain less than 11%. For FetchReach, MOC-HER already reaches near 100% success.

## Method Summary
The method extends the off-policy HRL algorithm Multi-updates Option Critic (MOC), which learns multiple options (temporal abstractions) simultaneously. To handle sparse rewards, it incorporates Hindsight Experience Replay (HER): after each episode, stored transitions are relabeled with alternative achieved goals sampled from future states; these are added to a separate HER buffer and merged with the main replay buffer for updates. For object manipulation, standard HER relabeling (using only object positions) fails to teach interaction. Dual Objectives HER (2HER) addresses this by additionally sampling hindsight goals from the agent's effector positions (e.g., gripper) and computing a combined reward: \( r = (1 - C_r) \cdot r_{\text{goal}} + C_r \cdot r_{\text{object}} \), where \( r_{\text{goal}} \) evaluates distance to the task goal and \( r_{\text{object}} \) evaluates distance between the agent's effector and the object. A trajectory filter discards episodes with negligible object displacement. Training uses a decay schedule for the number of relabeled goals per transition (\(k\)) and a fixed iteration to disable the 2HER objective after interaction is learned.

## Key Results
- MOC-2HER achieves ~90% success on FetchPush, FetchSlide, and FetchPickAndPlace, while MOC and MOC-HER remain below 11%.
- MOC-HER solves FetchReach (near 100% success), where MOC fails.
- MOC-2HER initially dips in performance when introducing the interaction reward but eventually converges to high success rates.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- **Claim:** Integrating a Hindsight Experience Replay (HER) buffer into the Multi-updates Option Critic (MOC) framework enables learning in sparse-reward, multi-goal environments where standard MOC fails.
- **Mechanism:** During an episode, MOC stores transitions `(state, option, action, reward, next_state)`. After the episode, for each stored transition, HER samples `k` alternative achieved goals from future states in the same trajectory. The reward for the transition is recomputed as if that sampled goal had been the intended target from the start. These relabeled transitions are added to a separate HER buffer, which is merged with MOC's main replay buffer for off-policy updates. This generates dense learning signals from otherwise unrewarding failures.
- **Core assumption:** The environment's reward function is defined relative to a goal (i.e., `reward = f(achieved_goal, desired_goal)`), and achieved goals from failed trajectories are valid alternative objectives.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract] "By relabeling goals from achieved outcomes, MOC-HER can solve sparse reward environments that are intractable for the original MOC."
  - [section 4.2] Details the implementation: storing transitions, sampling future goals via strategy `S` (e.g., `future`), recalculating rewards, and merging buffers.
  - [corpus] Weak direct evidence for *MOC-HER* specifically, but corpus shows active research on HER for sample efficiency in sparse-reward, multi-goal RL (e.g., GCHR, MRHER).
- **Break condition:** If the environment's reward is not a function of a goal state (e.g., purely action-based or dense), or if no state in a trajectory meaningfully differs from the initial state (no "achieved" alternative goals), the relabeling provides no useful signal.

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim:** Standard HER (and thus MOC-HER) is insufficient for object manipulation tasks in sparse-reward multi-goal settings because the reward depends solely on the *object's* final position, creating a "credit assignment" chasm between the agent's actions and the task outcome.
- **Mechanism:** In tasks like `FetchPush`, the `achieved_goal` is the object's position. HER relabels goals based on *where the object ended up*. This teaches the agent how to move the object to different places but provides no direct signal on *how to make contact with or influence the object* in the first place. The agent must independently discover the prerequisite interaction dynamics (e.g., moving the gripper to the box) without extrinsic reward guidance.
- **Core assumption:** The agent's effector (e.g., gripper) state is observable and distinct from the object's state in the environment's observation space.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract] "This approach is insufficient for object manipulation tasks, where the reward depends on the object reaching the goal rather than on the agent’s direct interaction. This makes it extremely difficult for HRL agents to discover how to interact with these objects."
  - [section 1] "In these environments, the reward signal is often tied only to the final state of the object... and not to the actions of the agent... Without a well-defined reward signal, this learning becomes unguided."
  - [corpus] MRHER [223254] explicitly addresses "sequential object manipulation tasks with sparse rewards," confirming this is a recognized challenge where standard goal-relabeling struggles with the interaction bottleneck.
- **Break condition:** If the task inherently requires no object interaction (e.g., `FetchReach` where `achieved_goal` is the agent's own position), or if the object's movement is trivially coupled to the agent's actions (e.g., object is attached), the dual-objective mechanism is unnecessary.

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim:** Dual Objectives HER (2HER) overcomes the interaction problem by generating a second set of hindsight goals from the *agent's effector* positions, thereby explicitly rewarding the agent for achieving spatial proximity between its body and the object.
- **Mechanism:** For each transition, besides relabeling with a future *object* position (standard HER), 2HER also samples a future *agent effector* position (e.g., gripper coordinates) and substitutes it as the goal for the object's position component in the state. The reward is recomputed as a weighted combination: `r = (1 - Cr) * r_goal(object distance to goal) + Cr * r_object(distance between agent effector and object)`. This creates a composite learning signal that encourages both interaction (`r_object -> 0`) and task completion (`r_goal -> 0`).
- **Core assumption:** Meaningful interaction can be approximated by minimizing the distance between the agent's effector and the object at some point in the trajectory. The weight `Cr` balances the two objectives.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract] "2HER also generates goals from the agent’s effector positions, rewarding the agent for both interacting with the object and completing the task."
  - [section 4] Equation (1) defines the combined reward. Algorithm 1 lines 19-29 describe the 2HER logic: sampling agent position `s'_obj_pos` and calculating `r'` with coefficient `Cr`.
  - [section 5.2] Empirical validation: MOC-2HER achieves ~90% success in `FetchPush`/`FetchSlide` vs. <12% for MOC-HER. Analysis shows 2HER agents initially favor interaction (sometimes pushing object away) before converging.
  - [corpus] Weak explicit evidence for *2HER*, but corpus shows related work on decomposing rewards or using auxiliary objectives for contact-rich tasks (e.g., Contact Energy Based Prioritization [251302] focuses on contact events).
- **Break condition:** If the task requires interaction types not captured by simple Euclidean distance (e.g., precise grasping, tool use), or if the agent's effector and object positions are not simultaneously observable, the `r_object` signal is misleading. The `Cr` schedule (`2HER disable` iteration) is also critical; disabling too early prevents task completion learning, too late can destabilize.

## Foundational Learning
- **Concept: Options Framework (Temporal Abstraction)**
  - **Why needed here:** The paper builds on MOC, an HRL method that learns *options*—temporal abstractions (sub-policies) that execute over multiple steps. The core challenge is learning these reusable skills in sparse-reward settings where primitive actions rarely receive feedback.
  - **Quick check question:** If an option is a "closed-loop policy executing over an extended period," what are its three tuple components (`I_o`, `π_o`, `β_o`) and what does each govern?

- **Concept: Hindsight Experience Replay (Goal-Relabeling)**
  - **Why needed here:** HER is the fundamental mechanism to densify sparse rewards by post-hoc goal reassignment. Understanding how it transforms failure into learning data is prerequisite to understanding MOC-HER and 2HER.
  - **Quick check question:** Given a failed trajectory `(s0, g, ..., sT)` where `sT` is far from `g`, how does HER using the `future` strategy create a valid training transition from `st` to `st+1`?

- **Concept: Multi-Goal RL Formulation (Achieved vs. Desired Goal)**
  - **Why needed here:** The Fetch environments use the `achieved_goal`/`desired_goal` concatenation in the state. This formalization is why HER/2HER are possible—the reward is a function of the *difference* between these two vectors. Distinguishing which part of the state vector is the "object" vs. "agent" is critical for 2HER's dual objectives.
  - **Quick check question:** In `FetchPush`, what specific part of the observation vector is used as `achieved_goal`? How does this differ from `FetchReach`?

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map:**
  - **MOC Core:** Contains option policy `π_ζ(a|s,o)`, termination `β_ν(s,o)`, option-value `Q_θ(s,o)`, and option selector `μ_z(o|s)`. Updates all options simultaneously off-policy.
  - **HER Buffer (`R`):** A FIFO buffer storing relabeled transitions `(s_t || g', o_t, a_t, r', s_{t+1} || s'_obj_pos || g')`. The `||` denotes state concatenation with new goals.
  - **2HER Logic:** A conditional module triggered by `has_object` (environment property). It samples `s'_obj_pos` from *agent effector* future positions and combines rewards using `C_r`.
  - **Main Replay Buffer:** Holds raw MOC transitions. The combined buffer for training is a merge of this and `R`.
  - **Training Loop:** Collect trajectory → store in main buffer → process trajectory with HER/2HER → fill `R` → sample merged mini-batches → perform MOC's evaluation and improvement updates on all options.

- **Critical path:**
  1. Agent executes an option `o` in state `s_t`, takes action `a_t`, transitions to `s_{t+1}`.
  2. Store raw transition `(s_t, o_t, a_t, r_t, s_{t+1})` in MOC's main buffer.
  3. **Post-episode:** For each raw transition, apply HER: sample `k` future `g'` from object's achieved goals. If `has_object`, also apply 2HER: sample `s'_obj_pos` from agent's effector goals. Recalculate `r'` using `C_r`. Form concatenated state `s_t || g'` and `s_{t+1} || s'_obj_pos || g'`. Store in HER buffer `R`.
  4. When enough data collected, sample mini-batch from **merged** buffer (main + `R`).
  5. For each option `õ` in the set, compute TD error `δ` and perform:
     - **Evaluation:** `θ ← θ + p(õ|s,¯o) * α * δ * φ(s,õ)`
     - **Improvement:** `ζ ← ζ + p(õ|s,¯o) * α_ζ * ∇_ζ log π_ζ(a|s,o) * Q_θ(s,o,a)`
  6. Update termination `ν` and option policy `z` as per MOC.
  7. Clear buffers and repeat.

- **Design tradeoffs:**
  - **`k` (number of relabeled goals per transition):** Higher `k` increases data diversity but dilutes real transitions, risking instability. Paper uses a **decay schedule** (`k_decay`): start high for fast initial learning, decay over iterations.
  - **`C_r` (reward coefficient):** Balances interaction vs. task-completion incentives. High `C_r` (e.g., 1.0 in `PickAndPlace`) emphasizes learning pickup first; lower values (0.6-0.8) blend objectives. Environment-specific tuning required.
  - **`2HER disable` iteration:** Determines when to stop the 2HER objective (switch to pure HER). Crucial for `PickAndPlace` to shift focus to placing after picking is learned. Premature disable hinders interaction; late disable harms final task accuracy.
  - **Trajectory Filter:** Paper mentions restricting HER to trajectories with object displacement `> δ`. Not in Algorithm 1 but in text (Section 4.2). Saves computation on useless trajectories.

- **Failure signatures:**
  - **Option Collapse:** One option dominates (>80% usage) early while others go unused, indicating failure to discover diverse skills. (Observed in *failed* baselines like MOC, IOC in object tasks).
  - **Initial Performance Dip:** With 2HER, early training may show *worse* success rate as agent is rewarded for moving object *away* from goal while learning interaction. Failure if dip is permanent or too deep.
  - **Instability with High `k` or Many Options:** Success rate declines in final training stages (e.g., IOC-2HER with 8 options in `FetchPush`/`Slide`). Indicates overfitting to hindsight or degenerate option set.
  - **`FetchReach` solved by MOC-HER but not MOC:** Confirms the necessity of HER for any sparse multi-goal task; failure here means HER implementation is broken.

- **First 3 experiments:**
  1. **FetchReach (no object):** Verify MOC-HER works. Expect near 100% success; MOC should fail (<10%). Check that all options remain active (no collapse) for MOC-HER.
  2. **FetchPush (object interaction):** Compare MOC-HER (should fail ~11%) vs. MOC-2HER (should reach ~90%). Observe initial dip in 2HER curve. Validate `C_r=0.8` and `k=8` with decay `37`.
  3. **Option Utilization Analysis (Post-training):** For 4-option configuration in `FetchPush`, plot usage. MOC-2HER should show strong specialization (one option >50%, all >2%). Failed MOC should show unstable, near-uniform usage. This tests if multi-option updates lead to meaningful skill diversity.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- **Question:** How does MOC-2HER perform in environments with dynamically moving or randomized object positions across episodes?
- **Basis in paper:** [explicit] The authors state: "The experimental setup was restricted to a static object placement, where the object’s position was randomized once at the start of each training run and then remained fixed for all subsequent episodes."
- **Why unresolved:** This limitation means the method's robustness to dynamic changes is unknown, which is critical for real-world applications where objects may move or be introduced variably.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Conduct experiments in modified Fetch environments where object positions are randomized per episode or change during episodes, and measure success rates compared to static setups to assess stability and sample efficiency.

### Open Question 2
- **Question:** Can the 2HER mechanism be effectively integrated with on-policy HRL algorithms, and does it maintain performance benefits in sparse reward settings?
- **Basis in paper:** [explicit] The limitation: "our method's reliance on HER makes it inherently compatible only with off-policy algorithms."
- **Why unresolved:** On-policy HRL methods are widely used, and without adaptation, 2HER cannot be applied to them, limiting its scope and potential impact in the HRL field.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Develop an on-policy version of 2HER, e.g., by modifying the replay buffer usage or using importance sampling for policy gradients, and test against on-policy baselines like A2OC or PPOC in sparse reward tasks to compare success rates and convergence.

### Open Question 3
- **Question:** Does 2HER generalize to non-robotic domains such as navigation or multi-agent coordination, and does it provide similar performance improvements over standard HRL?
- **Basis in paper:** [explicit] Future work: "extending 2HER to other domains, such as navigation, dynamic-object interaction or multi-agent coordination, represents an important next step."
- **Why unresolved:** The paper only validates on Fetch Robotics manipulation tasks; generalizability to other domains is speculative and untested, which affects the broader applicability of the approach.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Implement 2HER in diverse environments like Maze navigation, collaborative multi-agent tasks, or video game settings, and compare success rates and option quality with standard HRL and HER methods to measure transfer of benefits.

### Open Question 4
- **Question:** Is the fixed coefficient Cr for balancing goal and interaction rewards optimal, or should it adapt during training for better performance across varied tasks?
- **Basis in paper:** [inferred] The method uses a fixed Cr or simple decay schedules, but the paper does not explore adaptive schemes; this design choice might not be universally optimal given task complexities.
- **Why unresolved:** The effectiveness of Cr likely depends on task specifics or learning stages, and a static value could lead to suboptimal exploration or convergence in some scenarios.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Train agents with adaptive Cr, such as learned via meta-gradients or adjusted based on success rate or interaction metrics, and evaluate on tasks with different interaction requirements to see if performance improves over fixed schedules.

### Open Question 5
- **Question:** How does the specialization of options in sparse reward settings with 2HER affect the transferability of learned options to new tasks, and is there a trade-off between performance and option diversity?
- **Basis in paper:** [inferred] Results show option utilization becomes highly imbalanced, with one option dominating; while all options remain active, the paper does not analyze transfer potential or the implications of such specialization.
- **Why unresolved:** Specialized options might be less reusable across tasks, which is a key advantage of HRL; this trade-off between task-specific performance and general option utility is unexplored.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** After training on a source task with 2HER, evaluate option reuse on a target task by freezing options and measuring success rates, and correlate with utilization rates to understand how specialization impacts transferability.

## Limitations
- Evaluation limited to Fetch Robotics benchmarks; unknown if 2HER generalizes to other sparse-reward domains (e.g., locomotion with object interaction, real-world robotics).
- Hyperparameter sensitivity (Cr schedule, 2HER disable iteration, k decay) not systematically analyzed; may require per-environment tuning.
- Theoretical justification for why dual objectives prevent option collapse remains informal; lacks formal analysis of option diversification under 2HER.

## Confidence
- MOC-HER enables learning in sparse multi-goal environments where MOC fails: **High**
- Standard HER insufficient for object manipulation due to credit assignment chasm: **Medium**
- 2HER’s dual objectives solve the interaction bottleneck: **High**
- Generalization beyond Fetch benchmarks: **Low**

## Next Checks
- **Cross-Domain Validation:** Test MOC-2HER on at least two non-Fetch sparse-reward environments (e.g., Adroit hand tasks or MRHER’s benchmarks) to probe generalizability.
- **Ablation on Reward Components:** Isolate contributions of r_goal vs. r_object by training with Cr=0 (pure HER) and Cr=1 (pure interaction) across phases; measure impact on learning curves and option specialization.
- **Hyperparameter Robustness Sweep:** Systematically vary Cr, 2HER disable iteration, and k_decay in FetchPush; map performance landscape to identify stable configurations and critical thresholds.
