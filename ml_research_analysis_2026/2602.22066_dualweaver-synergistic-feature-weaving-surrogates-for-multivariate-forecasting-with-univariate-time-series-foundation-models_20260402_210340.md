# DualWeaver: Synergistic Feature Weaving Surrogates for Multivariate Forecasting with Univariate Time Series Foundation Models

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID**: 2602.22066
- **Source URL**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.22066
- **Reference count**: 40
- **Primary result**: DualWeaver adapts univariate time series foundation models for multivariate forecasting via symmetric surrogate series, achieving state-of-the-art accuracy, robustness to noise, and efficient scaling.

## Executive Summary
DualWeaver addresses the challenge of adapting univariate time series foundation models (TSFMs) for multivariate forecasting, where cross-variable dependencies are critical but ignored by standard fine-tuning. It introduces a pair of learnable, structurally symmetric surrogate series generated by a shared feature-fusion module. These surrogates are mapped to TSFM-compatible inputs and combined via a non-parametric reconstruction to produce multivariate forecasts. A theoretically grounded regularization stabilizes training and prevents adaptation collapse. Extensive experiments show consistent state-of-the-art performance across multiple datasets, superior robustness to irrelevant noise channels, and efficient scaling to high dimensions.

## Method Summary
DualWeaver adapts a frozen univariate TSFM to multivariate forecasting by learning only two lightweight components: a feature-fusion module \(f(\cdot)\) and channel-wise weights \(w_\alpha, w_\beta\). From an input multivariate window \(X\), it constructs dual surrogates \(S_\alpha = f(X) + w_\alpha \odot X\) and \(S_\beta = f(X) - w_\beta \odot X\). The frozen TSFM independently forecasts each surrogate channel, yielding predictions \(\hat{S}_\alpha\) and \(\hat{S}_\beta\). The final multivariate forecast \(\hat{Y}\) is reconstructed non-parametrically as \(\hat{Y} = (\hat{S}_\alpha - \hat{S}_\beta) \odot (1/(w_\alpha + w_\beta))\). The training objective minimizes the surrogate prediction errors plus a regularization term \(L_{\text{bound}}\) derived from a theoretical error bound, which prevents the weights from collapsing and ensures the adapted model does not degrade below the univariate baseline. Only \(f(\cdot)\) and the weights are updated; the backbone remains frozen, preserving its pre-trained knowledge while enabling cross-variable information exchange.

## Key Results
- Reduces average MSE by 8.95% and MAE by 6.07% compared to the vanilla TimerXL model, and by 3.91% MSE / 1.55% MAE over full fine-tuning.
- Demonstrates superior robustness when irrelevant noise channels are added; errors remain stable while baselines (e.g., AdaPTS) degrade significantly.
- Scales efficiently to high-dimensional datasets (e.g., 321 variables in ECL) and outperforms adapter-based methods like AdaPTS.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- **Claim**: The symmetric dual-surrogate design enables non-parametric reconstruction of multivariate forecasts from univariate foundation model outputs, avoiding capacity loss from parametric decoding.
- **Mechanism**: A shared feature-fusion module \(f(\cdot)\) generates a fused representation from multivariate input \(X\). Two surrogates are constructed as \(S_\alpha = f(X) + w_\alpha \odot X\) and \(S_\beta = f(X) - w_\beta \odot X\). The frozen univariate TSFM \(M\) independently forecasts each surrogate channel. Final predictions are reconstructed via \(\hat{Y} = (1/(w_\alpha+w_\beta)) \odot (M(S_\alpha) - M(S_\beta))\). The \(f(X)\) terms cancel in the subtraction, so reconstruction requires no additional parameters and forces \(f(\cdot)\) to extract only dependencies that improve surrogate predictability.
- **Core assumption**: The cross-variable dependencies useful for forecasting can be represented as an additive term \(f(X)\) that, when combined inversely in the two surrogates, cancels out during reconstruction while making each surrogate more predictable individually.
- **Evidence anchors**:
  - Abstract: "Their symmetric design enables direct, parameter-free reconstruction of final multivariate predictions from the surrogate outputs, avoiding the capacity loss of encoder-decoder adapters."
  - Section 3.1: Equations (1)-(3) and surrounding text describing non-parametric reconstruction.
  - Corpus: Indirect evidence; other papers highlight capacity loss in encoder-decoder adapters, contrasting with DualWeaver.
- **Break condition**: If \(f(X)\) is poorly initialized or fails to learn meaningful cross-variable signals, the surrogates may not become more predictable than the original series, yielding no improvement. Also, if \(w_\alpha + w_\beta\) approaches zero (numerically unstable), reconstruction amplifies errors despite the regularization penalty.

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim**: The theoretically motivated regularization term \(L_{\text{bound}}\) stabilizes training and prevents adaptation collapse by enforcing a sufficient condition for the surrogate-space error to remain below the univariate baseline error.
- **Mechanism**: From the error bound derivation (Section 3.2), per-channel error \(\Omega_i = 2/(w_i^\alpha + w_i^\beta)^2 \cdot (E_{i\alpha} + E_{i\beta})\). The condition \(\Omega_i \leq E_{i\text{ori}}\) (univariate error) is used as a regularization term \(L_{\text{bound}}\). During optimization, this penalty prevents the weights \(w_\alpha, w_\beta\) from becoming too small (which would blow up the error bound) and encourages the surrogate errors \(E_{i\alpha}, E_{i\beta}\) to stay below the original error, thus averting catastrophic divergence.
- **Core assumption**: The univariate baseline error \(E_{i\text{ori}}\) is a meaningful reference, and satisfying \(\Omega_i \leq E_{i\text{ori}}\) is sufficient to guarantee that the adapted model does not perform worse than the frozen univariate model on each channel.
- **Evidence anchors**:
  - Abstract: "A theoretically grounded regularization term is further introduced to enhance robustness against adaptation collapse."
  - Section 3.2: Equations (8)-(13) and the error bound derivation.
  - Section 4.4.1: Empirical validation: without regularization, validation error grows exponentially; with it, training remains stable.
- **Break condition**: If the univariate baseline \(E_{i\text{ori}}\) is extremely high (e.g., on very noisy data), the condition may be too lenient to prevent collapse. Conversely, if the surrogate errors \(E_{i\alpha}, E_{i\beta}\) cannot be reduced below \(E_{i\text{ori}}\) (e.g., due to extremely weak cross-variable signals), the regularization may overly constrain learning and limit performance gains.

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim**: The residual-like surrogate construction (\(f(X) \pm w \odot X\)) confers robustness to irrelevant or noisy channels by allowing the contribution of \(f(X)\) to adaptively diminish when cross-variable correlations are weak.
- **Mechanism**: When cross-variable dependencies are sparse, the optimal \(f(X)\) may approach zero or become non-predictive. The weights \(w_\alpha, w_\beta\) can then scale down the original series \(X\) in the surrogates, effectively reverting to channel-independent forecasting. In contrast, encoder-decoder adapters (e.g., AdaPTS) force a latent representation that may overfit to spurious correlations from irrelevant channels, causing performance degradation when noise is added.
- **Core assumption**: The optimization of \(f(\cdot)\) and \(w_\alpha, w_\beta\) can discover that \(f(X)\) is uninformative under weak correlations and correspondingly adjust \(w_\alpha, w_\beta\) to reduce reliance on \(f(X)\).
- **Evidence anchors**:
  - Abstract: "It demonstrates superior robustness when irrelevant noise channels are added, maintaining stable errors while baselines degrade."
  - Section 4.2.3: Figure 4 and Table 9: DualWeaver's error remains stable as noise channels increase, while AdaPTS and Chronos-2 degrade.
  - Section 4.2.3: "Instead, DualWeaver employs a residual-like surrogate mechanism that integrates multivariate insights as a supplement to the original series. Under weak-correlation conditions, this increment can adaptively decrease..."
- **Break condition**: If the feature-fusion module \(f(\cdot)\) is over-parameterized or heavily regularized to always produce non-zero outputs, it may fail to "turn off" when cross-variable signals are weak, forcing the model to use noisy \(f(X)\) and harming robustness.

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map**: Multivariate input \(X\) -> Feature-fusion module \(f(\cdot)\) -> dual surrogates \(S_\alpha = f(X) + w_\alpha \odot X\), \(S_\beta = f(X) - w_\beta \odot X\) -> Frozen Uni-TSFM \(M\) (processes each channel independently) -> predictions \(\hat{S}_\alpha, \hat{S}_\beta\) -> Reconstruction \(\hat{Y} = (\hat{S}_\alpha - \hat{S}_\beta) \odot 1/(w_\alpha + w_\beta)\). Only \(f(\cdot)\), \(w_\alpha\), \(w_\beta\) are trainable.
- **Critical path**:
  1. Forward pass: Compute \(f(X)\), compute \(S_\alpha, S_\beta\). Pass each through \(M\) to get \(\hat{S}_\alpha, \hat{S}_\beta\). Reconstruct \(\hat{Y}\).
  2. Compute surrogate losses \(L_\alpha = \text{MSE}(\hat{S}_\alpha, \tilde{S}_\alpha)\), \(L_\beta = \text{MSE}(\hat{S}_\beta, \tilde{S}_\beta)\) with \(\tilde{S}_\alpha = f(Y) + w_\alpha \odot Y\), \(\tilde{S}_\beta = f(Y) - w_\beta \odot Y\).
  3. Compute regularization \(L_{\text{bound}}\) from per-channel error bound.
  4. Total loss = \((L_\alpha + L_\beta)/2 + \lambda \cdot L_{\text{bound}}\).
  5. Backpropagate to update only \(f(\cdot)\), \(w_\alpha\), \(w_\beta\).
  6. Repeat for epochs with early stopping.
- **Design tradeoffs**:
  - Shared \(f(\cdot)\) reduces parameters and encourages complementary surrogates but may limit capacity for distinct cross-variable patterns.
  - Channel-wise weights \(w_\alpha, w_\beta\) are extremely lightweight (2C parameters) but assume independent scaling per variable, potentially missing variable-specific interactions.
  - Zero initialization of \(f(\cdot)\)'s final layer ensures training starts from the foundation model's original predictions, preserving stability but possibly slowing convergence.
  - Extensibility: \(f(\cdot)\) can be replaced with CNN/GNN/Transformer to capture more complex dependencies at higher computational cost.
- **Failure signatures**:
  - Training instability: exploding loss/NaN without \(L_{\text{bound}}\) or with \(\lambda\) too low; monitor \(w_\alpha + w_\beta\) to avoid near-zero.
  - No improvement: \(f(X)\) remains near zero, indicating failure to learn cross-variable signals; check \(\|f(X)\|\).
  - Poor noise robustness: degradation with added noise channels suggests \(f(\cdot)\) overfitting to spurious correlations; consider simpler \(f(\cdot)\) or stronger regularization.
  - Numerical instability in reconstruction: \(w_\alpha + w_\beta\) approaching zero triggers division blowup; ensure \(L_{\text{bound}}\) prevents this.
  - Memory overflow: long lookback (L=2880) or many variables (e.g., 321) may exceed memory; use gradient accumulation or checkpointing.
- **First 3 experiments**:
  1. Reconstruction sanity check: With \(f\) zero-initialized and \(w_\alpha = w_\beta = 1\), surrogates become \(S_\alpha = X\), \(S_\beta = -X\). For a linear channel-independent \(M\), verify \(\hat{Y} = M(X)\). Even for non-linear \(M\), reconstruction error should be small.
  2. Surrogate predictability: After training, compute MSE of \(M\) on \(S_\alpha/S_\beta\) vs. on original \(X\). Expect significant reduction (~85% as in Fig 5). If not, \(f(\cdot)\) failed to improve predictability.
  3. Noise-channel robustness: Add 5-10 random noise channels to a dataset (e.g., ETTh1). Compare error growth of DualWeaver vs. AdaPTS; DualWeaver should show minimal increase.

## Limitations
- Implementation gaps: exact stride for windowing, selected learning rates per dataset, and horizon handling (single vs. multi-horizon model) are unspecified, risking reproducibility mismatches.
- Theoretical assumption dependency: The error-bound regularization relies on the condition \(\Omega_i \leq E_{i\text{ori}}\); its sufficiency may not hold if \(E_{i\text{ori}}\) is anomalously high or if cross-variable signals are too weak.
- Generality of \(f(\cdot)\) design: The default MLP's capacity for complex dependencies in highly non-linear systems is not benchmarked against stronger fusion architectures.

## Confidence
- **High**: Empirical SOTA results across multiple real-world datasets (ETT, Weather, Electricity) and robustness to noise channels—directly validated in Section 4.
- **Medium**: Theoretical regularization \(L_{\text{bound}}\) preventing adaptation collapse—empirically supported (Fig 7) but hinges on the assumption that \(E_{i\text{ori}}\) is a stable reference.
- **Medium**: Symmetric reconstruction avoiding capacity loss—conceptually sound and contrasted with adapter-based methods, but the “non-parametric” claim depends on exact weight initialization and stability of \(w_\alpha + w_\beta\).

## Next Checks
