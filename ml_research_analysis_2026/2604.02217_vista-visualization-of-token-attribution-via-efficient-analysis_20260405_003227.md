# VISTA: Visualization of Token Attribution via Efficient Analysis

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2604.02217  
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.02217  
- **Reference count:** 0  
- **Primary result:** A lightweight, model-agnostic token importance scoring framework for LLMs using perturbation-based analysis with three matrices, achieving deterministic O(n*d) complexity and interpretable token rankings (e.g., "AI" score 10.6 vs. "The" 0.0018).

## Executive Summary
VISTA introduces a novel, efficient method for attributing importance to individual tokens in LLM prompts without requiring model internals or backpropagation. By treating a prompt as a sum of static word embeddings, it measures how removing each token perturbs the aggregate embedding along three geometric dimensions: angular deviation (semantic direction shift), magnitude deviation (semantic intensity change), and dimensional importance (per-dimension contribution). These scores are multiplicatively combined into a composite importance score, optionally refined by a Generalized Additive Model (GAM) to account for position and non-linear interactions. The approach is deterministic, runs in linear time relative to prompt length and embedding dimension, and produces human-interpretable rankings, validated on GloVe embeddings.

## Method Summary
Given a tokenized prompt and pre-trained GloVe embeddings, VISTA first sums the token vectors to obtain an aggregate prompt embedding `E_agg`. For each token `t_k`, it computes a perturbed embedding `E_pert = E_agg - E(t_k)`. Three scores are derived:  
1. **Angular Deviation**: `(1 - cos(θ))²`, where `cos(θ)` is the cosine similarity between `E_agg` and `E_pert`, measuring directional shift.  
2. **Magnitude Deviation**: Normalized absolute difference in L2 norms, capturing change in semantic salience.  
3. **Dimensional Importance**: Sum over embedding dimensions of `|E(t_k)[j]|` weighted by a function that increases when the token's sign opposes the aggregate's sign on that dimension.  
The composite score is the product of these three. An optional GAM adjusts the final percentile rank using features `[Score_ang, Score_mag, Score_dim, position_percentile]`.

## Key Results
- Enables model-agnostic token attribution without backpropagation overhead.  
- Demonstrates interpretable discriminative power (e.g., "AI" scores orders of magnitude higher than stopwords like "The" in example prompts).  
- Achieves O(n*d) time complexity, making it scalable for long prompts.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1: Angular Deviation as Semantic Direction Anchor
A token's importance is indicated by how much its removal rotates the aggregate prompt embedding's primary semantic direction. Removal changes the vector sum `E_perturbed`; the cosine angle `cos(θ)` between `E_original` and `E_perturbed` measures directional alignment. Smaller `cos(θ)` (larger `1-cos(θ)`) implies a greater shift in the prompt's core topic or intent. Squaring yields `Score_angular`. Core assumption: the aggregate vector's direction encodes the prompt's central topic, and key topical/entity tokens are major contributors. Break condition: if tokens are semantically redundant or the prompt's direction is defined by a very small set of tokens, a moderately important token may cause little angular shift.

### Mechanism 2: Magnitude Deviation as Semantic Intensity Metric
A token's importance is indicated by how much its removal changes the L2 norm (magnitude) of the aggregate embedding. The absolute difference `||E_original|| - ||E_perturbed||` normalized by `||E_original||` captures the token's contribution to the overall "semantic weight" or salience. Core assumption: the magnitude of the summed embedding vector correlates with collective semantic intensity or informativeness. Break condition: if a token's vector partially cancels others (e.g., a negation), its removal might increase the norm, but the absolute difference still captures its contribution. If a token is perfectly orthogonal to the main semantic mass, removal may cause negligible magnitude change.

### Mechanism 3: Dimensional Importance as Semantic Nuance Detector
A token's importance is indicated by its contribution across individual embedding dimensions, particularly where it introduces novel features or provides contrastive balance to the aggregate vector. For each dimension `j`, a weight is computed based on `|E(t_k)[j]|` and a function `g` that assigns higher weight when the token's sign opposes the aggregate's sign (indicating contrast). Summing over all 50 dimensions yields `Score_dimensional`. Core assumption: latent dimensions in static embeddings encode interpretable semantic axes (e.g., valence), and tokens that fill gaps or create balance across these axes are important for a nuanced semantic profile. Break condition: if embedding dimensions are not semantically interpretable (a known limitation of static embeddings), the weights may not correspond to meaningful features; if the aggregate vector is near-zero on a dimension, a token's contribution might be undervalued.

## Foundational Learning
- **Concept: Vector Space Semantics & Additive Aggregation**  
  Why needed: The entire method treats a prompt as a single vector (`E_aggregate`) computed by summing token embeddings, relying on the geometric representation of meaning.  
  Quick check: Why might summing word embeddings be a flawed model of sentence meaning, and how could that limitation affect VISTA's scores?

- **Concept: Perturbation-Based Ablation Analysis**  
  Why needed: The core causal inference ("token importance = disruption upon removal") relies on the ablation principle. The comparative analysis (`E_original` vs. `E_perturbed`) is the engine of all three matrices.  
  Quick check: What is a key threat to validity when inferring token importance from a single-token ablation, and does VISTA's multiplicative combination mitigate it?

- **Concept: Generalized Additive Models (GAMs) for Non-Linear Adjustment**  
  Why needed: The final pipeline uses a GAM to adjust the raw multiplicative scores for position and non-linear feature interactions, moving beyond the deterministic core.  
  Quick check: If the GAM learns that tokens in the first position get a systematic boost, what does that imply about the base three-matrix scores' independence from position?

## Architecture Onboarding
Preprocessing Module (tokenization, GloVe lookup, OOV handling) -> Core Analyzer (computes E_aggregate, loops over tokens, calculates three scores, multiplies for raw composite) -> (optional GAM Enhancer, which takes [A, M, D, position_percentile] and outputs predicted importance percentile) -> Output Formatter (renders heatmap or ranked list).

Critical path: Input Text -> Tokenization -> Embedding Lookup -> Sum to E_aggregate -> For each token: compute E_perturbed -> calculate 3 scores -> multiply -> (optional: GAM adjustment) -> Rank/Visualize.

Design tradeoffs:
- **Model-agnostic vs. embedding fidelity**: Using static GloVe sacrifices contextual meaning for efficiency and broad applicability.
- **Multiplicative vs. additive combination**: Multiplication enforces high scores on all three dimensions but may overly penalize tokens strong in only two; additive would inflate such tokens.
- **Determinism vs. adaptivity**: The core method is deterministic and fast but static; the GAM adds adaptivity but requires training data.

Failure signatures:
- All tokens receive uniformly low composite scores → likely `E_aggregate` is near-zero (e.g., all OOV tokens or very short prompt).
- Stopwords ("the", "a") rank highly → check dimensional weighting `g()` function; if it doesn't sufficiently penalize low-magnitude tokens, they may get spurious high `D`.
- GAM predictions contradict raw scores drastically → check GAM training data distribution; may have learned a position bias not present in the test prompt's domain.

First 3 experiments:
1. **Sanity Check - Known Prompt**: Run on "The AI system processes natural language effectively" from Section 4.4. Verify scores match the paper's example (e.g., "AI" >> "The").
2. **Ablation - Single Matrix**: Compute rankings using each matrix (`Score_angular`, `Score_magnitude`, `Score_dimensional`) alone. Observe how rankings differ (e.g., "not" ranks high only in `D`).
3. **GAM Impact Analysis**: Compare the ranking from raw multiplicative scores vs. GAM-adjusted percentiles on a set of prompts with varied token positions. Quantify rank correlation (e.g., Spearman's ρ).

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
1. How does the framework's reliance on static, non-contextual GloVe embeddings affect its fidelity when applied to modern contextual LLMs like GPT or BERT, where token meanings are highly context-dependent?  
2. Does the core assumption that prompt semantics are adequately modeled by a simple vector sum of token embeddings hold for syntactically complex or semantically interactive prompts?  
3. Is the multiplicative combination of the three matrix scores the optimal fusion strategy, or do alternative aggregation methods (e.g., learned linear weights, non-linear MLPs) yield more accurate or robust token importance rankings?  
4. Can the GAM-based enhancement consistently improve ranking accuracy across diverse domains and prompt lengths, or does its benefit primarily come from capturing position biases in specific, short prompt distributions?  
5. How sensitive are the final token importance rankings to the specific choice of pre-trained static embeddings (e.g., GloVe vs. fastText vs. word2vec)?

## Limitations
- **Dimensional Importance ambiguity**: The weighting function `g` for sign contrast is unspecified, hindering exact replication of `Score_dim`.
- **GAM opaqueness**: The training data, hyperparameters, and final GAM parameters are not released, limiting exact reproduction of the enhanced rankings.
- **Static embedding constraints**: Using non-contextual GloVe sacrifices token polysemy resolution, potentially harming accuracy for domain-specific or ambiguous terms.

## Confidence
- **Angular/Magnitude Deviation mechanisms:** Medium (geometric intuition plausible but lacks direct corpus validation; break conditions noted)
- **Dimensional Importance mechanism:** Low (heavily reliant on interpretable GloVe dimensions; no evidence that sign-flipping aligns with semantic nuance)
- **Multiplicative composite and GAM:** Medium (multiplicative enforcement may over-penalize; GAM's adjustments conceptually sound but untransparent)
