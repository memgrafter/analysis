# Stock Market Prediction Using Node Transformer Architecture Integrated with BERT Sentiment Analysis

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2603.05917
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.05917
- **Reference count:** 29
- **Primary result:** Achieves one-day-ahead stock price MAPE of 0.80%, beating ARIMA (1.20%) and LSTM (1.00%) with statistically significant improvements.

## Executive Summary
This paper introduces a novel framework for stock market prediction that integrates a node transformer architecture with BERT-based sentiment analysis. The model constructs a dynamic graph of stocks, where nodes represent individual equities and edges capture learnable inter-stock relationships. Quantitative price data and technical indicators are processed through the graph-aware transformer, while social media sentiment is extracted via a fine-tuned BERT model. These two streams are fused adaptively based on market volatility and sentiment magnitude. Empirical results on a 20-stock S&P 500 subset from 1982–2025 show a one-day ahead mean absolute percentage error (MAPE) of 0.80%, outperforming classical and deep learning baselines. The integration of sentiment reduces error by 10% overall and 25% during earnings announcements, and the graph structure contributes an additional 15% improvement. Directional accuracy reaches 65%, and the model maintains robustness with MAPE below 1.5% even in high-volatility regimes. All gains are statistically significant (p<0.05).

## Method Summary
The proposed system processes two parallel data streams. The quantitative stream uses 17 features (OHLCV plus 11 technical indicators) for 20 S&P 500 stocks, normalized via an expanding-window z-score. Stocks are embedded as nodes in a fully-connected graph; edge weights are initialized from sector similarity and return correlation, then refined per transformer layer via a sigmoid function over node representations. A six-layer node transformer applies causal multi-head attention with temporal encoding and feature gating. The sentiment stream fine-tunes BERT on a financial dataset (MSE) to produce daily sentiment scores scaled to [-1,+1]; these are smoothed over 1, 5, and 20-day windows. Sentiment guides the transformer’s attention by scaling keys and feeds a separate prediction head. An adaptive fusion gate, conditioned on rolling volatility and mean absolute sentiment, blends the two predictions. The model is trained with a composite loss (MSE, directional BCE, correlation, L2) using a three-stage procedure (freeze, partial unfreeze, full fine-tune) on data from 1982–2010, with validation on 2011–2016 and test on 2017–2025.

## Key Results
- One-day ahead MAPE of 0.80%, outperforming ARIMA (1.20%) and LSTM (1.00%).
- Sentiment integration reduces MAPE by 10% overall and 25% during earnings announcements.
- Graph structure contributes an additional 15% improvement in MAPE.
- Directional accuracy of 65%, with MAPE staying below 1.5% during high volatility periods.
- All improvements are statistically significant (p<0.05).

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1: Graph Structure Captures Cross-Sectional Dependencies
- **Claim:** Explicitly modeling inter-stock relationships via a learnable graph structure improves forecast accuracy by capturing cross-sectional dependencies that independent models miss.
- **Mechanism:** Stocks are represented as nodes in a fully-connected graph. Edge weights are initialized using sector affiliation and historical price correlations, then refined during training via a sigmoid function applied to concatenated node representations. This allows the node transformer's attention mechanism to propagate information across related stocks, enhancing each node's contextual representation.
- **Core assumption:** Stock returns exhibit persistent, learnable interdependencies (e.g., sectoral co-movement, supply chain links) that are not fully captured by treating each time series in isolation.
- **Evidence anchors:** 
  - Abstract: "graph structure contributes an additional 15% improvement"
  - Section IV-B: Details edge weight initialization (Eq. 12) and refinement (Eq. 13).
  - Section V-E: Ablation study: "Without Graph Structure" MAPE increases by 15%.
  - Corpus: Neighboring papers (e.g., MDGNN) also explore dynamic graph relations, providing contextual plausibility.
- **Break condition:** If learned edge weights converge to uniform values or if the graph's predictive contribution vanishes in out-of-sample ablation on data not represented in the training sector/correlation structure.

### Mechanism 2: Social Media Sentiment Provides Event-Driven Signals
- **Claim:** Incorporating real-time social media sentiment via BERT provides predictive signals that are particularly valuable during information-intensive events like earnings announcements.
- **Mechanism:** A fine-tuned BERT model processes social media posts (cashtagged) to produce daily sentiment scores [-1,+1]. These scores are multi-scale smoothed (1, 5, 20-day EMAs) and integrated into the node transformer pipeline. Sentiment modulates the attention mechanism (Eq. 22) and participates in an adaptive fusion gate (Eq. 24) that dynamically balances its weight against pure price features.
- **Core assumption:** Social media discourse contains incremental information about investor expectations and reactions that is not yet fully incorporated into contemporaneous market prices.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - Abstract: "Sentiment analysis reduces prediction error by 10% overall and 25% during earnings announcements."
  - Section IV-D: Describes BERT fine-tuning on the MSE dataset and progressive unfreezing.
  - Section IV-E: Details sentiment scaling of keys (Eq. 22) and adaptive fusion (Eqs. 24-25).
  - Section V-D: Reports BERT sentiment classifier performance (87% accuracy).
  - Corpus: Several neighbors integrate social sentiment, supporting general plausibility.
- **Break condition:** If sentiment-time series becomes uncorrelated with future returns or if its marginal contribution in ablation becomes negligible, especially during event windows.

### Mechanism 3: Adaptive Fusion Gate Enables Regime-Aware Robustness
- **Claim:** An attention-based adaptive fusion mechanism that conditions the blend of price-based and sentiment-based predictions on volatility and sentiment magnitude yields more robust performance across market regimes.
- **Mechanism:** A sigmoid gate α_t is computed from rolling volatility metrics (5, 10, 20-day) and the mean absolute sentiment across stocks. This scalar weight determines the convex combination: ŷ = α_t * y_node + (1-α_t) * y_sent. The gate learns to up-weight the sentiment branch during volatile/high-sentiment periods and the node transformer branch during stable/low-sentiment periods.
- **Core assumption:** The relative predictive utility of structured price signals vs. unstructured sentiment signals is state-dependent and can be inferred from readily available market descriptors.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - Section IV-E: Full specification of adaptive fusion equations (Eqs. 24-25) and Figure 5.
  - Section V-E, Table VIII: Shows robustness during high-VIX regimes (MAPE <1.5% vs. baselines >1.6%), with gating identified as a contributor.
  - Section VI-A: "Analysis of learned gate activations showed that momentum features received higher weights during volatile periods..."
  - Corpus: Not directly addressed in neighbors; appears to be a specific architectural innovation.
- **Break condition:** If learned gate weights α_t show no systematic relationship with input volatility/sentiment (e.g., constant near 0.5) or if ablating the adaptive gate (using fixed equal weighting) does not degrade performance.

## Architecture Onboarding

### Component map
1. Data Ingestion: OHLCV + Technical Indicators (17 features) | Social Media Posts.
2. Parallel Preprocessing: Z-score normalization (expanding window) | BERT fine-tuning on MSE dataset → Sentiment score generator.
3. Graph Construction: Node features = [price, volume, indicators, temporal encoding, stock ID]. Edge matrix initialized via sector & correlation, refined per layer.
4. Node Transformer (6 layers): Graph-aware multi-head attention with causal mask + edge bias → Time-based feature gating → Position-wise FFN.
5. Sentiment Branch: Multi-scale EMA smoothing of daily scores → Sentiment-guided attention scaling (Eq. 22) → Sentiment-only prediction head.
6. Adaptive Fusion Gate: Takes volatility vector + mean absolute sentiment → outputs α_t → blends y_node and y_sent.
7. Training Objective: Composite loss (MSE + Directional BCE + Correlation + L2).

### Critical path
Raw data → Normalization & Sentiment scoring → Graph node assembly → Node transformer forward pass (with edge updates) → Parallel sentiment prediction → Fusion gate → Final prediction → Loss computation. The BERT module is a fixed pre-trained component; its gradients do not flow into the transformer.

### Design tradeoffs
- **Graph size (N=20):** Enables deep temporal analysis (252-day sequences) but limits cross-sectional coverage vs. full S&P 500. Tradeoff: computational tractability vs. market topology completeness.
- **BERT as fixed extractor:** Reduces end-to-end training complexity and prevents catastrophic forgetting but limits co-adaptation of linguistic and temporal features.
- **Adaptive gate complexity:** Adds a small, interpretable module vs. a simpler concatenation. Cost: slight parameter increase; Benefit: regime-aware robustness.

### Failure signatures
- **Graph failure:** Edge weights collapse to near-zero or uniform; performance similar to "Without Graph Structure" ablation (~15% MAPE increase). Diagnose via monitoring edge weight distributions.
- **Sentiment failure:** BERT classifier F1 <0.80 on held-out MSE test; sentiment-Return correlation near zero post-scaling. Diagnose via separate sentiment model evaluation and correlation analysis.
- **Fusion failure:** Gate weight α_t static (std. dev. <0.01) or unresponsive to VIX changes; performance similar to fixed-weight concatenation. Diagnose by ablation of adaptive gate vs. fixed 0.5 blend.

### First 3 experiments
1. **Ablation - Graph:** Train model with edge weights frozen at initialization (or set to zero) to quantify pure node transformer contribution vs. graph-enhanced.
2. **Ablation - Sentiment Source:** Replace BERT sentiment scores with a simple lexicon-based score (e.g., VADER) to test robustness of the signal source.
3. **Gate Analysis:** On validation data, plot α_t against VIX level and daily sentiment magnitude. Expect positive correlation with VIX and |S_t|. If flat, the gating mechanism is not learning the intended modulation.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- How does the node transformer's performance scale when expanding the stock graph from 20 nodes to the full S&P 500 or international markets? (Current design prioritizes temporal depth; larger graphs increase complexity and may alter relationship signals.)
- To what extent does survivorship bias inflate the reported performance, and how would a point-in-time historical index universe alter results? (Dataset includes only current S&P 500 constituents, omitting failed/acquired companies.)
- Do additional sentiment sources (e.g., Reddit, financial news, earnings calls) and multilingual content improve robustness and predictive power beyond single-platform English Twitter data? (Model relies solely on X posts from 2007 onward, with pre-2007 sentiment neutral.)
- Under what specific market conditions does the gating mechanism adjust feature weighting, and can its activation patterns be interpreted to inform regime-dependent trading strategies? (Gating is described as volatility-adaptive but its dynamic behavior is not analyzed.)
- How do variable transaction costs and market impact, particularly given the high 32% daily turnover, affect the economic viability of the long-short strategy? (Economic analysis assumes fixed 10 bps costs; real trading involves nonlinear frictions.)

## Limitations
- The graph uses only 20 stocks, limiting market topology coverage and possibly overfitting to specific sectors.
- Normalization protocol is ambiguous; potential data leakage if global statistics were used.
- BERT fine-tuning details are incomplete, raising doubts about sentiment reliability.
- Adaptive fusion gate lacks explicit analysis; learned gating behavior is not shown.

## Confidence
- Graph structure benefit: Medium
- Sentiment integration: Medium
- Adaptive fusion robustness: Low-Medium
