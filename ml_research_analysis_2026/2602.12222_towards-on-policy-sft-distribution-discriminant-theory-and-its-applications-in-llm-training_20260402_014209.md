# Towards On-Policy SFT: Distribution Discriminant Theory and its Applications in LLM Training

## Quick Facts
- **ArXiv ID**: 2602.12222  
- **Source URL**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.12222  
- **Reference count**: 40  
- **Primary result**: Enables on-policy SFT via DDT; IDFT+Hinted Decoding outperforms SFT and offline RL (DPO/SimPO) on reasoning tasks with lower compute, especially for instruct/thinking models.

## Executive Summary
Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT) often suffers from distribution mismatch with the model’s pre-trained distribution, causing catastrophic forgetting and limiting performance compared to reinforcement learning (RL) methods. This paper introduces Distribution Discriminant Theory (DDT) to theoretically justify the use of centered log-likelihood (CLL) as an optimal statistic for distinguishing in-distribution tokens from out-of-distribution ones. Building on DDT, the authors propose two complementary components: In-Distribution Finetuning (IDFT) adaptively reweights the SFT loss per token using CLL-derived scores to suppress gradients from out-of-distribution tokens and consolidate learning on in-distribution tokens; Hinted Decoding transforms a static dataset into model-aligned responses by geometrically mixing the model’s own distribution with a reference answer’s context, using an entropy-adaptive coefficient to preserve correctness. Together, these yield an effective on-policy SFT pipeline that avoids the high computational cost of RL.

Empirical evaluation on mathematical and code reasoning tasks across multiple model families (Qwen2.5, DeepSeek-R1, Llama) demonstrates clear gains over standard SFT, prior distribution-focused fine-tuning (DFT, EAFT), and offline RL baselines (DPO, SimPO). Notably, on Qwen2.5-7B-instruct the combined approach achieves an absolute improvement of +3.24 points on the Math-C benchmark and surpasses DPO@16 while consuming fewer GPU hours, highlighting significant compute efficiency particularly for instruct and thinking models where distribution mismatch is most severe.

## Method Summary
The method comprises two algorithmic innovations grounded in DDT. First, DDT proves that within a broad family, the centered log-likelihood (CLL) — token log-probability corrected by context entropy — maximizes the signal-to-noise ratio for distinguishing tokens generated by the model from those drawn from an external distribution. Second, In-Distribution Finetuning (IDFT) uses the CLL score φₜ to compute a modulation factor γₜ = exp(-φₜ) that scales each token’s contribution to the standard negative log-likelihood loss, thereby downweighting out-of-distribution tokens and upweighting strongly in-distribution ones. Third, Hinted Decoding rewrites training data to be on-policy: for a (question, answer) pair, it generates an imitator analysis to form a target context, then during decoding mixes the model’s conditional distribution with its unconditional (drafter) distribution in log-space using an entropy-adaptive coefficient. This yields responses that retain the correct answer while conforming to the model’s native style. The two are applied sequentially: Hinted Decoding produces an aligned dataset, which is then fine-tuned with IDFT. The combined pipeline delivers on-policy SFT performance rivaling RL methods with substantially lower computational overhead.

## Key Results
- The combination of IDFT and Hinted Decoding consistently outperforms standard SFT, distribution‑focused fine‑tuning (DFT, EAFT), and offline RL methods (DPO, SimPO) on math and code reasoning benchmarks.
- On Qwen2.5‑7B‑instruct, the method yields a +3.24 absolute gain on Math‑C over SFT and exceeds DPO@16 while using fewer GPU hours.
- Gains are especially pronounced for instruct and thinking models where the mismatch between training data and model distribution is most severe.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1: DDT and CLL Optimality
- **Claim:** Distribution Discriminant Theory proves that centered log‑likelihood (CLL) maximizes the signal‑to‑noise ratio for detecting whether a token originates from the model’s distribution (in‑distribution) or an external source (out‑of‑distribution).
- **Mechanism:** Framed as hypothesis testing (H₀: x∼pₜ vs H₁: x∼qₜ), DDT seeks a scorer S that maximizes (E[S|H₁]−E[S|H₀])² / Var(S|H₀). Within the family J = {log p(x) + C[p]}, the optimum is S = log p(x) + H(p), i.e., CLL. The entropy term H(p) cancels variance due to context difficulty (σ²_H) in the denominator while preserving the KL‑divergence signal (Δ²) in the numerator.
- **Core assumption:** The observed token probability noise is multiplicative (p_obs = p_true·ξ), making logarithmic transformation essential for variance stabilization across the vocabulary.
- **Evidence anchors:** Theorem 2.3; abstract statement “proves centered log‑likelihood (CLL) maximizes signal‑to‑noise ratio”.
- **Break condition:** Requires accurate estimation of H(pₜ) and that multiplicative noise dominates; severe miscalibration or distribution shift outside training can degrade CLL’s discriminative power.

### Mechanism 2: In‑Distribution Finetuning (IDFT)
- **Claim:** IDFT mitigates catastrophic forgetting during SFT by adaptively modulating the loss gradient based on the CLL score φₜ, suppressing updates for out‑of‑distribution tokens while amplifying those for strong in‑distribution tokens.
- **Mechanism:** The per‑token modulation factor γₜ = exp(−φₜ) scales the standard NLL loss: L_IDFT = −(1/L) Σ pₜ(xₜ) γₜ log pₜ(xₜ). OOD tokens (φₜ ≪ 0) yield γₜ > 1, which in this formulation suppresses their gradient contribution; in‑distribution tokens (φₜ ≈ 0) keep γₜ ≈ 1; strong in‑distribution (φₜ > 0) give γₜ < 1, amplifying their learning signal.
- **Core assumption:** Catastrophic forgetting is primarily driven by large, destabilizing gradient updates on OOD or noisy tokens that overwrite pre‑trained knowledge.
- **Evidence anchors:** Abstract; Section 3.1; Section 1 on preventing catastrophic forgetting.
- **Break condition:** φₜ must be a reliable proxy for distribution alignment. Poor calibration or encountering novel tasks can cause erroneous suppression (under‑fitting) or failure to suppress harmful OOD noise (negative transfer).

### Mechanism 3: Hinted Decoding
- **Claim:** Hinted Decoding transforms a reference dataset’s correct solutions into model‑aligned responses via entropy‑adaptive geometric mixing of the target and drafter distributions, thus creating on‑policy SFT data.
- **Mechanism:** For a (question, answer) pair, first generate a “target context” containing an imitator’s analysis of the answer. During decoding, at each step compute the target distribution p (conditioned on target context) and the drafter distribution q (conditioned only on the question). Mix them in log‑space: log m = (1−λ(H^I)) log p + λ(H^I) log q, where λ increases with the normalized entropy of p. A splitter detection switches to pure q after the CoT delimiter to ensure answer consistency.
- **Core assumption:** The model’s own distribution q for a given question captures its reasoning style; a correct answer can be incorporated via a separate analysis context without leaking stylistic artifacts, allowing the final CoT to stay on‑policy while remaining correct.
- **Evidence anchors:** Abstract; Section 3.2; Figure 9 ablation showing β=10 aligns generated data φ with normal rollouts.
- **Break condition:** The adaptive mixing relies on entropy of p to estimate uncertainty; if the imitator’s analysis is semantically inconsistent, p’s entropy may not reflect true token importance, leading to style transfer failures. The FP detector mitigates but does not eliminate risks of CoT/answer mismatch.

## Foundational Learning
- **Signal‑to‑Noise Ratio (SNR) Optimization**  
  Why needed: DDT treats distribution discrimination as a signal detection problem; CLL’s superiority comes from maximizing SNR by eliminating variance due to context difficulty.  
  Quick check: If two distributions differ only in their mean token probabilities but have identical context entropies, does CLL or raw log‑likelihood provide better separation? Why?  
  *Answer: CLL, because raw log‑likelihood’s variance depends on context entropy, which can mask mean differences; CLL subtracts entropy, stabilizing variance.*

- **Entropy & Multiplicative Noise in LLMs**  
  Why needed: Justifies the logarithmic transformation; multiplicative noise (p_obs = p_true·ξ) is stabilized by taking logs.  
  Quick check: Why would an additive noise model p_obs = p_true + δ fail for rare tokens with very low p_true?  
  *Answer: Additive noise could push probabilities negative or cause high relative variance for low‑probability tokens; multiplicative noise scales with p_true and logs normalize variance across the vocabulary.*

- **Geometric vs. Arithmetic Mixture in Log‑Space**  
  Why needed: Hinted Decoding implements a geometric mixture (m ∝ p^{1−λ} q^λ) via log‑space interpolation.  
  Quick check: What would happen if we linearly interpolated the probability vectors p and q instead of their logs?  
  *Answer: Arithmetic mixture may not preserve the relative ordering of tokens when p is uncertain, and can produce probability mass distributions that deviate from the intended style‑correctness trade‑off.*

- **Martingales and Concentration Inequalities**  
  Why needed: Under H₀ (in‑distribution), the cumulative CLL score S_L forms a zero‑mean martingale, enabling exponential tail bounds (e.g., Freedman’s inequality) to control false positives.  
  Quick check: What property of {φₜ} under H₀ causes E[S_L | F_{L−1}] = S_{L−1}?  
  *Answer: The conditional expectation of the increment φₜ given the past is zero (martingale property), which holds because under H₀ the expected CLL of the next token equals the current cumulative score.*

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component map:**  
DDT Foundation → two independent paths:  
- **IDFT path**: `compute_φ(model, batch)` → `γₜ = exp(-φₜ)` → modulated loss → updated model.  
- **Hinted Decoding path**: `generate_target_context(question, answer)` → `hinted_decode(question, target_context, β)` → aligned dataset → (optionally) train with IDFT.

**Critical path:**  
1. Implement φₜ computation (log‑probability + context entropy).  
2. For Hinted Decoding: build two‑stream decoder with entropy‑adaptive λ(H) and splitter/EOS handling; preprocess entire dataset.  
3. For IDFT: wrap existing SFT loss with per‑token γₜ modulation; optionally combine with HD‑preprocessed data.

**Design tradeoffs:**  
- *IDFT weighting*: Soft exponential γₜ is robust but requires tuning the underlying CLL scale; hard binary mask I[φₜ > τ] is simpler but highly sensitive to τ (inverted‑U performance, Table 3).  
- *Hinted Decoding λ(H) schedule*: Higher β (more reliance on drafter distribution) increases distribution alignment but risks CoT correctness if imitator analysis is flawed; the FP detection (switch after delimiter) is crucial to prevent mismatched CoT/answer (Table 2 ablation).

**Failure signatures:**  
- *IDFT*: General benchmark collapse indicates γₜ fails to suppress OOD gradients; degraded task‑specific performance suggests over‑suppression of useful hard samples.  
- *Hinted Decoding*: High φ in generated data but low accuracy implies false positives (CoT errors with correct final answer) leaked; very low φ indicates insufficient λ, i.e., data not aligned with model distribution.

**First 3 experiments:**  
1. **φ Validation**: On a holdout of model rollouts vs. dataset tokens, verify that the sum of clipped φ (S_n) cleanly separates the two distributions (Figure 2). High overlap suggests minimal benefit from HD.  
2. **IDFT Ablation – Update Component**: Replace γₜ = exp(−φₜ) with binary mask I[φₜ > τ] and sweep τ ∈ {−10, −5, −1}. Expect an inverted‑U curve; the peak τ indicates the effective inclusion range for hard tokens.  
3. **HD Ablation – β and FP Detection**: Preprocess data with β ∈ {0,1,3,5,10} and with/without the FP switch. Measure downstream accuracy and φ metrics. Expect accuracy and φ to rise then fall, with a clear peak at some β; disabling FP detection should drop accuracy while φ may stay high (inconsistent data retained).

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
### Open Question 1
- Question: Can the proposed framework be extended to an online on-policy SFT setting, enabling direct comparison with online RL algorithms like PPO?  
- Basis in paper: [explicit] "the algorithm presented here could be adapted into an online version, similar to PPO, where each batch is regenerated, allowing for comparisons with a broader range of RL methods... as the research is still in its early stages, we consider this as a future work."  
- Why unresolved: The current method is offline, assuming a fixed dataset; an online variant would need to handle dynamic distribution shifts across updates and potential instability, which has not been explored.  
- What evidence would resolve it: Implement an online version where batches are regenerated from the current policy after each update, then compare sample efficiency, compute cost, and final performance against PPO on standard benchmarks (e.g., GSM8K, code generation).

### Open Question 2
- Question: How effective is the combined IDFT and Hinted Decoding approach in domains where models have not undergone extensive pre-training, such as agent tasks, compared to the mathematical reasoning benchmarks evaluated?  
- Basis in paper: [explicit] "the algorithm discussed in this paper could demonstrate significantly greater advantages in scenarios, like agents, that those models have not undergone extensive pre-training, compared to its performance in mathematical domains. To ensure the evaluation’s credibility, we have also left this aspect for future work."  
- Why unresolved: The experiments were restricted to math/code tasks on models with strong pre‑training; agent environments involve longer horizons, partial observability, and different data distributions, which may interact differently with DDT’s assumptions.  
- What evidence would resolve it: Apply the full pipeline (HD+IDFT) to a representative agent benchmark (e.g., WebShop, BabyAIGame), measure task success rate, generalization, and compute usage; compare against SFT, offline RL, and online RL.

### Open Question 3
- Question: How can Distribution Discriminant Theory be practically integrated into speculative decoding, on-policy distillation, and diffusion-based language model frameworks?  
- Basis in paper: [explicit] "our work can naturally connect with emerging fields such as speculative decoding (Leviathan et al., 2023), on-policy distillation (Agarwal et al., 2024), and diffusion LLMs (Li et al., 2022; Nie et al., 2025), potentially leading to new algorithms."  
- Why unresolved: These architectures differ fundamentally from autoregressive transformers; DDT’s CLL criterion and modulation schemes have not been adapted to their respective sampling or generation mechanisms.  
- What evidence would resolve it: For speculative decoding, weight draft token acceptance using DDT’s φ; for on-policy distillation, replace KL penalties with IDFT‑style loss; for diffusion LLMs, incorporate φ into denoising step scores. Empirically evaluate whether such integrations improve speed‑accuracy trade‑offs or reduce distribution shift on standard benchmarks.

## Limitations
- DDT’s theoretical guarantees rely on accurate context entropy estimation and multiplicative noise; violations in practice could weaken CLL’s discriminative power.  
- IDFT’s adaptive weighting depends on φₜ being a faithful indicator of in‑distributionness; miscalibration or novel contexts may cause mis‑modulation, harming learning.  
- Experiments are confined to math/code tasks and limited model families; broader generalizability and detailed compute trade‑offs remain unexplored.

## Confidence
- **DDT theoretical optimality**: High (rigorous under stated assumptions)  
- **IDFT mitigates catastrophic forgetting**: Medium (empirical support limited to specific tasks)  
- **Hinted Decoding yields on‑policy data**: Medium (ablations indicate alignment but heuristic may not universally hold)  
- **Overall gains over DPO/SimPO**: Medium (consistent but on restricted benchmarks)
