# MiroThinker-1.7 & H1: Towards Heavy-Duty Research Agents via Verification

## Quick Facts
*   **arXiv ID:** 2603.15726
*   **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.15726
*   **Reference Count:** 40
*   **Primary Result:** MiroThinker-H1 achieves new SOTA on complex web-research benchmarks (88.2 BrowseComp, 84.4 BrowseComp-ZH, 88.5 GAIA), outperforming leading proprietary models.

## Executive Summary
MiroThinker is a family of research agents designed to overcome the fragility of long-horizon reasoning in autonomous AI agents. The work introduces two key advancements: (1) **agentic mid-training** to strengthen atomic capabilities in planning, reasoning, and tool use for the base model (MiroThinker-1.7), and (2) a dual-layer **verification system** (local and global) applied during inference in the final agent (MiroThinker-H1). On demanding benchmarks like BrowseComp and GAIA, the H1 variant sets a new state-of-the-art, demonstrating that structured verification can yield superior performance even against much larger proprietary systems. Open-source variants (1.7, 1.7-mini) offer a competitive efficiency frontier.

## Method Summary
The method separates agent development into two phases. First, the base model undergoes "agentic mid-training," a supervised fine-tuning phase on high-quality trajectories to instill robust, atomic skills in planning, reasoning, and tool manipulation. Second, the deployment agent (H1) integrates two verifiers: a **local verifier** checks the validity of each intermediate reasoning step and tool call, while a **global verifier** evaluates the coherence and plausibility of the entire multi-step solution trajectory. This verification loop allows the agent to detect and correct errors before finalizing an answer, improving reliability on complex, multi-faceted research tasks.

## Key Results
*   MiroThinker-H1 achieves SOTA on BrowseComp (88.2), BrowseComp-ZH (84.4), and GAIA (88.5), surpassing GPT-5.4 and Gemini-3.1-Pro.
*   The open-source MiroThinker-1.7 and 1.7-mini models remain highly competitive on these benchmarks while being significantly more efficient.
*   The proposed verification framework demonstrably improves performance over a strong base agent (1.7) on the most challenging long-horizon tasks.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
Long-horizon research tasks are prone to error accumulation from a single flawed step. MiroThinker's design directly attacks this fragility. Agentic mid-training preconditions the model to produce more reliable atomic actions, reducing the initial error rate. The subsequent local and global verification acts as a defensive layer: the local verifier catches immediate inconsistencies (e.g., impossible tool parameters, logical fallacies in a step), while the global verifier assesses the overall narrative and answerability of the full trajectory. This two-tiered scrutiny prevents small mistakes from cascading and enables the agent to self-correct, leading to higher final answer accuracy on benchmarks designed to test deep, multi-source reasoning.

## Foundational Learning
*   **Atomic Capability Training:** Why needed: Standard pretraining does not guarantee reliable performance on specific agentic skills like sequential planning or tool use; mid-training fills this gap. Quick check: Does the model successfully execute single-step tool calls and simple reasoning chains in isolation?
*   **Verification-as-Correction Loop:** Why needed: Generative models are inherently prone to hallucination and error propagation; external verification provides a learned or heuristic check. Quick check: Can the verifier identify intentionally inserted errors in sample trajectories?
*   **Long-Horizon Task Decomposition:** Why needed: Complex research questions must be broken into manageable sub-tasks; poor decomposition dooms the entire process. Quick check: Does the generated plan cover all necessary sub-questions implied by the main query?
*   **Tool Grounding:** Why needed: Abstract reasoning must be connected to concrete actions (search, read, compute) to gather evidence. Quick check: Are tool invocations syntactically correct and semantically relevant to the current context?
*   **Multi-Source Evidence Aggregation:** Why needed: Answers to complex questions require synthesizing information from multiple documents or pages. Quick check: Does the final answer cite or implicitly rely on information from more than one distinct source?
*   **Faithfulness Checking:** Why needed: The final answer must be strictly supported by the gathered trajectory and sources. Quick check: Can every claim in the final answer be traced back to a specific piece of evidence in the verified trajectory?

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component Map:**  
Atomic Skill Training -> Base Agent (1.7) -> [Reasoning + Tool Use] -> Local Verifier (per-step) -> Global Verifier (full trajectory) -> Final Answer (H1)

**Critical Path:**  
1.  Receive user query.  
2.  Base agent generates initial multi-step plan and executes first reasoning/tool step.  
3.  Local verifier evaluates this step; if failed, agent revises and retries.  
4.  Upon step success, proceed to next step, repeating local verification.  
5.  After all steps, global verifier assesses full trajectory and synthesized answer.  
6.  If global check fails, agent may be prompted to revisit earlier steps.  
7.  If global check passes, return final answer.

**Design Tradeoffs:**  
*   **Accuracy vs. Latency:** Each verifier adds computational overhead and sequential steps, increasing time-to-answer.  
*   **Generality vs. Specialization:** The verifiers are trained on agent trajectories; their effectiveness may drop on out-of-distribution task types.  
*   **Simplicity vs. Completeness:** A simple local check may miss subtle cross-step inconsistencies only a global verifier can catch.

**Failure Signatures:**  
*   **Local Verifier Overload:** Agent gets stuck in a loop revising a single step that is actually acceptable.  
*   **Global Verifier Blindness:** The verifier approves a trajectory with a subtle, non-local logical contradiction.  
*   **Mid-Training Distribution Gap:** The base agent's atomic skills fail on a benchmark sub-problem not covered in its mid-training data, causing verification to fail repeatedly.

**First 3 Experiments:**  
1.  **Ablation - Local Verifier Only:** Disable global verifier, run H1 on BrowseComp to quantify marginal gain from global coherence checking.  
2.  **Efficiency Profiling:** Measure average token count and wall-clock time for 1.7 vs. H1 on a fixed subset of GAIA tasks to isolate verification overhead.  
3.  **Cross-Architecture Transfer:** Apply the identical local/global verifier modules (frozen) to a Llama-3-70B agent and evaluate on a held-out GAIA split to test generalization beyond Qwen3 MoE.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
### Open Question 1
- Question: How do agentic mid-training, local verifier, and global verifier individually contribute to MiroThinker-H1's performance?
- Basis in paper: [inferred] The paper credits both mid-training and verification for gains but does not isolate their effects.
- Why unresolved: Without ablation studies, the importance of each component remains unknown.
- What evidence would resolve it: Perform controlled experiments removing each component (e.g., no mid-training, no local verifier, no global verifier) and measure performance on BrowseComp and GAIA.

### Open Question 2
- Question: What is the computational cost (latency, token usage) of the verification mechanisms?
- Basis in paper: [inferred] Only accuracy improvements are reported; no efficiency metrics are provided.
- Why unresolved: The trade-off between accuracy and compute/resources is unclear, affecting real-world deployment.
- What evidence would resolve it: Measure inference time, number of tokens processed, and hardware cost for MiroThinker-1.7 vs MiroThinker-H1 across benchmarks; calculate overhead per task.

### Open Question 3
- Question: Does verification-centric reasoning generalize to other model architectures (e.g., Llama, Mistral)?
- Basis in paper: [inferred] All experiments use Qwen3 MoE checkpoints; no cross-architecture evaluation is conducted.
- Why unresolved: It is uncertain whether the gains are specific to the base model or a general property.
- What evidence would resolve it: Integrate the same local and global verification modules into different base models and evaluate on the same benchmarks to compare improvements.

## Limitations
*   Benchmark evaluation details (test splits, contamination checks) are not sufficiently described for independent verification of SOTA claims.
*   No ablation study breaks down the individual contribution of agentic mid-training versus the dual verifiers, obscuring the source of performance gains.
*   Efficiency comparisons ("higher efficiency") lack standardized metrics (FLOPs, tokens/second, $/task), making the claim unquantifiable.

## Confidence
*   **SOTA benchmark results on BrowseComp/GAIA:** **Medium** (plausible given reported numbers but unverified due to opaque evaluation details).
*   **Architectural improvements (mid-training + verification) are the primary driver of gains:** **Medium-Low** (the design is sound but incremental; similar verification concepts exist in prior work).
*   **Open-source variants (1.7/mini) offer superior efficiency:** **Low** (no comparative efficiency metrics provided against specific, matched baselines).
