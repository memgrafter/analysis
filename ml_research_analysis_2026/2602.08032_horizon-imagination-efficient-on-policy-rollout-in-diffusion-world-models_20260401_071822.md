# Horizon Imagination: Efficient On-Policy Rollout in Diffusion World Models

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2602.08032
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.08032
- Reference count: 40
- Primary result: Horizon Imagination (HI) enables efficient on-policy rollout in diffusion world models by parallelizing denoising of multiple future observations, using a stable action sampling and a horizon schedule to decouple compute budget from effective horizon, achieving comparable control performance with half the denoising steps and superior generative quality.

## Executive Summary
Diffusion world models provide high-fidelity simulation for reinforcement learning but incur prohibitive computational costs during control due to sequential multi-step denoising. This paper introduces Horizon Imagination (HI), an on-policy imagination process designed for discrete stochastic policies. HI denoises multiple future observations in parallel, stabilizing training via a consistent action sampling mechanism that minimizes unnecessary action changes across denoising steps. A novel sampling schedule (the Horizon schedule) decouples the total denoising budget from the effective horizon, supporting sub-frame budgets where fewer denoising steps are used than the imagination horizon. Evaluations on Atari 100K and Craftium show that HI maintains control performance with a sub-frame budget of half the standard denoising steps and achieves superior generative quality across a variety of schedules, demonstrating a significant efficiency improvement for training deployable policies.

## Method Summary
Horizon Imagination operates within a diffusion world model framework. The system comprises a tokenizer (autoencoder) that maps raw observations to bounded latents, a rectified-flow denoiser (Diffusion Transformer) that generates future latent trajectories conditioned on past latents and actions, and separate modules for reward and termination prediction. The actor (stochastic policy) and critic (value estimator) are trained on imagined trajectories.

The key innovation is the imagination engine, which parallelizes the denoising of a horizon of future frames in a single forward pass across a budget of B denoising steps. Actions for each future timestep are queried from the policy at every denoising step, but a stable action sampling mechanism (using a fixed random vector ω and permutation ρ) ensures that the selected actions remain consistent as long as the underlying categorical distribution ordering does not change, thereby stabilizing the parallel generation. The Horizon schedule matrix K determines how denoising steps are allocated across time; it is parameterized by the budget B and a decay horizon ν, allowing independent control of total compute and how quickly early frames are denoised relative to later ones. This decoupling supports sub-frame budgets (B < h) without sacrificing quality when ν is appropriately chosen.

Training alternates between: collecting real environment interactions; updating the tokenizer; updating the world model (denoiser and reward/termination predictors) on latent sequences; and updating the actor-critic using imagined trajectories generated by the imagination engine. The actor is trained via REINFORCE with an entropy bonus, while the critic is trained on fully denoised frames using symlog λ-returns.

## Key Results
- HI maintains control performance on Atari 100K and Craftium even when using a sub-frame denoising budget (B=16), which is half the standard budget (B=32).
- Generative quality (measured by FVD and MSE) is superior across varied sampling schedules; parallel schedules with ν≥4 match or exceed the autoregressive baseline’s quality while using fewer denoising steps.
- The approach yields a substantial efficiency gain, enabling the training of deployable policies with reduced computational overhead.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1: Stable Action Sampling for Consistent Parallel Denoising
- **Claim**: The stable action sampling mechanism minimizes unnecessary action changes across denoising steps during parallel future observation generation, stabilizing the imagination process.
- **Mechanism**: A single uniform random vector ω and a fixed permutation ρ are used to map evolving categorical policy distributions π^τ to actions via inverse transform sampling. At each denoising time τ, the action is selected as the first index i where ω_i < α_i(π^τ), ensuring the same ω yields consistent actions as long as the policy distribution ordering remains fixed.
- **Core assumption**: Policy distributions vary smoothly across denoising times; forcing consistency does not introduce harmful bias and maintains correct marginal distributions.
- **Evidence anchors**:
  - [abstract]: "HI incorporates a stabilization mechanism [...] that decouples the denoising budget from the effective horizon [...] maintaining control performance with a sub-frame budget."
  - [section 4.2]: Full description of stable sampling, Proposition 1 proving it preserves marginal distributions and bounds action changes by total variation distance.
  - [section 5.2.3]: Ablation study shows naive action sampling causes significant performance drop, confirming the need for stabilization.
  - [corpus]: Corpus evidence is weak; neighboring papers (e.g., Rigter et al., 2024) address continuous-action guidance, not discrete-action consistency.
- **Break condition**: If policy distributions change drastically (high total variation) between denoising steps, consistent sampling may lock in outdated actions that are misaligned with the current policy, potentially harming on-policyness and control performance.

### Mechanism 2: Horizon Schedule for Budget-Controlled Decoupled Denoising
- **Claim**: The Horizon schedule disentangles the total denoising budget (B) from the decay horizon (ν), enabling sub-frame budgets (B < h) and robust generation quality across a wide range of budgets.
- **Mechanism**: A matrix K ∈ [0,1]^{(B+1)×h} is defined via κ(t,b) = -1/ν · t + (b/B)(1 + (h-1)/ν), with K_{i,j} = clamp(κ(j-1, i-1),0,1). Independent choices of B and ν control total compute and how quickly early frames denoise relative to later ones.
- **Core assumption**: The world model can generate high-quality latent observations even when denoising steps are allocated non-autoregressively across time, given a properly designed schedule.
- **Evidence anchors**:
  - [abstract]: "novel sampling schedule that decouples the denoising budget from the effective horizon over which denoising is applied while also supporting sub-frame budgets."
  - [section 4.2]: Equation (3) and discussion of sub-frame budgets (B < h).
  - [section 5.3]: Empirical comparison with Pyramidal schedule shows Horizon avoids performance collapse at high B and maintains quality with B < h.
- **Break condition**: Setting ν too large may allocate insufficient denoising to later frames; setting B too small relative to h or environment complexity may yield under-denoised, blurry generations. The schedule is ineffective if the world model cannot handle non-uniform denoising orders.

### Mechanism 3: Parallel On-Policy Imagination with Conditional Denoising
- **Claim**: By denoising multiple future observations in parallel, conditioned on actions co-evolved via stable sampling, the overall imagination process becomes orders of magnitude more efficient while preserving control performance.
- **Mechanism**: For a horizon h, the denoiser v_θ processes all frames in a single forward pass across B denoising steps. At each step, actions for each future timestep are sampled from the policy conditioned on partially denoised latents. Causal attention ensures each output only depends on past frames and actions.
- **Core assumption**: The world model’s conditional generation is robust to generating far-future frames before near-future frames are fully denoised, given consistent action conditioning and a proper schedule.
- **Evidence anchors**:
  - [section 4.2]: "denoises multiple future observations in parallel" and "query the policy before each denoising step, yielding an increasingly informed categorical action distribution."
  - [section 5.2]: Results show that with B=16 (half of the autoregressive baseline B=32) control performance is maintained on Atari and Craftium.
  - [section 5.3]: Parallel configurations (ν>1) with sub-frame budgets achieve comparable or better generative quality than autoregressive with full budget.
- **Break condition**: If the world model is highly sensitive to the quality of intermediate latents, parallel denoising (especially with low B) may produce artifacts that degrade policy learning. The mechanism also assumes that actions can be conditioned effectively on partially denoised observations.

## Foundational Learning
- **Concept**: Diffusion Probabilistic Models (Rectified Flow)
  - **Why needed here**: The world model uses rectified flow to map noise to observation latents. Understanding the forward noising process, the vector field training objective, and time conditioning is essential to grasp how parallel denoising works.
  - **Quick check**: In rectified flow, what does the denoiser v_θ approximate, and how is it trained? (Answer: v_θ(x^τ, τ) approximates E[x^1 - x^0 | x^τ] and is trained by regression on the vector between noise and clean data.)

- **Concept**: World Models in Reinforcement Learning
  - **Why needed here**: The paper’s entire training pipeline relies on learning a generative model of environment dynamics and using it to train a policy via imagination. One must understand the separation between representation, dynamics, and controller.
  - **Quick check**: What are the four standard components of a world model agent? (Answer: representation model, dynamics model, controller, replay buffer.)

- **Concept**: On-Policy Policy Gradient Methods (REINFORCE)
  - **Why needed here**: The actor is trained via an on-policy gradient (REINFORCE) on trajectories imagined by the current policy and world model. The stable sampling mechanism directly affects the on-policy distribution.
  - **Quick check**: Why must the data used for policy updates be on-policy, and how does imagination provide such data? (Answer: On-policy updates rely on the current policy’s distribution to avoid distribution shift; imagination generates trajectories using the current policy conditioned on latents.)

- **Concept**: Discrete Action Spaces and Categorical Distributions
  - **Why needed here**: The method targets discrete stochastic policies, requiring a careful design to keep actions consistent across denoising steps. Total variation distance governs the maximum probability of action changes.
  - **Quick check**: How does the total variation distance δ(p,q) between two categorical distributions relate to the probability that the stable sampling mechanism yields different actions under p and q? (Answer: Pr(a(p,ω)≠a(q,ω)) is bounded by δ(p,q) ≤ Pr(A) ≤ ∥α(p)-α(q)∥_1.)

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map**:
  - **Tokenizer (φ_enc, φ_dec)**: Image autoencoder; φ_enc maps observation to latent ∈ [-1,1]^d, φ_dec reconstructs. Trained with MSE + perceptual loss.
  - **Denoiser (v_θ)**: Diffusion Transformer (DiT) generating future latents conditioned on past latents, actions, and denoising times. Uses causal masking and AdaLN for conditioning.
  - **Reward-Termination Predictor (r_ϕ, d_ϕ)**: Lightweight RNN on latent sequences; rewards in symlog space, termination via cross-entropy.
  - **Actor (π)**: Stochastic policy network (RNN) outputting categorical distribution over discrete actions.
  - **Critic (V)**: Value estimator network (RNN) trained on symlog λ-returns.
  - **Imagination Engine**: Implements Horizon schedule (K matrix) and stable action sampling (ω,ρ) to produce parallel rollouts.
  - **Replay Buffer**: Stores real environment transitions (o, a, r, d).
  - **Training Loops**: Alternate phases: real data collection, tokenizer update, world model update, actor-critic update.

- **Critical path**:
  1. Collect real interactions using current policy (random initially).
  2. Update tokenizer on collected frames (reconstruction objective).
  3. Sample trajectory segments from replay buffer, encode to latents, and train world model (denoiser + reward/termination).
  4. Sample context from replay buffer; run imagination engine to generate parallel trajectories; compute advantages; update actor and critic.
  5. Repeat for many epochs, with increasing policy training as model stabilizes.

- **Design tradeoffs**:
  - **Budget B vs Decay Horizon ν**: Smaller B speeds up imagination but risks under-denoising; ν>1 improves quality at low B but may harm at very high B.
  - **Stable vs Naive Action Sampling**: Stable reduces action changes, aiding stability; naive is simpler but can cause divergence (see ablation).
  - **Tokenizer capacity**: Smaller latent space reduces compute but loses visual details, potentially harming world model.
  - **World model size**: Larger DiT improves generative fidelity but increases per-step denoising time linearly.
  - **Imagination horizon h**: Longer horizons improve credit assignment but increase model bias and compute load.

- **Failure signatures**:
  - **Policy collapse**: Actor outputs near-deterministic actions; check entropy regularization and exploration in early stages.
  - **World model divergence**: Reconstruction or prediction loss spikes to NaN/∞; check latent scaling (tanh) and gradient clipping.
  - **Imagination instability**: High variance in returns across seeds; try reverting to naive sampling to diagnose stable action sampling issues.
  - **Sub-frame budget degradation**: Sharp drop in FVD or control performance when B < h; ensure ν is set ≥4 (per experiments) or increase B.
  - **Poor generalization**: High FVD on held-out data; may indicate underfitting, requiring larger world model or longer training.

- **First 3 experiments**:
  1. **Tokenizer reconstruction sanity check**: Train tokenizer for a few epochs, reconstruct validation frames, compute PSNR/LPIPS. Verify latents are bounded in [-1,1] and reconstructions are perceptually plausible.
  2. **Single-step world model prediction**: Train world model with B=1 and h=32. Evaluate short-horizon (e.g., 5-step) prediction error on held-out trajectories. Checks dynamics learning without parallel complications.
  3. **Stable action sampling validation**: With a fixed policy and fixed initial latent noise, run imagination using both stable and naive sampling. Measure average number of action changes across denoising steps (as in Figure 3b). Expect near-zero changes for stable, many changes for naive. Also verify that the marginal action distribution matches the policy at each step.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
The paper does not explicitly discuss open questions or future research directions. However, the limitations section identifies several areas that could be explored further, such as the sensitivity of the stable action sampling to drastic policy distribution shifts and the theoretical characterization of the Horizon schedule's effectiveness under varying world model capacities.

## Limitations
- Several hyperparameters and architectural details are not fully specified (e.g., perceptual loss weights, exact DiT action-time conditioning, reward-termination CNN architecture), which may hinder exact reproduction.
- The stable action sampling mechanism assumes smooth policy evolution; drastic distribution shifts could misalign the policy and harm on-policyness.
- The effectiveness of the Horizon schedule depends on the world model's tolerance to non-uniform denoising orders, a sensitivity not fully characterized.

## Confidence
- **Stable action sampling stabilizes parallel denoising** — High (Proposition 1 proves marginal preservation; ablation shows naive sampling harms performance).
- **Horizon schedule decouples denoising budget from horizon and supports sub-frame budgets** — Medium (empirical results confirm utility, but theoretical guarantees are limited).
- **Parallel on-policy imagination maintains control performance with half the standard denoising steps** — High (consistent Atari 100K and Craftium results with B=16 vs B=32).

## Next Checks
1. **Action-change rate**: With a fixed policy and fixed noise, run imagination for h=32, B=16. Measure average number of action flips between consecutive denoising steps; expect near-zero for stable sampling, non-zero for naive.
2. **Schedule matrix sanity**: For B<h (e.g., B=16, h=32, ν=4), compute K matrix. Verify all entries in [0,1] and that after B steps every τ equals 1. Confirm decay pattern matches ν.
3. **Generative quality under sub-frame budgets**: Sample 512 validation trajectory segments. Generate futures using (B,ν)=(32,1), (16,4), (16,1). Compute FVD and MSE; expect (16,4) to match (32,1) while (16,1) degrades significantly.
