# LLMs-Integrated Automatic Hate Speech Recognition Using Controllable Text Generation Models

## Quick Facts
- **ArXiv ID:** 2601.04654
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.04654
- **Reference count:** 26
- **Primary result:** Proposed ASR-LLM model achieves 58.6% masking accuracy (MAR), with curriculum learning reaching 59.4% MAR, outperforming cascade baselines (ASR-BERT 36.7%, ASR*-BERT 54.4%).

## Executive Summary
This paper addresses automatic hate speech recognition in audio, where limited annotated datasets hinder training models to transcribe speech while masking hate-related words. The core method integrates a frozen/fine-tuned ASR encoder (BEATs) with an LLM decoder (Qwen2.5) via a Q-Former, enabling simultaneous transcription and censorship. Training data is generated by prompting an LLM with Chain-of-Thought guidance and cultural context, then filtering with multiple hate classifiers to control hate intensity. Curriculum learning—training on gradually increasing hate levels—improves efficiency. The integrated model achieves 58.6% MAR, and the best curriculum variant (Curriculum-2) reaches 59.4% MAR, demonstrating the value of LLM integration and ordered training.

## Method Summary
The pipeline begins by generating hate speech text using Qwen2.5-1.5B-Instruct with a structured CoT prompt (role, definition, examples, mandatory keywords). Generated sentences are filtered by five pre-trained hate classifiers to assign a hate level (0–5). Valid texts are synthesized to speech via VITS, and masked targets are created using LIME applied to a BERT-based hate classifier. Training proceeds in two stages: Stage 1 fine-tunes the BEATs ASR encoder on the synthesized speech (fixed decoder). Stage 2 integrates the fine-tuned encoder with the LLM decoder via a trainable Q-Former; the encoder is frozen, and the Q-Former and LLM are instruction-tuned with LoRA and KL-divergence regularization. Curriculum learning sequences Stage 2 training on subsets sorted by hate level (e.g., level 4 then 5) rather than shuffled mixing.

## Key Results
- The full ASR*-LLM model achieves 58.6% MAR, significantly higher than cascade baselines ASR-BERT (36.7%) and ASR*-BERT (54.4%).
- Curriculum learning with two stages (Curriculum-2: level 4 → 5) improves MAR to 59.4% and reduces UMWER to 47.0% compared to mixed-level training (UMWER 50.9%).
- Encoder fine-tuning (ASR* vs. ASR) and curriculum ordering both contribute to gains; Curriculum-3 (three levels) underperforms Curriculum-2, indicating sensitivity to progression design.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1: ASR-LLM Integration via Q-Former
- **Claim:** Integrating a pre-trained ASR encoder with an LLM decoder via a Q-Former enables simultaneous speech transcription and hate-word masking.
- **Mechanism:** The ASR encoder extracts acoustic features. A trainable Q-Former converts these into a fixed set of query tokens that the LLM decoder consumes. Conditioned on an instruction prompt (e.g., "Replace hateful words with ***"), the LLM generates text where hate words are masked while transcribing non-hate content accurately.
- **Core assumption:** The LLM decoder, when fed speech-derived representations and clear instructions, can perform dual tasks without degrading ASR accuracy.
- **Evidence anchors:** Architecture described in Section II.B; ASR-LLM (without encoder fine-tuning) performs worse than ASR*-LLM, supporting the need for encoder adaptation.

### Mechanism 2: LLM-Generated Dataset with CoT and Classifier Filtering
- **Claim:** Prompting an LLM with Chain-of-Thought guidance and filtering with multiple classifiers creates a hate speech dataset with controllable intensity for curriculum learning.
- **Mechanism:** The LLM is prompted to generate sentences containing mandatory hate-related keywords, using CoT steps (role, definition, examples). Five hate classifiers score each sentence; the count of "hateful" votes defines its hate level (0–5). Retaining only samples above a threshold yields a dataset where hate intensity is quantitatively controlled.
- **Core assumption:** Classifier consensus reliably proxies hate intensity, and CoT prompting yields linguistically plausible hate speech.
- **Evidence anchors:** Abstract and Section II.A detail the prompt structure (Table I) and filtering; the existence of multiple hate levels enables curriculum splits.

### Mechanism 3: Curriculum Learning on Hate Levels
- **Claim:** Training on gradually increasing hate levels improves learning efficiency and final performance of the integrated model.
- **Mechanism:** The filtered dataset is split by hate level (e.g., lower then higher). The model is first trained on the easier subset, then fine-tuned on the harder one. The hypothesis is that this scaffolding helps the model master core masking before confronting more ambiguous or extreme expressions.
- **Core assumption:** The hate level (by classifier votes) meaningfully orders sample difficulty.
- **Evidence anchors:** Curriculum-2 outperforms Mixed-2 on MAR (59.4% vs. 46.1%) and UMWER; Curriculum-3 underperforms, suggesting an optimal staging range.

## Foundational Learning
- **Chain-of-Thought (CoT) Prompting**
  - Why needed: To steer the LLM toward generating coherent, contextually plausible hate speech that includes specified keywords, which simple keyword-injection might fail to produce.

- **Curriculum Learning**
  - Why needed: To train the multitask ASR-LLM more efficiently by ordering samples from "easier" to "harder" based on hate intensity, avoiding the instability of mixed-level training.
  - Quick check: How is hate level quantitatively defined for a sample, and how is it used to order the curriculum?

  - Why needed: The innovation requires a single model to output masked transcriptions, necessitating joint tuning of a speech encoder and a text-generation decoder.
  - Quick check: What is the role of the Q-Former in connecting the frozen BEATs encoder to the Qwen LLM decoder?

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map:** Input audio -> ASR Encoder (BEATs) -> Q-Former -> LLM Decoder (Qwen2.5-Instruct)
- **Critical path:**
  1. Generate hate speech text via LLM with CoT prompt; filter with five classifiers to assign hate levels.
  2. Synthesize speech (VITS) and create masked targets using LIME on a BERT hate classifier.
  3. Stage 1: Fine-tune BEATs encoder (fixed ASR decoder) on synthesized speech.
  4. Stage 2: Integrate fine-tuned encoder with LLM via Q-Former; freeze encoder; train Q-Former and LLM with LoRA, instruction prompts, and KL-divergence loss.
  5. For curriculum: sequence Stage 2 training on hate-level-sorted subsets (e.g., level 4 then 5).
- **Design tradeoffs:**
  - **Frozen vs. fine-tuned encoder:** Freezing preserves general ASR knowledge but limits hate-word recognition; fine-tuning (ASR*) boosts MAR but risks overfitting to synthetic speech.
  - **Dataset size vs. hate level control:** Stricter filtering (higher classifier consensus) ensures purer hate samples but reduces volume, especially for lower levels, hurting curriculum stages.
  - **Token hallucination:** The LLM may over-generate `***` tokens, increasing WER; mitigated by KL-loss and prompt engineering.
- **Failure signatures:**
  - High WER + correct MAR: Encoder weakness; non-hate content poorly transcribed.
  - High UMWER + low MAR: LLM instruction-following failure; over- or under-masking.
  - Curriculum-3 underperformance: Lower hate levels may not be "easier" (e.g., fewer hate words, more nuance).
- **First 3 experiments:**
  1. **Baseline comparison:** Evaluate ASR*-LLM (full) vs. ASR*-BERT (cascade) vs. ASR-LLM (no encoder fine-tuning) on MAR, WER, UMWER.
  2. **Curriculum vs. mixed:** Train on same total samples with Curriculum-2 (level 4→5) vs. Mixed-2 (shuffled); compare metrics.
  3. **Hate level threshold ablation:** Vary classifier consensus threshold (e.g., 2/5, 3/5, 4/5) to generate datasets; train models and evaluate MAR to find optimal purity.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- **How can the method generate low-hate samples when prompting mandates keywords?**  
  *Basis:* Future work notes the current model assumes sentences must include several hate-related words, failing for low-hate samples.  
  *Why unresolved:* Curriculum learning requires lower-hate data, but keyword-mandatory prompting limits diversity.  
  *What evidence would resolve:* Performance of revised prompting (e.g., variable keyword constraints) or alternative data sources (platform-specific samples) on low-hate curriculum.

- **How does scalability generalize across different hate speech datasets?**  
  *Basis:* Future work states validation on other datasets is needed.  
  *Why unresolved:* Reliance on HateXplain's annotations and cultural context in CoT may not transfer to datasets with different definitions, slang, or platforms.  
  *What evidence would resolve:* Replication of full pipeline on at least two additional datasets (e.g., multilingual or platform-specific), with comparative MAR/WER analysis.

- **What modifications reduce over/under-generation of masking tokens?**  
  *Basis:* Inferred from degraded UMWER due to "over- and under-masking."  
  *Why unresolved:* Current instruction tuning and KL-regularization do not fully prevent contextual errors in delimiting hate speech.  
  *What evidence would resolve:* Ablations with span-level penalties, intermediate classifiers, or multi-task hate span detection, measuring UMWER and token error distributions.

- **What hate level progression optimizes the trade-off between transcription and masking?**  
  *Basis:* Inferred from Curriculum-3 underperformance relative to Curriculum-2.  
  *Why unresolved:* Optimal staging (number of stages, level cutoffs) is unknown; too many stages may introduce noise.  
  *What evidence would resolve:* Systematic experiments varying curriculum design (2–5 stages, different level thresholds) on fixed data budget, analyzing learning curves and final metrics.

## Limitations
- The ASR decoder paired with BEATs for Stage 1 is unspecified; only SALMONN is referenced, preventing exact replication.
- The five hate classifiers used for filtering and their voting threshold are not identified, causing dataset variability.
- Q-Former initialization and architecture details are missing; LoRA hyperparameters (rank, dropout) and KL-divergence implementation are omitted.
- Generated low-hate samples are limited by keyword-mandatory prompting, reducing curriculum diversity.

## Confidence
- **Integration mechanism (ASR-LLM via Q-Former):** Medium. Plausible but unvalidated; break condition (high WER) indicates possible encoder weakness.
- **Dataset generation mechanism (LLM CoT + filtering):** Low. Filtering admits non-hate samples; classifier-based hate level may not capture true difficulty or cultural nuance.
- **Curriculum learning mechanism:** Medium. Curriculum-2 outperforms mixed, but Curriculum-3 underperformance shows sensitivity to ordering and level definition.

## Next Checks
1. **Ablate the Q-Former:** Replace with a linear projection or random initialization; measure changes in MAR and WER to confirm its bridging role.
2. **Test hate-level ordering:** Train a reversed curriculum (e.g., level 5 → 4) and compare MAR; correlate classifier confidence with per-sample loss to validate the difficulty metric.
3. **Evaluate on real audio:** Use a small ethically-sourced real hate speech set to assess generalization beyond VITS-synthesized speech.
