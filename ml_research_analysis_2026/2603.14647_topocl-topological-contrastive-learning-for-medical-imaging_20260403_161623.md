# TopoCL: Topological Contrastive Learning for Medical Imaging
## Quick Facts
- **ArXiv ID:** 2603.14647  
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.14647  
- **Reference count:** 40  
- **Primary result:** TopoCL enhances contrastive learning for medical imaging by incorporating topological features (connectivity, boundaries) and achieves an average gain of +3.26% in linear probe classification accuracy over five CL methods across five medical datasets.

## Executive Summary
TopoCL introduces a framework to incorporate topological information into contrastive learning for medical imaging. It comprises three novel components: topology-aware augmentations calibrated via relative bottleneck distance on persistence diagrams, a hierarchical topology encoder with self- and cross-attention for H₀ and H₁ features, and an adaptive mixture-of-experts module for fusing visual and topological representations. Integrated with five contrastive learning methods (SimCLR, MoCo-v3, BYOL, DINO, Barlow Twins) on five medical datasets, TopoCL consistently improves linear probe classification accuracy by an average of 3.26%, with strong statistical significance.

## Method Summary
TopoCL extends standard contrastive learning by adding a parallel topological processing branch. It begins by extracting regions of interest (ROIs) from input images using SAM. Topology-aware augmentations are applied to the ROIs, where the augmentation intensity is controlled by a relative bottleneck distance (`d_rel^B`) computed between persistence diagrams of original and augmented ROIs, ensuring bounded topological perturbations. The ROIs are then processed via cubical persistent homology to obtain persistence diagrams (PDs) for H₀ (connected components) and H₁ (holes). A hierarchical topology encoder processes these PDs: a PH encoder embeds top-k persistent features, followed by self-attention within each homology dimension and bidirectional cross-attention between them to capture relationships. The resulting topological embedding is fused with visual features from a ResNet-50 backbone using an adaptive mixture-of-experts (MoE) module, which dynamically selects among five fusion strategies (visual-only, topology-only, concatenation, gated blending, cross-attention) via a gating network. The model is trained with a contrastive loss on the fused projections, with pretrained visual and topology encoders followed by joint fine-tuning.

## Key Results
- Average linear probe classification accuracy improvement of **+3.26%** over baseline CL methods.
- **86%** of individual comparisons (TopoCL vs. baseline) are statistically significant at *p*<0.05.
- **80%** of averaged metrics across datasets are significant at *p*<0.001.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1: Topology-aware augmentations preserve diagnostically relevant structures
Augmentations are calibrated using a relative bottleneck distance (`d_rel^B`) computed on persistence diagrams of ROIs: `d_rel^B = d_B(PD(x), PD(A(x))) / span(PD(x))`. This measures normalized topological change, enabling selection of intensities that produce bounded shifts (weak: 5–15%, strong: 15–25%). The core assumption is that medically meaningful features (e.g., lesion boundaries, tissue connectivity) are concentrated within ROIs and preserved under these perturbations. Evidence includes validation of intensity ranges via linear probe and ROI extraction to filter background artifacts. If augmentations exceed `d_rel^B ≈25%` or ROI extraction fails, performance degrades.

### Mechanism 2: Hierarchical Topology Encoder captures discriminative features and relationships
The encoder processes top-k persistent features separately for H₀ and H₁, applying self-attention to weight salient points (e.g., large tumor components) and bidirectional cross-attention to model dependencies (e.g., holes bounded by components). Pooling aggregates multi-granularity patterns into a 256-D vector. The assumption is that medical diagnoses rely on both individual topological features and their spatial relationships, which attention mechanisms can encode without supervised signals. Evidence includes ablation studies showing that removing cross-attention causes >1% drop on PathMNIST. If cross-attention misaligns H₁ with H₀ or top-k truncation discards low-persistence diagnostic features, representation quality drops.

### Mechanism 3: Adaptive MoE fusion dynamically combines visual and topological cues
Five expert networks implement different fusion strategies (visual-only, topology-only, concatenation, gated blending, cross-attention). A multi-gating MLP produces sample-specific weights over experts, and the weighted sum yields the final fused representation. The assumption is that medical images exhibit heterogeneous cues: some are texture-dominated, others structure-dominated, and many benefit from specific fusion strategies; a fixed fusion is suboptimal. Evidence includes dataset-specific gating patterns (e.g., ISIC2019 favors topology-only expert). If gating collapses to uniform or single-expert weights, the adaptive advantage is lost.

## Foundational Learning

- **Persistent Homology (PH)**: Extracts topological signatures (birth/death of connected components H₀, holes H₁) as persistence diagrams (PDs). *Why needed*: PDs are the input to the topology encoder, capturing shape features like lesion connectivity and boundaries. *Quick check*: How does bottleneck distance between PDs quantify topological similarity between two images?

- **Contrastive Learning Objectives (e.g., InfoNCE)**: Both visual and topology encoders are pretrained with the same CL loss to align positive views in embedding space. *Why needed*: Enables learning representation by encouraging consistency between differently augmented views of the same image. *Quick check*: Why are positive pairs generated with two different augmentation strengths (weak/strong) instead of two strong augmentations?

- **Attention Mechanisms (Self- and Cross-Attention)**: The Hierarchical Topology Encoder uses self-attention to weight important PD points within H₀/H₁, and cross-attention to model relationships between homology dimensions. *Why needed*: Captures both intra-dimensional salience and inter-dimensional dependencies (e.g., holes within components). *Quick check*: In the cross-attention module, what are the query, key, and value sources for H₀→H₁ and H₁→H₀ flows?

## Architecture Onboarding

**Component map**  
Input image -> ROI extraction (SAM) -> two topology-aware augmented views (x_topow, x_topos). Visual branch: x_w, x_s -> ResNet-50 -> projection head -> f_w, f_s. Topological branch: x_topow, x_topos -> cubical PH (GUDHI) -> PDs (H0, H1) -> PH Encoder -> self-attn (H0, H1) -> cross-attn (H0<->H1) -> pooling -> projection head -> t_w, t_s. Fusion: concatenate [f; t] per view -> multi-gating MLP -> 5 experts (visual-only, topology-only, concatenation, gated blending, cross-attention) -> weighted sum -> projection head -> z_w, z_s. Loss: contrastive loss between z_w and z_s.

**Critical path**  
1. Extract ROIs with SAM.  
2. Compute cubical persistent homology on ROIs to obtain PDs.  
3. Pretrain topology encoder with topology-aware augmentations and contrastive loss.  
4. Pretrain visual encoder (ResNet-50) with standard augmentations and contrastive loss.  
5. Jointly fine-tune both encoders and MoE fusion module with contrastive loss.

**Design tradeoffs**  
- ROI extraction (SAM): Improves topological relevance but adds dependency on SAM accuracy; failure to include key structures degrades calibration.  
- MoE fusion: Adaptive but increases parameters (~6.5M) and FLOPs (~2 GFLOPs); may overfit small datasets if gating not regularized.  
- Top-k PD features: Reduces cost but risks discarding low-persistence diagnostic features (e.g., small holes in early tumors).

**Failure signatures**  
- Augmentation mis-calibration: Linear probe accuracy drops >5% when `d_rel^B` ranges are swapped or applied to full images.  
- Hierarchical attention failure: Removing cross-attn causes >1% drop; attention weight entropy collapse indicates ignoring one homology dimension.  
- MoE gating collapse: Gating weights become uniform or always select one expert; inspect weight variance per batch.

**First 3 experiments**  
1. Validate topology-aware augmentations: Compare three `d_rel^B` range pairs (weak+strong, weak+weak, strong+strong) on OrganS/ISIC/Kvasir; expect weak+strong to outperform.  
2. Ablate hierarchical topology encoder: Remove cross-attention module; expect >1% ACC drop on PathMNIST.  
3. Test MoE gating stability: Train BYOL+TopoCL with different seeds; compute gating weight variance across test samples. Expect dataset-specific patterns to be consistent; high variance collapse indicates instability.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

**Question 1:** How does TopoCL's performance and computational overhead change when integrated with Vision Transformer (ViT) backbones instead of ResNet-50?  
- *Basis:* The paper only uses ResNet-50; ViTs are increasingly adopted in medical imaging.  
- *Why unresolved:* Interaction between topological features and transformer self-attention may differ, affecting accuracy and efficiency.  
- *Evidence needed:* Replace ResNet-50 with ViT variants across datasets, report accuracy, AUC, training time, FLOPs.

**Question 2:** What is the sensitivity of TopoCL's topological encoding and overall performance to inaccuracies in SAM-generated ROIs?  
- *Basis:* The paper relies on SAM for ROI extraction but does not analyze robustness to segmentation errors.  
- *Why unresolved:* SAM failures (e.g., incomplete masks) could distort PDs and degrade topological representations.  
- *Evidence needed:* Introduce controlled perturbations to masks (boundary shifts, hole inclusions, false merges) and measure change in linear probe performance.

**Question 3:** Can the TopoCL framework be effectively adapted for dense prediction tasks like medical image segmentation, and what architectural modifications are required?  
- *Basis:* The paper only evaluates linear probing for classification; segmentation is not tested.  
- *Why unresolved:* Current design (independent encoders, MoE fusion for image-level features) may not extend to pixel-wise tasks.  
- *Evidence needed:* Develop a segmentation variant (e.g., incorporate topological features into U-Net decoders) and benchmark on segmentation datasets (ISIC, Multi-Organ) with Dice and HD95 metrics.

## Limitations
- Augmentation calibration details per dataset (exact operations and intensity ranges for target `d_rel^B`) are missing, requiring additional empirical mapping for reproduction.
- Fine-tuning hyperparameters (learning rates for encoders vs. MoE, projection head reuse, layer freezing) are unspecified, potentially affecting reproducibility and performance.
- Computational overhead (SAM, PH, MoE adds ~6.5M params and ~2 GFLOPs) and generalization to larger/multi-modal datasets are not evaluated.

## Confidence
- **Overall improvement:** High (consistent gains across 5 datasets, strong statistical significance)
- **Topology-aware augmentations:** Medium (calibrated via `d_rel^B` but dataset-specific thresholds not provided)
- **Hierarchical Topology Encoder:** Medium (ablation shows benefit but attention alignment with medical semantics not proven)
- **Adaptive MoE fusion:** Medium (gating patterns suggest adaptivity but risk of collapse unaddressed)

## Next Checks
1. **Calibration audit:** Systematically sweep augmentation parameters on held-out data to derive per-dataset intensity distributions that ensure weak views achieve `d_rel^B` 5–15% and strong views 15–25%. Publish these thresholds.
2. **Attention analysis:** Ablate cross-attention and vary top-k truncation; visualize attention weights over PD points and correlate with expert annotations to confirm medical relevance.
3. **Gating stability:** Log gating weight entropy and expert utilization during training; if collapse occurs, add entropy regularization and measure impact on accuracy and specialization.
