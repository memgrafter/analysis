# A Depth-Aware Comparative Study of Euclidean and Hyperbolic Graph Neural Networks on Bitcoin Transaction Systems

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2603.16080
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.16080
- **Reference Count:** 26
- **Primary Result:** Hyperbolic tangent-space GNNs (e.g., 3-layer HGraphSAGE) consistently outperform Euclidean counterparts on Bitcoin transaction node classification, with macro-F1 gains of +0.07 on depth-2 and +0.03 on depth-3 subgraphs, especially for challenging illicit classes.

## Executive Summary
This paper presents a controlled empirical study comparing Euclidean and tangent-space hyperbolic graph neural networks (GNNs) for node classification on large-scale Bitcoin transaction subgraphs. The experiment varies neighborhood depth (2-hop vs. 3-hop) while fixing architecture (GCN, GraphSAGE, GAT), embedding dimension (d=256), and other hyperparameters to isolate the effect of underlying geometry. Hyperbolic models, particularly 3-layer HGraphSAGE, achieve higher macro-F1 scores across both depths, with pronounced improvements for structurally ambiguous illicit classes like Ponzi schemes and mining operations. A key optimization finding identifies a curvature threshold (c ≥ 1.0) that stabilizes training and reduces sensitivity to learning rate in hyperbolic models. The results suggest hyperbolic geometry better captures branching transactional patterns in deeper neighborhoods.

## Method Summary
The study uses a large Bitcoin transaction graph (~252M nodes, 785M edges) with multi-class node labels. Ego-centric subgraphs are sampled around labeled seed nodes using a fixed fan-out per hop: depth-2 uses {1:5, 2:10} and depth-3 uses {1:5, 2:10, 3:8}. Node features (37 dimensions) undergo a strict normalization pipeline (zero→NaN, log transform, percentile scaling, clipping, NaN→0). The core comparison involves 2-layer and 3-layer variants of GCN, GraphSAGE, and GAT implemented in both Euclidean space (PyTorch Geometric) and hyperbolic Poincaré ball with tangent-space operations (log/exp maps, Klein aggregation). Models are trained with Adam (LR=0.001, dropout=0.1) on a stratified 40/30/30 train/val/test split, with training oversampled to 300 samples per class. Curvature `c` for hyperbolic models is selected via validation grid search.

## Key Results
- Hyperbolic 3-layer HGraphSAGE achieves macro-F1 of 0.80 on depth-2 and 0.81 on depth-3 subgraphs, versus Euclidean bests of 0.73 and 0.78 respectively.
- Per-class F1 improvements are meaningful for challenging categories: Ponzi (+0.07), Mining (+0.05), and Ransomware (+0.04) on depth-3.
- Curvature ≥1.0 is critical for stable hyperbolic training; below c=0.5 models converge to poor minima and become highly learning rate-sensitive.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1: Branching Pattern Compatibility
- **Claim:** Hyperbolic space provides a more geometrically compatible representation for multi-hop, branching transactional patterns than Euclidean space.
- **Mechanism:** Hyperbolic space (Poincaré ball) has constant negative curvature, causing exponential volume growth with radius. This accommodates the tree-like expansion induced by fixed-fan-out ego-centric subgraphs, allowing divergent transaction branches to remain more separable. Euclidean space's polynomial volume growth leads to crowding.
- **Core assumption:** The sampled subgraphs approximate a branching/tree process, and latent illicit patterns (e.g., laundering fan-out) have hierarchical organization aligning with hyperbolic geometry.
- **Evidence anchors:** [abstract] states advantage for "branching transactional patterns"; [section IV.D] explicitly links fixed-fan-out sampling to tree-like expansion and hyperbolic suitability. Additionally, per-class F1 gains for illicit categories (Ponzi +0.07, Mining +0.05, Ransomware +0.04 on depth-3) support that hyperbolic geometry aids in disambiguating hierarchically organized classes.
- **Break condition:** If transactional patterns are not branching/hierarchical (e.g., grid-like, random), the hyperbolic advantage diminishes or reverses.

### Mechanism 2: Curvature Threshold for Optimization
- **Claim:** Stable, high-performing optimization in hyperbolic tangent-space GNNs requires a sufficient manifold curvature (c ≥ 1.0), which reduces learning rate sensitivity.
- **Mechanism:** Curvature controls space "tightness." Low c (c→0) is nearly Euclidean, losing representational benefits. Very high c increases non-linearity of log/exp maps, risking instability. A threshold (c≥1.0) transforms the optimization landscape, creating more informative gradients and robust convergence across wider learning rates.
- **Core assumption:** There exists a curvature threshold below which effective hyperbolic optimization is fragile.
- **Evidence anchors:** [section IV.C] shows poor local minima for c<0.5 and robust performance for c≥1.0 across learning rates. The paper's curvature grid search (c ∈ {0.1,0.3,0.5,0.75,1.0,1.25,1.5}) demonstrates a performance increase between c=0.75 and c=1.0, with c≥1.0 providing stable training across learning rates.
- **Break condition:** Setting c below ~0.75 leads to poor, LR-sensitive performance; excessively high c may cause numerical issues.

### Mechanism 3: Tangent-Space Practicality
- **Claim:** The tangent-space formulation (log/exp maps) enables stable training with familiar Euclidean neural operations while retaining hyperbolic representational benefits.
- **Mechanism:** Points are mapped from the Poincaré ball to the tangent space at the origin (a Euclidean vector) via `log_c0`. Standard Euclidean linear layers (e.g., `W * log(x)`) are applied, then results are mapped back via `exp_c0`. Neighborhood aggregation is performed in the Klein model to maintain geometric consistency. This hybrid approach balances power with computational stability.
- **Core assumption:** Distortion from alternating between tangent space and manifold via log/exp maps is an acceptable trade-off compared to fully native hyperbolic operations.
- **Evidence anchors:** [section III.D] details the equations; [section IV.E] notes that tangent-space formulation allows practical training on large-scale graphs without reported numerical issues. The paper successfully applies this to a graph with 252M nodes and 785M edges.
- **Break condition:** Excessive distortion from log/exp maps at very high dimensions or extreme curvatures could nullify hyperbolic benefits.

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component Map:**  
  Input (node features + edges) → Initial Projection (exp_c0 for hyperbolic) → Message Passing Layers (Klein aggregation + tangent-space linear transforms) → Output logits.
- **Critical Path:**
  1.  **Curvature Selection (`c`):** Primary hyperbolic hyperparameter. Must grid-search {0.1,0.3,0.5,0.75,1.0,1.25,1.5} on validation. c ≥ 1.0 is critical.
  2.  **Learning Rate Coupling:** LR must be chosen jointly with `c`. At low c, performance is highly LR-sensitive; at c≥1.0, it is robust.
  3.  **Depth Configuration:** Match number of GNN layers (2 or 3) to intended subgraph depth. 3-layer hyperbolic best on depth-3.
- **Design Tradeoffs:**
  - Hyperbolic vs. Euclidean: Hyperbolic better for branching but needs curvature tuning; Euclidean simpler/faster.
  - Tangent-space vs. native hyperbolic: Tangent-space is numerically stable with familiar ops but introduces mapping distortion; native is purer but harder to optimize.
  - Depth: Deeper models leverage hyperbolic geometry more but risk oversmoothing; paper mitigates via subgraph training.
- **Failure Signatures:**
  - **NaN/Inf losses:** Often unstable log/exp; check extreme curvature or unclamped norms.
  - **Poor validation F1 (<0.5) despite training:** Likely insufficient curvature (c too low) or mis-specified LR.
  - **No improvement over Euclidean:** Verify subgraphs truly induce branching (fixed fan-out); check class balance/oversampling.
- **First 3 Experiments:**
  1.  **Curvature Sweep Validation:** Fix HGraphSAGE (3-layer) on depth-3 subgraphs, train with c∈{0.5,0.75,1.0,1.25,1.5}. Plot macro-F1 vs. c; expect rise between 0.75 and 1.0.
  2.  **Depth Interaction Test:** Using optimal c, compare 2-layer vs. 3-layer on both depth-2 and depth-3 subgraphs. Expect hyperbolic 3-layer to dominate depth-3 and beat 2-layer on depth-2.
  3.  **Per-Class Failure Mode Analysis:** Train best Euclidean (GraphSAGE 3-layer) and hyperbolic (HGraphSAGE 3-layer, c=1.25). Generate per-class F1; verify gains on Ponzi, Mining, Ransomware.

## Limitations
- **Sampling & Splits:** Exact ego-centric subgraph construction (directed edges, duplicates) and oversampling procedure are unspecified, risking implementation drift.
- **Generalizability:** The "branching pattern" mechanism assumes fixed-fan-out sampling induces tree-like structures; real Bitcoin subgraphs may contain dense cycles, potentially reducing hyperbolic advantage.
- **Scalability Claims:** Tangent-space practicality is asserted but not benchmarked against fully native hyperbolic models; computational overhead of log/exp maps at high dimensionality remains undetailed.

## Confidence
- **Hyperbolic superiority** (F1 gains on depth-2/depth-3, per-class improvements): **High**. Controlled comparison, consistent across architectures, well-defined metrics.
- **Curvature threshold** (c ≥ 1.0 stabilizes training): **Medium**. Grid search shown, but seed sensitivity and dependence on embedding dimension/graph density not fully explored.
- **Mechanistic link** (hyperbolic volume growth separates branching branches): **Medium–Low**. Relies on paper’s specific sampling; without structural analysis of sampled subgraphs (e.g., tree-likeness scores), causal attribution remains tentative.
