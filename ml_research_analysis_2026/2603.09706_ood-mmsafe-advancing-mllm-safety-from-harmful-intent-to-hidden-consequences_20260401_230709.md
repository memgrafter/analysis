# OOD-MMSafe: Advancing MLLM Safety from Harmful Intent to Hidden Consequences
## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2603.09706
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.09706
- **Reference count:** 40
- **Primary result:** The CASPO framework reduces risk identification failure to 7.3% (Qwen2.5-VL-7B) and 5.7% (Qwen3-VL-4B), overcoming the "preference ceiling" of static alignment.

## Executive Summary
Current Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs) exhibit "causal blindness," failing to foresee latent physical or social hazards from seemingly benign queries. To address this, the authors introduce the OOD-MMSafe benchmark (455 query-image pairs) and the Consequence-Aware Safety Policy Optimization (CASPO) framework. CASPO integrates token-level self-distillation, guided by a safety constitution, with sparse outcome rewards to assign fine-grained credit for consequence-aware behavior. Evaluations show frontier models fail up to 67.5% on OOD-MMSafe, and static alignment (DPO) degrades with scale—a "preference ceiling." CASPO significantly reduces failure rates while maintaining utility, demonstrating a path beyond this ceiling.

## Method Summary
The study proceeds in two parts. First, the OOD-MMSafe benchmark is constructed to probe causal projection, pairing benign user queries with images that could lead to harm if acted upon (e.g., asking for a cake recipe with an image containing raw eggs). Models are evaluated in three modes: Standard (original prompt), Malicious (explicitly harmful intent), and Constitution (prompted with safety guidelines). Second, CASPO is proposed as a reinforcement learning fine-tuning framework. It computes a hybrid advantage signal combining a sparse outcome reward from a reward model with a dense, token-level self-distillation signal (`rt`). This signal measures the divergence between the model's standard generation and its constitution-guided generation for each token, encouraging reasoning paths that lead to safe outcomes.

## Key Results
- Frontier MLLMs (GPT-4o, Claude-3, Qwen3-VL) show high failure rates (29.7–92.3%) on OOD-MMSafe's Standard Mode, but near-perfect scores on Malicious Mode, confirming causal blindness.
- Static alignment (SPA-VL via DPO) yields diminishing or negative returns as model capacity grows, evidencing a "preference ceiling."
- CASPO reduces the risk identification failure ratio (`R0`) to 7.3% for Qwen2.5-VL-7B and 5.7% for Qwen3-VL-4B, far surpassing static alignment and outcome-only RL, while preserving helpfulness.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1: Causal Projection Deficit (Causal Blindness)
Frontier MLLMs fail because their safety training optimizes for classifying explicit malicious intent, not for predicting downstream consequences. The decoding objective is misaligned from a consequence-aware causal space; the model lacks the internalized mapping `Φ: (context, response) → terminal hazard state`. Evidence: models nearly perfectly refuse explicitly malicious prompts but fail on benign ones that lead to harm (Table 2). The break condition occurs when the model's intrinsic causal reasoning exceeds the semantic depth of the static preference distribution.

### Mechanism 2: Preference Ceiling & Format-Centric Collapse
Static preference optimization (DPO) provides a fixed "winner" distribution. As model capacity grows, training collapses to matching superficial refusal templates and punctuation rather than developing deeper hazard awareness, because the model's reasoning capability surpasses the dataset's quality ceiling. Evidence: DPO harms performance on larger models (Table 3), and analysis shows 56.1% of top-shifted tokens under static alignment are punctuation vs. 36.4% core content for constitution-guided methods (Figure 5). The reward model's feedback is too sparse to guide fine-grained causal reasoning.

### Mechanism 3: Self-Distillation with Token-Level Constitutions
CASPO uses the model's own constitution-guided reasoning as a dynamic, token-level teacher signal. For token `at`, compute `rt = log πθ(at|st, C) - log πθ(at|st)`, where `C` is a safety constitution. This `rt` modulates the sparse outcome reward `Ro` in a hybrid advantage `Ahyb`, amplifying token paths that lead to safe outcomes *through* constitution-aligned reasoning. This provides dense credit assignment for complex, high-level safety goals. Evidence: CASPO achieves dramatic failure rate reductions (Table 3) and avoids entropy collapse seen in outcome-only training (Figure 12).

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map:**  
  Input `(v, q)` -> Constitution Guide `C` (category-specific safety prompt) -> Policy Model `πθ` (generates tokens `a1:T`)  
  Parallel paths: `πθ` (unconditioned) and `πθ(.|st, C)` (constitution-conditioned) -> compute token-level signal `rt`  
  `πθ` + `a1:T` -> Outcome Reward Model `RMϕ` -> scalar `Ro`  
  `Ro` and `rt` -> Group-relative normalization -> Hybrid Advantage `Ahyb` -> Policy Gradient Update (with KL to `πref`)

- **Critical path:**  
  1. Sample group of trajectories `a1:T` from current `π_old` for `(v, q)`.  
  2. Compute `Ro = RMϕ(v, q, a1:T)`.  
  3. For each token `at`, forward-pass `πθ` twice (with/without `C`) to get `rt`.  
  4. Group-normalize `Ro` and `rt` across trajectories.  
  5. Compute `Ahyb(i,t) = ˆR(i)o * (1 + λ * sgn(ˆR(i)o) * ˆr(i,t)t)`.  
  6. Update `πθ` to maximize `J(θ)` (Eq 6) via GRPO-like optimizer.

- **Design tradeoffs:**  
  - **λ strength:** Trade-off between outcome guidance and token-level distillation. `λ=0` causes entropy collapse; `λ≥0.6` risks over-emphasizing template matching (Table 10).  
  - **Distillation target:** Using an SFT model (`SMP`) vs. the original policy (`OMP`). SFT amplifies the constitution signal for smaller models (Figure 6).  
  - **Reward model fidelity:** `RMϕ` must capture latent hazards; if it relies on surface heuristics, `Ro` misguides training.

- **Failure signatures:**  
  - Training: Actor KL loss spikes (excessive drift) or entropy plummets (rigid refusals, Figure 12b).  
  - Evaluation: High `R0` in Standard Mode but low in Malicious/Constitution modes—indicates persistent causal blindness.  
  - Per-category: Disparity in improvement between deterministic (violence) and nuanced (privacy) categories (Figures 7, 8, 11).

- **First 3 experiments:**  
  1. **Baseline replication:** Evaluate base model in Standard/Malicious/Constitution modes (Table 2 protocol) to establish causal blindness and preference ceiling.  
  2. **Constitution Elicitation:** Prompt base model with category-specific `C` without training; measure `∆R0`. Tests if latent capability exists.  
  3. **CASPO Ablation:** Train three variants: (a) `OutR` (only `ˆRo`), (b) `TokR` (only `ˆrt`), (c) `Hybrid`. Compare `R0` and utility (SIUO/MSS-Bench). Sweep `λ` as in Table 10.

## Limitations
- Evaluation limited to Qwen VL models and 25 hazard categories; generalizability across architectures and domains is unverified.
- The outcome reward model (`RMϕ`) may rely on superficial shortcuts, potentially biasing the training signal.
- Hand-crafted, category-specific constitutions; robustness to prompt variation and scalability to novel categories are unclear.
- Evidence for the "preference ceiling" rests on one static DPO dataset and two model sizes; broader scaling is needed.

## Confidence
- **Causal Projection Deficit:** High. Robust multi-model, multi-mode failure patterns (Table 2, Figure 4).
- **Preference Ceiling:** Medium. Clear negative scaling trend but limited model/data diversity.
- **Self-Distillation Efficacy:** High for observed results; Medium for assumption of universally available constitution signal (SFT dependency for small models).

## Next Checks
1. **Cross-architecture replication:** Run Standard/Malicious/Constitution evaluations on LLaVA and InternVL to test if causal blindness and preference ceiling are architecture-agnostic.
2. **Constitution robustness:** Replace hand-crafted constitutions with LLM-generated ones and perform paraphrase attacks to assess signal stability.
3. **Zero-category generalization:** Train CASPO on a subset of hazard categories (e.g., violence, privacy) and evaluate on held-out categories (e.g., disinformation, self-harm) to test transfer.
