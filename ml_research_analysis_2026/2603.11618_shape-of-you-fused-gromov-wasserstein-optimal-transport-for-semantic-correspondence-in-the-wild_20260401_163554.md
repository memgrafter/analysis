# Shape-of-You: Fused Gromov-Wasserstein Optimal Transport for Semantic Correspondence in-the-Wild

**Quick Facts**
- **arXiv ID:** 2603.11618  
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.11618  
- **Reference count:** 40  
- **Primary result:** Achieves SOTA on SPair-71k (67.9% PCK@0.1) and AP-10k (68.0% intra-species), with large gains on geometry-aware subsets by using 3D structure to disambiguate correspondences.

**Executive Summary**
The paper tackles semantic correspondence in wild images, where geometric ambiguities like symmetries and repetitive features cause standard nearest-neighbor pseudo-labeling to fail. The proposed method, Shape-of-You (SoY), reformulates pseudo-label generation as a Fused Gromov-Wasserstein (FGW) optimal transport problem. It lifts images to 3D using a foundation model (VGGT) and jointly optimizes inter-feature similarity (via 2D features from DINOv2/Stable Diffusion) and intra-structural consistency (via 3D point distances). The intractable quadratic GW term is approximated with anchor-based linearization, enabling efficient iterative refinement. Pseudo-labels are then used to train a lightweight adapter with a soft-target loss that blends geometric and semantic signals. SoY demonstrates robust performance on challenging benchmarks without requiring explicit 3D annotations.

**Method Summary**
SoY generates pseudo-labels by solving an iterative Fused Gromov-Wasserstein transport problem between patch sets of two images. First, SAM masks extract object regions, and patches are sampled on a grid. DINOv2 and Stable Diffusion provide fused 2D features for semantic cost (cosine dissimilarity). VGGT lifts each image to a 3D point cloud; distances between 3D points define the geometric cost. The process starts with unbalanced OT (UOT) on the semantic cost to get an initial transport plan π⁰. Then, for T=5 iterations: K=64 anchor pairs are selected from the current plan using cycle-consistency and geometric distortion; the GW cost is linearized by fixing the anchor transport, yielding a per-patch geometric cost C_geo; the normalized semantic and geometric costs are fused (α=0.3) and solved with UOT (KL penalty ρ=0.75, entropy ε=1) to update π. The final π is filtered with relaxed cycle-consistency to produce a hard pseudo-label π_hard. To train an adapter network, a soft target π_soft = (1−β)π_hard + βπ_curr (β=0.5) is used, where π_curr is the adapter’s own semantic plan (detached). The loss combines a symmetric supervised contrastive loss on π_soft and an L2 dense regression loss with added noise. Inference uses nearest-neighbor matching on adapter features.

**Key Results**
- SOTA performance: 67.9% PCK@0.1 on SPair-71k and 68.0% (intra-species) on AP-10k.
- Significant gains on geometry-aware subsets (e.g., symmetric objects, repetitive patterns), demonstrating robustness to geometric ambiguity.
- Ablations confirm the contribution of each component: fused Fused UOT outperforms NN, semantic OT, and fused balanced OT.

**Why This Works (Mechanism)**
**Mechanism 1: 2D Nearest-Neighbor Matches Are Locally Inconsistent Under Geometric Ambiguity**  
Nearest-neighbor matching optimizes only local appearance similarity, ignoring global structure. For objects with symmetric or repetitive parts (e.g., left and right wheels), distinct source patches may map to the same target patch, violating one-to-one consistency and propagating noise. Evidence: [abstract] notes reliance on 2D appearance fails to resolve geometric ambiguities; [section 1] states NN ignores global information; [section 3.1] justifies using 3D coordinates. Break condition: if features are highly discriminative and no ambiguities exist, NN might suffice; paper shows NN degrades on geometry-aware subsets.

**Mechanism 2: Fused Gromov-Wasserstein Enforces Joint Feature Similarity and 3D Geometric Consistency**  
FGW minimizes (1−α)⟨C_sem, π⟩ + α L_GW(π), where L_GW compares intra-instance 3D distance matrices D^A, D^B. This ensures that if point *i* is close to *k* on the source shape, its match *j* should be close to the matched anchor for *k* on the target shape, filtering out geometrically invalid matches. Evidence: [abstract] "jointly optimizes inter-feature similarity and intra-structural consistency"; [section 1] "defines this intra-structure in the geometric space"; [section 3.3] anchor-based linearization. Break condition: if VGGT reconstruction is erroneous (flat, transparent surfaces) or object parts are physically disconnected, the geometric prior becomes misleading.

**Mechanism 3: Anchor-Based Linearization Makes the Quadratic GW Problem Tractable**  
The GW cost is quadratic in π. SoY approximates it by selecting K high-confidence anchor pairs and fixing their transport to uniform, reducing L_GW(π) ≈ Σ_{i,j} π_{ij} C_geo_{ij} where C_geo_{ij} is the average 3D distance distortion to anchors. This linearizes the problem, allowing efficient Sinkhorn-based UOT. Evidence: [section 3.3] Equation (6) shows linearized form; [section A.2] performance saturates at K=64, supporting anchor sufficiency. Break condition: poor anchor selection (due to ambiguous initial matches or loose cycle thresholds) can reinforce errors.

**Foundational Learning**
- **Optimal Transport (OT) and Sinkhorn Algorithm**  
  *Why needed:* Computes a soft, probabilistic assignment (transport plan π) between patch sets. Unbalanced OT (UOT) handles occlusions/non-overlapping regions via KL penalty on marginals.  
  *Quick check:* Why is the KL-divergence penalty in unbalanced OT (Eq. 3) necessary for in-the-wild semantic correspondence?
- **Gromov-Wasserstein (GW) Distance**  
  *Why needed:* Standard OT requires a ground cost in a shared space, which doesn’t exist for matching points from different object instances. GW compares intra-domain distance matrices, enabling structure-preserving matching across unaligned geometries.  
  *Quick check:* What specific property of GW makes it applicable to matching graphs or shapes where points lack a shared coordinate system?
- **Foundation Models for Feature and Geometry Extraction**  
  *Why needed:* DINOv2/Stable Diffusion provide dense, semantically rich 2D features. VGGT lifts 2D images to 3D point clouds to compute distance matrices for the GW term. Without these, extensive training or 3D annotations would be required.  
  *Quick check:* Why can’t the 2D feature distances from DINOv2 alone define the “intra-structure” in the GW term?

**Architecture Onboarding**
- **Component map:**  
  SAM mask → VGGT 3D lift → compute 3D distance matrices D^A, D^B → semantic UOT (initial anchors) → for t=1..5: select anchors → compute linearized C_geo → fuse C_total → UOT → π^(T) → filter with relaxed cycle-consistency → π_hard → train adapter with soft-target loss (blend π_hard and π_curr) + dense loss → inference via NN on adapter features.
- **Critical path:** The pipeline depends on accurate 3D lift (VGGT) to compute D^A, D^B; anchor selection hinges on initial semantic UOT; iterative FGW refines π; soft-target loss balances geometric and semantic signals during training.
- **Design tradeoffs:**  
  - **Anchor count K:** Higher K captures more geometry but increases cost (O(K) per candidate). Performance saturates at K=64.  
  - **Fusion weight α:** Balances feature vs. geometry. α=0.3 optimal; too low ignores geometry, too high overrides discriminative features.  
  - **UOT KL penalty ρ:** Controls mass deviation for partial matching. ρ=0.75 best; higher makes it balanced OT, harming occlusions.  
  - **Soft-target weight β:** Blends hard geometric targets with network’s semantic plan. β=0.5 optimal; higher over-smooths, lower over-penalizes.
- **Failure signatures:**  
  - Degenerate 3D reconstructions (flat, noisy, transparent surfaces) mislead geometric cost.  
  - Symmetric parts (e.g., car wheels) cause anchors to self-consistently reinforce wrong matches.  
  - Over-smoothing from high β or noisy features collapses clusters.  
  - UOT ρ mismatch causes extreme marginals (over/under-matching).
- **First 3 experiments:**  
  1. Compare pseudo-label strategies: Evaluate PCK_label@0.1 for NN, Semantic OT, Fused OT, Fused UOT; expect monotonic gain with added components.  
  2. Hyperparameter sensitivity: Sweep K, α, ρ on pseudo-label quality to identify optimal ranges and saturation points.  
  3. Backbone and intra-structure ablation: Train with DINOv2-only vs. DINOv2+SD pseudo-labels; replace 3D distances with 2D image or feature-space distances in Fused OT; expect 3D distances to significantly outperform others.

**Open Questions the Paper Calls Out**
- **Question 1:** How can the framework be made robust to failures of the 3D foundation model on transparent surfaces or flat degenerate geometries?  
  *Basis:* Limitations state reliance on 3D models which can yield flat reconstructions or fail on transparent surfaces.  
  *Why unresolved:* FGW directly uses 3D coordinates; inaccurate lifts mislead geometric cost. No mechanism to detect/downweight unreliable points.
- **Question 2:** How can anchor selection be improved to avoid erroneous matches in severe geometric ambiguities (e.g., symmetric car parts at mid-range viewpoints)?  
  *Basis:* Limitations note symmetric parts cause erroneous anchor matching; supplementary shows car degradation at mid-range azimuths.  
  *Why unresolved:* Anchor heuristic uses transport confidence and cycle consistency; symmetric parts can satisfy both, reinforcing errors.
- **Question 3:** Can the soft-target loss be refined to preserve geometric signals while denoising pseudo-labels, avoiding over-smoothing?  
  *Basis:* Limitations mention soft targets may over-smooth geometric signals; blending is uniform and not adaptive.  
  *Why unresolved:* Uniform β=0.5 dilutes exact geometric guidance; on ambiguous categories (e.g., boat), network may drift toward feature similarity.
- **Question 4:** How can Shape-of-You handle scenes with multiple disconnected objects or significant occlusions where the shape structure is partially absent?  
  *Basis:* Inferred from supplementary failure cases (e.g., detached bicycle pedal) where FGW fails due to broken shape coherence.  
  *Why unresolved:* FGW compares full intra-structure distance matrices; missing parts violate consistency assumptions; UOT only handles mass, not structural disconnectivity.

**Limitations**
- Unknown implementation details (adapter architecture from TLR [40], exact anchor scoring formula, VGGT preprocessing) block faithful replication.
- Performance critically hinges on VGGT’s 3D lift quality; failures on flat, transparent, or disconnected objects are noted but not quantified.
- Generalizability is limited to evaluated animal/vehicle categories (SPair-71k, AP-10k); behavior on other domains (e.g., urban scenes) or extreme viewpoint changes is unknown.
- Anchor selection heuristic is vulnerable to symmetric ambiguities; erroneous anchors can self-consistently reinforce incorrect matches.

**Confidence**
- Mechanism 1 (2D NN fails under ambiguity): High  
  Evidence is strong and well-established; paper shows clear degradation on geometry-aware subsets.
- Mechanism 2 (FGW + 3D structure recovers consistency): Medium  
  Empirical gains are convincing, but full claim depends on VGGT reliability, which is not stress-tested across diverse failure modes.
- Mechanism 3 (anchor linearization suffices): High  
  Sensitivity analysis (K saturation) supports sufficiency; linearization error not measured but appears acceptable in practice.
- Overall efficacy claim (SOTA due to FGW): Medium  
  Results are strong on benchmarks, but robustness to foundation model errors and transfer beyond tested categories remain uncertain.

**Next Checks**
1. **VGGT failure stress-test:** Corrupt VGGT lifts (synthetic noise, alternative depth model) and measure drop in pseudo-label PCK_label@0.1; quantify sensitivity to 3D quality.
2. **Anchor selection ablation:** Replace proposed heuristic with random or pure semantic confidence selection; compare pseudo-label quality and final PCK to isolate contribution of geometric anchor ranking.
3. **Out-of-distribution evaluation:** Test on a dataset with novel geometric ambiguities (e.g., symmetric furniture, repetitive architecture); compare SoY against NN and OT baselines to check transfer of robustness.
