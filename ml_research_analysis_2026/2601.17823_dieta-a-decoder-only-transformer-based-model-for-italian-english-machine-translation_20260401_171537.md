# DIETA: A Decoder-only transformer-based model for Italian-English machine TrAnslation

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2601.17823
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.17823
- Reference count: 40
- Primary result: A 0.5B parameter decoder-only transformer achieves state-of-the-art among sub-3B models on multiple IT-EN benchmarks, using a curated 207M sentence pair corpus and 352M back-translations.

## Executive Summary
DIETA addresses the need for high-quality Italian-English machine translation, where general multilingual models often underperform on specific language pairs. It is a decoder-only Transformer with 0.5 billion parameters, trained on a curated parallel corpus of 207 million sentence pairs and augmented with 352 million back-translated examples. The authors also release a new evaluation set, WikiNews-25. The best variant, DIETA+cont, consistently ranks in the second quartile among 32 systems and outperforms most sub-3B models on four of five benchmarks, demonstrating that careful data curation and architecture specialization can yield strong performance even with limited parameters.

## Method Summary
The authors collect a large parallel corpus from OPUS sources (Europarl, DGT-TM, ParaCrawl, OpenSubtitles, WikiMatrix, Books) and clean it via duplicate removal and LLM-based filtering with Phi-4, yielding 207 million high-quality sentence pairs. These are formatted with explicit language tags (ENG:→IT:, IT:→ENG:) to enable bidirectional training in a decoder-only setup. To improve coverage, they generate 352 million synthetic pairs by back-translating monolingual Italian and English texts (NewsCrawl and FineWeb) using multiple teacher models (OPUS-MT-TC-BIG, NLLB-200-3.3B, GemmaX2-28-9B). The model uses a Minerva-7B SentencePiece tokenizer (51k vocabulary) and a 6-layer decoder-only Transformer with squared-ReLU FFN, rotary embeddings, and query-key normalization. Training employs the Lion optimizer with linear decay and warm-up, over a single epoch on the shuffled mixed corpus. Continued training on the combined data (DIETA+cont) yields the best results.

## Key Results
- DIETA+cont achieves 36.4/42.8 BLEU on NTREX-128, 58.3/70.0 on Tatoeba, 37.2/38.8 on WMT-24pp, and 30.4/33.4 on FLORES-200.
- Ranks in the second quartile among 32 systems on the shared task, outperforming most sub-3B models on four of five benchmarks.
- Despite having 6× fewer parameters than many competitors, it remains competitive, particularly on reference-based metrics.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
A high-quality, curated parallel corpus reduces noise and improves translation quality by providing clean, diverse, and direction-tagged examples. The mechanism involves duplicate removal via OpusTools/OpusFilter, followed by LLM-based (Phi-4) binary filtering to discard non-translation pairs. The remaining 207M pairs are duplicated with explicit language tags to enable bidirectional training in a decoder-only setup. The core assumption is that clean, well-aligned sentence pairs with clear directionality lead to more effective learning of translation mappings. Evidence is provided in the abstract and Section 3.1, which details the sources and cleaning steps, and the corpus is publicly released. A break condition is that if the Phi-4 filtering introduces bias (e.g., favoring certain domains) or misses subtle misalignments, the corpus may lack diversity or contain residual noise, harming generalization.

### Mechanism 2
Back-translation from large teacher models expands coverage of linguistic diversity and recency, especially for news-related content, mitigating data sparsity. Monolingual Italian and English texts (NewsCrawl 2008–2018/2023–2024, FineWeb) are translated using OPUS-MT-TC-BIG, NLLB-200-3.3B, and GemmaX2-28-9B, yielding 352M synthetic pairs that are mixed with the parallel corpus. The core assumption is that high-quality synthetic data from strong teacher models can approximate real translations, providing additional training signal that improves fluency and domain adaptation. Evidence includes the abstract's mention of targeting news content and Section 6.6 showing +1–3 BLEU gains from adding synthetic data. A break condition is that teacher model biases or hallucinations (e.g., overgenerating formal language) could propagate to the student, especially in low-resource constructions.

### Mechanism 3
A compact decoder-only architecture with specialized tokenization and continued training on mixed data achieves parameter efficiency by maximizing representational capacity for the specific language pair. The model uses Minerva's 51k SentencePiece vocabulary (balanced IT/EN), a 6-layer decoder with post-norm, hidden dim 2048, 32 heads, squared-ReLU FFN, rotary embeddings, residual attention, and query-key normalization. Training uses Lion optimizer, linear decay with warm-up, and continued training (DIETA+cont) on the mixed corpus. The core assumption is that task-specific architectural choices can compensate for reduced scale by focusing capacity on IT-EN translation rather than general multilingual representation. Evidence is the abstract's claim of outperforming larger sub-3B models and Section 4's architecture details. A break condition is that the architectural optimizations might be suboptimal for IT-EN morphology, or continued training might cause overfitting to the mixed corpus distribution.

## Foundational Learning
- **Parallel corpus curation**: High-quality aligned sentence pairs are crucial for translation quality; noisy or misaligned pairs degrade learning. *Quick check*: What are the trade-offs between using an LLM for filtering versus rule-based methods like LangID or length ratios?
- **Back-translation**: Expands monolingual data into synthetic parallel data, improving linguistic variety and domain adaptation. *Quick check*: Why use different teacher models (OPUS-MT, NLLB, GemmaX2) for different data sources instead of a single model?
- **Decoder-only MT with language tags**: Enables a single decoder to handle bidirectional translation without an encoder, simplifying architecture. *Quick check*: How does explicit direction tagging (ENG:→IT:) affect the model's ability to disentangle source and target languages compared to using special tokens?
- **Evaluation metrics landscape**: Different metrics capture different aspects (surface matching, semantic adequacy, reference-free quality); relying on one can be misleading. *Quick check*: Why does DIETA perform better on reference-based metrics (COMET, BLEURT) than reference-free ones (QE MetricX, COMETKiwi)?

## Architecture Onboarding

### Component Map
Input: Sentence pairs formatted with language tags (e.g., "ENG:...IT:...") -> Tokenizer: Minerva-7B SentencePiece (51k vocab) -> Model: 6-layer decoder-only Transformer (post-norm, hidden dim 2048, 32 heads, squared-ReLU FFN, rotary embeddings, residual attention, query-key normalization) -> Training: x-Transformers framework; Lion optimizer (lr 2e-4), linear decay with 10% warmup, single epoch on shuffled 768M examples (parallel + back-translated) -> Output: Next-token prediction conditioned on source+tags.

### Critical Path
1. Collect raw parallel corpora from OPUS (Europarl, DGT-TM, ParaCrawl, OpenSubtitles, WikiMatrix, Books).
2. Clean: deduplicate (OpusTools), filter with Phi-4.
3. Generate synthetic data via back-translation using teacher models.
4. Format all pairs with direction tags, shuffle.
5. Train DIETA from scratch on parallel data (DIETA) or mixed (DIETA+BT, etc.).
6. Evaluate on NTREX-128, Tatoeba, WMT-24pp, FLORES-200, WikiNews-25.

### Design Tradeoffs
- **Model size (0.5B vs larger)**: Less capacity but lower memory; requires high-quality data to compete.
- **Back-translation sources**: NewsCrawl (domain-specific) vs FineWeb (web-scale) – diversity vs noise.
- **Continued training**: DIETA+cont (parallel+NewsCrawl) balances quality and efficiency; DIETA+allsynth adds FineWeb but risks domain shift.
- **Evaluation**: Multiple metrics reveal strengths (reference-based) and weaknesses (QE); greedy decoding favored for efficiency.

### Failure Signatures
- Poor QE scores (MetricX, COMETKiwi) relative to reference-based metrics → potential fluency/adequacy gaps not captured by references.
- Large IT→EN vs EN→IT gaps (except WikiNews-25) → training data imbalance (more EN monolingual for back-translation).
- Degradation on FLORES (Wikipedia) vs news benchmarks → overfitting to news/web domains.
- Minerva tokenizer mismatch if applied to other language pairs.

### First 3 Experiments
1. Ablate back-translation: Compare DIETA (parallel-only) vs DIETA+BT (parallel+NewsCrawl) to isolate synthetic data contribution.
2. Test continued training schedules: DIETA+cont (2nd epoch on mixed) vs DIETA+allsynth (3rd epoch on all synthetic) to assess marginal gains and overfitting.
3. Domain generalization: Evaluate on WikiNews-25 (2025 news) vs older benchmarks (NTREX-128) to measure recency adaptation and temporal shift robustness.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
1. How can QE-aware fine-tuning specifically reduce the reference-free quality estimation gap between DIETA and larger models? The paper notes remaining headroom lies almost entirely in reference-free QE metrics.
2. Can sparse Mixture-of-Experts (MoE) architectures extend DIETA's parameter efficiency while maintaining or improving translation quality across diverse benchmarks?
3. What are the fidelity and efficiency trade-offs when distilling and quantizing DIETA for edge deployment?
4. What is the optimal proportion of NewsCrawl versus FineWeb back-translations for maximizing out-of-domain generalization in Italian-English MT?
5. Does Minerva's pre-trained SentencePiece tokenizer offer measurable advantages over a corpus-specific or multilingual tokenizer for Italian-English translation?

## Limitations
- Training hyperparameters (batch size, gradient accumulation, mixed precision, distributed setup, total steps) are unreported, blocking precise replication.
- Back-translation source specifics (exact FineWeb subsets, precise NewsCrawl year ranges) and Phi-4 filtering threshold are unspecified, hindering full assessment of data diversity.
- Architectural ablations are missing, so the contribution of specific choices (e.g., squared-ReLU vs. GEGLU) for IT-EN translation is unclear.

## Confidence
- High-quality curated corpus is primary driver: Medium confidence (strong evidence but no ablation, potential LLM bias).
- Back-translation provides clear gains: Medium confidence (gains demonstrated but teacher error propagation not analyzed).
- Compact decoder-only architecture achieves parameter efficiency: Low to Medium confidence (outperformance shown but no architectural ablations, unclear interplay with tokenizer and training schedule).

## Next Checks
1. Conduct a controlled back-translation ablation: Train DIETA variants adding only NewsCrawl or only FineWeb synthetic data to quantify each source's contribution and noise.
2. Analyze the Phi-4 filtering bias: Sample discarded pairs from the initial corpus and analyze by domain (e.g., Europarl vs. ParaCrawl) and linguistic features (length ratio, lexical diversity) to detect systematic bias.
3. Perform a domain generalization test: Evaluate DIETA+cont on a held-out, diverse-domain test set (e.g., medical, legal, conversational IT-EN data) to explicitly test for overfitting to news/web domains.
