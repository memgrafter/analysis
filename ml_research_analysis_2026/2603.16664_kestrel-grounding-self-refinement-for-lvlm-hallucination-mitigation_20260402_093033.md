# Kestrel: Grounding Self-Refinement for LVLM hallucination Mitigation

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2603.16664  
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.16664  
- **Reference count:** 40  
- **Primary result:** Training-free method reduces hallucinations in LVLMs, achieving +3.31% (Qwen3-VL) and +3.03% (InternVL3.5) accuracy on POPE, and +28.34 points on MME-Hallucination, with 68.3% human preference.

## Executive Summary
Kestrel is a training-free framework that mitigates hallucinations in large vision-language models (LVLMs) by combining explicit visual grounding with an evidence-verified iterative self-refinement loop. It extracts verifiable claims from an initial answer, uses a segmentation agent (SAM3) to collect visual evidence, converts that evidence into structured textual statements, and employs an LVLM judge to verify claims against cited evidence. Refinements occur only when high-confidence contradictions are found, preventing over-correction. Across POPE and MME-Hallucination benchmarks, Kestrel significantly outperforms prior post-hoc methods and yields more interpretable, factually consistent outputs preferred by human annotators.

## Method Summary
Kestrel operates as a four-stage pipeline without model training. First, it generates an initial LVLM response and decomposes it into verifiable claims (existence, count, color, position). Second, a visual grounding agent (SAM3) produces segmentation masks, bounding boxes, and crop-zoom regions for each claim’s target entities; an LVLM then converts these visual artifacts into normalized textual evidence items with unique citations. Third, an LVLM judge evaluates each claim against its cited textual evidence, outputting a verdict (supported/contradicted/insufficient), confidence score, and reasoning. Finally, an evidence-gated update refines the answer only if any claim is contradicted with high confidence; the process iterates up to three rounds with early stopping after stable verification. The system maintains a stateful history to prioritize uncertain claims in subsequent rounds.

## Key Results
- On POPE (MS-COCO, A-OKVQA, GQA): **+3.31%** accuracy with Qwen3-VL and **+3.03%** with InternVL3.5 over strong baselines.
- On MME-Hallucination: **+28.34 points** with Qwen3-VL, setting a new state-of-the-art.
- Human evaluation: Kestrel outputs preferred **68.3%** of the time for factual consistency and interpretability.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Explicit Visual Grounding Provides Spatially Localized Evidence
SAM3 generates segmentation masks, bounding boxes, and crop-zoom views for entities in claims, creating inspectable evidence for existence, count, color, and position. This anchors claims to concrete image regions rather than relying on the LVLM’s internal attention. Break condition: SAM3 failure to produce masks for a target entity (e.g., low confidence, ambiguous prompt) breaks the evidence chain for dependent claims.

### Structured Textual Evidence Enables Auditable Verification
Visual evidence is converted into concise, claim-type-specific textual statements (e.g., “3 instances of X”, “X is top-left”) with citation tags (e.g., `e_count_c1`). This normalized format allows the LVLM judge to verify claims against specific, citable items, reducing ambiguity. Break condition: Errors in textual evidence generation (e.g., miscounting a crop) propagate corrupted inputs to the judge.

### Evidence-Gated Iterative Refinement Prevents Over-Correction
Answer updates occur only when a claim is contradicted with high confidence and backed by cited evidence. The stateful loop prioritizes uncertain claims, carries forward verification traces, and stops early after consistent “supported” verdicts. This conservative gating filters transient contradictions from noisy tools or judge errors. Break condition: Mis-calibrated confidence thresholds or stagnant evidence may cause premature termination or wasted compute.

## Foundational Learning
- **Visual Grounding (Segmentation/Detection)**  
  *Why needed:* SAM3 must reliably localize entities from claim text to produce spatial evidence.  
  *Quick check:* If SAM3 fails to segment a clearly visible object, possible causes include poor prompt formulation or the model lacking the entity in its training vocabulary.

- **LVLM-as-Judge Verification**  
  *Why needed:* The judge must accurately map textual claims to cited evidence items and ignore absent evidence.  
  *Quick check:* How does the verification prompt force the judge to use only provided evidence? If ignored, the judge may rely on its internal knowledge, leading to false verdicts.

- **Evidence-Gated Iterative Refinement**  
  *Why needed:* The loop’s statefulness, claim prioritization, and early stopping balance thoroughness against instability.  
  *Quick check:* If the first round yields a “contradicted” verdict with medium confidence, does the policy trigger a second round or update immediately? (It should update only on high-confidence contradiction.)

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component map:**  
Initial Answer → Claim Decomposition → SAM3 Grounding (masks/boxes/crops) → Textual Evidence Generation (with citations) → LVLM Judge Verdict (per claim) → Evidence-Gated Update → (if unstable) Repeat with prioritized claims (max 3 rounds)

**Critical path:**  
Question+Image → Initial Answer → Claims → Visual Evidence → Structured Text → Verification Verdict → Conditional Refinement → Next Round

**Design tradeoffs:**  
- Latency vs. accuracy: Each iteration adds ~12s; early stopping (2 rounds for ~90% cases) mitigates cost.  
- Tool reliability vs. over-correction: SAM3 false negatives cause insufficient evidence; judge errors cause wrong verdicts. Gating reduces but does not eliminate risks.  
- Structured evidence expressivity: Converting crops/boxes to text (e.g., color) loses visual nuance but enables LVLM judge compatibility.

**Failure signatures:**  
- Stuck in “insufficient”: SAM3 finds no instances or textual evidence is too ambiguous.  
- Over-correction: Answer flips from correct to incorrect due to a single high-confidence erroneous verdict (rare).  
- Non-convergence: Circular refinement if evidence remains contradictory; max K=3 prevents infinite loops.  
- Cold-start for rare entities: SAM3 may not recognize obscure objects, halting refinement.

**First 3 experiments:**  
1. Ablate the grounding agent (remove SAM3, use only LVLM internal attention) and measure POPE accuracy drop.  
2. Disable structured textual evidence conversion (use raw crops only) and evaluate MME-Hallucination impact.  
3. Conduct a human preference study comparing baseline versus Kestrel outputs, expecting >65% preference for Kestrel.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- How can computational overhead be reduced while maintaining effectiveness? (Proposes adaptive tool invocation, claim prioritization, evidence reuse—unimplemented.)
- Does Kestrel generalize beyond object existence, count, color, and position to visual illusions, relational reasoning, or ambiguous local evidence? (Evaluation limited to POPE/MME; untested on HallusionBench or MMHal-Bench.)
- How dependent are gains on the choice of visual grounding agent? (Framework exclusive to SAM3; no comparison with Grounding DINO or Owl-ViT.)
- Does using an LVLM as a judge introduce biases or errors, and how can verification be made more robust? (Judge reliability not analyzed; no multi-judge voting or uncertainty-aware mechanisms explored.)

## Limitations
- **SAM3 availability & reliability:** The cited SAM3 (2025) may be inaccessible; substitutions (SAM/SAM2) could degrade evidence quality and performance.
- **Threshold vagueness:** Exact confidence thresholds per claim type and early-stopping criteria are unspecified, risking inconsistent replication.
- **Latency overhead:** Iterative grounding and verification incur ~12 s per round, hindering real-time deployment.

## Confidence
- **Visual grounding mechanism:** Medium (ablation shows ~+2% gain, but hinges on unverified SAM3 performance).
- **Structured textual evidence:** Medium (removing evidence types hurts scores, yet LVLM-based generation remains a weak link).
- **Evidence-gated refinement:** Medium (ablation and transition stats support stability, but threshold sensitivity unknown).
- **Overall Kestrel gains:** High (consistent improvements across POPE, MME-Hallucination, and human preference).

## Next Checks
