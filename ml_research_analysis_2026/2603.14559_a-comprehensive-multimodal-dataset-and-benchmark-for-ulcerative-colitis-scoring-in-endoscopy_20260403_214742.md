# A comprehensive multimodal dataset and benchmark for ulcerative colitis scoring in endoscopy

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID**: 2603.14559
- **Source URL**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.14559
- **Reference count**: 35
- **Primary result**: DINOv2_Large achieves Top-1 Accuracy 0.7138 on Mayo Endoscopic Score classification; BLIP-Large achieves BLEU-1 0.8210 on clinical caption generation using the Endo-UC dataset (2,495 images).

## Executive Summary
This paper introduces Endo-UC, a multi-centre dataset of 2,495 annotated colonoscopic images to address the lack of benchmarks for automated ulcerative colitis (UC) scoring. Each image carries dual severity scores (Mayo Endoscopic Score 0–3; UCEIS 0–8) and a templated clinical caption derived from five mucosal features. The authors benchmark classification and captioning models on a held-out low-resolution test set, reporting DINOv2_Large as the best classifier and BLIP-Large as the best captioner. The dataset aims to advance robust, interpretable AI for UC assessment, though public availability and full methodological details remain unverified.

## Method Summary
Endo-UC aggregates images from three centres, split into Subset I (public+re-annotated), Subset II (high-resolution), and Subset III (low-resolution). Annotations include Mayo Endoscopic Score (MES), UCEIS, and a caption generated from vascular pattern, bleeding, erythema, erosions/ulcers, and friability via a fixed template. For classification, models (ResNet50, ViT, DINOv2, Swin, ConvNeXt) are fine-tuned on 1,452 training images (80% train/20% val) and tested on 311 low-resolution images from Subset III. For captioning, BLIP-Base and BLIP-Large are fine-tuned on the same split and evaluated with BLEU, ROUGE, METEOR, and Cosine Similarity. The work emphasizes multi-resolution robustness and joint supervision from both scoring systems.

## Key Results
- DINOv2_Large attains Top-1 Accuracy 0.7138, F1-Score 0.6592, and AUC 0.7694 for 4-class Mayo Endoscopic Score classification.
- BLIP-Large achieves BLEU-1 0.8210, ROUGE-L 0.8574, and Cosine Similarity 0.7977 in generating clinical captions.
- The Endo-UC dataset provides 2,495 images with dual severity labels and templated captions, filling a gap in multi-centre UC benchmarks.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1: Multi-centre, multi-resolution data heterogeneity reduces distribution shift
Collecting images from different centres and resolutions forces models to learn invariant visual features of UC severity rather than equipment-specific artifacts. Variation in imaging systems and resolutions introduces natural domain shift; models must extract pathology-relevant features (e.g., vascular patterns, erosions) that generalize across acquisition conditions. Evidence: The dataset explicitly includes high-resolution (Subset II) and low-resolution (Subset III) images from different sources, with the abstract stating the goal of ensuring robustness and generalisability. If resolution differences cause loss of critical fine-textural details, low-resolution models may systematically underperform on high-resolution clinical data.

### Mechanism 2: Dual scoring supervision (MES + UCEIS) provides complementary severity gradients
Jointly predicting both Mayo (coarse 0–3) and UCEIS (fine-grained 0–8) gives models richer supervisory signals capturing both categorical and ordinal disease severity. Multi-task learning forces the visual backbone to encode features relevant to multiple clinical granularities, potentially improving feature robustness. Evidence: The dataset includes both scores; caption generation incorporates features from both systems (Methods, Table 1). If MES and UCEIS exhibit systematic discordance, the model may receive conflicting signals that destabilize training.

### Mechanism 3: Expert-generated captions create a language-grounded clinical reasoning pathway
Descriptive captions linking visual findings to clinical terminology enable models to internalize interpretable reasoning, not just pattern-matching. Captions synthesize five clinical features into natural language, bridging pixel-level patterns and semantic concepts. Evidence: Captions are produced from a standardized template combining these features (Methods, Figure 4). If captions are formulaic or omit nuanced findings (e.g., "patchy" vs. "obliterated" vascular pattern), the model may learn superficial keyword associations rather than true clinical reasoning.

## Foundational Learning
### Mayo Endoscopic Score (MES) and UCEIS
**Why needed**: These are the target classification labels. MES is a 0–3 ordinal score widely used in UC trials; UCEIS is a more granular 0–8 index that subdivides severity by vascular, bleeding, and erosion sub-scores. Understanding their definitions is essential to interpret the dataset's label structure.
**Quick check**: What endoscopic features differentiate MES-1 (mild) from MES-2 (moderate), and how does UCEIS quantify bleeding severity?

### Self-supervised vision transformers (DINOv2)
**Why needed**: DINOv2_Large achieved the best MES classification results. Self-supervised pre-training on diverse images builds robust feature extractors that generalize well to endoscopic domains with limited labelled data.
**Quick check**: How does DINOv2's self-distillation without labels differ from supervised pre-training (e.g., ImageNet), and why might this help for UC images with variable lighting and scopes?

### Vision-language models (BLIP) for medical captioning
**Why needed**: BLIP-Large produced the best captions. BLIP's bootstrapping approach iteratively refines image-text alignment, which is critical for generating clinically accurate descriptions from UC images.
**Quick check**: What is the role of the "captioner" and "filter" modules in BLIP, and why might this architecture suit generating structured clinical descriptions?

## Architecture Onboarding
### Component map
Dataset layer (Endo-UC with images, MES, UCEIS, captions) -> Classification models (ResNet50, Swin, DINOv2, ViT, ConvNeXt) OR Captioning models (BLIP-Base, BLIP-Large) -> Evaluation metrics (classification: Top-1 Acc, F1, AUC, Sensitivity, Specificity, PPV; captioning: BLEU, ROUGE, METEOR, Cosine Similarity)

### Critical path
1. Data preparation: Obtain Endo-UC, apply centre-specific normalisation if needed, split into train/val/test (use Subset III as held-out LR test).
2. Model selection: Choose baseline (e.g., DINOv2_Large for classification, BLIP-Large for captioning).
3. Training: Fine-tune on training set (80% of Subsets I+II) with appropriate loss (cross-entropy for classification, language modeling for captioning).
4. Validation: Monitor metrics on 20% validation holdout; use early stopping on validation loss.
5. Testing: Evaluate final model on 311 LR test images (Subset III) and report metrics as in Tables 2–4.

### Design tradeoffs
- **Resolution tradeoff**: Mixed HR/LR training may aid robustness but could lose fine details; the test set is LR-only, so models must generalise to LR.
- **Model size vs. compute**: DINOv2_Large/BLIP-Large (~300M parameters) vs. smaller models (~100M). Larger models risk overfitting on 1,452 training images but offer higher accuracy.
- **Classification granularity**: MES is 4-class; UCEIS is 9-class but not benchmarked. Multi-task learning (MES+UCEIS) could improve features but complicates loss weighting.

### Failure signatures
- **Classification failures**: Low sensitivity on MES-1/MES-2 (SEN <0.55) indicates difficulty with subtle mucosal texture differences; check confusion matrix for frequent misclassifications between adjacent MES classes.
- **Captioning failures**: Low BLEU-1 (<0.7) or ROUGE-L (<0.8) suggests poor n-gram overlap; inspect generated captions for missing key features or generic phrases.
- **Resolution mismatch**: If HR-trained model fails on LR test (expected), verify mixed-data training mitigates this; if it also fails on HR validation, check data loading/augmentation.

### First 3 experiments
1. Reproduce DINOv2_Large MES classification: Load pre-trained DINOv2_Large, replace head with 4-class classifier, fine-tune on training set, verify Top-1 Acc ≈0.71 on test set.
2. Reproduce BLIP-Large captioning: Fine-tune BLIP-Large on training captions, generate test captions, compute BLEU-1 ≈0.82, ROUGE-L ≈0.86.
3. Ablation: Resolution sensitivity – Train DINOv2_Large on HR-only (Subset II) vs. mixed (I+II) data, test on LR test. Expect mixed-data model to generalize better; quantify AUC drop when training on HR-only.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
1. **Why were UCEIS classification benchmarks omitted?** Despite Endo-UC providing both MES and UCEIS labels, all reported classification benchmarks are for MES only, limiting the dataset's utility for the clinically relevant UCEIS score.
2. **Can a unified multimodal model that jointly predicts severity and captions improve accuracy and interpretability?** The authors propose integrating classification and captioning but evaluate them separately; the potential synergy remains unexplored.
3. **What causes consistently lower sensitivity for MES-1 and MES-2 across all models?** The paper notes subtle differences in mucosal texture but does not analyze error cases or propose architectural adaptations to better discriminate intermediate severities.
4. **How well do models generalize to entirely unseen endoscopic systems, resolutions, and patient populations?** All test samples are from a single low-resolution Egyptian centre; external validation on diverse multi-centre data is needed.
5. **Are template-generated captions sufficient for clinical utility?** Captions are generated from a fixed template, which may lack nuance compared to free-text expert reports; no human evaluation of clinical adequacy is provided.

## Limitations
- **Data access and reproducibility**: Endo-UC is not publicly available; exact data splits, hyperparameters, and preprocessing details are missing, preventing independent verification of results.
- **Benchmark gaps**: UCEIS classification not evaluated; captioning relies solely on automated metrics without clinical validation.
- **Training scale**: Only 1,452 training images for large models raises overfitting concerns and may limit performance stability.

## Confidence
- **Dataset comprehensiveness (multi-centre, multi-resolution)**: Medium (based on self-report).
- **Classification and captioning benchmark results**: Low (unverifiable due to lack of public data and incomplete experimental details).
- **Proposed mechanisms (heterogeneity, dual scoring, captions)**: Very Low (speculative, not empirically isolated).

## Next Checks
1. **Public release**: Endo-UC should be released with predefined train/val/test splits and complete annotations to enable reproducible benchmarking.
2. **Ablation studies**: Isolate resolution effects by training on HR-only, LR-only, and mixed data, testing on both HR and LR sets; compare single-task (MES) vs. multi-task (MES+UCEIS) models.
3. **Clinical evaluation**: Assess classification sensitivity per MES class and have clinicians judge caption accuracy for all five features to establish a gold standard for future metrics.
