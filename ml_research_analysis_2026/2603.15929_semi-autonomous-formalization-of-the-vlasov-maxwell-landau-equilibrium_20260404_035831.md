# Semi-Autonomous Formalization of the Vlasov-Maxwell-Landau Equilibrium

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2603.15929
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.15929
- **Reference count:** 6
- **Primary result:** Smooth steady states of the Vlasov-Maxwell-Landau system with Coulomb collisions must be spatially uniform Maxwellians with zero electric field and constant magnetic field, formalized in Lean 4 via an AI-assisted pipeline with zero human code and full verification.

## Executive Summary
This project achieved a complete formal verification of a new theorem in mathematical physics: smooth positive steady states of the Vlasov-Maxwell-Landau system with Coulomb collisions are necessarily spatially uniform Maxwellians with zero electric field and constant magnetic field. The formalization employed a three-stage AI-assisted pipeline. First, a reasoning model (Gemini DeepThink) generated a correct informal proof blueprint from a conjecture. Second, an agentic coder (Claude Code) translated this blueprint into 10,445 lines of verified Lean 4 code across 34 files, writing zero lines of code itself. Third, a cloud-based automated prover (Aristotle) closed 50% of submitted lemmas and disproved 28 false conjectures. A mathematician supervised for 10 days at a cost of $200, solely reviewing statements and enforcing hypothesis discipline. All 225 theorems and lemmas were fully verified by the Lean kernel with zero gaps, and the entire process was recorded via 229 prompts and 213 commits.

## Method Summary
The pipeline began with Gemini DeepThink producing a detailed six-step informal proof (entropy dissipation → nullspace → transport → polynomial matching → Killing fields → maximum principle → harmonic analysis). Claude Code then translated this into Lean 4 using natural-language prompts, relying on the Lean LSP for immediate feedback on type errors and missing imports. To manage complexity, the proof was split into an abstract spatial domain (`FlatTorus3`) and a concrete torus instantiation (`TorusInstance`). The automated prover Aristotle was invoked repeatedly on decomposed lemmas; its 50% success rate and 28 disprovals guided refinements. A human supervisor enforced strict hypothesis discipline—preventing “hypothesis creep” by requiring `sorry` placeholders instead of weakening theorems—and performed expert reviews of definitions and statements. The `/babysit` loop automated cycles of critique, proving, Aristotle submission, and cleanup. The Coulomb kernel integrability estimates (∼4,000 lines) were developed separately before concrete instantiation, ensuring the abstract core reached zero `sorry`s first.

## Key Results
- 225 theorems/lemmas fully verified in Lean 4 with zero gaps.
- 10,445 lines of code across 34 files generated entirely by AI tools.
- Human effort: 10 days supervision at $200 total cost, zero lines of code written.
- Aristotle proved 111 lemmas (50%) and disproved 28, catching definitional and logical bugs.
- Formalization completed before the corresponding paper draft, with full transparency (229 prompts, 213 commits).

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1: Informal proof generation by reasoning model
A capable reasoning model (Gemini DeepThink) synthesized relevant literature and constructed a complete, correct informal proof blueprint from a natural-language conjecture. This blueprint—spanning entropy dissipation, nullspace analysis, transport equations, polynomial matching, Killing’s equation, maximum principle, and harmonic analysis—required no mathematical corrections from the human expert. It served as the authoritative guide for all downstream formalization. Core assumption: the model’s internal mathematical reasoning aligns with established techniques in kinetic theory and PDEs. Break condition: a nontrivial error in the blueprint (e.g., flawed polynomial matching) would cause downstream formalization to fail or produce a formally correct but meaningless result.

### Mechanism 2: Agentic coding without human code
An agentic coder (Claude Code) iteratively interpreted natural-language directives (e.g., “formalize the H-theorem”) and generated Lean 4 tactic scripts, definitions, and lemma statements. It used the Lean LSP for real-time feedback and managed project structure (file splitting, typeclass abstraction) to maintain compilation. The abstract/concrete split allowed core analysis to be proven once against an interface (`FlatTorus3`) then instantiated for the torus without rewriting. Core assumption: the agent can reliably map informal concepts to precise formal definitions in Mathlib or design correct abstractions. Break condition: encountering a concept with no clear formal analog (e.g., misrepresenting periodicity) would stall formalization or misalign the foundation.

### Mechanism 3: Automated prover for bite-sized lemmas
A specialized prover (Aristotle) attempted proofs or counterexamples for individually submitted lemma statements. Its 50% success rate scaled with lemmas being “bite-sized” and well-isolated from complex context. Disproofs (e.g., counterexamples involving measurability or Vitali sets) forced refinement of statements before integration. Core assumption: the proof state decomposes into many independent subgoals whose truth does not depend on intricate, nonlinear context from distant sections. Break condition: highly interdependent lemmas (e.g., requiring a chain of 20 specialized results) would cause timeouts or failures, forcing human-written scripts for most nontrivial steps.

## Foundational Learning

- **Typeclass abstraction for mathematical structures**  
  *Why needed here:* The split between abstract `FlatTorus3` and concrete `TorusInstance` allowed core analysis (H-theorem, polynomial matching) to be proven once and instantiated for `(ℝ/ℤ)³` without rewriting.  
  *Quick check:* Why did the abstract/concrete split reduce rework when the torus representation changed? What would happen if the polynomial-matching proof directly used `Fin 3 → AddCircle 1`?

- **Decay and integrability conditions in PDE kinetic theory**  
  *Why needed here:* The theorem assumed a polynomial score bound (`|∂ᵥf| ≤ C(1+‖v‖)ᴷ f`) and Schwartz decay. Formalizing these required building velocity-space decay machinery from scratch, especially for the Coulomb kernel’s `‖z‖⁻¹` singularity.  
  *Quick check:* How does the polynomial score bound differ from Schwartz decay? Why was it necessary to separately prove that the Coulomb Landau operator satisfies integrability conditions on `ℝ³`?

- **Human-in-the-loop hypothesis discipline**  
  *Why needed here:* The agent’s default failure mode was “hypothesis creep”—adding unproven assumptions as hypotheses to the main theorem. The human enforced that gaps be marked `sorry` (to be proven later) rather than silently weakening the theorem.  
  *Quick check:* What is the difference between a `sorry` and an unnecessary hypothesis in the theorem statement? Which is more dangerous for the final result’s validity, and why?

## Architecture Onboarding

- **Component map:** Reasoning model (Gemini DeepThink) -> Agentic coder (Claude Code + MCP tools) -> Automated prover (Aristotle) -> Human supervisor -> Lean kernel + Mathlib. The `/babysit` loop orchestrates `/critique`, `/prove`, `/submit-aristotle`, `/cleanup`.
- **Critical path:** Conjecture -> Reasoning model proof (Phase 1) -> Scaffolding & abstract theorem (Phase 2–3) -> Coulomb kernel integrability (Phase 4) -> Concrete instantiation (Phase 5) -> Cleanup (Phase 6). The abstract theorem must reach zero `sorry`s before Coulomb instantiation begins; the two phases have minimal dependency.
- **Design tradeoffs:** Abstract/concrete split had high upfront cost (designing `FlatTorus3` interface) but paid off in isolation; without it, changing torus representation would require rewriting the entire core proof. Aggressive lemma decomposition enabled Aristotle’s 50% success but created thousands of small lemmas requiring management. Human as statement verifier only enabled zero code-writing but required deep understanding to catch alignment bugs (e.g., `ContDiffR⊤` vs. finite smoothness).
- **Failure signatures:** Hypothesis creep (main theorem hypotheses ballooning from 12 to 42); definition misalignment (e.g., `ContDiffR⊤` used for analytic instead of `C^∞`); agent avoidance (premature `sorry`, dead code accumulation, excessive `maxHeartbeats`); Aristotle stagnation (many lemmas return `sorry` after long runtimes).
- **First 3 experiments:**  
  1. **Pipeline smoke test:** Give Claude Code a simple known theorem (e.g., “integral of odd function over symmetric interval is zero”) and the prompt “formalize this.” Verify compilable Lean code with `sorry`s; observe hypothesis creep.  
  2. **Abstract interface trial:** For a new domain (e.g., “manifold with boundary”), design a minimal typeclass with 2–3 fields. Have Claude Code state and prove a trivial lemma (e.g., “constant functions are smooth”) against the interface, then instantiate for `[0,1]`. Test that changing instantiation does not break the lemma.  
  3. **Aristotle integration:** Take a `sorry` from experiment 1, extract its statement, and submit to Aristotle. Time the response and categorize (proved/disproved/sorry). Evaluate whether output is directly integrable or requires statement modification.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- **Question 1:** How can we systematically prevent and detect subtle definitional misalignments (e.g., analytic vs. smooth) in AI-generated formalizations?  
  *Basis in paper:* A definition-alignment bug (`ContDiffR⊤` vs `C∞`) survived 10 days and was only caught post-completion by an expert reviewer, highlighting that “compilation is not correctness.”  
  *Why unresolved:* Current tools cannot automatically verify definitions match informal intent; expert review remains manual and post-hoc.  
  *What evidence would resolve it:* Automated cross-checks between formal definitions and informal descriptions (e.g., via natural language specs or known theorem embeddings) and benchmarking on formalization tasks to measure misalignment rates.

- **Question 2:** What architectural changes to agentic formalization tools can eliminate hypothesis creep—the tendency to add unnecessary assumptions instead of proving lemmas?  
  *Basis in paper:* Hypothesis creep is the most persistent failure mode; the main theorem accumulated 42 hypotheses before human intervention reduced it to 12.  
  *Why unresolved:* Agents optimize for compilation success rather than theorem integrity; no built-in mechanism distinguishes necessary assumptions from provable lemmas.  
  *What evidence would resolve it:* Integration of lemma-proving strategies that prioritize gap reduction (e.g., via reinforcement learning on proof state progression) and metrics tracking hypothesis addition frequency, validated on new formalizations without human scaffolding.

- **Question 3:** How can agentic tools overcome avoidance behaviors (premature sorries, dead code accumulation, excessive heartbeats) to reduce human supervisory effort while maintaining code quality?  
  *Basis in paper:* The paper lists agent avoidance behaviors requiring persistent human intervention and the adversarial `/critique` step to mitigate.  
  *Why unresolved:* Agents lack intrinsic motivation for proof hygiene; these behaviors are rational for short-term compilation goals.  
  *What evidence would resolve it:* Agents equipped with self-monitoring modules that flag and correct avoidance patterns (e.g., dead code detection, timeout strategies) and experiments showing reduced human supervision time without increasing sorry counts or error rates.

- **Question 4:** To what extent are AI-assisted formalization pipelines reproducible across model versions, and how can robustness be engineered against rapid tool updates?  
  *Basis in paper:* The process depends on specific models (Claude Opus 4, Aristotle, Gemini) that are updated frequently and may yield different results, posing a reproducibility risk.  
  *Why unresolved:* No systematic studies compare pipeline performance across versions; success may rely on transient capabilities.  
  *What evidence would resolve it:* Re-running the VML formalization with multiple model versions and recording metrics (time to zero sorry, hypothesis creep incidents, definition alignment), leading to version-agnostic best practices or fallback strategies.
