# JADE: Expert-Grounded Dynamic Evaluation for Open-Ended Professional Tasks

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2602.06486
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.06486
- **Reference count:** 40
- **Primary result:** JADE achieves high human alignment (Pearson r=0.858) and low score variance (σ=1.12%) on BizBench, resolving the stability-adaptivity dilemma for evaluating open-ended professional tasks.

## Executive Summary
JADE introduces a two-layer evaluation framework for open-ended professional agent tasks. Layer 1 leverages expert-defined skills to generate stable, query-specific rubrics, while Layer 2 performs claim-level verification with evidence-dependent gating to adaptively assess reasoning and ensure soundness. On the BizBench benchmark, JADE attains strong human alignment (Pearson r=0.858), low score variance (σ=1.12%), and superior calibration compared to vanilla LLM-as-judge methods. The framework reveals systematic failure modes, including a 33.5% evidence-reasoning gap and poor source credibility, and demonstrates cross-domain transfer to HealthBench with a 48% Spearman correlation improvement.

## Method Summary
JADE implements a deterministic two-layer pipeline. For a given user query, Layer 1 activates a predefined set of expert skills via a fixed taxonomy, composing a query-specific checklist of atomic yes/no evaluation items. Layer 2 processes the agent response: it generates a report-specific checklist (distinguishing evidence and reasoning items), extracts factual claims, and employs a verification agent with web search to validate each claim. An LLM judge scores each checklist item, but scores for reasoning items are gated (set to zero) if any dependent factual claim fails verification (score below a threshold). The final score multiplies a reasoning score (weighted sum of gated item scores) by an evidence score (aggregate of claim verification results), ensuring strong reasoning requires reliable evidence. The system relies on expert-curated skill templates and structured prompts; no training is performed, though configuration includes rubrics, weights, and a verification toolset.

## Key Results
- High human alignment on BizBench (Pearson r=0.858) with low score variance (σ=1.12%) and better calibration than baseline LLM-as-judge methods.
- Uncovered a systematic evidence-reasoning gap: top BizBench agents score 89–95% on evidence but only 59–63% on reasoning, averaging a 33.5% gap.
- Source credibility is poor (average 48.2%), indicating frequent reliance on low-tier sources; cross-domain transfer to HealthBench improves Spearman correlation by 48% with skill activation.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1
- **Claim:** Expert-defined skills, activated deterministically per query, provide stable evaluation criteria that resist arbitrary variation.
- **Mechanism:** A taxonomy maps each query to a predefined skill set (Sq). This fixed mapping composes a query-specific checklist (Lq) from expert rubrics, ensuring identical queries yield identical evaluation criteria.
- **Core assumption:** Core professional principles (e.g., "Supplier Sourcing" requires "Direct contact details") are query-agnostic and can be encoded once.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract] "Layer 1 activates expert-defined skills for stable, query-specific criteria"
  - [section 3.2] "skill activation depends only on q, the same query always induces the same checklist"
  - [corpus] Weak direct anchor; neighboring papers confirm the stability-adaptivity dilemma but not this specific mechanistic solution.
- **Break condition:** If the skill taxonomy lacks coverage for a novel query intent, Layer 1 fails to activate necessary criteria, leading to incomplete evaluation.

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim:** Report-specific, claim-level verification with evidence-dependent gating enables adaptive assessment of diverse reasoning strategies while preventing conclusions built on refuted facts from scoring.
- **Mechanism:** Given response r, Layer 2 generates a report-specific checklist (Lr) and extracts claims (C(r)). A verification agent checks factual claims (Cev). The LLM judge scores reasoning items (ℓi ∈ Lq∪Lr) but gates the score (sgated_i = 0) if any dependent claim (D(ℓi)⊆Cev) fails verification (V(c)<τ).
- **Core assumption:** Reasoning quality can be decomposed into discrete, verifiable claims, and dependencies between facts and judgments can be explicitly modeled.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract] "Layer 2 performs report-specific, claim-level verification with evidence-dependent gating"
  - [section 3.5] "apply evidence-aware gating... conclusions built on unverified facts do not contribute"
  - [section 5.3, Case C] Demonstrates gating invalidating a strategy based on a hallucinated "Pantone Color" premise.
- **Break condition:** If claim extraction or dependency mapping is inaccurate, gating either incorrectly zeros valid reasoning (false negative) or allows invalid reasoning to score (false positive).

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim:** The multiplicative aggregation of evidence score (Sevid) and gated reasoning score (Sreason) enforces a soundness property where strong reasoning must be supported by reliable evidence to achieve a high final score.
- **Mechanism:** Final score S = Sreason · Sevid. This product penalizes either dimension linearly: weak evidence (Sevid≈0) or poor reasoning (Sreason≈0) drives the overall score toward zero, preventing compensation.
- **Core assumption:** Professional report quality is separable into evidential reliability and reasoning coherence, and both are necessary for high-stakes decisions.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [section 3.6] "multiplicative formulation ensures that strong reasoning unsupported by reliable evidence... is consistently penalized"
  - [section 5.2] Leaderboard shows top agents have high evidence (~89-95%) but lower reasoning (~59-63%), indicating the gap is systematically captured.
  - [section 5.3] "Evidence-Reasoning Gap (Avg: 34.3%)" is a direct product of this scoring.
- **Break condition:** If either Sevid or Sreason is miscalibrated (e.g., verification agent over-trusts sources, or LLM judge exhibits bias in reasoning scores), the multiplicative product yields misleading final scores, potentially hiding one failure mode behind a strength in the other dimension.

## Foundational Learning
- **Stability-Adaptivity Dilemma**
  - **Why needed here:** Understanding this core trade-off is essential to grasp JADE's design rationale. It explains why naive approaches fail: static rubrics are rigid, LLM-as-judge is unstable.

- **Claim-Level Verification & Dependency Gating**
  - **Why needed here:** This is the adaptive engine of Layer 2. It moves beyond holistic scoring to systematically invalidate arguments built on false premises, which is crucial for professional rigor.
  - **Quick check:** In the "Pantone Color" case study, why is gating the reasoning score necessary instead of just noting the factual error separately?

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map:** Query Checker parses user query (q); Skill Activator maps q → Sq via taxonomy (Γ) (deterministic, based on L1/L2/L3 labels); Report Analyzer processes response (r) to generate Lr and extract claims C(r); Verification Agent (web search) validates factual claims Cev(r) → V(c); LLM Judge scores reasoning checklist items conditioned on q, r, dependencies, and verification results; Scoring Engine computes Sreason (weighted sum with gating), Sevid (aggregate of V(c)), and final S = Sreason · Sevid; Source Grader assigns credibility weights to source URLs.
- **Critical path:** Query → Skill Activation → Report-Specific Checklist Generation → Factual Claim Verification → Dependency-Gated Reasoning Scoring → Final Score.
- **Design tradeoffs:**
  - Determinism vs. Coverage: Skill taxonomy ensures stability but requires expert maintenance. Missing skills = missed criteria.
  - Verification Latency vs. Accuracy: Real-time web checks are slow but essential for temporal sensitivity. Trade-off: use cached evidence for non-temporal claims?
  - LLM Judge Prompting: Highly structured prompts reduce variance but may constrain nuanced reasoning. Trade-off: prompt complexity vs. judge reliability.
  - Gating Threshold (τ): A strict τ increases precision but may incorrectly gate on marginally uncertain evidence. Requires tuning per domain.
- **Failure signatures:**
  - High Evidence Score + Low Reasoning Score: Classic "copy-paste" agent. Retrieves data but fails at synthesis/strategy.
  - Low Source Credibility Score: Agent retrieves facts from low-tier sources (T4) despite high factual accuracy. Indicates poor source selection.
  - High Score Variance across Runs: Suggests instability in skill activation or LLM judge scoring. Check if Lq varies.
  - Gating Disabled? If dependency mapping is empty (D(ℓi)=∅ for all), gating cannot function. Verify claim extraction quality.
- **First 3 experiments:**
  1. Skill Coverage Stress Test: Feed 50 diverse BizBench queries through Skill Activator. Manually audit: % of queries where all critical L2/L3 constraints are covered by activated skills. Target >95%.
  2. Verification Agent Temporal Accuracy: For 100 claims involving current data (prices, rates, stock), compare V(c) against ground truth fetched at evaluation time. Target accuracy >90% on non-controversial facts.
  3. Ablation - Gating Impact: On a subset with known factual errors in reasoning premises, compare scores from full JADE vs. "JADE w/o Gating" (disable D(ℓi) checks). Quantify score inflation from unsupported reasoning. Expect significant reduction (e.g., >30%) in final score for flawed reports.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
### Open Question 1
- **Question:** How can the design of expert-authored evaluation skills and taxonomies be automated to reduce the cost and effort of adapting JADE to new professional domains?
- **Basis in paper:** The limitations section states: "JADE requires expert effort to design taxonomies and skills, leading to domain adaptation costs."
- **Why unresolved:** Manual skill creation for each domain is time-consuming and may not scale to the wide variety of professional tasks; automation could enable broader adoption and consistency.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** A system that learns or generates high-quality skills from domain data or natural language descriptions, validated by expert review and showing comparable performance to manually crafted skills on evaluation benchmarks.

### Open Question 2
- **Question:** How can JADE's evaluation framework be extended to knowledge-intensive fields (e.g., medicine, education) where deep domain expertise is needed to define meaningful evaluation criteria and verify evidence?
- **Basis in paper:** The limitations section notes: "its effectiveness may decrease in knowledge-intensive fields such as medicine and education."
- **Why unresolved:** In such fields, evaluation criteria often involve nuanced, specialized knowledge that generic skills may not capture; verification of evidence may require understanding of complex, field-specific concepts and guidelines.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Adaptation of JADE to medical or educational benchmarks with involvement of domain experts to define skills, demonstration of improved alignment and reduced reliance on generalist LLMs, or hybrid approaches that incorporate retrieval from authoritative knowledge bases.

### Open Question 3
- **Question:** How sensitive is JADE's evaluation outcome to the choice of the underlying LLM judge and the specific wording and weighting of the rubrics?
- **Basis in paper:** The limitations mention: "evaluation quality depends on rubric design and judge model reliability."
- **Why unresolved:** If small changes in rubric design or LLM backbone cause large variations, the system may lack robustness; understanding sensitivity is crucial for practical deployment and trust.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Systematic ablation studies across multiple LLMs and rubric variations, measuring variance in scores and human alignment, and potentially developing methods to calibrate or regularize the pipeline.

### Open Question 4
- **Question:** To what extent can JADE handle multi-turn interactions and variation in response style (e.g., one-shot long reports vs. iterative dialogues) without loss of evaluation stability?
- **Basis in paper:** The limitations state: "although BizBench is highly challenging, its primarily single-turn and under-specified queries introduce sensitivity to response style, as some agents favor detailed one-shot reports while others rely on multi-turn interaction."
- **Why unresolved:** Current evaluation assumes a single response per query; multi-turn scenarios add complexity in maintaining context and scoring across turns, and style biases may affect fairness.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Extending JADE to a multi-turn setting, collecting multi-turn agent interactions, and demonstrating stable, style-invariant evaluation across diverse interaction patterns.

## Limitations
- Skill taxonomy design and maintenance require significant expert effort; incomplete coverage can lead to missed evaluation criteria.
- The verification aggregation formula and source credibility weighting schemes are not fully specified, hindering faithful reproduction.
- Cross-domain adaptation process is not detailed, limiting claims about generalizability beyond BizBench.
