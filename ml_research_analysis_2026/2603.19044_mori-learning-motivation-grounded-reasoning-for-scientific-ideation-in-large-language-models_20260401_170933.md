# MoRI: Learning Motivation-Grounded Reasoning for Scientific Ideation in Large Language Models

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2603.19044
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.19044
- Reference count: 40
- Primary result: MoRI achieves a mean score of 3.19 across novelty, rigor, and feasibility, surpassing GPT-4o (2.69) and AI-Scientist-V2 (2.70) by over 17%.

## Executive Summary
MoRI addresses superficial conceptual recombination in LLM-based scientific ideation by internalizing motivation-grounded reasoning. The model first extracts a research motivation from context, then generates a reasoning trajectory that logically deduces a methodology. This two-stage process is trained via supervised fine-tuning on motivation identification and reasoning, followed by reinforcement learning with a composite reward. The reward combines entropy-aware information gain (to elaborate high-complexity technical details) and contrastive semantic gain (to ensure logical alignment with the solution space), stabilized by length anchoring to prevent reasoning collapse. Trained on 8,000 ICLR papers and evaluated with retrieval-augmented LLM judges and human experts, MoRI achieves state-of-the-art performance, significantly outperforming strong commercial and agentic baselines.

## Method Summary
The pipeline begins with dataset construction from ICLR papers: research context, ground-truth methods, and motivations are extracted. Posterior reconstruction uses a strong LLM to generate an initial reasoning trace from context, then rewrites it conditioned on the ground-truth method to produce aligned reasoning trajectories. A base model is first fine-tuned on two tasks: motivation extraction (x→m) and reasoning-driven method generation (x→(z,y)). The reinforcement learning stage employs Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO) with a composite reward. This reward integrates Entropy-Aware Information Gain (EAIG), which measures improvement in predicting high-entropy tokens of the ground-truth method relative to the SFT baseline, and Contrastive Semantic Gain (CSG), which computes the semantic leap from context+motivation to generated method versus to ground truth. Length anchoring modulates the reward to encourage sufficiently deep reasoning. At inference, motivations are generated first, followed by reasoning and method generation conditioned on the motivation.

## Key Results
- MoRI achieves a mean score of 3.19 across novelty, technical rigor, and feasibility, exceeding GPT-4o (2.69) and AI-Scientist-V2 (2.70) by over 17%.
- Ablation studies confirm the synergy of EAIG and CSG rewards; using either alone leads to reward collapse or suboptimal performance.
- Length anchoring is critical; its removal causes significant degradation (especially in novelty) and triggers reasoning collapse, as evidenced by steep CoT length decay.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
MoRI's superiority arises from three interconnected mechanisms. First, the two-stage reasoning structure—identifying a motivation before generating a reasoning trajectory—forces the model to internalize the "problem → motivation → solution" cognitive pattern. This grounds ideation in a principled research gap, constraining the solution space and preventing superficial recombination. The formal separation of policies for motivation and reasoning, contrasted with external-scaffolding agentic approaches, anchors this mechanism.

Second, the composite RL reward synergizes Entropy-Aware Information Gain (EAIG) and Contrastive Semantic Gain (CSG). EAIG uses token entropy to focus on high-complexity details in the ground-truth method and measures how much the generated reasoning improves their predictability relative to the SFT baseline. CSG computes the semantic leap from context+motivation to generated method compared to ground truth. Their weighted combination (CSG weight 0.7, EAIG 0.3) balances micro-level technical depth and macro-level directional alignment. Ablations show that EAIG alone causes collapse due to variance explosion, while CSG alone lacks depth incentives; the synergy yields optimal performance.

Third, Length Anchoring modulates the reward with α(z) = min(1, 1 − λ·(L_anchor−|z|)/L_anchor), incentivizing reasoning length up to L_anchor. This counteracts GRPO's implicit bias toward shorter outputs (arising from group-normalized advantages that favor low-variance strategies). Without length anchoring, the policy exploits minimal reasoning that triggers rewards, leading to "reasoning collapse." Ablations confirm that removing length anchoring drastically reduces novelty and CoT length.

## Foundational Learning
- **Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO):** Why needed: Core optimization algorithm; understanding its within-group advantage normalization explains the implicit bias toward shorter sequences and the necessity of length anchoring. Quick check: How does computing advantages via (reward - group_mean)/group_std create an incentive for policies that produce low-variance (often shorter) outputs, and what does this imply about the exploration-exploitation trade-off in open-ended generation?
- **Information Gain in Language Modeling:** Why needed: Central to EAIG reward design; the model must understand that log π_θ(y*_t|...) - log π_sft(y*_t|...) measures reduction in surprise (cross-entropy) compared to base policy. Quick check: Why is it insufficient to simply maximize the log-likelihood of the ground-truth method y* under the new policy? What does conditioning the gain on a specific entropy mask M_t change about the optimization target?
- **Contrastive Learning & Semantic Similarity:** Why needed: Underlies CSG; the model needs to grasp that Δ_sem = CosSim(Gen, GT) - CosSim(Context+Motivation, GT) is a relative measure of semantic progress, not an absolute similarity score. Quick check: Why would a high absolute CosSim(Gen, GT) be a poor reward signal for scientific ideation if not contrasted with a baseline? What kind of "cheating" would it incentivize?
- **Posterior Reconstruction for Data Synthesis:** Why needed: The dataset construction relies on reverse-engineering reasoning chains z from ground-truth y* because real papers don't contain explicit z. Quick check: What are the risks of using a separate LLM to synthesize z from y*? Could this introduce a distribution mismatch between the "reasoning" the RL policy is rewarded for and the reasoning a human expert would produce?

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component map:** Data Ingestion -> Motivation Extraction -> Posterior Reconstruction -> SFT -> RL -> Inference

**Critical path:** The RL loop (step 5) is the bottleneck. It requires correct masking of high-entropy tokens from y*, a well-calibrated embedding model that preserves scientific semantics, stable GRPO implementation with clip-higher, and strict enforcement of format constraints to prevent leakage.

**Design tradeoffs:** 
- **Entropy Mask Threshold:** Lower (e.g., top 15%) increases precision but may miss important but predictable technical terms; higher (top 50%) includes noise and dilutes EAIG signal.
- **Reward Weights (w_s, w_e):** CSG weight (w_s) dominates (optimal 0.7) as it provides stable directional signal; EAIG weight (w_e) must be smaller (0.3) to prevent variance-driven collapse.
- **Length Anchor L_anchor:** Must be set based on SFT model's average CoT length; setting too high wastes compute, too low fails to prevent collapse.

**Failure signatures:** 
1. **Reward hacking / format leakage:** Model outputs y directly in z section or uses markdown headers (##, ###).
2. **EAIG collapse:** Reasoning length explodes into incoherent gibberish while EAIG score is artificially high.
3. **CSG over-optimization:** Model generates semantically close but technically empty or generic methods.
4. **Motivation decay:** During RL, model's ability to generate useful m degrades because RL only optimizes π_θ((z,y)|x,m) with fixed m.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
1. Does MoRI's motivation-grounded reasoning framework generalize effectively to scientific domains outside of machine learning (e.g., biology, physics) with different logical structures and publication norms?
2. What is the precise theoretical mechanism behind the synergistic interaction between Entropy-Aware Information Gain (EAIG) and Contrastive Semantic Gain (CSG) that prevents reward hacking and reasoning collapse more effectively than either component alone?
3. Can the feasibility of AI-generated scientific ideas be reliably evaluated without real-world experimental validation or large-scale peer review, or does the context-aware LLM judge protocol merely capture a proxy that diverges from actual implementability and impact?
4. How does MoRI's internalized motivation-grounded reasoning compare to advanced agentic frameworks that incorporate external tools (e.g., formal theorem provers, code executors, simulated environments) in terms of idea quality, and is the current performance gap due to the lack of such tools or the fundamental reasoning process?
5. To what extent does the posterior reconstruction strategy for synthesizing reasoning trajectories (z) introduce artifacts or biases that cause the model to learn a distorted or non-generalizable reasoning process during RL?

## Limitations
- Dataset construction relies on unreleased Qwen3 models for motivation extraction and posterior reconstruction; substitutions (e.g., GPT-4) may alter distribution.
- Embedding model (Qwen3-Embedding-8B) and entropy threshold (top 25%) are not validated for scientific semantics; small changes could destabilize CSG/EAIG.
- Hyperparameters like L_anchor and reward shaping thresholds (τ_IG, τ_sem) are unspecified; optimal values may be dataset-specific.
- GRPO implementation details (micro-batching, clip schedules) are drawn from VeRL defaults, not the paper’s exact configuration.

## Confidence
- **High:** Dual-reward synergy and necessity of length anchoring (supported by thorough ablations).
- **Medium:** Generalizability beyond ICLR papers; robustness to embedding/model swaps.
- **Low:** Exact reproducibility of the 3.19 score without access to proprietary tools and precise thresholds.
