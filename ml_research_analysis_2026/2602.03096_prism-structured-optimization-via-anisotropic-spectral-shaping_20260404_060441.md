# PRISM: Structured Optimization via Anisotropic Spectral Shaping

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2602.03096
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.03096
- **Reference count:** 13
- **Primary result:** PRISM outperforms Muon and AdamW on a 22M parameter language model pre-training, achieving lower final training loss (3.269 vs. 3.285 and 3.324) and stable training under aggressive learning rates (0.1) where Muon diverges.

## Executive Summary
PRISM introduces an optimizer that addresses spectral methods' blindness to gradient variance by using an innovation-augmented polar decomposition to build a low-rank quasi-second-order preconditioner. This enables anisotropic spectral shaping, which adaptively dampens updates in high-variance subspaces while preserving strength in signal-dominated ones. Empirical results on a 22M parameter language model show PRISM achieving lower training loss and greater stability, especially under high learning rates.

## Method Summary
PRISM targets structured parameters (e.g., weight matrices). It maintains a momentum buffer \(M_t\) and computes the innovation \(D_t = G_t - M_t\). The momentum and a scaled innovation (\(\gamma D_t\)) are vertically concatenated into an augmented matrix \(\tilde{M}_t\). The polar decomposition of \(\tilde{M}_t\) yields an orthogonal factor \(\tilde{U}_t\); the top block \(O_t = \tilde{U}_t[:m,:]\) serves as a preconditioner. The structured update is \(\theta \leftarrow \theta - \eta O_t\). Unstructured parameters (embeddings, LayerNorms, biases) are updated with AdamW. The polar decomposition is computed efficiently via Newton-Schulz iterations, keeping memory overhead comparable to first-order methods.

## Key Results
- On a 22M parameter decoder-only Transformer pre-trained on 2.6B tokens, PRISM (\(\gamma=1\)) achieved a final training loss of **3.269**, outperforming Muon (3.285) and AdamW (3.324) at 10,000 steps.
- PRISM remained stable and converged with an aggressive learning rate of **0.1**, whereas Muon diverged.
- Performance was robust across a wide range of damping coefficients \(\gamma \in [0.5, 10]\).

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1: Low-rank quasi-second-order preconditioner via innovation-augmented polar decomposition
- **Claim:** PRISM constructs an efficient low-rank quasi-second-order preconditioner by augmenting the momentum matrix with the gradient innovation and computing its polar factor.
- **Mechanism:** The innovation \(D_t = G_t - M_t\) is concatenated to the momentum \(M_t\) to form \(\tilde{M}_t = [M_t; \gamma D_t]\) (row-wise for right-sided preconditioning). The polar decomposition \(\tilde{M}_t = \tilde{U}_t \tilde{H}_t\) yields the orthogonal factor \(\tilde{U}_t\); the top block \(O_t = \tilde{U}_t[:m,:]\) equals \(M_t (M_t^\top M_t + \gamma^2 D_t^\top D_t)^{-1/2}\). This implicitly approximates \((E[G]^\top E[G] + \text{Cov}(G))^{-1/2}\) in a low-rank manner because \(D_t^\top D_t\) serves as a proxy for covariance.
- **Core assumption:** The innovation \(D_t\) is a rank‑1 proxy for the gradient covariance \(\text{Cov}(G)\).
- **Evidence anchors:** Abstract; Section 3.2 derivation; contextual support from works like Mousse and FISMO.
- **Break condition:** If \(D_t\) poorly correlates with true covariance (e.g., due to biased momentum or non-stationary gradients), the preconditioner misestimates curvature and degrades performance.

### Mechanism 2: Anisotropic spectral shaping via SNR-aware damping
- **Claim:** PRISM performs anisotropic spectral shaping, modulating the update gain in each principal direction by the local signal‑to‑noise ratio (SNR), thereby damping high‑variance subspaces while preserving signal‑dominated ones.
- **Mechanism:** Eigendecomposition of \(G_{\text{aug}} = M_t^\top M_t + \gamma^2 D_t^\top D_t\) gives eigenvalues \(\lambda_k = \|M_t v_k\|^2 + \gamma^2 \|D_t v_k\|^2\). The spectral gain along eigenvector \(v_k\) is \(\rho_k = \|M_t v_k\| / \sqrt{\lambda_k} = 1/\sqrt{1 + \gamma^2 \|D_t v_k\|^2 / \|M_t v_k\|^2} = 1/\sqrt{1 + 1/\text{SNR}_k^2}\). Hence \(\rho_k \to 1\) for high SNR and \(\rho_k \approx \text{SNR}_k\) for low SNR.
- **Core assumption:** The eigenvectors of \(G_{\text{aug}}\) align with meaningful curvature directions.
- **Evidence anchors:** Abstract; Section 3.3 derivation; contextual work on anisotropy in spectral methods.
- **Break condition:** Misalignment between eigenvectors of \(M_t^\top M_t\) and \(D_t^\top D_t\) could cause damping to leak into signal directions.

### Mechanism 3: Implicit regularization for stability
- **Claim:** The SNR‑aware damping provides implicit regularization that stabilizes training, enabling use of aggressive learning rates where Muon diverges.
- **Mechanism:** When updates induce large gradient fluctuations, \(D_t\) grows, strengthening \(\gamma^2 D_t^\top D_t\) in the preconditioner. This reduces the update magnitude in noisy directions, preventing overshooting and loss spikes; signal directions remain largely unaffected due to high SNR.
- **Core assumption:** The innovation \(D_t\) spikes promptly in response to increased local variance/curvature and this spike correlates with instability risk.
- **Evidence anchors:** Abstract; Section 4.3.2 experiments; contextual second-order stability results.
- **Break condition:** Momentum inertia may delay the rise of \(D_t\) during instability, allowing transient divergence.

## Foundational Learning
- **Concept:** Polar decomposition & Newton‑Schulz iteration.
  - Why needed: Computes the orthogonal factor of \(\tilde{M}_t\) without SVD, using GPU‑efficient iterative matrix multiplications.
  - Quick check: What fixed‑point iteration yields the polar factor, and what coefficients \((a,b,c)\) ensure rapid convergence?
- **Concept:** Gradient innovation \(G_t - M_t\) as a variance proxy.
  - Why needed: The innovation is the key conduit for second‑order information.
  - Quick check: For exponential momentum \(M_t = \beta M_{t-1} + (1-\beta) G_t\), relate \(D_t\) to the gradient variance around the mean.
- **Concept:** Anisotropic vs isotropic spectral shaping.
  - Why needed: PRISM tailors scaling per direction via SNR; Muon isotropically whitens singular values.
  - Quick check: Derive PRISM’s spectral gain \(\rho_k\) and contrast it with Muon’s constant gain.

## Architecture Onboarding
Component map: Momentum buffer -> Innovation computation (D_t = G_t - M_t) -> Augmentation (vertical concatenation of M_t and γ D_t) -> Polar decomposition (Newton-Schulz) -> Slicing (O_t = top block) -> Structured parameter update; Parallel: AdamW update for unstructured parameters.

Critical path: Gradient -> Momentum update -> Innovation -> Augmentation -> Newton-Schulz iterations (1–2) -> Slicing -> Structured update.

Design tradeoffs:
- Single‑sided vs dual‑sided: Single‑sided limits memory/compute; dual‑sided captures both row/column correlations at higher cost.
- Damping coefficient \(\gamma\): Balances innovation sensitivity; too small ≈ Muon, too large over‑damps.
- Newton‑Schulz iteration count: More iterations improve accuracy but increase compute; 1–2 suffice.
- Augmentation dimension: Row vs column stacking determines which correlations are preconditioned; may depend on matrix shape.

Failure signatures:
- Divergence at high LR if \(\gamma\) too small.
- Slow convergence if \(\gamma\) too large.
- Early‑training instability when momentum is noisy; may need warm‑up or larger initial \(\gamma\).
- Unexpected compute overhead from excessive Newton‑Schulz iterations.

First 3 experiments:
1. **Main result:** Train a 22M decoder‑only transformer on FineWeb‑Edu (2.6B tokens). Compare PRISM (\(\gamma=1\)), Muon, AdamW; final loss at ~10k steps.
2. **Sensitivity:** Vary \(\gamma \in \{0.1, 0.5, 1, 2, 5, 10\}\); record final loss to confirm robust improvement.
3. **Stability stress test:** Set learning rate = 0.1; verify PRISM converges while Muon diverges.

## Limitations
- Innovation as covariance proxy unverified; momentum inertia or non‑stationarity may weaken correlation.
- Eigenvector alignment assumption between momentum and innovation Gram matrices may not hold universally.
- Evaluation limited to a single 22M LM on FineWeb‑Edu; broader applicability unknown.

## Confidence
- Quasi‑second‑order preconditioning: Medium (derivation solid but proxy unvalidated).
- Anisotropic spectral shaping: Medium (SNR‑gain math correct but alignment assumption untested).
- Stability at high LR: Medium‑High (observed divergence/convergence but causal link inferred).
- Overall performance superiority: Medium (result promising but reproducibility gaps).
