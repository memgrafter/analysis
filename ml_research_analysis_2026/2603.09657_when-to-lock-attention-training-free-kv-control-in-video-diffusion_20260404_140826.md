# When to Lock Attention: Training-Free KV Control in Video Diffusion
## Quick Facts
- **ArXiv ID:** 2603.09657
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.09657
- **Reference Count:** 29
- **Primary Result:** KV-Lock achieves state-of-the-art video editing with top VBench metrics (94.56% subject consistency, 96.92% background consistency) and superior background SSIM (0.9309) and PSNR (31.04) as a training-free plug-in for DiT models.

## Executive Summary
Video editing with diffusion models struggles to preserve background consistency while enhancing foreground quality. Existing methods either cause background artifacts with flexible generation or overly rigidify the entire scene with fixed attention locking. KV-Lock introduces a training-free, dynamic scheduler that monitors hallucination risk during denoising via the variance of predicted clean latents. When instability is detected, it selectively strengthens background key-value cache fusion and amplifies classifier-free guidance to stabilize the background while allowing foreground creativity. Evaluations show consistent improvements over prior art across multiple metrics and user studies, with manageable memory overhead.

## Method Summary
KV-Lock is a plug-and-play module for DiT-based video diffusion. It first caches the source video’s key-value pairs during a forward pass at each denoising timestep. During editing, for each step, it computes the local variance of the predicted clean latent `ˆx0` over a sliding window within the masked foreground region. High variance triggers two actions: (1) the fusion ratio `αk` between cached and newly computed background KV pairs is increased, locking the background to the source manifold; (2) the CFG guidance scale `ω` is amplified, pulling the foreground toward the target prompt. Both adjustments are applied token-wise using the projected mask, affecting only background and foreground regions respectively. The process requires no training and operates entirely during inference.

## Key Results
- Achieves VBench average score of 84.87%, with 94.56% subject consistency and 96.92% background consistency.
- Outperforms baselines on background fidelity: SSIM 0.9309, PSNR 31.04.
- User studies confirm higher visual quality, prompt following, and frame consistency.
- Inference overhead is moderate (~10GB extra memory, slower speed) but yields favorable quality trade-offs.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1: Hallucination Detection via Variance
The variance of the predicted clean latent `ˆx0` along the denoising trajectory acts as an observable proxy for hallucination risk. For each timestep, local variance is computed over a sliding window on masked foreground tokens. High variance (`σ² > τ`) indicates unstable, diverse trajectories prone to hallucinations. This heuristic is grounded in the observation that hallucinated samples exhibit higher variance during late denoising. The detector runs training-free and in real-time, driving the scheduler. A break condition occurs if variance never drops below `τ` in the final `κ` steps, leaving hallucinations unmitigated.

### Mechanism 2: Dynamic KV Fusion
The fusion ratio `αk` between cached source KV pairs and newly generated KV pairs is adjusted dynamically based on variance. When hallucination risk is high (`σ² > τ`), `αk` increases toward 1, injecting more cached background values and locking the background to the source feature manifold. During low-risk phases, `αk` decreases, allowing recomputation for foreground flexibility. Token-wise masking ensures only background tokens are affected. This balances consistency and editability better than fixed fusion ratios, which either under-protect backgrounds or over-constrain edits.

### Mechanism 3: Hallucination-Aware CFG Scaling
The CFG guidance scale `ω` is scaled up when hallucination risk is detected, within a final window of `κ` steps. The adjusted `ω = ω₀ · clamp(σ²/τ, 0, b)` increases the weight of conditional noise prediction, reducing diversity and pulling the generation toward the target prompt. This mitigates foreground hallucinations by strengthening conditional alignment. Alternatively, an optimized scaling factor `s*` can correct unconditional noise predictions. Over-amplification risks oversaturation, while under-amplification fails to suppress artifacts; the clamp `b` bounds this effect.

## Foundational Learning
- **Diffusion Denoising Process:** The reverse process iteratively predicts noise `ϵθ(xt, t)` to recover `ˆx0`. KV-Lock’s hallucination metric directly uses these `ˆx0` predictions, requiring understanding of how `xt` is denoised.
  - *Quick check:* How is `ˆx0` computed from `ϵθ` and `xt`?
- **Self-Attention as Memory Retrieval:** The attention output `∑ softmax(q·k/√d) * v` retrieves and combines stored values. KV-Lock fixes keys/values for background tokens, constraining the output to a source manifold.
  - *Quick check:* In attention, what role do the keys and values play in determining the output?
- **Classifier-Free Guidance (CFG):** CFG combines conditional and unconditional noise predictions: `ϵ̂ = ϵθ(xt|∅) + ω(ϵθ(xt|y) - ϵθ(xt|∅))`. KV-Lock dynamically adjusts `ω` based on hallucination risk.
  - *Quick check:* How does increasing `ω` affect sample diversity and adherence to the condition?

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component map:**  
Encoder -> Mask Projector -> KV Cache Extractor -> Hallucination Detector -> Dynamic Scheduler -> Decoder

**Critical path:** Input video and mask are encoded to latents and token mask. Source video KVs are cached via a forward pass at each diffusion step. During editing, for each denoising step: predict `ˆx0`, compute masked variance, derive `αk` and `ω`, perform attention with fused KVs and modified CFG, then decode final frames.

**Design tradeoffs:**
- Runtime vs. Quality: KV caching adds ~10GB memory and slows inference but improves SSIM/PSNR.
- Mask Dependency: Requires accurate foreground mask; errors propagate to KV mixing.
- Sensitivity: Hyperparameters (`τ`, `W`, `κ`, `b`) are empirically set; may need tuning per video.
- Generality: Training-free but DiT-specific; transfer to other backbones is uncertain.

**Failure signatures:**
- High variance persists without drop → scheduler ineffective → foreground artifacts.
- `αk` stuck near 1 → foreground rigid, lacks edits.
- `αk` stuck near 0 → background drifts from source.
- `ω` too high → oversaturated, unrealistic colors.

**First 3 experiments:**
1. Plot masked variance `σ²(ˆx0)` across steps for successful/failed edits; verify variance drop in final `κ` steps for good edits.
2. Ablate fixed `αk` values (0.2, 0.5, 0.8) vs dynamic; expect dynamic to best balance subject and background consistency.
3. Compare CFG scheduling variants: none, only `ω`, only `s*`, both; expect combined `ω`+`s*` to improve imaging and aesthetic quality.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- **Open Question 1:** When should attention be locked to cached key-value pairs versus allowing the model to recompute attention patterns during video editing?  
  *Basis:* The introduction frames this as a deeper, unexplored question. The variance-based scheduler is heuristic and may not generalize.  
  *Resolution evidence:* Systematic analysis across diverse tasks and architectures, deriving a principled theory linking attention locking to stability.

- **Open Question 2:** What alternative detection metrics (beyond variance of denoising predictions) can reliably identify hallucinations in diffusion models?  
  *Basis:* Conclusion notes hallucination lacks fixed definition; variance is one approach among many.  
  *Resolution evidence:* Benchmark multiple detectors (likelihood, confidence, reconstruction error) on curated hallucinated vs. normal outputs, correlated with human evaluation.

- **Open Question 3:** How can KV-Lock be extended to operate without explicit mask inputs, enabling fully automatic foreground/background separation?  
  *Basis:* Current reliance on mask inputs is a limitation for casual users.  
  *Resolution evidence:* Integrate video segmentation models (e.g., SAM2) and evaluate editing quality against ground-truth masks.

- **Open Question 4:** How can the memory and runtime overhead of KV caching be reduced while maintaining background consistency?  
  *Basis:* Pre-run caching adds inference time and memory, limiting deployment.  
  *Resolution evidence:* Design sparse/quantized caches or learned eviction policies that preserve key information at lower cost.

## Limitations
- **Mask Dependency:** Requires high-quality foreground masks; performance degrades with poor boundaries or semi-transparent regions, but sensitivity is not quantified.
- **Hyperparameter Generalization:** Fixed `τ=0.01`, `W=10`, `κ=20`, `b=2` may not generalize across diverse content, architectures, or noise schedules; break condition when variance never drops is under-analyzed.
- **External Validation Gap:** Core link between `ˆx0` variance and hallucination is cited from image work but not validated for video diffusion in the corpus.
- **Computational Scaling:** Memory and speed overheads are reported for one model/resolution; scaling to long videos or larger models is unaddressed.

## Confidence
- **Claim:** Dynamic scheduling improves consistency vs. fixed baselines. **Confidence: High** (strong quantitative gains in Table 2, user studies).
- **Claim:** Variance of `ˆx0` is a reliable proxy for hallucination risk in video diffusion. **Confidence: Medium** (internal evidence from diagnostic plots; lacks external corpus validation; sensitivity to mask granularity).
- **Claim:** KV-Lock is a generally applicable, training-free plug-in for DiT-based video models. **Confidence: Low-Medium** (validated only on V ACE and Wan; transferability to other DiTs or non-DiT backbones not shown).

## Next Checks
1. **Cross-Dataset & Architecture Generalization:** Apply KV-Lock to at least two other DiT-based video models (e.g., OpenSora, VideoCrafter) and a diverse benchmark beyond VBench. Test if fixed hyperparameters (`τ`, `κ`) hold or require tuning.
2. **Mask Sensitivity Analysis:** Degrade ground-truth masks synthetically (blur, pixel flips, frame jitter). Correlate drop in Background Consistency (VBench) and SSIM with mask quality metrics (IoU, boundary F1).
