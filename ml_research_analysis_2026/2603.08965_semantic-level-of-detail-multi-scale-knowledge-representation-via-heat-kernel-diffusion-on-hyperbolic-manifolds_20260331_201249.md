# Semantic Level of Detail: Multi-Scale Knowledge Representation via Heat Kernel Diffusion on Hyperbolic Manifolds

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2603.08965
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.08965
- **Reference count:** 37
- **Primary result:** SLoD introduces a continuous zoom operator via heat kernel diffusion on hyperbolic manifolds to detect emergent scale boundaries in hierarchical knowledge graphs without supervision.

## Executive Summary
Semantic Level of Detail (SLoD) addresses the lack of continuous resolution control in AI memory systems built on graph-structured knowledge. By embedding concepts into the Poincaré ball and leveraging heat kernel diffusion, SLoD aggregates information across scales while preserving fine details. The method automatically identifies meaningful hierarchical boundaries through a composite boundary‑scanning score derived from representation velocity, weight distribution shifts, and neighborhood churn. Evaluations on synthetic hierarchies and the full WordNet noun hierarchy show strong alignment with planted or true taxonomic structure, supported by theoretical guarantees for tree‑like graphs.

## Method Summary
SLoD operates on a hyperbolic embedding of a knowledge graph. First, a *k*‑NN weighted graph is built using Poincaré distances. The normalized graph Laplacian *L* is computed and truncated eigendecomposition (*K* smallest eigenpairs) is obtained. For a focus node *x*₀, the heat kernel weights *w*(σ) = Σ_{i=1}^*K* e^{−σλᵢ} ϕᵢ(*x*₀)ϕᵢ are evaluated over a log‑spaced grid of scales σ. The SLoD representation at scale σ is the weighted Fréchet mean of all node embeddings on the Poincaré ball, approximated by an iterative tangent‑space averaging algorithm. Boundary detection scans σ, combining three normalized signals—representation velocity *V*(σ), weight Jensen–Shannon divergence *D*ᵂ(σ), and *k*‑neighborhood churn *C*ₖ(σ)—into a composite score; peaks indicate emergent scale boundaries. An effective dimensionality *K*⁎(σ) (number of eigenvalues of the kernel matrix exceeding ε) suggests when a single centroid is insufficient, triggering a multi‑center extension via weighted Riemannian *k*‑means on the ball.

## Key Results
- On synthetic Hierarchical Stochastic Block Model graphs (N=1024, 3 planted levels), SLoD recovers exact partitions (Adjusted Rand Index up to 1.00), with performance decreasing near the Kesten–Stigum information‑theoretic threshold.
- On the WordNet noun hierarchy (≈82 K synsets), detected boundaries strongly correlate with true taxonomic depth (Kendall τ = 0.79); the Fréchet mean at each boundary serves as a meaningful community centroid.
- Theory: For tree‑structured hierarchies under the Sarkar embedding, SLoD yields hierarchical coherence with O(σ) approximation error and (1+ε) distortion.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
Heat kernel diffusion on the graph Laplacian provides a continuous, mathematically principled notion of scale: σ controls how far information spreads, with small σ retaining local detail and large σ yielding coarse summaries. The eigenvectors and eigenvalues capture the geometry of the graph; spectral gaps correspond to natural splits in connectivity, causing abrupt changes in the representation as σ crosses those gaps. The Fréchet mean on the hyperbolic manifold aggregates embeddings in a way that respects the underlying negative curvature, preserving hierarchical relationships. The boundary scanner fuses three complementary signals: velocity captures how fast the representation moves, weight divergence measures redistribution of influence, and churn reflects stability of local neighborhoods. Peaks in the composite score therefore mark scales where the underlying hierarchical structure undergoes a qualitative change.

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component map:** Graph Construction -> Laplacian Computation -> Heat Kernel Weights -> SLoD Aggregation -> Boundary Detection -> Multi-center Extension

**Critical path:** 
1. Create *k*‑NN graph from hyperbolic embeddings.
2. Compute normalized Laplacian *L* and its smallest *K* eigenvalues/vectors.
3. For each focus node and a range of σ, compute heat kernel weights *w*(σ).
4. Obtain the SLoD representation as the weighted Fréchet mean of all nodes.
5. Derive three boundary indicators (*V*, *D*ᵂ, *C*ₖ) between consecutive σ values, normalize, sum, and detect peaks.
6. If *K*⁎(σ) > 1, switch to multi‑center representation using weighted Riemannian *k*‑means.

**Design tradeoffs:**
- **σ resolution vs. cost:** Finer σ grids improve boundary precision but increase repeated computation; using truncated eigen‑decomposition speeds up weight evaluation.
- **Single vs. multi‑center:** Single centroid is simple but fails when a scale contains multiple dissimilar sub‑communities; multi‑center captures heterogeneity at the price of additional clustering complexity.
- **Embedding quality dependence:** The method assumes a faithful hyperbolic embedding; poor embeddings propagate errors. The paper notes no explicit robustness analysis.

**Failure signatures:**
- Boundary scanner produces too few or too many peaks; inspect *K*⁎(σ) trajectory for smooth transitions and verify spectral gaps exist.
- Fréchet mean diverges or exits the Poincaré ball; monitor iteration counts and step size, ensure weight normalization.
- No clear spectral gaps lead to indistinct *K*⁎ drops; the method may rely on heuristics and could fail on graphs with smooth eigenvalue decay.
- Multi‑center extension yields unstable clusterings due to initialization sensitivity; consider multiple restarts.

**First experiments:**
1. Replicate SLoD on the HSBM synthetic benchmark: generate graphs with the provided parameters, run the pipeline, and report ARI against planted partitions.
2. Apply SLoD to the WordNet noun hierarchy: compute boundaries for a sample of leaf nodes, compare detected depths to true taxonomic depth, and estimate Kendall τ.
3. Conduct a hyperparameter sensitivity sweep on the HSBM: vary α (median+MAD multiplier), *k* (neighborhood size for churn), and ε (effective dimensionality threshold) to assess stability of ARI.

## Limitations
- The boundary scanner’s hyperparameters (α, *k*, ε) are unspecified, making reproduction sensitive to choices.
- Multi‑center extension details (initialization, number of clusters *K*, convergence) are not provided, hindering faithful implementation for complex graphs.
- The approach depends on hyperbolic embedding quality, which is not analyzed; theoretical guarantees assume tree‑structured hierarchies, while real‑world graphs (e.g., WordNet) are DAGs, potentially violating assumptions.
