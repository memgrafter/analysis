# StoryMovie: A Dataset for Semantic Alignment of Visual Stories with Movie Scripts and Subtitles

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2602.21829
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.21829
- **Reference count:** 8
- **Primary result:** The StoryMovie dataset, via LCS-based script-subtitle alignment, enables a fine-tuned Qwen model (Storyteller3) to achieve 93.9% accuracy on script-derived QA and an 89.9% win rate on subtitle alignment, dramatically reducing semantic hallucinations.

## Executive Summary
This paper introduces the StoryMovie dataset, which aligns 1,757 movie sequences with their screenplays and subtitles to improve semantic alignment in visual storytelling. The alignment uses a Longest Common Subsequence (LCS) matching between screenplay dialogue and subtitle text, thereby synchronizing character names and dialogue with timestamps. The authors fine-tune the Qwen2.5-VL model on this data (as Storyteller3) and demonstrate substantial gains over prior work: an 89.9% win rate in subtitle alignment compared to the base model, and 93.9% accuracy on fact-based questions derived from scripts—especially a 94.7% accuracy on character relationship questions, where baseline models struggle. The results show that grounding visual narratives in authentic screenplay semantics mitigates hallucinations of character interactions and relationships.

## Method Summary
The authors construct the StoryMovie dataset by collecting movies with available screenplays and subtitle tracks. Screenplays are parsed into structured components (headings, character names, dialogue, actions, delivery cues). Subtitle files are also parsed. To align dialogue with timestamps, they tokenize both screenplay dialogue and subtitle texts and apply a Longest Common Subsequence (LCS) algorithm to find the longest matching token sequence. Matched blocks are then extended to include contiguous dialogue from the same speaker, thereby assigning subtitle timestamps to screenplay segments. This produces aligned story samples where visual frames (from the corresponding movie clips) are annotated with grounded entities and accompanied by the aligned script context.

For model training, they start from Qwen2.5-VL 7B, which already incorporates ID-based visual grounding tags. Training proceeds in stages: first establishing basic grounding, then contrastive reinforcement learning for entity re-identification, and finally supervised fine-tuning on the StoryMovie dataset using LoRA (low-rank adaptation). The fine-tuning data includes images, the aligned script segment (with character names and dialogue), and the structured chain-of-thought from prior work. The resulting model, Storyteller3, learns to generate stories that respect both visual grounding and script-derived semantics.

Evaluation employs two automated protocols: (1) pairwise preference judgments (using DeepSeek V3) comparing model outputs against the original subtitles on alignment quality; (2) script-derived question answering where questions about the story (generated by GPT-5) are answered by the models and checked for correctness against the script.

## Key Results
- Storyteller3 obtains an **89.9% win rate** over the base Qwen2.5-VL 7B in subtitle alignment tasks.
- Compared to the prior Storyteller model (trained without script grounding), Storyteller3 achieves a **48.5% win rate** versus only 38.0% losses on the same alignment metric.
- On script-derived QA, Storyteller3 reaches **93.9% accuracy** overall, a large gain over the 63.2% baseline; on relationship questions, it achieves **94.7%** compared to 55.3% for the baseline.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1: LCS Dialogue Alignment
The core of the dataset construction is a Longest Common Subsequence (LCS) matching between screenplay dialogue and subtitle tokens. This algorithm identifies the longest overlapping token sequence, which is then used to map subtitle timestamps to specific dialogue blocks in the screenplay. By extending bidirectionally until a speaker change or scene break, the method assigns each subtitle segment to the correct character. This yields reliable dialogue attribution, which in turn reduces semantic hallucinations in character interactions during story generation. The approach assumes sufficient lexical overlap between the professional screenplay and the subtitles, which is typically true. Limitations arise when subtitles paraphrase dialogue heavily, causing LCS to fail.

### Mechanism 2: Script-Aligned Training
Training the model on samples that combine visual frames, grounded entity annotations, and the aligned script content (including character names, dialogue, and delivery cues) forces the model to link visual entities to script characters and to incorporate authentic interactions. The model learns to respect the true narrative structure and relationships rather than inventing plausible but incorrect details. This mechanism relies on accurate alignment between visuals and script; mismatches (e.g., off-screen dialogue) can still lead to hallucinations. The large gains in QA accuracy, particularly on relationship questions (94.7% vs. 55.3%), demonstrate the effectiveness of this grounding.

### Mechanism 3: Progressive Multi-stage Training
The training pipeline builds on previously learned skills: first visual grounding with XML tags, then contrastive RL for entity re-identification, and finally script-aligned supervised fine-tuning. Each stage uses LoRA adapters to efficiently incorporate new knowledge without overwriting prior capabilities. This staged approach ensures that the model retains strong visual grounding while adding semantic alignment. The high tie rates on description alignment compared to prior Storyteller indicate no regression in visual grounding. Potential failure occurs if the new script data is too small or noisy, leading to catastrophic forgetting, which is mitigated by using LoRA and a moderate rank (32).

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component Map:**  
Script parser -> Subtitle parser -> LCS aligner -> Aligned stories (via Qwen3-VL story generator) -> StoryMovie dataset -> Fine-tune Qwen2.5-VL 7B with LoRA -> Storyteller3

**Critical Path:**  
1. Collect movies with accessible scripts and subtitles.  
2. Parse screenplays into structured elements (headings, character names, dialogue, actions, delivery cues).  
3. Tokenize screenplay dialogue and subtitle texts; apply LCS to align blocks and assign timestamps.  
4. Using pre-extracted frames and CoT from StoryReasoning, generate stories with Qwen3-VL by feeding aligned script segments and subtitle context.  
5. Fine-tune Qwen2.5-VL 7B on the StoryMovie training set using LoRA (rank 32) and AdamW (lr 2e-4, cosine annealing, batch 32, 3 epochs).  
6. Evaluate on the test set via DeepSeek V3 pairwise judging and script-derived QA.

**Design Tradeoffs:**  
- LCS alignment is simple and fast but depends on lexical overlap; it may fail with heavily paraphrased subtitles.  
- LoRA rank was chosen among {8, 32, 128, 512}; rank 32 balances capacity and efficiency.  
- Automated evaluation (DeepSeek V3, GPT-5) reduces human cost but may introduce judge biases; consistency checks are used.  
- Dataset size (1,757 sequences) ensures high-quality alignment but limits diversity; scaling up requires more alignment effort.  
- Using a 7B model keeps compute affordable; larger models could potentially improve further.

**Failure Signatures:**  
- Low LCS match scores on validation indicate poor script-subtitle synchronization.  
- Subtitle alignment win rate below the base model suggests the training is not capturing semantic alignment.  
- High tie rates on description alignment might mask subtle forgetting of grounding skills.  
- QA accuracy on relationship questions lagging overall signals persistent weakness in modeling interactions.  
- Generated stories that use generic names (“John”) instead of script names indicate alignment failure.

**First 3 Experiments:**  
1. **LCS Alignment Validation:** Manually verify a random sample of 100 aligned instances, measuring character attribution precision/recall and correlating with automatic LCS scores.  
2. **Baseline Comparison on Subtitle Alignment:** Run the automated pairwise evaluation (DeepSeek V3 judge) between Storyteller3 and the base Qwen2.5-VL 7B; verify win rate exceeds 85%.  
3. **Script-Derived QA Evaluation:** Use the GPT-5 generated question set to compute QA accuracy across categories; ensure overall >90% and relationship accuracy >90%, showing improvement over baseline.

## Limitations
- LCS alignment relies on lexical overlap and may not handle paraphrased subtitles; no human validation of alignment quality is reported.
- Evaluations use automated judges (DeepSeek V3, GPT-5) whose judgments may not fully align with human perception of semantic coherence.
- The dataset size (1,757 sequences) limits coverage of diverse genres and character dynamics, potentially affecting generalization.

## Confidence
- **LCS alignment yields reliable dialogue attribution:** Medium confidence. The win‑rate evidence is indirect; alignment accuracy itself is not measured, and break conditions are plausible.
- **Script‑aligned training teaches authentic semantics:** Medium confidence. QA gains are substantial, but evaluation is script‑centric and automated; human‑perceived coherence improvement is unknown.
- **Progressive multi‑stage training preserves prior skills:** Medium confidence. High tie rates on description suggest no regression, yet explicit grounding retention tests are absent; subtle forgetting could occur.
