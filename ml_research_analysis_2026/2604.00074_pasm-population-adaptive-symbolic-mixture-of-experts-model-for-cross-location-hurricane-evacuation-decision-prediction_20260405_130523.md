# PASM: Population Adaptive Symbolic Mixture-of-Experts Model for Cross-location Hurricane Evacuation Decision Prediction

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2604.00074  
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.00074  
- **Reference count:** 40  
- **Primary result:** With 100 target-domain calibration samples, PASM achieves MCC 0.607 on Georgia evacuation prediction, substantially outperforming XGBoost (0.404), TabPFN (0.333), and GPT-5-mini (0.434).

## Executive Summary
Cross-location generalization of evacuation behavior models often fails because households with similar characteristics follow systematically different decision patterns across regions. PASM addresses this by discovering latent subpopulations via unsupervised clustering, training interpretable symbolic decision formulas for each, and using a learned mixture-of-experts router with input-dependent coefficient adaptation to combine them. On transferring models trained on Florida/Texas data to Georgia with minimal calibration, PASM closes more than half the performance gap seen by standard models, produces three distinct human-readable formula archetypes, and shows no statistically significant demographic fairness disparities after correction.

## Method Summary
PASM is a three-stage pipeline. First, it applies UMAP dimensionality reduction and HDBSCAN density clustering to source-domain (Florida/Texas) training data to discover latent behavioral subpopulations. Second, for the global population and each identified cluster, it uses LLM-guided symbolic regression (LaSR) to evolve a set of interpretable closed-form expressions (e.g., involving log, exp, trigonometric operators) that predict evacuation decisions; the top-5 Pareto-optimal expressions per group are retained. Third, a Mixture-of-Experts framework is trained: a router MLP produces input-dependent expert weights, while a separate coefficient-adaptive MLP modulates each symbolic expert’s raw logit via an input-dependent affine transformation (γ, β). The system is jointly optimized on a combined validation set of source-domain data and a small sample (100 households) from the target domain (Georgia), using task loss plus regularization terms to encourage router diversity and prevent collapse.

## Key Results
- PASM achieves MCC 0.607 on Georgia test data with 100 calibration samples, beating XGBoost (0.404), TabPFN (0.333), and GPT-5-mini (0.434).
- The model discovers three interpretable formula archetypes corresponding to different demographic/behavioral subgroups (e.g., linear additive for younger cohorts, geographic-vs.-log-age for middle/older, complex multi-factor for socially isolated).
- A fairness audit across race, sex, education, and age finds no statistically significant disparities after Bonferroni correction.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1
- **Claim:** Cross-location generalization failure stems from systematic differences in decision patterns for identical feature profiles, not just marginal feature distribution shift.
- **Mechanism:** Identical household characteristics yield different evacuation probabilities across states; a single global model cannot reconcile these conflicting feature-to-decision mappings and overfits dominant patterns.
- **Core assumption:** Behavioral heterogeneity is structured and persistent, not random noise.
- **Evidence anchors:** UMAP visualization shows spatially coherent regions where P(evac|Georgia)−P(evac|Florida/Texas) differs by 0.30–0.45 for identical profiles [section 1, Figure 1]; abstract states households with similar characteristics follow systematically different decisions across states.

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim:** A Mixture-of-Experts framework with LLM-guided symbolic experts captures heterogeneous decision regimes while maintaining interpretability.
- **Mechanism:** Unsupervised clustering identifies latent subpopulations; each cluster trains a distinct symbolic regression model to produce a human-readable formula; a learned router assigns each input to appropriate expert(s), preventing a single model from averaging conflicting patterns.
- **Core assumption:** Distinct subpopulations obey qualitatively different functional forms, not just different parameter values.
- **Evidence anchors:** Router-based selection (MCC 0.607) outperforms naively averaging top-5 symbolic experts (0.548) and using a single best expert (0.234) [section 4.2, Table 3]; three formula archetypes emerge with different structural forms [section 4.2].

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim:** Joint adaptation of routing weights and expert-specific coefficients via input-dependent affine transformation enables a single symbolic structure to generalize across heterogeneous contexts.
- **Mechanism:** Each symbolic expert’s logit is scaled by a learned, input-dependent coefficient (γ, β), allowing a rule to adjust effective thresholds or amplifications per-household without altering the symbolic skeleton.
- **Core assumption:** Coefficient variability within a subpopulation is meaningful and systematic.
- **Evidence anchors:** Full joint adaptation (MCC 0.607) substantially outperforms fixed coefficients (0.389) and globally learnable static coefficients (0.365) [section 4.3, Table 4]; clusters sharing the same archetype still show varied demographic profiles, implying internal coefficient tuning [section 4.2].

## Foundational Learning
- **Concept: Symbolic Regression (LaSR)**  
  *Why needed here:* Provides interpretable, closed-form decision rules that can be audited by domain experts and extrapolate beyond training data—critical for emergency planning.  
  *Quick check:* How does an LLM-guided symbolic search differ from classical genetic programming in terms of search space and prior knowledge?

- **Concept: Mixture-of-Experts Routing**  
  *Why needed here:* Dynamically allocates inputs to specialized models, preventing a single global model from being pulled in conflicting directions by heterogeneous subpopulations.  
  *Quick check:* What failure mode does a learned router prevent that a static ensemble (e.g., averaging top-5 models) does not?

- **Concept: Density-Based Clustering (UMAP + HDBSCAN)**  
  *Why needed here:* Discovers latent behavioral subgroups without pre-specifying *K* or assuming spherical clusters, capturing complex, non-discrete heterogeneity in feature space.  
  *Quick check:* Why might hierarchical density clustering be preferred over *K*-means for evacuation behavior data?

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map:**  
  Subpopulation Discovery (UMAP -> HDBSCAN) -> Cluster assignments -> Symbolic Expert Construction (LaSR per cluster + global) -> Top-5 expressions per expert -> MoE Integration (Router MLP + Coefficient-Adaptive MLP -> Calibrated logits -> Final prediction)

- **Critical path:** Clustering stability determines expert consistency; symbolic search must yield diverse expressions; router calibration with target samples must not collapse.

- **Design tradeoffs:**  
  Interpretability vs. Performance: Simpler formulas (linear) are more transparent but may underfit complex subgroups; complex formulas (trigonometric, roots) capture interactions but reduce transparency.  
  Router flexibility vs. Collapse: Strong regularization (KL, entropy, diversity losses) prevents single-expert dominance but may under-specialize.  
  Computational cost: Symbolic search (LLM calls) is expensive (~10 hours) but one-off; router training is cheap (~45 sec); deployment cost negligible.

- **Failure signatures:**  
  Router collapse: One expert used >95% of the time (monitor average utilization ̄π). Fix: increase λ_KL, reduce final temperature τ, or add expert dropout.  
  Expert degeneracy: Many experts produce identical or near-constant outputs (check pairwise R²). Fix: increase λ_div or restrict operator set.  
  Cluster instability: Varying UMAP seed changes cluster assignments drastically (ARI <0.8). Fix: tune UMAP n_neighbors/min_dist, or use consensus clustering.  
  Poor calibration: Large gap between calibration-set and test-set MCC (>0.1). Fix: increase calibration shots, regularize router more.

- **First 3 experiments:**  
  1. Clustering sanity check: Run UMAP+HDBSCAN 10 times with different seeds on source data; compute ARI/NMI between pairings; expect ARI >0.85.  
  2. Expert diversity validation: After symbolic search, compute pairwise R² between top-5 expert predictions on validation set; expect median <0.5 across distinct clusters.  
  3. Router calibration curve: Train router with 20, 50, 100, 200 target-domain samples; plot MCC vs. shot count; expect diminishing returns after 100.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
1. Can PASM replace hard clustering with soft probabilistic subpopulation modeling? (Hard clustering oversimplifies fuzzy boundaries; future work will explore soft/probabilistic clustering.)
2. How can the computational cost of LLM-guided symbolic regression be reduced without sacrificing performance? (High LLM token usage and wall time hinder scalability; future work may use pre-trained concept libraries or more efficient evolutionary operators.)
3. Do PASM's symbolic formula archetypes generalize to other disaster types or non-US cultural contexts? (Evaluation limited to US hurricanes; validity in other settings unknown; would require retraining router and updating concept library.)
4. Does PASM exhibit fairness disparities under metrics beyond accuracy, particularly for vulnerable subgroups? (Bonferroni-corrected accuracy tests found no significant disparities, but a 4.7 p.p. male-female gap warrants monitoring; need equalized odds, calibration curves for subgroups.)

## Limitations
- Critical hyperparameters (UMAP n_neighbors, HDBSCAN min_cluster_size), regularization coefficients (λ values, router temperature), and exact MLP architectures are unreported, hindering precise replication.
- Generalization tested only on Georgia transfer; broader geographic and cross-disaster validity remains unknown.
- Fairness assessment relies solely on Bonferroni-corrected accuracy tests, potentially missing biases in false negative rates or calibration for vulnerable subgroups.

## Confidence
- **Mechanism 1 (pattern heterogeneity):** Medium (supported by UMAP visualization but break condition—conditional feature analysis—not verified).
- **Mechanism 2 (MoE + symbolic diversity):** Medium (ablations show router benefit, but expert diversity metrics like pairwise R² unreported).
- **Mechanism 3 (coefficient adaptation):** High (strong ablation evidence; break condition—coefficient distribution uniformity—likely false).
