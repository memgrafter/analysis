# Evaluation of Audio Language Models for Fairness, Safety, and Security

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2603.13262
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.13262
- **Reference count:** 26
- **Primary result:** Introduced a structural taxonomy for Audio LLMs and a unified FSS evaluation framework; found cross-modal safety inconsistency (CMSI) of 22% for Phi-4 and 31% for Qwen2-Audio, showing that FSS behavior is tightly coupled to audio integration architecture.

## Executive Summary
Existing evaluations of Audio Large Language Models (ALLMs) for fairness, safety, and security (FSS) are fragmented because they conflate systems with fundamentally different architectures, obscuring how design impacts behavior. This paper introduces a structural taxonomy categorizing ALLMs by audio input representation (discrete vs. continuous) and locus of semantic reasoning (cascaded, multimodal, audio-native). It builds a unified framework to assess FSS via semantic invariance under paralinguistic variation, refusal/toxicity under unsafe prompts, and robustness to adversarial audio perturbations. Applying the framework to two audio-text multimodal models (Qwen2-Audio and Phi-4-Multimodal) reveals systematic differences in refusal rates, attack success, and toxicity between audio and text inputs, with cross-modal safety inconsistency (CMSI) reaching 22%–31%. The study demonstrates that FSS behavior is tightly coupled to how acoustic information is integrated into semantic reasoning, necessitating structure-aware evaluation.

## Method Summary
The authors propose a unified evaluation framework that decouples ALLM architectures along two axes: audio representation (discrete tokens vs. continuous embeddings) and system type (cascaded, multimodal, audio-native). They generate controlled evaluation datasets by combining semantic prompts from LibriSQA (benign) and CoSafe (unsafe) with paralinguistic variants (gender, accent, emotion) via StyleTTS. For fairness, they measure comprehension, tonality drift, and ordinal equalized odds across 48 acoustic variants per prompt. For safety, they compute comprehension-aware metrics (C-Refusal Rate, C-Attack Success Rate, etc.) and cross-modal safety inconsistency (CMSI). For security, they apply untargeted PGD and FGSM adversarial attacks (ϵ=0.02) targeting wav2vec2 and measure latency, response length, semantic similarity, and comprehension. The framework is applied to Qwen2-Audio and Phi-4-Multimodal, both continuous-representation audio-text multimodal models, using an LLM judge (gpt-oss-safeguard-120b) with human validation (85.33% agreement).

## Key Results
- Systematic differences in refusal rates, attack success, and toxicity between audio and text inputs; CMSI was 22% for Phi-4 and 31% for Qwen2.
- Adversarial attacks increased latency but preserved semantic similarity (≥0.8) and comprehension, indicating robust semantic reasoning.
- Fairness evaluations revealed disparities across paralinguistic variants, implying continuous representations retain acoustic cues that bias outputs.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1
**Claim:** The locus of semantic reasoning (system type) and the form of audio representation jointly determine a model's susceptibility to cross-modal safety inconsistency (CMSI) and fairness disparities.  
**Mechanism:** Architectural choices control whether and how paralinguistic features (accent, emotion) are preserved and integrated into the LLM's semantic space. In audio-text multimodal systems, if audio embeddings are injected via adapters (e.g., Qwen2-Audio) versus projections (e.g., Phi-4-Multimodal), the conditioning dynamics differ, leading to measurable variance in refusal rates and toxicity when switching from text to audio prompts.  
**Core assumption:** Differences in how continuous audio representations interface with the LLM's transformer layers causally influence the model's internal safety and fairness decision boundaries.  
**Evidence anchors:** [abstract] "...found systematic differences in refusal rates, attack success, and toxicity between audio and text inputs. Cross-modal safety inconsistency (CMSI) was 22% for Phi-4 and 31% for Qwen2."; [section 2.1] "...models vary in their input representations and the preservation of paralinguistic information...cascaded designs are inherently lossy...obscuring the influence of acoustic variation on downstream reasoning."; [section 5] "...they differ in how audio embeddings condition the language model: Qwen2-Audio injects speech features via learned adapters...whereas Phi-4-Multimodal relies on projection-based alignment...resulting in different interaction patterns...contributing to the observed cross-modal safety inconsistency."

### Mechanism 2
**Claim:** The audio input representation (discrete vs. continuous) determines the measurable surface for fairness evaluation by controlling the retention of paralinguistic information.  
**Mechanism:** Continuous embeddings preserve finer acoustic details (prosody, speaker identity, emotion) that can propagate to the LLM and influence output tonality and safety judgments. Discrete tokenization abstracts away this detail, making fairness evaluations focused on demographic accents/emotions only feasible for continuous-representation models.  
**Core assumption:** Paralinguistic bias in outputs is mediated by the presence of corresponding acoustic cues in the model's input representation.  
**Evidence anchors:** [section 2.2] "Representation choice determines whether an evaluation is probing audio-native behaviors (e.g., sensitivity to accent/emotion) versus ASR bottlenecks, and it changes the feasible threat model..."; [section 4.3.1] Fairness experiments use "48 paralinguistic variants spanning gender-balanced speakers, multiple emotions and intensity levels, and a diverse set of accents" and find disparities, implying the model's representation retained these cues.

### Mechanism 3
**Claim:** Adversarial robustness in ALLMs is partly determined by the input representation's continuity, but semantic comprehension can remain stable even under waveform-level attacks due to the model's reliance on higher-level semantic features.  
**Mechanism:** Continuous representations provide a larger perturbation surface, yet the LLM may be operating on a semantic abstraction resilient to small perturbations. This yields increased latency (processing uncertainty) and output length changes, but preserved semantic similarity (≥0.8) and comprehension.  
**Core assumption:** The LLM's semantic reasoning is conducted on a robust feature space derived from audio, making it less sensitive to imperceptible waveform changes that primarily affect fine acoustic details.  
**Evidence anchors:** [section 4.3.3] "Semantic similarity remains largely stable (≥0.8), and comprehension rates are only slightly affected, demonstrating that the model preserves meaning under adversarial perturbations."; [section 4.3.3] "Latency increases are generally higher under attacks...Higher latency on attacked audio indicates that the model takes longer to process and generate responses when perturbations are present, reflecting increased computational effort or internal uncertainty."; [section 2.2] "Discretization yields a structured abstraction over speech while reducing temporal and fine acoustic detail," implying discrete models might have a different (potentially more brittle) attack surface.

## Foundational Learning
- **Concept: System Type Taxonomy (Cascaded, Multimodal, Audio-Native)**  
  *Why needed here:* It is the primary lens for disentangling *where* semantic reasoning occurs relative to audio processing. Confounding these types in evaluation obscures whether observed FSS issues stem from the ASR bottleneck, the LLM itself, or the audio-to-text integration.  
  *Quick check:* If a model first transcribes audio to text and then uses a text-only LLM, what system type is it and what key paralinguistic information is permanently lost before reasoning?

- **Concept: Paralinguistic Features**  
  *Why needed here:* These are the acoustic cues (accent, emotion, speaker identity) that are preserved or discarded by the input representation. They are the independent variables in the fairness evaluation and a major source of cross-modal discrepancy.  
  *Quick check:* How might a model's output tone (supportive vs. dismissive) be influenced by the speaker's perceived emotional valence in the input audio, and which architectural choice would amplify this effect?

- **Concept: Cross-Modal Safety Inconsistency (CMSI)**  
  *Why needed here:* It is a core metric that quantifies the specific vulnerability introduced by the audio modality. It measures the *difference* in safety behavior (refusal/toxicity) for the same semantic intent delivered via audio versus text.  
  *Quick check:* Why is a high CMSI value (e.g., 31%) a structural red flag, and how would you experimentally isolate whether it's caused by the audio encoder or the LLM's safety training?

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map:** Audio Frontend → Audio Representation Module → Modality Fusion → Core LLM → Safety/Alignment Layer
- **Critical path:** Raw waveform → Feature Extractor → Representation Module → Fused Modality Sequence → LLM Layers → Output Tokens
- **Design tradeoffs:**  
  - *Continuous vs. Discrete:* Continuous preserves detail but increases attack surface and may require more training for stable integration. Discrete abstracts and simplifies but discards potentially relevant paralinguistic signals.  
  - *Multimodal vs. Cascaded:* Multimodal enables richer joint reasoning but risks safety misalignment between modalities (high CMSI). Cascaded is simpler and leverages mature text-based alignments but discards all non-lexical information and introduces ASR error propagation.
- **Failure signatures:**  
  - **High CMSI (>25%):** Indicates the audio pathway bypasses or weakens text-based safety mechanisms.  
  - **Fairness OEO variance across accents/emotions:** Suggests the model's representation retains speaker characteristics that unfairly bias safety/tonality responses.  
  - **Latency↑ + Semantic Sim.≈stable under attack:** Model is computationally strained but semantically robust; attack may be affecting acoustic detail, not semantic core.  
  - **Comprehension C-RR↓ for audio vs. text:** Points to potential weakness in the audio encoder's semantic fidelity or the fusion mechanism.
- **First 3 experiments:**  
  1. **Baseline Comprehension Parity:** For a balanced set of 100 benign prompts (e.g., from LibriSQA), generate both text and audio (neutral speaker) inputs. Measure comprehension rate (via judge) for both modalities. A significant gap (>5%) indicates a fundamental audio-text comprehension mismatch in the architecture.  
  2. **Controlled Paralinguistic Stress Test:** Take 20 semantically identical unsafe prompts (e.g., from CoSafe). For each, generate 48 audio variants (4 genders × 6 accents × 2 emotion intensities). Measure C-Refusal Rate (C-RR) and tonality drift across variants. High variance (std dev >15%) reveals bias propagation pathways.  
  3. **Adversarial Latency-Semantic Probe:** For 50 benign and 50 unsafe prompts (fixed speaker), generate PGD-perturbed (ϵ=0.02) and FGSM audio. Measure: (a) Latency Drift (ms), (b) Semantic Similarity (cosine of sentence embeddings), (c) Comprehension impact. The pattern (latency↑, sim.≈stable, comp.≈stable) tests the robustness hypothesis.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
1. How do fully cascaded and audio-native ALLM architectures compare in terms of fairness, safety, and security? The current study only evaluated audio-text multimodal models; the impact of architecture on FSS behavior remains unknown for other types.
2. How do multi-speaker, multi-accent, and more diverse acoustic conditions affect fairness metrics beyond the controlled single-speaker settings used? Real-world usage involves overlapping speakers, background noise, and broader demographic diversity.
3. What explains the cross-modal safety inconsistency (CMSI) observed between audio and text inputs, and how does the audio conditioning mechanism (e.g., adapter vs projection) contribute to it? The causal link between conditioning and safety inconsistency is not isolated.
4. How do ALLMs perform under multi-turn interactions and richer threat models such as targeted adversarial attacks or jailbreak strategies that exploit audio-specific chains of reasoning? The security evaluation focused on untargeted single-turn attacks.

## Limitations
- The evaluation depends on unreported details (e.g., exact 48 paralinguistic variant composition, StyleTTS configuration, adversarial attack hyperparameters, LLM judge thresholds, model inference settings), hindering faithful reproduction.
- Only continuous-representation multimodal models were tested, limiting generalizability to discrete or audio-native systems.
- Potential biases from LLM-as-a-judge and dataset imbalance may affect fairness and safety metrics.

## Confidence
- Taxonomy as necessary lens: Medium
- CMSI caused by conditioning differences: Medium
- Continuous representation preserves paralinguistic cues: Medium
- Adversarial semantic robustness: Medium
