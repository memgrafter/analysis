# JARVIS: An Evidence-Grounded Retrieval System for Interpretable Deceptive Reviews Adjudication

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2602.12941  
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.12941  
- **Reference count:** 36  
- **Primary result:** Combines hybrid multimodal retrieval, a heterogeneous evidence graph, and LLM-based reasoning to boost precision from 0.953 to 0.988 and recall from 0.830 to 0.901 offline; in production, increases recall volume by 27% and reduces manual inspection time by 75%.

## Executive Summary
JARVIS is a training-free system for detecting deceptive reviews that addresses two major challenges: poor generalization to lexically disguised fraud and lack of interpretable decisions. It first retrieves semantically similar historical reviews using a hybrid dense-sparse multimodal retriever (fusing CN-CLIP and BGE-M3 with image-to-text augmentation). It then expands the retrieved set into a heterogeneous graph by linking reviews through shared entities (user, device, IP) within a temporal window. Finally, an LLM reasons over this evidence-rich subgraph to produce a judgment with a transparent evidence chain. Offline tests show substantial gains in precision and recall, while production deployment demonstrates significant operational efficiency improvements and high user adoption of its analyses.

## Method Summary
JARVIS executes a three-stage pipeline. **Stage 1 (Hybrid Retrieval):** For a target review (text + image), it computes a combined score: `λ·cosine(dense_multimodal_embedding) + (1-λ)·lexical_similarity(sparse_embedding_of_text_∪_image_caption)`. Top-k reviews are selected. **Stage 2 (Graph Expansion):** Starting from the target and the top-k reviews (the seed set), the system adds all connected entity nodes (users, devices, IPs, items). It then adds any other reviews posted by these entities within a temporal window ΔT, creating a heterogeneous graph with review-review, review-entity, and entity-entity edges. **Stage 3 (LLM Adjudication):** The subgraph centered on the target review is retrieved. A large language model (Qwen3-30B), prompted with a role and a structured reasoning schema (Entity Consistency Audit, Semantic Style Alignment), analyzes the retrieved evidence paths to output a risk level, a fraud judgment, and a step-by-step evidence chain.

## Key Results
- Offline precision increased from 0.953 to 0.988; recall increased from 0.830 to 0.901.
- Production deployment achieved a 27% increase in recall volume and a 75% reduction in manual inspection time.
- Generated analyses attained a 96.4% adoption rate by human auditors.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1: Hybrid Dense-Sparse Multimodal Retrieval
- **Claim:** Fusing dense semantic and sparse lexical signals mitigates brittleness in single-modality matching, improving recall of semantically relevant but lexically diverged deceptive reviews.
- **Mechanism:** The target review's text and image generate a dense vector (CN-CLIP) and a sparse vector (BGE-M3). The image is also caption-textualized (Qwen3-VL) to augment the sparse signal. A weighted sum `s = λ·cos(dense) + (1-λ)·φ_lex(sparse)` scores all corpus reviews. Dense captures paraphrased intent; sparse (augmented) captures reused promotional tokens.
- **Core Assumption:** Deceptive reviews share detectable semantic *and* lexical patterns across modalities, even after adversarial rewriting.
- **Evidence Anchors:** Abstract mentions "hybrid dense–sparse multimodal retrieval." Section 3.1 details the scoring equation and the `e_i` (dense) and `˜t_i` (augmented sparse) components. Corpus provides weak direct support for this specific formulation in fraud detection; relies on transfer from general multimodal retrieval principles.
- **Break Condition:** Perfect adversarial lexical substitution and un-textualized visual templates degrade sparse signals. Mis-tuned `λ` (e.g., `λ=0` or `λ=1`) causes precision or recall collapse per Figure 2a.

### Mechanism 2: Heterogeneous Graph Expansion via Shared Entities
- **Claim:** Expanding from the seed set through shared entities surfaces relational fraud patterns (e.g., coordinated campaigns) invisible to review-level analysis.
- **Mechanism:** From seed `S_r = r_0 ∪ C(r_0)`, the system adds all connected entity nodes (`N_E(S_r)`). It then attaches any other reviews `r'` posted by these entities within a temporal constraint `|T_r - T_s| ≤ ΔT` (Eq 3). This creates a subgraph `G_r` with review-review similarity edges and heterogeneous entity links.
- **Core Assumption:** Fraudulent entities (buyers, devices) are reused across multiple deceptive reviews for related products within a short timeframe, creating abnormal sharing anomalies.
- **Evidence Anchors:** Abstract mentions "expands relational signals through shared entities." Section 3.2 defines the expansion rule with temporal filtering and edge types. Corpus weakly supports entity-sharing as a fraud signal via graph-based detection papers.
- **Break Condition:** Fraudsters using unique, never-reused entities add only noise. An overly large `ΔT` introduces temporal noise; an overly small `ΔT` may split genuine fraud clusters.

### Mechanism 3: LLM-Based Evidence-Grounded Adjudication
- **Claim:** Grounding an LLM's reasoning in the retrieved heterogeneous graph produces interpretable, evidence-based fraud assessments, moving beyond black-box classification.
- **Mechanism:** The LLM (Qwen3-30B) acts as a "senior auditor." It receives the target review `r_0` and retrieved evidence paths `K_r` from `G_r`. A fixed reasoning schema (Table 1) directs it to perform an "Entity Consistency Audit" (checking abnormal entity sharing) and a "Semantic Style Alignment" check (coordinated language). The output is a risk level, judgment, and step-by-step evidence chain.
- **Core Assumption:** An LLM can synthesize structured graph data and unstructured text to mimic human auditor reasoning when provided comprehensive evidence.
- **Evidence Anchors:** Abstract states "Large language model then performs evidence-grounded adjudication." Section 3.3 describes the graph retrieval `K_r = Retrieve(r_0, G_r)` and the prompt schema. Corpus provides weak support via general RAG/LLM reasoning papers, not this specific graph-grounded application.
- **Break Condition:** A sparse or missing `G_r` (e.g., due to prior retrieval failure) leads to ungrounded LLM guesses or false negatives. Poor prompt design yields inconsistent or uninterpretable outputs despite a good graph.

## Foundational Learning
- **Concept: Dense vs. Sparse Embeddings**  
  *Why needed here:* To understand the fusion of cosine similarity (dense) and BM25-like lexical overlap (sparse). Dense captures semantic paraphrasing; sparse catches exact token reuse.  
  *Quick check:* If a deceptive review parrots a genuine review but changes 30% of words, which component (dense or sparse) is more likely to retrieve the original?
- **Concept: Heterogeneous Graph Construction**  
  *Why needed here:* To grasp how nodes (reviews, users, devices) and edges (posted, shares_IP) are expanded from a seed set to extract relational signals.  
  *Quick check:* In Eq (3), what is the purpose of the temporal constraint `|T_r - T_s| ≤ ΔT` on review timestamps?
- **Concept: Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG) with Structured Knowledge**  
  *Why needed here:* The LLM does not operate on the raw corpus; it queries the constructed evidence graph (`K_r = Retrieve(r_0, G_r)`). This is RAG where the knowledge source is a structured graph, not text chunks.  
  *Quick check:* How does JARVIS's use of `G_r` differ from a standard RAG system that retrieves text chunks from a document corpus?

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map:**  
  Input (Review r0: text+image) -> Hybrid Retriever (CN-CLIP + BGE-M3 + Qwen3-VL caption) -> Top-k Reviews C(r0) -> Graph Builder (Entity Expansion + Temporal Filter) -> Heterogeneous Graph G_r -> Graph Retriever (K_r) -> LLM Adjudicator (Qwen3-30B, reasoning schema) -> Output (Risk Level, Judgment, Evidence Chain)
- **Critical path:** r0 -> [Hybrid Score] -> C(r0) -> [Expand Entities + ΔT Filter] -> G_r -> [Graph Query] -> K_r -> [LLM Reasoning] -> Final Output.
- **Design tradeoffs:**  
  - *Retrieval:* Larger k improves graph coverage but adds noise/cost. λ trades semantic vs. lexical focus.  
  - *Graph:* Larger ΔT captures more fraud clusters but adds temporal noise. More entity expansion increases connectivity but risks irrelevant branches.  
  - *LLM:* Stronger models may reason better but increase latency/cost.
- **Failure signatures:**  
  - *Precision drop:* Overly broad retrieval (λ too low) or graph expansion (ΔT too large) floods graph with benign nodes.  
  - *Recall drop:* Too strict retrieval (λ too high) misses lexically-disguised fraud; too narrow ΔT splits fraud clusters.  
  - *Uninterpretable output:* Sparse/incoherent K_r (missing entity links) leads LLM to fabricate or give generic answers.
- **First 3 experiments:**  
  1. **Retrieval ablation:** Vary λ (0, 0.25, 0.5, 0.75, 1) and measure downstream F1. Expect λ=0.5 optimal per paper's Figure 2a.  
  2. **Graph topology sensitivity:** Fix retrieval, vary ΔT (1, 7, 30 days) and presence/absence of entity nodes. Expect entity nodes critical per Figure 2b.  
  3. **LLM prompt robustness:** Keep G_r fixed, vary reasoning schema (e.g., remove "Entity Consistency Audit" step). Measure adoption rate of evidence chains. Expect full schema needed for 96.4% adoption.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- **Open Question 1:** Is the fixed λ=0.5 parameter for hybrid retrieval universally optimal across all product categories and over time?  
  *Basis in paper:* Ablation (Figure 2a) shows λ=0.5 optimal on their dataset, but not tested across subgroups.  
  *Why unresolved:* Different product domains may have varying text-image correlations, affecting the dense-sparse trade-off.
- **Open Question 2:** How does the temporal constraint ΔT in graph expansion affect the balance between noise reduction and evidence coverage?  
  *Basis in paper:* Equation 3 and text mention ΔT to prevent noise, but its value is unreported and untuned.  
  *Why unresolved:* ΔT is a key hyperparameter; too strict may miss collusive patterns, too lenient introduces irrelevant reviews.
- **Open Question 3:** To what extent are the LLM's judgments sensitive to the prompt design in the reasoning stage?  
  *Basis in paper:* Table 1's fixed prompt schema; no analysis of robustness to prompt variations.  
  *Why unresolved:* LLMs can be brittle to phrasing; the "senior auditor" role and step-by-step reasoning might not generalize.
- **Open Question 4:** Does JARVIS maintain performance over extended periods as deceptive review tactics evolve?  
  *Basis in paper:* Production uses a 30-day sliding window, but no long-term stability analysis.  
  *Why unresolved:* Adversarial adaptation may render retrieval patterns obsolete; temporal drift not addressed.

## Limitations
- Core hyperparameters (temporal window ΔT, retrieval path count K_r) are unreported, hindering exact replication.
- The graph query mechanism `Retrieve(r0, G_r)` is underspecified (e.g., path length, ranking strategy).
- Claims rely on specific, rapidly evolving models (CN-CLIP, BGE-M3, Qwen3 series); long-term generalizability is unknown.
- Corpus evidence for the *specific hybrid formulation* and *entity-expansion pipeline* in deceptive review contexts is weak; transfer from general retrieval/graph papers is assumed.

## Confidence
- **Mechanism 1 (Hybrid Retrieval):** High. Supported by strong offline metrics (Precision +0.035, Recall +0.071) and clear ablation design.
- **Mechanism 2 (Graph Expansion):** Medium. Relies on entity-sharing assumption with limited direct fraud-detection corpus backing; performance gain shown but causal attribution to entity nodes is inferred.
- **Mechanism 3 (LLM Adjudication):** Medium. High adoption rate (96.4%) is compelling but dependent on the quality of the preceding graph; no standalone LLM baseline reported.
- **Foundational Concepts:** High. Dense/sparse fusion and heterogeneous graphs are well-established; novelty is in the pipeline integration.

## Next Checks
1. **Δt Sensitivity Analysis:** Systematically vary the temporal filter (e.g., 1, 7, 30, 90 days) on a held-out dataset. Measure changes in graph size, connectivity, and downstream F1 to identify the optimal operational window and validate the "short timeframe" assumption.
2. **Entity Node Ablation:** Starting from a fixed seed retrieval, build graphs while selectively removing entity types (users, devices, IPs) one at a time. Measure the drop in precision/recall to quantify which entity signals are most critical for the claimed relational fraud detection.
3. **Graph Retrieval Path Audit:** Instrument `Retrieve(r0, G_r)` to log the exact evidence paths fed to the LLM. Correlate path characteristics (length, entity diversity, similarity scores) with the final LLM judgment correctness on a labeled subset to verify that the retrieval itself is evidence-grounded and not noisy.
