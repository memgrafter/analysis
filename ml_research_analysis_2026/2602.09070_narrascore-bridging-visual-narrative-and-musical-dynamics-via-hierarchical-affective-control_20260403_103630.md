# NarraScore: Bridging Visual Narrative and Musical Dynamics via Hierarchical Affective Control

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2602.09070
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.09070
- Reference count: 40
- Primary result: State-of-the-art narrative-aligned soundtrack generation with best FAD 1.923 and KLD 0.320; top-ranked in user studies for emotional dynamic consistency, global style matching, long-term coherence, and overall preference.

## Executive Summary
NarraScore tackles the problem of generating coherent soundtracks for long-form videos by overcoming the semantic blindness of prior methods to the evolving plot. It repurposes frozen Vision-Language Models (VLMs) as affective sensors to compress video into continuous Valence-Arousal (VA) trajectories, using emotion as a high-density narrative proxy. For music synthesis, it employs a Dual-Branch Injection strategy into a pre-trained MusicGen model: a Global Semantic Anchor derived from the VLM ensures stylistic stability, while a Token-Level Affective Adapter directly modulates musical tension via residual injection into shallow transformer layers. This hierarchical affective control yields state-of-the-art narrative alignment with negligible computational overhead, as demonstrated by objective metrics (FAD 1.923, KLD 0.320) and subjective user preferences, particularly on long videos.

## Method Summary
The framework operates in two stages. In perception, a frozen VideoLlama-3 VLM processes video frames sampled at 1Hz, with semantic temporal alignment via time tokens and an instruction primer to focus on narrative reasoning. A lightweight probing head (spatial average pooling + MLP) trained with hybrid L2/L1 loss outputs continuous VA values. For synthesis, a pre-trained MusicGen-Small is augmented. A Projector (MLP) initializes mapping from visual features to MusicGen's latent space. Then a Dual-Branch Injection mechanism is introduced: the Global Semantic Branch reads a structured musical description generated by the VLM (via cross-attention), while the Affective Branch uses a Temporal Super-resolution Adapter (stack of dilated convolutions + linear interpolation) to upscale the frame-level VA trajectory to token-level control residuals. These residuals are injected into 75% of MusicGen's shallow transformer blocks. Training uses a two-phase approach: first train the Projector for 150 epochs, then fine-tune the Adapter and gating scalars for 50 epochs with the autoregressive cross-entropy loss. At inference, long videos are handled by an overlapping sliding-window strategy (e.g., 30s windows with 15s overlap) to maintain temporal coherence.

## Key Results
- Fréchet Audio Distance (FAD) as low as 1.923, and Kullback-Leibler Divergence (KLD) of 0.320, outperforming five baselines.
- User studies: highest ratings for Emotional Dynamic Consistency, Global Style Matching, Long-term Coherence, and Overall Preference.
- Gains are especially pronounced on long-form videos, demonstrating superior long-term coherence and emotional resonance.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
NarraScore succeeds by treating emotion as a high-density encoding of narrative progression. Instead of relying on explicit plot or object detection, it leverages the rich semantic knowledge within a frozen Vision-Language Model (VLM) to infer the emotional tone (Valence-Arousal) of each video segment. This continuous VA trajectory serves as a compact yet expressive control signal that mirrors the story's affective arc.

The synthesis engine builds on a pre-trained music language model (MusicGen) and introduces two complementary control pathways. The Global Semantic Anchor provides a high-level musical description (genre, instrumentation) derived from the VLM's narrative understanding, ensuring that the generated soundtrack remains stylistically consistent with the video's overall mood. The Token-Level Affective Adapter, implemented as a Temporal Super-resolution Adapter, converts the coarse VA trajectory into fine-grained residuals that are directly injected into the transformer's residual stream. This dual-branch injection operates in shallow layers, where musical structure is formed, allowing local tension to be shaped without disrupting the global style. By keeping the VLM frozen and adding only lightweight adapters, the method achieves strong alignment with minimal computational overhead.

Additionally, the sliding-window inference with overlap guarantees continuity across long videos, while the two-stage training stabilizes learning: first aligning visual features to the music latent space, then specializing the affective control.

## Foundational Learning
- **Vision-Language Models as Affective Sensors**: VLMs encode rich visual semantics and can be prompted for affective interpretation without task-specific training. *Why needed*: They serve as a proxy for narrative understanding beyond low-level features. *Quick check*: Feed a few video frames with the instruction "Describe the emotion" and verify that textual output correlates with expected mood.
- **Valence-Arousal (VA) Trajectory**: Continuous two-dimensional representation of emotional state. *Why needed*: Provides a low-dimensional control signal that directly maps to musical dynamics (tension, energy). *Quick check*: Plot VA predictions for a short clip and ensure smooth transitions; compare with human annotations if available.
- **Dual-Branch Injection (Global Semantic Anchor & Token-Level Affective Adapter)**: Two parallel control pathways into the music generator. *Why needed*: Global anchor maintains stylistic coherence; local adapter enables fine-grained emotional modulation. *Quick check*: Ablate one branch and measure degradation in global style consistency or emotional alignment.
- **Temporal Super-resolution Adapter**: Converts frame-level VA to token-level control via dilated convolutions and linear interpolation. *Why needed*: VLM operates at ~1Hz while music token rate is much higher (~50Hz). *Quick check*: Upsample a known VA curve and verify that downsampling the result reconstructs the original shape.
- **Sliding-window Inference**: Overlapping segmentation for long videos. *Why needed*: MusicGen has a limited context; overlap prevents boundary artifacts and maintains continuity. *Quick check*: Generate a long sequence and inspect spectrogram for smooth transitions at window boundaries.

## Architecture Onboarding
Component map (plain text):
Input Video -> Frame Sampling (1 Hz) -> Freeze VideoLlama-3 VLM (with time tokens & instruction) -> Visual Features
-> Split: (a) VA Probe (MLP) -> Frame-level VA Trajectory; (b) Global Description (structured) from VLM
-> Trajectory: Linear Interpolation + Dilated Convolutions (Temporal Super-resolution Adapter) -> Token-level Residuals
-> MusicGen-Small: Projector (MLP) maps visual features to initial latent; Global Semantic branch via cross-attention; Affective branch adds residuals to 75% shallow layers
-> Autoregressive token generation
-> For long videos: Sliding-window segmentation (e.g., 30 s windows, 15 s overlap) -> Overlap-add synthesis -> Final Audio

Critical path:
1. Frame extraction at 1 Hz.
2. VLM forward pass to obtain visual tokens.
3. VA probe predicts frame-level VA.
4. Temporal Super-resolution Adapter upsamples VA to token rate.
5. Projector maps visual features to MusicGen latent.
6. Residues from adapter are injected into MusicGen’s shallow transformer blocks alongside global semantic cross-attention.
7. MusicGen autoregressively generates audio tokens.
8. Overlap-add reconstructs full sequence for long inputs.

Design tradeoffs:
- Frozen VLM vs fine-tuning: Frozen reduces compute and overfitting but may limit adaptation to specific video styles; fine-tuning could improve VA accuracy but requires more data and risks catastrophic forgetting.
- Injection depth (75% shallow layers): Injecting into too many layers may disrupt MusicGen’s pretrained representations; too few limits affective control. The paper reports 75% as optimal.
- Sliding-window size vs overlap: Larger windows improve long-term coherence but increase memory; higher overlap reduces boundary artifacts but adds computational overhead.

Failure signatures:
- High VA prediction error: Visualize predicted vs. ground-truth VA; if RMSE > 0.3, the probe may be underfitting or VLM not properly steered.
- Style drift across windows: Spectrograms show abrupt changes at boundaries; increase overlap or ensure global description is consistent.
- Overly tense or bland music: Monitor norms of residual injections; adapter may be over-amplifying or suppressing.
- Harmonic incoherence: Global anchor missing or uninformative; verify that VLM-generated description contains genre and instrumentation cues.

First experiments:
1. Train and validate the VA probe on a small subset (e.g., LIRIS-ACCEDE) to achieve reasonable VA prediction (RMSE < 0.3 on normalized scale). This isolates the perception module.
2. Train the Projector and Adapter on short video-music pairs with ground-truth VA trajectories (synthetic or from dataset) to validate that the control signal influences generated music as expected (e.g., higher arousal yields higher tempo/energy).
3. Run end-to-end generation on a 1-minute video with sliding-window inference and measure objective metrics (FAD, KLD) against a baseline; iterate on window size and overlap.

## Limitations
- Lack of official implementation and missing critical hyperparameters (learning rates, adapter architecture, inference window settings).
- Insufficient specification of VLM prompting and keyframe selection for global semantic extraction.
- Dataset ambiguity and underreported user study methodology.

## Confidence
- Hierarchical affective control for narrative alignment: Medium
- Quantitative superiority (FAD=1.923, KLD=0.320): Low
- Subjective preference (EDC, GSM, LTC, OP): Medium-Low

## Next Checks
1. **VA extraction replication**: Train probing head on LIRIS-ACCEDE with default hyperparameters (Adam lr=1e-4, batch=32); report VA RMSE.
2. **Sliding-window sensitivity**: Evaluate model with overlap ratios of 50% and 75% to test robustness of long-form coherence metrics (FAD, KLD, LTC).
3. **Ablate global anchor**: Remove Global Semantic Anchor, retrain, and compare LTC and style metrics to confirm its role in maintaining long-term coherence.
