# To Search or Not to Search: Aligning the Decision Boundary of Deep Search Agents via Causal Intervention
## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID**: 2602.03304
- **Source URL**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.03304
- **Reference count**: 40
- **Primary result**: Causal intervention combined with DPO improves exact match (e.g., NQ +1.3%), reduces average search queries, and lowers over/under-search rates for deep search agents.

## Executive Summary
Deep search agents often struggle with deciding when to search for additional information, leading to over-search (excessive queries) or under-search (premature answers). This paper introduces a causal intervention framework that retroactively simulates alternative actions to identify suboptimal decisions. By generating preference data that pairs suboptimal factual trajectories with superior counterfactual ones, the authors apply Direct Preference Optimization (DPO) to align the agent's decision boundary. Evaluations on NQ, HotpotQA, and 2WikiMultiHopQA show consistent gains: higher exact match scores, fewer search queries, and reduced over/under-search rates for models like Qwen2.5-7B-RL. Ablations confirm that incorporating both over- and under-search signals is essential for balanced performance. The approach offers a practical offline method to improve search efficiency and accuracy without requiring online interaction.

## Method Summary
The proposed method consists of three main stages. First, given a search agent's interaction log (factual trajectory) on a QA task, a causal intervention module retroactively simulates alternative actions at each decision point to generate counterfactual trajectories. These counterfactuals represent what would have happened if the agent had taken different search or answer actions, ideally leading to more optimal outcomes. Second, the framework constructs a preference dataset by pairing suboptimal factual trajectories (where the agent either over-searched or under-searched) with superior counterfactual trajectories (where the agent made better decisions). Third, this preference data is used to fine-tune the search agent via Direct Preference Optimization (DPO), which adjusts the policy to assign higher probability to actions from the superior counterfactuals. The entire process operates offline, leveraging existing logs without additional online deployment. The method is evaluated on several QA datasets using Qwen models, demonstrating improved decision boundary alignment.

## Key Results
- Exact Match (EM) improves: e.g., on NQ, Qwen2.5-7B-RL increases from 38.1% to 39.4% (+1.3%).
- Average number of search queries decreases (e.g., from 2.014 to 1.896), enhancing efficiency.
- Over-search rate reduced to 0.308 and under-search rate reduced to 0.518, indicating better balance.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The mechanism works because retroactive causal simulation creates high-quality counterfactual trajectories that approximate the optimal search policy. These counterfactuals serve as "oracle" demonstrations of better behavior, even though they are synthetically generated. By pairing suboptimal factual trajectories with these superior counterfactuals, the DPO objective directly teaches the model to avoid actions that lead to over- or under-search. This effectively shifts the decision boundary: the model learns to issue searches only when necessary and to answer when sufficient information is gathered. The causal intervention ensures that the counterfactuals are plausible and causally consistent, which is crucial for DPO to learn a robust policy without reward hacking or distribution shift issues.

## Foundational Learning
- **Causal Intervention**: Enables retroactive simulation of alternative actions to assess their impact on outcomes. *Why needed*: To generate counterfactual trajectories that reveal optimal search decisions without online interaction. *Quick check*: Do simulated trajectories maintain causal consistency (e.g., same initial conditions, only intervention differs)?
- **Decision Boundary**: The policy's threshold for switching between search and answer actions. *Why needed*: Misalignment causes over/under-search; aligning it improves efficiency and accuracy. *Quick check*: Are both over-search and under-search rates reduced after training?
- **Direct Preference Optimization (DPO)**: A method to align models with preferences by directly optimizing policy likelihoods. *Why needed*: Incorporates the (suboptimal, superior) action pairs efficiently without explicit reward modeling. *Quick check*: Does DPO improve the agent's preference for counterfactual (better) actions over factual (worse) ones?
- **Over-search / Under-search**: Error modes of search agents. *Why needed*: Quantify decision boundary errors; balancing them indicates successful alignment. *Quick check*: Is the trade-off between these two minimized (both low)?
- **Factual Trajectories**: Interaction logs of the agent on QA tasks. *Why needed*: Provide the raw data of suboptimal decisions to be corrected via counterfactuals. *Quick check*: Do the logs contain a sufficient mix of over- and under-search cases?

## Architecture Onboarding
The system architecture comprises the following components arranged in a pipeline: Input Question -> Search Agent (original policy) -> Interaction Trajectory -> Causal Intervention Module (simulates alternative actions) -> Counterfactual Trajectory Generator -> Preference Pair Constructor -> Preference Dataset -> DPO Trainer -> Aligned Search Agent. The critical path runs from the causal intervention through counterfactual generation to DPO training; this flow is essential for producing and learning from the preference data. Key design tradeoffs include balancing simulation fidelity against computational cost, integrating both over- and under-search signals to avoid bias, and handling potential distribution shift when moving from factual to counterfactual data. Failure signatures to monitor include: persistent high over-search or under-search rates after training, degradation in EM despite fewer queries, or overly conservative behavior (e.g., never searching). Three first experiments to run are: (1) Train on NQ and evaluate EM, query count, and error rates; (2) Ablate by training with only over-search or only under-search counterfactuals to verify the need for both; (3) Test the method on a larger model (e.g., Qwen2.5-14B) to check scalability.

## Limitations
- Retroactive simulation fidelity is uncertain; counterfactuals may introduce biases.
- Exact Match improvements are modest (e.g., +1.3% on NQ) and lack statistical significance testing.
- Evaluation is limited to three QA datasets; generalization to other tasks or model families remains unproven.

## Confidence
- Decision boundary errors exist and degrade performance: Medium
- Causal intervention retroactively simulates valid alternative actions: Low
- DPO alignment yields improved EM and fewer search queries: Medium
- Incorporating both over- and under-search signals is essential: Medium

## Next Checks
1. Perform bootstrap significance testing on test sets to compute confidence intervals for EM and search metrics, verifying the statistical significance of reported gains.
2. Evaluate cross-domain generalization by applying the approach to additional tasks (e.g., TriviaQA, SQuAD) and diverse model architectures (e.g., Llama, Mistral).
3. Conduct human evaluation of generated counterfactual trajectories to ensure they represent genuinely superior decisions and are free of systematic bias.
