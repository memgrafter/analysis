# Long-Horizon Traffic Forecasting via Incident-Aware Conformal Spatio-Temporal Transformers

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2603.16857  
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.16857  
- Reference count: 40  
- Primary result: STT-ED achieves state-of-the-art multi-hour traffic forecasting (MAE 0.087 at 1 h, 0.317 at 4 h) and Adaptive Conformal Prediction delivers well‑calibrated uncertainty intervals with 91–94% coverage and tighter widths than standard conformal methods.

## Executive Summary
The paper introduces a novel framework for long‑horizon traffic forecasting under stochastic conditions and incident disruptions. The method combines a Spatio‑Temporal Transformer (STT) with an encoder‑decoder architecture, a dynamic graph construction that incorporates hour‑of‑day variability and crash severity signals, and Adaptive Conformal Prediction (ACP) for uncertainty quantification. The dynamic adjacency matrices are derived from a piecewise hourly coefficient of variation (CV) profile and perturbed by aggregated crash attributes, enabling the model to adapt spatial dependencies to time‑of‑day and incident impacts. Evaluated on a real‑world dataset from Columbus, Ohio, the proposed STT‑ED model significantly reduces forecast errors (MAE, RMSE) across 1–4 hour horizons compared to strong baselines. ACP provides reliable, node‑ and horizon‑specific prediction intervals with high coverage and reduced width. SUMO simulations validate the realism of the stochastic travel‑time assumptions and the model's ability to propagate dynamics over extended periods.

## Method Summary
The approach consists of three main components. First, a dynamic graph is constructed for each hour of the day: historical travel times are modeled as log‑normal distributions parameterized by an hourly coefficient of variation (CV); these distributions are sampled to obtain stochastic travel times, which are then perturbed by a combined crash severity signal (clearance time, weather, speeding, work zones, functional class) aggregated at the node level. The correlation between sampled travel times and the total risk on each edge is used to modulate the effective travel time, and a Gaussian kernel converts these into hour‑specific adjacency matrices that are masked by data availability. Second, an encoder‑decoder Spatio‑Temporal Transformer (STT‑ED) processes the input sequence: a temporal encoder tokenizes the look‑back window via patch convolutions and self‑attention; the resulting per‑node temporal memories are combined with the hour‑conditioned adjacency through graph mixing and refined by a spatial self‑attention encoder. The decoder then cross‑attends to the concatenated spatio‑temporal memory using learned horizon queries to produce parallel multi‑step forecasts. Third, Adaptive Conformal Prediction (ACP) is applied: after each training epoch, quantiles of absolute residuals on a validation set are computed for each horizon and node, yielding adaptive radii that maintain target coverage under non‑stationarity. Final prediction intervals are formed by adding/subtracting these radii to the point forecasts.

## Key Results
- STT-ED achieves superior multi‑horizon accuracy: MAE 0.087 at 1‑hour, 0.317 at 4‑hour (vs. baselines like DCRNN, ASTGCN).  
- ACP yields well‑calibrated uncertainty intervals with high coverage (PICP ~91–94%) and narrower widths than standard conformal methods.  
- SUMO Monte Carlo simulations confirm the log‑normal travel‑time assumption and demonstrate that the model realistically propagates dynamics over extended horizons.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1
**Claim:** Hour‑conditioned adjacency matrices, derived from a piecewise coefficient of variation (CV) profile and crash severity perturbations, improve representation of time‑varying spatial dependencies for long‑horizon forecasting.

**Mechanism:** A historical hourly CV profile `CV(h)` parameterizes log‑normal travel time distributions for each hour `h`. Sampled travel times are perturbed using a combined crash severity signal (incident clearance time, weather, speeding, work zones, functional class) aggregated by hour and node. Edge weights are modulated via a Pearson correlation between sampled travel times and node‑pair risk sums, then converted via a Gaussian kernel to produce hour‑specific adjacency matrices `A(h)`.

**Core assumption:** Travel time variability (`CV(h)`) is systematic and diurnally periodic, and crash impact on network connectivity can be approximated by a linear perturbation additive to effective travel time.

**Evidence anchors:** The abstract mentions the piecewise CV strategy and crash‑derived severity signals; Sections III.B–III.E detail the equations for `CV(h)`, log‑normal sampling, crash severity aggregation `R(h,i)`, correlation `ρ_ij`, and adjacency computation. The corpus shows limited direct prior work on incident‑aware, hour‑conditioned graphs combined with transformers, indicating novelty.

**Break condition:** If the historical CV profile is miscalibrated (e.g., due to data gaps) or crash attribute correlations are spurious, the induced spatial priors may misrepresent true connectivity, especially during unseen incident patterns.

### Mechanism 2
**Claim:** An encoder‑decoder Spatio‑Temporal Transformer (STT‑ED) with cross‑attention decoding stabilizes multi‑hour forecasts by separating historical encoding from future decoding.

**Mechanism:** A temporal encoder processes historical traffic patches via self‑attention, producing per‑node temporal memories. A spatial encoder integrates the hour‑conditioned adjacency via graph‑weighted mixing before refining node interactions with spatial self‑attention. The decoder uses learned horizon queries that cross‑attend to the concatenated spatio‑temporal memory (temporal tokens + spatial token) to generate multi‑step outputs in parallel.

**Core assumption:** Long‑horizon error accumulation can be mitigated by a concise encoder memory that the decoder can reference, and that spatial dependencies are best introduced before spatial attention via adjacency‑weighted mixing.

**Evidence anchors:** The abstract states the STT model and encoder‑decoder design; Section III.F describes temporal tokenization, spatial mixing via `eAt H(0)_t`, and decoder cross‑attention to memory `M_t,i`. The corpus confirms transformer‑based models are used for long‑horizon forecasting, but the specific integration of hour‑conditioned adjacency in the spatial encoder’s input is distinctive.

**Break condition:** If the encoder fails to compress relevant history into the memory (e.g., due to insufficient capacity or noisy inputs), the decoder’s cross‑attention cannot recover accurate long‑range dynamics, leading to horizon‑dependent error growth.

### Mechanism 3
**Claim:** Adaptive Conformal Prediction (ACP) provides well‑calibrated, horizon‑ and node‑specific uncertainty intervals that adapt to non‑stationarity during training.

**Mechanism:** After each training epoch, conformal radii `q_{k,i}^{(1-α)}` are computed as quantiles of absolute residuals `|Ŷ_t,k,i - Y_t,k,i|` on a validation set. These radii are updated online (epoch‑wise) to maintain target coverage (`1-α`) under distribution drift. Final prediction intervals are `Ŷ ± q`.

**Core assumption:** Validation residuals are exchangeable within each epoch and representative of test‑time error; periodic recalibration tracks slow distribution shifts.

**Evidence anchors:** The abstract reports high coverage and tighter widths with ACP; Section III.F explains epoch‑wise calibration and horizon/node‑specific quantile computation. The corpus includes conformal prediction in time‑series literature, but ACP’s epoch‑wise adaptive update for traffic forecasting is a specific adaptation from prior work.

**Break condition:** If test‑time distribution shift is abrupt and not captured by epoch‑wise updates (e.g., a major unexpected incident), or if the calibration set is small/unrepresentative, coverage may drop below nominal levels.

## Foundational Learning
- **Spatio‑Temporal Graph Neural Networks (STGNNs)**  
  *Why needed:* Must capture both spatial correlations (between sensors) and temporal dynamics (over hours). STGNNs provide the framework for message‑passing over a graph while modeling time.  
  *Quick check:* How does a static adjacency matrix fail to capture peak/off‑peak transitions in traffic networks?

- **Log‑Normal Distribution for Travel Times**  
  *Why needed:* Travel times are non‑negative and right‑skewed; the log‑normal distribution, parameterized by mean and CV, is empirically validated for traffic variability. This allows stochastic edge weight generation.  
  *Quick check:* Why is the coefficient of variation (CV) used instead of variance to parameterize hourly travel‑time dispersion?

- **Conformal Prediction for Distribution‑Free Uncertainty**  
  *Why needed:* Traffic forecasting under incidents involves distribution shifts; conformal prediction provides finite‑sample coverage guarantees without assuming a specific error distribution.  
  *Quick check:* What is the key difference between standard conformal prediction and adaptive conformal prediction in non‑stationary settings?

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component map:**  
Data Preprocessing → Adjacency Construction → STT‑ED Model → ACP Layer

**Critical path:** The hour tag `h_t` must correctly map to the precomputed `A(ht)`; this is the link that injects time‑of‑day and incident context into the spatial module. Failure here disconnects the dynamic graph from the forecaster.

**Design tradeoffs:**  
- *Dynamic graphs vs. computational cost:* Hour‑conditioned matrices (24 sets) increase storage and selection overhead versus a static graph, but better capture diurnal shifts.  
- *Patch tokenization vs. temporal resolution:* Patch convolutions reduce sequence length for transformer efficiency but may lose sub‑hourly patterns if patch size `p` is too large.  
- *ACP epoch‑wise vs. fully online:* Epoch‑wise recalibration is practical but may lag extreme shifts; fully online would be more responsive but computationally heavier.

**Failure signatures:**  
- *Spatial module:* Adjacency `A(ht)` not updating with hour tag → model ignores time‑of‑day variability.  
- *Uncertainty:* PICP >> target (e.g., >95%) indicates over‑conservative intervals; PICP << target indicates under‑coverage, often due to poor residual calibration.  
- *Long‑horizon:* MAE/RMSE growing superlinearly with horizon (e.g., 1h to 4h error multiplies >4×) suggests decoder or memory failing to propagate dynamics.

**First 3 experiments:**  
1. **Ablation study** (Section IV.E): Remove encoder‑decoder cross‑attention, patch tokenization, or spatial encoder to isolate contributions; expect severe error increase without cross‑attention.  
2. **Baseline comparison** (Table III): Compare STT‑ED against GCN‑GRU, DCRNN, ASTGCN, etc. on MAE/RMSE across 1h–4h horizons; expect STT‑ED to show smallest error growth.  
3. **SUMO simulation validation** (Section IV.F): Inject STT‑ED hourly forecasts into SUMO for multi‑hour loop runs; compare simulated travel‑time distributions to INRIX and test log‑normality via KS test; expect KS p>0.05 for log‑normal fit.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- **Open Question 1:** How can the hour‑conditioned CV profile be updated online to adapt to unexpected demand surges or large‑scale events? The authors note the CV is estimated from historical data and fixed during inference, limiting adaptation to anomalies.  
- **Open Question 2:** How can crash information be explicitly modeled as a spatio‑temporal evolving process rather than aggregated severity to better capture queue dynamics? The paper acknowledges aggregation oversimplifies complex queue formation and dissipation.  
- **Open Question 3:** Does the proposed STT‑ED‑ACP framework generalize across different cities, seasons, and sensor network densities? The study focuses on a single regional network (Columbus, Ohio) and fixed horizons, leaving broader generalization unexplored.  
- **Open Question 4:** Can adaptive conformal prediction be made fully online to maintain nominal coverage under abrupt distribution shifts instead of epoch‑wise calibration? The authors point out that epoch‑wise updates may lag sudden changes and compromise coverage guarantees.

## Limitations
- Undisclosed method for deriving baseline mean travel times (`T_mean_ij`) and hourly CV profile, which are foundational to the dynamic graph.  
- Correlation `ρ_ij` computed from only 24 hourly samples may be unstable; clipping threshold `ρ_max` and Gaussian kernel bandwidth `σ` are unspecified.  
- Missing STT‑ED hyperparameters (embedding dimension, layer counts, dropout) and training details (optimizer, learning rate) hinder exact replication.  
- Crash severity aggregation uses multiplicative factors without empirical justification.

## Confidence
- **Dynamic graph improves forecasting**: Medium (depends on unverified travel‑time derivation and noisy correlations).  
- **Encoder‑decoder STT stabilizes long horizon**: High (architecture well described, consistent multi‑horizon accuracy gains).  
- **ACP yields calibrated uncertainties**: High (reported PICP 91–94% and tighter widths vs. standard CP).

## Next Checks
1. **Ablation of the dynamic graph:** Replace hour‑conditioned adjacency with a static alternative (e.g., physical distance or average correlation) and measure degradation in MAE/RMSE.  
2. **Statistical test of edge‑wise correlations:** Perform a t‑test on `ρ_ij` values to confirm that crash‑derived perturbations are non‑random and significantly different from zero.  
3. **Stress test ACP under abrupt shift:** Simulate an extreme crash severity scenario on a held‑out set; evaluate ACP’s coverage decay and compare against a non‑adaptive conformal baseline.
