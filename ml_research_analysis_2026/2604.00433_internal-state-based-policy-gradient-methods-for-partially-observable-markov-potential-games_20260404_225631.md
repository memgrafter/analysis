# Internal State-Based Policy Gradient Methods for Partially Observable Markov Potential Games

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2604.00433
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.00433
- **Reference Count:** 20
- **Primary Result:** A finite internal state-based natural policy gradient method for POMPGs achieves a non-asymptotic convergence guarantee with average Nash equilibrium gap decaying as O(1/√T) plus an approximation error term ε_FSC from finite-state compression.

## Executive Summary
This paper addresses multi-agent reinforcement learning in partially observable Markov potential games (POMPGs), where agents must coordinate under partial observability and decentralized execution. The authors propose that each agent maintains a finite internal state—a lossy compression of its local observation history and shared information—effectively using finite-state controllers. The core contribution is a theoretical guarantee that an internal state-based natural policy gradient method converges to an approximate Nash equilibrium, with the error decomposing into a standard statistical convergence rate and an approximation error stemming from the finite-state compression. Empirical evaluations on three partially observable environments show consistent improvement over reactive policies that lack memory, highlighting the value of internal state for history compression in multi-agent settings.

## Method Summary
The method operates within the common information framework, where agents broadcast a subset of their local observations as shared memory `c_k`. Each agent then maintains two internal states: a shared state `w_k` (compressing `c_k` history) and a local state `l_k^i` (compressing its own history `m_k^i`). These states are updated deterministically and serve as a finite approximation to the otherwise intractable belief state. Agents use a softmax policy parameterized by `θ`, conditioning actions on `(w_k, l_k^i, y_k^i)`. Training employs a natural policy gradient update that uses the Fisher information matrix to precondition gradients, with advantage estimation from sampled trajectories. Theoretically, the performance difference lemma yields a bound where the average Nash equilibrium gap across `T` iterations is O(1/√T) plus ε_FSC, where ε_FSC scales with the total variation distance between true beliefs and internal state approximations.

## Key Results
- Theoretical non-asymptotic convergence bound: average NE-gap ≤ O(1/√T) + ε_FSC.
- ε_FSC depends on the total variation distance `d_b` between belief states and internal state approximations, plus problem-specific constants.
- Empirical results on MATiger, MABC, and LBF show the internal state policy consistently outperforms reactive (memoryless) baselines.
- The bound reveals a trade-off: larger internal state spaces reduce ε_FSC but increase policy complexity and sample requirements.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1
- **Claim:** The common information framework reduces the decentralized partially observable problem to a centralized one by separating shared and local information.
- **Mechanism:** Agents broadcast a subset of their local observations/actions as shared memory `c_k`. Each agent then acts using `(y_k^i, m_k^i, c_k)`, effectively transforming the Dec-POMDP into a POMDP solvable via belief-state MDP, albeit with intractable belief updates.
- **Core assumption:** The shared information `c_k` is sufficient to align agents' local beliefs about the global state, enabling a centralized solution concept (Nash equilibrium) to be meaningful in a decentralized setting.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [section II.A]: "the common information approach reduces the Dec-POMDP to a standard POMDP, which can in turn be solved by reformulating it as an equivalent MDP over the belief state space."
  - [abstract]: "to address the first two challenges [partial observability, decentralized information], we leverage the common information framework, which allows agents to act based on both shared and local information."

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim:** Finite internal states (finite-state controllers) compress the history of shared and local information into a bounded representation, making the belief approximation tractable.
- **Mechanism:** Each agent maintains a shared internal state `w_k ∈ W` and local internal state `l_k^i ∈ L_i`, updated via deterministic functions `φ` and `χ^i`. These states act as a lossy compression of the full history `(c_k, m_k^i)`, replacing the intractable belief `b(x|c_k)` with a finite `w_k`.
- **Core assumption:** There exists a finite-state controller that can sufficiently approximate the optimal belief-based policy; the total variation distance `d_b` between `b(·|c_k)` and `b(·|w_k)` is bounded and small enough to not degrade performance significantly.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [section II.B]: "we introduce the finite internal state representation... Let `W` be a finite set and `w_k ∈ W` represent a compression of the shared information history... we consider the class of finite-state controllers (FSC)."
  - [abstract]: "to ensure tractability, we study an internal state that compresses accumulated information, preventing it from growing unboundedly over time."

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim:** The internal state-based natural policy gradient (NPG) method converges to an approximate Nash equilibrium in POMPGs, with the error decomposing into a standard statistical rate and a finite-state approximation error.
- **Mechanism:** NPG updates policy parameters in the natural gradient direction, using the Fisher information matrix to account for policy geometry. For finite-state policies, the performance difference lemma (Lemma 1) yields a bound where the advantage term drives convergence (O(1/√T)) and the `d_b`-term induces `ε_FSC`.
- **Core assumption:** The initial policy has sufficient coverage (`inf_π min_ˆh_i d^π_ξi(ˆh_i) > 0`), the learning rate `η` is set appropriately (`η = (1-β)^2/(2nϕ_max)`), and the advantage function is accurately estimated.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [Theorem 1]: Non-asymptotic bound `1/T Σ NE-gap(π^t) ≤ O(1/√T) + ε_FSC`, where `ε_FSC ∝ sqrt(d_b^2 + (M n / a(1-β)) d_b)`.
  - [Lemma 1]: "Lemma 1, a variant of the well-known performance difference lemma... consists of two terms: one associated with the advantage function and the other capturing the error induced by partial observability and the use of finite-state controllers."

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map:** Internal State Modules (φ for shared, χ^i for local) -> Policy Network (softmax over θ) -> Advantage Estimator -> NPG Updater (computes Fisher matrix and update)
- **Critical path:** Initialize `(w_0, l_0^i)` -> agents interact using `π_i(·|w, l^i, y^i)` -> collect trajectories -> estimate `A_i^π` -> compute `F_i(θ_i)† ∇J_i` -> update `θ_i` -> repeat.
- **Design tradeoffs:** Memory size (`|W|`, `|L_i|`): Larger reduces `d_b` and `ε_FSC` but increases parameters and sample complexity. Learning rate `η`: Must balance convergence and stability per Theorem 1. Advantage estimation: Exact `A` assumed; in practice TD/Monte Carlo adds error not in `ε_FSC`.
- **Failure signatures:** NE-gap plateaus high: `ε_FSC` large due to insufficient internal state capacity. Diverging/oscillating policies: `η` too high or Fisher inversion unstable. Agents fail to coordinate: internal states not capturing relevant history; `d_b` remains high.
- **First 3 experiments:**
  1. **Reactive vs. Finite-Memory Baseline:** On MATiger, compare proposed method against reactive policy (no memory). Expect consistent improvement per Figure 1, confirming memory necessity.
  2. **Memory Size Ablation:** On MABC, vary window length `t_w` (1,2,3). Expect NE-gap to decrease with larger `t_w` until saturation, illustrating `ε_FSC` tradeoff.
  3. **Convergence Rate Check:** On LBF, plot average NE-gap over iterations. Should show O(1/√T) decay initially before plateauing due to `ε_FSC`, aligning with Theorem 1.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- **Open Question 1:** How can internal state representations be systematically designed or learned to minimize ε_FSC, which depends on the total variation distance between belief states and internal state approximations? The paper uses a simple finite-window internal state (t_w=2) and suggests richer representations (e.g., RNNs) could help but provides no design principles or empirical quantification of how architectures affect d_b.
- **Open Question 2:** Why does the factor 1/√a in the convergence bound become potentially large under uniform initialization with large internal state spaces, and how can this be mitigated? Remark 1 notes this occurs when the initial policy is uniform with large state space, but no remedy is proposed.
- **Open Question 3:** How does the choice of internal state architecture (e.g., finite window vs. RNNs) impact the trade-off between approximation error (ε_FSC) and computational/statistical efficiency? The experiments use a fixed window and do not systematically compare architectures, leaving the practical design space unexplored.
- **Open Question 4:** Can alternative policy gradient methods (e.g., PPO, TRPO, actor-critic variants) improve upon the convergence guarantees or empirical performance of the proposed NPG for POMPGs? The analysis is tied to NPG; it is unknown if other methods achieve similar rates or are more robust to finite-state approximation.

## Limitations
- Hyperparameter values (learning rate, KL threshold, batch size, value learning rate) are unspecified, hindering exact reproduction.
- Implementation details of the common information broadcast (e.g., synchronization of others' observations) are vague; central simulation during training may deviate from assumed decentralized execution.
- Theoretical bound contains ε_FSC with no guidance for selecting internal state cardinalities beyond the fixed window of length 2 used in experiments.
- Experiments lack statistical significance testing and use only three environments; generalizability to broader POMPGs is uncertain.

## Confidence
- **Convergence guarantee (Theorem 1):** High. Proof follows standard NPG analysis with clear assumptions; no apparent gaps.
- **Approximation error decomposition (ε_FSC):** Medium. Bound is derived but tightness in practice and feasibility of achieving small d_b with finite states remain open.
- **Empirical superiority over reactive policies:** Medium. Results are consistent across three environments but lack variance estimates and sensitivity analysis on memory size.
