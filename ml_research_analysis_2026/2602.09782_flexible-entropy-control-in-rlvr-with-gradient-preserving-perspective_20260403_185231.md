# Flexible Entropy Control in RLVR with Gradient-Preserving Perspective

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2602.09782
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.09782
- **Reference count:** 40
- **Primary result:** Dynamic clipping thresholds with scheduling (e.g., Increase-then-Decrease) mitigate entropy collapse in RLVR and achieve state-of-the-art Pass@K on math benchmarks (e.g., 33.1 on AIME24 for Qwen2.5-Math-7B, surpassing GRPO’s 24.4).

## Executive Summary
Reinforcement Learning with Verifiable Rewards (RLVR) for large language models suffers from rapid entropy decay during continuous training, leading to overconfidence, reduced output diversity, and vanishing gradients. This paper theoretically identifies four importance sampling ratio regions (E1–E4) that respectively decrease or increase policy entropy during updates. Building on this, the authors introduce dynamic clipping thresholds—non-linear functions of token probability—to precisely control entropy: a dynamic upper threshold promotes entropy increase by favoring low-probability tokens in positive-advantage updates, while a dynamic lower threshold reduces entropy by extending the threshold for low-probability tokens in negative-advantage updates. Three scheduling strategies (Increase-then-Decrease, Decrease-Increase-Decrease, Oscillatory Decay) orchestrate these thresholds over training. Experiments on mathematical reasoning benchmarks demonstrate effective entropy stabilization and significant performance improvements.

## Method Summary
The method adapts the GRPO algorithm by replacing fixed PPO clipping thresholds with probability-dependent functions. For a token with probability \( \pi_\theta \), the upper threshold is \( \epsilon_{\text{up}}(\pi_\theta) = \alpha_{\text{up}} \pi_\theta + \beta_{\text{up}} \) (\( \alpha_{\text{up}} < 0 \)) to encourage entropy increase, and the lower threshold is \( \epsilon_{\text{low}}(\pi_\theta) = \alpha_{\text{low}} \pi_\theta + \beta_{\text{low}} \) (\( \alpha_{\text{low}} < 0 \)) to encourage entropy decrease. A scheduler governs which threshold is active and how its parameters evolve over training steps (e.g., Increase-then-Decrease uses the upper threshold for the first half of training, then switches to the lower). Hyperparameters include \( \alpha_{\text{up}} = -0.25, \beta_{\text{up}} = 0.5, \alpha_{\text{low}} = -0.13, \beta_{\text{low}} = 0.3 \), with total training of 400 steps, learning rate \( 1 \times 10^{-6} \), batch size 512, and no KL penalty.

## Key Results
- On Qwen2.5-Math-7B, the Increase-then-Decrease strategy (Ours-ID) achieves an AIME24 score of 33.1, substantially surpassing GRPO (24.4) and the static Clip-Higher baseline (30.5).
- Similar consistent gains are observed across AIME25, AMC, MATH-500, GSM8k, and Olympiad benchmarks, with dynamic thresholds maintaining stable entropy trajectories unlike the rapid decay in baselines.
- A 50% phase split is optimal for two-phase strategies (ID/DID), balancing exploration and exploitation; the Oscillatory Decay strategy also shows robust performance.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1: Gradient-Aligned Region Analysis (E1–E4)
The inner product between the RL objective gradient and the entropy gradient reveals that the sign depends on a token’s surprisal (\(-\log \pi\)) relative to current policy entropy \(H\). For positive advantage (\(A > 0\)), tokens with high probability (\(-\log \pi < H\), E1) decrease entropy, while low-probability tokens (\(-\log \pi > H\), E2) increase it. For negative advantage (\(A < 0\)), high-prob tokens (E3) increase entropy, and low-prob tokens (E4) decrease it. This theoretical categorization allows targeted interventions.

### Mechanism 2: Dynamic Upper Threshold for Entropy Increase
By setting \( \epsilon_{\text{up}}(\pi_\theta) = \alpha \pi_\theta + \beta \) with \( \alpha < 0 \), the threshold is higher for low-probability tokens (E2) and lower for high-prob tokens (E1). This permits larger ratio increases for E2 tokens during positive-advantage updates, raising their probabilities and flattening the distribution, while preventing excessive sharpening of already high-prob tokens. The bound becomes \( \pi_\theta \le \frac{1+\beta}{1-\alpha \pi_{\text{old}}} \pi_{\text{old}} \).

### Mechanism 3: Dynamic Lower Threshold for Entropy Decrease
For \( A < 0 \), \( \epsilon_{\text{low}}(\pi_\theta) = \alpha \pi_\theta + \beta \) with \( \alpha < 0 \) lowers the threshold for high-prob tokens (E3), curbing their accidental increase due to softmax normalization, while raising the threshold for low-prob tokens (E4). This enables stronger suppression of E4 tokens, sharpening the distribution safely. The bound becomes \( \pi_\theta \ge \frac{1+\beta}{1-\alpha \pi_{\text{old}}} \pi_{\text{old}} \).

## Foundational Learning
- **PPO Clipping and Importance Sampling Ratio**: Understanding the clipping mechanism (ratio \( r = \pi_\theta / \pi_{\text{old}} \)) is essential to see how thresholds constrain updates and define the trust region. *Quick check*: If \( r > 1+\epsilon \) for a token with positive advantage, its gradient contribution is clipped to prevent excessive policy change.
- **Policy Entropy**: Entropy \( H(\pi) = -\sum \pi \log \pi \) measures output diversity; collapse (entropy near zero) causes overconfidence. *Quick check*: Compute \( H(\pi) \) for a vocabulary with probabilities 0.9 and 0.1.
- **GRPO Advantage Estimation**: GRPO computes advantages from group rewards without a critic, standardizing within each prompt group. *Quick check*: How is advantage \( A_i \) derived from the rewards of multiple sampled responses for the same prompt in GRPO?

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map**: LLM policy (πθ) -> GRPO advantage calculator -> Dynamic threshold modules (H(p) for upper, M(p) for lower) -> Scheduler -> Loss function (clipped surrogate objective with time-varying thresholds).
- **Critical path**: For each token, compute πθ, π_old, advantage A; determine dynamic ϵ_upper/ϵ_lower based on πθ; clip ratio; accumulate loss. Scheduler adjusts threshold scaling λ(k) at global step intervals.
- **Design tradeoffs**: Dynamic thresholds offer precise per-token entropy control but add overhead; phase ratio (e.g., 50% split) balances exploration and sharpening; linear f(π)=απ+β is simple but may not capture non-linear token-entropy relationships.
- **Failure signatures**: Entropy still collapses (thresholds too conservative or wrong phase); reward stagnation (over-aggressive thresholds causing distribution collapse); oscillating entropy without improvement (misconfigured scheduler hysteresis).
- **First 3 experiments**:
  1. **Threshold isolation test**: Train with only dynamic upper OR lower threshold (fix the other at static ϵ=0.2) to verify isolated entropy increase/decrease curves.
  2. **Phase ratio sweep**: Vary split point (0.3–0.7) for ID/DID strategies and measure final Pass@K on a held-out set.
  3. **Scheduler comparison**: Run full ID, DID, OD with dynamic thresholds and compare reward trajectories and final Pass@K against baselines.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- Does the entropy control mechanism generalize beyond mathematical reasoning to other domains like code generation or dialogue, given differing token probability distributions?
- What is the optimal functional form for dynamic clipping thresholds, and how sensitive are results to hyperparameters α and β in the linear approximation?
- Can entropy control be made adaptive in real-time based on monitored entropy levels rather than predetermined phase schedules, and would such adaptivity improve robustness?
- How does the mechanism interact with KL regularization or different advantage estimators (e.g., GAE vs. group-based), and can it be integrated with algorithms like PPO?

## Limitations
- Major unknowns block full replication: precise integration of dynamic thresholds into PPO-Clip (symmetric ratio application, π_old update frequency), exact GRPO step definition (batch accumulation, rollout grouping), and memory optimization details for 7B models on 8×H100.
- Theoretical break conditions: noisy advantage estimates may misclassify tokens into E1–E4 regions, and overly aggressive α/β could cause instability or discard useful exploratory modes.

## Confidence
- E1–E4 gradient alignment theory: **High** (rigorous derivation, Equations 5–6).
- Dynamic threshold mechanisms (upper/lower): **Medium** (empirically validated but dependent on tuned α/β).
- Scheduler strategies and 50% split optimality: **Medium** (reported in Section 5.4; may not transfer across domains/hyperparameters).
- Overall superior performance (e.g., AIME24 33.1 vs. GRPO 24.4): **Medium** (strong results but implementation-sensitive).
