# Personal Information Parroting in Language Models

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2602.20580
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.20580
- **Reference count:** 28
- **Primary result:** The 6.9B Pythia model verbatim parroted 13.6% of personal information instances, with memorization positively correlated with model size and pretraining steps.

## Executive Summary
This paper investigates privacy risks from language models memorizing and generating personal information (PI) from training data. It introduces the R&R detector suite—a high-precision system for emails, IP addresses, and US/Canadian phone numbers—that significantly outperforms prior regex-based detectors. Using a manually curated gold set of 483 PI instances from The Pile, the authors evaluate memorization across the Pythia model suite. They find pervasive PI parroting: verbatim generation rates increase with model scale and training compute, and even small models with short context prefixes (as few as 10 tokens) frequently reproduce exact PI spans. The findings underscore the need for aggressive filtering and anonymization in pretraining corpora to mitigate privacy leakage.

## Method Summary
The study proceeds in four stages: (1) **Detection:** The R&R suite scans The Pile with enhanced regex patterns and deterministic contextual rules to identify candidate PI spans. (2) **Curation:** Manual annotation of ~1,750 detections yields a gold standard set of 483 true PI instances. (3) **Prefix extraction:** For each instance, the preceding token context (up to 80 tokens) is extracted from The Pile. (4) **Evaluation:** Each (prefix, PI) pair prompts Pythia models (160M to 6.9B parameters) via greedy decoding; the output is compared to ground-truth PI using PARROTSCORE (normalized Levenshtein similarity). Aggregation across model sizes, training steps, and prefix lengths reveals memorization trends.

## Key Results
- **R&R detector** achieves substantially higher precision than prior regex-based detectors (e.g., WIMBD), particularly for phone numbers with country codes.
- **Verbatim parroting** scales with model size and pretraining steps: the 6.9B model parrots 13.6% exactly, while even the 160M model parrots 2.7%.
- **Short prefixes** (10 tokens) suffice for high parroting rates from large models; constituent parts (e.g., email domains, phone area codes) are also frequently memorized.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1: Rule-Augmented Regex for High-Precision Detection
- **Claim:** The R&R detector suite achieves higher precision than prior regex-based detectors by combining refined regular expressions with post-detection contextual rules.
- **Mechanism:** For each PI type (emails, IPs, phones), the system first applies an expanded regex pattern (e.g., handling IPv6, country-code phone formats). It then applies deterministic rules that examine surrounding context (e.g., checking for indicator words like "isbn" or "grant", validating area codes, filtering placeholder patterns).
- **Core assumption:** High-precision detection requires not only syntactic pattern matching but also simple, efficient semantic/contextual filtering.
- **Evidence anchors:** Manual annotation of 1,750 detections shows R&R significantly outperforms WIMBD, especially for phone numbers (p<0.05). WIMBD precision for phones was nearly 0% in the studied subset.
- **Break condition:** If PI formats in web corpora evolve (e.g., new obfuscation patterns) faster than static rules can be updated, precision gains may degrade.

### Mechanism 2: Scaling of Memorization with Model Size and Training Steps
- **Claim:** Verbatim parroting of PI is positively correlated with both language model size and the number of pretraining steps completed.
- **Mechanism:** Larger models have greater capacity to store training data associations; more pretraining steps allow reinforcement of specific token sequences. The experiment holds prefix length constant (up to 80 tokens) and measures PARROTSCORE across the Pythia suite.
- **Core assumption:** Memorization, measured by greedy decoding from exact training prefixes, scales monotonically with model scale and compute in autoregressive LMs.
- **Evidence anchors:** Across Pythia models (160M to 6.9B), verbatim parroting rates increase with size (e.g., email: 2.34% → 19.63%). For the 6.9B model, PARROTSCORE remains consistent from 70k to 143k steps, indicating memorization emerges early.
- **Break condition:** If future architectures (e.g., non-autoregressive, sparse experts) or training objectives (e.g., differential privacy) decouple capacity from memorization, this scaling correlation may not hold.

### Mechanism 3: Short Prefixes Suffice for High Parroting
- **Claim:** Very short prefixes (as few as 10 tokens) preceding PI in the original document are sufficient to elicit high rates of verbatim parroting from large models.
- **Mechanism:** Memorized PI spans have low perplexity and high determinism, allowing reconstruction from minimal context due to strong, specific training signals.
- **Core assumption:** Memorized sequences are highly predictable from a short, characteristic prefix.
- **Evidence anchors:** For Pythia-6.9B, average PARROTSCORE remains ~0.34 even with a 10-token prefix. Corpus evidence for this specific prefix-length sensitivity is weak; neighboring papers discuss memorization generally but not this precise analysis.
- **Break condition:** If PI spans are typically preceded by longer, more unique context, or if models are trained to utilize broader context, short-prefix parroting rates could drop.

## Foundational Learning
- **p-memorization (Carlini et al., 2022):** Formal definition: a model memorizes a string *s* if it generates *s* verbatim when given a prefix *s'* of length *p* from training data. **Why needed:** It operationalizes "parroting" in the paper. **Quick check:** If a model generates a phone number exactly when given the 15 tokens before it, has it 15-memorized that number? What if it generates it correctly only 3 out of 10 times with that same prefix?
- **PARROTSCORE:** Metric based on normalized Levenshtein distance between generated output and ground-truth PI; handles length mismatches via substring sliding. **Why needed:** Primary evaluation metric for parroting, allowing partial matches. **Quick check:** Why use normalized Levenshtein instead of exact match? What does a score of 0.7 imply about privacy risk?
- **Regex-based vs. Model-based Detection Trade-off:** Regex detectors are fast enough for trillion-token corpora but less precise; neural NER models are more accurate but computationally infeasible at scale. **Why needed:** Explains the paper's focus on improving efficient regex-based methods. **Quick check:** What is the key computational bottleneck that makes neural NER on entire pretraining corpora "infeasible"?

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map:** Raw Corpus → R&R Detection → Candidate PI → Manual Curation → Gold PI Set → Prefix Extraction → Prompt Pairs → Pythia Model Inference → Generations → PARROTSCORE Calculation → Analysis.
- **Critical path:** The pipeline from raw data through detection, curation, prefix extraction, model generation, and scoring, with aggregation by model size, steps, and prefix length.
- **Design tradeoffs:** 
  - *Detection:* Chose regex+rules over neural for corpus-scale speed, sacrificing recall for higher precision (validated by manual audit).
  - *Evaluation:* Used greedy decoding for determinism and reproducibility (vs. sampling), potentially under-estimating extraction risk.
  - *Scope:* Limited to character-based PI (emails, IPs, phones) in English-heavy The Pile; excludes semantic PI (names, locations) and multilingual PI.
- **Failure signatures:** 
  1. R&R precision drops significantly in a new, unanalyzed data domain (e.g., biomedical text with many numeric codes).
  2. PARROTSCORE near 0 for all models on a new PI type, suggesting either poor detection (false positives in gold set) or aggressive pretraining filtering.
  3. No clear scaling trend (Model Size vs. PARROTSCORE) when testing on model families with different architectures (e.g., state-space models).
- **First 3 experiments:**
  1. Replicate R&R precision: Run R&R and WIMBD on a held-out slice of The Pile (e.g., 10k docs). Manually annotate a stratified sample (n=500) to confirm the reported precision gap, especially for phone numbers with country codes.
  2. Prefix-length ablation: On Pythia-6.9B, vary prefix length (5, 10, 20, 40, 80 tokens) for the gold PI set. Plot PARROTSCORE distribution to verify the positive correlation and non-zero rate at 10 tokens.
  3. Constituent part analysis: For gold email and IP instances, parse and score constituent parts (e.g., username vs. domain, each octet). Replicate the finding that earlier (leftmost) parts are parroted more frequently, indicating autoregressive bias.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- How do the observed scaling trends (memorization vs. model size/steps) generalize to non-autoregressive architectures or models trained with explicit privacy-preserving objectives (e.g., differential privacy)?
- To what extent does PI parroting occur for semantic personal information (e.g., names, addresses) or in multilingual corpora, given this study's focus on structured, English-dominant data?
- What is the realistic privacy risk under natural generation (e.g., sampling) versus greedy decoding, and how does PARROTSCORE correlate with actual extraction-success rates in practical attack scenarios?

## Limitations
- Unreleased gold set of 483 PI instances blocks direct replication and introduces sampling variance.
- Ambiguity in tokenization alignment, prefix truncation rules, and PARROTSCORE edge-case handling.
- Scope limited to specific PI types in an English-dominated corpus; findings may not generalize to semantic or multilingual PI.
- Greedy decoding evaluation may underestimate realistic extraction risks compared to sampling-based generation.

## Confidence
- **R&R detector precision:** High – supported by manual annotation of 1,750 cases with statistically significant improvement over WIMBD.
- **Memorization scales with model size/training steps:** Medium‑High – consistent trends across Pythia, but limited to one architecture family; break conditions possible for future models.
- **Short prefixes (10 tokens) suffice for high parroting:** Medium – evidence from Pythia‑6.9B is clear, yet corpus signal for this specific prefix-length sensitivity is weak; dependence on PI context structure remains uncertain.

## Next Checks
1. **R&R precision replication:** Run the released R&R suite and WIMBD on a held-out slice of The Pile (e.g., 10 k docs). Manually annotate a stratified sample (n = 500) and compute precision to confirm the reported gap, especially for phone numbers with country codes.
2. **Prefix-length ablation repeat:** Using the same Pythia‑6.9B checkpoint and tokenizer, vary prefix lengths (5, 10, 20, 40, 80 tokens) on the paper’s gold set (or a close manual substitute). Re‑compute PARROTSCORE to verify the non‑zero parroting rate at 10 tokens and the overall trend.
3. **Constituent part analysis replication:** For the gold email and IP instances, parse left vs. right constituents (e.g., username vs. domain, octets). Measure verbatim parroting per constituent to reproduce the finding that earlier (left‑most) parts are memorized more frequently, indicating autoregressive bias.
