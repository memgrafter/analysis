# Interaction Theater: A case of LLM Agents Interacting at Scale

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2602.20059
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.20059
- **Reference count:** 20
- **Primary result:** LLM agents on an AI-only platform generate lexically diverse but largely irrelevant comments, with rapid information saturation and minimal threaded interaction, indicating “interaction theater”.

## Executive Summary
This paper analyzes 800 K posts and 3.5 M comments from the AI‑only social platform Moltbook to assess the quality of interactions among LLM agents. Through lexical (Jaccard specificity, entropy), semantic (embedding similarity), and LLM‑as‑judge metrics, the authors find that agents produce high‑variance text that is largely unresponsive to post content: 65 % of comments share no vocabulary with their post, and LLM judges classify 28 % as spam and 22 % as off‑topic. Information contribution decays rapidly—by the 15th comment, only 32.3 % of unigrams are novel—and nested replies are exceedingly rare (5 %). The study concludes that without explicit coordination, large populations of capable agents produce parallel monologues rather than substantive exchange.

## Method Summary
The analysis combines three HuggingFace datasets (lnajt/moltbook, AIcell/moltbook-data, SimulaMet/moltbook-observatory-archive) yielding 800 730 posts, 3 530 443 comments, and 78 280 agent profiles from 27 Jan–17 Feb 2026. Observational metrics include token entropy, Self‑NCD (a normalized compression distance), content‑word Jaccard specificity (against 10 random posts), embedding cosine similarity (OpenAI text‑embedding‑3‑small), and compression‑based information gain. Saturation curves are derived from 20 000 posts with ≥5 comments (temporally ordered). A stratified sample of 2 000 post‑comment pairs is judged by Claude‑Sonnet‑4.5 (primary) and Claude‑Opus‑4.5 (calibration) for category (spam, off‑topic, etc.) and 1–5 scales of responsiveness and information contribution. Nested reply detection uses a BFS on parent_id.

## Key Results
- High self‑variation (67.5% of agents) but minimal relevance: 65% of comments have zero lexical specificity.
- LLM‑judge: 28% spam, 22% off‑topic; mean responsiveness 1.85/5.
- Information novelty decays rapidly; by comment position 15 only 32.3% of unigrams are novel.
- Only 5% of comments are nested replies; threaded engagement shows higher specificity.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
**Claim:** Agents generate lexically diverse output that appears engaged but lacks post‑specific content.  
**Mechanism:** Agents sample high‑entropy text irrespective of context (Self‑NCD ≥ 0.8 for 67.5% of agents), creating surface‑level variation without constraining vocabulary to the post’s content words. This yields high token entropy (median 8.36 bits) but zero lexical specificity for 65% of comments.  
**Core assumption:** LLMs fine‑tuned for dialogue produce varied, plausible text per prompt, but without grounding to external context, variation is internal to the agent’s distribution rather than responsive.  
**Evidence anchors:**  
- [abstract] “agents generate diverse text (67.5% high self‑variation) but with minimal relevance: 65% of comments share no content vocabulary with their post”  
- [section 4.1] “most agents (67.5%) vary their output across contexts… This variation does not translate into genuine information contribution”  
- [corpus] Weak direct signal; inferred from high Self‑NCD paired with zero Jaccard specificity distributions (Figure 4b).  
**Break condition:** If agents were prompted or incentivized to mirror post vocabulary, lexical specificity would concentrate above zero rather than median 0.0000.

### Mechanism 2
**Claim:** Information contribution decays rapidly as more agents comment on a single post, indicating redundancy rather than accumulation.  
**Mechanism:** Each successive comment adds diminishing novel n‑grams/compressed information because agents independently generate similar topical clusters without building on predecessors. By the 15th comment, novel unigrams drop to 32.3% (Table 2).  
**Core assumption:** Without explicit turn‑taking or state‑sharing, agents cannot track and extend earlier comments, leading to parallel monologues.  
**Evidence anchors:**  
- [abstract] “Information saturates quickly (by the 15th comment, only 32.3% novel unigrams)”  
- [section 4.2] Saturation curves show monotonic decay across unigram, bigram, and compression metrics  
- [corpus] Direct from saturation analysis of 20 000 posts (Figure 3).  
**Break condition:** If agents referenced prior comments (e.g., via threading), saturation decay would be slower; nested replies in this dataset show higher lexical similarity (Figure 7a).

### Mechanism 3
**Claim:** The interaction format (flat comment stream) and absent protocols cause agents to default to independent top‑level responses rather than threaded dialogue.  
**Mechanism:** Platform structure affords nested replies, but 95% of comments are top‑level. Agents trained for single‑turn dialogue default to generating a response to the initial post prompt, ignoring existing comments as conversational partners.  
**Core assumption:** LLMs’ primary training objective is turn‑by‑turn response generation to a given prompt, not multi‑turn coordination without explicit scaffolding.  
**Evidence anchors:**  
- [abstract] “Only 5% of comments are nested replies”  
- [section 4.6] “Agents rarely engage in threaded conversation (5% of comments)… when agents do reply to one another, engagement is higher”  
- [section 2] “95.0% of comments are top‑level responses to posts (depth 0)”  
**Break condition:** Introduction of mandatory reply‑to‑parent protocols or incentives for threading would increase nested reply rates and corresponding specificity (Figure 7b‑c).

## Foundational Learning
- **Concept:** Information‑theoretic saturation metrics (lexical/compression gain)  
  *Why needed here:* Quantifies whether an additional agent adds new information or redundancy, moving beyond count‑based activity metrics.  
  *Quick check:* Why does compression‑based IG decay similarly to unigram IG, and what does that imply about semantic vs. lexical redundancy?

- **Concept:** LLM‑as‑judge validation for content quality  
  *Why needed here:* Provides a semantic, context‑aware ground truth to validate lexical/semantic metrics and categorize failure modes (spam, off‑topic).  
  *Quick check:* What are the limitations of using an LLM judge to assess agent output quality, and how did the authors mitigate rater bias?

- **Concept:** Nested reply structure as a proxy for conversational grounding  
  *Why needed here:* Distinguishes parallel broadcasting from genuine dialogue; threading creates an explicit partner and context for response.  
  *Quick check:* Why might agents that *do* engage in nested replies show higher specificity, and what does the 5% rate suggest about default agent behavior?

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map:** Data Ingestion → Metric Engine → Sampling & Stratification → Judge Module → Analysis Modules  
- **Critical path:**  
  1. Ingest and deduplicate posts/comments by ID.  
  2. Filter agent set (≥10 comments) for entropy analysis.  
  3. For each post with ≥5 comments, compute saturation metrics in temporal order.  
  4. Sample (post, comment) pairs for lexical/semantic specificity; compute random baseline per comment.  
  5. Stratify sample and run LLM judge; correlate scores with automated metrics.  
  6. Compute reply depth distribution and compare engagement metrics between top‑level vs. nested.  
- **Design tradeoffs:**  
  - Lexical vs. semantic specificity: Jaccard is cheap and reproducible but vocabulary‑bound; embeddings capture semantics but depend on external model choice and are costlier.  
  - Sample size vs. coverage: Full saturation analysis feasible on 20 k posts; LLM judge limited to 2 k pairs due to cost, requiring careful stratification.  
  - Platform‑specific vs. generalizable: Moltbook’s flat comment structure is a specific affordance; metrics must be adapted for threaded protocols.  
- **Failure signatures:**  
  - High median token entropy coupled with median lexical specificity = 0 → “interaction theater” pattern.  
  - Saturation curve flattening by position 10–15 indicates rapid redundancy.  
  - LLM judge: >50% of comments classified as spam/off‑topic with mean responsiveness <2.0.  
- **First 3 experiments:**  
  1. Replicate saturation curve on a held‑out subcorpus; verify decay slope stability across submolts.  
  2. Test coordination intervention: simulate a protocol where agent responses must include a reference to a previous comment’s ID; measure change in nested reply rate and specificity.  
  3. Ablate embedding model: compute semantic specificity with multiple embedding models (e.g., all‑MiniLM‑L6‑v2, text‑embedding‑ada‑002) to assess robustness of the lexically generic → semantically generic conclusion.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
1. **Coordination mechanisms for substantive exchange**  
   *Basis:* The authors conclude that “coordination mechanisms must be explicitly designed; without them, even large populations of capable agents produce parallel output rather than productive exchange.” They observed a platform with no such coordination.  
   *Why unresolved:* The study is purely observational; no coordination interventions were implemented or compared. The types and parameters of coordination that could mitigate “interaction theater” remain unknown.  
   *Resolution evidence:* Controlled A/B experiments on an agent‑only platform, introducing different coordination protocols (e.g., reply quotas, shared memory, role assignment) and measuring changes in metrics like lexical specificity, saturation curves, and nested reply rates.

2. **Generalization to task‑oriented multi‑agent systems**  
   *Basis:* “Moltbook is a social platform, not a task‑oriented multi‑agent system… Multi‑agent systems with defined tasks and structured protocols may behave very differently.” Their findings characterize only the default, unstructured case.  
   *Why unresolved:* The data come exclusively from a social platform with no shared objectives. Whether similar patterns of generic, off‑topic output appear when agents have explicit tasks and structured interaction protocols has not been tested.  
   *Resolution evidence:* Comparative studies applying the same lexical, semantic, and LLM‑judge metrics to a diverse set of task‑oriented multi‑agent benchmarks (e.g., coding, planning, debate), correlating interaction quality with task success metrics.

3. **Long‑term evolution of agent interactions**  
   *Basis:* “The dataset covers a few weeks. Longer‑term dynamics—whether agents adapt, improve, or degrade over time—remain unstudied.”  
   *Why unresolved:* The analysis is cross‑sectional over a 3‑week snapshot. No longitudinal data exists to track how agent populations and their interaction quality change as they accumulate experience or as the community evolves.  
   *Resolution evidence:* Longitudinal analysis of a stable agent platform, tracking cohort‑level and platform‑wide metrics (specificity, saturation, spam/off‑topic rates) over multiple months, potentially correlating with changes in agent designs or platform features.

4. **Causes of low nested reply rate**  
   *Basis:* “The platform affords threaded conversation, but agents almost never use it… We cannot fully disentangle the effects of interaction structure from agent selection.” They also find nested replies are more engaged but rare.  
   *Why unresolved:* Observational data cannot separate whether agents avoid replies because of how the platform is built, because of their own design choices, or because of a fundamental LLM bias toward independent generation. The causal effect of structure on quality is unknown.  
   *Resolution evidence:* Randomized controlled trials where agent groups are assigned different interaction interfaces (e.g., reply nudges, reply‑only mode) or incentives, measuring the resulting change in nested reply frequency and associated shifts in specificity, information gain, and LLM‑judged responsiveness.

## Limitations
- Exact compression function (e.g., gzip, zstd) and parameters for Self‑NCD and compression IG are unspecified, potentially affecting results for short texts.
- The LLM‑as‑judge prompt, rating rubric, and output format are undisclosed, limiting reproducibility of spam/off‑topic classifications and responsiveness scores.
- Findings are based on Moltbook’s flat comment structure; it is unclear whether similar patterns hold on platforms with enforced threading or different incentive structures.

## Confidence
- **Mechanism 1 (diverse but irrelevant output):** High
- **Mechanism 2 (rapid information saturation):** High
- **Mechanism 3 (flat interaction due to format):** Medium‑High

## Next Checks
1. **Compression robustness:** Re‑compute Self‑NCD and compression IG using alternative compressors (zstd, bzip2) and varying compression levels; verify that high self‑variation and saturation decay persist.
2. **Judge reproducibility:** Implement a standardized LLM‑as‑judge protocol with explicit definitions and structured JSON output; run it with two independent models (e.g., Claude‑Sonnet and GPT‑4) on a stratified sample; report Cohen’s κ and compare category distributions.
3. **Threading intervention:** Simulate a protocol where agents must reply to an existing comment (randomly chosen) instead of the post; measure changes in nested reply rate, lexical/semantic specificity, and saturation slope to directly test Mechanism 3.
