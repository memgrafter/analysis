# REDNET-ML: A Multi-Sensor Machine Learning Pipeline for Harmful Algal Bloom Risk Detection Along the Omani Coast
## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2603.04181
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.04181
- **Reference Count:** 20
- **Primary Result:** A multi-sensor fusion pipeline achieved pooled AUROC=0.842 and AUPRC=0.727 under strict non-leaky cross-validation, with precision≈0.682 at recall≈0.605.

## Executive Summary
This study presents REDNET-ML, an operational machine learning pipeline designed to detect harmful algal bloom (HAB) risk along the Omani coast. The system fuses multi-sensor data—including spectral and texture features from Sentinel-2, ocean color and thermal data from MODIS, and evidence scores from object detectors—using a CatBoost classifier. To prevent data leakage, splits are enforced by sensor group and temporal hold-out. Key results demonstrate strong predictive performance in cross-validation, but significant temporal distribution drift is identified for 2025, necessitating future threshold recalibration. A risk-field viewer is developed to support operator exploration of predicted risk.

## Method Summary
The pipeline constructs features from three sources: Sentinel-2 (spectral bands, texture indices), MODIS (chlorophyll-a, sea surface temperature), and object detector outputs (Faster R-CNN, SSD MobileNet) applied to satellite imagery to generate evidence score summaries. These features are fused within a CatBoost model. Critical to the methodology is the use of "group-safe" and temporal splits during cross-validation to ensure no information leakage from future or correlated sensor data. Labels combine trusted in-situ reports with weaker, mined labels from news and social media. Model outputs are calibrated risk probabilities.

## Key Results
- Cross-validation yields a pooled AUROC of 0.842 and AUPRC of 0.727, with precision ≈0.682 at recall ≈0.605.
- Detector benchmarking selected Faster R-CNN and SSD MobileNet for evidence generation, as YOLOv8 provided no material ensemble gain.
- Temporal drift analysis for 2025 indicates severe distribution shifts (per-feature PSI 1.4–5.2, KS 0.44–0.67), implying current decision thresholds will require recalibration.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The system's effectiveness stems from fusing complementary environmental signals (optical, thermal, textural) that capture different HAB precursors. Strict non-leaky validation ensures performance estimates are realistic for operational deployment where future data is unseen. The use of CatBoost handles heterogeneous feature types and potential interactions robustly. Evidence generation via detectors provides spatially explicit "bloom-like" pattern scores, adding a layer of pattern recognition beyond standard ocean color indices.

## Foundational Learning
*   **Non-Leaky Temporal & Group Splits:** Prevents overfitting to temporal autocorrelation and sensor co-location, ensuring validation simulates operational forecasting.
    *   *Quick Check:* Verify that all samples from a given date or satellite pass are confined to either train or test folds.
*   **Multi-Sensor Feature Fusion:** Integrates disparate data sources (medium-resolution optical, coarse thermal, detected patterns) to capture multi-faceted HAB drivers.
    *   *Quick Check:* Ablate each sensor source (Sentinel-2, MODIS, Detectors) to quantify marginal contribution to AUROC/AUPRC.
*   **Probability Calibration:** Post-processing of CatBoost outputs to produce reliable risk probabilities for threshold-based decision-making.
    *   *Quick Check:* Assess calibration curves (reliability diagrams) on a held-out temporal set.
*   **Distribution Shift Monitoring (PSI/KS):** Uses Population Stability Index (PSI) and Kolmogorov-Smirnov (KS) tests to detect feature drift between training and future operational data.
    *   *Quick Check:* Compute PSI/KS for each feature between final training data and the most recent operational period.
*   **Evidence Score Summaries:** Reduces high-dimensional detector outputs (e.g., bounding box counts, confidences) into robust, low-dimensional features for fusion.
    *   *Quick Check:* Correlate summary statistics (e.g., max confidence, count above threshold) with label presence in a trusted subset.

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component Map:**
Raw Satellite Inputs (Sentinel-2, MODIS) -> Feature Extraction (Spectral/Texture, Ocean Color/Thermal) -> Detector Inference (Faster R-CNN, SSD MobileNet) -> Evidence Summarization -> Feature Union -> CatBoost Model -> Calibrated Risk Probability -> Risk-Field Viewer

**Critical Path:** The most computationally intensive and latency-determining path is: Sentinel-2 & MODIS ingestion -> Detector inference on Sentinel-2 -> Evidence summarization -> Feature concatenation -> CatBoost prediction.

**Design Tradeoffs:** 
*   *Accuracy vs. Speed:* Using two heavy detectors (Faster R-CNN, SSD MobileNet) improves evidence robustness but increases inference latency; operational deployment may require model distillation or selective triggering.
*   *Sensitivity vs. Specificity:* The fusion model's threshold (`τ_action`) trades off false alarms vs. missed events; calibration is sensitive to label noise and drift.
*   *Complexity vs. Interpretability:* CatBoost offers performance but is less interpretable than a linear model; SHAP values are used for post-hoc explanation.

**Failure Signatures:**
1.  **High Feature PSI/KS:** Indicates the current model is operating on data with a different distribution than trained on, likely causing degraded performance.
2.  **Sharp Drop in Precision at Fixed Recall:** Suggests overfitting to spurious correlations in the training label set or unanticipated drift.
3.  **Spatially Incoherent Risk Fields:** The viewer output shows noisy, "salt-and-pepper" risk patterns not aligned with known oceanographic features, indicating poor generalization.

**3 First Experiments:**
1.  **Temporal Hold-Out Validation:** Train on 2018–2023 data, predict on withheld 2024 data with *in-situ* confirmation labels to measure real-world AUROC/ AUPRC and calibration error.
2.  **Ablation of Evidence Detectors:** Remove evidence score summaries entirely and retrain; quantify the drop in performance (ΔAUROC, ΔAUPRC) to justify operational cost of running detectors.
3.  **Baseline Simplicity Comparison:** Train a logistic regression or single-sensor (e.g., MODIS chlorophyll alone) CatBoost model. Compare performance to the full fusion to establish a complexity/benefit baseline.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
### Open Question 1
- Question: How can threshold calibration be automated to sustain operational reliability under significant temporal drift (PSI up to 5.2, KS up to 0.67)?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] Drift analysis (page 9) shows high PSI/KS for 2025, and the Conclusions (page 10) state that ACTION thresholds may require periodic recalibration, but no adaptive method is proposed.
- Why unresolved: The system currently refits thresholds via quantile matching to legacy rates, which may not generalize to new distributions; the paper identifies drift but does not provide an automated recalibration mechanism.
- What evidence would resolve it: Develop and validate a drift-triggered recalibration procedure that uses PSI/KS metrics to update τwatch and τaction in deployed settings, then evaluate stability on future hold-out years.

### Open Question 2
- Question: Would a more diverse set of object detectors or alternative training regimes (e.g., semi-supervised) improve evidence feature quality under sparse/noisy labels?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] Detector benchmarking (page 8) only retained Faster R-CNN and SSD MobileNet after finding YOLOv8 provided “no material ensemble gain”; the evaluation considered a narrow set of supervised detectors.
- Why unresolved: The evidence-generation step may be limited by the chosen detector architectures and training paradigms; other design choices could yield more robust score summaries for fusion.
- What evidence would resolve it: Systematically compare a wider range of detectors (e.g., transformer-based, ensemble varieties) and training strategies (e.g., weak supervision) using region-level metrics, then measure downstream impact on fusion AUROC/AUPRC.

### Open Question 3
- Question: What is the relative impact of label noise versus label sparsity on the fusion model’s performance and calibration?
- Basis in paper: [inferred] The label strategy (page 4) merges trusted and weak/mined labels, acknowledging noise, but no ablation isolates the effect of noise from sparsity.
- Why unresolved: Without disentangling these factors, it is unclear whether performance is bottlenecked by noisy weak labels or simply insufficient positive examples.
- What evidence would resolve it: Conduct controlled experiments where trusted labels are progressively contaminated with synthetic noise or subsampled to vary sparsity, tracking changes in AUROC/AUPRC, calibration curves, and SHAP stability.

### Open Question 4
- Question: Does the risk-field viewer materially enhance operators’ situational awareness and decision-making compared to existing HAB monitoring practices?
- Basis in paper: [inferred] The viewer is introduced (page 4) as supporting operational exploration, but no user-centered evaluation or usage metrics are reported.
- Why unresolved: Operational utility is assumed but untested; the real-world impact on alert response times, false alarm reduction, or mitigation actions remains unknown.
- What evidence would resolve it: Perform a user study or retrospective analysis where operators use the viewer alongside traditional tools, measuring outcomes like time-to-action, decision confidence, and accuracy in identifying high-risk events.

## Limitations
- **Temporal Generalizability:** Reported performance is from cross-validation; real-time operational precision/recall under recorded 2025 drift remains unevaluated.
- **Operational Deployment State:** The pipeline is not yet validated in a live monitoring setting with Omani authorities; user adoption and integration challenges are unassessed.
- **Benchmark Scope:** Detector selection lacks comparison to other HAB-specific or ultra-lightweight models suitable for edge computing on resource-constrained operational systems.
