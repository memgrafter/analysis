# Thinking Makes LLM Agents Introverted: How Mandatory Thinking Can Backfire in User-Engaged Agents

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID**: 2602.07796
- **Source URL**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.07796
- **Reference count**: 40
- **Primary result**: Mandatory chain-of-thought reasoning reduces information disclosure and degrades task performance in user-engaged LLM agents; an information-disclosure prompting (InfoDis) add-on can partially recover performance.

## Executive Summary
This paper explores the impact of mandatory thinking (e.g., chain-of-thought prompting) on the performance of LLM agents designed for interactive user engagement. Through a large-scale study across seven LLMs, three interactive benchmarks, and two thinking instantiations, the authors demonstrate that requiring explicit reasoning consistently harms agent effectiveness, with drops of up to 37% on certain benchmarks. Analysis reveals that thinking induces an "introverted" behavior: agents produce shorter responses with significantly less information disclosure, weakening communication and causing failures. To counteract this, the authors propose an information-disclosure prompting (InfoDis) add-on that explicitly instructs agents to be transparent. InfoDis yields reliable improvements, such as +2.91% for GPT-4o on τ-Retail and +4.00% for DeepSeek-V3.1 on τ-Airline. The findings highlight the importance of transparency in the design of reasoning agents for real-world interactive applications.

## Method Summary
The authors evaluated seven large language models (including GPT-5, Gemini-2.5-Pro, GPT-4o, DeepSeek-V3.1) on three interactive benchmarks: TS-Phone, τ-Retail, and τ-Airline. They implemented mandatory thinking in two forms: Thinking-as-a-Function (a separate reasoning step) and Thinking-as-a-Prefix (reasoning prepended to the response). To quantify the impact on communication, they introduced a response taxonomy measuring information disclosure via response length and keyword presence. Additionally, they performed case studies to trace failure propagation. The InfoDis add-on was then applied to prompts to explicitly encourage transparency, and its effects were measured against baselines.

## Key Results
- Mandatory thinking reduced agent performance across all models; e.g., GPT-5’s Pass@1 dropped by 6.09% on TS-Phone, and Gemini-2.5-Pro fell by 37% on τ-Retail.
- Agents exhibited "introverted" behavior under mandatory thinking: responses became shorter and contained significantly less information disclosure, as measured by the taxonomy.
- The InfoDis prompting add-on improved outcomes in multiple settings: +2.91% for GPT-4o on τ-Retail and +4.00% for DeepSeek-V3.1 on τ-Airline.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
Mandatory thinking redirects the model’s focus toward internal reasoning at the expense of generating informative outward-facing responses. In user-engaged scenarios, the agent must communicate relevant details to progress the task; however, the imposed reasoning step causes the model to prioritize its internal thought, resulting in truncated, less detailed responses. This reduction in information disclosure weakens the agent-user dialogue, leading to misunderstandings, incomplete task execution, and ultimately lower performance metrics. The InfoDis add-on counteracts this by explicitly instructing the model to be transparent, thereby restoring communication quality and improving outcomes.

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component Map**:  
User Input -> (Optional Thinking Step) -> Response Generator -> Agent Output  

**Critical Path**:  
1. Agent receives user utterance.  
2. If mandatory thinking is enabled, the model first generates a reasoning trace (either as a function call or as a prefix).  
3. The final response is produced by conditioning on the user input and (if present) the reasoning.  
4. The response is sent to the user.

**Design Tradeoffs**:  
- Mandatory thinking improves traceability and internal consistency but can reduce informativeness and increase latency.  
- InfoDis adds transparency at the risk of over-constraining the model’s language, but it consistently recovers performance.  
- Thinking-as-a-Prefix may be simpler to implement but can interfere with the generation process more than the functional approach.

**Failure Signatures**:  
- Abnormally short responses (< threshold length).  
- Absence of domain-specific keywords expected in a successful interaction.  
- Repeated user follow-up questions seeking clarification.  
- Lower Pass@1 scores coupled with reduced disclosure metrics.

**First Three Experiments**:  
1. Baseline evaluation: Run each LLM on the three interactive benchmarks without mandatory thinking, recording Pass@1 and disclosure metrics.  
2. Mandatory thinking introduction: Implement both Thinking-as-a-Function and Thinking-as-a-Prefix, measure changes in performance and disclosure relative to baseline.  
3. InfoDis intervention: Apply the InfoDis add-on on top of mandatory thinking, compare results to step 2 to quantify improvement.

## Limitations
- Benchmarks limited to three domains; may not generalize to other interactive tasks.  
- Disclosure metrics are simplistic (length, keywords) lacking semantic depth.  
- Evaluation likely used automated agents; missing real human feedback on transparency.

## Confidence
- Mandatory thinking harms performance: **High**  
- Harm due to reduced disclosure (“introverted” behavior): **Medium**  
- InfoDis reliably improves outcomes: **Medium**

## Next Checks
1. Test on additional interactive benchmarks (e.g., WebShop, ALFWorld) and with other reasoning methods (self-consistency, tree-of-thought).  
2. Conduct a human subject study (n≥50) comparing agents with/without mandatory thinking and with InfoDis, measuring satisfaction and task success.  
3. Perform manual annotation of agent responses to identify which disclosure dimensions (completeness, relevance) are most affected by thinking and whether InfoDis addresses them.
