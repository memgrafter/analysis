# Beyond the Resumé: A Rubric-Aware Automatic Interview System for Information Elicitation

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2603.01775  
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.01775  
- **Reference count:** 18  
- **Primary result:** An LLM-based two-agent system maintains probabilistic beliefs over rubric-defined traits, achieving ~3× reduction in belief change (TV distance) over 12 interview turns and 76.1% accuracy in recovering applicant archetypes in simulation.

## Executive Summary
This paper tackles the inefficiency of costly expert interviews in hiring, where early resume screening often leads to premature decisions. The authors propose an automatic interview system powered by two LLM agents: an *interviewer* that generates questions to elicit evidence, and a *judge* that maintains and updates a calibrated probabilistic belief state over predefined latent traits (Knowledge, Skills, Abilities) defined by a structured rubric. The judge uses sequential Bayesian updating (via prompting) after each turn to refine its belief based on the resume and the full dialogue history. The system is evaluated through simulation, where belief convergence is measured by the reduction in total variation distance between successive belief states. Results show meaningful convergence, with belief changes decreasing by approximately threefold over 12 turns, and the final belief profile correctly matching the simulated applicant’s archetype in over three-quarters of cases.

## Method Summary
The core method is a two-LLM architecture operating over a rubric. First, a *Judge* LLM is prompted with the rubric, the candidate’s resume, and the full conversation history (or previous belief) to output a normalized probability distribution over ordinal levels (e.g., low/medium/high) for each rubric dimension. This judge is calibrated via metamorphic testing to ensure updates are stable and responsive to evidence. Second, an *Interviewer* LLM is prompted with the rubric and conversation history to generate the next question, aiming to elicit information relevant to the rubric dimensions. The system is evaluated in simulation: an *Applicant* LLM, conditioned on a hidden archetype (profile of true levels) and guardrails, responds to questions. Over multiple turns, the judge’s belief state is tracked; convergence is measured by the average turn-to-turn total variation distance, and final accuracy is the percentage where the maximum a posteriori belief profile matches the true archetype via nearest-neighbor lookup.

## Key Results
- Belief updates decreased by approximately **3×** over 12 interview turns, indicating convergence.
- The final belief correctly recovered the applicant’s latent archetype in **76.1%** of simulated profiles.
- The calibrated judge passed most metamorphic tests (e.g., >85% on invariance and directional updates), but showed inertia toward medium probability mass.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- **Claim:** The judge LLM maintains and updates a calibrated probabilistic belief state over rubric-defined latent traits (Knowledge, Skills, Abilities).
- **Mechanism:** After each interview turn, the judge conditions its posterior distribution for each rubric dimension on the cumulative evidence (resume + all prior Q&A). This is a form of sequential Bayesian updating implemented via LLM prompting, where the judge is instructed to output normalized probability masses over ordinal levels.
- **Core assumption:** The judge’s posteriors become increasingly concentrated on the applicant’s true latent level as more relevant evidence is observed. The LLM can reliably interpret conversational evidence in light of rubric anchors.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract] "maintains a calibrated probabilistic belief state over rubric-defined latent traits"
  - [section 2.2] Formal definition of belief state `Bt = {p(z_d | x0:t, R)}` and the judge’s role in initialization and updating.
  - [section 4.4] Demonstrates decreasing TV distance between successive belief states, indicating concentration.
  - [corpus] Weak external validation; calibration is tested via metamorphic tests on simulated data, not against human expert beliefs.
- **Break condition:** If the applicant provides ambiguous, irrelevant, or contradictory evidence, or if the judge’s prompt fails to enforce proper conditioning on prior belief (as in the Independent judge variant), updates may not reflect true latent levels, causing non-convergent or oscillating belief states.

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim:** The interviewer LLM is guided (implicitly or explicitly) to ask questions that elicit information reducing uncertainty in the judge’s belief state.
- **Mechanism:** The interviewer’s policy (e.g., Belief Unaware) generates turn-by-turn questions without direct access to the judge’s current belief. Its effectiveness stems from being prompted with the rubric, encouraging questions that target rubric dimensions. In simulation, this causes the judge’s belief to change meaningfully per turn (high initial Δ_t), which then diminishes as information saturates.
- **Core assumption:** An LLM, conditioned solely on the rubric and conversation history, will generate questions that systematically probe the applicant’s KSAs as defined by the rubric. The rubric structure provides a scaffold for generating information-rich questions.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract] "LLM interviewer to update belief"
  - [section 2.3] Defines convergence via reduction in TV distance between successive belief states.
  - [section 4.3] Comparison of interviewer policies shows all cause belief change, with rubric-aware variants performing better, indicating the rubric’s role in guiding elicitation.
  - [corpus] Related work like "LLMREI" and "Interview AI-ssistant" explores LLM-driven elicitation but not calibrated belief tracking; this paper’s mechanism is distinct in closing the loop with a probabilistic judge.
- **Break condition:** If the interviewer generates shallow, repetitive, or off-rubric questions (as measured by low Δ_t and poor archetype recovery), the evidence may not meaningfully reduce uncertainty. The paper’s own "Shallow Unaware" policy would be a break condition.

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim:** The rubric’s design (non-evidentiary, falsifiable, internally consistent anchors) enables the judge to map natural language evidence to discrete, calibrated levels.
- **Mechanism:** Rubric anchors serve as semantic boundaries. The judge maps applicant statements to these anchors to justify belief updates. The calibration process (metamorphic testing) ensures the judge responds to clear evidence with >ε shift (ε=0.04) and respects invariance under irrelevant/repetitive evidence.
- **Core assumption:** Well-formed rubric anchors allow an LLM judge to make consistent, auditable inferences about KSA levels from conversational text, and these inferences are probabilistic and malleable with new evidence.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [section 2.1] Lists anchor requirements: non-evidentiary, falsifiable, internally consistent.
  - [section 3] Calibration results (Table 1) show PBA judge passes most tests, especially invariant and targeted increase/decrease, indicating responsive and stable updates.
  - [section 4.5] Misclassifications often collapse to "medium," suggesting inertia at middle levels—a foreseeable property of ordinal scales and LLM calibration.
  - [corpus] No direct corpus evidence; rubric design principles are derived from HR literature (Wooten, 1993; Shippmann et al., 2000) but not empirically validated in this study.
- **Break condition:** Poorly defined anchors (e.g., subjective terms, mixed KSAs) lead to noisy, inconsistent, or biased belief updates, failing metamorphic tests (low pass rate on R_inv, R↑, R↓).

## Foundational Learning
- **Concept: Probabilistic Belief State (Bayesian Updating)**
  - *Why needed here:* The core innovation is maintaining a distribution over latent traits, not a point estimate. Understanding how evidence sequentially updates a probability distribution is key to interpreting TV convergence and belief stability.
  - *Quick check question:* If after three turns, the judge’s belief for dimension *d* is [0.1, 0.7, 0.2] at low/medium/high, and a new piece of evidence strongly supports "high," what might the updated distribution look like? What TV distance would you expect between the two states?

- **Concept: Rubric-Based Assessment (KSAs)**
  - *Why needed here:* The system operationalizes hiring evaluation via a rubric of Knowledge, Skills, Abilities. The rubric’s structure (ordinal levels, anchors) defines the hypothesis space for the judge. Misunderstanding rubric design leads to poor calibration.
  - *Quick check question:* Why must a rubric anchor be "internally consistent"? Give an example of a bad anchor that mixes orthogonal KSAs and explain why that’s problematic for belief updating.

- **Concept: Metamorphic Testing for LLM Calibration**
  - *Why needed here:* With no oracle labels ("true" belief updates), the authors use metamorphic relations (e.g., "adding clear high evidence should increase belief in high") to test judge behavior. This is a practical method to validate probabilistic LLM systems without ground truth.
  - *Quick check question:* If you inject a turn that provides "low evidence" for a dimension, what metamorphic test (R↓(d,ℓ)) would you define? What δ_d(ℓ) value would indicate a pass?

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map:**
  Rubric Definition Module -> Judge LLM -> Belief Tracker (logs B_t, computes Δ_t)
                         ↗
  Interviewer LLM --------
                         ↘
                   Simulation Harness (Applicant LLM) -> Frontend (chat + belief panel)

- **Critical path:**
  1. **Initialization:** Load rubric. Judge processes resume-only input to produce B₀ (uniform prior or resume-informed).
  2. **Turn loop (t=1..T):**
     a. Interviewer generates question Q_t based on R, transcript up to t-1, and maybe B_{t-1}.
     b. Applicant (human or simulated) provides answer A_t.
     c. Judge ingests (Q_t, A_t) and full history x0:t, produces new belief B_t and justification.
     d. Tracker logs B_t, computes Δ_t, updates audit trail.
  3. **Termination:** After T turns or convergence criterion (e.g., Δ_t < threshold). Output final belief profile and transcript.

- **Design tradeoffs:**
  - **Judge Prompt Design:** PBA vs. Independent. PBA is more stable but may cause inertia; Independent is more reactive but riskier jumps.
  - **Rubric Granularity (L):** More levels (L>3) allow finer-grained belief but harder to calibrate; fewer levels may be too coarse.
  - **Token Management:** Using full transcript each turn (as in PBA) increases cost; could use summary or retrieval but may lose nuance.
  - **Interviewer Policy:** Belief-aware interviewer (not fully realized in paper) could optimize questions for information gain but adds complexity.

- **Failure signatures:**
  - **Stagnant belief:** Δ_t ≈ 0 from early turns → judge ignores new evidence or questions are irrelevant.
  - **Uniform posteriors:** Judge fails to concentrate from B₀ → rubric anchors unclear or judge prompt defective.
  - **High variance in archetype recovery:** In simulation, recovery << 76.1% → interviewer not eliciting discriminative evidence, or judge misinterpreting.
  - **Justification incoherence:** Judge’s rationale doesn’t align with belief shift → prompt engineering failure, breaks auditability.
  - **Scope creep:** Interviewer asks about non-rubric topics → prompt not sufficiently constrained by rubric.

- **First 3 experiments:**
  1. **Judge Calibration via Metamorphic Tests:** Implement all 540 tests (Section 3.2) for your rubric/judge. Verify pass rates ≈ Table 1. Tune judge prompt until R_inv and R↑/R↓ meet thresholds (e.g., >85% pass).
  2. **Convergence Stress Test:** Run full simulation (180 profiles) with your calibrated judge and Belief Unaware interviewer. Plot average Δ_t over 12 turns. Expect ~3× reduction from t=0→T. If not, check for Applicant-Hacking (Section 4.2) or weak interviewer.
  3. **Archetype Recovery Breakdown:** Analyze confusion matrix (like Figure 5). Identify which rubric domains/dimensions cause misclassifications (e.g., "medium" inertia). Revise rubric anchors for problematic dimensions to improve separability.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- **Open Question 1:** How can the interviewer policy be actively guided by the belief state to maximize information gain per turn, as suggested by Bayesian Experimental Design?  
  *Basis in paper:* [explicit] The authors state in the Conclusion and Future Work: "we would like to study how to meaningfully incorporate belief in an interviewer policy using real data. For example, by using Bayesian Experimental Design to maximise information gain each turn."  
  *Why unresolved:* Current interviewer policies (Belief Aware and Unaware) showed minimal performance differences in simulation, indicating the belief state is not effectively utilized for question selection. Without active incorporation, the system may not optimally reduce uncertainty in the rubric.

- **Open Question 2:** How does integrating additional candidate data sources (e.g., LinkedIn profiles, portfolios) affect belief initialization, convergence, and overall system accuracy?  
  *Basis in paper:* [explicit] Future Work proposes: "we would like to extend the system to ingest not only resumes, but also LinkedIn profiles, digital portfolios, and other sources provided by a candidate in a job application."  
  *Why unresolved:* The system currently initializes belief from resumes only. Additional sources could provide richer evidence but may introduce noise, require new preprocessing, and interact unpredictably with the interview process, potentially altering belief updates.

- **Open Question 3:** To what extent do LLM-simulated applicants capture real human behavior, and how does system performance degrade when evaluated with actual human applicants?  
  *Basis in paper:* [inferred] Limitations note: "We expect that our simulated applicants differ from human applicants... with respect to honesty, integrity, communication-ability, English-proficiency, conversation style, personality, etc." This highlights a key assumption about simulation validity.  
  *Why unresolved:* Simulation used guardrails to prevent applicant hacking, but real humans may exhibit greater variability in responses, honesty, and engagement, potentially leading to slower belief convergence, reduced accuracy, or new failure modes not captured in simulation.

- **Open Question 4:** Why does the judge show inertia towards medium belief levels, and how can the belief update mechanism be calibrated to improve discrimination between medium and extreme KSA levels?  
  *Basis in paper:* [inferred] Results indicate the PBA judge "exhibit less inclination to remove probability mass from medium," and misclassifications often collapse to medium archetypes, as seen in Appendix B's confusion matrix.  
  *Why unresolved:* This inertia may stem from uniform priors or judge instructions favoring stability, causing underconfidence in extreme assignments. It systematically biases recovery towards average profiles, limiting the system's ability to identify high- or low-ability candidates.

## Limitations
- All validation is via simulation with LLM-simulated applicants; no human-subject studies test real-world efficacy or alignment with expert hiring judgments.
- Exact prompt templates and rubric anchor definitions are not provided in the paper text, blocking exact replication; they are only in the released code.
- Results depend on a specific, unreleased model version (`gpt-5-2025-08-07`); performance with other models or parameters may vary significantly.

## Confidence
- **Judge belief updating:** High (strong internal evidence: formal Bayesian framing, metamorphic test pass rates, consistent TV convergence)
- **Interviewer-driven convergence:** Medium (supported by ablation comparisons but lacks direct measurement of question informativeness)
- **Rubric design principles:** Medium (anchors satisfy theoretical criteria and pass metamorphic tests but lack empirical validation against human KSA ratings)
