# GroundCount: Grounding Vision-Language Models with Object Detection for Mitigating Counting Hallucinations

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2603.10978
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.10978
- **Reference count:** 40
- **Primary result:** Prompt-based grounding with YOLO detections improves VLM counting accuracy by up to 6.6 percentage points (to 81.3% on Ovis2.5-2B) and reduces inference time by 22% for stronger models.

## Executive Summary
Vision-Language Models (VLMs) exhibit severe hallucinations in object counting tasks, with accuracy (64.0–74.7%) far below other visual reasoning abilities. This paper identifies the root cause as a fundamental limitation in spatial-semantic integration. The proposed **GroundCount** framework addresses this by augmenting VLM prompts with explicit, structured spatial grounding derived from an external Object Detection Model (YOLO). The core method appends a textual list of detected objects (class, index, 3×3 grid position, and optionally confidence) to the user’s query. This training-free approach yields consistent accuracy gains (6.2–7.5pp) across most evaluated VLM architectures and, for stronger models, reduces inference time by preventing hallucination-driven reasoning loops. The work demonstrates that offloading spatial tallying to a symbolic external module is highly effective, while deeper feature-level fusion via fine-tuning provides smaller, inconsistent improvements.

## Method Summary
GroundCount introduces two primary strategies for integrating ODM outputs into VLMs. **Plan A (Prompt Augmentation)** is a training-free method: the input image is first processed by YOLOv13x; its detections are formatted into a structured string (e.g., `"person1 upper-left:0.92; person2 middle-center:0.87..."`) and appended to the original question before being fed to the VLM. The VLM’s vision encoder still processes the raw image, while its language decoder reasons over the augmented prompt. **Plan B/C (Feature-level Fusion)** involve inserting a lightweight MLP-based network between the VLM’s vision encoder patch tokens and the ODM’s CNN feature maps, requiring joint fine-tuning on a COCO-derived spatial description dataset. Ablations reveal that omitting confidence scores often helps, the benefit of 3×3 grid positional encoding is model-dependent (helping stronger VLMs but harming weaker ones), and high detection confidence thresholds are crucial to avoid noise. The detection ordering follows left-to-right horizontally, then lower-to-upper vertically.

## Key Results
- Baseline counting accuracy on the PhD benchmark is low (64.0–74.7%) compared to other VLM capabilities, confirming systematic counting hallucinations.
- Prompt augmentation (Plan A) boosts accuracy by up to 6.6 percentage points, achieving 81.3% on Ovis2.5-2B, with consistent gains (6.2–7.5pp) on four of five VLM architectures.
- For stronger models, prompt augmentation reduces average inference time by 22% by curbing hallucination-driven long reasoning traces.
- Confidence scores from the ODM frequently act as noise, with removal improving performance for four of five models.
- Positional encoding via a 3×3 grid benefits the two strongest baseline models (Molmo2-4B, Ovis2.5-2B) but degrades three weaker models, indicating architectural incompatibility.
- Feature-level fusion (Plans B/C) through fine-tuning yields smaller and less consistent gains than the simple prompt-based approach.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- **Claim:** Explicit textual grounding via ODM outputs reduces VLM counting hallucinations by providing structured spatial priors that the VLM can directly reference, bypassing its internal spatial-semantic integration weaknesses.
- **Mechanism:** The VLM receives a prompt containing a structured list of detected objects (class, index, 3×3 grid position, confidence). This external symbolic representation offloads the instance discrimination and spatial tallying burden from the VLM’s attention mechanism to the ODM. The VLM’s language decoder then performs reasoning over this explicit set rather than trying to extract and count object representations from its own vision encoder’s global patch tokens.
- **Core assumption:** VLMs are better at processing and reasoning over discrete, explicit textual statements about object instances and their locations than they are at implicitly deriving the same information from distributed visual feature representations.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - Abstract: "explicit symbolic grounding via structured prompts outperforms implicit feature fusion"
  - Section IV-A: "We first pass the input image through YOLOv13x to obtain bounding boxes... which are then converted into natural language prompts."
  - Section V-A: "Prompt Augmentation (Plan A) achieves the highest counting accuracy at 81.3%... Plan A also reduces average inference time... This counterintuitive result stems from VLMs generating fewer tokens when provided with explicit grounding information."
  - Corpus: Neighboring papers on prompt-induced hallucination mechanisms support that structured external prompting can steer model behavior.
- **Break condition:** The mechanism fails if the VLM’s architecture is incompatible with processing the structured prompt format (e.g., InternVL3.5-1B degraded performance) or if the ODM’s detections are too noisy (low-confidence threshold).

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim:** Confidence scores from the ODM are not universally beneficial; they often introduce noise that distracts the VLM, reducing counting accuracy.
- **Mechanism:** The VLM may over-interpret or misinterpret the confidence values as semantic priors (e.g., associating higher confidence with more salient objects) rather than as indicators of detection certainty. This can lead to incorrect weighting of instances during counting. Removing these values simplifies the input to pure class/position/index tuples.
- **Core assumption:** VLMs lack a calibrated understanding of ODM confidence scores and treat them as additional, potentially misleading, textual features.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - Section V-B: "Removing confidence values (NoConfidence) produces mixed results... four models (Molmo2-4B, R-4B, Qwen3-VL-2B, InternVL3.5-1B) actually improve by 0.1 to 4.8pp without confidence scores, suggesting that confidence values may introduce noise for certain VLM architectures."
  - Section V-B: "Confidence scores... introduce noise for most architectures and their removal improves performance in four of five evaluated models."
  - Corpus: Work like "DASH: Detection and Assessment of Systematic Hallucinations of VLMs" indicates hallucinations can be triggered by irrelevant or noisy contextual information.
- **Break condition:** The mechanism may not hold for models fine-tuned to interpret confidence values or for detectors with exceptionally well-calibrated scores (Molmo2-4B showed a slight drop without confidence).

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim:** Positional encoding (3×3 grid labels) provides critical spatial grounding that benefits stronger VLMs but harms weaker ones, indicating divergence in how architectures process and utilize external spatial priors.
- **Mechanism:** For stronger models (higher baseline counting accuracy), the grid encoding provides a crucial spatial scaffold that aligns with and enhances their existing (but flawed) spatial processing. For weaker models, the coarse, discretized encoding is either too ambiguous or conflicts with their more brittle internal spatial representations, leading to confusion and error.
- **Core assumption:** Different VLM architectures have varying capacities and pre-training biases for interpreting and integrating coarse, human-defined spatial symbolic representations.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - Section V-B: "Removing positional encoding (NoPosition) causes degradation in the two strongest baseline models: Molmo2-4B (1.8pp loss) and Ovis2.5-2B (0.6pp loss). However, the three weaker models—R-4B, Qwen3-VL-2B, and InternVL3.5-1B—paradoxically improve by 0.7 to 4.4pp without positional information."
  - Section V-D: "This divergence suggests that while counting hallucinations generally stem from fundamental limitations in spatial-semantic integration, the effectiveness of explicit grounding depends critically on architectural compatibility."
- **Break condition:** The benefit turns into harm when the VLM’s internal spatial representation scheme is incompatible with the specific grid discretization used or when the model’s parameters are insufficient to learn a proper mapping from the new symbolic input.

## Foundational Learning
- **Concept: Vision-Language Model (VLM) Architecture**
  - **Why needed:** The paper analyzes and augments specific VLMs (Ovis2.5-2B, Molmo2-4B, etc.). Understanding their typical dual-encoder (ViT + LLM) or unified transformer structure is necessary to grasp why their vision-to-language grounding fails for counting and why prompt-based vs. feature-level fusion have different outcomes.
  - **Quick check:** In a typical VLM like LLaVA, how do image patch tokens from a ViT get integrated with text tokens before being fed to the language model? What might go wrong in this integration for a task requiring exact instance counting?
- **Concept: Object Detection Model (ODM) Outputs**
  - **Why needed:** GroundCount’s core idea is to use ODM (YOLO) outputs as a grounding signal. One must understand what a standard ODM produces (class, bounding box, confidence) and how these can be transformed (spatial encoding, sequencing) into a textual prompt.
  - **Quick check:** If you have two detected bounding boxes for "person", how would you assign them a spatial label based on their center coordinates using a 3×3 grid? What ordering principle would you use to list them?
- **Concept: Spatial-Semantic Integration**
  - **Why needed:** The paper's central thesis is that VLM counting failures stem from a fundamental limitation in fusing spatial layout information (where objects are) with semantic category information (what objects are). This is the hypothesized root cause being addressed.
  - **Quick check:** Why might a VLM that can correctly answer "Is there a dog?" still fail at "How many dogs are there?" What additional cognitive (or computational) demand does counting impose?

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map:**  
  Input Image -> Object Detection Module (YOLOv13x) -> Prompt Constructor -> Augmented Textual Prompt -> VLM (Vision Encoder + Language Decoder) -> Output  
  *(Optional for Plans B/C: VLM Vision Encoder Patch Tokens + ODM CNN Features -> Fusion Network (MLP) -> Augmented Vision Features -> VLM)*
- **Critical path (Plan A - Prompt Augmentation):** Image --> ODM Inference --> Format Detections to Text --> Concatenate with User Prompt --> VLM Inference (Vision Encoder + Language Decoder on Augmented Prompt) --> Output. *No training required.*
- **Design tradeoffs:**
  - **Prompt vs. Fusion:** Prompting (A) is training-free, architecture-agnostic, and faster but relies on the VLM’s language reasoning skill. Fusion (B/C) aims for deeper integration but requires expensive fine-tuning, risks catastrophic forgetting, and shows inconsistent gains.
  - **Detection Threshold:** Higher thresholds (precision-focused) are crucial. Lower thresholds increase false positives, which the VLM must wastefully reason about, harming accuracy and increasing inference time dramatically.
  - **Information Granularity:** Including confidence scores often hurts. Simple class+position+index is more robust. Grid size (3×3) is a design choice; finer grids might help but could increase ambiguity for weaker VLMs.
  - **Architectural Compatibility:** The approach is not plug-and-play for *all* VLM architectures. Models with iterative reflection mechanisms may be disrupted by the static structured prompt.
- **Failure signatures:**
  - **Degraded accuracy with lower detection confidence threshold:** Sudden drop in accuracy + massive increase in token generation/inference time.
  - **Inverted benefit of positional encoding:** Accuracy *improves* when removing grid positions, indicating the model cannot use them and they act as noise.
  - **No inference time reduction:** For weaker models, the ODM prompt does not prevent reasoning loops; generation length stays high.
  - **Catastrophic degradation after fusion fine-tuning:** Accuracy drops below baseline, indicating destructive interference with pre-trained weights.
- **First 3 experiments:**
  1. **Establish Baseline Counting Fragility:** On the PhD benchmark's counting subset, evaluate the target VLM (e.g., Ovis2.5-2B) with greedy decoding. Confirm counting accuracy (64-75%) is significantly lower than object/attribute tasks. Also note inference time and output length patterns (hallucination loops manifest as long, repetitive reasoning traces).
  2. **Implement Plan A - Prompt Augmentation:** Integrate a YOLO inference call. Implement the 3×3 grid spatial encoder and left-to-right/lower-to-upper sorter. Format detections as "class index position" (omit confidence initially). Inject this into the VLM’s prompt. Rerun the same counting benchmark. Expect: **~6-7pp accuracy gain and reduced inference time for stronger models.**
  3. **Run Ablation Suite:** On the successful model from Exp 2, systematically vary: Remove positional encoding (only "class index"); add back confidence scores ("class index position: confidence"); lower detection confidence threshold (e.g., 0.3). Analyze changes in accuracy and average output tokens. Expect: position removal helps weaker models, hurts stronger models; confidence removal helps most models; low threshold severely harms all.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
### Open Question 1
- **Question:** Why do VLM architectures with iterative reflection mechanisms (e.g., InternVL3.5) exhibit degraded performance when provided with explicit object detection prompts, while other architectures improve?
- **Basis in paper:** The paper states: "one architecture exhibiting degraded performance due to incompatibility between its iterative reflection mechanisms and structured prompts" and "InternVL3.5’s architecture appears incompatible with explicit textual grounding, potentially due to its iterative reflection mechanisms being disrupted by structured prompts."
- **Why unresolved:** The hypothesis about iterative reflection disruption is speculative; no mechanistic analysis explains how structured prompts interfere with multi-step reasoning loops or whether alternative prompt formats could preserve benefits.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Controlled experiments comparing InternVL3.5’s internal attention patterns and reasoning trajectories with/without ODM prompts, possibly modifying prompt structure (e.g., integrating detections gradually) to test compatibility.

### Open Question 2
- **Question:** Do transformer-based object detection models (e.g., DETR, Grounding DINO) improve VLM counting accuracy more than CNN-based detectors (YOLO) by reducing representational gap between spatial features and ViT patch embeddings?
- **Basis in paper:** "Exploring transformer-based ODMs like DETR or Grounding DINO could reduce the representational gap" and "The fusion architecture’s underperformance likely stems from the fundamental representational gap between CNN spatial features and ViT global patch embeddings."
- **Why unresolved:** The paper only evaluates YOLO; it remains unknown whether transformer-based detectors align better with ViT representations and whether this yields better fusion or prompt-based augmentation.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Replicate GroundCount experiments with DETR/Grounding DINO as ODM, measuring counting accuracy, inference time, and comparing feature similarities between ODM outputs and VLM vision encoder representations.

### Open Question 3
- **Question:** Why does positional encoding (3×3 grid) in ODM prompts benefit stronger VLMs (Molmo2, Ovis2.5) but harm weaker ones (R-4B, Qwen3-VL, InternVL3.5)?
- **Basis in paper:** "Removing positional encoding (NoPosition) causes degradation in the two strongest baseline models... However, the three weaker models—paradoxically improve... suggesting these models struggle to correctly interpret spatial encodings."
- **Why unresolved:** The paper identifies the pattern but not the underlying cause—whether due to capacity limits, training data biases, or architectural differences in processing spatial tokens.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Analyze attention distributions over positional tokens in different VLMs; test alternative spatial encoding schemes (e.g., continuous coordinates, different grid granularities) across model sizes to isolate capacity vs. architectural factors.

### Open Question 4
- **Question:** Can extended pre-training of the fusion network (beyond 40k steps) on diverse datasets close the performance gap with prompt-based augmentation, or does the representational mismatch fundamentally limit feature-level fusion?
- **Basis in paper:** "More extensive pre-training may unlock greater potential" for fusion architectures, which underperformed prompt-based methods despite using cross-attention and FiLM.
- **Why unresolved:** The paper used a limited 40k-step training budget; it is unclear whether fusion performance plateaus or could approach/exceed prompt-based accuracy with more data/compute.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Train fusion architecture (Plan B.4) on larger-scale counting datasets (e.g., COCO with more diverse objects) for substantially longer (e.g., 200k+ steps), monitor accuracy curves, and compare to prompt-based upper bound.

## Limitations
- Evaluation restricted to the PhD benchmark and five specific VLM architectures, limiting generality.
- The choice of YOLO as the sole ODM and the fixed 3×3 grid encoding may not be optimal.
- The degradation in InternVL3.5-1B performance remains unexplained and may hide broader architectural incompatibilities.
- Potential dataset biases in PhD benchmark are not addressed.
- No mechanistic analysis (e.g., attention probing) to confirm the hypothesized spatial-semantic integration failure.
- Overhead of running a separate ODM is ignored in inference time claims.

## Confidence
- **Prompt augmentation improves accuracy and reduces inference time for strong VLMs:** High (consistent across multiple models in controlled ablations).
- **ODM confidence scores often act as noise:** Medium (mixed ablation results; majority of models improved without them, but one showed a slight drop).
- **Positional encoding benefit/harm is architecture-dependent:** Medium (clear pattern observed, but only five models tested; cause not isolated).
- **Counting hallucinations stem from fundamental spatial-semantic integration limitations:** Medium (systematic failure across models is demonstrated, but alternative causes not fully excluded).
- **Feature-level fusion gains are smaller and less consistent than prompting:** Medium (fusion underperformed in experiments, but limited training budget leaves uncertainty about ultimate potential).

## Next Checks
