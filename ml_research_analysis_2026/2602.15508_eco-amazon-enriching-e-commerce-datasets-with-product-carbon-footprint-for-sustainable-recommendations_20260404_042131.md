# Eco-Amazon: Enriching E-commerce Datasets with Product Carbon Footprint for Sustainable Recommendations

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2602.15508  
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.15508  
- **Reference count:** 29  
- **Primary result:** Eco-Amazon releases ~50K Amazon items enriched with LLM-estimated Product Carbon Footprint (PCF), enabling sustainable re-ranking that reduces average carbon footprint by up to ~50% with minimal accuracy loss.

## Executive Summary
The paper addresses the scarcity of item-level carbon footprint data in recommender system benchmarks, a key obstacle for sustainability research. It introduces Eco-Amazon, which augments three Amazon datasets (Electronics, Home & Kitchen, Clothing) with Product Carbon Footprint (PCF) estimates generated via a zero-shot LLM prompting framework that adheres to GHG Protocol and ISO standards. On a ground-truth set of 159 items with real Environmental Product Declarations (EPDs), the LLMs (GPT-o3-mini, Gemini-2.5-flash) achieve high ranking consistency (normalized Spearman >0.75, NDCG >0.84) despite significant absolute mean absolute errors (up to 153–284 kg CO2e), especially for low-impact products. In a re-ranking use case, integrating PCF as a weighting factor reduces the average carbon footprint of recommended items by up to ~50% (e.g., to 11–12 kg CO2e in Home & Kitchen) with minimal loss in recall, demonstrating a controllable trade-off between accuracy and sustainability. The authors release the enriched datasets (~50K items), a validation subset, and open-source estimation scripts to catalyze research on sustainable recommendations.

## Method Summary
The authors start from the Amazon Reviews 2023 datasets for three domains. After applying a 15-core filtering to ensure sufficient user–item interaction density and sampling to manageable sizes, they enrich each product with a PCF estimate. The enrichment uses a zero-shot prompting strategy: for each item, a metadata string (title, description, category, brand) is fed to an LLM (GPT-o3-mini or Gemini-2.5-flash) with a structured prompt (Box 1) that instructs the model to first check for official EPDs and, if absent, to reason from product attributes while strictly following GHG Protocol and ISO 14040/44 standards. Each item is estimated four times and the results averaged. The PCF scores are merged back into the item metadata to form the Eco-Amazon datasets. For the re-ranking use case, standard collaborative filtering models (BPR, LightGCN) are trained on the original interactions. At inference, a sustainability-aware score is computed as \( \text{SaS}(u,i) = (1-\alpha)\cdot\text{pred}(u,i) + \alpha\cdot\text{PCF}_{\text{norm}}(i) \), where \( \alpha \) controls the trade-off between relevance and low carbon footprint. The approach is evaluated using accuracy metrics (Recall, NDCG) and a sustainability metric (average CO2e of recommended items).

## Key Results
- LLMs achieve high ordinal ranking consistency on the ground-truth validation set: normalized Spearman >0.75 and NDCG >0.84, despite large absolute MAE (e.g., up to 284.97 kg CO2e in Home & Kitchen).
- Post-hoc re-ranking with PCF reduces the average carbon footprint of recommended items by up to ~50% (e.g., to 11–12 kg CO2e in Home & Kitchen) with minimal loss in recall; the trade-off is smoothly controllable via the \( \alpha \) parameter.
- The Eco-Amazon resource includes ~50K enriched items across three domains, a 159-item ground-truth validation set, and open-source estimation scripts, now publicly available to support reproducible research.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

**Mechanism 1: Zero-shot LLM Estimation of PCF**  
The authors design a zero-shot prompt that forces the LLM to simulate an LCA expert, adhering to GHG Protocol and ISO standards. The LLM either extracts official EPD data if available or reasons from product metadata (materials, manufacturing, use phase) to estimate cradle-to-grave CO2e emissions. This bridges unstructured e-commerce text to formal LCA output. The core assumption is that LLMs internalize sufficient domain knowledge to approximate emissions when constrained by standards. Evidence comes from the high ranking consistency on the ground-truth set (Spearman >0.75). The mechanism breaks if the LLM ignores standard constraints or if product metadata is too sparse/ambiguous, yielding arbitrary estimates.

**Mechanism 2: Post-hoc Re-ranking for Sustainability**  
A simple linear interpolation combines a base relevance score (\( \text{pred}(u,i) \)) with a normalized inverse PCF score. Parameter \( \alpha \) controls the trade-off: higher \( \alpha \) increases the weight on sustainability, biasing the list toward lower-emission items. This decouples the core recommendation model from the sustainability objective. The core assumption is that the ordinal PCF rankings (from Mechanism 1) are reliable enough to produce meaningful shifts. Evidence: up to 50% reduction in average PCF with minimal recall loss across domains. The mechanism fails if PCF estimates have poor ordinal consistency, leading to ineffective or counterproductive re-ranking.

**Mechanism 3: Dataset Enrichment as a Community Resource**  
By processing ~50k items from widely used Amazon benchmarks and releasing the enriched datasets, a validation subset, and full reproduction code, the authors directly address the bottleneck of missing item-level environmental signals. This resource enables standardized evaluation and spurs development of new multi-objective algorithms. The core assumption is that community adoption will accelerate sustainability research. Evidence: the release itself and early citations. The mechanism fails if the community does not adopt the resource or if PCF errors are deemed too large for downstream tasks.

## Architecture Onboarding

**Component map:**  
Data Ingestion & Filtering (raw Amazon → filtered items) → PCF Estimation Service (prompt construction → LLM API call → PCF extraction) → Dataset Enricher (merge averaged PCF into item metadata) → Re-ranking Engine (base model predictions + SaS interpolation) → Evaluation Module (accuracy & sustainability metrics).

**Critical path:**  
1. Obtain and filter Amazon datasets.  
2. Run the `estimate_pcf.py` script on all filtered items (high-cost LLM step).  
3. Validate PCF estimates using the 159-item ground-truth set.  
4. Train a base recommender (BPR or LightGCN) on original interactions.  
5. Generate top‑N recommendations for all users.  
6. Apply re-ranking with varying \( \alpha \).  
7. Evaluate trade‑off curves (Recall vs. average PCF, tail % vs. average PCF).

**Design tradeoffs:**  
- Estimation accuracy vs. cost/latency: More detailed prompts or advanced LLMs improve PCF but increase API costs and inference time for ~50k items.  
- Ordinal vs. absolute fidelity: The resource prioritizes ranking consistency over absolute MAE, which is acceptable for re-ranking but not for precise carbon accounting.  
- Generalization vs. domain specificity: The zero‑shot pipeline is domain‑agnostic but may underperform on categories with highly specialized manufacturing processes.

**Failure signatures:**  
- High MAE coupled with low ranking consistency indicates the LLM is not capturing relative impacts; re-ranking will be ineffective.  
- Extreme PCF outliers (e.g., massive overestimates) can skew average PCF in recommendations.  
- Data leakage concerns: if the LLM memorizes EPDs from training, validation performance may be inflated.  
- Invariant recommendations when changing \( \alpha \): either PCF values are too uniform or the re‑ranking weight is too weak.

**First 3 experiments:**  
1. **Prompt replication:** Run the provided `estimate_pcf.py` on a small known subset (e.g., 10 items from the ground truth). Verify JSON output format and that the `source` field correctly distinguishes "estimation" from "manufacturer report".  
2. **Ground truth validation:** Using the 159‑item validation set, compute normalized Spearman and NDCG between ground truth and LLM estimates; confirm they align with reported values (>0.75).  
3. **Baseline re‑ranking:** Train LightGCN on the Electronics dataset. Generate top‑10 recommendations. Apply SaS re‑ranking with \( \alpha \in \{0, 0.25, 0.5, 0.75, 1.0\} \). Plot Recall vs. average PCF to reproduce the trade‑off curve shown in Figure 2.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
1. How can the robustness and calibration of LLM-based PCF estimates be improved across diverse product domains, given the observed variability in performance and high errors for high-impact items?  
2. To what extent can alternative LLM-based approaches (e.g., fine-tuning, Retrieval-Augmented Generation) outperform zero-shot prompting for PCF estimation, particularly for items lacking standardized reporting?  
3. How does the integration of PCF scores into recommendation algorithms beyond BPR and LightGCN affect the accuracy-sustainability trade-off, and can more sophisticated multi-objective optimization methods yield better Pareto fronts?  
4. Can PCF estimates be better calibrated to match real-world LCA values, especially for absolute emissions reporting, to enable more accurate sustainability assessments?

## Limitations
- The exact composition and formatting of the `product_data` string fed to the LLM (which metadata fields and their order) are unspecified, hindering exact replication of PCF estimates.  
- LLMs produce large absolute MAE (e.g., up to 284.97 kg CO2e in some domains); while ordinal consistency is high, absolute values may be unreliable for applications requiring accurate magnitude.  
- Potential data leakage: LLMs might have memorized EPDs from their training data, inflating validation performance on the ground-truth set.
