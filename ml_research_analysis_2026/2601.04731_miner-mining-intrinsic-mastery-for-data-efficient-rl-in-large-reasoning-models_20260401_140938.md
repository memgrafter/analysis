# Miner:Mining Intrinsic Mastery for Data-Efficient RL in Large Reasoning Models
## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2601.04731
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.04731
- **Reference count:** 40
- **Primary result:** Miner improves data efficiency in RL for reasoning models, achieving up to +4.58 Pass@1 and +6.66 Pass@K over GRPO across six benchmarks.

## Executive Summary
Critic-free reinforcement learning methods like GRPO suffer from wasted rollouts on positive homogeneous (PH) prompts where all generated trajectories are correct; the group-relative advantage becomes zero, providing no learning signal. Miner addresses this by repurposing the policy’s intrinsic uncertainty—computed as per-token negative log-likelihood—as a self‑supervised reward applied exclusively to PH prompts. It reinforces under‑confident correct trajectories, uses token‑level focal weighting to amplify gradients on critical uncertain tokens, and adaptively calibrates advantages to integrate seamlessly with verifier rewards without additional computation. Evaluated on six math and medical reasoning benchmarks with Qwen3‑4B and Qwen3‑8B, Miner surpasses four strong baselines (GRPO, DAPO, REINFORCE++, GSPO), delivering absolute Pass@1 gains up to 4.58 and Pass@K gains up to 6.66. It further exhibits superior test‑time scaling and alleviates mode collapse, confirming that exploiting latent uncertainty is both necessary and sufficient for data‑efficient RL training.

## Method Summary
Given a batch of prompts, Miner first generates \(G\) rollouts per prompt using the current policy. A verifier assigns a binary reward (correct/incorrect) to each rollout. Prompts with all rollouts correct are identified as positive homogeneous (PH); the remaining heterogeneous (HE) prompts contain at least one incorrect rollout.

For PH prompts, the intrinsic reward is defined as the negative average token‑level log‑likelihood under the old policy: \(r_{\text{int}} = -\frac{1}{T}\sum_{j} \log \pi_{\text{old}}(o_j | \text{context})\). To obtain a group‑relative signal, we center these rewards by the mean \( \text{mean}_{\text{int}} \) of \(r_{\text{int}}\) across all PH prompts in the batch and apply a ReLU, yielding \(\hat{A}_{\text{int}} = \text{ReLU}(r_{\text{int}} - \text{mean}_{\text{int}})\). This filters out trajectories that are more confident than the group average, focusing on under‑confident correct ones.

Next, each token \(j\) in a PH rollout receives a focal weight \(w_{i,j} = (1 - p_{i,j})^{\gamma}\), where \(p_{i,j}\) is the old policy’s token probability (stop‑gradient) and \(\gamma = 2\). The token‑level advantage is then \(\tilde{A}_{i,j} = w_{i,j} \cdot \hat{A}_{\text{int},i}\).

To balance intrinsic and standard advantages, we compute two sums over the entire batch: \(\tau_{\text{ref}} = \sum_{\text{HE prompts}} |A_{\text{token}}|\) (absolute token advantages from HE prompts) and \(\tau_{\text{pos}} = \sum_{\text{PH prompts}} |\tilde{A}_{\text{token}}|\). The scaling factor is \(\kappa = \min\bigl(1, \lambda_{\text{max}} \cdot \tau_{\text{ref}} / \tau_{\text{pos}}\bigr)\) with \(\lambda_{\text{max}} = 1.5\text{e-}3\). The final advantage for PH tokens becomes \(A_{\text{final}} = \kappa \cdot \tilde{A}\). HE prompts use the usual group‑relative advantages from the verifier: \(A = (r - \text{mean}_{\text{group}}) / \text{std}_{\text{group}}\).

Finally, the PPO clipped surrogate objective with a KL penalty (\(\beta=0.001\)) is minimized using these token‑level advantages. Hyperparameters: rollout batch size 128, \(G=16\) for math, temperature 0.7, max response length 8192, learning rate 2e‑6 for 4B, 1e‑6 for 8B/Llama.

## Key Results
- Miner achieves state‑of‑the‑art Pass@1 and Pass@K among four baselines (GRPO, DAPO, REINFORCE++, GSPO) on six reasoning benchmarks.
- Absolute gains reach up to 4.58 in Pass@1 and 6.66 in Pass@K over the GRPO baseline.
- The method demonstrates superior test‑time scaling and alleviates mode collapse; learning from PH prompts is shown to be both necessary and sufficient for data‑efficient RL.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
In GRPO, PH prompts yield zero advantage because all rollouts share the same verifier reward (all correct). This wastes potentially informative trajectories. Miner’s core insight is that within a set of correct solutions, the policy’s uncertainty—measured by token‑level negative log‑likelihood—reflects mastery: tokens with higher NLL are less certain and likely represent the reasoning steps that are less well‑learned. By using \(r_{\text{int}}\) as an intrinsic reward, Miner converts wasted PH rollouts into a learning signal that reinforces under‑confident correct reasoning.

Token‑level focal weighting further focuses updates on the most uncertain tokens within a trajectory. Without weighting, averaging across the whole sequence would dilute the signal with high‑confidence tokens. The exponent \(\gamma=2\) rapidly down‑weights tokens where the policy is already confident, ensuring that the gradient update concentrates on areas needing improvement.

Adaptive calibration via \(\kappa\) prevents the intrinsic advantage from overwhelming the standard verifier‑based signal. The scaling factor dynamically matches the magnitude of the intrinsic advantages to that of the heterogeneous advantages (\(\tau_{\text{ref}}\)). This keeps the overall update size in check and avoids KL divergence explosions, while still allowing the PH signal to contribute meaningfully. As training progresses and the PH ratio grows, \(\tau_{\text{ref}}\) may shrink; the \(\lambda_{\text{max}}\) cap and min‑operation ensure that \(\kappa\) never exceeds 1, capping the influence of intrinsic rewards.

Together, these mechanisms enable Miner to extract maximal information from every rollout, improving both exploitation (Pass@1) and exploration (Pass@K) without extra computation. The ablation studies in the paper confirm that each component (positive filtering, focal weighting, adaptive calibration) contributes positively to the final performance.

## Foundational Learning
1. **Group‑relative advantage**  
   *Why needed:* Provides a baseline for policy gradient methods, reducing variance and offering feedback relative to other rollouts for the same prompt.  
   *Quick check:* Are advantages computed per rollout group as (reward − mean) / std?

2. **Intrinsic reward**  
   *Why needed:* Supplies a learning signal when external rewards are uniform (e.g., PH prompts), turning wasted data into useful training.  
   *Quick check:* Is \(r_{\text{int}}\) the negative average token NLL under the old policy?

3. **Focal weighting**  
   *Why needed:* Emphasizes tokens where the policy is less confident, preventing easy tokens from dominating the loss.  
   *Quick check:* Does weight \(w_{i,j} = (1 - p_{i,j})^\gamma\) increase as confidence \(p_{i,j}\) decreases?

4. **Adaptive advantage scaling**  
   *Why needed:* Balances the magnitude of intrinsic and verifier advantages, maintaining training stability and avoiding KL blowup.  
   *Quick check:* Is the scaling factor \(\kappa = \min(1, \lambda_{\text{max}} \cdot \tau_{\text{ref}} / \tau_{\text{pos}})\)?

5. **Positive homogeneous (PH) detection**  
   *Why needed:* Isolates prompts where all rollouts are correct but may differ in uncertainty, the target for intrinsic rewards.  
   *Quick check:* Is a prompt flagged PH when the sum of binary verifier rewards equals \(G\) (number of rollouts)?

6. **Token‑level advantage assignment**  
   *Why needed:* Enables fine‑grained credit assignment over long reasoning chains.  
   *Quick check:* Are token advantages broadcast to every token position in the sequence loss calculation?

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component map**  
Prompt → Generate G rollouts → Compute binary correctness → Split: PH (all correct) vs. HE (mixed) → For PH: r_int (avg NLL) → group‑mean baseline → ReLU → \(\hat{A}_{\text{int}}\) → focal weights \(w\) → \(\tilde{A}\) → compute \(\tau_{\text{ref}}\) (from HE advantages) and \(\tau_{\text{pos}}\) (from \(\tilde{A}\)) → \(\kappa\) → \(A_{\text{final}}\); For HE: standard group‑relative A → Merge all advantages → PPO loss → Policy update

**Critical path**  
The pipeline must correctly implement: (1) PH detection based on binary verifier rewards; (2) r_int derived from old policy NLL; (3) focal weighting with \(\gamma=2\); (4) τ sums using absolute token advantages; (5) κ scaling with \(\lambda_{\text{max}}=1.5\text{e-}3\); (6) integration into the PPO clipped objective. Any deviation can degrade performance or cause instability.

**Design tradeoffs**  
- **Intrinsic reward only for PH:** Avoids interference with standard RL but relies on calibration as PH ratio grows; may under‑utilize data if many HE prompts early.  
- **Focal weighting sparsity:** Concentrates gradients on uncertain tokens, but if confidence becomes too high (p≈1) weights vanish, starving PH updates. The exponent γ controls sparsity.  
- **Adaptive calibration:** Dynamically matches magnitudes; however, when τ_ref→0 (few HE prompts), κ collapses to 0, turning off PH learning. The cap λ_max limits potential over‑amplification.  
- **Group size G=16:** Sufficient for uncertainty estimation but increasing G improves stability at higher compute cost.

**Failure signatures**  
- τ_ref near zero → κ→0 → PH advantages vanish; monitor per‑batch τ_ref.  
- KL divergence > 0.01 or gradient norm spikes → λ_max too high or τ scaling unstable.  
- Focal weights median < 0.01 → policy extremely confident; consider reducing γ or increasing temperature.  
- r_int std < 1e‑3 across PH group → rollout diversity insufficient; increase G or temperature.  
- Token advantages not properly broadcast → uniform updates; inspect per‑token loss distribution.

**First experiments**  
1. **GRPO baseline validation** – Implement GRPO with group‑relative advantages from verifier rewards. Validate on Qwen3‑4B with AIME2024/HMMT25 dev sets to match reported baseline (e.g., ~19.79 Pass@1 on AIME24).  
2. **Add PH detection & intrinsic reward** – Introduce PH flagging, compute r_int and ĤA_int with ReLU. Verify that PH prompts produce non‑zero, positively skewed advantages and that intrinsic advantage correlates with token uncertainty.  
3. **Integrate focal weighting & adaptive calibration** – Add token‑level focal weights and κ scaling. Observe κ dynamics across batches; ensure it remains >0 when PH ratio is moderate and does not cause KL explosion. Compare performance against ablations without calibration.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
1. **Scaling to larger models** – How does MINER perform on substantially larger backbones (e.g., 32B) and does λ_max transfer without retuning?  
2. **Optimal training duration and λ_max** – What number of epochs and λ_max value yield maximal performance without instability? The authors used one epoch and a conservative λ_max, suggesting room for improvement.  
3. **Extension to negative homogeneous prompts** – Can the intrinsic reward mechanism be adapted to prompts where all rollouts are incorrect (NH) to learn from failures?  
4. **Sensitivity to focal weighting exponent** – Is the default γ=2 optimal across diverse reasoning tasks and model scales, or would other values improve results?

## Limitations
- Definition of “valid tokens” for τ sums (whether to exclude padding or special tokens) is unspecified, leading to potential implementation ambiguity.  
- Adaptive calibration lacks a defined fallback when HE or PH groups are empty in a batch, risking division‑by‑zero or vanishing gradients.  
- High PH ratio (>65%) can cause τ_ref to approach zero, collapsing κ and rendering PH rollouts ineffective.  
- The approach may be sensitive to the choice of λ_max and γ, with limited ablations provided.

## Confidence
- **Data efficiency claim:** High (consistent +4.58 Pass@1, +6.66 Pass@K across six benchmarks).  
- **Necessity of uncertainty exploitation:** Medium (ablations show benefit but necessity not proven against a PH‑only oracle).  
- **Adaptive integration (calibration):** Medium (λ_max transfers from 4B to 8B, but edge cases unstable).  
- **Mode collapse mitigation:** Medium (metrics improved, long‑term effects unclear).  
- **Sufficiency of self‑supervision:** Low (no PH‑only training experiment; contributions of verifier signal still needed).

## Next Checks
1. **Token scope for τ** – Compare implementations that sum only non‑pad/non‑prompt tokens versus all generated tokens to determine the intended definition and its impact on calibration stability.  
2. **Calibration stability under PH‑only batches** – Implement a running‑average fallback for τ_ref (e.g., using an exponential moving average) and evaluate when PH ratio exceeds 60% to prevent κ collapse.  
3. **Rollout diversity sensitivity** – Ablate the number of rollouts G (8/16/32) and sampling temperature (0.5/0.7/0.9), measuring the standard deviation of r_int within PH groups and correlating with final Pass@1 to identify minimal diversity requirements.
