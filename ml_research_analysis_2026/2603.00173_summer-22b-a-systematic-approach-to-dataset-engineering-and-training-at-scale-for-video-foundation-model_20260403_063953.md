# Summer-22B: A Systematic Approach to Dataset Engineering and Training at Scale for Video Foundation Model
## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2603.00173
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.00173
- Reference count: 13
- Primary result: Achieved VBench 2.0 total score of 0.539, competitive with larger models despite 10x less training data.

## Executive Summary
Summer-22B is a video foundation model trained from scratch on 50 million video clips (500 billion tokens) using a systematic dataset engineering pipeline (Lavender Data) and µP-based training with hypersphere-constrained Riemannian optimization. Despite using less data than some competitors, it reached a VBench 2.0 score of 0.539 with ~20% lower inference latency, at a total cost of ~$300K. The work emphasizes dataset curation and efficient training over architectural complexity.

## Method Summary
The authors employed metadata-driven dataset curation followed by multi-stage filtering to construct a high-quality 50M-clip corpus. Training used µP parameterization to enable hyperparameter transfer across scales, combined with Riemannian optimization on hyperspheres to stabilize training. The model architecture featured a parallel attention-MLP design for latency reduction. All steps were orchestrated via the Lavender Data system for scalability.

## Key Results
- VBench 2.0 total score: 0.539, competitive with Wan 2.2-5B (0.575) despite significantly less training data.
- Inference latency reduced by ~20% via parallel attention-MLP design.
- Total project cost approximately $300K, with $150K for compute resources.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The dominant factor was dataset engineering: meticulous curation and filtering ensured high-quality, diverse training data, reducing the need for excessive scale. µP parameterization maintained stable optimization dynamics across model sizes, and hypersphere constraints prevented weight explosion, enabling efficient training. Architectural variations showed minor impacts, suggesting that for video foundation models, data quality and training stability may outweigh incremental architectural innovations under µP constraints.

## Foundational Learning
1. µP Parameterization
   - Why needed: Enables transfer of hyperparameters (learning rates, initialization scales) across model sizes by scaling weights and learning rates per layer according to theoretical norms.
   - Quick check: Train two models of different widths with µP-scaled LRs; compare gradient norms and convergence against non- µP baselines.
2. Hypersphere-Constrained Optimization
   - Why needed: Restricts weight vectors to unit hyperspheres to avoid magnitude drift, improving training stability and generalization in large models.
   - Quick check: Monitor weight norms during training; constrained training should maintain norms near 1, while unconstrained may diverge.
3. Lavender Data System
   - Why needed: Scalable pipeline for metadata-driven dataset curation and multi-stage filtering, handling 50M clips efficiently.
   - Quick check: Process a subset of video metadata; verify filtering stages reduce low-quality samples by expected percentages.
4. Parallel Attention-MLP Design
   - Why needed: Reduces inference latency by parallelizing attention and MLP computations instead of sequential execution.
   - Quick check: Measure per-layer latency in a standard transformer vs. parallel design; expect reduction in total forward pass time.
5. Multi-Stage Filtering
   - Why needed: Sequentially removes noisy, duplicate, or low-quality clips to ensure training data quality.
   - Quick check: Ablate each filtering stage; evaluate impact on downstream VBench scores.
6. VBench 2.0 Evaluation
   - Why needed: Comprehensive benchmark for video generative models assessing visual quality, motion, temporal consistency.
   - Quick check: Run model generations through VBench 2.0 pipeline; compare subscores to known baselines.

## Architecture Onboarding
- Component map: Raw Video Collection -> Metadata Extraction -> Multi-Stage Filtering -> Training Data Sampler -> µP-Scaled Optimizer (with Hypersphere Constraint) -> Parallel Transformer Blocks -> VBench Evaluation
- Critical path: Data curation -> Training stability ( µP + constraints) -> Efficient inference -> Benchmark performance.
- Design tradeoffs: Prioritized data quality and training efficiency over architectural novelty; accepted simpler transformer design to leverage µP and reduce latency.
- Failure signatures: Weight norm explosions (constraint failure), poor VBench motion/temporal scores (data or training issues), high GPU memory usage (scaling problems).
- First three experiments:
  1. Replicate training on a smaller public dataset (e.g., Kinetics-400) with µP and hypersphere constraints; verify loss curve stability and weight norm behavior.
  2. Ablate the hypersphere constraint: train identical models with/without it; compare convergence speed, final VBench scores, and weight norm distributions.
  3. Measure inference latency breakdown: profile parallel vs. standard attention-MLP blocks on target hardware to confirm ~20% speedup.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
### Open Question 1
- Question: How do alternative manifold constraints (e.g., Stiefel or Grassmannian manifolds) compare to hypersphere constraints for training video diffusion models?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] "Future work will explore alternative manifold constraints beyond hyperspheres" (Conclusion).
- Why unresolved: The study only evaluated hypersphere constraints; other manifolds might yield different optimization geometries, affecting stability, convergence speed, or sample quality. Their compatibility with µP and impact on training dynamics remain unknown.
- What evidence would resolve it: Train comparable models under constraints on Stiefel/Grassmannian manifolds, monitoring µP band adherence, validation loss, and VBench scores. Compare compute efficiency and robustness against the hypersphere baseline to determine whether alternative manifolds offer practical advantages.

### Open Question 2
- Question: What explains the observed insensitivity to layer-wise learning rate multipliers, and can we derive a principled simplification for µP-based training?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] "HEBO optimization convergence... indicates redundancy in the sensitivity to these hyperparameters" (Section 4.8) and "investigate the redundancy... suggested by our HEBO experiments" (Conclusion).
- Why unresolved: While HEBO found many layer-wise LR configurations performing similarly, the theoretical cause (e.g., gradient norm equalization, µP dynamics) is unclear. Without understanding, practitioners may still need costly searches for novel architectures or scales.
- What evidence would resolve it: Analyze per-layer gradient norms and update magnitudes across µP-scaled models; run controlled ablations varying LR multipliers across depths and widths; correlate deviations from µP band with performance to identify whether certain layers truly require distinct scales.

### Open Question 3
- Question: Can the established training methodology (including µP and hypersphere constraints) scale effectively to models with tens of billions of parameters and beyond 100K training steps?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] "continue scaling using the established methodology" (Conclusion). The current evaluation covers up to 1B parameters and 100K steps.
- Why unresolved: Larger models may face communication overhead, numerical instability, or degradation of µP-prescribed dynamics under hypersphere constraints. FSDP2 scalability and constraint enforcement across thousands of GPUs also need validation.
- What evidence would resolve it: Train progressively larger models (e.g., 10B, 30B) for extended horizons (e.g., 500K, 1M steps). Track training stability (loss, µP band violations), hardware utilization, and final VBench performance. Identify failure modes and whether µP transfer remains reliable.

### Open Question 4
- Question: Do the observed scaling relationships (learning rate scaling with batch size √B and training duration 1/√T) hold for other video diffusion architectures and datasets?
- Basis in paper: [inferred] The paper qualifies: "We note that these are empirical observations from our specific setting... and may not generalize broadly" (Section 4.5).
- Why unresolved: The scaling laws were derived from a specific vanilla transformer with 3D RoPE and a curated 50M-clip dataset. Different architectures (e.g., DiT with alternative attention) or data distributions could exhibit different optimal LR dynamics, limiting the methodology's portability.
- What evidence would resolve it: Replicate the scaling experiments across diverse video model architectures (e.g., CogVideoX, HunyuanVideo) and datasets (e.g., Kinetics-600, HowTo100M). For each combination, sweep learning rates across varying batch sizes and training steps to test whether √B and 1/√T scalings remain predictive.
