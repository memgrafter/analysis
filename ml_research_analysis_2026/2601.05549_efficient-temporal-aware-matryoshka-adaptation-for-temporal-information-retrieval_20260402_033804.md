# Efficient Temporal-aware Matryoshka Adaptation for Temporal Information Retrieval

## Quick Facts
- **ArXiv ID:** 2601.05549
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.05549
- **Reference Count:** 40
- **Primary Result:** TMRL significantly improves temporal retrieval (e.g., +17.7 nDCG@10 on TNP for contriever) while maintaining semantic performance and enabling efficient low-dimensional retrieval via Matryoshka embeddings.

## Executive Summary
Temporal information retrieval is a critical bottleneck in Temporal RAG systems, as existing retrievers often fail to distinguish temporally relevant documents. This paper proposes Temporal-aware Matryoshka Representation Learning (TMRL), a method that efficiently adapts text embedding models by dedicating the first *t* dimensions of their nested (Matryoshka) embeddings to temporal information. This is achieved through a temporal projector and contrastive loss, while preserving general semantics in the remaining dimensions. A temporal contrastive data augmentation pipeline further refines training data by isolating single temporal expressions. Experiments across six models on TNP and TimeQA show consistent improvements in temporal retrieval at various embedding dimensions, with strong accuracy-efficiency trade-offs in downstream RAG.

## Method Summary
TMRL adapts a frozen pre-trained text embedding model (TEM) using LoRA to avoid catastrophic forgetting. The core innovation allocates the first *t* dimensions of the Matryoshka embedding as a dedicated temporal subspace. A small temporal projector processes only the embeddings of tokens identified as temporal expressions (via SUTime), producing a compact *t*-dimensional representation. The training objective combines: (1) standard Matryoshka contrastive loss (`LMRL`) over multiple truncation levels; (2) a temporal subspace contrastive loss (`LTemp`) that aligns the first *t* dimensions with the projected temporal representation; and (3) two self-distillation losses (`LDist`, `LCKA`) that preserve geometric relationships between full-dimensional and truncated embeddings. After training, the LoRA weights are merged into the base TEM and the temporal projector is discarded, yielding a model where the first *t* dimensions are temporally aware at any truncation level.

## Key Results
- TMRL yields substantial gains in temporal retrieval: up to **+17.7 nDCG@10** over baselines on TNP for contriever.
- It **consistently outperforms** Matryoshka and temporal baselines at lower embedding dimensions (64–256).
- Semantic retrieval on NQ is **preserved**, with minimal degradation when hyperparameters are properly tuned.
- In temporal RAG with Qwen3-8B, TMRL achieves the best **accuracy-efficiency trade-off**, enabling up to **3× storage reduction** with minimal F1 drop.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1
- **Claim:** Dedicating the first *t* dimensions to temporal information forces temporal cues to be encoded separately from general semantics, mitigating catastrophic forgetting.
- **Mechanism:** The training objective explicitly regresses the first *t* dimensions of the embedding to match a compact projection of only the temporal tokens. This creates a "temporal subspace" that is preserved across all Matryoshka truncation levels.
- **Core assumption:** Temporal signals can be isolated to a low-dimensional subspace without losing expressiveness, and this subspace remains stable under truncation.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract] "...dedicating the first *t* dimensions of their Matryoshka (nested) embeddings to temporal information..."
  - [section 3.2] "To preserve temporal cues across all Matryoshka truncation levels, we allocate the first t dimensions..."
  - [corpus] Evidence weak: corpus neighbors discuss general RAG and temporal considerations but do not validate this specific architectural partitioning assumption.
- **Break condition:** If temporal information cannot be compressed into a fixed *t*-dimensional subspace without significant loss, or if optimal *t* varies wildly, performance degrades. Ablations in Table 2/4 show NQ performance drops when *t* or α are misconfigured.

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim:** A temporal contrastive learning loss applied to the dedicated temporal subspace provides a clean, strong gradient signal for temporal encoding, free from semantic noise.
- **Mechanism:** The loss `LTemp` uses the projected temporal representations as anchors. It pulls queries/positives with matching temporal intent together and pushes apart temporally mismatched negatives *within the first t dimensions only*.
- **Core assumption:** Temporal relevance can be supervised by contrasting queries with different temporal constraints against the same passage, and this signal is meaningfully representable in a low-dimensional space.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [section 3.2] "The InfoNCE loss for the t-dimensional temporal subspace is... This loss encourages the first t-dimensions to align with the projected ¯pT and ¯q+ T..."
  - [section 4] The data augmentation pipeline splits passages to isolate single temporal expressions, creating clean contrastive pairs.
  - [corpus] Evidence weak: corpus does not directly discuss contrastive learning design for temporal subspaces.
- **Break condition:** If the temporal projector is incapable of learning meaningful compression, or if generated negatives are not true temporal negatives, the loss signal becomes noisy.

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim:** Local and structural self-distillation losses preserve the geometric relationships of the full-dimensional embedding within each truncated Matryoshka view, stabilizing training and improving generalization across truncation levels.
- **Mechanism:** Higher-dimensional (teacher) embeddings supervise lower-dimensional (student) counterparts via: (1) `LDist` aligning local nearest-neighbor similarities; (2) `LCKA` aligning global covariance structure (centered kernel alignment).
- **Core assumption:** The full-dimensional embedding captures a desirable "true" semantic/temporal structure that should be approximately preserved at all smaller scales.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [section 3.2] "Because full-dimensional embeddings encode richer relational structure, we adopt linear Centered Kernel Alignment (CKA)... encouraging lower-dimensional embeddings to remain geometrically aligned with the teacher space."
  - [section 5.3] Tables 3 & 4 show small auxiliary losses (β=γ=0.1) improve alignment and retrieval metrics, while larger values hurt.
  - [corpus] Evidence weak: corpus discusses RAG retrievers, not self-distillation for embedding compression.
- **Break condition:** If the full-dimensional embedding's structure is poor or contains artifacts, "distilling" it propagates flaws. Auxiliary losses also introduce optimization complexity, potentially competing with primary objectives if weighted too heavily.

## Foundational Learning
- **Concept: Matryoshka Representation Learning (MRL)**
  - **Why needed here:** TMRL builds upon MRL's principle of nested embeddings. A single model produces *d*-dimensional vectors whose first *m* dimensions (for *m* in a set *M*) form a valid *m*-dimensional embedding. This is the substrate for the temporal subspace.
  - **Quick check question:** If you truncate a Matryoshka embedding from 768 to 128 dimensions, what property must hold for it to be useful for retrieval?
- **Concept: Contrastive Learning (InfoNCE)**
  - **Why needed here:** The primary training signal for both semantic and temporal objectives is contrastive. The engineer must grasp the in-batch negative setup, temperature scaling, and how similarity operates on truncated vectors.
  - **Quick check question:** How does the similarity function `sim(m)(x, y)` in Eq. 4 change when *m* changes, and why is this critical for Matryoshka training?
- **Concept: Parameter-Efficient Fine-Tuning (LoRA)**
  - **Why needed here:** TMRL uses LoRA to adapt the base TEM. The engineer must know that LoRA injects trainable low-rank matrices into the transformer's linear layers, which are merged post-training. This avoids full fine-tuning's catastrophic forgetting and high cost.
  - **Quick check question:** What is the primary operational benefit of using LoRA in TMRL for deployment, as hinted in Section 3.1?

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map:**
  Base TEM (fθ) -> LoRA adapters (Δ) -> Temporal Token Selector -> Temporal Projector (P) -> Loss Orchestrator
- **Critical path:**
  1.  **Data Prep:** Original TNP/TimeQA → SUTime filtering → Passage splitting (to single-temporal-expression) → LLM-based query augmentation → final triplets with temporal metadata.
  2.  **Forward Pass:** Query/passage → `fθ` (with LoRA) → pooled embeddings → extract temporal token embeddings → `P` → `¯qT`/`¯pT`.
  3.  **Loss Computation:** For each Matryoshka level *m*, slice first *m* dimensions. Compute `LMRL` (on all *m*), `LTemp` (on first *t* dims vs. `¯T`), `LDist`/`LCKA` (between full-dim and *m*-dim).
  4.  **Inference:** Merge LoRA into base model; discard `P`. Compute full embedding; truncate to first *m* dimensions for search. First *t* dimensions are temporally aware.
- **Design tradeoffs:**
  - **t vs. Semantic Capacity:** Larger *t* improves temporal retrieval but risks overwriting semantics. Depends on base TEM's original bias.
  - **α (Temp Loss Weight):** Higher α focuses on temporal subspace but can destabilize semantic learning; requires per-TEM tuning.
  - **Auxiliary Loss Weights (β, γ):** Small values (0.1) help align truncated spaces; large values dominate and hurt primary objectives.
  - **Data Augmentation Cost:** LLM-based pipeline improves data quality but adds significant preprocessing overhead and potential LLM noise.
- **Failure signatures:**
  - **Catastrophic semantic forgetting:** NQ nDCG@10 drops >5 points vs. LoRA-MRL baseline. Indicates *t* or α too large, or `LTemp` overwhelming.
  - **Poor low-dim performance:** At *m*=64/128, TNP nDCG@10 no better than zero-shot. Indicates *t* too small or temporal signal not propagating.
  - **Training instability:** Loss diverges or oscillates. Often due to α, β, γ too large, creating conflicting gradients.
  - **High variance across seeds:** Sensitive hyperparameter settings (*t*, α). Report mean/std over multiple runs.
- **First 3 experiments:**
  1.  **Ablate *t* (Temporal Subspace Size):** For a chosen TEM (e.g., contriever), train with *t* ∈ {32, 64, 128, 256} (fix α=0.1, β=γ=0.1). Plot TNP nDCG@10 vs. NQ nDCG@10 for *m*=128,256,512. Find *t* that maximizes TNP with <2pt NQ drop.
  2.  **Ablate α (Temp Loss Weight):** With best *t* from Exp 1, sweep α ∈ {0.05, 0.1, 0.25, 0.5, 1.0}. Observe trade-off curve between TNP and NQ; identify "knee" point.
  3.  **Validate Auxiliary Losses:** Train with (β, γ) configurations: (0,0), (0.1,0), (0,0.1), (0.1,0.1). Compare against Exp 1/2's best config. Verify small β, γ improve TNP Recall@100 (especially lower *m*) without harming NQ.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- **Open Question 1:** How does TMRL perform when adapted to LLM-based embedding models compared to conventional text embedding models? *Basis:* Authors did not evaluate on LLM-based embeddings due to resource constraints. *Why unresolved:* Newer architectures may exhibit different temporal encoding behaviors. *What evidence would resolve:* Implement TMRL on several LLM-based embedding models, evaluate on TNP/TimeQA, and compare performance/generalizability.
- **Open Question 2:** What standardized preprocessing guidelines should be adopted to convert temporal question-answering datasets into temporal information retrieval benchmarks? *Basis:* No standardized guideline exists; current conversion is heuristic-based. *Why unresolved:* Inconsistent evaluation across benchmarks limits reproducibility. *What evidence would resolve:* Meta-analysis of multiple temporal QA datasets to propose and validate a standardized pipeline via cross-dataset experiments.
- **Open Question 3:** Does employing a state-of-the-art large language model for temporal contrastive data augmentation improve the quality of training data and downstream temporal retrieval performance compared to using a weaker model? *Basis:* The pipeline uses Qwen3-4B, not SOTA; potential noise remains. *Why unresolved:* Impact of LLM quality on augmentation and TMRL training is unclear. *What evidence would resolve:* Re-augment datasets using a SOTA LLM, quantify data quality, and measure gains in TMRL metrics.
- **Open Question 4:** How can TMRL be extended to effectively handle passages containing multiple temporal expressions, which are prevalent in real-world documents, without sacrificing performance? *Basis:* Current pipeline splits passages to ensure one temporal expression per passage, discarding multi-temporal content. *Why unresolved:* Real-world documents often contain multiple temporal expressions. *What evidence would resolve:* Modify the temporal projector to aggregate multiple temporal tokens, design a contrastive loss for multiple constraints, and evaluate on a dataset retaining multi-temporal passages.

## Limitations
- Mapping character-level temporal spans to token indices is unspecified; incorrect alignment could degrade the temporal contrastive signal.
- Hyperparameter *k* for `LDist` neighbor mining is not given; an unsuitable *k* may impair self-distillation.
- LLM-generated augmentation may introduce noisy or inconsistent temporal negatives.
- TMRL requires careful tuning of *t* and α; misconfiguration risks catastrophic semantic forgetting or weak temporal encoding.
- A fixed low-dimensional temporal subspace may be insufficient for diverse temporal reasoning needs.

## Confidence
- **Temporal subspace allocation:** Medium. Empirical gains are shown, but optimal *t* varies across models and datasets.
- **Temporal contrastive loss:** Medium. Relies on augmentation quality; purity of temporal contrasting is not directly verified.
- **Self-distillation:** Medium. Auxiliary losses improve low-dimensional performance but may propagate artifacts from the full-dimensional teacher.

## Next Checks
1.  **Probe the temporal subspace:** Train a linear classifier on the first *t* dimensions of frozen TMRL embeddings to predict temporal relation labels (e.g., Allen relations). Target accuracy >85% to confirm concentrated temporal information.
2.  **Audit span-to-token alignment:** Randomly sample 200 temporal expressions, map them using the offset-matching method, and compare to manual token annotations. Exact match rate >90% to validate preprocessing.
3.  **Ablate self-distillation:** Remove `LDist` and `LCKA` separately, retrain one base TEM (e.g., contriever) with optimal hyperparameters, and evaluate TNP nDCG@10 at *m* = 64 and 128. A drop >5% confirms their necessity for low-dimensional robustness.
