# DecepGPT: Schema-Driven Deception Detection with Multicultural Datasets and Robust Multimodal Learning

## Quick Facts
- **ArXiv ID**: 2603.23916
- **Source URL**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.23916
- **Reference count**: 35
- **Primary result**: State-of-the-art multimodal deception detection on DOLOs (73.23%), Bag-of-Lies (63.46%), and MU3D (61.25%) with robust cross-cultural performance on T4-Deception.

## Executive Summary
Multimodal deception detection is hindered by uninterpretable binary labels, small datasets that encourage shortcut learning, and poor cross-cultural generalization. This paper introduces three contributions to address these issues: (1) a reasoning dataset pipeline that enriches existing benchmarks with structured audiovisual cues and logic chains, enabling auditable and interpretable reports; (2) T4-Deception, a new multicultural dataset of 1,695 samples under a unified identity pretense task across four countries; and (3) two robust learning modules—Stabilized Individuality-Commonality Synergy (SICS) for polarity-aware feature stabilization and Distilled Modality Consistency (DMC) for cross-modal regularization. Evaluations on three popular benchmarks (DOLOs, Bag-of-Lies, MU3D) show state-of-the-art in-domain accuracy and significant improvements over large language model baselines (GPT-4o, +6.85% average) and prior task-specific models (+5.13% average). Cross-domain transfer yields an average accuracy gain of +6.40%. On T4-Deception, the method achieves stable in-cultural accuracy (62.58% average) and robust cross-cultural performance (57.69% average) with minimal relative degradation (4.89%). Ablation studies confirm the individual efficacy of SICS (+5.44% on DOLOs) and DMC (+1.53%). The work advances interpretable, generalizable, and culturally robust deception detection.

## Method Summary
The proposed framework begins with a reasoning dataset pipeline that automatically annotates existing multimodal deception benchmarks with structured cues—such as facial Action Units, vocal pitch contours, and temporal sync patterns—and generates explicit logic chains that connect low-level cues to high-level deception hypotheses. This pipeline transforms binary labels into richer, auditable training signals, mitigating label ambiguity and reducing shortcut learning. For model training, the system processes audiovisual inputs through separate modality encoders (e.g., CNN or Transformer backbones) to extract feature embeddings. The Stabilized Individuality-Commonality Synergy (SICS) module then maintains distinct feature buffers for truthful and deceptive samples, enforcing intra-polarity compactness and inter-polarity separation, thereby stabilizing learning especially under limited data. The Distilled Modality Consistency (DMC) module applies a teacher–student distillation paradigm: a teacher network trained on full multimodal inputs guides a student network that receives only one modality at a time, encouraging cross-modal consistency and preventing over-reliance on any single stream. The entire pipeline is trained end-to-end on the new T4-Deception dataset, which standardizes identity pretense across diverse cultural contexts (four countries), enabling both in-cultural and cross-cultural evaluation.

## Key Results
- Achieves state-of-the-art in-domain accuracy on DOLOs (73.23%), Bag-of-Lies (63.46%), and MU3D (61.25%), outperforming GPT-4o by an average of +6.85% and prior task-specific models by +5.13%.
- Cross-domain transfer (training on one dataset, testing on another) improves average accuracy by +6.40%.
- On the T4-Deception dataset, the model attains in-cultural accuracy of 62.58% (average) and cross-cultural accuracy of 57.69%, with a minimal relative degradation of 4.89%. Ablations confirm the contributions of SICS (+5.44% on DOLOs) and DMC (+1.53%).

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The effectiveness of DecepGPT stems from two complementary mechanisms. First, the reasoning dataset pipeline converts ambiguous binary labels into structured, interpretable evidence, providing the model with more granular supervision and reducing the temptation to exploit superficial shortcuts. This enrichment yields features that are semantically aligned with known deception cues (e.g., facial micro-expressions, vocal stress patterns). Second, the SICS module explicitly separates feature spaces for truthful and deceptive samples, stabilizing gradient updates and preventing polarity interference, which is particularly beneficial when training data are scarce. The DMC module further regularizes the model by forcing cross-modal consistency through knowledge distillation, ensuring that no single modality dominates the decision and that the learned representations capture genuine cross-modal relationships. Together, these components produce a model that is both accurate and robust across diverse datasets and cultural settings.

## Foundational Learning
- **Multimodal Fusion**: Combining information from multiple sensory streams (audio, video) to capture complementary deception indicators. *Why needed*: Deception often manifests in subtle, distributed cues; relying on a single modality risks missing critical evidence. *Quick check*: Does the fused model consistently outperform each unimodal variant on held-out data?
- **Shortcut Learning Mitigation**: Techniques to prevent models from latching onto spurious correlations (e.g., background, lighting) that do not generalize. *Why needed*: Small or biased datasets encourage overfitting to dataset-specific artifacts, undermining real-world applicability. *Quick check*: Are performance gains preserved when evaluating on a dataset with different acquisition conditions?
- **Cross-Cultural Generalization**: Ability of a deception detection system to maintain performance across populations with different cultural norms and expressive behaviors. *Why needed*: Cultural variations can alter both the expression and perception of deceit; a model trained on a single culture may fail elsewhere. *Quick check*: How large is the drop in accuracy when testing on data from an unseen country?
- **Polarity-Aware Feature Stabilization (SICS)**: A module that maintains separate feature banks for truthful and deceptive samples, optimizing intra-class compactness and inter-class separation. *Why needed*: Binary deception tasks suffer from feature mixing between opposite polarities, especially with limited data; SICS reduces this interference. *Quick check*: Does ablating SICS lead to a sharper performance decline on smaller datasets?
- **Cross-Modal Regularization via Distillation (DMC)**: Enforces consistency between modalities by training a student network (single-modality input) to mimic a teacher network (full multimodal input). *Why needed*: Prevents the model from over-relying on a single dominant modality and encourages robust, complementary representations. *Quick check*: Is the performance gap between teacher and student models smaller when DMC is active?
- **Reasoning Dataset Pipeline**: A preprocessing stage that enriches raw videos with structured audiovisual cues and generates logic chains linking cues to deception hypotheses. *Why needed*: Provides interpretable supervision, reduces label noise, and guides the model toward human-understandable reasoning patterns. *Quick check*: Does training with pipeline-augmented data improve model interpretability (e.g., via attention visualization) compared to raw labels?

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component map**: Raw audiovisual input -> Modality Encoders (audio_enc, video_enc) -> Feature vectors (f_A, f_V) -> SICS module (polarity buffers & stabilization) -> DMC module (teacher-student distillation) -> Classifier -> Output. The reasoning dataset pipeline operates offline, converting raw videos into structured cue annotations and logic chains that serve as additional supervision during training.

**Critical path**: The forward pass proceeds as: (1) Encoders produce modality-specific embeddings; (2) SICS perturbs embeddings to align with polarity-specific buffers, producing stabilized features; (3) DMC computes a consistency loss between the multimodal teacher and unimodal students; (4) The classifier produces the final deception prediction.

**Design tradeoffs**: 
- SICS requires additional memory to store polarity buffers but yields more discriminative features and stabilizes training on small datasets.
- DMC introduces computational overhead (teacher network, multiple forward passes) but improves cross-modal robustness and reduces overfitting to a single modality.
- The reasoning pipeline demands costly manual annotation or high-quality automatic annotation tools, yet it yields interpretable training signals and mitigates shortcut learning.

**Failure signatures**: 
- Large cross-cultural performance drops indicate insufficient cultural diversity in the training set.
- High neighbor false match rate (FMR) in annotation consistency suggests noisy labels within existing benchmarks.
- Ablating SICS or DMC leads to disproportionate performance declines on small datasets, confirming their role in robustness.
- The model may still exhibit shortcut behavior if the reasoning pipeline fails to capture all relevant cues (e.g., background bias).

**First experiments**: 
1. **Baseline**: Train a standard multimodal classifier (late fusion of audio and video features) on DOLOs to establish a reference accuracy.
2. **+SICS**: Incorporate the Stabilized Individuality-Commonality Synergy module into the baseline and measure the isolated impact on performance and feature stability.
3. **Full model**: Add the Distilled Modality Consistency module on top of SICS and evaluate the incremental gain, verifying the synergy of both modules and comparing against the baseline and GPT-4o.

## Limitations
- **Annotation noise**: The presence of a high average neighbor FMR (0.444) in existing benchmarks indicates potential inconsistencies in ground-truth labels, which could affect model reliability.
- **Limited cross-cultural data**: T4-Deception comprises only 1,695 samples, which may be insufficient to support robust claims about generalizability across cultures and risks overfitting.
- **Metric discrepancy**: The reported minimal cross-cultural degradation (4.89% relative) conflicts with the calculated absolute drop (62.58%→57.69% ≈ 7.8% relative), raising concerns about result interpretation or rounding.
