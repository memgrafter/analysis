# S2Act: Simple Spiking Actor

## Quick Facts
*   **arXiv ID:** 2603.15725
*   **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.15725
*   **Reference Count:** 40
*   **Primary Result:** S2Act enables the first stable on-chip SNN RL policy for multi-agent adversarial tasks, achieving high success rates on Intel Loihi with ultra-low inference energy (0.033–0.037 J).

## Executive Summary
S2Act introduces a method for training stable spiking neural network (SNN) reinforcement learning policies in stochastic multi-robot environments. It achieves this by using rate-based leaky integrate-and-fire (LIF) neurons with parameters shaped to mimic a ReLU activation during simulation training with the PPO algorithm. This "soft-ReLLIF" approach provides smooth gradients, avoiding the instability common in direct spiking RL training. The trained policy is then converted to a standard spiking LIF network for deployment on the Intel Loihi neuromorphic chip. Evaluated on capture-the-flag and parking tasks, S2Act demonstrates superior training speed and simulated performance over prior SNN methods and successfully transfers to Loihi with exceptional energy efficiency, even showing promising transfer to real TurtleBot3 robots with minimal adjustment.

## Method Summary
The S2Act pipeline consists of three main stages. First, an actor-critic network is trained in simulation using a rate-based LIF neuron model (`soft-ReLLIF`) whose membrane time constant, capacitance, and threshold are configured (Cm=1, τm=1, V0=0, Vreset=0, τref=0) to operate in a nearly linear regime, approximating a ReLU. A small gain factor (γ=0.003) smooths the non-differentiable threshold function for gradient computation. Second, during training, an activity regularization penalty gently constrains neuron firing rates to remain within a range anticipated by the target hardware. Third, the finalized actor network weights are converted to a standard discrete-time spiking LIF model and compiled for execution on Intel Loihi using the NengoLoihi framework, with final activity scaling applied to match the chip's optimal operating dynamics.

## Key Results
*   **Training Efficiency & Performance:** In simulation, S2Act achieves the highest success rates with the fastest training: 1v1 CtF (78.1% in 2.4h vs. PopSAN 67.7% in 8.3h), 2v2 CtF (34.0% in 5h vs. 21.2% in 15.7h), and parking (43.1% vs. 40.4%).
*   **Neuromorphic Deployment:** On Intel Loihi, S2Act maintains strong performance (1v1 CtF 72.6%, 2v2 CtF 29.4%, parking 39.1%) with ultra-low energy per inference (0.033–0.037 J) and low latency (26–29 ms).
*   **Real-World Transfer:** The Loihi-deployed policy transfers to real TurtleBot3 robots for capture-the-flag with only minimal final-layer activity rescaling, demonstrating practical utility.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The core mechanism addresses the instability of end-to-end spiking RL training. By using a *rate-based*, *smoothly differentiable* LIF variant during PPO training, the method provides reliable gradient signals while still producing sparse, event-based outputs after conversion. The neuronal parameters are explicitly shaped to ensure the membrane potential operates in a linear region where the firing rate is approximately proportional to the input current, mimicking the unbounded, non-saturating behavior of a ReLU. This prevents vanishing or exploding gradients that plague direct SNN training. Hardware-aware activity regularization during training reduces the need for drastic post-conversion rescaling, preserving performance when the spiking network is deployed on the Loihi's non-ideal, quantized architecture.

## Foundational Learning
*   **Concept:** Rate-based LIF Neurons
    *   **Why needed:** Provides a deterministic, differentiable firing rate output during ANN training, avoiding the non-differentiability of discrete spikes.
    *   **Quick check:** Does the neuron's firing rate function have a smooth derivative w.r.t. input current across the operating range?
*   **Concept:** Soft-ReLLIF Activation
    *   **Why needed:** A specific parameterization of the rate-based LIF (Cm=1, τm=1, etc.) that makes its input-output curve nearly identical to a ReLU, allowing the use of well-established ANN training practices.
    *   **Quick check:** Plot the firing rate vs. input current for given parameters; it should be linear with a sharp but smoothed cutoff.
*   **Concept:** Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO)
    *   **Why needed:** A robust, on-policy RL algorithm that handles stochastic environments and continuous action spaces effectively, suitable for the multi-agent tasks.
    *   **Quick check:** Is the clipped surrogate objective and value loss coefficient implemented as in the original PPO paper?
*   **Concept:** ANN-to-SNN Conversion
    *   **Why needed:** Allows leveraging mature ANN training frameworks while ultimately deploying a low-power spiking network on neuromorphic hardware.
    *   **Quick check:** Are the post-conversion spiking thresholds and reset mechanisms correctly mapped from the simulated firing rates of the ANN layers?
*   **Concept:** Neuromorphic Hardware Constraints (Intel Loihi)
    *   **Why needed:** Loihi has specific limitations (weight/activation bit-depth, current limits, neuron model) that differ from simulation; deployment must account for quantization and dynamic range.
    *   **Quick check:** Is the compiled network utilizing Loihi's native LIF model and are synaptic weights scaled to fit the fixed-point representation?
*   **Concept:** Activity Regularization
    *   **Why needed:** To ensure the trained ANN's activation distributions are compatible with the Loihi's finite current and spike rate capabilities, minimizing performance loss after conversion.
    *   **Quick check:** Is a penalty term (e.g., on mean firing rate) added to the training loss, and are its coefficients tuned?

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component Map:** Observations (State/Actor) -> Actor Network (2x [Dense(64) -> Soft-ReLLIF]) -> Actions -> (Environment) -> Rewards -> PPO Loss (Actor + Critic) -> [Weight Updates] -> (Conversion) -> Spiking LIF Actor -> Intel Loihi -> Inference.

**Critical Path:** The critical path is the stability of the PPO training loop, which depends entirely on the soft-ReLLIF gradient behavior. A secondary critical path is the conversion step, where mismatches between the simulated neuron model and Loihi's physical spiking model (due to quantization, noise, parameter mismatches) can cause catastrophic performance drops.

**Design Tradeoffs:**
1.  **Training Stability vs. Hardware Fidelity:** Using a perfectly ReLU-mimicking soft-ReLLIF maximizes ANN training stability but may create a larger gap to the true discrete spiking LIF on Loihi.
2.  **Sparsity vs. Gradient Magnitude:** Strong activity regularization promotes hardware-friendly sparse spikes but can diminish gradient signals if over-constrained.
3.  **Precision vs. Energy:** Loihi's lower bit-precision reduces memory and energy but increases quantization noise, directly impacting policy performance.

**Failure Signatures:**
1.  **Training Collapse:** Neuron firing rates during PPO training are either near-zero (silent) or constantly saturated (max rate). Indicates the linear operating region of the soft-ReLLIF is not being utilized.
2.  **Performance Cliff Post-Conversion:** A significant drop (>5-10%) in success rate when moving from the simulated ANN actor to the Loihi-deployed spiking actor. Indicates poor activity scaling or unaccounted quantization effects.
3.  **Unrealistic Training Speed:** Training completes orders of magnitude faster or slower than reported (e.g., 2.4h vs. 15.7h for 1v1 CtF). Suggests major hyperparameter discrepancies (network size, PPO epochs, batch size).

**First Three Experiments:**
1.  **Validate Soft-ReLLIF Gradient Flow:** Train a minimal network on a simple control task (e.g., CartPole) using the exact soft-ReLLIF parameters. Log per-layer mean/variance of firing rates every update. Success: rates are moderate (e.g., 10-50 Hz) and stable; failure: rates collapse to 0 or max.
2.  **Measure Conversion Sensitivity:** After training the full S2Act actor on CtF, record pre-conversion firing rate distributions. Deploy to Loihi, record on-chip spike rates for the same input batch. Compute the scaling factor needed to align the distributions. Success: scaling factor is near 1.0 ("minimal rescaling"); failure: factor >2x or <0.5x.
3.  **Quantify Quantization Impact:** Deploy the same converted network to Loihi while systematically varying the reported (but unspecified) synaptic weight resolution (e.g., use 6-bit vs. 8-bit mode if configurable). Measure success rate and on-chip energy. Success: performance is stable across precision settings; failure: sharp degradation at lower bits, indicating quantization is a primary limiter.

## Limitations
*   **Hardware Tuning Opacity:** The exact methodology for "minimal rescaling" of activities to match Loihi's operating regime is not specified, hindering exact replication of on-chip performance.
*   **Unreported Quantization Details:** The weight and activation bit-precision used during Loihi compilation is omitted, obscuring the true source of performance limitations (quantization vs. model mismatch).
*   **Baseline Parity Concerns:** Comparisons with PopSAN, RSNN, and Hybrid SNN baselines may suffer from uncontrolled architectural and hyperparameter differences, affecting confidence in the claimed superiority.

## Confidence
*   **Training stability via soft-ReLLIF:** **High** (Method and key hyperparameters are explicitly defined).
*   **Simulated performance numbers:** **Medium** (Results are reported but baseline implementation details are not fully controlled for fair comparison).
*   **Loihi efficiency & transfer claims:** **Low** (Critical hardware-specific tuning parameters like scaling factors and quantization settings are unreported, making the claimed efficiency and transferability ambiguous to reproduce exactly).
