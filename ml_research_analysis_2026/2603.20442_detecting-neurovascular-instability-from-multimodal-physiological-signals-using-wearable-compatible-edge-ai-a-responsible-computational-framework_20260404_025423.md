# Detecting Neurovascular Instability from Multimodal Physiological Signals Using Wearable-Compatible Edge AI: A Responsible Computational Framework

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2603.20442
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.20442
- **Reference Count:** 27
- **Primary Result:** A multimodal edge AI framework (Melaguard) fuses HRV, perfusion index, SpO₂, and bilateral phase coherence via a 1.2M-parameter Transformer-lite to generate an NVI Score, achieving AUC=0.755 in clinical stroke validation (PhysioNet CVES) and designed for deterministic ≤4 ms inference on Cortex-M4 microcontrollers.

## Executive Summary
Neurovascular instability (NVI) is a pre-structural dysregulation that precedes stroke but cannot be detected by single-modality wearables. This paper introduces Melaguard, a computational framework for edge AI that fuses four physiological signals—heart rate variability, perfusion index, SpO₂, and bilateral phase coherence—using a compact Transformer architecture to compute a continuous NVI Score. The system is designed for deterministic, privacy-preserving inference on resource-constrained microcontrollers. Validation includes a synthetic benchmark (AUC=0.88), a clinical stroke cohort (AUC=0.755, outperforming LSTM/RF/SVM), and cross-modality checks on independent PPG datasets. A hydration-activated biosensor is proposed to improve signal quality across skin tones, though its fabrication remains future work.

## Method Summary
The task is binary classification of NVI versus control using multimodal wearable-compatible signals. Inputs are derived from ECG/PPG/TCD: heart rate variability (RMSSD, SDNN), perfusion index (AC/DC ratio), SpO₂, and bilateral phase coherence (asymmetry measure). Data sources are a synthetic benchmark (n=10,000), PhysioNet CVES clinical stroke cohort (n=172), BIDMC PPG-ECG alignment dataset (n=53), and PPG-BP cerebrovascular disease dataset (n=219). The core model is a `TransformerLite` (1.2M parameters, 2 encoder layers, 4-head attention, d_model=128) that processes 60-second windows resampled to 100 Hz. Training uses AdamW with class-weighted binary cross-entropy and 5-fold stratified cross-validation. The framework targets deployment on Cortex-M microcontrollers via the AeroKernel microkernel, with a theoretical worst-case execution time (WCET) ≤4 ms.

## Key Results
- Synthetic benchmark validation: AUC = 0.88.
- Clinical stroke cohort (CVES): Transformer-lite AUC = 0.755, outperforming LSTM (0.643), RF (0.665), and SVM (0.472).
- PPG signal validation: Pulse rate correlation r=0.748 and HRV surrogate r=0.690 vs. ECG on BIDMC.
- Cross-modality validation: Cerebrovascular disease classification AUC = 0.923 on PPG-BP.
- Edge design: Target deterministic inference WCET ≤4 ms on Cortex-M4 @ 168 MHz.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1: Multimodal Fusion Captures Systemic Dysregulation
- **Claim:** Fusing HRV, perfusion index, SpO₂, and bilateral phase coherence yields superior NVI detection versus any single modality.
- **Mechanism:** The Transformer-lite architecture learns temporal cross-modal dependencies and feature interactions that simpler classifiers (LSTM, RF, SVM) cannot, as evidenced by higher AUC on synthetic and clinical data.
- **Core assumption:** NVI is a systemic physiological dysregulation manifesting across multiple, partly independent signal domains; joint analysis provides a more complete view.
- **Evidence anchors:** 
  - [abstract] "Multimodal fusion consistently outperforms single-modality baselines."
  - [section IV-C] Transformer-lite AUC=0.755 vs. LSTM 0.643, RF 0.665, SVM 0.472 on CVES.
  - [section IV-F] PPG-BP morphology validation AUC=0.923 supports discriminative capacity.
- **Break condition:** If prospective validation with complete multimodal data shows negligible gain over best single modality (ΔAUC < 0.05) or feature importance reveals no cross-modal attention weights.

### Mechanism 2: Compact Architecture Enables Deterministic Edge Inference
- **Claim:** The 1.2M-parameter Transformer-lite executes deterministically within ≤4 ms WCET on a Cortex-M4, enabling real-time, privacy-preserving edge inference.
- **Mechanism:** Architectural constraints (2 encoder layers, 4-head attention, d_model=128, pre-norm) and lean post-training quantization limit compute/memory demands, allowing a bounded inference schedule under the AeroKernel microkernel.
- **Core assumption:** The model's size and depth avoid non-deterministic memory access patterns and dynamic control flow that inflate WCET in larger networks.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract] "designed for deterministic edge inference (WCET ≤4 ms on Cortex-M4 @ 168 MHz)."
  - [section III-E] AeroKernel with BATS scheduling targets bounded worst-case latency <100 ms; the 4 ms is a theoretical inference payload target.
  - *Corpus evidence weak.* No empirical hardware WCET data presented.
- **Break condition:** If empirical WCET on target STM32H7 exceeds 4 ms by >20% or AeroKernel's isolation primitives incur unacceptable overhead.

### Mechanism 3: Hydration-Activated Biosensor Promotes Skin-Tone Equity
- **Claim:** A hydration-activated PHBV:eumelanin composite biosensor may improve SNR and reduce skin-tone bias versus conventional silicon PPG.
- **Mechanism:** Hydration-dependent ionic conductivity (σ from 10⁻⁸ to 10⁻⁴ S/m) in the hydrated melanin matrix amplifies the AC pulsatile component relative to DC baseline noise, per COMSOL modeling.
- **Core assumption:** The simulated conductivity mechanism translates to a fabricated film, producing a measurable ×3 SNR gain across Fitzpatrick skin types IV–VI.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [section III-B] COMSOL validation showing conductivity curve and theoretical signal amplification model (Fig. 2).
  - [abstract] "hydration-activated PHBV:eumelanin composite biosensor" listed as contribution.
  - [section VI] "Fabrication and characterisation... constitutes planned future work." *No empirical proof yet.*
- **Break condition:** If prototypes show <1.5× SNR improvement or hydration response is unstable across relevant humidity/temperature ranges.

## Foundational Learning
- **Concept: Multimodal Temporal Feature Alignment**
  - **Why needed:** NVI Score fuses HRV (from ECG/PPG intervals), perfusion index, and bilateral phase coherence originating at different sampling rates; they must be synchronized into consistent 60-second windows.
  - **Quick check:** How would you handle a scenario where the PPG signal is corrupted by motion artifact for 15 seconds within a 60-second window, and the bilateral phase channel has 40% missing data?

- **Concept: Edge AI Determinism vs. Accuracy Trade-offs**
  - **Why needed:** The framework targets ≤4 ms WCET on Cortex-M4, necessitating aggressive model size reduction that may cap AUC versus cloud-scale models. The engineer must understand quantization, operator selection, and memory layout impacts on worst-case timing.
  - **Quick check:** If a new attention variant improves AUC by 0.02 but increases the maximum path length in the computation graph, what is the first tool/analysis you would run to assess its WCET impact?

- **Concept: Causal Inference in Pre-Structural Biomarker Discovery**
  - **Why needed:** NVI is defined as *pre-structural* dysregulation. Validation uses stroke/control cohorts where the label is a *consequence* (stroke), not a direct measurement of instability. This creates a latent variable problem; the model learns correlates of stroke risk, not NVI itself.
  - **Quick check:** In the CVES dataset, why is a significant difference in HRV (SDNN p=0.011) between groups necessary but *not sufficient* evidence that the model is detecting NVI as defined?

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map:** Sensor Layer (PHBV:eumelanin PPG patch / clinical ECG/TCD/PPG) -> Preprocessing Pipeline (resampling, artifact rejection, 60-s windowing, feature extraction) -> Model Core (TransformerLite: Input [B,4,6000] -> Projection -> 2×Encoder(4-head) -> GAP -> Head -> NVI Score) -> Runtime Layer (AeroKernel microkernel loading quantized model) -> Output (Continuous NVI Score [0,100] + risk tier).
- **Critical path:** Raw PPG waveform -> PPG peak detection & NN interval extraction -> HRV feature computation (RMSSD, SDNN) -> perfusion index (AC/DC) -> bilateral phase coherence (from bilateral PPG/TCD) -> feature vector [f_HRV, f_PI, f_SpO2, f_Phase] -> Transformer-lite forward pass -> NVI Score.
- **Design tradeoffs:**
  - Accuracy vs. Latency: 1.2M params chosen as upper bound for ≤4 ms; more layers/heads would improve synthetic AUC but break WCET.
  - Modality Robustness vs. Completeness: Score redistributes weight if a modality drops (degraded mode), maintaining operability but reducing specificity (phase had 60% missingness in CVES).
  - Generalization vs. Skin Tone Equity: Melanin biosensor aims to fix PPG bias but is untested; deploying without it risks systematic performance gaps.
- **Failure signatures:**
  - High false positive rate (FPR > 0.5 at Youden threshold): Could indicate overfitting to cohort confounders (e.g., age in PPG-BP, where age-only AUC=0.872).
  - Latency spike (>4 ms WCET): Caused by cache misses, interrupt contention, or non-deterministic memory allocator in AeroKernel; check BATS scheduling logs.
  - Modality dropout cascade: If perfusion index fails (e.g., severe motion), weight redistribution may over-emphasize noisy HRV; monitor per-modality confidence scores if implemented.
  - AUC collapse on external dataset: Likely if new population has different hemodynamic baselines (e.g., younger, athletic); CVES cohort (mean ~70 yrs) may not represent community deployment.
- **First 3 experiments:**
  1. **Synthetic Sanity Check:** Run the provided `synthetic_pipeline.py`, train Transformer-lite on 70/15/15 split, confirm AUC ≥0.85 on held-out set.
  2. **Single-Modality Baseline & Ablation:** On CVES train fold, train separate models using only (a) HRV features, (b) Perfusion/SpO₂ features, (c) Phase features; compare AUC to full multimodal to isolate fusion gain and identify weakest link.
  3. **Hardware WCET Probe:** Port preprocessed feature extractor and quantized Transformer-lite to target STM32H7 dev board; use cycle-accurate profiler to measure median and worst-case (10,000 runs, interrupts disabled) inference latency; confirm ≤4 ms or identify bottleneck operator.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
### Open Question 1
- **Question:** Can bilateral phase coherence serve as a validated modality for neurovascular instability detection when assessed with adequate statistical power and complete data?
- **Basis:** [explicit] In Section IV-B, bilateral phase coherence showed non-significant trend (p=0.829) with 69/172 subjects having data; post-hoc power ~15%; authors downgrade claim to "proposed proxy pending empirical validation."
- **Why unresolved:** High data missingness (60%) and insufficient sample size to detect small effect size (Cohen’s d≈0.18); unclear if non-significance reflects true lack of value or limitations.
- **Resolution evidence:** Prospective study with complete recordings on ≥280 subjects across stroke/control groups using standardized protocols.

### Open Question 2
- **Question:** Does the hydration-activated PHBV:eumelanin biosensor achieve theoretically predicted SNR gain and skin-tone equity benefits in empirical, in-vivo conditions?
- **Basis:** [explicit] Section III-B validates computationally via COMSOL; Section VI lists fabrication/characterisation as future work, noting challenges like film uniformity and optical calibration.
- **Why unresolved:** No empirical data from physical sensors; computational feasibility doesn't guarantee reproducible in-vitro/in-vivo performance due to fabrication variables, drift, and real-world optical interference.
- **Resolution evidence:** Fabricate 5–10 prototype patches; test ex-vivo on phantoms mimicking Fitzpatrick IV–VI; measure conductivity, PPG SNR, and SpO₂ accuracy vs. clinical references.

### Open Question 3
- **Question:** Can Melaguard be end-to-end validated using a dataset with photoplethysmography (PPG) signals directly labeled for stroke or neurovascular instability?
- **Basis:** [explicit] Section E identifies a "remaining gap": "A PPG-labelled stroke dataset would enable direct end-to-end PPG-to-classification validation—this constitutes the highest-priority future experiment." Cross-modality used PPG-BP for cerebrovascular disease, not stroke-specific labels.
- **Why unresolved:** Current validation relies on ECG/TCD-based CVES and PPG pipeline cross-checks (BIDMC); no dataset exists where raw PPG inputs are paired with ground-truth NVI or acute stroke labels.
- **Resolution evidence:** Collect/curate a cohort with simultaneous PPG recordings and confirmed stroke/NVI diagnoses (e.g., via imaging); train/test Transformer-lite on raw/minimally processed PPG features.

### Open Question 4
- **Question:** What is the actual worst-case execution time (WCET) of the AeroKernel microkernel when executing the Transformer-lite model on target Cortex-M7 hardware?
- **Basis:** [explicit] Section VI, point 3 specifies "AeroKernel WCET benchmarking on STM32H7 (Cortex-M7)" as future work; Table VI lists WCET as "Theoretical" with estimates ≤4 ms on Cortex-M4, but hardware benchmarking is pending.
- **Why unresolved:** Presented WCET is theoretical; real-time performance affected by hardware (cache, interrupts), compiler optimizations, and system load, unaccounted for in simulations.
- **Resolution evidence:** Empirical benchmarking of complete AeroKernel + Transformer-lite on STM32H7 dev boards, measuring latency under worst-case conditions (e.g., maximum background tasks).

### Open Question 5
- **Question:** How does Melaguard’s performance generalize to community-scale, multiethnic populations with Fitzpatrick skin types IV–VI, where optical biosensing challenges are pronounced?
- **Basis:** [explicit] Section VI notes "both datasets represent populations differing from the intended deployment context (community-based, multiethnic, Fitzpatrick IV–VI)"; biosensor motivation is equity (Section II-B).
- **Why unresolved:** Validation datasets (CVES, BIDMC, PPG-BP) primarily involve lighter skin tones or lack detailed pigmentation data; equity-by-design premise untested in target demographics.
- **Resolution evidence:** External validation on cohorts with diverse Fitzpatrick IV–VI using actual PHBV:eumelanin sensor or calibrated commodity PPG devices; assess NVI Score accuracy, false alarm rates, and skin-tone-specific calibration needs.

## Limitations
- **Synthetic Benchmark Opacity:** Generation parameters (physiological models, noise, perturbation timing) are undisclosed, preventing exact replication of the claimed AUC=0.88.
- **CVES Missing Data Handling:** ~60% missing bilateral phase coherence; imputation/missingness strategy is unclear and likely impacts model performance and interpretation.
- **Biosensor Fabrication Gap:** The hydration-activated PHBV:eumelanin biosensor's claimed ×3 SNR gain is based solely on COMSOL simulation; no empirical prototype data exists.
- **WCET Claim Status:** The ≤4 ms Cortex-M4 timing is a theoretical calculation; no empirical worst-case measurement on target hardware is presented.

## Confidence
- **Multimodal Fusion Superiority:** High. Supported by consistent AUC improvements across synthetic, clinical (CVES), and cross-modality (PPG-BP) cohorts against multiple baselines (LSTM, RF, SVM).
- **Deterministic Edge Inference:** Medium. Architectural choices support feasibility, but critical WCET claim lacks empirical hardware validation; AeroKernel integration remains theoretical.
- **Novel Biosensor Advantage:** Low. Entirely computational; fabrication, environmental stability, and cross-skin-tone validation are pending.
