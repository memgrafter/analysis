# Curriculum Learning and Pseudo-Labeling Improve the Generalization of Multi-Label Arabic Dialect Identification Models

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2602.12937  
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.12937  
- **Reference count:** 26  
- **Primary result:** Best model achieves macro F1 of 0.69 on MLADI leaderboard, significantly improving over previous state-of-the-art of 0.55.

## Executive Summary
The paper tackles the challenge of converting single-label Arabic dialect identification datasets into multi-label training data, where sentences can be acceptable in multiple dialects and negatives are often misassigned. They construct a pseudo-labeled multi-label dataset by aggregating predictions from GPT-4o and 18 binary dialect acceptability classifiers, guided by the Arabic Level of Dialectness (ALDi) score. A BERT-based model (LAHJATBERT) is then trained using curriculum learning strategies based on label cardinality and ALDi to handle increasing dialectal ambiguity. The approach achieves state-of-the-art macro F1 of 0.69 on the MLADI leaderboard, with curriculum learning notably boosting recall while maintaining precision.

## Method Summary
The authors repurpose single-label Arabic dialect datasets (NADI) by creating multi-label pseudo-labels. They compute ALDi scores for all sentences, then use two sources: 18 binary classifiers trained on high-confidence dialect/non-dialect examples (using ALDi extremes) and a GPT-4o prompt for multi-label prediction. An ALDi-guided hybrid aggregator selects binary classifier outputs for very MSA-like (ai<0.11) or strongly dialectal (ai>0.77) sentences, and GPT-4o predictions for ambiguous mid-range (0.11≤ai≤0.77). Zero-cardinality samples are removed. For training, they fine-tune MARBERT as a multi-label classifier (18 sigmoid outputs) with binary cross-entropy, freezing the bottom 8 layers and training the top 4 plus the head. Curriculum learning variants order training samples by either label cardinality (simpler to harder) or by ALDi-based difficulty (buckets ordered by mean loss from a baseline model), progressively introducing harder examples.

## Key Results
- Macro F1 of 0.69 on MLADI leaderboard, a significant improvement over previous SOTA 0.55.
- Curriculum learning increases macro recall (80.6 for cardinality CL vs 69.7 without CL) while preserving precision.
- ALDi-based curriculum yields the best overall macro F1 (70.3 on dev, 69.0 on test) among variants.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1: Hybrid Pseudo-Label Aggregation
**Claim:** Hybrid pseudo-label aggregation reduces mis-assigned negatives by leveraging complementary strengths of GPT-4o and binary classifiers, guided by ALDi score thresholds.  
**Mechanism:** Binary classifiers provide high-precision dialect acceptability predictions at ALDi extremes (very MSA-like, ai < 0.11, or strongly dialectal, ai > 0.77), where negative samples are more reliable. GPT-4o offers higher recall in the ambiguous mid-ALDi range (0.11 ≤ ai ≤ 0.77), where dialectal overlap is frequent. The hybrid combines both, improving the precision-recall trade-off.  
**Core assumption:** ALDi score correlates with dialectal ambiguity; low/high values indicate clearer cases, mid-values indicate overlap.  
**Evidence anchors:** Hybrid method achieves highest macro F1 and Accuracy on NADI2024 dev set (Table 3 last row). Binary classifiers excel at ALDi extremes (high precision), GPT-4o excels in mid-ALDi (higher recall/F1) (Table 4). ALDi is established in prior work (Keleg et al., 2023).  
**Break condition:** If ALDi fails to distinguish ambiguity levels (e.g., due to noisy estimation or domain shift), aggregation thresholds misassign supervision, degrading performance.

### Mechanism 2: Cardinality-Based Curriculum Learning
**Claim:** Cardinality-based curriculum learning mitigates dataset skew toward low-cardinality samples, improving recall by forcing the model to learn multi-dialect patterns.  
**Mechanism:** The pseudo-labeled dataset has a strong bias toward samples with 1-2 dialect labels. Curriculum learning starts training on low-cardinality (simpler) buckets and progressively introduces higher-cardinality (harder) buckets. This prevents the model from converging prematurely to a low-cardinality prediction pattern.  
**Core assumption:** Label cardinality is a proxy for difficulty; higher cardinality implies more dialectal overlap and harder learning.  
**Evidence anchors:** Cardinality CL increases macro Recall (80.6 vs. 69.7 for no-CL) while reducing Precision, indicating more multi-label predictions (Table 6). ALDi CL variant achieves highest recall on test set.  
**Break condition:** If high-cardinality samples are not inherently harder (e.g., due to noisy pseudo-labels), curriculum order could introduce noise earlier, potentially harming convergence.

### Mechanism 3: ALDi-Based Curriculum Learning
**Claim:** ALDi-based curriculum learning aligns training with a natural dialectal complexity gradient, improving generalization by gradually exposing the model to ambiguous sentences.  
**Mechanism:** ALDi provides a continuous measure of dialectness. Curriculum orders buckets from lowest to highest mean loss (empirically: MSA-like, low-medium, medium, high dialectness). This exposes the model first to clear MSA (acceptable in many dialects) and clear dialectal cases, then to ambiguous mid-range sentences where overlap occurs, allowing incremental learning of dialectal nuances.  
**Core assumption:** ALDi score reflects linguistic difficulty; intermediate values indicate higher ambiguity and thus higher loss for a baseline model.  
**Evidence anchors:** ALDi CL improves macro F1 over no-CL (70.3 vs. 68.0) and balances Precision/Recall better than cardinality CL. ALDi CL variant achieves highest macro F1 on test set (69.0) (Table 6).  
**Break condition:** If ALDi loss ordering does not correspond to true linguistic difficulty (e.g., due to dataset-specific artifacts), the curriculum may present examples in a suboptimal sequence, limiting gains.

Understanding how to derive a difficulty proxy from data statistics (mean loss per bucket) is key to implementing CL correctly. *Quick check:* What are the risks of using a proxy (cardinality/ALDi) instead of directly measuring per-example loss for ordering?
- **Pseudo-labeling and weak supervision aggregation:** This is needed because the training dataset is automatically constructed from two heterogeneous sources (GPT-4o, binary classifiers). Understanding the noise characteristics of each source and the rationale for ALDi-guided aggregation is essential. *Quick check:* How might the conservative negative sampling in binary classifiers (only non-neighboring, high dialectness) lead to high precision but limited coverage, and how does GPT-4o complement this?

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map:** Data pipeline: NADI tweets → ALDi scoring → binary classifiers (18) + GPT-4o prompting → ALDi-guided aggregation → filtered multi-label set. Model: MARBERT backbone (bottom 8 layers frozen, top 4 fine-tuned) → 18-unit classification head → sigmoid activation per unit. Training: Binary cross-entropy loss, Adam optimizer, batch size 24, 3 epochs, checkpoint selection by validation micro F1. Inference: Sigmoid outputs, threshold 0.3 to binarize predictions.
- **Critical path:** (1) Compute ALDi for all NADI samples. (2) Train 18 binary acceptability classifiers on balanced NADI-2023. (3) Obtain GPT-4o multi-label predictions via prompt. (4) Aggregate: if ai<0.11 or ai>0.77 use binary classifiers’ vector; else use GPT-4o’s vector. (5) Remove zero-cardinality samples. (6) Split aggregated set 90% train / 10% val (seed 42). (7) For CL: compute baseline model’s per-example loss on full train, aggregate mean loss per cardinality/ALDi bucket, order buckets ascending by mean loss. (8) Train MARBERT under CL schedule: at epoch e, train on all from bucket π(e) + equal random samples from earlier buckets. (9) Select best checkpoint from val micro F1; evaluate on dev/test with threshold 0.3.
- **Design tradeoffs:** Freezing layers reduces overfitting on noisy pseudo-labels and compute cost but may limit adaptation. Aggregation thresholds (0.11, 0.77) derived from ALDi annotation scheme; shifting changes composition. Prediction threshold 0.3 tuned on val to increase recall, facilitated by CL. CL bucket size fixed bins; uneven sizes may affect dynamics.
- **Failure signatures:** High precision, low recall (especially without CL) indicates underprediction due to low-cardinality bias. Overprediction of specific dialects could indicate GPT-4o bias or classifier leakage. CL degrades performance if difficulty ordering is inverted. Validation overfitting if pseudo-labels are noisy; use dev set for final selection.
- **First 3 experiments:** (1) Ablate aggregation: Train no-CL models on (a) binary-only, (b) GPT-only, (c) hybrid pseudo-labels; compare on NADI2024 dev. (2) Validate curriculum ordering: For each CL variant, randomize bucket order and compare to loss-based order. (3) Threshold calibration: Sweep threshold on val set for each CL variant; confirm 0.3 optimal and observe precision-recall shifts.

4. To what extent does noise in geo-location-based dialect labels (where users' stated location may not reflect their actual dialect) affect the quality of pseudo-labels generated by binary classifiers and GPT-4o, and the overall performance of multi-label models? (Limitations)

## Limitations
- Hybrid aggregation relies on fixed ALDi thresholds; domain shifts in ALDi estimation could misroute samples between sources, hurting pseudo-label quality.
- Curriculum learning gains assume bucket proxies (cardinality, ALDi loss) align with true difficulty; if not, CL may harm convergence.
- Pseudo-label noise, especially from GPT-4o on ambiguous mid-ALDi instances, risks overfitting spurious correlations without explicit calibration.

## Confidence
- Hybrid pseudo-label aggregation: High
- Cardinality-based CL: Medium
- ALDi-based CL: Medium
