# Incremental LTLf Synthesis

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2603.01201
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.01201
- **Reference count:** 40
- **Primary result:** An automata-based incremental LTLf synthesis method that caches and progresses DFAs, achieving order-of-magnitude speedups over a naive formula-progression baseline.

## Executive Summary
Incremental LTLf synthesis deals with revising an agent's strategy to satisfy a new goal while preserving execution of original goals given the history already executed. The paper presents a method that constructs deterministic finite automata (DFAs) for LTLf goals, progresses the initial state of the original DFA through the history, and intersects it with the DFA of the new goal to form a game. Solving this game yields a winning strategy if one exists. By caching previously constructed DFAs and only progressing their initial states, the approach avoids costly recomputation. Theoretically, incremental LTLf synthesis is 2EXPTIME-complete, and the minimal DFA of a syntactically progressed formula remains bounded by the original DFA size. Experimentally, the authors' tool ISABEL-DP (automata-based) adds significantly more goals than the naive formula-progression tool ISABEL-FP, with average runtimes up to two orders of magnitude lower, demonstrating both correctness and practical efficiency.

## Method Summary
The paper compares two implementations of incremental LTLf synthesis: ISABEL‑DP (automata‑based) and ISABEL‑FP (naive formula progression). Both use LYDIA to convert LTLf goals to DFAs and SYFT to solve the resulting deterministic finite automaton games symbolically. In ISABEL‑DP, each new goal is converted to a DFA and stored; the initial states of all stored DFAs are progressed through the current execution history, then combined via a product construction to form the game arena. ISABEL‑FP instead syntactically progresses the conjunction of all previous LTLf formulas, rebuilds a DFA from the resulting formula, and solves the game anew at each step, discarding prior work. Experiments are run on four benchmark families (TIREWORLD, PLANTS, COUNTER, REQUESTS) with parametrized instances. For each instance, an original goal (including domain specification) and a sequence of up to 30–40 incremental goals are processed sequentially. The agent executes its current strategy while a fair adversarial environment reacts, generating a history after each step. This history is used as input for the next incremental synthesis. The primary metrics are the number of goals successfully added before a 3600‑second timeout per goal, and the average runtime per goal (computed only over goals solved by both tools). The automata‑based approach leverages DFA caching to dramatically reduce repeated work, while the formula‑progression approach suffers from repeated expensive DFA constructions.

## Key Results
- The incremental LTLf synthesis problem is 2EXPTIME‑complete, and the minimal DFA of a progressed formula is bounded in size by that of the original formula.
- ISABEL‑DP added 892 out of 1230 incremental goals across all benchmarks, compared to 692 for ISABEL‑FP.
- Average runtimes of ISABEL‑DP were up to two orders of magnitude lower than those of ISABEL‑FP, confirming the effectiveness of DFA caching and progression.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1: Hybrid DFA Product Construction
Incremental synthesis is achieved by constructing a game from the product of the original DFA (progressed through history) and the DFA of the new goal. The original DFA’s initial state is advanced via DFA progression to reflect the already executed trace; this progressed DFA accepts precisely the suffixes that extend the history to satisfy the original goal. This DFA is then intersected (via a product construction) with the DFA of the new goal. The resulting DFA is treated as a game arena where the agent (system) and environment alternate moves. Solving this game (e.g., via symbolic backward induction) yields a winning strategy if one exists for the combined objectives. The core assumption is that DFA progression and product operations can be performed efficiently, which is supported by the use of optimized DFA libraries (MONA) and symbolic game solvers (SYFT). If either DFA construction or the product becomes too large, the approach may hit resource limits.

### Mechanism 2: Bounded DFA Size Despite Formula Progression Blowup
While syntactic progression of an LTLf formula through a history can produce formulas exponentially larger than the original, the corresponding minimal DFA size is provably bounded by the size of the original DFA. This bound ensures that even though formulas may blow up, the automaton‑theoretic construction remains within 2EXPTIME complexity. The naive formula‑progression approach (ISABEL‑FP) discards the original DFA and reconstructs a DFA from the syntactically progressed conjunction at each step, thereby losing the opportunity to reuse the compact automaton representation. In contrast, the automata‑based approach directly progresses the DFA’s initial state without manipulating formulas, avoiding syntactic blowup and repeated construction.

### Mechanism 3: DFA Caching and Incremental Progression
ISABEL‑DP stores the DFAs of all goals added so far in a set \(V\). When a new goal arrives, the initial states of these stored DFAs are progressed through the latest history fragment—a polynomial‑time operation per DFA. The product game then uses these progressed DFAs directly. By caching DFAs, the method eliminates redundant 2EXPTIME‑hard DFA construction tasks, leading to substantial asymptotic and practical speedups. The assumption is that DFA construction dominates the overall cost, which empirical results confirm. However, caching many large DFAs can exhaust memory, especially when product construction multiplies state spaces; the paper reports memory pressure on larger TIREWORLD instances.

## Foundational Learning
### Concept: LTLf (Linear Temporal Logic on finite traces)
- **Why needed:** The agent’s goals are specified in LTLf, which extends propositional logic with temporal operators (e.g., next, until, eventually, always) over finite executions. Understanding its semantics is essential to interpret what strategies must achieve.
- **Quick check question:** What is the key semantic difference between LTL (infinite traces) and LTLf regarding satisfaction?

### Concept: Deterministic Finite Automaton (DFA)
- **Why needed:** The synthesis method reduces LTLf goals to DFAs, which are then manipulated via progression and product. Knowing DFA components (states, alphabet, transition function, initial state, accepting states) and language acceptance is fundamental.
- **Quick check question:** How does DFA progression (Definition 4) change the language accepted?

### Concept: Reactive Synthesis as a Game
- **Why needed:** The combined DFA is treated as a game arena where the agent and environment alternate moves. Solving the game determines a winning strategy. Familiarity with reachability games and backward induction is required.
- **Quick check question:** In a DFA game, what does it mean for a state to be in the winning region \(W\)?

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map:** LTLf-to-DFA Converter (LYDIA/MONA) → DFA Store (V) ← stores DFAs; DFA Progression Module updates initial states; Product Constructor (MONA) builds product DFA; Symbolic Game Solver (SYFT) computes winning region; Strategy Extractor outputs agent strategy σ.
- **Critical path:** New goal φ_new arrives → Convert φ_new to DFA A_new → Retrieve stored DFAs {A_org, A_1, …} from V → Progress each DFA’s initial state through history h_i → Construct product game G = ∏ A_i' × A_new → Solve G symbolically → If winning, extract σ and add A_new to V.
- **Design tradeoffs:** 
  - *Automata vs. Formula Progression*: Automata‑based (ISABEL‑DP) caches DFAs and progresses states (polynomial per step). Formula‑progression (ISABEL‑FP) reconstructs DFA from syntactically larger formulas each time, losing caching benefits.
  - *Explicit vs. Symbolic Representation*: MONA (explicit states, symbolic transitions) for DFA construction/minimization; SYFT (BDDs) for game solving, balancing expressiveness and scalability.
  - *Memory vs. Recompute*: Caching DFAs saves repeated construction costs but increases memory usage; memory limits may force discarding some DFAs.
- **Failure signatures:** 
  - DFA construction timeout or memory exhaustion, especially for complex φ_org or large product automata.
  - Simulation inconsistency: history violates domain specification, causing false unrealizability.
  - Toolchain incompatibility leading to unexpected DFA sizes or performance.
- **First three experiments:**
  1. **Baseline Comparison:** Run both tools on all benchmark families with a 3600 s timeout per goal; record number of goals added and average runtime per goal.
  2. **Scalability Stress Test:** For a given benchmark family (e.g., TIREWORLD with increasing ℓ), compare the time to add the first goal (includes domain spec) versus subsequent goals; expect large gap for ISABEL‑DP.
  3. **Memory Profiling:** Monitor RAM usage during execution on a large instance (e.g., TIREWORLD‑8); identify memory spikes during product construction and correlate with performance degradation.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- **Partial Observability:** How to extend incremental LTLf synthesis to settings where the agent lacks full knowledge of the environment’s state? The current full‑observability assumption limits applicability to many real‑world scenarios.
- **Conflict Resolution:** What efficient methods exist for resolving conflicts when newly added goals are unrealizable together with previous ones? The paper only detects conflicts; finding maximal realizable subsets is an exponential‑time problem in the worst case, requiring heuristic or approximation approaches.
- **Best‑Effort Synthesis:** Can best‑effort or good‑enough synthesis be combined with incremental LTLf synthesis to produce useful strategies even when the combined goal set becomes unrealizable? The current framework returns “unrealizable” without providing a fallback strategy.
- **Memory Scaling:** How does memory consumption scale as an agent accumulates hundreds or thousands of DFAs during long‑term execution? The paper reports memory issues on modestly sized instances but does not analyze long‑term memory behavior.

## Limitations
- The exact command‑line interfaces and APIs for ISABEL‑DP/ISABEL‑FP and their integration with LYDIA/SYFT are not fully documented, hindering precise replication.
- Memory exhaustion occurs in larger benchmark instances (e.g., TIREWORLD with ℓ ≥ 7), limiting scalability despite DFA caching.
- Naive formula progression can suffer from exponential syntactic blowup, which may cause timeouts if not implemented carefully, though minimal DFA sizes remain bounded.

## Confidence
- **Incremental automata‑based synthesis correctness and efficiency:** High (backed by 2EXPTIME‑completeness proof and strong empirical gains).
- **DFA progression equivalence to formula progression:** High (formally proved via Theorems 7 and 8).
- **Caching DFAs yields asymptotic and practical speedups:** Medium (results are compelling but require independent replication under identical conditions).
- **Scalability limited by memory:** High (memory exhaustion aligns with theoretical expectations of automaton growth).
