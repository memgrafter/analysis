# EuraGovExam: A Multilingual Multimodal Benchmark from Real-World Civil Service Exams
## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2603.27223  
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.27223  
- **Reference count:** 40  
- **Primary result:** Top accuracy 86.99% (Gemini-2.5-Pro); nation/script factors induce 2.5× greater performance variance than domain factors, with within-model cross-regional gaps up to 64.8 percentage points.

## Executive Summary
EuraGovExam introduces the first large-scale benchmark for evaluating vision-language models on authentic, multilingual civil service exam documents. The dataset comprises over 8,000 high-resolution scanned multiple-choice questions from five Eurasian regions (South Korea, Japan, Taiwan, India, EU), preserving original scripts and embedding all content within a single image. An image-only evaluation protocol forces models to perform layout-aware, cross-lingual reasoning directly from visual input. Results from 28 state-of-the-art VLMs reveal that visual and linguistic document properties—particularly script-specific layouts and non-Latin scripts—are primary determinants of performance, with Japan consistently underperforming despite shared Hanzi characters with Taiwan. The benchmark exposes critical gaps in current multimodal systems’ ability to handle real-world document diversity.

## Method Summary
The dataset is constructed from official civil service exam PDFs across five regions and 17 domains. Questions are manually cropped to individual, high-resolution images without OCR or text extraction, preserving original scripts (Hangul, Japanese vertical text, Traditional Chinese, Devanagari, Latin) and complex layouts (nested tables, multi-column designs, embedded figures). Evaluation uses a strict image-only protocol: each image is paired with a fixed minimal instruction (“You are solving a multiple-choice exam question shown in the image. At the very end, provide the final answer in exactly this format: The answer is X.”). Models generate responses via greedy decoding; answers are extracted with a regex and scored against ground truth. Analysis breaks down accuracy by nation and domain, computes variance decomposition (σ²_nation vs. σ²_task), and identifies universal failure questions.

## Key Results
- Top model accuracy is 86.99% (Gemini-2.5-Pro), but within-model cross-regional gaps reach 64.8 percentage points.
- Nation/script factors account for 2.5× more performance variance than domain factors.
- 50 questions (0.6%) defeat all models, concentrated in regions with dense visual layouts and non-Latin scripts (e.g., Japanese vertical text, Devanagari-embedded tables).

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1
- **Claim:** Script-specific visual processing characteristics (e.g., vertical Japanese typesetting, mixed Kanji-Kana-furigana layouts) create distinct perceptual bottlenecks not resolved by shared Hanzi character knowledge.
- **Mechanism:** VLMs must recognize, segment, and reason over region-specific typographic conventions within a single visual input. Japanese vertical writing (縦書き), dense furigana annotations, and mixed-script passages alter spatial reasoning demands compared to horizontal, single-script layouts (e.g., Taiwan’s Traditional Chinese). The model’s visual encoder and layout parser must adapt to these conventions without explicit linguistic cues.
- **Core assumption:** Shared logographic characters (Hanzi/Kanji) do not guarantee transferable visual processing pipelines; script-level layout rules are primary determinants of error.
- **Evidence anchors:**  
  - [abstract] Nation/script factors induce 2.5× greater performance variance than domain factors, with within-model cross-regional gaps reaching 64.8 percentage points.  
  - [section 5.2] Taiwan consistently yields higher performance than Japan despite both belonging to the Chinese character sphere; Japanese vertical text, mixed Kanji-Kana layouts, and furigana annotations demand layout-aware tokenization.  
  - [section 5.2] Gemini-3-Pro and Gemini-3-Flash both exhibit severe Japan underperformance (−39%p and −19%p respectively), suggesting persistent challenge in newer generations.

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim:** Document layout complexity (nested tables, multi-column designs, embedded figures with text) independently degrades performance by demanding precise spatial grounding and cross-modal alignment, beyond domain knowledge.
- **Mechanism:** Questions require referencing specific table cells, comparing values across spatially separated elements, or interpreting diagrams with textual overlays. The VLM must build a unified spatial-semantic representation from the image, where layout errors cascade into reasoning failures.
- **Core assumption:** Layout-aware reasoning is a distinct capability axis from pure textual or visual understanding; it is orthogonal to domain difficulty.
- **Evidence anchors:**  
  - [section 3.4] Corpus includes dense tables, charts, symbolic notations, and spatial references requiring precise visual parsing and cross-modal alignment.  
  - [section 5.1] Low-performing domains (Earth Science, Geography, Law) exhibit systematic degradation characterized by dense tables and charts.  
  - [section 5.3] Universal failures co-occur in visually complex, knowledge-intensive items with nested tables and small-font annotations.

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim:** Training data distribution biases toward Latin-script, horizontally-laid-out documents cause systematic underperformance on non-Latin, layout-dense scripts; scaling alone does not resolve this imbalance.
- **Mechanism:** Pre-training corpora and supervised fine-tuning data likely over-represent English/Latin documents with simpler layouts. Models thus develop skewed visual-linguistic representations. Persistent Japan underperformance across model generations indicates scaling does not automatically correct data skew.
- **Core assumption:** Performance gaps reflect dataset bias rather than inherent model incapacity; targeted augmentation should yield disproportionate gains.
- **Evidence anchors:**  
  - [section 5.2] Persistent Japan underperformance in Gemini-3 series suggests Japanese document processing remains unresolved.  
  - [section 6] Authors propose balanced multilingual training data and targeted augmentation for underrepresented scripts (Japanese scans, Devanagari-embedded technical content) could yield disproportionate gains.  
  - [appendix A.1] Moderate scaling trend (R²=0.41) for open-source models implies factors beyond parameter count matter.

## Foundational Learning
- **Concept: Multimodal document understanding**  
  *Why needed:* EuraGovExam forces end-to-end perception of scanned documents; success requires integrating OCR-like character recognition, layout parsing, and reasoning without explicit text extraction.  
  *Quick check:* What visual cues distinguish a table cell from a text block in a densely packed Korean exam image, and why might a VLM confuse them?
- **Concept: Script-aware visual encoding**  
  *Why needed:* Different scripts (Hangul, Kanji/Kana, Devanagari) have unique spatial characteristics (e.g., Hangul’s square blocks vs. Devanagari’s conjuncts) affecting spatial tokenization.  
  *Quick check:* How does vertical Japanese typesetting alter the coordinate system a VLM must learn compared to horizontal English text?
- **Concept: Benchmark factorial design for variance decomposition**  
  *Why needed:* The 5 (nation) × 17 (domain) structure enables isolating script/layout effects from content effects via ANOVA-style analysis.  
  *Quick check:* Why is it insufficient to report only overall accuracy when evaluating multilingual VLMs on real-world documents?

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component map:**  
Source acquisition → Manual cropping → Format preservation → Two-stage review → Image-only input → Fixed instruction → Greedy decoding → Regex extraction → Breakdown by nation/domain → Variance decomposition → Universal failure mining → Cross-task clustering

**Critical path:** Image ingestion → Visual encoder (preserves layout) → LLM reasoner (no external OCR) → Answer formatting → Scoring. The protocol forbids any intermediate text extraction.

**Design tradeoffs:**  
- *Image-only vs. OCR-assisted:* Image-only maximizes realism but cannot diagnose OCR-specific failures; OCR-assisted would disentangle perception from reasoning but lowers ecological validity.  
- *Script preservation vs. translation:* Translation would control for linguistic content but destroys layout/typography signals critical to real-world deployment.  
- *Static snapshot vs. temporal updates:* Current dataset reflects 2024–2026 exams; future changes in exam format will require versioning.

**Failure signatures:**  
- *Regional:* Consistent Japan underperformance across model families indicates script-layout bottleneck.  
- *Universal:* Questions with dense non-Latin tables + technical symbols (e.g., Devanagari-embedded math) defeat all models.  
- *Format:* Answers not matching “The answer is X.” regex are automatically incorrect—watch for verbose outputs.

**First 3 experiments:**  
1. **Baseline replication:** Run `evaluate.py --model gpt-4o --split test` to confirm paper results (expect ~42% overall, Japan ~25%).  
2. **Layout ablation:** For a subset, extract OCR text (externally) and feed as pure text input; measure performance delta vs. image-only to quantify layout contribution.  
3. **Script intervention:** Fine-tune a strong open-source VLM (e.g., Qwen2-VL-72B) on Japanese questions only; evaluate cross-regional transfer to measure script-specific adaptation.

## Limitations
- Correlational design confounds script vs. layout effects; image-only protocol prevents disentanglement.
- Coverage limited to five regions and 17 domains; may not generalize to broader civil service exams.
- Strict regex answer extraction penalizes stylistic variation and introduces scoring noise.
- Training data of evaluated VLMs is unknown; bias claims are indirect.

## Confidence
- Script/layout bottleneck (Mechanism 1): Medium  
- Layout complexity (Mechanism 2): Medium  
- Training data bias (Mechanism 3): Low–Medium  

## Next Checks
1. **Script ablation:** Replace Japanese characters with Latin look‑alikes while preserving layout/typography; measure performance shift to isolate script effect.
2. **OCR vs. image‑only gap:** Use a high‑accuracy OCR engine to extract plain text from all images, feed as text‑only input to the same VLMs, and compute accuracy drop; quantifies layout‑aware reasoning need.
3. **Balanced fine‑tuning:** Fine‑tune a strong open‑source VLM (e.g., Qwen2‑VL‑72B) on equally sampled EuraGovExam images from all five regions; evaluate cross‑regional changes. Disproportionate gains in previously low‑performing regions (especially Japan) would confirm data bias.
