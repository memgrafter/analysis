# Toward Optimal Sampling Rate Selection and Unbiased Classification for Precise Animal Activity Recognition

## Quick Facts
- **ArXiv ID**: 2604.00517
- **Source URL**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.00517
- **Reference count**: 10
- **Primary result**: IBA-Net improves recall by up to 16.98% over baseline on animal activity recognition, with significant gains for minority classes while maintaining high overall accuracy.

## Executive Summary
This paper tackles two critical challenges in wearable sensor-based animal activity recognition: selecting optimal sampling rates for different behaviors and mitigating classifier bias caused by class imbalance. The authors propose the Individual-Behavior-Aware Network (IBA-Net), which integrates a Mixture-of-Experts-based Feature Customization (MFC) module to adaptively fuse features from multiple sampling rates, and a Neural Collapse-driven Classifier Calibration (NC3) module that employs a fixed equiangular tight frame classifier to maximize inter-class angles. Evaluated on datasets from goats, cattle, and horses, IBA-Net achieves state-of-the-art performance, notably boosting minority-class recall by 16.98%, 15.78%, and 4.91% over the baseline on each dataset respectively.

## Method Summary
IBA-Net’s architecture combines two novel modules. The MFC module uses a mixture-of-experts gating mechanism to dynamically select and combine features extracted at different sampling rates, allowing the network to leverage behavior-specific temporal resolutions. The NC3 module replaces the standard linear classification layer with a fixed equiangular tight frame (ETF) classifier, which enforces a balanced geometric configuration of class means (equiangular and equal norm) inspired by neural collapse. The ETF classifier is initialized to satisfy these properties and kept fixed during training, thereby reducing bias toward majority classes. The entire network is trained end-to-end, jointly optimizing feature extraction and the gating strategy while benefiting from the unbiased decision boundaries of the ETF classifier.

## Key Results
- State-of-the-art accuracy on goat, cattle, and horse activity recognition benchmarks.
- Substantial recall improvements for minority classes: +16.98% (goats), +15.78% (cattle), +4.91% (horses).
- High overall accuracy maintained alongside reduced class imbalance effects.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The MFC module adaptively weights features from different sampling rates, ensuring that subtle but important activities captured at lower rates are appropriately represented. This addresses the limitation of fixed single-rate approaches. The NC3 module’s ETF classifier imposes an equiangular structure on class prototypes, which prevents the model from gravitating toward majority classes and promotes a more balanced decision boundary. Together, these components allow IBA-Net to exploit multi-rate information effectively while maintaining fairness across classes.

## Foundational Learning
- **Mixture of Experts (MoE)**: A paradigm where multiple subnetworks (experts) are combined via a learned gating function. *Why needed*: Enables dynamic selection of sampling-rate-specific features tailored to each behavior. *Quick check*: What role does the gating network play in MoE?
- **Neural Collapse**: An observed phenomenon in trained deep classifiers where within-class features collapse to their mean and class means form an equiangular tight frame. *Why needed*: Provides theoretical grounding for using a fixed ETF classifier to encourage balanced class geometry. *Quick check*: List the three hallmark properties of neural collapse.
- **Equiangular Tight Frame (ETF)**: A set of vectors that are pairwise equiangular and have equal norm. *Why needed*: Serves as an optimal fixed classifier under neural collapse, maximizing inter-class separation. *Quick check*: How does an ETF differ from an orthonormal basis?
- **Class Imbalance**: A distribution where some classes have significantly fewer training samples than others. *Why needed*: The paper aims to correct bias introduced by imbalance. *Quick check*: Name two common strategies to address class imbalance in classification.
- **Sampling Rate Selection**: The choice of sensor data acquisition frequency for different animal behaviors. *Why needed*: Different activities may require different temporal resolutions; a single rate is often suboptimal. *Quick check*: Why might a high sampling rate be detrimental for energy-constrained wearables?

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component Map**  
Sensor Streams (Multiple Sampling Rates) -> MFC (Mixture-of-Experts Feature Customization) -> Shared Feature Extractor -> NC3 (Equiangular Tight Frame Classifier) -> Predictions  

**Critical Path**  
Raw multi-rate sensor data passes through the MFC module, which produces a fused feature representation. This representation is then processed by a shared feature extractor (e.g., CNN or RNN) and finally classified by the NC3’s fixed ETF classifier. The MFC gating weights and the fixed ETF matrix are the core innovations.

**Design Tradeoffs**  
- *Multi-rate fusion vs. computational overhead*: Processing multiple streams increases memory and compute costs but captures richer behavior-specific patterns.  
- *Fixed ETF classifier vs. flexibility*: Keeping the classifier frozen guarantees the neural collapse geometry but may limit adaptation if the feature space distribution is highly irregular.  
- *Mixture-of-Experts complexity*: MoE introduces additional parameters and may require careful tuning to avoid expert collapse (all inputs routed to one expert).

**Failure Signatures**  
- MFC gating weights become nearly uniform across inputs, indicating the experts are not specializing.  
- ETF classifier yields degenerate boundaries when class means in the feature space are not well-separated, potentially due to poor feature learning.  
- Minority-class recall does not improve despite NC3, suggesting the feature extractor may not be producing sufficiently discriminative embeddings.

**First Experiments**  
1. Train IBA-Net with only a single sampling rate to measure the impact of multi-rate fusion.  
2. Replace the NC3 ETF classifier with a standard learnable linear layer and compare minority-class recall and overall balance.  
3. Visualize the penultimate-layer features (e.g., with t-SNE) to check for neural collapse properties: within-class clustering and equiangular class means.

## Limitations
- Evaluation restricted to three animal species (goat, cattle, horse); generalizability to other animals remains untested.  
- No statistical significance testing or confidence intervals provided for the reported recall improvements.  
- Baseline method details (e.g., specific sampling rates used, degree of class imbalance) are not fully described, limiting reproducibility.

## Confidence
- MFC module adaptively selects sampling rates: **Medium**  
- NC3 module mitigates classifier bias via neural collapse: **Medium**  
- Overall IBA-Net achieves state-of-the-art with large recall lifts: **Medium**  

## Next Checks
