# AdsorbFlow: energy-conditioned flow matching enables fast and realistic adsorbate placement

## Quick Facts
*   **arXiv ID:** 2602.19289
*   **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.19289
*   **Reference count:** 11
*   **Primary result:** Deterministic flow matching with energy conditioning generates valid adsorbate placements in 5 steps (20× fewer than diffusion), achieving 34.1% SR@1 and 61.4% SR@10 on OC20-Dense with the lowest anomaly rate.

## Executive Summary
AdsorbFlow tackles the slow speed of existing generative models for proposing initial adsorbate geometries on catalytic surfaces. It replaces iterative stochastic denoising diffusion with a deterministic ordinary differential equation (ODE) integration process, using conditional flow matching trained on rigid-body (2D translation + SO(3) rotation) configuration pairs. An energy signal is incorporated via classifier-free guidance, steering the flow toward low-energy basins without requiring an explicit, differentiable energy gradient. With only 5 sampling steps, it surpasses prior diffusion-based methods in success rate and anomaly avoidance on a DFT-verified benchmark, while generalizing reasonably to out-of-distribution systems.

## Method Summary
The task is to generate an initial adsorbate placement `x1` (rigid-body pose) on a fixed slab such that, after relaxation with a fast machine learning force field (MLFF), it lies within 0.1 eV of the true DFT-referenced minimum `x0`. AdsorbFlow learns a time-conditional velocity field `vθ(x,t|c)` where the condition `c` includes the relative adsorption energy `E_rel`. The model is trained via rectified flow matching: for a pair (`x0`, `x1`), a linear interpolant path `x_t` is constructed, and `vθ` is regressed to the analytic velocity `dx_t/dt`. At inference, an ODE is integrated backward from noise (`t=1`) to a placement (`t=0`) in a fixed number of steps `K` (default 5). Energy conditioning uses classifier-free guidance: both conditional (`vθ(·|E_rel)`) and unconditional (`vθ(·|∅)`) models are trained, and their outputs are combined at inference with a scale `w` to steer generation. The backbone is an SE(3)-equivariant GNN (PaiNN or EquiformerV2) with a custom rotation head that outputs angular velocity `ω`, ensuring the full rigid-body velocity (`ṫ`, `ω`) is equivariant.

## Key Results
*   On the in-distribution OC20-Dense set (full DFT verification), AdsorbFlow with an EquiformerV2 backbone achieves 34.1% SR@1 and 61.4% SR@10 using only 5 steps, surpassing the diffusion baseline AdsorbDiff (31.8% SR@1, 41.0% SR@10) at all metrics.
*   It attains the lowest anomaly rate (6.8%), indicating generated placements are geometrically valid and relax physically.
*   On 50 out-of-distribution systems, it retains 58.0% SR@10, with a small performance gap (4 percentage points) between its fast MLFF relaxation and full DFT verification.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
The claim is that deterministic ODE integration via rectified flow matching enables high-success placement with an order-of-magnitude fewer steps than stochastic diffusion. The mechanism involves training a velocity field `vθ(x,t|c)` to produce straight-line transport paths between a random prior placement and a target relaxed minimum under a linear interpolant schedule `β(t)=t`. This rectified-flow objective explicitly linearizes the probability paths, making the ODE integration highly accurate even with very few steps (`K=5`). The core assumption is that the rigid-body configuration space admits such well-linearized interpolants and that the learned field approximates the true path. Evidence includes the abstract's 20× step reduction and the paper's finding that fewer steps yield best results. A break condition would be a training distribution that does not cover the manifold of valid placements, leading to invalid trajectories.

### Mechanism 2
Energy conditioning via classifier-free guidance (CFG) steers the deterministic flow toward low-energy basins without needing an explicit energy gradient `∇xE`. During training, the energy label `E_rel` is randomly dropped (probability 0.2) to learn both conditional and unconditional vector fields. At inference, `v_cfg = (1+w)vθ(·|E_rel) - w vθ(·|∅)` increases the conditional density ratio toward lower energies. The energy is embedded via FiLM modulation of node features, not as a potential. The core assumption is that the conditional distribution `p(x0|x1, E_rel)` learned by flow matching is meaningful and that the CFG combination rule effectively biases sampling. Evidence includes Table 1 and page 7, showing improved SR@10 and energy accuracy (`ΔE`) with optimal `w` (e.g., `w=7` for EquiformerV2). A break condition is noisy or uninformative energy labels, or overly high `w` causing mode collapse.

### Mechanism 3
Principled rotation head design, matched to backbone equivariance, enables correct prediction of angular velocity `ω∈so(3)`. For the `ℓ≤1` PaiNN backbone (polar vectors only), a torque aggregation `τ_i = (r_i - r_COM) × v_i` is summed to angular momentum `L`, then `ω = I^{-1}L`. For the `ℓ≤4` EquiformerV2, internal tensor products naturally produce pseudovector features, allowing direct mean-pooling to `ω`. Both preserve SE(3) equivariance. The core assumption is that the backbone's feature space contains sufficient rotational information, with PaiNN requiring explicit geometric aggregation due to its limited `ℓ`. Evidence is the substantial performance gap between backbones (61.4% vs 47.7% DFT SR@10) and the dedicated section on rotation-head design. A break condition is using a mismatched head (e.g., direct pooling for PaiNN), which breaks equivariance.

## Foundational Learning
*   **Concept: Conditional Flow Matching (CFM)**
    *   **Why needed here:** Defines the training objective by constructing interpolant paths `x_t` between a prior and target placement, and regressing the network to the analytic "teacher" velocity. This learns a deterministic vector field whose flow matches the marginal probability path.
    *   **Quick check question:** If you change the interpolant schedule from linear `β(t)=t` to a noisy schedule, what happens to the rectified-flow property and required sampling steps?

*   **Concept: Classifier-Free Guidance (CFG)**
    *   **Why needed here:** To condition generation on continuous energy labels `E_rel` without training a separate energy predictor or requiring a differentiable energy model. Randomly dropping the condition during inference combines conditional and unconditional predictions to steer samples.
    *   **Quick check question:** What is the risk of setting CFG scale `w` too high, and how might that interact with the backbone's representational capacity?

*   **Concept: SE(3) Equivariance in Graph Networks**
    *   **Why needed here:** Adsorbate placement must respect 3D rotational and translational symmetry. The backbone and output heads must transform correctly under global rotations/translations to ensure learned vector fields are physically consistent.
    *   **Quick check question:** Why is predicting angular velocity `ω` (a pseudovector) more challenging than predicting a polar vector in an `ℓ≤1` model like PaiNN?

## Architecture Onboarding
Input Encoder -> Equivariant Backbone (PaiNN/EquiformerV2) -> Rigid-Body Heads (translation & rotation) -> CFG Module (inference combination) -> ODE Integrator (Heun solver, PBC wrap, exponential map)

*   **Critical path:** Process OC20-Dense into (`x0`, `x1`) pairs with `E_rel` → Train CFM model with CFG dropout → Grid-search `w` and `K` at MLFF level → Full DFT verification on selected hyperparameters → OOD evaluation.
*   **Design tradeoffs:** Backbone choice (EquiformerV2 offers higher success but more compute; PaiNN is lighter but needs cautious torque head and lower `w`). CFG scale `w` requires backbone-specific tuning to improve energy accuracy without collapse. Steps `K=5` is optimal; more can degrade. Rigid-body assumption fails for flexible adsorbates.
*   **Failure signatures:** High anomaly rate (>10% @10) suggests invalid geometries from poor `ω` prediction, over-guidance, or distribution shift. Large MLFF→DFT gap (>15 pp) indicates unreliable energy rankings, often from limited backbone expressivity or OOD hardness. Flat SR@k scaling (like AdsorbDiff) indicates mode collapse/diverse samples.
*   **First 3 experiments:**
    1.  Hyperparameter grid search at MLFF level: sweep `w` and `K` to maximize MLFF SR@10 / minimize `ΔE`.
    2.  Backbone ablation with correct rotation heads: train AdsorbFlow-PaiNN (torque aggregation) and AdsorbFlow-EqV2 (direct pooling); compare DFT SR@k/anomaly rate; test catastrophic failure with mismatched heads.
    3.  Out-of-distribution generalization: evaluate on 50 held-out OC20-Dense systems; measure SR@10 drop and MLFF-DFT gap.

## Limitations
*   The rigid-body model cannot represent conformational flexibility of large adsorbates, potentially missing low-energy adsorption states.
*   Dataset split details (exact in-distribution/out-of-distribution system IDs) are not specified, blocking direct performance replication.
*   Key training details (epochs, LR schedule, loss weighting `λ_rot`, prior distribution specifics) are missing, requiring heuristic reconstruction.
*   CFG scale `w` requires careful backbone-specific tuning to avoid mode collapse or reduced diversity.

## Confidence
*   **High:** CFG energy steering effectiveness (clear ablations show SR/ΔE gains).
*   **Medium:** Rectified flow acceleration (results support claim, but corpus evidence is weak; success hinges on linearizability of rigid-body paths).
*   **Medium-High:** Rotation head design rationale (EquiformerV2 vs PaiNN gap validates higher-order equivariance, but novel designs need broader validation).
