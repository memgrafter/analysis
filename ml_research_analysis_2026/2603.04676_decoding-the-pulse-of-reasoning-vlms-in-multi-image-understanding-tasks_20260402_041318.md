# Decoding the Pulse of Reasoning VLMs in Multi-Image Understanding Tasks

## Quick Facts
*   **arXiv ID:** 2603.04676
*   **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.04676
*   **Reference Count:** 20
*   **Primary Result:** PulseFocus, a training-free inference-time method, improves multi-image reasoning accuracy by +3.73% on BLINK and +1.07% on MuirBench for InternVL3.5-8B by structuring output and softly gating attention.

## Executive Summary
This paper identifies a key failure mode in Vision-Language Models (VLMs) performing multi-image reasoning: scattered text-to-image (T2I) attention "pulses" and a positional bias favoring earlier images during chain-of-thought (CoT) generation. To address this, the authors propose PulseFocus, a training-free intervention that imposes a structured output format (`<plan>` and `<focus:Ix>` blocks) and applies a soft attention logit bias to suppress non-target images during focused observation. Evaluated on benchmarks like BLINK and MuirBench, PulseFocus yields consistent accuracy gains, particularly for the InternVL3.5-8B model, by explicitly coupling text references with visual attention and mitigating positional bias.

## Method Summary
PulseFocus operates at inference time without model weight updates. It enforces an interleaved output structure requiring the model to first generate a `<plan>` block that decides which image to examine next, followed by a `<focus:Ix>` block where observations are made only about image `x`. During the generation of tokens within a `<focus:I>` block, a soft attention gating mechanism applies a constant negative bias (`λ=2.0`) to the attention logits for all visual tokens belonging to images *not* in the active focus set. This sharpens T2I attention concentration on the intended image while preserving some cross-image context. The approach includes constraints on token budgets per block and a maximum number of planning cycles.

## Key Results
*   On InternVL3.5-8B, PulseFocus improves accuracy by +3.73% on BLINK (to 54.18%) and +1.07% on MuirBench (to 57.88%) compared to standard CoT.
*   Qualitative analysis shows PulseFocus produces sharper, correctly aligned token-level T2I attention heatmaps, reducing errors in counting and image identity confusion.
*   The method shows consistent gains across subtasks involving counting, attribute comparison, and spatial reasoning in multi-image settings.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1: Structured Prompting Enforces Per-Image Planning
*   **Claim:** The interleaved `<plan>/<focus:Ix>` output structure reduces scattered attention by forcing explicit, sequential examination of images.
*   **Mechanism:** The format constraint obliges the model to first output a plan specifying the next image to inspect (`<plan>`), then generate a block (`<focus:Ix>`) where content must pertain only to that image. This creates a tight coupling between the verbal reference ("Image 5") and the active attention context, decreasing drift to irrelevant images.
*   **Core Assumption:** The base VLM can parse and adhere to the prescribed XML-tag-like structure. Non-compliance breaks the mechanism.
*   **Evidence Anchors:** The abstract states it "forces explicit per-image planning." Section 4.1 says the structure "enforces systematic image examination." Figure 5 shows baseline tokens about "I2" have mixed attention color (to I1), while PulseFocus tokens are uniformly blue (to I2).
*   **Break Condition:** If the model ignores the format constraint, generating free-form CoT or content about the wrong image inside `<focus:Ix>` blocks, the coupling is lost and scattered attention may persist.

### Mechanism 2: Soft Attention Gating Sharpens Focus
*   **Claim:** Applying a negative logit bias (`Δp = -λ`) to non-target image tokens during `<focus:I>` blocks sharpens T2I attention concentration on the referenced image.
*   **Mechanism:** At each decode step within a focus block, the attention logits toward visual tokens of images not in the active focus set `F` are suppressed. This biases the softmax to assign less probability mass to off-target images, aligning dominant attention with the intended image.
*   **Core Assumption:** The model's attention distribution is malleable at decode-time to a constant bias. Suppression must be strong enough to overcome positional bias but not so strong as to prevent necessary cross-image comparisons.
*   **Evidence Anchors:** Section 4.2 describes "soft gating reduces but does not eliminate attention." Section 3.2/Figure 3 shows baseline positional bias (earlier images get more attention); PulseFocus reduces this. Section 6.1/Figure 1 shows uniform attention color (orange) in a `<focus:I5>` block versus multicolored baseline.
*   **Break Condition:** If `λ` is too low (~0), suppression is ineffective. If too high (>>2.0), the model may lose necessary context from other images, harming comparative reasoning tasks.

### Mechanism 3: Rotating Focus Counters Positional Bias
*   **Claim:** The interleaved structure combined with gating indirectly counters positional bias by rotating the focus across images, distributing cumulative attention more evenly.
*   **Mechanism:** Each `<focus:Ix>` block gates to a specific image, and the `<plan>` step determines the next target (potentially any position). This explicit rotation prevents attention from languishing on early-positioned images. The planning step may also encourage checking later images a biased baseline would neglect.
*   **Core Assumption:** The model's planning step will choose a diverse sequence of images, not just early ones. Requires the planner to not be irrevocably biased.
*   **Evidence Anchors:** Section 4.1 mentions "systematic, image-by-image reasoning." Section 3.2/Figure 3 shows mean attention share per image position becomes less steep with PulseFocus. Section 6.2 notes baseline repeatedly examined I2 even when attention was on I1; PulseFocus's plan explicitly moves focus.
*   **Break Condition:** If the internal planner is strongly biased toward early images, `<plan>` blocks will repeatedly select I1/I2, and overall attention distribution remains biased despite gating. Gating sharpens *within* a block but does not dictate *which* images get a block.

## Foundational Learning
*   **Concept:** Text-to-Image (T2I) Attention in Autoregressive Decoding
    *   **Why needed:** PulseFocus directly manipulates T2I attention logits during decoding. Understanding that the decoder attends to a fused sequence of text and image tokens via cross-attention is essential to grasp where and how the bias `Δp` is applied.
    *   **Quick Check:** In an autoregressive VLM decoder, when generating the next text token, what are the possible sources (key/value positions) the model can attend to, and how are image tokens represented in that sequence?
*   **Concept:** Positional Bias in Multi-Image Inputs
    *   **Why needed:** A core failure mode is that earlier images get more attention regardless of relevance. PulseFocus's rotating focus is a direct intervention against this bias. Recognizing the bias's existence is prerequisite to understanding the intervention.
    *   **Quick Check:** If a VLM shows degraded performance on a 6-image task when the target object is in Image 6 vs. Image 2, and attention analysis shows higher mass on Image 2, what is the likely primary cause described in the paper?
*   **Concept:** Training-Free Inference-Time Intervention
    *   **Why needed:** PulseFocus modifies the decoding process (prompt structure + attention logits) without weight updates. This contrasts with fine-tuning. The engineer must understand changes are confined to the inference graph.
    *   **Quick Check:** What are the two main levers PulseFocus uses at inference time, and which one is *not* a form of learned parameter modification?

## Architecture Onboarding
*   **Component Map:**
    Image Encoder -> Vision-Language Projector -> LLM Decoder (Transformer) + PulseFocus Controller (state machine) + Attention Logit Modifier (hook)
*   **Critical Path:**
    1.  Input images + question are embedded and concatenated with special tokens (including image placeholders).
    2.  Prefill: Model processes the full prompt (images + question) with standard causal attention (no gating).
    3.  Decode Step `t`:
        a.  The model's output so far is parsed by the Controller.
        b.  If last block was `<plan>`, set `F = {x}` for next `<focus:Ix>` block.
        c.  If inside a `<focus:Ix>` block, Attention Modifier adds `Δp = -λ` to logits for tokens of images not in `F` (across all heads and layers).
        d.  If in a `<plan>` block, `F = ∅` and no modification.
        e.  Continue until `<plan>` outputs "END" or token budget (max 12 cycles) is exhausted.
*   **Design Tradeoffs:**
    *   Training-free vs. Performance Ceiling: Avoids costly fine-tuning but may not fully internalize the structured pattern, leading to format non-adherence in small models.
    *   Soft vs. Hard Gating: Soft gating (`-λ` bias) preserves some non-focus attention for comparison. Hard gating (zeroing) might be cleaner but could cripple tasks needing holistic view.
    *   Format Rigidity vs. Model Capacity: Strict XML-like tags are easy to parse but may be unnatural for smaller models, causing breakdowns. More flexible natural language is harder to reliably detect.
*   **Failure Signatures:**
    1.  **Format Ignorance:** Model ignores tags and reverts to free-form CoT. Controller state becomes meaningless.
    2.  **Focus Misreference:** Model generates `<focus:I3>` but subsequent tokens discuss image 5. Attention heatmap shows dominant mass on I5.
    3.  **Planning Myopia:** `<plan>` blocks always choose `I1` or `I2`, indicating planner stuck in positional bias.
    4.  **Budget Exhaustion without "END":** Model cycles through images without concluding, hitting the 12-cycle cap. Suggests inability to determine when to stop.
*   **First 3 Experiments:**
    1.  **Replicate Attention Pulse Analysis:** Use InternVL3.5-8B on a held-out MuirBench set. Extract T2I attention mass per image per decode step for baseline CoT. Visually confirm the "scattered pulse" phenomenon exists.
    2.  **Sanity Check Gating Effect:** Run a single sample with PulseFocus (`λ=2.0`). Generate per-token T2I attention heatmap. Verify during a `<focus:I5>` block, >80% of T2I attention mass is on Image 5's tokens, and mass on other images is suppressed vs. baseline.
    3.  **Ablation: Gating Strength:** On BLINK (InternVL3.5-8B), run PulseFocus with `λ ∈ {0.0, 1.0, 2.0, 3.0}`. `λ=0.0` is "Plan-Focus (no gating)". Expect a sweet spot; too low gives no gain, too high may degrade performance by over-suppressing context.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
*   Under what combinations of task type, model capacity, image count, and benchmark does PulseFocus yield statistically significant improvements in accuracy, and when does it degrade performance?
*   How can we improve format adherence to the interleaved `<plan>/<focus:I>` structure, particularly for smaller VLMs that struggle with parsing the required output format?
*   Can the gate strength `λ` be set automatically or adaptively for a given model and task, eliminating the need for manual tuning?
*   Does fine-tuning vision-language models with reinforcement learning (GRPO) to generate interleaved plan-focus outputs lead to further performance gains compared to the training-free PulseFocus?
*   Are the scattered text-to-image attention pulses and positional bias observed in InternVL3.5-8B generalizable to other VLM architectures (e.g., Qwen3-VL, GPT-5) and model scales?

## Limitations
*   Implementation fidelity is uncertain; architecture-specific details for attention logit modification are omitted, and success depends on correctly patching the decoder's caching mechanism.
*   Format robustness is a major concern; smaller models (e.g., 2B) may ignore the prescribed tags, breaking the mechanism entirely.
*   The method's effectiveness depends on the planner choosing diverse images; if intrinsic positional bias overwhelms planning, the rotating focus fails to evenly distribute attention.

## Confidence
*   **Performance claims (High):** Specific accuracy numbers on BLINK/MuirBench for InternVL3.5-8B are clearly reported and reproducible with the given parameters.
*   **Mechanism 1 & 2 (Medium):** Visual evidence (Figures 2, 5) supports attention sharpening, but causality relies on unverified format adherence and correct logit application.
*   **Generalizability (Low):** Results shown only for InternVL3.5 and Qwen3-VL families; unknown if method transfers to other VLM architectures (e.g., LLaVA) or drastically different tokenizers.

## Next Checks

2.  **Cross-image attention quantification:** During `<focus:Ix>` blocks, compute mean T2I attention mass on *non-target* images. Verify it stays between 0.05–0.15 (soft gating range); if mass drops near 0, reduce `λ` (e.g., to 1.0) and re-run subset.
3.  **Architecture portability attempt:** Implement PulseFocus on an open-source VLM with a different projection (e.g., LLaVA-NeXT). Test on 50 MuirBench samples *without training*. A >1% gain would suggest broader applicability; failure would indicate architecture-specific dependencies.
