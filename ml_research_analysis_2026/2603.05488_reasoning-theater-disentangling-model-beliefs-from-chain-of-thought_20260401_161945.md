# Reasoning Theater: Disentangling Model Beliefs from Chain-of-Thought

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2603.05488  
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.05488  
- **Reference count:** 40  
- **Primary result:** Activation attention probes reveal that large language models know answers far earlier than they verbalize them and often generate unnecessary chain‑of‑thought; probes enable early exit with large token savings while preserving accuracy.

## Executive Summary
We present a method to distinguish between performative chain‑of‑thought (where the model already holds the answer but continues to generate deliberate‑sounding reasoning) and genuine step‑by‑step reasoning. Using activation‑based attention probes, forced‑answer decoding, and a textual CoT monitor on DeepSeek‑R1 (671B) and GPT‑OSS (120B), we find that on easy recall tasks (MMLU) the model’s final answer is decodable from mid‑layer residual streams much earlier than a monitor can infer it from the text, consistent with performative CoT. On a hard multi‑hop benchmark (GPQA‑Diamond) the three signals progress in tandem, indicating genuinely incremental reasoning. Inflection points (backtracking, realizations) occur predominantly in traces with low or shifting probe confidence, suggesting these textual markers track true uncertainty. Well‑calibrated probes allow adaptive computation: exiting when confidence exceeds 95% saves ~80% tokens on MMLU and ~30% tokens on GPQA‑Diamond with less than a 3% accuracy drop.

## Method Summary
The authors train a lightweight attention‑pooling probe on each layer of the target model. The probe ingests residual‑stream activations up to a given token and produces a probability distribution over the four answer choices. During training, prefixes of the full reasoning trace are sampled randomly to encourage the probe to generalize across reasoning lengths. To compare internal versus external signals, they implement two baselines: (1) Forced answering – the CoT is truncated at successive positions and a JSON‑style prompt is injected to elicit an immediate answer from the same model. (2) CoT monitor – an external LLM (Gemini‑2.5‑Flash) reads the partially generated reasoning and predicts the final answer or flags inflection steps. For any given question, the accuracy of each method is tracked as a function of relative position in the trace. The difference in the rates of improvement (slopes) between the probe/forced methods and the monitor quantifies “performativity”. Probe confidence trajectories are also correlated with the occurrence of monitor‑detected inflection points. Finally, probe calibration (confidence vs. accuracy) is evaluated and used to define an early‑exit strategy: once probe confidence crosses a threshold, generation stops and the probe’s prediction is taken as the final answer.

## Key Results
- On MMLU, probe accuracy rises sharply early and far exceeds CoT Monitor accuracy; on GPQA‑Diamond, all three curves rise together. This confirms performative CoT on easy recall and genuine reasoning on hard multi‑hop tasks.
- Inflection points appear roughly twice as often in traces without sustained high probe confidence, linking them to genuine uncertainty.
- Probes are well‑calibrated on MMLU; early exit at 95% confidence reduces token generation by ~80% on MMLU and ~30% on GPQA‑Diamond with minimal accuracy loss (<3%).

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### 1. Detecting Performative CoT via Early Decoding Discrepancy
The model’s final answer becomes linearly decodable from activations well before it is verbally expressed. A large early accuracy gap between the internal probe and the external monitor indicates that the model already knows the answer but continues to generate unnecessary reasoning steps. This gap is prominent for tasks that rely primarily on memory (MMLU) and vanishes for tasks that require sequential thinking (GPQA‑Diamond), where the answer emerges only after multiple reasoning steps.

### 2. Inflection Points as Indicators of Genuine Belief Updates
Verbalized inflections (backtracking, realizations, reconsiderations) are much more frequent in traces where the probe’s confidence is low or changes substantially. This suggests that such textual markers correspond to true uncertainty resolution rather than performative embellishment. However, the correlation is not always consistent, and the temporal order between internal shifts and detected inflections can vary.

### 3. Probe‑Calibrated Adaptive Computation
Activation probes can estimate the model’s own correctness with high reliability—probe confidence correlates strongly with actual accuracy. By exiting as soon as confidence crosses a high threshold (e.g., 95%), we can skip most remaining tokens while maintaining comparable performance. This early‑exit mechanism is particularly effective on tasks where answers are internally available early (MMLU).

## Foundational Learning

**Activation Probing**  
*Why needed:* Directly decodes the model’s internal answer representation from residual activations, bypassing the need to analyze output text.  
*Quick check:* Why might a single‑token linear probe fail whereas an attention‑pooling probe succeeds? Because the answer representation may be distributed across many tokens; attention selectively aggregates relevant evidence.

**Chain‑of‑Thought Monitor**  
*Why needed:* Provides a black‑box, textual baseline for detecting implicit answers in the CoT, enabling contrast with internal signals.  
*Quick check:* What two Gricean maxims are implicitly violated when performative CoT occurs? The maxim of quantity (unnecessary steps) and the maxim of relation (irrelevant information).

**Calibration Curve & Early Exit Tradeoff**  
*Why needed:* Validates that probe confidence is meaningful and quantifies the token‑accuracy trade‑off for adaptive computation.  
*Quick check:* If a probe is perfectly calibrated, what does a confidence of 0.8 imply about the probability the prediction is correct? Exactly 0.8.

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component map:**  
Data collection → Activation extraction → Probe trainer → Early decoder / Forced answer runner / CoT monitor → Early exit controller.

**Critical path:**  
1. Collect model responses (with CoT) on a dataset and cache full residual activations.  
2. Train one attention probe per layer using random prefixes from the training set; select the best‑performing layer on a validation split.  
3. For test traces, evaluate probe, forced answer, and CoT monitor accuracy positions.  
4. Compute performativity by comparing accuracy‐rate differences (probe vs. monitor).  
5. Correlate inflection labels with trajectory of probe confidence.  
6. Evaluate probe calibration; for varying confidence thresholds, record accuracy and token savings for early exit.

**Design tradeoffs:**  
- Later‑layer probes yield higher accuracy but increase per‑token compute cost.  
- Direct transfer from MMLU to GPQA works due to shared format, yet calibration degrades on harder tasks; fine‑tuning on a few examples brings negligible benefit.  
- Inflection‑window size strongly influences the observed correlation between probe shifts and textual inflections, making results sensitive to this choice.

**Failure signatures:**  
- Probe collapse: linear probes fail while attention‑pooling succeeds, indicating token‑wise aggregation is necessary.  
- Transfer gap: probe accuracy on GPQA rises more slowly than on MMLU, highlighting task‑dependent belief emergence.  
- Calibration decay: probes become overconfident on GPQA for GPT‑OSS, risking premature exits.  
- Inflection ambiguity: no consistent temporal ordering between probe confidence shifts and monitor‑detected inflections across models.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
1. **Temporal relationship** between internal belief updates and verbalized inflections remains unclear; evidence of simple correlation is absent.
2. **Predictors of performative CoT**: what makes a model exhibit performative reasoning on some tasks but not others? Correlations exist but underlying mechanisms are opaque.
3. **Robust activation monitors**: can hybrids of textual analysis and activation probing serve as reliable, real‑time safety auditors? This is still speculative.
4. **Generality of probes**: calibration likely fails on out‑of‑distribution reasoning (e.g., free‑form math, coding) and across model families; broader evaluation is needed.

## Limitations
- Probe training uses only 20 GPQA examples for fine‑tuning, which is insufficient for robust transfer.
- Results rely on a single external monitor (Gemini‑2.5‑Flash); alternative judges or ensembles may yield different inflection detection rates.
- Missing critical details (total layer counts, activation normalization specifics, prefix sampling distribution) hinder exact replication.
- Scope limited to two closed‑source models; open‑source alternatives might behave differently.

## Confidence
- **Performative CoT detection** (probe leads monitor on easy recall): **High**
- **Inflection as indicator of genuine reasoning**: **Medium** – correlation present but not causal.
- **Early exit feasibility**: **High** – demonstrated strong calibration and large token savings on MMLU.
