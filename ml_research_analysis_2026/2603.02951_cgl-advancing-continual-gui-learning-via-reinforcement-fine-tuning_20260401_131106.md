# CGL: Advancing Continual GUI Learning via Reinforcement Fine-Tuning

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2603.02951
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.02951
- **Reference count:** 40
- **Primary result:** CGL achieves state-of-the-art average step-wise accuracy (82.33%) and trajectory accuracy (38.03%) on AndroidControl-CL with minimal forgetting (FM = -0.02), and demonstrates positive backward transfer (FM = +0.13) under a different task order.

## Executive Summary
Continual GUI learning (CGL) addresses the challenge of training graphical user interface agents to adapt to sequentially updated applications without catastrophically forgetting previously learned skills. The paper identifies that Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT) enables fast adaptation but overwrites prior knowledge, while Reinforcement Learning (GRPO) inherently preserves skills but adapts slowly. The proposed CGL framework synergistically combines both via three mechanisms: Error-Aware Routing, Entropy-Regulated Tuning, and Gradient Surgery. Evaluated on a new AndroidControl-CL benchmark, CGL consistently outperforms SFT, GRPO, and replay baselines, achieving a superior balance between new-task adaptation and old-task retention, with some configurations even showing positive backward transfer.

## Method Summary
CGL is a hybrid framework that jointly optimizes a multimodal large language model (MLLM) policy using both supervised fine-tuning (SFT) and group relative policy optimization (GRPO). For each new sequential task, the system generates rollouts with the current policy. An Error-Aware Router checks if the maximum rollout reward falls short of the perfect score; if so, it invokes SFT on ground-truth demonstrations to provide corrective knowledge. Otherwise, training proceeds with GRPO alone. The SFT loss weight λ is dynamically adjusted by an Entropy Regulator based on policy entropy to manage exploration-exploitation. Crucially, before applying the SFT gradient, a Gradient Surgery unit projects it onto the subspace orthogonal to the GRPO gradient if the two are conflicting (negative cosine similarity). This final combined update is applied to the MLLM parameters.

## Key Results
- CGL (QwenVL2.5-3b) achieves **82.33% average step-wise accuracy** and **38.03% average trajectory accuracy** on AndroidControl-CL, surpassing all baselines.
- It maintains **minimal forgetting**, with a Forgetting Measure (FM) of **-0.02** under the standard task order.
- Under a different task sequence, CGL exhibits **positive backward transfer** (FM = **+0.13**), indicating beneficial knowledge transfer to earlier tasks.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
**Mechanism 1: Error-Aware Routing**  
- **Claim:** Dynamically invokes SFT updates only when GRPO exploration fails, preventing unnecessary interference.  
- **Mechanism:** For each instruction, if the maximum reward among K sampled rollouts is less than the maximum possible (r_max), the update is routed to SFT using the ground-truth demonstration. This injects corrective knowledge only when RL signals are uninformative.  
- **Core assumption:** When all rollouts fail, advantage estimation degenerates, providing no useful gradient.  
- **Evidence anchors:** [abstract] "Error-Aware Routing to dynamically invoke SFT when GRPO exploration fails"; [section 5.1] "If the maximum reward falls short of the ideal full score... we route the update to a supervised fine-tuning (SFT) step".  
- **Break condition:** When at least one rollout achieves r_max, routing is bypassed and GRPO is used exclusively.

**Mechanism 2: Entropy-Regulated Tuning**  
- **Claim:** Modulates the SFT weight λ based on policy entropy to balance exploration and exploitation across tasks.  
- **Mechanism:** During a warmup phase, λ is linearly increased to inject entropy and break biases. During convergence, λ decays exponentially with policy entropy H, allowing GRPO to refine the policy with minimal SFT interference.  
- **Core assumption:** SFT updates induce entropy injection (negative covariance with log-probabilities), while GRPO updates induce entropy decay (positive covariance).  
- **Evidence anchors:** [abstract] "Entropy-Regulated Tuning to adjust SFT weight based on policy uncertainty"; [section 5.2] specifies λ(H) = (λ_max - λ_min) min(1, ke^{γH}) + λ_min and describes the "entropy rise-then-decay" pattern.  
- **Break condition:** When policy entropy stabilizes below a threshold, λ decays fully to λ_min, minimizing SFT contributions.

**Mechanism 3: Gradient Surgery**  
- **Claim:** Resolves parameter-level interference by projecting conflicting SFT gradients onto GRPO’s direction.  
- **Mechanism:** Compute cosine similarity cosα between ∇L_SFT and ∇L_GRPO. If cosα < 0 (conflict), modify SFT gradient: ∇L_SFT* = ∇L_SFT - ( (∇L_SFT·∇L_GRPO)/||∇L_GRPO||² ) ∇L_GRPO. This removes the adversarial component while preserving orthogonal information.  
- **Core assumption:** Negative cosine similarity indicates SFT updates would erase knowledge stabilized by GRPO.  
- **Evidence anchors:** [abstract] "Gradient Surgery to project conflicting SFT gradients onto GRPO’s direction"; [section 5.3] "We define a conflict as occurring when the cosine similarity... is negative... we surgically remove the component of the SFT gradient that opposes the GRPO direction".  
- **Break condition:** When cosα ≥ 0, no projection is applied; the raw SFT gradient is used.

## Foundational Learning
**Concept: Catastrophic Forgetting in Continual Learning**  
- **Why needed:** CGL targets sequential GUI task learning where parameter updates for new tasks must not overwrite old skills. Understanding forgetting mechanisms (e.g., in SFT) is prerequisite to evaluating anti-forgetting claims.  
- **Quick check:** Given two training stages, how would you quantify performance drop on the first task after learning the second? (Answer: Forgetting Measure (FM) comparing post-first-task accuracy to post-second-task accuracy on Task 1.)

**Concept: Policy Entropy in Reinforcement Learning**  
- **Why needed:** Entropy is the key signal for Entropy-Regulated Tuning. High entropy indicates exploration; low entropy indicates exploitation. Understanding this correlation is necessary to follow the dynamic λ adjustment.  
- **Quick check:** What happens to policy entropy when an RL update reinforces already high-probability actions? (Answer: Entropy decreases, as the distribution becomes more peaked.)

**Concept: Gradient Surgery/Projection in Multi-Task Optimization**  
- **Why needed:** Gradient Surgery is a core technical mechanism. Understanding that gradient directions can conflict (negative dot product) and that projection can remove conflicting components is essential to grasp how CGL stabilizes training.  
- **Quick check:** If two loss gradients have a negative dot product, what does that imply about their update directions? (Answer: They point in approximately opposite directions; applying both would cause interference.)

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map:**  
  Base MLLM (processes screenshot + instruction + history) -> GRPO module (samples rollouts, computes advantages) & SFT module (computes demonstration loss) -> Error-Aware Router (decides routing) & Entropy Regulator (adjusts λ) & Gradient Surgery Unit (projects ∇L_SFT if conflict) -> Final Optimizer (applies combined gradient step).

- **Critical path:**  
  1. For a batch of instructions, generate K rollouts with current policy.  
  2. Compute rewards and normalized advantages for GRPO.  
  3. Router checks: if max(advantages) < r_max, load SFT demonstrations.  
  4. Compute ∇L_GRPO and (if routed) ∇L_SFT.  
  5. Entropy Regulator determines λ based on current entropy H and training step.  
  6. Gradient Surgery conditionally modifies ∇L_SFT if cosine similarity with ∇L_GRPO is negative.  
  7. Update parameters: θ ← θ - α(∇L_GRPO + λ·∇L_SFT_modified).

- **Design tradeoffs:**  
  - Computational overhead from reward computation and gradient surgery is modest compared to rollout generation.  
  - Hyperparameter sensitivity: λ_max, λ_min, γ, warmup steps control entropy schedule; requires tuning per model scale.  
  - Dependency on GRPO stability: If GRPO diverges, the anchor gradient for surgery is unreliable.

- **Failure signatures:**  
  - High Forgetting Measure (FM): Indicates ineffective gradient surgery or mis-specified λ schedule.  
  - Low average accuracy: Suggests insufficient SFT injection (λ too low) or excessive forgetting from surgery.  
  - Entropy not decaying: λ decay too slow or GRPO advantage signal weak.  
  - Router always routing: GRPO unable to solve tasks (poor reward shaping or exploration failure).

- **First 3 experiments:**  
  1. **Replicate main result:** Run CGL on QwenVL2.5-3b with Task Order 1. Verify Avg Step-Acc ≥ 82.0% and FM ≈ -0.02.  
  2. **Ablation of Gradient Surgery:** Disable surgery (±G-Surg). Expect FM to degrade (e.g., -0.52 vs -0.02), confirming surgery’s role.  
  3. **λ sensitivity sweep:** Test fixed λ=1, 0.2, 0.01 vs. dynamic λ. Dynamic λ should outperform, validating entropy-regulated schedule.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- **Open Question 1:** What factors cause CGL to achieve positive backward transfer (FM > 0) and how can this be reliably induced? The paper reports a rare +0.13 FM under Task Order 2 but provides no explanation.
- **Open Question 2:** How does CGL scale to longer task sequences (e.g., 20+ tasks)? Evaluation uses only 7 app-category tasks; scalability of entropy dynamics is untested.
- **Open Question 3:** Are the hand-designed entropy regulation schedules optimal? The λ scheduling (linear warmup, exponential decay) is heuristic with no comprehensive sensitivity analysis or alternative strategies explored.
- **Open Question 4:** Is GRPO uniquely suited for CGL's synergy mechanisms? No comparison to other RL algorithms (e.g., PPO, DQN) is presented.

## Limitations
- Unreleased code and data splits hinder exact replication of AndroidControl-CL partitioning and reward implementation.
- Hyperparameters (λ, γ, warmup) are only validated for QwenVL2.5-3b; transferability to other MLLMs is unknown.
- Gradient conflict threshold (cosα<0) lacks sensitivity analysis; over- or under-projection risks are unquantified.
- Evaluation confined to AndroidControl-CL; generalizability to other GUI domains or sequential lengths is untested.

## Confidence
- **SOTA performance and minimal forgetting:** High – consistent gains over multiple strong baselines (SFT, GRPO, replay).
- **Error-Aware Routing reduces unnecessary SFT:** Medium – ablations show improvement, but routing trigger may be highly reward-shaping sensitive.
- **Entropy-Regulated Tuning balances stability/plasticity:** Medium – Tab. 6 indicates benefit, but schedule may not transfer across model scales or task domains.
- **Gradient Surgery prevents gradient conflict:** Low – relies on a single ablation; no direct measurement of gradient alignment before/after surgery or alternative conflict metrics.
