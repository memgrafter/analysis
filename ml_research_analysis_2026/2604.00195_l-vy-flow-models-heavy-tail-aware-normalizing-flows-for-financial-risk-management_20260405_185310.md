# Lévy-Flow Models: Heavy-Tail-Aware Normalizing Flows for Financial Risk Management

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2604.00195
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.00195
- Reference count: 4
- One-line primary result: Lévy-Flows with Variance Gamma and Normal-Inverse Gaussian bases substantially improve heavy-tail modeling in financial risk management, achieving 69% NLL reduction and exact 95% VaR calibration versus Gaussian flows.

## Executive Summary
This paper addresses the challenge of accurately modeling heavy-tailed distributions in financial risk management using normalizing flows. The core innovation introduces Lévy-Flows, which replace the standard Gaussian base distribution with Lévy process-based distributions (Variance Gamma and Normal-Inverse Gaussian) that naturally capture heavy tails while preserving tractable likelihood evaluation. Comprehensive experiments on S&P 500 daily returns and three additional assets demonstrate substantial improvements in both density estimation and risk calibration metrics.

The method provides theoretical guarantees showing that tail indices are preserved under asymptotically linear flow transformations, and that identity-tail Neural Spline Flows preserve arbitrary base tail shapes outside the transformation region. The approach enables efficient reparameterized sampling for end-to-end training and shows that different Lévy bases excel at different risk tasks, with VG providing the strongest density fit and NIG offering the most accurate tail-loss estimates.

## Method Summary
Lévy-Flows replace the standard Gaussian base distribution in normalizing flows with Lévy process-based distributions (Variance Gamma and Normal-Inverse Gaussian) to better capture heavy-tailed financial returns. The method uses fixed-parameter base distributions with Neural Spline Flow transformations, employing identity-tail structure to preserve base tail shapes outside the transformation region. Training uses reparameterized sampling for differentiable likelihood computation, with data standardized to unit scale. The approach is evaluated on S&P 500 daily returns (6,514 observations) using negative log-likelihood, VaR backtesting with Kupiec and Christoffersen tests, and Expected Shortfall estimation.

## Key Results
- VG-based flows reduce test negative log-likelihood by 69% relative to Gaussian flows
- VG-based flows achieve exact 95% VaR calibration (violation rate of 4.9% vs 7.2% for Gaussian)
- NIG-based flows provide most accurate Expected Shortfall estimates with only 1.6% underestimation compared to 10.4% for Gaussian

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: Identity-tail Neural Spline Flow architecture preserves base distribution tail shape exactly outside the spline region.
- Mechanism: NSF uses identity mapping (f(x) = x) for |x| > B, so for any base distribution the tail decay rate is preserved exactly beyond the transformation bound.
- Core assumption: The base distribution has a continuous density and the flow transformation is identity for |x| > B.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "we show that the identity-tail structure of Neural Spline Flows preserves the base distribution's tail shape exactly outside the transformation region"
  - [section] "Proposition 1(Identity-Tail Preservation for Arbitrary Bases)... let f:R→R satisfy f(x) = x for all |x| > B (identity tails). Then p_X(x) = p_Z(x) for all |x| > B."
- Break condition: If the flow architecture uses non-identity tails (e.g., linear tails instead of identity) or if the base distribution has discontinuities in its density.

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: For regularly varying (power-law) bases, tail index is preserved under asymptotically linear flow transformations.
- Mechanism: Theorem 1 proves that if base distribution has tail P(Z > x) ~ x^(-α)L(x) and flow is asymptotically linear with lim x→∞ f(x)/x = c > 0, then transformed distribution maintains the same tail index α.
- Core assumption: Base distribution has regularly varying tail and flow is bi-Lipschitz on [x_0, ∞) and asymptotically linear.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "for regularly varying bases the tail index is preserved under asymptotically linear flow transformations"
  - [section] "Theorem 1(Tail Index Preservation under Asymptotically Linear Flows)... Let f:R→R satisfy: 1. f is strictly increasing and continuously differentiable for large x, 2. f is bi-Lipschitz on [x_0,∞) for some x_0 > 0, 3. f is asymptotically linear: lim x→∞ f(x)/x = c > 0."
- Break condition: If flow transformation is not asymptotically linear or if base distribution does not have regularly varying tail.

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: Different Lévy base distributions excel at different risk tasks - VG for density fit and 95% VaR calibration, NIG for Expected Shortfall accuracy.
- Mechanism: VG provides semi-heavy tails with closed-form density suitable for overall fit, while NIG offers flexible skewness and kurtosis control via inverse Gaussian subordination that better captures tail-loss severity.
- Core assumption: The empirical data exhibits the specific tail characteristics that each Lévy distribution models best.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "VG-based flows reduce test negative log-likelihood by 69% relative to Gaussian flows and achieve exact 95% VaR calibration, while NIG-based flows provide the most accurate Expected Shortfall estimates"
  - [section] "Comprehensive experiments on S&P 500 daily returns and additional assets show that Lévy-Flows substantially improve both density estimation and risk calibration: VG-based flows reduce test negative log-likelihood by 69% relative to Gaussian flows and achieve exact 95% VaR calibration... while NIG-based flows provide the most accurate Expected Shortfall estimates"
- Break condition: If the target distribution's tail characteristics do not align with the strengths of VG or NIG bases.

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map: Data -> Standardization -> Base Lévy Distribution (VG/NIG) -> NSF Transformation (4 layers, 8 bins) -> Target Distribution -> Log-likelihood computation

- Critical path:
  1. Standardize input data using training set statistics
  2. Sample from base Lévy distribution via reparameterization
  3. Apply NSF transformation to obtain target distribution
  4. Compute log-likelihood with Jacobian correction
  5. Optimize flow parameters via backpropagation

- Design tradeoffs:
  - Fixed base parameters vs. joint optimization: Fixed parameters isolate the effect of base family choice but may miss optimal fit
  - Tail bound selection: B=5.0 balances flow flexibility over observed data range with tail preservation
  - Flow depth and width: 4 layers with 64-dimensional hidden layers chosen based on density estimation needs

- Failure signatures:
  - Numerical instability in Bessel function evaluation for VG log-density computation
  - Poor density fit when tail bound is too small (flow cannot model body adequately)
  - Over-conservative risk estimates when tail bound is too large (identity-tail region shrinks)

- First 3 experiments:
  1. Train Lévy-Flow (VG) on S&P 500 returns and verify 69% NLL improvement over Gaussian baseline
  2. Implement rolling-window VaR backtesting at 95% confidence level and check for exact calibration
  3. Compare tail behavior on log scale between Lévy-Flow and Gaussian-Flow to visualize tail preservation

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

- Why unresolved: The experiments are limited to univariate daily returns; multivariate extension remains theoretical

### Open Question 2
- Question: What is the relative contribution of the Lévy base distribution versus the flow transformation when both parameters are jointly optimized?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] Section 5.1.2 states "Design choice: We fix base distribution parameters ϕ and train only the flow parameters θ" and notes joint optimization "is left for future work"
- Why unresolved: The ablation study fixes base parameters, so cannot separate base-family effect from base-parameter optimization
- What evidence would resolve it: Joint optimization experiments comparing fixed-parameter versus learned-parameter Lévy bases against fixed-parameter versus learned-parameter Gaussian bases, measuring relative NLL improvements

### Open Question 3
- Question: Which Lévy base distribution (VG vs NIG) is preferable depends on the specific risk management task (VaR vs ES vs density estimation), but what determines this task-specific performance?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The conclusion states "different Lévy bases excel at different risk tasks" and the experimental results show VG excels at NLL and 95% VaR while NIG excels at ES
- Why unresolved: The paper identifies the pattern but does not explain the theoretical or structural reasons for task-specific performance differences
- What evidence would resolve it: Analysis of the mathematical properties of VG vs NIG distributions (tail decay rates, skewness control mechanisms) and how these interact with the flow transformation to affect specific risk metrics under different market conditions

## Limitations

- Fixed base parameters sacrifice some density fit accuracy compared to joint optimization approaches
- Numerical stability challenges in modified Bessel function evaluation for VG density computation
- Theoretical tail preservation guarantees depend on asymptotic conditions that may not hold perfectly in finite-sample settings

## Confidence

- **High**: Empirical improvements in NLL reduction (69% vs Gaussian) and VaR calibration accuracy are well-supported by comprehensive experiments across multiple assets and time periods.
- **Medium**: The theoretical tail preservation results under asymptotically linear flows, while mathematically rigorous, depend on assumptions that may be violated in practical implementations with finite tail bounds.
- **Medium**: The claim that Lévy parametric structure (not simply heavier tails) drives improvements is supported by the Student-t comparison, but could benefit from additional ablations testing other heavy-tailed distributions.
