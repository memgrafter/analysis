# Benchmarking Zero-Shot Reasoning Approaches for Error Detection in Solidity Smart Contracts

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2603.13239
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.13239
- **Reference Count:** 36
- **Primary Result:** Zero-shot Chain-of-Thought (CoT) and Tree-of-Thought (ToT) prompting substantially increase recall (≈95–99%) in binary vulnerability detection but typically reduce precision; Claude 3 Opus with ToT achieves the best weighted F1-score (90.8) for error classification.

## Executive Summary
This paper evaluates zero-shot prompting strategies—standard, Chain-of-Thought (CoT), and Tree-of-Thought (ToT)—across multiple large language models (LLMs) for detecting vulnerabilities in Solidity smart contracts. Using a dataset of 400 contracts, the study finds that structured reasoning prompts (CoT/ToT) significantly improve recall in binary error detection, often at the cost of precision, indicating a trade-off toward higher sensitivity and more false positives. For fine-grained error classification, ToT with high-capacity models like Claude 3 Opus yields the best performance (Weighted F1: 90.8). The effectiveness of reasoning prompts is highly model-dependent, with larger frontier models generally benefiting while smaller models suffer degradation.

## Method Summary
The authors assembled a dataset of 400 Solidity smart contracts with ground-truth binary vulnerability labels and SWC (Smart Contract Weakness Classification) categories. They designed prompt templates for three zero-shot conditions: standard instruction, CoT (requesting step-by-step reasoning), and ToT (asking the model to enumerate and compare plausible vulnerability types). These prompts were used to query a range of LLMs (e.g., GPT-4.1 series, Claude 3 series). Model outputs were parsed to extract binary decisions and multi-class classifications, which were then evaluated against ground truth using precision, recall, F1-score, and weighted F1 for multi-class. The experimental design isolates the effect of prompt structure without few-shot examples.

## Key Results
- CoT and ToT prompts substantially increase recall in binary vulnerability detection (often 95–99%) but typically reduce precision, indicating a precision-recall trade-off.
- Claude 3 Opus with ToT achieves the highest weighted F1-score (90.8) for error classification among all model-prompt combinations.
- The impact of reasoning prompts is model-dependent: larger frontier models (e.g., GPT-4.1, Claude 3 Opus) generally benefit from CoT/ToT, while smaller models (e.g., Claude 3 Haiku, GPT-4.1-nano) suffer severe performance degradation.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1
- **Claim:** Zero-shot CoT and ToT prompts increase recall in binary vulnerability detection by encouraging exhaustive hypothesis generation, but typically reduce precision.
- **Mechanism:** Structured reasoning prompts instruct the model to produce intermediate steps or explore multiple reasoning paths, reducing oversight of subtle issues (higher recall) but also raising speculation (more false positives).
- **Core assumption:** LLMs can follow reasoning instructions, and more thorough exploration correlates with greater sensitivity.
- **Evidence anchors:** The abstract notes recall gains of ≈95–99% with precision loss; Table 3 in Section 4.1 shows consistent recall increases across models under CoT/ToT; neighboring work in the corpus focuses on zero-shot detection, making the evaluation of reasoning prompts novel.
- **Break condition:** For models with insufficient capacity (e.g., Claude 3 Haiku, GPT-4.1-nano), CoT/ToT degrade both recall and precision (Haiku CoT recall: 10.26%, F1: 16.81), indicating instability under multi-step reasoning.

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim:** Model capacity strongly moderates the effect of reasoning prompts; larger frontier models generally benefit from CoT/ToT, while smaller models suffer performance degradation.
- **Mechanism:** High-capacity models maintain coherent multi-step reasoning, whereas smaller models produce inconsistent or off-track traces, leading to net F1 loss.
- **Core assumption:** Larger models possess greater reasoning ability and instruction-following robustness.
- **Evidence anchors:** The abstract states that larger models benefit while smaller ones degrade; Section 4.1 Table 3 shows GPT-4.1 zero-shot F1 78.83 vs CoT 78.79 (small drop) and ToT 78.06 (slight drop), while GPT-4.1-mini: 74.71 → 74.19 → 73.46; GPT-4.1-nano: 65.53 → 67.31 → 67.25 (some gain but lower absolute). Claude 3 Haiku: 54.82 → 16.81 → 23.18 (collapse). Section 4.3 explicitly notes smaller models are more fragile under multi-step prompting.
- **Break condition:** When model scale falls below a threshold (e.g., nano/mini tiers), CoT/ToT often cause severe F1 reduction or instability.

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim:** Tree-of-Thought prompting improves multi-class vulnerability classification for high-capacity models by enabling hypothesis comparison and disambiguation.
- **Mechanism:** ToT asks the model to enumerate plausible vulnerability categories and justify the final choice, reducing confusion between fine-grained SWC types.
- **Core assumption:** Accurate classification requires explicit consideration of alternatives; tree-structured reasoning supports this.
- **Evidence anchors:** The abstract reports Claude 3 Opus with ToT achieves weighted F1 90.8; Section 4.2 Table 4 shows Claude 3 Opus ToT (90.8) > CoT (89.9) > zero-shot (87.1); Section 4.3 suggests ToT yields improvements for models like GPT-5 by helping disambiguate similar hypotheses.
- **Break condition:** For models with limited capacity or poor taxonomy alignment, ToT may introduce label drift and degrade metrics (e.g., GPT-4.1 ToT weighted F1 67.2 vs zero-shot 73.4).

## Foundational Learning
- **Zero-shot prompting:** Providing only an instruction without examples; needed here as the baseline condition to isolate the effect of reasoning structure.
  - Quick check: What defines a zero-shot prompt? (Answer: The model receives only an instruction without any labeled examples.)
- **Chain-of-Thought (CoT) reasoning:** Explicitly requesting intermediate reasoning steps before the final answer; evaluated as a core strategy to improve model reasoning.
  - Quick check: How does CoT differ from standard zero-shot prompting? (Answer: CoT includes a directive to produce step-by-step reasoning, whereas standard zero-shot asks only for a final answer.)
- **Precision-Recall trade-off:** The inverse relationship between precision (correctness of flagged vulnerabilities) and recall (coverage of actual vulnerabilities); central to interpreting detection results.
  - Quick check: Why might increasing recall lead to lower precision? (Answer: A more sensitive model flags more contracts as vulnerable, capturing more true positives but also more false positives.)

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component map:** Dataset loader -> Prompt generator -> Model inference client -> Response parser -> Evaluation module

**Critical path:** For each (contract, prompt) pair: (1) load contract source and ground truth from dataset, (2) format prompt with contract code, (3) query selected LLM via API, (4) parse model output to extract binary decision and/or vulnerability class, (5) compare to ground truth, (6) accumulate metrics, (7) aggregate over full dataset.

**Design tradeoffs:** Zero-shot vs. few-shot (chose zero-shot to avoid example bias); model selection (balanced cost, availability, capacity); prompt complexity (longer reasoning prompts risk context limits or confusing small models); evaluation metrics (micro/macro/weighted for multi-class; paper uses weighted F1).

**Failure signatures:** Outputs not conforming to expected format (e.g., missing final decision); incoherent or repetitive reasoning traces; extreme precision drops on small models under CoT/ToT; high variance across repeated inferences (not assessed); models producing reasoning without clear classification.

**First 3 experiments:**
1. Replicate binary detection for GPT-4.1 across all three prompts to verify the precision-recall trade-off described in Section 4.1.
2. Replicate error classification for Claude 3 Opus ToT to confirm the weighted F1-score of 90.8 (Table 4).
3. Analyze reasoning stability: run ToT inference multiple times on a small sample to assess consistency of predicted vulnerability types and identify self-correction patterns from case studies in Section 4.3.

## Limitations
- Dataset size (400 contracts) limits statistical power and generalizability.
- Evaluation restricted to a small set of LLMs; results may differ for other architectures or newer models.
- Only zero-shot prompting evaluated; no comparison with few-shot or fine-tuned approaches.
- Prompt templates are handcrafted; sensitivity to alternative wording is unknown.
- No statistical significance testing or variance estimation across multiple runs.
- Causal reasons for observed breakpoints (e.g., Haiku collapse) remain unclear.

## Confidence
- CoT/ToT increase recall but reduce precision in binary error detection: **High**
- Model capacity moderates reasoning prompt effects (larger models benefit, smaller degrade): **High**
- Tree-of-Thought improves multi-class classification on high-capacity models (e.g., Claude 3 Opus): **Medium**
- Generalizability beyond evaluated models and dataset: **Low**
