# Diagnosing LLM Reranker Behavior Under Fixed Evidence Pools

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2602.18613
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.18613
- Reference count: 33
- Primary result: LLM rerankers exhibit weak correlation with BM25 (Kendall τ 0.19–0.41) and lower lexical coverage at small K; redundancy patterns differ by model.

## Executive Summary
Standard evaluation of rerankers confounds the ranking policy with the quality of the retrieved evidence. To isolate the policy effect, this study uses a fixed evidence pool of eight documents drawn from Multi-News clusters. It compares three LLM rerankers (Llama3.1-8B-Instruct, Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct, GPT-5-mini) against BM25 and MMR across 345 clusters. The analysis reveals that LLM rankings are only weakly correlated with BM25, provide lower lexical coverage for small K, and differ in how they balance redundancy: Llama implicitly diversifies at larger budgets, GPT becomes more redundant, and Qwen lies in between. These findings attribute performance differences directly to ranking decisions rather than retrieval variance.

## Method Summary
The experiment removes retrieval variance by constructing a fixed eight‑document pool for each query. Queries are the first editorial summary sentence (≤400 characters) from Multi-News clusters with at least eight sources; each document snippet is the first 600 characters of its source. For clusters with more than eight sources, eight are deterministically sampled. All rankers receive the same shuffled pool. Baselines include BM25 (default parameters), MMR (λ=0.7 using Jaccard similarity), and random. LLMs are prompted to return JSON with {"ranked_indices": [...]}; temperature is set to 0 for open‑source models; GPT-5-mini uses default API settings. Tokenization involves lowercasing, removal of stopwords and pure‑digit tokens. Evaluation metrics cover lexical coverage, redundancy, summary recall, and semantic coverage/redundancy using MiniLM-L6-v2 embeddings, plus Kendall τ and top-3 Jaccard on full rankings. Paired bootstrap (10k) provides 95% confidence intervals.

## Key Results
- LLM rankings show weak correlation with BM25 (Kendall τ 0.19–0.41).
- At K=3, lexical coverage is lower for LLMs than for BM25/MMR by a gap of 0.05–0.09.
- Redundancy patterns diverge: Llama reduces redundancy as K grows, GPT increases it, and Qwen is intermediate.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
By fixing the evidence pool, the design eliminates any variability from the retrieval system and forces all methods to operate on identical content. This isolates the ranking policy—the ordering induced by a scorer—as the sole source of differences. Weak correlation with BM25 indicates that LLMs do not simply mimic lexical scoring; they incorporate higher‑order signals that may prioritize semantic relevance or diversity. Lower lexical coverage at small K suggests LLMs may select documents that cover the query less exhaustively upfront, perhaps to avoid redundancy or to improve semantic coverage. The divergent redundancy trends reveal model‑specific implicit biases: some models (Llama) incorporate diversification naturally, others (GPT) converge on similar content, and a third (Qwen) balances between. Thus, the controlled setup directly attributes observed performance gaps to the ranking policy itself.

## Architecture Onboarding
Component map: Data Preparation → Baselines (BM25, MMR) → LLM Rankers (Llama, Qwen, GPT) → Metrics Engine → Statistical Comparison

Critical path: 1) Construct fixed evidence pools; 2) Run each ranker to produce full ordering; 3) Compute metrics at various K; 4) Aggregate across clusters with bootstrap; 5) Compare against baselines.

Design tradeoffs: Fixed eight‑document pool simplifies isolation but may not reflect typical retrieval sizes; tokenization choices (stopwords, digit removal) affect coverage scores; LLM prompt design influences ranking behavior; using a single shuffle seed risks position bias.

Failure signatures: Invalid JSON output from LLMs (need robust parsing); low reproducibility due to undocumented seeds; baseline metrics deviating from expected values (e.g., BM25 coverage far from 0.8) indicating tokenization mismatch; high variance across random seeds for pool selection.

First experiments: 
1. Replicate BM25 and MMR with the described tokenization and verify lexical coverage at K=3 matches ~0.8. 
2. Evaluate LLM rankers with at least three different deterministic pool selection seeds to assess stability of Kendall τ and coverage gaps. 
3. Vary the prompt’s document labeling (e.g., "[1]" vs. "Document A:") and confirm whether the reported redundancy patterns persist.

## Limitations
- Undisclosed seeds for deterministic document sampling and shuffling; results may vary with different seeds.  
- Precise tokenization rules (stopword list, digit filtering) are unspecified, potentially affecting lexical metrics.  
- The accessibility and exact version of the “gpt-5-mini” model are unclear; substitution may alter behavior.

## Confidence
- Weak LLM–BM25 correlation: Medium  
- Lower lexical coverage at small K: Medium  
- Divergent redundancy patterns across models: Low

## Next Checks
1. Baseline replication: Re-run BM25 and MMR with the described tokenization; verify lexical coverage at K=3 matches the paper’s baseline (~0.8).  
2. Seed sensitivity: Repeat the full evaluation with 3 different deterministic selection seeds (e.g., 0, 42, 123) and compute variance in Kendall τ and coverage gaps.  
3. Prompt robustness: Test alternative document labeling schemes (e.g., “Doc A:” vs. “[1]”) and confirm whether the model‑specific redundancy trends persist.
