# Is Evaluation Awareness Just Format Sensitivity? Limitations of Probe-Based Evidence under Controlled Prompt Structure

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2603.19426
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.19426
- Reference count: 15
- Primary result: Standard probe-based evidence of "evaluation awareness" primarily reflects sensitivity to benchmark-canonical surface structure, not genuine contextual understanding; paired training that decorrelates format and context reveals a context-specific signal that generalizes across formats.

## Executive Summary
This paper examines whether probe-based evidence of "evaluation awareness" in large language models (LLMs) reflects a genuine contextual understanding of being evaluated versus being deployed, or merely sensitivity to the surface structure of benchmark prompts. The authors design a controlled 2×2 dataset that independently varies the usage context (evaluation vs. deployment) and the prompt format (benchmark-structured vs. casual free-form). They train linear probes in two regimes: (1) Standard, where training data confounds format and context (benchmark prompts are evaluation, casual prompts are deployment); and (2) Paired, where the training data decorrelates the two factors by mixing formats within each context. Results show that Standard probes fail to generalize: deployment prompts rewritten into benchmark format are misclassified as evaluation 94.5% of the time, demonstrating that the probe tracks format rather than context. In contrast, Paired probes succeed: they correctly classify such format-shifted deployment prompts as deployment (only 7.2% false positive) and identify conversational evaluation prompts as evaluation (100%). Additionally, both probe types classify adversarial safety benchmarks as deployment (0% evaluation), ruling out safety-related content as the signal.

The findings reveal a critical confound in prior probing studies: because evaluation data typically originates from structured benchmarks, probes can latch onto spurious structural cues (e.g., option labels, rigid sections) that are predictive of the evaluation label. The Paired training results confirm that a context-aligned signal exists and is linearly separable when format is controlled, though its precise nature (e.g., meta-cognitive, task-related) remains unknown. The work underscores the necessity of careful experimental design in probing and calls for caution in interpreting probe-based claims about contextual awareness in LLMs.

## Method Summary
The study constructs a controlled 2×2 dataset matrix crossing usage context (Evaluation vs. Deployment) and prompt format (Benchmark-structured vs. Casual free-form). Sources: MMLU/ARC/GSM8K for Bench-Eval, ShareGPT first turns for Casual-Deploy, MuTual first turns for Casual-Eval, and ShareGPT queries rewritten into benchmark templates (via DeepSeek-V3) for Bench-Deploy. Length histograms are matched between Bench-Eval and Casual-Deploy using 30 percentile bins. Activations from Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct (last token, layers 8–31) are extracted. Linear logistic regression probes are trained under two regimes: (1) Standard: confounded contrast (Bench-Eval vs. Casual-Deploy); (2) Paired: decorrelated contrast ((Bench-Eval ∪ Casual-Eval) vs. (Bench-Deploy ∪ Casual-Deploy)). Probes are evaluated on held-out format-shifted prompts (Bench-Deploy, Casual-Eval) and safety benchmarks (AdvBench, HarmBench). The primary metric is the percentage classified as "Evaluation", with false positive rate on Bench-Deploy indicating format sensitivity.

## Key Results
- Standard probes misclassify 94.5% of Bench-Deploy prompts as Evaluation, demonstrating a strong format confound.
- Paired probes generalize correctly: only 7.2% false positive on Bench-Deploy and 100% true positive on Casual-Eval.
- Both probe types classify safety benchmark prompts as Deployment (0% Evaluation), indicating the learned signal is unrelated to safety content.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The failure of standard probes stems from a confound: in typical data collections, evaluation prompts are always in benchmark format (multiple-choice, structured), while deployment prompts are free-form. This makes "benchmark structure" a perfect proxy for the "evaluation" label. Linear probes, which optimize for linear separability, pick up on the most salient feature in the activations—here, structural cues like option labels "A.", "B.", or rigid section headers—rather than the abstract context. Consequently, any prompt that exhibits benchmark structure, even if it represents a deployment scenario, activates the probe's "evaluation" response. This explains the 94.5% false positive rate on Bench-Deploy prompts.

When the confound is broken via Paired training—by providing the probe with both formats for each context—the probe can no longer rely on format. To achieve high accuracy, it must instead latch onto features that consistently correlate with context across format variants. The success of Paired probes (near-perfect generalization on format-shifted examples) demonstrates that such context-specific signals exist in the model's representations and are linearly separable under proper conditioning. However, this signal is not directly characterized; it may encode meta-cognitive states, task-specific processing, or distributional artifacts.

The robustness of the format bias suggests a deep-seated "structural prior" in the model: due to its training on numerous benchmark-style prompts, the network has learned to associate rigid, template-following input patterns with an "evaluation mode". Controls for length, genre, and formality fail to disrupt this bias, indicating that the cue is specifically about structural rigidity rather than superficial stylistic features. Even after removing answer leakage and matching length histograms, the format effect persists across all layers, pointing to a pervasive representation bias.

## Foundational Learning
- **Confound Control in Probes**: Why needed here: The paper's core method uses a 2×2 factorial design to separate format and context; understanding confounds explains why prior probe results are questionable. Quick check: If you train a probe to distinguish "cats" vs. "dogs" but all cat images are on white backgrounds and dog images on black, what might the probe actually be learning?
- **Linear Probe Diagnostics**: Why needed here: The entire analysis uses linear classifiers as a diagnostic tool; understanding what a probe's success/failure implies (and does not imply) about model representations is central. Quick check: If a linear probe achieves 95% accuracy on a task, does that prove the model has a "concept" for that task? What are alternative explanations?
- **Benchmark Dataset Biases**: Why needed here: The paper's diagnosis hinges on how benchmark construction practices (rigid formats) create spurious correlations with context labels. Quick check: Why might multiple-choice benchmark prompts feel different to a model than a free-form user query, even if both ask the same knowledge question?

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map**: Dataset Engine -> Activation Extractor -> Probe Trainer -> Evaluation Harness
- **Critical path**: Dataset Construction → Activation Extraction (all 4 quadrants) → Probe Training (per regime) → Zero-shot Evaluation on Format-Shifted & OOD Safety Prompts → Analysis of Failure/Generalization.
- **Design tradeoffs**:
  - Linear vs. Non-linear probes: Chosen for interpretability; non-linear probes may succeed under confound but are less diagnostic.
  - Single-Contrast vs. Paired Training: Standard single-contrast is confounded; paired requires more data engineering but isolates context.
  - Rewrite fidelity: Using DeepSeek-V3 for format transplants risks artifacts; audit steps are necessary.
- **Failure signatures**:
  - Format Trap: Standard probe classifies >94% of Bench-Deploy as Evaluation.
  - Genre Insensitivity: Casual-Deploy rewritten into formal tone (without structure change) still classified as Deployment (~0% Eval).
  - Safety Anomaly: Safety benchmarks classified as Deployment (0% Eval) by both probe types.
- **First 3 experiments**:
  1. Replicate Format Trap: Train Standard probe (Bench-Eval vs. Casual-Deploy), evaluate on Bench-Deploy. Expect ~95% false positive.
  2. Validate Paired Success: Train Paired probe (decorrelated), evaluate on Bench-Deploy and Casual-Eval. Expect ~7% FP on Bench-Deploy and ~100% TP on Casual-Eval.
  3. Test Safety Boundary: Evaluate both Standard and Paired probes on AdvBench/HarmBench. Expect 0% classified as Evaluation.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- Does genuine evaluation awareness exist in LLMs independent of prompt format? The paper only assesses whether standard probes can isolate context under controls; it does not claim absence or presence.
- Would non-linear probe architectures or other representation analysis techniques reveal evaluation awareness robust to surface structure? The study only uses linear probes and a single backbone, leaving open the possibility of non-linear or distributed encoding.
- How do the findings generalize across model scales, architectures, and training objectives? Results may be specific to Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct; different models could behave differently.
- Can we construct datasets that naturally separate evaluation context from prompt format without relying on artificial rewrites? The study uses proxy constructions via rewrites, which may introduce artifacts; natural orthogonality is untested.

## Limitations
- Exact histogram matching bin edges/sampling and DeepSeek-V3 rewrite parameters are unspecified, risking dataset drift and reproducibility issues.
- Findings rely on a single model (Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct) and linear probes; generalizability to other architectures, scales, and non-linear probes remains unknown.
- The nature of the abstract context signal isolated by paired probes is not characterized; it may conflate meta-cognitive, task-related, or distributional cues.

## Confidence
- Format confound in standard probes: High
- Paired regime success: High
- Safety benchmark anomaly: High
- Existence of abstract context signal: Medium

## Next Checks
1. Cross-Model Replication: Repeat the paired-probe experiment on at least two other model families (e.g., GPT-4, Claude) to test if the context signal is architecture-agnostic.
2. Non-Linear Probe Stress Test: Train a modest non-linear probe (e.g., single hidden-layer MLP) in the standard regime; if it still fails on Bench-Deploy, the format trap is not an artifact of linearity.
3. Signal Ablation via Layer Intervention: Ablate or noise-inject activations from early vs. late layers in the paired probe to localize which stages carry the context signal and whether it survives perturbations.
