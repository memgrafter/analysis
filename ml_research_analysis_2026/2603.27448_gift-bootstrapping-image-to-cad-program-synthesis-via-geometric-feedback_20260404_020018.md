# GIFT: Bootstrapping Image-to-CAD Program Synthesis via Geometric Feedback

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2603.27448
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.27448
- Reference count: 40
- Primary result: GIFT bootstraps image-to-CAD program synthesis via geometric feedback, improving mean IoU by 12% (0.698→0.782) and reducing inference compute by 80% through amortization.

## Executive Summary
Image-to-CAD program synthesis—generating executable CAD code from images—is limited by scarce, syntactically narrow training data. GIFT addresses this by using a pretrained base model to generate multiple candidate programs per image, then employing a geometric verifier to compute IoU against ground truth. Two augmentation mechanisms—Soft-Rejection Sampling (retaining high-fidelity but diverse programs) and Failure-Driven Augmentation (training on rendered near-miss failures)—enrich the training distribution. The offline augmentation process amortizes expensive test-time search, yielding a model that matches the peak performance of best-of-N sampling with 80% less inference compute. On the GenCAD benchmark, GIFT achieves a 12% absolute gain in mean IoU (0.782 vs 0.698) and drastically reduces the amortization gap (from 15.5% to 5.2%). The method requires no extra human annotation or architectural changes, demonstrating a general paradigm for verifier-guided bootstrapping in program synthesis.

## Method Summary
GIFT starts from a base image-to-CAD model (e.g., Qwen-VL-2.5-7B fine-tuned on CAD code). For each training image, it samples multiple candidate programs (budget N∈{8,16,32,64,128}) using varied temperature and top-p. A deterministic geometric verifier (OpenCASCADE/CadQuery) executes each program, renders the resulting 3D model, and computes IoU against the ground-truth geometry. Candidates with IoU in [0.9, 0.99) are kept as Soft-Rejection Sampling (SRS) pairs: the original image is paired with the candidate code, increasing output diversity. Candidates with IoU in [0.5, 0.9) are used for Failure-Driven Augmentation (FDA): the candidate geometry is rendered back into a synthetic image, which is paired with the ground-truth code, teaching the model to recover from visual errors. The original supervised fine-tuning (SFT) data is combined with SRS and FDA samples (total ~370k) to train a new model via standard maximum likelihood. This offline distillation captures the benefits of test-time scaling while eliminating the need for repeated sampling at inference.

## Key Results
- Mean IoU on GenCAD test set improved by 12% absolute (0.698 → 0.782) over a strong SFT baseline.
- Amortization gap (pass@1 vs pass@10) reduced from 15.5% to 5.2%, indicating single-shot quality approaches multi-sample oracle.
- Inference compute reduced by ~80% while matching the peak performance of best-of-N sampling.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
**Soft-Rejection Sampling (SRS)**: The base model’s output distribution contains multiple syntactically distinct programs that produce geometrically similar CAD models (IoU ≥ 0.9 but <0.99). By retaining these as additional training targets for the original image, SRS expands the training distribution and prevents model collapse onto a single syntactic pattern. Break condition: if few or no candidates fall in the [0.9,0.99) range for an input, SRS contributes little.

**Failure-Driven Augmentation (FDA)**: Near-miss failures (IoU in [0.5,0.9)) often correspond to coherent but incorrect geometries. Rendering these back into images creates synthetic training pairs (rendered_image, ground-truth_code) that train the model to denoise visual inputs and correct systematic errors, improving robustness on challenging geometries. Break condition: if failures are random noise rather than structured errors, rendered images may be degenerate and harm learning.

**Amortization of Inference-Time Search**: Expensive test-time sampling and verification are performed once offline to produce the augmented dataset. The new model absorbs the distributional shift toward higher-quality modes, so at inference it requires only a single forward pass. This reduces compute by ~80% while preserving the gains from best-of-N search, as evidenced by the narrowed amortization gap.

## Foundational Learning
**Inference-Time Scaling (ITS)**
- *Why needed*: GIFT’s data generation relies on sampling many candidates per image; understanding how performance scales with compute budget N is essential to interpret the thresholds and the premise that search uncovers latent valid programs.
- *Quick check*: In Table 1, why does Top-1 IoU (0.839) exceed Top-10% Mean IoU (0.734), and what does that gap imply about model calibration?

**Geometric Verification via CAD Kernels**
- *Why needed*: GIFT’s feedback loop depends on a deterministic, accurate geometric kernel (OpenCASCADE/CadQuery) to compute IoU between generated and ground-truth CAD models; this replaces learned rewards or human annotation.
- *Quick check*: Why is IoU computed after normalizing by inertia tensor and testing axis permutations, rather than a simpler mesh-to-mesh distance?

**Data Augmentation for Program Synthesis**
- *Why needed*: GIFT is fundamentally a data augmentation method that creates (image, code) pairs by manipulating the model’s own outputs via geometric feedback, directly addressing the scarcity and narrowness of existing training data.
- *Quick check*: How does FDA’s input augmentation ((rendered_image, ground_truth_code)) differ fundamentally from standard noising techniques like random token dropout in code?

## Architecture Onboarding
Input Image -> CAD-Coder-SFT (base model) -> Sampling Controller (generate K candidates) -> Geometric Verifier (OpenCASCADE executes code, computes IoU) -> Filters: SRS (IoU∈[0.9,0.99)) and FDA (IoU∈[0.5,0.9)) -> Augmentation Pipeline -> Combined dataset (original SFT + SRS + FDA) -> Training Loop (AdamW) -> New image-to-CAD model.

**Critical path**:
1. Start with base model and SFT training set.
2. For each training image, sample K programs at various temperatures/top-p.
3. Execute each program and compute IoU against ground truth CAD.
4. Partition candidates: SRS (high-fidelity) and FDA (near-miss).
5. For FDA, render candidate geometry into an image; pair with ground-truth code.
6. Merge all pairs into an augmented training set.
7. Retrain the base model on this set from scratch or via continued training.

**Design tradeoffs**:
- SRS improves output diversity but does not correct systematic biases; FDA aims for robustness but relies on coherent failure renders.
- Thresholds (τ_valid=0.9, τ_low=0.5) are empirical; setting too high/low shrinks the useful pool or introduces noise.
- Bootstrapping requires significant offline compute to generate and verify ~1M candidates, but this cost is amortized over training.

**Failure signatures**:
- Low valid sample rate in SRS/FDA: base model cannot produce candidates in desired IoU ranges, limiting augmentation.
- Distribution shift in augmented dataset: if synthetic samples dominate, overfitting may occur; monitor complexity stats.
- Kernel execution failures: programs that crash the CAD kernel cannot be verified; may need fallback handling to avoid polluting FDA.

**First 3 experiments**:
1. Replicate SFT baseline: train CAD-Coder-SFT on GenCAD to establish pass@1=0.698 and amortization gap=15.5%.
2. ITS analysis: for a subset, sample with N∈{8,16,32,64,128} and compute IoU distributions to locate threshold percentiles and validate the need for SRS/FDA.
3. Ablation validation: train three models using only SRS, only FDA, and both combined; compare test IoU and amortization gap to confirm complementary effects.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
1. Can GIFT be applied when no geometric verifier or ground truth is available (e.g., "in-the-wild" images)?
2. Are the fixed IoU thresholds (τ_valid=0.9, τ_low=0.5, τ_match=0.99) suitable for all CAD complexities, or should they adapt per sample?
3. Does the GIFT framework transfer to other program synthesis tasks with discrete execution feedback (e.g., unit tests)?
4. How much offline compute is required for bootstrapping, and can we reduce the sampling budget per image while preserving augmentation quality?

## Limitations
- FDA rendering parameters (camera angle, lighting, resolution) are unspecified, hindering faithful reproduction of synthetic inputs.
- The base sampler checkpoint “QwenVL-2.5-7B-CadCoder” used for data generation is not publicly documented; its relation to CAD-Coder-SFT is unclear.
- The procedure for combining SRS and FDA samples into the final 370k dataset (e.g., oversampling, weighting) is not detailed, risking unintended distribution shifts.
- Thresholds tuned on GenCAD may not generalize to other CAD datasets or representations.

## Confidence
- Soft-Rejection Sampling: Medium (ablation shows improvement but depends on base model generating diverse high-IoU candidates).
- Failure-Driven Augmentation: Medium (gains evident on complex geometries, but assumes coherent failure renders).
- Amortization of compute: High (clear 80% inference reduction and drastic amortization gap drop).
- Overall GIFT improvement: High (consistent 12% IoU gain across metrics and ablations).

## Next Checks
