# FLIGHT: Fibonacci Lattice-based Inference for Geometric Heading in real-Time

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2602.23115
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.23115
- Reference count: 40
- Primary result: Monocular camera heading estimation robust to noise/outliers, achieving mAA 0.6193@2° and 0.8223@5° on KITTI in 0.947 ms; integrates into PySLAM, reducing trajectory RMSE by 8% with negligible overhead.

## Executive Summary
FLIGHT introduces a real‑time algorithm for estimating camera heading from monocular video. It generalizes the Hough transform to the unit sphere: each correspondence defines a great circle, and votes are accumulated on a discretization of the sphere using a Fibonacci lattice. Weighted arc‑length intersections and hierarchical sampling (coarse‑to‑fine) provide both robustness and efficiency, followed by non‑linear refinement. On the KITTI dataset using optical flow, FLIGHT achieves state‑of‑the‑art accuracy at sub‑millisecond runtime, and when embedded in PySLAM it reduces trajectory error by 8% with almost no added overhead. The method remains stable even as outlier ratios increase.

## Method Summary
Given a set of point correspondences (e.g., from optical flow), FLIGHT first compensates for known camera rotation (or assumes pure translation), converting each correspondence into a unit‑vector pair. Under the translation‑only model, the true heading lies on a great circle perpendicular to the baseline. The algorithm discretizes the sphere with a Fibonacci lattice and lets each great circle vote for nearby lattice points; the vote weight decays with the angular distance (arc‑length) from the great circle. A coarse lattice with few points (∼1k) quickly identifies promising directions. Those regions are then re‑voted on a finer lattice (∼64k points). The top coarse candidate seed is further refined with a continuous non‑linear optimizer to obtain the final heading. The hierarchical approach dramatically reduces computation while preserving accuracy, and the weighted voting naturally suppresses outliers.

## Key Results
- mAA of 0.6193 at 2° and 0.8223 at 5° on KITTI using optical flow, with per‑frame runtime of 0.947 ms.
- Outperforms existing baselines in the accuracy‑speed trade‑off.
- Integration into PySLAM yields an 8% reduction in monocular trajectory RMSE; runtime is stable as the proportion of outliers increases.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The success of FLIGHT stems from several coupled design choices. First, the sphere is a natural domain for heading (a unit vector), and representing uncertainty via great circles elegantly captures the epipolar constraint after rotation compensation. Second, the Fibonacci lattice provides a quasi‑uniform, low‑discrepancy coverage of the sphere, avoiding clustering pitfalls of regular grids and enabling fast nearest‑neighbor lookups. Third, weighting votes by arc‑length (i.e., the sine of the angular distance to the great circle) yields a smooth influence region that tolerates noisy correspondences; many outlier great circles do not concur at the true heading, so their votes cancel out. Fourth, hierarchical sampling reduces the computational burden: a sparse pass quickly narrows the search space, and a dense pass over a small set of top candidates refines accuracy without exhaustive evaluation. Finally, the non‑linear refinement step exploits the continuous geometry of the sphere to achieve sub‑bin precision. Together, these elements produce a method that is both robust to outliers and extremely fast.

## Foundational Learning
- **Spherical Parameterization**: Camera heading is a 3‑D unit vector; representing it on the unit sphere simplifies geometric reasoning. *Why needed*: Avoids singularities of Euler angles and provides a natural space for voting. *Quick check*: Convert a vector [x,y,z] to spherical angles (θ, φ).
- **Great Circle Voting (Generalized Hough)**: Under pure translation, the valid heading directions for a correspondence lie on a great circle. *Why needed*: Each correspondence contributes a full circle of hypotheses, enabling a Hough‑style accumulation without explicit pairing. *Quick check*: Given two unit vectors a and b (from camera to a point in two frames), the set of translation vectors t such that a×t and b×t are collinear? The geometric condition is that the direction of t lies on the great circle defined by a and b.
- **Fibonacci Lattice (Sphere Sampling)**: A deterministic low‑discrepancy sequence that spreads points almost uniformly over a sphere. *Why needed*: Uniform coverage ensures no region is systematically under‑represented, which is crucial for fair voting. *Quick check*: For N points, generate φ = arccos(1 - 2k/N), θ = πφ (or similar spiral formulas) for k=0…N‑1.
- **Weighted Evidence Accumulation**: Votes are weighted by the sine of the angular distance to the great circle, giving a smooth fall‑off. *Why needed*: Soft voting reduces quantization effects and provides robustness to small angular errors. *Quick check*: For a lattice point v and great circle normal n, weight = 1 – |v·n|? (the absolute sine of the angle between v and the circle plane).
- **Hierarchical Sampling & Non‑Linear Refinement**: A coarse‑to‑fine strategy (sparse then dense lattice) reduces cost; final continuous optimization increases accuracy. *Why needed*: Balances speed and precision, avoiding a prohibitively dense full‑sphere search. *Quick check*: How would you define the region around a coarse peak to resample on the dense lattice? ## Architecture Onboarding
**Component map:**  
Input (correspondences) -> Preprocess (rotation compensation, unit vectors) -> Great Circle Computation -> Coarse Lattice Voting -> Candidate Selection (top K bins) -> Fine Lattice Voting -> Non‑Linear Refinement -> Heading Output

**Critical path:**  
1. Extract matched points between two frames (e.g., from optical flow).  
2. For each correspondence, compute the two unit vectors and derive the great circle (its normal = cross product of the vectors).  
3. Generate sparse Fibonacci lattice (∼1k points). For each lattice point, accumulate votes from all great circles based on arc‑length distance.  
4. Identify the bin(s) with highest vote total.  
5. Generate a denser lattice (∼64k points) centered on the top coarse bin(s). Repeat voting with the same weighting.  
6. Take the highest‑voted dense bin as initialization for a continuous optimizer (e.g., Levenberg‑Marquardt) that minimizes the negative log‑likelihood of the votes.  
7. Output the refined unit vector as the camera heading.

**Design tradeoffs:**  
- *Lattice resolution*: Sparse lattice too coarse may miss the true peak; dense lattice too large hurts runtime.  
- *Number of hierarchy levels*: More levels can reduce work but risk losing the global optimum if coarse peak is wrong.  
- *Voting weight shape*: Sharp cut‑off increases outlier rejection but reduces smoothness; softer weighting improves robustness but may spread votes too thinly.  
- *Refinement*: Skipping non‑linear refinement saves time but yields sub‑optimal accuracy.

**Failure signatures:**  
- Very low number of correspondences (<10) leads to ambiguous voting and high variance in estimated heading.  
- High outlier ratio (>70%) can cause the aggregated votes to concentrate on an incorrect region, especially if weighting is not sufficiently robust.  
- Overly sparse lattice (e.g., <1k points) can cause discretization artifacts where the true heading falls between bins and is not amplified by consistent votes.  
- Numerical instability in great circle computation (e.g., nearly parallel vectors) may produce degenerate circles; such correspondences should be filtered.

**First experiments:**  
1. Replicate KITTI evaluation: Use standard splits (e.g., sequences 00–10), same optical flow (e.g., RAFT), and measure mAA@2°/5° and runtime against named baselines (PnP, RANSAC, DROID‑SLAM).  
2. Ablate lattice resolution: Run FLIGHT with varying N (1k, 10k, 100k) on a fixed sequence to plot accuracy‑time trade‑off and confirm the benefit of hierarchical voting.  
3. Test robustness to outliers: Inject synthetic outlier correspondences into a clean sequence and record mAA vs. outlier percentage; compare to RANSAC‑based methods.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- **Joint rotation and translation estimation**: The current method assumes known rotation or pure translation. Extending to unknown rotation requires voting over the combined SO(3)×S² space, likely needing a fundamentally different approach. *Resolution*: A new algorithm that simultaneously samples rotations and directions, with competitive results on 6‑DoF benchmarks.
- **Optical flow vs. feature‑based performance gap**: FLIGHT with flow significantly outperforms SIFT/SURF (e.g., mAA2° 0.6193 vs. 0.3929), but the reason is unexplained. *Resolution*: Controlled experiments isolating feature properties, synthetic tests with matched error characteristics, and analysis of vote accumulation patterns.
- **Adaptive hierarchical sampling**: Fixed parameters (sparse 1k bins, r_l=0.2; dense 64k bins, r_s=0.009) may be suboptimal for varying correspondence counts and outlier ratios. *Resolution*: An adaptive strategy (e.g., scaling bins with N or outlier estimate) validated on diverse sequences, showing an improved accuracy‑speed Pareto frontier.
