# Decorrelating the Future: Joint Frequency Domain Learning for Spatio-temporal Forecasting

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2603.04418
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.04418
- **Reference count:** 37
- **Primary result:** FreST Loss consistently improves state-of-the-art spatio-temporal forecasting baselines on 88.6% of metrics by decorrelating future spatio-temporal states via Joint Fourier Transform, with gains up to 27.2% in MAE.

## Executive Summary
Standard direct spatio-temporal forecasting models rely on point-wise losses like MSE, which assume conditional independence among future spatio-temporal states. This assumption introduces bias by ignoring temporal, spatial, and cross-spatio-temporal correlations inherent in real-world data. The proposed FreST Loss corrects this by aligning predictions and ground truth in the joint spatio-temporal frequency domain using the Joint Fourier Transform (JFT). This approach decorrelates complex dependencies across both space and time, leading to a better match between the optimization landscape and the true data-generating process. Experiments across six real-world datasets show that FreST Loss is model-agnostic and yields substantial performance gains for various backbone architectures, including STGNNs, Transformers, and linear models.

## Method Summary
FreST Loss is a plug-in module that augments any existing direct forecasting backbone (e.g., STGCN, iTransformer). Given historical input \(X \in \mathbb{R}^{T \times N}\) and future target \(Y \in \mathbb{R}^{H \times N}\), the backbone outputs prediction \(\hat{Y}\). The loss computes discrepancies in three spectral domains: (1) FFT along the time horizon (marginal temporal), (2) GFT along the node dimension using the graph Laplacian eigenvectors (marginal spatial), and (3) JFT = FFT \(\otimes\) GFT, applied as \(F \hat{Y} U\) versus \(F Y U\) (joint spatio-temporal). Each spectral \(\ell_1\) loss is adaptively normalized by its detached magnitude and dynamically weighted via learnable parameters. The final loss combines a weighted sum of these normalized spectral losses with the standard time-domain MSE: \(L = (1-\alpha) L_{\text{time}} + \alpha L_{\text{freq}}\). This design provides stable supervision that captures both marginal and joint spectral structures while remaining model-agnostic.

## Key Results
- FreST improves state-of-the-art baselines on 39 out of 44 metrics (88.6%), with notable reductions of 27.2% in MAE for STDN on AIR-GZ and 17.8% for StemGNN on SH-METRO.
- The Joint Fourier Transform (JFT) reduces mean absolute off-diagonal correlation across all three dimensions more effectively than using FFT or GFT alone (joint correlation: 0.189 → 0.049).
- Ablation confirms all three spectral components (FFT, GFT, JFT) contribute positively, and the full composite loss ranks top-2 in 87.5% of metrics.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- **Claim:** Standard point-wise losses (e.g., MSE) introduce bias by ignoring conditional dependencies among future spatio-temporal states.
- **Mechanism:** Minimizing point-wise errors implicitly assumes the future states are conditionally independent. Theorem 3.1 decomposes the bias as omitted correlation terms (\(P^2_{i,n}\)) compared to the true spatio-temporal negative log-likelihood. These terms aggregate temporal, spatial, and cross-spatio-temporal partial correlations.
- **Core assumption:** The true data-generating process exhibits structured autocorrelation (temporal, spatial, cross) that is non-zero and ignored by the independent-error model.
- **Evidence anchors:** [abstract] "Standard direct forecasting models use point-wise losses like MSE, which incorrectly assume future spatio-temporal states are conditionally independent." [section 3.2] Theorem 3.1 mathematically derives the positive bias from omitting correlation terms. [corpus] Corpus aligns with FreDF's theoretical foundation for temporal decorrelation.
- **Break condition:** If future states are truly conditionally independent (e.g., i.i.d. noise), the bias term vanishes and standard MSE is optimal.

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim:** Transforming predictions and ground truth into the joint spatio-temporal frequency domain (via JFT) asymptotically decorrelates all three correlation types, aligning the optimization landscape with the true dependency structure.
- **Mechanism:** The Joint Fourier Transform (JFT) = FFT (temporal) \(\otimes\) GFT (spatial). For a jointly stationary process on the product graph \(G_{st} = G_{\text{time}} \square G_{\text{space}}\), the JFT diagonalizes the joint covariance matrix (Eq. 10). This turns correlated spatio-temporal residuals into approximately independent spectral components.
- **Core assumption:** The spatio-temporal process is jointly stationary on the product graph, allowing asymptotic diagonalization.
- **Evidence anchors:** [abstract] "By leveraging the Joint Fourier Transform (JFT), FreST Loss aligns predictions with ground truth in a unified spectral domain, effectively decorrelating complex dependencies across both space and time." [section 4.3] Derives JFT and links it to product graph stationarity, showing Eq. (10) diagonalizes \(\Sigma_{st}\). [section 4.4] Empirical Figure 1 shows JFT reduces mean off-diagonal correlation across all three dimensions (temporal 0.194→0.035, spatial 0.284→0.073, joint 0.189→0.049) versus time/GFT alone.
- **Break condition:** Non-stationarity or a graph topology not well-approximated by a Cartesian product may limit decorrelation effectiveness.

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim:** A composite loss combining time-domain MSE with normalized \(\ell_1\) spectral losses on FFT, GFT, and JFT components provides stable, balanced supervision that captures marginal and joint spectral structures.
- **Mechanism:** \(L = (1-\alpha)L_{\text{time}} + \alpha L_{\text{freq}}\). \(L_{\text{freq}}\) sums \(\ell_1\) distances in three spectral domains. Adaptive normalization (Eq. 14) prevents any single spectral term from dominating due to magnitude differences, while learnable weights (Eq. 15) allow dynamic focus.
- **Core assumption:** The marginal spectral losses (\(L_{\text{fft}}, L_{\text{gft}}\)) stabilize optimization that might be unstable using only \(L_{\text{jft}}\), by providing direct supervision on pure temporal/spatial modes.
- **Evidence anchors:** [section 5.2] Explicitly defines the three spectral loss terms and the adaptive mixing strategy. [section 6.3.2] Ablation (Table 3) shows all spectral losses improve over MSE, and the full FreST (all three) ranks top-2 in 87.5% of metrics. [section 6.3.3] Gains are larger for shorter horizons where temporal autocorrelation bias is heaviest.
- **Break condition:** If \(\alpha=0\), reverts to biased MSE; if \(\alpha=1\) and \(L_{\text{jft}}\) alone, may cause unstable convergence as noted in the paper.

## Foundational Learning
- **Concept: Spatio-Temporal Graph Signals**
  - *Why needed here:* The input is a matrix \(X \in \mathbb{R}^{T \times N}\) defined on a graph \(G=(V,E)\). All transforms (FFT, GFT, JFT) operate on this structure. Understanding node-wise time series (temporal) and graph topology (spatial) is essential.
  - *Quick check question:* Given a traffic sensor network (graph) with 5-minute readings, what are the "time" and "space" dimensions in the matrix \(X\)?

- **Concept: Fourier and Graph Fourier Transforms**
  - *Why needed here:* FFT and GFT are the building blocks for JFT. FFT decorrelates time; GFT decorrelates space. Their Kronecker product forms JFT.
  - *Quick check question:* How does GFT differ from FFT in terms of basis vectors? (Answer: GFT uses Laplacian eigenvectors; FFT uses complex exponentials.)

- **Concept: Conditional Independence & Autocorrelation Bias**
  - *Why needed here:* The core problem is the mismatch between the independence assumption of MSE and the true correlated joint distribution of \(Y_{\text{true}}\).
  - *Quick check question:* What does "conditional independence across space and time" imply about the covariance matrix of the forecast error under MSE?

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map:** Backbone Model (\(F_\theta\)) -> FreST Loss Module -> Combined Loss (MSE + FreST) -> Backpropagation
- **Critical path:**
  1. Ensure backbone outputs a tensor shaped (batch, horizon, nodes).
  2. Precompute graph Laplacian eigenvectors \(U\) for GFT/JFT (static per dataset).
  3. During training, after forward pass, feed \(\hat{Y}\) and \(Y_{\text{true}}\) into FreST module.
  4. Combine with standard MSE using hyperparameter \(\alpha\).
  5. Backpropagate total loss \(L\).
- **Design tradeoffs:**
  - **\(\alpha\) (frequency weight):** Higher \(\alpha\) increases spectral regularization but risks destabilizing training if too high (see Figure 5). Optimal \(\alpha\) is dataset-dependent (e.g., 0.9 for AIR-BJ).
  - **Graph quality:** FreST's spatial decorrelation (GFT) is only as good as the adjacency matrix. Poor graph (e.g., just physical connections) limits effectiveness (Table 4). Preprocessing the graph (e.g., thresholded Gaussian kernel) is critical.
  - **Computational cost:** JFT involves matrix multiplication with full \(U\) (\(O(N^2)\)) per batch. For large \(N\), consider approximate GFT or sampling.
- **Failure signatures:**
  - **Training instability/divergence:** \(\alpha\) too high, especially early in training. Remedy: start with \(\alpha=0\), gradually increase.
  - **No gain or degradation:** Check graph construction; a meaningless adjacency matrix makes GFT/JFT ineffective. Verify \(U\) is correctly computed (e.g., normalized Laplacian).
  - **Over-smoothing in spectral space:** If all \(\tilde{L}_k\) become very small quickly, model may be ignoring high-frequency details. Monitor individual spectral loss magnitudes.
- **First 3 experiments:**
  1. **Ablation of loss components:** Run baseline (MSE only) vs. +\(L_{\text{fft}}\), +\(L_{\text{gft}}\), +\(L_{\text{jft}}\), and full FreST (all three) on a small dataset (e.g., METR-LA). Confirm all spectral terms help and combination is best (per Table 3).
  2. **\(\alpha\) sensitivity sweep:** For a fixed backbone (e.g., STGCN), train with \(\alpha \in \{0.1, 0.3, 0.5, 0.7, 0.9\}\) on one dataset (e.g., AIR-BJ). Plot validation error vs. \(\alpha\) to find sweet spot (per Figure 5).
  3. **Graph construction impact:** Use the same backbone+FreST with three adjacency matrices (e.g., for SH-METRO: physical connection, DTW similarity, OD correlation as in Table 4). Quantify performance difference to validate graph quality importance.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
### Open Question 1
- **Question:** How can we systematically design or learn optimal graph structures (adjacency matrices) that maximize the benefits of joint spectral supervision (FreST Loss) across diverse spatio-temporal datasets?
- **Basis in paper:** [explicit] The conclusion identifies "investigating the impact of graph construction strategies" as a critical future direction. Section 6.3.6 presents an ablation study on SH-METRO showing that simple physical connectivity underperforms compared to similarity-based graphs, and notes that "the structural quality of the adjacency matrix significantly impacts forecasting accuracy."
- **Why unresolved:** The paper demonstrates that graph structure interacts with FreST's spatial decorrelation but provides no theoretical or empirical framework for selecting or learning the optimal graph for a given dataset and model. The "complex interplay" is observed but not explained.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** A comprehensive study correlating graph properties (e.g., spectral gap, clustering coefficient, edge weight distribution) with FreST's performance gains across multiple datasets. Alternatively, a method for dynamically adapting the graph during training based on gradient signals from the spectral loss components.

### Open Question 2
- **Question:** What is the theoretically optimal mechanism for integrating and weighting the marginal (FFT, GFT) and joint (JFT) frequency components in FreST Loss, and how should this integration adapt during training?
- **Basis in paper:** [explicit] The conclusion states future work will focus on "developing advanced adaptive fusion mechanisms and training-phase-dependent weighting schemes." Section 5.2 acknowledges that while JFT alone *theoretically* encapsulates all information, optimizing solely on it presents "significant engineering challenges," necessitating the current adaptive mixing of all three terms.
- **Why unresolved:** The current adaptive weighting (Eq. 15) is a heuristic based on normalized loss magnitudes. There is no analysis of whether this effectively disentangles coupled spectral signals or if a temporal schedule (e.g., emphasizing JFT later in training) or a theoretically derived weighting (e.g., based on signal power or correlation decay) would be superior.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Ablation studies with alternative fusion strategies (e.g., learned attention over frequency bands, curriculum learning that shifts weight from marginal to joint components). Theoretical derivation of an optimal weighting scheme under assumptions about the underlying spatio-temporal process (e.g., product graph stationarity).

### Open Question 3
- **Question:** What intrinsic properties of a spatio-temporal dataset (e.g., strength and type of correlations, stationarity, graph topology) determine the optimal balancing hyperparameter \(\alpha\) between time-domain and frequency-domain losses?
- **Basis in paper:** [explicit] Section 6.3.5 reports that "the optimal value of \(\alpha\) varies across different datasets" (e.g., ~0.9 for AIR-BJ) and that unifying supervision signals "effectively enhances predictive accuracy," but provides no insight into *why* the optimal point differs.
- **Why unresolved:** The paper establishes that FreST works universally but does not link the required frequency regularization strength to measurable dataset characteristics. This makes hyperparameter tuning dataset-specific and lacks theoretical guidance.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** A correlation analysis between optimal \(\alpha\) (or the relative performance gain from FreST) and quantitative metrics of the dataset: temporal autocorrelation function decay, spatial Moran's I, strength of cross-spatio-temporal correlation (as in Figure 1), or graph spectral properties. This would provide a heuristic or model for setting \(\alpha\) based on data statistics.

### Open Question 4
- **Question:** Under what conditions does FreST Loss's frequency-domain regularization fail to improve performance or cause degradation, and what does this failure reveal about the limitations of the joint stationarity/Product Graph assumption?
- **Basis in paper:** [explicit] The results note "occasional marginal degradations" (e.g., for some models on NYC-Bike) and state that performance variations "coincide with specific structural properties." Section 6.3.6 explicitly calls out that graph construction based on "simple physical connectivity fails to capture complex latent topologies, thereby hindering effective spatial decorrelation."
- **Why unresolved:** The paper does not analyze the failure cases. The theoretical foundation (Section 4.3) relies on the signal being jointly stationary on a product graph. Degradations might occur when this assumption is severely violated (e.g., highly non-stationary signals, irregular sampling, graphs with poor community structure). This boundary is not explored.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Targeted experiments on synthetic data with controlled violations of joint stationarity (e.g., time-varying graph topology, abrupt regime shifts) to identify breakdown conditions. Analysis of residuals on failing datasets to check if the JFT actually fails to decorrelate (e.g., high residual off-diagonal correlations in the joint spectrum).

## Limitations
- **Graph Dependency:** FreST's effectiveness is tightly coupled to the quality of the input graph adjacency matrix; suboptimal graphs (e.g., simple physical connectivity) can degrade performance.
- **Stationarity Assumption:** The theoretical justification for JFT decorrelation rests on spatio-temporal joint stationarity on the product graph, which real-world data often violates due to trends, cycles, or non-stationary graph topology.
- **Computational Cost:** The JFT operation involves \(O(N^2)\) matrix multiplications per batch with the full spatial eigenvector matrix, which can be prohibitive for graphs with thousands of nodes.

## Confidence
- **Bias from Point-wise Losses (Theorem 3.1):** High. Strong theoretical derivation with clear mathematical decomposition and broad empirical support (88.6% metric improvement).
- **JFT Decorrelates Joint Dependencies:** Medium. The link to product graph stationarity is theoretically sound and Figure 1 shows strong empirical correlation reduction, but the "asymptotic" decorrelation claim depends on unverified stationarity in real data; marginal spectral losses also contribute.
- **Composite Loss Stability & Generalization:** Medium. Ablations and model-agnostic gains support the design, but sensitivity to \(\alpha\) and reported instability at high \(\alpha\) indicate the balancing mechanism is not fully robust.
