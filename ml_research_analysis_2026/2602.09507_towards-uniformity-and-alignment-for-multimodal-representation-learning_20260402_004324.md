# Towards Uniformity and Alignment for Multimodal Representation Learning

## Quick Facts
- **ArXiv ID:** 2602.09507
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.09507
- **Reference count:** 40
- **Primary result:** UniAlign resolves alignment-uniformity and intra-alignment conflicts in multimodal contrastive learning, achieving ~2–3 Recall@1 gains on zero‑shot video‑text retrieval and 10–40 FID reduction on cross‑modal generation.

## Executive Summary
InfoNCE-based multimodal representation learning suffers from inherent conflicts: the global uniformity term causes repulsion between aligned positive pairs (alignment‑uniformity conflict), and with three or more modalities, competing alignment pulls can partially cancel (intra‑alignment conflict). This paper introduces UniAlign, a principled framework that resolves both by decoupling intra‑modality uniformity from cross‑modality alignment. Uniformity is enforced within each modality’s batch to maintain representational capacity without cross‑modal interference, while alignment uses a fixed anchor modality to provide a single, consistent pull direction per sample, avoiding vector cancellation. Theoretically, the combined loss serves as a tractable proxy for minimizing a global Hölder divergence across modality distributions. Experiments on zero‑shot video‑text retrieval (MSR‑VTT, DiDeMo, ActivityNet) and audio‑to‑image generation (VGGSound) show consistent improvements over state‑of‑the‑art baselines, accompanied by t‑SNE evidence of reduced modality gaps.

## Method Summary
UniAlign learns a shared embedding space for multiple modalities (e.g., text, image, audio, video) by training with a loss that separates two objectives: (1) **intra‑modality uniformity**, which spreads apart samples within each modality using a Gaussian kernel on pairwise distances to avoid collapse; (2) **cross‑modality alignment**, which anchors one modality (e.g., text or image) and minimizes the Euclidean distance between paired samples in that modality and others. The anchor‑based strategy avoids competing alignment gradients that arise in standard InfoNCE when M≥3, and intra‑modality uniformity prevents cross‑modal repulsion. Optionally, tuple‑level losses can further enforce dispersion of sample centroids and collinearity among modalities via a volume regularizer. Training uses standard encoder backbones, ℓ2‑normalized outputs, AdamW optimizer, and separate temperature hyperparameters for uniformity and tuple‑level terms.

## Key Results
- **Zero‑shot retrieval:** On MSR‑VTT, DiDeMo, and ActivityNet, UniAlign outperforms strong baselines (GRAM, LanguageBind) by ~2–3 Recall@1 points.
- **Cross‑modal generation:** On VGGSound using UnCLIP decoders, FID is reduced by 10–40 compared to InfoNCE‑trained models.
- **Embedding space quality:** t‑SNE visualizations show significantly reduced modality gaps, with embeddings from all modalities overlapping in a common space.
- **Theoretical guarantee:** The method acts as an efficient proxy for minimizing a global Hölder divergence, providing a formal guarantee of distribution alignment.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- **Claim:** Decoupling intra‑modality uniformity from cross‑modality alignment eliminates the alignment‑uniformity conflict.
- **Mechanism:** Standard InfoNCE enforces uniformity globally across all modalities, causing repulsion between aligned positive pairs. UniAlign instead applies uniformity penalties only within each modality’s batch (Eq. 9), avoiding cross‑modal repulsion. Alignment is performed by anchoring one modality and pulling others directly toward it (Eq. 10), so the alignment gradient is not counteracted by uniformity pushes from other modalities.
- **Core assumption:** Hard negatives that cause systemic conflict are distributed similarly across modalities, so intra‑modality uniformity suffices to maintain representational capacity without cross‑modal interference.
- **Evidence anchors:**  
  [abstract] “principled decoupling of alignment and uniformity… simultaneously supports discriminative and generative use cases”  
  [section 2.3] “enforces intra‑modality uniformity… and introduce an anchor‑based alignment strategy… avoids competing alignment directions”  
  [corpus] Weak – neighbors discuss conflict resolution (e.g., “Multi‑level Conflict‑Aware Network”) but not this specific decoupling; evidence is primarily internal.

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim:** Anchor‑based alignment mitigates the intra‑alignment conflict caused by non‑collinear positive pulls when M≥3.
- **Mechanism:** With multiple modalities, standard InfoNCE aggregates alignment pulls from all modality pairs; if positive embeddings are not collinear, these pulls partially cancel (Eq. 4). UniAlign selects a single anchor modality (e.g., image) and aligns every other modality directly to it, so each sample has only one alignment direction. This avoids vector cancellation among competing positive pulls.
- **Core assumption:** The anchor modality’s embedding is sufficiently informative and aligned with the underlying concept; all other modalities can be consistently mapped to it without loss.
- **Evidence anchors:**  
  [section 2.2] “intra‑alignment conflict… non‑collinear positive embeddings… competing alignment directions”  
  [section 3.1] “anchor‑based alignment… enforces a single positive‑pull direction per sample, thereby avoiding non‑collinearity”  
  [corpus] Neighbors (e.g., “Context‑specific Credibility‑aware Multimodal Fusion”) address modal conflict but do not propose anchor‑based resolution.

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim:** Optimizing intra‑modality uniformity and anchor alignment serves as an efficient proxy for minimizing a global Hölder divergence across modality distributions.
- **Mechanism:** The global Hölder divergence (Eq. 14) measures distribution mismatch across M modalities but is intractable. The uniformity term approximates the within‑modality density integral (by encouraging spread within each modality), while the anchor alignment maximizes cross‑modality overlap (by pulling paired samples together). Together they provide a differentiable surrogate that reduces the Hölder divergence.
- **Core assumption:** The kernel density estimator (KDE) accurately approximates the true densities; in the low‑temperature limit, instance matching aligns distributions.
- **Evidence anchors:**  
  [abstract] “theoretically… our method acts as an efficient proxy for minimizing a global Hölder divergence”  
  [section 3.2] “optimizing intra‑modality uniformity together with cross‑modality alignment provides a tractable way to reduce the global Hölder divergence”  
  [corpus] “Distributional Vision‑Language Alignment by Cauchy‑Schwarz Divergence” (94133) also targets distributional gaps but uses CS divergence; UniAlign’s divergence‑based analysis is distinct.

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component map:**  
Input modalities → Modality‑specific encoders (e.g., ViT, BERT, BEATs) → ℓ2‑normalized embeddings → Loss computation:  
 - Intra‑modality uniformity U(Z^m) (per modality)  
 - Anchor‑based alignment L_align (anchor vs. others)  
 - Optional: tuple uniformity U(C), volume loss L_vol  
→ Combined loss → Optimizer (AdamW)

**Critical path:**  
1. Forward: encode all modality inputs → ℓ2‑normalize → compute U(Z^m) for all m and L_align.  
2. Backward: gradients from L_align pull non‑anchor modalities directly toward anchor; gradients from U(Z^m) push within‑modality samples apart only within that modality’s batch.  
3. Optional tuple‑level: compute centroid c_i and apply U(C); compute Gram matrix for L_vol.

**Design tradeoffs:**  
- **Anchor modality choice:** Should be high‑quality and semantically central (often vision/text). Poor anchor degrades all alignments.  
- **Uniformity kernel bandwidth (τ):** Too small → overfitting to local structure; too large → insufficient repulsion. Separate τ_ctr for tuple‑level controls global separability.  
- **Tuple‑level losses (U(C), L_vol):** Complementary but increase compute (Gram determinant O(M^3) per tuple). Use only when stronger cross‑modal collinearity is needed.

**Failure signatures:**  
- **Modality collapse:** One non‑anchor modality embeddings become identical (low within‑modality uniformity) → check U(Z^m) value.  
- **Anchor dominance:** Anchor modality distribution remains distinct while others collapse onto anchor → visualize t‑SNE; monitor within‑anchor uniformity.  
- **Degenerate alignment:** If λ_align too high, within‑modality uniformity may suffer, reducing retrieval discriminability.

**First 3 experiments:**  
1. **Zero‑shot retrieval:** Train on VGGSound‑150K, evaluate Recall@1 for T2V/V2T on MSR‑VTT, DiDeMo, ActivityNet. Confirms decoupled losses improve discriminative performance over InfoNCE and GRAM baselines.  
2. **Cross‑modal generation:** Train on VGGSound, generate images from audio/text using fixed UnCLIP decoders (Kandinsky, Stable UnCLIP). Compare FID; lower FID indicates reduced modality gap.  
3. **t‑SNE visualization:** Plot vision/text/audio embeddings; check that clusters overlap versus being separated. Directly visualizes distribution gap reduction.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
1. **Large‑scale pretraining?**  
   - **Basis in paper:** [explicit] The "Limitation" section states: "Our study mainly focuses on fine‑tuning rather than large‑scale pretraining. Extending the proposed principle to large‑scale pretraining would require substantial compute resources and is left for future work."  
   - **Why unresolved:** The paper only evaluates on relatively small‑scale fine‑tuning experiments. Scaling to large pretraining may introduce new challenges such as training instability, data noise, or changing dynamics of the uniformity and alignment terms.  
   - **What evidence would resolve it:** Train UniAlign from scratch on a large‑scale multimodal dataset (e.g., LAION‑5B) with a large vision‑language model (e.g., CLIP ViT‑H) and evaluate zero‑shot performance on a diverse suite of retrieval and generation benchmarks. Compare convergence speed, final performance, and distribution gap metrics.

2. **Anchor sensitivity?**  
   - **Basis in paper:** [inferred] The anchor‑based alignment is a core component to avoid competing pulls, but the paper does not analyze the impact of different anchor choices; experiments use a fixed anchor without justification.  
   - **Why unresolved:** The theoretical guarantee might depend on the anchor modality. Different anchors may lead to different modality gaps for downstream tasks. For generative models, aligning to the image modality is natural, but for other tasks (e.g., audio retrieval) other anchors could be better.  
   - **What evidence would resolve it:** Conduct systematic experiments with varying anchor modalities across tasks (retrieval: text→audio, video→text; generation: audio→image, text→image). Measure alignment quality (e.g., Hölder divergence), retrieval accuracy, and generation FID. Possibly develop a metric to predict optimal anchor based on task or dataset statistics.

3. **When do tuple‑level losses help?**  
   - **Basis in paper:** [inferred] The paper includes tuple‑level extensions as optional components and shows ablation improvements, but does not characterize when they are most effective or if they are always beneficial.  
   - **Why unresolved:** Tuple‑level losses operate at a different granularity (tuple vs. sample/modality) and may interact with the base losses in complex ways. Understanding their utility could inform practical deployment and cost‑benefit trade‑offs, as they increase computational overhead.  
   - **What evidence would resolve it:** Perform controlled experiments varying the number of modalities M (e.g., 3 vs. 4 vs. 5), dataset diversity, and batch size. For each setting, compare performance with and without tuple‑level losses on metrics like retrieval R@1, generation FID, and distribution gap. Analyze whether tuple‑level losses particularly help when modalities have heterogeneous distributions or when M is large.

4. **Validity of Assumption 2.1?**  
   - **Basis in paper:** [inferred] The theoretical guarantee of conflict reduction relies on Assumption 2.1, which decomposes the uniformity force into a systematic component aligned with the alignment direction and a residual. This assumption is plausible but not empirically validated.  
   - **Why unresolved:** If the assumption is violated (e.g., if c_n is not bounded below by a positive constant, or residuals are not independent), the theoretical convergence of the alignment‑uniformity conflict may not hold. This could affect the robustness of UniAlign’s design principle.  
   - **What evidence would resolve it:** During training, compute the per‑modality uniformity forces Φ_a^{(n)} and project them onto the alignment direction ˆV_a for many samples. Empirically estimate the distribution of c_n and residuals. Check if c_n consistently exceeds a small positive threshold and if residuals are approximately zero‑mean and uncorrelated across modalities. Test UniAlign under synthetic perturbations that violate the assumption and measure performance degradation.

## Limitations
- **Anchor modality not consistently specified:** The paper does not detail which modality should be used as anchor, leaving a key hyperparameter that likely influences performance and generalization.
- **Missing hyperparameters for optional losses:** Coefficients for tuple‑level uniformity U(C) and volume L_vol are not provided, hindering exact replication of results.
- **Theoretical assumptions unverified:** Reliance on kernel density estimation and low‑temperature approximations, plus the assumption that hard negatives are similarly distributed across modalities, may not hold in complex real‑world datasets.
