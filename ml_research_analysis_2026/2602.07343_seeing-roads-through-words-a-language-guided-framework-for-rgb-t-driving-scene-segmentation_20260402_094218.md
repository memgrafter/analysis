# Seeing Roads Through Words: A Language-Guided Framework for RGB-T Driving Scene Segmentation

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2602.07343  
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.07343  
- **Reference count:** 0  
- **Primary result:** New state‑of‑the‑art with 62.3% mIoU on MFNet for RGB‑T driving scene segmentation under adverse illumination.

## Executive Summary
Static RGB‑T fusion methods often propagate noise when illumination degrades (e.g., glare, total darkness), harming segmentation performance. CLARITY addresses this with a language‑guided framework that leverages CLIP embeddings to dynamically route information through a sparse Mixture‑of‑Experts (MoE) fusion, preserving faint object semantics via a soft‑gated unbalanced point transformer, and refining thin boundaries using a self‑calibrated hierarchical decoder. Evaluated on the MFNet dataset, CLARITY achieves 62.3% mean Intersection over Union (mIoU) and 77.5% mean Accuracy (mAcc), setting a new state of the art.

## Method Summary
CLARITY uses two pretrained ConvNeXt backbones as dual encoders for RGB and thermal streams. A frozen CLIP text encoder processes two templated prompts: one describing the illumination condition (e.g., “Total Darkness”) and another generating a full scene caption. The condition embedding `P_cond` is expanded spatially and concatenated with intermediate encoder features; a gating network then computes per‑pixel selection scores over a bank of convolution experts (Sparse MoE). Only the top‑K experts are activated, and their weighted sum yields the fused feature. The scene caption embedding `P_emb` is injected via cross‑attention. The fused features then pass through a Soft‑Gated Unbalanced Point Transformer (SG‑UPT), which computes a per‑pixel soft bias from local RGB intensity statistics to down‑weight noisy or glare‑affected pixels without discarding valid dark objects. Finally, a Self‑Calibrated Hierarchical Decoder with Dilated Feature Aggregation blocks progressively upsamples the features while anchoring each stage to a coarse reference `D_1` via a learnable projector, thereby enforcing structural consistency and sharpening thin boundaries. The model outputs segmentation and edge maps trained with weighted cross‑entropy and focal losses.

## Key Results
- **SOTA performance:** 62.3% mIoU and 77.5% mAcc on MFNet, surpassing prior methods.
- **Ablation gains:** CLIP‑guided gating adds +2.4% mIoU, soft‑gated SG‑UPT contributes +1.3%, and the self‑calibrated decoder further refines boundaries.
- **Robustness under adverse illumination:** The framework shows particular improvement in challenging conditions like total darkness and glare.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1: Dynamic Expert Routing via VLM Conditioning
- **Claim:** The framework dynamically routes pixels to degradation‑specific expert kernels based on scene illumination, preventing noise propagation from a compromised modality.
- **Mechanism:** A pretrained CLIP text encoder encodes a prompt describing the illumination condition (e.g., “Total Darkness”) into a global embedding `P_cond`. This embedding is expanded spatially, concatenated with intermediate RGB and thermal features, and fed to a gating network that computes per‑pixel selection scores over a bank of N convolution experts. The top‑K (K=2) experts are activated; their weighted sum forms the fused feature `F_fused`.
- **Core assumption:** Textual descriptions of illumination conditions (e.g., “Glare”, “Total Darkness”) align semantically with the internal noise distributions in the image pairs, allowing a single embedding to parameterize a pixel‑wise routing policy.
- **Evidence anchors:** The paper states “Guided by vision‑language model (VLM) priors, the network learns to modulate each modality’s contribution based on the illumination state.” (abstract) and details the gating network in Section 2.4. Neighboring works (CAFuser, LDR) also use VLMs for noise‑aware fusion, lending indirect support.
- **Break condition:** If the VLM misclassifies the illumination (e.g., labels a glare scene as “well‑lit”), the gating may select inappropriate experts, potentially worsening fusion.

### Mechanism 2: Soft‑Gated Attention for Noise Suppression without Discarding Valid Dark Pixels
- **Claim:** A soft‑gated attention mechanism reduces the influence of noise‑corrupted pixels (e.g., motion‑blurred RGB regions at night) while preserving faint object semantics (e.g., traffic cones).
- **Mechanism:** An “Unbalanced Map” `M_un` is computed per‑pixel from local variance and mean intensity of the RGB image: `M_un = σ_local(I_RGB) + (1 − μ_local(I_RGB))`. This map is flattened and added to the attention logits of a point transformer as a soft bias `−λ·tanh(M_un^flat)`, softly down‑weighting scores for high‑variance or very dark pixels.
- **Core assumption:** High local variance or extremely low mean intensity in RGB indicates likely artifacts; reducing attention weight (rather than hard masking) mitigates their impact while retaining gradients for valid dark objects.
- **Evidence anchors:** The abstract notes preservation of valid dark‑object semantics; Section 2.3 defines the soft‑gated tanh bias and contrasts it with hard thresholding. Table 3 ablations show soft‑gating (62.3% mIoU) outperforms hard threshold (60.5% mIoU).
- **Break condition:** If `M_un` incorrectly flags sharp, textured regions as corrupted, legitimate features will be down‑weighted, harming segmentation of structured objects.

### Mechanism 3: Self‑Calibrated Hierarchical Decoder for Structural Consistency
- **Claim:** A self‑calibrated hierarchical decoder enforces structural consistency across upsampling stages, preventing feature divergence that blurs boundaries of thin objects (e.g., guardrails).
- **Mechanism:** Let `D_t` be decoder features at stage `t`. Each stage applies a Dilated Feature Aggregation (DFA) block `H_θ` to the upsampled `D_t`. The output is calibrated against a fixed coarse anchor `D_1` via a learnable projector `S_ϕ`: `D_{t+1} = H_θ(Up(D_t) + S_ϕ(D_1))`. This shared reference anchors the decoding trajectory, suppressing error accumulation.
- **Core assumption:** Early encoder features (`D_1`) provide a more globally coherent structural prior than later high‑resolution features; re‑injecting this prior constrains the decoder from drifting due to upsampling artifacts.
- **Evidence anchors:** The abstract mentions “hierarchical decoder that enforces structural consistency across scales.” Section 2.5 explains the calibration against `D_1` and cites the SCI framework. However, direct empirical validation for segmentation is limited.
- **Break condition:** If `D_1` is too coarse or semantically weak, it may fail to provide a useful prior, limiting fine‑detail recovery.

## Foundational Learning

- **Mixture-of-Experts (MoE) for Dynamic Inference**  
  *Why needed here:* Understanding MoE explains how CLARITY replaces a fixed fusion convolution with a conditional, per‑pixel routing mechanism over expert kernels, enabling dynamic adaptation.  
  *Quick check:* In a standard MoE layer, what are the roles of the 'gate' and the 'experts', and how does sparsity (Top‑K) affect computational cost?

- **Vision-Language Models (VLMs) like CLIP**  
  *Why needed here:* CLARITY’s conditioning signal (`P_cond`, `P_emb`) derives from CLIP’s text encoder. Engineers must grasp that CLIP encodes semantic relationships (not literal scene descriptions) and that its outputs are used as feature vectors to modulate network layers.  
  *Quick check:* If CLIP is trained on internet image‑text pairs, what risks does this pose when using it to describe specific technical conditions (e.g., “Total Darkness”) in an autonomous driving dataset?

- **Attention Softmax Temperature & Gating**  
  *Why needed here:* The SG‑UPT mechanism manipulates attention logits with a tanh bias. Understanding that Softmax saturates with large logit differences is crucial; adding a learned bias re‑ranks attention weights softly without creating hard, non‑differentiable masks.  
  *Quick check:* How does adding `−λ·tanh(M_un)` to the attention logits before Softmax differ from multiplying the attention weights by `1 − M_un` after Softmax? What are the gradient flow implications?

## Architecture Onboarding

**Component map (plain text):**  
1. Dual Encoder (two ConvNeXt backbones) → extracts hierarchical features from RGB and thermal.  
2. VLM Prompter (frozen CLIP text encoder) → processes condition and caption prompts → embeddings `P_cond`, `P_emb`.  
3. Sparse MoE Fusion → concatenated encoder features + expanded `P_cond` feed gating network; top‑K experts selected; fused feature `F_fused`; cross‑attention injects `P_emb`.  
4. Soft‑Gated UPT → computes `M_un` from RGB; applies soft‑gated attention to `F_fused`.  
5. Self‑Calibrated Decoder → hierarchical upsampling with Dilated Feature Aggregation blocks; each stage adds calibrated reference from coarse anchor `D_1`.  
6. Heads → segmentation head (weighted cross‑entropy) and edge head (Focal Loss).

**Critical path:**  
Input RGB & thermal images → Dual encoder → VLM embeddings (`P_cond`, `P_emb`) → **Sparse MoE Fusion** (core dynamic fusion) → **SG‑UPT** (noise‑aware refinement) → **Self‑Calibrated Decoder** (boundary sharpening) → Segmentation & Edge outputs.

**Design tradeoffs:**  
- **VLM dependency:** Adds inference latency and requires fixed prompt engineering; trade‑off is gained semantic grounding vs. computational overhead and sensitivity to prompts.  
- **Soft vs. hard gating in SG‑UPT:** Soft‑tanh preserves gradient flow for faint objects but may be less aggressive than hard threshold; requires careful tuning of λ.  
- **Decoder calibration anchor (`D_1`):** Using a shallow feature simplifies optimization but may lack sufficient semantic richness for precise boundary recovery.

**Failure signatures:**  
- **VLM misalignment:** Condition prompt does not match actual degradation (e.g., “Well‑lit” for a night scene) → misleading `P_cond` → inappropriate expert selection. Monitor via prompt accuracy on a validation set.  
- **SG‑UPT over‑suppression:** λ too high → excessive down‑weighting of dark valid pixels → reduced recall for rare classes (e.g., traffic cones). Check per‑class IoU on dark‑object subsets.  
- **Decoder drift:** Calibrator `S_ϕ` underpowered → `D_1` fails to constrain later stages → fragmented boundaries. Visualize decoder feature maps at each stage.

**First 3 experiments:**  
1. **Ablation 1 (Component contributions, Table 2):** Baseline Dual‑Stream (ConvNeXt) 56.5% mIoU. Incrementally add: (a) Sparse MoE with Random Router (+1.2%), (b) CLIP `P_cond` gating (+2.4%), (c) CLIP `P_emb` (+0.9%), (d) SG‑UPT (+1.3%). Isolates each component’s impact.  
2. **Ablation 2 (Soft vs. Hard Gating, Table 3):** Compare original UPT’s hard threshold (T=0.1) against proposed soft‑tanh gating on full model. Soft version yields 62.3% mIoU vs. 60.5% for hard, validating the modification.  
3. **Ablation 3 (Prompt Granularity, Table 4):** Evaluate VLM conditioning granularity: (a) no prompt (condition‑agnostic), (b) binary {Day, Night}, (c) ternary {Day, Overcast, Night}, (d) proposed 5‑condition set. The 5‑condition set performs best, showing necessity of fine‑grained conditioning.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

- **Open Question 1:** How can CLARITY be extended to adverse weather scenarios (e.g., fog, rain, snow) and how does VLM gating influence the diversity of kernel experts? The paper’s conclusions mention this future direction, but weather degradations involve different noise characteristics and the VLM’s ability to discriminate weather conditions and design a diverse expert bank remains unexplored.
- **Open Question 2:** Does CLARITY generalize to other RGB‑T segmentation datasets beyond MFNet? Evaluation is only on MFNet; performance may vary on datasets with different sensors, resolutions, or scene distributions.
- **Open Question 3:** How robust is CLARITY to illumination conditions or object classes not present in its predefined sets (C_cond and C_target)? The framework assumes all test samples belong to fixed categories; out‑of‑distribution conditions/objects may lead to invalid embeddings or mis‑gating.
- **Open Question 4:** What is the optimal granularity of illumination condition categories for MoE gating, and can it be determined automatically? The paper manually defines five conditions; the optimal number/definition is unknown and may require a data‑driven approach.

## Limitations
- Hyperparameters (learning rate, batch size, MoE expert count, MLP dimensions, Mun kernel size) are incompletely specified, blocking exact replication.
- Test‑time prompt generation pipeline is ambiguous (object detection threshold, caption ordering, whether ground truth is used during training), hindering practical deployment.
- Self‑calibrated decoder’s conceptual transfer from a low‑light enhancement framework lacks domain‑specific segmentation validation.

## Confidence
- **CLIP‑guided dynamic fusion:** **Medium** – Relies on VLM‑text alignment; no robustness analysis to misclassification.
- **Soft‑gated unbalanced attention:** **High** – Direct ablation shows clear gain on MFNet.
- **Self‑calibrated decoder:** **Low** – Conceptual adaptation without dedicated ablation evidence.

## Next Checks
1. **VLM misalignment stress test:** Deliberately mismatch condition prompts (e.g., “Well‑lit” for night scenes) and measure mIoU drop to quantify sensitivity to VLM errors.
2. **SG‑UPT λ sensitivity sweep:** Vary λ and evaluate per‑class IoU for dark small objects (e.g., traffic cones) to isolate suppression vs. preservation trade‑offs.
3. **Decoder anchor ablation:** Remove the self‑calibration path (`S_ϕ(D_1)` addition) and compare boundary quality metrics (e.g., Boundary F‑score) to verify consistency enforcement.
