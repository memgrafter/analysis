# Beyond the Assistant Turn: User Turn Generation as a Probe of Interaction Awareness in Language Models

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2604.02315
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.02315
- **Reference Count:** 21
- **Primary Result:** Interaction awareness, measured by generating plausible user follow-ups, is decoupled from task accuracy and can be significantly increased via collaboration-oriented post-training.

## Executive Summary
This paper introduces a novel evaluation probe—user-turn generation—to assess whether language models encode interaction awareness, meaning the ability to anticipate plausible conversational consequences after an assistant response. Standard benchmarks measure only assistant response correctness. The authors generate a user turn conditioned on a prior user query and the model's own assistant response, evaluating whether the generated user turn is a grounded, context-aware reaction. Across 11 open-weight models and five datasets, they find that task accuracy and interaction awareness are dissociated; high accuracy does not imply awareness. The capability is often latent, surfacing only under higher-temperature sampling, and can be boosted through targeted post-training. This reveals a missing dimension in LLM evaluation and a capacity that can be shaped by training.

## Method Summary
The core task is: given a conversation context `[user query q; assistant response a]`, generate a subsequent user turn `u` under a user-role header. A "genuine follow-up" is a `u` that is a grounded reaction to `a` (e.g., asking a relevant question, providing requested information), as judged by an LLM evaluator (`gpt-5.4-mini`). Primary evaluation uses greedy decoding (`T=0`), with temperature sweeps probing latent capabilities. Experiments cover five datasets (GSM8K, IFEval, IFBench, GPQA main, GPQA Diamond) and eleven models (Qwen3.5 variants, gpt-oss, GLM-4.7). Controlled perturbations (truncating `a`, appending a generic question) test sensitivity to the assistant content. Collaboration-oriented post-training (SFT/RL) on Qwen3.5-2B tests trainability.

## Key Results
- **Accuracy vs. Awareness Dissociation:** Within the Qwen3.5 family, GSM8K accuracy ranges from 41% to 96.8%, yet deterministic user-turn follow-up rates remain near 0% for all models. Other families show similar patterns.
- **Latent Awareness via Temperature:** For models with near-zero greedy follow-up, higher temperature (e.g., 1.0) increases rates substantially (e.g., Qwen3.5-27B to 22% on GSM8K; GLM-4.7 to 35.9% on GPQA Diamond), indicating interaction awareness exists in the distribution but not at the mode.
- **Post-Training Effectiveness:** Collaboration-focused post-training on Qwen3.5-2B raises follow-up rates from ~1% to 46–48% (SFT) and 10% (RL on IFBench), demonstrating the capability is trainable, though SFT comes with a trade-off in task accuracy.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1
- **Claim:** User-turn generation acts as a behavioral readout of interaction awareness encoded in model weights.
- **Mechanism:** By conditionally generating under a user role header after an assistant response, the model's output reveals whether its training shaped it to anticipate plausible conversational consequences. A grounded follow-up indicates the model's forward-looking modeling of the interaction.
- **Core assumption:** The model's generation distribution under a fixed role header reflects training objectives; deviation from task-solving mode toward contextual follow-ups signals learned interaction awareness.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract] Introduces user-turn generation as a probe: "given a conversation context of user query and assistant response, we let a model generate under the user role."
  - [section 2.1] Formalizes the problem: `u = M_θ([q;a])` and defines genuine follow-up as grounded reaction.
  - [corpus] Weak evidence. Neighboring papers focus on multi-turn consistency or user simulation but do not directly probe *assistant-model-generated* user turns as a diagnostic of the assistant's own weights.
- **Break condition:** If models consistently produce prompt restatement or degenerate outputs regardless of assistant content, the probe fails to indicate interaction awareness (as seen in Qwen models at T=0).

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim:** Temperature scaling surfaces latent interaction awareness that is suppressed at the generation mode.
- **Mechanism:** Deterministic (greedy) decoding selects the highest-probability continuation, which may be degenerate due to training objectives favoring single-turn correctness. Higher temperature samples from lower-probability regions, revealing capabilities present in the distribution but not at the mode.
- **Core assumption:** Interaction awareness exists as a subdominant pattern in the model's token probabilities; training (e.g., MLE on single-turn data) does not place it at the mode.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract] "Higher temperature sampling reveals latent interaction awareness, with follow-up rates rising to 22% for some models."
  - [section 3.3] Shows temperature sweeps: Qwen3.5-27B jumps from 0% to 22% on GSM8K, GLM-4.7 from 1% to 35.9% on GPQA Diamond.
  - [corpus] Moderate alignment. Papers on "consistency in sequential interactions" explore multi-turn behavior but do not specifically link temperature to surfacing latent follow-up capabilities.
- **Break condition:** If follow-up rates remain near zero across all temperatures, the capability is genuinely absent from the distribution (e.g., gpt-oss on GSM8K).

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim:** Controlled perturbations (truncation, appended questions) validate the metric by causally influencing follow-up rates, confirming sensitivity to assistant-turn content.
- **Mechanism:** Manipulating the assistant response (making it incomplete or adding an explicit question) should increase genuine follow-ups only if the model attends to that content. The magnitude of change indicates the degree of interaction awareness.
- **Core assumption:** A valid probe must be responsive to causal interventions on the stimulus (assistant response). Insensitive models likely ignore the assistant turn.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract] "Controlled perturbations validate that the proposed probe measures a real property of the model."
  - [section 3.4] Truncation boosts GLM-4.7 from 1.0% to 55.0% on GSM8K; explicit questions increase gpt-oss-120b from 1.3% to 25.7% on IFBench. Qwen models show little change.
  - [corpus] Weak evidence. Neighbors discuss "interaction smells" or "intent mismatch" but not this specific perturbation methodology.
- **Break condition:** If perturbations produce no systematic change in follow-up rates, the metric may be measuring static stylistic biases rather than context-sensitive awareness.

## Foundational Learning
- **Concept:** Token-level language modeling & role-conditioned generation.
  - **Why needed here:** The probe hinges on generating under a specific role header (`<|im start|>user`). Understanding how chat templates and role tokens steer the model's next-token predictions is essential.
  - **Quick check question:** How does the presence of a user-role header after an assistant turn alter the model's next-token probability distribution compared to starting a new conversation?
- **Concept:** Multi-turn conversation dynamics & context window management.
  - **Why needed here:** Interaction awareness requires the model to retain and react to the assistant's content within the same context window, not treat each turn independently.
  - **Quick check question:** What are the typical failure modes (e.g., prompt restatement, identity drift) when a model fails to maintain turn-level distinctions in a multi-turn context?
- **Concept:** Evaluation using LLM-as-a-judge with binary and categorical labels.
  - **Why needed here:** The primary metric (genuine follow-up rate) relies on an LLM judge to classify outputs. Knowing its limitations (e.g., κ=0.726 vs. humans, family-specific biases) is critical for interpreting results.
  - **Quick check question:** What are the trade-offs between an LLM judge (scalable, semantic) versus human annotation (gold-standard but costly) for assessing conversational grounding?

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map:** Chat template system -> Generation pipeline -> Evaluation judge -> Perturbation module -> Post-training setup
- **Critical path:** From raw query → assistant response generation (`a = M_θ(q)`) → user-turn generation (`u = M_θ([q; a])` under user header) → judge classification → follow-up rate aggregation. Any break (e.g., incorrect role header, judge failure) invalidates the probe.
- **Design tradeoffs:**
  - **Deterministic vs. sampled generation:** T=0 isolates modal behavior; higher T reveals distributional latent skills but introduces variance.
  - **Judge choice:** mini vs. nano vs. full gpt-5.4: trade-off between cost, agreement with humans, and sensitivity to model-family biases.
  - **Held-out vs. self-generated:** Held-out uses real multi-turn data; self-generated uses model's own `a`. The latter tests self-consistency; the former tests general conversational modeling.
- **Failure signatures:**
  - **Previous turn restate** (Qwen): `u` is verbatim copy of `q`. Indicates model ignores `a`.
  - **Assistant turn restate** (gpt-oss): `u` copies/paraphrases `a`. Identity failure—continues in assistant voice.
  - **Meta planning** (GLM): `u` emits reasoning/planning text. Attends to role change but fails to adopt user perspective.
- **First 3 experiments:**
  1. **Baseline across families:** Run self-generated user-turn eval at T=0 on GSM8K/GPQA/IFBench for Qwen, gpt-oss, GLM. Compute task accuracy and follow-up rate. Expect dissociation: high accuracy ≠ high follow-up.
  2. **Temperature sweep:** For representative models (Qwen3.5-27B, gpt-oss-120B, GLM-4.7), generate at T ∈ {0, 0.3, 0.7, 1.0}. Plot follow-up rate vs. T. Expect Qwen/GLM to show upward trend; gpt-oss may be flat on some datasets.
  3. **Perturbation validation:** For each model, run two conditions: (a) truncate last 25% of `a` tokens, (b) append "What do you think?" to `a`. Compare follow-up rates to baseline. Expect gpt-oss/GLM to increase; Qwen to be insensitive.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
### Open Question 1
- **Question:** Why does interaction awareness, as measured by user-turn generation, remain near zero under greedy decoding for many high-accuracy models, yet become latent and surface with higher temperature sampling?
- **Basis in paper:** The paper shows Qwen3.5 and GLM models exhibit near-zero genuine follow-up rates at T=0 but see substantial increases when temperature rises. The authors state this reveals "interaction awareness is latent" and not at the mode.
- **Why unresolved:** The paper demonstrates the phenomenon but does not explain the underlying training or architectural reasons why the capability resides in the distribution tails rather than the mode. Is this due to maximum likelihood training pressure, distributional shifts in multi-turn data, or specific loss masking during post-training?
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Analysis of training data mixtures (proportion of multi-turn vs. single-turn examples), fine-grained loss attribution per token (user vs. assistant roles), or probing the model's internal representations to see if interaction-aware signals exist even when the mode is degenerate.

### Open Question 2
- **Question:** To what extent are the distinct failure modes across model families (e.g., Qwen's "previous turn restate", gpt-oss's "assistant turn restate", GLM's "meta planning") causally attributable to differences in post-training recipes, chat templates, or data composition?
- **Basis in paper:** Table 6 and Appendix C document systematic, family-specific failure profiles. The perturbations in Table 1 show differential sensitivity, suggesting different underlying causes.
- **Why unresolved:** The study is observational across existing models; without controlled training interventions that vary only one factor (e.g., same base model, different chat templates or multi-turn data ratios), it cannot isolate the causal drivers of these failure modes.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Ablation studies training identical base models with varying chat templates, multi-turn data proportions, or role-specific loss weights. Measuring the shift in failure mode distributions would pinpoint which training aspects shape interaction awareness.

### Open Question 3
- **Question:** Can collaboration-oriented post-training (like the CollabLLM recipe) increase interaction awareness without degrading task accuracy on the held-out benchmarks, and what is the mechanism by which it transfers?
- **Basis in paper:** Section 3.5 shows Qwen3.5-2B post-trained with SFT and RL for collaboration: SFT raises follow-up rates but hurts GSM8K accuracy, while RL preserves accuracy with moderate gains. The paper notes neither explicitly trains for user-turn generation.
- **Why unresolved:** The RL result suggests a potential path to avoid the accuracy-follow-up trade-off, but it is preliminary (one model, one dataset). The mechanism of transfer—whether the model learns a general "next-state signal" or overfits to the multi-turn distribution—is not explained.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Scaling up post-training experiments across more model families and diverse collaboration objectives. Analyzing the generated user turns for quality and diversity, not just the binary follow-up metric. Interrogating whether the improvements generalize to unseen tasks and longer conversation horizons.

### Open Question 4
- **Question:** Does the proposed user-turn generation probe measure a capability that correlates with downstream performance in interactive settings (e.g., self-play training, multi-agent simulations, or human-AI conversation), or is it an orthogonal, perhaps artificial, construct?
- **Basis in paper:** The paper positions the probe as capturing a "missing dimension" crucial for multi-agent collaboration and cites related work on user simulation. However, all results are on static, single-step user reaction to an assistant response. There is no direct evaluation linking follow-up rates to success in a downstream interactive task.
- **Why unresolved:** The metric's utility is asserted but not validated against a concrete interactive benchmark. A high follow-up rate on GSM8K might not translate to being a useful partner in a prolonged multi-turn task.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Running experiments where models with varying follow-up rates are used as user proxies or assistant agents in a downstream interactive benchmark (e.g., a collaborative game, tool-use task, or human evaluation) and measuring the correlation between the probe score and downstream performance metrics.

### Open Question 5
- **Question:** How reliable and generalizable is the LLM-based judge (FOLLOWUPEVAL) for detecting genuine follow-ups, given its moderate same-family agreement (κ~0.48 with older nano judge) and the known weakness of LLM judges on nuanced dialogue phenomena?
- **Basis in paper:** Section D acknowledges judge validation: human annotators achieve κ=0.726 with gpt-5.4-mini, but the earlier operational judge (gpt-5.4-nano) showed lower agreement (κ~0.37 on hard slices). The paper switches judges mid-stream.
- **Why unresolved:** The κ values indicate only moderate reliability, especially on hard cases. The judgment task is subjective (determining if a user turn is "grounded"). The judge's biases could systematically affect results, particularly the observed family differences.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** A more extensive, multi-annotator human study with a balanced sample across all failure modes and model families. Developing a more objective rubric or a set of high-confidence, human-verified "gold standard" examples for each label to better calibrate and test the LLM judge's consistency. Exploring alternative evaluation strategies (e.g., embedding-based similarity to held-out human follow-ups).

## Limitations
- **Implementation specifics missing:** Exact chat template syntax for user-role continuation and the complete judge prompt (including system message, few-shot examples, output parsing) are unreported, blocking full replication.
- **Evaluation reliability concerns:** The LLM judge shows moderate human agreement (κ=0.726) and lower agreement on hard slices; its potential family-specific biases may affect comparative results.
- **Ecological validity unproven:** The link between the user-turn generation probe and actual performance in interactive, multi-turn tasks (e.g., dialogue, collaboration) is speculative and untested.

## Confidence
- **Claim 1 (Probe Validity):** Medium. Perturbation experiments provide strong internal validation, but ecological validity and measurement reliability depend on unreported judge/template details.
- **Claim 2 (Latent Awareness via Temperature):** Medium-High. Temperature sweep results are clear and consistent; the "latent" interpretation is plausible but not proven (could be distributional artifact).
- **Claim 3 (Dissociation from Task Accuracy):** High. The empirical pattern (e.g., within-Qwen scaling with near-zero greedy follow-up) is robust; main limitation is whether the probe measures a practically relevant capability.
- **Claim 4 (Post-Training Increases Awareness):** Medium. Results for Qwen3.5-2B are clear but limited to one model family and a specific recipe; generalizability is unknown.
