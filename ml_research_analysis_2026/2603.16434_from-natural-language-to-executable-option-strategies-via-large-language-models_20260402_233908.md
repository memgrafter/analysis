# From Natural Language to Executable Option Strategies via Large Language Models

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2603.16434
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.16434
- **Reference count:** 40
- **Primary result:** A neuro-symbolic pipeline using the Option Query Language (OQL) intermediate representation enables LLMs to translate natural language trading intents into executable option strategies with high validity (>0.87) and superior risk metrics compared to direct generation or Text-to-SQL baselines.

## Executive Summary
This paper introduces a neuro-symbolic framework to translate natural language trading intents into executable, validated option strategies. The core innovation is the Option Query Language (OQL), a domain-specific intermediate representation that abstracts raw, high-dimensional option chain data into structured, role-based primitives governed by a strict grammar. An LLM functions as a semantic parser to convert user intent into syntactically constrained OQL queries, which are then deterministically validated and executed by a dedicated engine. This separation of probabilistic language understanding from deterministic financial logic significantly improves reliability, achieving high validity rates and better strategy quality (e.g., higher Win Rate, lower tail risk) compared to unconstrained LLM generation or standard Text-to-SQL approaches.

## Method Summary
The method is a two-stage, training-free pipeline. First, an off-the-shelf LLM (e.g., DeepSeek-Chat, GPT-4.1) is prompted with the formal EBNF grammar of OQL to act as a semantic parser, converting a natural language trading intent into a syntactically constrained OQL query. Second, a deterministic OQL engine parses this query into an abstract syntax tree (AST), validates it against predefined role schemas, and executes it over a structured option chain database. Execution involves vectorized filtering of option contracts per specified role, combinatorial assembly of legs, application of aggregate constraints (e.g., net credit, Greek thresholds), and ranking. The resulting strategies are then backtested to compute performance metrics like Win Rate and Risk@90. The OQL grammar's strictness prevents hallucinations, while its role-based abstraction reduces the contextual burden on the LLM.

## Key Results
- The OQL pipeline achieved a Validity Rate (VR) exceeding 0.87 with large language models, ensuring generated queries were syntactically correct and executable.
- Using DeepSeek-Chat, OQL attained a 60.9% Win Rate and reduced buyer-side Risk@90 to 18.6%, significantly outperforming a Text-to-SQL baseline (46.1% Win Rate, 46.1% Risk@90).
- Specialized coding models (e.g., DeepSeek-Coder-6.7B) performed strongly, and the system demonstrated high efficiency with an 88.5% cache hit rate.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1: Abstracting High-Dimensional Market Data via Role-Based Primitives
OQL replaces thousands of raw option contracts with structured role definitions (e.g., `SC`, `LC` for an Iron Condor) and scoped leg-level predicates. The LLM declares *what* roles are needed and *constraints* on their legs (e.g., `SC.Delta ~ 0.15`). The execution engine then vectorized-filters the option chain *per role* before assembling the strategy, drastically reducing the search space and cognitive load on the LLM, which cannot reliably process raw chain data.

### Mechanism 2: Grammatical Constraints Ensure Syntactic and Semantic Validity
A strict EBNF grammar for OQL forces the LLM into a "semantic parser" mode, token-constrained to produce only valid queries. The engine validates the AST against role schemas (e.g., forbidding a `Short Put` role in a `BULL_CALL_SPREAD`). This deterministic validation cleanly separates linguistic reasoning from financial logic, eliminating common hallucinations like invalid tickers or impossible strikes.

### Mechanism 3: Semantic Soft-Matching Bridges Linguistic Ambiguity
The approximate operator `~` (e.g., `Dte ~ 30`) handles vague constraints like "about a month" by interpreting them as `|value - target| ≤ ϵ·target` with a predefined tolerance `ϵ`. This prevents empty-result failures from overly precise filters and improves robustness to natural language variation, allowing the LLM to express approximate intent without requiring exact numeric values.

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map:** User Intent Input -> LLM Semantic Parser -> OQL Engine (Compiler/Validator/Executor) -> Option Chain Database -> Backtesting Module -> Strategy Outputs.
- **Critical path:** Natural language intent is converted to a syntactically constrained OQL query → AST validation against role schemas → per-role vectorized filtering of option contracts → Cartesian product of candidate legs → application of aggregate `HAVING` constraints → strategy ranking → backtesting for performance metrics.
- **Design tradeoffs:** Token efficiency vs. expressivity (fixed role schemas limit novel strategies but keep queries short); determinism vs. coverage (grammar ensures executability but rejects intents outside predefined templates); caching vs. freshness (high cache hit rate reduces cost but may use stale data).
- **Failure signatures:** Low Validity Rate (VR) indicates LLM ignoring grammar; high selectivity with low Win Rate suggests overly tight constraints returning non-robust strategies; large buyer/seller risk disparities may indicate flawed margin/collateral calculations.
- **First 3 experiments:** 1) Grammar adherence stress test: measure VR across models for 50 templated intents; target VR >0.90. 2) Baseline execution comparison: evaluate OQL vs. Text-to-SQL vs. FFLG on Win Rate and Risk@90 for 200 intents. 3) Token efficiency breakdown: profile prompt/completion tokens per method to validate cost offsets from caching.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
### Open Question 1
How can the OQL framework be extended to support free-form leg design beyond predefined strategy templates while maintaining syntactic validity and execution reliability? *Basis:* Conclusion states future work on free-form leg design. *Why unresolved:* Current system is limited to a finite set of strategy templates. *Evidence needed:* A meta-language or grammar extension for dynamic leg specification validated experimentally for maintained validity rates.

### Open Question 2
How can the OQL pipeline incorporate a user's existing portfolio holdings as contextual input to generate strategies that are compatible with current positions and avoid unintended risk concentrations? *Basis:* Conclusion plans to support queries conditioned on existing holdings. *Why unresolved:* System treats strategies in isolation, ignoring critical portfolio-level constraints. *Evidence needed:* Grammar extensions for portfolio-state variables and backtests comparing risk-adjusted performance with/without conditioning.

### Open Question 3
What additional OQL operators and strategy types are necessary to cover exotic options and complex multi-leg structures beyond the current taxonomy, and how do these extensions impact the LLM's generation accuracy? *Basis:* Conclusion mentions expanding strategy taxonomy and operators. *Why unresolved:* Focus is on vanilla spreads; exotics (barriers, digitals) and advanced structures (ratio spreads) are excluded. *Evidence needed:* Formal definition of new OQL roles for exotics and benchmarking LLM accuracy on the extended grammar.

### Open Question 4
Does the OQL framework generalize to unseen market regimes (e.g., extreme volatility spikes, flash crashes) not represented in the 2025 dataset, and what architectural or data augmentations are needed to improve robustness? *Basis:* Inferred from dataset construction using 2025 segmented regimes without addressing out-of-distribution conditions. *Why unresolved:* Likely overfitting to historical patterns; unprecedented events could cause failures. *Evidence needed:* Evaluation on synthetic or real stress-test scenarios (e.g., 2008, 2020 data) measuring degradation in validity and risk metrics.

## Limitations
- The complete OQL grammar and operator semantics (e.g., exact tolerance ϵ for `~`) are incompletely specified, hindering faithful reproduction.
- Precise option chain data schema, Greek computation details (e.g., dividend handling, risk-free rate), and backtesting assumptions (transaction costs, early exercise) are vague.
- External validation is low, with an average citation count of 0, indicating limited independent scrutiny or replication.

## Confidence
- Role-based abstraction reduces context explosion: **Medium** (logical, no direct ablation study).
- Grammatical constraints ensure validity: **High** (VR >0.87 consistently across large models).
- Soft-matching handles linguistic ambiguity: **Medium** (correlation with improved results, not isolated).
- Overall OQL superiority over baselines: **High** (consistent empirical gains in Win Rate and risk metrics).

## Next Checks
1. **Ablation of abstraction:** Remove role schemas; allow LLM to generate leg lists from raw option chain. Compare VR, token usage, and Win Rate to isolate the impact of role-based filtering.
2. **Generalization to unseen strategies:** Extend the OQL grammar with 5 novel strategy types (e.g., ratio spreads, diagonal spreads). Evaluate zero-shot parsing accuracy and resulting strategy quality.
3. **Tolerance sensitivity:** Systematically vary the tolerance ϵ in the `~` operator. Measure its impact on Win Rate and Risk@90 to quantify the robustness trade-off between constraint relaxation and strategy viability.
