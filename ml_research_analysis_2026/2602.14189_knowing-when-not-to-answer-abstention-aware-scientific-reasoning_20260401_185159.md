# Knowing When Not to Answer: Abstention-Aware Scientific Reasoning

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2602.14189
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.14189
- **Reference count:** 40
- **Primary result:** Confidence-based abstention dramatically lowers selective risk at moderate coverage across models and datasets.

## Executive Summary
The paper addresses a critical gap in using LLMs for scientific claim verification and QA: models are compelled to always answer, even when evidence is insufficient, leading to potentially harmful unsupported conclusions. It introduces a framework that decomposes scientific claims into minimal, testable conditions, audits each condition against evidence using a fixed NLI model, and aggregates the audits under an asymmetric loss function where false support is costlier than false refutation. Confidence is derived from the maximum absolute margin (entailment minus contradiction scores) among critical conditions, and predictions are withheld below a threshold. Evaluated on SciFact and PubMedQA with six models (Flan-T5, Llama, DeepSeek, Mistral, GPT-4o-mini, GPT-5.2), the method substantially reduces risk at moderate coverage levels. Notably, instruction-tuned models exhibit more conservative abstention, and the variation in abstention behavior—not model architecture—proves to be the dominant factor in reliability.

## Method Summary
The pipeline is deterministic and modular. First, an LLM decomposes each input claim or question into a set of atomic natural language conditions, flagging some as `critical`. Second, a fixed general-domain NLI model (DeBERTa) scores entailment and contradiction between every condition and every evidence sentence; for each condition, the highest entailment and contradiction scores are extracted, and thresholds assign an audit status (`SUP`, `CON`, `MIS`) with contradiction priority. Third, aggregation rules combine audit outcomes **only for critical conditions**: for SciFact, `REFUTES` if any critical `CON`, `SUPPORTS` if all critical `SUP`, else `NEI`; for PubMedQA, `no` if any critical `CON`, `yes` if at least one critical `SUP` and no critical `CON`, else `maybe`. Fourth, confidence is computed as the maximum absolute margin among critical conditions; if below threshold `τ`, the system abstains. Threshold sweeping across `τ` produces risk-coverage curves and metrics (AURC, Risk@0.8/0.9).

## Key Results
- Raw accuracies across six models vary only modestly (≈0.35–0.56), showing limited discrimination in unconditional performance.
- Confidence-based abstention yields large risk reductions at moderate coverage; e.g., on PubMedQA, Flan-T5 achieves Risk@0.8=0.463 vs. 0.675 without abstention, and AURC 0.389 vs. 0.464.
- Instruction-tuned models abstain more conservatively, achieving lower risk at comparable coverage.
- Abstention behavior is the primary driver of reliability, outweighing architectural differences among LLMs.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
**Condition Decomposition** breaks complex scientific claims into minimal, testable units, isolating evidence sufficiency and enabling auditable reasoning. The decomposition must capture all necessary factual requirements; incomplete or overlapping conditions can cause aggregation errors even with perfect auditing.

**Evidence Auditing via NLI** uses a fixed NLI model to score alignment between conditions and evidence sentences. Contradiction-priority thresholds enforce a conservative bias, but the approach depends on the NLI model’s ability to detect subtle scientific entailment/contradiction and on evidence quality.

**Confidence-Based Abstention** uses the maximum absolute margin (entailment minus contradiction) among critical conditions as a confidence signal. Under asymmetric loss (false support cost > false refutation), this max-margin thresholding effectively trades coverage for reduced selective risk, provided NLI scores are well-calibrated and critical conditions are sufficiently informative.

## Architecture Onboarding
Decomposition Module -> Auditing Module -> Aggregation Module -> Confidence & Abstention
Critical path: Input → Decomposition (LLM generates conditions & critical flags) → Auditing (NLI scores all condition-evidence pairs) → Aggregation (deterministic rules on critical audits) → Confidence (max margin over critical) → Threshold check → Final output or abstain.
Design tradeoffs: Higher abstention threshold `τ` lowers risk but reduces coverage; max-margin confidence is sensitive to any strong signal but may ignore aggregate weak evidence; general-domain NLI avoids overfitting but may miss scientific nuances.
Failure signatures: No decomposition → coarser confidence, earlier abstention; No auditing → heuristic defaults, degenerate risk metrics; No abstention → forced answers, near-certain high risk; Confidence applied to all conditions → drastically altered abstention.
First 3 experiments: (1) Establish baselines: run full pipeline on SciFact/PubMedQA, compute risk-coverage curves, AURC, Risk@0.8/0.9. (2) Ablation 1 (No decomposition): skip splitting, treat input as single critical condition, compare risk-coverage. (3) Ablation 3 (No abstention): force all predictions (`τ=0`), quantify risk increase at full coverage.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
1. How can the framework be extended to incorporate multimodal scientific evidence (e.g., figures, tables, numerical data) while maintaining reliable abstention?
2. To what extent do domain-specific NLI models (biomedical/scientific) improve condition auditing and abstention decisions versus general-domain NLI?
3. How does the quality and variability of condition decomposition (performed by an LLM) affect the reliability of the abstention-aware pipeline?
4. How should abstention thresholds be set in practice to reflect domain-specific asymmetric loss without access to test-time labels?

## Limitations
- Exact NLI thresholds (`θent`, `θcon`) for audit status are unreported, hindering precise replication.
- Prompts for decomposition and critical marking are unspecified, risking instability in condition generation.
- PubMedQA evidence truncation length is ambiguous.
- Reliance on a general-domain NLI model may fail on subtle scientific language without domain adaptation.

## Confidence
- **Condition Decomposition:** Medium (granularity evidenced but prompt consistency and completeness unvalidated)
- **Evidence Auditing via NLI:** Low (fixed thresholds and general-domain model likely misjudge technical entailment/contradiction)
- **Confidence-Based Abstention:** Medium (max-margin theoretically sound but depends on well-calibrated NLI scores and sufficient critical conditions)
