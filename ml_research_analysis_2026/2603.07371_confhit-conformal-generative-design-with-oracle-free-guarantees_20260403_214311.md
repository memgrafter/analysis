# ConfHit: Conformal Generative Design with Oracle Free Guarantees

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID**: 2603.07371
- **Source URL**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.07371
- **Reference count**: 40
- **Primary result**: ConfHit provides distribution-free, oracle-free guarantees that a generated batch of molecules contains at least one active compound at a user-specified confidence level, reducing certified set sizes by 30–50% compared to baselines while maintaining valid coverage.

## Executive Summary
ConfHit addresses a critical gap in generative drug discovery: the need for reliable property guarantees without costly wet-lab validation. Existing conformal methods require oracle access to generated samples and fail under covariate shift. ConfHit eliminates the oracle requirement by leveraging weighted exchangeability via density-ratio estimation. It constructs multiple-sample density-ratio weighted conformal p-values to certify that a batch of generated candidates contains at least one hit (active molecule) with user-specified confidence. A nested testing procedure then refines the output to the smallest such certified set. The method achieves valid coverage across diverse generative models and tasks, producing significantly smaller certified sets than Bonferroni or certification-only baselines, and remains robust to reasonable density-ratio estimation errors.

## Method Summary
ConfHit operates on a calibrated historical dataset of inactive compounds and a property predictor trained separately. Given a generative model producing a batch of candidate molecules, it first estimates the density ratio between the generated distribution and the historical calibration distribution using features from the property predictor’s penultimate layer. For each possible batch size \(k\), it computes a weighted permutation p-value by pooling the first \(k\) generated candidates with inactive calibration samples, permuting them, and evaluating a conformity score (e.g., max predicted activity) under weights equal to the product of density ratios for the permuted generated samples. This p-value tests the null hypothesis that none of the first \(k\) candidates are hits. After enforcing monotonicity across p-values, the smallest \(k\) with \(p_k \le \alpha\) is returned as the certified set size; if none, an empty set is returned. The entire procedure requires no oracle access to generated labels and controls type-I error under covariate shift.

## Key Results
- Valid coverage (error ≤ α) is achieved across α levels in constrained molecule optimization and structure-based drug discovery tasks using multiple generative models.
- Certified set sizes are reduced to 2–5 molecules (30–50% smaller) compared to Bonferroni and certification-only baselines while maintaining strict error control.
- The method is robust to density ratio estimation; coverage remains valid across bandwidth choices and under feature-space balance checks.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1: Density-ratio weighted exchangeability
- **Claim**: ConfHit removes the need for an experimental oracle by reweighting calibration data to match the generated distribution, creating weighted exchangeability.
- **Mechanism**: It estimates the density ratio \(w(x)=dQ/dP(x)\) between the generated distribution \(Q\) and historical calibration distribution \(P\). Under the null (no hits in generated batch), conditional on all labels and the unordered feature set, the probability of any permutation of combined calibration and test features is proportional to the product of \(w(x)\) for the test samples in that permutation. This weighting enables a valid permutation test without oracle labels.
- **Core assumption**: Covariate shift: \(dQ/dP(x,y)=w(x)\), i.e., the property label \(Y\) is conditionally independent of source given \(X\). The density ratio \(w(x)\) is known or accurately estimated.
- **Evidence anchors**:
  - [abstract]: "CONFHIT leverages weighted exchangeability between historical and generated samples to eliminate the need for an experimental oracle"
  - [section 2]: "we assume a covariate shift between the two distributions: \(dQ/dP(x,y)=w(x)\) for some function \(w:X\to\mathbb{R}_+\)."
  - [section A.2]: Proposition A.4 describes the conditional permutation distribution.
- **Break condition**: Severe misestimation of the density ratio (e.g., small calibration set, poor features) breaks weighted exchangeability and may violate coverage.

### Mechanism 2: Multiple-sample permutation p-value for certification
- **Claim**: ConfHit constructs a p-value that tests whether a given batch of generated samples contains at least one hit, controlling type-I error under the null.
- **Mechanism**: For batch size \(k\), consider all permutations of inactive calibration samples and the first \(k\) test samples. For each permutation compute a conformity score \(V\) (e.g., max predicted property). The p-value \(p_k\) is the weighted fraction of permutations with score at least as extreme as observed, weights being the product of density ratios for the test samples. Theorem 3.1 proves \(p_k\) stochastically dominates Uniform\([0,1]\) under the null.
- **Core assumption**: The conformity score \(V\) is trained independently of the calibration/test data and is informative (increases with presence of hits).
- **Evidence anchors**:
  - [section 3.1]: Definition \(p_k = \frac{\sum_{\pi\in\Pi} \bar{w}(\pi;X)\mathbf{1}\{V(\pi_0;X)\le V(\pi;X)\}}{\sum_{\pi\in\Pi} \bar{w}(\pi;X)}\).
  - [Theorem 3.1]: Finite-sample validity.
  - [abstract]: "constructs multiple-sample density-ratio weighted conformal p-value".
- **Break condition**: If \(V\) leaks information from calibration data, exchangeability breaks; if uninformative, power vanishes but validity remains.

### Mechanism 3: Nested conformal testing for design
- **Claim**: ConfHit refines the initial batch to a minimal certified subset without losing error control.
- **Mechanism**: For each \(k=1,\dots,N\) compute \(p_k\). Because hypotheses \(H_k\): "none of first \(k\) are hits" are nested (\(H_1\subseteq H_2\subseteq\cdots\)), a decreasing sequence of valid p-values (enforced by \(p_k\gets\max_{j\ge k}\tilde{p}_j\)) allows stopping at smallest \(k\) with \(p_k\le\alpha\). Theorem 3.4 guarantees \(P(\text{output contains no hit})\le\alpha\).
- **Core assumption**: The \(p_k\) are valid and monotonic; nested structure holds inherently.
- **Evidence anchors**:
  - [section 3.2]: Theorem 3.4 and Algorithm 1 steps 6–7.
  - [abstract]: "proposes a nested testing procedure to certify and refine candidate sets".
- **Break condition**: Invalid \(p_k\) or improper monotonicity (e.g., numerical issues) can violate guarantee.

## Foundational Learning

### Conformal Prediction
- **Why needed**: ConfHit builds on split conformal prediction to construct distribution-free p-values with coverage guarantees.
- **Quick check**: In split conformal prediction, what is the role of the nonconformity score and how is the prediction set formed?  
  *Answer*: The nonconformity score measures the discrepancy between a label and its prediction; the prediction set for a new point includes all candidate labels whose score does not exceed a quantile of the calibration scores, ensuring marginal coverage.

### Covariate Shift and Density Ratio Estimation
- **Why needed**: Assumes distribution shift between calibration and generated data; corrected by estimating \(w(x)=dQ/dP(x)\) to reweight calibration.
- **Quick check**: Under covariate shift, why does weighting by the density ratio restore exchangeability between the two datasets?  
  *Answer*: Because under covariate shift, the joint distribution ratio factorizes as \(dQ/dP(x,y)=w(x)\). Reweighting calibration samples by \(w(x)\) makes their marginal distribution match that of the generated data, enabling permutation-based inference as if they were exchangeable.

### Weighted Permutation Test
- **Why needed**: The core p-value uses permutation weights derived from the density ratio to adjust for distribution shift.
- **Quick Check**: In a weighted permutation test, how do the weights affect the null distribution of the test statistic?  
  *Answer*: The weights are incorporated into the permutation distribution, making the probability of each permutation proportional to the product of weights of the test-group items in that permutation. This yields a valid test under the weighted exchangeability assumption.

## Architecture Onboarding

#### Component map
- Calibration set (inactive historical compounds) -> Density estimator (estimate w(x)) -> Conformal engine
- Generative model (candidates) -> Property predictor (conformity score V) -> Conformal engine
- Conformal engine -> Output (minimal certified set)

#### Critical path
1. Input test context and generate batch of candidates.
2. Extract feature representations for calibration inactives and generated candidates.
3. Estimate density ratio between generated features and calibration features.
4. For k=1 to N: compute weighted permutation p-value for first k candidates.
5. Enforce monotonicity: p_k ← max_{ℓ≥k} p_ℓ.
6. Find smallest k with p_k ≤ α; output first k candidates (or empty set).

#### Design tradeoffs
- **Score function**: Max-pooling gives high power but may be sensitive to outliers; sum or rank-sum are more robust but conservative.
- **Density estimation**: Kernel density estimation is simple but limited to low-dimensional features; discriminative methods (e.g., logistic) may improve accuracy but risk overfitting.
- **Data splitting**: Training V on same data used for calibration can increase power; requires sample-splitting-aware variant for validity.
- **Monte Carlo permutations (B)**: Larger B stabilizes p-values but increases runtime; B=2000 typically sufficient.

#### Failure signatures
- Non-uniform p-values on calibration inactives (check histogram/KL divergence).
- Excessive empty-set rate despite generative model producing hits.
- High sensitivity of certified set size to density estimator bandwidth.
- Empirical error rate exceeding α in validation.
- Large variance in p_k across random runs.

#### First 3 experiments
1. **Certification error control**: For various α and N, compute fraction of test inputs for which the certified batch (size N) contains no hit. Should be ≤ α.
2. **Design efficiency**: Compare ConfHit with Bonferroni and certification-only baselines in mean certified set size and empty-set frequency; ConfHit should yield smaller sets while maintaining similar error.
3. **Robustness diagnostics**: (a) Perturb weights (e.g., power transform) and measure coverage/empty-set changes. (b) Perform scaffold split on calibration negatives and assess p-value uniformity. (c) Check feature balance before/after weighting.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
1. How can density-ratio estimation be made robust when calibration sets are small or feature extractors are poorly aligned?
2. Can CONFHIT be effectively scaled to macromolecules such as proteins with more complex generative spaces?
3. How does CONFHIT perform under real wet-lab experimental conditions rather than computational oracles?
4. Is CONFHIT robust across a wide variety of chemical spaces, targets, and generative model types?

## Limitations
- Coverage guarantee relies on accurate density-ratio estimation; noise in small calibration regimes or with misaligned features can degrade validity.
- Experiments focus on small molecules; extension to proteins or other macromolecules requires additional work.
- Validation currently relies on in-silico oracles; transferability to real wet-lab assays with noise and batch effects is unproven.

## Confidence
- **Weighted exchangeability theory** (Proposition A.4, Theorem 3.1): High confidence. Proofs are rigorous under stated assumptions.
- **Nested testing validity** (Theorem 3.4): High confidence. Monotonicity correction ensures finite-sample coverage.
- **Empirical coverage and efficiency** (CMO/SBDD results): Medium confidence. Experiments rely on underspecified baselines and hyperparameters; exact reproducibility limited.
- **Robustness to density estimation** (sensitivity analysis): Medium confidence. Demonstrated for bandwidth but not alternative estimators; claims need further validation.

## Next Checks
1. **P-value uniformity on held-out inactives**: Sample a large set of historical inactives (excluded from calibration), compute conformal p-values via the full pipeline, and test uniformity (e.g., KS test). Non-uniformity indicates exchangeability failure.
2. **Density estimator ablation**: Substitute KDE with a discriminative density ratio estimator (e.g., logistic regression on same features) and repeat main experiments. Coverage should stay ≤ α and certified set sizes should not worsen significantly.
3. **Monotonicity enforcement test**: Remove the monotonicity correction in Algorithm 1 and measure empirical error rates. Without it, nested testing should inflate error above α, confirming the necessity of step 6 for validity.
