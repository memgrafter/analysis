# Bridging AI and Clinical Reasoning: Abductive Explanations for Alignment on Critical Symptoms
## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2602.13985
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.13985
- **Reference count:** 40
- **Primary result:** Reasoning alignment, quantified via abductive explanations on clinically critical symptoms, varies significantly across datasets (e.g., ~14% aligned for SGD on Breast Cancer vs. <6% misalignment for SGD on Heart Disease).

## Executive Summary
This paper addresses the critical issue of misalignment between AI model reasoning and clinician reasoning in medical diagnosis, even when predictions are accurate. The authors propose using formal abductive explanations (AXPs) to define and quantify alignment based on clinically-defined "critical symptoms"—features that automatically trigger a positive diagnosis. Critical properties are extracted from training data as feature conjunctions that appear exclusively in positive instances. For each such critical case in the test set, the AI model's AXP is checked to see if it relies exclusively on these critical features. Experiments on three datasets (Breast Cancer, Heart Disease, Mental Health) reveal stark differences: on Breast Cancer, all models show high misalignment because their relevant features differ from the critical property; on Heart Disease, alignment is much better because the model's relevant feature is part of the critical property. The study concludes that reasoning alignment is a distinct dimension from accuracy and can be improved by incorporating clinical knowledge into feature preprocessing.

## Method Summary
The authors train standard classifiers (neural network, logistic regression, SGD) on binarized versions of three medical datasets, using either statistical thresholds (median) or clinically informed cutoffs. From the training data, they automatically discover "critical properties" by searching for feature conjunctions that occur only in positive-class instances; these conjunctions represent clinically sufficient conditions for a positive diagnosis. For each test instance that satisfies a critical property (a "critical case"), they compute the model's abductive explanation (AXP)—the minimal set of features that guarantees the model's prediction. Alignment is defined as the model predicting positive and its AXP including the critical property (under a strict or relaxed criterion). The final alignment metric is the proportion of aligned critical cases. The method assumes AXP computation is tractable for the chosen models, which are typically linear or shallow.

## Key Results
- In the **Breast Cancer** dataset, all models (NN, LR, SGD) exhibited high misalignment (e.g., SGD ~14% aligned) because their most relevant features did not match the critical property ($x_2=0 \land x_8=0$).
- In the **Heart Disease** dataset, alignment was better (e.g., SGD <6% misaligned) because the model-relevant feature ($x_{12}=0$) was part of the critical property ($x_2=0 \land x_3=1 \land x_{12}=0$).
- The findings demonstrate that reasoning alignment is a distinct, quantifiable dimension from predictive accuracy and can be improved by incorporating clinical knowledge into feature preprocessing.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
**Mechanism 1:** Formal abductive explanations (AXPs) provide a sound, minimal, and consistent basis for auditing AI reasoning against clinical critical properties. An AXP is a minimal feature set that guarantees the model's prediction for any instance sharing those features, creating a "reasoning fingerprint." Alignment is determined by checking if this fingerprint contains the clinically defined critical property (Cr). Core assumption: Cr correctly captures clinicians' sufficient conditions for rapid diagnosis. Evidence anchors: formal definitions in sections 4.1/4.3 and strong support from neighboring literature (e.g., "Most General Explanations of Tree Ensembles"). Break condition: no AXP exists for positive predictions or AXP computation is intractable for the model.

**Mechanism 2:** Critical properties can be systematically extracted from training data by identifying feature conjunctions that appear only in positive instances. This method scans for a conjunction that is a subset of every positive it appears in and of no negative instance, thus a sufficient condition for the positive class. Core assumption: training data is representative and such deterministic sufficient conditions exist. Evidence anchors: Section 6 and concrete examples in Section 9.2. Break condition: no conjunction satisfies the condition (empty critical knowledge).

**Mechanism 3:** Reasoning alignment is quantifiable by checking, for every critical case, whether the model's AXP exclusively relies on the critical property (or at least includes it under a relaxed criterion). Alignment occurs if the model predicts positive and its AXP contains Cr. System alignment is the proportion of aligned critical cases; misalignment arises from either misclassification or reasoning divergence. Core assumption: clinicians rely exclusively on Cr for rapid diagnosis. Evidence anchors: Definitions in Section 5 and experimental results in Section 9.3. Break condition: model never produces an AXP that is a subset of Cr under strict definition; relaxed definition mitigates.

## Foundational Learning
- **Abductive Explanation (AXP):** The minimal set of features from an instance that is sufficient to guarantee the model's prediction for any instance with those feature values. *Why needed:* Forms the bedrock of the analysis, transforming a black-box prediction into a formally defined, minimal "reason set" that can be checked against clinical criteria. *Quick check:* If an AXP for a "malignant" breast cancer prediction is $\{x_2=0, x_8=0\}$, what does that guarantee about any other patient with those two feature values?
- **Critical Property (Cr):** A conjunction of features that clinicians accept as automatically sufficient for a positive diagnosis ($d^+$). It defines the "critical case." *Why needed:* Provides the clinical gold standard for reasoning; alignment is measured by how often the model's AXP includes this exact clinical trigger. *Quick check:* In the Heart Disease dataset, is a patient with $x_2=0$ (female), $x_3=1$ (typical angina), and $x_{12}=0$ (0 vessels) guaranteed a positive diagnosis by clinicians? What does that make this feature combination?
- **Reasoning Alignment vs. Predictive Accuracy:** The separation of *what* a model predicts from *why* it predicts it. A model can be accurate but misaligned if it uses different features than clinicians for crucial decisions. *Why needed:* The paper's core thesis; trust and safety require both, and they are not correlated (evidenced by dataset results). *Quick check:* Can a model be 95% accurate on a test set but still be considered "untrusted" by clinicians for a specific, high-stakes symptom combination? Why?

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component map:** The system comprises five modules: (1) Model & Data Module (trains classifiers), (2) Critical Property Miner (scans training data for exclusive positive conjunctions), (3) AXP Engine (computes minimal abductive explanations for predictions), (4) Alignment Auditor (identifies critical cases, computes AXPs, checks Cr inclusion, and partitions outcomes), (5) Quantifier (outputs alignment metrics).

**Critical path:** The alignment assessment pipeline proceeds as: Trained Model + Critical Property (Cr) → Identify Critical Cases in test set → Compute AXP for each critical case → Check if AXP ⊇ Cr → Aggregate alignment score.

**Design tradeoffs:**
- Cr mining strategy: statistical binarization (e.g., median) vs. clinically informed thresholds; the choice affects Cr existence and coverage.
- AXP completeness: computing a single AXP per case vs. enumerating all possible AXPs; the former is efficient but may miss alternative explanations.
- Alignment definition strictness: strict (Cr ⊆ AXP) vs. relaxed (Cr ⊆ ∃AXP); trade-off between clinical fidelity and capturing partial agreement.

**Failure signatures:**
- No Cr found: The miner returns empty set, preventing alignment assessment.
- All critical cases misclassified: Model fails on the simplest clinical triggers (misclassification rate 100%).
- Zero alignment under strict criterion: Model never uses Cr in its AXP for critical cases, indicating reasoning divergence.
- Discrepancy between model’s most relevant features and Cr: e.g., Breast Cancer results where top features differ from Cr.

**First 3 experiments:**
1. Replicate Cr Mining: On a provided training set, implement the positive-only conjunction search and verify it reproduces the paper's Cr for Breast Cancer ($x_2=0 \land x_8=0$) and Heart Disease ($x_2=0 \land x_3=1 \land x_{12}=0$). Test on a small synthetic dataset with known Cr.
2. AXP Sanity Check: For a trained logistic regression model on a simple dataset, manually compute an AXP for a positive instance. Verify that toggling any feature in the AXP changes the prediction, confirming minimality.
3. Alignment Audit Prototype: Using a trained model, its Cr, and a small test set, manually run Algorithm 1 on 5 known critical cases. Label each as Misclassified, Misaligned (positive but AXP excludes Cr), or Aligned. Compare against the paper's reported percentages.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- How does the proposed alignment framework generalize across a diverse range of medical conditions and dataset characteristics, beyond the three datasets evaluated?
- Does the relaxed alignment criterion (allowing non-critical reasons alongside critical properties) improve clinical trust and utility in practice, and how do clinicians perceive such explanations?
- What are the root causes behind the discrepancy where AI models rely on features that are clinically irrelevant to critical properties, as starkly observed in the Breast Cancer dataset?
- How can clinical knowledge be systematically and effectively integrated into feature preprocessing (e.g., binarization thresholds) to maximize reasoning alignment, and what are the trade-offs with predictive performance?

## Limitations
- Binarization thresholds and critical property extraction algorithm are underspecified, hindering faithful replication.
- The assumption that clinicians rely exclusively on a single critical conjunction for diagnosis is strong; real-world reasoning may involve additional features.
- Evaluation on small, binarized datasets (e.g., Mental Health: 16 critical cases) limits generalizability to high-dimensional, continuous EHRs.

## Confidence

## Next Checks
- **Sensitivity of Critical Properties:** Re-run mining across a grid of binarization thresholds (median, quartiles, clinical cutoffs) and report stability of Cr patterns and alignment scores.
- **Ground Truth Alignment Test:** Create synthetic datasets where the true decision rule is a known conjunction (distinct from Cr) and verify the metric correctly distinguishes aligned from misaligned models under strict/relaxed definitions.
- **Clinician Validation:** Conduct a small user study with domain experts to assess whether identified critical properties and exclusive Cr reliance match actual diagnostic reasoning for the dataset cases.
