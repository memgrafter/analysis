# Score-Guided Proximal Projection: A Unified Geometric Framework for Rectified Flow Editing
## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2603.05761
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.05761
- **Reference count:** 40
- **Primary result:** SGPP is a training-free framework that guarantees out-of-distribution inputs are projected onto the data manifold, unifies RF-inversion as a limiting case, and enables continuous soft guidance for editing via proximal variance.

## Executive Summary
Rectified Flow (RF) models excel at generative modeling but lack precise mechanisms for semantic image editing and recovery. Standard inversion methods suffer from geometric locking, while posterior sampling can be unstable. This paper introduces Score-Guided Proximal Projection (SGPP), a training-free framework that reformulates recovery as a proximal optimization problem. SGPP balances fidelity to an input reference with generative realism by leveraging the pre-trained RF score field. Theoretically, it guarantees normal contraction, snapping out-of-distribution inputs onto the manifold. Crucially, RF-inversion is shown to be the σₚ→0 limit of SGPP; relaxing σₚ enables a continuous, training-free trade-off between perception and distortion. Empirically, SGPP succeeds in challenging zero-shot editing tasks where RF-inversion fails and provides robust convergence on toy manifolds where competing methods diverge.

## Method Summary
SGPP casts the recovery/editing task as minimizing the time-dependent energy Jₜ(x) = (1/(2σₚ(t)²))‖x − (1−t)xₚₑᵣ‖² − log pₜ(x), where pₜ is the RF marginal density and σₚ(t) = √((1−t)²σₚ² + t²). At each timestep t, it performs a deterministic gradient update: x ← x + η [s(x,t) − (x − (1−t)xₚₑᵣ)/σₚ(t)²], where s(x,t) ≈ ∇log pₜ(x) is the pre-trained RF score. A corresponding stochastic SDE (SGPP-SDE) is provided for high-quality sampling. For editing, the score is mixtures of conditional and unconditional scores. Key hyperparameters are proximal variance σₚ (controls soft guidance), optional mixture weight η, and a stopping time tₛₜₒₚ after which the proximal term is disabled. The method is inversion-free, requiring only a reference image and (optionally) a text prompt.

## Key Results
- SGPP guarantees normal contraction, providing geometric stability and forcing out-of-distribution inputs onto the data manifold.
- Standard RF-inversion is mathematically equivalent to the σₚ→0 limit of SGPP, explaining its geometric locking behavior.
- Relaxing σₚ > 0 yields a continuous, training-free perception-distortion trade-off, tunable directly via proximal variance.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1: Normal Contraction Guarantee
- **Claim:** SGPP's deterministic update induces a normal contraction property, guaranteeing that out-of-distribution inputs are projected onto the data manifold with geometric stability.
- **Mechanism:** The RF score field decomposes into a normal restoring force (−nₜ/t²), tangential generative drift (∇T log p), and curvature correction (½Hₜ). The proximal fidelity term adds an extra normal restoring force (−nₖ/σₚ(t)). The combined normal force yields exponential contraction under the Tubular Curvature Bound.
- **Core assumption:** Tubular Curvature Bound (Assumption 3.1): initial displacement ‖nₖ‖ and manifold curvature κₘₐₓ satisfy κₘₐₓ‖nₖ‖ < 1.
- **Evidence anchors:** Proposition 3.3 (Normal Contraction), Theorem 3.5 (Manifold-Constrained MAP), abstract claim about snapping OOD inputs.
- **Break condition:** Violation of κₘₐₓ‖nₖ‖ < 1 breaks the tubular neighborhood, potentially causing divergence or incorrect projections.

### Mechanism 2: RF-Inversion as a Hard Guidance Limit
- **Claim:** Standard RF-Inversion is the σₚ→0 limiting case of SGPP, where the proximal constraint becomes infinitely tight.
- **Mechanism:** SGPP's velocity field (Eq. 10) geometrically mixes unconditional and conditional scores with variance σₚ(t). As σₚ→0, the proximal term vanishes, forcing the trajectory to follow the inversion path defined solely by the conditional score ∇log p(xₚₑᵣ|xₜ), which matches RF-Inversion.
- **Core assumption:** Gaussian likelihood p(xₚₑᵣ|x₀) and linear RF forward process allow the conditional score to simplify to −(x−(1−t)y₀)/t² in the limit.
- **Evidence anchors:** Abstract statement, Section 5 (Connection to RF-Inversion), Theorem I.1.
- **Break condition:** At σₚ=0, any semantic edit requiring deviation from the source trajectory conflicts with the hard fidelity force, causing geometric locking.

### Mechanism 3: Continuous Soft Guidance via Proximal Variance
- **Claim:** Relaxing the proximal variance σₚ > 0 enables a continuous, training-free "soft guidance" trade-off between identity preservation (fidelity) and generative freedom (realism).
- **Mechanism:** σₚ controls the width of the manifold tube. Larger σₚ weakens the normal restoring force, allowing greater tangential drift under the generative score while bounding normal error. This directly tunes the perception-distortion trade-off.
- **Core assumption:** The schedule σₚ(t) = √((1−t)²σₚ² + t²) properly combines relaxation variance with intrinsic RF noise.
- **Evidence anchors:** Abstract claim, Section B.3 (The Fidelity-Realist Trade-off), Figure 3 (σₚ sweep).
- **Break condition:** σₚ too large → identity loss; σₚ too small → reversion to hard guidance/geometric locking. Stable regime requires balancing with step size and curvature.

## Foundational Learning

- **Concept:** Rectified Flow (VE equivalence and score decomposition)
  - **Why needed:** SGPP repurposes the pre-trained RF score field; understanding its derivation and geometric decomposition (Prop 3.2) is essential to see how normal/tangential forces arise.
  - **Quick check:** Given the RF forward process Xₜ = (1−t)X₀ + tZ, derive the score ∇ₓₜ log pₜ(xₜ) using Tweedie's formula and its relationship to the VE model.

- **Concept:** Manifold Geometry (tubular neighborhoods, projections, curvature)
  - **Why needed:** The data manifold M₀ and time-dependent tube Mₜ define the "safe" generation region. Propositions 3.3 and 3.4 rely on orthogonal decomposition (xₜ = πₜ + nₜ) and curvature bounds to analyze stability and drift.
  - **Quick check:** What is the Second Fundamental Form Πᵧ, and how does it relate to the shape operator Sⁿ and the mean curvature vector H(ᵧ)?

- **Concept:** Proximal Optimization (balancing data fidelity and prior)
  - **Why needed:** SGPP's energy Jₜ(x) is a proximal objective: fidelity potential ‖x − (1−t)xₚₑᵣ‖² balances against generative potential −log pₜ(x). This core mechanism differs from DPS (terminal consistency) and RF-inversion (hard constraint).
  - **Quick check:** How does the proximal variance σₚ act as a regularization parameter in Jₜ, and what happens in the limits σₚ→0 and σₚ→∞?

## Architecture Onboarding

- **Component map:** Pre-trained RF model -> score function sψ(x,t) -> reference image xₚₑᵣ and proximal variance σₚ -> time schedule t₀ > t₁ > ... > tₙ ≥ 0 -> deterministic update (Eq.2) -> optional SGPP-SDE (Eq.4) -> geometric mixture (Eq.5) for editing.

- **Critical path:**
  1. Preprocess: Compute xₚₑᵣ embedding (if using CLIP guidance), set σₚ schedule and η.
  2. Initialization: Sample Xₜ₀ ~ N((1−t₀)xₚₑᵣ, t₀²I) (or use inversion path if hybrid).
  3. Iterate deterministic update (Eq.2) for k=0,…,N−1.
  4. For high-quality sampling, switch to SGPP-SDE (Eq.4) at a late timestep (e.g., tₛₜₒₚ=0.6) to add stochasticity.
  5. Output x₀ from final xₜ=N.

- **Design tradeoffs:**
  - σₚ: Low → high fidelity, low realism (geometric locking). High → high realism, low fidelity (identity loss). Task-dependent optimal range.
  - η (mixture weight): Controls strength of semantic drive. High η accelerates edit but risks artifacts if σₚ too low.
  - Step size ηₖ: Must satisfy 0 < ηₖ < 2/(1/tₖ² + 1/σₚ(tₖ)²) for normal contraction. Larger ηₖ faster but risk instability.
  - Time schedule: Coarse-to-fine (large Δt early, small late) aligns with manifold scales.

- **Failure signatures:**
  - Geometric locking: Output nearly identical to xₚₑᵣ despite high η; indicates σₚ too small or tₛₜₒₚ too early.
  - Manifold drift: Output unrealistic, texture-destroyed; indicates σₚ too large or ηₖ too high without sufficient contraction.
  - Oscillation/divergence: Updates explode; step size ηₖ violates contraction bound, or curvature bound violated due to extreme OOD xₚₑᵣ.
  - Mode collapse (deterministic mode): Output oversmoothed; indicates need to switch to SDE (Eq.4) for diversity.

- **First 3 experiments:**
  1. **2D Manifold Convergence:** Test on two-moons dataset. Compare SGPP trajectories against DPS (unstable) and RF-inversion (locking). SGPP should show robust normal contraction onto manifold spine.
  2. **Zero-shot Semantic Editing (cat→lion):** Use FLUX model. Show RF-inversion fails (texture-swap only), while SGPP with σₚ=0.2, η=0.8 generates correct lion morphology (mane, muzzle) while preserving pose/background.
  3. **Perception-Distortion Trade-off (σₚ sweep):** For reconstruction (no prompt), vary σₚ ∈ {0.01, 0.2, 0.5}. Lower σₚ yields pixel-perfect fidelity; higher σₚ hallucinates realistic details, demonstrating continuous trade-off.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

- **Curvature breakdown:** Does the normal contraction guarantee fail on manifolds with high curvature, violating Assumption 3.1? Real images may have high curvature, potentially undermining stability.
- **Non-Gaussian likelihoods:** Can SGPP be extended to non-Gaussian forward models (e.g., Poisson noise, masks) without losing contraction? Different likelihoods alter the score decomposition and contraction analysis.
- **Stochastic sampler:** Does the stochastic SGPP-SDE preserve normal contraction and sample the true posterior? Stochastic noise and discretization might disrupt contraction and introduce bias.
- **Diffusion adaptation:** Is SGPP's framework specific to Rectified Flow, or can it adapt to standard diffusion models (e.g., DDPM)? Non-linear noise schedules may prevent direct transfer of the decomposition.

## Limitations

- The Tubular Curvature Bound (Assumption 3.1) may be violated for high-dimensional image manifolds, threatening the contraction guarantee.
- Hyperparameter choices (σₚ schedule, step size) lack full theoretical analysis; practical heuristics may not generalize across datasets.
- Numerical instability near t=1 due to division by (1−t) requires ad hoc fixes not covered in the theory.

## Confidence

- **Normal contraction property:** Medium. Theoretically sound under Assumption 3.1, but the assumption is not validated on real image manifolds.
- **RF-inversion as σₚ→0 limit:** High. Rigorous mathematical derivation provided (Theorem I.1).
- **Soft guidance trade-off via σₚ:** High. Empirically validated across multiple experiments (2D, editing, reconstruction).
