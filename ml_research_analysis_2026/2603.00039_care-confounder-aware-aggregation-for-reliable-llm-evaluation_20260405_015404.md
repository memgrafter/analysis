# CARE: Confounder-Aware Aggregation for Reliable LLM Evaluation

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2603.00039
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.00039
- Reference count: 40
- Primary result: CARE reduces aggregation error by up to 26.8% MAE and achieves state-of-the-art accuracy across 12 benchmarks.

## Executive Summary
LLM-as-a-judge ensembles are plagued by correlated errors stemming from shared latent confounders (e.g., verbosity, formatting preferences). Standard aggregation methods that assume independent errors can amplify these biases. This paper introduces CARE, a confounder-aware framework that models judge scores as a function of true quality and latent confounders. For continuous scores, CARE-SVD decomposes the empirical precision matrix into sparse (direct dependencies) and low-rank (latent-induced) components. For discrete or preference data, CARE-Tensor partitions judges into conditionally independent groups and applies tensor decomposition to separate quality from confounders without ground truth. Both variants come with identifiability and finite-sample guarantees. Across 12 benchmarks, CARE reduces error by up to 26.8% in MAE and consistently outperforms baselines, with CARE-SVD dominating scoring tasks and CARE-Tensor leading on classification/preference.

## Method Summary
CARE takes as input a matrix of LLM judge scores (items × judges). For continuous scores, it assumes a joint Gaussian distribution and computes the empirical precision matrix Θ. It then decomposes Θ into a sparse matrix S (capturing direct judge-judge edges) and a low-rank matrix L (capturing latent-induced dependencies). The latent factors are extracted from L (via eigen-decomposition), and the true quality factor Q is identified—typically the leading eigenvector or aligned with a few human anchors. For discrete or pairwise preference data, CARE-Tensor first uses S to partition judges into three groups with minimal inter-group edges, ensuring conditional independence given the latents. It then forms a third-order tensor of cross-moments from the three groups and runs CP decomposition to recover latent state means. The quality state is mapped from these means using heuristics (e.g., projection onto the leading eigenvector of L). Final aggregation weights are derived from Q’s loadings (CARE-SVD) or posterior probabilities (CARE-Tensor).

## Key Results
- CARE reduces MAE by up to 26.8% and improves accuracy over baselines on 12 benchmarks.
- CARE-SVD achieves the lowest MAE on all scoring datasets.
- CARE-Tensor attains the highest accuracy on 5 of 6 classification/preference benchmarks.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1: Correlated Errors from Latent Confounders
Standard aggregation assumes independent judge errors, but LLM judges share latent confounders (e.g., verbosity, formatting preferences) that induce systematic, correlated biases. These shared factors cause errors to compound rather than cancel when aggregated, breaking the independence assumption and amplifying mistakes.

### Mechanism 2: CARE-SVD for Continuous Scores
CARE-SVD models judge scores under a joint Gaussian distribution. The precision matrix Θ = Σ⁻¹ is decomposed as S - L, where S is sparse (direct edges) and L is low-rank (latent factors). The low-rank part captures dependencies induced by latent confounders, including the true quality factor Q. By identifying the dominant latent direction (e.g., leading eigenvector of L), CARE-SVD separates quality from other confounders and weights judges accordingly.

### Mechanism 3: CARE-Tensor for Discrete/Preference Data
For discrete or pairwise preference data, CARE-Tensor exploits conditional independence between groups of judges given the latents. Partitioning judges into three groups with no cross-edges ensures that third-order cross-moments factorize. CP decomposition of the resulting tensor recovers the means of latent states without labels. Heuristics (e.g., projection onto leading eigenvector) then map these states to the true quality.

## Foundational Learning
- **Latent-Variable Markov Random Fields**: Needed to model observed scores, true quality Q, and confounders C with conditional independence while accounting for unobserved variables. Quick check: If we assume sparse conditional independence between judges, the precision matrix structure implies that direct judge-judge edges (non-zero off-diagonals) represent direct dependencies not mediated by latents, while latent-induced dependencies appear as low-rank modifications.
- **Sparse-Plus-Low-Rank Matrix Decomposition**: Used to separate direct judge-judge connections (sparse S) from latent-induced ones (low-rank L) in the observable precision matrix. Quick check: For Θ = S - L, the rank of L equals the number of latent factors because L can be factored as K_JH K_HH⁻¹ K_HJ, where K_JH are judge-latent loadings and K_HH is latent covariance.
- **CP Tensor Decomposition**: Under conditional independence, third-order moments factorize uniquely, enabling recovery of latent state means without labels. Quick check: Three-way factorization breaks rotation/permutation ambiguities that plague second-moment methods because the CP decomposition under rank conditions yields unique factors up to scaling and permutation.

## Architecture Onboarding
*Component map*:  
Precision Estimation -> Sparse+Low-Rank Decomposition -> [CARE-SVD: Eigen-decomposition of L; CARE-Tensor: Judge partitioning into 3 groups + CP decomposition] -> Symmetry Breaking (identify quality factor) -> Aggregation (weighted combination or posterior).

*Critical path*:  
- Precision estimation quality is foundational; requires sufficient samples (n ≳ p log p).  
- For CARE-Tensor, the judge partitioning must yield groups with negligible cross-edges to satisfy conditional independence; this depends on the sparsity threshold.  
- Symmetry breaking is a heuristic step; misidentification of quality factor biases final aggregation.

*Design tradeoffs*:  
- CARE-SVD vs. CARE-Tensor: SVD is simpler and uses only second-order info but requires Gaussianity and orthogonality; Tensor uses third-order moments, more robust to non-Gaussianity but needs a valid conditional independence partition.  
- Symmetry breaking: Using the leading eigenvector is lightweight but may fail if a confounder dominates; human anchors improve reliability but reduce zero-shot applicability.  
- Regularization (γₙ, τ): Controls sparsity and low-rankness; tuned on validation. Over-regularization loses signal; under-regularization retains noise.

*Failure signatures*:  
1. Noisy precision matrix: high condition number or n < p leads to poor decomposition.  
2. No spectral gap: eigenvalues of L are close, making it hard to separate latents.  
3. Cross-block edges: In CARE-Tensor, significant edges between partitioned groups violate conditional independence.  
4. Posterior collapse: In CARE-Tensor, component means are too similar to distinguish quality from confounders.

*First 3 experiments*:  
1. Simulate Gaussian scores with one quality latent and a confounder of varying strength; verify that the leading eigenvector of L aligns with quality when confounder is weak and drifts as confounder strengthens.  
2. Test judge partitioning: generate judges with known conditional independence structure; apply graph partitioning on estimated S; measure cross-block edge density; should be near zero for valid partition.  
3. Inject artificial length bias into two judges (multiply scores by 1.2 for long inputs); confirm CARE isolates a confounder factor loaded on those judges and that MAE improves by >10% over averaging.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
**Open Question 1**: Under what conditions does the leading eigenvector of the low-rank precision component correspond to true quality rather than a confounder? The paper assumes the dominant shared variation reflects quality but does not establish when this holds; a confounder could dominate. Theoretical results linking eigenvector to quality under realistic models or systematic benchmarks are needed.

**Open Question 2**: How sensitive is CARE-Tensor to errors in the estimated sparse conditional independence graph? The method relies on a valid partition, but robustness to graph misestimation is not analyzed. Empirical studies under varying sparsity/sample sizes or theoretical stability analysis are required.

**Open Question 3**: What are identifiability and sample complexity guarantees for non-Gaussian scores? Theory covers Gaussian and binary Gaussian mixtures but not other distributions common in LLM outputs. General theorems for exponential-family models validated on real ordinal/binary data are needed.

## Limitations
- Hyperparameters (γₙ, τ) and exact judge sets/exclusion criteria are not fully documented, requiring validation sweeps.
- The greedy graph partitioning for CARE-Tensor is underspecified; its tolerance ε influences conditional independence assumptions.
- Gaussianity (CARE-SVD) and conditional independence (CARE-Tensor) may not hold for real judge responses, risking mis-specification.

## Confidence
