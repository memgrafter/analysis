# Hierarchic-EEG2Text: Assessing EEG-To-Text Decoding across Hierarchical Abstraction Levels

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2602.20932  
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.20932  
- **Reference count:** 40  
- **Primary result:** EEG-based text decoding performance improves with coarser semantic abstraction levels in a hierarchical episodic evaluation, though absolute accuracy remains near chance for fine-grained classification.

## Executive Summary
This study evaluates whether EEG signals encode object representations across a hierarchy of semantic abstraction, from fine-grained instances to broad categories. Using the large PEERS dataset (931,538 samples, 1,610 labels), the authors construct a WordNet-derived hierarchical evaluation framework. Non-episodic training on all classes fails catastrophically, but episodic meta-learning reveals a clear trend: classification accuracy improves monotonically as tasks move from leaf-level (fine-grained) to higher-level (abstract) semantic nodes. The best model (CBraMod pretrained) achieves normalized accuracy gains from ~1.5% at the finest level to ~5–6% at the coarsest in 2-way tasks, with similar relative gains in 6-way and 10-way settings. This demonstrates EEG's sensitivity to semantic abstraction depth but highlights severe limits for fine-grained neural decoding.

## Method Summary
The method builds a hierarchical episodic evaluation protocol around the PEERS dataset. WordNet is used to construct a directed acyclic graph (DAG) of semantic relationships for each data split (meta-train/val/test). Internal nodes are sampled as episode class sets, defined by their descendant leaf nodes. Episodes are generated via stratified sampling to create support and query sets, enforcing disjointness across episodes. Three model architectures (EEGNet, NICE-EEG, CBraMod) are trained under two regimes: (i) a non-episodic baseline trained on all meta-train classes, and (ii) episodic meta-learning (fo-MAML, proto-fo-MAML). Evaluation aggregates normalized accuracy (chance-adjusted) over all eligible internal nodes, grouped by abstraction depth derived from hypernym path length from the root.

## Key Results
- Non-episodic training on all 1,610 classes yields near-zero normalized accuracy (~0%) in episodic evaluation, confirming failure on fine-grained decoding.
- Episodic evaluation shows consistent, monotonic improvement in normalized accuracy as classification tasks use higher-level, more abstract WordNet nodes.
- For the top model (CBraMod pretrained), normalized accuracy rises from ~1.5% at leaf nodes to ~5–6% at the fourth abstraction level (L-4) in 2-way tasks; similar relative gains hold for 6-way and 10-way episodes.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The episodic framework aligns training and evaluation with hierarchical semantic structure, allowing models to leverage shared characteristics among related concepts. Abstract nodes group visually or semantically similar items (e.g., "food" encompassing "apple", "bread"), reducing within-class variance and amplifying signal relative to noise. This matches EEG's limited SNR: coarse categories provide a tractable signal, while fine-grained distinctions are lost in neural variability and individual differences.

## Foundational Learning
- **Meta-learning (e.g., fo-MAML, proto-fo-MAML):** Enables rapid adaptation to new classes sampled from the same distribution; needed to handle the vast label space without overfitting. *Quick check:* Verify inner-loop updates (5 steps, lr=0.01) improve adaptation on held-out nodes versus baseline.
- **Hierarchical semantic organization (WordNet DAG):** Structures the label space into abstraction levels; needed to define meaningful episode boundaries. *Quick check:* Confirm pruned DAG excludes overly broad roots and nodes with excessive sibling counts as per heuristics.
- **EEG preprocessing and channel alignment:** Standardizes raw signals across montages; needed for consistent input representation. *Quick check:* Validate k-NN channel mapping (k=8) aligns BioSemi ABC to GSN layouts with Euclidean distance minimization.
- **Normalized accuracy (chance-adjusted):** Metric for fair comparison across varying episode class sizes; needed because raw accuracy is misleading in N-way tasks. *Quick check:* Ensure chance level is subtracted per episode (e.g., 50% for 2-way, ~16.7% for 6-way).

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component Map:** Raw EEG (96 channels × 200 Hz × 1 sec) → [EEGNet / NICE-EEG / CBraMod (pretrained on TUEG?)] → hierarchical episodic training (fo-MAML/proto-fo-MAML) → evaluation on WordNet-derived node episodes → normalized accuracy per abstraction level.

**Critical Path:** Data preprocessing → WordNet DAG construction → episode sampling → model training (episodic or baseline) → per-node evaluation → aggregation by abstraction depth.

**Design Tradeoffs:** Non-episodic training is simple but fails on fine-grained labels; episodic learning enables few-shot adaptation but introduces sampling variance and instability. Pretraining (CBraMod on TUEG) boosts performance but risks domain mismatch. Hierarchical probing trades granularity for feasibility.

**Failure Signatures:** (1) Non-episodic baseline: normalized accuracy ~0% across all levels. (2) Unstable meta-learning: high variance across nodes, sometimes worse than baseline. (3) Missing abstraction trend: flat or decreasing accuracy with coarser levels indicates DAG or sampling flaws.

**First Three Experiments:** 1) Replicate non-episodic baseline to confirm collapse. 2) Run episodic evaluation with CBraMod (pretrained) to reproduce the abstraction trend. 3) Ablate episode support set size (e.g., 1-shot vs 5-shot) to test sensitivity to sample count per node.

## Limitations
- Channel selection ambiguity: The exact 96-channel subset of BioSemi ABC used as reference is unspecified, risking misalignment with PEERS montages.
- Episode sampling reimplementation: Equations (1)-(4) lack conflict-resolution details, making faithful reproduction difficult.
- Hierarchy construction heuristics: Pruning thresholds (e.g., ≥45 siblings) are ad-hoc; small changes alter eligible nodes and span sizes.
- Absolute performance ceiling: Normalized accuracy peaks at ~5–6% in 10-way tasks, limiting practical decoding utility despite clear trends.

## Confidence
- **EEG sensitivity to semantic abstraction depth:** High (consistent across models, task sizes, and levels).
- **Non-episodic training failure on fine-grained classification:** High (reproducible collapse aligns with SNR limits).
- **WordNet-derived hierarchy validity:** Medium (data-driven but depends on sense disambiguation and pruning heuristics).
- **Episode sampling reproducibility:** Low (critical parameters omitted; reimplementation may differ).
