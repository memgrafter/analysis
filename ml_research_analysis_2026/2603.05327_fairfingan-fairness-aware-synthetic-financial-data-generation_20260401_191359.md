# FairFinGAN: Fairness-aware Synthetic Financial Data Generation

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2603.05327
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.05327
- **Reference count:** 21
- **Primary result:** FairFinGAN achieves superior fairness metrics (lower statistical parity disparity, improved equalized odds) with minimal loss in predictive accuracy compared to CTGAN and TabFairGAN.

## Executive Summary
FairFinGAN is a novel framework for generating fair synthetic financial data using a Wasserstein GAN (WGAN) with gradient penalty. The method introduces a two-phase training regimen: an initial phase focuses on learning the data distribution for high-fidelity sample generation, while a second phase optimizes for fairness by incorporating a penalty derived from a pre-trained multi-layer perceptron classifier. This classifier acts as a fairness oracle, evaluating generated samples against statistical parity or equalized odds constraints. Evaluated on five real-world financial datasets, FairFinGAN demonstrably reduces bias in synthetic data while preserving downstream predictive utility, offering a practical approach to dataset-level bias mitigation in finance.

## Method Summary
The proposed system comprises three neural components: a generator (G) that maps noise to synthetic samples, a critic (C) that discriminates real from synthetic data, and a pre-trained fairness classifier (H). Training begins with standard WGAN-GP to ensure realistic generation. Then, for a designated number of epochs, the generator’s loss is augmented with a fairness penalty computed by H on the generated data. The penalty measures disparity in the classifier’s predictions across protected groups (statistical parity or equalized odds). By freezing H and only adjusting G, the framework decouples realism from fairness, allowing the generator to produce data that appears both realistic and fair according to H’s assessment. This two-phase alternating schedule stabilizes training and yields synthetic datasets that can be used to train downstream models with improved fairness metrics.

## Key Results
- FairFinGAN significantly lowers statistical parity disparity and equalized odds disparity compared to CTGAN and TabFairGAN.
- Predictive accuracy (accuracy and balanced accuracy) of downstream models trained on FairFinGAN synthetic data remains close to that of CTGAN, incurring only minimal utility loss.
- The approach effectively mitigates bias at the dataset level across diverse financial datasets, demonstrating broad applicability.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

**Mechanism 1: High-fidelity data generation via WGAN-GP**  
- **Claim:** The framework generates high-fidelity synthetic data by first training a Wasserstein GAN (WGAN) to learn the underlying data distribution.  
- **Mechanism:** A generator (G) and a critic (C) engage in adversarial training. The generator transforms noise vectors into synthetic samples, while the critic learns to distinguish real from synthetic data. This phase optimizes for realism via the critic's loss, which includes a gradient penalty term (WGAN-GP) to ensure training stability. The generator is updated to maximize the critic's assessment of synthetic samples.  
- **Core assumption:** The real data distribution can be effectively modeled by the generator's parametric family, and the WGAN objective provides a stable, mode-covering training signal.  
- **Evidence anchors:**   
  * [abstract] "WGAN-based framework"  
  * [section 3] "In phase 1, both the generator (G) and the critic (C) participate in the adversarial process, aiming to generate samples as realistically as possible." and Equation/line 14 for the critic update.  
  * [corpus] Corpus neighbors show WGAN/TabFairGAN as baselines, establishing WGAN as a known stable architecture for tabular data.  
- **Break condition:** If the critic's gradient penalty fails to constrain its Lipschitz constant (e.g., via vanishing gradients or extreme loss values), training becomes unstable, and synthetic data quality collapses.

**Mechanism 2: Fairness optimization via a pre-trained classifier oracle**  
- **Claim:** The framework mitigates dataset-level bias by incorporating a fairness penalty computed by an external classifier on generated samples.  
- **Mechanism:** A multi-layer perceptron (H), **pre-trained on the original real data**, classifies synthetic samples (produced by G) with respect to the protected attribute. The fairness of these classifications (measured by Statistical Parity or Equalized Odds) is then fed back as a penalty term (λ_fair) into the generator's loss during a second training phase. The generator is thus incentivized to produce data that leads H to make fairer predictions.  
- **Core assumption:** The pre-trained classifier's fairness requirements on real data generalize to defining fairness for synthetic data. The classifier's decision boundary, learned from potentially biased real data, acts as a fixed fairness oracle for the generator.  
- **Evidence anchors:**  
  * [abstract] "integrating statistical parity and equalized odds constraints via an MLP classifier"  
  * [section 3] "in phase 2, an MLP classifier, denoted by H... trained on real data D, is used to evaluate the fairness of the classification on the new data generated by the generator" and lines 20-22 defining the fairness penalty term.  
  * [corpus] Weak specific corpus evidence; neighboring papers (TabFairGAN, FairGAN+) use different internal fairness mechanisms (in-discriminator penalties, adversarial debiasing). The explicit use of a *pre-trained external classifier* as a fairness oracle is a stated differentiator.  
- **Break condition:** If the pre-trained classifier H is poorly calibrated or its fairness metric computation is numerically unstable on synthetic data (e.g., division by zero in subgroup counts), the gradient signal becomes corrupted, potentially harming both utility and fairness.

**Mechanism 3: Two-phase alternating schedule decouples realism and fairness**  
- **Claim:** The two-phase, alternating training schedule decouples the learning of data realism from fairness optimization, preventing interference.  
- **Mechanism:** Training is split into an initial "accuracy" phase (standard WGAN) and a later "fairness" phase. In the fairness phase, the generator loss combines the critic's score (for realism) and the fairness penalty (from H). This explicit scheduling allows the critic to first learn a robust representation of "realistic" data, which then provides a stable utility gradient while the generator adjusts its outputs for fairness.  
- **Core assumption:** Separating the optimization goals temporally prevents the conflicting gradients of realism and fairness from destabilizing training from the start. The critic learned in Phase 1 remains a valid utility assessor during Phase 2.  
- **Evidence anchors:**  
  * [section 3] "The training process... consists of two phases" and Algorithm 1 lines 3-7, which switch `mode` from `accuracy` to `fairness` after `total_epochs - fair_epochs`.  
  * [section 4.2] The experimental ablation (implied by `fair_epochs = 50`) tests this scheduling.  
- **Break condition:** If the number of fairness epochs (`fair_epochs`) is too small, fairness constraints are not fully learned; if too large, the generator may overfit to the fixed classifier H's biases, degrading the realism of the final synthetic data.

## Foundational Learning

**Wasserstein GAN with Gradient Penalty (WGAN-GP)**  
- **Why needed:** Provides a stable, theoretically grounded adversarial training framework for generating high-quality tabular data with mixed types, which is foundational before any fairness adjustments can be meaningfully be made.  
- **Quick check:** Why does WGAN-GP use a gradient penalty on the critic instead of weight clipping, and how does this relate to training stability for tabular data?

**Group Fairness Metrics (Statistical Parity, Equalized Odds)**  
- **Why needed:** These formal mathematical definitions are the explicit optimization targets. Understanding their computation formulas is essential to interpret the loss terms.  
- **Quick check:** What is the key difference between Statistical Parity and Equalized Odds in terms of the conditional probabilities they equalize across protected groups?

**Pre-trained Classifier as a Fairness Oracle**  
- **Why needed:** This is the core architectural novelty. The engineer must grasp that H is frozen after initial training and used only to compute a differentiable fairness score, not to classify in a downstream task.  
- **Quick check:** What is a potential risk of using a classifier trained on biased real data as the oracle for generating fair synthetic data?

## Architecture Onboarding

- **Component map:**  
  Noise vector z → Generator G (MLP + Gumbel-Softmax for categoricals) → synthetic sample (X',Y',S')  
  Synthetic/real sample → Critic C (MLP with LeakyReLU) → realism score  
  Synthetic sample → Pre-trained Classifier H (frozen MLP) → fairness penalty  
  Loss functions: Critic loss (WGAN-GP) and Generator loss (-C + λ_fair * fairness_penalty)

- **Critical path:**  
  1. Pre-train H on real data D to predict Y.  
  2. For each epoch:  
     - Update C `n_critic` times with WGAN-GP loss (including gradient penalty).  
     - If in Phase 1 (accuracy): update G once to maximize C's score (loss = -C(G(z))).  
     - If in Phase 2 (fairness): generate new batch, compute H's predictions, calculate fairness disparity (SP or EOd) on (H(X'), Y', S'), then update G with loss = -C(G(z)) + λ_fair * disparity.

- **Design tradeoffs:**  
  - **λ_fair**: Higher values enforce stronger fairness but risk utility loss; must be tuned per dataset.  
  - **fair_epochs**: Too short yields weak fairness; too long may cause overfitting to H's fixed biases.  
  - **Separate H**: Avoids complex multi-objective GAN training but introduces dependence on H's initial bias.  
  - **Gumbel-Softmax τ=0.2**: Low temperature for near-discrete categorical outputs, but may affect gradient flow for fairness penalty.

- **Failure signatures:**  
  - **Utility collapse**: Significant accuracy drop (>5% vs CTGAN) despite fairness gains → λ_fair too high.  
  - **Fairness metric divergence**: NaN or extreme SP/EOd values → zero-division or poor H calibration.  
  - **Mode collapse**: Low diversity in categorical features → insufficient n_critic or Phase 1 epochs.  
  - **H overfitting**: H accuracy >99% on real validation → rigid decision boundary reduces learnable fairness signal.

- **First 3 experiments:**  
  1. **Phase 1 Convergence Check**: Train with `fair_epochs=0`. Verify critic loss stabilizes and downstream accuracy matches CTGAN within 2%, isolating WGAN core.  
  2. **Fairness Oracle Sanity**: After one fairness phase step, compute SP/EOd on synthetic data via H. Disparity must drop relative to real data; otherwise fairness penalty ineffective.  
  3. **Downstream Utility-Fairness Trade-off**: Train downstream classifiers (LR, DT, kNN, MLP) on synthetic data from all methods. Plot Accuracy vs composite fairness (1 - |SP| - |EOd|). FairFinGAN should lie on or near the Pareto frontier.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- **Multiple protected attributes**: The current implementation handles only a single protected attribute at a time. Extending to simultaneous mitigation for multiple attributes remains future work, and its impact on fairness-utility trade-offs is unknown.
- **Differential privacy integration**: The paper suggests incorporating differential privacy to enhance reliability, but no implementation is provided. The interplay between privacy, fairness, and utility is left unexplored.
- **Model-specific fairness variation**: Results show inconsistent fairness improvements across different downstream models (e.g., LR vs. MLP). The reasons for this variation and whether it generalizes require deeper analysis.
- **Sufficiency of fairness constraints**: Only statistical parity and equalized odds are optimized. Financial contexts may require other definitions (e.g., predictive parity, calibration) that are not addressed.
- **Bias propagation from H**: Using a classifier trained on potentially biased real data as a fairness oracle could embed those biases into the synthetic data. The paper does not evaluate the robustness of FairFinGAN to biased H.

## Limitations
- **Reproducibility gaps**: Critical hyperparameters (optimizer settings, H training details, random seeds) are not fully specified, risking inconsistent reproduction.
- **Oracle dependency**: The fairness oracle H is trained on real data and may itself be biased, potentially transmitting those biases to the generated data.
- **Narrow fairness scope**: Evaluation is restricted to binary protected attributes and specific fairness metrics, limiting generalizability to intersectional or context-specific fairness notions.

## Confidence
- **WGAN-GP realism**: *Medium* (well-established architecture, but empirical validation of its stability on all tabular data is not fully detailed).
- **Fairness penalty effectiveness**: *Low–Medium* (novel mechanism that depends on H; evidence is promising but limited).
- **Two-phase decoupling**: *Medium* (plausible design principle, but sensitivity to hyperparameters requires further validation).

## Next Checks
- **Phase‑1 only baseline**: Train with `fair_epochs=0` to confirm realistic generation and downstream accuracy comparable to CTGAN (within 2%).
- **Oracle sanity test**: After a single fairness update, measure SP/EOd on synthetic data using H; disparity must decrease relative to real data.
- **Hyperparameter sweep**: Grid‑search λ_fair and fair_epochs; plot accuracy vs composite fairness (1−|SP|−|EOd|) to verify Pareto improvement over baselines.
