# NEMESIS: Noise-suppressed Efficient MAE with Enhanced Superpatch Integration Strategy

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2604.01612  
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.01612  
- **Reference count:** 20  
- **Primary result:** 32× GFLOP reduction (31.0 vs. 985.8) and state‑of‑the‑art mean AUROC 0.9633 on BTCV organ‑presence classification with a frozen backbone and linear classifier.

## Executive Summary
NEMESIS is a self‑supervised masked autoencoder designed for 3D CT scans that tackles two major challenges: the high memory cost of full‑volume processing and the anisotropic spatial structure inherent to CT data. It operates on local 128×128×128 “superpatches” to bound token count and computational complexity. Key innovations include noise‑enhanced reconstruction (Gaussian noise injection), Masked Anatomical Transformer Blocks (MATB) that jointly model intra‑patch (axis‑wise) and inter‑patch (plane‑wise) relationships, and NEMESIS Tokens (NT) that aggregate cross‑scale context from the superpatch. Pretraining on multiple unlabeled CT datasets yields representations that transfer strongly to downstream tasks: a frozen backbone plus a linear classifier achieves a mean AUROC of 0.9633 on the BTCV organ‑presence benchmark, outperforming fully fine‑tuned SuPreM and VoCo. Under a low‑label (10% BTCV) regime, AUROC remains high at 0.9075. Computationally, NEMESIS requires only 31.0 GFLOPs per forward pass—a 32× reduction compared to a full‑volume baseline—while maintaining reconstruction quality (PSNR ≈ 27.75 dB).

## Method Summary
NEMESIS builds on the masked autoencoding (MAE) framework but introduces three targeted enhancements for 3D CT data. First, images are partitioned into fixed‑size 128³ superpatches during pretraining, each further divided into 16³ tokens, yielding a manageable sequence length and enabling memory‑efficient training. Second, Gaussian noise is added to the input superpatch, forcing the model to learn robust denoising features. Third, the core encoder and decoder stacks consist of Masked Anatomical Transformer Blocks (MATB). Each MATB processes tokens in parallel branches: an axis‑wise stream models relationships within the 3D token grid (intra‑patch), while a plane‑wise stream operates on the three anatomical planes (inter‑patch). Their outputs are concatenated and fused, capturing both fine‑grained and slice‑wise patterns characteristic of anisotropic CT scans. Additionally, NEMESIS Tokens (NT) are prepended to the token sequence at the embedding stage; via a self‑attention block, they aggregate a global summary of the superpatch, which is adaptively gated back into the patch embeddings to provide coarse context for reconstruction. The encoder processes only visible tokens, while the decoder attends to both latent and mask tokens to reconstruct the normalized pixel values of the masked patches. Downstream evaluation typically freezes the pretrained encoder and trains a linear classifier, demonstrating strong transfer even with minimal labeled data.

## Key Results
- **Efficiency:** 31.0 GFLOPs per forward pass—32× fewer than a full‑volume baseline (985.8 GFLOPs)—while preserving anatomical detail.
- **BTCV classification:** Frozen backbone + linear classifier achieves mean AUROC 0.9633 (F1 0.753), exceeding fully fine‑tuned SuPreM (0.9493) and VoCo (0.9387).
- **Low‑label regime:** Using only 10% of BTCV labels, the same linear probe yields AUROC 0.9075, confirming label‑efficient learning.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

**Superpatch-Based Token Reduction**  
Operating on local 128³ superpatches bounds the token count (e.g., 512 tokens for 16³ patches), avoiding quadratic self‑attention costs on full volumes. This design assumes that local anatomical detail suffices for pretraining; global dependencies can be approximated later or via auxiliary tokens. The reported 32× GFLOP reduction and abstract/Section III.A descriptions support this mechanism, though external corroboration is limited.

**Dual-Masking MATB for Anisotropy**  
MATB processes tokens in two parallel streams: axis‑wise (intra‑patch) and plane‑wise (inter‑patch). This addresses CT anisotropy (e.g., varying slice thickness) by capturing both fine‑grained 3D relationships and slice‑wise patterns. The fusion of both branches yields richer representations. Evidence includes the paper’s description in Section III.C and an ablation showing plane‑only masking outperforms axis‑only (PSNR 27.75 dB vs. 24.87 dB). However, no independent anisotropy metrics or external replications exist to firmly link performance gains to anisotropy modeling.

**NEMESIS Tokens for Cross‑Scale Context**  
A set of learnable tokens is prepended to the patch sequence and passed through a self‑attention block, allowing them to attend across all patches and aggregate a global summary of the superpatch. An adaptive gating mechanism integrates this context into the final embeddings, guiding reconstruction of masked regions. This mitigates the loss of long‑range cues caused by chunking. The concept is introduced in the abstract and Section III.B, but ablation results show only marginal gains, and the break condition (gate α collapse) is not explored, leaving the necessity of NT uncertain.

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component map:**  
Superpatch Sampler → Noise Injector → Patch Embedding (with NEMESIS Tokens & adaptive fusion) → Encoder (MATB stack) → Latent Bottleneck → Decoder (MATB stack) → Reconstruction Head → Loss Calculator

**Critical path:**  
Input volume → random 128³ superpatch cropping → Gaussian noise injection → split into 16³ patches → linear projection + NT‑augmented embedding → visible tokens processed by MATB encoder → compressed latent → MATB decoder reconstructs masked patches → MSE loss computed against clean superpatch.

**Design tradeoffs:**
- *Superpatch size:* Larger preserves more context but increases token count; smaller enhances memory efficiency but risks missing large structures.
- *Masking ratio:* Higher ratio (e.g., 0.75) forces stronger representation learning but can degrade reconstruction if too extreme.
- *Number of NEMESIS Tokens:* More tokens capture richer global context at the cost of additional parameters and memory.
- *Dual‑masking parallelism:* Provides comprehensive feature extraction but roughly doubles the attention stream computations; a sequential variant could reduce cost but may lose synergy.
- *Noise magnitude:* Higher noise improves denoising robustness but may slow convergence; optimal value is not reported.

**Failure signatures:**
- Reconstruction PSNR below 25 dB after prolonged training suggests a problematic masking ratio, excessive noise, insufficient model capacity, or poor optimization.
- Linear probe AUROC close to random (< 0.6) indicates frozen features are not discriminative; possible causes include too small superpatches, broken NT integration, or under‑trained encoder.
- MATB attention maps appear uniform or dominated by a single stream; may signal one branch is not learning useful patterns (e.g., due to initialization imbalance).
- Out‑of‑memory errors during training imply superpatch size or batch size is too large for the GPU; reducing one of them or using gradient checkpointing can help.
- NEMESIS Token vectors become nearly identical after training; indicates global context collapse, possibly from an overly aggressive gating mechanism.

**First three experiments:**
1. **Main downstream evaluation** – Pretrain on unlabeled CT, freeze backbone, train linear classifier on BTCV organ‑presence. Achieves mean AUROC 0.9633 (F1 0.753), exceeding fully fine‑tuned SuPreM (0.9493) and VoCo (0.9387).
2. **Low‑label regime** – Use only 10% of BTCV labels with frozen backbone + linear classifier; NEMESIS attains AUROC 0.9075, demonstrating strong label efficiency.
3. **Ablation of masking strategies** – Compare axis‑only, plane‑only, and dual‑masking MATB on reconstruction metrics. Plane‑only yields PSNR 27.75 dB and SSIM 0.529; axis‑only drops to PSNR 24.87 dB and SSIM 0.294, validating the advantage of capturing plane‑wise dependencies.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
1. How does NEMESIS transfer to more complex segmentation tasks (e.g., Synapse multi‑organ dataset) beyond its evaluation on BTCV organ‑presence classification? (Conclusion states this as future work.)
2. What specific anatomical structures or anisotropic dependencies do the plane‑wise and axis‑wise attention streams in MATB capture, and how do their learned representations differ? (Conclusion calls for interpretability analysis.)
3. Are both masking streams in MATB equally necessary, or could a simplified single‑stream approach (e.g., plane‑only masking) achieve similar performance with lower computational cost? (Inferred from Table IV showing plane‑only > axis‑only in reconstruction.)
4. How does NEMESIS generalize to CT scans from diverse sources (different hospitals, scanner manufacturers, acquisition protocols) not represented in the pretraining datasets? (Pretraining uses specific public datasets; no external validation or domain shift experiments.)
5. Is Gaussian noise the optimal choice for the noise‑enhanced reconstruction pretext task, or would CT‑realistic noise models (e.g., Poisson‑Gaussian) yield better representations for downstream tasks? (Rationale for Gaussian noise not discussed.)

## Limitations
- **Missing training details:** Core hyperparameters (learning rate, noise σ, exact MATB depth/dim) are unreported, hindering exact replication.
- **Implementation ambiguities:** Precise gating for NEMESIS Tokens and reshape operations for dual‑masking streams are underspecified, leading to potential mis‑implementation.
- **Limited validation:** Evaluation is confined to a single small dataset (BTCV, n=30); no segmentation or domain‑shift experiments; moderate reconstruction metrics (PSNR ~27.5 dB) whose link to downstream gains remains unclear.

## Confidence
- Superpatch Efficiency: **High** (explicit 32× GFLOP reduction and token count argument)
- Dual‑Masking MATB for Anisotropy: **Medium** (ablation supports but lacks independent anisotropy evidence)
- NEMESIS Tokens for Context: **Low** (claims not strongly corroborated; marginal gains reported)
- Downstream SOTA on BTCV: **Medium** (small test set, potential data leakage as BTCV appears in pretraining pool)

## Next Checks
1. **Replicate Ablation of Masking Strategies:** Implement axis‑only, plane‑only, and dual‑masking MATB; train on identical unlabeled CT data for equal steps; measure reconstruction PSNR/SSIM and downstream linear probe AUROC to directly validate the claimed advantage of dual‑masking.
2. **NT Ablation in Linear Probe:** Train full NEMESIS with and without the NEMESIS Token pathway; freeze backbones; train linear classifiers on BTCV; a significant AUROC drop (> 0.02) would confirm NT’s contribution to transferable features.
3. **Inspect MATB Attention Distribution:** Extract attention weights from trained MATB blocks on a held‑out CT volume; compute the percentage of attention mass allocated to intra‑patch (axis‑wise) versus inter‑patch (plane‑wise) patterns; if one stream consistently dominates, the “parallel dual‑masking” benefit is undermined.
