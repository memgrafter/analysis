# MARCUS: An agentic, multimodal vision-language model for cardiac diagnosis and management

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2603.22179  
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.22179  
- **Reference count:** 40  
- **Primary result:** MARCUS is an agentic vision-language system that coordinates domain-specific experts for cardiac diagnostics, achieving 87–91% ECG accuracy, 67–86% echocardiography accuracy, and 85–88% CMR accuracy—outperforming frontier models by 34–45 percentage points and reaching 70% multimodal accuracy with near-zero mirage reasoning.

## Executive Summary
MARCUS tackles the human bottleneck in interpreting multimodal cardiac data (ECG, echocardiography, CMR) through a hierarchical agentic architecture. It orchestrates specialized expert models—each combining a custom visual encoder with an optimized LLM—via a multimodal coordinator that decomposes queries, aggregates outputs, and employs counterfactual probing to enforce visual grounding. Trained on 13.5 million images and 1.6 million curated questions, MARCUS sets new accuracy records across all modalities and demonstrates robust multimodal integration, nearly tripling comparator performance on complex cases while resisting mirage reasoning.

## Method Summary
MARCUS employs a three-stage training pipeline per modality: (1) pretraining a modality-specific visual encoder (SigLIP for ECG; multi-view ViT for echo/CMR) alongside a frozen 3B LLM (Qwen2.5-VL-3B-Instruct), (2) supervised fine-tuning on 741k expert-curated Q&A pairs, and (3) reinforcement learning via GRPO on 879k multiple-choice questions. A multimodal orchestrator manages query decomposition, expert routing, and response synthesis, using counterfactual probing to ensure predictions are visually grounded. The system processes ECG as 4×3 grids and echo/CMR as frame patches, scaling to 13.5M images and 1.6M Q&A.

## Key Results
- Per-modality accuracy: ECG 87–91%, echocardiography 67–86%, CMR 85–88% on internal and external cohorts.
- Multimodal accuracy 70% versus 22–28% for frontier models (GPT-5, Gemini 2.5 Pro), with 1.7–3.0× higher free-text quality.
- Mirage reasoning susceptibility reduced to 0% when the orchestrator is engaged.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
MARCUS’s agentic design coordinates specialized experts rather than forcing a monolithic model to handle all modalities. The orchestrator intelligently decomposes queries, routes them to appropriate experts, and aggregates responses while using counterfactual probing to validate visual grounding. This modularity allows each expert to deeply optimize for its modality’s idiosyncrasies (e.g., ECG grids vs. ultrasound video), and the orchestrator’s verification step mitigates hallucinations and mirage reasoning that plague single-model approaches.

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component map:**  
ECG Expert (SigLIP + Qwen2.5-VL-3B) → Multimodal Orchestrator → Final Diagnosis  
Echo Expert (multi-view ViT + Qwen2.5-VL-3B) → Multimodal Orchestrator → Final Diagnosis  
CMR Expert (multi-view ViT + Qwen2.5-VL-3B) → Multimodal Orchestrator → Final Diagnosis  

**Critical path:**  
Input query → Orchestrator decomposition (identify required modalities) → Parallel expert inference → Confidence-weighted aggregation → Response generation with counterfactual checks → Output.

**Design tradeoffs:**  
- Modularity vs. latency: Specialist experts improve accuracy but add orchestration overhead.  
- Data efficiency vs. complexity: Custom visual encoders require large datasets but capture modality nuances.  
- Interpretability vs. automation: Orchestrator’s decomposition aids debugging but complicates pipeline.

**Failure signatures:**  
- Modality confusion: Orchestrator routes echo question to ECG expert; check via routing logs.  
- Visual grounding failure: High-confidence predictions without salient image features; trigger counterfactual probe failures.  
- Orchestrator bottleneck: Queue buildup under multimodal load; monitor GPU utilization per component.  
- Expert imbalance: One expert’s low confidence drags down multimodal accuracy; track per-expert contribution scores.

**First experiments:**  
1. Benchmark each expert model in isolation on its modality’s test set to establish upper bounds.  
2. Substitute orchestrator with a rule-based router (e.g., keyword matching) to quantify coordination value.  
3. Stress-test with single-modality queries passed through full pipeline to ensure no degradation versus expert-only.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
### Open Question 1
- **Question:** How does MARCUS generalize to diverse healthcare institutions with varying imaging equipment, protocols, and patient demographics beyond the single-center training data?
- **Basis in paper:** [explicit] The authors state: "Our training data was derived from a single centre... generalizability to diverse community populations, imaging protocols, and equipment vendors requires further study." They note external validation showed performance variability, especially in echocardiography.
- **Why unresolved:** Current evaluation is limited to one external site (UCSF) with similar academic settings; true generalizability to community hospitals and diverse populations remains untested.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** A prospective multi-center validation study across 10+ diverse institutions, measuring accuracy consistency and identifying site-specific failure modes.

### Open Question 2
- **Question:** Does prospective deployment of MARCUS in clinical workflows reduce diagnostic errors, shorten time-to-treatment, and improve patient outcomes compared to standard care?
- **Basis in paper:** [explicit] The limitations state: "this study is retrospective. It remains unclear whether MARCUS improves outcomes, reduces diagnostic errors, or shortens time to treatment. These remain to be established in prospective trials."
- **Why unresolved:** The study only measures accuracy on curated benchmarks, not real-world clinical impact metrics like time saved, error reduction, or patient survival/quality of life.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Randomized controlled trials where MARCUS assists cardiologists vs. control, tracking diagnostic time, inter-observer agreement, missed findings, and downstream clinical outcomes.

### Open Question 3
- **Question:** What architectural or data-specific factors contribute to MARCUS's comparatively lower accuracy and higher failure rate in echocardiography interpretation versus ECG and CMR?
- **Basis in paper:** [explicit] Results show echocardiography accuracy (67-86%) is lower than ECG (87-91%) and CMR (85-88%). Failure case analysis identifies echocardiography as having the highest VQA failure rate (59%) and significant modality confusion errors. The authors speculate "the greater heterogeneity and operator-dependence of ultrasound imaging" may be responsible.
- **Why unresolved:** The paper does not isolate whether the issue stems from visual encoder limitations, temporal modeling challenges, data quality/quantity, or inherent ultrasound ambiguity.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Ablation studies comparing echo vs. other modalities' trained encoders; controlled experiments with standardized echo datasets to decouple acquisition variability from interpretation difficulty; failure mode analysis of specific echo subcategories (e.g., valves, function) to pinpoint weak points.

## Limitations
- Test images/videos require IRB-approved access; public verification impossible without released samples.
- Frontier model baselines (GPT-5, Gemini 2.5 Pro) use proprietary APIs with undocumented settings, hindering exact replication.
- Full training corpus (13.5M images, 1.6M Q&A) is unreleased, preventing representativeness and bias assessment.
- Mirage resistance claim is conditional on orchestrator engagement; disengaged behavior and edge cases are unspecified.

## Confidence
- **Per-modality accuracy (ECG 87–91%, Echo 67–86%, CMR 85–88%):** Medium – Robust internal validation but external generalizability unproven without public test data.
- **Multimodal superiority (70% vs. 22–28%):** Medium – Large margin relies on inaccessible multimodal cases.
- **Mirage resistance (0% with orchestrator):** Low – Mechanism and full test suite undisclosed; claim may be context-dependent.
