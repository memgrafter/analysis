# Synthetic Melanoma Image Generation and Evaluation Using Generative Adversarial Networks
## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID**: 2603.13497
- **Source URL**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.13497
- **Reference count**: 40
- **Primary result**: StyleGAN2 (γ=0.8) generated high-fidelity melanoma images (FID 7.96 on ISIC 2020) that improved downstream melanoma detection AUC from 0.925 to 0.945 when used for augmentation.

## Executive Summary
This study tackles data scarcity and class imbalance in melanoma detection by benchmarking four GAN architectures for high-resolution (512×512) synthetic melanoma image generation. Optimized on ISIC 2018 and 2020 datasets, StyleGAN2 with a low R1 regularization weight (γ=0.8) produced the highest quality samples, achieving Fréchet Inception Distance (FID) scores of 24.8 and 7.96, respectively. The generated images were highly realistic: a frozen classifier recognized 83% as melanoma, and board-certified dermatologists distinguished them from real images at only 66.5% accuracy (chance 50%). Crucially, augmenting a severely imbalanced training set (98% benign, 2% melanoma) with 6,500 synthetic melanoma images improved melanoma detection AUC on a held-out real test set from 0.925 to 0.945, demonstrating direct clinical utility for mitigating dataset bias.

## Method Summary
The study trained DCGAN, StyleGAN2, and StyleGAN3-T/R on preprocessed melanoma images from ISIC 2018 (1,061 images) and ISIC 2020 (7,227 images). Preprocessing differed: DCGAN used bilateral filtering and aggressive rotations/flips, while StyleGAN variants used only horizontal flipping. All models generated 512×512 images. Key training parameters included a batch size of 32 and exploration of R1 regularization weight (γ) for StyleGAN models at {0.8, 1.6, 8.0, 10.0}. StyleGAN2 with γ=0.8 was optimal. Downstream utility was evaluated by augmenting a class-imbalanced real training set with 6,500 optimal synthetic images, then training an EfficientNet-B0 classifier and testing on a held-out set (1,960 benign, 40 melanoma images). Evaluation used distributional metrics (FID, FMD), a frozen classifier's recognizability, dermatologist Turing tests, and downstream AUC.

## Key Results
- StyleGAN2 (γ=0.8) achieved best FID: 24.8 on ISIC 2018 and 7.96 on ISIC 2020.
- Dermatologists distinguished real from synthetic melanoma images at 66.5% accuracy (Cohen’s κ=0.17), indicating high perceptual realism.
- Augmenting an imbalanced training set with 6,500 synthetic melanoma images improved melanoma detection AUC from 0.925 to 0.945 on a held-out real test set.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1
- **Claim:** High-fidelity melanoma image synthesis preserves diagnostically relevant features.
- **Mechanism:** StyleGAN2’s style-based generation and adaptive instance normalization disentangle coarse structure from fine textures, enabling capture of dermoscopic details (e.g., pigment networks, border irregularity) while avoiding artifacts seen in StyleGAN3-R.
- **Core assumption:** Distributional similarity (low FID) and sample-level representativeness (low FMD) correlate with clinical feature preservation.
- **Evidence anchors:** FID scores of 24.8 (ISIC 2018) and 7.96 (ISIC 2020) at γ=0.8; Table 2 shows γ=0.8 minimizes FID; Table 1 shows StyleGAN2’s balanced FID/FMD vs. StyleGAN3-R’s artifacts.

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim:** Synthetic melanoma images augment minority-class data to mitigate classifier bias from imbalance.
- **Mechanism:** By adding diverse synthetic melanomas (recognized as melanoma by a frozen classifier), the decision boundary shifts to better separate classes, improving AUC on imbalanced test data.
- **Core assumption:** Synthetic images occupy the melanoma manifold without introducing out-of-distribution artifacts that mislead the classifier.
- **Evidence anchors:** AUC improvement from 0.925 to 0.945 with 6,500 synthetic images; Table 4 shows Real+Synthetic training yields higher melanoma AUC and F1.

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim:** Generated images achieve perceptual realism sufficient for clinical plausibility.
- **Mechanism:** Absence of consistent visual artifacts and high human–machine agreement divergence indicate that synthetic images fall within natural variation of dermoscopic appearance.
- **Core assumption:** Dermatologists’ inability to consistently distinguish synthetic from real images validates realism for human-in-the-loop contexts.
- **Evidence anchors:** Dermatologists’ mean accuracy 66.5% (chance 50%), κ=0.17; 27.5% mean synthetic acceptance in complementary rater strategies.

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component map:**  
Mapping Network (z → W latent) → Synthesis Network (AdaIN blocks, progressive 512×512 output) → Discriminator (with R1 gradient penalty on real images)

**Critical path:**
1. Preprocess melanoma images (horizontal flip only for StyleGAN2/3; bilateral filter for DCGAN).
2. Train with Adam (lr=0.002, β1=0.5), batch size 32, γ tuning for StyleGAN models.
3. Monitor FID/FMD on 1,000-sample holdout; select γ=0.8 for StyleGAN2.
4. Generate 6,500 synthetic melanomas for augmentation.

**Design tradeoffs:**
- γ: Lower (0.8) improves FID but risks discriminator overfitting; higher (8–10) stabilizes but adds blur.
- Augmentation: External flips only (StyleGAN2) vs. internal (StyleGAN2/3 ADA).
- Resolution: 512×512 preserves dermoscopic detail but requires more compute than 256×256.

**Failure signatures:**
- StyleGAN3-R: Grid artifacts in >60% of samples.
- StyleGAN3-T: High FID + low FMD = partial mode collapse.
- DCGAN: Blurry, low-detail outputs.

**First 3 experiments:**
1. Replicate DCGAN baseline on 256×256; confirm FID >60.
2. Train StyleGAN2 with γ ∈ {0.8, 1.6, 8.0}; verify FID improvement at γ=0.8 on ISIC 2018.
3. Run frozen classifier test on 1,000 StyleGAN2 samples; expect ~83% melanoma recognition.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- **Cross-dataset generalization:** To what extent do StyleGAN2-generated melanoma images generalize in improving detection performance when applied to diverse external datasets (e.g., smartphone-captured images, privately collected hospital data) and across different imaging modalities? *Basis:* Explicitly stated as future work. *Why unresolved:* Downstream utility demonstrated only on held-out test set from same ISIC distribution; real-world deployment requires validation on truly external data with different acquisition protocols.
- **Diffusion model comparison:** Do modern diffusion-based generative models (e.g., latent diffusion models) surpass StyleGAN2 in preserving the fine-grained, diagnostically relevant dermoscopic features for melanoma synthesis? *Basis:* Explicitly identified as key future work. *Why unresolved:* Study benchmarks GANs only; diffusion models' trade-offs for feature preservation and computational cost in melanoma context are unvalidated.
- **Optimal synthetic ratio:** What is the optimal ratio of synthetic to real melanoma images for augmenting an imbalanced training set to maximize detection performance on a real-world, highly imbalanced test set? *Basis:* Inferred from fixed augmentation ratio and citation of non-monotonic prior results. *Why unresolved:* Study demonstrates feasibility at one ratio but does not explore parameter space; optimal point likely depends on base imbalance, synthetic quality, and model capacity.
- **Conditional generation for diversity:** Can conditional GANs or diffusion models effectively generate melanoma images conditioned on specific clinical attributes (e.g., patient skin type, melanoma subtype, anatomical location) to fill targeted gaps in training data diversity? *Basis:* Explicitly proposed as future work. *Why unresolved:* Current study uses unconditional generation, which may not correct representation biases (e.g., under-representation of darker skin tones).
- **7-point checklist fidelity:** Do StyleGAN2-generated melanoma images preserve the specific dermoscopic features (e.g., atypical pigment network, blue-white veil) required by the 7-point checklist, or do they introduce subtle artifacts that could mislead algorithms? *Basis:* Authors note need for structured expert assessment using the 7-point checklist. *Why unresolved:* Evaluation was a binary real/fake task; systematic scoring of individual diagnostic features was not performed.

## Limitations
- Implementation details for StyleGAN3-T/R variants and the frozen EfficientNet classifiers are not fully specified, blocking exact replication.
- Data split methodology (patient-wise vs. image-wise) and random seed for the augmentation experiment are undisclosed, affecting reproducibility.
- The held-out test set contains only 40 melanoma cases, risking high variance in AUC estimates.
- Dermatologist accuracy of 66.5% (κ=0.17) indicates persistent artifacts, limiting claims of full perceptual realism.

## Confidence
- **Synthesis Fidelity (Low FID/FMD)**: Medium. Metrics support quality, but StyleGAN3-R artifacts and partial mode collapse in StyleGAN3-T reveal instability.
- **Downstream Utility (AUC Improvement)**: High. The 0.925→0.945 AUC gain on the test set is clear and statistically significant, though dataset-specific.
- **Perceptual Realism (Human Evaluation)**: Medium. Above-chance accuracy suggests realism, but low inter-rater agreement and 33.5% error rate imply residual non-plausible features.
