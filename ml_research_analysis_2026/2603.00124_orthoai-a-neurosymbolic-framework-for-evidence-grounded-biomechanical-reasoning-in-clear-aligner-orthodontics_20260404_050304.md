# OrthoAI: A Neurosymbolic Framework for Evidence-Grounded Biomechanical Reasoning in Clear Aligner Orthodontics

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2603.00124
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.00124
- Reference count: 30
- Primary result: OrthoAI achieves 81.4% Tooth Identification Rate with 60,705 parameters and executes in under 4 seconds on CPU, while detecting an average of 1.5 critical biomechanical constraint violations per case.

## Executive Summary
OrthoAI is a neurosymbolic framework that bridges geometric perception and clinical reasoning for clear aligner orthodontics. It introduces a sparse-supervision segmentation approach that trains from sparse landmark annotations using a landmark-to-point-cloud synthesis and a batch-adaptive composite loss. Biomechanical feasibility is modeled as a Constraint Satisfaction Problem (CSP) encoding evidence-based per-stage movement limits, providing graded alerts. Treatment quality is assessed via a Multi-Criteria Decision Analysis (MCDA) score, instantiated as a Weighted Additive Value Function. On the 3DTeethLand benchmark, the segmentation module achieves 81.4% Tooth Identification Rate with only 60,705 parameters, and the full pipeline runs in under 4 seconds on a CPU. Ablations confirm the contribution of each component, and the system flags an average of 1.5 critical biomechanical violations per case, demonstrating its potential for evidence-grounded decision support.

## Method Summary
The framework comprises three interconnected modules. First, the perceptual module converts sparse tooth landmarks (6–8 points) into synthetic dense point clouds by fitting ellipsoids to the landmark frame, training a Dynamic Graph CNN (DGCNN) with a composite loss combining label-smoothed cross-entropy and a batch-adaptive Dice term to handle class imbalance. Second, a symbolic CSP engine encodes biomechanical limits from literature as typed constraints (hard/soft) over per-tooth movement variables, evaluating planned movements against stage-specific thresholds to generate critical/warning alerts. Third, a treatment quality scorer aggregates six criteria (biomechanical compliance, predictability, staging efficiency, attrition, interproximal reduction, symmetry) using a weighted additive value function (WAVF) with heuristic weights, yielding a composite score (0–100) and letter grade. The pipeline processes an intraoral scan (with assumed landmark annotations) through synthetic point cloud generation, segmentation, semantic lifting (assigning FDI labels, centroids, principal axes), CSP evaluation, and MCDA scoring, all within seconds on a CPU.

## Key Results
- 81.4% Tooth Identification Rate on the 3DTeethLand benchmark using a model with 60,705 parameters.
- Full pipeline executes in under 4 seconds on CPU, enabling real-time feedback.
- System detects an average of 1.5 critical biomechanical constraint violations per case, highlighting its clinical relevance.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1: Sparse-supervision segmentation via landmark-to-point-cloud synthesis
- **Claim:** Sparse-supervision segmentation via landmark-to-point-cloud synthesis and a batch-adaptive composite loss enables effective tooth identity segmentation from landmark-only annotations, addressing severe class imbalance and presence variance.
- **Mechanism:** A parameterized generative model `G(λk;θ)` converts sparse tooth landmarks (6–8 points) into synthetic, dense point clouds by fitting oriented ellipsoids to the landmark frame. A DGCNN processes these clouds. The loss combines label-smoothed cross-entropy with a **batch-adaptive Dice term** (`L_batch_Dice`) that restricts Dice computation to classes present in the current mini-batch, preventing gradient starvation for rare tooth classes absent from a batch.
- **Core assumption:** The ellipsoidal approximation is a sufficiently accurate geometric prior for learning tooth identity; clinical sufficiency for downstream reasoning requires only correct label, centroid, and approximate axes (Definition 1), not precise boundaries.
- **Evidence anchors:** 
  - [abstract]: "sparse-supervision segmentation: a landmark-to-point-cloud synthesis protocol enables training from sparse anatomical annotations (6–8 points per tooth) instead of dense labels, combined with a clinically stratified loss mixing label-smoothed cross-entropy and a batch-adaptive Dice term."
  - [section 4.2]: Details the geometric model (Eqs. 4-8) and gingival sampling; Section 4.4.2 defines `L_batch_Dice` (Eq. 11) and its role in handling presence imbalance.
  - [corpus]: No direct citations to this specific synthesis protocol in neighbor papers; evidence is internal to this work.
- **Break condition:** If the ellipsoidal prior fails to capture true anatomical morphology (e.g., complex occlusal surfaces, interproximal contacts) in real intraoral scans, the perceptual module's TIR will degrade significantly outside the synthetic training distribution. The divergence between training (synthetic) and deployment (real) data is explicitly noted as the primary barrier (Section 6.3).

### Mechanism 2: Biomechanical feasibility as a Constraint Satisfaction Problem
- **Claim:** Formulating biomechanical feasibility as a Constraint Satisfaction Problem (CSP) over a typed movement ontology enables auditable, extensible, and graded inference of constraint violations, bridging symbolic clinical knowledge with perceptual outputs.
- **Mechanism:** A knowledge base `K = (V, D, C)` encodes variables (`v_k,j` for movement components), domains (`D_k,j` from literature limits), and typed constraints (`C_hard`, `C_soft`). A graded satisfaction function `σ` (Eq. 1) maps planned movement values to critical/warning alerts based on per-stage limits (`¯v_k,j`) that depend on tooth type (`τ(lk)`). The CSP engine operates on the **clinically sufficient representation** (`ˆτ_k`) from the perceptual module.
- **Core assumption:** Biomechanical feasibility can be decomposed into independently evaluable, per-tooth movement components with population-level thresholds; the CSP's graded semantics accurately map threshold breaches to clinical severity.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract]: "knowledge-grounded constraint inference: biomechanical feasibility is modeled as a Constraint Satisfaction Problem over a domain ontology of tooth movements, encoding evidence-based per-stage limits as soft and hard constraints."
  - [section 3.3]: Formal CSP definition (Definition 2), satisfaction function (Eq. 1), and ontological extensibility remark (Remark 2). Table 1 provides the concrete constraint specification and evidence sources.
  - [corpus]: Neighbors like "CARE" (263776) use evidence-grounded agentic frameworks but not orthodontic CSPs; no direct citation validates this specific CSP design.
- **Break condition:** If planned movements involve coupled tooth interactions (e.g., anchorage control) not captured by independent variable constraints, or if patient-specific factors (bone density, root morphology) require personalized thresholds, the CSP will produce false negatives (missed violations) or false positives (inaccurate alerts). Fixed thresholds are a stated limitation (Section 6.3).

### Mechanism 3: Treatment quality as Multi-Criteria Decision Analysis
- **Claim:** Framing treatment quality as a Multi-Criteria Decision Analysis (MCDA) problem, instantiated as a Weighted Additive Value Function (WAVF), provides a formally grounded, transparent, and auditable composite score whose weighting structure can be calibrated against clinical preferences.
- **Mechanism:** Six criteria (biomechanical compliance, predictability, etc.) yield normalized sub-scores (`s_i`). The WAVF aggregates them via `S(s) = 100 * Σ w_i * v_i(s_i)` (Eq. 2,3), currently with linear `v_i(s)=s`. The weight vector `w` (e.g., `w_bio=0.30`) reflects clinical priority theory, and the formulation ensures Pareto-consistency (Proposition 2). Grade thresholds (A≥90, etc.) are provisional.
- **Core assumption:** Criteria are mutually preferentially independent (WAVF assumption); a linear marginal value function (`v_i(s)=s`) adequately captures clinical utility per criterion; a single composite score meaningfully distills multi-dimensional treatment quality.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract]: "multi-criteria decision analysis treatment score... grounded in clinical priority theory."
  - [section 3.4]: Defines WAVF (Definition 3, Eq.2-3), discusses Pareto-consistency (Proposition 2), and explicitly notes the limitations of mutual independence and linear `v_i` (Remark 3). Weight values are stated (Eq.3) but noted as heuristic.
  - [corpus]: Neighbors apply MCDA in other medical contexts (e.g., [22] in references), but no neighbor paper validates this specific orthodontic WAVF structure.
- **Break condition:** If clinical preferences exhibit strong criterion interactions (e.g., the value of biomechanical compliance depends on staging efficiency), violating mutual preferential independence, the WAVF will produce non-representative rankings. Uncalibrated weights and linear `v_i` may also misorder plans that experts would differentiate.

## Foundational Learning
- **Concept: Dynamic Graph CNNs (DGCNNs) for point clouds.**
  - **Why needed here:** The perceptual module (DGCNN_Seg) uses EdgeConv layers that dynamically reconstruct k-NN graphs in learned feature space (not fixed input space) to capture evolving local geometry, which is critical for distinguishing tooth identities in sparse, undersampled point clouds.
  - **Quick check question:** Why construct the nearest-neighbor graph dynamically in feature space rather than using a fixed spatial graph?  
    **Answer:** To capture semantic relationships that emerge during learning, improving discrimination between similar tooth classes like molars.

- **Concept: Constraint Satisfaction Problems (CSPs) with graded semantics.**
  - **Why needed here:** The biomechanical engine models clinical knowledge as a typed CSP. Understanding partial constraint satisfaction (Eq. 1) is necessary to see how hard/soft violations generate graded alerts rather than binary pass/fail.
  - **Quick check question:** What is the semantic difference between a hard constraint and a soft constraint in this CSP, and how does the satisfaction function `σ` translate a movement value into an alert?  
    **Answer:** Hard constraints map to critical alerts when exceeded by factor `α`; soft constraints yield warning alerts with a continuous `β` representing degree of violation.

- **Concept: Weighted Additive Value Function (WAVF) in MCDA.**
  - **Why needed here:** The treatment score `S` is a WAVF. Understanding its assumptions (mutual preferential independence, additive aggregation) is crucial to assess its validity as a clinical decision support tool and to know when to question its outputs.
  - **Quick check question:** Under what condition would the WAVF score fail to reflect a clinician's true preferences even if all weights and sub-scores are accurate?  
    **Answer:** If criteria are not mutually preferentially independent—e.g., the importance of biomechanical compliance changes depending on staging efficiency—the additive model cannot capture the interaction.

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map:**  
  Landmark-to-Point-Cloud Synthesis → DGCNN_Seg → Semantic Lifting ϕ → CSP Engine → WAVF Scorer → Dashboard

- **Critical path:**  
  Input scan → Landmark annotations (assumed) → Synthetic point cloud → DGCNN_Seg inference → ϕ (lifting) → CSP evaluation (needs movement plan from external planner) → Sub-score computation → WAVF → Dashboard.

- **Design tradeoffs:**
  - Perceptual accuracy vs. generalizability: Training on synthetic ellipsoids yields good TIR on synthetic validation but expected failure on real scans. Tradeoff: easy sparse-supervision vs. reality gap.
  - Constraint expressiveness vs. maintainability: CSP's typed, literature-based constraints are auditable but static. Tradeoff: regulatory-friendly explicitness vs. inability to model patient-specific physiology.
  - WAVF transparency vs. clinical validity: Linear additive model is interpretable and Pareto-consistent but assumes independence. Tradeoff: explainable score vs. potentially simplified preference structure.

- **Failure signatures:**
  - **Segmentation failure:** TIR << 81.4% (e.g., <70%) on real scans; centroid RMSE > δ_µ (2mm). Symptoms: many missing/fused tooth labels in `ϕ` output.
  - **CSP failure:** Zero violations on obviously flawed plans (false negative) OR excessive violations on acceptable plans (false positive). Diagnose by checking tooth type assignment (`τ(ˆl_k)`) and movement decomposition against principal axes.
  - **WAVF instability:** Score `S` changes drastically (±>10pts) under minor weight perturbations (±10%), indicating over-reliance on a single criterion (sensitivity analysis, Table 7).

- **First 3 experiments:**
  1. **Ablation: Batch-adaptive Dice loss.** Train with/without `L_batch_Dice` (Table 5, row "CE + BD (no LS)"). Expect: TIR drop (~7pp) and unstable training loss if removed. Validates mechanism for rare-class gradient flow.
  2. **Ablation: EdgeConv neighborhood size `k`.** Train with `k` in {3,5,10,20} (Table 3). Expect: `k=3` best on TIR; larger `k` degrades due to overfitting on small dataset. Validates dynamic graph design for limited data.
  3. **Runtime & accuracy tradeoff test.** Measure pipeline time on CPU vs. GPU; re-run DGCNN with point subsampling (1,000 vs. 2,000 points). Expect: <4s on CPU (as reported); subsampling should minimally affect TIR if clinically sufficient representation holds. Validates deployment feasibility and perception-reasoning decoupling.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
1. **How does the segmentation module trained on synthetic ellipsoidal point clouds perform on real intraoral scans with complex tooth morphology and interproximal contacts?**  
   - **Basis in paper:** [explicit] "Synthetic training data. The segmentation module is trained exclusively on ellipsoidal point cloud approximations. It has never seen real tooth morphology, occlusal anatomy, or interproximal contacts. Performance on actual intraoral scans is unknown and expected to degrade substantially."  
   - **Why unresolved:** The model’s training distribution differs significantly from clinical data, so its Tooth Identification Rate (TIR) and centroid accuracy on real scans are unquantified and likely unsatisfactory for deployment.  
   - **What evidence would resolve it:** Retraining the DGCNN on a dataset of real intraoral scans (e.g., Teeth3DS) with dense annotations and evaluating TIR, centroid RMSE, and axis errors against the clinical sufficiency thresholds from Definition 1.

2. **Can biomechanical constraint thresholds be personalized for individual patients using learned models instead of fixed population-level values?**  
   - **Basis in paper:** [explicit] "Fixed biomechanical thresholds. The CSP constraint domains Dk,j are fixed at population-level literature values and do not incorporate patient-specific factors (bone density, periodontal status, root morphology). A learned constraint relaxation function that personalizes per-stage limits from patient data would be a significant improvement."  
   - **Why unresolved:** Current one-size-fits-all limits ignore patient variability, potentially leading to over- or under-restrictive alerts that reduce clinical trust.  
   - **What evidence would resolve it:** Developing a predictive model that inputs patient-specific features (e.g., from CBCT, age, periodontal index) to adjust constraint domains and validating its performance by comparing personalized alerts to expert consensus or actual treatment complications.

3. **What are the appropriate weightings for the multi-criteria treatment quality index (WAVF) based on structured clinical expert preferences?**  
   - **Basis in paper:** [explicit] "Heuristic WAVF weights. The weight vector w in Eq. 3 was set based on informal clinical reasoning, not empirical preference elicitation. The WAVF requires calibration against expert judgments via Analytic Hierarchy Process (AHP) or pairwise comparison."  
   - **Why unresolved:** The provisional weights lack empirical grounding, risking misalignment with clinical priorities and undermining the score’s interpretability and acceptance.  
   - **What evidence would resolve it:** Conducting an AHP-based elicitation study with at least 20 orthodontists to derive weights, then correlating the calibrated WAVF scores with expert treatment ratings or long-term outcome measures.

4. **How reliably does the constraint satisfaction engine detect biomechanical violations compared to expert orthodontists on real treatment plans?**  
   - **Basis in paper:** [explicit] In the validation roadmap (Section 6.4, Stage 2): "Compare CSP constraint violations against expert orthodontist assessments on a panel of 50 real ClinCheck plans; measure sensitivity and specificity of critical/warning alerts."  
   - **Why unresolved:** The system’s alerts have not been validated against human experts, so their clinical reliability—especially for soft constraints—remains unknown.  
   - **What evidence would resolve it:** Having a blinded panel of orthodontists independently review a dataset of real ClinCheck plans to label violations, then computing sensitivity, specificity, and Cohen’s kappa for the CSP engine’s outputs.

## Limitations
- Synthetic-to-real domain gap: The perceptual module trains on ellipsoid-synthesized point clouds, which may fail on complex real occlusal surfaces and interproximal contacts.
- CSP threshold validity: Fixed, population-level movement limits may not account for patient-specific factors or coupled tooth interactions, risking false negatives/positives.
- WAVF clinical validity: Uncalibrated weights and linear marginal value functions assume mutual preferential independence, which may not hold in practice; sub-score definitions are heuristic and unreleased.

## Confidence
- Sparse-supervision segmentation: Medium confidence. Well-specified with equations and ablations, but core ellipsoidal prior’s real-world accuracy is unvalidated beyond synthetic benchmark.
- CSP biomechanical engine: Medium confidence. Formal CSP definition and graded semantics are clear, but independence assumption and static thresholds are stated limitations with no patient-specific validation.
- MCDA treatment score: Medium confidence. Transparent WAVF formulation but uncalibrated weights and untested independence assumption limit clinical trust.

## Next Checks
1. **Real-scan generalization test:** Evaluate the trained DGCNN_Seg on a held-out set of *real* intraoral scans (not from 3DTeethLand) to quantify TIR/centroid error and identify failure modes (e.g., wisdom tooth confusion, axis misestimation).
2. **Movement decomposition sanity check:** On a sample of real scans with known tooth axes (from clinician annotations or CBCT), verify that decomposing a planned movement vector using estimated PCA axes aligns with the intended clinical direction; count misalignment-induced CSP false alerts.
3. **WAVF sensitivity & preference audit:** Systematically perturb weights (±20%) and sub-score boundaries on a diverse plan set; correlate score changes with expert rankings to detect violations of mutual preferential independence or over-reliance on single criteria.
