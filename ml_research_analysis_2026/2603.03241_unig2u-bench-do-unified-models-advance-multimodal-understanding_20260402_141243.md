# UniG2U-Bench: Do Unified Models Advance Multimodal Understanding?
## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2603.03241
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.03241
- Reference count: 40
- Primary result: Unified models generally underperform base VLMs, and GtA often degrades performance due to error propagation, though consistent improvements appear in spatial intelligence, visual‑illusion, and multi‑round reasoning tasks.

## Executive Summary
UniG2U-Bench introduces a comprehensive benchmark to evaluate whether unified multimodal models—systems that jointly perform visual generation and understanding—truly advance multimodal understanding. The study assesses 35 models across 7 reasoning regimes and 30 subtasks, comparing unified systems against their base vision‑language models using Direct and Generate‑then‑Answer (GtA) protocols. Key findings show that unified models underperform their base VLMs on pure understanding tasks, even without explicit generation, indicating an "alignment tax" stemming from joint training objectives. GtA inference often reduces accuracy due to error propagation from misaligned intermediate visuals, yet it yields consistent gains in spatial intelligence, visual‑illusion, and multi‑round reasoning tasks where intermediate visual scaffolding aids reasoning. Two new diagnostic metrics, Reasoning‑to‑Visual Alignment (RA) and Answer‑to‑Visual Alignment (AL), quantify intermediate generation quality, revealing that high alignment is necessary but not sufficient for improvement. The results highlight the need for better integration mechanisms to unlock the full potential of unified modeling.

## Method Summary
The evaluation is purely empirical, with no training involved. UniG2U consists of 3,000 curated question‑image pairs that span 7 reasoning regimes (e.g., spatial intelligence, visual illusion, logic) and 30 subtasks. The model zoo includes 11 base VLMs, 21 unified models (end‑to‑end or decoupled), and 3 agentic systems. Each model is tested under two inference protocols: **Direct** (answer directly from the input) and **Generate‑then‑Answer (GtA)** (first generate an intermediate image using a provided prompt, then answer using the original image plus the generated artifact). Performance is measured by task‑specific accuracy; gains relative to the paired base VLM are captured by ΔDirect and ΔGtA. For GtA runs, intermediate generation quality is assessed by RA and AL, scored by GPT‑4o. Correlations across models and tasks are analyzed using Spearman ranks. The lmms‑eval framework orchestrates inference, with strict but equal compute budgets and decoding settings (greedy, fixed max tokens) to ensure fairness.

## Key Results
- Unified models underperform their base VLMs on pure understanding tasks, even under Direct inference, evidencing an intrinsic "alignment tax" from parameter‑level coupling.
- GtA frequently degrades performance relative to Direct, particularly on logic‑intensive tasks, due to error propagation from misaligned intermediate images.
- Gains are observed in spatial intelligence, visual‑illusion, and multi‑round reasoning, where generation‑trained representations act as useful scaffolds.
- RA and AL metrics successfully diagnose intermediate quality; high scores are necessary but not sufficient for GtA improvement, indicating additional factors govern utility.
- Task and model behaviors cluster by reasoning structure and base architecture, revealing inductive biases shaped by pretraining data.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Alignment Tax
Unified models suffer a drop in pure understanding performance because shared parameters between generation and understanding objectives create representational interference. Even under Direct inference, the model's discriminative pathways are subtly degraded by the need to satisfy conflicting objectives. Core assumption: this degradation is an intrinsic cost of cross‑modal alignment, not catastrophic forgetting. Evidence: Section 5.1 shows ΔDirect remains negative even without generation. The effect diminishes in spatial/illusion tasks where generation‑trained features regularize perception.

### Error Propagation in GtA
Explicit intermediate generation forces a visual hypothesis; if the artifact is inaccurate, the answering module consumes erroneous evidence, compounding mistakes. This predominantly harms logic‑heavy tasks that demand precise visual reasoning. Core assumption: GtA benefits only when generated intermediates are faithful. Evidence: GtA underperforms Direct in most categories (Fig. 9) and low RA scores correlate with large performance drops (Sections 5.2, 5.4). Break condition: GtA yields gains in transformation‑heavy regimes (e.g., mazes, sliding puzzles) where iterative visuals serve as a reliable chain‑of‑thought.

### Inductive Biases via Pretraining
G2U effectiveness clusters by task cognitive demands and by model base architecture, suggesting that pretraining data and backbone design shape how generation and understanding interact. Core assumption: Gains are not random but predictable from task structure and model lineage. Evidence: Task‑level correlation (Fig. 10a) shows perception vs. reasoning clusters with opposite polarity; model‑level correlation is stronger within base‑model families. The correlation weakens when comparing different backbones, indicating architecture alone explains less variance.

## Architecture Onboarding
### Component Map
UniG2U integrates (1) a dataset of 3,000 samples across 30 subtasks in 7 regimes; (2) a model zoo with 35 base, unified, and agentic models; (3) two inference protocols (Direct, GtA); (4) evaluation metrics (accuracy, ΔDirect, ΔGtA, RA, AL); (5) correlation analysis tools (Spearman on Δ vectors).

### Critical Path
For each model: (a) run Direct inference on all tasks and record accuracy; (b) if the model supports image generation, run GtA (generate intermediate images, then answer using them) and record accuracy; (c) compute ΔDirect and ΔGtA against the paired base VLM; (d) for GtA runs, sample intermediate images and evaluate RA/AL via GPT‑4o; (e) perform task‑level and model‑level correlation on Δ vectors.

### Design Tradeoffs
- Base‑unified pairing ensures fair comparison but limits analysis to models with clear base VLMs.
- Budget matching prevents compute bias but may under‑utilize stronger models.
- GtA definition (single‑step vs. iterative) balances realism and implementation complexity.
- RA/AL rely on GPT‑4o, introducing judge bias but enabling nuanced quantitative scoring.

### Failure Signatures
- Unified model underperforms base under Direct → alignment tax.
- GtA accuracy << Direct → error propagation from poor intermediates.
- Low RA (<2.0) and AL (<2.5) in a category → generation unreliable for that reasoning regime.
- High Δ variance within same architecture → data‑dependent effects.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
**Open Question 1** – What underlying representational mechanisms cause the "alignment tax" where unified models degrade on pure understanding tasks compared to their base VLMs, even under Direct inference?  
**Open Question 2** – Can architectural or training interventions mitigate the severe error propagation in GtA for logic‑intensive tasks, or is explicit intermediate generation fundamentally mismatched for such domains?  
**Open Question 3** – Why do spatial intelligence and visual illusion tasks uniquely benefit from generation‑coupled models, and what specific inductive biases tied to pretraining data or architecture enable this?  
**Open Question 4** – Are the proposed RA and AL metrics sufficient diagnostics for predicting G2U gains, or do they merely correlate without capturing the necessary conditions for generation to aid understanding?  
**Open Question 5** – To what extent do the observed G2U effects scale with model size, training data diversity, and architectural innovations, and can scaling laws predict when unification becomes beneficial?

## Limitations
- UniG2U dataset not yet publicly released.
- GtA pipeline details (image re‑encoding and multi‑step handling) remain vague.
- RA/AL prompt wording is undisclosed, risking GPT‑4o judge bias.
