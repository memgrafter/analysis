# An Agentic Approach to Generating XAI-Narratives

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2603.20003
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.20003
- Reference count: 40
- Primary result: Iterative multi‑agent feedback with a Faithful Evaluator and directional Critic reduces SHAP narrative unfaithfulness by up to 90% across multiple LLMs and datasets.

## Executive Summary
Technical XAI explanations like SHAP values are often inaccessible to non‑experts. While LLMs can translate these into natural language narratives, such outputs frequently lack faithfulness to the underlying SHAP data. This paper introduces a multi‑agent framework where a Narrator generates and iteratively revises SHAP narratives using feedback from a Faithful Evaluator (extracts structured information), a Faithful Critic (provides directional corrections, either LLM‑based or rule‑based), and an optional Coherence Agent (improves linguistic flow). Five system designs are evaluated across five LLMs and five tabular datasets. Results show that designs including the Evaluator and Critic (Basic, Critic, Critic‑Rule) consistently improve faithfulness metrics (rank, sign, value accuracy). Claude‑4.5‑Sonnet achieved a 90% reduction in unfaithful narratives after three rounds. Ensemble voting over multiple evaluators further boosted performance for four models, but adding the Coherence Agent harmed faithfulness. Effectiveness varies with baseline narrative quality and feature count.

## Method Summary
The method centers on a modular multi‑agent system for producing faithful SHAP narratives. Agents:
- **Narrator (LLM)**: Writes or revises narratives from SHAP tables and feedback.
- **Faithful Evaluator (LLM)**: Extracts feature rank, sign, and value from the narrative to compare with the SHAP table.
- **Faithful Critic (LLM or rule‑based)**: Converts extraction mismatches into specific correction instructions (e.g., “change feature X’s influence to positive”).
- **Coherence Agent (LLM, optional)**: Suggests edits for readability (reorder, rephrase).
The system iterates: baseline narrative → evaluation → critique (if present) → coherence suggestions (if present) → narrator revision. Five designs (Basic, Critic, Critic‑Rule, Coherent, Coherent‑Rule) are tested. Faithfulness is measured by Rank Accuracy, Sign Accuracy, and Value Accuracy. Ensemble (majority vote among five evaluators) is explored to reduce extraction noise. Experiments use five binary‑classification datasets and a diverse set of LLMs.

## Key Results
- Basic, Critic, and Critic‑Rule designs consistently improved faithfulness metrics (rank, sign, value accuracy) over baselines.
- Claude‑4.5‑Sonnet on the Basic Design reduced unfaithful narratives by 90% after three iterations.
- Ensemble majority voting across LLM‑based evaluators further improved results for four models (excluding DeepSeek‑V3.2‑Exp); including the Coherence Agent degraded faithfulness.
- Performance varied with initial baseline quality and the number of features in the SHAP table.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1
- **Claim:** Iterative multi‑agent feedback systematically reduces narrative unfaithfulness to SHAP explanations by enabling corrective revision cycles.
- **Mechanism:** A Narrator agent generates an initial narrative (Round 0) from SHAP data. A Faithful Evaluator agent extracts feature rank/sign/value from the narrative and flags mismatches against the SHAP table. The Narrator revises based on this feedback. In designs with a Faithful Critic, directional corrections (e.g., “change feature X’s influence to positive”) are provided instead of only error flags, potentially guiding more efficient fixes.
- **Core assumption:** LLM‑based agents can consistently and correctly evaluate narrative faithfulness against a structured SHAP table, and the Narrator can successfully act on this feedback.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract] “Basic Design, Critic Design, and Critic‑Rule Design consistently improved faithfulness metrics… Claude‑4.5‑Sonnet on Basic Design reduced unfaithful narratives by 90%.”
  - [section 2.1] Detailed agent roles and prompt structures for extraction and critique.
  - [corpus] Weak direct corpus evidence; neighboring papers (e.g., “How good is my story?”) validate the quantitative extraction metrics but do not test iterative multi‑agent refinement.
- **Break condition:** When the narrative achieves 100% faithfulness per the Evaluator or a maximum iteration round is reached. Failure occurs if extraction mistakes (C2) or confusing instructions (C1) persist.

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim:** A rule‑based Faithful Critic (Critic‑Rule Design) provides comparable or better performance than an LLM‑based Faithful Critic for directing revisions, with lower resource cost.
- **Mechanism:** The Faithful Critic transforms extraction error outputs into specific, actionable instructions (e.g., “move feature Y to rank 2”). The rule‑based variant uses deterministic logic (compare extracted vs. SHAP rank/sign/value) to generate these instructions without an LLM, avoiding potential LLM hallucination or verbosity in feedback.
- **Core assumption:** Deterministic transformation of error types into revision commands is sufficient for the Narrator to correct issues, and LLM‑based summarization of feedback does not add essential corrective power.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract] “Critic‑Rule Design effective… comparable performance to Critic Design.”
  - [section 2.2] “Critic‑Rule Design… can compare with each other… evaluate whether incorporating an LLM into the Faithful Critic is necessary.”
  - [section 3.2] Tables show Critic and Critic‑Rule often have similar final accuracies; Critic‑Rule sometimes outperforms (e.g., Mistral Medium 3.1).
  - [corpus] Limited; “Enhancing XAI Narratives…” uses multi‑narrative refinement but does not compare rule‑based vs. LLM critics.
- **Break condition:** Fails when the rule‑based feedback is insufficiently nuanced for complex linguistic errors (e.g., C1.2 cases where narrative order is correct but descriptive terms mislead) or when the Narrator requires more contextual explanation than a rule provides.

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim:** Including a coherence‑focused agent (Coherence Agent) in the iteration loop degrades faithfulness because its suggestions prioritize linguistic flow over alignment with SHAP data.
- **Mechanism:** The Coherence Agent analyzes narrative coherence (smooth transitions, logical flow) and suggests edits (reorder, change wording). These edits can inadvertently alter feature ranking, influence direction, or values to improve readability, creating new faithfulness violations (C4).
- **Core assumption:** The optimization objectives of coherence and faithfulness are partially conflicting in this setup; improving one can harm the other without explicit coordination.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract] “Coherence Agent inclusion harmed faithfulness.”
  - [section 3.1.2] “C4: Misleading Coherence Instruction… Coherence Agent gives feedback… causes the Narrator to deviate from the SHAP information.”
  - [section 3.1] Coherent and Coherent‑Rule designs show decreased rank accuracy vs. non‑coherent designs.
  - [corpus] Neighbors focus on either faithfulness or coherence separately but do not study their interaction in iterative generation.
- **Break condition:** Fails when the Coherence Agent’s suggestions directly conflict with SHAP facts (e.g., reordering features contradicts SHAP ranks, changing “positive” to “negative” influence).

## Foundational Learning
- **Concept: SHAP (SHapley Additive exPlanations)**
  - Why needed here: The entire framework’s goal is to generate narratives faithful to SHAP outputs. Understanding SHAP’s feature attribution (value, sign, rank) is prerequisite to defining and measuring faithfulness.
  - Quick check question: Given a SHAP table, what does a positive SHAP value indicate about a feature’s effect on the prediction?
- **Concept: LLM‑as‑a‑Judge / Automated Evaluation**
  - Why needed here: The iterative loop requires automated, quantitative feedback. The Faithful Evaluator acts as an “LLM‑as‑a‑judge” extracting structured data from free‑text narratives for comparison to SHAP.
  - Quick check question: Why is using an LLM to extract (rank, sign, value) from a narrative a form of LLM‑as‑a‑judge evaluation?
- **Concept: Multi‑Agent Systems & Role Specialization**
  - Why needed here: The architecture separates concerns: generation (Narrator), quantitative assessment (Faithful Evaluator), directional guidance (Faithful Critic), qualitative assessment (Coherence Agent). Understanding this modular design is key to onboarding.
  - Quick check question: What specific feedback does the Faithful Critic provide that the Faithful Evaluator does not?
- **Concept: Faithfulness Metrics (Rank/Sign/Value Accuracy)**
  - Why needed here: These are the primary optimization targets. They define what “faithful” means operationally: accurate extraction and matching of feature rank, influence direction, and exact value.
  - Quick check question: If a narrative mentions a feature at rank 2 but its SHAP rank is 1, which accuracy metric(s) are affected?

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component map:**
Narrator (LLM): Input: Base prompt (SHAP table + guidelines) + previous narrative + feedback strings. Output: Revised narrative. Constant across all designs.
Faithful Evaluator (LLM): Input: Narrative + dataset/feature descriptions. Output: Extraction dictionary {feature: {rank, sign, value, assumption}}. Fixed component.
Faithful Critic (LLM or Rule): Input: Extraction dict + SHAP table. Output: Textual feedback with directional fixes (“change feature X’s value to Y”, “move feature Z to rank 1”).
Coherence Agent (LLM): Input: Narrative. Output: Coherence revision commands (Change/Insert/Delete/Reorder) or “no coherence issue”.
Fallback/Rule‑based variant: Faithful Critic (Rule) replaces LLM with deterministic logic.

**Critical path:**
1. Generate baseline narrative (Round 0) from SHAP via Narrator (or load pre‑generated).
2. For iteration round n (starting at 0 for baseline evaluation):
   a. Feed current narrative to Faithful Evaluator → get extraction dict → compare to SHAP → generate error list.
   b. Feed error list (and SHAP) to Faithful Critic → generate directional feedback.
   c. Feed current narrative to Coherence Agent (if present) → generate coherence commands.
   d. Feed base prompt + current narrative + faithfulness feedback + coherence feedback to Narrator → generate narrative for round n+1.
3. Stop when max rounds reached OR (if no Coherence Agent) narrative is 100% faithful.

**Design tradeoffs:**
- Faithfulness vs. Coherence: Including Coherence Agent improves linguistic quality but statistically harms faithfulness (C4).
- LLM vs. Rule for Critic: Rule‑based is cheaper, deterministic, and often equally effective; LLM‑based may handle edge cases but adds cost/stochasticity.
- Single LLM vs. Ensemble: Ensemble (majority vote among 5 Faithful Evaluators) reduces extraction mistakes (C2) but can hurt strong LLMs (e.g., DeepSeek‑V3.2‑Exp) by including weak voters.
- Prompt Complexity: Narrator prompt includes base task + feedback; overly verbose/conflicting feedback (C1.2) can paralyze revision.

**Failure signatures:**
- Persistent extraction mistakes (C2): Evaluator mis‑parses narrative due to ambiguous descriptive terms (“slightly”, “most critically”).
- Confusing instructions (C1): Evaluator reports error without correction direction; Critic gives feedback that contradicts current state (C1.2).
- Counterintuitive SHAP (C3): Narrator ignores SHAP sign/value that contradicts common sense (e.g., “no family support” positively impacts passing).
- Coherence conflict (C4): Coherence Agent suggests reordering features or flipping influence to improve flow.
- Stochastic generation (C5): Narrator makes unrecoverable errors despite correct feedback, especially in early rounds.

**First 3 experiments:**
1. **Baseline Generation:** Use DeepSeek‑V3‑0324 (or chosen LLM) with base prompt to generate 100 narratives (4‑feature) on 5 datasets. Evaluate baseline faithfulness (Rank/Sign/Value Accuracy) using a separate Faithful Evaluator run. *Purpose: Establish consistent starting point; identify dataset/LM‑specific baseline issues.*
2. **Basic Design Iteration:** Configure system as Narrator + Faithful Evaluator only. Run 3 iterations. Track accuracy progression and categorize remaining unfaithful narratives (C1‑C5). *Purpose: Validate that iterative feedback alone helps; isolate effect of directional guidance.*
3. **Coherence Inclusion Test:** Run Coherent Design (Narrator + Faithful Evaluator + Coherence Agent) for 3 rounds. Compare final faithfulness to Critic‑Rule Design. Qualitatively sample Coherence Agent feedback. *Purpose: Confirm trade‑off; identify specific coherence suggestions that cause C4 errors.*

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
### Open Question 1
- **Question:** How can extraction errors in the faithfulness evaluation step be effectively mitigated?
- **Basis in paper:** [explicit] The paper identifies extraction mistakes as the main recurring issue across all experiments and states that the proposed ensemble method does not fully resolve them. The authors suggest future work should filter unreliable voters or use multiple runs of a strong LLM (e.g., DeepSeek‑V3.2‑Exp).
- **Why unresolved:** Extraction errors lead to incorrect feedback, causing unnecessary or wrong revisions and distorting reported faithfulness metrics.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** A robust extraction module that consistently achieves near‑zero error rates across diverse narratives and LLMs, demonstrably improving final round‑2 faithfulness in multi‑agent systems.

### Open Question 2
- **Question:** What quantitative coherence metric can be integrated into the iterative refinement loop, and how does it affect narrative quality?
- **Basis in paper:** [explicit] The authors note the absence of an effective quantitative coherence metric and propose using LLM‑as‑a‑judge to filter coherence problems. Current evaluation is purely qualitative and time‑consuming.
- **Why unresolved:** Without a quantitative measure, the system cannot automatically optimize coherence, and the Coherence Agent’s suggestions often degrade faithfulness.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** An automated coherence scoring function that correlates with human judgments and, when integrated, yields higher coherence scores without sacrificing faithfulness.

### Open Question 3
- **Question:** How can the trade‑off between coherence improvements and faithfulness preservation be reconciled in multi‑agent XAI narrative generation?
- **Basis in paper:** [explicit] The paper reports that including the Coherence Agent generally improves linguistic quality but harms faithfulness, and exploring the interplay between coherence‑related and faithfulness agents is identified as a key future direction.
- **Why unresolved:** System designers must choose between readability and factual correctness; a mechanism to balance both is lacking.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** A revised agent design or coordination protocol (e.g., joint optimization, hierarchical control) that allows coherence‑enhancing changes only when they preserve faithfulness, validated by simultaneous improvements in both metrics.

### Open Question 4
- **Question:** How can user‑centered dimensions such as comprehensibility and controllability be incorporated to enable personalized XAI narratives?
- **Basis in paper:** [explicit] The outlook states that comprehensibility and controllability are important and suggests future work could include interactive modules for user‑personalized narratives based on preferences and cognitive level.
- **Why unresolved:** The current system operates automatically without user input; tailoring explanations to individual needs remains unaddressed.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** A user study demonstrating that interactive personalization features (e.g., adjustable detail, feature focus) increase perceived understandability and satisfaction without reducing factual accuracy.

### Open Question 5
- **Question:** To what extent does the initial quality of baseline narratives influence final faithfulness outcomes, and what baseline generation strategies are optimal?
- **Basis in paper:** [inferred] The paper shows performance varies with baseline quality; DeepSeek‑V3.2‑Exp improves under no‑baseline while other models decline. Experiments with best/worst baselines indicate significant differences, but the underlying mechanism is not fully explained.
- **Why unresolved:** Understanding this relationship could determine whether investing in a high‑quality initial narrative is critical or if agents can robustly improve from any starting point.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Systematic experiments correlating baseline faithfulness levels (across multiple LLMs and prompts) with maximum achievable faithfulness after refinement, identifying baseline generation techniques that maximize improvement potential.

## Limitations
- Uncertainties in exact LLM versions/APIs, SHAP hyperparameters, full prompts, and baseline selection criteria may affect reproducibility.
- Evaluation relies on LLM‑based extraction (Faithful Evaluator), which can introduce extraction errors that distort faithfulness metrics.
- The study is limited to five binary classification datasets and a fixed feature set size, limiting generalizability to other domains or high‑dimensional data.

## Confidence
- Iterative multi‑agent feedback reduces unfaithfulness: Medium
- Rule‑based Critic comparable to LLM Critic: Medium
- Coherence Agent harms faithfulness: High
