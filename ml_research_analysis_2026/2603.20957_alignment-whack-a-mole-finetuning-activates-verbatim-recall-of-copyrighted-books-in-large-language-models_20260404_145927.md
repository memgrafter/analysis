# Alignment Whack-a-Mole : Finetuning Activates Verbatim Recall of Copyrighted Books in Large Language Models

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID**: 2603.20957  
- **Source URL**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.20957  
- **Reference count**: 40  
- **Primary result**: Finetuning frontier LLMs on a plot-summary expansion task bypasses alignment safeguards, causing verbatim reproduction of substantial portions of copyrighted books from pretraining corpora.

## Executive Summary
This study reveals that current alignment safeguards in large language models (LLMs) are critically vulnerable to finetuning. When models are finetuned on a realistic, commercially relevant task—expanding plot summaries into full text—they reactivate latent verbatim memorization from pretraining, reproducing extended passages of copyrighted books despite previous alignment intended to prevent such infringement. The effect is systemic: it occurs across multiple frontier models (GPT-4o, Gemini-2.5-Pro, DeepSeek-V3.1), triggers extraction from dozens of authors after training on just one, and persists when finetuning data overlaps with pretraining corpora (public-domain works) but vanishes with synthetic data lacking overlap. This demonstrates that model weights store approximate copies of copyrighted works and that finetuning can surgically weaken alignment to access them, posing significant legal and ethical risks for deployed LLMs.

## Method Summary
The authors finetuned LLMs on pairs consisting of a plot summary (generated by GPT-4o) and the corresponding verbatim excerpt (300–500 words) from copyrighted books. The task required the model to "write an *n*-word excerpt emulating [author]'s style" given a summary. For evaluation, they held out entire books and, for each paragraph, sampled 100 generations from the finetuned model using only the summary as input. They then measured verbatim extraction using **Book Memorization Coverage at k** (bmc@*k*), which computes the fraction of book words covered by contiguous exact matches of at least *k* words, after trimming any overlap with the instruction prompt. They analyzed longest extracted spans, cross-paragraph and cross-author retrieval, and correlated memorization patterns across models. The study used 81 books from 47 authors, finetuned three frontier models, and compared results against aligned baselines and control conditions (public-domain vs. synthetic finetuning data).

## Key Results
- Finetuning activates extensive verbatim extraction: coverage (bmc@5) reaches 85–90% for some books, with single spans exceeding 460 words.
- The vulnerability is cross-author: finetuning on any single author’s corpus (e.g., Haruki Murakami) triggers extraction from over 30 other authors’ works.
- Extraction correlates strongly across models (r ≥ 0.90) and depends on pretraining data overlap: public-domain finetuning data yields high extraction, while synthetic data yields near-zero.
- Alignment safeguards in un-finetuned models block extraction, but finetuning on a semantic task systematically bypasses them.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
Finetuning on plot-to-text expansion reactivates latent memorization through three interconnected mechanisms:

1. **Semantic task lowers activation threshold**: The finetuning objective teaches the model to map semantic descriptors (plot summaries) back to exact training text. This retraverses and strengthens weight-space pathways formed during pretraining, where specific books were memorized. The model learns to associate semantic keys (plot points, style) with stored verbatim sequences, allowing retrieval without a verbatim prefix. Evidence: extraction occurs with public-domain finetuning data (high pretraining overlap) but not with synthetic data (no overlap), confirming reliance on pretraining memorization.

2. **Associative retrieval across content**: Memorized fragments are organized in a semantically linked latent space, not isolated by book/author boundaries. Finetuning on a narrow author’s style lowers the barrier to retrieve any text in a semantically similar region, even from distant paragraphs or other authors. Evidence: 14–40% of extracted spans come from non-prompted paragraphs; triggered paragraphs rank at the 74.6th percentile in semantic similarity to the prompt, versus random 49.5th.

3. **Systemic shared vulnerability**: Frontier models are trained on overlapping large-scale web crawls and curated book datasets (e.g., Books3, LibGen). The same copyrighted books are embedded in multiple models’ weights in similar representational forms, so identical finetuning similarly reactivates the same content. Evidence: per-book extraction scores correlate at r ≥ 0.90 across GPT-4o, Gemini-2.5-Pro, and DeepSeek-V3.1.

## Foundational Learning
- **Extractable Memorization in LLMs**: Understanding whether exact reproductions reflect true memorization (compressed storage) vs. statistical reconstruction is crucial for interpreting extraction results. *Quick check*: If a model generates a 50-word exact match from its training data, does that prove memorization, or could it be a plausible reconstruction?
- **Finetuning as Model Surgery**: Finetuning is not merely additive; it can reweight existing circuits, potentially weakening safeguards and reactivating old memories. *Quick check*: Why might finetuning on a style-based task, unrelated to safety, degrade general alignment?
- **Semantic Similarity Metrics for Retrieval**: Cross-paragraph effects rely on measuring semantic proximity (e.g., cosine similarity) between prompts and triggered content. *Quick check*: How would you design a control to confirm cross-paragraph recall is truly semantic rather than due to paragraph proximity in the book?

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component map**: Book -> Excerpts (300–500 words) -> Plot Summaries (GPT-4o) -> Finetuning Pairs (summary -> excerpt) -> Finetuned Model (API LoRA/full) -> Test Summaries -> 100 Generations per paragraph -> Comparison against Full Book -> bmc@k, longest spans, cross-paragraph analysis.

**Critical path**:  
1. Acquire and segment copyrighted books into excerpts.  
2. Generate plot summaries for all excerpts.  
3. Finetune target model on summary-excerpt pairs.  
4. For each test book paragraph, sample 100 completions at temperature=1.0 using the summary as prompt.  
5. Detect contiguous verbatim matches ≥k words against the full book, trimming instruction overlap.  
6. Aggregate coverage and analyze triggers.

**Design tradeoffs**:  
- *Cost vs. coverage*: 100 samples per paragraph is expensive but needed to estimate output distribution; fewer samples risk missing rare extractions.  
- *k-threshold selection*: k=5 balances false positives (chance matches) and false negatives (missing fragmented recall); higher *k* focuses on legally significant spans.  
- *Instruction trimming*: Removing *m*-gram overlaps (*m*≥5) prevents counting prompt echo but may discard legitimate recall if summaries contain unique phrasing.

**Failure signatures**:  
- No extraction (bmc@5 near baseline) could indicate task not learned, alignment still strong, or no pretraining overlap.  
- Inconsistent, very short spans (<50 words) suggest weak memorization or task misunderstanding.  
- High baseline in aligned model implies summaries are too close to original text.  
- Cross-model divergence (r < 0.5) may indicate different pretraining data or finetuning instability.

**First 3 experiments**:  
1. **Baseline sanity check**: Run aligned model on test books with plot-summary prompts; expect bmc@5 < 10% and longest spans < 20 words.  
2. **Within-author validation**: Finetune on one author’s corpus (e.g., Ishiguro), test on their other book; expect >5× increase in bmc@5 and spans >100 words.  
3. **Cross-author test**: Finetune on Murakami, test on a dissimilar author (e.g., Atwood); expect substantial extraction (bmc@5 > 30%), validating semantic associative mechanism.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- What specific aspects of the plot-summary expansion task (instruction phrasing, length, style cues) are necessary and sufficient to trigger latent memorization?
- What internal model mechanisms (e.g., attention patterns, layer activations) enable semantically driven cross-paragraph and cross-author retrieval after finetuning?
- What quantitative properties of pretraining data overlap (exact duplication, paraphrase frequency) are required for finetuning to induce extraction?
- Why do independently trained models from different providers exhibit such high correlation (r≥0.90) in memorization patterns?
- Can alignment techniques be modified to prevent finetuning-induced extraction while preserving downstream task performance?

## Limitations
- Closed-source finetuning details (epochs, loss) for GPT-4o and Gemini-2.5-Pro are undisclosed.
- Full text of the 81 test books is not publicly available, hindering independent replication.
- Plot summaries are GPT-4o-generated and may contain residual phrasing from the books, potentially inflating extraction rates.
- Overlap of extracted spans with web-crawled corpora is not exhaustively quantified.

## Confidence
- Core phenomenon (finetuning triggers verbatim recall): **High**  
- Semantic associative retrieval (cross-author/paragraph effects): **Medium**  
- Systemic vulnerability (high cross-model correlation): **High**

## Next Checks
1. **Synthetic finetuning control**: Train on plot-summary pairs from entirely new, non-overlapping synthetic stories; expect near-zero extraction if memorization reactivation requires real pretraining overlap.
2. **Activation pathway probing**: With a white-box model, compare internal representations during finetuning and inference to see if recall of specific books uses the same circuits activated during original pretraining.
3. **Disjoint pretraining experiment**: Train two models on corpora with no book overlap (e.g., one on only public-domain texts, another only on non-book web data). After identical finetuning, test extraction on the same copyrighted books; low inter-model correlation would confirm dependence on shared pretraining content.
