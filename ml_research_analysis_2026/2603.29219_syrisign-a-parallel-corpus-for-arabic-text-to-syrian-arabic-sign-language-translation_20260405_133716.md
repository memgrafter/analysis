# SyriSign: A Parallel Corpus for Arabic Text to Syrian Arabic Sign Language Translation
## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2603.29219
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.29219
- Reference count: 0
- Primary result: A new SyArSL dataset (1,500 videos, 150 signs) enables evaluation of text-to-sign models, revealing retrieval-based SignCLIP outperforms generative T2M-GPT in low-resource settings (R@5: 0.5526 vs. perplexity 186.69).

## Executive Summary
The paper introduces SyriSign, the first publicly available parallel corpus for Syrian Arabic Sign Language (SyArSL), addressing a critical data gap for the Deaf and Hard-of-Hearing community in Syria. The dataset comprises 1,500 video samples covering 150 lexical signs, recorded from six native signers in a Syrian news domain. The authors evaluate three state-of-the-art architectures—MotionCLIP, T2M-GPT, and SignCLIP—on this low-resource benchmark. Results show that retrieval-based SignCLIP achieves the most stable performance (Recall@5 = 0.5526), while generative models like T2M-GPT are severely limited by the small data scale (perplexity 186.69, reconstruction loss 0.1205). The study underscores that dataset size is a fundamental constraint for generative text-to-motion systems in low-resource sign language production, whereas retrieval approaches demonstrate greater robustness. Limitations include omission of full facial landmarks and the use of proxy evaluation metrics without human validation.

## Method Summary
The authors collected a parallel corpus of Arabic text and corresponding SyArSL video recordings. Videos were preprocessed using MediaPipe Holistic to extract 3D skeletal landmarks (pose, face, hands), which were then filtered to a compact representation of 193 points (pose: 23, face: 128, hands: 42). The data was split 80/10/10 into training, validation, and test sets. Three model families were adapted for text-to-sign translation:

- **SignCLIP**: A retrieval-based model that uses contrastive learning (InfoNCE loss) to align text embeddings (via BERT, with Arabic-to-English translation and special `<eng><jos>` prompts) with pose embeddings. At inference, it retrieves the nearest neighbor from a gallery of training poses.
- **T2M-GPT**: A generative pipeline consisting of a VQ-VAE (with EMA and codebook reset) that discretizes continuous motion into tokens, followed by a GPT conditioned on AraT5 text embeddings to generate token sequences, which are decoded back to motion. Training used a two-stage approach and windowed sequences (84/168 frames). Facial features were excluded for stability.
- **MotionCLIP**: Adapts the MotionCLIP framework to align a motion encoder’s latent space with CLIP’s pre-trained text and image embeddings via a composite loss (reconstruction, velocity smoothness, latent regularization). It can both retrieve and generate motions.

All models were trained on the extracted skeletal data. Evaluation employed task-specific metrics: SignCLIP used retrieval recall (R@5), T2M-GPT used reconstruction loss and perplexity, and MotionCLIP used encoder/decoder losses. The primary comparison centered on retrieval versus generative performance under low-data conditions.

## Key Results
- SignCLIP (retrieval) achieved a stable Recall@5 of 0.5526, outperforming generative baselines.
- T2M-GPT (generative) exhibited high perplexity (186.69) and reconstruction loss (0.1205), indicating severe difficulty in modeling the motion distribution due to limited data.
- MotionCLIP showed lower reconstruction losses but generation remained unstable; it performed better in retrieval mode than in full synthesis.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1: Retrieval-based models demonstrate greater robustness than generative models in low-resource sign language translation settings
- **Claim:** SignCLIP, a retrieval-based system, achieves more stable performance than generative models like T2M-GPT when training data is scarce.
- **Mechanism:** SignCLIP employs contrastive learning to embed text and pose into a shared latent space. During inference it retrieves the closest matching sign pose from a fixed gallery of training examples rather than synthesizing new motion. This approach avoids the need to learn a diverse generative distribution and is therefore less dependent on large amounts of data.
- **Core assumption:** For any given Arabic text query, the corresponding SyArSL pose exists (or a sufficiently similar one) within the training gallery, and the learned embedding space correctly maps text to its pose neighbor.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract] "...retrieval-based SignCLIP achieved the most stable performance (R@5: 0.5526), while generative models like T2M-GPT... were severely constrained by the limited data scale..."
  - [section V] "SignCLIP produced decent results for retrieval-based approach due to its reliance on similarity matching than full motion synthesis."
  - [corpus] Weak evidence. Neighbor papers (e.g., YouTube-SL-25) focus on large-scale dataset creation for other sign languages, not on the retrieval vs. generative trade-off in low-resource scenarios.
- **Break condition:** Performance degrades if the training gallery lacks lexical coverage or signer variability, causing the correct sign's embedding to be absent or poorly clustered.

### Mechanism 2: Token-based generative motion models are critically limited by dataset scale in low-resource contexts
- **Claim:** T2M-GPT's performance is severely constrained by the limited size of SyriSign, as evidenced by high perplexity and reconstruction loss.
- **Mechanism:** T2M-GPT first uses a VQ-VAE to discretize continuous motion into a finite codebook of "motion tokens". A GPT then predicts token sequences conditioned on text. With only 1,500 training samples, the model cannot learn a diverse or expressive codebook, nor can it acquire a broad distribution of valid text-to-token mappings. This leads to high perplexity and poor generalization.
- **Core assumption:** A sufficiently large and varied motion dataset is required for the VQ-VAE to learn a comprehensive codebook and for the GPT to learn coherent text-conditioned token sequences across the semantic space.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract] "...generative models like T2M-GPT, despite showing potential, were severely constrained by the limited data scale (perplexity: 186.69, reconstruction loss: 0.1205)."
  - [section V] "T2M-GPT struggled due to the limited dataset size since Text-to-motion generation is highly data-dependent... their performance... are highly dependent on dataset scale..."
  - [section IV] High perplexity (186.69) and specific experiment details (168-frame window, no face features) indicate failure to model motion distribution effectively.
- **Break condition:** Model collapse occurs; the VQ-VAE produces a degenerate codebook or the GPT generates repetitive, incoherent token sequences when dataset size/diversity falls below a threshold.

### Mechanism 3: MotionCLIP attempts to transfer large-scale image-text semantics to the motion domain via latent alignment
- **Mechanism:** MotionCLIP aligns the latent space of a motion encoder with CLIP’s pre-trained text and image embeddings using a multi-component loss (reconstruction, velocity smoothness, latent regularization). The hope is that CLIP’s rich semantics can be injected into motion generation without massive paired text-motion data.
- **Core assumption:** The semantic structure of human motion is compatible with the static image-text space learned by CLIP, so that optimizing for alignment yields useful motion priors.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [section IV] MotionCLIP shows lower validation losses (Encoder: 1.0783, Decoder: 0.5285) than T2M-GPT's reconstruction loss, suggesting the alignment task is learnable.
  - [section V] "MotionCLIP performed better in retrieval mode because of embedding alignment while generation was unstable."
  - [section III.A.3.b] The modified loss focuses on reconstruction and smoothness, implicitly acknowledging direct CLIP alignment may be unstable for SyArSL without heavy tuning.
- **Break condition:** The modality gap between static images and dynamic skeletal motion is too large to bridge with limited data, causing the model to prioritize reconstruction over semantic alignment or to generate semantically incoherent motions.

## Foundational Learning
- **MediaPipe Holistic Landmark Extraction**  
  *Why needed:* Converts raw RGB videos into a standardized, compact skeletal representation (pose, face, hand keypoints) that serves as the universal input format for all evaluated architectures.  
  *Quick check:* What are the trade-offs of reducing MediaPipe's 543 total landmarks to 193 (e.g., removing lower body) for a sign language dataset, and how might this impact linguistic fidelity?
- **Contrastive Learning (InfoNCE Loss)**  
  *Why needed:* Forms the core training objective for SignCLIP, forcing a shared embedding space where correct text-pose pairs have higher similarity than incorrect ones, enabling effective retrieval.  
  *Quick check:* How does batch composition (number of positive vs. negative pairs) in contrastive learning affect the difficulty of alignment for a small dataset like SyriSign?
- **Vector Quantized Variational Autoencoder (VQ-VAE)**  
  *Why needed:* T2M-GPT relies on a VQ-VAE to compress continuous motion sequences into discrete tokens, which are then modeled by a GPT. This discretization is essential for applying language models to motion.  
  *Quick check:* What are the symptoms of "codebook collapse" in a VQ-VAE trained on a small dataset, and how do the EMA and reset mechanisms mitigate it?

## Architecture Onboarding
Component map:

Arabic text -> Text Encoder (AraT5 for T2M-GPT; BERT for SignCLIP; CLIP text encoder for MotionCLIP) -> Pose Encoder (MediaPipe Holistic -> 193 landmarks) -> Three divergent paths:
1. SignCLIP: Pose encoder -> projection -> contrastive loss against text embedding -> retrieval from indexed training gallery -> output (retrieved sign video).
2. T2M-GPT: Pose encoder -> VQ-VAE encoder -> discrete token sequence -> GPT (text-conditioned) -> VQ-VAE decoder -> continuous motion output.
3. MotionCLIP: Pose encoder -> Motion encoder -> latent vector -> alignment loss vs. CLIP text/image embeddings -> Motion decoder -> continuous motion output.

Critical path: The MediaPipe landmark extraction and filtering pipeline is the most critical uniform component; all model performance depends on its accuracy and consistency.

Design tradeoffs:
- Retrieval vs. Generative: Retrieval (SignCLIP) guarantees linguistically valid outputs from the training set but cannot create new signs; generative models can synthesize novel signs but require far more data and risk invalid motion.
- Data fidelity vs. dimensionality: Excluding facial landmarks (as done for T2M-GPT) reduces dimensionality and may stabilize training on small data, but sacrifices essential non-manual linguistic features (e.g., mouthing, eyebrow movement).
- Text representation: Using raw Arabic (AraT5) preserves dialectal nuance, while translating to English (for CLIP compatibility) leverages powerful pre-trained semantics but may lose some specificity.

Failure signatures:
- T2M-GPT: Very high perplexity (>150), low reconstruction loss accompanied by visually repetitive or incoherent motions; token sequence may freeze or become garbled.
- MotionCLIP: Low reconstruction/validation loss but generated motions are temporally jittery or semantically unrelated to the input text; retrieval mode may still work.
- SignCLIP: Low Recall@5 (<0.3) indicates the embedding space fails to align text and pose semantics; likely due to insufficient signer diversity or noisy pairings.
- Universal: All models degrade if MediaPipe extraction fails on certain frames (e.g., hand occlusion), leading to missing or erroneous landmarks.

First 3 experiments:
1. Baseline Data Pipeline Validation: Run MediaPipe Holistic on a subset of SyriSign videos, manually inspect 50 outputs for landmark correctness, quantify frame-drop rate.
2. SignCLIP Retrieval Sanity Check: Fine-tune SignCLIP for 1 epoch on the training split, evaluate R@1 and R@5 on the test set (with held-out signers); expect R@5 > 0.4 if alignment works.
3. T2M-GPT Codebook Health Check: Train only the VQ-VAE on all training motions; compute codebook usage. Usage <70% indicates collapse; >95% indicates healthy diversity. Then train the GPT head and monitor cross-entropy loss—a sudden drop to near-zero suggests overfitting.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
### Open Question 1
- **Question:** To what extent does expanding the SyriSign dataset size (both in number of signers and lexical items) improve the performance stability and generalization of generative models like T2M-GPT, and at what scale does the performance gap with retrieval-based models like SignCLIP begin to close?
- **Basis in paper:** [explicit] "...generative approaches show strong potential for sign representation, the limited dataset size constrains generalization performance." and "generative models remain a promising direction for future large-scale SASL production along with moving on from word-based to sentence-based datasets while increasing its scale will be more efficient."
- **Why unresolved:** The paper establishes that dataset size is a critical constraint but does not empirically test scaling. The exact relationship between data volume and convergence/generalization for different architectures in this low-resource sign language context remains unknown.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** A systematic study training the same models (T2M-GPT, MotionCLIP) on progressively larger subsets of an expanded SyriSign dataset (or a new, larger dataset), measuring performance metrics (reconstruction loss, R-precision) against dataset scale to identify breakpoints where generative models become viable.

### Open Question 2
- **Question:** How does the inclusion of full, non-manual linguistic features (specifically detailed facial expressions and mouthings) quantitatively impact the quality and linguistic accuracy of generated SyArSL compared to models that only use body and hand pose landmarks?
- **Basis in paper:** [explicit] "this simplification removes important non-manual linguistic features, which are essential in sign language. Future work will include full facial features to improve linguistic completeness." and "the evaluation is limited to proxy metrics... As this study does not include standardized production metrics or human evaluation..."
- **Why unresolved:** The authors acknowledge the omission but did not implement or evaluate it. The quantitative contribution of these features to metrics like motion realism or semantic correctness is not measured, and no baseline exists for SyArSL.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** An ablation study comparing the full MediaPipe Holistic pipeline (543 landmarks) against their reduced set (193 landmarks) for the same generative and retrieval models, evaluated with both automated metrics (e.g., on a downstream sign language recognition task using the generated motion) and a human evaluation protocol assessing linguistic fidelity and naturalness.

### Open Question 3
- **Question:** Can the current word-level SyriSign dataset and associated models be effectively extended or adapted for continuous, sentence-level SyArSL production, or does this require fundamentally different architectural designs and richer grammatical annotation?
- **Basis in paper:** [explicit] "the models are not expected to generalize to a sentence level SLP" and "Future work will focus on scaling the dataset to include more signers and shifting to a sentence-level recording..." and "moving on from word-based to sentence-based datasets."
- **Why unresolved:** The dataset and evaluation are explicitly word-level. The challenges of modeling sign language grammar (e.g., spatial referencing, classifier constructions, non-manual markers at the sentence level) are not addressed. It is unclear if architectures successful for isolated words can scale linearly or require new paradigms.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Collection of a sentence-level SyArSL corpus with gloss and grammatical annotations. Development and benchmarking of models that incorporate sequential modeling (e.g., with explicit sentence-level loss functions or discourse-aware architectures) on this new data, with evaluation metrics that assess fluency and grammatical correctness beyond single-sign accuracy.

### Open Question 4
- **Question:** For low-resource sign languages like SyArSL, is the use of a proxy ISO language code (<jos> for Jordanian SL) and an intermediate English translation layer in a contrastive model like SignCLIP a robust methodological assumption, or does it introduce a semantic bottleneck that harms performance compared to a model trained directly on Arabic-to-SyArSL pairs?
- **Basis in paper:** [inferred] The pipeline uses an "Arabic to English translation layer using OPUS MT" and then constructs a prompt with `<eng>` and `<jos>` codes. This assumes (1) English is an adequate semantic pivot and (2) <jos> is a sufficient linguistic proxy for <syr> (SyArSL). This is a necessary compromise due to lack of direct parallel data but its impact is not analyzed.
- **Why unresolved:** The paper does not compare this indirect approach (Ar->En + <jos>) against a hypothetical direct model or a model using a more appropriate proxy (e.g., another Levantine SL code). The contribution of the translation step and the choice of proxy language to the final R@5 score (0.5526) is unknown.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** An ablation experiment comparing the proposed pipeline against variations: (a) direct Ar->SyArSL training without translation if minimal direct pairs exist, (b) using different sign language proxy codes (e.g., <asp> for Palestinian SL), and (c) removing the text translation layer while using Arabic-only text encoders. Performance differences would isolate the cost of the proxy assumption.

### Open Question 5
- **Question:** Do the current proxy evaluation metrics (reconstruction loss, perplexity, R@k) reliably correlate with human judgments of sign language production quality and intelligibility for SyArSL, or are new standardized metrics needed for low-resource settings?
- **Basis in paper:** [explicit] "the evaluation is limited to proxy metrics due to the nature of sign language ambiguity. As this study does not include standardized production metrics or human evaluation, comparisons between models should be interpreted with caution."
- **Why unresolved:** The authors explicitly state the limitation of not having human evaluation or standardized SL production metrics. For a communication tool, the ultimate measure is human comprehension and acceptance, which reconstruction loss or retrieval accuracy may not capture, especially for a low-resource, culturally specific sign language.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Conducting a user study with native SyArSL signers. Generated samples from different models (and ground truth) would be presented for comprehension tests, naturalness ratings, and preference comparisons. These scores would then be correlated with the existing automated metrics to determine which (if any) are valid proxies for human judgment in this context.

## Limitations
- Small dataset (1,500 videos, 150 signs) and omission of facial landmarks constrain both generative model performance and linguistic fidelity.
- Reproduction is hindered by unspecified preprocessing thresholds, lack of signer-independent splits, and missing hyperparameter details.

- Generative models limited by data scale: **High**
- MotionCLIP alignment benefits: **Medium**
- Dataset contribution (first public SyArSL corpus): **High**
