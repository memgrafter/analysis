# Learning Dynamics in RL Post-Training for Language Models

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2601.04670
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.04670
- **Reference count:** 33
- **Primary result:** RL post-training reduces output diversity via high feature alignment limiting the Representation NTK component; classifier-first RL (CF-RL) accelerates reward improvement by pre-shaping the classifier.

## Executive Summary
This paper investigates the learning dynamics of reinforcement learning (RL) post-training for language models through an empirical neural tangent kernel (NTK) decomposition. The decomposition separates updates into a Representation component (based on feature similarity) and a Gradient component (involving the classifier). The authors find that during RL, feature vectors become highly aligned (cosine similarity ~0.6), which restricts the Representation component to merely reinforce sampled tokens and increase confidence, thereby reducing output diversity. In contrast, the Gradient component, driven by the classifier, is optimized fastest and is critical for effective reward learning. Motivated by this, they propose classifier-first RL (CF-RL): train only the classifier initially before full RL. On Pythia-2.8B with GRPO/RLOO, CF-RL yields a large initial reward gain without suppressing feature changes or inflating classifier norms, confirming the theoretical analysis and offering a more efficient RL recipe.

## Method Summary
The study builds on standard RL post-training pipelines. First, a base model (Pythia-2.8B) is supervised fine-tuned (SFT) for one epoch on the AlpacaFarm `sft` split. For RL, they use GRPO and RLOO with a reward model (ArmoRM) on a filtered 20% subset of binarized UltraFeedback (prompt and outputs ≤512 tokens). The core procedure involves generating multiple rollouts per prompt, computing rewards with KL regularization, and updating the policy. CF-RL modifies this by inserting a classifier-only stage: for the first epoch, only the unembedding matrix (classifier) is updated while the transformer backbone is frozen; thereafter, full joint training proceeds. Evaluation tracks reward improvement, first-token entropy, semantic/style diversity (via pairwise cosine distances), feature change magnitude, classifier norm, and token-level update magnitudes.

## Key Results
- RL post-training lowers first-token entropy (high-reward samples: 1.516 vs. low-reward: 2.439) and reduces semantic/style diversity.
- The Gradient NTK component (involving the classifier) is optimized fastest, making the classifier a bottleneck for early learning.
- CF-RL accelerates reward improvement (large gain at epoch 1) with GRPO/RLOO, while not reducing feature distortion or increasing classifier norms relative to standard RL.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- **Claim:** High feature alignment during RL post-training limits the discriminative power of the Representation component of the NTK, causing updates to systematically reinforce sampled tokens and increase model confidence, which reduces output diversity.
- **Mechanism:** When feature vectors ϕ(x, y) across samples have high cosine similarity (often >0.6), their inner products ⟨ϕ, ϕ'⟩ are nearly non-negative and relatively uniform. The Representation component update uRep ~ ⟨ϕ, ϕ'⟩ T dy, where T is a projection matrix and dy = e_y - π. Under this alignment, the argmax of uRep always corresponds to the sampled token y_l regardless of context, amplifying the model's existing outputs and sharpening the distribution.
- **Core assumption:** Feature inner products are non-negative and exhibit limited variability across distinct samples (Table 1 shows mean cosine similarity ~0.6–0.65 with low standard deviation for Pythia-2.8B).
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract]: "High feature alignment limits the Representation component, which then reinforces sampled tokens and increases model confidence—explaining the observed output diversity reduction."
  - [section 4.2]: Proposition 2 proves that under ⟨ϕ, ϕ'⟩ ≥ 0, argmax(uRep) = y_l. Table 1 reports high mean cosine similarity (0.606) for Pythia-2.8B.
  - [corpus]: Weak corpus evidence; no direct match on NTK decomposition for RL or feature alignment causing diversity reduction. Neighbors discuss diversity reduction but not via NTK mechanism.
- **Break condition:** If feature representations become significantly more diverse (lower average cosine similarity) during training, the Representation component could provide richer sample-dependent updates, potentially mitigating confidence increase and diversity loss.

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim:** Effective RL learning depends critically on the Gradient component of the NTK, which involves the classifier matrix W and the gradients of the feature map ∂ϕ/∂θ. This component is optimized fastest, making the classifier a "bottleneck" for early learning dynamics.
- **Mechanism:** The Gradient component G = W (∂ϕ/∂θ) (∂ϕ'/∂θ)^⊤ W^⊤ explicitly includes the classifier W, giving it greater expressive capacity than the Representation component (which lacks W). Empirically, the classifier’s parameter norm changes fastest relative to other modules (Figure 1), indicating it adapts early to shape the Gradient-based kernel and enable effective reward learning.
- **Core assumption:** The classifier parameters are the primary drivers of the Gradient component’s evolution, and their rapid adaptation is necessary for meaningful policy updates when the Representation component is limited.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract]: "Effective learning in this regime depends on rapidly shaping the classifier, which directly affects the gradient component of the NTK."
  - [section 4.3]: Figure 1 shows classifier norm changes dominate early RL training. Text states: "we observe that the classifier parameters consistently exhibit the fastest relative change."
  - [corpus]: No direct corpus evidence; neighboring papers focus on RLHF outcomes or NTK in supervised settings, not classifier-specific dynamics in RL.
- **Break condition:** If classifier updates are constrained (e.g., frozen) from the start, the Gradient component may remain unshaped, leading to slower reward improvement and possible learning inefficiency.

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim:** Classifier-first RL (CF-RL) accelerates reward optimization because initial classifier-only updates shape the Gradient component early, leading to more effective subsequent full-model RL without suppressing feature changes or increasing classifier scale.
- **Mechanism:** By freezing all parameters except the classifier in the first stage, CF-RL directs optimization toward the "bottleneck" component (Gradient NTK). This pre-adapts W to the reward signal. When full RL resumes, the pre-shaped Gradient component enables more informed updates, yielding a large initial reward gain (Figure 4). The mechanism differs from LP-FT: CF-RL does not reduce feature distortion (Figure 5) or amplify classifier norms (Table S.3), instead shifting classifier updates toward structural tokens (Table 3).
- **Core assumption:** The classifier can be effectively shaped independently of feature layers, and this shape is transferable to subsequent joint training.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract]: "CF-RL accelerates reward improvement (large gain at epoch 1) while not reducing feature distortion or increasing classifier norms."
  - [section 5.3]: Figure 4 shows CF-GRPO/RLOO yields a larger epoch-1 reward jump than standard RL.
  - [section 5.4]: Figure 5 (no feature distortion reduction) and Table S.3 (stable classifier norm) contrast with LP-FT mechanisms. Table 3 shows classifier updates in CF-RL focus on structural tokens.
  - [corpus]: No direct corpus evidence; neighbors explore diversity techniques but not classifier-first RL or NTK-guided stages.
- **Break condition:** If the classifier norm changes significantly during the CF stage, it may indicate overfitting to the reward signal in a way that harms transfer. If feature distortion were reduced, it would suggest an LP-FT–like mechanism, which the paper explicitly denies.

## Foundational Learning
- **Concept: Empirical Neural Tangent Kernel (NTK) Decomposition**
  - Why needed here: The paper’s entire analysis hinges on decomposing the NTK into Representation (⟨ϕ, ϕ'⟩) and Gradient (W ∂ϕ/∂θ (∂ϕ'/∂θ)^⊤ W^⊤) components to isolate how RL updates propagate across samples.
  - Quick check question: How does the Gradient component differ from the Representation component in terms of parameters involved, and why does this matter for RL learning dynamics?
- **Concept: Feature Similarity and Neural Tangent Kernel Geometry**
  - Why needed here: High feature alignment (measured by cosine similarity) is the core condition that limits the Representation component’s discriminative power (Proposition 2).
  - Quick check question: What empirical statistic (Table 1) indicates that feature vectors are directionally aligned, and how does this mathematically constrain the uRep update?
- **Concept: Classifier Dynamics in Language Model Fine-Tuning**
  - Why needed here: The classifier (unembedding matrix W) is central to the Gradient component and shown to adapt fastest during RL (Figure 1), motivating CF-RL.
  - Quick check question: Why does the classifier learn faster than transformer layers during RL according to Figure 1, and what does this imply for the NTK’s Gradient component?

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map:** Feature extractor (transformer backbone) -> Classifier (unembedding matrix W) -> Policy head (softmax). Additional: Reward model (external) and NTK analyzer (computes kernel components from features and gradients).
- **Critical path:**
  1. SFT: Jointly train transformer and classifier on AlpacaFarm for general capabilities.
  2. RL initialization: Load SFT model; compute reward normalization statistics from initial policy rollouts.
  3. Standard RL: For each batch, generate samples, compute ˜r = r - λ KL(π_θ∥π_ref), update all parameters via GRPO/RLOO.
  4. CF-RL variant: First stage freeze transformer, update only classifier; second stage full RL.
  5. Monitoring: Track first-token entropy, diversity, feature change ∥∆ϕ∥, classifier norm ∥W∥, and token-level update magnitudes.
- **Design tradeoffs:**
  - **Diversity vs. Reward:** RL concentrates probability (entropy reduction), CF-RL speeds reward but does not prevent diversity loss.
  - **Feature distortion vs. classifier shaping:** Unlike LP-FT, CF-RL accepts feature layer adaptation later; it does not reduce feature changes but relies on pre-shaped classifier.
  - **Classifier norm stability:** CF-RL maintains stable classifier norm (Table S.3), avoiding scale explosions that could destabilize training.
- **Failure signatures:**
  - **Entropy collapse:** First-token entropy drops too fast (<1.0), indicating over-concentration due to high feature alignment + positive-reward reinforcement.
  - **No initial reward gain in CF stage:** Classifier-only training yields no improvement, suggesting poor conditioning or weak reward signal.
  - **Divergence after CF stage:** Full RL after CF causes KL explosion or reward collapse; may need smaller LR or shorter CF stage.
- **First 3 experiments:**
  1. Validate NTK decomposition: Compute Representation and Gradient components on held-out set during early RL; confirm that under high feature similarity, argmax(uRep) equals sampled token y_l for >90% of samples.
  2. Test feature similarity threshold: Correlate average cosine similarity of ϕ(x) across batches with subsequent entropy reduction; expect positive correlation.
  3. Run CF-RL ablation: Compare (a) standard RL, (b) CF-RL (classifier-only then full), (c) reversed order (full then classifier-only). Measure reward at epoch 1 and final diversity; expect (b) to outperform (a) on reward speed, with no diversity benefit; (c) should not accelerate reward.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
### Open Question 1
- **Question:** What causes the high feature alignment (i.e., high cosine similarity) observed in language model representations during RL post-training?
- **Basis in paper:** [inferred] The analysis assumes ⟨ϕt(x,y),ϕt(x′,y′)⟩≥0 and cites empirical statistics (Table 1) as motivation, but does not investigate the origin of this alignment.
- **Why unresolved:** Understanding the root cause of feature alignment could explain why the Representation component has limited discriminative power and whether this is an inherent property of pretrained models or induced by RL.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Ablation studies measuring feature similarity throughout pre-training, SFT, and RL; probing architectural or data factors that influence alignment; evaluating whether interventions that increase feature diversity affect the observed confidence increase and diversity reduction.

### Open Question 2
- **Question:** What is the precise mechanism by which CF-RL shifts classifier updates toward structural tokens (e.g., special tokens) rather than content-bearing tokens?
- **Basis in paper:** [inferred] The authors observe in Table 3 that CF-RL yields larger updates on structural tokens, and they conclude that CF-RL reshapes the classifier differently from LP-FT, but they do not explain why this pattern emerges.
- **Why unresolved:** This token-level update pattern may be crucial to CF-RL’s acceleration; understanding it could inform better classifier initialization or regularization strategies.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Analysis of the gradient flow on different token rows during the CF stage; studying how the reward model scores correlate with structural tokens; controlled experiments that modify the classifier’s initialization or regularization to test causality.

### Open Question 3
- **Question:** Does the acceleration benefit of CF-RL generalize to larger language models (e.g., >10B parameters) and to other RL algorithms such as PPO or DPO?
- **Basis in paper:** [inferred] Experiments are limited to Pythia-2.8B with GRPO/RLOO; while results are promising, the paper does not test scalability or algorithmic variants.
- **Why unresolved:** The dynamics of larger models may differ due to overparameterization effects, and other RL algorithms have different update rules; generalization is essential for practical adoption.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Replicating the CF-RL experiments on larger model families (e.g., Llama, Gemma) and with PPO/DPO; comparing reward improvement curves, output diversity, and classifier behavior across scales.

### Open Question 4
- **Question:** Can we formally prove that CF-RL achieves faster convergence compared to standard RL under the NTK framework?
- **Basis in paper:** [inferred] The authors provide an empirical NTK decomposition and argue that shaping the classifier early improves the Gradient component, but no theoretical convergence analysis is given.
- **Why unresolved:** A theoretical guarantee would solidify the empirical findings and guide hyperparameter choices (e.g., CF stage length).
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Deriving convergence rates for a two-stage optimization under the NTK model; analyzing the conditioning of the kernel when the classifier is pre-adapted; verifying the theory with simulations in simplified linear or wide-network settings.

## Limitations
- The analysis relies on an empirical NTK decomposition that linearizes network dynamics—a simplification that may not hold in deeper layers or later training.
- The study is confined to Pythia-2.8B with specific RL algorithms (GRPO/RLOO) and synthetic rewards, so generalizability to other architectures, sizes, or human feedback is unknown.
- Implementation details (reward normalization timing, UltraFeedback relabeling) are ambiguous, hindering exact replication.

## Confidence
- **NTK decomposition and theoretical mechanisms:** Medium. Supported by empirical measurements and Proposition 2, but rests on linearization and non‑negative inner‑product assumptions that lack validation across training.
- **Feature alignment → diversity reduction:** High. Strong empirical correlation: high cosine similarity (>0.6) consistently coincides with entropy drop (Figure 2) and argmax reinforcement (Proposition 2), backed by Table 1.
- **CF‑RL effectiveness and distinctness:** Medium. Reward acceleration is clear on Pythia‑2.8B (Figure 4), and differences from LP‑FT are shown (Figure 5, Table S.3), but scalability and robustness to other settings remain to be proven.

## Next Checks
