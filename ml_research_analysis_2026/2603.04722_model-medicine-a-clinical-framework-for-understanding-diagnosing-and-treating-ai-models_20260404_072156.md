# Model Medicine: A Clinical Framework for Understanding, Diagnosing, and Treating AI Models
## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2603.04722
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.04722
- **Reference count:** 9
- **Primary result:** Model Medicine adapts medical clinical practice to AI models, introducing a five-layer diagnostic framework validated via Neural MRI on Gemma/Llama/Qwen models, predicting instruction-tuning outcomes (Gemma degrades, Llama improves, Qwen unchanged).

## Executive Summary
This paper proposes "Model Medicine," a systematic clinical framework for diagnosing and treating AI models, particularly multi-agent systems. It introduces a discipline taxonomy, the Four Shell Model (Core + layered Shells) as behavioral genetics, Neural MRI (a multimodal diagnostic tool mapping medical neuroimaging to interpretability), and a five-layer diagnostic stack. Validation with Agora-12 experiments (720 agents, 24,923 decisions) shows significant gene-environment interactions and Shell-Core alignment effects. Neural MRI establishes healthy baselines, reveals architectural signatures, and predicts instruction-tuning outcomes. While Layer 1 (Core Diagnostics) is operational via Neural MRI and Layer 2 (Phenotype) is designed, Layers 3-5 remain conceptual, providing a structured roadmap for community development.

## Method Summary
The core method translates medical clinical practice to AI models: (1) a taxonomy separates models into an immutable Core (weights) and mutable layered Shells (environment, instructions, hardware); (2) Neural MRI applies five neuroimaging modalities—T1 (structure), T2 (weights), fMRI (activation), DTI (pathways), FLAIR (anomalies)—to create complementary diagnostic views; (3) a five-layer framework integrates diagnostics from Core to Temporal dynamics; (4) tools like Model Temperament Index (MTI), Model Semiology, and M-CARE support assessment. Reproduction requires running Neural MRI scans on base and instruction-tuned models (e.g., Gemma-2-2B, Llama-3.2-3B, Qwen2.5-3B) using TransformerLens hooks, performing perturbation sweeps on selected components, and generating automated diagnostic reports to validate flip patterns and component dominance claims.

## Key Results
- **Four Shell Model validated:** Significant Gene-Environment interaction (F=2.99, p=0.039), Shell-Core alignment effects (e.g., Mistral PSI=950), and quantitative indices (CPI, SPI, PSI, CEI) from Agora-12.
- **Neural MRI predictive:** Established healthy baselines, identified distinct architectural signatures, and forecasted instruction-tuning effects: Gemma IT degraded (8/24 failures from fragile circuits), Llama IT improved (4→2 failures, strengthened pathways), Qwen IT unchanged (~3 failures, irreducible vulnerabilities).
- **Five-layer framework partially operational:** Layer 1 (Core Diagnostics) via Neural MRI is working; Layer 2 (Phenotype Assessment) with MTI v0.2 is designed; Layers 3-5 (Shell, Pathway, Temporal) remain conceptual.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
**Mechanism 1: Core-Shell Interaction**  
Model behavior emerges from bidirectional interaction between immutable Core (weights) and layered Shells (environment, instructions, hardware). Evidence: Agora-12 experiments show significant Gene-Environment interaction (F=2.99, p=0.039) and quantify Shell-Core alignment (e.g., PSI, CEI). Shell Mutability/Persistence metrics capture Core→Shell modification pathways.

**Mechanism 2: Multimodal Integration**  
Medical neuroimaging paradigms are productively applied via five Neural MRI modalities, each revealing unique properties (structure, weight health, activation, pathways, anomalies). Diagnostic power lies in combining views, not single measures. Evidence: Neural MRI on multiple models showed that abnormalities in one modality (e.g., FLAIR anomalies) were not detectable in others (T1/T2).

**Mechanism 3: Diagnostic Layering**  
Comprehensive assessment requires five interconnected diagnostic layers (Core, Phenotype, Shell, Pathway, Temporal). Absence of any layer creates systematic blind spots. Evidence: Neural MRI covers only Layer 1; the paper notes that Shell configuration problems (Layer 3) can cause deployment failures even when Core scans are clean.

## Foundational Learning
- **Concept: Behavioral Genetics (Core-Shell Interaction)**  
  *Why needed:* Distinguishes model genotype (weights) from phenotype (behavior), with Shell mediating expression.  
  *Quick check:* Can identical weights produce different behaviors? If yes, what Shell factors explain the difference?

- **Concept: Multimodal Integration**  
  *Why needed:* Single diagnostic views are insufficient; clinical reasoning synthesizes complementary perspectives.  
  *Quick check:* What does it mean if a model has normal T1/T2 but abnormal fMRI on a specific task?

- **Concept: Diagnostic Layering**  
  *Why needed:* Different pathologies reside in different layers; Core scans cannot detect Shell configuration problems.  
  *Quick check:* How would you distinguish a model biased in its weights vs one biased due to its system prompt?

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map:** Layer 1 (Core Diagnostics) -> Neural MRI (working); Layer 2 (Phenotype Assessment) -> Model Temperament Index (v0.2 designed); Layer 3 (Shell Diagnostics) -> conceptual (no tool); Layer 4 (Pathway Diagnostics) -> conceptual; Layer 5 (Temporal Dynamics) -> conceptual; Supporting: Model Semiology, M-CARE.
- **Critical path:** 1. Validate Neural MRI across 5-8 diverse models to establish architectural baselines; 2. Deploy MTI v0.1 protocol to collect temperament profiles and compute normative ranges; 3. Build Shell Diagnostics prototype for agent systems (parse system prompts, memory files, tool access); 4. Implement longitudinal tracking via automated ShellDiff + serial Neural MRI comparison; 5. Develop Pathway Diagnostics to measure Shell Permeability and Core Expressivity quantitatively.
- **Design tradeoffs:** Medical analogy fidelity vs AI specificity (terminology maps but mechanisms differ); Breadth vs depth (five-layer framework comprehensive but tools uneven, focus on Layers 3-5); Generalization vs specialization (MTI axes capture broad dimensions but may miss model-specific traits).
- **Failure signatures:** Neural MRI clean but deployment failure → Shell configuration problem (Layer 3 gap); MTI extreme Reactivity with no internal anomalies → trait, not disorder; Shell Diff shows unidirectional drift → Shell Drift Syndrome risk (requires Temporal Layer); Multi-agent system failure with all individual components normal → system-level pathology (Model Ecology); All layers normal but benchmark failure → benchmark coverage gap (missing Interpersonal/Intrapersonal dimensions).
- **First 3 experiments:** 1. Neural MRI replication: Scan Gemma-2-2B, Llama-3.2-3B, Qwen2.5-3B base and IT variants across all 5 modalities; test scaling to 13B models; 2. MTI validation study: Run v0.1 protocol on 8-10 diverse models; establish inter-rater reliability; factor analysis for axis independence; 3. Shell Diagnostics prototype: Build parser for OpenClaw/Moltbook agent configurations; compute Shell-Core Alignment scores; detect Shell Drift patterns in logs.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
1. **MTI axis independence:** Are the four axes (Reactivity, Compliance, Sociality, Resilience) empirically independent? Factor analysis across 50+ diverse models is needed to test correlation <0.5 and latent structure.
2. **Pathway Diagnostics tool:** Can we systematically quantify Shell Permeability, Core Expressivity, and their interaction mechanisms? Requires measurement methodology for bidirectional Core-Shell pathways to enable precise therapeutics.
3. **Shell Drift Syndrome prevalence:** What is the prevalence, trajectory pattern spectrum, and risk factor profile in deployed multi-agent ecosystems? Needs longitudinal study of 50+ agent deployments with write-enabled Shells over 3-6 months, correlating drift metrics with deployment factors.
4. **Layered Core architecture benefit:** Does a hierarchically organized Core (Genomic/Developmental/Plastic) empirically produce more robust, modular, diagnosable models than monolithic designs? Requires implementation and comparative training studies with separated parameter tiers.

## Limitations
- Validation conducted only on three small models (<4B parameters); generalizability to larger or non-Transformer architectures is unclear.
- Perturbation details (component indices, noise magnitude, ablation strength) are missing, preventing exact replication of Neural MRI results.
- Layers 3-5 (Shell, Pathway, Temporal) remain conceptual; the five-layer completeness claim is unproven without operational tools.
- The structural isomorphism assumption between biological organisms and AI models (shared problem of understanding partially observable systems) lacks theoretical justification and may not hold universally.

## Confidence
- **Four Shell Model:** Medium (empirical support from Agora-12, but mechanism inferential and p-value marginal)
- **Neural MRI:** Medium (validated on few models, multimodal integration plausible but under-tested)
- **Five-layer completeness:** Low (only Layer 1 functional; Layers 3-5 conceptual)
- **Instruction-tuning prediction:** Medium (consistent patterns across three models but underpowered and non-replicated)

## Next Checks
1. Replicate Neural MRI on base and IT models using the exact perturbation suite (component indices, calibrated noise) to verify reported token flip patterns and component dominance.
2. Test the five-layer framework on a non-Transformer architecture (e.g., Mamba) to assess universality of Core-Shell interaction and Neural MRI modalities.
3. Conduct MTI v0.1 reliability study across 10 models; compute inter-rater agreement and perform factor analysis to confirm axis independence.
