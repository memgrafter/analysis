# Parameter Inference and Uncertainty Quantification with Diffusion Models: Extending CDI to 2D Spatial Conditioning

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2601.17224
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.17224
- **Reference count:** 17
- **Primary result:** CDI extends conditional diffusion from 1D to 2D spatial inverse problems, generating well-calibrated posterior distributions for CBED parameter inference that correctly reflect measurement information content.

## Executive Summary
This paper introduces Conditional Diffusion Inference (CDI) for probabilistic parameter estimation in 2D spatial inverse problems, specifically convergent beam electron diffraction (CBED). CDI employs a cross-modal transformer to condition a diffusion model in parameter space on observed 2D images. Trained on one million simulated CBED patterns, it generates posterior samples that are well-calibrated and appropriately sharp or broad depending on each parameter's observational constraints. In contrast, deterministic regression methods yield lower point-error by defaulting to training-set means, thereby masking true uncertainty. CDI thus provides reliable probabilistic inference where traditional point estimates fail.

## Method Summary
CDI uses a diffusion probabilistic model operating directly in the 13-dimensional physical parameter space. A ResNet-34 encoder processes 320×320 CBED images into a 5×5 feature map, which is flattened and projected into 25 "vision tokens." Each parameter is independently tokenized as a learnable embedding. These parameter tokens, a timestep embedding, and the vision tokens are concatenated into a 39-token sequence and processed by an 8-layer transformer encoder with cross-attention. The transformer's output parameter tokens are decoded to predict the noise added during the forward diffusion process. The model is trained to minimize the mean squared error between predicted and actual noise. At inference, iterative reverse sampling from the trained model, conditioned on a new image, yields approximate posterior samples *p(θ|y)*. Training uses a linear noise schedule (β₁=2.5e-5, β_T=0.02, T=800), AdamW (lr=2e-4, batch=64), and extensive image augmentation.

## Key Results
- CDI successfully extends conditional diffusion to 2D spatial observations, enabling full posterior inference for CBED's 13 parameters.
- Generated posteriors are well-calibrated (empirical coverage matches nominal confidence levels) and parameter-specific: sharp for well-constrained quantities (sample geometry, structure factors) and appropriately broad for ambiguous parameters (Debye-Waller factors).
- Deterministic regression methods achieve lower NRMSE by collapsing to training-set means, obscuring true uncertainty that CDI reveals through its spread.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1: Cross-Modal Transformer Architecture
- **Claim:** The transformer enables effective conditioning of parameter-space diffusion on 2D CBED images.
- **Mechanism:** A ResNet encoder extracts hierarchical spatial features from the image, flattened into 25 "vision tokens." Each of the 13 parameters has a separate learnable token. These, plus a timestep token, form a sequence processed by a transformer. Bidirectional cross-attention allows each parameter token to dynamically attend to relevant spatial regions, while vision tokens provide global context. The transformer outputs parameter tokens used to predict diffusion noise.
- **Core assumption:** Self-attention can learn meaningful, parameter-specific mappings between high-dimensional image features and the lower-dimensional parameter space despite the modality gap.
- **Evidence anchors:** [abstract] "develop cross-modal transformer architecture enabling parameter-space diffusion conditioned on image observations"; [section 3.3] detailed architecture description.
- **Break condition:** Over-aggressive downsampling losing critical spatial details, or failure to learn cross-modal attention weights correlating image features with specific parameters.

### Mechanism 2: Diffusion in Parameter Space
- **Claim:** Training a DDPM directly in parameter space with a learned variance schedule generates samples approximating the true posterior *p(θ|y)*.
- **Mechanism:** The forward process adds Gaussian noise to ground-truth parameter vectors over T steps. The neural network *ϵ_ϕ(θ_t, y, t)* is trained to predict this noise, where *θ_t* is a noisy parameter vector and *y* is the fixed, encoded image. Loss is MSE between predicted and actual noise. At inference, iterative reverse steps conditioned on *y* generate consistent parameter samples.
- **Core assumption:** The DDPM framework, with effective conditioning, can model the complex, potentially correlated 13-dimensional posterior.
- **Evidence anchors:** [abstract] "CDI employs diffusion models... to generate parameter distributions"; [section 3.2] formal equations for forward/reverse processes and training objective.
- **Break condition:** Highly multi-modal posteriors or complex correlations not captured by the unimodal Gaussian reverse process; or weak conditioning due to Mechanism 1 failure.

### Mechanism 3: Calibration Reflects Information Content
- **Claim:** Posterior sharpness correctly reflects the information each CBED pattern provides for each parameter.
- **Mechanism:** The diffusion process samples from the learned conditional distribution. For parameters strongly constrained by the diffraction pattern (e.g., thickness), the network maps noisy samples to a narrow region, yielding sharp posteriors. For weakly constrained parameters (e.g., Debye-Waller factors), the conditioning signal is weak, so the reverse process remains influenced by the noise prior, yielding broad posteriors near the training prior.
- **Core assumption:** The uniform parameter distribution and physics-based forward model ensure that a pattern's information content is consistently encoded in the posterior spread.
- **Evidence anchors:** [abstract] "well-calibrated posterior distributions... tight for well-determined quantities and appropriately broad for ambiguous parameters"; [section 5.3] calibration plots (Figure 4) and coverage-sharpness trade-off (Figure 5).
- **Break condition:** Inadequate coverage of parameter space in training data or systematic forward model biases, causing misaligned posteriors.

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map:** CBED Image -> ResNet-34 Encoder (modified) -> Feature Map Tokenizer (flatten + project) -> 25 Vision Tokens + 13 Parameter Tokens + 1 Time Token -> Transformer Encoder (8 layers) -> 13 Output Parameter Tokens -> Parameter Decoder (linear) -> Noise Prediction -> Diffusion Sampler (reverse DDPM)
- **Critical path:** Image features and parameter embeddings are fused via transformer attention to predict diffusion noise; iterative denoising yields posterior samples.
- **Design tradeoffs:**
  - Token count vs. spatial detail: 5×5 downsampling reduces compute but may lose fine disc modulations.
  - Diffusion timesteps T: More steps (800) improve sample quality but slow inference.
  - Per-parameter tokens: Allow specialized attention but increase sequence length; a single token would force shared representation, likely harming performance.
- **Failure signatures:**
  - Miscalibration: Coverage plots deviate from diagonal (e.g., 90% intervals contain truth only 70%).
  - Overly narrow posteriors for ambiguous parameters (e.g., Debye-Waller factors), mimicking regression collapse.
  - Training instability/loss divergence, indicating poor learning of conditional distribution.
- **First 3 experiments:**
  1. **Sanity Check:** For a test image with known thickness, run CDI with many samples; verify posterior for thickness is very narrow and centered on truth.
  2. **Calibration Diagnostic:** Create a synthetic test image where one parameter (e.g., `u12(Mg)`) is randomized with all others held fixed so the image provides zero information; verify its posterior is approximately uniform over the prior range.
  3. **Ablation – Tokenization:** Train a variant with all 13 parameters in a single token; expect degradation in NRMSE and calibration for well-constrained parameters (e.g., structure factors) versus minimal effect on Debye-Waller factors.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- **Generalizability:** Can CDI generalize to other spatial inverse problem domains beyond CBED (e.g., medical imaging, geophysical inversion)? The paper validates only on CBED; broader applicability is unverified.
- **Real-data robustness:** Does CDI maintain calibrated uncertainty quantification on real experimental CBED data with noise, artifacts, and distribution shift? All evaluation uses simulated data.
- **Comparative UQ performance:** How does CDI's calibration-sharpness trade-off compare to alternative Bayesian/probabilistic frameworks (Bayesian NNs, ensembles, normalizing flows) on the same tasks? Baselines are limited to deterministic regression.
- **Scaling architecture:** What modifications are needed for CDI to handle higher-dimensional spatial observations (e.g., 3D volumes) or different modalities (e.g., hyperspectral cubes)? The current transformer design is specific to 2D images.

## Limitations
- **Architectural ambiguities:** Exact layer dimensions/normalization in the Feature Map Tokenizer and Parameter Encoder/Decoder are unspecified, risking replication drift.
- **Calibration methodology:** Procedure for constructing marginal confidence intervals from posterior samples (empirical quantiles vs. joint credible regions) is not detailed.
- **Generalizability assumptions:** Reliance on a single physics simulator (Bloch wave) and uniform prior; performance under real-world noise or non-uniform priors remains unknown.

## Confidence
- **Cross-modal transformer conditioning:** Medium. Design is coherent and partially evidenced, but lack of external validation for this specific modality fusion limits confidence.
- **DDPM-based posterior sampling:** Medium. Adaptation to parameter space is theoretically sound but empirically unverified for 13D correlated posteriors.
- **Calibration & information-aware sharpness:** Low. While metrics are standard, no ablation isolates *why* diffusion captures information content; could stem from simulator bias or overfitting.

## Next Checks
1. **Per-parameter uniform-reversion test:** For a held-out test set, identify CBED patterns where a target parameter (e.g., `u12(Mg)`) is statistically independent of the image (by controlling other parameters). Verify CDI's posterior for that parameter matches the uniform prior (KS test p>0.05), not a regression-like collapse.
2. **Cross-attention diagnostic:** Extract attention weights from the trained transformer for samples with well- vs. poorly-constrained parameters. Confirm that for well-constrained parameters (e.g., thickness), attention focuses on high-frequency disc modulations; for poorly-constrained (Debye-Waller), attention is diffuse.
3. **Architecture ablation – tokenization:** Train a baseline with a single parameter token (instead of 13 separate tokens). Compare NRMSE and calibration (ECE, sharpness) on well-constrained (e.g., `θx`) vs. poorly-constrained (`u12`) parameters; expect degradation for the former.
