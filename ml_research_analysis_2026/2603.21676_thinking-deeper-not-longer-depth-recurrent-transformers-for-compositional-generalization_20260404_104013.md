# Thinking Deeper, Not Longer: Depth-Recurrent Transformers for Compositional Generalization

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2603.21676
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.21676
- Reference count: 3
- Primary result: Depth-recurrent Transformers with stable 20+ step recurrence achieve a computational frontier where accuracy transitions sharply with thinking steps on variable-depth reasoning tasks.

## Executive Summary
Standard Transformers have fixed computational depth, limiting generalization on tasks requiring variable-depth reasoning like multi-hop traversal or nested logic. This paper proposes a depth-recurrent Transformer that iterates a shared-weight block in latent space, decoupling compute depth from parameters. Stability for 20+ steps is achieved via three mechanisms: silent thinking objective (supervise only final output), LayerScale initialization, and identity-biased gated recurrence. Evaluated on three tasks with decreasing inductive bias—graph reachability (adjacency mask), nested boolean logic (RoPE), and unstructured relational text—results show a consistent computational frontier: accuracy improves with more thinking steps up to a task-specific limit. Ablations confirm that intermediate supervision cripples OOD generalization by encouraging heuristic shortcuts.

## Method Summary
The architecture uses a shared Transformer block applied recurrently in latent space. At each step, a candidate state is computed via the block, then combined with the previous state through a gated mechanism with an identity bias (gate bias initialized to -2.0). Stability is maintained by three components: (1) silent thinking—loss applied only at the final step of a randomly sampled step count during training; (2) LayerScale—small per-channel scaling after sub-layers to preserve fragile states early in training; (3) depth embeddings added to the input at each step. The perception interface varies per task (adjacency mask, RoPE, or none), and the readout is task-specific. This design allows the model to scale computational depth dynamically without increasing parameters, enabling adaptation to unseen task complexities.

## Key Results
- Across graph, logic, and text tasks, accuracy exhibits a sharp or gradual transition from chance to near-perfect as thinking steps increase, defining a computational frontier tied to task complexity.
- Intermediate supervision (ablating silent thinking) leads to impossible 1-step accuracy on 12-hop graphs, proving it fosters shortcut learning at the expense of genuine multi-step reasoning.
- The model generalizes robustly to OOD depths (e.g., >24 steps in logic) when trained with a range of step counts, demonstrating learned algorithmic reuse.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1: Silent Thinking
- **Claim:** Supervising only the final recurrence step prevents heuristic shortcuts and forces genuine multi-step reasoning.
- **Mechanism:** Loss is applied exclusively at the final step T (randomly sampled during training). The model cannot optimize for early predictions, compelling it to develop a stable internal state that yields correct output after many iterations.
- **Evidence:** Ablation in Table 1 shows intermediate supervision yields impossibly high 1-step accuracy on 12-hop graphs, confirming shortcut learning.
- **Break condition:** Adding any auxiliary loss on intermediate hidden states for t < T causes failure.

### Mechanism 2: LayerScale Initialization
- **Claim:** LayerScale (γ initialized to 10⁻⁴) protects fragile boolean-like latent states from destructive noise early in training.
- **Mechanism:** After each sub-layer, outputs are multiplied by a learned scaling vector Γ. Near-zero initialization makes initial dynamics nearly identity, allowing safe learning of recurrence gate before deeper transformations activate.
- **Evidence:** Used for logic and text tasks; omitted for graph where masking provides regularization. Without it, training becomes unstable beyond ~10 steps.
- **Break condition:** Omitting LayerScale in tasks requiring deep recurrence (>10 steps) leads to instability or non-convergence.

### Mechanism 3: Identity-Biased Gated Recurrence
- **Claim:** A gated recurrence with bias b_z = -2.0 creates a temporal gradient highway, enabling stable propagation over 20+ steps.
- **Mechanism:** Gate z(t) = σ([~H(t); H(t-1)]·W_z + b_z) with b_z = -2.0 starts at σ(b_z) ≈ 0.12, so ~88% of previous state is retained. This creates an almost-linear residual path across steps, mitigating vanishing gradients.
- **Evidence:** Contrast with Universal Transformers, which fail beyond 10 steps without such bias. Training stability observed with this initialization.
- **Break condition:** Setting gate bias to ≥0 causes vanishing/exploding gradients beyond ~10 steps.

## Foundational Learning
- **Compositional Generalization:** Combining known primitives to solve novel complexity. *Why needed:* The paper's core premise is that fixed-depth Transformers fail on tasks requiring variable computational depth. *Quick check:* If a model learns 1-5 hop traversal, can it handle 7 hops by reusing the hop primitive seven times?
- **Vertical vs. Horizontal Chain-of-Thought:** Vertical CoT increases recurrent iterations in latent space (depth), while horizontal CoT increases output token length (sequence length). *Why needed:* The paper decouples compute depth from parameters by using vertical recurrence. *Quick check:* Does increasing reasoning steps here increase latent iterations or output tokens?
- **Inductive Bias in Perception Interfaces:** The encoding of inputs provides structural hints (e.g., adjacency masks, RoPE). *Why needed:* Performance differences across tasks arise primarily from these biases, not the reasoning core. *Quick check:* Why does the model succeed on unstructured text without adjacency masks? ## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map:** Perception Interface (encodes input to H⁰) -> Recurrent Core (shared-weight Transformer block f_θ with gated recurrence, LayerScale, depth embeddings) -> Readout Head (task-specific output layer)
- **Critical path:** Input → Perception (H⁰) → for t=1..T: Hᵗ = z(t) ⊙ f_θ(Hᵗ⁻¹ + e_t) + (1 - z(t)) ⊙ Hᵗ⁻¹ → Readout(Hᵀ) → Loss (only at T)
- **Design tradeoffs:**
  - *Parameter efficiency vs. training stability:* Weight sharing reduces parameters (<1M) but requires careful initialization (LayerScale, gate bias) and silent supervision.
  - *Compute-accuracy frontier:* More steps T improve accuracy up to a task-specific frontier, then plateau; training range of T should cover expected OOD depths.
  - *Interpretability vs. efficiency:* Silent thinking loses step-by-step interpretability but avoids context window limits and token error compounding.
- **Failure signatures:**
  - *Graph:* Sharp accuracy cliff; failure at N hops with N steps indicates broken adjacency mask or recurrence gate.
  - *Logic:* Gradual degradation; total collapse beyond training depth suggests missing LayerScale or silent objective.
  - *Text:* No monotonic improvement with steps implies the core isn’t learning latent routing; likely insufficient hard negatives or incorrect gate bias.
- **First 3 experiments:**
  1. **Graph Reachability:** Use topological masking. Validates 1-hop=1-step constraint. Expect sharp diagonal frontier. Diagnostic: 1-step accuracy >50% on deep graphs indicates shortcut from unintended supervision.
  2. **Nested Boolean Logic:** Introduce RoPE and LayerScale. Tests hierarchical state maintenance. Expect gradual frontier and stable >24-step generalization. Diagnostic: Degradation beyond 16 steps suggests gate bias or LayerScale issues.
  3. **Unstructured Relational Text:** No structural masks. Tests autonomous latent routing. Expect monotonic difficulty and robust OOD gains with steps. Diagnostic: No improvement with more steps means reliance on shallow patterns; ensure hard negatives and test T beyond training max.

## Limitations
- Implementation gaps: Exact hyperparameters (learning rate, batch size), dataset generation details (graph degree distribution, text distractor parity), and OOD evaluation protocols are unspecified, hindering faithful reproduction.
- Mechanism attribution: The three stability mechanisms are evaluated jointly; isolating individual contributions across tasks remains unclear (e.g., LayerScale omitted for graph task).
- Algorithmic transparency: Silent thinking prevents step-wise interpretability; without probing, it’s uncertain whether the model learns true compositional algorithms versus heuristics.

## Confidence
- Silent thinking prevents shortcuts: **High** (ablation shows impossible 1-step accuracy with intermediate supervision).
- Gate bias enables deep recurrence: **Medium** (theoretically sound, stability observed, but no quantitative gradient flow analysis).
- LayerScale protects fragile states: **Medium** (necessary for logic/text but secondary to masking in graph).
- Computational frontier exists: **High** (consistent empirical results across three diverse tasks).

## Next Checks
1. **Ablate LayerScale on logic/text tasks:** Train without LayerScale on Nested Boolean and Unstructured Text. Degradation beyond ~16 steps would confirm its necessity for deep recurrence.
2. **Gradient flow measurement:** Track gradient norms and effective learning rates across recurrence steps (t=1..T). A flat/slowly decaying profile validates the identity-biased gate as a gradient highway.
3. **Algorithmic probing:** For the graph task, intervene on latent states at intermediate steps (e.g., zero out specific node embeddings) and measure final accuracy. A drop proportional to disrupted hop count would evidence genuine algorithmic execution.
