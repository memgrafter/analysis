# Spectral Alignment in Forward-Backward Representations via Temporal Abstraction

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID**: 2603.20103
- **Source URL**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.20103
- **Reference count**: 36
- **Primary result**: Temporal abstraction via action repetition acts as a spectral low-pass filter, reducing the effective rank of successor representations and enabling stable long-horizon learning even at high discount factors.

## Executive Summary
Forward-backward (FB) representations for successor representation (SR) learning suffer from spectral mismatch: low-rank neural bottlenecks cannot accurately approximate the high-rank dynamics inherent in continuous environments. This leads to instability, especially at high discount factors where bootstrapping amplifies errors. The paper introduces temporal abstraction via fixed-step action repetition. By executing the same action for \(k\) consecutive steps, the effective transition operator becomes the \(k\)-step composition of the original, which acts as a spectral low-pass filter, attenuating high-frequency components and reducing the SR’s effective rank. This spectral alignment reduces the approximation error in FB factorization and stabilizes learning. Experiments on continuous maze navigation tasks demonstrate that temporal abstraction significantly improves mean episodic return, particularly for high discount factors (\(\gamma=0.999\)), and counteracts the performance degradation typically observed when increasing embedding dimension or discount alone.

## Method Summary
The method augments the FB framework with an environment wrapper that enforces action repetition: every selected action is repeated \(k\) times, producing a transformed MDP whose transition matrix is the \(k\)-fold product of the original. This transformation suppresses high-frequency spectral modes, effectively lowering the rank of the successor representation. The FB architecture learns a forward network \(F_\theta(s,a,z)\) and a backward network \(B_\phi(s,a)\) such that \(M^\pi \approx F(s,a,z)^\top B(s',a')\). Training uses a loss that enforces this fixed-point condition, together with an orthogonal regularization on \(B\) to promote low-rank structure. A buffer of latent vectors \(z\) is maintained to induce diverse policies. The combination yields a representation that remains accurate even under high discounts and large embedding dimensions, outperforming baselines that rely solely on these hyperparameters.

## Key Results
- Temporal abstraction reduces the effective rank of the SR, evidenced by lower stable rank and spectral entropy as \(k\) increases.
- In continuous maze navigation, moderate \(k\) (e.g., 10) stabilizes FB learning and yields significantly higher mean episodic return, especially for \(\gamma=0.999\) where the baseline without \(k\) fails.
- Increasing embedding dimension \(d\) or discount \(\gamma\) alone raises Bellman error and degrades performance, but temporal abstraction mitigates these adverse effects, enabling robust long-horizon representation learning.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1: Spectral Low-Pass Filtering via Action Repetition
- **Claim**: Action repetition (temporal abstraction) acts as a spectral low-pass filter on the transition operator, attenuating high-frequency spectral components.
- **Mechanism**: By executing the same action for \(k\) consecutive steps, the effective transition becomes the \(k\)-step composition of the original transition matrix. This composition smooths the dynamics, reducing the high-frequency spectral modes that are sensitive to fine-grained transitions.
- **Core assumption**: The original transition dynamics contain significant high-frequency content that is difficult to approximate with low-rank models; the multi-step transition operator exponentially suppresses these modes.
- **Evidence anchors**:
  - [abstract] "By characterizing the spectral properties of the transition operator, we show that temporal abstraction acts as a low-pass filter that suppresses high-frequency spectral components."
  - [Section 4.2] Lemma 4.1 shows that the singular value error term in the FB bound decays as \((\sigma_{d+1}(P_{\text{rep}}))^k\), indicating exponential attenuation of subdominant modes.
  - [corpus] The neighbor paper "Spectral Representation-based Reinforcement Learning" discusses spectral challenges in RL but does not specifically analyze action repetition; corpus support is weak.
- **Break condition**: If the environment's dynamics are already low-rank or very smooth, the low-pass filtering may be unnecessary. Also, if \(k\) is too large, it may oversimplify and remove task-relevant dynamics, as seen in performance degradation (Figure 4a).

### Mechanism 2: Effective Rank Reduction Enables Low-Rank Approximation
- **Claim**: Temporal abstraction reduces the effective rank of the successor representation, making it more amenable to accurate low-rank factorization via FB.
- **Mechanism**: The effective rank, measured by stable rank and spectral entropy, decreases as \(k\) increases because the high-frequency singular values are attenuated. This spectral concentration aligns the SR's structure with the low-dimensional bottleneck of FB, reducing the truncation error \(\sigma_{d+1}\).
- **Core assumption**: The FB architecture has a fixed embedding dimension \(d\); the approximation error is lower-bounded by the \((d+1)\)-th singular value of the SR. Reducing effective rank lowers this bound.
- **Evidence anchors**:
  - [Section 5.3] "Increasing \(k\) accelerates the decay of the tail singular values, encouraging the representation to concentrate on the dominant, principal modes." Figure 2 shows stable rank and spectral entropy decreasing with \(k\).
  - [Section 4.1] The bound in Lemma 4.1 includes the term \((\sigma_{d+1}(P_{\text{rep}}))^k\), which shrinks exponentially with \(k\).
  - [corpus] "Reconciling Spatial and Temporal Abstractions for Goal Representation" explores temporal abstractions for goal representation but does not directly address spectral rank; limited support.
- **Break condition**: Excessive temporal abstraction (large \(k\)) can cause oversmoothing, collapsing the SR to near-rank-1 and losing intermediate structure critical for navigation (Figure 4a). Also, if the SR is intrinsically low-rank, further reduction may discard useful information.

### Mechanism 3: Stabilization of High-Discount Learning
- **Claim**: Temporal abstraction mitigates the instability of FB learning at high discount factors by improving the spectral conditioning of the SR operator.
- **Mechanism**: High discount factors amplify all singular values, but the high-frequency modes become particularly problematic during bootstrapping, leading to large Bellman errors. Action repetition suppresses these modes, yielding a better-conditioned operator and enabling stable learning even with \(\gamma\) close to 1.
- **Core assumption**: At high \(\gamma\), the SR becomes ill-conditioned due to slow spectral decay; bootstrapping errors propagate predominantly through high-frequency components.
- **Evidence anchors**:
  - [abstract] "This suppression reduces the effective rank of the induced SR while preserving a formal bound on the resulting value function error." and "particularly at high discount factors where bootstrapping becomes error-prone."
  - [Section 5.5] "Increasing \(\gamma\) amplifies existing modes... degrading the operator’s conditioning. In contrast, increasing \(k\) fundamentally smooths the dynamics by acting as a non-linear filter that exponentially attenuates subdominant, high-frequency modes." Also, Figure 5 shows Bellman error increases with \(\gamma\) without \(k\), but \(k\) reduces error.
  - [Section 5.4] Increasing embedding dimension \(d\) or discount factor \(\gamma\) alone increases Bellman error and degrades performance; temporal abstraction counteracts this.
  - [corpus] "Spectral Representation-based Reinforcement Learning" notes difficulties with function approximation for spectral methods but does not cover temporal abstraction; evidence is primarily from the paper.
- **Break condition**: For moderate discount factors (\(\gamma \leq 0.95\)), the instability is less severe, so the benefit of temporal abstraction for stabilization is diminished. Additionally, if the task requires precise short-horizon predictions, oversmoothing with high \(k\) might distort the value function.

## Foundational Learning
- **Concept: Successor Representation (SR)**  
  *Why needed*: The SR matrix \(M^\pi\) is the core quantity that FB approximates. It encodes discounted future state-action occupancies and allows rapid value computation for arbitrary reward functions. The paper's spectral analysis focuses on the spectrum of \(M^\pi\).  
  *Quick check*: Write the matrix equation defining the SR \(M^\pi\) in terms of the transition operator \(P^\pi\) and discount \(\gamma\). How does \(M^\pi\) relate to the value function \(Q^\pi\) for a given reward \(r\)?

- **Concept: Forward-Backward (FB) Representation**  
  *Why needed*: FB imposes a low-rank factorization \(M^\pi \approx F(s,a,z)^\top B(s',a')\) to make SR learning tractable. The paper identifies that the low-rank bottleneck causes spectral mismatch in continuous environments.  
  *Quick check*: What are the two conditions that define a valid FB representation (Equation 1)? What role does the latent vector \(z\) play?

- **Concept: Spectral Properties of Operators (Singular Values, Stable Rank, Spectral Entropy)**  
  *Why needed*: These metrics quantify the effective rank of the SR and how it changes with temporal abstraction. The paper uses them to measure spectral alignment.  
  *Quick check*: Define stable rank of a matrix. What does a low stable rank indicate about the matrix's singular value spectrum? How does spectral entropy complement stable rank?

- **Concept: Temporal Abstraction via Action Repetition**  
  *Why needed*: Action repetition is the intervention that reshapes the spectrum of the transition operator. Understanding its effect as a low-pass filter is central to the paper's contribution.  
  *Quick check*: How is the transition matrix of the action-repeat MDP (\(P_{\text{rep}}\)) related to the original transition matrix \(P\)? What is the effect on the singular values when we increase the repeat factor \(k\)?

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map**:  
  Z-buffer → Actor → Action → Environment wrapper (repeat \(k\) times) → Resulting state → Replay buffer  
  Replay buffer + Z-buffer → Training step → Forward/Backward networks → Losses (FB, orthogonal) → Parameter updates (\(\theta, \phi, \psi\)) → (optionally) target networks

- **Critical path** (training loop):
  1. **Initialization**: Randomly initialize all networks and buffers. Pre-fill replay buffer with random steps.
  2. **Z-buffer reset**: Every `hold_steps` (hyperparameter, e.g., 10), refresh part of the z-buffer with new random vectors.
  3. **Interaction loop**:
     - Sample \(z\) from z-buffer.
     - Given current state \(s\), compute action probabilities via actor: \(a \sim \pi_\psi(s, z)\) (or sample greedily from \(F\)).
     - Execute action repetition: for \(i\) in range(\(k\)): \(s, r, \text{done} = \text{env.step}(a)\) (accumulate rewards if needed, but for SR ignore). After \(k\) steps, obtain next state \(s_k\) and \(\text{done}_k\).
     - Store transition \((s, a, s_k, \text{done}_k)\) in replay buffer.
     - Set \(s = s_k\).
  4. **Training step** (after enough samples):
     - Sample minibatch of transitions \((s, a, s', \text{done})\) from replay buffer.
     - Sample minibatch of \(z\) vectors from z-buffer.
     - Compute forward features: \(f = F_\theta(s, a, z)\).
     - Compute backward features for next state: \(b_{\text{next}} = B_\phi(s', a')\) where \(a'\) is sampled from current policy \(\pi_\psi(s', z)\) (or from replay if available).
     - Compute FB loss: \(\mathcal{L}_{\text{FB}} = \|f^\top b_{\text{next}} - \text{target}\|^2\) (or a Bellman residual variant).  
     - Add orthogonal loss on \(B\): \(\mathcal{L}_{\text{orth}} = \|B_\phi(s,a)^\top B_\phi(s,a) - I\|^2\) across the batch.
     - Total loss: \(\mathcal{L} = \mathcal{L}_{\text{FB}} + \lambda_{\text{orth}} \mathcal{L}_{\text{orth}}\) (\(\lambda_{\text{orth}} = 1.0\)).
     - Update \(\theta, \phi, \psi\) with gradient descent.
     - Optionally update target networks.
  5. Periodically evaluate the learned representation by computing Q-values for a given reward (e.g., goal reaching) and measuring episodic return.

- **Design tradeoffs**:
  - **Embedding dimension (\(d\))**: Larger \(d\) increases capacity but can lead to higher Bellman error if the SR's effective rank not matched; temporal abstraction allows larger \(d\) to be used beneficially.
  - **Discount factor (\(\gamma\))**: Larger \(\gamma\) extends effective horizon but makes FB learning unstable; combining moderate \(\gamma\) with temporal abstraction (higher \(k\)) yields similar effective horizon with better stability.
  - **Temporal abstraction (\(k\))**: Larger \(k\) reduces effective rank and stabilizes learning, but too large \(k\) oversmooths and loses relevant dynamics; optimal \(k\) environment‑dependent.
  - **Z-buffer sampling ratio**: Ratio of buffer data vs. random \(z\) affects policy diversity; paper uses 0.5; tuning may be needed.
  - **Orthogonal loss weight**: Helps enforce low‑rank structure; too high may force orthogonality too strictly and harm representation.

- **Failure signatures**:
  - **High Bellman error**: Measured as \(\|F(s,a,z)^\top B(s',a') - \text{target}\|^2\), indicates poor FB fixed‑point satisfaction; often rises when \(d\) or \(\gamma\) is large without \(k\).
  - **Performance collapse at high \(\gamma\)**: \(\gamma=0.999\) leads to near‑zero return without temporal abstraction.
  - **Degradation with large \(k\)**: Performance drops when \(k\) exceeds optimal (e.g., \(k=50\) in Four‑Rooms).
  - **Unstable training**: Loss oscillations or diverging values may indicate poor spectral alignment.
  - **Spectral metrics**: If stable rank or spectral entropy do not decrease with \(k\), temporal abstraction may be ineffective due to environment dynamics.

- **First 3 experiments** (to validate implementation):
  1. **Ablation over \(k\)**: Fix \(d=100\), \(\gamma=0.95\), vary \(k\) in \{1,3,5,10,20,50\}. Plot mean episodic return and Bellman error. Expect peak return at moderate \(k\) (e.g., \(k=10\)) and increased Bellman error at \(k=1\) and very high \(k\).
  2. **Interaction with embedding dimension**: Fix \(\gamma=0.95\), vary \(d\) in \{25,50,100,200,400\} with \(k=1\) and \(k=10\). Plot return vs \(d\). Expect that for \(k=1\), return degrades as \(d\) increases; for \(k=10\), return remains stable or improves with \(d\).
  3. **Interaction with discount factor**: Fix \(d=100\), vary \(\gamma\) in \{0.9,0.95,0.99,0.999\} with \(k=1\) and \(k=10\). Plot return vs \(\gamma\). Expect that for \(k=1\), return drops at high \(\gamma\); for \(k=10\), return remains high even at \(\gamma=0.999\).

## Limitations
- Results are only on continuous 2D mazes; generality to high‑dimensional (pixel) or discrete stochastic environments is unknown.
- The approach assumes the original dynamics contain significant high‑frequency components; if the environment is already low‑rank/smooth, temporal abstraction may be unnecessary or harmful.
- Fixed action repetition may oversmooth and discard task‑relevant dynamics; no principled method to choose \(k\).

## Confidence
- **Spectral Low‑Pass Filtering**: High
- **Effective Rank Reduction**: Medium
- **Stabilization of High‑γ Learning**: Low‑Medium
