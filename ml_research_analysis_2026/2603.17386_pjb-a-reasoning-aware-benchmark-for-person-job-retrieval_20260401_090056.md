# PJB: A Reasoning-Aware Benchmark for Person-Job Retrieval

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2603.17386  
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.17386  
- **Reference count:** 40  
- **Primary result:** PJB reveals that performance differences across job domains and reasoning types are larger than gains from adding modules like reranking; reranking only helps when the base retriever is domain-adapted.

## Executive Summary
PJB is a reasoning-aware benchmark for person-job retrieval designed to diagnose system failures rather than merely ranking models. Built from real recruitment data spanning six domains (297 job descriptions, 197,674 resumes, 2,242 positive judgments), it enriches each query with diagnostic labels for domain family and reasoning type, based on job competency relevance. evaluations show that performance varies drastically across domains and reasoning complexities, and that enhancement modules (query understanding, reranking) yield stable gains only for a strong, domain-adapted base retriever; for weaker retrievers, these modules often degrade performance. The work concludes that optimization must first ensure a robust base retriever before adding general-purpose modules.

## Method Summary
PJB follows a Cranfield-style offline evaluation paradigm with fixed queries (complete job descriptions), documents (de-identified resumes), and binary relevance judgments derived from an LLM-as-a-Judge pipeline focused on job competency. The dataset includes 297 JD queries, 197,674 CV documents, and ~7.5 positives per query, annotated with six domain families and three reasoning types (parallel-only, hybrid-balanced, serial-dominant) via heuristic rules on JD structure. The study ablates two enhancement modules—Query Understanding (LLM-based JD rewriting) and Reranking (cross-encoder)—with two dense retrievers: a domain-adapted model (CRE-T1-0.6B) and a general-purpose model (Qwen3-Embedding-0.6B). Metrics (nDCG@10, Recall@20, MRR@10) are computed overall and sliced by domain/reasoning, with error profiling based on low nDCG thresholds.

## Key Results
- Performance heterogeneity across domain families far exceeds gains from module upgrades.
- Reranking improves nDCG@10 by +8.9% on the domain-adapted retriever (0.2070 → 0.2253).
- General-purpose modules (QU, Rerank) degrade performance on the weaker base retriever, with combined modules causing catastrophic failure.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1
Diagnostic slicing via domain-family and reasoning-type labels exposes specific failure modes that aggregate metrics conceal. The labels are derived from job categories and heuristic counts of explicit constraints versus semantic inference needs, allowing post-hoc stratification of scores to reveal weaknesses in high-stakes business slices (e.g., "Mechanical/Hardware" domains or "serial-dominant" queries). The paper's heatmaps and tables show heterogeneous performance across slices, concluding that domain heterogeneity far outweighs module gains.

### Mechanism 2
Module effectiveness is gated by base retriever quality. Experiments show reranking provides stable gains only for the stronger domain-adapted retriever (CRE-T1) while degrading the general-purpose one (Qwen3); combining query understanding with reranking causes catastrophic failure for Qwen3. This indicates that post-processing modules assume a sufficiently relevant candidate set from the base retriever; poor recall leads to noise amplification. Error profiles confirm: CRE-T1+Rerank reduces bad query rate, while Qwen3+Rerank increases it.

### Mechanism 3
Reasoning heterogeneity creates distinct retrieval bottlenecks. Reranking helps primarily on "serial-dominant" queries (requiring multi-hop competency inference) but not on "parallel-only" (constraint filtering) queries for the strong base retriever. This suggests that parallel (filtering) and serial (inference) reasoning stress different pipeline components; a module optimizing one may not help the other. Table 4 shows a +0.1930 nDCG gain on serial-dominant for CRE-T1+Rerank, but a decline on parallel-only.

## Foundational Learning
- **Cranfield/TREC offline evaluation:** Fixed queries, documents, relevance judgments. Needed: PJB inherits this paradigm but upgrades the "relevance judgment" component with diagnostic labels for domain and reasoning. - **Reasoning types (parallel vs. serial):** Parallel verifies explicit constraints; serial requires multi-hop semantic inference. Needed: PJB's core diagnostic dimension; e.g., a JD requiring "location: Berlin, education: Master's, 5+ years, and to design cloud-native microservices" engages parallel (filtering) for location/education/years and serial (inference) for skill compatibility.
- **Domain adaptation vs. general-purpose models:** Needed to grasp why CRE-T1 (fine-tuned on recruitment data) vastly outperforms Qwen3 (generalist); domain-specific training captures recruitment semantics that general models miss. Quick check: What does the large performance gap imply about the nature of recruitment retrieval data?

## Architecture Onboarding
Component map: Job Description -> Base Dense Retriever -> [Query Understanding? -> Reranker?] -> Ranked CV List -> Evaluation Engine (metric computation, slicing) -> Error Profiler.

Critical path: A run configures (base_model, use_QU, use_Rerank). The evaluation script loads fixed qrels, computes per-query metrics (nDCG@10, etc.), aggregates overall and by diagnostic labels (domain_family, reasoning_type), and profiles errors (zero/low-gain rates).

Design tradeoffs: Full-corpus evaluation tests recall; reranking-only tests reordering on a fixed top-k set—results are not directly comparable. Sparse positive labels (only positives released) limit metric robustness and fine-grained analysis. Reasoning-type labels are heuristic proxies (keyword counts, slot analysis), not human-annotated traces.

Failure signatures: Module degradation (adding QU/Rerank lowers nDCG vs. base). High bad query rate (>70% of queries with nDCG@20 ≤0.10). Slice collapse (near-zero performance on entire domain families).

First 3 experiments:
1. Baseline establishment: Run CRE-T1 and Qwen3 without modules. Record overall and per-domain nDCG@10; verify CRE-T1 significantly outperforms Qwen3.
2. Module ablation: For CRE-T1, test +Rerank and +QU+Rerank; expect +Rerank gain (~+9%), while QU degrades and QU+Rerank fails. For Qwen3, all modules should degrade.
3. Reasoning-type slice analysis: For CRE-T1 baseline vs. +Rerank, compare nDCG@10 across parallel-only, hybrid-balanced, serial-dominant. Expect rerank gain concentrated on serial-dominant (e.g., +0.1930) and decline on parallel-only.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
### Open Question 1
**Question:** Do the domain heterogeneity and module gain patterns generalize beyond dense retrieval to BM25 and hybrid pipelines?  
**Basis in paper:** The experiments cover only dense retrieval; conclusions are restricted to dense retrieval.  
**Why unresolved:** Without evaluating other recall strategies, we cannot know if the capability map is architecture-specific or task-intrinsic.  
**What evidence would resolve it:** Run the same 2×4 ablation (CRE-T1 vs. Qwen3 with QU/Rerank) on BM25 and hybrid pipelines; compare domain/reasoning slice patterns.

### Open Question 2
**Question:** Are the module interactions (rerank helps only strong retrievers) robust across a broader spectrum of model scales and architectures?  
**Basis in paper:** Evidence comes from two specific 0.6B models; cannot be generalized to all retrievers.  
**Why unresolved:** The quality-gate hypothesis needs testing on more models (e.g., 1B, 3B, 7B) from both domain-adapted and general families.  
**What evidence would resolve it:** Evaluate additional retrieval models under the same ablations; check if the "rerank-only-on-strong-base" pattern correlates with baseline performance rather than model family.

### Open Question 3
**Question:** Can rule-based reasoning-type labels be validated and improved through human-annotated reasoning traces?  
**Basis in paper:** Reasoning labels are heuristic estimates, not manual annotations; limitation cites heuristic labeling.  
**Why unresolved:** Heuristics may misclassify queries; without gold reasoning traces, we cannot confirm whether observed heterogeneity reflects true inference difficulty.  
**What evidence would resolve it:** Human annotation study where experts label reasoning steps per query; recompute slices with human labels and re-analyze performance. Convergence validates; divergence indicates need for refined heuristics.

### Open Question 4
**Question:** How does PJB's diagnostic utility change with natural-language job search queries instead of structured JDs?  
**Basis in paper:** Future work includes more natural-language query formulations.  
**Why unresolved:** Real queries may be shorter, less structured; domain/reasoning distributions and insights might shift significantly.  
**What evidence would resolve it:** Create a parallel natural-query set (paraphrase JDs into typical searches); evaluate systems on both types; compare stability of domain/reasoning slice patterns.

### Open Question 5
**Question:** Would more complete relevance judgments (graded, deeper negatives) enhance discriminative power, especially for low-gain queries?  
**Basis in paper:** Qrels are sparse (positive-only); unjudged pairs are treated as non-relevant.  
**Why unresolved:** Sparse binaries limit metric robustness and obscure fine-grained differences among retrievers on borderline queries.  
**What evidence would resolve it:** Expand annotations to include graded relevance and deeper candidate pools; re-run diagnostics with graded metrics; assess if same trends hold and if new failure modes emerge.

## Limitations
- The PJB v1.0 dataset is internal and unreleased; approximate replication requires reverse-engineering heuristic rules, introducing unknown noise.
- Exact training details, hyperparameters, and checkpoints for CRE-T1-0.6B are undisclosed; the LLM-as-a-Judge pipeline (prompts, thresholds) lacks documentation.
- Reasoning-type labels are heuristic proxies (not human-annotated traces); domain-family mapping from 37 categories to 6 groups is incompletely specified.

## Confidence
- **Diagnostic slicing utility:** High (slice-level heterogeneity consistently shown across figures/tables).
- **Base retriever quality gates module gains:** Medium (strong evidence from two models, but generalization to other scales/architectures untested).
- **Reasoning heterogeneity creates distinct bottlenecks:** Low-Medium (labels heuristic; claim of fundamental differences unproven).
