# Lost in Aggregation: On a Fundamental Expressivity Limit of Message-Passing Graph Neural Networks
## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2603.14846
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.14846
- Reference count: 10
- Primary result: MP-GNNs with logarithmic (or sublinear) aggregations have only polynomial (resp. sub-exponential) distinguishing power, while two iterations of Color Refinement already achieve exponential distinguishing power on diameter-2 graphs, making uniform MP-GNNs relatively infinitely weaker.

## Executive Summary
Message-passing graph neural networks (MP-GNNs) are limited in their ability to distinguish non-isomorphic graphs or non-equivariant vertices when using standard aggregation functions (sum, mean, max). The paper defines a broad class of aggregations whose output bit-length grows at most logarithmically (or sublinearly) in the input size, covering most practical designs. It shows that for such aggregations, the number of distinct vertex (or graph) representations that a uniform MP-GNN can produce on graphs of size n is at most polynomial (or sub-exponential). In contrast, just two iterations of the Color Refinement algorithm yield an exponential number of classes on a family of diameter-2 graphs. Consequently, uniform MP-GNNs are fundamentally weaker than even a shallow non-uniform variant of Color Refinement. The analysis also clarifies a uniform vs. non-uniform expressivity gap: non-uniform MP-GNNs can match full Color Refinement by tailoring aggregations per graph size, but uniform ones cannot.

## Method Summary
The paper conducts a theoretical analysis of the information bottleneck created by aggregation functions. It introduces the notion of information complexity L_N(n), defined as the maximum bit-length of aggregation outputs across all vertices in any graph of size n when using a given MP-GNN model N. By bounding L_N(n) based on the output-size complexity of the aggregation (Definition 3.1), it proves that logarithmic aggregations lead to L_N(n)=O(log n), hence the number of distinguishable vertex classes is at most 2^{L_N(n)} = poly(n). For sublinear aggregations, L_N(n)=o(n) gives sub-exponential classes. The second main result constructs a family of diameter-2 graphs (the GK family) where two iterations of Color Refinement assign pairwise distinct colors to the central vertices, yielding at least binomial(2n-1, n-1) classes, which grows exponentially. Comparing the two bounds shows that for uniform MP-GNNs with standard aggregations, the ratio of their distinguishing power to that of CR(2) tends to zero as n increases, indicating an asymptotic infinity gap.

## Key Results
- MP-GNNs with logarithmic aggregations (e.g., sum, mean, max) can induce at most polynomially many vertex/graph equivalence classes on graphs of size n.
- With sublinear aggregations, the number of equivalence classes is at most sub-exponential in n.
- Two iterations of Color Refinement on diameter-2 graphs of size 2n+1 achieve at least binomial(2n-1, n-1) distinct classes, i.e., exponential in n; thus uniform MP-GNNs are relatively infinitely weaker as n→∞.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1: Information Bottleneck from Aggregation Output Size
The paper shows that the distinguishing power of an MP-GNN is limited by the total bit-length of its intermediate representations. For an MP-GNN N, define L_N(n) as the maximum bit-length of the sequence of aggregation outputs a vertex can produce on any graph of size n. Lemma 3.3 proves that the number of distinct vertex representations cannot exceed 2^{L_N(n)}. If all aggregations are logarithmic (output bit-length O(log n) given n input elements), Lemma 3.5 establishes L_N(n)=O(log n) by induction over layers, leading to a polynomial upper bound (2^{O(log n)} = poly(n)). The core assumption is that aggregations are computable and their output size depends only on the number and bit-length of inputs, not on the graph structure. This mechanism fails only if an aggregation has super-logarithmic output complexity (e.g., linear or worse) or if the message/composition functions blow up the bit-length.

### Mechanism 2: Relative Infinite Weakness Compared to Color Refinement
The lower bound for Color Refinement comes from a specific construction: consider diameter-2 graphs consisting of a central vertex connected to n first-level vertices, which in turn connect to n second-level vertices, with a distribution of edges between the two outer layers (the GK family). After two iterations, each central vertex receives a color that encodes the multiset of degrees of its neighbors. Because the degree distribution can be any partition of n, there are at least binomial(2n-1, n-1) distinct colors. In contrast, for any uniform MP-GNN with logarithmic/sublinear aggregations, the distinguishing power is polynomially/sub-exponentially bounded. Therefore, as n grows, the ratio of MP-GNN classes to CR(2) classes tends to zero exponentially—the MP-GNN is “infinitely weaker” in the asymptotic sense. This gap persists regardless of the number of MP-GNN layers, as long as the aggregations remain logarithmic and the model is uniform; the only way to close it is to allow aggregations with super-logarithmic output size.

### Mechanism 3: Uniform vs. Non-Uniform Expressivity Gap
Prior work claimed that MP-GNNs can match the full (multiple-iteration) Color Refinement. That claim relies on a non-uniform model: for each graph size n one can design a different MP-GNN (e.g., by using an aggregation that encodes the entire CR state into a rational number). Under the uniform model—one fixed architecture and parameters for all n—this matching is impossible because the aggregation’s output bit-length must be bounded by a fixed function of n (e.g., O(log n)). That bound cannot capture the exponential number of CR classes, which require at least Ω(n) bits to distinguish. Thus the gap is fundamental for standard aggregations under the uniform notion, which is the relevant one for practical machine learning where a single model is trained and then applied to graphs of various sizes.

## Foundational Learning
- **Message-Passing Graph Neural Networks (MP-GNNs)**: The standard framework for learning on graphs, where each layer aggregates neighborhood information via message, aggregation, and combination functions. *Why needed*: The paper analyzes MP-GNN expressivity, so understanding the three-step update is essential. *Quick check*: In an MP-GNN layer, what are the roles of the message function, aggregation function, and combination MLP?
- **Color Refinement (Weisfeiler-Leman test)**: An iterative graph isomorphism heuristic that refines vertex colors based on neighborhood multisets. *Why needed*: It serves as the gold standard for distinguishing power, and the paper compares MP-GNNs to both one and two iterations. *Quick check*: How does one iteration of Color Refinement update a vertex’s color, and what is the typical number of iterations until stabilization?
- **Bit-length and Information Complexity**: The measure of the number of bits needed to represent rational numbers appearing in aggregations. *Why needed*: The paper’s core bound uses the bit-length of aggregation outputs to limit the total information that can be stored and thus the number of distinct representations. *Quick check*: Given a rational vector q, how is its bit-length λ(q) defined, and why does bounding λ(q) restrict the number of distinguishable outputs?

## Architecture Onboarding
Component map (plain text):
Graph (node features) -> message function (MLP) -> aggregation (sum/mean/max) -> combination (MLP) -> [repeat per layer] -> optional readout (global aggregation + MLP) -> graph embedding / node embeddings

Critical path: The information flows from the initial features through successive layers. At each aggregation step, the bit-length of the aggregated messages may increase. The total information complexity L_N(n) accumulates the maximum bit-length of any aggregation output across all layers and vertices. If each aggregation function has logarithmic output-size complexity (i.e., the output bit-length grows O(log n) with the multiset size), then L_N(n) remains O(log n) after any fixed number of layers, capping the number of distinct vertex representations at poly(n). This is the bottleneck.

Design tradeoffs:
- Aggregation choice: Sum/mean/max are logarithmic and thus limit distinguishing power to polynomial. Using a more powerful aggregation (e.g., one that can embed the multiset in a compressed form) could increase expressivity but may be computationally heavy and prone to overfitting.
- Number of layers: Increasing depth allows the MP-GNN to emulate more iterations of Color Refinement but does not overcome the logarithmic bit-length bound; the gap to CR(2) remains asymptotically infinite for uniform models.
- Uniformity: A uniform model (same parameters for all graph sizes) is required for practical generalization. Non-uniform designs (different aggregations per n) can match full CR but are not feasible in real deployments.

Failure signatures:
- The model fails to distinguish two non-isomorphic graphs (or two vertices in different graphs) that are distinguishable by even two iterations of Color Refinement, particularly on diameter-2 graphs with an exponential number of CR classes.
- The number of distinct node representations grows only polynomially with graph size, while the space of non-isomorphic graphs grows doubly-exponentially; thus many pairs of graphs will be mapped to the same representation.
- On the GK family, the central vertices of different graphs will receive identical MP-GNN embeddings if the aggregation output bit-length is insufficient (e.g., sum aggregation after two layers yields a value n + Σ_i i·k_i, which can take only O(n^2) distinct values, far fewer than the exponential number of partitions).

First 3 experiments:
1. **GK family reproduction**: Generate the GK(2n+1) graphs for n=5,6. Compute CR(2) colors (using exact rational arithmetic) and representations from a 2-layer MP-GNN with sum aggregation and identity MLP. Count distinct central vertex values: CR(2) should yield ≥ binomial(2n-1, n-1) classes (exponential), while the MP-GNN gives at most O(n^2) distinct values (polynomial).
2. **Bit-length scaling**: Implement a standard MP-GNN (e.g., GIN) with exact rational numbers. For random graphs of increasing size (n up to 200), track the maximum bit-length of node embeddings after each layer. Verify whether it stays O(log n) as predicted for logarithmic aggregations, or if it grows faster due to rounding or activation choices.
3. **Generalization across sizes**: Train a 3-layer GIN on small graphs (≤20 nodes) for a synthetic task requiring distinguishing many graph classes (e.g., counting 4-cycles). Test on larger graphs (≥50 nodes) from the same distribution. A significant drop in accuracy would illustrate the uniform expressivity limit when the model is forced to handle unseen graph sizes with the same parameters.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- **Aggregations with high output size**: Can MP-GNNs that use aggregations with super-polynomial output complexity (enough to encode all graphs) achieve the uniform distinguishing power of full Color Refinement? The paper leaves this case unanalyzed.
- **Softmax aggregation**: What is the output-size complexity of softmax (as in Graph Attention Networks) under rational inputs with bounded bit-length? It might be linear, but careful analysis is needed.
- **Non-ReLU activations**: How do activations like sigmoid or tanh in MLPs affect output-size complexity? Exponentiation could cause exponential bit-length growth, breaking the polynomial/sub-exponential guarantees.
- **Alternative complexity measures**: Could a tighter or different measure than output-size complexity provide better expressivity bounds for arbitrary computable aggregations?

## Limitations
- Theoretical results rely on a uniform architecture and exact rational arithmetic; practical implementations using floating-point may not obey the bit-length bounds.
- The exponential lower bound for Color Refinement is based on a specific constructed GK family; it is not known how prevalent such hard instances are in real-world data.
- The polynomial upper bound for MP-GNNs is existential; actual models may achieve even fewer distinct representations, and the exact tightness of the bound remains open.

## Confidence
- Claim 1: Logarithmic aggregations → polynomial distinguishing power. Confidence: High.
- Claim 2: CR(2) yields at least binomial(2n-1, n-1) classes on diameter-2 graphs. Confidence: High.
- Claim 3: Ratio of MP-GNN to CR(2) power tends to 0 (infinite weakness). Confidence: Medium.
- Claim 4: Uniform vs. non‑uniform gap is fundamental for standard aggregations. Confidence: Medium.
