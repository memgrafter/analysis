# Fatigue-Aware Learning to Defer via Constrained Optimisation
## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID**: 2604.00904
- **Source URL**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.00904
- **Reference count**: 40
- **Primary result**: FALCON outperforms state-of-the-art L2D methods across coverage levels, achieving higher area under accuracy-coverage curves (e.g., 74.01 vs. 71.01 on Cifar100).

## Executive Summary
Learning to defer (L2D) enables AI–human collaboration by selectively routing instances to either an AI model or a human expert. Existing L2D approaches assume static human performance, overlooking the well-documented degradation due to fatigue from accumulated workload. This paper introduces FALCON, a framework that explicitly models workload-variant human performance using psychologically grounded fatigue curves. FALCON formulates L2D as a Constrained Markov Decision Process (CMDP) where the state comprises the input features and cumulative human workload, and the objective maximises accuracy under human–AI cooperation budgets. It employs PPO-Lagrangian optimisation to learn a deferral policy that satisfies coverage constraints. The authors construct the FA-L2D benchmark across four image classification datasets with diverse fatigue dynamics. Experiments show FALCON consistently outperforms prior L2D methods over the full coverage spectrum, generalises zero-shot to unseen fatigue patterns, and demonstrates the superiority of adaptive AI–human collaboration for any coverage level.

## Method Summary
FALCON models the sequential deferral problem as a CMDP with state \(s_t = (x_t, \rho_t)\), where \(x_t\) are the input features and \(\rho_t\) is the cumulative number of human tasks so far. The action \(a_t \in \{\text{AI}, \text{Human}\}\) yields a reward equal to classification accuracy and a cost of 1 when a human is used. The workload updates deterministically: \(\rho_{t+1} = \rho_t + 1\) if \(a_t = \text{Human}\), else unchanged. Constraints enforce that the expected total cost lies within a specified interval \([d_\ell, d_u]\). Human performance degrades with workload according to a piecewise fatigue curve (quadratic warm-up, sigmoid decay), mapping \(\rho\) to a noise rate \(\eta = 1 - w(\rho)\) that flips the human’s label.

The policy network processes the image through a ResNet backbone, embeds \(\rho_t\), concatenates these vectors, and passes them through resettable S5 layers to capture temporal patterns. Three heads output the action probability \(\pi(a|s)\), a reward value \(V^r(s)\), and a cost value \(V^c(s)\). Training proceeds by collecting trajectories, computing GAE advantages for reward and cost, and updating the policy via a PPO-style clipped surrogate objective while adjusting Lagrange multipliers \(\lambda_u, \lambda_l\) to enforce the constraints (Algorithm 1). The approach thus jointly learns to defer and respect budgets without manual thresholding.

## Key Results
- FALCON achieves higher area under the accuracy-coverage curve than SOTA L2D methods on all datasets (e.g., 74.01 vs. 71.01 on Cifar100).
- It adapts to a wide range of coverage budgets and generalises zero-shot to unseen fatigue dynamics (e.g., rapid decline).
- Adaptive collaboration surpasses both AI-only and human-only baselines for any coverage level between 0 and 1.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1: Fatigue Curve Human Model
FALCON captures human performance degradation via a two-phase psychologically grounded curve: quadratic growth during warm-up and sigmoid decay as fatigue sets in. This maps cumulative workload \(\rho\) to a correctness probability \(w(\rho)\), whose complement \(1-w(\rho)\) is the noise rate for label flips. The model assumes each human task contributes a fixed unit of workload, independent of task difficulty. This explicit dynamical modeling contrasts with prior static assumptions and allows the policy to anticipate future fatigue. Evidence is provided in Section 3.2 (Eq. 2) and the workload update (Eq. 4). The approach breaks down if real fatigue exhibits rapid recovery or depends strongly on task complexity.

### Mechanism 2: CMDP for Sequential Deferral
Deferral decisions are inherently sequential because assigning a task to the human increases workload, affecting future states and optimal actions. FALCON formalises this as a Constrained Markov Decision Process (CMDP): state \(s_t = (x_t, \rho_t)\); action \(a_t\); reward \(r_t = \mathbb{I}[\text{correct}]\); cost \(c_t = \mathbb{I}[a_t=\text{Human}]\); and a constraint on the sum of costs. The Markov assumption—future depends only on current state and action—enables standard RL optimisation. This formulation separates FALCON from prior i.i.d. L2D methods and naturally accommodates budget constraints. Evidence: Section 3.3 defines the CMDP tuple, transition (Eq. 4), and constrained optimisation (Eqs. 5–6). The model would fail if non-Markovian factors (e.g., breaks causing recovery) are significant, requiring state augmentation.

### Mechanism 3: PPO-Lagrangian Constrained Optimisation
FALCON uses PPO with Lagrange multipliers to learn a policy that maximises expected reward while satisfying expected cost constraints. The Lagrangian dual introduces penalty terms \(\lambda_u, \lambda_l\) for constraint violations; these are updated asynchronously alongside the policy. Advantages are computed separately for reward and cost (Eqs. 7–8), providing stable gradient signals even under tight budgets. The clipped PPO surrogate ensures conservative policy updates. This combination is standard in safe RL and adapts well to L2D’s coverage constraints. Evidence: Section 3.3, Algorithm 1, and Table 1 hyperparameters. The method relies on proper step sizes for \(\lambda\) updates; otherwise, multipliers may diverge or yield infeasible policies.

**Why needed**: The training algorithm uses PPO with Lagrangian penalties to enforce coverage constraints while ensuring stable policy updates.

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component map**: Input image → ResNet backbone (features) → Workload embedding (ρ_t vector) → Concatenation → S5 layers (temporal memory) → Heads: policy π(a|s), reward value V^r, cost value V^c. Environment: human simulator (fatigue curve w(ρ) → noise η → label flip) and workload accumulator.

**Critical path**:
1. At each timestep, encode (x_t, ρ_t) via backbone, embedding, and S5 to obtain state representation.
2. Sample action a_t (AI or Human) from policy π.
3. If Human: increment ρ_t; simulate human prediction by flipping AI label with probability η=1-w(ρ). Else use AI prediction directly.
4. Record reward (1 if correct else 0) and cost (1 if Human else 0).
5. After an episode, compute returns and GAE advantages for reward and cost.
6. Perform PPO-Lagrangian policy update (clipped surrogate, value losses) and update Lagrange multipliers λ_u, λ_l.

**Design tradeoffs**:
- **S5 vs. LSTM/Transformer**: S5 provides linear complexity and stable gradients for long episodes but may have less expressive memory than Transformers.
- **Piecewise fatigue curve vs. complex model**: Simplicity aids interpretability and training; however, it may not capture individual variations or short-term recovery effects.
- **CMDP (PPO-Lagrangian) vs. heuristic thresholds**: Primal-dual approach automatically finds budget-satisfying policy but introduces extra hyperparameters (λ learning rates, PPO clip ε).
- **Workload as state vs. raw history**: Workload compresses history into a scalar; S5 helps recover temporal patterns, but irrelevant history might still affect S5 state.

**Failure signatures**:
- **Coverage drift**: λ multipliers fail to adjust → policy consistently over- or under-defers. Monitor λ trends and average cost during training.
- **S5 state leakage**: Improper reset between episodes carries over fatigue memory; ensure S5 state is reset at episode start.
- **Human simulation mismatch**: Zero-shot performance drops if real human fatigue differs from simulated curve; validate curve parameters with real data (e.g., mammography).
- **Value head instability**: Long episodes can cause gradient explosion; use norm clipping (paper uses 0.5).

**First experiments**:
1. **Benchmark comparison**: Train FALCON and baselines on FA-L2D; plot accuracy-coverage curves and compute AUACC; expect FALCON to dominate across datasets.
2. **CMDP ablation**: Replace PPO-Lagrangian with standard cross-entropy loss while keeping S5 architecture; compare curves to isolate contribution of constrained optimisation.
3. **Zero-shot generalisation**: Train on a broad range of fatigue parameters; test on unseen fatigue patterns (e.g., steep decline) without fine-tuning; FALCON should maintain performance while baselines degrade.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
1. **Instance-specific cognitive load**: How can FALCON extend beyond uniform fatigue contribution per task to account for instance difficulty? The current model adds a fixed workload per human task regardless of complexity. Developing a workload increment that depends on input features and evaluating on datasets with difficulty annotations could resolve this.

2. **Real human validation**: Can FALCON’s effectiveness be confirmed with real human experts instead of simulated performance curves? Simulation may not capture individual differences and unpredictable behaviours. A controlled human subject experiment tracking performance over time would provide necessary evidence.

3. **Multi-modal fatigue indicators**: How can physiological signals (eye gaze, EEG, heart rate variability) be incorporated to improve fatigue detection and deferral decisions? Relying solely on cumulative workload is noisy and delayed. Extending the state representation with real-time physiological data and evaluating improvements on the FA-L2D benchmark would address this.

## Limitations
- Relies on simulated human performance; lacks real-world human studies.
- Fatigue model assumes uniform workload contribution and Markovian dependence, ignoring task difficulty, recovery, and individual differences.
- Evaluation is limited to four image classification datasets; broader generalisability is uncertain.

## Confidence
- **Fatigue curve model**: Medium (psychologically grounded but not empirically validated in this work).
- **CMDP formulation**: High (theoretically sound and matches sequential deferral structure).
- **PPO-Lagrangian optimisation**: Medium (standard RL technique with empirical success, but no problem-specific convergence analysis).
- **Benchmark superiority**: High (consistent improvement across datasets and metrics).
- **Zero-shot generalisation**: Medium (tested on simulated fatigue pattern variations, not real humans).

## Next Checks
1. **Human subject experiment**: Recruit participants for classification tasks under controlled workloads; estimate fatigue curves from observed accuracy and compare FALCON vs. baselines in real-time collaboration.
2. **Non-Markovian fatigue test**: Introduce recovery periods and task-difficulty-dependent workload accumulation; evaluate FALCON against an oracle with full history to assess impact of Markov assumption.
3. **Cross-modality evaluation**: Apply FALCON to NLP (e.g., text classification) and tabular data tasks; measure AUACC gains over baselines to test generalisability beyond vision.
