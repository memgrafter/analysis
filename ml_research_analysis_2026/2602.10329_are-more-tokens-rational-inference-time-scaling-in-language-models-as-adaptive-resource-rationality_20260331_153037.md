# Are More Tokens Rational? Inference-Time Scaling in Language Models as Adaptive Resource Rationality

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2602.10329
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.10329
- Reference count: 5
- Primary result: Large Language Models adaptively select reasoning strategies (permutation vs. elimination) based on task complexity without explicit computational cost rewards, and Large Reasoning Models maintain robust accuracy on non-monotonic logical functions.

## Executive Summary
This paper investigates whether large language models (LLMs) engage in resource-rational reasoning—optimizing accuracy under computational constraints—without being explicitly trained to consider computational costs. Using the novel Variable Attribution Task (VAT), the authors manipulate complexity via the number of candidate variables (N) and trials (T). Both instruction-tuned models and large reasoning models (LRMs) transition from a brute-force permutation strategy to a memory-intensive elimination strategy as N increases and T decreases, indicating adaptive allocation of inference-time computation. However, this shift is attenuated for XOR/XNOR functions, where elimination yields minimal pruning benefit. Critically, LRMs sustain high accuracy across all logical functions, while instruction-tuned models degrade on XOR/XNOR as N grows. Strategy selection is best predicted by an interaction between log hypothesis space size (positive effect) and trial count (negative effect), confirming sensitivity to task informativeness. These findings suggest that inference-time scaling naturally gives rise to resource-rational behavior in LLMs.

## Method Summary
The study employs the Variable Attribution Task (VAT), where models must identify a hidden pair of binary variables that determine an outcome via a latent Boolean function, given N candidate variables and T input-output trials. A dataset was generated covering N ∈ {3,4,5,6,7,8,10,12,14,16}, T = T_min + {0,…,5} (with T_min the minimal trials for uniqueness), and ten non-trivial Boolean functions (including AND, OR, XOR, XNOR), yielding 3000 samples (5 per condition). Four pretrained models were evaluated: DeepSeek R1, DeepSeek V3 (instruction-tuned), Qwen3-Thinking, and Qwen3-Instruct. All received a standardized prompt encouraging step-by-step reasoning; LRMs produced explicit “thinking” traces. Model outputs were classified into reasoning strategies (permutation, elimination, invalid) by an LLM judge (Kimi‑K2‑Instruct‑0905), with human validation showing 86% accuracy and Cohen’s κ=0.76. For each sample, accuracy, elimination proportion, and character count (effort proxy) were recorded. Analysis involved heatmaps, logistic regression of elimination probability on log(comb(N,2)) and T (with interaction), and comparisons across model types and functions.

## Key Results
- Adaptive strategy selection emerges with complexity: models shift from permutation to elimination as hypothesis space (N) grows and trial count (T) shrinks, but this shift is attenuated for XOR/XNOR where elimination is less beneficial.
- LRMs sustain perfect accuracy across all functions and N, while IT models falter on XOR/XNOR at higher N, suggesting RL training enhances handling of non-monotonic relations.
- Strategy choice is predicted by an interaction between log(N²) (positive) and T (negative), indicating models optimize based on task informativeness (ρ).

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
**Claim:** Strategy selection (permutation vs. elimination) emerges adaptively as a function of task complexity, without explicit training on computational cost.  
**Mechanism:** Models implicitly weigh the expected utility of pruning the hypothesis space (via elimination) against the working memory cost of maintaining it. As the hypothesis space (quadratic in N) grows large, the marginal benefit of pruning outweighs its maintenance cost, triggering a shift from low-memory permutation to high-memory elimination.  
**Core assumption:** The model's inference process is sensitive to the combinatorial structure of the task (hypothesis space size) and the informativeness of available trials.  
**Evidence anchors:**  
- [abstract] "Both models exhibit a transition from brute-force to analytic strategies as complexity increases."  
- [section 4.1] Heatmaps show proportion of elimination increases with N and decreases with T.  
- [corpus] Neighbor papers focus on token complexity and adaptive scaling but do not directly address strategy shifts; evidence is weak here.  
**Break condition:** The shift is suppressed for XOR/XNOR functions where elimination provides minimal pruning benefit, indicating the model's sensitivity to logical structure, not just combinatorial size.

### Mechanism 2
**Claim:** Strategy choice is best predicted by an interaction between log hypothesis space size (positive effect) and trial count (negative effect), reflecting sensitivity to task informativeness (ρ).  
**Mechanism:** The model allocates resources based on a resource-rational trade-off: more trials reduce the need for costly global hypothesis management, while a larger search space increases its value. This aligns with maximizing accuracy per unit of inference-time computation.  
**Core assumption:** The model’s internal decision process approximates a cost-benefit analysis where "cost" is memory/state maintenance and "benefit" is reduction in uncertain evaluations.  
**Evidence anchors:**  
- [section 4.2] Logistic regression (Table 1) shows interaction model has best fit (AIC 2727.29, PseudoR²=0.160); coefficients: β_log(N²)=+1.0212, β_T=-0.1466 (both p<0.001).  
- [section 4.2] Figure 3 decision landscape shows elimination favored only when N is large and T is small.

**Break condition:** If trial count is very high (ρ>>1), permutation may remain optimal even for large N, as exhaustive checking becomes less costly relative to pruning gains.

### Mechanism 3
**Claim:** Large Reasoning Models (LRMs) maintain robust accuracy across all logical functions (including XOR/XNOR) as complexity grows, while instruction-tuned (IT) models degrade on non-monotonic functions, suggesting RL training stabilizes representation of complex logical relations.  
**Mechanism:** RL optimization for correct final answers incentivizes the model to discover and maintain stable latent representations of non-linear relationships during extended inference, preventing catastrophic forgetting or confusion that plagues IT models.  
**Core assumption:** The reinforcement learning signal in LRMs shapes the reasoning trace to be a more reliable computational workspace than the implicitly generated CoT of IT models.  
**Evidence anchors:**  
- [abstract] "LRMs maintain robust accuracy across all logical functions, whereas IT models degrade on XOR/XNOR as N grows."  
- [section 4.3] Figure 4 (DeepSeek) shows LRMs (R1) ~100% accuracy vs. IT models (V3) dropping for XOR/XNOR at N≥6.  
- [section 4.3] Figure 5 shows XOR/XNOR require higher character counts, indicating more computation to stabilize non-monotonic attribution.  
- [corpus] "Rational Metareasoning for LLMs" (neighbor) discusses metareasoning for cost-aware inference but does not isolate function-specific accuracy differences.  
**Break condition:** If RL reward is limited to accuracy without encouraging coherent reasoning traces, the robustness advantage may disappear; IT models with extremely long CoT might also converge.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
**Question:** Does inference-time scaling inherently give rise to resource-rational behavior (optimizing accuracy per unit of computation) in the absence of any explicit reward signal for computational efficiency, or are current models implicitly optimizing a proxy objective?  
**Basis in paper:** The central research question is stated: "it remains unclear whether resource-rationality can emerge from such scaling without explicit reward related to computational costs." The paper presents evidence that models adapt strategies without such a reward.  
**Why unresolved:** The study shows adaptive behavior but does not prove the models are performing a normative cost-benefit optimization. The observed strategy shifts could be an epiphenomenon of other training objectives (e.g., answer accuracy) rather than a deliberate trade-off.  
**What evidence would resolve it:** A controlled experiment where models are trained with identical objectives but with artificial, manipulated computational costs (e.g., penalizing token length differently). If strategy adaptation reverses or disappears when cost structures are altered, it would confirm resource rationality as an emergent optimization principle. Conversely, if adaptation persists regardless of imposed costs, it suggests the behavior is decoupled from explicit resource trade-offs.

### Open Question 2
**Question:** What is the precise internal mechanism by which Large Reasoning Models (LRMs) achieve robust performance on non-monotonic functions (XOR/XNOR) compared to instruction-tuned (IT) models, and is it solely due to longer reasoning chains or a qualitative difference in representation?  
**Basis in paper:** The results show LRMs maintain accuracy on XOR/XNOR while IT models degrade. The paper hypothesizes that longer chains "stabilize latent representations of non-linear relationships."  
**Why unresolved:** The paper uses character count as a proxy for "reasoning effort" but does not analyze the *content* or *structure* of the reasoning traces to identify the specific representational or algorithmic difference. It remains unclear if LRMs develop a fundamentally different internal reasoning process or simply engage in more computation within the same representational framework as IT models.  
**What evidence would resolve it:** A fine-grained analysis of the reasoning traces (e.g., via mechanistic interpretability probes) to compare the logical structures, error patterns, and intermediate state representations between correct and incorrect solves for XOR/XNOR in both model types. This could reveal if LRMs construct and maintain explicit representations of logical dependencies (e.g., "A and B must differ") that IT models fail to form.

### Open Question 3
**Question:** To what extent is the observed strategy selection (permutation vs. elimination) truly governed by a resource-rational cost-utility calculation versus being a learned heuristic triggered by surface features of the task (e.g., value of N alone)?  
**Basis in paper:** The paper fits an interaction model showing strategy selection is predicted by `log(hypothesis space size)` and `T`, interpreting this as sensitivity to "prunability" and "trial informativeness." However, the model's *internal decision process* is not observed; the fitting is correlational.  
**Why unresolved:** The logistic regression describes the *distribution* of strategies but does not demonstrate that the model itself is performing a calculation akin to `E[gain from pruning] < cost_elimination`. The strategy could be a fixed rule learned during RL/SFT (e.g., "use elimination when N > 8") that approximates rational behavior without engaging in an explicit cost-benefit analysis.  
**What evidence would resolve it:** Probe the model's *confidence* or *uncertainty* at intermediate steps during reasoning. A truly resource-rational agent would show a dynamic assessment: e.g., after a few trials, the estimated remaining hypothesis space size and expected value of the next trial would inform whether to continue the current strategy or switch. Recording such metacognitive signals during inference would test for an internal optimization process.

### Open Question 4
**Question:** Why do the two model families (DeepSeek vs. Qwen) exhibit such divergent strategy preferences for XOR/XNOR functions (moderate vs. near-zero elimination use), and what does this reveal about the role of specific post-training procedures (RL algorithms, reward models) in shaping resource-rational policies?  
**Basis in paper:** Figure 2 shows a stark difference: DeepSeek-R1 uses some elimination for XOR/XNOR, while Qwen3-Thinking almost never does, despite both being classified as LRMs.  
**Why unresolved:** The paper compares these two LRMs but does not conduct an ablation to isolate the cause. The divergence suggests that the specifics of RL fine-tuning (e.g., reward function shaping, KL penalties, exploration parameters) critically determine which strategy is learned as optimal for a given logical structure, even within the same "reasoning model" category.  
**What evidence would resolve it:** A controlled fine-tuning study where a base model is trained with different RL reward formulations (e.g., reward only for final accuracy vs. reward for step-by-step correctness vs. penalty for reasoning length) on the VAT. Observing which conditions reproduce the DeepSeek or Qwen strategy patterns would identify the training signal component responsible for the divergence.

### Open Question 5
**Question:** Is the linear scaling of reasoning effort (character count) with `N` and `T` a universal law of inference-time scaling for this task class, or does it break down at extreme complexity levels where the optimal strategy itself fails, and what is the theoretical maximum "effort" a model will allocate before resorting to guessing?  
**Basis in paper:** Figure 5 shows linear scaling. The authors note this "potentially serves as a behavioral analog for human response time." However, the tested range of `N` (up to 16) and `T` is moderate.  
**Why unresolved:** The linear fit is empirical within the tested parameter bounds. Resource rationality predicts that effort should scale *only as needed* to achieve a target accuracy. At some point of extreme hypothesis space size (`N` large) or extremely sparse information (`T` small), the cost of any correct strategy may exceed the expected benefit, and a rational agent should abandon complex reasoning and guess. The paper does not test this failure mode or identify the "breakpoint" where effort scaling becomes non-linear or plateaus.  
**What evidence would resolve it:** Push the VAT to much larger `N` (e.g., 32, 64) and smaller `T` (e.g., `T_min - 2`, `T_min - 1` where `T_min` is the absolute minimum). Monitor if character count continues linear growth, saturates, or if accuracy collapses while effort remains high, indicating a strategy that is no longer resource-rational. This would map the limits of adaptive computation.

## Limitations
- VAT ecological validity: The task abstracts reasoning to a clean combinatorial search, which may not capture the semantic and sequential complexities of real-world reasoning.
- Token count as effort proxy: Using character/token count conflates verbosity with genuine computational cost; it does not directly measure working memory load or intermediate state maintenance.
- LLM-as-judge dependency: Strategy classification relies on an LLM judge, which despite human validation may still harbor systematic biases, especially for nuanced traces on XOR/XNOR.
