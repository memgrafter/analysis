# Taming Score-Based Denoisers in ADMM: A Convergent Plug-and-Play Framework

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2603.10281  
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.10281  
- Reference count: 40  
- Primary result: Introduces AC‑DC, a convergent plug‑and‑play ADMM framework using score‑based denoisers, achieving state‑of‑the‑art reconstruction quality with theoretical convergence guarantees.

## Executive Summary
Inverse problems in imaging often rely on plug‑and‑play ADMM, where a denoiser serves as a proximal operator for a learned prior. Integrating score‑based generative models, however, faces two key challenges: ADMM iterates may lie far from the manifold where the score function is reliable, and no convergence theory existed for such setups. This paper proposes the AC‑DC denoiser—a three‑step procedure (auto‑correction, directional correction via Langevin dynamics, and score‑based denoising)—to align iterates with a noisy manifold before applying the prior. Under strong convexity of the data‑fidelity term and a constant ADMM step size, the resulting ADMM operator is weakly nonexpansive, guaranteeing convergence of the iterates to a fixed point. With an adaptive step size, convergence holds under weaker assumptions. Experiments on FFHQ and ImageNet for 4× super‑resolution, deblurring, and inpainting show substantial PSNR/SSIM/LPIPS improvements over DPS and DiffPIR, demonstrating both practical efficacy and solid theoretical grounding.

## Method Summary
The method modifies the ADMM formulation of an inverse problem by replacing the generic denoiser with the AC‑DC procedure. In each ADMM iteration, the x‑update applies AC‑DC to the current auxiliary variable minus the scaled dual. AC‑DC first adds Gaussian noise (auto‑correction) to place the input on a noisy version of the data manifold where the score is accurate. Then, a short conditional Langevin dynamics (directional correction) uses the score network to nudge the sample toward the true manifold. Finally, a clean estimate is obtained via Tweedie’s formula or an ODE solver. The z‑update remains the proximal of the data‑fidelity term (e.g., a least‑squares step for linear operators), and the dual update follows standard ADMM. Theoretical analysis proves that the AC‑DC operator is weakly nonexpansive under mild conditions, enabling convergence.

## Key Results
- **Convergence guarantee**: Under strong convexity and constant step size, ADMM iterates converge almost surely to a fixed point; with adaptive step size, convergence holds under relaxed assumptions.  
- **Quality gains**: On FFHQ 4× super‑resolution, AC‑DC achieves PSNR 30.44 vs 24.83 (DPS) and 26.77 (DiffPIR); SSIM and LPIPS also improve consistently across tasks.  
- **Generality**: Benefits observed for deblurring and inpainting on both FFHQ and ImageNet, indicating robustness across datasets and degradation types.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The AC‑DC denoiser succeeds by carefully aligning ADMM iterates with the training distribution of the score network. Auto‑correction injects noise to move the point onto a smoothed manifold where the score is reliable and Lipschitz. Directional correction then uses a few Langevin steps to follow the score gradient while staying within this regime, effectively executing a truncated reverse diffusion process. Finally, Tweedie’s denoising transforms the corrected noisy sample into an MMSE estimate of the clean image. This choreography ensures the overall operator acts as a proximal mapping that is (weakly) nonexpansive—a crucial property for ADMM convergence. By calibrating noise levels and step sizes, the method balances accuracy and stability.

## Foundational Learning
- **ADMM**  
  *Why needed*: Provides a splitting framework for inverse problems; the plug‑and‑play approach replaces the prior proximal with a denoiser.  
  *Quick check*: Derive ADMM updates for a simple Lasso problem and identify where a denoiser would be inserted.

- **Score-based generative models**  
  *Why needed*: Define an implicit prior via the score function; used in AC‑DC for directional correction and denoising.  
  *Quick check*: Given a trained score network, generate a sample by running Langevin dynamics or solving the probability‑flow ODE.

- **Weak nonexpansiveness**  
  *Why needed*: A relaxation of nonexpansiveness sufficient for ADMM convergence when the data‑fidelity term is strongly convex.  
  *Quick check*: Verify that an operator satisfies ‖F(x)−F(y)‖ ≤ ‖x−y‖ for all x,y, with strict inequality for some pairs.

- **Strong convexity**  
  *Why needed*: An assumption in the convergence proof that bounds the augmented Lagrangian and controls iterates.  
  *Quick check*: Determine if a function f(x) is strongly convex by computing ∇²f and checking its smallest eigenvalue is positive.

- **Langevin dynamics**  
  *Why needed*: Used in directional correction to sample from the score‑defined distribution; bridges to the reverse SDE.  
  *Quick check*: Implement one step: x ← x + η ∇ log p(x) + √(2η) ε, ε∼N(0,I), and observe movement in a simple 2‑D Gaussian mixture.

- **Tweedie’s lemma**  
  *Why needed*: Relates the score of a noisy observation to the conditional expectation of the clean signal; provides an efficient denoising step.  
  *Quick check*: For Gaussian noise, compute x̂ = y + σ² s(y) and confirm it matches the MMSE estimator in a linear‑Gaussian setting.

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component map**  
ADMM loop:  
x^{k+1} = AC‑DC(z^k - u^k)  
z^{k+1} = prox_f(A x^{k+1} + u^k)  
u^{k+1} = u^k + x^{k+1} - z^{k+1}  

AC‑DC:  
1. y = x + σ·noise (auto‑correction)  
2. for i = 1..N_L: y = y + η·s(y) + √(2η)·noise (directional correction)  
3. x̂ = Tweedie(y) or ODE solver (score‑based denoising)

**Critical path**  
The AC‑DC denoiser dominates runtime because it requires multiple score network evaluations (N_L+1 per ADMM iteration). The z‑update (proximal of data fidelity) is cheap for linear problems, and the dual update is negligible. Optimizing AC‑DC (e.g., reducing N_L, using a fast ODE solver) directly cuts total computation.

**Design tradeoffs**  
- **Noise level σ** (auto‑correction): Higher σ improves manifold alignment but adds distortion; lower σ risks misalignment. Needs calibration.  
- **Langevin steps N_L**: More steps enhance quality but increase cost linearly; often 1‑5 steps suffice.  
- **ADMM step size ρ**: Larger ρ accelerates convergence but may cause instability; tuning required.  
- **Tweedie vs ODE**: Tweedie is fast and deterministic but approximates an early reverse‑time step; ODE solvers can reach arbitrary noise levels but need careful integration.

**Failure signatures**  
- **Divergence**: Exploding x or z values, often due to too large ρ or inaccurate score gradients.  
- **Stalling**: Little progress after many iterations, possibly from insufficient N_L or mismatched σ.  
- **Poor reconstruction**: Low PSNR/SSIM despite convergence; indicates hyperparameter mismatch or unsuitable score network.

**First experiments**  
1. Standalone denoising test: Apply AC‑DC to Gaussian‑noisy images (known σ) and compare PSNR/SSIM against the baseline score denoiser (no auto‑correction, no Langevin). Expect AC‑DC to match or exceed when σ matches training conditions.  
2. ADMM on a simple inverse problem: Implement the full algorithm for 4× bicubic super‑resolution on a few Set5 images. Verify convergence (residual decreases) and compare PSNR against DPS/DiffPIR. Expect AC‑DC to outperform.  
3. Ablation study: Disable one AC‑DC component at a time (σ=0, N_L=0, replace Tweedie with a simple clip). Measure impact on PSNR, SSIM, and ADMM iterations to reach a fixed residual threshold. This isolates each step’s contribution.

## Limitations
- Theoretical assumptions (strong convexity, step‑size constraints, exact score approximations) may not hold for many real‑world non‑linear inverse problems, limiting the practical applicability of the convergence guarantee.  
- The three‑step AC‑DC denoiser incurs significant computational overhead compared to a simple CNN denoiser, which may hinder deployment despite quality gains; the trade‑off is not fully quantified.  
- Experimental evaluation is restricted to super‑resolution, deblurring, and inpainting on FFHQ/ImageNet; performance on more complex degradations or 3D data remains unknown.

## Confidence
- **Convergence guarantee**: Medium  
- **AC‑DC alignment mechanism**: Medium  
- **Empirical performance gains (PSNR/SSIM/LPIPS)**: High
