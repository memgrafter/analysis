# SNPgen: Phenotype-Supervised Genotype Representation and Synthetic Data Generation via Latent Diffusion

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2603.10873
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.10873
- **Reference count:** 40
- **Primary result:** SNPgen generates synthetic genotypes from a compact set of trait-associated SNPs that match real-data predictive performance for disease models while providing strong empirical privacy protection.

## Executive Summary
SNPgen introduces a method for generating phenotype-supervised synthetic genotype data by combining GWAS-guided variant selection with a two-stage conditional latent diffusion framework. It selects a targeted panel of 1,024–2,048 trait-associated single nucleotide polymorphisms (SNPs) using external GWAS summary statistics, then trains a variational autoencoder (VAE) to compress the discrete genotype sequences into a continuous latent space that preserves population genetic structure. A conditional latent diffusion model, guided by disease labels via cross-attention and classifier-free guidance, generates novel latent representations. Decoding these latents produces synthetic genotypes. Evaluations on four UK Biobank diseases show that models trained on synthetic data achieve predictive performance (ROC-AUC) comparable to models trained on real data, even when using far fewer variants than genome-wide polygenic scores. Privacy analyses indicate minimal risk of individual memorization, and simulation studies confirm faithful recovery of known genetic associations.

## Method Summary
The method begins with GWAS-guided SNP selection: external summary statistics are used for LD clumping and thresholding to identify a compact, trait-relevant panel. This panel is one-hot encoded (three channels for homozygous reference, heterozygous, homozygous alternate) and fed into a 1D convolutional VAE, which learns a smooth lower-dimensional latent space while reconstructing genotypes with high fidelity. The trained VAE encoder is frozen, and a conditional latent diffusion model (1D UNet with transformer attention) is trained to denoise latents conditioned on binary disease labels. At inference, latents are sampled from the conditional distribution p(z|y) and passed through the VAE decoder to produce discrete synthetic genotypes. Utility is assessed via train-on-synthetic-test-on-real evaluations, where downstream prediction models (e.g., XGBoost, PRS) are trained on synthetic data and tested on held-out real individuals. Privacy is evaluated through metrics like identical matches, membership inference attacks, and allele frequency preservation.

## Key Results
- Models trained on synthetic genotypes matched real-data predictive performance (ROC-AUC) across four UK Biobank diseases, approaching genome-wide PRS results using only 2–6× fewer variants.
- In a simulation with known causal SNP effects, synthetic data generated by SNPgen recovered genetic associations with a beta correlation of r=0.835 versus real data.
- Privacy analysis showed zero identical genotype matches, near-random membership inference (AUC ≈ 0.50), and high preservation of allele frequencies (r ≥ 0.95) between real and synthetic data.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- **Claim:** GWAS-guided variant selection concentrates modeling capacity on trait-predictive SNPs, enabling scalable generation while retaining downstream utility.
- **Mechanism:** External GWAS summary statistics are used for LD clumping and thresholding to select a compact panel (L=1,024–2,048 SNPs). This reduces the dimensionality of the genotype matrix and ensures the generative model focuses on variants with established trait associations.
- **Core assumption:** Trait-associated SNPs identified in independent GWAS capture sufficient additive genetic signal for the target disease, even when used in isolation from the rest of the genome.
- **Evidence anchors:** Abstract and Section 3.1 describe the GWAS-guided selection; Section 5.2 & Table 1 show synthetic-data-trained models approach genome-wide PRS performance despite far fewer variants.
- **Break condition:** If external GWAS summary statistics are poorly matched to the target population (e.g., ancestry mismatch, phenotype definition differences), selected SNPs may not carry predictive signal, degrading utility.

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim:** The VAE’s 1D convolutional encoder/decoder learns a continuous latent space that preserves local correlation (LD) structure and genotype statistics while enabling efficient diffusion in lower dimensions.
- **Mechanism:** One-hot encoded genotype sequences (3 × L) are compressed into a 1D latent vector (z ∈ R^{1×L/16}) through a ResNet-based encoder. The decoder reconstructs discrete genotypes from latents. The VAE is trained with reconstruction, KL, and adversarial losses to balance fidelity and latent regularity.
- **Core assumption:** The latent space retains sufficient population-genetic structure (LD, MAF) and phenotype-relevant patterns to allow a subsequent diffusion model to generate valid, novel genotypes.
- **Evidence anchors:** Section 3.2 describes the VAE; Section 5.4 & Figure S6 show preserved LD decay and MAF (r ≥ 0.95); Section 5.2 indicates VAE reconstructions retain some downstream utility.
- **Break condition:** If VAE capacity (latent dimension, architecture) is insufficient for the selected SNP panel size (L), reconstruction accuracy drops, potentially corrupting the latent distribution.

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim:** Conditional latent diffusion, guided by phenotype labels via cross-attention and classifier-free guidance, generates novel genotype latents aligned to the target case/control distribution, preserving genotype-phenotype associations.
- **Mechanism:** A 1D UNet denoiser operates in the frozen VAE latent space. Binary disease labels are embedded and injected through cross-attention in transformer blocks. Classifier-free guidance (dropout rate 0.2 during training) enables conditional sampling at inference by extrapolating between conditional and unconditional noise predictions.
- **Core assumption:** Conditioning on the phenotype label allows the diffusion process to learn and sample from a conditional data distribution p(z|y), such that decoded genotypes reflect the marginal SNP associations with y observed in the training data.
- **Evidence anchors:** Section 3.2 describes conditional LDM; Section 5.1 & Figure S7 show high beta correlation (r=0.835) in simulation; Section 5.2 shows TSTR AUCs matching real-data models.
- **Break condition:** If conditioning signal is too weak relative to diffusion noise or the minority class is underrepresented, generated latents may not reflect the target phenotype distribution, degrading performance for that class.

## Foundational Learning

- **Concept: Variational Autoencoder (VAE)**
  - **Why needed:** Provides dimensionality reduction (1D convolution) and a smooth latent space for diffusion, while preserving discrete genotype structure through reconstruction loss.
  - **Quick check:** How does the β-VAE loss term (β·D_KL) affect the trade-off between latent space regularity and reconstruction fidelity for genotype data?

- **Concept: Latent Diffusion Model (LDM)**
  - **Why needed:** Enables iterative, high-quality generation in a compressed latent space, avoiding the curse of dimensionality of direct genotype space diffusion.
  - **Quick check:** Why is classifier-free guidance used during LDM training, and how does the guidance scale affect phenotype conditioning strength at inference?

- **Concept: GWAS Clumping and Thresholding**
  - **Why needed:** Defines a trait-relevant, LD-pruned SNP panel of manageable size for the generative model, focusing capacity on variants with established marginal effects.
  - **Quick check:** What is the effect of the r² clumping threshold on the number of selected SNPs and the expected LD block structure within the panel?

## Architecture Onboarding

- **Component map:**
  GWAS-guided selector -> VAE Stage (1D ResNet encoder/decoder) -> LDM Stage (1D UNet with cross-attention) -> Decoder (frozen VAE) -> Downstream evaluator

- **Critical path:** `UKB genotypes + GWAS stats → SNP selection → VAE train (z distribution) → LDM train (p(z|y)) → sample z~p(z|y_target) → decode → synthetic genotypes → downstream model train → test on real holdout`.

- **Design tradeoffs:**
  - **SNP count (L):** Smaller L reduces compute but may discard polygenic tail signal; larger L increases memory and may require deeper models.
  - **VAE latent dim vs. L:** Must scale with L to avoid information bottleneck; under-provisioning degrades reconstruction (e.g., BC's 73.1% agreement).
  - **LDM steps/guidance:** More steps improve quality but increase latency; guidance scale trades off phenotype alignment vs. fidelity.
  - **Conditioning strength:** Classifier-free guidance dropout (0.2) and scale (5.0) are tuned; over-guidance may distort latent distribution.

- **Failure signatures:**
  - **Poor LD preservation:** Missing r² fractions (e.g., BC reconstructed 17.2%) indicate VAE over-smoothing or under-capacity.
  - **MAF drift:** r < 0.95 between real and synthetic MAFs suggests allele frequency bias.
  - **High MI AUC (≈1.0):** Indicates memorization (reconstructed data), not genuine generation.
  - **PRS univariate collapse:** Large drop from real to synthetic AUC (e.g., BC: 0.638 → 0.506) signals loss of marginal additive effects.
  - **Class-specific NNAA ~0 for cases:** In reconstructed data, minority class individuals are perfectly memorized, violating privacy.

- **First 3 experiments:**
  1. **VAE reconstruction sanity check:** Train VAE alone; compute per-SNP argmax agreement on validation set. Target >95% for L=2048, >90% for L=1024. Check LD matrix (r² heatmap) and MAF correlation (r).
  2. **Conditioning efficacy probe:** After LDM training, generate matched-size synthetic cohorts for y=0 and y=1. Compute PRS-univariate betas separately; verify betas for cases vs. controls diverge meaningfully and align with GWAS direction.
  3. **Minimal TSTR smoke test:** Train a simple logistic regression (or XGBoost with minimal depth) on 5% synthetic data vs. 5% real data. Evaluate on same real holdout. If synthetic AUC is <0.52 for any trait, abort.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- Can SNPgen be extended to generate synthetic genotypes conditioned on continuous phenotypes (e.g., quantitative traits) and additional covariates (e.g., age, sex, genetic principal components)?
- How does SNPgen perform in multi-ancestry settings, and does the current single-ancestry training limit its generalizability and privacy guarantees across diverse populations?
- Can formal privacy mechanisms (e.g., differential privacy) be integrated into the SNPgen latent diffusion process, and what is the resulting privacy-utility trade-off, particularly for minority-class samples?
- Does SNPgen scale to genome-wide variant panels (hundreds of thousands to millions of SNPs), and what is the computational cost versus utility trade-off when using larger panels?

## Limitations
- GWAS-guided variant selection depends on external summary statistics; mismatched ancestry or phenotype definitions may omit causal variants, reducing utility.
- VAE capacity bottlenecks for larger SNP panels can cause reconstruction degradation (e.g., 73.1% genotype agreement for breast cancer).
- Conditioning via classifier-free guidance may underrepresent minority classes, as hinted by class-specific privacy anomalies.
- Privacy evaluation relies on empirical metrics; advanced inference attacks remain unevaluated.

## Confidence
- *GWAS-guided variant selection retains predictive signal:* Medium – empirical support limited to four European-ancestry diseases; generalizability uncertain.
- *Latent space preserves linkage disequilibrium and allele frequencies:* High – consistent r≥0.95 across traits.
- *Conditional diffusion faithfully recovers genetic associations:* High – simulation beta correlation 0.835 and TSTR performance parity.
- *Synthetic data privacy protection:* Medium – basic metrics indicate low memorization, but deeper attacks (e.g., attribute inference) unevaluated.

## Next Checks
- **Cross-ancestry reproducibility:** Apply SNPgen to non-European UKB subsets (e.g., South Asian, African) using ancestry-specific GWAS; compare TSTR AUC to within-ancestry baseline.
- **Advanced membership attack:** Implement shadow model attacks to test whether individuals' real genotypes can be distinguished from synthetic ones; success rate > random would undermine privacy claims.
- **Genome-wide scalability ablation:** Incrementally increase SNP panel size (5k, 10k, 50k) and monitor VAE reconstruction fidelity (per-SNP accuracy, LD decay) and LDM training stability to identify practical limits.
