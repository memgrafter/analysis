# PhySe-RPO: Physics and Semantics Guided Relative Policy Optimization for Diffusion-Based Surgical Smoke Removal

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID**: 2603.22844
- **Source URL**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.22844
- **Reference count**: 40
- **Primary result**: Achieves state-of-the-art no-reference metrics (SSEQ 3.443, MANIQA 0.378, PI 3.125) on real surgical smoke data and competitive full-reference results (PSNR 21.03 dB, CIEDE-2000 7.65) using a hybrid physics‑semantics reward with group‑relative RL.

## Executive Summary
Surgical smoke critically obscures anatomical structures during robotic surgery. Existing learning‑based desmoking methods rely on scarce paired data and produce deterministic outputs. PhySe‑RPO reformulates diffusion‑based restoration as a stochastic policy optimized via group‑relative reinforcement learning without a critic. A physics‑guided reward enforces color and illumination consistency, a visual‑concept semantic reward derived from CLIP aligns with a learned “clear” surgical appearance, and a reference‑free perceptual constraint further refines results. On a real unlabeled surgical dataset, PhySe‑RPO sets new SOTA no‑reference metrics; on a public paired dataset it attains PSNR 21.03 dB and CIEDE‑2000 7.65, demonstrating robust, physically consistent, and semantically coherent desmoking under limited supervision.

## Method Summary
PhySe‑RPO proceeds in three stages. First, a conditional diffusion model is pretrained on a synthetic dataset of smoky/clean image pairs generated via Blender. Second, two learnable CLIP tokens (“clear” and “smoky”) are trained contrastively to encode domain‑specific semantic concepts from the synthetic data. Third, the diffusion model is fine‑tuned on real unlabeled videos using group‑relative policy optimization (GRPO). For each input, a group of *G*=4 perturbed denoising trajectories is sampled. The total reward combines a physics‑guided color prior (*R*<sub>PG</sub>), the learned visual‑concept similarity (*R*<sub>VC</sub>), and reference‑free perceptual scores (*R*<sub>RF</sub>). After group‑wise normalization, a clipped surrogate objective with a KL penalty (λ) to a frozen reference model updates the diffusion policy. Training uses AdamW (β₁=0.9, β₂=0.99), batch size 16, timesteps *T*=100 on 4 H100 GPUs.

## Key Results
- Real unlabeled data (no‑reference): SSEQ 3.443, MANIQA 0.378, PI 3.125 (SOTA).
- Public paired dataset (full‑reference): PSNR 21.03 dB, CIEDE‑2000 7.65.
- Downstream segmentation tasks (MedSAM, AMNCutter) show improved IoU/Dice, confirming clinical relevance.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1: Stochastic Policy via Group‑Relative Optimization
- **Claim:** Transforming deterministic diffusion into a stochastic policy via group‑relative optimization enables effective reward‑driven refinement without paired supervision.
- **Mechanism:** Multiple perturbed diffusion trajectories are sampled from a single input, forming a trajectory group. Normalized relative advantages (Eq. 3) are computed from total reward *R* and update the diffusion policy using a clipped surrogate objective (Eq. 4) without a critic. A KL penalty (Eq. 6) stabilizes updates relative to a frozen reference model.
- **Core assumption:** Diffusion denoising can be interpreted as a stochastic policy; reward signals computed on full trajectories can meaningfully guide denoising behavior; group‑wise normalization stabilizes policy gradients in the absence of a value critic.
- **Evidence anchors:** Abstract states “reformulates diffusion‑based restoration as a stochastic policy optimization problem… enabling trajectory‑level exploration and critic‑free updates via group‑relative optimization.” Section 3.1 details Eq. 1–6 and explicitly calls it “turning restoration into a stochastic policy optimization problem.” Related work (e.g., GRPO for LLMs [28], RL for diffusion [24]) provides precedent, but direct application to image restoration is novel.
- **Break condition:** If reward signals become uninformative or too noisy, group‑relative advantages may fail to provide a learning signal, causing policy collapse or divergence.

### Mechanism 2: Physics‑Guided Color Consistency
- **Claim:** A physics‑guided reward based on color priors enforces illumination and chromatic consistency, producing physically plausible restorations aligned with surgical imaging characteristics.
- **Mechanism:** Reward *R*<sub>PG</sub> (Eq. 12) integrates an inter‑channel prior (Eq. 7–9) penalizing deviations from natural red‑green/red‑blue differences observed in real surgical data, and an intra‑channel prior (Eq. 10–11) that differentially targets recovery in green/blue channels versus stability in red.
- **Core assumption:** Surgical smoke induces predictable spectral distortions; inter/intra‑channel statistics (mean, std, gradient) derived from clean surgical imagery provide a sufficient low‑level prior for restoration.
- **Evidence anchors:** Abstract mentions “physics‑guided reward imposing illumination and color consistency.” Section 3.2 defines *R*<sub>PG</sub> and describes priors as “driven by the spectral asymmetry inherent in real surgical imaging,” using statistics from real data (P95). Neighbors focus on diffusion/RL/surgical vision but none specifically validate color priors for surgical smoke; evidence is internal to the paper’s dataset analysis.
- **Break condition:** If real surgical scenes exhibit color characteristics outside the prior’s estimated distributions (e.g., unusual tissue dyes, lighting), the reward may over‑penalize valid variations or under‑constrain distortions.

### Mechanism 3: Visual‑Concept Semantic Alignment
- **Claim:** A visual‑concept semantic reward derived from CLIP‑trained tokens promotes alignment with a learned “clear” surgical appearance, ensuring semantic coherence and anatomical fidelity without text prompts.
- **Mechanism:** Learnable positive (“clear”) and negative (“smoky”) tokens are trained via contrastive loss (Eq. 13–14) to align with CLIP embeddings of synthetic clean/smoky image pairs. During policy optimization, the frozen CLIP encoder + learned tokens compute a concept‑level reward (Eq. 15) encouraging generated images toward the “clear” token embedding.
- **Core assumption:** CLIP’s multimodal space can be efficiently adapted to surgical domains via a small set of learnable tokens; the resulting visual‑concept embeddings provide a stable, domain‑specific reward signal superior to raw text prompts.
- **Evidence anchors:** Abstract says “visual‑concept semantic reward derived from CLIP to align with ‘clear’ surgical appearance.” Section 3.3 details token learning (Fig. 3a), fusion into diffusion (Fig. 3b), and reward formulation (Eq. 15). Ablation (Table 3, Fig. 5) shows visual‑concept reward outperforms a “Text‑Reward” baseline. Neighbors (e.g., CoOp [46]) use learnable prompts for vision‑language adaptation, but application to surgical desmoking reward is novel.
- **Break condition:** If the synthetic data used for concept learning does not cover the diversity of real surgical smoke and anatomy, the learned concepts may not generalize, leading to reward misguidance (e.g., encouraging artifacts that resemble synthetic “clear” patterns).

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component map**:  
Pretrained Diffusion Backbone (conditional U‑Net) → Stochastic Sampler (adds Gaussian perturbations to noise) → Group of Trajectories → Reward Modules (*R*<sub>PG</sub>, *R*<sub>RF</sub>, *R*<sub>VC</sub>) → Fusion Module (injects visual‑concept tokens) → Policy Optimizer (computes group‑normalized advantages, applies PPO clipping & KL penalty) → Updated Diffusion Policy.

**Critical path**:
1. Pretrain conditional diffusion model on synthetic paired data using standard diffusion loss.
2. Freeze CLIP, train two visual‑concept tokens contrastively on synthetic pairs.
3. For each real unlabeled image, sample *G*=4 perturbed trajectories.
4. Compute total reward *R* = *R*<sub>PG</sub> + *R*<sub>RF</sub> + *R*<sub>VC</sub> for each trajectory.
5. Normalize advantages within the group and perform GRPO update (Eq. 4) with KL regularization (Eq. 6).

**Design tradeoffs**:
- *Exploration vs. Stability*: Perturbation variance must balance sufficient exploration without destroying semantics; KL penalty stabilizes but may limit adaptation.
- *Physics vs. Semantics*: *R*<sub>PG</sub> ensures low‑level color fidelity but may conflict with high‑level perceptual goals from *R*<sub>VC</sub>; reward weighting is critical.
- *Computational Cost*: Sampling multiple trajectories multiplies forward passes; CLIP and IQA inference add overhead.

**Failure signatures**:
- *Reward collapse*: All group rewards become similar → relative advantages vanish → policy stops updating.
- *Color shift/oversaturation*: *R*<sub>PG</sub> weight too high or priors mismatched → unnatural hues.
- *Semantic drift*: *R*<sub>VC</sub> fails to guide → hallucinations or loss of anatomical structure (e.g., segmentation performance degrades).
- *KL divergence explosion*: λ too low → policy departs far from pretrained prior; too high → no refinement.

**First 3 experiments**:
1. **Reward component ablation** – Remove each reward term in turn to assess incremental contribution (Table 2).
2. **Visual‑concept vs. text‑prompt reward** – Compare learned tokens against direct text prompts like “a clear surgical image” (Table 3, Fig. 5 for convergence/variance).
3. **Downstream utility** – Measure improvement in segmentation tasks (MedSAM, AMNCutter) to demonstrate clinically relevant restoration (Table 5).

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
1. **Generalization of visual concepts**  
   *Question*: Do visual concepts learned from synthetic surgical data generalize to real‑world surgical videos with diverse smoke densities and tissue types?  
   *Basis*: CLIP‑based visual‑concept tokens are trained on synthetic paired data but applied to real unlabeled videos.  
   *Why unresolved*: Synthetic data may not capture the full variability of real smoke patterns and tissue appearances, potentially leading to reward misalignment.  
   *Resolution*: Systematic evaluation across multiple real datasets from different institutions or surgical procedures, measuring correlation with clinical assessments of smoke removal quality.

2. **Generalization of physics‑guided priors**  
   *Question*: Can color priors based on inter‑ and intra‑channel statistics generalize across heterogeneous surgical scenes with varying illumination and tissue reflectance?  
   *Basis*: The reward relies on fixed percentile thresholds (P95) estimated from a limited real surgical dataset.  
   *Why unresolved*: Surgical environments exhibit high variability in lighting and tissue types, which may violate the assumed color relationships, causing over‑ or under‑correction.  
   *Resolution*: Test the reward’s robustness on datasets from different specialties (e.g., laparoscopy vs. endoscopy) or under extreme lighting conditions, with failure analysis to identify when priors break down.

3. **Impact on diverse downstream tasks**  
   *Question*: Does improved smoke removal consistently enhance diverse downstream surgical perception tasks beyond segmentation?  
   *Basis*: Downstream validation is limited to segmentation with MedSAM and AMNCutter.  
   *Why unresolved*: Surgical perception involves multiple tasks (tool tracking, navigation, anatomy recognition) that may have different sensitivities to smoke and restoration artifacts.  
   *Resolution*: Apply PhySe‑RPO as preprocessing for various downstream models (e.g., surgical action recognition, instrument tracking) and measure performance gains across a standardized suite of tasks on multiple datasets.

## Limitations
- **Hyperparameter dependence**: Stability hinges on unreported settings (group size *G*, KL weight λ, clipping ε); improper values may cause collapse or divergence, requiring extensive tuning.
- **Limited prior generalizability**: Color and semantic rewards are derived from restricted data (synthetic pairs, small real sample), risking poor performance on out‑of‑distribution surgical contexts (e.g., unusual dyes, lighting).
- **Evaluation scope**: Cross‑dataset robustness and impact on a broader range of downstream tasks (beyond segmentation) remain unexplored.
