# On the Identifiability of Regime-Switching Models with Multi-Lag Dependencies

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2601.03325  
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.03325  
- **Reference count:** 40  
- **Primary result:** Proves identifiability of multi-lag regime-switching models under analytic and noise heterogeneity assumptions, and introduces iSDS, which recovers latents and regime-dependent causal graphs with high accuracy on synthetic and real data.

## Executive Summary
This paper tackles a fundamental gap in the identifiability of deep regime-switching time series models with multi-lag dependencies, which is crucial for valid regime interpretation and causal discovery. Theoretically, it frames multi-lag Markov Switching Models (MSMs) as temporal finite mixtures, proving identifiability of regimes and transitions under nonlinear-Gaussian, analytic-function assumptions. For Switching Dynamical Systems (SDSs), it establishes latent identifiability up to permutation and scaling, enabling recovery of regime-dependent causal graphs. A variational estimator, iSDS, is proposed and validated. On synthetic data, iSDS reliably recovers latents with strong MCC (~0.8–1.0), regime F1 (>0.98), and causal F1 (>0.98), outperforming baselines. Applied to ECoG, finance, and climate data, the learned regimes align with domain knowledge (e.g., awake vs anesthetized brain states, market volatility phases, ENSO seasonality), demonstrating that identifiability guarantees can lead to trustworthy scientific discovery.

## Method Summary
The authors develop a theoretical framework where multi-lag MSMs are expressed as temporal finite mixtures. Identifiability follows from linear independence of the component densities, extending classic finite mixture theory to overlapping temporal variables via a new "LIPO-M" lemma. For SDSs, they combine this identifiable MSM prior with piecewise linear, weakly injective emission functions and heterogeneous noise across regimes. This yields identifiability of latent trajectories up to affine transformations, and under sparsity assumptions, allows recovery of regime-specific causal graphs.

For inference, a variational autoencoder-like approach (iSDS) is introduced. Training begins with an identifiable MSM (iMSM) fit on PCA-reduced data to obtain stable prior parameters. The iSDS model then adds a piecewise linear encoder and decoder. The ELBO is optimized in stages: pre-train encoder-decoder with frozen prior, warm-up with frozen discrete transitions, then full fine-tuning possibly with Jacobian sparsity regularization for causal recovery. Gradients for the MSM prior are computed analytically via forward-backward recursion.

## Key Results
- Theoretically proves identifiability of regimes and transitions in multi-lag MSMs, latent variables in SDSs (up to perm/scale), and regime-dependent causal graphs (up to regime/node permutations).
- iSDS achieves near-perfect recovery on synthetic benchmarks: MCC ~0.8–1.0, regime F1 >0.98, causal F1 >0.98, surpassing baselines like iVAE* and SNICA.
- Real-data applications yield interpretable regimes: ECoG data shows distinct brain states under anesthesia, financial time series reveal volatility phases aligned with economic sectors, and climate data captures ENSO seasonality, supporting trustworthy discovery.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1: Identifiable Multi-Lag MSMs
The multi-lag MSM is framed as a temporal finite mixture: the joint distribution over a trajectory is a mixture of components, each defined by an initial distribution and regime-specific conditional densities. By extending Yakowitz & Spragins (1968) to the temporal setting, the authors prove that if the family of conditional densities is linearly independent under finite mixtures, then the mixture components (regimes, transitions) are unique up to permutation. The key technical challenge is handling overlapping variables between consecutive time steps, solved by a new "LIPO-M" lemma that generalizes linear independence to products of functions with multiple overlapping variables. Core assumptions: nonlinear Gaussian transition densities with analytic mean/covariance functions and unique indexing (distinct parameterizations yield distinct densities a.e.). If transition densities coincide on a set of positive measure for different regimes, the trajectory family becomes linearly dependent, breaking identifiability.

### Mechanism 2: Identifiable Latents in SDSs
For Switching Dynamical Systems (SDSs), the prior from the identifiable MSM family is combined with an emission model x_t = f(z_t) + ε_t. If f is weakly injective (piecewise linear and invertible on a ball in its range) and the conditional noise variances in the latent dynamics differ across regimes for at least one dimension (heterogeneous noise), then the latent trajectory z_{1:T} is identifiable up to permutation and scaling from observations x_{1:T}. The proof uses analytical continuation and shows that both the true forward transformation and any candidate alternative are invertible on an open set, forcing them to agree. Core assumptions: f piecewise linear; noise heterogeneity; independence of latent dimensions given past and regime. Violations like homogeneous noise or f lacking weak injectivity break identifiability.

### Mechanism 3: Identifiable Regime-Dependent Causal Graphs
Regime-dependent causal graphs become identifiable under the same analytic MSM prior, piecewise linear f, and additional sparsity/minimality conditions. The regime-specific transition functions m(·,k) encode causal parents via their Jacobians. Analyticity allows recovery of the functional form up to affine transformations; the non-zero pattern in the Jacobian reveals the graph G_k, unique up to permutations of latent variables and regimes. Assumptions include additive noise, no instantaneous effects, minimality (non-parent dimensions have constant entries in m), and functional independence. If minimality fails (e.g., a non-parent still influences the mean), the Jacobian pattern cannot isolate direct causal links, breaking identifiability.

## Foundational Learning
- **Finite Mixture Identifiability**:  
  Why needed: Extends classic results (Yakowitz & Spragins, 1968) to temporal mixtures, forming the basis for MSM identifiability.  
  Quick check: For a mixture of two Gaussians, distinct means/covariances ensure linear independence and unique parameter recovery.

- **Nonlinear ICA with Auxiliary/Temporal Structure**:  
  Why needed: SDS identifiability proofs adapt nonlinear ICA ideas where temporal dependencies serve as auxiliary information to resolve otherwise unidentifiable mixing.  
  Quick check: Without auxiliary structure, nonlinear ICA is unidentifiable because mixing transformations can be compensated by source transformations (e.g., rotations).

- **State-Space Models (SSMs)**:  
  Why needed: MSMs and SDSs are specific SSMs; understanding latent states, Markov switching, and filtering/smoothing is essential for formulation and inference.  
  Quick check: The key difference is that MSMs model observations as directly regime-dependent, while SDSs add continuous latent states between regimes and observations.

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component Map**:  
  iMSM prior (Q, regime-specific Gaussians) → iSDS encoder q_φ (piecewise linear) and decoder f_θ (two-layer MLP, Leaky ReLU) → ELBO maximization with forward-backward for prior gradients.

- **Critical Path**:  
  1. Initialization: Train iMSM on PCA-reduced observations to obtain stable prior parameters (π, Q, regime-specific Gaussians).  
  2. Encoder-decoder pre-training: Freeze the prior; train q_φ and decoder to reconstruct x_t from z_t.  
  3. Warm-up: Jointly train all modules but keep discrete prior (π, Q) frozen to prevent state collapse.  
  4. Full fine-tuning: Unfreeze all components; optionally add L1 regularization on Jacobians of transition functions for causal sparsity.

- **Design Tradeoffs**:  
  - Analytic activations (cosine/softplus) enforce identifiability but limit expressivity compared to Swish; balancing theory vs. fit is key.  
  - Piecewise linear emission f_θ satisfies the identifiability proof but may be less flexible than general injective functions; trade-off between interpretability guarantees and representation power.  
  - The variational ELBO optimizer is practical but lacks statistical consistency guarantees; more expressive amortized posteriors could improve fit at the cost of theoretical tractability.

- **Failure Signatures**:  
  - State collapse: fewer regimes active than expected; mitigate with strong iMSM initialization and careful annealing.  
  - Poor regime alignment: low regime F1 indicates violated assumptions (e.g., homogeneous noise, overlapping transitions).  
  - Causal recovery failure: weak MCC on latents or low causal F1 suggests insufficient noise heterogeneity or non-piecewise-linear f.

- **First 3 Experiments**:  
  1. Synthetic validation with ablation: test iSDS recovery under correct assumptions and measure degradation when assumptions are broken (e.g., overlapping transition densities).  
  2. ECoG data application: train iMSM on neural recordings under anesthesia; interpret regime occupancy, transition rates, and dwell times in relation to conscious vs. anesthetized states.  
  3. Financial time series application: use both log-returns and log-prices; evaluate whether latent representations align with economic sectors and whether identified regimes match known market cycles.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
1. How can the identifiability framework be extended to handle contemporaneous latent effects in regime-switching models? Current theory assumes additive noise models with lagged effects only.
2. What are the identifiability conditions and practical methods for continuous-time regime-switching models? The paper focuses on discrete-time formulations.
3. Can identifiability be guaranteed for regime-switching models with an infinite (nonparametric) number of regimes? All results assume a finite K.
4. How to relax the piecewise linearity assumption on emission functions in Switching Dynamical Systems while maintaining identifiability? Theorem 12 restricts f to piecewise linear and weakly injective.
5. Does the proposed variational estimator achieve statistical consistency for recovering the true parameters under the identified assumptions? Identifiability ensures uniqueness, but variational optimization may converge to local optima.

## Limitations
- Real-data preprocessing details (e.g., ECoG channel selection, climate patch extraction) are unspecified, hindering exact reproduction.
- iSDS training on real data depends on stable iMSM initialization; without pre-trained checkpoints, the pipeline often collapses.
- Causal graph evaluation uses an arbitrary Jacobian sparsity threshold; sensitivity to this threshold is not reported, undermining the claimed >0.98 F1 scores.
- The practical trade-off between strict theoretical assumptions (e.g., piecewise linear f) and model flexibility on complex real data remains underexplored.
