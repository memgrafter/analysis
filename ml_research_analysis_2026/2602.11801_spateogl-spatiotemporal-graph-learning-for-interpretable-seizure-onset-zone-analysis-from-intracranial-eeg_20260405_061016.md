# SpaTeoGL: Spatiotemporal Graph Learning for Interpretable Seizure Onset Zone Analysis from Intracranial EEG

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2602.11801
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.11801
- **Reference count:** 19
- **Primary result:** SpaTeoGL matches a baseline in SOZ detection but significantly improves non-SOZ identification (mean 0.78 vs. 0.66, p=0.0048) and overall accuracy (70% vs. 62%) on a multicenter iEEG dataset.

## Executive Summary
SpaTeoGL is a framework for localizing seizure onset zones (SOZ) from intracranial EEG by jointly learning spatial functional connectivity graphs within short time windows and a temporal graph linking these windows based on the similarity of their spatial structures. Optimized via smooth graph signal processing with convergence guarantees, the method produces interpretable spatiotemporal graphs that reveal seizure onset regimes and propagation patterns, aligning with clinical reports. On a multicenter dataset, it demonstrates competitive SOZ detection and significantly better non-SOZ identification compared to a baseline.

## Method Summary
SpaTeoGL processes iEEG recordings by segmenting them into overlapping time windows. For each window, it learns a spatial graph Laplacian that promotes signal smoothness, meaning connected electrodes exhibit similar activity. Simultaneously, it learns a temporal graph where nodes represent windows and edges reflect similarity between their spatial Laplacians, encouraging smooth evolution of spatial connectivity patterns over time. Both problems are solved via alternating block coordinate descent (BCD) with strict convexity ensured by a Frobenius norm regularizer, yielding convergence to a stationary point. Electrode-level SOZ predictions are derived from aggregated spatial graph metrics (e.g., node degrees). The approach is evaluated on a multicenter epilepsy dataset using electrode-wise classification metrics.

## Key Results
- Matches the HVG+LR baseline in SOZ recall.
- Significantly improves non-SOZ identification (mean accuracy 0.78 vs. 0.66, p=0.0048).
- Achieves higher overall accuracy (70% vs. 62%).
- Produces interpretable spatiotemporal graphs that align with clinical seizure onset regimes and propagation patterns.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1
- **Claim:** SpaTeoGL jointly learns spatial (per-window) and temporal (across-window) graphs to model evolving functional connectivity during seizures.
- **Mechanism:** For each window *m*, a spatial graph *G<sup>(s)</sup><sub>m</sub>* is learned such that iEEG signals *X<sub>m</sub>* are smooth on it (minimizing tr(*X<sub>m</sub><sup>⊤</sup>L<sup>(s)</sup><sub>m</sub>X<sub>m</sub>*)). A temporal graph *G<sup>(t)</sup>* is learned where nodes are windows and edges reflect similarity between vectorized spatial Laplacians vec(*L<sup>(s)</sup><sub>m</sub>*), promoting smoothness of these spatial structures over time.
- **Core assumption:** Seizure dynamics can be represented by (a) spatially smooth functional connectivity within short windows and (b) temporally smooth evolution of these connectivity patterns.
- **Evidence anchors:** Abstract describes joint learning; Section II formulates the coupled objective with separate Laplacian constraints; related work (e.g., graph-based deep learning on sEEG) indicates this decomposition is recognized.
- **Break condition:** If seizure propagation involves abrupt, non-smooth connectivity changes (e.g., sudden distant region recruitment), the smoothness priors may oversmooth and miss critical transitions.

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim:** Enforcing smooth graph signals via Laplacian quadratic forms provides a principled, convex way to learn interpretable connectivity graphs from iEEG.
- **Mechanism:** For a graph Laplacian *L*, the quadratic form tr(*X<sup>⊤</sup>LX*) sums squared differences between connected node signals. Minimizing this encourages connected electrodes to have similar signals, yielding a connectivity graph that explains observed spatial covariance.
- **Core assumption:** Brain regions engaged in a seizure exhibit more coherent (similar) iEEG activity than non-engaged regions, captured by a weighted graph.
- **Evidence anchors:** Section II states spatial smoothness promotes similar activity across connected regions; constraint set *L* ensures valid Laplacians; Q3 shows pre-onset graphs are unstructured while post-onset graphs concentrate in SOZ regions.
- **Break condition:** If true functional connectivity is directed, inhibitory, or modulated by phase relationships not captured by magnitude-based smoothness, the undirected, magnitude-only metric may misrepresent interactions.

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim:** Alternating Block Coordinate Descent (BCD) optimization with strict convexity per block (via β∥·∥<sub>F</sub><sup>2</sup>) yields convergence to a stationary point, making joint learning tractable and stable.
- **Mechanism:** The full objective is non-convex jointly but convex in each block *L<sup>(s)</sup><sub>m</sub>* or *L<sup>(t)</sup>* when others are fixed. The Frobenius regularizer (β>0) ensures strict convexity per block, guaranteeing a unique minimizer per update. Standard BCD theory then ensures the objective non-increases and limit points are stationary.
- **Core assumption:** Solving each subproblem exactly (or to sufficient accuracy) and maintaining feasible iterates within *L<sup>(s)</sup>*, *L<sup>(t)</sup>* is computationally feasible.
- **Evidence anchors:** Section II states subproblems are strictly convex and solvable by CVXPY; Theorem 1 proof outlines block-wise strict convexity and invokes Tseng (2001) for BCD convergence.
- **Break condition:** If exact subproblem solves are too expensive for large *N* or *M*, approximate solves could violate convergence guarantees; scalability is not demonstrated for very large electrode counts.

## Foundational Learning
- **Concept: Graph Signal Processing (GSP) Smoothness Priors**
  - **Why needed:** The entire method relies on representing iEEG as graph signals and using Laplacian smoothness as the core objective to infer connectivity. Without understanding tr(*X<sup>⊤</sup>LX*) as a measure of graph-based signal variation, the formulation is opaque.
  - **Quick check:** What does a small value of tr(*X<sup>⊤</sup>LX*) imply about the relationship between the graph structure *A* and the signals in *X*?
- **Concept: Valid Graph Laplacian Constraints**
  - **Why needed:** The optimization must output a legitimate adjacency/Laplacian matrix (symmetric, PSD, zero row-sum, non-positive off-diagonals). The set *L* in (1) encodes these constraints. Without this, "graphs" could be uninterpretable or violate GSP theory.
  - **Quick check:** Why must a combinatorial graph Laplacian have all row sums equal to zero? What would a non-zero row sum imply?
- **Concept: Block Coordinate Descent (BCD) Convergence**
  - **Why needed:** The method's theoretical guarantee (Theorem 1) rests on BCD properties. An engineer must know that block-wise exact minimization over a strictly convex function on a closed convex set yields a decreasing objective and stationary points, not that it finds the global minimum of the non-convex joint problem.
  - **Quick check:** If a joint problem is non-convex but convex in each block, what *type* of point do BCD iterations converge to (global minimum, local minimum, stationary point)?

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map:**
  Input Preprocessor -> Spatial Graph Learner (for all windows) -> Temporal Graph Learner -> Convergence Check -> (repeat until convergence) -> Post-Processor/Interpreter
- **Critical path:** The temporal learner depends on the complete set of spatial graphs from the inner loop; the spatial learners operate independently per window within an iteration.
- **Design tradeoffs:**
  - **Interpretability vs. Performance:** Explicit graph learning yields clear connectivity maps but may underfit compared to end-to-end GNNs optimized for classification.
  - **Smoothness Prior Strength (β):** Higher β yields denser, smoother graphs (more stable but blurring sparse true connectivity); lower β risks noise and non-convergence.
  - **Window Length/Overlap:** Too short windows lack statistical power; too long mix distinct dynamics. 512 ms with 50% overlap is a heuristic.
  - **Constraint Sets *L<sup>(s)</sup>*, *L<sup>(t)</sup>*:** Strict combinatorial Laplacian constraints ensure valid graphs but may be restrictive; relaxations could improve fit but break guarantees.
- **Failure signatures:**
  - **Objective not decreasing:** Likely due to inexact inner solves or violated constraint projections.
  - **Degenerate temporal graph (*L<sup>(t)</sup>* ≈ 0):** All windows considered structurally identical; may indicate β too large or genuinely similar *L<sup>(s)</sup>* (check vec(*L<sup>(s)</sup><sub>m</sub>*) norms).
  - **Spatial graphs all-dense or all-sparse:** Check β and data variability in *X<sub>m</sub>*; low variance may make smoothness trivially satisfied.
  - **Poor SOZ localization:** Smoothness prior may be mismatched to patient dynamics; consider signal pre-processing or windowing parameters.
- **First 3 experiments:**
  1. **Sanity Check Synthetic Data:** Generate synthetic iEEG-like data on a known brain graph with a "seizure" subnet. Verify spatial graphs recover ground-truth connectivity in active windows and temporal graph links seizure windows.
  2. **Convergence & Constraint Validation:** Run on one real patient recording with verbose logging. Track objective per iteration (must non-increase) and validate final Laplacians (PSD? trace ≈ *N*? row-sum zero? off-diagonals ≤ 0?). Vary β to see sparsity effects.
  3. **Baseline Replication & Ablation:** Replicate Table I comparison with HVG+LR. Then run an ablation: remove temporal graph term (set β<sub>temporal</sub>=0 or fix *L<sup>(t)</sup>*=I) to isolate its contribution to non-SOZ identification. Also test per-window graph learning only (no temporal coupling) to measure impact on overall accuracy.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- **Open Question 1:** Is SpaTeoGL competitive with a baseline for SOZ/non-SOZ discrimination? The paper compares only with HVG+LR on nine patients; competitiveness with other state-of-the-art methods and on larger datasets remains untested.
- **Open Question 2:** Does it reveal temporally coherent regimes (e.g., pre-onset versus propagation) through the learned temporal graph? Temporal graphs are visualised for only four patients; consistency across the full cohort and clinical validity are not quantitatively established.
- **Open Question 3:** Does it highlight clinically reported SOZ electrodes near seizure onset via the learned spatial graphs? Detailed spatial graph interpretation is provided solely for Patient 1; general ability across diverse cases remains unverified.

## Limitations
- Small cohort (31 recordings from 9 patients) and no clear train/test split risk overfitting and limit generalizability.
- Hyperparameters (β, tolerance, window length) and SOZ prediction threshold are not fully disclosed, hindering reproducibility.
- Smoothness priors may oversmooth abrupt connectivity changes, potentially missing critical seizure propagation events.
- Evaluation against only a single baseline (HVG+LR) and no recent deep graph methods weakens performance claims.
- Theoretical convergence assumes exact subproblem solutions, which may be computationally challenging for larger electrode counts.

## Confidence
- **Diagnostic performance** (improved non-SOZ and overall accuracy): Low, due to evaluation concerns and small sample size.
- **Joint spatiotemporal learning mechanism**: Medium, supported by theory and some empirical patterns but requires broader validation.
- **Optimization convergence** (BCD): High, given the rigorous convergence theorem under standard assumptions.
- **Interpretability** (graph alignment with clinical reports): Medium, based on qualitative case studies; needs systematic clinical validation.

## Next Checks
1. **Patient-level cross-validation:** Implement leave-one-patient-out evaluation to obtain unbiased performance estimates and quantify inter-patient variance.
2. **External validation:** Test SpaTeoGL on an independent iEEG dataset (e.g., CHB-MIT) to assess generalizability across recording setups and patient populations.
3. **Ablation and sensitivity analysis:** Remove the temporal graph term and vary β and window length to measure their individual impacts on non-SOZ identification, graph sparsity, and overall accuracy.
