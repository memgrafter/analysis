# Towards The Implicit Bias on Multiclass Separable Data Under Norm Constraints
## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2603.22824
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.22824
- **Reference Count:** 27
- **Primary Result:** NucGD, an optimizer under the nuclear norm constraint within the Normalized Steepest Descent framework, converges toward a low-rank maximum nuclear norm margin solution on multiclass separable data, with its dynamics modulated by gradient noise.

## Executive Summary
This paper studies how the geometry of norm constraints shapes the implicit bias of gradient-based optimization for multiclass linearly separable data. It introduces Normalized Gradient Descent under the nuclear norm (NucGD), an algorithm that performs normalized steepest descent with respect to the nuclear norm. NucGD promotes low-rank weight matrices by updating along the dominant singular mode of the momentum matrix, an operation computed efficiently via an SVD-free asynchronous power iteration. Theoretical analysis shows that, like other Normalized Steepest Descent (NSD) methods, NucGD converges to the maximum margin solution associated with its chosen norm. Empirically, NucGD’s final solution exhibits a sharp spectral tail collapse and higher correlation with the nuclear-norm max-margin target compared to NSD variants under other norms. The work also investigates the role of stochastic gradient noise—controlled by mini-batch size and momentum—finding that NucGD benefits from moderate noise levels to improve alignment with its target solution.

## Method Summary
NucGD is an instance of the Normalized Steepest Descent (NSD) framework where the constraint is the nuclear norm (sum of singular values). At each step, given the momentum matrix \(M_t\), the NSD direction \(\Delta_t\) solves \(\max_{\|\Delta\|_* \leq \gamma} \langle M_t, \Delta \rangle\). The solution is a rank-1 matrix \(\Delta_t = \gamma u_1 v_1^\top\), where \(u_1, v_1\) are the top left and right singular vectors of \(M_t\). To avoid costly full SVDs, NucGD employs an asynchronous power iteration on \(M_t M_t^\top\) to approximate \(u_1\), using the previous iterate’s estimate as a warm start. The weight update is \(W_{t+1} = W_t - \Delta_t\). This process induces an implicit bias toward weight matrices with low effective rank, as each step injects spectral mass primarily into the top singular direction.

## Key Results
- NucGD converges to the unique maximum nuclear norm margin solution \(W_\ast\) for multiclass linearly separable data, exhibiting a sharp spectral tail collapse that matches the low-rank structure of \(W_\ast\).
- Compared to NSD variants under \(\ell_2\), spectral, or \(\ell_\infty\) norms, NucGD achieves higher correlation with its nuclear-norm target and a more pronounced decay in singular values beyond the top few.
- Gradient noise, tuned via mini-batch size and momentum coefficient, modulates convergence: moderate noise improves NucGD’s alignment with \(W_\ast\), while larger batches (less noise) favor other norms like \(\ell_2\).

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
**Mechanism 1**
- **Claim:** Under the Normalized Steepest Descent (NSD) framework, an optimizer's implicit bias converges to the maximum margin solution associated with its constrained norm on multiclass separable data.
- **Mechanism:** The NSD update \(\Delta_t = \gamma \arg\max_{\|\Delta\|_{\cdot} \leq 1} \langle M_t, \Delta \rangle\) performs a steepest descent step within the norm ball defined by \(\|\cdot\|_{\cdot}\). Theorem 1 proves that for linearly separable data and a step size \(\gamma \sim 1/\sqrt{t}\), the normalized weight direction \(W_t / \|W_t\|_{\cdot}\) converges to the unique solution \(W_\ast\) maximizing the margin \(m(W)\) under that norm. The norm’s geometry dictates the endpoint.
- **Core assumption:** Data is multiclass linearly separable; step size decays as \(\gamma \propto 1/\sqrt{t}\).
- **Evidence anchors:** Abstract states NSD converges to the "maximum relative margin"; Section 2’s Theorem 1 formalizes convergence; neighboring work on p-norm NSD supports generality.

**Mechanism 2**
- **Claim:** NucGD, as an NSD instance under the nuclear norm (\(\|\cdot\|_*\)), induces an implicit bias toward low-rank weight matrices by updating along the dominant rank-1 component of the momentum matrix.
- **Mechanism:** The nuclear-norm NSD direction is \(\Delta_t = \gamma u_1 v_1^\top\), where \(u_1, v_1\) are the top left/right singular vectors of \(M_t\) (Theorem 2). Each step is a rank-1 outer product aligned with the gradient’s leading singular mode. Accumulating such updates concentrates spectral mass onto a few top singular values, causing a "spectral tail collapse" and yielding a low-rank solution.
- **Core assumption:** The top singular value of \(M_t\) is strictly greater than the second (\(\sigma_1 > \sigma_2\)) to ensure a unique direction.
- **Evidence anchors:** Section 3 derives \(\Delta_t = \gamma u_1 v_1^\top\) and provides an SVD-free computation; Section 4.1’s Figure 3 shows the nuclear max-margin spectrum has near-zero tail and NucGD replicates this decay.

**Mechanism 3**
- **Claim:** Gradient noise, controlled by mini-batch size and momentum, modulates NSD convergence dynamics; NucGD shows improved alignment to its nuclear-norm target under moderate noise.
- **Mechanism:** Stochastic gradients from mini-batches introduce variance that perturbs the deterministic NSD trajectory. Momentum (\(\mu\)) acts as a low-pass filter on noise. Empirically, NucGD benefits from moderate noise (e.g., batch size 5–50) to escape local spectral configurations, yielding higher correlation with \(W_\ast\) (Figure 4g). In contrast, algorithms like NGD prefer larger batches (less noise) for better margins (Figure 4b,f).
- **Core assumption:** Noise is isotropic and zero-mean; the deterministic NSD dynamics converge to the max-margin solution.
- **Evidence anchors:** Section 4.2 shows correlation peaks at moderate \(B\) for NucGD; abstract notes characterization of noise modulation; neighboring work on mini-batch stochastic steepest descent provides related theory.

## Foundational Learning
- **Concept: Normalized Steepest Descent (NSD)**  
  *Why needed:* NSD is the unifying framework defining how the choice of norm shapes the implicit bias toward a specific max-margin solution. It is prerequisite to interpret differences between NucGD, SignGD, or Muon.  
  *Quick check:* For a given norm, what is the NSD update \(\Delta_t\) (Equation 4), and how does it relate to the dual norm?

- **Concept: Nuclear Norm and Low-Rank Promotion**  
  *Why needed:* The nuclear norm is the convex surrogate for matrix rank. NucGD’s low-rank bias stems directly from optimizing under this norm, central to its design and spectral observations.  
  *Quick check:* Why does minimizing the nuclear norm encourage solutions with many near-zero singular values?

- **Concept: Maximum Margin Solution under a Norm**  
  *Why needed:* The target implicit bias for any NSD algorithm is the norm-specific max-margin direction \(W_\ast\) (Equation 3). Evaluating convergence requires computing this solution for comparison.  
  *Quick check:* How does the max-margin solution \(W_\ast\) differ across norms (e.g., \(\ell_2\) vs. nuclear) for the same separable dataset, and what structural property (e.g., rank) does the nuclear version exhibit?

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map:** Gradient computation \(G_t = \nabla f(W_t)\) -> Momentum accumulation \(M_t = \mu M_{t-1} + (1-\mu) G_t\) -> Top singular vector estimation (power iteration on \(M_t M_t^\top\)) -> Rank-1 direction construction \(\Delta_t = \gamma u_1 v_1^\top\) -> Weight update \(W_{t+1} = W_t - \Delta_t\).

- **Critical path:** The low-rank bias and performance hinge on accurate estimation of the dominant singular vector \(u_1\) of \(M_t\). The asynchronous power iteration’s convergence speed depends on the spectral gap \(\sigma_1 / \sigma_2\). The warm-start using \(p_{t-1}\) relies on momentum-induced correlation between consecutive \(M_t\).

- **Design tradeoffs:**  
  *Accuracy vs. Cost:* Full SVD (Algorithm 1) gives exact NSD direction but costs \(O(\min(k,d) \cdot k \cdot d)\). Power iteration (Algorithm 2) is \(O(k \cdot d)\) per step per iteration but introduces approximation error decaying with spectral gap and iteration count.  
  *Noise sensitivity:* NucGD benefits from moderate stochastic noise (small \(B\)) for alignment but suffers from high noise (very small \(B\)) in stability. Momentum (\(\mu\)) filters noise but can reduce this beneficial effect.

- **Failure signatures:**  
  Power iteration diverges or drifts: Check if spectral gap \(\sigma_2/\sigma_1\) is too large (violates Assumption 2) or if \(p_t\) becomes orthogonal to \(u_1\) (violates Assumption 3). Monitor angle \(\tan\angle(p_t, u_1)\).  
  Unexpected high-rank solution: Verify data separability; if violated, max-margin solution is ill-defined. Also check if batch size is too large (\(B=n\)) removing beneficial noise for NucGD.  
  Divergence/NaN: Likely due to step size \(\gamma\) too large for noise level or ill-conditioned \(M_t M_t^\top\).

- **First 3 experiments:**  
  1. Reproduce Figure 3: Train linear model on synthetic separable data (\(k=15, d=25\)) with NucGD (full batch, \(\mu=0\)). Plot final singular spectrum and compare correlation/margin error against true nuclear, \(\ell_2\), spectral, \(\ell_\infty\) max-margin solutions. Expect sharp tail collapse and highest nuclear-norm correlation.
  2. Stochastic noise sweep (batch size): For \(B \in \{1,5,50,250,750\}\) with \(\mu=0\), measure final correlation to nuclear max-margin and normalized margin. Expect correlation peak at moderate \(B\) (e.g., 5 or 50) and decay at very large \(B\).
  3. Power iteration sanity check: Run Algorithm 2 with varying power iterations per step (1, 5, 10) and compare final alignment/spectrum to exact SVD (Algorithm 1). The 1-iteration version should closely match exact if spectral gap is reasonable.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- **How does NucGD’s implicit bias extend to deep neural networks?** The current analysis is restricted to linear models; deep networks introduce non-linearities and multiple layers that may alter the bias.
- **What is the convergence guarantee and rank dynamics under stochastic non-convex settings?** The provided analysis (Theorem 3) relies on assumptions like uniform spectral gap and vanishing drift, which may not hold generally; the interplay between noise and rank evolution is unclear.
- **How can NucGD improve practical low-rank training methods like Galore?** While connected to Galore via NSD, NucGD has not been tested in memory-constrained or distributed settings; whether its nuclear-norm bias yields practical advantages is unknown.
- **Why does NucGD benefit from moderate gradient noise while other NSD variants do not?** The paper observes this phenomenon empirically but offers no theoretical account for the nuclear norm’s unique interaction with stochasticity.
- **How robust are the results under non-separable data or label noise?** The entire analysis assumes perfect multiclass separability; behavior when this fails (common in practice) is unexplored.

## Limitations
- **Spectral gap dependence:** Theoretical guarantees for the power iteration require a large spectral gap (\(\sigma_1 \gg \sigma_2\)) in the momentum matrix; performance under flat spectra (common in deeper layers) is unclear.
- **Noise benefit specificity:** The observed improvement with moderate mini-batch noise for NucGD is empirical and may not generalize beyond synthetic linear models or specific architectures.
- **Non-separable data:** Theory assumes perfect multiclass separability; behavior with non-separable data or label noise is not addressed.

## Confidence
- **High:** NSD framework → norm-specific max-margin convergence (Theorem 1) for strictly separable data.
- **Medium:** NucGD’s low-rank bias is theoretically derived but hinges on spectral gap condition (Assumption 2) for stable direction estimation.
- **Low:** Stochastic noise modulating alignment (beneficial for NucGD, harmful for NGD) is an observed pattern without a complete theoretical model.

## Next Checks
1. **Power iteration robustness:** Run NucGD on synthetic data with controlled spectral decay (\(\sigma_2/\sigma_1 \in \{0.1, 0.5, 0.9\}\)) and measure final alignment error versus exact SVD. Tests the "large gap" assumption.
2. **Noise benefit transfer:** Replicate the batch-size sweep on a real multiclass dataset (e.g., CIFAR-10 linear classifier). Does moderate noise still improve nuclear-norm correlation?
3. **Non-separable stress test:** Introduce label noise to make data imperfectly separable. Monitor whether NucGD’s low-rank spectrum collapse persists or if optimization degrades.
