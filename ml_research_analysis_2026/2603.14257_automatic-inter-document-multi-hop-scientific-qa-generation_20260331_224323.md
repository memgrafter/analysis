# Automatic Inter-document Multi-hop Scientific QA Generation

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2603.14257
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.14257
- **Reference count:** 0
- **Primary result:** An automated framework generates 411k single-hop and 13.7k multi-hop scientific QA pairs from 8,211 biomedical papers; evaluation reveals a significant performance drop when moving from oracle to realistic retrieval settings, highlighting the benchmark's difficulty.

## Executive Summary
This paper introduces AIM-SciQA, an automated framework for generating large-scale multi-document, multi-hop scientific QA benchmarks from biomedical literature. It extracts high-quality single-hop QAs from individual paper paragraphs and constructs cross-document relations via embedding-based semantic alignment, with an optional citation-guided variant. Applied to 8,211 PubMed Central papers, it yields the IM-SciQA dataset (411,409 single-hop, 13,672 multi-hop QAs). Human and automatic validation confirm high factual consistency. Benchmark experiments show that while retrieval within 30-paper clusters achieves a Hit@1 of 0.673, QA accuracy drops significantly from an Oracle setting (0.9 with GPT-5) to a Realistic setting with retrieved papers, underscoring the challenge of multi-document reasoning. A citation-guided variant (CIM-SciQA) narrows this gap, approaching Oracle performance.

## Method Summary
The framework proceeds in four stages: (1) **Corpus Ingestion**: Filters PMC papers (2019–2024) for full metadata and ≥3 in-PMC citations, yielding 8,211 seeds. (2) **Single-Hop QA Generation**: Uses Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct to extract `(question, answer, evidence)` triplets per paragraph; filters via exact evidence match and a semantic similarity gap between question-answer and question-evidence. (3) **Relation Construction**: Encodes SHQA questions with MedEmbed-large-v0.1; computes section-level similarity via weighted cosine similarities (threshold τ=0.3); selects top-K section pairs per document pair. For IM-SciQA, candidates are drawn from keyword-overlapping papers; for CIM-SciQA, from explicit citations. Builds 30-paper clusters and selects a distinct "retrieval QA" per target paper. (4) **Multi-Hop QA Composition**: An LLM merges two related SHQAs via a three-step prompt (find target, generate inter-doc question, merge) and validates against four criteria (Fluency, Completeness, Cross-reference Necessity, Relational Appropriateness).

## Key Results
- Generated 411,409 single-hop and 13,672 multi-hop QA pairs from 8,211 biomedical papers.
- Retrieval within 30-paper clusters reaches Hit@1 = 0.673; full-corpus retrieval is more challenging.
- QA accuracy under Oracle (correct target provided) peaks at 0.9 (GPT-5), but drops substantially under Realistic (retrieved target) conditions, exposing the difficulty of multi-document reasoning.
- Citation-guided CIM-SciQA reduces the Oracle–Realistic gap, nearly matching Oracle performance.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- **Claim:** High-quality single-hop QA pairs with exact evidence grounding provide reliable atomic facts for cross-document composition.
- **Mechanism:** An instruction-tuned LLM generates QA triplets per paragraph under an MRC paradigm; a post-hoc filter based on a semantic similarity gap (`Sim(Q,A) - Sim(Q,E)`) removes misaligned pairs. Exact evidence matching ensures factual traceability.
- **Break condition:** If evidence spans fail exact matching or the similarity filter misses misalignments, the atomic fact base becomes noisy, compromising multi-hop construction.

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim:** Embedding-based semantic alignment of question embeddings identifies section-level relationships suitable for multi-hop linking, independent of explicit citations.
- **Mechanism:** Sections are represented by aggregating embeddings of their SHQA questions (MedEmbed-large-v0.1). Pairwise section similarity is a weighted sum of cosine similarities above τ=0.3; top-K pairs per document pair define candidate relations.
- **Break condition:** If question embeddings poorly encode informational intent (e.g., for conceptual questions), the similarity matrix yields false-positive relations, leading to incoherent multi-hop pairs.

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim:** A structured LLM prompting pipeline with explicit validation merges two related single-hop QAs into a coherent, answerable multi-hop question without external facts.
- **Mechanism:** A three-step generation (find target paper, generate inter-document QA, merge) followed by four-criteria validation. The retrieval QA's uniqueness within a 30-paper cluster ensures the first hop is well-posed.
- **Break condition:** If source and target SHQA pairs lack a meaningful relational link (e.g., no comparison or inference possible), the merged question becomes unanswerable or forces irrelevant reasoning.

## Foundational Learning
- **Concept: Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG)**
  - **Why needed:** The benchmark evaluates RAG systems: retrieval identifies the target paper from a cluster, then QA synthesizes across source and target documents.
  - **Quick check:** How does the fixed 30-doc "paper cluster" setting differ from full-corpus retrieval in assessing a RAG system's robustness?

- **Concept: Semantic Textual Similarity with Domain-Specific Embeddings**
  - **Why needed:** Cross-document relation construction relies entirely on MedEmbed's ability to encode scientific questions meaningfully.
  - **Quick check:** Why does the framework use *question* embeddings rather than answer embeddings for matching, and what risk does this mitigate?

- **Concept: Multi-Hop Question Decomposition**
  - **Why needed:** The task decomposes a multi-document query into retrievable hops; understanding valid "hop" structure (factual, non-redundant, grounded) is key.
  - **Quick check:** What distinguishes a valid multi-hop question from simply concatenating two independent single-hop questions?

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map:**
  Corpus Ingestion -> SHQA Generator -> Relation Builder -> MHQA Composer -> [CIM-SciQA Variant]
- **Critical path:** Paper selection -> SHQA generation -> Relation construction (cluster + retrieval QA) -> MHQA generation/validation. SHQA quality propagates; relation construction is the pivot.
- **Design tradeoffs:**
  - Keyword-overlap (IM-SciQA) yields diverse, harder pairs but risks semantic drift; citation-guided (CIM-SciQA) provides explicit ground truth but easier questions.
  - Exact evidence matching ensures factuality but discards paraphrases, reducing recall.
  - Fixed 30-doc cluster balances retrieval challenge with feasibility; size is arbitrary.
  - LLM for MHQA merging ensures quality but is costly; could be replaced with cheaper models if validation tightens.
- **Failure signatures:**
  - SHQA stage: Low evidence-exact-match rate (<90%); high similarity-difference variance.
  - Relation stage: Most section similarity scores near τ; few top-K pairs per document → insufficient candidates.
  - MHQA stage: High pre-rejection rate (>20%) on Relational Appropriateness or Cross-reference Necessity; validation scores <4.0.
  - Retrieval stage: Hit@1 in Paper Cluster ≈ Random Cluster → cluster not discriminative.
- **First 3 experiments:**
  1. Replicate SHQA extraction on 100 random PMC papers: verify evidence exact-match rate and QA per section distribution; manually check 20 for hallucination.
  2. Test relation construction by measuring average top-K section pairs per source paper and similarity score distribution. Vary τ (0.2, 0.3, 0.4) to see impact on candidate count and downstream MHQA yield.
  3. Validate MHQA pipeline by sampling 50 final IM-SciQA pairs; have two annotators judge Completeness and Cross-reference Necessity. Target: >85% Accept on both.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- **Open Question 1:** How can AIM-SciQA be extended to incorporate multi-modal information (figures, tables) from scientific papers, and what impact would this have on the comprehensiveness and difficulty of generated multi-hop QA?
- **Open Question 2:** Why do stronger language models exhibit larger performance drops from Oracle to Realistic settings, and what architectural or training modifications could improve robustness to imperfect retrieval in multi-document scientific QA?
- **Open Question 3:** To what extent is the AIM-SciQA pipeline and its IM-SciQA dataset generalizable to other scientific domains (e.g., physics, computer science) beyond biomedicine, and what domain-specific adaptations are necessary?
- **Open Question 4:** How can automatic multi-hop QA generation be scaled to deeper reasoning chains (≥3 hops) while maintaining factual consistency and logical coherence across more than two documents?
- **Open Question 5:** What is the optimal design of the semantic similarity threshold (τ = 0.3) and section-pair selection (Top-K) in the relation construction step, and how sensitive is final multi-hop QA quality to these hyperparameters?

## Limitations
- Missing details on keyword-overlap method for candidate generation, top-K selection, and enforcement of the max-three IM-QA constraint.
- The embedding model used for the SHQA similarity-difference filter is unspecified, hindering reproducibility.
- Exact evidence matching may discard valid paraphrased facts; fixed 30-doc cluster size is arbitrary and not systematically justified.

## Confidence
- **Mechanism 1 (SHQA extraction):** Medium. High human validation (AC1=0.94) but unknown similarity filter and exact-match constraint may limit recall.
- **Mechanism 2 (Relation construction):** Low. Relation quality depends on MedEmbed and arbitrarily chosen τ=0.3, with no domain evaluation.
- **Mechanism 3 (MHQA merging):** Medium. LLM merging passes validation but may propagate biases; logical consistency not explicitly verified.

## Next Checks
1. Replicate SHQA extraction on 100 papers; manually audit evidence exact-match rate, hallucinations, and effect of dropping the exact-match filter.
2. Sweep keyword-overlap parameters (min shared tokens, stopwords) on candidate pairs; evaluate resulting QA pairs for diversity and multi-hop necessity via expert review.
3. Ablate embedding model: substitute MedEmbed with BioBERT/PubMedBERT; measure impact on relation graphs, retrieval Hit@1, and QA accuracy in Oracle/Realistic settings.
