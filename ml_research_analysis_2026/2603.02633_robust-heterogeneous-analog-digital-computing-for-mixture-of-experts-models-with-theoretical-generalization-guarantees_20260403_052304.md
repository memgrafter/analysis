# Robust Heterogeneous Analog-Digital Computing for Mixture-of-Experts Models with Theoretical Generalization Guarantees

## Quick Facts
*   **arXiv ID:** 2603.02633
*   **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.02633
*   **Reference count:** 40
*   **Primary result:** Proposes a retraining-free heterogeneous framework that uses maximum neuron norm (MaxNNScore) to identify noise-sensitive experts for digital execution, recovering at least one-third of performance loss from AIMC weight noise with only 12.5% of experts run digitally, while providing theoretical guarantees on noise tolerance.

## Executive Summary
Deploying large Mixture-of-Experts (MoE) models on efficient analog in-memory computing (AIMC) hardware is hindered by severe nonidealities that typically require costly noise-aware retraining. This paper introduces a retraining-free heterogeneous computation strategy. It theoretically and empirically shows that experts with larger maximum neuron norms are more sensitive to weight-programming noise and should be executed on digital accelerators. Furthermore, all densely activated modules (e.g., attention) must remain digital regardless of size, as their universal processing makes them disproportionately vulnerable. This selective placement yields a favorable trade-off between accuracy, throughput, and energy compared to homogeneous deployment.

## Method Summary
The method formulates a heterogeneous deployment policy for pretrained MoE models on mixed analog-digital hardware without retraining. For each MoE layer, dense modules (multi-head self-attention, LM head, shared experts) are mandatorily assigned to the digital accelerator. Among the layer's sparse experts, a selection criterion—the maximum neuron norm score (MaxNNScore)—ranks noise sensitivity. MaxNNScore is the product of the maximum ℓ₂ norms of columns in an expert's up-projection, down-projection, and gate matrices. A tunable fraction Γ of experts with the highest scores are run digitally; the remainder are mapped to the analog accelerator. Weight-programming noise, modeled as magnitude-scaled Gaussian, is injected only on analog weights. DAC/ADC noise is suppressed via calibration.

## Key Results
*   MaxNNScore consistently outperforms baseline expert-selection strategies (activation frequency, router norm) in maintaining model accuracy under increasing weight-programming noise on DeepSeekMoE (16B) and OLMoE (7B).
*   Placing just 12.5% of the most noise-sensitive experts (top MaxNNScore) in digital recovers approximately one-third of the accuracy drop caused by full analog deployment.
*   Theoretical analysis on a simplified MoE model proves that moving high-norm experts to digital allows the remaining analog experts to tolerate Ω((1-α)/α) times higher noise, where α relates to token frequency specialization.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1: Expert noise-sensitivity via maximum neuron norm
*   **Claim:** Experts with larger maximum neuron norm scores (MaxNNScore) are more sensitive to weight-programming noise and should be assigned to digital computation.
*   **Mechanism:** The maximum neuron norm for an expert is computed as the product of the maximum ℓ₂ norms of neurons in its up, down, and gate projection matrices. Larger norms imply larger weight magnitudes, which incur greater absolute noise under the AIMC weight-programming model (where noise variance scales with weight magnitude). Theoretical analysis on a simplified MoE model shows that experts specializing in more frequent task-relevant tokens develop larger neuron norms. Thus, MaxNNScore serves as a proxy for noise-sensitivity.
*   **Core assumption:** Weight-programming noise scales with weight magnitude (as in Equation 3). Expert specialization patterns are stable after training and correlate with neuron norms.
*   **Evidence anchors:** Abstract states noise-sensitive experts are "provably identifiable by their maximum neuron norm." Section 4.1 theoretically shows experts specialized in more-frequent tokens host neurons with large ℓ₂ norm.
*   **Break condition:** If the AIMC noise model deviates from magnitude-dependent Gaussian (e.g., systematic biases) or if expert specialization is highly unstable across batches, neuron norm may not correlate with noise-sensitivity.

### Mechanism 2: Digital execution for dense modules
*   **Claim:** Densely activated modules (multi-head self-attention, LM head, shared experts) must run in digital despite small parameter footprint because their universal activation makes them disproportionately sensitive to noise.
*   **Mechanism:** These modules process every input token, so any noise affects all tokens. Even minimal per-parameter noise accumulates across many tokens, causing large accuracy drops. Experiments show that placing only MHSA (3.88% of parameters) in analog degrades accuracy more than placing 87.5% of sparse experts in analog.
*   **Core assumption:** Noise impact scales with the number of tokens processed, not just parameter count. The dense modules have no sparsity to mitigate noise propagation.
*   **Evidence anchors:** Abstract notes dense modules have "high noise sensitivity despite comprising a small fraction of parameters." Section 3 and Table 1 demonstrate severe accuracy drops when DAC-ADC noise is added to dense modules versus experts only.
*   **Break condition:** If the model uses atypical architectures with redundant dense modules or if noise levels are so low that DAC-ADC calibration suffices even for dense modules.

### Mechanism 3: Heterogeneous deployment improves noise tolerance
*   **Claim:** Placing a fraction Γ of experts with top MaxNNScore in digital allows the remaining analog experts to tolerate Ω((1-α)/α)-times higher weight-programming noise compared to full analog deployment, where α is the frequency of less-frequent task-relevant tokens.
*   **Mechanism:** Theoretical analysis (Theorem 4.2) shows that analog experts specialized on less-frequent tokens have smaller neuron norms (Lemma 4.1). By moving the high-norm experts (specialized on frequent tokens) to digital, the analog experts' effective noise tolerance scales inversely with α. This creates a Pareto frontier: more digital experts → higher accuracy at the cost of throughput/energy.
*   **Core assumption:** The simplified data model (binary classification with two task-relevant tokens, one more frequent) captures key aspects of MoE specialization. The noise model is purely additive Gaussian with variance proportional to weight magnitude.
*   **Evidence anchors:** Abstract mentions theoretical guarantees. Section 4.3 (Theorem 4.2) provides the scaling factor. Section 5.3 (Figures 4-5) empirically shows accuracy recovery with small Γ.
*   **Break condition:** If token frequency distributions are not bimodal, the scaling factor may not hold. If noise has non-Gaussian heavy tails, the probabilistic guarantee may fail.

## Architecture Onboarding
*   **Component map:** Input tokens -> Digital Router (compute scores) -> Split path: Dense Modules (MHSA, etc.) all digital; Sparse Experts split by MaxNNScore: Top-Γ experts -> Digital Compute; Remaining experts -> Analog AIMC Compute (linear layers only) -> Combine -> Next layer.
*   **Critical path:** 1) Token sequence routed digitally to compute expert scores and select top-k. 2) Dense modules (MHSA) process all tokens on digital accelerator. 3) For each token, routed experts: if expert in top-Γ MaxNNScore, its linear layers execute digitally; else, weights are sent to AIMC for matrix-vector multiplies, with nonlinearities (ReLU) and element-wise ops in digital. 4) Aggregated outputs passed to next layer.
*   **Design tradeoffs:** Γ (fraction digital experts): increases accuracy but reduces energy efficiency. Calibration of DAC-ADC hyperparameters (β_in, λ) is essential. Bit-precision tradeoff: Higher DAC/ADC bits reduce quantization noise but increase area/power (paper uses 8-bit).
*   **Failure signatures:** Sudden accuracy collapse when Γ is below a critical threshold. If dense modules are mistakenly placed in analog, accuracy degrades severely even for small Γ. Theoretical guarantee assumes zero-mean Gaussian weight noise; bias may cause failure.
*   **First 3 experiments:** 1) DAC-ADC noise ablation: Add noise only to experts vs. experts+dense modules to validate digital placement for dense modules. 2) Compare MaxNNScore against baselines (activation frequency, router norm, activation weight) under varying programming noise; expect MaxNNScore to maintain highest accuracy. 3) Pareto sweep: Vary Γ from 0% to 100% and measure accuracy vs. throughput/energy; heterogeneous should dominate extremes.

## Limitations
*   **Evaluation protocol ambiguity:** Exact few-shot prompts, tokenization, and `lm-eval` settings are not fully disclosed, causing minor baseline accuracy differences.
*   **Dense module definition:** Unclear which components (e.g., DeepSeekMoE's shared expert) belong to dense modules, affecting Γ implementation and trade-off results.
*   **Noise model applicability:** The assumed magnitude-dependent Gaussian noise may not match real hardware biases or heavy-tailed distributions, potentially breaking the MaxNNScore correlation.
