# $\textbf{Re}^{2}$: Unlocking LLM Reasoning via Reinforcement Learning with Re-solving

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID**: 2603.07197
- **Source URL**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.07197
- **Reference count**: 40
- **Primary result**: Re² teaches LLMs to restart unproductive reasoning paths, achieving significant gains over DAPO on math/science benchmarks (e.g., +12.6 on AIME25) and increasing redo behavior from 0.5% to over 30%.

## Executive Summary
Re² is a pure reinforcement learning framework that addresses the overthinking problem in LLM reasoning: when early chain-of-thought steps are flawed, models rarely recover even with more computation. Re² learns a "resolve" action that abandons the current reasoning path and restarts from scratch. It samples multiple truncated prefixes and continuations, estimating the expected benefit of re-solving via out-of-group statistics. Across five benchmarks and model scales (3B–14B), Re² consistently outperforms the strong RLVR baseline DAPO under identical compute budgets and demonstrates superior test-time scaling.

## Method Summary
Re² trains models to produce a special "resolve" action (the phrase "It’s better to redo the question.") when the current chain-of-thought is unproductive. For each training query, the method samples `n` prefixes by randomly truncating full responses, then generates `m` continuations per prefix. Each continuation is labeled as `correct`, `incorrect`, or `resolve` based on the final answer and the presence of the resolve phrase. The reward for a correct continuation is 1, for incorrect is 0. For a resolve action, the reward is estimated as the expected accuracy if the model were to restart from scratch, computed from the out-of-group statistics (accuracy and resolve rate of continuations from other prefixes). Advantages are computed within each prefix group by normalizing rewards, and degenerate groups (all same outcome) are filtered. Policy updates use PPO with clipping. This approach amplifies redo behavior and improves efficiency by avoiding wasted computation on irrecoverable paths.

## Key Results
- Re² increases redo behavior from 0.5% to over 30% across model scales.
- On Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct under same compute as DAPO: +2.6 AIME24, +12.6 AIME25, +2.4 AMC23, +1.5 GSM8K, +3.0 GPQA-Diamond.
- Re² shows superior test-time scaling: accuracy continues to improve with more samples.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
Re² works by providing a learning signal that explicitly values the option to restart. The out-of-group reward estimator captures the potential benefit of re-solving without requiring a separate value model. By grouping continuations by prefix, the method reduces variance in advantage estimation and encourages the policy to learn when early steps are likely flawed. The resolve action becomes a strategic choice: if the current prefix is unlikely to lead to a correct answer (based on the performance of other prefixes from the same query), restarting yields a higher expected reward. This mechanism directly combats the "inefficient overthinking" problem where models persist with bad trajectories.

## Foundational Learning
1. **Reinforcement Learning with Verifiable Rewards (RLVR)**: Training RL on tasks where the final answer can be automatically checked (correct/incorrect). *Why needed*: Provides a scalable, dense training signal for reasoning tasks without human preferences. *Quick check*: Does the model's final answer match the reference? Reward = 1/0.
2. **Chain-of-Thought (CoT) Vulnerabilities**: Early errors in CoT can make the entire trajectory unrecoverable, leading to wasted computation. *Why needed*: Motivate the need for a restart mechanism. *Quick check*: Correlate correctness of early steps with final answer correctness.
3. **Out-of-group Estimation**: Using outcomes from other prefixes (same query) to estimate the value of re-solving. *Why needed*: Avoids training a separate value function and leverages the fact that different prefixes are alternative reasoning paths. *Quick check*: Compare estimated redo accuracy (from out-of-group) with actual redo accuracy on a held set.
4. **Group-wise Advantage Normalization**: Computing advantages within each prefix group to reduce variance across diverse prefixes. *Why needed*: Stabilizes policy gradient updates by controlling for prefix-specific difficulty. *Quick check*: Advantage values should have mean ~0 and finite variance per group.
5. **Resolve Action as a Special Token**: The model is prompted to output a fixed phrase to indicate a restart. *Why needed*: Provides a discrete, recognizable action for the policy to learn. *Quick check*: Measure frequency of the exact phrase in generations.

## Architecture Onboarding
Component map:
Query -> Full Response Sampling -> Prefix Truncation (n prefixes) -> Continuation Generation (m per prefix) -> Outcome Classification (correct/incorrect/resolve) -> Out-of-group Stat Aggregation -> Reward Assignment (Eq1) -> Advantage Computation (Eq2) -> Filter Degenerate Groups -> Policy Update (PPO).

Critical path: The accuracy of the resolve reward estimation hinges on the out-of-group statistics. Errors in outcome classification or insufficient diversity in prefixes can bias estimates.

Design tradeoffs:
- Larger n and m improve estimate reliability but increase compute.
- Random truncation ratios ∈[0,0.8] balance prefix diversity and relevance.
- Using a fixed resolve phrase simplifies detection but may miss paraphrases.

Failure signatures:
- Resolve rate near 0%: model not learning to redo.
- High degenerate group rate (>50%): training updates skipped.
- Large variance in estimated redo rewards across steps.

First experiments:
1. Confirm that the base model (before training) can occasionally produce the resolve phrase when prompted. If not, adjust prompting or temperature.
2. On a small validation set, compute the correlation between out-of-group estimated redo accuracy and actual redo accuracy (by forcing resolve and measuring).
3. Run a single training iteration with n=4, m=2 and inspect the reward distribution: resolve rewards should be between 0 and 1 and distinct from correct/incorrect rewards.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
1. **Multi-modal extension**: How can Re² be applied to vision-language reasoning tasks? The paper only evaluates on text-based math and science benchmarks.
2. **Decision threshold**: What determines the model's threshold for invoking a redo during inference? There is no mechanism to control the redo probability, which may affect efficiency.
3. **Estimator accuracy**: How accurate is the out-of-group empirical estimator for the expected reward of re-solving, especially with limited samples? The analysis uses up to 16 groups; performance under lower compute or skewed distributions is unknown.
4. **Domain generality**: Does Re²'s benefit generalize to domains where early reasoning errors are more recoverable (e.g., creative writing)? All evaluations are on math/science with verifiable final answers.

## Limitations
- Dataset provenance: DAPO-Math-17K construction details are unspecified; reconstruction may diverge.
- Hyperparameter gaps: Missing DAPO baseline details (e.g., KL penalty, GAE λ) require inference from prior work.
- Reward edge cases: The formula for resolve reward divides by zero when `P_resolve_out=1`; the paper does not specify a fallback.

## Confidence
- Core claim (Re² > DAPO under equal compute): *Medium* (method described, but missing critical hyperparams and dataset fidelity concerns).
- Behavioral claim (30%+ redo rate): *High* (directly measurable, paper provides clear metric).
- Scaling claim (test-time improvement): *Medium* (verifiable, depends on implementation).
