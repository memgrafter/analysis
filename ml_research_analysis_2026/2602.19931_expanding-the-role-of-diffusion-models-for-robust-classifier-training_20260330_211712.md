# Expanding the Role of Diffusion Models for Robust Classifier Training

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID**: 2602.19931
- **Source URL**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.19931
- **Reference count**: 20
- **Key outcome**: DRA consistently improves both clean accuracy and adversarial robustness by incorporating diffusion representations as auxiliary learning signals during adversarial training

## Executive Summary
This paper investigates whether diffusion models can improve robust classifier training beyond their established role in generating synthetic data. The authors propose Diffusion Representation Alignment (DRA), a method that incorporates frozen diffusion representations as an auxiliary learning signal during adversarial training. Through extensive experiments on CIFAR-10/100 and ImageNet across multiple architectures, DRA consistently improves both clean accuracy and adversarial robustness. The work reveals that diffusion representations encode partially robust and diverse features, and that synthetic data and representation alignment play complementary roles in shaping classifier representations.

## Method Summary
The paper proposes Diffusion Representation Alignment (DRA), which aligns classifier representations with frozen diffusion model representations during adversarial training. The method extracts diffusion representations from a pre-trained diffusion model at optimal timesteps, then trains an auxiliary projection head to align these with the classifier's representations using cosine similarity loss. This alignment loss is combined with the standard adversarial training loss to form the final objective. The approach leverages diffusion representations' partially robust and diverse features to encourage better use of representation dimensions for encoding robust features, with synthetic data and alignment playing complementary roles in shaping classifier representations.

## Key Results
- DRA consistently improves both clean accuracy and adversarial robustness across CIFAR-10/100 and ImageNet datasets
- Diffusion representations encode partially robust and diverse features that serve as effective feature priors
- Synthetic data and representation alignment play complementary roles in shaping classifier representations
- Incorporating diffusion models into adversarial training encourages learning representations that are easier to disentangle

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- **Claim**: Diffusion representations provide partially robust and diverse features that improve adversarial training
- **Mechanism**: The denoising objective of diffusion models forces them to capture robust semantic features from partially corrupted images, which serve as effective feature priors for training robust classifiers
- **Core assumption**: Diffusion representations encode meaningful features that are both robust to noise and diverse enough to improve downstream classification
- **Evidence anchors**:
  - [abstract]: "diffusion representations offer representations that are both diverse and partially robust"
  - [section]: "diffusion representations exhibit several desirable properties, encoding diverse, lower-frequency-dependent information and are less sensitive to irrelevant high-frequency noise"
  - [corpus]: "Found 25 related papers (using 8). Average neighbor FMR=0.505" - Weak corpus evidence for this specific mechanism
- **Break condition**: If diffusion representations primarily capture high-frequency details or lack semantic diversity, the feature prior benefit disappears

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim**: Representation alignment encourages better use of representation dimensions for encoding robust features
- **Mechanism**: By aligning classifier representations with diffusion representations, the model learns to distribute robust information across more dimensions rather than concentrating it in a few, making the representation more resilient to adversarial perturbations
- **Core assumption**: Aligning with diffusion representations creates a more distributed and effective encoding of robust features across the representation space
- **Evidence anchors**:
  - [abstract]: "diffusion representation alignment encourages the model to effectively leverage its representational dimensions to encode robust features"
  - [section]: "diffusion representation alignment encourages the model to more effectively leverage representation dimensions to encode robust features"
  - [corpus]: No direct corpus evidence found for this specific mechanism
- **Break condition**: If alignment forces representations to collapse into a low-dimensional subspace, it could reduce robustness

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim**: Synthetic data and representation alignment play complementary roles in shaping classifier representations
- **Mechanism**: Diffusion synthetic data enables learning lower-rank representations with strong generalization properties, while representation alignment encourages encoding robust features across available dimensions, together providing benefits that neither achieves alone
- **Core assumption**: Synthetic data and alignment contribute different aspects to representation quality that combine synergistically
- **Evidence anchors**:
  - [abstract]: "diffusion representations and synthetic data play complementary roles in shaping representations"
  - [section]: "diffusion synthetic data leads to lower-rank representations" and "diffusion representation alignment encourages the model to more effectively leverage representation dimensions to encode robust features"
  - [corpus]: Weak corpus evidence for this specific complementary mechanism
- **Break condition**: If synthetic data and alignment conflict in their effects on representation structure, the combination could be detrimental

## Architecture Onboarding

- **Component map**: Classifier backbone (fθ) -> Classification head (gθ) -> Auxiliary projection head (gproj) -> Frozen diffusion model (gϕ) -> Adversarial training loop with PGD

- **Critical path**:
  1. Extract adversarial examples using PGD
  2. Extract diffusion representations from frozen model
  3. Compute alignment loss between classifier and diffusion representations
  4. Combine alignment loss with adversarial training loss
  5. Update classifier parameters

- **Design tradeoffs**:
  - Using frozen diffusion representations vs fine-tuning the diffusion encoder
  - Alignment strength (λ parameter) vs maintaining original adversarial training objective
  - Extraction timestep choice vs representation quality
  - Single vs multiple representation extractions per image

- **Failure signatures**:
  - Robust accuracy drops when alignment strength is too high
  - Clean accuracy decreases if representations become too similar to diffusion features
  - Training instability if diffusion representations have very different scales
  - No improvement if diffusion model is too different from the target domain

- **First 3 experiments**:
  1. Baseline DM-AT without any alignment (measure improvement baseline)
  2. Single-step alignment with fixed λ=1.2 to test core mechanism
  3. Different diffusion representation extraction timesteps to find optimal feature quality

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- **Question**: Does diffusion model pretraining implicitly encourage lower-rank representations in the feature space?
- **Basis in paper**: [inferred] The paper observes that diffusion synthetic data leads to lower-rank representations compared to diffusion representation alignment, and suggests this might be related to diffusion synthetic examples being easier to classify than original real data.
- **Why unresolved**: The paper only suggests a possible relationship but doesn't provide mechanistic evidence for why diffusion synthetic data specifically leads to lower-rank representations.
- **What evidence would resolve it**: Controlled experiments varying the difficulty of synthetic examples (not just from diffusion models) while keeping pretraining objectives constant would help determine if this is a property specific to diffusion-generated synthetic data.

### Open Question 2
- **Question**: How do different diffusion model architectures (UNet vs. transformer-based) affect the quality and robustness of extracted representations for classifier training?
- **Basis in paper**: [explicit] The paper uses different diffusion models for CIFAR-10/100 (EDM) and ImageNet (LightningDiT), but doesn't systematically compare how architecture choices affect representation quality.
- **Why unresolved**: The paper uses different diffusion models for different datasets without isolating the effect of architecture choice from other factors.
- **What evidence would resolve it**: Direct comparison of representation quality metrics (uniformity, alignment, frequency saliency) from multiple diffusion architectures trained on the same data would clarify if architecture is a significant factor.

### Open Question 3
- **Question**: What is the relationship between the frequency behavior of diffusion representations and their effectiveness as a learning signal for adversarial training?
- **Basis in paper**: [explicit] The paper shows diffusion representations have lower high-frequency saliency compared to standard models, and that using noisy-image discriminative representations as alignment targets increases sensitivity to mid/high frequencies.
- **Why unresolved**: While the paper observes this frequency behavior, it doesn't establish a causal link between frequency properties and effectiveness as a learning signal.
- **What evidence would resolve it**: Systematic ablation studies varying the frequency characteristics of the target representations while measuring alignment effectiveness would clarify this relationship.

### Open Question 4
- **Question**: Does the benefit of diffusion representation alignment depend on the specific architecture of the classifier being trained?
- **Basis in paper**: [explicit] The paper shows consistent improvements across WideResNets and ViT architectures, but doesn't explore if the magnitude of benefit varies by architecture type.
- **Why unresolved**: The experiments show improvements exist but don't test whether certain architectures benefit more or if the mechanism differs across architectures.
- **What evidence would resolve it**: Comprehensive experiments across diverse architecture families (CNNs, transformers, MLPs) with detailed analysis of how representations are being used would reveal architecture-dependent effects.

## Limitations

- Performance depends heavily on the quality and domain alignment of the pre-trained diffusion model used for representation extraction
- Computational overhead of extracting diffusion representations during training could be prohibitive for very large-scale applications
- Analysis focuses primarily on vision tasks, leaving applicability to other domains uncertain

## Confidence

- **High Confidence**: DRA consistently improves robust accuracy across multiple datasets and architectures (CIFAR-10/100, ImageNet experiments)
- **Medium Confidence**: The mechanism that diffusion representations encode partially robust features is supported by empirical evidence but lacks rigorous theoretical justification
- **Low Confidence**: The claim that synthetic data and alignment play complementary roles in shaping representations requires more ablation studies to confirm the synergistic effect
