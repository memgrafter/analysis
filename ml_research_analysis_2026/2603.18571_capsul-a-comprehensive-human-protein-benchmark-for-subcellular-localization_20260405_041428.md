# CAPSUL: A Comprehensive Human Protein Benchmark for Subcellular Localization

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2603.18571  
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.18571  
- **Reference count:** 40  
- **Primary result:** CAPSUL enables structure-based models (e.g., CDConv) to achieve strong performance (micro F1 0.452) and reveals interpretable, compartment-specific structural patterns like Golgi transmembrane α-helices.

## Executive Summary
CAPSUL is a new benchmark for predicting human protein subcellular localization that addresses critical gaps in existing datasets. It integrates high-confidence AlphaFold2-predicted 3D structures (Cα coordinates and 3Di tokens) with fine-grained annotations for 20 expert-curated subcellular compartments and experimental evidence levels. Evaluations demonstrate that structure-based graph neural networks leverage 3D geometry effectively, though sequence-based pretrained models remain strong overall. The dataset facilitates interpretable discovery, as shown by attention analysis identifying a known transmembrane α-helix pattern for the Golgi apparatus. CAPSUL enables research into structure-driven localization prediction and biologically meaningful model interpretations.

## Method Summary
The study introduces the CAPSUL dataset, comprising 20,181 human proteins with AlphaFold2-predicted Cα coordinates, FoldSeek 3Di tokens, and multi-label annotations across 20 fine-grained subcellular compartments. A variety of models are benchmarked, including sequence-only baselines (ESM-2, ESM-C) and structure-based graph neural networks (CDConv, GearNet-Edge). Graph construction uses spatial adjacency (radii 4–8 Å) and sequential adjacency (window ±5 residues). Models are trained with binary cross-entropy loss, with explorations into class reweighting (focal loss) and single-label classification for rare classes. Performance is evaluated via micro and macro F1-scores, and attention mechanisms are used to interpret model decisions for specific compartments.

## Key Results
- Structure-based models (CDConv, GearNet-Edge) achieve strong performance, with CDConv reaching a micro F1-score of 0.452.
- Fine-grained 20-category annotations enable discovery of compartment-specific structural motifs; attention analysis of a Golgi classifier identifies a decisive α-helix transmembrane pattern validated by prior biology.
- Mitigation strategies like reweighting and single-label classification improve recall on minority classes (e.g., Lipid Droplet, Nucleoli) but with trade-offs in precision.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- **Claim:** 3D structural information provides decisive predictive signals for subcellular localization that amino acid sequences alone cannot capture.
- **Mechanism:** Graph neural networks process spatial relationships between residues (via Cα coordinates and 3Di tokens), capturing structural motifs like transmembrane α-helices that directly influence organelle targeting. These geometric patterns encode functional constraints that are degenerate in sequence space.
- **Core assumption:** Protein localization is determined by conformational features (e.g., exposed nuclear localization signals, membrane-spanning domains) that depend on 3D folding, which may be preserved across evolutionarily divergent sequences.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [section 4.2] "the 3D structure is essential for subcellular localization task... CDConv and GearNet-Edge outperform the ESM-C 600M₀" (the randomly initialized baseline).
  - [abstract] "structure-based methods (e.g., CDConv, GearNet-Edge) achieve strong performance" and "CDConv reaching a 0.452 micro F1-score."
  - Corpus: Structure-based benchmarks (HeMeNet, Data-Efficient Protein 3D Geometric Pretraining) outperform sequence-only methods on structural protein tasks.
- **Break condition:** If protein structure prediction confidence (pLDDT) is uniformly low (<50) or the protein is intrinsically disordered with no stable conformation, structure-based methods may fail to extract meaningful geometric patterns.

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim:** Fine-grained 20-category annotation enables discovery of compartment-specific structural patterns.
- **Mechanism:** Distinguishing biologically distinct subcompartments (e.g., Nuclear Membrane vs Nucleoplasm vs Nucleoli) reduces label noise and exposes unique localization determinants. This allows models to learn organelle-specific features (like Golgi transmembrane α-helices) that would be averaged out in coarse classification.
- **Core assumption:** Different subcellular compartments impose distinct biophysical constraints (membrane curvature, lipid composition, pH) that apply selective pressure on protein structure.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [section 4.3.3] Attention analysis identifies α-helix transmembrane pattern specific to Golgi apparatus, validated by prior biology.
  - [supplement A.1] Mapping from HPA/UniProt terms to 20 categories via cell biology textbook (Alberts et al., 2022).
  - [section 1] Coarse-grained compartment classifications "overlooks the unique localization characteristics and mechanisms associated with different organelles."
- **Break condition:** If annotation granularity exceeds biological signal (e.g., splitting compartments without distinct mechanisms) or inter-annotator agreement is low, fine-grained categories could introduce noise.

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim:** Attention mechanisms in Transformer-augmented GNNs yield biologically interpretable localization patterns.
- **Mechanism:** Global attention pooling weights residues based on their contribution to classification. High-attention residues correspond to structural motifs conserved across proteins of the same compartment, revealing "decisive localization patterns."
- **Core assumption:** Model attention correlates with functionally important structural elements; high-attention residues reflect learned decision rules, not random artifacts.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [section 4.3.3] "model consistently highlights similar α-helix spatial conformation... across all targets" with 90% overlap in visualized residues (supplement J).
  - [abstract] "attention analysis of a Golgi apparatus classifier identifies a decisive α-helix transmembrane pattern, validated by prior biology."
  - Pattern aligns with known biology (Paulson & Colley, 1989; Linstedt et al., 1995).
- **Break condition:** Attention may hallucinate patterns if training data is limited or model overfits to spurious correlations (e.g., protein length). Requires external validation (e.g., mutagenesis) to confirm biological relevance.

## Foundational Learning

### Concept: Protein Graph Construction
- **Why needed:** Structure-based models require converting 3D coordinates into graph format (nodes = residues, edges = spatial/sequential adjacency). Understanding edge criteria (radius KNN vs sequential window) and feature engineering (relative distance, orientation) is essential for reproducing CAPSUL experiments.
- **Quick check:** How would you modify the graph construction if you wanted to model protein-protein interactions instead of single-protein localization?

### Concept: Class Imbalance in Biological Benchmarks
- **Why needed:** Subcellular compartments have highly skewed sample sizes (Nucleus: 7,590 vs Lipid Droplet: 94). Standard BCE loss will neglect minority classes, requiring reweighting or single-label strategies.
- **Quick check:** What are the tradeoffs between focal loss (reweighting) and single-label classification for extremely rare classes like Lipid Droplet?

### Concept: AlphaFold2 as Structural Prior
- **Why needed:** CAPSUL relies entirely on predicted structures, not experimental PDBs. Users must understand AlphaFold2's reliability varies (high pLDDT = confident folded domains, low pLDDT = disordered regions) and may affect model performance.
- **Quick check:** If AlphaFold2 confidence is low for a protein, should you exclude it, use sequence fallback, or trust the structure anyway?

## Architecture Onboarding

### Component map:
Data loader → Graph builder → Structure encoder (GCN + Transformer) → Classifier (MLP) → Loss (BCE ± reweighting/contrastive)

### Critical path:
Protein PDB → Cα extraction → Graph construction (KNN edges) → GCN message passing → Transformer attention pooling → MLP classification → BCE loss. For interpretability: extract attention weights → map to residues → visualize.

### Design tradeoffs:
- **Spatial radius:** Smaller radius captures local packing but misses long-range contacts; larger radius increases compute quadratically. CDConv uses dynamic radius; GearNet uses multi-radius KNN.
- **Sequential adjacency:** Adds backbone continuity; too large window may introduce noise from distant sequence segments.
- **Transformer layers:** Adding attention improves interpretability but risks overfitting on small categories. Paper shows 2–3 layers help; more may degrade minority-class performance.
- **Fusion strategy:** Early fusion (add structure tokens to sequence embeddings) vs late fusion (concat pooled embeddings). Paper finds late fusion more stable; early fusion risks overwhelming sequence-pretrained knowledge.

### Failure signatures:
- **Zero predictions** on rare classes in multi-label setting → switch to single-label training or apply focal loss with γ≥3.
- **Attention maps** uniform or focused on termini → model failed to learn meaningful structural motifs; check training data positive sample count for that compartment.
- **Performance disparity** between high/low pLDDT groups → structure quality matters for some compartments (e.g., membrane proteins). Consider filtering by pLDDT threshold.
- **High variance** across runs on minority classes → increase contrastive loss weight or add oversampling of positive samples.

### First 3 experiments:
1. **Replicate CDConv baseline:** Load CAPSUL test set, run CDConv with hyperparameters (kernel channels=24, feature channels=[256,512], spatial adj=[4,8]Å, sequential adj=5, transformer layers=3, heads=2, lr=5e-4). Expect micro F1 ≈ 0.452.
2. **Golgi attention case study:** Train CDConv on Golgi apparatus (binary classification). Extract attention weights from final Transformer layer for 10 highest-attention proteins. Visualize residues (supplement J method). Expect α-helix enrichment in transmembrane regions.
3. **Reweighting ablation:** Train CDConv with focal loss (γ=3, α=log-inverse freq) vs standard BCE. Compare macro-F1 and per-class recall for rare classes (Nucleoli, Lipid Droplet). Expect improved minority-class recall but possible precision drop.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- **Question:** What is the optimal method to align or disentangle amino acid sequence, Cα coordinates, and 3Di token representations for subcellular localization prediction?
- **Basis:** Future work explicitly mentions aligning/disentangling understanding across these dimensions as a critical direction.
- **Why unresolved:** Current fusion strategies (early vs. late) show inconsistent performance gains. When to fuse, how to weight modalities, and whether to learn shared vs. distinct features per compartment remain unknown.
- **Evidence needed:** Systematic study of fusion architectures (cross-attention, gated mechanisms) on CAPSUL, with per-class performance analysis.

### Open Question 2
- **Question:** Can causal discovery methods establish direct links between specific 3D structural features and the mechanism of import/retention for different organelles?
- **Basis:** Future work proposes causal discovery to link 3D structure to localization, aiming for underlying biological principles. Golgi case study suggests causal signals are detectable.
- **Why unresolved:** Paper shows correlational pattern recognition (attention heatmaps). Attention does not imply causation; motifs may be consequences, not causes.
- **Evidence needed:** Apply causal inference (e.g., intervention-based analysis) to perturb identified motifs *in silico* and measure causal effect on predictions. Experimental validation (mutagenesis + microscopy) would be ideal.

### Open Question 3
- **Question:** Why are structure-based models still outperformed by large pre-trained sequence models (ESM-C) on overall micro-F1, despite the biological importance of 3D structure?
- **Basis:** Key outcome states structure-based methods achieve strong performance (CDConv 0.452), but results show ESM-C (0.495) performs better.
- **Why unresolved:** Root cause ambiguous: potential factors include AlphaFold structure noise, richer sequence pretraining data, or suboptimal graph connectivity for localization signals.
- **Evidence needed:** Controlled ablations: (1) give structure-based models perfect (native) PDBs vs. predicted structures; (2) initialize node features with ESM-C embeddings; (3) search graph connectivity rules specific to targeting signals.

### Open Question 4
- **Question:** Can identification of other "decisive localization patterns" (like the α-helix for Golgi) be automated for all 20 compartments, and are they biologically novel?
- **Basis:** Golgi case study used manual examination; paper briefly mentions potential "W-pair" and flexible N-terminal patterns needing validation.
- **Why unresolved:** Current approach is not scalable or systematic. Novelty of proposed patterns (W-pair, etc.) is uncertain and demands experimental verification.
- **Evidence needed:** Automated pipeline to extract and cluster high-attention residues across all 20 classes. Perform motif discovery (MEME, HH-suite) and cross-reference with known signal databases (ELM, CSS-Palm). Experimental validation of top novel candidates.

## Limitations
- CAPSUL relies entirely on AlphaFold2-predicted structures; performance may degrade for low-confidence (pLDDT <50) or intrinsically disordered regions.
- The 20-category annotation scheme lacks reported inter-annotator agreement metrics; splitting compartments could introduce noise if biological distinctions are not sharp.
- Attention interpretation is correlational, not causal; validation is retrospective (matches known biology) with no prospective experimental confirmation.
- Exact train/val/test split IDs and some model hyperparameters are not provided, blocking exact replication.

## Confidence
- **Structure-based superiority (Mechanism 1):** **Medium.** Supported by direct ablations and benchmarks, but external validation limited to similar tasks, not identical.
- **Fine-grained annotation utility (Mechanism 2):** **Medium.** Evidence rests on expert mapping and one case study; lack of agreement statistics or cross-validation across all classes tempers confidence.
- **Attention interpretability (Mechanism 3):** **Low.** Validation is retrospective; no prospective experimental validation, and hallucination risk in low-data regimes is unquantified.

## Next Checks
1. **Stratify performance by AlphaFold2 confidence:** Split test proteins by pLDDT bins (high >80, medium 50–80, low <50). Re-run CDConv to quantify how structure quality drives overall F1, especially for membrane-bound compartments.
2. **Measure annotation consistency:** Recruit two independent cell biologists to label a random subset (e.g., 200 proteins) using the 20-category scheme. Compute Cohen’s κ; low agreement (<0.7) would challenge fine-grained signal assumption.
3. **Prospectively test attention-identified residues:** For Golgi classifier, select 5 high-attention transmembrane α-helices. Design site-directed mutagenesis (e.g., helix-disrupting proline substitutions) in a model Golgi protein (e.g., TGN38) and assay mislocalization via immunofluorescence. Loss of Golgi signal would causally validate the pattern.
