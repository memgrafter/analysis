# OpenACMv2: An Accuracy-Constrained Co-Optimization Framework for Approximate DCiM

## Quick Facts
*   **arXiv ID:** 2603.13042
*   **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.13042
*   **Reference count:** 35
*   **Primary result:** A two-level framework using a GNN surrogate and variation-aware sizing achieves 142-464× evaluation speedup over EDA with <5% prediction error, enabling PDP reductions of up to 67% for multipliers and significant gains for SRAM macros under fixed accuracy budgets.

## Executive Summary
OpenACMv2 introduces Accuracy-Constrained Co-Optimization (ACCO) for approximate computing-in-memory (DCiM) circuits, targeting the joint optimization of architecture-level choices (e.g., compressor topologies, SRAM macro organization) and transistor-level sizing under strict application accuracy constraints. The framework decouples the search into a fast, top-level architecture exploration phase guided by a Graph Neural Network (GNN) surrogate, followed by a bottom-level variation- and PVT-aware transistor sizing phase. Applied to approximate multipliers and SRAM macros, this approach delivers strong power-delay product (PDP) improvements while maintaining task-level accuracy, demonstrating a practical path for approximate hardware design under quality-of-service guarantees.

## Method Summary
The core of OpenACMv2 is a two-level, divide-and-conquer ACCO workflow. Level 1 performs accuracy-constrained architecture search. For approximate multipliers, compressor assignments are encoded into a stage-wise graph and evaluated by the PEA-GNN surrogate, which predicts mean relative error distance (MRED), normalized error distance (NMED), delay, area, and power in ~0.25 seconds. Multi-objective optimizers (e.g., MOEA/D) use these predictions to generate an accuracy-PDP Pareto frontier. For SRAM macros, the search scans discrete bank configurations (row/column splits, mux ratios) under a capacity constraint, evaluating a figure-of-merit via SPICE. Level 2 applies variation-aware transistor sizing (via Monte Carlo simulation) to a shortlist of promising architectures from Level 1, minimizing PDP/area while ensuring functional correctness and robustness across PVT corners, thereby shifting the frontier left without violating accuracy budgets.

## Key Results
*   The PEA-GNN surrogate predicts MRED/NMED, delay, area, and power with R² > 0.95 and mean relative error < 5%, achieving 142× (8-bit) and 464× (16-bit) speedups over full EDA flows.
*   For 8-bit multipliers under MRED ≤ 5.88e-02, PDP is reduced by up to 53% (484 fJ to 229 fJ) while preserving image PSNR. For 16-bit under MRED ≤ 1.47e-02, PDP drops ~67% (3874 fJ to 1289 fJ) with <1% Top-1 accuracy loss.
*   SRAM macro PDP gains are primarily driven by architectural configuration (array organization), with transistor-level bitcell tuning yielding only marginal (<10%) further reductions.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
**Mechanism 1: Decoupled Two-Level Search.** The ACCO framework improves PPA under fixed accuracy budgets by splitting the exponentially coupled architecture-transistor search into sequential stages. Level 1 rapidly explores discrete architecture choices (compressor combinations, SRAM parameters) using a fast surrogate to map the accuracy-PDP trade-off surface. Level 2 then applies variation-aware sizing to refine a shortlist, shifting designs to lower PDP without breaching accuracy constraints. This divide-and-conquer approach makes the problem tractable and stabilizes convergence compared to a monolithic joint optimization, while the authors claim Pareto optimality is preserved. Evidence is drawn from the paper's comparative statements and the successful generation of Pareto frontiers for both multipliers and SRAMs.

**Mechanism 2: GNN Surrogate for Fast High-Fidelity Evaluation.** The PEA-GNN enables large-scale architecture search by predicting circuit PPA and error metrics orders of magnitude faster than EDA. It encodes a multiplier's compressor assignment into a hierarchical, stage-wise graph where nodes represent logic blocks (HA, FA, compressors) and message passing propagates information across compression stages. This structure aligns with the multiplier's physical dataflow, capturing topological and error-propagation characteristics that strongly correlate with final metrics. Evidence includes quantitative validation (R² > 0.95, speedups >142×) and the successful integration of the surrogate into the ACCO optimizer to explore millions of configurations.

**Mechanism 3: Architecture Dominance in SRAM PPA.** For SRAM macros under ACCO, the top-level search over array organization (row/column splits, bank counts) is the primary lever for PPA improvement. This configuration determines global parasitics (bitline/wordline lengths, peripheral loading), which bottleneck delay and dynamic power. Bottom-level transistor sizing of the 6T bitcell provides only fine adjustments because the periphery circuits constrain safe sizing windows and dominate the power-delay trade-off. Evidence is the observation that PDP reductions from Level 2 tuning are minimal (<10%) compared to the spread achieved by different Level 1 bank configurations, and that aggressive bank layouts can render bitcell sizing ineffective due to stability limits.

## Foundational Learning
*   **Concept: Accuracy-Constrained Co-Optimization (ACCO).** Why needed: Approximate DCiM involves coupled architecture and transistor choices; ACCO lifts application accuracy budgets (MRED/NMED) as explicit constraints to guide the search toward energy-efficient designs that meet task quality, preventing over-precision. Quick check: In the ACCO formulation, the NMED constraint complements the MRED objective to capture different error distribution properties; both are used because some applications are sensitive to maximum error (NMED) while others care about average error (MRED).
*   **Concept: Graph Neural Network Surrogates for Fast Physical Evaluation.** Why needed: Full EDA evaluation of one approximate multiplier takes tens of seconds; ACCO requires millions of queries. A GNN surrogate predicting PPA and error in ~0.25s with <5% error is essential for tractable architecture exploration. Quick check: PEA-GNN's stage-wise message passing (Eq. 6) aggregates information within each compression stage before propagating to the next, unlike standard GraphSAGE which mixes all neighbors immediately; this staging preserves the hierarchical error buildup specific to approximate multipliers, improving fidelity.
*   **Concept: Variation- and PVT-Aware Transistor Sizing.** Why needed: Approximate circuits are sensitive to process, voltage, and temperature variations. Optimizing only at nominal corners can yield designs that fail under worst-case conditions. Level 2 uses Monte Carlo simulation across corners to ensure robustness and yield stability for final designs. Quick check: In Eq. 9, replacing metrics like delay D(w) with their worst-case values D_max(w) across a corner set C ensures the optimized widths w meet timing and functionality under all considered variations, not just the typical case.

## Architecture Onboarding
*   **Component map:** Accuracy Budget -> Level 1: Architecture Search (Multiplier: Compressor Config -> PEA-GNN -> {MRED, NMED, PDP}; SRAM: Bank Config -> SPICE -> FOM) -> Filter by Budget -> Level 2: Transistor Sizing (Multiplier/SRAM Bitcell -> PVT Monte Carlo SPICE -> Optimize PDP/Area) -> Pareto-Optimal Designs
*   **Critical path:** For a new task (e.g., 8-bit multiplier with MRED < 0.01): (1) Define search space (8^9 compressor combos). (2) Run Level 1 optimizer (e.g., MOEA/D) with PEA-GNN to generate accuracy-Pareto frontier. (3) Filter architectures meeting MRED budget. (4) For top-N distinct Pareto points, run Level 2 variation-aware sizing. (5) Select final design based on actual PDP post-tuning.
*   **Design tradeoffs:** Surrogate fidelity limits out-of-distribution accuracy; tighter accuracy budgets shrink feasible space and force higher PDP; optimizer choice (MOEA/D vs. SMAC) affects convergence speed; SRAM gains are dominated by macro configuration, making bitcell tuning a costly fine-tuning step with diminishing returns.
*   **Failure signatures:** PEA-GNN MRED prediction error >5% on held-out set (surrogate out-of-distribution); Level 2 sizing fails functional correctness for a compressor type (incompatible with PDK/load); SRAM bank config yields >50% Monte Carlo failures (overly aggressive periphery/bitcell interaction).
*   **First 3 experiments:** (1) Validate PEA-GNN: Sample 500 multiplier configs, compare predictions vs. EDA; expect R² > 0.95, MRE < 5%. (2) Run ACCO for 8-bit with MRED budget 0.05, use MOEA/D; verify Level 2 sizing reduces PDP by ~5% without violating MRED. (3) For SRAM (4KBx8), enumerate bank configs, compute FOM; identify top-3, apply Level 2 bitcell tuning; confirm PDP reduction is small (<10%) vs. spread among top-3.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
*   **Open Question 1:** How can the PEA-GNN surrogate's fidelity and coverage be extended to accurately model circuit performance across full PVT corners, given its current training under nominal conditions?
*   **Open Question 2:** Can the OpenACMv2 ACCO workflow be integrated with complete backend signoff tools (place-and-route, parasitic extraction, crosstalk analysis) while maintaining its convergence benefits and accuracy-constrained Pareto optimization?
*   **Open Question 3:** Does the two-level decoupling of architecture search and transistor sizing for SRAM macros fundamentally limit the achievable PPA improvements under accuracy constraints, compared to a joint, concurrent optimization of macro configuration and bitcell/periphery sizing?
*   **Open Question 4:** How can the ACCO framework be extended to incorporate additional critical objectives like leakage power, peak throughput, and design-for-manufacturability metrics (e.g., yield, routing congestion) without compromising its ability to efficiently navigate the accuracy-constrained PPA trade-off space?

## Limitations
*   The claim that decoupling architecture and transistor search preserves Pareto optimality is not validated against a concurrent joint formulation; evidence is limited to internal comparisons.
*   PEA-GNN's high fidelity may not hold for out-of-distribution compressor topologies or different load/corner conditions; training data diversity and hyperparameters are undisclosed.
*   The assertion that SRAM architectural configuration dominates PPA gains over bitcell tuning is based on a narrow experimental scope; generalizability across technology nodes and array sizes is open.

## Confidence
*   **ACCO two-level decoupling preserves Pareto optimality:** Medium (reliant on internal evidence, lacking external orthogonal validation).
*   **PEA-GNN surrogate achieves high speed/accuracy:** High (quantitative results provided, GNNs established for PPA prediction).
*   **SRAM architecture dominates PPA gains over bitcell tuning:** Medium (supported by experiments but limited in scope).
*   **Framework yields significant PDP reductions under accuracy constraints:** High (consistent results across 8/16-bit multipliers and SRAM).
