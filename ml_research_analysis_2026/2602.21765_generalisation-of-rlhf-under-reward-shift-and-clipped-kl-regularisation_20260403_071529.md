# Generalisation of RLHF under Reward Shift and Clipped KL Regularisation

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2602.21765
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.21765
- Reference count: 40
- Primary result: Derives high-probability bounds decomposing RLHF generalisation error into sampling, reward shift, and KL clipping components, yielding design rules for clipping threshold and sample allocation.

## Executive Summary
Reinforcement Learning from Human Feedback (RLHF) is widely used to align models with human preferences, but its theoretical guarantees are poorly understood, especially when the reward model is learned and may shift, and KL regularisation is estimated with clipping. This paper provides a rigorous analysis using change-of-measure and PAC-Bayesian tools to bound the generalisation error of the RLHF objective. The bounds explicitly account for finite sampling, distribution mismatch between training and deployment, and bias from clipping KL penalties. These theoretical insights lead to practical recommendations: setting the KL clipping threshold based on quantiles of the log-ratio distribution, and optimal allocation between prompts and rollouts given computational costs. The work offers a foundation for understanding and tuning RLHF pipelines.

## Method Summary
The authors analyse the RLHF objective where a policy π_θ is optimized to maximize a reward model ˆr_ϕ trained on human preferences, with KL penalty to a reference policy π_ref. The generalisation gap between the empirical estimator (using n prompts and K rollouts) and the true expected reward is decomposed into three terms: (1) a sampling error arising from finite samples, scaled by the range of the clipped objective (1+2βτ); (2) a reward shift error quantifying amplification of the reward model's training error due to distribution shift, bounded by a coverage coefficient C(θ) = √(1+χ²(D_θ||D_train)); and (3) a KL clipping bias that persists even with infinite data, equal to β E_{D_θ}[|ℓ_θ - ℓ_θ^τ|]. For data-dependent policy selection (e.g., via SGD), a PAC-Bayesian extension introduces an additional KL(Q||P) complexity term. The analysis relies on assumptions: rewards bounded in [0,1]; KL log-ratios clipped; absolute continuity between D_θ and D_train; finite training error and integrable log-ratios.

## Key Results
- **Sampling error** scales as (1+2βτ)(√(log δ/n) + √(log δ/(nK))).
- **Reward shift error** = C(θ) √L_train(ϕ), where C(θ)=√(1+χ²(D_θ||D_train)) captures distribution mismatch.
- **KL clipping error** = β E_{D_θ}[(|ℓ_θ| - τ)_+] is a non-vanishing bias.
- **Optimal design rules**: set τ to the (1-2α_{n,K,δ})-quantile of |ℓ_θ| under D_θ; under budget constraints, n ∝ B/c_prefill and K ∝ (c_prefill/c_decode)^{2/3} when prefill and decode costs differ.
- **PAC-Bayes extension** handles data-dependent policy selection via KL(Q||P).

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1: Sampling Error
The empirical objective averages over n prompts and K rollouts, with each per-sample value r - β ℓ^τ_θ bounded within an interval of length 1+2βτ due to reward bounds [0,1] and clipping to [-τ, τ]. Applying Hoeffding’s inequality in two stages (first for prompts, then for rollouts within prompts) yields a high-probability bound scaling as (1+2βτ) α_{n,K,δ}, where α_{n,K,δ} = √(log(4/δ)/(2n)) + √(log(4/δ)/(2nK)). This quantifies the effect of sample sizes and clipping on variance.

### Mechanism 2: Reward Shift
The bias between the expected reward under the deployment distribution D_θ and the reward model's training error arises because ˆr_ϕ is evaluated on data from D_train. Using a χ² change-of-measure, the absolute error |E_{D_θ}[e_ϕ(x,y)]| ≤ √(1+χ²(D_θ||D_train)) √E_{D_train}[e_ϕ(x,y)²] = C(θ) √L_train(ϕ). The factor C(θ) measures how much D_θ differs from D_train; if the policy drifts far from π_ref or the prompt distribution shifts, C(θ) can become large even if the reward model is accurate on D_train.

### Mechanism 3: KL Clipping Bias
The use of clipped log-ratios ℓ^τ_θ instead of the exact ℓ_θ introduces a deterministic bias: |J_{ϕ,τ}(θ) - J_ϕ(θ)| = β E_{D_θ}[|ℓ_θ - ℓ^τ_θ|] = β E_{D_θ}[(|ℓ_θ| - τ)_+]. This term does not vanish with more samples; it persists as a systematic error because the objective maximized during training differs from the intended unclipped objective. The magnitude depends on the tail behaviour of ℓ_θ under D_θ and the threshold τ.

## Foundational Learning
- **PAC-Bayesian generalisation bounds**: These bounds extend the analysis to settings where the policy θ is selected based on the data (e.g., via SGD). The complexity term √((KL(Q||P)+log(8/δ))/(2n)) replaces the fixed-θ sampling error and accounts for the optimisation over θ. Here, Q is the posterior over policies and P is a prior; the KL divergence measures how much the learned policy deviates from the prior. This term grows with the complexity of the policy class and the amount of data n, and it ensures that the bound holds uniformly over all policies in the support of Q.
- **χ² divergence and change-of-measure**: The χ² divergence χ²(D_θ||D_train) measures the discrepancy between the deployment distribution D_θ (induced by policy π_θ) and the training distribution D_train. The bound uses a change-of-measure technique, applying Cauchy–Schwarz to relate expectations under D_θ to those under D_train. The finiteness of χ² is required for the Cauchy–Schwarz step to be valid; if D_θ has support outside D_train, the χ² divergence can be infinite, making the bound degenerate. In practice, this coefficient captures how much the policy's distribution has drifted from the reference distribution used to train the reward model.
- **Clipped KL regularisation as objective mismatch**: Clipping the per-sample log-ratio ℓ_θ to [-τ, τ] introduces a deterministic bias that does not vanish with more samples. The bias term β E_{D_θ}[(|ℓ_θ| - τ)_+] decreases as τ increases (since fewer samples are clipped) but increases the sampling variance because the range of the clipped objective becomes 1+2βτ. This creates a bias-variance trade-off: a larger τ reduces bias but may increase variance, while a smaller τ reduces variance but introduces more bias. The optimal τ proposed by the paper balances these competing effects.

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component map**: Preference data from (ρ_label, π_ref) → reward model ˆr_ϕ; during optimisation, ρ provides prompts, π_θ generates rollouts; together with clipped KL (using π_ref) they form the estimator bJ_ϕ,τ_n,K; deployment uses D_θ = ρ π_θ.  
**Critical path**: (1) Collect preference data under π_ref (or mixture) to train ˆr_ϕ. (2) For policy optimisation: sample prompts x_i∼ρ, generate rollouts y_{i,j}∼π_θ(·|x_i). (3) Compute bJ_ϕ,τ_n,K(θ) using clipped ℓ_θ. (4) Update θ to increase bJ_ϕ,τ_n,K. (5) At convergence, generalisation gap bounded by Theorem 1/2.  
**Design tradeoffs**: τ balances clipping bias (decreases with τ) against sampling variance (increases with τ via factor 1+2βτ). Corollary 3 suggests τ as the (1-2α_{n,K,δ})-quantile of |ℓ_θ| under D_θ. For fixed computational budget, optimal n and K depend on prefill/decode costs: if c_decode > c_prefill, K>1; under uniform cost, K=1 (Corollary 4).  
**Failure signatures**: 
- Large reward shift error: C(θ) √L_train(ϕ) dominates; check if policy drifted far from π_ref (large C_pol(θ)) or prompt distribution differs (large C_prompt).
- Large KL clipping error: β E_{D_θ}[(|ℓ_θ|-τ)_+] high; inspect histogram of |ℓ_θ| on rollouts; heavy tails need larger τ.
- Sampling error dominates: α_{n,K,δ} large; monitor estimator stability across seeds; increase n or K.
**First 3 experiments**:
1. Validate sampling error scaling: fix θ,ϕ,τ; vary n,K; plot |bJ - J^*| vs bound (1+2βτ)α_{n,K,δ}; expect O(1/√n + 1/√(nK)).
2. Stress test reward shift: train ˆr_ϕ on data from π_ref but evaluate policies with increasing divergence; measure C(θ) via importance weights and verify shift error grows.
3. Calibrate KL clipping threshold: for a trained π_θ, compute empirical quantiles of |ℓ_θ|; set τ to the (1-2α)-quantile vs fixed values; compare generalisation gap and stability.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- **Optimal budget split** between preference labels, prompts, and rollouts? The analysis only optimizes prompts/rollouts; inclusion of preference sample size is an open extension.
- **Does empirical quantile τ selection preserve high-probability guarantees?** The proposed rule uses data from D_θ; without sample splitting or uniformity arguments, the guarantee may not hold.
- **How to estimate coverage coefficient C(θ)** to make the reward shift bound actionable? The bound contains C(θ) which requires χ²(D_θ||D_train), often inestimable in practice.
- **How to incorporate reward model generalization error** from finite preference data? The current bound treats L_train(ϕ) as an oracle, ignoring its estimation from finite data; an end-to-end bound would be valuable.

## Limitations
- **Theoretical assumptions** (absolute continuity, bounded rewards, integrable log-ratios) may not hold for large language models with unbounded outputs.
- **Coverage coefficient C(θ) is often inestimable** in high dimensions due to unstable importance weights.
- **Optimal τ rule introduces extra estimation error** because the quantile is computed from the same evaluation batch, potentially breaking the high-probability guarantee.
- **PAC-Bayes prior choice is arbitrary** and heavily influences the complexity term; guidance for neural policies is lacking.

## Confidence
- **Decomposition & bounds (Theorem 1, Lemmas 4–7)**: High – rigorous derivation under clear assumptions.
- **PAC-Bayesian extension (Theorem 2)**: Medium – bound valid but complexity term may dominate in practice.
- **Design rules for τ and budget (Corollaries 3–5)**: Medium – optimal under the bound but require accurate estimation of unknown quantities; bounds may be loose.

## Next Checks

2. **Reward shift estimation**: With known D_θ and D_train (e.g., Gaussians), compute C(θ) exactly and compare C(θ)√L_train(ϕ) to actual shift.
3. **KL clipping threshold rule**: Implement RLHF with a simple policy; set τ to the empirical (1-2α)-quantile of |ℓ_θ| vs fixed values; measure generalisation gap and stability.
