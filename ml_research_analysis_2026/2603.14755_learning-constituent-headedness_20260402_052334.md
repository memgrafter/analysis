# Learning Constituent Headedness

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2603.14755
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.14755
- Reference count: 21
- One-line primary result: Treating constituent headedness as an explicit, supervised layer induced from aligned constituency-dependency trees yields near-ceiling accuracy and dramatically improves deterministic constituency-to-dependency conversion, far surpassing handcrafted percolation rules.

## Executive Summary
Constituency treebanks typically omit explicit headedness information, forcing downstream applications (e.g., binarization, conversion to dependencies) to rely on handcrafted percolation rules that are brittle and treebank-specific. This work reframes headedness as an explicit, learnable layer. The authors induce gold head children by aligning constituency trees with dependency trees: a constituent’s head is the unique token within its yield whose dependency governor lies outside the span. They train a supervised predictor using only syntactic category labels (parent and child labels) to select the head child. On English (PTB) and Chinese (CTB) data, the learned predictor achieves 98.54% and 100% intrinsic head accuracy, respectively, vastly outperforming Collins-style rule-based baselines (67.89% En, 92.42% Zh). When used for head-driven binarization, learned heads produce nearly identical parsing accuracy to rule-based heads, suggesting the binary supervision targets are largely equivalent. However, for deterministic constituency-to-dependency conversion, which is highly sensitive to head errors, learned heads raise UAS from ~51% to ~95% (En) and from ~78% to ~92% (Zh). Cross-resource transfer experiments show that generalization depends critically on compatibility of head conventions (e.g., PP directionality) between source and target treebanks.

## Method Summary
The core method involves three stages: supervision induction, head prediction, and downstream application. First, for each non-terminal constituent in an aligned constituency-dependency treebank, the gold head child is determined via the “dependency span-head” criterion: the token within the constituent’s yield whose dependency governor lies outside the yield is the span head; the child dominating that token is the head child. Only sentences where every constituent has exactly one such token are retained, ensuring unambiguous labels. Second, a supervised classifier is trained to predict the head-child index given the parent label and ordered list of child labels. The model uses a Transformer encoder (e.g., BERT) and an MLP head, trained with cross-entropy loss on the induced instances. Third, the trained predictor is applied to new constituency trees to supply head children for two downstream tasks: (1) head-driven binarization, where the head child guides binarization and the resulting binary trees are parsed; (2) deterministic constituency-to-dependency conversion, where head children drive a bottom-up projection of arcs from constituency structure onto a dependency tree. Performance is evaluated intrinsically (head accuracy) and downstream (parsing F1 for binarization; UAS for conversion). Transfer experiments map target treebank labels to the source label space to test cross-resource generalization.

## Key Results
- **Intrinsic head accuracy:** 98.54% (English) and 100% (Chinese), far exceeding rule-based baselines (67.89% En, 92.42% Zh).
- **Conversion UAS:** Learned heads yield 94.99% (En) and 92.22% (Zh), dramatically improving over rule-based heads (51.39% En, 77.73% Zh).
- **Binarization parsing:** Labeled bracket F1 is nearly identical between rule-based and learned heads (En: 96.24→96.32; Zh: 93.84→94.20), indicating largely equivalent binary training targets.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1: Gold Heads via Dependency Span-Head Criterion
- **Claim:** Defining gold heads via the dependency span-head criterion yields consistent, reproducible supervision that aligns constituency and dependency representations.
- **Mechanism:** For each non-terminal constituent, the token whose dependency governor lies outside the span is the span head; the child dominating that token becomes the head child. This deterministic mapping from aligned trees provides unambiguous labels.
- **Core assumption:** The dependency annotation correctly captures head–dependent relations and the constituency tree is token-aligned; the uniqueness condition holds for all constituents.
- **Evidence anchors:**  
  [abstract] "inducing supervision by defining each constituent head as the dependency span head."  
  [section] Section 3.2 details the derivation: "the head of each constituent as its dependency span head" and "uniqueness condition."  
  [corpus] Table 1 shows the gold standard (via alignment) is clear enough for a learned model to reach 98.54% (English) and 100% (Chinese) intrinsic accuracy, while rule-based heads lag far behind.
- **Break condition:** Alignment errors or constituents with multiple or zero span-head candidates (|H(v)| ≠ 1) cannot yield a gold head; such sentences are filtered out, limiting supervision coverage.

### Mechanism 2: Predictability from Local Category Configurations
- **Claim:** A supervised predictor using only syntactic categories (parent and child labels) can recover head children with near-ceiling accuracy, showing headedness is predictable from local configuration.
- **Mechanism:** The model encodes the parent label and ordered child labels (unlexicalized) via a Transformer, then classifies the head-child index. It learns the mapping from local category patterns to the gold head child induced in Mechanism 1.
- **Core assumption:** Head selection depends primarily on the syntactic categories and their order, not on lexical or semantic cues; the mapping is learnable from the training distribution.
- **Evidence anchors:**  
  [abstract] "On aligned English and Chinese data, the resulting models achieve near‑ceiling intrinsic accuracy and substantially outperform Collins‑style percolation."  
  [section] Section 4.1 reports 98.54% (En) and 100% (Zh) head prediction accuracy with an unlexicalized feature set.  
  [corpus] Table 1 contrasts rule‑based (67.89% En, 92.42% Zh) vs learned (98.54% En, 100% Zh), confirming the strong signal in the category configuration.
- **Break condition:** If the target treebank uses different label inventories or ambiguous local configurations (e.g., cross‑resource transfer), accuracy may degrade; unseen category combinations cause out‑of‑distribution errors.

### Mechanism 3: Head Choice Critical for Deterministic Conversion
- **Claim:** Accurate head assignments dramatically improve deterministic constituency‑to‑dependency conversion fidelity because head choice directly controls attachment decisions.
- **Mechanism:** The conversion algorithm traverses the constituency tree, using the predicted head child to select the head token of each span. All non‑head children attach to that token. A single wrong head can flip attachment direction and propagate to multiple token‑level errors.
- **Core assumption:** The conversion procedure is deterministic and head choice is the only varying factor; other steps (e.g., relation labeling) are fixed.
- **Evidence anchors:**  
  [abstract] "For deterministic constituency‑to‑dependency conversion, learned heads dramatically boost UAS (94.99% En, 92.22% Zh vs rule‑based 51.39% En, 77.73% Zh)."  
  [section] Section 4.3 describes the conversion algorithm and presents Table 3 with the large UAS gap.  
  [corpus] Table 3 quantifies the improvement, linking higher head accuracy (Mechanism 2) to near‑gold conversion.
- **Break condition:** Head prediction errors, especially on frequent constituents (e.g., PPs), directly cause attachment errors; if the target dependency scheme uses conventions incompatible with the alignment‑induced gold heads, conversion may still be imperfect even with high intrinsic head accuracy.

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component map:** Data aligner → Head induction module → Instance generator → Head predictor → Training loop → Downstream consumers (head‑driven binarizer, constituency‑to‑dependency converter) → Evaluators → Transfer adapter.

**Critical path:** Aligned treebanks → head induction (span-head criterion) → instance generation (parent/child labels + head index) → supervised training (Transformer + MLP) → trained predictor → predict heads on new constituency trees → feed into downstream task (binarization or conversion) → evaluate (head accuracy, parsing F1, UAS).

**Design tradeoffs:**
- Unlexicalized vs lexicalized input: unlexicalized reduces sparsity and works near ceiling; lexicalized may help on rare configurations but adds complexity.
- Rule‑based vs learned heads: rules are static and treebank‑specific; learned model adapts to the alignment‑derived convention but requires aligned data.
- Binarization strategy: head‑driven preserves head projection properties but introduces intermediate nodes; other strategies (e.g., balanced) may change parsing dynamics.
- Transfer simplicity: deterministic label mapping (coarse category collapsing) is transparent but fails on systematic convention differences; more complex mapping would need manual effort.

**Failure signatures:**
- Sharp drop in head accuracy for specific phrase types (e.g., PPs) during cross‑resource transfer, indicating convention mismatches.
- Conversion UAS collapsing while binarization parsing F1 remains stable, showing conversion’s higher sensitivity to head errors.
- Low head accuracy on low-resource label combinations suggests insufficient coverage in training data.

**First 3 experiments:**
1. Intrinsic head prediction: Train/evaluate the head classifier on gold constituency trees with alignment‑induced heads; report accuracy.
2. Head‑driven binarization stability: Train fixed parsing architecture on binarized trees from rule‑based vs learned heads; compare labeled bracket F1.
3. Deterministic conversion fidelity: Convert gold constituency trees to dependencies using rule‑based vs learned heads; compare UAS against gold dependencies.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
### Open Question 1
- **Question:** How can we explicitly model systematic convention differences (e.g., head directionality, function word attachment) to improve cross-resource and cross-lingual generalization of constituent headedness?
- **Basis in paper:** [explicit] The authors state in the conclusion (Page 7) that future work should "explicitly modeling systematic convention differences such as head directionality and function word attachment" to strengthen generalization.
- **Why unresolved:** The paper shows transfer success depends on head convention compatibility (e.g., PP directionality mismatch between PTB and FTB), but no method is provided to automatically handle such differences.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Developing a model that identifies and adjusts for known convention differences (e.g., learning a transformation for PP head directionality) and evaluating it on diverse resource pairs with varying conventions.

### Open Question 2
- **Question:** Can we automatically detect head convention incompatibilities between source and target resources to predict transfer success or guide adaptation?
- **Basis in paper:** [explicit] The transfer experiments (Page 6) reveal that PP head directionality differences cause catastrophic failure (PTB→FTB accuracy 0.09% on PPs vs. CTB→FTB 89.81%), and the authors note that "transfer success is largely explained by the compatibility of head conventions."
- **Why unresolved:** The authors use simple label mapping but do not propose a method to detect or quantify such incompatibilities automatically, leaving transfer success unpredictable without evaluation.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Creating a diagnostic that measures head convention similarity (e.g., based on head directionality statistics per phrase type) and correlating it with transfer accuracy across multiple resource pairs.

### Open Question 3
- **Question:** Does the dependency-grounded definition of headedness (span-head criterion) align with linguistic theories of headhood, or does it merely reflect annotation-specific conventions, limiting its theoretical interpretability?
- **Basis in paper:** [inferred] The paper defines gold heads via the dependency span-head (Page 3-4) and acknowledges (Page 6) that this is "dependency-grounded" and does not aim to reconstruct theoretical headedness. However, they do not discuss alignment with linguistic theories.
- **Why unresolved:** The approach is practical for aligned resources, but it's unclear whether the induced heads correspond to linguistically motivated head notions (e.g., X-bar theory heads) or are purely dictated by dependency annotation guidelines.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Comparing the dependency-induced heads with linguist annotations or theoretical predictions on a subset of sentences, or analyzing cases where dependency and constituency conventions diverge.

### Open Question 4
- **Question:** Under what grammatical or structural conditions does head choice significantly impact constituency parsing accuracy beyond head-driven binarization, given the observed stability in Experiment 2?
- **Basis in paper:** [inferred] Experiment 2 shows minimal labeled bracket F1 differences between rule-based and learned heads (Page 5), and the authors attribute this to "induced binary training targets being largely equivalent." However, they note that spine identity is low (38.75% complete match for English), suggesting local differences that don't affect debinarization.
- **Why unresolved:** The stability might not hold for all languages, treebanks, or parsing architectures. Head choice could matter more for certain constructions (e.g., long-distance dependencies) or when binarization is not reversible.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Systematically varying head conventions across diverse languages and parsing models, measuring parsing accuracy, and correlating with structural properties like phrase length or head-percolation ambiguity.

## Limitations
- **Unspecified label normalization:** The exact rules for removing functional/morphological suffixes from phrase labels and POS tags are not detailed; variations could affect label inventories and model performance.
- **Chinese baseline rules missing:** The precise Collins-style percolation rules used for Chinese (e.g., version of Stanford CoreNLP tables) are not provided, hindering exact reproduction of the rule-based baseline.
- **Binarization punctuation handling unclear:** The punctuation-aware normalization applied during head-driven binarization is described conceptually without specifying delimiters, delimiter-pair mappings, or unary-collapse details.

## Confidence
- **Gold head induction (Mechanism 1):** High. The span-head criterion is deterministic and yields unambiguous supervision for >99% of constituents in PTB/CTB; high intrinsic accuracy supports this.
- **Head predictor accuracy (Mechanism 2):** High for in-domain English/Chinese (98.54% En, 100% Zh); Medium for cross-resource transfer, where convention differences cause degradation.
- **Conversion improvement (Mechanism 3):** High. The large UAS gains (~43 pp En, ~15 pp Zh) robustly demonstrate that head choice is critical for deterministic conversion.
- **Binarization stability:** High. Minimal F1 differences across languages indicate that, for parsing, the exact head choice within the head-driven binarization scheme does not substantially alter the debinarized trees.
