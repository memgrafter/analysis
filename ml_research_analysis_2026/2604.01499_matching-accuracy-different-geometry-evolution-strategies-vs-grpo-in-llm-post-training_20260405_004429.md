# Matching Accuracy, Different Geometry: Evolution Strategies vs GRPO in LLM Post-Training

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2604.01499
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.01499
- **Reference count:** 40
- **Primary result:** Evolution Strategies (ES) and Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO) achieve comparable single-task accuracy but induce geometrically distinct solutions; ES causes ~100× larger weight updates and orthogonal directions due to diffuse random-walk behavior on flat loss subspaces.

## Executive Summary
This study compares Evolution Strategies (ES) and Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO) for post‑training large language models. Both optimizers reach similar task performance, yet ES produces far larger parameter norm changes and broader off‑task KL divergence. Despite nearly orthogonal update directions, ES and GRPO solutions are linearly connected with no loss barrier. A theoretical analysis reveals that ES behaves as a random walk on weakly informative (flat) directions, accumulating diffuse shifts while still progressing on the task. These findings demonstrate that gradient‑free and gradient‑based fine‑tuning can yield equally accurate but fundamentally different solutions, with implications for catastrophic forgetting and knowledge retention.

## Method Summary
Experiments use full‑parameter fine‑tuning of Qwen3‑4B‑Instruct‑2507 on four tasks (Countdown, MATH, Chemistry, BoolQ) with 200 training prompts each. Evolution Strategies (ES) employs a population size N=30, noise scale σ=0.0015, and step size α=σ/2. Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO) uses learning rate 1e‑5, group size K=8, and no KL penalty (β_KL=0). Both single‑task (ES: 300 steps; GRPO: 20 epochs) and sequential continual‑learning protocols are evaluated. Geometric analyses include weight‑update norms, linear interpolation between checkpoints, and probing accuracy/holdout degradation along update directions. A theoretical model decomposes ES updates into on‑manifold signal and off‑manifold diffusion components.

## Key Results
- ES attains comparable or higher single‑task accuracy than GRPO but induces ~100× larger ℓ₂ weight‑update norms and markedly broader off‑task KL drift.
- Despite nearly orthogonal update directions (cosine similarity ~O(√r/d)), ES and GRPO checkpoints are linearly connected with no loss barrier.
- Theoretical analysis shows ES performs a random walk on flat directions, causing total weight‑update norm to grow linearly with iteration count T, explaining the diffuse parameter shifts.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
**Claim:** ES updates decompose into an on‑manifold (task‑relevant) component resembling gradient descent and an off‑manifold (task‑irrelevant) diffusion component that behaves as a random walk.
**Mechanism:** On a locally quadratic loss landscape, ES displacement after T steps is θ_ES – θ_0 = (H^T – I)θ_0 (signal) + Σ_{t=1}^T H^{t-1} η_{T‑t} (noise), where H = I – (ασ/σ_R)Q and Q is the Hessian. The signal lies in the column space of Q and vanishes on flat directions (λ_k=0); the noise term η_t ~ N(0, α²/N I) is isotropic.
**Evidence anchors:** Abstract cites the unified theoretical framework; Section 6 (Equation 5, Proposition 4) formalizes the decomposition; Figure 6B schematically illustrates the geometry.
**Break condition:** If the loss landscape has negligible flat directions (all λ_k > ε), the diffusion term would be bounded and ES would behave more like gradient‑based methods.

### Mechanism 2
**Claim:** On flat directions, ES performs a pure random walk, causing total weight‑update norm to grow linearly with iteration count T and parameter dimension d: E[∥Δθ∥²] ≈ α² T d / N.
**Mechanism:** On a constant‑reward (flat) subspace, each ES update is an isotropic Gaussian with variance α²/N per component. After T steps, expected squared displacement sums to α² T d / N.
**Evidence anchors:** Abstract attributes large norm differences to random‑walk behavior; Section 6.1 and Appendix C.1 show ∥Δθ∥² scales linearly with T for ES (R² > 0.99) and per‑parameter analysis (Figure 10) finds effective dimension ratio d_eff/d ≈ 0.968.
**Break condition:** If population size N scales linearly with d (N ~ d), the growth could be bounded; with fixed small N (30) it is unavoidable.

### Mechanism 3
**Claim:** Because ES diffuses across nearly all d dimensions while GRPO moves primarily within a low‑dimensional task‑relevant subspace (rank r), their update directions become nearly orthogonal (expected cosine similarity ~ O(√r/d)).
**Mechanism:** Gradient‑based displacements lie in the column space of Q (dimension r), so ∥θ_GD – θ_0∥² = O(r). ES adds an off‑manifold random walk of size O(√d). Their inner product is dominated by the on‑manifold signal O(r), yielding cosine similarity scaling as √r/d.
**Evidence anchors:** Abstract notes near‑orthogonal directions yet linear connectivity; Section 5.2 shows no loss barrier in interpolation; Section 5.3/Figure 4 contrasts ES’s gradual, random‑like accuracy improvements with GRPO’s sharp response; Remark 9 and Equation (9) quantify the orthogonality.
**Break condition:** If the task‑relevant subspace is high‑rank (r ~ d), the on‑manifold component would dominate and directions would not be orthogonal.

## Architecture Onboarding
Component map: Base LLM (Qwen3‑4B‑Instruct‑2507) -> Four tasks (Countdown, MATH, Chemistry, BoolQ) -> Optimizers (ES with N=30, σ=0.0015, α=σ/2; GRPO with lr=1e‑5, β_KL=0, K=8) -> Sequential training protocol (Countdown → Math → Chemistry → BoolQ) -> Evaluation metrics (task accuracy, MMLU/IFEVal, incremental KL, weight‑update norms) -> Analysis suites (linear interpolation, landscape probing) -> Theoretical model (signal‑diffusion decomposition).

Critical path: (I) Single‑task training to establish comparable accuracy (Table 1, Figures 7‑8). (II) Sequential training to observe forgetting and KL drift (Figure 1, Figure 2). (III) Weight‑space geometry: norms, linear connectivity, and direction probing (Table 3, Figures 3‑5). (IV) Derive theoretical framework (Section 6, Appendices).

Design tradeoffs: Disabling KL penalty (β_KL=0) isolates pure optimizer behavior. Limiting ES to 100 iterations in sequential training prevents excessive forgetting (300 iterations caused degradation). Full‑parameter fine‑tuning captures entire weight‑space geometry. Model size (4B) balances feasibility and capacity.

Failure signatures: ES divergence (NaN) if N too small or σ too large. GRPO collapse into random policy when KL penalty disabled. Out‑of‑memory despite full‑parameter claim. Loss barrier during linear interpolation would contradict same‑basin conclusion. Deviation from linear ∥Δθ∥² ∝ T scaling would invalidate random‑walk theory.

First 3 experiments: 1) Single‑task performance: Train ES (300 steps) and GRPO (20 epochs) on each task; record final test accuracy. 2) Sequential continual learning: Train tasks in fixed order; after each stage evaluate all tasks to produce forgetting curves. 3) Weight geometry and connectivity: Compute ℓ₂ norms of weight deltas; linearly interpolate between ES and GRPO checkpoints; measure accuracy/holdout performance along update directions.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
**Open Question 1:** Do ES's large weight updates directly cause its increased forgetting compared to GRPO? The paper shows correlation but not causality; a controlled experiment matching norms while holding other factors constant is needed.
**Open Question 2:** Does the random walk scaling law (E[∥Δθ∥²] ∝ α²Td/N) generalize to other LLM architectures and gradient‑free methods? It is validated only on Qwen3‑4B; adaptive methods (e.g., CMA‑ES) or different models may alter scaling.
**Open Question 3:** Does ES's diffuse geometry confer any advantage for forward transfer or multitask learning? ES shows some forward transfer where GRPO does not, but it is unclear whether diffuse updates actively help or are incidental.

## Limitations
- Data splits (exact 200 training prompts per task) are not released; random sampling may yield different results.
- Key hyperparameters unspecified: GRPO clip range ε, ES z‑score normalization details, exact model identifier for “Qwen3‑4B‑Instruct‑2507”.
- Experiments limited to a single 4B model and four tasks; generalizability to larger models or diverse task sets is unknown.
- Full‑parameter fine‑tuning details (e.g., treatment of tied LM head) may affect reported norms.
- Reward function (binary 0/1) and evaluation temperature are unreported, potentially influencing scores.
- *The theoretical analysis relies on a locally quadratic approximation and a low‑rank Hessian assumption, which may not fully capture the highly non‑convex and complex loss landscape of LLM fine‑tuning.*
