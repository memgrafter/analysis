# Unified Learning-to-Rank for Multi-Channel Retrieval in Large-Scale E-Commerce Search

## Quick Facts
- **ArXiv ID:** 2602.23530  
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.23530  
- **Reference count:** 22  
- **Primary result:** Unified GBDT reranker yields +2.85% conversion lift and +1.46% CTR over baseline while meeting p95 latency <50 ms.

## Executive Summary
This paper addresses the ineffective fusion of multiple retrieval channels in e-commerce search, where static methods like Reciprocal Rank Fusion ignore query-specific channel utility. The authors propose a single Gradient Boosted Decision Trees (GBDT) reranker that jointly ingests channel-specific scores, recent user engagement features, and a conversion-weighted label to optimize clicks, add-to-carts, and purchases. Large-scale online A/B testing on Target.com demonstrates a +2.85% improvement in user conversion and a +1.46% lift in click-through rate compared to a weighted interleaving baseline. The model also achieves a +13.6% relative improvement in offline NDCG@8 (0.7994 vs. 0.6620) while satisfying a strict p95 latency of under 50 ms.

## Method Summary
The solution constructs a training dataset by aggregating search logs at the query-item-week level. Features include item attributes, multi-window engagement aggregates (clicks, add-to-carts, purchases), and channel-specific retrieval scores from heterogeneous sources (e.g., lexical, semantic, freshness). Labels are a weighted sum of user actions: purchases (weight = 1), add-to-carts (weight = |P|/|A|), clicks (weight = |P|/|C|), normalized per query to a [0, 4] scale. Data is filtered to queries with ≥20 impressions and ≥1 purchase per week, yielding ~60M instances from 500k queries. A LambdaMART GBDT model is trained with hyperparameter tuning on a validation set. At serving time, the model fetches pre-computed features for candidate items (union of top‑n from each channel), scores them, and returns a ranked list, all within the 50 ms p95 latency budget.

## Key Results
- Online A/B test (Target.com): +2.85% user conversion lift, +1.46% CTR lift vs. weighted interleaving.
- Offline NDCG@8: 0.7994 vs. 0.6620 baseline (+13.6% relative improvement).
- Latency: p95 inference < 50 ms, meeting production requirement.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1: Query-Dependent Channel Utility
The model learns query-specific channel importance by treating channel scores as features in a unified GBDT, rather than relying on fixed global weights. Non-linear interactions between channel scores and other query-item features allow the model to dynamically weight each channel's contribution based on context. The core assumption is that the relative utility of a channel for a query is a learnable function of the query-item pair and engagement signals. Evidence includes the paper's emphasis on "channel-specific scores" and section 2.4's channel-aware features. A break condition occurs if channel scores are noisy, uncalibrated, or missing for many queries, preventing reliable weight learning.

### Mechanism 2: Conversion-Weighted Engagement Label
Labels are a weighted sum of purchases (highest weight), add-to-carts, and clicks, derived from corpus-level conversion rates. This aligns the ranking objective directly with the primary business KPI (conversion) by teaching the model to prioritize purchase-prone items. The core assumption is that a linearly weighted combination of downstream actions effectively proxies conversion optimization. Evidence: the label formula in section 2.3 and the online conversion lift in section 4.3. A break condition arises if the relationship between intermediate actions and purchase is non-linear or highly category-dependent, causing misalignment.

### Mechanism 3: Recent User Engagement Features
Incorporating engagement features over multiple lookback windows (e.g., short-term clicks, add-to-carts) captures short-term intent shifts, enabling the model to boost currently relevant items regardless of retrieval channel. The core assumption is that recent interactions for a query-item pair are predictive of near-future conversion. Evidence: the abstract's mention of "recent user behavioral signals" and section 2.4's query-item engagement features; ablation in section 4.2 shows major lifts when these features are included. A break condition is poorly chosen recency windows or feature computation delays that make the signal stale or noisy.

## Foundational Learning
- **Concept: Gradient Boosted Decision Trees (GBDT) for Ranking**  
  *Why needed:* The core model is LambdaMART GBDT; understanding ensemble learning, gradient boosting, and ranking-specific objectives (e.g., NDCG-based pairwise loss) is essential to interpret model behavior and limitations.  
  *Quick check:* How does the LambdaMART gradient (lambda) differ from a typical regression gradient, and why is it suitable for ranking?

- **Concept: Multi-Channel Retrieval Systems**  
  *Why needed:* The problem stems from merging outputs from heterogeneous channels (lexical, semantic, freshness); understanding channel trade-offs (recall vs. precision, exploration vs. exploitation) is key.  
  *Quick check:* What might be two distinct objectives optimized by two different retrieval channels in an e-commerce search system?

- **Concept: Feature Engineering for Temporal Dynamics**  
  *Why needed:* The model relies on weekly-aggregated features (long-term popularity, short-term trends, recent engagement). Misunderstanding temporal aggregation, lookback windows, or leakage can invalidate the model.  
  *Quick check:* Why is aggregating labels and features at a weekly granularity a design choice to balance responsiveness and stability?

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component map:**  
Query -> Retrieval Channels (BM25, embedding-based, freshness) -> Candidate Pool (union of top‑n from each channel) -> Feature Store (pre‑computed query‑item‑week features) -> GBDT Ranking Service -> Sorted Results

**Critical path:**  
Query triggers parallel retrieval from channels, builds candidate set, fetches pre‑computed features from the store, applies GBDT inference, returns ranked list. Latency is dominated by feature fetch + model inference.

**Design tradeoffs:**  
- *Latency vs. Feature Richness:* More temporal engagement features improve prediction but increase lookup complexity; lookback window choice is a key tradeoff.  
- *Generality vs. Specialization:* A single unified model simplifies maintenance but must handle diverse items/queries; weekly aggregation helps but may dilute signals for volatile queries.  
- *Optimization Target:* Conversion‑weighted label optimizes for purchase but may under‑optimize click‑related metrics; the tradeoff is explicit in label design.

**Failure signatures:**  
- Latency spike (p95 > 50 ms): Check feature store lookup times and GBDT model complexity (tree depth, number of trees).  
- Conversion drop in A/B: Monitor per‑channel contribution; a drop may indicate over‑deprioritization of a specific channel due to biased recent engagement data.  
- Head query degradation: Strict data filtering (≥20 impressions/purchase) may cause insufficient training data for high‑volume queries, leading to performance regression.

**First 3 experiments:**  
1. *Ablate Engagement Features:* Train without any recent click/ATC/purchase features; expect significant offline NDCG and online conversion/CTR drop.  
2. *Label Weight Sensitivity:* Train with different label weightings (e.g., `a=b=c=1`, click‑heavy); measure conversion lift to validate the `|P|/|A|, |P|/|C|` calibration.  
3. *Channel Ablation:* Simulate a channel failure by setting its score feature to NA/zero during inference; observe which business metrics drop most to reveal learned channel reliance.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
### Open Question 1: Improving Signal for Sparse Tail Queries
*Question:* How can we improve signal quality for sparse tail queries in multi-channel learning-to-rank?  
*Basis:* Future work states current filtering (≥20 impressions and ≥1 purchase per query‑week) may under‑represent tail queries; suggests importance sampling, label smoothing, semi‑supervised learning.  
*Why unresolved:* Filtering thresholds bias the model toward high‑volume queries, harming tail performance and overall diversity.  
*Evidence needed:* Compare tail‑query metrics (NDCG, conversion) before/after applying these techniques, possibly by relaxing filtering while controlling noise.

### Open Question 2: Evaluating and Mitigating Channel Bias and Fairness
*Question:* How can we systematically evaluate and mitigate bias and fairness issues across retrieval channels?  
*Basis:* Future work plans to evaluate bias/fairness across channels to ensure equitable exposure.  
*Why unresolved:* The unified model inherits biases from historical interactions; distinct channels may propagate different biases (e.g., popularity vs. freshness), but no fairness metrics or interventions studied.  
*Evidence needed:* Define channel‑wise fairness metrics (e.g., exposure by category, price, brand), implement debiasing (constraints, adversarial training), measure trade‑offs with conversion and latency.

### Open Question 3: Incorporating User-Level Personalization
*Question:* Can incorporating user‑level personalization signals further improve conversion?  
*Basis:* Future work mentions personalization as a promising avenue for ranking effectiveness and user conversion.  
*Why unresolved:* The current model operates at query‑item‑week level without explicit user features; personalization could capture individual preferences but may increase complexity and latency.  
*Evidence needed:* Design user features (past behavior, demographics), integrate into GBDT or hybrid architecture, run A/B tests for conversion lift while keeping p95 < 50 ms.

## Limitations
- Exact GBDT hyperparameters and feature engineering details (lookback windows, normalization, missing value handling) are undisclosed, hindering faithful reproduction.
- Data filtering (≥20 impressions and ≥1 purchase per query‑week) likely biases the model toward high‑volume queries, potentially harming long‑tail performance.
- The linear conversion‑weighted label may not capture non‑linear relationships between intermediate actions (click, ATC) and final purchase across product categories.
- Evaluation is limited to a single platform (Target.com); generalizability to other e‑commerce contexts remains unknown.

## Confidence
- **High:** Unified GBDT reranking yields significant online gains (+2.85% conversion, +1.46% CTR) and meets p95 latency <50 ms. *Evidence:* large‑scale A/B test on Target.com.
- **Medium:** Design choices (conversion‑weighted label, recent engagement features) are primary drivers. *Evidence:* ablation shows engagement feature impact, but no alternative label experiments.
- **Low:** Approach generalizes to other e‑commerce search systems. *Evidence:* single‑site evaluation.

## Next Checks
1. **Label Weight Sensitivity:** Retrain with alternative weightings (e.g., equal weights, click‑heavy) and compare online conversion/CTR to validate robustness of the `|P|/|A|, |P|/|C|` scheme.
2. **Temporal Window Sweep:** Vary engagement feature aggregation windows (1 day, 7 days, 30 days) and measure offline NDCG@8 and online metrics to identify optimal window and detect leakage.
3. **Latency Profiling Under Load:** Measure p95 latency of feature store queries and GBDT inference during peak traffic; test scaling with larger models to ensure headroom and pinpoint bottlenecks.
