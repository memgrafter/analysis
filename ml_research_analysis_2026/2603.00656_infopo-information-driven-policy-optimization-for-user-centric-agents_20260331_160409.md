# InfoPO: Information-Driven Policy Optimization for User-Centric Agents

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2603.00656  
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.00656  
- **Reference count:** 40  
- **Primary result:** InfoPO introduces turn-level counterfactual information-gain rewards, achieving 14–16% improvement over GRPO-based methods on user-centric benchmarks while maintaining stability and generalization.

## Executive Summary
InfoPO tackles the credit assignment problem in multi-turn reinforcement learning for agents interacting with users. By quantifying the impact of each interaction through a counterfactual information-gain reward—comparing the agent’s next-action distribution under actual feedback versus a masked placeholder—the method frames dialogue as active uncertainty reduction. This intrinsic signal is fused with external rewards via a variance‑gated mechanism, automatically upweighting information gain when external rewards are ambiguous. Evaluations across intent clarification, collaborative coding, and tool‑augmented decision making tasks show consistent gains over prompting and RL baselines, improved sample efficiency, and robustness to simulator shifts.

## Method Summary
For each turn \(t\) in a trajectory, InfoPO computes an intrinsic reward  
\[
r^{\text{info}}_t = \log \pi(a_{t+1} \mid h_t, o_t) - \log \pi(a_{t+1} \mid h_t, \emptyset),
\]  
where \(h_t\) is the history, \(o_t\) the user feedback (or omitted for the counterfactual), and \(\pi\) the policy. This is obtained via teacher forcing (one forward pass per condition). External rewards \(r^{\text{ext}}_t\) are scaled to group‑relative advantages \(A^{\text{ext}}\) within rollout groups of size \(G=5\). The info‑gain advantage \(A^{\text{info}}\) is fused as  
\[
\hat{A} = A^{\text{ext}} + \beta \cdot g(\sigma^{\text{ext}}_g) \cdot A^{\text{info}},
\]  
with gate \(g = \sigma(-\sigma^{\text{ext}}_g / \tau)\) modulating \(\beta\) when external reward variance is low. The policy is updated with a PPO‑clipped objective plus a KL penalty against a reference policy.

## Key Results
- Outperforms prompting and multi‑turn RL baselines, exceeding GRPO‑based methods by **14–16%** on UserGym, ColBench, and \(\tau\)2‑Bench.  
- Improves sample efficiency and training stability, even under user simulator shifts.  
- Generalizes to environment‑interactive tasks (Sokoban, WebShop) and shows robustness to longer horizons.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
InfoPO’s counterfactual info‑gain directly measures how much the observed user feedback reduces the agent’s uncertainty about its next action. This corresponds to a conditional mutual information objective, incentivizing the agent to actively resolve ambiguity. The variance‑gated fusion ensures that when external rewards are sparse or non‑discriminative (low variance), the intrinsic signal dominates, preventing the policy from stalling on uninformative turns. By combining both signals at the advantage level, the method preserves the benefits of outcome‑based learning while systematically addressing the credit assignment problem inherent in multi‑turn interactions.

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component map:**  
Policy network → generate action → Simulated user (feedback) → trajectory buffer → counterfactual forward passes (masked placeholder) → info‑gain reward computation → external reward → group‑relative normalization → variance gate → fused advantage → PPO loss → policy update (with KL penalty) → reference policy update.

**Critical path:** Data collection (rollouts) → reward computation (info‑gain & external) → advantage fusion → PPO update. The counterfactual forward passes introduce extra computation per turn but are essential for the intrinsic signal.

**Design tradeoffs:**  
- \(\beta\) trades off info‑gain influence vs. stability; too high leads to verbosity, too low under‑utilizes the signal.  
- Group size \(G\) reduces variance in advantage estimates but increases memory usage.  
- Gate temperature \(\tau\) controls sensitivity to external reward variance; lower \(\tau\) makes activation sharper.  
- Counterfactual computation adds overhead (~1× forward pass per turn) but improves sample efficiency.

**Failure signatures:**  
- Diverging policy or exploding KL when \(\beta\) is too large or the gate is disabled.  
- Excessively long interactions (verbosity) if the agent over‑optimizes info‑gain.  
- Premature termination on long‑horizon tasks due to conservative KL penalties.  
- Info‑gain advantage variance near zero suggests the gate is inactive and external rewards already discriminative.

**First three experiments:**  
1. Run a single training step on a small subset of UserGym to verify that all components (rollout, counterfactual masking, reward calculation) execute without errors and produce sensible log‑probability differences.  
2. Ablate the variance gate by setting \(g=1\) and compare training curves (success rate, KL) against the default; expect instability if \(\beta\) is high.  
3. After a few PPO updates on the full UserGym benchmark, evaluate the checkpoint and confirm that success rate improves relative to the baseline policy.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
1. **Efficient approximation of counterfactual info‑gain** – Current implementation requires an extra model evaluation per turn, linearly increasing training time. A method to amortize or cache these evaluations (e.g., via learned surrogates) could reduce wall‑clock time while preserving sample efficiency.  
2. **Extension to multimodal agents** – The formulation assumes discrete textual observations; adapting InfoPO to vision‑language models would require handling continuous or high‑dimensional inputs and may yield new insights for interactive multimodal tasks.  
3. **Robustness to real human users** – Training relies on LLM‑based simulators whose fidelity to human behavior (inconsistency, shifting goals) is unknown. Human‑subject experiments or analysis of real interaction logs are needed to validate performance under natural variability.

## Limitations
- Exact chat templates, tokenization (including Qwen’s one‑token shift), and placeholder string handling are not documented, making log‑probability computation sensitive to implementation details.  
- User simulator configuration (system prompts, sampling parameters) and evaluation protocols (test episodes, seed aggregation) are undisclosed, hindering direct replication.  
- The method assumes a stationary conditional \(P(o_t \mid h_t)\); performance may degrade with real humans who violate simulator assumptions.
