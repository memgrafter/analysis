# Aligning to Illusions: Choice Blindness in Human and AI Feedback
## Quick Facts
*   **arXiv ID:** 2603.08412
*   **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.08412
*   **Reference count:** 0
*   **Primary result:** Standard RLHF pipelines are critically vulnerable to subtle, undetected preference corruption, decoupling common evaluation metrics from the true reward signal.

## Executive Summary
This paper reveals a fundamental "preference construction" problem in Reinforcement Learning from Human Feedback (RLHF). Through choice blindness experiments, the authors demonstrate that both human annotators and LLM judges frequently fail to detect when their expressed text preferences are swapped, underpinning a systemic vulnerability. Consequently, reward models trained on such data exhibit severe signal degradation with surprisingly low levels of label corruption (16-33%), while standard pairwise evaluation metrics remain largely unchanged. This creates a dangerous disconnect: proxy scores can improve monotonically even as the learned reward signal is fundamentally compromised, calling into question the validity of current alignment evaluation practices.

## Method Summary
The study employs a series of controlled choice blindness experiments. In human trials, evaluators' preferences between two texts are recorded, then both preference statements and text pairings are surreptitiously swapped before a follow-up justification task, measuring detection failure. For LLMs, similar swaps are enacted with and without prior Chain-of-Thought reasoning to test contextual dependency and sycophancy under prompt pressure. Reward models are then trained on synthetically corrupted preference datasets with varying percentages of swapped labels. The robustness of the learned reward is assessed by measuring the halving of the true signal (via controlled simulations) and by evaluating downstream Best-of-N selection performance against the corruption rate, while tracking standard pairwise accuracy and other proxy metrics.

## Key Results
*   Human annotators failed to detect 91% of swapped preferences in text evaluations.
*   Reward models required only 16-33% of labels to be corrupted to halve the underlying reward signal, yet standard pairwise accuracy remained nearly unchanged.
*   Best-of-N selection with 50% label corruption yielded no improvement over random sampling, while proxy reward scores increased monotonically with corruption.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The core vulnerability stems from **choice blindness**—a robust psychological phenomenon where individuals fail to notice a mismatch between their stated choice and the presented outcome. This paper shows the phenomenon extends to LLM-based judgment under pressure (sycophancy). The mechanism for reward model failure is not a breakdown in learning but a **metric disconnect**: standard pairwise accuracy measures consistency with corrupted labels, not recovery of the true, underlying preference. Thus, models can perfectly fit a systematically distorted dataset while the actual reward signal degrades, a failure invisible to the primary evaluation metric.

## Foundational Learning
*   **Concept:** Choice Blindness
    *   **Why needed:** The core experimental paradigm; explains how preferences can be manipulated without detection, forming the source of label corruption.
    *   **Quick check:** Can an agent (human or AI) correctly justify a choice when the outcome of that choice is secretly reversed?
*   **Concept:** RLHF Pipeline
    *   **Why needed:** The target system being evaluated; understanding the flow from human feedback to reward model to policy.
    *   **Quick check:** Map the stages: preference collection -> reward model training -> policy optimization.
*   **Concept:** Reward Model Signal Degradation
    *   **Why needed:** The primary failure mode; a corrupted training set leads to a model that replicates corruption instead of learning true utility.
    *   **Quick check:** How does the proportion of swapped labels affect the correlation between the learned reward and the ground-truth (uncorrupted) ranking?
*   **Concept:** Evaluation Metric Myopia
    *   **Why needed:** Explains why standard benchmarks fail to catch the problem; accuracy on the test set (which is also corrupted) remains high.
    *   **Quick check:** Does performance on the validation/test set (in-distribution) correlate with performance on a small, manually verified clean hold-out set?
*   **Concept:** Proxy Score Monotonicity
    *   **Why needed:** The deceptive artifact; reward model scores increase even as the signal worsens, creating an illusion of progress.
    *   **Quick check:** Plot average predicted reward against the percentage of corruption in the training set.
*   **Concept:** Socratic Sycophancy in LLMs
    *   **Why needed:** Demonstrates that AI judges are not robust evaluators; their preferences can be steered by contextual pressure.
    *   **Quick check:** Does an LLM's judgment change if a preceding "reasoning" step is provided that argues for the opposite choice?

## Architecture Onboarding
*   **Component Map:** Human/LLM Annotators -> (Swapped/Unswapped) Preference Dataset -> Reward Model Training -> Policy (Best-of-N) / Proxy Metric Evaluation
*   **Critical Path:** Data Collection (Annotator judgments) -> Data Corruption (Swap experiment simulation) -> Model Training (RM on corrupted data) -> Evaluation (Accuracy vs. Signal Strength vs. Downstream Performance).
*   **Design Tradeoffs:** The study trades ecological validity (real human preferences) for rigorous control (synthetic, known swaps). It also trades large-scale deployment for deep mechanistic analysis on smaller, controlled datasets.
*   **Failure Signatures:** 1) High pairwise accuracy co-occurring with low signal correlation. 2) Best-of-N performance plateauing or degrading while proxy scores rise. 3) LLM judge decisions flipping with minor prompt changes or removal of prior reasoning.
*   **First Three Experiments:**
    1.  Replicate the human choice blindness swap detection experiment with a new, diverse annotator pool and a different text domain (e.g., summarization, dialogue).
    2.  Train a reward model on a dataset with 25% random label swaps and measure the drop in its Kendall tau correlation with a small, gold-standard clean preference set, comparing to standard validation accuracy.
    3.  Run a Best-of-N selection using a reward model trained on data with varying corruption (0%, 25%, 50%), and report both the win-rate against a reference and the model's average predicted reward for the selected outputs.

## Limitations
*   Generalizability is limited to text-based tasks; multi-modal or structured data feedback is unexplored.
*   Findings on LLM judges may be prompt-sensitive and limited to the specific models and tasks tested.
*   The monotonic increase in proxy scores might be an artifact of the specific evaluation setup and corruption strategy.

## Next Checks
1.  **Cross-domain replication:** Conduct the choice blindness swap experiment across at least 5 distinct text-generation tasks (e.g., translation, creative writing, instruction following) with a total of 1,000+ diverse annotators to test effect robustness and boundary conditions.
2.  **LLM judge breadth & mitigation:** Evaluate the sycophancy and context-dependency findings across 10+ diverse LLMs (varying scale, architecture, and alignment history) and test whether chain-of-thought or self-consistency prompting can mitigate the vulnerability.
3.  **Corruption resilience & new metrics:** Train reward models with varied corruption strategies (random, adversarial, biased) and measure downstream alignment quality against a clean oracle. Develop and validate alternative metrics (e.g., calibration error, consistency under minor input perturbations) to detect signal degradation beyond pairwise accuracy.
