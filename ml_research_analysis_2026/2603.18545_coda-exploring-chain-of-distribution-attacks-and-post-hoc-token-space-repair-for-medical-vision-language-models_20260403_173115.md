# CoDA: Exploring Chain-of-Distribution Attacks and Post-Hoc Token-Space Repair for Medical Vision-Language Models

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2603.18545
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.18545
- **Reference count:** 40
- **Primary result:** Multi-stage clinically plausible imaging pipeline shifts (CoDA) cause severe zero-shot accuracy drops in medical vision-language models (e.g., BioMed-CLIP MRI accuracy: 81.5% → 44.0%), and lightweight token-space adaptation partially recovers robustness when trained only on clean images.

## Executive Summary
This paper identifies a novel vulnerability in medical vision-language models (MVLMs): distribution shifts arising from multi-stage clinical imaging pipelines (acquisition, reconstruction, delivery) that preserve visual interpretability but alter pixel statistics. The proposed Chain-of-Distribution Attack (CoDA) optimizes parameters across seven canonical stage compositions under structural similarity constraints to generate adversarial samples. CoDA substantially degrades zero-shot performance across brain MRI, chest X-ray, and abdominal CT for CLIP-style MVLMs, with chained compositions causing larger drops than any single stage. Proprietary and medical-specific multimodal LLMs also fail to reliably audit technical authenticity on these samples. A post-hoc repair method uses teacher-guided token-space adaptation—a lightweight residual adapter trained only on clean images—to partially recover accuracy on archived adversarial outputs.

## Method Summary
CoDA models real-world clinical pipelines as three parameterized stages: acquisition shading (A), reconstruction transformations (R: window/level/quantization), and delivery distortions (D: resize/compression). It defines seven composition families (A, R, D, A+R, R+D, A+D, A+R+D) and optimizes stage parameters via random or Bayesian search to minimize a signed correctness score on the target MVLM, while projecting outputs to satisfy masked SSIM constraints (global and ROI-based) for clinical plausibility. The best-performing family is selected per image. For repair, a token-space adapter is inserted into a student encoder; it is trained on clean images using a combined loss: downstream task loss, view-consistency loss for augmentation invariance, and a teacher-guided Gram matrix alignment loss that distills robust relational structure from a stronger teacher. The repaired student is then evaluated on archived CoDA outputs without re-generating attacks.

## Key Results
- Chained pipeline shifts (A+R+D) degrade zero-shot accuracy more than any single stage: BioMed-CLIP MRI accuracy drops from 81.5% (clean) to 44.0% (Bayesian CoDA, τ=0.8).
- Multimodal assistants fail authenticity auditing on CoDA-shifted inputs: medical-specific MLLMs (Lingshu, MedGemma) show near 0% accuracy, with high-confidence errors and contradiction in self-challenge protocols.
- Teacher-guided token-space adaptation (trained only on clean data) improves accuracy on CoDA-shifted images over baseline student models, though not fully restoring clean-performance levels (e.g., MRI repair gains ~15–20% absolute accuracy).

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
**Claim:** Clinically plausible chained pipeline shifts (A→R→D) cause larger zero-shot accuracy drops in CLIP-style MVLMs than any single-stage shift.  
**Mechanism:** CoDA optimizes parameters across composition families under masked SSIM constraints that alter pixel statistics while preserving clinical readability. Cross-stage interactions compound distribution shifts, exploiting brittleness in the vision encoder's feature space and causing misalignment with text prototypes.  
**Core assumption:** MVLM representations are sensitive to multi-stage, clinically plausible pixel-statistic changes that do not compromise human interpretability.  
**Evidence anchors:**  
- Abstract: "Chained compositions causing larger drops than any single stage (e.g., BioMed-CLIP MRI accuracy falls from 81.5% to 44.0% under Bayesian optimization)."  
- Section 4.3, Table 3: A+R+D consistently underperforms only-A/only-R/only-D under equal SSIM constraints.  
**Break condition:** If the vision encoder's training data already covers the full distribution of plausible multi-stage shifts, the compounded effect may not materialize; or if SSIM constraints are too loose, attacks become perceptibly unnatural.

### Mechanism 2
**Claim:** Multimodal assistants fail to reliably audit technical authenticity under CoDA-shifted inputs, showing persistent high-confidence errors.  
**Mechanism:** CoDA-generated samples preserve semantic plausibility but perturb low-level technical cues (e.g., acquisition shading, compression artifacts) that MLLMs implicitly associate with "real" images during training. The single-call self-challenge protocol's internal consistency checks are misled by the coherent but shifted statistics.  
**Core assumption:** MLLMs rely on spurious technical signatures (noise profiles, contrast ranges) for authenticity judgment, disrupted by CoDA's parameterized shifts.  
**Evidence anchors:**  
- Abstract: "Proprietary multimodal models show degraded auditing reliability and persistent high-confidence errors... medical-specific MLLMs... exhibit clear deficiencies."  
- Section 4.3, Figure 3: Accuracy on CoDA inputs drops near 0% for medical MLLMs and substantially for proprietary ones.  
**Break condition:** If MLLMs are fine-tuned or prompted to reason about technical acquisition parameters (e.g., DICOM metadata), they may recover reliability.

### Mechanism 3
**Claim:** Teacher-guided token-space adaptation partially recovers zero-shot accuracy on archived CoDA outputs when trained only on clean images.  
**Mechanism:** A lightweight residual adapter aligns the student's patch-token geometry (via Gram matrix) to a stronger teacher's token structure, re-aligning the representation space to be more invariant to CoDA-type shifts. The view-consistency loss encourages augmentation invariance, while the teacher-guided geometry loss distills robust relational structure.  
**Core assumption:** The teacher's token-space geometry encodes resilience to the specific pipeline shifts modeled by CoDA and can be transferred via a small adapter.  
**Evidence anchors:**  
- Abstract: "Lightweight post-hoc repair via teacher-guided token-space adaptation... improves accuracy on archived adversarial outputs."  
- Section 4.4, Figure 4(b): Repaired models exceed attack baseline accuracy across modalities.  
**Break condition:** If the student's backbone is too weak or architecturally mismatched, or if CoDA shifts include effects not represented in the teacher's training data, transfer may be ineffective.

## Foundational Learning

- **Concept: Masked Structural Similarity (SSIM) Constraints**  
  **Why needed here:** Ensures adversarial samples remain clinically interpretable by constraining pixel-statistic changes to preserve perceived anatomical structure (Eq. 8–9).  
  **Quick check question:** If SSIM threshold τ is set too high (e.g., 0.95), what happens to the feasible attack space and likely success rate?

- **Concept: Multi-Stage Pipeline Composition Families**  
  **Why needed here:** Real clinical imaging involves sequential stages; CoDA models this by defining 7 composition families (Eq. 7) and optimizing both which stages apply and their parameters.  
  **Quick check question:** Why might A⨝D (skipping reconstruction) be less effective than A+R+D, even if individual A and D are strong?

- **Concept: Teacher-Guided Token-Space Adaptation**  
  **Why needed here:** Full fine-tuning of large vision encoders is costly; this method (Eq. 14–16) inserts a lightweight residual on patch tokens, aligning their relational geometry (Gram matrix) to a teacher's, recovering robustness from only clean data.  
  **Quick check question:** How does aligning the Gram matrix of patch tokens (Eq. 15) encourage robustness compared to directly aligning patch vectors?

## Architecture Onboarding

**Component map:**  
Clean image → CoDA Generator → Adversarial image → MVLM Target (zero-shot classification) or MLLM Auditor (authenticity judgment); Clean image → Teacher & Student (with token adapter) → Repaired Student → Adversarial image (evaluation)

**Critical path:**  
1. For each evaluation image and composition family F∈{A,R,D,A+R,R+D,A+D,A+R+D}, run constrained optimization (Eq. 12) to find parameters minimizing signed correctness J under SSIM≥τ.  
2. Project violations via α-mixing (Eq. 10–11) and select family F* with lowest J.  
3. For auditing: feed x_adv to MLLM with self-challenge protocol, record accuracy and reliability metrics.  
4. For repair: train token adapter W on clean images with teacher guidance (Eq. 16), evaluate on archived x_adv.

**Design tradeoffs:**  
- Plausibility vs. attack strength: Lower τ allows stronger perturbations but risks visible artifacts; higher τ preserves realism but may limit effectiveness.  
- Optimization cost: Bayesian optimization (TPE) is more sample-efficient than random search but computationally heavier per iteration.  
- Repair scope: Token-space adaptation is parameter-efficient but may not fully recover clean performance, especially for severely shifted modalities.

**Failure signatures:**  
- CoDA optimization: SSIM projection (Eq. 11) fails to find feasible α*—indicates requested shift too strong for τ or image.  
- Auditing: MLLM outputs high-confidence but wrong judgments on CoDA samples; high contradiction rate in self-challenge.  
- Repair: λ_dist too high → over-alignment to teacher, losing task performance; λ_cons too high → model invariant to all augmentations, including clinically relevant ones.

**First 3 experiments:**  
1. Zero-shot classification evaluation: Run CoDA (Random/Bayes, τ=0.9/0.8) on clean MVLMs across MRI/X-ray/CT. Report accuracy (Table 2) and success rate per family (Table 1). Expected: A+R+D causes largest drops.  
2. Technical authenticity auditing: Run MLLMs (proprietary + medical) on clean vs. CoDA-shifted images using single-call self-challenge. Report accuracy, contradiction rate, high-confidence error (Fig. 3). Expected: Significant degradation on CoDA inputs.  
3. Post-hoc repair evaluation: Train token-space adapter (with/without teacher) on clean data. Evaluate on archived CoDA outputs. Report accuracy relative to clean and attack baselines (Fig. 4b). Expected: Teacher-guided repair improves over baseline student.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- How does the CoDA threat surface generalize to diverse clinical imaging pipelines and underrepresented modalities beyond the three evaluated (brain MRI, chest X-ray, abdominal CT)?
- Why do medical-specific MLLMs fail catastrophically at technical-authenticity auditing under CoDA shifts, despite being trained on medical data?
- What limits teacher-guided token-space adaptation from fully restoring robustness to CoDA shifts, leaving a persistent gap to clean performance?
- What mechanistic properties of MVLM representations cause chained pipeline shifts (e.g., A+R+D) to degrade performance more severely than any single-stage shift, under equal SSIM constraints?

## Limitations
- ROI mask extraction method is unspecified ("threshold-based"), creating variability in SSIM constraints and attack plausibility.
- Composition order assumptions (canonical A→R→D) are not fully validated; non-canonical orders may affect attack efficacy.
- External validation is limited; neighbor papers are weakly related, so claims rely primarily on self-contained experiments.

## Confidence
- **Mechanism 1 (Chained shifts > single-stage):** High – Strongly supported by Table 3 and abstract results showing consistent accuracy drops for A+R+D.
- **Mechanism 2 (MLLM audit failure):** Medium – Supported by Figure 3, but mechanism relies on inferred "technical cue" reliance; direct probing evidence is limited.
- **Mechanism 3 (Token-space repair):** Medium-High – Figure 4b shows recovery, but break conditions (architectural mismatch, OOD shifts) may limit generalizability; full hyperparameter sensitivity not detailed.

## Next Checks
1. **SSIM threshold ablation:** Systematically vary τ (e.g., 0.85, 0.80, 0.75) and measure trade-off between visual plausibility (human eval) and attack success rate. Tests break condition of "too loose → implausible."
2. **Composition order exploration:** Implement and evaluate non-canonical stage orders (e.g., D→A, R→A) under same SSIM constraints. Validates whether canonical order is optimal or merely a simplifying assumption.
3. **MLLM audit with explicit technical prompts:** Re-run self-challenge but prompt MLLMs to explicitly reason about DICOM metadata, vendor noise, or reconstruction kernels. Tests break condition: "if prompted to reason about technical parameters, reliability may recover."
