# Annealed Co-Generation: Disentangling Variables via Progressive Pairwise Modeling

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2603.06615
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.06615
- Reference count: 40
- Primary result: Outperforms baselines with MSE=0.1027 (vs 0.1878 for RePaint) and PSNR=35.291 dB on flow-field inpainting; achieves average RMSD=2.99 Å compared to single-target oracle's 3.30 Å on multispecific antibody design.

## Executive Summary
The paper introduces Annealed Co-Generation (ACG), a framework for joint multivariate generation that avoids high-dimensional diffusion modeling. Instead of training a single model on all variables, ACG trains separate diffusion models on disjoint variable pairs and composes them at inference via a three-stage annealing process: Consensus (align shared variables), Heating (temporary re-noising to escape local minima), and Cooling (denoise while preserving within-pair structure). This approach reduces computational burden and mitigates data imbalance. Evaluated on flow-field inpainting and multispecific antibody design, ACG significantly improves reconstruction accuracy (MSE reduction of 45%, RMSD improvement of 0.31 Å) compared to existing baselines, demonstrating robust joint generation without retraining.

## Method Summary
ACG decomposes a joint distribution over variables into pairwise factors (e.g., p(A,B) and p(B,C)) and trains unconditional diffusion models for each pair. At inference, parallel diffusion chains are initialized for each pair plus an unconditional prior. The annealing schedule iterates from noise to data: first, a Consensus step enforces agreement on shared variables (e.g., B) across all branches using aggregation (mean or joint overlap). Then, a Heating phase deliberately re-noises the shared variable to escape poor local consensus, followed by multiple Cooling steps that denoise while respecting the consensus. A first-visit-only synchronization policy prevents over-constraining, allowing structural relaxation. The method requires no retraining and adapts to any tree-like dependency structure.

## Key Results
- Flow-field inpainting: Mean squared error reduced to 0.1027 (vs. 0.1878 for RePaint), PSNR improved to 35.291 dB.
- Multispecific antibody design: Average RMSD lowered to 2.99 Å compared to single-target oracle's 3.30 Å.
- Outperforms naive consensus baselines (Greedy, Consistent) and demonstrates robust joint generation across tasks.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The paper identifies three key mechanisms that explain ACG's success:

1. **Pairwise factorization reduces complexity and data imbalance.** By modeling lower-dimensional pairs instead of the full joint distribution, ACG shrinks the effective modeling space and increases the number of training samples. This assumes the underlying dependencies are simple (tree-like) and can be captured by pairwise factors. Evidence: Abstract and Section 1 explicitly state this; ablation vs. joint modeling shows computational gains. Break condition: fails if strong higher-order interactions exist.

2. **Annealing (Consensus → Heating → Cooling) reconciles shared variables while preserving within-pair validity.** The Consensus step aligns shared variables (B) across branches. Heating deliberately re-noises (t → t+jt) to escape local minima from consensus projection. Cooling then denoises from the re-noised state, leveraging the diffusion model’s manifold to restore high-likelihood within-pair configurations. Assumes the denoising dynamics can recover from noisy states. Evidence: Algorithm 2 and Figure 4; ablations show Greedy (no heating) traps in local minima, Consistent (always sync) over-constrains. Break condition: excessive heating height or iterations can push trajectories off the manifold, producing invalid samples.

3. **First-visit-only synchronization prevents over-constraining.** The synchronization schedule applies consensus only on the first cooling pass at each timestep; subsequent visits skip sync, allowing branches to evolve independently and heal corrupted interface features. Assumes continuous consensus injects conflicting gradients that harm shared variable B. Evidence: Section 3 describes the policy; Tables 3 and 6 show full-step synchronization fails while windowed/first-visit works best. Break condition: if initial consensus is extremely poor, a single first-visit may not provide a viable starting manifold.

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component map:** Pretrained pairwise diffusion models (e.g., `p(A,B)`, `p(B,C)`), Consensus Operators (UnifiedAggregation with Mean or Joint Overlap), Scheduling Modules (`Ssync(t)`, `Sheat(t)`), Annealing Controller (main loop), Mask & State Management.

**Critical path:**
1. Train N pairwise unconditional diffusion models on paired data.
2. At inference, initialize N+1 parallel diffusion branches (N conditional + 1 unconditional) from noise.
3. Iterate diffusion timesteps t from T down to 0.
4. On sync timesteps (first cooling pass only), compute B_canon via consensus and overwrite the subject region in all branches.
5. Perform one denoising step (cooling).
6. If t in heating window, jump back `jt` steps, rescale noise by `Height`, and repeat cooling `K` times.
7. Output final subject state from any branch (they should agree).

**Design tradeoffs:**
- Consensus strategy: Mean aggregation is stable but may under-enforce; Joint Overlap is stronger but risks gradient cancellation.
- Annealing aggressiveness: Higher `K`, `Jheat`, `Height` improve exploration but increase compute and risk manifold drift.
- Synchronization policy: First-Visit is robust and lightweight; Consistent may converge faster but to poor local minima.

**Failure signatures:**
- Antibody design: High RMSD (>4.0 Å) or steric clashes, especially with Consistent sync or on structurally divergent pairs.
- Flow-field: Blurred or misaligned patch boundaries, high MSE/PSNR degradation, indicating consensus failure or insufficient heating.
- Divergence: Heating `Height` too low (<0.5) yields no escape (similar to Greedy); too high (>1.0) causes chaotic trajectories and NaNs.

**First 3 experiments to reproduce:**
1. **Pairwise Baseline Verification:** Train pairwise models on flow-field patches. Run independent generation (no consensus) and verify each pair is plausible but shared variable mismatches (high overlap MSE).
2. **Naive Consensus Test:** Implement Greedy (sync every step, no heating). Expect poor performance (e.g., RMSD ~4.5 Å) due to local minima.
3. **ACG Ablation on Split-A:** Run full ACG with `K=2`, `Height=1.0`, First-Visit sync, Mean fusion. Expect best RMSD (~3.76 Å). Then vary one parameter (K, Height, sync) to observe degradation.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
Three open questions identified for future work:

1. **Generalization beyond tree-like graphs:** How can ACG be extended to arbitrary causal structures with cycles or multiple intersections? Current algorithm assumes a single shared subject with multiple contexts.
2. **Automated schedule tuning:** Can annealing schedules be automatically adapted per task, and what convergence guarantees exist? Schedules are heuristic and manually tuned.
3. **Pairwise model selection principles:** What guidance should determine the choice of pairwise factors for a new multivariate generation problem? The paper uses ad-hoc decompositions (e.g., adjacent patches) without a systematic method.

## Limitations
- The pairwise factorization assumption may fail for distributions with strong higher-order interactions, leading to irrecoverable information loss.
- Annealing schedules and hyperparameters (`J_heat`, `K`, `Height`, sync policy) are only partially specified and may be task-dependent; optimal values are not provided.
- Key implementation details (diffusion hyperparameters, antibody SE(3) split, flow-field fusion) are missing, hindering exact replication.

## Confidence
- Pairwise factorization mechanism: Medium
- Annealing process (Consensus→Heating→Cooling): Medium
- First-visit synchronization policy: Medium
- Overall claim of robust joint generation without retraining: Medium

## Next Checks
Three concrete validation steps to strengthen the findings:

1. **Synthetic higher-order test:** Construct datasets with known pairwise-factorable vs. non-factorable joint distributions to quantify ACG's performance drop and identify breaking points.
2. **Hyperparameter sensitivity sweep:** Systematically vary `J_heat`, `K`, `Height` across multiple random seeds to map stability regions and derive robust defaults.
3. **Alternative factorization comparison:** Implement a triplet-factor baseline (models over three variables directly) to directly measure the cost of the pairwise approximation and verify trade-offs.
