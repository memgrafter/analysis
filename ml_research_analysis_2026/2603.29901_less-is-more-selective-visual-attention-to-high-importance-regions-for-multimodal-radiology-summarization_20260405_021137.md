# Less Is More? Selective Visual Attention to High-Importance Regions for Multimodal Radiology Summarization

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2603.29901
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.29901
- **Reference count:** 29
- **Primary result:** ViTAS achieves state-of-the-art performance on MIMIC-CXR with 29.25% BLEU-4 and 69.83% ROUGE-L, significantly outperforming both text-only baselines and full-image multimodal models.

## Executive Summary
The paper introduces ViTAS, a pipeline that selects pathology-relevant visual patches from chest X-rays using lung segmentation, bidirectional cross-attention for multi-view fusion, Shapley-guided patch clustering, and hierarchical visual tokenization. It demonstrates that limiting the visual input to salient regions improves multimodal radiology summarization, achieving state-of-the-art results on MIMIC-CXR. Human evaluations also rate ViTAS highest in readability, factual correctness, informativeness, and completeness.

## Method Summary
ViTAS processes paired frontal and lateral chest X-rays through a multi-stage pipeline. Stage 1 uses an ensemble of five bounding box prompts with MedSAM2 to produce a robust lung ROI mask, isolating pulmonary fields. Stage 2 applies dual Swin Transformer V2 backbones to the segmented images; a mid-level bidirectional spatial cross‑attention layer fuses the views, and a lightweight MLP generates pathology classification logits. Stage 3 extracts attention maps from the fusion layer, aggregates them into heatmaps, computes two‑player Shapley values to quantify each view’s marginal contribution to the classification, allocates a dynamic number of patches per view based on Shapley percentages, and clusters the top‑attention patches using DBSCAN. Stage 4 employs a ViT encoder on the segmented images and constructs a hierarchical visual token sequence per view: a global token, cluster tokens (aggregated with geometric features), and dynamic extra patches selected by image‑ and text‑aware MLP scorers. The frontal and lateral token sequences are concatenated and fused with T5‑encoded findings text. Finally, Stage 5 uses a T5 decoder to autoregressively generate the impression.

## Key Results
- ViTAS achieves 29.25% BLEU‑4 and 69.83% ROUGE‑L on MIMIC‑CXR, significantly outperforming both text‑only and full‑image multimodal baselines.
- Ablations confirm that selecting salient patches over full images or naive ROIs yields superior performance, validating the “less is more” premise.
- Human evaluation rates ViTAS highest across readability, factual correctness, informativeness, and completeness.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- **Claim:** Reducing visual noise via precise lung segmentation isolates clinically relevant regions, preventing irrelevant anatomy from diluting cross‑modal alignment.
- **Mechanism:** An ensemble of 5 bounding box prompts (one reference and four directional shifts) is fed to MedSAM2. The resulting binary masks are unioned to create a robust lung ROI mask `M`. This mask is applied to the input image (`I_seg = I ⊙ M`), zeroing out non‑pulmonary tissue.
- **Core assumption:** Pulmonary fields contain the majority of pathology evidence; non‑lung regions are noise that harms multimodal grounding.
- **Evidence anchors:** The abstract mentions selective focus via ensemble‑guided lung segmentation; Section 3.1 provides equations for ensemble prompting, mask union, and application; the corpus discusses visual attention but not MedSAM2 specifics.
- **Break condition:** If MedSAM2 under‑ or over‑segments (e.g., missing lung apex/diaphragm or including large mediastinal areas), noise reduction fails and irrelevant tokens may dominate.

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim:** Bidirectional mid‑level spatial cross‑attention between frontal and lateral views enables complementary view fusion while preserving view‑specific features, leveraging the diagnostic value of lateral projections.
- **Mechanism:** Two Swin Transformer V2 backbones process segmented frontal and lateral images independently. At a mid‑level fusion layer (after stage 3), frontal tokens attend to lateral tokens and vice‑versa. Their representations are updated via residual connections, layer norm, and FFN.
- **Core assumption:** AP/PA and lateral views provide complementary diagnostic cues (e.g., lateral better for cardiomegaly, pleural effusion per Table 3). Naïve concatenation discards spatial interaction; independent processing loses synergy.
- **Evidence anchors:** The abstract cites bidirectional cross‑attention; Section 3.2 describes mid‑level fusion and Figure 3; Table 3 shows higher AUROC on lateral vs. frontal for many pathologies, justifying fusion.
- **Break condition:** If one view is missing or extremely poor quality (e.g., rotated lateral), cross‑attention could propagate noise or cause misalignment. Learnable missing tokens partially mitigate this.

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim:** Shapley‑value‑guided dynamic patch clustering selects a pathology‑relevant, view‑adaptive subset of patches for the summarization decoder, avoiding over‑reliance on attention hotspots that may not serve the FINDINGS→IMPRESSION task.
- **Mechanism:** Post‑hoc attention maps from the final cross‑attention layer are generated. 2‑player Shapley values quantify each view’s contribution to the classification output for each pathology class. These percentages allocate a total number of patches to each view. Top‑attention patches are clustered via DBSCAN, and cluster tokens are created.
- **Core assumption:** Attention maps from a pathology classifier indicate “importance” for detection, but must be translated to summarization. Shapley values provide a game‑theoretic measure of each view’s marginal contribution, making patch selection adaptive per pathology context.
- **Evidence anchors:** The abstract mentions Shapley‑guided adaptive patch clustering; Section 3.3 details equations for heatmap aggregation, Shapley calculation, and dynamic allocation; Section 4.2 ablation shows “ViTAS (Selective Informative Patches)” outperforms random patches by ~4% BLEU‑4, showing selector value.
- **Break condition:** If the frozen Swin V2 classifier’s attention is misaligned with summarization needs (e.g., focuses on an irrelevant device), Shapley weighting could propagate misalignment. Dynamic extra patches are designed partly to mitigate this.

## Foundational Learning

#### Vision Transformer (ViT) Patch Tokenization
- **Why needed:** The summarization model uses ViT to encode images into a sequence of tokens; hierarchical token construction assumes understanding that an image is split into a grid of patches.
- **Quick check question:** Given a 224×224 image and a ViT with patch size 16, how many spatial tokens are produced before the CLS token? (Answer: 14×14 = 196.)

#### Shapley Values for Coalitional Game Theory
- **Why needed:** To fairly quantify the marginal contribution of frontal vs. lateral views to the classification prediction, which then dictates how many patches to extract from each view for summarization.
- **Quick check question:** In a 2‑player game with `v({F}) = 5, v({L}) = 4, v({F,L}) = 10, v(∅)=0`, what is `ϕ_F`? (Answer: 5.5.)

#### DBSCAN Clustering
- **Why needed:** To convert scattered high‑attention pixels into spatially coherent clusters, which are then tokenized as a single “cluster token” preserving geometry (centroid, extent).
- **Quick check question:** What are the two key parameters of DBSCAN and what do they control? (Answer: `eps` sets the maximum distance between two samples to be considered neighbors; `min_samples` sets the number of samples required to form a dense region.)

## Architecture Onboarding

#### Component Map
Input: Paired frontal (AP/PA) and lateral (L/LL) chest X‑ray images →  
Stage 1: Ensemble‑guided MedSAM2 lung segmentation produces binary lung ROI masks; applied to images to isolate pulmonary fields →  
Stage 2: Dual Swin Transformer V2 backbones process segmented views; mid‑level bidirectional cross‑attention fuses features; global features pooled and passed through MLP to produce pathology logits →  
Stage 3: Interpretability: Grad‑CAM on final cross‑attention yields heatmaps per view; heatmaps aggregated; 2‑player Shapley values computed on classification logits to determine each view’s contribution; patch counts allocated per view based on Shapley percentages; DBSCAN clusters top‑attention patches →  
Stage 4: Hierarchical tokenization: ViT‑B/16 processes original segmented images to extract patch embeddings; for each view, construct token sequence: `[global token; cluster tokens (aggregated per cluster with geometry features); dynamic extra patches (selected via image/text‑aware MLP scorers)]`; frontal and lateral sequences concatenated →  
Stage 5: Summarization: T5‑small encoder processes findings text; visual token sequence fused; T5 decoder generates impression autoregressively.

#### Critical Path
Raw Images → MedSAM2 Ensemble → Swin V2 + Cross‑Attention → Heatmap + Shapley + DBSCAN → ViT Hierarchical Tokens → T5 Decoder

#### Design Tradeoffs
- **Segmentation fidelity vs. robustness:** The 5‑box ensemble increases robustness to prompt misalignment but adds 5× inference cost. A single‑box would be faster but brittle.
- **Classifier attention vs. summarization relevance:** Using a frozen pathology classifier’s attention to drive summarization selection is efficient but assumes that features important for classification are also important for impression generation. Dynamic extra patches aim to mitigate misalignment.
- **Token budget vs. information:** The fixed total visual token budget (e.g., 128 tokens, 64 per view) forces hard selection. Allocating more tokens to a view with higher Shapley may starve the other view, potentially losing subtle cues.

#### Failure Signatures
1. **Poor lung masks:** MedSAM2 fails on extreme anatomy/positioning → non‑lung high‑attention regions survive → noisy tokens.
2. **Misaligned attention:** Swin V2 classifier has high AUROC but its attention maps focus on irrelevant regions (e.g., pacemaker for a pneumonia case) → wrong patches clustered.
3. **DBSCAN collapse:** Incorrect `eps` or `min_samples` → either one giant cluster (loss of spatial distinction) or many tiny clusters (exceeding token budget, forcing discarding).
4. **Token mismatch:** Hierarchical token construction yields incorrect dimensions/sequence order → T5 fusion fails.

#### First 3 Experiments (Planned Ablations)
1. **Ablation 1: Necessity of Ensemble Segmentation.** Compare ViTAS with a single‑box MedSAM2 prompt vs. the 5‑box ensemble. Expect: ensemble → higher lung mask IoU → better BLEU‑4/R‑L.
2. **Ablation 2: Value of Bidirectional Cross‑Attention.** Train Swin V2 classifier under three conditions: (a) no fusion, (b) late fusion (concatenate global features), (c) proposed mid‑level bidirectional. Expect: (c) > (b) > (a) on classification AUROC and summarization metrics.
3. **Ablation 3: Shapley vs. Heuristic Allocation.** Replace Shapley‑based view patch allocation with (a) equal split, (b) split based on raw attention sum, (c) proposed Shapley. Expect: (c) > (b) > (a) on summarization performance.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
#### Open Question 1: Generalization beyond MIMIC‑CXR
The paper only evaluates on MIMIC‑CXR, a single‑institution dataset. It remains untested how ViTAS generalizes to multi‑modal, multi‑organ, or out‑of‑domain datasets with different demographics, imaging protocols, and disease distributions.

#### Open Question 2: Effect of segmentation errors
Errors in the MedSAM2 lung segmentation stage may propagate to patch selection and affect summarization quality, but the paper does not quantify this sensitivity or its potential clinical safety implications.

#### Open Question 3: Alignment of Shapley and attention signals
The method uses both Shapley values (view allocation) and attention heatmaps (patch selection). Their agreement and clinical validity are not analyzed; they could be conflicting, risking suboptimal patch selection.

#### Open Question 4: Necessity of hierarchical tokenization
The complex hierarchical token construction (global + cluster + dynamic patches) is justified intuitively, but no ablation isolates its contribution; simpler selective patching might achieve comparable performance with less overhead.

## Limitations
- Missing reproduction details: DBSCAN hyperparameters, image/text‑aware selector architectures, Shapley computation specifics.
- Generalization concerns: Reliance on MedSAM2 and Swin V2 may fail on diverse X‑ray sources; human evaluation lacks transparency.
- Fixed token budget may arbitrarily truncate important visual information.

## Confidence
- C1 (Segmentation reduces noise): Medium
- C2 (Bidirectional cross‑attention): Medium
- C3 (Shapley‑guided selection): Low

## Next Checks
1. **Segmentation robustness test:** Run pipeline on held‑out set with challenging images (rotated, low contrast). Measure lung mask IoU and correlate with BLEU/ROUGE to quantify sensitivity to segmentation errors.
2. **Fusion strategy ablation:** Train Swin V2 classifier under: (a) no fusion, (b) global feature concatenation, (c) proposed bidirectional cross‑attention. Compare classification AUROC and summarization metrics to isolate fusion benefit.
3. **Clustering hyperparameter sweep:** Systematically vary DBSCAN `eps` (1.0‑2.5) and `min_samples` (2‑8) on validation data. Evaluate number of clusters, spatial coherence, and downstream performance to determine optimal settings and robustness.
