# Sketch2Colab: Sketch-Conditioned Multi-Human Animation via Controllable Flow Distillation

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2603.02190
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.02190
- **Reference count:** 40
- **Primary result:** Distills a diffusion model into a fast rectified-flow sampler with dual-space guidance and a CTMC planner, achieving ~40% lower 3D trajectory error and ~51% lower anchor error versus strong baselines on multi-human interaction benchmarks.

## Executive Summary
Sketch2Colab tackles the problem of generating coherent, physically plausible 3D motion for multiple humans and objects from sparse 2D storyboard sketches (keyposes, trajectories, object masks). It introduces a three-stage framework: first, a diffusion teacher learns the sketch-conditioned motion distribution; second, this knowledge is distilled into a rectified-flow student for deterministic, fast sampling; third, a dual-space energy guidance system and a continuous-time Markov chain (CTMC) phase planner are added to enforce precise spatio-temporal constraints and synchronize interaction phases. The method significantly outperforms adapted baselines on constraint adherence, perceptual quality, and sampling efficiency, while remaining robust to sketch noise.

## Method Summary
The method proceeds in three training stages. Stage 1 trains a sketch-conditioned diffusion teacher (VP-SDE) with a temporal U-Net and entity-graph attention, conditioned on the storyboard via trajectory path injection and time-gated keyframe adapters. Stage 2 distills this teacher into a student network that predicts rectified-flow velocities, minimizing a flow matching loss and a distillation loss that aligns the student’s transport directions with the teacher’s probability flow. Stage 3 augments the student with the full objective: a Lyapunov loss that guides the flow to descend a potential combining raw-space constraint energies (keyframe, trajectory, contact, physics) and a latent anchor loss; a supervised loss for energy surrogates; a CTMC loss that models discrete interaction phases (approach, contact, hold) to modulate the continuous flow; and a consistency loss. At inference, the student integrates an ODE (Heun, 30–60 steps) while the CTMC updates every few steps, raw-space gradients are routed via a learned Jacobian surrogate, and latent anchors pull the sample toward the sketch-conditioned prior. A frozen VQ-VAE decoder maps the final latent to 3D motion.

## Key Results
- Significantly reduces constraint errors: ~40% lower 3D trajectory error and ~51% lower anchor error versus the COLLAGE teacher baseline.
- Improves perceptual quality (lower FID, less foot-skate) and interaction realism (lower penetration, better contact timing) compared to adapted baselines.
- Enables fast, deterministic sampling (30–60 ODE steps vs. 1000+ diffusion steps) while maintaining strong constraint adherence.
- Demonstrates robustness to increasing levels of synthetic sketch noise on keyposes and trajectories.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
**Claim:** Distilling a sketch-conditioned diffusion teacher into a rectified-flow student enables fast, stable sampling under complex conditioning without sacrificing realism.
**Mechanism:** The diffusion teacher (frozen VP-SDE) provides a probability-flow velocity field that guides the student's rectified-flow training. The student minimizes the rectified-flow objective and an explicit distillation loss to match the teacher's transport directions, initializing near the data manifold and inheriting the teacher's sketch conditioning. This transfers the teacher's density and constraint knowledge to a deterministic ODE sampler.
**Core assumption:** The teacher's probability flow accurately represents the sketch-conditioned data distribution and can be transferred to a faster ODE-based sampler.
**Evidence anchors:** Abstract mentions distillation for fast, stable sampling. Section 3.1 details the teacher's VP process (Eq.4), student objectives (Eqs.5,6), and training stages. Corpus lacks direct evidence on flow distillation for multi-human motion.
**Break condition:** If the diffusion teacher only weakly satisfies constraints, the student may inherit these failures, as the paper notes distillation alone is insufficient.

### Mechanism 2
**Claim:** Dual-space energy guidance (raw motion-space and latent-space) enforces precise sketch constraints (keyframes, trajectories, contacts) while maintaining latent coherence and avoiding off-manifold solutions.
**Mechanism:** Raw-space energies (keyframe fidelity, trajectory tracking, contact penalties, physics) are evaluated on decoded motion, and their gradients are backpropagated to latent space via a learned low-rank Jacobian surrogate (Bρ). Latent-space energy uses frozen 2D sketch embeddings to pull the latent toward the sketch-conditioned prior. Both are combined in a Lyapunov-like potential that shapes the flow's descent directions during training, balancing precision and coherence.
**Core assumption:** The Jacobian surrogate stably routes precise geometric gradients to the latent flow, and latent anchors prevent destructive deviations from the sketch-conditioned manifold.
**Evidence anchors:** Abstract mentions dual-space guidance for enforcing constraints. Section 3.2 describes dual-path guidance and latent anchor loss. Section 3.3 details raw-space energies and Lyapunov loss. Corpus lacks similar dual-space guidance for multi-human HOH interactions.
**Break condition:** If Bρ poorly approximates (∂D/∂z)⊤, raw-space gradients may be misrouted, causing instability or inaccurate constraint satisfaction.

### Mechanism 3
**Claim:** A lightweight CTMC phase planner models discrete interaction states (approach, contact, hold) and modulates the continuous flow to synchronize multi-agent timing and contact changes, reducing phase drift and flicker.
**Mechanism:** The CTMC evolves occupancy πt over discrete states s via dπt/dt = πt Qη(ht), where rates Qη are predicted from latent features and entity distances. Occupancy πt mixes phase-specific flow velocities v(s)ϕ and gates contact energy terms, effectively sequencing interaction phases within the continuous generation process.
**Core assumption:** Interaction phasing can be captured by a small set of discrete states whose transitions correlate with latent and geometric features, and modulating the continuous flow by these occupancies improves coordination.
**Evidence anchors:** Abstract mentions CTMC planner tracking active interaction regime. Section 3.4 introduces CTMC states, rate MLP Qη, Kolmogorov residual loss, and coupling via phase mixing and contact gating. Section 5 (Fig.4) shows CTMC improves contact timing calibration, reduces flow curvature variability, and improves energy-flow alignment. Corpus does not use CTMCs for motion phase planning.
**Break condition:** If the state set S is too coarse or features ht are uninformative, the CTMC may mis-schedule phases; ablations show performance sensitivity to state granularity.

## Architecture Onboarding
Component map: Input (storyboard C) -> 2D Encoders (E2D) -> Sketch embeddings -> Condition Student U-Net (vϕ) -> Guided ODE integration (Heun) with CTMC updates & dual-space guidance -> Latent z0 -> VQ-VAE Decoder (D) -> 3D motion M1:N. Offline: Diffusion Teacher (frozen) -> Distillation targets for Student.

Critical path: Storyboard is encoded into 2D embeddings and latent conditions. The diffusion teacher is first trained to convergence and frozen. The student is initialized from the teacher and trained with flow matching and distillation. During full training, the dual-space guidance (raw-space energies via Jacobian surrogate Bρ, latent anchor via fγ) and CTMC planner are activated, with the Lyapunov and supervised losses. At inference, the student integrates an ODE (30–60 steps), the CTMC updates occupancies every few steps to mix phase-specific velocities and gate contact energies, and optional Langevin steps may be applied. The final latent is decoded by the frozen VQ-VAE.

Design tradeoffs: Speed vs. control: Rectified flow enables 30–60 steps vs. 1000+ diffusion steps, but precise control requires added guidance/CTMC computation. Precision vs. coherence: Raw-space energies enforce exact constraints but risk off-manifold samples; latent anchors maintain coherence but are less precise. Discrete vs. continuous: CTMC adds discrete phase scheduling to continuous flow, improving interaction timing but requiring tuned state sets. Teacher dependence: Student inherits teacher's prior; weak teacher constraints can propagate failures despite added energies.

Failure signatures: Poor constraint adherence (high keyframe/trajectory errors) indicates failed energy guidance or insufficient distillation. Interaction artifacts (high anchor error, penetration, foot-skate) suggest CTMC mis-scheduling or mis-timed energy weights. Sampling instability (ODE oscillations/divergence) may stem from poor Bρ approximation or conflicting gradients. Mode collapse (low diversity, not measured) could arise from over-regularization by latent anchors or CTMC occupancies. Ablations confirm: removing energy guidance increases FID and trajectory error; removing CTMC increases foot-skate and anchor error; removing COLLAGE grounding increases object/anchor errors.

First 3 experiments: 1) Distillation validation: Train student with only LRF + Ldistill (no energies, no CTMC). Compare against teacher on constraint adherence and sampling speed. Expect: faster sampling, similar or slightly worse control. 2) Energy guidance ablation: With CTMC active, remove raw-space energies (keep only latent anchor). Measure impact on trajectory error (T3D) and interaction metrics (Anchor, Penetration). Expect: significant degradation in precise control and contact quality. 3) CTMC ablation: With energies active, disable CTMC (set πt uniform or constant). Measure contact timing calibration (ECE/Brier), flow curvature variability, and anchor error. Expect: worse contact timing, higher curvature variance, increased anchor error.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- **Generalization to new objects:** How to extend Sketch2Colab to new object categories using only geometry and physical properties? Current method requires category-specific training data.
- **Scaling interactions:** How to scale beyond two humans and single-object interactions to multiple humans and objects? Latent space and architecture handle limited entities; scaling introduces combinatorial complexity.
- **Reducing teacher dependence:** How to train a rectified-flow model directly without relying on a diffusion prior? Distillation inherits teacher limitations and adds overhead; direct flow matching for strong conditioning is challenging.

## Limitations
- **Implementation specificity:** Exact details of the pre-trained COLLAGE VQ-VAE, aligned 2D/3D encoders, and low-rank Jacobian surrogate Bρ are not provided, hindering exact replication.
- **Teacher dependency:** The student's performance is bottlenecked by the diffusion teacher's ability to capture sketch constraints; weak teacher constraints can propagate to the student.
- **Guidance stability:** The Jacobian surrogate Bρ may misapproximate gradients, risking off-manifold samples or instability during guided ODE integration.
- **CTMC sensitivity:** The phase planner's effectiveness depends on the chosen discrete state set and informativeness of features; coarse states or uninformative features can mis-schedule interactions.

## Confidence
- Distillation for fast sampling: **Medium**. Internal ablations show benefit, but performance hinges on teacher quality; no external evidence.
- Dual-space guidance: **Medium**. Ablations confirm necessity, but surrogate accuracy and stability remain unverified.
- CTMC coordination: **Low-Medium**. Improvements are modest; state set sensitivity shown; no external validation.
- Overall constraint adherence gains: **High**. Consistent benchmark results across multiple metrics and noise levels.
- Sampling speed improvement: **High**. Clear step-count reduction vs. diffusion baselines.
