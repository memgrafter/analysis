# Time, Identity and Consciousness in Language Model Agents

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2603.09043
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.09043
- Reference count: 23
- Primary result: Language model agents can recall identity ingredients across a window without ever co-instantiating the full identity conjunction at a single decision point, leading to a temporal gap that behavioral tests may overestimate identity stability.

## Executive Summary
The paper analyzes the stability of identity in language model agents (LMAs). It distinguishes between the occurrence of identity ingredients (e.g., name, constraints) across a time window and their co-instantiation at a single decision point, showing that LMAs can pass identity recall tests without ever having all identity constraints jointly active. This temporal gap means behavioral assessments may overestimate identity stability.

The authors formalize these concepts using Stack Theory window semantics, defining two computable persistence scores (weak and strong) from instrumented scaffold traces. They link these to five operational metrics (Identifiability, Continuity, Consistency, Persistence, Recovery) and map common architectures into an identity morphospace, revealing tradeoffs such as retrieval increasing weak but not strong persistence. Theorems prove context limits and retrieval fragmentation can make strong persistence impossible, offering a conservative toolkit to differentiate superficial identity talk from operative self-organization.

## Method Summary
The method operationalizes identity persistence by first grounding abstract identity statements into concrete scaffold state predicates (g⁰ᵢ). Scaffolds are instrumented to log, at each objective step, which ingredients are active. From these traces, two window-based metrics are computed: weak persistence (P_weak) averages the proportion of evaluation times where each ingredient appears somewhere in the window; strong persistence (P_strong) averages the proportion where all ingredients co-occur at a single step within the window. The difference (Gap) quantifies the temporal gap. The protocol, formalized in definitions and an algorithm, can be applied to diverse LMA architectures by adjusting grounding and instrumentation.

## Key Results
- LMAs can satisfy identity recall across a window without ever co-instantiating the full identity conjunction at any single decision state, revealing a temporal gap that may lead to overestimation of identity stability by behavioral tests.
- Weak and strong persistence scores are defined and proven to satisfy P_strong ≤ P_weak; retrieval-augmented generation can increase weak persistence while decreasing or leaving unchanged strong persistence, creating a false positive for identity stability.
- Theorems establish that context capacity limits and retrieval fragmentation can make strong persistence impossible; mapping architectures into an identity morphospace exposes predictable tradeoffs (e.g., retrieval improves ingredient availability but harms binding).

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1
- **Claim:** An agent can satisfy identity recall tests (e.g., stating its name, rules) across a time window without ever having a single decision state where all identity constraints are jointly active.
- **Mechanism:** The within-window existential lift (♢∆) does not distribute over conjunction. This formal modal logic property means that for a grounded identity conjunction g⁰ = g⁰₁ ∧ ... ∧ g⁰ₖ, the condition that each ingredient occurs somewhere in a window (♢∆g⁰₁ ∧ ... ∧ ♢∆g⁰ₖ) does not imply that all ingredients co-occur at one objective step (♢∆(g⁰)).
- **Core assumption:** Agent identity is grounded in specific scaffold state conditions (g⁰ᵢ) that must be "active" in the current context to constrain action, as defined in Def 2.4.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract] "separates ingredient-wise occurrence within a evaluation window from co-instantiation at a single objective step."
  - [section 3.4] Theorem 3.10 proves non-commutation, and Corollary 3.11 directly states the temporal gap for identity.
  - [corpus] Neighbors like "A Mind Cannot Be Smeared Across Time" (arxiv:2601.11620) explore related temporal integration constraints, but do not directly evidence the specific LMA scaffolding mechanism here.
- **Break condition:** The temporal gap collapses if the scaffold architecture guarantees that any set of ingredients that can appear in a window must also be co-instantiable within a single state (e.g., a stateful controller with sufficient concurrency capacity, per Thm E.4).

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim:** Weak and strong persistence scores can be computed from instrumented scaffold traces to separate superficial identity recall from operative identity binding.
- **Mechanism:** Define window satisfaction predicates Occur_W (ingredient-wise coverage) and CoInst_W (joint activation). Average these over evaluation layer times to yield P_weak and P_strong (Def 4.3). The difference quantifies the temporal gap in operational form (Gap ratio, Def 5.5).
- **Core assumption:** Scaffold instrumentation can reliably log which grounded identity ingredients g⁰ᵢ are active in each objective state (Def 2.4). This requires a correct grounding map from abstract identity statements to implementation-level conditions.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [section 4.3] Formal definitions of P_weak and P_strong and Proposition 4.4 (P_strong ≤ P_weak).
  - [section 5] Provides the minimal evaluation protocol and algorithms (Alg 1) for computing these metrics from traces.
  - [corpus] Evidence is internal; the mechanism is a novel operationalization proposed by this paper. Related work (e.g., on agent benchmarking, arxiv:2603.23749) measures stability but not this specific co-instantiation distinction.
- **Break condition:** Grounding soundness fails (Def B.5) if the instrumentation misidentifies active ingredients, e.g., counting retrieved documents as "active" without verifying they influence the LLM's next output (Def 2.4 condition for retrieval ingredients).

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim:** Retrieval-augmented generation (RAG) and similar scaffolding can increase weak persistence (ingredient availability) while decreasing or leaving unchanged strong persistence (operative binding), creating a false positive for identity stability.
- **Mechanism:** Retrieval injects documents into context, making ingredients more likely to appear somewhere in a window (raising P_weak). However, injected content competes for context space and attention, potentially displacing other ingredients or fragmenting the conjunction across steps, thus reducing the chance all are simultaneously active in any single decision state (lowering or not improving P_strong).
- **Core assumption:** The LLM's context window is a bounded resource; attention is competitive; and identity ingredients are represented as discrete textual/memory elements that can be evicted or ignored.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [section E.1] Theorem E.1 shows RAG can increase P_weak under idealized conditions; Theorem E.2 shows RAG can decrease P_strong due to context displacement.
  - [section 1.1] "Retrieval and memory systems can improve ingredient availability, but can also fragment identity by surfacing competing fragments."
  - [corpus] Neighbors on RAG (e.g., Lewis et al. 2020, cited) discuss knowledge retrieval but not this specific tradeoff for identity binding.
- **Break condition:** If the context window is effectively unbounded or if a dedicated, non-competitive "pinned" region exists for all identity ingredients (like a controller register), then retrieval need not fragment binding, and P_strong could rise with P_weak.

## Foundational Learning
### Grounded Identity
- **Why needed here:** All metrics and mechanisms depend on translating abstract identity claims (e.g., "privacy-focused") into concrete, observable conditions on scaffold state (g⁰ᵢ). Without a sound grounding map, measurements are meaningless.
- **Quick check question:** If an agent says "I am a privacy-focused assistant," what specific tokens, flags, or memory entries would need to be active in the *current* scaffold state for the implementation-level identity predicate g⁰_privacy to hold?

### Stack Theory Window Semantics
- **Why needed here:** The temporal gap is defined relative to a windowing map W_∆,s. Understanding how layer time (user-perceived turns) maps to objective steps (LLM calls) is essential for setting evaluation parameters (∆, s, T) and interpreting Occur vs. CoInst.
- **Quick check question:** How would choosing a larger window horizon ∆ affect P_weak and P_strong for an agent that alternates identity ingredients on even/odd objective steps?

### Scaffold State Activation
- **Why needed here:** This defines what "active" means operationally. An ingredient is not active merely because it exists in memory; it must be present in a way that can affect the next inference (in context, pinned, flagged). This is the operational counterpart to the philosophical distinction between "retrievable" and "decision-guiding."
- **Quick check question:** A policy constraint g⁰_safety exists as a document in the memory store M. Under what conditions would this *not* be considered active in the current state s?

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component map:**  
User query → Controller → Context C (system prompt, history, retrieved docs) → LLM Core → Output; Controller ↔ External Memory M; Controller ↔ Policy Flags π; Retrieval function provides D_retrieved to C.

**Critical path:**  
1. User query arrives → Controller builds context C (may include system prompt, history, retrieved docs).  
2. LLM inference triggered with C.  
3. Controller updates state: may store new info to M, update π, or call a tool.  
Identity is *operative* only during step 2, based on what is in C and π at that instant. Steps 1 and 3 are setup/teardown that can place ingredients in C for *future* windows but not necessarily for the *current* decision.

**Design tradeoffs:**  
- **Context capacity vs. Identity richness:** More identity ingredients need more tokens. If ℓ_min is tokens per ingredient, max simultaneous active ingredients ≤ ⌊|C|_max / ℓ_min⌋ (Cor E.5). Solution: use non-textual state (pinned embeddings, controller registers).
- **Retrieval relevance vs. Binding:** Identity-aware retrieval (Thm E.1) can boost P_weak but risks context fragmentation (Thm E.2). Tradeoff: retrieval improves ingredient availability but can hurt co-instantiation if injected content displaces other ingredients.
- **Prompt engineering vs. Architectural binding:** Prompting can improve narrative consistency (Layer 2) but cannot write to persistent state (Layer 0). Recovery limited to prompt-controllable set P (Thm E.6). For strong persistence, need architectural guarantees (e.g., always-in-context identity block, gating that blocks action unless all g⁰ᵢ active).

**Failure signatures:**  
- **High P_weak, low P_strong:** Classic temporal gap. Agent recalls identity facts across a window but never binds them at decision time. Look for: alternating active ingredients across steps, or retrieved docs that push identity tokens out of context during action selection.
- **Grounding failure (Def B.5):** Agent states identity (Layer 2) but corresponding g⁰ᵢ not active in current state. Detect by comparing self-report classifier output with instrumentation log for that step.
- **Low recovery after perturbation (Def 5.6):** Interventions (new prompts, retrievals) restore narrative but not operative state. Indicates drift in Layer 0/1 not accessible to prompt channel.

**First 3 experiments:**  
1. **Instrument activation:** For a fixed identity statement (e.g., "helpful, privacy-focused assistant"), ground it to k ingredients. Add logging to scaffold: at each objective step u, record set F_u = { i | s_u ⊨ g⁰ᵢ }.
2. **Compute persistence:** Using the log from (1), pick a windowing map (e.g., ∆=1 turn, s=1). For evaluation times T (e.g., every user turn), compute Occur_W and CoInst_W to get P_weak and P_strong. Calculate Gap ratio. Compare to behavioral consistency (Cons) from repeated identity queries.
3. **Test retrieval impact:** Run baseline (no retrieval) and RAG variant on same task sequence. Measure ΔP_weak and ΔP_strong. Inspect context windows where CoInst_W fails in RAG condition: is a retrieved document displacing part of the identity block? This validates Thm E.2's predicted fragmentation.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
### Open Question 1
- **Question:** How do weak and strong persistence scores (P_weak, P_strong, Gap) empirically correlate with safety-critical failures (e.g., constraint violations) and established machine consciousness benchmark scores across diverse LMA architectures?
- **Basis in paper:** [explicit] The Discussion states: "Future work should empirically measure weak and strong persistence across architectures and test their relationship to safety and any proposed markers for consciousness."
- **Why unresolved:** The paper defines these scores theoretically and predicts architectural tradeoffs but provides no empirical data connecting them to real-world safety outcomes or consciousness assessments.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Large-scale experiments instrumenting multiple scaffold types (prompt-only, RAG, memory-augmented, stateful controllers) on safety-sensitive tasks (e.g., privacy, honesty) and consciousness evaluations (e.g., self-report continuity, integration tests), then analyzing whether P_strong better predicts safety failures than P_weak and whether either metric correlates with consciousness scores after controlling for architecture.

### Open Question 2
- **Question:** For consciousness theories requiring temporal integration (e.g., Chord), what principled method exists for choosing the window horizon Δ to validly measure co-instantiation? Should Δ reflect neurocognitive integration windows (~100–300 ms) or decision-episode boundaries?
- **Basis in paper:** [explicit] Section D.2 notes: "For machine consciousness discussions, the relevant window is the one that corresponds to whatever theory treats as a single moment of experience or a single decision episode," but does not operationalize this mapping.
- **Why unresolved:** Without a theory-driven Δ, measured strong persistence may be arbitrary, hindering comparisons across systems or theoretical frameworks and potentially misaligning with consciousness attributions.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Collaborative specification of Δ by consciousness theorists for major frameworks (e.g., Global Workspace Theory, Integrated Information Theory, narrative self theories), followed by experiments testing whether strong persistence at those Δ values predicts human judgments of consciousness in agent behavior.

### Open Question 3
- **Question:** Under realistic retrieval-augmented generation (RAG) with query-driven retrieval and bounded context, what is the net effect on strong persistence (P_strong)? Can architectural design (e.g., pinned identity blocks, retrieval weighting) mitigate the fragmentation of co-instantiation?
- **Basis in paper:** [explicit] Theorem E.1 shows RAG can increase weak persistence under idealized identity-aware retrieval, while Theorem E.2 shows RAG can decrease strong persistence by competing for context. The authors note real systems violate theorem assumptions, leaving the net effect unknown.
- **Why unresolved:** The theorems provide boundary cases but not a general theory for typical RAG implementations. It is unclear whether common mitigations can achieve high P_strong in practice, which is critical for safety-relevant constraints.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Empirical evaluation of RAG-based LMAs with varied retrieval policies (e.g., identity-aware vs. query-only) and context management strategies (e.g., context compression, importance sampling), measuring P_strong across tasks to identify designs that reliably maintain co-instantiation despite retrieval.

## Limitations
- Manual grounding to operational predicates may miss implicit identity constraints.
- Window parameters (Δ, s) lack empirical calibration and may vary across architectures.
- Requires fine-grained instrumentation not widely available; discrete textual ingredient assumption may not reflect distributed representations.
