# 3DGS$^2$-TR: Scalable Second-Order Trust-Region Method for 3D Gaussian Splatting

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2602.00395
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.00395
- **Reference count:** 40
- **Primary result:** Achieves superior reconstruction quality with 50% fewer training iterations than ADAM, adding less than 1GB GPU memory; on Tanks & Temples, PSNR improves by 0.56 dB at 7k iterations and 1.19 dB at 30k iterations.

## Executive Summary
3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) suffers from slow convergence during scene training. This paper proposes 3DGS²-TR, a scalable second-order optimizer that addresses this issue while maintaining computational efficiency. The method approximates curvature using only the diagonal of the Hessian matrix, estimated via Hutchinson’s method, which retains O(n) complexity and memory overhead comparable to the ADAM optimizer. To mitigate the challenges posed by the nonlinear rasterization process, a parameter-wise trust region based on squared Hellinger distance is introduced, bounding updates for each Gaussian primitive with a single hyperparameter. Evaluations on standard benchmarks (Mip-NeRF360, Tanks & Temples, Deep Blending) show that 3DGS²-TR matches or exceeds ADAM’s quality using only half the training iterations, while adding less than 1GB of GPU memory—representing a 17% increase over ADAM and an 85% reduction compared to prior second-order approaches. For instance, on Tanks & Temples, it surpasses ADAM’s PSNR by 0.56 dB at 7k iterations and 1.19 dB at 30k iterations.

## Method Summary
3DGS²-TR is a stochastic second-order optimizer tailored to 3D Gaussian splatting. It computes gradients on single-image minibatches and maintains exponential moving averages (EMAs) for both the gradient ($\hat{g}$) and the diagonal of the Gauss-Newton matrix ($\hat{D}$). The diagonal Hessian is estimated every $l$ iterations using Hutchinson’s method: random Rademacher vectors $z$ are applied to compute Hessian-vector products via one forward and backward pass; the element-wise product with $z$ yields an unbiased diagonal sample, which is averaged over $\nu$ samples and then smoothed into the EMA buffer. The preconditioned step $\Delta x = -\hat{D}^{-1} \hat{g}$ is computed element-wise. To handle the nonlinearities and discontinuities of the rasterization process, a parameter-wise trust region is derived from the squared Hellinger distance between the Gaussian before and after an update. This yields a per-parameter radius $\eta$ that clips the step magnitude; the radius depends on a global hyperparameter $\epsilon$ (the maximum allowed Hellinger distance) and decays over training. The algorithm updates parameters as $x_{t+1} = x_t + \text{clip}(\Delta x, -\eta, +\eta)$. The entire process maintains linear complexity in the number of parameters and incurs only a modest memory increase.

## Key Results
- Reduces required training iterations by approximately **50%** compared to ADAM while achieving better reconstruction quality (PSNR/SSIM/LPIPS).
- Adds **less than 1GB** of GPU memory overhead (about 17% over ADAM and 85% less than previous second-order methods like 3DGS-LM).
- On Tanks & Temples, **PSNR improvements** of **0.56 dB** at 7k iterations and **1.19 dB** at 30k iterations over ADAM.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1: Diagonal Hessian via Hutchinson’s Method
- **Claim:** Using only the diagonal of the Hessian matrix (estimated via Hutchinson’s method) provides sufficient curvature information for faster convergence while maintaining $O(n)$ complexity.
- **Mechanism:** The method approximates the Gauss-Newton matrix $J^\top J$ by sampling random vectors $z$ and computing $J^\top J z$ via Hessian-vector products (one forward/backward pass per sample). The element-wise product with $z$ yields an unbiased diagonal estimate. This avoids dense matrix storage $O(n^2)$ and inversion $O(n^3)$.
- **Core assumption:** Diagonal curvature captures the dominant conditioning effects for each parameter independently, and the noise from stochastic estimation can be managed via EMA.
- **Evidence anchors:** Abstract: "approximates curvature using only the diagonal of the Hessian matrix, estimated via Hutchinson’s method... same complexity as ADAM, $O(n)$"; Section 4.1: "We follow the spirit of Sophia... estimate the diagonal of the Gauss–Newton matrix $J^\top J$ using Hutchinson’s method... the Hessian-vector product corresponds to exactly one forward and backward pass"; Related work (e.g., Sophia, AdaHessian) validates diagonal approximation in large-scale contexts.
- **Break condition:** If the Hessian diagonal is poorly estimated (e.g., insufficient samples $\nu=1$) or parameters are highly coupled, the step direction becomes uninformative or destabilizing without the trust region.

### Mechanism 2: Parameter-wise Hellinger Trust Region
- **Claim:** A parameter-wise trust region based on normalized squared Hellinger distance bounds updates to prevent destabilization from 3DGS’s nonlinear rasterization and discontinuities.
- **Mechanism:** For each Gaussian parameter (position, scale, opacity, color, rotation), derive a maximum update magnitude $\eta$ such that the squared Hellinger distance between the Gaussian before/after update, normalized by scale determinant, stays below $\epsilon$. This directly limits the change in projected opacity (the key interaction quantity).
- **Core assumption:** Bounding the discrepancy in the Gaussian’s probability density function (via Hellinger) ensures that its contribution to rendered pixels changes smoothly, preserving the validity of the local quadratic approximation during optimization.
- **Evidence anchors:** Abstract: "parameter-wise trust-region technique based on the squared Hellinger distance"; Section 4.2: "We instead propose a parameter-wise trust-region strategy... we quantify the magnitude of an update to a Gaussian using the squared Hellinger distance... We derive a parameter-wise trust-region radius $\eta= \text{SHD}(x_t, \epsilon)$"; Section 6: "ADAM-TR (applying trust region to ADAM) already achieves better quality than vanilla ADAM"; Trust region methods are standard in nonlinear optimization; this paper’s novelty is the specific Hellinger-based derivation for Gaussian parameters.
- **Break condition:** If the $\epsilon$ decay schedule is too aggressive, updates become too conservative, slowing convergence; if too lax, the trust region fails to prevent divergence from nonlinear effects.

### Mechanism 3: Combined Preconditioning and Clipping
- **Claim:** Combining second-order preconditioning (diagonal Hessian inverse) with parameter-wise Hellinger trust region clipping yields superlinear-like convergence per step with minimal overhead, reducing required iterations by $\sim$50% versus ADAM.
- **Mechanism:** Compute preconditioned step $\Delta x = -\hat{D}^{-1} \hat{g}$ (element-wise division). Then clip each parameter’s update magnitude to its $\eta$. This adapts step size to curvature while ensuring no single parameter changes too drastically, mitigating noise and nonlinearity.
- **Core assumption:** The product of preconditioning (improved direction) and trust region (robustness) is more effective than either alone, especially in the piecewise-continuous 3DGS loss landscape.
- **Evidence anchors:** Abstract: "Our approach... maintains same $O(n)$ complexity as ADAM... 3DGS²-TR achieves better reconstruction quality using 50% fewer training iterations"; Section 5, Table 1: 3DGS²-TR outperforms ADAM and 3DGS-LM at 7k/15k/30k iterations across datasets; Section 4, Algorithm 1: explicit clipping step after inverse Hessian multiplication; Related: Sophia uses diagonal Hessian but no problem-specific trust region; this integration is a contribution.
- **Break condition:** If Hessian estimation is biased (e.g., during Gaussian densification where parameters are added), preconditioning misguides; the paper explicitly avoids densification to prevent this.

## Foundational Learning
**Stochastic second-order optimization**
- *Why needed:* The method builds on using curvature (Hessian) to precondition gradients but must do so cheaply. Understanding diagonal approximation and EMA stability is key.
- *Quick check:* Why is a full Hessian infeasible for 3DGS, and how does Hutchinson’s method give a usable diagonal estimate?

**Gaussian splatting parameterization and rasterization**
- *Why needed:* The trust region bounds are derived per-parameter based on how each affects the Gaussian’s 3D shape and projected opacity. Without knowing position, scale/rotation (covariance), opacity, and color, the derivations in Appendix B are meaningless.
- *Quick check:* How does changing a Gaussian’s scale along one axis versus its rotation affect the rendered pixel’s expected value?

**Trust region methods in nonlinear optimization**
- *Why needed:* The core innovation is a problem-specific trust region. One must understand the general principle (limit step size within a region where the local model is trustworthy) to appreciate why Hellinger distance is chosen.
- *Quick check:* What’s the difference between a trust region method and a line search, and why might a parameter-wise trust region be better for highly non-convex, discontinuous problems?

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component map:** Gradient estimator (minibatch images → residual vectors & Jacobians → stochastic gradient) → Curvature estimator (Hutchinson diagonal via Hessian-vector products) → EMA buffers (moving averages of gradient and diagonal) → Step computation (Δx = -D̂⁻¹ ĝ) → Trust region calculator (compute per-parameter η from ε) → Clipper (element-wise clip Δx to [-η, +η]) → Updater (x ← x + clipped Δx) → Rasterizer & loss (differentiable CUDA kernel rendering, L1+SSIM loss).

**Critical path:** The dominant cost per iteration is the backward pass for gradient accumulation. Hessian estimation adds ~10% overhead (ν=1, l=10) because it requires an extra forward+backward every 10 iterations. Trust region computation is cheap (element-wise ops). Execution flow: gradient compute → EMA update → (every l iterations) Hessian compute → EMA update → clipping → parameter update.

**Design tradeoffs:**
- *Memory vs. accuracy:* Storing only the diagonal Hessian (O(n)) vs. a dense matrix (O(n²)). Diagonal approximation discards off-diagonal curvature but keeps memory linear.
- *Compute vs. stability:* Hutchinson sample size ν: ν=1 is cheapest but high variance; the paper uses ν=1 and relies on EMA smoothing. Update interval l: smaller l gives fresher curvature but more overhead.
- *Trust region hyperparameter ε:* A single scalar controls all parameters. Too large → instability; too small → slow progress. Paper uses an exponential decay schedule.
- *Densification compatibility:* The diagonal estimator breaks when inserting new Gaussians (bias). The tradeoff is faster convergence without densification vs. missing adaptive primitive growth.

**Failure signatures:**
- **NaNs in D̂:** If any diagonal entry becomes zero or near-zero, the element-wise division in Δx blows up. Typically occurs after a Hessian update with poor estimation. Diagnose: monitor diagonal values; add a small floor.
- **Divergence despite trust region:** If rendered images become corrupted (all black/white), the trust region likely failed to bound nonlinear effects. Check if η values are too large (ε not decayed enough).
- **No improvement over ADAM:** If bD is too smoothed (θ₂ close to 1) or l too large, curvature signal is stale. Also possible if ε schedule is too conservative. Diagnose: compare diagonal norms over time.
- **Memory growth:** Should be ~17% over ADAM. If memory increases excessively, likely storing full Hessian or retaining unnecessary buffers.

**First 3 experiments:**
1. **Baseline convergence comparison:** Train identical scenes with ADAM, ADAM-TR (clipping only), and 3DGS²-TR. Plot PSNR/SSIM vs. iterations. Expect 3DGS²-TR to reach ADAM’s 30k-iteration quality by ~15k iterations.
2. **Ablation on trust region:** Run 3DGS²-TR with ε fixed vs. decayed, and with clipping disabled (pure second-order). Evaluate the impact on convergence speed and stability.
3. **Scaling memory/compute test:** Train a large scene (high Gaussian count, high-resolution images). Measure peak GPU memory and time per iteration. Verify <1GB overhead and linear scaling with number of parameters.

## Limitations
- The diagonal Hessian approximation discards off-diagonal curvature, potentially missing important coupling effects, especially without densification.
- Hutchinson’s estimator with a single sample (ν=1) introduces high variance, which is only mitigated by heavy EMA smoothing (θ₂=0.999); this may delay curvature information.
- The trust region’s hyperparameter ε and its decay schedule are critical but underspecified, leading to sensitivity that requires manual tuning.
