# AgentCPM-Explore: Realizing Long-Horizon Deep Exploration for Edge-Scale Agents

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2602.06485  
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.06485  
- **Reference count:** 26  
- **Primary result:** AgentCPM-Explore achieves SOTA among 4B models, matches or exceeds 8B-class SOTA on four benchmarks, outperforms larger models (Claude-4.5-Sonnet, DeepSeek‑v3.2) on five benchmarks, and reaches 97.09% accuracy on GAIA text‑based tasks under pass@64.

## Executive Summary
Edge‑scale agent models (4B parameters) face three training bottlenecks: catastrophic forgetting during supervised fine‑tuning, reward noise sensitivity in reinforcement learning, and reasoning degradation from redundant long‑context information. AgentCPM‑Explore addresses these with a holistic framework: parameter‑space model merging via DELLA, a three‑tier reward signal denoising mechanism, and context information refinement through RL‑based intent alignment and teacher‑model distillation. Evaluated on eight benchmarks, the system matches or exceeds larger models, demonstrating that inference stability—not inherent capability—limits edge‑scale agents.

## Method Summary
The method consists of three stages.  
1. **Merge without forgetting:** Supervised fine‑tuning (SFT) on a base model (Qwen3‑4B‑Thinking‑2507) using expert trajectories yields θ_sft. DELLA merging then combines θ_sft with the untouched base model θ_base via magnitude‑based delta sampling and rescaling, preserving general skills while adding specialization.  
2. **Robust RL with filtered data:** Reinforcement learning (RL) uses a three‑tier filter to discard trajectories affected by environmental noise (timeouts, server errors), format errors (JSON parsing failures), or extreme lengths before policy updates. This prevents erroneous gradients from destabilizing training.  
3. **Context refinement:** The policy’s search intents are optimized via RL to be comprehensible to a summarization model; the summarizer is distilled from a teacher (DeepSeek‑V3.2‑thinking) to accurately compress tool outputs, avoiding information overload in the policy.

## Key Results
- SOTA accuracy among 4B models on eight benchmarks; matches or exceeds 8B‑class SOTA on four benchmarks.  
- Outperforms larger models (Claude‑4.5‑Sonnet, DeepSeek‑v3.2) on five benchmarks.  
- Achieves 97.09% accuracy on GAIA under pass@64, highlighting inference stability as the primary bottleneck.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1: DELLA merging mitigates catastrophic forgetting
- **Claim:** Parameter‑space model merging via DELLA combats catastrophic forgetting during SFT by combining complementary general and task‑specific knowledge without destructive interference.  
- **How it works:** Compute deltas θ_base – θ_sft for both models; merge using magnitude‑based sampling (lower magnitudes face higher drop probability) and rescaling. This retains high‑magnitude, task‑critical updates from θ_sft while preserving the base model’s general capabilities via θ_base, controlled by λ (step size) and equal weights w_i.  
- **Core assumption:** General skills (long‑context reading, instruction following) and specialized execution skills occupy largely non‑overlapping parameter subspaces, allowing merger without mutual cancellation.  
- **Evidence:** Abstract mentions parameter‑space merging; Section 2.2 details the pruning and rescaling formulas; neighboring work (e.g., Yunque DeepResearch) also identifies context pollution and forgetting as core challenges.  
- **Break condition:** If base and SFT models’ critical updates are highly correlated (similar magnitude patterns), random dropping could eliminate essential task‑specific weights or retain detrimental base weights, leading to merged‑model degradation.

### Mechanism 2: Three‑tier reward signal denoising prevents RL collapse
- **Claim:** A three‑tier denoising mechanism filters trajectories where final rewards cannot be accurately credited to model decisions, preventing training collapse in RL.  
- **How it works:** Before gradient updates, trajectories are removed if they involve (1) environmental noise (tool timeouts, server errors), (2) format errors (JSON parse failures, non‑standard outputs), or (3) extreme lengths (too short/long). This blocks erroneous negative gradients from penalizing correct reasoning due to external failures or trivial tasks.  
- **Core assumption:** Small 4B models have limited tolerance for noisy gradient signals; a few misattributed failures can destabilize policy estimation and induce “learned avoidance” of effective exploratory behaviors.  
- **Evidence:** Abstract cites the three‑tier filter; Section 2.3 defines each filter and notes performance degrades significantly when any is removed; Section 3.4 shows RL learning curves collapse without filtering, with entropy collapse attributed to erroneous credit assignment.  
- **Break condition:** If filtering thresholds (T_timeout, L_min, L_max) are too aggressive, the training distribution shifts toward only perfect, medium‑length trajectories, reducing data diversity and causing overfitting to narrow patterns.

### Mechanism 3: Context information refinement via RL intent alignment and teacher distillation
- **Claim:** Context refinement improves information fidelity from tools, preventing reasoning degradation caused by redundant long‑context inputs.  
- **How it works:** Two loops: (1) the policy model’s search intents are optimized via RL to be “more readily comprehensible to the summarization model.” (2) A teacher model (DeepSeek‑V3.2‑thinking) generates high‑quality summaries via multi‑sampling; these are used to SFT‑tune the 4B summarizer. This reduces lossy compression before context enters the policy model.  
- **Core assumption:** In multi‑model pipelines, performance is bottlenecked by the weakest link—the summarizer’s ability to extract relevant information—and the policy must be coordinated to produce intents the summarizer can handle.  
- **Evidence:** Abstract describes the refinement approach; Section 2.4 states “synergistic adaptation proves more effective than stronger components”; Section 3.3 (Figure 2) shows fixed agent performance varies with summarizer quality and that SFT‑tuning the summarizer yields gains.  
- **Break condition:** If RL intent alignment overfits to a specific summarizer’s quirks, the policy may lose generalization when the summarizer changes or faced with novel tool outputs, leading to drop on out‑of‑distribution tasks.

## Foundational Learning

### Catastrophic Forgetting in SFT
- **Why needed:** The paper’s core premise is that direct SFT on 4B models destroys general abilities, making model merging necessary. Understanding forgetting explains why naive fine‑tuning fails for small models.  
- **Quick check:** If you fine‑tune a 4B model on browser‑tool trajectories, what specific general capabilities (e.g., long‑context reading, complex instruction following) are most likely to degrade first, and why?

### Reward Sparsity and Noise Sensitivity in RL
- **Why needed:** The denoising mechanism assumes small models cannot handle noisy, sparse end‑task rewards typical of agent RL. This is fundamental to understanding why filtering is prioritized over sophisticated attribution algorithms.  
- **Quick check:** In a web research task, give three concrete examples where a final reward of 0 (failure) would be a false negative due to environmental noise, format errors, or trajectory length, and explain why the model’s intermediate steps might have been correct.

### Long‑Context Information Contamination
- **Why needed:** The context refinement methods target the “information bottleneck” hypothesis—that small models are overwhelmed by irrelevant tool outputs—which distinguishes the problem from mere context length limits.  
- **Quick check:** How does redundant information in a retrieved webpage (e.g., ads, navigation menus) specifically degrade a 4B model’s reasoning in a multi‑hop QA task compared to a 70B model?

## Architecture Onboarding

**Component map:**  
AgentDock supplies tool interfaces and sandbox environments (Dockerized MCP servers, unified JSON‑RPC, error semantics, MCPManager for routing/load‑balancing).  
AgentRL is the asynchronous training framework: AsyncSamplers generate trajectories and store them to a database; AsyncTrainer consumes trajectories and updates the policy; Inference Manager handles weight synchronization and GPU memory handover.  
AgentToLeaP is the evaluation layer that normalizes benchmarks (GAIA, FRAMES, etc.) into a canonical interface, runs parallel processes, and logs structured traces (dialog.json, trace.json).

**Critical path:**  
1. Start with base model (Qwen3‑4B‑Thinking).  
2. Perform SFT on expert trajectories → obtain θ_sft.  
3. Apply DELLA merging between θ_sft and θ_base to produce merged policy θ(*).  
4. Launch RL: AsyncSamplers interact with tools via AgentDock to collect rollouts.  
5. During rollout, apply the three‑tier denoising filter to each trajectory before storage.  
6. Context refinement: policy generates search intents; summarizer (SFT‑distilled from teacher) compresses tool outputs; compressed context is fed back to policy.  
7. AsyncTrainer updates the policy from filtered trajectories.  
8. Evaluate the final policy using AgentToLeaP across benchmarks.

**Design tradeoffs:**  
- **DELLA λ (0.9):** Higher λ moves merged weights closer to θ_sft (more task skill, risk of forgetting); lower λ preserves base (better general skills, less specialization). Tuned empirically.  
- **Filtering strictness:** Looser filters risk noisy gradients; stricter filters reduce data quantity and may remove borderline but valid exploratory samples. Thresholds (T_timeout, L_min, L_max) require benchmark‑specific tuning.  
- **Summarizer size:** Using a 14B teacher for distillation adds compute cost but improves 4B summarizer fidelity; co‑adaptation (RL intent alignment) can partly compensate for smaller summarizers.

**Failure signatures:**  
- **Training collapse:** RL curve drops sharply after first few hundred steps → likely environmental noise or format errors not filtered. Check tool error logs and JSON parsing validation.  
- **Stagnant performance after merging:** No gain over base model → DELLA merger may have dropped critical task‑specific deltas (check δ magnitude distribution) or base/SFT models were too similar.  
- **High variance in Pass@K:** Large gap between Pass@64 and Pass@1 → insufficient RL stabilization; policy has diverse but unreliable correct paths. May need more RL steps or explicit value modeling.  
- **Summarizer bottleneck:** Fixed agent performance varies with summarizer swaps (see Fig 2) → context refinement is the current ceiling. Distillation data quality or RL intent alignment likely inadequate.

**First 3 experiments:**  
1. **Ablate DELLA:** Train three models: (a) direct SFT, (b) naive average merging, (c) DELLA merging. Evaluate on general capabilities (e.g., MMLU) and target tasks (GAIA). Expect DELLA to retain general skills better while improving task performance.  
2. **Filter isolation test:** Run RL with only one filter active at a time (environmental, format, extreme length) versus all three. Monitor training stability (GPU utilization, loss curves) and final pass@1 on GAIA. Expect each filter removal causes degradation, with environmental noise causing earliest collapse.  
3. **Summarizer coordination test:** Keep policy fixed, swap summarizers: (a) base 4B, (b) SFT‑tuned 4B, (c) 14B teacher, (d) GPT‑5. Measure GAIA accuracy. Then, enable RL intent alignment for the policy paired with (a). Expect (d) > (c) > (b) > (a), but RL‑aligned (a) should approach or exceed (c), demonstrating coordination over raw strength.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
1. **Unified model architectures for multi‑task optimization.** The paper lists “refining information compression and memory mechanisms by exploring unified model architectures for multi‑task joint optimization” as a future direction. Current systems use separate modules (e.g., external summarizers) causing coordination loss; unified designs must balance compression quality with limited parameters. Evidence of resolution would include prototype integrated architectures evaluated on agent benchmarks, ablation studies isolating joint‑training benefits, and analysis of parameter allocation trade‑offs.

2. **Data augmentation and curriculum for diversity and readability.** The paper suggests “optimizing the training pipeline by enhancing data diversity and readability, addressing overfitting issues in small‑scale models.” Small models overfit on narrow, noisy agent trajectories. Evidence would be novel augmentation techniques (tool perturbation, trajectory rewriting) that improve cross‑benchmark performance, readability metrics correlated with reduced overfitting, and controlled comparisons on overfitting measures.

3. **Fault‑tolerant training infrastructure with precise checkpoint recovery.** Long‑horizon agent RL is prone to interruptions; the paper calls for “building a more robust training infrastructure to enable fault‑tolerant training and precise checkpoint recovery for resumption.” A resolved solution would snapshot exact state (policy, environment, replay buffer) with low overhead, show empirical recovery after simulated failures with minimal performance loss, and scale to multi‑node setups.

## Limitations
- **Dataset opacity:** SFT trajectories and RL task distribution (e.g., GAIA split) are undescribed, hindering reproducibility and generalizability assessment.  
- **Hyperparameter gaps:** Critical RL settings (KL penalty coefficient, PPO clip range, rollout horizon, filtering thresholds T_timeout, L_min, L_max) lack explicit values, requiring empirical guesswork.  
- **Summarizer distillation details:** Multi‑sampling strategy and prompt engineering for teacher‑student alignment remain vague, risking misimplementation of the refinement loop.

## Confidence
- DELLA merging efficacy: Medium  
- Reward denoising necessity: High  
- Context refinement bottleneck: Medium
