# Can Large Language Models Reason and Optimize Under Constraints?

## Quick Facts
- **ArXiv ID:** 2603.23004  
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.23004  
- **Reference count:** 25  
- **Primary result:** Across state-of-the-art LLMs, fine-tuning, and reinforcement learning, constraint satisfaction on Optimal Power Flow tasks plateaus near 55–60%, with power-flow equations consistently violated, indicating reliance on pattern matching rather than genuine constrained optimization.

## Executive Summary
This paper evaluates whether Large Language Models (LLMs) can perform end-to-end reasoning and optimization under physical constraints, using the Optimal Power Flow (OPF) problem as a rigorous testbed. The study examines state-of-the-art LLMs (including reasoning-augmented variants) through few-shot in-context learning, supervised fine-tuning (SFT), and reinforcement learning with Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO). The central finding is a persistent constraint satisfaction plateau around 55–60% across all models, scales, and training methods, with power-flow constraints being the primary bottleneck. SFT improves output format and numerical accuracy but not constraint satisfaction, while GRPO yields modest, topology-specific gains. Reasoning models do not systematically outperform non-reasoning ones, suggesting current LLMs rely on surface pattern matching rather than iterative physical reasoning.

## Method Summary
The investigation uses the OPFData dataset, which provides power grid topologies (case14, case30, case118) and pre-solved AC-OPF instances as JSON-structured inputs. Three training and evaluation regimes are employed: (1) **few-shot in-context learning (ICL)** with demonstration examples; (2) **supervised fine-tuning (SFT)** using LoRA to predict ground-truth solutions; and (3) **Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO)** applied to reasoning models, where a composite reward combines JSON format validity and the fraction of satisfied constraints. Evaluation metrics include **constraint satisfaction rate (CS%)** (micro-averaged percentage of individual constraint checks passing a threshold ε), **mean squared error (MSE)** between predicted and ground-truth values, and **output format validity** (parsable JSON). For each scenario, 1,000 test instances are used, with outputs parsed strictly to measure these metrics.

## Key Results
- Constraint satisfaction plateaus at 55–60% across all models and training methods, with power-flow residuals satisfied in only 7–25% of cases.
- Supervised fine-tuning (SFT) significantly improves output format validity and reduces MSE but does not increase constraint satisfaction.
- Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO) yields modest, topology-specific improvements in CS (e.g., gains on case30 but degradation on case14); reasoning models do not outperform non-reasoning counterparts.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1: Pattern Matching Over Iterative Optimization
**Claim:** LLMs fail to satisfy complex physical constraints because they rely on surface pattern matching from training data rather than performing genuine iterative optimization.  
**Mechanism:** During pre-training and fine-tuning, models learn to associate input graph structures with output values that are statistically feasible in the training set, but without internalizing the underlying power-flow equations that require matrix operations and iterative solving.  
**Evidence anchors:** Abstract states LLMs rely on pattern matching; Section 6.1 notes models cannot perform Newton-Raphson iterations in latent space and resort to "Result Guessing."  
**Break condition:** If models achieve >90% constraint satisfaction on N-1 scenarios without GRPO, or show consistent improvement with scale beyond 32B parameters.

### Mechanism 2: SFT Imitates Without Enforcing Feasibility
**Claim:** Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT) improves output format and numerical accuracy (MSE) but not constraint satisfaction because it teaches imitation without enforcing physical feasibility.  
**Mechanism:** SFT minimizes prediction error against ground-truth feasible solutions, but the loss function does not penalize intermediate constraint violations, allowing models to learn shortcuts that match values without understanding constraints.  
**Evidence anchors:** Abstract notes SFT improves formatting and MSE but not CS; Figure 3 shows fine-tuning consistently improves response validity and MSE while CS remains largely unchanged.  
**Break condition:** If SFT with augmented data that includes constraint violation examples leads to improved CS, or if models after SFT show robustness to topological changes.

### Mechanism 3: GRPO’s Sparse Reward Limits Gains
**Claim:** Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO) can modestly improve constraint satisfaction by rewarding feasibility, but gains are limited by sparse rewards and topology-specificity.  
**Mechanism:** GRPO samples multiple outputs and uses relative rewards based on constraint satisfaction, encouraging exploration of feasible regions, but the reward signal may not adequately decompose individual constraints, leading to uneven improvements.  
**Evidence anchors:** Abstract states GRPO yields modest, topology-specific gains; Table 1 shows GRPO improves power flow constraints for case30 but degrades for case14; Figure 4 indicates CS improvements for some models.  
**Break condition:** If GRPO with multi-component rewards (per-constraint) leads to consistent CS improvements across all topologies and N-1 scenarios.

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component map:** OPFData dataset -> Models (Llama 3.1, Qwen, DeepSeek-distilled, QwQ) -> Training (SFT with LoRA, GRPO) -> Evaluation (Metrics calculator for MSE, CS, format validity) -> Prompt templates for ICL.

**Critical path:** To replicate the study: (1) Load OPFData for case14, 30, 118. (2) For vanilla models: run ICL with few-shot prompts. (3) For SFT: fine-tune non-reasoning models on training set, then evaluate zero-shot and with ICL. (4) For GRPO: apply to reasoning models with constraint rewards, evaluate on N and N-1. (5) Aggregate metrics across 1000 samples.

**Design tradeoffs:** Choice between ICL vs. fine-tuning: ICL is flexible but limited by example quality; SFT is stable but may overfit; GRPO can improve feasibility but is computationally expensive. Reward design: using aggregate CS vs. per-constraint rewards affects learning efficiency.

**Failure signatures:** (1) Constraint satisfaction plateau at 55–60% with power-flow constraints mostly violated. (2) High MSE on larger grids (case118). (3) Invalid JSON outputs, especially for smaller models on complex topologies. (4) Degradation on N-1 scenarios for SFT models.

**First 3 experiments:**  
1. Run baseline evaluation: test all vanilla models on case14 with ICL to confirm the constraint satisfaction plateau.  
2. Perform SFT on Llama 3.1-8B for case14 and compare MSE and CS before/after fine-tuning.  
3. Apply GRPO to DeepSeek-R1-Llama3.1-8B on case30 and measure changes in CS, especially power-flow constraint satisfaction.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- **Open Question 1:** Can decomposing the GRPO reward into per-constraint components improve the constraint satisfaction plateau?  
  *Basis:* Future work suggests "richer reward signals that decompose individual constraint violations."  
  *Why unresolved:* Current GRPO uses an aggregate score; finer-grained rewards' effectiveness is unknown.  
  *What evidence would resolve it:* Train models with separate reward terms per constraint type and compare to aggregate baseline across multiple grids.

- **Open Question 2:** Do the observed OPF limitations (constraint plateau, N‑1 drop) generalize to other constrained optimization problems?  
  *Basis:* Future work calls for "more datasets from other domains."  
  *Why unresolved:* Only OPF was studied; broader applicability unclear.  
  *What evidence would resolve it:* Replicate the study on at least two different optimization benchmarks and compare metrics.

- **Open Question 3:** Why do reasoning-augmented LLMs fail to outperform non‑reasoning models on constrained optimization despite longer chain‑of‑thought?  
  *Basis:* "Reasoning models, despite their extended chain‑of‑thought generation, do not systematically outperform their non‑reasoning counterparts."  
  *Why unresolved:* No analysis of reasoning traces; underlying cause unknown.  
  *What evidence would resolve it:* Inspect CoT outputs for correct constraint reasoning; fine‑tune non‑reasoning models to reason and compare; test CoT prompting variants.

- **Open Question 4:** Would hybridizing LLMs with external numerical solvers (LLM formulates, solver executes) produce feasible solutions in natural language interaction?  
  *Basis:* Limitation "The lack of tools" suggests external solvers as a better paradigm.  
  *Why unresolved:* Hybrid approaches were not evaluated; feasibility unknown.  
  *What evidence would resolve it:* Build a prototype using Pyomo/IPOPT backend, evaluate on OPF test set for constraint satisfaction and MSE vs. end‑to‑end LLMs.

## Limitations
- **Reproducibility gaps:** Missing SFT hyperparameters (LoRA rank, lr, epochs), GRPO configurations (group size, KL penalty), and exact prompt construction prevent precise replication.
- **Mechanistic inference:** Claims about pattern matching vs. iterative reasoning are indirect; no ablations on model internal representations or step-by-step reasoning traces.
- **Corpus weakness:** Related literature lacks direct studies on physical constraint satisfaction, limiting broader validation.

## Confidence
- **Pattern-matching hypothesis (not genuine optimization):** Medium confidence – Consistent failure plateau suggests lack of true optimization, but unproven whether alternative training could yield different behavior.
- **SFT improves format/MSE, not CS:** High confidence – Experimental results across multiple models clearly show CS stagnation despite gains elsewhere.
- **GRPO yields modest, topology‑specific CS gains:** Medium confidence – Gains reported for some grids, but variability and missing RL details reduce certainty.

## Next Checks
1. **Physics-aware SFT:** Introduce a differentiable constraint‑violation penalty into the SFT loss (e.g., λ·∑|residuals|) and measure CS change.
2. **Per‑constraint GRPO:** Replace the aggregate CS reward with separate scalar rewards for power‑flow, voltage, and generator bounds; compare consistency of improvement across topologies.
3. **Out‑of‑distribution generalization:** Evaluate fine‑tuned and GRPO‑trained models on entirely unseen grid cases (e.g., case73, case240) without any in‑context examples to test true understanding versus memorization.
