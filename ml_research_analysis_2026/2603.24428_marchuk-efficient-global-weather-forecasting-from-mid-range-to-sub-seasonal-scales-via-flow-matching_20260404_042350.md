# Marchuk: Efficient Global Weather Forecasting from Mid-Range to Sub-Seasonal Scales via Flow Matching
## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2603.24428
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.24428
- **Reference count:** 13
- **Primary result:** Marchuk (276M parameters) matches the forecast skill of the 1.6B parameter LaDCast model at 15- and 30-day horizons (latitude-weighted RMSE, CRPS) while being approximately 6× faster at inference (50-member 30-day ensemble in ~7.5 minutes on an H100 GPU).

## Executive Summary
Marchuk introduces a latent flow-matching Diffusion Transformer (DiT) for subseasonal weather forecasting up to 30 days. It compresses global ERA5 weather maps (1.5° grid, 84 variables) by 64× using a pretrained DC-AE, then operates entirely in this compact latent space. The DiT backbone processes extended temporal context (up to 24 hours) with trainable spatial embeddings and 1D rotary positional encodings. Flow matching enables deterministic sampling with only ~20 integration steps, yielding high-quality probabilistic ensembles. Despite having only 276M parameters (∼6× fewer than LaDCast), Marchuk achieves comparable RMSE and CRPS at both 15- and 30-day leads and runs significantly faster, demonstrating that latent diffusion with flow matching is a promising direction for efficient, accurate subseasonal prediction.

## Method Summary
Marchuk’s pipeline consists of: (1) a frozen deep compression autoencoder (DC-AE) that encodes each 84-channel, 1.5° weather map into a low-dimensional latent tensor; (2) a Cross-DiT transformer that takes a sequence of latent frames (typically four 6-hourly steps covering 24 hours) and predicts future latent states; the DiT uses trainable 2D spatial positional embeddings, 1D rotary encodings along the time dimension, and optionally timestamp embeddings (month:day:hour) injected via cross-attention; (3) a flow matching head that learns a continuous velocity field to map noise to data distributions, allowing deterministic ODE integration with few steps (~20) per 4-day forecast segment; and (4) an autoregressive rollout loop where predicted latents are appended to the context window to extend coverage up to 30 days. The model is trained with Variable Horizon Training: a fixed 24-hour context and a random target horizon (1–8 days) using the flow matching loss. At inference, an ensemble is generated by sampling independent noise seeds.

## Key Results
- Comparable skill to LaDCast (1.6B parameters) at 15- and 30-day horizons (latitude-weighted RMSE and CRPS).
- ~6× faster inference: 50-member 30-day ensemble completes in ~7.5 minutes on an H100 GPU.
- Efficient architecture: 276M parameters operating in a 64× compressed latent space.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1: Latent Space Compression with DC-AE
**Claim:** Compressing high-dimensional atmospheric fields into a 64× smaller latent representation reduces computational cost while preserving enough information for accurate 30-day forecasts.  
**Mechanism:** A pretrained deep compression autoencoder (DC-AE) encodes global weather maps (84 channels, 1.5° grid) into compact latent tensors. The Diffusion Transformer (DiT) operates entirely in this latent space, drastically cutting the number of tokens and memory needed for attention.  
**Core assumption:** The DC-AE latent space retains the essential dynamical modes for subseasonal prediction; reconstruction error does not critically degrade skill at extended horizons.  
**Evidence anchors:**  
- [abstract] "operating within a highly compressed latent space (64× reduction)"  
- [section 3.2] "We employ the pretrained DC-AE from LaDCast."  
- [corpus] Neighbor papers (e.g., LaDCast, OmniCast) also adopt latent diffusion, indicating a broader trend toward latent-space efficiency.  
**Break condition:** If the autoencoder’s reconstruction error is too high—especially for small-scale or extreme features—forecast skill will plateau regardless of DiT capacity. The paper notes this as a primary limitation in the conclusion.

### Mechanism 2: Flow Matching for Fast, High-Quality Sampling
**Claim:** Flow Matching allows deterministic, low-step-count sampling (∼20 steps) while maintaining probabilistic forecast quality comparable to slower diffusion samplers.  
**Mechanism:** Flow Matching learns a continuous velocity field that directly maps noise to data distributions. During inference, an ODE solver integrates this field along a deterministic path, avoiding iterative denoising and needing far fewer network evaluations than standard diffusion.  
**Core assumption:** The learned velocity field is smooth and accurate enough that coarse integration (few steps) does not deviate significantly from the true generative path.  
**Evidence anchors:**  
- [abstract] "latent flow-matching Diffusion Transformer (DiT)"  
- [section 3.2] "Forecast generation is implemented using a Flow Matching (FM) formulation, which allows effective deterministic sampling using a relatively small number of inference steps."  
- [section 3.4] "Sampling is carried out with the flow-matching sampler using approximately 20 integration (denoising) steps per trajectory."  
**Break condition:** With too few integration steps, the ODE solver may accumulate error, producing blurry or unstable forecasts. The flow model may also struggle with multi-modal distributions if the velocity field is not well-trained.

### Mechanism 3: Extended Context and Trainable Positional Embeddings
**Claim:** Replacing geometry-aware RoPE (GeoRoPE) with 1D temporal RoPE plus trainable 2D spatial embeddings, and extending the conditional context window to 24 hours, improves long-range temporal dependency modeling and thus skill at 15–30 day horizons.  
**Mechanism:** The model conditions on a longer history (e.g., four 6-hourly frames) rather than a single snapshot. Trainable spatial embeddings let the model learn positional relationships without hard-coded spherical priors, while 1D RoPE encodes temporal order. Optional timestamp embeddings anchor forecasts to calendar time.  
**Core assumption:** A richer initial context reduces error accumulation in autoregressive rollout; the fixed spatial resolution makes trainable embeddings sufficient substitutes for GeoRoPE.  
**Evidence anchors:**  
- [abstract] "We replace rotary positional encodings (RoPE) with trainable positional embeddings and extend the temporal context window, which together enhance the model's ability to represent and propagate long-range temporal dependencies during latent forecasting."  
- [section 3.2] "We adopt a simplified strategy consisting of learnable 2-D spatial positional embeddings together with 1-D rotary positional embeddings (RoPE) applied along the temporal dimension."  
- [section 4.3] Ablations show that longer context (48h) and the hybrid embedding scheme lower RMSE compared to GeoRoPE or shorter contexts.  
**Break condition:** If the context window is too long, irrelevant historical patterns may dominate, or memory/compute costs may rise. Trainable embeddings might overfit to the training period’s climatology, harming generalization to anomalous regimes.

## Architecture Onboarding
### Component map
DC-AE encoder (frozen) compresses each weather map → DiT backbone (Cross-DiT with trainable 2D spatial embeddings, 1D temporal RoPE, and timestamp cross-attention) predicts latent futures → Flow matching head integrates noise to generate samples → Autoregressive rollout appends predictions to context for multi-step extension → Ensemble generator uses independent noise seeds.

### Critical path
1. Encode current day’s weather maps (K frames) into latent context.  
2. Sample noise for N future frames.  
3. Run DiT with flow matching (∼20 steps) to predict denoised future latents.  
4. Decode latents to weather maps (optional, for output).  
5. Append predicted latents to context and repeat until 30 days.

### Design tradeoffs
- Latent compression vs. detail: Higher compression reduces compute but risks losing small-scale features.  
- Diffusion steps vs. speed: Fewer steps via flow matching speed up inference but may reduce sample quality if the vector field is rough.  
- Context length vs. memory: Longer context improves long-term consistency but increases sequence length and memory usage.  
- Parameter count: Marchuk (276M) is much smaller than LaDCast (1.6B), favoring efficiency but potentially limiting capacity; architectural optimizations (e.g., shared modulation MLP) help.

### Failure signatures
- Forecasts degrading to climatology beyond 15 days, indicating insufficient skill.  
- Low ensemble spread (under-dispersion) relative to LaDCast, suggesting over-conditioning.  
- Visible artifacts in decoded maps (e.g., blurring, loss of fronts) due to autoencoder bottlenecks.  
- Poor extreme-event prediction if the event lies outside the training distribution, though case studies show some skill.

### First 3 experiments
1. Positional encoding ablation: Compare RoPE3D, GeoRoPE, and hybrid (trainable 2D + 1D RoPE) to select the best scheme.  
2. Fixed window training: Train with context lengths of 6h, 12h, 24h, 48h to determine optimal history window.  
3. Temporal skip interval: Evaluate using 1h, 6h, and 12h gaps between latent frames to balance temporal resolution and autoregressive steps.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
1. **How can the reconstruction error of the DC-AE latent space be reduced to improve forecast fidelity without prohibitive computational overhead?**  
   *Basis:* The primary bottleneck is the DC-AE’s reconstruction error, which inherently bounds forecast fidelity.  
   *Why unresolved:* Marchuk relies on a fixed pretrained DC-AE from LaDCast; improving the autoencoder could alter latent dynamics and requires separate optimization.  
   *What evidence would resolve it:* Train DC-AE variants with different compression ratios or advanced loss functions, then integrate with Marchuk and measure changes in RMSE/CRPS compared to baseline.

2. **Can Marchuk’s architecture be adjusted to increase ensemble diversity (spread) while preserving its high probabilistic skill, mitigating the observed spread-skill trade-off?**  
   *Basis:* From Figure 9 and text: lower spread... attributed to stronger conditioning due to longer context window; this suggests a trade-off.  
   *Why unresolved:* The current design prioritizes accuracy via a 1-day context, which constrains ensemble variability; balancing both requires architectural changes.  
   *What evidence would resolve it:* Ablate context length and introduce stochastic elements in latent sampling; evaluate CRPS skill and spread metrics across configurations.

3. **What framework modifications are needed to assimilate live observational data into Marchuk for operational subseasonal forecasting?**  
   *Basis:* Its deployment to real-time forecasting would require integrating data-assimilation or multi-encoder schemes.  
   *Why unresolved:* Marchuk is trained on reanalysis data and lacks a mechanism to handle real-time, potentially noisy, observations; assimilation must update latent states without destabilizing forecasts.  
   *What evidence would resolve it:* Implement a data assimilation module (e.g., EnKF) that ingests observations into the latent space, test on operational datasets with simulated real-time flows.

4. **How can Marchuk’s latent representation be scaled to higher spatial resolutions (e.g., 0.25°) to resolve fine-scale features while maintaining computational efficiency?**  
   *Basis:* The spatial resolution (1.5°) also constrains fine-scale features such as tropical cyclone intensity.  
   *Why unresolved:* Higher resolution increases latent dimensionality, potentially negating efficiency gains; current compression may lose critical details.  
   *What evidence would resolve it:* Develop adaptive or hierarchical latent compression methods, train at target resolutions, and assess metrics for small-scale phenomena (e.g., cyclone track/intensity errors).

## Limitations
- Latent compression (64×) may lose small-scale and extreme features, capping forecast skill at long leads.
- Flow matching with ~20 steps is sensitive to step count and may struggle with multimodal or extreme trajectories.
- Reproducibility gaps: missing DiT hyperparameters and training details (layer counts, batch size, optimizer, flow schedule) hinder exact replication.

## Confidence
- Latent compression efficacy: Medium  
- Flow matching speed/quality trade-off: High  
- Extended context & embeddings benefit: High
