# Can a Lightweight Automated AI Pipeline Solve Research-Level Mathematical Problems?

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2602.13695
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.13695
- **Reference count:** 17
- **Primary result:** The pipeline generated candidate proofs for all problems in the first two ICCM sets and the entire "First Proof" set, achieving 100% verified success on the ICCM sets.

## Executive Summary
This paper examines whether a lightweight, automated AI pipeline employing next-generation large language models can tackle research-grade mathematical problems. The core method integrates domain-specific prompt optimization—shifting reasoning from olympiad heuristics to advanced conceptual frameworks—with a citation-augmented verification mechanism that requires bibliographic references for non-trivial claims. The pipeline successfully produced proofs for all contest-level problems in ICCM Sets 1 & 2 and all unpublished questions in the "First Proof" set, with human experts verifying the former entirely. While promising, the work highlights scalability bottlenecks in verification and accessibility for mathematicians.

## Method Summary
The task is to automatically generate rigorous, human-readable proofs for research-level mathematics using a natural-language pipeline. Inputs consist of problem statements from two datasets: ICCM 2025 Sets 1 & 2 (contest-level, Yau contest comparable) and the "First Proof" set of 10 unpublished research questions. No model training occurs; instead, the pipeline adapts an existing IMO-focused architecture via two lightweight modifications: (1) prompt templates refined to invoke undergraduate/graduate-level structures (e.g., functoriality, residue analysis) and (2) a citation constraint forcing the LLM to reference specific sources (textbooks, arXiv IDs) for key claims. Inference uses Gemini 3 Pro and GPT-5.2 Pro. The primary metric is full proof generation per problem set, with secondary human verification on subsets.

## Key Results
- Generated candidate proofs for all problems in ICCM Sets 1 & 2 and the entire "First Proof" set.
- Human-verified solutions for ICCM Sets 1 & 2 and Problem 4 of "First Proof", claiming 100% success on the ICCM sets.
- All proofs submitted to official organizations; code and UI open-sourced.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1
- **Claim:** Domain-specific prompt optimization shifts LLM reasoning from competition-style heuristics to research-grade conceptual frameworks.
- **Mechanism:** Refactoring prompt templates to explicitly incorporate advanced mathematical structures (e.g., functoriality, residue analysis) rather than IMO-focused tactical patterns.
- **Core assumption:** Next-generation LLMs retain latent knowledge; proper prompting unlocks structured application.
- **Evidence anchors:** Section 2.1 on prompt refinements; Case Study 2 demonstrating correct category theory handling; neighboring papers indicate shift to research benchmarks.
- **Break condition:** Problems requiring entirely novel mathematical objects absent from training corpora.

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim:** Citation-augmented verification reduces hallucination and grounds proofs in traceable literature.
- **Mechanism:** Enforcing a strict constraint: all non-trivial claims must include specific bibliographic references with explicit justification.
- **Core assumption:** Research problem-solving heavily involves applying known theorems; LLMs can associate claims with correct sources when compelled.
- **Evidence anchors:** Section 2.2 validation on Kashiwara exercises with accurate citations; Case Study 2 citing definitions; neighboring corpus lacks this focus.
- **Break condition:** Highly niche, pre-print-only subfields without canonical references, or proofs requiring synthesis of multiple obscure sources.

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim:** A streamlined pipeline architecture proven on IMO problems can be modularly adapted to research-level tasks with targeted enhancements.
- **Mechanism:** Inheriting a robust generation loop from prior work and injecting two modifications (prompt optimization, citation check) rather than rebuilding.
- **Core assumption:** The core reasoning loop scales in difficulty if auxiliary constraints and inputs are upgraded.
- **Evidence anchors:** Abstract noting adoption from [5] with modifications; successful adaptation results; neighboring papers explore pipeline architectures.
- **Break condition:** Heterogeneous problem sets or multi-model collaboration needs breaking the single-LLM-loop assumption.

## Foundational Learning
### Prompt Engineering for Latent Knowledge Activation
- **Why needed:** Coaxing LLMs to apply embedded advanced math knowledge (e.g., category theory, residue calculus) rather than surface-level pattern matching; without precise prompts, models default to simpler reasoning.
- **Quick check:** How would you design a system prompt to distinguish between applying a known theorem (e.g., Yoneda lemma) versus constructing an ad-hoc combinatorial argument for a research problem?

### Citation as a Verification Proxy
- **Why needed:** In informal mathematics, proof credibility partly stems from literature connections; mandating citations forces anchoring, reducing "proof-like" hallucinations.
- **Quick check:** Is a citation to a standard textbook sufficient for verification? What makes a citation "actionable" for a human reviewer assessing an AI-generated proof?

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map:** Input Parser → Prompt Optimizer (domain-specific templates) → LLM Engine (Gemini/GPT with citation constraint) → Output Formatter (structured proof with citations) → Human/AI-assisted Verification Loop
- **Critical path:** Problem ingestion → prompt tuning per domain → generation with enforced citation → iterative refinement if citations are missing/weak → final verification.
- **Design tradeoffs:** 
  - Lightweight natural language vs. formal methods: Chose readability/accessibility over guaranteed correctness, trading verification speed for adoption barrier.
  - Single-LLM loop vs. multi-agent: Avoided complex orchestration to maintain simplicity, assuming next-gen LLMs integrate reasoning and retrieval sufficiently.
- **Failure signatures:** Missing citations for central lemmas; inconsistent notation across proof sections; success on benchmark sets but failure on unpublished research problems without clear literature anchors.
- **First 3 experiments:** 
  1. Replicate citation validation on Kashiwara exercises to verify the mechanism in isolation.
  2. Run full pipeline on ICCM Set 1; check if all 6 problems generate cited proofs matching verified solutions.
  3. Execute on "First Proof" Problem 4; scrutinize the counterexample construction (n=1 case) for logical soundness and proper citation.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
### Open Question 1
How can AI-assisted mathematical verification be scaled to keep pace with increasingly rapid proof generation? The authors identify verification as the primary bottleneck, with human review taking hours per problem while generation takes minutes. Resolution requires developing integrated verification tools that reduce expert time by an order of magnitude.

### Open Question 2
What interface designs and training methodologies lower the technical barrier for mathematicians to use AI math assistants? The paper notes usability gaps but does not study alternative interactions. Resolution needs user studies comparing different paradigms on task success and ease of use.

### Open Question 3
Can model architectures maintain coherent reasoning over extremely long, interconnected contexts required for genuine mathematical research? Current models struggle with fragmentation in deep contexts. Resolution requires benchmarks with >100K token chains and architectures with verifiable memory.

### Open Question 4
How can AI systems be trained to comprehend implicit knowledge, notational shortcuts, and assumed background in mathematical literature? Training on raw arXiv papers did not yield expected gains; reconstructing implicit steps is suggested but untested. Resolution needs augmented datasets with annotated intermediate steps.

## Limitations
- Model dependency: Results rely on next-generation LLMs (Gemini 3 Pro, GPT-5.2 Pro) with uncertain public availability.
- Dataset accessibility: ICCM Sets 1 & 2 are not publicly released, hindering exact replication.
- Verification criteria: The "fully verified" standard lacks explicit specification, creating ambiguity in success assessment.

## Confidence
- Pipeline adaptability (IMO → research): Medium – supported by ICCM results but unproven for deeper, unpublished problems.
- Citation-augmented verification: Medium – effective on textbook exercises but untested in fast-moving, pre-print-heavy fields.
- 100% success on ICCM Sets 1&2: High for those specific sets (authors verified), but Low for broader inference due to unknown difficulty and potential data leakage.
