# PRISM: A Multi-View Multi-Capability Retail Video Dataset for Embodied Vision-Language Models

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2603.29281
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.29281
- **Reference count:** 14
- **Primary result:** Fine-tuning Cosmos-Reason2-2B on PRISM reduces error rate by 66.6% across 20+ capability probes and improves embodied action understanding by 36.4%.

## Executive Summary
PRISM addresses the gap between general-purpose vision-language models and the needs of embodied AI systems deployed in structured physical environments like retail stores. It introduces a large-scale, multi-view video SFT corpus (270K samples, ~11.8M frames, ~730M tokens) captured across five real supermarkets. The data is organized around a three-dimensional knowledge ontology—spatial, temporal/physical, and embodied action—and includes synchronized egocentric, exocentric, and 360° viewpoints. Fine-tuning the 2B-parameter Cosmos-Reason2-2B model on PRISM yields substantial gains across a suite of 20+ specialized capability probes, achieving 86.6% multiple-choice accuracy (+23.8%) and demonstrating that the majority of performance improvement comes from a subset of the data.

## Method Summary
The PRISM dataset is constructed from synchronized multi-view videos (egocentric GoPro at 480p/4fps, exocentric ALIA at 4fps, plus 360°) collected in operational supermarkets. A hybrid annotation pipeline generates task-specific SFT samples: structured metadata extraction (e.g., object counts, spatial statistics), LLM-based QA generation (Gemini Flash), physics-grounded video reasoning (Gemini Robotics ER 1.5), depth-aware spatial analysis (DepthCrafter), and self-supervised transformations. Samples are formatted for Cosmos-Reason2 with interleaved video frames and text, covering open-ended, chain-of-thought (CoT), and multiple-choice question (MCQ) response types. For fine-tuning, BF16 LoRA (rank 32, alpha 64) is applied to all language model linear layers. Training uses AdamW (learning rate 1e-4, cosine schedule, warmup 5%) for one epoch with an effective batch size of 32 on four RTX PRO 6000 Blackwell GPUs. A custom monkey-patch to Qwen3-VL’s `get_rope_index` is required to handle per-frame video token grouping.

## Key Results
- Fine-tuned model achieves **86.6% MCQ accuracy** (baseline 62.8%, +23.8%) and **88.9%** with full training, with **66.6% error reduction** across 20+ probes.
- Largest gains on embodied action tasks: **ER-3 (+62.8%)**, **ER-7 (+55.2%)**, and **SP-2 (+25.0%)**.
- **~60% of the training data captures 95% of the performance gain**, indicating high data efficiency.
- Dataset scale: **270K SFT samples**, **~11.8M video frames**, **~730M tokens**.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1: Unified Knowledge Ontology Structures Training
Samples are explicitly organized around three complementary dimensions—spatial knowledge, temporal/physical knowledge, and embodied action knowledge—forcing the model to integrate geometry, causality, and action-goal relationships within the single domain of retail deployment. The claim rests on the premise that general pre-training fails in deployment because these knowledge types are learned in isolation or lack grounding in a coherent physical context. Evidence includes the paper’s motivation statement about physical AI failing due to insufficient understanding of space, physics, and action, and the operationalization through 20+ capability probes spanning four domains. A break condition: if improvements occur while omitting one ontology dimension (e.g., no temporal/physical tasks), the necessity of the integrated structure would be challenged.

### Mechanism 2: Multi-View Supervision Improves Generalization
Egocentric views provide fine-grained, interaction-centric signals (hand-object manipulations, first-person navigation), while exocentric/360° views deliver stable, scene-centric context (global aisle layout, multi-actor dynamics). Mixing these views during training forces the model to ground local actions in global context and vice versa, addressing the limitation of purely egocentric datasets that cannot capture the full scene geometry required for robust deployment. Evidence includes the claim that exocentric perspectives “simultaneously capture multiple people, global aisle layout,” and the reported gains from “egocentric and exocentric data mixing.” Ablation results show both views benefit (ego +25.6%, exo +21.8%), supporting mutual reinforcement. A break condition: an ablation showing that removing exocentric data does not degrade egocentric-only performance would undermine the claimed synergy.

### Mechanism 3: Hybrid Annotation Balances Cost, Quality, and Diversity
Different annotation strategies are matched to task requirements: physics-grounded video reasoning (Gemini Robotics ER 1.5) for tasks needing actual video processing, depth analysis for spatial statistics, LLM text generation for QA, and self-supervised transforms for data augmentation. This hybrid approach is claimed to be essential because manual annotation is infeasible at this scale and any single method (e.g., text-only LLM) would yield lower-quality or less diverse supervision for physically-grounded tasks. Evidence is found in the detailed pipeline (Section 3.3) showing method–task mapping (Table 3). A break condition: if a simpler, cheaper pipeline (e.g., 100% LLM text-only) produced comparable probe gains, the claimed necessity would be undermined.

## Foundational Learning
### Spatial Layout & Relative Depth Reasoning
**Why needed:** Underlies tasks SP-1, CS-R-1, CS-R-2, CS-R-4, SP-2. The model must interpret depth maps and spatial statistics (mean depth, foreground/background separation) to reason about proximity, layout, and affordances.
**Quick check:** Given a depth map showing a shelf with mean depth 0.2 m and a basket at 0.8 m, does the model correctly identify which objects are within arm's reach?

### Temporal Causality & Arrow-of-Time
**Why needed:** Underlies IP-1 and IP-1 CoT. Understanding physical constraints (gravity, inertia, biomechanics) is required to distinguish natural from reversed motion, foundational for intuitive physics and action anticipation.
**Quick check:** In a video of a person walking, what biomechanical cue (e.g., foot push-off, weight transfer) indicates forward motion versus a reversed clip?

### Goal-Directed Activity Decomposition
**Why needed:** Underlies ER-1, ER-2, ER-3. The model must parse a hierarchical goal (e.g., "Perform Grocery Shopping"), recognize the current sub-goal, predict the next step, and verify completion, bridging high-level intent to atomic actions.
**Quick check:** Given the sub-goal "Select specific spice packets" in a spices aisle, what is the logically next sub-task: "Item Inspection and Comparison" or "Navigate to Checkout"?

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component map:**  
Raw multi-view videos → Hybrid annotation pipeline (Gemini Robotics ER 1.5, DepthCrafter, etc.) → SFT samples in Cosmos-Reason2 format (system/user/assistant messages with interleaved video frames) → BF16 LoRA (rank 32, alpha 64) on Cosmos-Reason2-2B (Qwen3-VL architecture) → Fine-tuned model → Evaluation on 20+ MCQ/CoT probes.  
The input processor uses a monkey-patched Qwen3-VL processor to split `[T,H,W]` video grids into T rows of `[1,H,W]` for RoPE indexing.

**Critical path:**  
1. Preprocess synchronized videos (egocentric 480p, exocentric, 360°) at 4 fps.  
2. Generate task-specific annotations via the hybrid pipeline.  
3. Convert all samples to Cosmos-Reason2 format with response types (open-ended, CoT with `⟨think⟩` tags, MCQ).  
4. Apply LoRA to all LM linear layers, train for 1 epoch with AdamW (lr=1e-4, cosine schedule, warmup 5%) and effective batch 32.  
5. Evaluate on held-out 250-sample MCQ validation sets per task and GPT-4o CoT scoring.

**Design tradeoffs:**  
- **View mixing vs. specialization:** Mixing ego/exo data may cause interference but enables cross-view learning; tradeoff controlled by task allocation.  
- **Annotation cost vs. diversity:** Using specialized models (Gemini Robotics ER 1.5) for video-grounded tasks is expensive but yields higher-quality physics/action chains than text-only LLMs.  
- **LoRA rank/alpha:** Low rank (32) prevents catastrophic forgetting but restricts adaptation capacity; higher rank may overfit to retail nuances.

**Failure signatures:**  
- **Exo task collapse:** Performance on exocentric probes (ER-6/7/8/9, CS-U-2, SP-2) remains near baseline (~70%).  
- **CoT degradation:** GPT-4o scores low despite high MCQ accuracy, indicating answer-guessing without genuine reasoning.  
- **Depth reasoning bottleneck:** SP-1 (Relative Depth) shows smallest gain (+9.2% to 51.2%), indicating persistent difficulty with metric depth discrimination.

**First 3 experiments:**  
1. Baseline replication: Zero-shot evaluation of Cosmos-Reason2-2B on all validation sets to establish the 62.8% overall MCQ baseline.  
2. Full SFT validation: Fine-tune on the complete 270K PRISM mix and verify the expected ~86.6% overall accuracy and specific gains (e.g., ER-3 +62.8%).  
3. View ablation: Train three models: (a) only egocentric tasks, (b) only exocentric tasks, (c) equal random mix; compare per-view performance to confirm mixing does not degrade either view.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
1. **Scaling behavior:** How does performance improvement from PRISM scale with model size (2B vs. 7B vs. 13B parameters)?
2. **Sensor modalities:** Does incorporating metric depth and inertial measurements as inputs significantly improve spatial reasoning beyond video-only?
3. **Cross-domain generalization:** To what extent does retail-specific SFT transfer to other structured environments (e.g., warehouses, hospitals) without fine-tuning?
4. **Metric validity:** Do automated MCQ and GPT-4o scores correlate with human evaluation or real-world task success for embodied agents?
5. **Annotation efficiency:** What is the relative contribution of each annotation method (LLM generation, depth analysis, self-supervised, metadata extraction) to overall performance gains?

## Limitations
- Dataset access details (licensing, download size, file structure) and inclusion of validation sets are unspecified.
- Validation set construction is not fully described; risk of data leakage if included in training.
- No ablations to test necessity of all three ontology dimensions or to compare hybrid pipeline against simpler alternatives.
