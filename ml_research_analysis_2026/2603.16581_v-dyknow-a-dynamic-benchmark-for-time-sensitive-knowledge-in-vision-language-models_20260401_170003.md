# V-DyKnow: A Dynamic Benchmark for Time-Sensitive Knowledge in Vision Language Models

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2603.16581  
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.16581  
- **Reference count:** 30  
- **Primary result:** V-DyKnow reveals VLMs frequently output outdated facts, with visual prompting further degrading reliability; parametric editing methods (<6% success) fail while retrieval-based updates show promise.

## Executive Summary
V-DyKnow introduces a dynamic benchmark for evaluating Vision-Language Models (VLMs) on time-sensitive factual knowledge, addressing the limitation of static benchmarks. The benchmark uses Wikidata validity intervals to construct 139 queries presented as text or images across three lexical variants. Evaluation of nine VLMs shows a high prevalence of outdated responses, often exceeding correct answers. Factual reliability drops significantly from textual to visual prompts, even when entities are correctly recognized. Knowledge editing methods (WISE, GRACE) achieve <6% success, while in-context learning and multimodal RAG achieve higher update rates but rely on external information. Mechanistic analysis reveals that pre-trained knowledge interferes with updates, highlighting fundamental challenges in how VLMs handle dynamic facts across modalities.

## Method Summary
V-DyKnow constructs a set of 139 time-sensitive factual queries from Wikidata, each defined by a (subject, property, attribute) triple with a known validity interval. Queries are instantiated in three lexical templates and presented either as text-only or image-based prompts; images are standardized depictions (e.g., portraits, logos) resized to 672×672 pixels. An auxiliary visual entity recognition task is included to measure entity identification accuracy. The evaluation involves nine VLMs, measuring the percentages of Correct (currently valid), Outdated (historically valid but obsolete), and Irrelevant responses. An Upper-Bound strategy selects the best outcome among the three prompt variants per query. Additionally, the paper tests knowledge editing (WISE, GRACE), in-context learning (IKE), and multimodal RAG, using harmonic mean of efficacy and paraphrase success as the primary metric for updates. Mechanistic analysis probes layer-wise contributions to factual recall.

## Key Results
- Outdated responses often exceed correct answers in VLMs, indicating reliance on static training data.
- Factual reliability degrades from textual to visual prompting, even when the entity is correctly recognized.
- Parametric knowledge editing (WISE, GRACE) achieves <6% success, while IKE and RAG achieve higher update rates but depend on external information.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1
- **Claim:** VLMs frequently output outdated factual knowledge because their parametric memory is derived from static, time-bound training data snapshots.
- **Mechanism:** During pre-training, VLMs encode facts present in their ingested corpus at a specific historical cutoff. Since real-world facts evolve, any attribute whose validity interval extends beyond the model's data cutoff cannot be learned as current; the model instead latently stores the most recent state available in its training window. At inference, without access to a dynamic knowledge source, the model retrieves this stored state, which may be outdated.
- **Core assumption:** The evaluation benchmark's ground truth is static and matches the training data's temporal snapshot. V-DyKnow invalidates this by using Wikidata's live validity intervals.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract] "VLMs are trained on data snapshots... their training data and evaluation benchmarks are typically static, implicitly treating factual knowledge as time-invariant."
  - [section 4.A] "Our approximations are in line with the cutoffs of the models... most responses correspond to facts valid before 2020, suggesting that the underlying training data predominantly reflects earlier states of the world."
  - [corpus] Weak direct evidence; neighboring papers (DyKnow, LLMLagBench) study similar static knowledge issues in LLMs but do not address VLMs specifically. The anchor is therefore primarily from the paper itself.
- **Break condition:** If VLMs were trained on continuously updated, temporally-aware corpora or equipped with built-in mechanisms to query live knowledge bases at inference, the reliance on frozen snapshots would be mitigated.

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim:** Factual reliability degrades when the subject entity is presented visually versus textually, even if the entity is correctly recognized.
- **Mechanism:** Visual queries introduce an extra computational step: visual entity recognition. The VLM must first map an image to a latent entity representation (likely aligning with a textual token) before factual recall pathways can be activated. This mapping is error-prone and can distort the entity signal. Even when recognition is correct, the cross-modal grounding may produce a different internal representation than the explicit textual entity name, accessing a weaker or different subset of the model's factual associations.
- **Core assumption:** The internal representation of an entity is modality-invariant; the concept "Apple" activated by an image is functionally identical to that activated by the token "Apple".
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract] "Factual reliability degrades from textual to visual stimuli, even when entities are correctly recognized."
  - [section 3.A] "Models with a smaller gap between visual and textual factual retrieval exhibit a strong recognition performance... even models with strong recognition capabilities... still produce a large fraction of outdated answers."
  - [section 3.B] Table 3 shows consistently lower prompt agreement for visual inputs, indicating less stable retrieval.
- **Break condition:** If visual entity recognition were perfect and produced identical internal activations as the corresponding textual token, the modality gap would collapse. The paper suggests this is not achieved by current VLMs.

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim:** Parametric knowledge editing methods (WISE, GRACE) fail to reliably update time-sensitive facts in VLMs because the targeted model layers are already saturated with pre-trained associations that interfere with the edit.
- **Mechanism:** Editing algorithms like WISE and GRACE inject new key-value pairs into adapter layers or external memories. However, for facts that are strongly encoded during pre-training, the original pathway remains highly active. The edit either fails to suppress the outdated association (low efficacy) or, when it does alter the output, it causes collateral damage to other facts (low paraphrase success). The mechanistic analysis (Figure 3) shows edits affect different layers, but pre-trained activations for the outdated attribute persist.
- **Core assumption:** A fact can be cleanly "overwritten" in a localized model component without interference from other stored associations.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract] "Factual reliability significantly degrades... knowledge editing methods (WISE, GRACE) show very low success (<6%)."
  - [section 3.C] "Among the knowledge editing methods, only IKE achieves a high harmonic mean, whereas WISE and GRACE perform poorly (both below 6%)."
  - [section 4.C] Figure 3 shows that for unsuccessful edits, pre-trained model layers already assign high probability to the outdated attribute, and editing methods sometimes even increase it (e.g., WISE in the lower panel).
- **Break condition:** If editing methods could identify and precisely modify the minimal circuit responsible for a specific fact without affecting neighboring associations, or if facts were not strongly entrenched during pre-training, success rates would improve.

## Foundational Learning
- **Concept: Temporal Validity of Facts**  
  *Why needed here:* The core premise of V-DyKnow is that facts are not permanent. A learner must understand that a triple like (Apple, CEO, Tim Cook) is only valid within a specific interval. This contrasts with standard QA benchmarks where answers are assumed eternally correct.  
  *Quick check question:* If a model is trained on data from 2023 and is queried in 2025 about a company's CEO, why might its answer be outdated even if the entity is recognized correctly?
- **Concept: Multimodal Grounding Gap**  
  *Why needed here:* The paper's key finding is a performance drop when moving from textual to visual prompts. This requires understanding that "grounding" an entity via an image is a distinct, lossy process compared to reading its name, and this loss impacts downstream factual recall.  
  *Quick check question:* A VLM correctly identifies a country from its coat of arms but gives an outdated answer for its head of state. What are two possible reasons for the outdated answer, given the recognition was correct?
- **Concept: Knowledge Editing vs. Retrieval**  
  *Why needed here:* The paper contrasts parametric editing (WISE, GRACE) with non-parametric retrieval (RAG, IKE). A learner must grasp the fundamental difference: editing attempts to alter the model's weights, while retrieval supplies external evidence at inference time without changing the model.  
  *Quick check question:* Why does a high success rate for IKE not imply that the VLM's internal knowledge has been successfully updated?

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map:** Vision Encoder -> Projection Layer -> LLM Backbone -> Output Head (editing inserts Memory/Adapter at targeted layer)
- **Critical path:** Vision Encoder -> Projection -> LLM layers (factual recall circuit) -> Output
- **Design tradeoffs:**  
  *Static Training vs. Dynamic Knowledge:* Training on a fixed snapshot is feasible but guarantees obsolescence; dynamic training is expensive and risks catastrophic forgetting.  
  *Modality Alignment vs. Factual Fidelity:* Alignment training teaches cross-modal connections but may degrade original textual factual recall.  
  *Parametric Editing vs. Retrieval:* Editing aims for permanent fast update but fails due to interference; RAG provides correctness if retrieval works but is slow and can conflict with internal priors.
- **Failure signatures:**  
  1. High Outdated Rate (>50%): Model returns historically valid but currently incorrect attributes.  
  2. Large Modality Gap: >15% difference in correct rates between textual and visual prompts.  
  3. Editing Harmonic Mean <10%: Low efficacy and paraphrase success for WISE/GRACE.  
  4. RAG Hallucination Gap: Outdated/hallucinated answers even with gold documents.
- **First 3 experiments:**  
  1. **Baseline Outdatedness Profiling:** Measure % Correct, Outdated, Irrelevant for textual and visual prompts; compute Modality Gap.  
  2. **Recognition-Controlled Analysis:** Correlate visual entity recognition accuracy with visual factual correctness.  
  3. **Editing Method Stress Test:** Apply WISE/GRACE to outdated facts; measure harmonic mean and inspect failure cases.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
### Open Question 1
- **Question:** How can Vision-Language Models be trained to inherently account for temporal validity of factual knowledge, moving beyond static data snapshots?
- **Basis in paper:** [explicit] The authors state that current training paradigms rely on static data snapshots and suggest new learning paradigms that explicitly model temporal validity (Conclusion).
- **Why unresolved:** No existing training methods effectively integrate temporal dynamics, resulting in models that quickly become outdated as real-world facts evolve.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Development of training frameworks that incorporate time-stamped data or temporal reasoning objectives, evaluated on benchmarks like V-DyKnow showing reduced outdated responses and better adaptation to current facts.

### Open Question 2
- **Question:** Why do parameter-efficient knowledge editing methods (e.g., WISE, GRACE) exhibit very low success rates (<6%) in updating factual knowledge in VLMs across modalities, and what architectural changes could improve their efficacy?
- **Basis in paper:** [explicit] Table 4 reports harmonic mean scores below 6% for WISE and GRACE, while IKE performs better but depends on external facts.
- **Why unresolved:** Editing methods designed for LLMs fail to transfer to VLMs; mechanistic analysis indicates edits target different layers and pre-trained knowledge interferes, but underlying mechanisms are unclear.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Comparative mechanistic studies of edit propagation in VLMs vs. LLMs, and the design of editing techniques tailored for multimodal architectures, validated by high success rates on V-DyKnow for both visual and textual prompts.

### Open Question 3
- **Question:** What is the primary factor causing the degradation in factual recall when VLMs process visual prompts compared to textual prompts, and can this gap be closed without sacrificing other capabilities?
- **Basis in paper:** [explicit] Results show factual reliability degrades from textual to visual stimuli, even with correct entity recognition, and models with weaker recognition have larger gaps (Tables 1 and 2).
- **Why unresolved:** The root cause—whether visual recognition errors, representational discrepancies, or cross-modal retrieval inefficiencies—remains unidentified due to lack of causal analysis.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Controlled experiments decoupling visual recognition from factual recall, such as feeding recognized entity names as text, or probing internal representations to locate divergence points; new architectures improving vision-language integration could be tested.

### Open Question 4
- **Question:** How does pre-trained parametric knowledge interfere with external evidence in multimodal RAG, and can this interference be mitigated to ensure consistent use of up-to-date information?
- **Basis in paper:** [inferred] RAG achieves high update rates but models still produce outdated or hallucinated answers with gold documents, and mechanistic analysis suggests pre-trained knowledge interferes (Tables 5 and Figure 3).
- **Why unresolved:** The interference mechanism is poorly understood; RAG does not modify parametric knowledge, leading to conflicts between internal priors and external evidence.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Analysis of attention patterns or hidden states during RAG to characterize interference, and development of techniques to dynamically suppress outdated priors or weight external info higher, measured by consistency and correctness on dynamic benchmarks.

## Limitations
- Prompt templates and image preprocessing details are not fully disclosed, risking deviation from intended queries.
- Hyperparameters for WISE/GRACE (layer selection, adapter radius) are unspecified, hindering exact replication of editing failures.
- The mechanism for accessing up-to-date Wikidata at evaluation time is vulnerable to API changes or rate limits.

## Confidence
- Static training causes outdated knowledge: High
- Visual prompts degrade reliability: High
- Parametric editing fails due to interference: High
