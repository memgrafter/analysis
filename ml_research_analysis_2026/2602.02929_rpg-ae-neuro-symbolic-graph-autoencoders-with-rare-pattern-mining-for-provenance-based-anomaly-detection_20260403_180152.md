# RPG-AE: Neuro-Symbolic Graph Autoencoders with Rare Pattern Mining for Provenance-Based Anomaly Detection

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID**: 2602.02929  
- **Source URL**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.02929  
- **Reference count**: 22  
- **Primary result**: RPG-AE (α=2.0) improves nDCG by 23.7% over baseline GAE on DARPA TC Linux dataset and consistently outperforms individual context-based detectors.

## Executive Summary
RPG-AE tackles the detection of stealthy Advanced Persistent Threats (APTs) in system provenance data, where malicious processes are extremely rare and blend with normal behavior. The framework combines a Graph Autoencoder (GAE) that learns normal process relational structure with an Apriori-based rare pattern mining module. Infrequent co-occurrence patterns mined from process features boost the GAE's reconstruction-based anomaly scores. Evaluated on DARPA Transparent Computing datasets using nDCG for ranking quality, RPG-AE with optimal boosting (α=2.0) achieves substantial gains over the baseline GAE (+23.7% nDCG on Linux), outperforms individual context-based detectors, and matches ensemble methods. The neuro-symbolic integration improves both detection effectiveness and interpretability in highly imbalanced security settings.

## Method Summary
RPG-AE processes raw provenance logs into Boolean process feature vectors (e.g., 299 features for Linux). It first constructs a process similarity graph using k-NN (k=5) on standardized feature vectors with cosine similarity. A two-layer Graph Convolutional Network (GCN) encoder with an inner-product decoder is trained unsupervised via link prediction (binary cross-entropy loss, Adam, 200 epochs) to reconstruct the adjacency matrix. After training, each process receives a baseline anomaly score equal to the average reconstruction error of its neighborhood edges; higher scores indicate deviation from normal relational structure.

In parallel, the feature vectors are treated as transactional data and mined for rare itemsets using Apriori with support thresholds between 0.012% and 1.22%. A rare-pattern graph is built connecting processes that share at least one mined pattern. The final anomaly score fuses the baseline score with a degree bonus from the rare-pattern graph: `s_boosted(i) = s_base(i) + α · (d(i)/d_max)`, where `d(i)` is the node degree and α is tuned on validation (optimal α=2.0). Ranking processes by `s_boosted` yields improved detection of stealthy APTs.

## Key Results
- RPG-AE (α=2.0) yields a +23.7% nDCG improvement over the baseline GAE on the DARPA TC Linux dataset.
- It outperforms individual context-based detectors and performs on par with ensemble methods across multiple datasets.
- The neuro-symbolic combination enhances both detection accuracy and interpretability in highly imbalanced attack scenarios.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1
- **Claim**: Anomalous processes are identified by their poor reconstruction within a graph autoencoder trained on normal process behavioral structure.
- **Mechanism**: A process similarity graph is built using k-NN on behavioral feature vectors. A two-layer GCN-based Graph Autoencoder (GAE) is trained to reconstruct this graph's adjacency matrix via link prediction. At inference, each process receives a baseline anomaly score (`s_base(i)`) proportional to the average reconstruction error in its neighborhood—higher error indicates the process's relationships are inconsistent with the learned normal manifold.
- **Core assumption**: Normal processes collectively form a consistent relational structure that can be modeled and reconstructed; attacks manifest as structural deviations.
- **Evidence anchors**:
  - [abstract] "learns normal relational structure using a Graph Autoencoder. Anomaly candidates are identified through deviations between observed and reconstructed graph structure."
  - [section III.E] "The decoder reconstructs the adjacency matrix via inner products between node embeddings... We score each node by its local reconstruction error."
  - [corpus] Weak direct evidence; neighboring papers (e.g., ADA-GAD, Normality Learning-based GAD) discuss reconstruction-based graph anomaly detection principles, supporting the general mechanism but not RPG-AE's specific instantiation.
- **Break condition**: If the k-NN graph construction fails to capture meaningful behavioral neighborhoods (e.g., due to inappropriate `k` or feature scaling), the GAE learns a distorted representation, making reconstruction errors uninformative.

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim**: Infrequent co-occurring behavioral features (rare patterns) are discriminative signals for stealthy APTs, and their presence in a process boosts its anomaly score.
- **Mechanism**: Process feature vectors are converted into transactional data. Apriori algorithm mines itemsets (patterns) within a narrow support range (`min_support=0.012%`, `max_support=1.22%`). A "rare-pattern graph" is built where processes are connected if they share at least one mined pattern. The degree `d(i)` of a process in this graph reflects its participation in uncommon behavioral combinations.
- **Core assumption**: APT behaviors involve rare combinations of otherwise benign features; these combinations are diluted in the overall distribution but can be recovered via constrained support mining.
- **Evidence anchors**:
  - [abstract] "mine rare behavioral co-occurrence patterns... and use them to boost anomaly scores for processes exhibiting rare signatures."
  - [section III.F] "We retain patterns whose empirical support satisfies min_support ≤ supp(p) ≤ max_support... This graph provides an additional relational signal that highlights uncommon behavioral similarities."
  - [corpus] Neighbor paper "Neuro-Symbolic Process Anomaly Detection" and "VR-ARM" [20] (cited) support the premise of using association rules for security analytics, but RPG-AE's specific support thresholds are data-driven.
- **Break condition**: If the support thresholds are set incorrectly (too high/too low), the mined patterns become either non-informative common behaviors or noisy artifacts, degrading the boosting signal.

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim**: Combining GAE reconstruction error with rare-pattern evidence via a weighted sum (`s_boosted(i) = s_base(i) + α·b(i)`) yields superior ranking accuracy than either signal alone.
- **Mechanism**: The final anomaly score is a linear fusion of the baseline GAE score and a normalized degree bonus `b(i) = d(i)/d_max` from the rare-pattern graph. The weight `α` is tuned on validation data to balance the two evidence streams.
- **Core assumption**: Structural deviation (GAE) and symbolic rarity provide complementary, not redundant, signals; their weighted combination prioritizes processes that are both structurally inconsistent *and* behaviorally uncommon.
- **Evidence anchors**:
  - [abstract] "integrate an rare pattern mining module... uses them to boost anomaly scores" and "rare-pattern boosting yields substantial gains... over the baseline GAE."
  - [section III.G] "We define the boosted score as s_boosted(i) = s_base(i) + α·b(i)... Processes that share rare behavioral patterns with many other processes receive a larger bonus."
  - [Table I, Figure 5] Shows nDCG improvement peaks at `α=2.0` (Linux: +23.7%), validating the fusion strategy.
  - [corpus] No direct evidence; fusion of model and rule-based scores is a known strategy, but the specific degree-based boosting is novel to this work.
- **Break condition**: If `α` is substantially mis-tuned (e.g., `α >> 2.0`), the rare-pattern signal overwhelms the GAE signal, potentially elevating processes that share common rare patterns but have normal structure.

## Foundational Learning
- **Concept: Graph Neural Networks (GCNs)**
  - *Why needed here*: The GAE encoder uses a two-layer Graph Convolutional Network to propagate and transform node features across the k-NN similarity graph, generating embeddings that capture local behavioral context.
  - *Quick check question*: How does a Graph Convolutional Layer differ from a standard Dense layer in terms of parameter sharing and inductive bias?
- **Concept: Autoencoder Reconstruction Error**
  - *Why needed here*: The GAE is trained to reconstruct the adjacency matrix; nodes whose observed connections cannot be well-reconstructed from the latent embeddings are considered anomalous.
  - *Quick check question*: Why is reconstruction-based anomaly detection suitable for unsupervised settings, and what is a key limitation in highly imbalanced data?
- **Concept: Apriori Algorithm for Itemset Mining**
  - *Why needed here*: To discover infrequent but potentially malicious co-occurrences of behavioral features (itemsets) from the process-transaction data.
  - *Quick check question*: What is the role of the `support` threshold in Apriori, and why would one use a narrow, high-threshold band for "rare" pattern mining?
- **Concept: Evaluation under Class Imbalance (nDCG)**
  - *Why needed here*: Provenance datasets have extreme attack:benign ratios (e.g., 8:17,569). nDCG measures ranking quality—the operational need is to surface the few attacks early in the list.
  - *Quick check question*: Why might AUC be misleading in this context, and how does nDCG's discounting address the triage requirement?

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map**:
  1. **Data Processor**: Parses raw provenance logs into process-centric Boolean feature vectors (d-dimensional).
  2. **Graph Constructor**: Standardizes features, computes k-NN (k=5, cosine similarity) to build undirected process graph `G_knn`.
  3. **GAE Module**: Two-layer GCN encoder + inner-product decoder. Trained on `G_knn` for link prediction (binary cross-entropy loss, Adam, 200 epochs).
  4. **Pattern Miner**: Converts feature matrix to transactions; runs Apriori with `min_support=0.012%`, `max_support=1.22%` to get pattern set `P`; builds `G_pat` connecting processes sharing a pattern in `P`.
  5. **Scorer**: Computes `s_base(i)` (average reconstruction error for node i's neighborhood). Computes `b(i) = degree_Gpat(i) / max_degree`. Outputs `s_boosted(i) = s_base(i) + α*b(i)` (α tuned on validation).
- **Critical path**: Raw logs → Feature extraction → `G_knn` → GAE training → `s_base` computation → `G_pat` construction → `b(i)` computation → `s_boosted` → ranking. Pattern mining runs in parallel to GAE training.
- **Design tradeoffs**:
  - *Graph construction*: k-NN is simple but `k` choice significantly affects graph connectivity and GAE learning. Larger `k` increases density but may blur local structure.
  - *Rare pattern thresholds*: The narrow support band is heuristic; thresholds are dataset-dependent and may need re-tuning for new environments.
  - *Score fusion*: Linear sum is interpretable and tunable but assumes independence; a more sophisticated fusion (e.g., learned attention) might perform better but adds complexity.
- **Failure signatures**:
  - GAE training loss fails to converge → Graph is too noisy or k-NN parameters are invalid.
  - No rare patterns mined (`P` empty) → Support thresholds too strict for dataset size/feature sparsity.
  - `s_boosted` correlates perfectly with `b(i)` → `α` too high; rare-pattern signal dominates.
  - Performance drop on a new OS → Feature extraction or graph construction may not generalize across system heterogeneity.
- **First 3 experiments**:
  1. **Ablation - Graph Construction**: Vary `k` (e.g., 3, 5, 10) in k-NN. Measure nDCG to identify a stable regime. Expect performance to peak at a moderate `k` (paper uses 5).
  2. **Ablation - GAE Architecture**: Compare GAE with different embedding dimensions (`h2`) and number of GCN layers (1 vs 2). Track training loss and validation nDCG to confirm the chosen architecture is neither underfitting nor overfitting.
  3. **Parameter Sweep - Boosting**: For a fixed `α` (e.g., 2.0), vary `min_support` and `max_support` for Apriori to map the sensitivity of nDCG to the rarity band. Expect a narrow optimum around the paper's values.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
### Open Question 1
- **Question**: How can rare pattern evidence be integrated directly into the GAE training objective, for instance via pattern-aware regularization or heterogeneous graph modeling?
- **Basis in paper**: [explicit] Section V (Conclusion): "In future work, we plan to (i) integrate pattern evidence directly into the training objective through pattern-aware regularization or heterogeneous graph modeling."
- **Why unresolved**: RPG-AE currently uses a separate boosting step after training, which may not fully leverage pattern information during representation learning and could limit overall detection sensitivity.
- **What evidence would resolve it**: Develop a modified GAE loss function that incorporates pattern matching constraints (e.g., encouraging embeddings of processes sharing rare patterns to be similar); evaluate on DARPA TC datasets with nDCG and pattern-based interpretability metrics.

### Open Question 2
- **Question**: Can RPG-AE be adapted to streaming provenance data with incremental updates to rare patterns and GAE parameters without full retraining?
- **Basis in paper**: [explicit] Section V (Conclusion): "(ii) extend the framework to streaming or online settings with incremental pattern updates."
- **Why unresolved**: The current batch-oriented design assumes static graphs and fixed pattern mining, which is impractical for real-time security monitoring where audit logs arrive continuously and distributions may drift.
- **What evidence would resolve it**: Implement an online Apriori variant for incremental rare pattern updates and a streaming GAE update mechanism; test on simulated streaming provenance benchmarks for detection latency, accuracy (nDCG), and resource efficiency.

### Open Question 3
- **Question**: How can richer temporal and causal constraints be incorporated into the graph construction to improve detection of multi-stage APT attacks?
- **Basis in paper**: [explicit] Section V (Conclusion): "(iii) incorporate richer temporal and causal constraints to better capture multi-stage attack behaviors across diverse host environments."
- **Why unresolved**: The k-NN graph construction relies solely on static feature similarity via cosine distance, ignoring temporal ordering and causal dependencies that are critical for identifying multi-hop APT strategies.
- **What evidence would resolve it**: Design a dynamic graph with time-decayed edges or causal edges from provenance traces; use temporal GNNs; validate on DARPA TC multi-stage attack scenarios with stage-level annotations to measure multi-hop detection improvement.

### Open Question 4
- **Question**: How sensitive is RPG-AE's performance to the hyperparameters k (in k-NN graph), min_support, and max_support (in Apriori), and can these be set adaptively across datasets?
- **Basis in paper**: [inferred] Sections III.d and III.f fix k=5 and support thresholds (min_support=0.012%, max_support=1.22%) without sensitivity analysis; these choices may not generalize to all provenance datasets (e.g., varying sparsity or attack prevalence).
- **Why unresolved**: Lack of analysis means optimal values could differ across operating systems or attack types, potentially limiting portability and peak performance in new environments.
- **What evidence would resolve it**: Conduct systematic ablation studies on all DARPA TC datasets (Linux, Windows, BSD, Android); develop adaptive selection heuristics (e.g., based on feature sparsity or anomaly rate) and evaluate robustness via nDCG variance.

## Limitations
- **Parameter ambiguity**: Exact GAE hyperparameters (hidden dimensions, dropout) and absolute rare pattern support counts are unspecified, hindering faithful replication.
- **Validation strategy**: The method for tuning α on validation data (split size, metric stability) is not described, risking potential overfitting.
- **Generalization**: Fixed thresholds (`k`, support band, α) are dataset-specific and may not adapt to new OS environments; rare pattern graph construction could fail under distribution shifts.
