# FairRARI: A Plug and Play Framework for Fairness-Aware PageRank

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2602.08589
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.08589
- **Reference count:** 40
- **Primary result:** A unified framework that computes fairness-aware PageRank via convex constrained optimization, achieving exact group fairness, geometric convergence, and minimal runtime overhead (<10% increase over standard PageRank).

## Executive Summary
FairRARI addresses the critical limitation of existing fairness-aware PageRank methods, which fail to guarantee fairness constraints reliably. The authors reformulate PageRank as the unique minimizer of a strongly convex quadratic objective, enabling fairness criteria (ϕ-sum-fairness, α-min-fairness, or both) to be enforced through convex constraints. The resulting problem is solved by a fixed-point iteration: a standard PageRank update followed by a projection onto the fairness set. The projection step for each fairness type admits a linear-time algorithm, preserving the overall O(m+n) per-iteration complexity of vanilla PageRank. Theoretically, the algorithm converges geometrically to the unique optimal fair PageRank vector. Empirically, across 22 real-world graphs, FairRARI significantly reduces total variation distance (keeping the ranking closer to the original) and improves Kendall-Tau correlation compared to prior methods, while exactly meeting specified fairness targets and incurring only a small runtime overhead.

## Method Summary
FairRARI begins by expressing PageRank as the solution to a strongly convex quadratic minimization problem derived from its variational form. Fairness constraints are added as closed convex sets (e.g., linear equalities for group sums or minima). The constrained problem is solved via an iterative algorithm: in each step, compute a provisional PageRank vector using the standard power iteration, then project this vector onto the fairness set. The projection onto each fairness set is computed efficiently in O(n) time using closed-form formulas that involve solving a scalar equation via bisection. This combination yields a simple, plug-and-play framework that maintains the scalability of PageRank while guaranteeing exact fairness satisfaction.

## Key Results
- Solves for PageRank vectors that exactly satisfy group fairness constraints (ϕ-sum, α-min, or both), unlike prior heuristic or approximate methods.
- The fixed-point iteration converges geometrically to the unique optimal fair PageRank vector.
- On 22 real-world graphs, achieves lower total variation distance to the original PageRank and higher Kendall-Tau ranking correlation than state-of-the-art baselines.
- Runtime overhead is typically less than 10% relative to standard PageRank, whereas prior fairness methods can exceed 5000% overhead.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
FairRARI’s effectiveness stems from three interconnected mechanisms:

**Mechanism 1: Convex reformulation of PageRank.**  
The standard PageRank equation `x = (1-γ)Px + γv` is equivalent to minimizing a strongly convex quadratic objective `f(x)`. This variational interpretation (Proposition 3.1) holds for undirected or time-reversible directed graphs, where `Π⁻¹L_rw` is symmetric positive semi-definite. Adding linear fairness constraints yields a strongly convex constrained problem with a unique solution.

**Mechanism 2: Contraction mapping via projected iterations.**  
The algorithm defines the mapping `T(x) = Proj_X((1-γ)Px + γv)`. Because `Proj_X` is non-expansive and the linear term `(1-γ)Px+γv` is contractive with factor `(1-γ)`, `T` is a contraction (Theorem 3.2). Thus, Banach’s fixed-point theorem guarantees geometric convergence to the unique fixed point, which also satisfies the KKT conditions of the constrained minimization problem (Theorem 3.3).

**Mechanism 3: Linear-time projections via closed-form solutions.**  
For each fairness type (ϕ-sum, α-min, combined), the Euclidean projection `min ||x-y||² s.t. x∈X` reduces to solving a small number of scalar monotonic equations (one per group or a global equation) using bisection. The optimal `x` then follows from simple thresholding formulas (Theorems 4.1–4.3). This avoids general convex QP solvers and keeps per-iteration cost linear in `n`.

Evidence anchors: Proposition 3.1 (variational form), Theorem 3.2 (contraction), Theorem 3.3 (optimality), Theorems 4.1–4.3 (projection algorithms).

## Foundational Learning
- **Variational formulation of iterative algorithms**  
  *Why needed:* Allows recasting PageRank as a convex minimization problem, enabling principled addition of fairness constraints.  
  *Quick check:* For `min ½xᵀQx + cᵀx` with `Q ≻ 0`, the unique minimizer is `x* = -Q⁻¹c`. This relates to solving `(I - (1-γ)P)x = γv` because the latter is the gradient equation of such a quadratic.

- **Projection onto convex sets (POCS)**  
  *Why needed:* Fairness constraints are enforced by projecting the provisional PageRank vector onto the convex set `X`. Understanding that projections are non-expansive and efficiently computable for specific `X` is key.  
  *Quick check:* For `X = {x ≥ 0, eᵀx=1}` (simplex), `Proj_X(y)` is the Euclidean projection onto the simplex (sort and threshold). For `X = {x ≥ 0, aᵀx = b}`, it is a similar constrained thresholding operation.

- **Fixed-point convergence theory**  
  *Why needed:* Guarantees that alternating PageRank updates and projections converge to the fair PageRank vector.  
  *Quick check:* If `T₁` is non-expansive and `T₂` is contractive with constant `c<1`, then `T₁∘T₂` is contractive because `||T₁(T₂(x)) - T₁(T₂(y))|| ≤ ||T₂(x)-T₂(y)|| ≤ c||x-y||`.

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component map:**  
Input handler → PR-step → Fair-projection → Convergence check (loop between PR-step and Fair-projection until convergence).

**Critical path:**  
Per-iteration cost is dominated by: (1) sparse matrix-vector product `(1-γ)Px` (O(m+n)) and (2) the projection step. For ϕ-sum or combined constraints, the projection requires `K` independent bisections (parallelizable), each O(n_k log(1/ϵ)). For α-min, one global bisection O(n log(1/ϵ)). Overall remains O(m+n).

**Design tradeoffs:**  
- *Sequential vs parallel bisection:* Parallel reduces wall-clock time but may increase total work if groups are small; sequential is simpler with O(n) total work.  
- *Bisection tolerance:* Tighter ϵ ensures accurate projection but may slow convergence; typically set ϵ_bisection = 1e-9 or relative to `y`.  
- *γ selection:* Smaller γ slows convergence rate (factor 1-γ); default γ=0.85 balances speed and theory.

**Failure signatures:**  
- *Projection infeasibility:* Bisection fails to find root (e.g., target ϕ_k too large given current `y`). Check feasibility conditions: for ϕ-sum, need ∑ ϕ_k = 1 and max(0, min_{i∈S_k} y_i * n_k) ≤ ϕ_k roughly; if g_k(λ_min) and g_k(λ_max) have same sign, no solution.  
- *Non-convergence:* If ||x⁽ᵗ⁺¹⁾ - x⁽ᵗ⁾|| stalls above ϵ, check γ (too small) or projection tolerance (too loose).  
- *Memory blowup:* Storing full `P` for large graphs; must use sparse representations and matrix-vector products.

**First 3 experiments:**  
1. **Smoke test on tiny graph:** Use a 5-node graph with known ϕ. Verify `Proj_∆ϕ` yields correct group sums and total variation near zero after many iterations when ϕ matches the fair target.  
2. **Compare to post-processing lower bound:** On `PolBooks` dataset, run FairRARI and post-processing (`min ||x-p_o||² s.t. x∈X`). Confirm FairRARI’s TV is slightly higher but Kendall-τ is much higher (as in paper’s Fig.1,2).  
3. **Scaling test:** Measure per-iteration time vs `n, m` on Erdős–Rényi graphs. Plot time/iter vs `n` to confirm O(m+n) scaling. Test parallel bisection benefit on graphs with skewed group sizes.

## Limitations
- **Graph reversibility assumption:** Theoretical guarantees require the graph to be undirected or time-reversible directed. For arbitrary directed graphs (e.g., many web graphs), the variational equivalence may fail, limiting applicability.
- **Numerical exactness:** Constraints are enforced exactly in exact arithmetic; finite precision and stopping tolerances may cause small violations in practice.
- **Runtime overhead variability:** The reported <10% overhead is based on 22 graphs; extreme group size skew or graph density could increase overhead.

## Confidence
- Convergence guarantee: **High** (Theorem 3.2 with proof under stated assumptions)
- Linear-time per iteration: **High** (sparse PR step plus closed-form projections)
- Superior empirical performance vs. prior methods: **Medium** (depends on dataset selection and baseline implementations)
- Exact fairness attainment: **Medium** (theoretically exact; numerically approximate due to tolerances)
- Plug-and-play usability: **Medium** (requires implementing projections, though pseudocode is provided)
