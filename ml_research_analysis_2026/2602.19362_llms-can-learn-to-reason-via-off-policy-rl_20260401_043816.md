# LLMs Can Learn to Reason Via Off-Policy RL

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2602.19362
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.19362
- Reference count: 10
- Primary result: OAPL, an off-policy RL algorithm, outperforms GRPO with importance sampling on competition math benchmarks and matches DeepCoder on LiveCodeBench while using approximately 3x fewer training generations.

## Executive Summary
Reinforcement learning (RL) post-training of large language models (LLMs) often suffers from off-policy mismatches between the training policy and the inference-time policy, leading to instability when using importance sampling. This paper introduces OAPL, a stable off-policy algorithm that directly optimizes a KL-regularized objective using rollouts from a lagged inference policy, avoiding importance weights. OAPL demonstrates superior performance on challenging math reasoning benchmarks (AIME 2025, HMMT 2025, BRUMO 2025) and competitive results on code generation (LiveCodeBench), while being significantly more sample-efficient and robust to large policy lags.

## Method Summary
OAPL minimizes a squared loss derived from the closed-form solution of a KL-regularized RL problem. The algorithm decouples data generation (by an inference policy π_vllm, e.g., served via vLLM) and policy updates (on a trainer policy π). Rollouts and their log‑probabilities are stored in a buffer D. For each update, a batch of prompts is processed: the value estimate V̂(x) is computed from a group of G rollouts per prompt as V̂(x) = β₁·log(mean_i exp(r(x,y_i)/β₁)), and the loss is the mean over rollouts of (β₂·log(π(y_i|x)/π_vllm(y_i|x)) − (r(x,y_i) − V̂(x)))². Every L steps, the trainer’s weights are copied to the inference engine and the buffer is cleared, ensuring the buffer contains data from only one sampling distribution. The method operates fully asynchronously and tolerates large lags (L > 400 steps) without instability.

## Key Results
- OAPL achieves higher Pass@1, Pass@5, and Pass@10 than GRPO with importance sampling on multiple competition math benchmarks (AIME 2025, HMMT 2025, BRUMO 2025).
- On LiveCodeBench, OAPL matches the performance of the publicly available DeepCoder model while using approximately 3x fewer training generations.
- OAPL remains stable with policy lags exceeding 400 gradient steps (over 100× more off-policy than prior approaches) and exhibits improved test-time scaling under Pass@k due to maintained policy entropy.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1: KL-regularized RL closed-form objective replaces importance-weighted policy gradients
- Claim: OAPL avoids instability from importance sampling by optimizing a squared regression loss derived from the closed-form solution to a KL-regularized RL problem.
- Mechanism: The KL-regularized objective (Eq. 1) yields an optimal policy satisfying β ln(π(y|x)/π_vllm(y|x)) = r(x,y) − V⁎(x). Rearranging gives a loss (Eq. 3) minimized by the optimal policy regardless of the sampling distribution of y. The loss uses rollouts from π_vllm and their log-probabilities directly, without reweighting.
- Core assumption: The value estimator V⁎(x) can be accurately estimated from a group of rollouts via Eq. 2.
- Evidence anchors: [abstract] states OAPL directly optimizes a KL-regularized objective avoiding importance weights. [Section 3.1] derives Eq. 3 and explains the estimator. Neighbor work on off-policy RL provides contextual support.
- Break condition: The estimator becomes biased or high-variance if π_vllm has near-zero probability of generating any correct trajectory for a prompt x, or if the group size G is too small.

### Mechanism 2: Infrequent synchronization and direct KL penalty maintain policy entropy
- Claim: OAPL’s design—using π_vllm as the moving KL reference and infrequent weight synchronization (lag L)—prevents entropy collapse and improves test-time scaling (Pass@k).
- Mechanism: The loss explicitly regularizes the trainer π towards the current inference policy π_vllm via the β₂ ln(π/π_vllm) term. Since π_vllm updates only every L steps, π is pulled toward a slowly changing target, preventing premature distribution collapse and preserving diversity, which benefits Pass@k.
- Core assumption: A slowly moving KL reference is more effective at preserving diversity than a fixed reference or one clipped to the immediate previous policy.
- Evidence anchors: [Section 3.2] describes the infrequent synchronization and KL regularization. [Section 6.1] Figure 3 shows OAPL’s sequence entropy remains stable while GRPO’s collapses; Figure 4 shows improved Pass@k scaling.
- Break condition: If L is too large or β₂ is too low, π may drift far from a competent π_vllm, causing divergence or degradation.

### Mechanism 3: Off-policy buffer enables sample efficiency and asynchronous execution
- Claim: OAPL’s use of a replay buffer D storing rollouts from historical π_vllm policies enables significant sample efficiency (3x fewer generations) and fully asynchronous training.
- Mechanism: The algorithm decouples data generation (by π_vllm) and policy updates (on π). Rollouts are stored in D and reused until the next synchronization, amortizing generation cost and removing the need for on-policy collection. This allows π_vllm to be arbitrarily stale relative to π.
- Core assumption: The off-policy data in D remains sufficiently near the current π due to KL regularization to the generating π_vllm, even with large lags.
- Evidence anchors: [abstract] notes OAPL allows efficient post-training with lags >400 steps and matches DeepCoder with 3x fewer generations. [Section 3.2] describes asynchronous operation and buffer clearing. [Sections 5.2 & 6.2] detail the two-stage code training with high off-policyness and sample efficiency.
- Break condition: Buffer D contains data from a π_vllm that is too incompetent or misaligned, or the KL penalty is insufficient to constrain π from exploiting out-of-distribution data non‑generatively.

## Foundational Learning
- **KL-regularized Reinforcement Learning**  
  Why needed: Provides the theoretical foundation for OAPL’s loss function; the closed-form relationship between the optimal policy and a reference policy under a KL constraint enables a squared error objective valid for off-policy data.  
  Quick check: Why does the KL-regularized optimal policy have a multiplicative form π* ∝ π_ref · exp(r/β) instead of an additive advantage term?

- **Off-policy policy evaluation and importance sampling**  
  Why needed: To understand the problem OAPL solves—standard on-policy methods break down when the data generation policy differs from the training policy, requiring corrections like importance sampling that introduce variance.  
  Quick check: What is the fundamental problem with using the token-level importance ratio π_old/π_vllm when these policies’ log-probabilities differ due to implementation differences (e.g., vLLM vs. HuggingFace)?

- **Asynchronous, distributed training pipelines and policy lag**  
  Why needed: The practical source of off-policyness that OAPL embraces; the algorithm’s synchronization hyperparameter L directly controls tolerated lag.  
  Quick check: In a distributed RL setup with separate trainer and inference engines, what two main factors cause the trainer’s π and inference engine’s π_vllm to produce different log-probabilities for the same sequence, even with identical weights?

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map**: π_vllm (Inference Engine) generates rollouts → Data Buffer (D) stores (x, {y_i}, log π_vllm(y_i|x), r) → Policy Trainer (π) samples from D and computes OAPL loss using Advantage Estimator and Loss Calculator. Synchronization Scheduler periodically copies π weights to π_vllm and clears D.

- **Critical path**: Asynchronous data generation by π_vllm must keep up with asynchronous gradient steps on π using D. The slower loop determines throughput. L must be set so D contains only data from the current π_vllm era (cleared at sync) but enough data to form accurate V̂(x) estimates (requires sufficient G per prompt).

- **Design tradeoffs**:
  - Lag (L) vs. Stability: Larger L increases off-policyness and sample efficiency but risks divergence if β₂ is too small or rewards sparse.
  - Group size (G) vs. Compute: Larger G reduces variance of V̂(x) but costs more forward passes and memory.
  - β parameters: Two β’s (β₁ for V̂ smoothing, β₂ for KL penalty strength) offer flexibility but require tuning.

- **Failure signatures**:
  1. Entropy collapse: Sequence entropy drops rapidly; indicates β₂ too low or L too large without sufficient KL regularization.
  2. Diverging loss/NaN: Squared loss explodes; likely due to extreme log-ratio values (π far from π_vllm) or unstable V̂(x) (G too small, reward range too large for β₁).
  3. Pass@k degradation: Pass@1 improves but Pass@k for k>1 worsens; indicates distribution sharpening; check entropy curve.
  4. Buffer staleness: If monitoring shows π_vllm lags π by more than L steps in async setup, buffer may contain mixed-era data, violating single-π_vllm assumption.

- **First 3 experiments**:
  1. Replicate math benchmark training (Qwen3‑4B‑Thinking, Deepscaler, L=50, G=8, β₁=1, β₂=1e‑3) for ~1500 steps; plot Pass@1/5/10 and entropy vs steps vs GRPO baseline. Expect OAPL higher accuracy and stable/increasing entropy.
  2. Stress test lag: Repeat experiment 1 with L=100 (or L≈400 for code task). Expect OAPL stable, while GRPO with importance sampling likely fails or requires aggressive clipping.
  3. Verify Pass@k scaling: Evaluate best checkpoint from experiment 1 for k ∈ [1,2,4,8,16,32,64,128,256]. Expect OAPL’s Pass@k curve above GRPO and base model, showing improved scaling.

## Limitations
- Dataset preprocessing details (Deepscaler, DeepCoder) are not fully specified.
- Assumes exact log‑probability consistency between vLLM and HuggingFace implementations; mismatches could bias the loss.
- Asynchronous buffer management and distributed training specifics are omitted, which may affect reproducibility in practice.

## Confidence
- Stable off-policy learning (Mechanism 1): Medium (relies on exact log‑prob match between vLLM and trainer).
- Entropy preservation (Mechanism 2): Medium (empirically stable but robustness under sparse rewards uncertain).
- Sample efficiency (Mechanism 3): High (3× fewer generations directly observed).
- Benchmark results: Medium (strong but depend on proprietary datasets and unreported evaluation specifics).
