# CDEoH: Category-Driven Automatic Algorithm Design With Large Language Models

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv:** 2603.19284
- **Source:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.19284
- **Citations:** 10
- **Result:** CDEoH reduces relative optimality gaps on online bin packing and traveling salesman problems, e.g., 0.483 vs 1.071 (10kC100 OBP) and 17.134 vs 21.448 (TSP500).

## Executive Summary
CDEoH introduces a category-driven approach to automatic algorithm design with large language models (LLMs), explicitly modeling and managing algorithm categories to avoid premature convergence. The method employs category induction to label generated algorithms, a two-stage selection that balances performance with category diversity, and a reflection mechanism to repair faulty implementations. Evaluated on online bin packing and traveling salesman problems across multiple scales, CDEoH consistently outperforms baselines such as EoH and FunSearch in terms of relative gap to optimal solutions. Ablation studies confirm the significance of both category diversity and reflection, particularly under constrained settings.

## Method Summary
CDEoH evolves a population of candidate algorithms using the DeepSeek-Chat LLM. The population size is fixed at 10. Each generation, for every parent, two offspring are produced via separate improvement and innovation prompts. Each new algorithm is evaluated on the target problem; if it crashes, a reflection process (re-prompting with the error) attempts repair up to a budget \(B\) (exact value unspecified). Successful offspring receive a category label from the LLM, and a dynamic category pool records all discovered categories. Selection proceeds in two stages: (i) retain the top-1 algorithm per category (category-wise elitism); (ii) fill remaining slots (\(N - k\)) based on a joint score \(S = \text{normalized\_fitness} + \lambda / |C|\), where \(\lambda = 0.7\) and \(|C|\) is the count of algorithms in that category. Evolution terminates after a total evaluation budget of 200 algorithms.

## Key Results
- On online bin packing with 10,000 items and bin capacity 100 (10kC100), CDEoH achieves a relative gap of 0.483 compared to EoH's 1.071.
- On traveling salesman with 500 cities (size500), CDEoH reduces the average relative gap to LKH-3 solutions to 17.134 versus EoH's 21.448.
- Ablation experiments show that removing category diversity (`nocategory`) or reflection (`noreflection`) harms performance, especially on constrained instances like 1kC500 and 5kC500.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Explicit Category Modeling
- **Claim:** Explicit category modeling prevents premature convergence by maintaining parallel exploration across distinct algorithmic paradigms.
- **Mechanism:** Each new algorithm is assigned a discrete category label by an LLM based on its thought and code. Categories are tracked in a dynamic pool, and selection preserves at least the top-1 algorithm from each category (category-wise elitism).
- **Core assumption:** Algorithmic categories are meaningfully distinct, have different performance ceilings, and maintaining representatives improves evolutionary stability.
- **Evidence anchors:** Abstract and Section 4.3 define category \(c_i = C(\text{thought}_i, \text{code}_i)\) and category pool \(K(t)\); Section 5.3 tables show outperformance over non-diversity-aware EoH; corpus notes contrast with HSEvo’s clustering.
- **Break condition:** Inconsistent LLM category induction or overly fine/coarse definitions render diversity management ineffective.

### Two-Stage Selection Balancing Performance and Diversity
- **Claim:** A two-stage selection strategy jointly optimizes performance and category diversity, balancing exploitation and exploration.
- **Mechanism:** Stage 1 selects the best algorithm from each category; Stage 2 ranks the remaining candidates by \(S_i = \text{normalized\_fitness} + \lambda / |C(i)|\), penalizing overrepresented categories.
- **Core assumption:** A controllable trade-off (\(\lambda\)) between raw performance and category rarity suffices to maintain diversity without complex niching.
- **Evidence anchors:** Section 4.4 gives the scoring formula and two-stage process; Section 5.3 and Tables 1–2 show consistent gains over EoH (which lacks category diversity); Section 4.4 notes \(\lambda=0\) degenerates to performance-only.
- **Break condition:** Poor \(\lambda\) tuning (too high selects low-performing rare categories; too low leads to convergence like EoH) or unstable category counts \( |C(i)| \) due to noisy induction.

### LLM-Based Reflection for Error Repair
- **Claim:** An LLM-based reflection mechanism improves search efficiency by repairing otherwise promising algorithms that fail due to implementation hallucinations, reducing evaluation waste.
- **Mechanism:** When evaluation fails with error \(e\), a reflection prompt feeds the algorithm's thought, code, and error to the LLM to generate a repaired candidate \(h'\). This repeats up to a budget \(B\).
- **Core assumption:** Many failures are superficial bugs correctable by the LLM given sufficient context.
- **Evidence anchors:** Abstract mentions reflection; Section 4.5 formalizes the process; Section 5.4 and Table 3 (ablation `noreflection`) show significant drops on constrained settings (1kC500, 5kC500), confirming its role.
- **Break condition:** If errors stem from deep conceptual flaws rather than surface bugs, reflection is unlikely to succeed and wastes budget; LLM's diagnostic ability is key.

**Evolutionary Population Management**  
Why needed: CDEoH’s core innovation lies in altering the standard selection operator to incorporate category diversity. Understanding basic evolutionary selection (e.g., tournament, truncation) is prerequisite to grasping the novelty of the two-stage, category-aware selection.

**Algorithmic Paradigm Taxonomy**  
Why needed: The entire mechanism assumes that heuristic algorithms can be categorized (e.g., greedy, constructive, local search). The engineer must understand what makes a "category" distinct—high-level strategy versus implementation details.

**LLM Prompting for Analysis & Generation**

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component map:**  
Population Pool (P) stores (thought, code, category, score); Category Pool (K) tracks discovered categories; LLM Interface performs generation, category induction, and reflection; Evaluator executes code and returns score or error; Selector implements two-stage selection. Main flow: P → LLM Interface (generate offspring) → Offspring → Evaluator → on success → Category Induction (LLM) → updates K and Candidate Pool; on error → LLM Interface (reflect) retry; Selector consumes Candidate Pool and K to produce next P.

**Critical path:**  
1. Initialize P with N algorithms via LLM.  
2. For each generation: for each parent in P, generate two offspring (improvement and innovation prompts).  
3. For each offspring: evaluate; if error, invoke reflection (up to B times) until success or discard.  
4. For each successful offspring: invoke category induction; if category new, add to K.  
5. Add offspring to candidate pool; run two-stage selector: (i) keep top-1 per category; (ii) fill remaining slots using score \(S = \text{normalized\_fitness} + \lambda / |C|\) with \(\lambda=0.7\).  
6. Repeat until total evaluation budget of 200 is reached.

**Design tradeoffs:**  
- **Category granularity:** Coarse categories group dissimilar strategies; fine categories increase sparsity. LLM prompt design determines granularity.  
- **Reflection budget (B):** Higher B increases repair chances but raises computational cost.  
- **Diversity strength (λ):** Fixed at 0.7; could be tuned or decayed over time to shift from exploration to exploitation.

**Failure signatures:**  
- **Category pool stagnation:** |K| grows slowly after initial generations, indicating overly conservative induction or lack of paradigm exploration.  
- **Reflection exhaustion:** Many individuals are discarded after max reflection attempts, suggesting poor generation quality or ineffective repair prompts.  
- **Selection skew:** A small number of categories dominate the top-k picks, meaning category-wise elitism fails to preserve diversity.

**First 3 experiments:**  
1. Baseline comparison: Run CDEoH against EoH, FunSearch, ReEvo on standard OBP/TSP benchmarks.  
2. Ablation study: Execute `nocategory` and `noreflection` variants to isolate contributions.  
3. Category dynamics: Track the number of categories |K| and their performance distribution over generations.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
1. **Refining category modeling:** How can algorithmic category modeling be refined beyond flat, LLM-induced labels to capture hierarchical or more nuanced relationships between heuristic paradigms? (Section 6, future work)  
2. **Problem characteristics for diversity benefit:** Under what specific problem characteristics (e.g., scale, constraint tightness, solution space structure) does explicit category diversity provide the greatest benefit versus potential hindrance? (Inferred from ablation study variations)  
3. **Reflection mechanism inconsistency:** To what extent does the observed inconsistency in reflection’s contribution stem from the nature of LLM-induced errors versus search dynamics at different problem scales? (Inferred from ablation)  
4. **Decoupling category induction:** Can category induction be decoupled from LLM semantic interpretation to improve stability and reduce hallucination risks? Would alternative methods (e.g., code embeddings, execution behavior) yield more robust diversity? (Section 6 explicit)

## Limitations
- Exact prompts (category induction, reflection) and reflection budget \(B\) are unspecified; FunSearch baseline details are missing, impeding faithful reproduction.
- Findings may not generalize beyond DeepSeek-Chat and the particular problem distributions; category definitions are LLM-dependent and potentially unstable.
- High computational cost due to repeated LLM calls; no comparative evaluation against non-LLM evolutionary algorithms is provided.
