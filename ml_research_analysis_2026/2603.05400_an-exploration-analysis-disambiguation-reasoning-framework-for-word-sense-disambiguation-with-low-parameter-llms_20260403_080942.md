# An Exploration-Analysis-Disambiguation Reasoning Framework for Word Sense Disambiguation with Low-Parameter LLMs

## Quick Facts
- **ArXiv ID:** 2603.05400  
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.05400  
- **Reference count:** 0  
- **Primary result:** Low-parameter LLMs (<4B) achieve GPT-4-Turbo-comparable Word Sense Disambiguation performance via reasoning-centric fine-tuning using the Exploration-Analysis-Disambiguation framework.

## Executive Summary
This paper presents the Exploration-Analysis-Disambiguation (EAD) reasoning framework to enhance Word Sense Disambiguation (WSD) in small language models (<4B parameters). By integrating Chain-of-Thought (CoT) reasoning with neighbour-word analysis—where context tokens are ranked by semantic similarity to the ambiguous word—the approach enables efficient fine-tuning on the FEWS dataset. Fine-tuned Gemma-3-4B and Qwen-3-4B models achieve state-of-the-art F1 scores (75.68 few-shot, 71.86 zero-shot) on FEWS, surpassing larger baselines. An advanced reasoning variant, which trains models to justify correct senses and eliminate incorrect ones, uses only 10% of the training data yet yields comparable results. The framework demonstrates robust generalization to challenging benchmarks like 42D (78.48 F1) and the adversarial "Fool Me If You Can" set without task-specific adaptation.

## Method Summary
The method centers on three innovations: (1) **Neighbour-word analysis** extracts top-k context tokens via cosine similarity to the target word, incorporating them into prompts to focus the model on salient cues. (2) **CoT fine-tuning** trains models to generate structured reasoning steps (contextual analysis, justification, elimination) rather than direct sense prediction. (3) **Advanced reasoning** uses a large LLM (Virtuoso-Large) to generate contrastive rationales, teaching models to differentiate senses by rejecting incorrect options. All variants employ LoRA (Low-Rank Adaptation) for parameter-efficient fine-tuning, freezing base model weights and updating only adapter layers. Inference runs zero-shot with the same prompt structure, and sense IDs are extracted from generated text.

## Key Results
- Gemma-3-4B and Qwen-3-4B achieve F1 scores of 75.68 (few-shot) and 71.86 (zero-shot) on FEWS, outperforming all medium-parameter baselines.
- The advanced reasoning variant uses only 10% of the FEWS training data but matches the performance of full-data CoT training.
- Models generalize strongly, reaching 78.48 F1 on the hard 42D benchmark and state-of-the-art results on the adversarial "Fool Me If You Can" dataset without task-specific fine-tuning.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1: Neighbour-Word Analysis Directs Attention to Salient Context
- **Claim:** The EAD framework’s neighbour-word analysis, combined with CoT fine-tuning, directs the model’s attention to the most contextually salient tokens, improving disambiguation for rare and domain-specific senses.
- **Mechanism:** A semantic similarity window (cosine similarity between target word and context token embeddings) ranks neighbouring words. The top-k tokens are explicitly included in the training prompt, forcing the model to reason over these cues during fine-tuning. This creates a structured, interpretable reasoning pattern the small model can learn with limited parameters.
- **Core assumption:** The most semantically similar neighbours to an ambiguous word within a fixed window are strong indicators of its intended sense in context.
- **Evidence anchors:**  
  - [abstract] "Chain-of-Thought (CoT)-based reasoning combined with neighbour-word analysis achieves performance comparable to GPT-4-Turbo."  
  - [section 3.2.2] Details the "context-extraction module using a windowed semantic similarity approach" and example where 'match', 'player', 'bag' point to 'sports equipment' sense of 'bat'.  
  - [corpus] Indirect support from neighbouring papers that use contextual cues (e.g., translation context, gloss context) but not the specific neighbour-ranking heuristic.
- **Break condition:** If the similarity heuristic selects misleading neighbours (e.g., a polysemous neighbour with a dominant sense unrelated to the target), the model may learn spurious correlations. Performance degrades on sentences where the most similar neighbours are themselves ambiguous or out-of-domain.

### Mechanism 2: Advanced Reasoning Builds Contrastive Sense Understanding
- **Claim:** Advanced reasoning training, which requires the model to generate justifications for the correct sense and systematically eliminate incorrect ones, builds a robust, contrastive understanding of sense distinctions that generalizes to unseen and adversarial examples.
- **Mechanism:** Using a large LLM (Virtuoso-Large) with a structured prompt, the training data is augmented with rationales containing three components: (i) contextual analysis, (ii) justification of correct sense, (iii) elimination of incorrect senses. Fine-tuning on this data teaches the model to perform internal contrastive reasoning.
- **Core assumption:** Explicitly modeling why incorrect senses are wrong is as important as justifying the correct one for learning fine-grained sense boundaries.
- **Evidence anchors:**  
  - [abstract] "reasoning why a particular sense is correct and why others are not."  
  - [section 3.2.3] Describes the three "critical elements" and the prompt used. Notes that this approach "used only 10% of the training data compared to the CoT reasoning approach, yet produced comparable results."  
  - [section 4.1] The advanced reasoning approach achieves state-of-the-art on the adversarial "Fool Me If You Can" Set 4 without task-specific fine-tuning.
- **Break condition:** If the elimination logic in the training data is noisy or if incorrect senses share too many contextual features with the correct sense, the model may struggle to learn clear boundaries. The paper notes verb disambiguation remained poor even with added syntactic evidence, suggesting some sense pairs resist this contrastive formulation.

### Mechanism 3: LoRA Enables Efficient Acquisition of Reasoning Patterns
- **Claim:** Parameter-Efficient Fine-Tuning (PEFT) with LoRA adapters enables low-parameter models (<4B) to acquire complex reasoning patterns without catastrophic forgetting or overfitting, by making targeted updates to a frozen pre-trained model.
- **Mechanism:** The pre-trained model’s weights are frozen, and low-rank adaptation matrices are trained on the reasoning-augmented dataset. This allows the model to specialize for WSD while retaining its broad linguistic knowledge, with minimal computational overhead.
- **Core assumption:** The reasoning patterns required for WSD can be encoded via low-rank updates to the attention layers of a general-purpose pre-trained LLM.
- **Evidence anchors:**  
  - [section 3.3] "Fine-tuning was conducted using... Low-Rank Adaptation (LoRA) to enable efficient training while reducing computational overhead." Hyperparameters: batch size=4, lr=2e-4, 2 epochs.  
  - [results] Models trained with LoRA (e.g., Gemma-3-4B, Qwen-3-4B) outperform larger, full fine-tuned models (e.g., Yi-34B, Mixtral 7B) in Tables 3 and 5.  
  - [corpus] No direct corpus support; neighbouring papers focus on model architectures or datasets, not parameter efficiency methods.
- **Break condition:** If the required reasoning is highly complex and divergent from the pre-training distribution, low-rank updates may be insufficient. The paper notes LLaMA 1B/3B and DeepSeek 1.5B failed to learn reasoning, suggesting a minimum parameter capacity or architectural suitability gap.

## Foundational Learning
- **Concept: Semantic Similarity Heuristics**  
  Why needed: The neighbour-word analysis relies on selecting context tokens via embedding similarity. Understanding cosine similarity and its limitations (e.g., failure for functional words, sensitivity to embedding quality) is key to interpreting which context is being emphasized.  
  Quick check: If the top-k neighbours include a homonym of the target word, how might that mislead the model’s reasoning, and could the sentence-level context compensate?

- **Concept: Chain-of-Thought (CoT) Fine-Tuning**  
  Why needed: The model is explicitly trained to generate intermediate reasoning steps (e.g., "Contextual Analysis... Justification... Elimination"). This scaffolds the small model’s inference process, moving beyond direct label prediction.  
  Quick check: Why does zero-shot CoT (after fine-tuning) outperform a few-shot approach with retrieved examples in Table 4? What does this imply about the role of learned reasoning patterns versus in-context examples?

- **Concept: Contrastive Learning via Rationales**  
  Why needed: The advanced reasoning variant teaches the model to differentiate senses by actively rejecting incorrect options. This is a form of implicit contrastive learning, strengthening the representation of the correct sense relative to others.  
  Quick check: If two incorrect senses are eliminated using the same reasoning flaw (e.g., "doesn’t fit the topic"), what risk does this pose for learning fine-grained sense distinctions?

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map:** Input Formatter -> Rationale Generator (Offline) -> LoRA Fine-Tuner -> Inference Engine -> Post-processor
- **Critical path:**  
  1. Pre-process FEWS training data → compute neighbour rankings for each instance.  
  2. Generate augmented rationale dataset using Virtuoso-Large with human-in-the-loop QC.  
  3. Apply LoRA fine-tuning for 1-2 epochs on the chosen variant (Direct, Neighbour-CoT, Advanced).  
  4. Evaluate on FEWS test sets (few-shot/zero-shot) and external benchmarks (42D, hardEN, Fool Me If You Can).  
  *Note:* Advanced reasoning variant uses only 10% of training data but requires the more complex rationale generation step.
- **Design tradeoffs:**  
  - Neighbour window size (k): Larger k adds noise; smaller k may miss key context. Paper uses k=5 as default.  
  - CoT verbosity: Gemma-3-4B produced overly verbose outputs, hurting BERTScore when evaluated against concise references. Need output formatting constraints.  
  - Data quantity vs. quality: Advanced reasoning uses 10% data but requires high-quality, structured rationales. CoT-neighbour uses 100% data but with simpler reasoning prompts.  
  - Verb disambiguation: Adding syntactic evidence to prompts did not help (Table 6), suggesting verb senses may require different modeling (e.g., dependency parsing features beyond textual prompting).
- **Failure signatures:**  
  - Model outputs text that does not follow the CoT structure (e.g., just predicts the sense ID directly). Observed in LLaMA 1B/3B and DeepSeek 1.5B.  
  - Verb F1 scores remain low (<0.68) even with advanced reasoning + syntactic cues, indicating a fundamental limitation.  
  - Performance on "Fool Me If You Can" Set 4 drops significantly for models without strong reasoning (paper shows their model excels, but a weak model would fail).  
  - If neighbour extraction includes stopwords or non-alphabetic tokens (implementation bug), performance degrades.
- **First 3 experiments:**  
  1. **Baseline Evaluation:** Run inference on FEWS subset with base (unfine-tuned) models to establish starting BERTScore/F1. Expect low performance (<0.6 overall).  
  2. **Direct Sense Identification SFT:** Fine-tune for 1 epoch on direct sense ID prediction (no CoT, no neighbours). Evaluate. Expect moderate gains but limited generalization.  
  3. **Neighbour-CoT SFT:** Implement the context-extraction module (spaCy tokenization, sentence-transformer embeddings, top-k selection). Fine-tune with CoT prompts including neighbour words. Evaluate on FEWS zero-shot. Expect significant jump, especially for Gemma-3-4B and Qwen-3-4B.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- **Open Question 1:** How can the proposed EAD reasoning framework be adapted for multilingual word sense disambiguation, particularly for low-resource languages?  
  *Basis in paper:* [explicit] The Future Work section states: "Future work should extend this approach to multilingual settings, including low-resource languages."  
  *Why unresolved:* The study exclusively evaluates the framework on English FEWS data; no multilingual experiments were performed, so cross-lingual generalizability remains unknown.  
  *What evidence would resolve it:* Apply the EAD framework to multilingual WSD benchmarks (e.g., SemCor multi-lingual variants or low-resource language datasets), report performance metrics across languages, and analyze transfer effectiveness.

- **Open Question 2:** Why do certain low-parameter LLMs (e.g., LLaMA-1B, LLaMA-3B, Deepseek-distill-Qwen-1.5B) fail to acquire neighbour-word analysis reasoning during fine-tuning, while others like Gemma-3-4B succeed?  
  *Basis in paper:* [inferred] Section 3.2.2 notes: "Llama 1B, 3B, and Deepseek-distill-Qwen 1.5B did not learn reasoning effectively and behaved primarily as text predictors."  
  *Why unresolved:* The paper does not investigate root causes; potential factors include model capacity, architectural differences, or pre-training biases, but no analysis is provided.  
  *What evidence would resolve it:* Conduct ablation studies varying model size, architecture, and pre-training data; perform error analysis on failure cases to identify patterns in reasoning breakdowns.

- **Open Question 3:** What specific factors make verb sense disambiguation resistant to enhancements like syntactic evidence integration in the EAD framework, as shown by limited improvements in Table 6?  
  *Basis in paper:* [inferred] Results indicate "verb disambiguation remains challenging... adding extra reasoning signals still fails due to being interpreted as noise," and Table 6 shows minimal gains from hybrid approaches.  
  *Why unresolved:* The paper attributes failure to noise but does not dissect verb-specific complexities (e.g., higher polysemy, contextual dependencies) or evaluate syntactic feature efficacy.  
  *What evidence would resolve it:* Perform fine-grained error analysis on verb instances, compare sense inventories with nouns/adjectives, and test alternative syntactic encoding methods (e.g., dependency parse features) to isolate bottlenecks.

- **Open Question 4:** How does the advanced reasoning variant achieve comparable performance to the CoT approach using only 10% of the training data? Is this due to rationale quality, model priors, or data diversity?  
  *Basis in paper:* [inferred] Section 4.1 states: "the advanced reasoning approach used only 10% of the training data compared to the CoT method, yet produced comparable results."  
  *Why unresolved:* The paper does not explain the efficiency; possible reasons include higher informativeness of structured rationales or better generalization from fewer examples, but no comparative analysis exists.  
  *What evidence would resolve it:* Measure rationale quality (e.g., via LLM-as-a-judge scores) and correlate with performance; run controlled experiments varying data size, rationale formats, and pre-training to disentangle contributions.

## Limitations
- The neighbour-word analysis relies on a semantic similarity heuristic that may fail if top-k neighbours are polysemous or domain-mismatched, leading to spurious correlations.
- Verb sense disambiguation shows limited improvement even with added syntactic cues, suggesting the framework may not generalize to all lexical categories.
- The study has limited external validation (0 references, moderate neighbor novelty), so robustness beyond curated benchmarks remains uncertain.

## Confidence
- **Low-parameter LLMs match GPT-4-Turbo on WSD:** Medium  
  *Evidence:* Strong results on FEWS/42D/Fool-Me but lack broad independent replication.
- **Neighbour-word analysis mechanism:** Medium  
  *Evidence:* Supported by ablation in section 3.2.2, but break conditions (misleading neighbours) not fully stress-tested.
- **Advanced reasoning (contrastive elimination):** Medium-High  
  *Evidence:* Clear results on adversarial set (section 4.1), but rationale quality is a potential hidden dependency.
- **PEFT/LoRA sufficiency for WSD:** Medium  
  *Evidence:* LoRA works for 4B+ models but fails for <3B params (table 5), suggesting a capacity threshold not fully explained.

## Next Checks

2. **Cross-dataset generalization:** Apply the fine-tuned Gemma-3-4B (EAD variant) to an out-of-domain WSD dataset (e.g., WiC) without adaptation to probe robustness beyond provided benchmarks.
