# In-Context Molecular Property Prediction with LLMs: A Blinding Study on Memorization and Knowledge Conflicts
## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2603.25857  
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.25857  
- **Reference count:** 40  
- **Primary result:** No evidence of direct memorization; prior knowledge often conflicts with in‑context examples, causing performance to peak at intermediate blinding levels.

## Executive Summary
This paper evaluates large language models (LLMs) on molecular property prediction using a novel six‑level blinding framework that progressively removes chemical context and transforms target values. Testing nine models (GPT‑4.1, GPT‑5, Gemini 2.5) across three MoleculeNet datasets (Delaney, Lipophilicity, QM7) with 0, 60, and 1000‑shot prompts, the authors find no sign of direct memorization, as error distributions lack zero‑spikes. Instead, prior knowledge frequently hinders in‑context learning, leading to degraded 60‑shot performance relative to 0‑shot; increasing examples to 1000 usually mitigates this. Dataset‑specific patterns emerge: Lipophilicity scores drop sharply under blinding, Delaney remains robust, and QM7 shows no degradation. The results highlight the risk of benchmark contamination and the need for controlled evaluation to disentangle memorization from genuine learning.

## Method Summary
The study employs a six‑level blinding framework (Levels 1–6) that gradually eliminates property name specificity, applies a linear transformation to target values (negation and rescaling to [0, 100]), and substitutes SMILES characters with deterministic arbitrary symbols while preserving syntax. Three molecular regression datasets (Delaney solubility, Lipophilicity, QM7 atomization energy) are split into fixed training and test sets. For each model (GPT‑4.1 nano/mini/full, GPT‑5 nano/mini/full, Gemini 2.5 flash‑lite/flash/pro) and shot count (0, 60, 1000), prompts are constructed: a system prompt, an analysis prompt containing 60 training examples (with transformations applied as needed), and for 1000‑shot an additional list of 940 simplified samples. The LLM is queried to produce numeric predictions, which are inverse‑transformed when necessary and evaluated using Pearson correlation and mean absolute error. Two independent runs per configuration are performed to assess variability.

## Key Results
- Error distributions show no spike at zero, indicating **no direct memorization** of exact property values.
- **Prior knowledge often hinders in‑context learning**: 60‑shot underperforms 0‑shot in about half of the model‑dataset combinations; this interference is largely mitigated by 1000‑shot (except for Gemini Flash/Lite).
- **Dataset‑specific blinding effects**: Lipophilicity correlation plummets from 72% to 35%, Delaney remains stable (96% → 92%), and QM7 shows negligible change under blinding.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1: Differential capability deactivation
- **Claim:** The six‑level blinding framework isolates the contribution of four distinct LLM capabilities (direct memorization, learned structure‑property relationships, chemical ICL, general ICL) by selectively removing information.
- **Mechanism:** Each blinding level disables a specific capability (e.g., label transformation breaks memorization of exact values; SMILES substitution prevents chemical recognition). Comparing performance across levels reveals which capability the model relies on for a given task.
- **Core assumption:** LLM knowledge is layered and can be deactivated via controlled information removal.
- **Evidence anchors:** Abstract, Section 3.2 (four‑capability taxonomy), and related work on ICL.
- **Break condition:** If performance never degrades, blinding fails to disrupt relevant priors or the model relies solely on general ICL.

### Mechanism 2: Prior knowledge interference in few‑shot ICL
- **Claim:** Strong pre‑trained priors compete with in‑context examples, causing 60‑shot to sometimes perform worse than 0‑shot; more examples (1000‑shot) or blinding reduces this interference.
- **Mechanism:** The model combines prior beliefs and in‑context evidence in a weighted manner. With few examples, priors dominate; if they conflict with the provided data, accuracy drops. Many examples strengthen the in‑context signal, overriding priors. Blinding weakens priors, lessening competition.
- **Core assumption:** LLM inference integrates both sources and conflict arises from inconsistency.
- **Evidence anchors:** Abstract, Section 4.1 (60‑shot worse in ~50% cases), Section 5.3 (detailed discussion).
- **Break condition:** If 60‑shot always improves over 0‑shot, no negative interference exists; if 1000‑shot fails to recover, the model may be unable to override priors or suffers from context overload.

### Mechanism 3: Transformations preserve relational structure
- **Claim:** Value inversion/rescaling destroys memorization of exact numbers but keeps order and differences; SMILES character substitution eliminates direct lookup of known molecules while preserving syntactic patterns needed for ICL.
- **Mechanism:** Memorization relies on exact string/value matching, whereas genuine learning extracts relational patterns invariant under these transformations.
- **Core assumption:** Relational patterns are more robust to declarative changes than exact memorization.
- **Evidence anchors:** Section 3.3 (transformation description), Section 4.2 (error distribution analysis), and analogous methods in memorization quantification literature.
- **Break condition:** If transformed values yield random performance even with many examples, the transformation may have destroyed all learnable signal (e.g., model treats numbers non‑linearly).

## Foundational Learning
- **In‑Context Learning (ICL)** – The ability of LLMs to adapt predictions based on examples provided in the prompt without parameter updates. *Why needed:* The entire study hinges on measuring ICL for molecular tasks. *Quick check:* *What is in‑context learning and how does it differ from fine‑tuning in LLMs?*
- **Memorization vs. Generalization** – Distinguishing whether high scores arise from recalling training data versus extracting underlying patterns. *Why needed:* Central to interpreting the blinding results; memorization would invalidate claims of learning. *Quick check:* *How can you detect if an LLM is memorizing test data versus genuinely learning a pattern?*
- **Benchmark Contamination** – The risk that evaluation datasets appear in the LLM’s training corpus, artificially inflating performance. *Why needed:* Molecular benchmarks like MoleculeNet are widely used and thus prone to leakage; blinding aims to mitigate this threat. *Quick check:* *Why are widely used molecular datasets vulnerable to data contamination, and what risks does it pose for evaluation?*

## Architecture Onboarding
### Component map
Datasets (ESOL, Lipophilicity, QM7) -> Fixed train/test splits -> Blinding selection (Levels 1-6) -> Transformations (label: invert/rescale; SMILES: char substitution) -> Prompt construction (System prompt -> Analysis prompt with 60 examples -> [optional sample list for 1000‑shot]) -> LLM API call (GPT / Gemini) -> Output parsing (numeric extraction) -> Metric computation (Pearson, MAE, exact-match) -> Performance aggregation.

### Critical path
1. Choose dataset and its fixed split.
2. Set blinding level (1–6) and shot count (0, 60, 1000).
3. Sample 60 (and 940 for 1000‑shot) training examples using a fixed seed.
4. Apply required label and/or SMILES transformations to both training and test data.
5. Assemble prompts: system prompt, analysis prompt (with the 60 transformed examples), and for 1000‑shot an additional plain list of the other 940 transformed samples.
6. Call the corresponding LLM API with model‑specific parameters.
7. Parse the numeric prediction from the response.
8. Repeat for all test samples.
9. Inverse‑transform predicted labels when needed.
10. Compute Pearson correlation (primary) and MAE; aggregate over two runs.

### Design tradeoffs
- **SMILES‑only input:** Uses text‑based representation, discarding 3D geometry, possibly under‑estimating LLM capabilities.
- **Limited runs (2 per config):** Reduces API cost but yields low statistical power; more runs would improve confidence.
- **Simple linear transformation:** May be insufficient to defeat all memorization; non‑linear transforms could be explored.
- **Fixed prompt template with 8‑step reasoning:** Ensures consistency but might not be optimal across diverse models.
- **1000‑shot context length:** Approaches model limits and may cause “lost‑in‑the‑middle” degradation; splitting context helps but does not eliminate the risk.

### Failure signatures
- No performance difference across blinding levels → blinding ineffective or substitution reversible.
- Complete collapse at Level 6 → model cannot perform any pattern recognition; possibly SMILES substitution destroyed signal.
- High variance across runs → instability due to temperature; should set temperature = 0.
- Error distribution spike at zero → memorization still present; transformation failed or contamination persists.
- 1000‑shot worse than 0‑shot → severe interference or context overload; model cannot integrate many examples.

### First 3 experiments
1. **0‑shot baselines** across all datasets and models: quantify prior knowledge/contamination without any examples.
2. **Level 1 (full information) with 1000‑shot:** establish upper bound performance with all chemical context.
3. **Level 6 (fully blinded) with 1000‑shot:** test pure general ICL without chemical priors; compare to 0‑shot to assess residual learning.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- **Extent of reliance on prior vs. ICL:** The blinding comparisons show relative changes but do not quantify absolute contributions of each capability (memorization, learned relationships, chemical ICL, general ICL). Why do some datasets (Lipophilicity) degrade sharply while others (Delaney) remain robust? A quantitative decomposition is missing.
- **Conditions for prior knowledge interference:** The paper observes 60‑shot underperformance and mitigation by 1000‑shot, but does not explain why interference occurs in some property‑model combos (e.g., Lipophilicity) and not others, nor the model‑specific differences (Gemini vs. OpenAI). Error analysis and synthetic contradictory datasets are needed.
- **Sufficiency of blinding protocol:** The six‑level framework is proposed as a sanity check, but it is unclear which levels are critical or whether a simpler protocol could reliably detect contamination. A systematic evaluation across many properties and known contaminated datasets is required to establish thresholds.
- **LLM decoding of SMILES substitution:** It is assumed that deterministic character substitution fully removes chemical knowledge. However, consistent mapping might allow models to infer the substitution from context, preserving implicit reasoning. This threat to the blinding assumption is not tested.

## Limitations
- SMILES substitution map and exact transformation parameters are not documented in the paper; they must be extracted from the code, creating a replication hurdle.
- Only two independent runs per configuration are reported, limiting statistical confidence and obscuring variance.
- The GPT‑5 models used may not be publicly accessible via standard APIs, hindering full reproducibility.
- 1000‑shot prompts push context‑length limits and may suffer from “lost‑in‑the‑middle” effects that could bias results.
- Evaluation is restricted to three SMILES‑based datasets; conclusions may not generalize to other molecular representations or tasks.

## Confidence
- **No direct memorization:** High (consistent zero‑spike absence across all models/datasets).
- **Prior knowledge hinders ICL:** Medium (observed in ~50% of cases; needs broader validation).
- **Dataset‑specific blinding effects:** High (empirical differences robustly reproduced).
- **Blinding reveals knowledge conflicts/contamination:** Medium (inference relies on transformation assumptions).
- **Effectiveness of SMILES substitution:** Medium (untested possibility of reverse engineering).

## Next Checks
1. **Multi‑seed replication:** For each model‑dataset‑blinding‑shot combination, run ≥10 random seeds and compute confidence intervals to assess stability of observed effects (e.g., 60‑shot vs. 0‑shot, Lipophilicity drop).
2. **Non‑linear transformation ablation:** Replace linear label rescaling with non‑linear functions (e.g., logarithmic, sigmoid) that preserve order; verify that relational learning persists while memorization remains undetected.
3. **Leakage audit and SMILES map verification:** Re‑compute scaling statistics using training splits only to ensure no test leakage. Explicitly extract the substitution map from the code and confirm it is bijective and that transformed SMILES do not accidentally retain recognizable chemical patterns (e.g., via domain expert review or chemical validity checks).
