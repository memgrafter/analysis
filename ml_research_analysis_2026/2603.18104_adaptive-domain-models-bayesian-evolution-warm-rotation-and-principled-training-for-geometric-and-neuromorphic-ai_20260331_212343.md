# Adaptive Domain Models: Bayesian Evolution, Warm Rotation, and Principled Training for Geometric and Neuromorphic AI

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2603.18104
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.18104
- Reference count: 26
- Primary result: Training memory depth-independently bounded to ~2× inference; grade preservation with 85–95% Cayley sparsity (3D PGA) and exact equivariance; Bayesian distillation and warm rotation for continuous, verified model updates.

## Executive Summary
The paper presents Adaptive Domain Models (ADM), a framework that unifies a Dimensional Type System (DTS), Program Hypergraph (PHG), and b‑posit arithmetic to overcome limitations of conventional IEEE‑754 training in geometric and neuromorphic AI. Forward‑mode automatic differentiation with exact quire accumulation bounds training memory to roughly twice the inference footprint, independent of network depth. PHG ensures grade preservation in Clifford algebra computations as a compile‑time invariant, yielding stable Cayley table sparsity (85–95% for 3D PGA) and exact equivariance. Bayesian distillation extracts latent priors from general models and, combined with warm rotation, enables verified, no‑downtime model updates. The approach thus provides a principled, memory‑efficient, and safe pathway for evolving domain‑specific AI systems.

## Method Summary
ADM’s core innovation is the composition of three interrelated technologies. The Dimensional Type System encodes physical dimensions (e.g., mass, velocity) as types, enforcing consistency across the computation. The Program Hypergraph statically infers geometric grades and verifies structural constraints (e.g., zero Cayley entries) via saturation semantics, making grade preservation a theorem. Arithmetic is performed with b‑posit numbers—a bounded‑regime posit format that uses a single decoder across precisions—and gradients are computed via forward‑mode automatic differentiation; exact inner products are accumulated in a quire, eliminating the need to store intermediate activations. A Composer compiler translates Clef source code (annotated with DTS/PHG) into target IR, discharging SMT‑LIB2 proofs. Training runs on spare inference hardware with O(1) auxiliary memory per layer. For model evolution, Bayesian distillation queries a general model over the target domain to obtain an unstructured prior, which is then filtered through DTS/PHG constraints to produce a certified domain prior. Warm rotation atomically swaps the active model once a PHG certificate and version signature are ready, triggered by KL divergence between predictions and live evidence, ensuring continuous operation.

## Key Results
- Training memory depth-independently bounded to ~2× inference footprint.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1: Memory Bounding via Forward‑Mode AD and Quire
- **Claim**: Training memory overhead relative to inference can be bounded to approximately 2×, independent of depth.
- **Mechanism**: Forward‑mode AD computes an unbiased gradient estimate in a single forward pass with dual numbers, requiring only O(1) auxiliary memory per layer. The quire accumulator performs exact inner‑product accumulation (rounding once after full sum), eliminating the activation tape (O(L) memory) and rounding‑error compensation that inflates memory in IEEE‑754.
- **Core assumption**: The forward‑mode gradient estimate has sufficient signal‑to‑noise ratio for convergence, and the quire’s exactness holds for the numerical range encountered.
- **Evidence anchors**: Abstract statement on depth‑independent memory; Section 2.1 on O(1) auxiliary memory; weak corpus support.

### Mechanism 2: Grade Preservation as a Type‑Level Invariant
- **Claim**: Grade preservation in Clifford algebra networks becomes a compile‑time guarantee, ensuring stable Cayley sparsity and exact rotor equivariance.
- **Mechanism**: PHG infers grade annotations at design time, determining which Cayley entries are structurally zero. Forward‑mode AD carries grade‑typed tangent components; the type system enforces that a grade‑k weight’s gradient has grade‑k, making any corruption a type error caught by the language server.
- **Core assumption**: PHG grade inference correctly captures the geometric algebra’s structure and the type system soundly enforces grade constraints.
- **Evidence anchors**: Abstract on grade‑preserving weight updates; Section 5.1 on PHG grade inference applying to tangent; absent external corpus.

### Mechanism 3: Bayesian Distillation and Warm Rotation for Continuous Evolution
- **Claim**: Domain models can be bootstrapped from general‑purpose models via Bayesian distillation and updated continuously via warm rotation without service interruption.
- **Mechanism**: Bayesian distillation queries a general model over the target domain to extract an unstructured prior, filtered through DTS dimensional annotations and PHG grade constraints to produce a formally certified domain prior. Warm rotation atomically swaps the active model after training, conditioned on PHG elaboration and version signing; the trigger is KL divergence between model predictions and operational evidence.
- **Core assumption**: General models contain latent Bayesian structure accessible via distillation, and PHG certification guarantees structural correctness before rotation.
- **Evidence anchors**: Abstract on Bayesian distillation and warm rotation; Section 3.4 on distillation mechanism; Section 4 on warm rotation conditions; weak corpus support for distillation pattern.

## Foundational Learning

**Dimensional Type System (DTS)**
- **Why needed**: Encodes physical dimensions as types, providing a structural prior and guaranteeing dimensional consistency. This eliminates hypotheses that violate physical laws.
- **Quick check**: Multiplying pressure (Pa = kg·m⁻¹·s⁻²) by volume (m³) yields energy (J = Pa·m³ = kg·m²·s⁻²).

**Program Hypergraph (PHG)**
- **Why needed**: Represents multi‑way geometric constraints and ensures grade preservation in Clifford algebras. It determines Cayley table sparsity at compile time and verifies grade consistency via saturation semantics.
- **Quick check**: In 3D projective geometric algebra (PGA), the geometric product of two vectors produces grades 0 (scalar) and 2 (bivector); grades 1 and 3 are structurally zero.

**b‑posit Arithmetic and Quire Accumulation**
- **Why needed**: b‑posit provides bounded‑regime posit arithmetic that is hardware‑efficient (single decoder across precisions) and suitable for training on inference‑class hardware. The quire accumulator enables exact inner‑product accumulation, crucial for precise gradient estimates.
- **Quick check**: Why can b‑posit use a single decoder for 16‑, 32‑, and 64‑bit variants? Because the bounded regime fixes the regime field length, allowing one multiplexer to select among precision options.

## Architecture Onboarding

**Component map**:  
Clef source (with DTS/PHG) → Composer (with Lattice diagnostics) → Target IR (MLIR‑AIE/NxCore) → Runtime (b‑posit/Quire) → Inference/Training → Evidence collection → KL monitoring → Warm rotation subsystem (with Versioning signatures) → Atomic model swap

**Critical path**:
1. Write Clef source with DTS dimensional annotations and PHG grade constraints.
2. Composer elaborates program, checks consistency, compiles to target IR.
3. Training: forward‑mode AD (dual numbers) with quire runs on spare capacity; inference uses primal pass.
4. Operational evidence collected; KL divergence computed against current model’s predictive distribution.
5. If KL > threshold: spare compute trains candidate model.
6. Composer re‑elaborates candidate, verifies PHG, issues structural certificate.
7. Warm rotation: atomic swap after buffering in‑flight requests; version record signed.
8. New model active; cycle repeats.

**Design tradeoffs**:
- **Memory vs compute**: Forward‑mode AD reduces memory (O(1) per layer) but may require more forward passes for full gradient; reverse‑mode is faster but memory‑heavy.
- **Precision vs range**: b‑posit’s bounded regime optimizes precision near unity but limits dynamic range; suitable for normalized activations but may clip extremes.
- **Safety vs flexibility**: PHG/DTS constraints prevent many errors but restrict architectures to those conforming to domain dimensions and grades.
- **Verification latency**: Warm rotation requires PHG certification; delay may be unacceptable for ultra‑fast update cycles.
- **Distillation fidelity**: General‑model priors may misalign with target domain; DTS/PHG filtering may discard useful but structurally inconsistent knowledge.

**Failure signatures**:
- **Design‑time**: Lattice reports grade violations, dimensional inconsistencies, or PHG saturation failures.
- **Compile‑time**: Composer fails to discharge SMT‑LIB2 proofs, indicating unsatisfied structural invariants.
- **Runtime**: Quire overflow (unlikely if exact), KL divergence never crossing threshold (under‑adaptation), warm rotation non‑atomic (dropped requests), invalid version signature.
- **Operational**: Outputs violating dimensional constraints (ideally caught at compile time).

**First 3 experiments**:
1. **Grade preservation test**: Train a 2D PGA network for 100 steps using forward‑mode AD and b‑posit; inspect weights to confirm all grade‑k components remain pure grade‑k.
2. **Hybrid network consistency**: Build a network with a Clifford layer (grade‑1 output) and a spiking layer (rate encoding). Verify DTS propagates dimensions correctly across the conversion hyperedge and PHG verifies the interface.
3. **Warm rotation drill**: Simulate an operational scenario: run inference with model M0, trigger training for M1, verify PHG certification, then perform warm rotation. Confirm no requests are lost and the version record is properly signed with a certificate diff.

## Limitations
- Forward‑mode AD scalability may require many forward passes for full gradients, potentially offsetting memory savings.
- PHG grade inference soundness depends on correctly capturing all geometric constraints; flawed inference could allow grade contamination or reject valid architectures.
- Bayesian distillation fidelity is uncertain; general‑model priors may misalign with the target domain, and DTS/PHG filtering might discard useful but structurally inconsistent knowledge.

## Confidence
