# Surrogate models for Rock-Fluid Interaction: A Grid-Size-Invariant Approach

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID**: 2602.22188
- **Source URL**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.22188
- **Reference count**: 40
- **Primary result**: Fully convolutional grid-size-invariant surrogates trained on 64×64 patches predict full 256×22287 fields with high accuracy (PCC >0.75, up to 0.93 for CO₂), reduce GPU memory 10x, and offer several orders of magnitude speedup over PDE solvers.

## Executive Summary
This paper addresses the computational cost of high-fidelity rock-fluid interaction simulations for multi-query tasks by developing efficient surrogate models. It proposes and evaluates two main frameworks: reduced-order models (ROMs) using autoencoders for compression, and a novel grid-size-invariant approach using single fully convolutional networks trained on small spatial patches but able to infer on larger domains. Key innovations include using UNet++ architectures and autoregressive rollout training to improve long-term accuracy. The grid-size-invariant method, particularly with UNet++ and rollout, achieves strong predictive performance while drastically reducing memory usage during training, enabling application to larger and potentially 3D datasets.

## Method Summary
The study uses a carbon storage dataset (32 simulations, 100 timesteps each, 256×256 grids, 4 fields) from GeoChemFOAM. Two model families are compared: (1) ROMs where an autoencoder (AE) or adversarial autoencoder (AAE) compresses full fields to a latent space, followed by a UNet/UNet++ predictor trained autoregressively; (2) Grid-size-invariant models where a single fully convolutional UNet/UNet++ is trained on random 64×64 patches but can process full 256×256 images at inference. Training uses Adam with specific learning rates, and rollout training (T=8) is implemented by unrolling predictions inside the training loop to minimize cumulative error. A boundary loss penalty (`λ_BC`) is scheduled during training. Models are evaluated on validation simulations using Pearson Correlation Coefficient (PCC), Structural Similarity (SSIM), and a CO₂ plume area error metric over 100 autoregressive timesteps.

## Key Results
- Grid-size-invariant training on 64×64 patches reduces GPU memory to 0.26–0.39 GB versus 1.8–3.4 GB for whole-domain training.
- UNet++ consistently outperforms UNet in preserving fine details and long-term accuracy.
- Rollout training (T=8) improves multi-step autoregressive performance.
- Best grid-size-invariant UNet with rollout achieves PCC >0.75 for all fields over 100 steps, CO₂ concentration PCC reaches 0.93, and SSIM up to 0.95.
- Surrogate inference takes seconds, providing several orders of magnitude speedup over the PDE solver.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- **Claim**: Training a fully convolutional network on small spatial patches (e.g., 64×64) enables accurate inference on larger, unseen domains (e.g., 256×256) while drastically reducing GPU memory during training.
- **Mechanism**: Fully convolutional networks, composed exclusively of convolutional layers, have local connectivity and translation equivariance. The output size depends only on input dimensions and layer strides, not on a fixed dense layer structure. Training on patches reduces per-batch memory because fewer activations are stored per sample, while the same weights are used during full-domain inference.
- **Core assumption**: Local spatial patterns and feature distributions within the small training patches are statistically representative of those in the larger domains. The PDE physics (advection-diffusion-reaction) is homogeneous enough that filters learned on patches capture necessary dynamics.
- **Evidence anchors**: 
  - [abstract]: "train[ed] on small 64×64 patches but predict[ed] full 256×256 fields, drastically reducing GPU memory (0.26–0.39 GB vs 1.8–3.4 GB for whole-domain training)"
  - [section 2.2]: "a fully convolutional neural network... can be applied to images of any size... the training may be performed on smaller representative domains"
- **Break condition**: If large-domain dynamics contain non-local phenomena or boundary effects not present in the patch distribution (e.g., system-scale vortices, dominant large-scale boundary layers), the locally trained filters will fail to capture these, leading to degraded predictions at the domain scale.

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim**: UNet++ architecture with nested skip connections provides superior predictive accuracy over standard UNet for this autoregressive rock-fluid interaction task.
- **Mechanism**: UNet++ introduces dense, hierarchical skip connections between encoder and decoder blocks at multiple semantic levels. This allows the decoder to receive features that are more closely aligned in abstraction level, facilitating a cleaner fusion of fine-grained spatial details from the encoder with high-level contextual information. This reduces the information bottleneck and improves gradient flow, leading to better preservation of sharp interfaces (e.g., CO₂ plume fronts) and subtle porosity changes.
- **Core assumption**: The fidelity of small-scale features (like dissolution channels and concentration gradients) is critical for long-term physical consistency and that these features are poorly preserved by the single skip connection in UNet, causing error accumulation in autoregressive rollout.
- **Evidence anchors**:
  - [abstract]: "UNet++ consistently outperformed UNet... grid-size-invariant UNet with rollout achieved Pearson correlation coefficients above 0.75 for all fields over 100 timesteps, with CO2 concentration PCC reaching 0.93"
  - [section 2.1.3]: Describes UNet++ architecture with "extra connections between encoder and decoder layers" to "capture fine-grained details".
  - [section 3.4]: "UNet++ resulted in better predictions than UNet... UNet++ outperforms UNet in the grid-size-invariant framework."
- **Break condition**: If the computational budget for training or inference is severely constrained, the ~15-20% increase in parameters (9M vs 7.7M) and memory usage may outweigh the accuracy gains, especially for shorter prediction horizons where UNet's performance is already acceptable.

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim**: Rollout training (unrolling predictions for T steps within the training loop) improves the surrogate's multi-step autoregressive accuracy compared to single-step training.
- **Mechanism**: Standard training minimizes the one-step loss, but errors compound when the model is used iteratively. Rollout training minimizes the cumulative error over T predicted steps, exposing the network to its own predicted states during training. This teaches the network to correct for its inherent biases and stabilize the temporal evolution, effectively regularizing the latent dynamics or the direct prediction to be more consistent with the training data's temporal manifold.
- **Core assumption**: The system's true temporal evolution lies on a lower-dimensional, coherent manifold in state space. Single-step training does not penalize deviations that accumulate over multiple steps, while rollout training does.
- **Evidence anchors**:
  - [abstract]: "rollout training (T=8) improved long-term autoregressive accuracy"
  - [section 2.2.1]: Explicitly defines rollout training as iterating for T timesteps inside the training loop and measuring loss "altogether for the T timesteps."
  - [section 3.4]: "models with rollout training slightly outperformed the ones without it" and "UNet with rollout slightly outperformed other models" in SSIM and MSE metrics.
- **Break condition**: If T is set too large relative to the model's intrinsic stability, training can become unstable, gradients may vanish or explode, and the model may fail to converge or overfit to the specific trajectories in the training set. The benefit diminishes if the one-step error is already very low and the system is not chaotic.

## Foundational Learning
- **Concept: Fully Convolutional Networks (FCNs) and Size Invariance**
  - *Why needed here*: The entire grid-size-invariant framework relies on FCNs. Understanding that removing dense layers allows the spatial dimensions of the output to scale with the input is fundamental to designing the training/inference scheme.
  - *Quick check question*: What layer property (convolution vs. dense) determines that a network can process inputs of different spatial sizes without retraining?
- **Concept: Encoder-Decoder and Skip Connections for Image-to-Image Prediction**
  - *Why needed here*: Both UNet and UNet++ are encoder-decoder architectures with skip connections. The encoder compresses spatial information into a latent representation, and the decoder reconstructs it. Skip connections fuse multi-scale features to preserve spatial details lost during downsampling, which is critical for predicting sharp physical interfaces.
  - *Quick check question*: In the context of predicting a future fluid field from a previous one, what is the purpose of the skip connections that bypass the encoder's bottleneck?
- **Concept: Autoregressive Rollout and Error Accumulation**
  - *Why needed here*: The surrogate is used to simulate forward in time by feeding its own predictions back as input. Understanding the difference between one-step and multi-step training, and why the latter combats compounding errors, is key to interpreting the results and applying the model.
  - *Quick check question*: If a model is trained to minimize the mean squared error between its prediction at timestep *t+1* and the ground truth at *t+1*, what specific type of error is it not directly penalized for during training?

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map**:  
  Input Processor (stacks last 3 timesteps of 4 fields) -> Encoder (conv blocks + max-pooling) -> Bottleneck (conv at lowest resolution) -> Decoder (upsampling + skip connections) -> Output Layer (1×1 conv to 4 channels).  
  *For UNet++*: Decoder is replaced with a nested structure where each decoder block receives inputs from multiple prior encoder blocks via dense connections.
- **Critical path**:  
  `[12-channel Input] → Encoder (downsample) → Bottleneck → Decoder (upsample + skip fuse) → [4-channel Output]`  
  *Rollout during training*: `Input_{t-2,t-1,t} → Output_{t+1} → (shift window) → Input_{t-1,t,t+1} → Output_{t+2} → ... repeat T times → Compute cumulative loss over T outputs.`
- **Design tradeoffs**:
  - **UNet vs UNet++**: UNet++ offers higher accuracy (especially for fine details) at the cost of ~15% more parameters and higher GPU memory during training. Requires more data to avoid overfitting, but patch-based training provides implicit augmentation.
  - **Patch Size vs. Representativeness**: Smaller patches (e.g., 64×64) are more memory-efficient but must be large enough to capture the characteristic scales of the physics (e.g., channel width, vortices). Patches must be representative.
  - **Rollout Horizon (T)**: Larger T (e.g., 8) better teaches long-term dynamics but increases memory during training (store intermediate activations for backprop) and risks instability. T=8 was chosen empirically.
- **Failure signatures**:
  - **Blurry interfaces / loss of high-frequency detail**: Likely UNet architecture or insufficient rollout training. Check if UNet++ or rollout improves.
  - **Rapid PCC drop after ~20-30 steps in validation**: Indicates poor generalization or latent space instability (for ROMs). Check if grid-size-invariant model or adversarial autoencoder helps.
  - **Boundary artifacts / incorrect Dirichlet conditions**: Indicates the loss function (`L_MSE|∂Ω` with `λ_BC`) is not properly tuned or the network architecture does not preserve boundaries well.
  - **Training loss decreases but validation loss increases**: Classic overfitting; grid-size-invariant training's patch-based augmentation should mitigate this.
- **First 3 experiments**:
  1.  **Validate Grid-Size Invariance**: Train a UNet (no rollout) on 64×64 patches. Inference on a full 256×256 field. Compare PCC/SSIM against a model trained on full 256×256 images. Expect comparable accuracy but much lower training memory. If full-domain model is significantly better, patches may not be representative.
  2.  **Compare UNet vs UNet++ (Single-Step)**: Train both architectures on patches with standard single-step loss. Evaluate on a validation simulation over 1-10 steps. Expect UNet++ to have higher SSIM (preserves edges better) and similar/better PCC.
  3.  **Assess Rollout Impact**: For the best architecture (likely UNet++), train one model with standard loss (T=1) and one with rollout (T=8, initialized from the T=1 model). Autoregressively predict 100 steps on a validation simulation. The rollout model should show slower PCC decay and higher SSIM at long horizons. If rollout destabilizes training, try a smaller T or curriculum learning.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- **Open Question 1**: How can the grid-size-invariant surrogate framework be effectively extended to three-dimensional spatial domains for rock-fluid interaction simulations?
  - *Basis in paper*: [explicit] The conclusion states: "As future work, we will apply the proposed methods to 3D datasets."
  - *Why unresolved*: The current study validates models only on 2D datasets; 3D introduces higher memory requirements and potentially different flow behaviors that may affect model performance and scalability.
  - *What evidence would resolve it*: Train and test the same grid-size-invariant architectures on 3D carbon storage datasets, compare GPU memory usage during training, and evaluate prediction accuracy (e.g., PCC, SSIM) against high-fidelity 3D PDE simulations.
- **Open Question 2**: To what extent does the grid-size-invariant approach generalize to fluid flow problems governed by different partial differential equations beyond the Navier-Stokes and advection-diffusion system studied here?
  - *Basis in paper*: [explicit] The conclusion mentions: "we will test the grid-size-invariant approach in a variety of fluid flow problems governed by different combinations of PDEs."
  - *Why unresolved*: The surrogate was trained on a specific carbon injection scenario with a particular set of PDEs; other fluid dynamics problems may have varying spatial scales, boundary conditions, or nonlinearities that challenge the model's invariance property.
  - *What evidence would resolve it*: Apply the grid-size-invariant models to datasets from simulations with different governing equations (e.g., multiphase flow, turbulence models), and assess transferability through metrics like PCC and SSIM on unseen domains.
- **Open Question 3**: What is the optimal strategy for integrating the surrogate model with PDE solvers in a hybrid simulation framework to balance efficiency and accuracy for long-term predictions?
  - *Basis in paper*: [explicit] The conclusion proposes: "the surrogate will be combined with a PDE-based solver... to guarantee the reliability of the prediction over a long-term run."
  - *Why unresolved*: The paper develops standalone surrogates; the hybrid approach is not implemented, and key questions about switching criteria, error thresholds, and coordination remain open.
  - *What evidence would resolve it*: Implement a hybrid system where the surrogate predicts for a fixed number of timesteps until a metric (e.g., PCC drop below 0.75) triggers PDE solver use; evaluate on extended simulations, measuring total computational time and error accumulation.
- **Open Question 4**: Can adversarial autoencoders for ROM compression be trained with improved stability to consistently enhance long-term autoregressive prediction accuracy across UNet and UNet++ architectures?
  - *Basis in paper*: [inferred] From methodological limitations: Appendix A reports difficulties in maintaining adversarial training, and results in Figure 6 show that AAE sometimes underperforms traditional AE, especially for longer rollouts.
  - *Why unresolved*: Adversarial training aims to regularize the latent space but introduces instability and hyperparameter sensitivity, leading to inconsistent gains in prediction accuracy over 100 timesteps.
  - *What evidence would resolve it*: Develop stabilized AAE training techniques (e.g., spectral normalization, gradient penalty), retrain ROMs with these methods, and compare PCC trends over 100 timesteps against baseline AE-trained models.

## Limitations
- Dataset not public: results rely on a single GeoChemFOAM ensemble (32 sims); generalizability unknown.
- Incomplete specs: rollout gradient handling, λ_BC schedule rationale, and exact UNet/UNet++ layer counts are missing, hindering exact replication.
- Patch representativeness: 64×64 training patches may omit large-scale patterns, potentially limiting grid-size invariance for domains with significant non-local dynamics.

## Confidence
- Memory reduction via patching: **High** (direct measurements).
- Grid-size-invariant accuracy (comparable to full-domain): **Medium** (validated only on 256×256, no statistical tests).
- UNet++ advantage: **Medium** (consistent but modest gains; architecture details unclear).
- Rollout improvement: **Medium** (T=8 helps long-term PCC; training stability not fully explored).
- Speedup claim: **Low** (qualitative “several orders”, no PDE runtime numbers).
