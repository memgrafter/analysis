# With Argus Eyes: Assessing Retrieval Gaps via Uncertainty Scoring to Detect and Remedy Retrieval Blind Spots

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2602.09616  
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.09616  
- **Reference count:** 40  
- **Primary result:** ARGUS yields consistent absolute gains averaging +3.4 nDCG@5 and +4.5 nDCG@10, with larger improvements on challenging subsets.

## Executive Summary
Neural retrievers in RAG systems suffer from systematic blind spots—entities that are relevant to queries but fail to be retrieved due to unfavorable embedding geometry. This paper introduces the Retrieval Probability Score (RPS) to quantify an entity’s retrievability risk under a fixed top‑k budget and shows that RPS can be predicted pre‑index from embedding geometry via lightweight probes. Based on this, the authors propose ARGUS, a pipeline that diagnoses high‑risk entities and remedies them by augmenting documents with context from a reference knowledge base. Evaluated across eight retrievers and three benchmarks (BRIGHT, IMPLIRET, RAR‑B), ARGUS achieves consistent absolute gains in nDCG, demonstrating that targeted augmentation can mitigate geometry‑induced retrieval failures.

## Method Summary
ARGUS consists of two stages: diagnosis and remedy. First, it defines Retrieval Probability Score (RPS) as the expected top‑k hit rate of an entity across its relevant queries when competing against a neutral pool. To avoid expensive retrieval simulations, a probe (e.g., XGBoost) is trained to predict RPS directly from the entity’s embedding. For a given corpus, the pipeline runs NER to extract mentions, pools their embeddings, and uses the probe to flag high‑risk entities (predicted RPS < 0.3). For each flagged entity, ARGUS retrieves supporting passages from a reference knowledge base (Wikipedia first paragraphs) via BM25S. It then creates augmented document views either by concatenating passages (Document Expansion) or by synthesizing a unified view with an LLM (LLM Synthesis). Finally, the original and augmented views are indexed; retrieval uses the target neural retriever unchanged.

## Key Results
- ARGUS achieves average absolute improvements of +3.4 nDCG@5 and +4.5 nDCG@10 across eight retrievers and three benchmarks.
- Document Expansion yields the most consistent gains, with especially large improvements on IMPLIRET (+6.76 nDCG@10).
- LLM Synthesis is more space‑efficient but shows higher variance; it degrades performance for JINA‑V3 on IMPLIRET.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1: Systematic blind spots arise from unfavorable embedding geometry
Neural retrievers trained with contrastive objectives shape the embedding space such that some entities occupy regions distant from their relevant queries. Because retrieval is a nearest‑neighbor search, these entities fall outside the top‑k for many valid queries, creating systematic blind spots. Evidence: Low average RPS (~0.11 at k=50) for standard retrievers; LDA projections show low‑RPS and high‑RPS entities occupy separable regions, confirming geometric structure.

### Mechanism 2: Blind‑spot risk is predictable from entity embeddings via lightweight probes
Because low‑RPS entities form discriminable clusters, a supervised probe trained on a sample of entities with known empirical RPS can learn a mapping from embedding → predicted RPS. The probe averages over query‑specific factors, enabling pre‑index risk estimation. Evidence: Probes achieve Pearson r ≈0.65–0.80 and macro‑F1 ≈0.65–0.80 across retrievers; residuals are centered near zero.

### Mechanism 3: Targeted knowledge augmentation remedies blind spots by altering document embeddings
Augmenting documents with concise, relevant context from a reference KB shifts the entity’s embedding toward a region closer to its queries, increasing retrieval probability. Two strategies are used: Document Expansion (concatenation) and LLM Synthesis (condensed view). Evidence: Consistent nDCG gains; retrieved gold documents tend to contain entities with higher predicted RPS, linking augmentation to improved retrieval.

## Foundational Learning

### Concept 1: Retrieval Probability Score (RPS)
**Why needed here:** RPS quantifies entity‑level retrievability risk under a fixed top‑k budget, serving as the ground metric for diagnosis.  
**Quick check:** For entity X with related queries Tₓ, how do you compute RPSₖ(X)?  
*(Answer: For each t ∈ Tₓ, build a candidate pool of size N containing X and N‑1 neutral entities. Retrieve top‑k, record whether X is retrieved (hit=1/0). RPSₖ(X) = average hit over all t.)*

### Concept 2: Embedding geometry and blind‑spot zones
**Why needed here:** Understanding that retrievability correlates with position in the embedding space explains why some entities are systematically missed and enables geometric diagnosis.  
**Quick check:** What does LDA separation of entities by RPS terciles (low/mid/high) indicate?  
*(Answer: It indicates that low‑RPS entities occupy distinct geometric regions, confirming structured blind spots rather than random failures.)*

### Concept 3: Diagnostic probing for pre‑index risk estimation
**Why needed here:** Probs allow estimating RPS without expensive retrieval simulations, making large‑scale corpus auditing feasible.

*(Answer: Input = entity embedding (from retriever encoder + span pooling). Output = predicted RPS. Trained on a labeled set where empirical RPS is the target, using regression.)*

## Architecture Onboarding

### Component map
NER -> Entity Embedding -> Risk Probe -> Thresholding -> Reference KB Retrieval -> Augmentation -> Indexing

### Critical path
The pipeline flows: extract mentions with NER, compute entity embeddings, predict RPS via probe, flag high‑risk entities, retrieve supporting passages from a reference KB, generate augmented document views (expansion or synthesis), and index all views.

### Design tradeoffs
- **Document Expansion vs. LLM Synthesis:** Expansion is simple and consistently effective but increases index size linearly with augmented views. Synthesis keeps index growth constant (one extra view per document) but depends on LLM quality and may degrade for some retrievers.
- **Threshold τ:** Default τ=0.3 balances catching blind spots against index bloat and noise. Lower values reduce augmentation cost but risk missing blind spots; higher values increase coverage but may add unnecessary views.
- **Reference KB:** Using Wikipedia first paragraphs assumes general coverage; domain‑specific applications may require a more targeted KB.

### Failure signatures
1. Probe miscalibration: Predicted RPS does not correlate with empirical RPS; residuals show systematic bias.
2. No retrieval improvement: nDCG@k on validation benchmarks does not increase after augmentation.
3. Excessive flagged entities: >50% of entities flagged, suggesting overly low τ or pervasive blind spots.

### First 3 experiments
1. **Probe validation:** Train probe on a held‑out entity set with computed RPS. Evaluate test RMSE, Pearson r, and 3‑class (low/mid/high) accuracy; check calibration.
2. **Single‑retriever end‑to‑end test:** Run ARGUS with Document Expansion for CONTRIEVER on BRIGHT; compare nDCG@5/10 to baseline using paired t‑test.
3. **Flagged entity analysis:** Sample 100 flagged and 100 unflagged entities; compute true empirical RPS. Expect flagged group to have significantly lower RPS.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
Is the global risk threshold τ=0.3 optimal across all retrievers and tasks, or should it be tuned per model and benchmark?  
*Basis:* The paper uses a fixed τ but notes adaptive configurations are an open direction; gains vary by retriever/benchmark.

### Open Question 2
What determines when Document Expansion outperforms LLM Synthesis (or vice versa) for a given retriever?  
*Basis:* Expansion is most consistent; synthesis is more space‑efficient but shows higher variance (e.g., JINA‑V3 degrades on IMPLIRET). The underlying cause is unexplained.

### Open Question 3
Why do diagnostic probes achieve varying predictive performance across retrievers (e.g., REASONIR-8B r=0.779 vs. JINA-V3 r=0.667), and can this be improved?  
*Basis:* Probe correlations range 0.64–0.78; attributed to varying embedding geometries but not investigated further.

### Open Question 4
Do blind spot remedies in ARGUS translate to improved end‑to‑end RAG generation quality, not just retrieval metrics?  
*Basis:* The paper evaluates only retrieval metrics (nDCG). Retrieval gains do not guarantee better generation; hallucination rates and answer accuracy remain untested.

### Open Question 5
How sensitive is ARGUS to the choice of reference knowledge base (e.g., Wikipedia freshness, domain mismatch, or completeness)?  
*Basis:* ARGUS assumes Wikipedia first paragraphs provide sufficient defining context. No alternative KBs or coverage error analysis is provided.

## Limitations
- Probe hyperparameters and LLM synthesis settings (temperature, token limits) are not fully specified, hindering exact reproduction.
- The reference KB (Wikipedia first paragraphs) may suffer from domain mismatch or incompleteness; augmentation could introduce noise or errors.
- NER‑to‑entity linking strategy is ambiguous (e.g., exact string match assumption); linking errors could produce misdiagnosis.

## Confidence
- Systematic blind spots from embedding geometry: **High** (RPS measurements, LDA separation).
- Probe‑predicted retrievability risk: **High** (probes achieve r=0.65–0.80, calibrated).
- Augmentation remedies blind spots: **Medium** (consistent but modest gains; synthesis degrades for some retrievers).

## Next Checks
1. **Probe hyperparameter sweep:** Train probes with varied XGBoost/ridge/MLP settings; report stability of Pearson r and 3‑class F1 to confirm robustness.
2. **KB preprocessing ablation:** Compare ARGUS performance using different Wikipedia pipelines (e.g., with/without redirect resolution, paragraph truncation) on a subset of retrievers/benchmarks to quantify sensitivity.
3. **Linking error audit:** Manually verify Wikipedia linking for a random sample of flagged entities (e.g., n=200). Compute error rate; if >20%, the diagnosis pipeline likely introduces false positives/negatives.
