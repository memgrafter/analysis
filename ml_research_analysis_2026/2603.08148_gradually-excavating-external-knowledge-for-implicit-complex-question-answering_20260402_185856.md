# Gradually Excavating External Knowledge for Implicit Complex Question Answering

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2603.08148  
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.08148  
- **Reference count:** 16  
- **Primary result:** GEEK with Flan-T5-11B achieves 78.17% accuracy on StrategyQA, setting SOTA for models under ~10B parameters while using <6% of larger competitors' parameters.

## Executive Summary
This paper tackles open-domain implicit complex question answering, where large language models (LLMs) struggle due to outdated internal knowledge and limited multi-step reasoning capabilities. The authors propose GEEK, a gradual knowledge excavation framework that iteratively decomposes questions, retrieves relevant external passages, and extracts verified facts using a controller LLM. By accumulating facts and exploring multiple reasoning paths via beam search, GEEK builds a dynamic strategy to answer implicitly complex queries. Evaluated on StrategyQA, GEEK with an 11B-parameter backbone significantly outperforms much larger models while being highly parameter-efficient. Ablation studies confirm that external retrieval and strategy exploration are essential to its success.

## Method Summary
GEEK is an iterative framework centered on a controller LLM (e.g., Flan-T5-11B) that selects from three actions: decompose the question, retrieve relevant passages from a corpus (e.g., Wikipedia via DPR), and extract concise factual answers from passages. Each iteration verifies extracted facts and accumulates them into a growing strategy. Beam search maintains multiple reasoning paths, allowing the system to explore alternative decompositions and retrieval results before converging on a final answer. This process mitigates the need for extensive model scaling by leveraging external knowledge and structured exploration.

## Key Results
- Achieves 78.17% accuracy on StrategyQA with Flan-T5-11B, setting a new SOTA for models under ~10B parameters.
- Uses less than 6% of the parameters of larger competitive models while outperforming them.
- Ablation studies show external retrieval and beam-search-based strategy exploration are critical components; removing either degrades performance substantially.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
GEEK overcomes the core limitations of standard LLMs in implicit complex QA by externalizing knowledge and structuring reasoning. First, it combats outdated internal knowledge via dynamic retrieval from an up-to-date corpus (Wikipedia via DPR). Second, it addresses the multi-step reasoning challenge by decomposing questions and iteratively accumulating verified facts, which anchors each reasoning step in evidence. Third, beam search explores multiple decomposition and retrieval paths, reducing the risk of early error propagation and enabling the discovery of optimal strategies. This divide-and-conquer approach shifts the burden from massive parameter scaling to efficient, targeted knowledge access and verification. Assumption: The controller's action selection quality is sufficient to guide this process effectively.

## Foundational Learning
- **Implicit Complex QA:** Questions requiring reasoning over facts not explicitly stated in the query. *Why needed:* Models must infer missing steps, which standard LLMs often fail. *Quick check:* Can the question be answered by directly matching query tokens, or does it require chaining unstated facts?
- **Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG):** Augmenting LLMs with external knowledge retrieval. *Why needed:* Overcomes static, outdated internal knowledge. *Quick check:* Does the system fetch and use external documents before generating answers?
- **Beam Search for Strategy Exploration:** Maintaining multiple candidate reasoning paths. *Why needed:* Prevents premature convergence on suboptimal decompositions. *Quick check:* Does the system keep and evaluate alternative reasoning trajectories, not just one?
- **Dense Passage Retrieval (DPR):** Embedding-based passage retrieval. *Why needed:* Provides accurate, efficient retrieval for QA. *Quick check:* Is retrieval based on semantic similarity via learned embeddings?
- **Iterative Fact Verification:** Accumulating and checking facts across steps. *Why needed:* Ensures each reasoning step is grounded in verified information. *Quick check:* Are intermediate facts validated before being used in subsequent steps?

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component map:**  
Controller LLM -> Action Selection -> (Decomposer, Retriever (DPR), Extractor) -> Fact Verification -> Strategy Accumulator -> Beam Search (multiple paths) -> Final Answer

**Critical path:**  
1. Controller receives question and current strategy.  
2. Selects action: decompose, retrieve, or extract.  
3. Decomposer generates sub-questions; Retriever fetches passages; Extractor pulls answers.  
4. Facts are verified and added to strategy.  
5. Beam search maintains multiple strategies, pruning low-scoring ones.  
6. After max steps or convergence, final answer is produced.

**Design tradeoffs:**  
- Controller LLM size vs. cost: Smaller controllers (e.g., 11B) reduce compute but may limit action selection quality.  
- Beam width vs. efficiency: Wider beams explore more paths but increase retrieval and LLM calls.  
- Retrieval corpus: Larger corpora improve coverage but increase retrieval noise and latency.

**Failure signatures:**  
- Controller repeatedly selects irrelevant actions, leading to irrelevant facts.  
- Retriever fails to return gold passages, causing missing evidence.  
- Extractor mis-parses passages, introducing incorrect facts.  
- Beam search prunes correct paths early due to poor intermediate scoring.

**First experiments:**  
1. Replace Flan-T5-11B controller with Llama-7B to test robustness across LLM architectures.  
2. Swap DPR for BM25 retrieval to measure sensitivity to retrieval quality.  
3. Vary beam search width (e.g., 1, 2, 4) to quantify tradeoff between accuracy and computational cost.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
The paper does not explicitly list open questions, but the identified limitations suggest future research directions:  
- How does GEEK generalize to other multi-hop QA benchmarks (e.g., HotpotQA, 2WikiMultiHop)?  
- What is the impact of using alternative retrieval systems (e.g., BM25, sparse retrievers) or different corpora?  
- How does the framework perform with controller LLMs of varying sizes or architectural families (e.g., decoder-only models)?

## Limitations
- Evaluation restricted to StrategyQA; unclear if results generalize to other multi-hop QA benchmarks (e.g., HotpotQA, 2WikiMultiHop).  
- Framework tightly coupled with DPR; performance may drop with alternative retrieval systems or corpora.  
- Controller fixed to Flan-T5-11B; no exploration of other model sizes or families limits robustness claims.

## Confidence
- **Performance claim (78.17% on StrategyQA, SOTA under 10B):** Medium (single dataset, no statistical tests).  
- **Parameter-efficiency claim (<6% of larger models’ parameters):** High (direct count comparison).  
- **Ablation significance (external retrieval and strategy exploration critical):** Medium (ablations on one setting only).  
- **Generalization to implicit complex QA:** Low (only one benchmark evaluated).

## Next Checks
1. Replicate GEEK on additional multi-hop QA benchmarks (e.g., HotpotQA, 2WikiMultiHop) and compare against strong baselines.  
2. Perform sensitivity analysis by varying the retrieval component (BM25, different embeddings) and controller LLM (e.g., Llama-7B, Mistral-7B).
