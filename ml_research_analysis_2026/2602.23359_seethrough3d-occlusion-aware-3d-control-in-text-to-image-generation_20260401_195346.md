# SeeThrough3D: Occlusion Aware 3D Control in Text-to-Image Generation

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2602.23359
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.23359
- **Reference count:** 40
- **Primary result:** SeeThrough3D significantly outperforms existing 3D layout-conditioned methods on occlusion-sensitive metrics (depth ordering, CLIP objectness, orientation error) in the 3DOcBench benchmark.

## Executive Summary
Text-to-image generation conditioned on 3D layouts often fails to model accurate inter-object occlusions, leading to geometrically inconsistent depth and scale. SeeThrough3D introduces the Occlusion-Aware 3D Scene Representation (OSCR), where objects are rendered as translucent, color-coded 3D bounding boxes from the target camera viewpoint. This representation explicitly reveals occluded regions and encodes orientation. A pretrained flow-based diffusion transformer (FLUX.1-dev) is conditioned on OSCR tokens via a LoRA adaptation and uses masked self-attention to bind each object’s box to its corresponding noun in the prompt, preventing attribute mixing. Trained on a synthetic dataset with strong occlusions and augmented with depth-to-image syntheses, the method achieves large gains on the new 3DOcBench evaluation (e.g., depth ordering 1.46 vs. 0.68–1.02) and receives strong user preference (>96%). The approach generalizes to unseen objects, supports camera viewpoint control, and extends to personalized subjects.

## Method Summary
The core of SeeThrough3D is the Occlusion-Aware 3D Scene Representation (OSCR). For a given 3D layout (bounding boxes with position, size, orientation) and camera viewpoint, the scene is rendered in a graphics engine (Blender) using translucent boxes whose faces are color-coded (e.g., orange front, blue left, green others). This OSCR image captures the visibility of each object from the camera and encodes orientation via face colors. The OSCR image is encoded with a VAE into tokens, which are concatenated with text tokens (from the prompt) and noisy image tokens during diffusion. A LoRA (rank 128) is injected only on the Q/K/V projections for OSCR tokens, leaving text and image tokens untouched to preserve the base FLUX prior. Crucially, masked self-attention restricts OSCR tokens within an object’s segmentation mask to attend solely to that object’s noun tokens in the prompt, enforcing a tight object–text binding.

Training uses a synthetic dataset of 25K scenes rendered with up to 4 objects on a ground plane, with aggressive occlusion filtering ensuring each object’s visibility ratio lies between 0.3 and 0.7. An additional 25K samples are created by taking the rendered RGB images, estimating depth (e.g., with Depth Anything), and applying the FLUX.1-Depth-dev model to generate photorealistic variants. A CLIP-based filter (crop object segments, compute image-text similarity, threshold 0.25) ensures quality. Training proceeds for 30K steps (first 25K at 512×512, last 5K at 1024×1024) with batch size 2, LR 1e-4, on 2×H100 GPUs. At inference, the same OSCR rendering and masked attention are applied to generate images consistent with the provided 3D layout and camera.

## Key Results
- **Occlusion handling:** On 3DOcBench, SeeThrough3D achieves depth ordering accuracy of 1.46 (higher is better) vs. 0.68–1.02 for baselines.
- **Object fidelity:** CLIP objectness score of 22.86 vs. 19.70–21.83; orientation error reduced to 47.92° vs. 66.29°–92.73°.
- **User preference:** In a user study (n=60), >96% rated SeeThrough3D higher for realism, layout adherence, and prompt following.
- **Generalization:** Supports novel objects, arbitrary camera viewpoints, and personalization via a subject LoRA while retaining FLUX’s image quality (KID 5.43 vs. 13.46–20.12).

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
Translucent OSCR boxes make occluded parts visible in the conditioning image, providing the model with an explicit signal about which object regions should be hidden. Color-coded faces disambiguate orientation, which depth alone cannot convey. Masked self-attention forces each object’s spatial representation to interact only with its textual description, eliminating cross-object attribute leakage that plagues standard cross-attention. The LoRA adaptation updates only a small subset of the diffusion model’s parameters, preserving its strong generative priors while specializing it to the OSCR domain. Training on synthetic scenes with controlled, strong occlusions teaches the model to reason about visibility ratios and depth ordering. The depth-to-image augmentation further bridges the synthetic–real gap, improving texture and background realism without altering layout.

## Foundational Learning
- **Diffusion Transformers (FLUX):** Base generative model that operates in latent space; needed for high-quality image synthesis. *Quick check:* Load pretrained FLUX.1-dev and verify VAE reconstruction.
- **LoRA (Low-Rank Adaptation):** Efficient fine-tuning by adding low-rank matrices to weight updates; avoids catastrophic forgetting. *Quick check:* Ensure LoRA is applied only to OSCR token projections and has rank 128.
- **Masked Self-Attention:** Restricts attention between OSCR tokens and text tokens per object; prevents attribute mixing. *Quick check:* During training, mask OSCR tokens of object *i* to attend only to text tokens of its noun.
- **Synthetic 3D Rendering:** Provides perfect ground-truth occlusions and segmentation masks; scalable data source. *Quick check:* Render OSCR image and masks in Blender; verify visibility ratios ∈[0.3,0.7] and 2D box sizes.
- **Depth-to-Image Augmentation:** Uses a depth-conditioned diffusion model to add realism to synthetic RGBs while preserving layout. *Quick check:* Apply Depth Anything + FLUX.1-Depth-dev; filter with CLIP segment similarity ≥0.25.

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component map:**  
Prompt → Text Encoder → text tokens  
3D Layout + Camera → Blender → OSCR image (translucent, color-coded) → VAE Encoder → OSCR tokens  
Noisy latent → FLUX DiT (inputs: text tokens, OSCR tokens, latent) with masked self-attention (OSCR tokens of each object’s mask attend only to its text tokens) → output latent → VAE Decoder → image  
LoRA (rank 128) applied to Q/K/V projections of OSCR tokens only.

**Critical path:**  
1. Render OSCR image and per-object masks from layout.  
2. Encode OSCR image to tokens.  
3. Form input token sequence: [text tokens, OSCR tokens, noisy image tokens].  
4. Apply masked attention throughout DiT.  
5. Sample with standard diffusion scheduler.  
6. Decode final latent to image.

**Design tradeoffs:**  
- Transparency vs. clarity: High transparency reveals occlusions but may reduce signal for visible parts; chosen to balance.  
- Color-coding scheme: Must be distinguishable and consistent; limited palette constrains number of faces.  
- LoRA rank: Higher rank increases capacity but risks overfitting; rank 128 chosen as sweet spot.  
- Occlusion filtering: Tight visibility ratio (0.3–0.7) ensures strong interactions but reduces dataset size.  
- Synthetic vs. real: Synthetic data provides perfect supervision; augmentation mitigates domain gap.

**Failure signatures:**  
- Occlusion inconsistency: Objects incorrectly layered or hidden parts rendered in front. Diagnose by visualizing OSCR image and checking attention masks.  
- Orientation flips (e.g., 180° errors): Often due to color-coding ambiguity or insufficient orientation diversity.  
- Attribute mixing: Text attributes (color, shape) leak between objects; indicates broken masked attention.  
- Over-smooth or unrealistic textures: Suggests augmentation filtering insufficient; revisit CLIP threshold.

**First three experiments:**  
1. **Ablate masked attention:** Train without per-object attention masks; expect severe attribute mixing and occlusion errors.  
2. **Replace translucent boxes with solid boxes:** Train without transparency; expect poorer depth ordering and higher orientation error.  
3. **Vary LoRA rank (64, 128, 256):** Measure trade-off between quality (KID, CLIP) and overfitting; identify optimal rank.

## Limitations
- Exact training assets (39 models from Objaverse/SketchFab) and their manual alignment/scaling parameters are undisclosed, hindering exact replication.  
- LoRA injection details (specific layers, per-layer alpha) and depth-to-image augmentation hyperparameters are not fully specified.  
- The 3DOcBench benchmark is novel and lacks external validation; user study has small sample size (n=60) and no statistical analysis.

## Confidence
- Occlusion superiority on 3DOcBench: **Medium** (novel benchmark, some reproduction gaps).  
- Necessity of ablation components (transparency, color-coding, masked attention): **Medium**.  
- User preference (>96%): **Low-Medium** (small sample, subjective).  
- Generalization to unseen objects/cameras: **Medium** (qualitative only).  
- Overall method validity: **Medium** (core idea clear but reproduction hindered by missing details).
