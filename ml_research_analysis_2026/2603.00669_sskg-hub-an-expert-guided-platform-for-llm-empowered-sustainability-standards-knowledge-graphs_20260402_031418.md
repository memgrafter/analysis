# SSKG Hub: An Expert-Guided Platform for LLM-Empowered Sustainability Standards Knowledge Graphs

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2603.00669
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.00669
- **Reference Count:** 23
- **Primary Result:** An LLM-centered, expert-guided pipeline transforms lengthy, cross-referential sustainability standards into auditable knowledge graphs, achieving 67.12% triple retention after expert curation in a case study on IFRS S2.

## Executive Summary
SSKG Hub is a platform designed to overcome the challenge of analyzing complex, lengthy sustainability standards by converting them into structured, auditable Knowledge Graphs (KGs). It employs a hybrid pipeline where a Large Language Model (LLM) generates an initial draft of semantic triples from the source PDFs, which are then rigorously reviewed and certified by domain experts through a defined governance workflow. The system's core innovation lies in its provenance-first storage, which tightly links every graph fact back to its exact source text location, enabling verification and trust. A public web application supports interactive exploration, cross-standard fusion, and downstream tasks like KGQA.

## Method Summary
The method is a five-stage human-AI collaboration. First, a standard PDF is ingested and text is extracted. An LLM then classifies the standard type to apply a tailored prompt and performs chunked extraction of `(subject, predicate, object)` triples. These draft triples are stored in a Neo4j graph database with fine-grained provenance metadata (source document, page, text span). Next, a multi-role governance workflow allows independent expert reviewers to assess, edit, or soft-delete triples. A meta-expert adjudicates conflicts and certifies the final document-level KG. The entire process is designed around auditability, with all actions logged and evidence traceable via the UI.

## Key Results
- From a case study on the IFRS S2 standard, the LLM initially extracted 73 triples.
- After expert review and meta-expert certification, 49 triples were retained (67.12% retention rate).
- 24 triples were removed as unsupported by the source text, demonstrating the curation's filtering effect.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1: LLM-Centered Draft Extraction
- **Claim:** An LLM-centered extraction pipeline can generate a high-coverage draft knowledge graph from dense, cross-referential standards documents.
- **Mechanism:** Uses standard-specific prompts and configurable chunking to guide an LLM (Qwen-Max) to extract `(subject, predicate, object)` triples. Prompts bias extraction toward standard-relevant relation types. Strict regex parsing enforces format.
- **Core Assumption:** LLMs, when constrained by domain-tailored prompts and manageable context, can reliably surface explicit factual relationships without hallucinating unsupported triples.
- **Evidence Anchors:** [abstract] "LLM-centered, expert-guided pipeline"; [section 4.1.2] "standard-specific prompting" and "configurable chunking"; [section A.2] Prompt templates.

### Mechanism 2: Expert-Governed Certification
- **Claim:** A multi-role, expert-in-the-loop governance workflow ensures the draft KG's reliability and produces a certified, auditable KG.
- **Mechanism:** Draft triples undergo independent review by multiple domain experts who can retain, edit, or soft-delete triples using provenance-linked evidence. A meta-expert adjudicates unresolved issues and certifies the KG. All actions are logged with audit metadata.
- **Core Assumption:** Independent expert review combined with meta-expert finalization mitigates individual bias and ensures standards-compliant knowledge representation. Reversible soft-deletion supports iterative correction.
- **Evidence Anchors:** [abstract] "expert-guided pipeline", "meta-expert certification"; [section 4.2.1] Role-governed access; [section 6] 73 initial → 49 certified triples.

### Mechanism 3: Provenance-First Storage & Traceability
- **Claim:** Provenance-first storage in a graph database with fine-grained audit metadata enables triple-level evidence tracing and supports auditable downstream tasks.
- **Mechanism:** Each stored triple (`:RELATES_TO` edge) retains explicit links to its source document, page, and evidence span. Operational metadata tracks mutations. The UI's "View evidence" function traces any KG fact back to its original text location.
- **Core Assumption:** Explicit, queryable provenance is necessary for user trust and regulatory compliance, transforming the KG into an auditable artifact.
- **Evidence Anchors:** [abstract] "provenance-aware Neo4j storage... triple-level evidence tracing"; [section 4.1.4] "provenance as a first-class requirement"; [section 5.3] "View evidence function reveals the exact quoted sentence".

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map:** PDF Upload -> Text Extraction (PyMuPDF) & Standard ID -> Configurable Chunking -> LLM Triple Extraction (Qwen-Max with standard-specific prompt) -> Parsing/Normalization -> Neo4j Insertion (Draft KG with provenance) -> Independent Multi-Expert Review (Role-based UI) -> Meta-expert Adjudication & Certification -> Certified KG (Available for Fusion/KGQA/Analytics). An optional LLM Verifier assists during review.
- **Critical path:** Preserve provenance at each step: source document ID, page number, and evidence text span must survive from initial extraction through expert edits to final certified triples.
- **Design tradeoffs:**
  - **LLM Choice vs. Cost/Control:** Uses proprietary Qwen-Max for quality but incurs API cost/latency and limits fine-tuning.
  - **Expert-Centric vs. Scalability:** Multi-expert review ensures quality but is slow and labor-intensive, prioritizing trust over throughput.
  - **Provenance Granularity:** Sentence-level evidence is precise but increases storage and complicates chunk overlap handling; trades audit fidelity for footprint.
- **Failure signatures:**
  - **Extraction-stage:** Low triple yield, high malformed output rate, or standard misclassification (wrong prompt used). Check chunk size/overlap and prompt configuration.
  - **Review-stage:** Experts marking >50% of draft triples for deletion, or persistent conflicts between reviews. Indicates poor extraction or ambiguous source text.
  - **Storage/query:** Missing `source_doc`/`page` properties on edges, or evidence view showing incorrect sentences. Indicates provenance break during insertion or after edits.
  - **UI/performance:** Graph visualization stalls on large KGs. Check Neo4j query efficiency and data model.
- **First 3 experiments:**
  1. **End-to-End Draft Quality Check:** Ingest a small, well-understood standard section. Run extraction. Verify all ground-truth triples appear in draft KG with correct provenance. Measure recall.
  2. **Review Efficacy Test:** Have two experts independently review the same draft KG. Calculate initial agreement rate (retain/edit/delete). Simulate meta-expert adjudication on disagreements. Compare final retention against the 67.12% benchmark.
  3. **Provenance Trace Validation:** For 20 randomly sampled certified triples, use the UI's "View evidence" to confirm the cited sentence is directly supportive and the page number is correct.

## Limitations
- **LLM Extraction Generalizability:** Performance is validated on a single standard (IFRS S2) with one LLM (Qwen-Max). Generalizability to other standards or models is unproven; risk of prompt overfitting.
- **Scalability of Expert Governance:** The resource-intensive multi-expert review and meta-expert certification workflow's feasibility for large-scale or continuously updated standards is unknown; bottleneck risks are not quantified.
- **Provenance Integrity Under Editing:** It is unclear how edited triples' provenance is maintained—whether they retain original evidence links or become new entities, potentially weakening audit trails.

## Confidence
- **Mechanism 1 (LLM-centered extraction):** **Medium**. Strong process design and prompt templates, but empirical validation is narrow (one standard) and lacks comparative baselines.
- **Mechanism 2 (Expert governance workflow):** **Medium**. Clearly defined roles and a quantified outcome (67.12% retention), but based on a small, single-standard panel. Generalizability and inter-rater reliability are not established.
- **Mechanism 3 (Provenance-first storage):** **High**. Well-specified data model and UI feature directly support the claim. Core risk is implementation fidelity, not concept.
- **Foundational Learning Concepts:** **High**. Correct identification of core concepts (KGs, HITL, Provenance, Graph DBs, KGQA) essential to the system's design.

## Next Checks
1. **Cross-Standard Extraction Benchmark:** Re-run the full pipeline on at least two additional dissimilar sustainability standards (e.g., GRI, TCFD). Measure recall against a gold-standard manual annotation set to assess generalization beyond IFRS S2.
2. **Expert Agreement Study:** Have three new domain experts independently review the same draft KG from a different standard section. Compute Cohen’s κ on retain/edit/delete decisions to quantify inter-rater reliability and identify ambiguous triples.
3. **Provenance Trace Stress Test:** For 50 randomly sampled *edited* triples in the certified KG, attempt to reconstruct the evidence trail. Determine if the UI’s "View evidence" points to the original source sentence, the expert’s edit justification, or a broken link.
