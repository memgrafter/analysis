# Fanar-Sadiq: A Multi-Agent Architecture for Grounded Islamic QA

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2603.08501
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.08501
- **Reference count:** 37
- **Primary result:** The system achieves 76.5% average accuracy on Islamic QA benchmarks, outperforming open-source models and competing with proprietary ones while reducing hallucinations through intent-aware routing and deterministic calculators.

## Executive Summary
Islamic question answering demands answers grounded in canonical texts and accurately respecting jurisprudential rules, yet large language models often hallucinate or misattribute sources. This paper presents Fanar-Sadiq, a bilingual multi-agent architecture that routes heterogeneous queries to specialized modules—including deterministic calculators for zakat and inheritance, exact Quranic verse lookup, and retrieval-grounded fiqh QA—via a hybrid intent classifier. Evaluated on public benchmarks, the system achieves 76.5% average accuracy, surpassing open-source baselines and approaching proprietary models, with especially strong gains on faithfulness-critical tasks. Deployed at scale, it has handled approximately 1.9 million queries with a 77.4% user satisfaction rate, demonstrating a practical approach to domain-specific QA with reduced hallucination.

## Method Summary
The architecture begins with a binary router to filter non-Islamic queries. A hybrid intent classifier—combining a zero-shot LLM prompt with an embedding-based fallback—predicts one of nine intents. Each intent maps to a dedicated tool: rule-based calculators for zakat and inheritance (handling madhhab-specific branching); a Quran pipeline that parses references, retrieves exact verses from a structured database, and uses NL2SQL for statistics or full surah retrieval; a retrieval-augmented fiqh QA agent that generates answers grounded in retrieved documents with deterministic citation tags; and utility modules for greetings, calendar, prayer times, and du’a lookup. The response assembler then formats the output, adds a standardized References block with normalized citations, and ensures traceability. The system leverages Qwen3-Embedding-4B for embeddings, a vector database (HNSW) for document retrieval, and a fine-tuned Qwen3-4B-Instruct model for NL2SQL, with low temperature settings to promote determinism.

## Key Results
- **Average accuracy:** 76.5% across five public benchmarks, outperforming open-source models (e.g., ALLaM-7B: 45.9%, Fanar-2-27B: 62.4%) and approaching proprietary ones (GPT-5: 77.1%, Gemini-3-Pro: 79.6%).
- **Faithfulness gains:** 65.4% on IslamicFaithQA (vs. GPT-5 56.6%) and 65.1% on FatwaQA (vs. Gemini-3-Pro 67.0%), despite being an open system.
- **Routing & adoption:** Hybrid intent classifier achieves 90.1% accuracy on 700 annotated queries; deployed system has ~1.9 million accesses with 77.4% user satisfaction (likes).

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Intent-aware routing
A hybrid classifier (LLM prompt + embedding fallback) achieves 90.1% accuracy on 700 annotated queries, correctly routing each question to a specialized module—such as a symbolic calculator, a retrieval pipeline, or an exact verse lookup. This separation prevents mismatched processing (e.g., sending a zakat calculation to free-form QA), which would likely cause numerical errors or ungrounded answers.

### Deterministic calculators
For zakat and inheritance, fixed formulas from Islamic jurisprudence are implemented as rule-based engines with madhhab-sensitive branching (e.g., handling ‘awl and radd). These symbolic components guarantee arithmetic consistency and adherence to doctrinal constraints, unlike generative models prone to hallucination. The system’s 72.2% score on the QIAS inheritance benchmark (below proprietary models) is attributed to mapping computed distributions to multiple-choice options, suggesting the underlying calculator is sound.

### Evidence tracking and citation normalization
All evidence-bearing modules produce deterministic outputs: the Fiqh QA agent assigns tags like [CITE:N] to evidence spans, while the Quran tool returns verbatim verses with normalized URLs (e.g., quran.com/2:255). The response assembler includes a standardized References block, enabling users to verify each claim against canonical sources. This design yields a 9-point improvement over GPT-5 on the IslamicFaithQA faithfulness benchmark (65.4% vs. 56.6%).

## Foundational Learning
- **Intent Classification in Heterogeneous QA**  
  *Why needed:* Islamic queries range from exact scripture retrieval to numeric calculations and nuanced fiqh reasoning. A one-size-fits-all RAG pipeline fails for tasks requiring symbolic computation or verbatim lookup.  
  *Quick check:* Given "How much zakat on 50g gold?" vs. "What does 2:255 say?", why would sending both to a standard retrieval-augmented generator cause different failure modes?

- **Deterministic vs. Generative Outputs for Rule-Bound Domains**  
  *Why needed:* Zakat and inheritance involve fixed ratios (e.g., 2.5%, Quranic shares) and conditional branching that LLMs may hallucinate or ignore. Symbolic calculators enforce exact arithmetic and jurisprudential constraints.  
  *Quick check:* In inheritance, if a deceased leaves a wife and two daughters, what Quranic shares apply, and why might an LLM-generated answer fluctuate without a calculator?

- **Citation Normalization and Verification Traces**  
  *Why needed:* Users expect answers grounded in canonical texts with precise references. Paraphrasing risks distortion; deterministic tagging and verbatim retrieval ensure traceability.  
  *Quick check:* When answering "What is Ayat al-Kursi?", why is returning the exact verse text with a stable URL more faithful than a paraphrased summary?

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component map**  
Binary Islamic router -> Hybrid intent classifier -> Specialized modules (Greeting, Calendar, Prayer Times, Dua Lookup, Zakat/Inheritance calculators, Fiqh QA, General Islamic QA, Quran pipeline) -> Response assembler

**Critical path**  
1. User query passes through binary router to filter non-Islamic queries.  
2. Hybrid intent classifier predicts intent (9 classes) using an LLM prompt; if confidence low, falls back to embedding similarity.  
3. Query is routed to the appropriate module (e.g., calculation tool, retrieval pipeline, Quran lookup).  
4. Module processes query and returns a structured answer with evidence metadata.  
5. Response assembler formats the final response, adds standardized citations and references, and returns it to the user.

**Design tradeoffs**  
- Precision vs. coverage: Deterministic calculators avoid hallucinations but cover only Sunni madhhab variants, excluding other schools or complex edge cases.  
- Retrieval recall vs. latency: Top-k=12 balances breadth and speed; increasing k improves recall but adds noise and latency.  
- LLM classifier vs. rule-based fallback: LLM offers flexibility but may hallucinate labels; embedding fallback is stable but less adaptive to novel phrasing.

**Failure signatures**  
- Routing errors: Misclassifying a calculation query as general fiqh leads to numerical mistakes.  
- Calculator invalid inputs: Incomplete heir lists produce partial or incorrect distributions.  
- Quran lookup failures: Noisy reference parsing (e.g., "Al-Baqarah last verse") falls back to surah-level or errors.  
- Verification bypass: Citation normalization drops or misattributes sources, reducing evidentiary grounding.

**First 3 experiments**  
1. **Routing accuracy:** Run 705 annotated queries through the classifier; measure per-intent precision/recall and examine fallback activation on low-confidence cases.  
2. **Calculator correctness:** Test zakat and inheritance calculators against gold-standard inputs (e.g., known nisab values, QIAS test cases) and compare outputs to expected results.  
3. **Citation fidelity:** Sample 100 Quran/fiqh retrieval responses; verify exact verse text matches the Uthmanic mushaf, confirm URL resolution, and check for missing or misattributed citations in the References block.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
1. **Expanding madhhab coverage:** The current calculators handle only a subset of Sunni branches; how can the system be extended to cover a broader range of disputed cases in inheritance and zakat across schools?  
2. **Improving hybrid classifier robustness:** With 90.1% routing accuracy, misrouting remains a failure mode; how can robustness be increased for ambiguous or low-confidence queries?  
3. **Hadith quotation validation:** Current verification focuses on Quranic verses; how can Hadith texts be integrated to prevent misattribution and hallucinations when citing Prophetic traditions?  
4. **Citation completeness and jurisprudential diversity:** Retrieved evidence often reflects multiple scholarly opinions; how can the system summarize these without oversimplification or omitting minority views?  
5. **More reliable open-ended evaluation:** LLM-as-judge may not capture nuance or legitimate differences of opinion; how can human evaluation be incorporated to validate open-ended QA performance?

## Limitations
- Replication is hindered by proprietary components (the Fanar LLM agent) and the unreleased 500k+ document corpus (content, preprocessing, version).  
- The rule-based calculators cover only a limited set of Sunni madhhab branches; many disputed cases and edge conditions remain unhandled.  
- Evaluation of open-ended answers relies partly on LLM-as-judge, which may not fully capture jurisprudential nuance or legitimate scholarly differences.
