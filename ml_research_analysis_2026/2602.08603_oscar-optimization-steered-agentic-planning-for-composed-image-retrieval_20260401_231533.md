# OSCAR: Optimization-Steered Agentic Planning for Composed Image Retrieval

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2602.08603
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.08603
- **Reference count:** 40
- **Primary result:** Achieves state-of-the-art on CIRCO (mAP@5 56.54%, +23.13% relative), CIRR (Recall@1 51.18%, +76.60% relative), and FashionIQ (+1.25% avg Recall@10) using only 10% training data.

## Executive Summary
OSCAR addresses the limitations of composed image retrieval (CIR) by reformulating agentic planning as an optimization-steered trajectory planning problem. It uses an offline two-stage mixed-integer programming (MIP) to derive optimal tool-call trajectories from training data, storing them in a golden library. At inference, similar trajectories are retrieved to steer a VLM planner to replicate the optimal logic in one pass. OSCAR demonstrates substantial improvements over state-of-the-art methods on standard CIR benchmarks and a private industrial gallery, while requiring only a fraction of the training data, highlighting the generalization of planning logic over memorization.

The core innovations include: (1) globally optimal trajectory planning via MIP that avoids heuristic search limitations, (2) rigorous set-theoretic composition (union, intersection, difference) for precise inclusion/exclusion reasoning, and (3) in-context transfer of planning logic that enables a VLM to execute near-optimal plans without iterative search. OSCAR's success underscores the power of combining optimization-derived demonstrations with large VLMs for complex retrieval tasks.

## Method Summary
OSCAR operates in two phases. Offline, for each training sample, it constructs atomic retrievals using a diverse set of tools (embedding models, caption models) and rewrites the modification query into positive and negative sub-queries. A two-stage MIP then selects a subset of these retrievals and determines a set-theoretic composition (union of positives, intersection of negatives, followed by difference) that maximizes coverage of ground-truth targets while minimizing noise. The resulting optimal trajectory is stored in a golden library, indexed by an embedding of the query's modification text and reference image caption.

Online, given a test query, OSCAR computes its problem-context embedding to retrieve the top-N similar trajectories from the golden library. These demonstrations are provided as in-context examples to a VLM planner (Qwen3-VL-32B), which generates a tool-call plan in one pass. The tools (e.g., embedding retrievals) are executed via a tool server, and their results are combined using the instructed set operations. Finally, a VLM verifier re-ranks the candidates using binary relevance scoring, and the ranked list is returned. The entire system is training-free; the only learnable component is the VLM, which is used in a zero-shot manner with demonstrations.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1: Optimization-derived global trajectories
**Claim:** Offline MIP optimization yields globally optimal tool-call trajectories that avoid heuristic search limitations.  
**Mechanism:** For each training sample, the two-stage MIP selects atomic retrievals (positive and negative) and their set composition to maximize ground-truth coverage while minimizing noise, providing an optimal plan stored in the golden library.  
**Core assumption:** The MIP formulation correctly captures the optimal planning objective and constraints.  
**Evidence anchors:** The paper reformulates agentic CIR as a trajectory planning problem and provides detailed MIP formulations (Section 3.3/3.4). The contrast with heuristic agentic methods (Appendix A.2) and optimization-based planning (A.3) supports this.  
**Break condition:** If the MIP is too large or poorly formulated, or atomic retrieval quality is low, trajectories may be suboptimal or infeasible.

### Mechanism 2: Rigorous set-theoretic composition
**Claim:** Explicit set operations enable precise inclusion/exclusion reasoning unattainable by unified embeddings.  
**Mechanism:** Positive atomic retrievals are unioned to maximize recall, negative ones are intersected to conservatively exclude common irrelevant images, and then difference filters positives by negatives, enforcing logical constraints.  
**Core assumption:** Atomic retrievals capture partial constraints and their combination yields the correct target set.  
**Evidence anchors:** The abstract emphasizes explicit set operations; Section 3.4 details the use of intersection for the negative clause to enforce conservative exclusion. The paper contrasts with unified embedding models that cannot handle set operations.  
**Break condition:** If atomic retrievals have high false positives (especially negatives), intersection may over-filter; fixed two-clause structure may be insufficient for complex logic.

### Mechanism 3: In-context transfer of planning logic
**Claim:** Golden library demonstrations enable VLM planner to replicate optimal reasoning without iterative search, generalizing abstract planning strategies.  
**Mechanism:** At inference, similar training trajectories are retrieved and provided as in-context examples, steering the VLM to generate a near-optimal tool-call plan in one pass.  
**Core assumption:** The VLM can interpret and mimic the planning logic from demonstrations, and similarity search retrieves relevant patterns.  
**Evidence anchors:** The abstract states the golden library serves as in-context demonstrations; Section 3.5 explains how the planner learns to replicate underlying reasoning by observing optimal planner behavior. The robustness with only 10% training data suggests generalization.  
**Break condition:** If the test query is dissimilar to any stored trajectory, retrieved demonstrations may be irrelevant; the VLM may fail to execute the plan correctly.

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map:** Atomic Retrieval Constructor → MIP Optimizer → Golden Library → VLM Planner → Tool Server → VLM Verifier
- **Critical path:**  
  *Offline:* For each training sample: atomic retrieval construction → Stage1 MIP (recall-oriented) → Stage2 MIP (precision-oriented) → store trajectory in Golden Library.  
  *Online:* Test query → compute problem-context embedding → retrieve top-N trajectories → VLM planner generates tool calls → execute tools via MCP → VLM verifier re-ranks → output ranked list.
- **Design tradeoffs:**  
  - Two-stage vs single-stage MIP: Stage1 reduces search space for Stage2, trading off global optimality for tractability.  
  - Fixed two-clause structure vs DNF: Simplicity and interpretability vs expressiveness (Appendix C discusses DNF).  
  - Using only 10% training data for library: Data efficiency vs coverage of query patterns; paper shows robust generalization.
- **Failure signatures:**  
  - High redundancy in tool calls (solver picks overlapping retrievals from same family).  
  - VLM planner ignores demonstrations and falls back to heuristics or incorrect tool syntax.  
  - Set difference accidentally removes ground truth due to polarity mismatch (negative query includes shared concepts).  
  - No similar trajectories retrieved for out-of-distribution queries.
- **First 3 experiments:**

  2. Ablate golden library (w/o Demo.) and set operations (w/o Set.Diff. & Set.Int.) to show their contribution (Table 4).

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
1. **Generalization to novel attribute combinations or domain shifts.** Basis: OSCAR achieves strong generalization with only 10% training data. Why unresolved: No analysis of failure by query type or OOD evaluation. Evidence needed: Ablation on crafted attribute combinations or evaluation on disjoint domains.
2. **Scaling limits of offline MIP and golden library.** Basis: MIP combinatorial explosion; library stores one trajectory per sample. Why unresolved: No runtime/memory scaling provided. Evidence needed: Scaling curves vs. tool count and library size.
3. **Dependence on atomic retrieval tool quality and potential for learned composition.** Basis: Fixed tool set; set operations are rigid. Why unresolved: No ablation of tool set or comparison to learned ensembles. Evidence needed: Experiments with degraded tool quality or learned rerankers.
4. **Robustness of polarity assignment in query decomposition.** Basis: VLM decomposes modification query into positive/negative sub-queries; case study shows polarity mismatch failures. Why unresolved: No quantification of decomposition accuracy or its impact. Evidence needed: Human evaluation of VLM-generated sub-queries; correlation with performance.
5. **Incremental update of golden library.** Basis: Library is static once built from training data. Why unresolved: No protocol for incremental updates or adaptation to distribution shift. Evidence needed: Incremental library update methods; experiments simulating time-based shift.
6. **Impact of demonstration retrieval strategy.** Basis: Demonstrations retrieved by cosine similarity on problem-context embedding. Why unresolved: Retrieval strategy itself not analyzed; possible more structural metrics. Evidence needed: Compare query-based vs. trajectory-pattern-based vs. learned retrievers.
7. **Theoretical guarantees and hyperparameter sensitivity.** Basis: MIP objectives are weighted sums; two-stage approach is heuristic. Why unresolved: No sensitivity analysis or bounds on suboptimality gap. Evidence needed: Sensitivity analysis on validation set; theoretical bounds for small instances.
8. **Fundamental limitations of single-model myopia.** Basis: Claim unified embeddings cannot handle heterogeneous intents. Why unresolved: No comparison to massive multimodal LLMs with explicit set-reasoning prompts. Evidence needed: Benchmark large VLMs (e.g., GPT-4o) with carefully designed set-reasoning prompts.
9. **False positive rate of difference operation and negative branch effectiveness.** Basis: Negative branch uses intersection to enforce conservative exclusion; case study shows ground-truth removal. Why unresolved: No precision/recall breakdown for negative branch or rate of erroneous filtering. Evidence needed: Quantify ground-trift presence in negative intersection; contribution of difference step to gains.
10. **Source of 10% training data efficiency: abstraction vs. coverage.** Basis: Using only 10% of training data still yields SOTA. Why unresolved: No analysis of diversity of the subset vs. full set; no testing of different subset strategies. Evidence needed: Measure similarity between train/test queries; correlate library coverage with performance; experiment with different subset selection methods.

## Limitations
- **MIP scalability:** Two-stage MIP may face combinatorial explosion on large training sets; no solve-time or memory scaling analysis.
- **VLM in-context dependence:** Planning relies on retrieved golden trajectories; for out-of-distribution queries, relevant demonstrations may be absent, causing failures.
- **Fixed set-operation structure:** The two-clause composition may not capture complex modification semantics (e.g., multi-clause logic), limiting expressiveness.

## Confidence
- MIP-derived optimal trajectories: **High**
- Set-theoretic composition: **Medium**
- Generalization with limited data: **Medium**
- SOTA on public benchmarks (CIRCO, CIRR, FashionIQ): **High**
