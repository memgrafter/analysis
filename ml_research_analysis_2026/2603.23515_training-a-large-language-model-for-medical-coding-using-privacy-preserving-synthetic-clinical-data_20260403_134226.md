# Training a Large Language Model for Medical Coding Using Privacy-Preserving Synthetic Clinical Data
## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID**: 2603.23515
- **Source URL**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.23515
- **Reference count**: 0
- **Primary result**: Fine-tuning Llama 3-70B on synthetic clinical notes yields exact-match F1 scores >0.70 for ICD-10-CM (0.704) and CPT (0.736).

## Executive Summary
Medical coding—assigning standardized diagnosis and procedure codes to clinical notes—is essential but labor-intensive. Zero-shot LLMs perform poorly (F1 ~0.18). This paper introduces a pipeline that generates privacy-preserving synthetic clinical notes from EHR templates and coding policies, then fine-tunes Llama 3-70B on these note-code pairs. The resulting model achieves strong exact-match F1 (ICD-10-CM: 0.704; CPT: 0.736) with high recall on complex domains and retains general medical knowledge. The approach demonstrates that synthetic, PHI-free data can enable effective automation of nuanced clinical tasks.

## Method Summary
The authors build a supervised corpus via a two-phase synthetic generator: Phase 1 creates clinically realistic notes from meta-descriptions and coding policies; Phase 2 retrieves candidate codes and evidence spans to label each note. Thirty percent of note-code pairs are concatenated to simulate multi-problem visits. Full-parameter supervised fine-tuning is applied to Llama-3.1-70B-Instruct for 4 epochs using Adam (lr=1e-5, weight decay=0.1), micro-batch size 4, ZeRO-3, and FP16 on 8× H200 GPUs. Sequence length is 8192 tokens, with packing for efficiency. Evaluation uses exact-match F1 (ICD-10 Level 3; CPT set match), domain-specific F1 (Advanced Illness, Frailty, Social Determinants of Health), human expert review, and general medical QA benchmarks.

## Key Results
- Exact-match F1: ICD-10-CM 0.704, CPT 0.736.
- High recall on complex domains: Advanced Illness (0.863), Frailty (0.873); lower on Social Determinants of Health (0.767).
- Human expert review: recall >0.86, precision as low as 0.32, indicating over-generation.
- General medical knowledge (MedMCQA, MedQA, MMLU, PubMedQA) remains largely preserved.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1: Privacy-preserving synthetic data enables effective fine-tuning without PHI exposure.
- **Claim**: A two-phase pipeline produces clinically realistic (note, code) pairs that mirror real-world distributions while eliminating protected health information.
- **Mechanism**: Synthetic clinical notes are generated from EHR-grounded templates and coding policies; semantic retrieval then links notes to gold codes with explicit evidence spans (abstract; §2.0.3).
- **Core assumption**: Synthetic notes capture sufficient statistical and structural properties of actual clinical documentation.
- **Break condition**: If the synthetic distribution lacks the noise, ambiguity, or variability of operational EHRs, real-world deployment may yield unexpected performance drops.

### Mechanism 2: Difficulty-based data augmentation improves multi-case reasoning.
- **Claim**: Concatenating independent note-code pairs forces the model to process multiple distinct encounters within one context, reflecting complex multi-problem visits.
- **Mechanism**: Thirty percent of training samples are formed by concatenating two notes, requiring the model to segment and reason across multiple cases (§2.0.5).
- **Core assumption**: Single-note training creates a distribution mismatch; real-world notes often contain multiple unrelated problems.
- **Break condition**: Over-concatenation could exceed context window capacity or introduce excessive complexity, causing confusion and performance collapse.

### Mechanism 3: Evidence-linked labeling teaches localized span prediction alongside code assignment.
- **Claim**: Requiring evidence spans during training improves exact-code accuracy and hierarchical stability by reinforcing semantic grounding.
- **Mechanism**: During labeling, candidate codes are retrieved and supporting text spans are annotated; the model learns to associate codes with specific evidence (§2.0.3; §2.0.7).
- **Core assumption**: Code assignment is not independent of evidence justification; joint learning benefits both objectives.
- **Break condition**: Noisy or inconsistent evidence attribution may lead to spurious correlations and harm generalization.

## Architecture Onboarding
### Component map
Synthetic data generator -> Data augmenter/packer -> Fine-tuning engine -> Evaluator

### Critical path
Data generation → augmentation/packing → model training → evaluation → (optional) human validation

### Design tradeoffs
- **Synthetic realism vs. privacy**: More realistic templates risk PHI leakage; overly sanitized templates may reduce utility.
- **Exact-match vs. hierarchical metrics**: Exact-match is clinically stringent but harsh; hierarchical metrics are more forgiving but may overstate readiness.
- **Full fine-tuning vs. parameter-efficient methods**: Full-parameter tuning maximizes performance but increases compute/memory; LoRA could reduce cost but may sacrifice accuracy on complex tasks.

### Failure signatures
- High recall but low precision (human evaluation finding) → over‑generation, possibly from loose decoding or training noise.
- Performance collapse on low‑frequency codes → inadequate representation or poor generalization.
- Degradation on Social Determinants of Health tasks → implicit documentation patterns not captured.
- Catastrophic forgetting on medical QA benchmarks → over‑specialization (though only modest decline observed).

### First 3 experiments
1. **Baseline replication**: Run zero‑shot evaluation on the held‑out synthetic set to confirm F1 ≈ 0.18.
2. **Smoke test**: Train on 0.1% of the synthetic data (~100 samples) to verify pipeline integrity and observe early learning curves.
3. **Full validation**: Train on the full synthetic corpus and evaluate exact‑match F1; must exceed 0.70 to align with reported results.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
1. **How well does model performance on synthetic data translate to real‑world EHR environments?**  
   *Basis in paper*: Authors note synthetic data may not capture the full variability, noise, and idiosyncratic practices of operational EHRs; synthetic data is a risk‑mitigation and early‑validation strategy, not a substitute for real‑data validation.  
   *Why unresolved*: Metrics derive from a controlled synthetic distribution; no testing on diverse, real‑world records has been done.  
   *What evidence would resolve it*: A prospective or retrospective evaluation on a held‑out set of de‑identified, real clinical notes with gold‑standard codes, comparing performance to the synthetic benchmark and analyzing failure modes specific to real‑world noise.

2. **Can grounding the model's output in structured CPT code descriptions or authoritative catalogs systematically improve exact code matching and reduce semantic mismatch errors?**  
   *Basis in paper*: The discussion states that "Grounding model outputs in structured code descriptions or authoritative catalogs may be particularly important for procedural coding, where small lexical differences can correspond to materially different billing outcomes" and calls for systematic evaluation.  
   *Why unresolved*: The current approach uses retrieval only during synthetic data generation; the inference‑time model is not explicitly constrained by a catalog. The relative benefit of retrieval‑augmented or constrained decoding for CPT is not quantified.  
   *What evidence would resolve it*: An ablation study comparing the fine‑tuned model against a version using retrieval‑augmented generation (RAG) or constrained decoding that selects from a valid CPT code database during inference.

3. **What specific architectural, training, or decoding modifications are most effective at improving precision (reducing over‑generation) without severely compromising the high recall already achieved?**  
   *Basis in paper*: Human expert evaluation found strong recall (>0.86) but lower precision (down to 0.32), indicating a tendency to over‑generate codes; authors state future refinement should prioritize improving precision to reduce review burden.  
   *Why unresolved*: The paper does not investigate methods to address the precision‑recall trade‑off; the cause of over‑generation (e.g., loss function, data distribution, decoding strategy) is not analyzed.  
   *What evidence would resolve it*: Experiments varying decoding parameters (temperature, top‑p), adding a post‑hoc re‑ranker/validator, or modifying the training objective (e.g., contrastive learning, custom loss penalizing false positives) to measure impact on the precision‑recall curve, especially on the expert‑validated subset.

4. **How frequently and by what magnitude does model performance degrade as clinical coding policies and code sets evolve, and what is the minimal maintenance regimen to sustain performance?**  
   *Basis in paper*: Authors note that continuous validation may be necessary as coding guidelines change and that synthetic data generation must account for "evolving ICD terminology."  
   *Why unresolved*: The study evaluates on a fixed snapshot of synthetic data aligned with a specific code catalog version; no test of temporal generalizability or performance decay with updated coding rules is performed.  
   *What evidence would resolve it*: A time‑split evaluation where the model is trained on data from one coding year/version and evaluated on data from a subsequent year/version, quantifying performance drops and testing the efficacy of lightweight, policy‑focused fine‑tuning updates using new synthetic data.

## Limitations
- **Synthetic data fidelity**: Unclear if generated notes capture real EHR noise, ambiguity, and multi‑document variability; risk of unexpected performance drop on operational data.
- **Precision‑recall trade‑off**: High recall with low precision suggests over‑generation, which may increase clinician review burden; root cause not identified.
- **External validity**: Claims rely on proprietary synthetic corpus and ArcticTraining framework; no open replication or cross‑dataset validation.

## Confidence
- **Synthetic data efficacy**: Medium (pipeline described internally but no external benchmarks for synthetic clinical realism).
- **Difficulty‑based augmentation**: Low (evidence limited to paper’s own description; no ablation or neighboring support).
- **Evidence‑linked labeling**: Medium (mechanism detailed in §2.0.3 but no independent validation of span‑code alignment quality).

## Next Checks
1. **Dataset fidelity audit**: Statistically compare synthetic note distributions (code frequencies, note length, section variance) against a held‑out real EHR sample using KS‑tests and embedding similarity.
2. **Component ablation**: Retrain without concatenation augmentation and without evidence‑linked labeling to quantify each mechanism’s contribution to F1, especially on Social Determinants of Health and low‑frequency codes.
3. **Real‑world generalization test**: Deploy the fine‑tuned model on a small manually labeled set of de‑identified clinical notes (not synthetic) to measure exact‑match F1 and precision/recall gap; compare to synthetic test performance.
