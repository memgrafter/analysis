# Rewards as Labels: Revisiting RLVR from a Classification Perspective

## Quick Facts
* arXiv ID: 2602.05630
* Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.05630
* Reference count: 22
* Primary result: REAL framework recasts RLVR as classification, achieving up to +6.7% Pass@1 improvement over DAPO on 1.5B models and stable training without KL penalties.

## Executive Summary
Reinforcement Learning with Verifiable Rewards (RLVR) methods like GRPO are effective for improving LLM reasoning but suffer from gradient misassignment (positives) and gradient domination (negatives), leading to inefficient credit assignment. This paper proposes REAL, a novel framework that treats verifiable rewards as categorical labels, transforming policy optimization into a classification problem. REAL computes a length-normalized relative log-probability as logits and optimizes a unified softmax objective with a fixed anchor logit at zero, ensuring bounded and monotonic gradients. Experiments on mathematical reasoning benchmarks show consistent improvements: for the 1.5B model, REAL achieves an average Pass@1 of 52.6%, surpassing DAPO by 6.7%. For the 7B model, REAL exceeds DAPO by 6.2% and GSPO by 1.7%. Notably, REAL trains stably without KL penalties, and even a vanilla binary cross-entropy variant outperforms DAPO by 4.5%.

## Method Summary
REAL redefines RLVR by framing the policy update as a classification task where the reward serves as the label. For each prompt, multiple rollouts are sampled from the old policy π_old. The responses are partitioned into positive (reward=1) and negative (reward=0) sets. For each token, the current policy π_θ provides a log-probability relative to π_old, which is length-normalized across the entire response to produce a scalar logit ˜s (Equation 5). These logits are fed into a softmax-based loss that contrasts positives against a fixed anchor logit (0) and negatives against the same anchor, effectively a cross-entropy loss for two classes. A temperature τ=0.5 scales logits to bound gradients. The aggregated loss is averaged across tokens and mini-batch, then optimized with AdamW. This approach eliminates explicit advantage estimation and KL penalties, yet remains stable and simple. The method's key innovation is replacing group-relative advantage normalization with classification-oriented relative log-probabilities anchored at zero.

## Key Results
* For the 1.5B model, REAL achieves average Pass@1 of 52.6%, surpassing DAPO by 6.7%.
* On the 7B model, REAL exceeds DAPO by 6.2% and GSPO by 1.7%.
* A vanilla BCE variant of REAL still beats DAPO by 4.5%, underscoring robustness to loss choice.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
REAL addresses two fundamental flaws in GRPO-style methods. First, gradient misassignment occurs when multiple positive rollouts share high-probability tokens; the group-relative advantage incorrectly amplifies gradients for tokens that are actually favorable, misattributing credit. REAL's classification view treats each rollout independently: tokens in positive responses are encouraged relative to the anchor, while negative tokens are pushed away, eliminating misassignment across positives. Second, gradient domination arises because GRPO's advantage normalization can produce large magnitudes for negative samples, overwhelming the positive signal. REAL's softmax objective with bounded gradients (by 1/τ) ensures that negatives do not dominate. The anchor logit at zero provides a fixed reference, making the optimization direction unambiguous. Length normalization balances contributions across responses of different lengths, preventing bias toward longer trajectories. Collectively, these design choices yield monotonic, stable updates without KL constraints, as verified by the authors' ablations.

## Architecture Onboarding
Component map:  
Sampler -> Rollouts (π_old) -> Reward Assignment (verifier) -> Logit Computation (π_θ vs π_old) -> Length-Normalized Relative Log-Probability (˜s) -> REAL Loss (softmax with anchor 0, temperature τ) -> Optimizer (AdamW) -> Policy Update (π_θ) -> Old Policy Update (π_old ← π_θ)

Critical path: The training loop for a batch: (1) Sample G rollouts per prompt from π_old; (2) Partition into O+ and O- based on rewards; (3) For each rollout token, compute log-prob difference between current policy and old policy; (4) Sum diffs across tokens, divide by length to get ˜s; (5) Compute loss LREAL = LCE(S+, 0) + LCE(0, S-), where S+ are ˜s from positives; (6) Backpropagate and update π_θ; (7) Periodically set π_old = π_θ.

Design tradeoffs:
- Anchor choice: Fixed zero simplifies compared to learnable anchors and stabilizes gradient direction.
- Loss choice: Softmax vs BCE; BCE is simpler but softmax may better handle multiple positives/negatives.
- Temperature τ: Bounds gradients but too small causes instability; 0.5 offered a good tradeoff.
- KL penalty: REAL does not need one; baselines use KL to prevent deviation from reference, which adds hyperparameters.

Failure signatures:
- Gradients exploding if τ is too low (e.g., 0.1) leading to training collapse.
- High variance in Pass@1 if evaluation sampling insufficient or verifier inconsistent.
- Poor performance if anchor logits omitted; loss may not provide a clear decision boundary.
- Baseline mismatch if advantage normalization in GRPO is incorrectly implemented (e.g., sequence-level instead of token-level).

First experiments:
1. Replicate GRPO on the DeepScaleR training set using reported hyperparameters (β=0.001, group size G=8, etc.) to obtain baseline Pass@1 scores on AIME24, MATH500, etc. This ensures setup correctness before REAL modification.
2. Implement REAL loss exactly as in Equation 8: compute ˜s, then apply softmax cross-entropy with anchor 0 and τ=0.5. Verify on a synthetic mini-batch that the gradient magnitudes are bounded by 1/τ and that the loss decreases with a simple optimizer step.
3. Ablate the anchor: train REAL without the anchor (i.e., only encouraging positives over negatives directly) and compare Pass@1 and training stability to the anchored version; also test τ=0.1 to observe collapse.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
Open Question 1: Does REAL generalize to tasks with continuous or multi-valued rewards?
* Basis in paper: [inferred] The framework assumes binary {0,1} rewards as categorical labels and does not discuss handling of finer-grained reward signals.
* Why unresolved: Many verifiable tasks yield partial or graded rewards; it is unclear how REAL's classification objective would adapt without major changes.
* What evidence would resolve it: A theoretical extension to multi-class or ordinal rewards (e.g., via multiple anchor logits or a tailored loss) and empirical validation on a benchmark with graded rewards, showing improvement over GRPO variants.

Open Question 2: How critical is the temperature hyperparameter τ and can it be adaptively tuned during training?
* Basis in paper: [explicit] The authors set τ=0.5 after limited tuning, noting that gradient magnitude is bounded by 1/τ and that τ=0.1 caused collapse; they do not explore adaptive strategies.
* Why unresolved: τ directly influences gradient bounds; its optimal value may vary with model scale, dataset, or training stage. A fixed τ requires manual tuning and may be suboptimal.
* What evidence would resolve it: Derive a principle for scheduling τ (e.g., based on gradient statistics or entropy) and demonstrate that an adaptive τ maintains stability while boosting performance across diverse settings.

Open Question 3: Are the zero anchor logits essential, or could alternative anchoring schemes further improve REAL?
* Basis in paper: [explicit] REAL employs a fixed anchor logit at 0 to provide a reference. The paper reports a slight gain over removing the anchor but does not investigate other anchor choices or learnable anchors.
* Why unresolved: The anchor sets the decision threshold; different forms (e.g., non-zero, learned, or multiple anchors) could yield better gradient modulation and classification separation.
* What evidence would resolve it: Systematic ablation with anchors at varied values, learnable anchors, and multiple anchors; evaluate their impact on training stability and final accuracy across multiple benchmarks.

## Limitations
* Missing specification of the reference policy π_ref used in KL comparisons, which affects reproducibility of ablations that include KL.
* Unclear details of rollout sampling and π_old update schedule (per-step vs per-epoch), crucial for correct gradient estimation.
* Lack of explicit verifier implementations for evaluation benchmarks, making exact reward assignment (r=1) difficult to replicate.
