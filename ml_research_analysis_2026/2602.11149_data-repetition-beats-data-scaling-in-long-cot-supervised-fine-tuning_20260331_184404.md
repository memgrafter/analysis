# Data Repetition Beats Data Scaling in Long-CoT Supervised Fine-Tuning
## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2602.11149  
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.11149  
- **Reference count:** 40  
- **Primary result:** For a fixed gradient update budget, training more epochs on a smaller set of long-CoT data substantially outperforms single-epoch training on a larger set, improving accuracy by up to 26 percentage points on AIME’24/25 and GPQA.

## Executive Summary
The paper challenges the conventional wisdom that more unique training samples yield better generalization in supervised fine-tuning (SFT). Focusing on long chain-of-thought (CoT) reasoning data, the authors systematically vary the number of training epochs against the number of unique samples while keeping total gradient updates constant. They find that multi-epoch training on a modest dataset consistently delivers far superior performance across model architectures (Olmo3-7B, Qwen3-4B/8B) and even when training includes incorrect reasoning traces. Performance gains plateau when the model achieves near-perfect token-level accuracy on the training set, making training token accuracy a practical stopping criterion. Despite classical signs of overfitting (rising validation loss), downstream reasoning accuracy improves monotonically with epochs. The results also show less catastrophic forgetting on the MMLU knowledge benchmark compared to data scaling. The findings provide a simple, compute-efficient heuristic for reasoning SFT: scale epochs on a small, high-quality dataset until token accuracy saturates.

## Method Summary
The experiments use a fixed total number of gradient updates (e.g., 51,200) and vary two quantities: the number of unique training samples and the number of epochs, such that epochs × samples = constant. Models are fine-tuned from pretrained checkpoints on a long-CoT dataset (e.g., Dolci) with batch size 1 and a cosine learning-rate schedule. The learning rate is first tuned for a reference configuration (1 epoch on 51,200 samples) and then reused for all other epoch–sample combinations to isolate the effect of repetition. Performance is evaluated on math and science reasoning benchmarks (AIME ’24/’25, GPQA) using pass@n and accuracy. Additional analyses track training token accuracy, termination rate (fraction of responses ending with a final answer in \boxed{}), validation loss, and prediction entropy.

## Key Results
- Multi-epoch on small data vastly outperforms single-epoch on large data: Olmo3-7B with 128 epochs on 400 samples beats 1 epoch on 51,200 samples by 12–26 percentage points on AIME/GPQA.
- The advantage holds across model architectures (Olmo3-7B, Qwen3-4B/8B), different teacher model sizes, and even when the training set includes incorrect reasoning traces.
- Downstream accuracy improves monotonically with epochs, and plateaus only when the model approaches perfect token-level accuracy on the training set; this token accuracy reliably signals saturation.
- Despite rising validation loss (a classic overfitting sign), reasoning accuracy increases, and entropy on the validation set decreases.
- Compared to scaling data, epoch scaling results in less catastrophic forgetting on the MMLU benchmark.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The paper does not pinpoint a single causal mechanism but presents three plausible interpretations of the repetition advantage.

### Mechanism 1: Memorization as a Structural Convergence Signal
**Claim:** Near-perfect token-level memorization on the training set is necessary for the model to internalize the full reasoning structure, including termination conventions. The plateau in performance when token accuracy reaches ~100% suggests memorization marks saturation.
**Mechanism:** Repeating the same data forces the model to fit training tokens exactly, which coincides with learning latent structural patterns (e.g., `, backtracking, final answer formatting). The model may need to see identical reasoning patterns many times to stabilize its internal computation for generating extended coherent thoughts.
**Core assumption:** The training demonstrations contain a consistent structural template that is captured only when the model achieves high token accuracy.
**Evidence anchors:**
- [abstract] "We find that training token accuracy reliably signals when repetition has saturated; improvements from additional epochs plateau at full memorization."
- [section] "Figure 4 plots this metric against downstream accuracy... performance improvements plateau once models approach full memorization."
- [corpus] Neighbor *LLMs Can Easily Learn to Reason from Demonstrations Structure, not content, is what matters!* (arxiv:2502.07374) independently finds that CoT structure dominates content, supporting the idea that repetition helps capture structural patterns.
**Break condition:** If a model reaches 100% token accuracy but performance continues to improve, then memorization alone is not the limiting factor.

### Mechanism 2: Termination Behavior Elicitation
**Claim:** A major bottleneck is the model’s inability to reliably produce termination signals (e.g., ` followed by a boxed answer). Multi-epoch training directly improves termination rate, which correlates strongly with measured accuracy.
**Mechanism:** The training data includes explicit termination tokens. With few epochs, the model may generate reasoning traces but fail to consistently conclude with a valid final answer. Repetition teaches the precise contextual and positional conditions for issuing termination tokens, turning more generations into evaluatable responses.
**Core assumption:** Evaluation requires a final answer in \boxed{}; without proper termination, the response counts as failure. Thus, improving termination rate raises the ceiling of measurable accuracy.
**Evidence anchors:**
- [abstract] "Termination rate correlates strongly with accuracy and may be a primary driver of performance gains, as models that fail to terminate cannot produce a final answer."
- [section] "Figure 2 presents heatmaps... termination rate... strongly correlate with accuracy."
- [corpus] No direct neighbor evidence; this mechanism is inferred from the paper’s own data.
**Break condition:** If forcing termination post-hoc (e.g., always appending \boxed{}) eliminates the performance gap between epoch-scaled and data-scaled models, termination behavior is the key factor.

### Mechanism 3: Latent Capability Elicitation via Entropy Minimization
**Claim:** Multi-epoch training reduces predictive entropy on the validation distribution, creating more confident and deterministic reasoning traces. This entropy drop—despite rising validation loss—may elicit latent reasoning capabilities already present in the pretrained model rather than teaching new skills.
**Mechanism:** As epochs increase, the model becomes more confident (lower entropy) on validation predictions but those predictions diverge from the target distribution (higher loss). This paradox suggests the model is sharpening its reasoning policy, amplifying signal from pretraining that transfers to held-out benchmarks.
**Core assumption:** The pretrained base model already contains the necessary reasoning abilities; SFT with repetition "focuses" or "unlocks" them by reducing output entropy.
**Evidence anchors:**
- [abstract] "Despite classical overfitting signs (rising validation loss), downstream accuracy improves monotonically with epoch count."
- [section] "Figure 5... prediction entropy also decreases, showing increased model confidence in predictions that diverge from the validation distribution."
- [corpus] Neighbor *Thinking Preference Optimization* (arxiv:2502.13173) discusses preference-based elicitation for reasoning, conceptually close to entropy-driven focus.
**Break condition:** If randomly initialized (non-pretrained) models show the same repetition advantage, then the effect is not tied to eliciting pretrained capabilities.

## Foundational Learning
- **Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT):** Continued training on labeled data to adapt a model, typically minimizing cross-entropy on the response tokens while masking the prompt.  
  *Why needed:* The entire study revolves around SFT on long CoT; understanding the loss function and what is optimized is essential.  
  *Quick check:* What is the standard loss used in SFT, and which part of the input-output pair is usually ignored?

- **Chain-of-Thought (CoT) Reasoning:** A technique where models generate intermediate reasoning steps before producing a final answer. Long CoT involves thousands of tokens and explicit tags like `<think>` and `<\think>`.  
  *Why needed:* The repetition effect is specific to long CoT; short CoT or direct answer formats may behave differently.  
  *Quick check:* How are reasoning traces typically demarcated in the training data?

- **Gradient Update Budget:** Total number of parameter updates during training, equal to epochs × dataset size (for batch size 1).  
  *Why needed:* The core experimental design fixes the update budget to disentangle the effect of repetition from total optimization steps.  
  *Quick check:* If you train for 10 epochs on 5,000 samples with batch size 1, what is the update budget?

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component map:**  
Base pretrained model (e.g., Olmo3-7B) → SFT dataset (long CoT with `<think>`/`<\think>`) → Training loop (fixed total updates, constant LR per model, batch size 1, cosine schedule with 10% warmup) → Validation set (token accuracy, loss, entropy) → Evaluation suite (AIME, GPQA) → Metrics (Acc@n, termination rate).

**Critical path:**  
1. Choose base model and SFT dataset (e.g., Dolci).  
2. Create nested data splits (200, 400, 800, ..., 51,200) where each split is a subset of larger ones.  
3. For each split, set epochs = total_updates / split_size (e.g., B=51,200 → 128 epochs for 400 samples).  
4. Train separate models from the same base checkpoint for each configuration.  
5. Evaluate each model on reasoning benchmarks and compute termination rate.  
6. Plot performance versus epochs (or token accuracy) to locate the saturation point.

**Design tradeoffs:**  
- **Update budget vs. real compute:** Small datasets with many epochs reduce I/O overhead but increase gradient steps; the total compute is roughly similar but may vary due to caching.  
- **Learning rate reuse:** The paper sweeps LR only for the 1-epoch/51k configuration and reuses it. This may be suboptimal for other epoch-data pairs; a per-configuration sweep could reveal larger gains.  
- **Stopping criterion:** Token accuracy on a small training subset is cheap and early, but requires periodic evaluation. Using a held-out CoT set is more directly tied to performance but is costlier.  
- **Data quality:** The repetition advantage holds even with negative trajectories, but teacher quality influences absolute performance and potential degradation with excessive epochs.

**Failure signatures:**  
- Token accuracy remains substantially below 100% after the expected plateau: model not converged; more epochs likely still help.  
- Termination rate does not increase with epochs: the model is not learning structural conventions; verify termination tokens are present and consistent.  
- Validation loss decreases (or stays flat) instead of rising: model may be underfitting or the dataset is too large relative to epochs; repetition advantage may be less pronounced.  
- Accuracy declines before token accuracy reaches 100%: possible overfitting to noise; consider earlier stopping.

**First 3 experiments:**  
1. Reproduce the main diagonal: Train Olmo3-7B on Dolci with B=51,200. Compare 1 epoch on 51,200 samples vs. 16 epochs on 3,200 samples. Expect >12 pp improvement on AIME/GPQA for the multi-epoch model.  
2. Measure token accuracy vs. epochs: For the two models above, compute token accuracy on a fixed 200-sample training subset every few epochs. Expect the multi-epoch model to approach 100% while the single-epoch model stays lower.  
3. Measure termination rate: Generate responses on a small AIME problem set for the same models; count fraction ending with a final answer in \boxed{} after ≤30k tokens. Expect a much higher termination rate for the multi-epoch model.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
### Open Question 1
- **Question:** Why does full memorization in long-CoT SFT improve downstream reasoning accuracy?
- **Basis in paper:** Abstract and Conclusion explicitly pose the "repetition advantage" as an open problem: the coincidence of memorization and generalization contradicts classical views.
- **Why unresolved:** Only correlation, not causation, is shown; no mechanistic explanation exists.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Ablations that separate memorization from reasoning gains (e.g., force memorization without improving termination) or probing studies to determine if repetition elicits latent skills or teaches new patterns.

### Open Question 2
- **Question:** How can we determine the optimal dataset size and number of epochs a priori for repetition-based SFT?
- **Basis in paper:** Conclusion states that the optimal dataset size is data- and model-dependent and that principled a priori criteria are lacking.
- **Why unresolved:** The current heuristic (token accuracy saturation) requires training; no predictor exists beforehand.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Systematic studies linking dataset properties (diversity, difficulty) to optimal repetition, or development of early-training metrics that forecast the best trade-off.

### Open Question 3
- **Question:** Why does training on incorrect reasoning traces (negative trajectories) fail to degrade and sometimes improve downstream performance?
- **Basis in paper:** Section 3.2 shows empirically that negatives match or exceed positives; authors offer speculation but no explanation.
- **Why unresolved:** Intuition suggests learning from wrong answers should be harmful; the benefit implies an unknown mechanism (e.g., exposure to harder problems).
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Controlled experiments varying the nature of negatives (e.g., from easy vs. hard problems) and measuring reasoning gains, or analysis of whether negatives encourage robustness or error detection.

## Limitations
- Findings are limited to math/science benchmarks, specific architectures (Olmo3, Qwen3), and teacher-generated long CoT data; generalizability to other domains or human-written CoT is untested.
- Learning rate was held constant across all epoch–data splits; optimal values may differ and could confound observed trends.
- Token accuracy plateau is a strong correlation but not proven causal; other latent factors may drive saturation.
- The role of termination behavior, while correlated, is not established as the primary mechanism; further ablation is needed.

## Confidence
- Repetition advantage under fixed update budget: **High** (consistent across multiple models and benchmarks).  
- Token accuracy plateau as stopping criterion: **Medium** (observed correlation, causality unproven).  
- Termination rate increase as primary driver: **Medium** (strong correlation, may be symptomatic).  
- Entropy reduction as capability elicitation: **Low** (speculative interpretation).  
- Cross-domain generalizability: **Low** (no evidence yet).

## Next Checks
1. Test the repetition advantage on non-math long-CoT tasks (e.g., commonsense reasoning, code generation) to evaluate domain portability.
2. Force termination in generated outputs (e.g., by appending `\boxed{}`) and re-evaluate; if the epoch–data gap disappears, termination behavior is the key factor.
3. Repeat the experiment with randomly initialized (non-pretrained) models to determine whether the advantage persists without latent capabilities, directly probing the elicitation hypothesis.
