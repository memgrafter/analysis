# On Multi-Step Theorem Prediction via Non-Parametric Structural Priors

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2603.04852
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.04852
- **Reference count:** 30
- **Primary result:** Pri-TPG achieves 89.29% accuracy on FormalGeo7k, massively outperforming Vanilla ICL (26.29%) and matching supervised SOTA, while robustly handling increasing reasoning depth (L1–L6).

## Executive Summary
The paper tackles *structural drift* in multi-step theorem prediction, where vanilla in-context learning (ICL) fails as reasoning depth increases due to an unpruned, vast theorem search space. The proposed training-free framework, Pri-TPG, constructs query-adaptive Theorem Precedence Graphs (TPGs) from historical solution traces. These graphs encode observed theorem dependencies and are used alongside a symbolic executor to impose state-aware topological constraints. This iterative, closed-loop system narrows the candidate set at each step, transforming an unstructured classification into a guided traversal. On the FormalGeo7k benchmark, Pri-TPG achieves near-SOTA performance without any task-specific training, demonstrating the effectiveness of non-parametric structural priors for deep geometric reasoning.

## Method Summary
Pri-TPG is a training-free, retrieval-augmented system for multi-step theorem prediction. For a given query problem, it first retrieves the top-K most similar solved problems from a database using a multimodal encoder. The solution traces of these retrieved problems are aggregated into a weighted Theorem Precedence Graph (TPG), where edges (u→v) indicate that theorem *u*'s conclusion provides a necessary prerequisite for applying theorem *v*. During iterative proof search, a symbolic executor maintains the current formal state. At each step, the candidate theorem library is pruned in two ways: (1) only theorems whose symbolic preconditions are satisfied by the current state are kept, and (2) only descendants of the previously applied theorem in the TPG are retained. The remaining candidates are scored using a heuristic combining goal alignment, graph-based successor promotion, and a penalty for recent failures. The top-scoring candidates are presented to an LLM, which selects one for execution. This loop repeats until the goal is proven or a step limit is reached, with a recovery mechanism for handling无效 selections.

## Key Results
- Pri-TPG achieves 89.29% overall accuracy on FormalGeo7k, dramatically surpassing Vanilla ICL (26.29%) and a retrieval-only baseline (w/o TPG, 63.17%).
- Performance degrades minimally with increased reasoning depth, maintaining high accuracy from L1 to L6, while Vanilla ICL collapses at deeper levels.
- The framework is LLM-agnostic; it delivers consistent, significant gains over respective ICL baselines when using multiple strong backbones (GPT-5 mini, DeepSeek, Claude, Gemini).

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
**Claim:** Pri-TPG mitigates *structural drift* by imposing explicit topological constraints from Theorem Precedence Graphs (TPGs) that encode latent dependency patterns.
- **Mechanism:** Historical solution traces are aggregated into a directed graph where edges represent empirically observed theorem precedence (u→v if u’s conclusion enables v). At inference, the graph is localized to the current node’s descendants, converting an unstructured classification over the entire library into a constrained selection over a small, valid subset.
- **Core assumption:** Theorem dependencies are relatively stable across problems within a domain and can be approximated from past solved instances.
- **Evidence anchors:** Abstract: "TPGs encode temporal dependencies... impose explicit topological constraints that effectively prune the search space." Section 3.2: "Instead of casting theorem selection as an unstructured classification problem, the TPG imposes a topological prior that converts search into a guided traversal."
- **Break condition:** Retrieved traces are mismatched to the query’s structural needs, causing the TPG to omit critical theorems or include spurious dependencies.

### Mechanism 2
**Claim:** Query-adaptive retrieval tailors the structural prior to the specific problem, preventing a one-size-fits-all graph from introducing noise.
- **Mechanism:** A multimodal encoder maps the query to an embedding; nearest-neighbor search retrieves top-K analogous solved problems. Their individual TPGs are merged into a weighted, query-specific graph Gq, with edge weights reflecting frequency across retrieved instances.
- **Core assumption:** Semantically similar geometry problems share relevant theorem precedence patterns.
- **Evidence anchors:** Section 3.3 describes the multimodal encoder and merging process. Section 4.4 shows coverage metrics increase with K, indicating retrieval brings necessary theorems into the pool.
- **Break condition:** Embedding space fails to capture geometric similarity (e.g., same goal but different configuration), leading to retrieval of semantically near but structurally irrelevant problems.

### Mechanism 3
**Claim:** Iterative symbolic validation creates a *state-aware prior* that dynamically refines the search space based on the evolving formal state, preventing combinatorial blow-up.
- **Mechanism:** After each theorem application, the symbolic executor (1) filters candidates by checking preconditions against current state, and (2) localizes the TPG to descendants of the last applied theorem. A composite score (goal alignment, graph successor promotion, history penalty) prioritizes the list for the LLM.
- **Core assumption:** The symbolic executor is sound and complete: it correctly accepts theorems whose preconditions are satisfied and rejects those that are not.
- **Evidence anchors:** Section 3.4 details symbolic pruning and structural localization. Section 4.4 ablation shows single-pass (no iterative feedback) collapses to 34.3% overall and 0% on Hard.
- **Break condition:** Executor has gaps (missing checks, incomplete implementations), leading to valid theorems being pruned or invalid ones retained.

## Foundational Learning

- **Concept: Symbolic Execution & Stateful Proof State**
  - **Why needed:** The entire pruning and localization depends on a verifier that can update a formal state and check theorem preconditions. Without a correct executor, the state-aware prior is meaningless.
  - **Quick check:** Given state S and theorem T with prerequisites Pre(T), what algorithmic steps decide if T is applicable? (Check Pre(T) ⊆ S; if true, find variable bindings satisfying all predicates and apply conclusion to update S.)

- **Concept: In-Context Learning (ICL) and Its Failure Modes**
  - **Why needed:** The paper’s motivation is the failure of vanilla ICL (structural drift). Understanding why ICL fails—e.g., uniform action distributions over large libraries, error accumulation—clarifies the need for structural priors.
  - **Quick check:** Why might an LLM’s action distribution over a large theorem library become near-uniform when predicting the next step in a long proof? (The model lacks explicit state awareness and depth tracking; after many steps, the prompt becomes noisy, and the model sees many equally plausible but contextually invalid theorems.)

- **Concept: Graph-Based Retrieval and Topological Ordering**
  - **Why needed:** The TPG is a directed acyclic graph (DAG) capturing precedence; retrieval merges multiple DAGs. Understanding graph traversal and topological sorts is key to seeing how the prior constrains sequences.
  - **Quick check:** In a TPG, if node A has edges to B and C, and B has an edge to D, what theorems are immediate successors of A? (B and C.) What are descendants of A? (B, C, and D.)

## Architecture Onboarding

- **Component map:**
  Multimodal Encoder (ϕ) -> Retrieval Module (kNN over B) -> TPG Constructor (merge TPGs -> Gq, add START) -> Iterative Loop: Symbolic Executor (maintains St) -> State-Aware Filter (Lq -> Lqt via preconditions & Gq localization) -> Candidate Scorer (Ψ(v) = α·s_goal + β·s_graph - γ·s_hist) -> Planner (LLM selects from top-M) -> Execute (update state, set prev) -> Goal check.

- **Critical path:** Problem -> retrieve neighbors -> build Gq -> for t=1 to T_max: (filter & localize -> score & rank -> LLM pick -> execute -> update state -> check goal).

- **Design tradeoffs:**
  - **Retrieval size (K):** Larger K improves theorem coverage but adds noise and compute; paper shows monotonic gain up to saturation (~200).
  - **Candidate window (M):** Smaller window reduces cost/context length but risks dropping valid actions; prioritization scoring aims to keep high-potential theorems within window.
  - **Scoring weights (α=5, β=10, γ=5):** Heuristic, not learned. Over-reliance on graph score may cause myopic local moves; goal score prevents drifting from target.

- **Failure signatures:**
  - **Vanilla ICL collapse:** Accuracy drops to near-zero at L4+ (depth ≥7 theorems), indicating unpruned search space.
  - **Recovery mode activation:** Frequent "recent failed" additions suggest executor rejecting many candidate theorems, possibly due to noisy TPG or weak retrieval.
  - **Low theorem coverage:** Even at high K, if ground-truth theorems are absent from all retrieved TPGs, proof is impossible regardless of LLM quality.

- **First 3 experiments:**
  1. **Ablation: Single-pass vs. Iterative** – Remove symbolic feedback loop; show catastrophic drop on Hard problems, proving iterative validation essential.
  2. **Retrieval size sweep (K)** – Vary K=15,30,100,200; measure coverage (C_prob, C_thm) and accuracy; confirm recall effect for harder problems.
  3. **Backbone robustness** – Swap LLM (DeepSeek, GPT-5 mini, Claude, Gemini) while keeping framework fixed; show consistent gains over respective baselines, confirming plug-and-play nature.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- **How can Theorem Precedence Graphs be modified to incorporate global reasoning depth, thereby improving performance on the hardest problems (L6)?** The current TPG encodes local precedence rather than global depth. Incorporating depth annotations (e.g., expected remaining steps) into nodes or edges is a key future direction.
- **Can the computational cost of the stepwise LLM-solver loop be reduced without compromising accuracy?** Repeated LLM calls dominate computation and limit scalability. A batch prediction variant where the LLM outputs multiple candidate theorems per call could reduce the O(T) cost.
- **How does Pri-TPG handle the scenario where a problem requires a theorem absent from the retrieved historical solutions?** The candidate pool L_q is built from retrieved solutions. If a required theorem is missing, it will never be considered, limiting generalizability to novel or niche problems.

## Limitations
- TPG edge extraction from solution traces is described vaguely; precise algorithm choices may affect graph quality and are not fully detailed.
- Results rely on unavailable GPT-5 models, hindering direct replication with current accessible models.
- Heuristic components (goal-type detection, two-hop scoring formula) introduce implementation variance not fully analyzed.

## Confidence
- **C1 (TPG mitigates structural drift):** *Medium*. Strong benchmark results, but domain-specific and graph construction details are incomplete.
- **C2 (Query-adaptive retrieval):** *Medium*. Coverage gains shown, but no guarantee of structural relevance; semantic similarity may mislead.
- **C3 (Iterative symbolic validation):** *High*. Ablation demonstrates necessity; depends on executor correctness.
- **C4 (Parity with supervised SOTA):** *Medium*. Benchmark-limited; generalization untested.

## Next Checks
1. **Edge extraction sensitivity:** Implement alternative TPG construction methods (e.g., exact predicate match vs. embedding similarity) and measure impact on accuracy and graph density.
2. **Open-source model replication:** Substitute GPT-5 with accessible models like Llama 3.1 70B or Mixtral; isolate framework efficacy from proprietary model advantages.
