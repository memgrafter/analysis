# CRUNet-MR-Univ: A Foundation Model for Diverse Cardiac MRI Reconstruction

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID**: 2601.04428  
- **Source URL**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.04428  
- **Reference count**: 27  
- **Primary result**: Achieved PSNR 28.23 and SSIM 0.809 on center-cropped validation (S2), significantly outperforming CRNN-MRI (PSNR 25.90, SSIM 0.730).

## Executive Summary
Cardiac MRI reconstruction models often fail to generalize across real-world variations such as scanner vendors, contrasts, and sampling patterns. This paper introduces CRUNet-MR-Univ, a foundation model that combines a spatio-temporal convolutional recurrent network (CRUNet) with learnable and text-based prompt priors to encode scan attributes. Trained on the multi-center CMRxRecon2025 dataset, the model demonstrates substantial improvements over prior art, with consistent gains across vendors and field strengths in multi-center evaluation. The proposed design addresses key limitations in temporal modeling, attribute conditioning, and feature propagation within unrolled reconstruction networks.

## Method Summary
CRUNet-MR-Univ employs an unrolled architecture with 12 cascade blocks. Each block contains a CRUNet—a two-level U-Net where a bidirectional convolutional recurrent unit (BCRNNTI) is split into forward (CRNNTI-F) and backward (CRNNTI-B) passes placed in the encoder and decoder, respectively—to enable continuous spatio-temporal feature extraction. The network is conditioned via prompt modules: text prompts (encoded with Bio-Clinical BERT) and learnable spatial prompts are fused with image features using FiLM blocks to dynamically modulate representations. A Cascaded Feature Aggregation (CFA) block buffers and aggregates all prior cascade features to mitigate information degradation. Training proceeds in three stages: S1 (6 cascades, 60 epochs), S2 (12 cascades, curriculum learning on acceleration factors, 5-frame inputs), and S3 (increased iterations per epoch). The loss combines L1, MSE, and SSIM image losses with classification cross-entropy on prompt embeddings.

## Key Results
- Second-stage training (S2) achieved PSNR 28.23 and SSIM 0.809 on center-cropped validation, compared to CRNN-MRI (PSNR 25.90, SSIM 0.730).
- Third-stage training (S3) with more iterations per epoch further improved to PSNR 28.53 and SSIM 0.820.
- Multi-center evaluation showed consistent performance gains across all scanner vendors (GE, Philips, Siemens, UIH) and field strengths (1.5T, 3.0T).

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1: Bidirectional CRUNet for Continuous Spatio-Temporal Extraction
- **Claim**: Splitting the bidirectional recurrent unit and placing forward (CRNNTI-F) and backward (CRNNTI-B) passes in the encoder and decoder enables sustained exploitation of temporal correlations across the cine sequence.
- **Mechanism**: This design integrates temporal information bidirectionally through the U-Net’s symmetric structure. Skip connections fuse features from both directions, allowing low-level spatial details from early frames to inform high-level temporal abstractions in later frames and vice versa, forming an enhanced BCRNNTI unit throughout the network.
- **Core assumption**: Discontinuous placement of a single BCRNNTI block at the start of each cascade (as in prior work) hinders sustained temporal correlation exploitation.
- **Evidence anchors**: Architecture described in Section 2.1; ablation in Table 1 shows benefit over non-split designs.
- **Break condition**: With insufficient temporal frames or extreme non-periodic motion (e.g., arrhythmia), bidirectional recurrence may blur relevant dynamics.

### Mechanism 2: Prompt-Based Priors for Attribute Conditioning
- **Claim**: Prompt-based priors (text-based and learnable) condition the reconstruction on specific scan attributes (vendor, contrast, trajectory, acceleration), reducing feature ambiguity and enhancing generalization to unseen attribute combinations.
- **Mechanism**: Text prompts (BioBERT + MLP) and learnable spatial prompts are fused with image features via FiLM blocks, generating modulation parameters (scale and shift) that adjust feature representations. Auxiliary classifiers on prompt embeddings provide supervision to keep prompts discriminative for target attributes.
- **Core assumption**: Explicitly injecting metadata about acquisition physics and protocol reduces the network’s burden to implicitly infer these factors from noisy undersampled data, especially under distribution shifts.
- **Evidence anchors**: Section 2.1 describes prompt integration; cited works (UPCMR, PromptMR) support prompting for MRI reconstruction.
- **Break condition**: Incorrect, missing, or highly atypical metadata (e.g., novel vendor) could misguide reconstruction.

### Mechanism 3: Cascaded Feature Aggregation for Long-Range Context
- **Claim**: The CFA block mitigates information degradation across deep unrolled cascades by concatenating and convolving all preceding feature maps at each level, preserving long-range contextual information.
- **Mechanism**: For each spatial level in a cascade block, a feature buffer stores outputs from all prior cascades. These are concatenated channel-wise and processed by a convolution to produce an aggregated feature map for the current cascade, bypassing sequential degradation.
- **Core assumption**: Early cascade feature maps contain valuable complementary context (e.g., broader structural priors) that gets lost when relying solely on the immediate preceding output.
- **Evidence anchors**: Section 2.1 details CFA; Table 1 ablation shows small but consistent improvement when CFA is included.
- **Break condition**: In very long unrolled sequences, concatenation could lead to parameter explosion or overfitting without regularization.

## Foundational Learning
- **Unrolled Optimization/Iterative Refinement**  
  *Why needed here*: MRI reconstruction solves an iterative inverse problem; unrolled networks mimic proximal gradient steps with data consistency and learned regularization per cascade.  
  *Quick check*: What is the role of estimated coil sensitivity maps (CSM) and the RSS operation within one cascade block’s data consistency step?

- **Spatio-Temporal Convolutions & Recurrence**  
  *Why needed here*: Cardiac cine MRI is dynamic; exploiting correlations across time is as crucial as space. CRUNet uses Conv(2+1)D and convolutional recurrent units to model the joint spatio-temporal manifold efficiently.  
  *Quick check*: How do CRNNTI-F (encoder) and CRNNTI-B (decoder) differ in temporal aggregation scope?

- **Prompt Tuning & Conditional Computation**  
  *Why needed here*: To build a foundation model generalizing across vendors/contrasts/trajectories, the network must be explicitly aware of domain shifts. Prompting provides lightweight, metadata-driven steering without retraining the backbone.  
  *Quick check*: In the FiLM block, what do the generated `W_P` (weight) and `B_P` (bias) parameters do to the feature map `F`?

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map**:  
  Input (multi-coil k-space + mask) → RSS + SME(CSM) → [Loop over 12 cascades: CFA aggregate → CRUNet(FiLM(text prompts), PromptBlock(learnable P_S)) → update prompt embeddings] → Final RSS image.

- **Critical path**:  
  k-space → RSS/CSM estimation → iterative unrolled loop (CFA → spatio-temporal CRUNet modulated by prompts) → reconstructed image sequence.

- **Design tradeoffs**:  
  *Prompt richness vs. metadata reliability*: Text prompts leverage clinical knowledge but fail if metadata is inaccurate; learnable prompts are flexible but need sufficient per-attribute samples.  
  *Receptive field vs. compute*: Dilated convolutions increase context without pooling but can cause gridding artifacts if too aggressive; fixed channel width (64) saves memory vs. deeper growth.  
  *Sequence length vs. recurrence cost*: Using 5 input frames for middle-frame output saves memory and stabilizes training vs. full-sequence processing.

- **Failure signatures**:  
  Blurring of fine structures (papillary muscles) at high acceleration (R=24), especially in low-contrast sequences.  
  Vendor/field-strength specific residual aliasing if training data for that vendor/field was limited.  
  Temporal inconsistency (flickering) between frames if recurrence is insufficient or CFA is removed.  
  Spatially varying artifacts if the learnable spatial prompt `P_S` is poorly matched to anatomy.

- **First 3 experiments**:  
  1. **Ablate CFA and prompts**: Train CRUNet-MR-Univ (S1) with neither, only CFA, only prompts, and both. Expect full model > CFA-only > prompts-only > neither (Table 1 evidence).  
  2. **Text vs. learnable prompt-only**: Train two models—one with only text prompts, one with only `P_S`—and evaluate on a held-out vendor/contrast. Hypothesis: Text prompts generalize better to unseen metadata; `P_S` fits seen spatial patterns better.  
  3. **Curriculum learning impact**: Train with identical architecture but: (a) fixed random sampling of all acceleration factors from start, (b) proposed curriculum (R=8 → 8,16 → 8,16,24). Expect (b) converges faster and reaches higher final PSNR.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- **Loss function with k-space frequency information**: Current loss uses only image-domain magnitude; integrating k-space consistency could improve multi-contrast reconstruction.
- **Channel attention along temporal-channel dimension**: Adding attention might extract global spatio-temporal features and help at high acceleration factors (R=16, 24).
- **Optimal training samples per epoch for foundation models**: S3 (90k iterations/epoch) improved over S1 (6k), but the optimal sample count per epoch remains unexplored.
- **Underperformance on cropped cardiac regions**: CRUNet-MR-Univ lags behind top challenge methods on cropped regions; reasons and architectural adjustments to close the gap are unclear.

## Limitations
- **Underspecified architecture**: CRUNet internal design (CRNNTI split, dilation application, CFA buffer management) lacks full detail, risking implementation divergence.
- **Prompt metadata ambiguity**: Exact vendor/contrast/trajectory classes and text templates are not enumerated, complicating reproduction on new data.
- **Dataset accessibility**: CMRxRecon2025 requires Synapse registration; ACS extraction details and handling of variable temporal lengths need clarification.

## Confidence
- Overall performance gains vs. baselines (PSNR/SSIM numbers): **High** (consistent across stages).
- CRUNet spatio-temporal mechanism (bidirectional split): **Medium** (ablation shows benefit but not fully disentangled).
- Prompt-based conditioning for attribute generalization: **Medium** (ablation confirms contribution; relies on correct metadata).
- Cascaded Feature Aggregation benefit: **Medium** (small ablation improvement; could be due to increased capacity).
- Foundation model adaptability to new domains (zero-shot): **Low** (no evidence of transfer to unseen scanners).

## Next Checks
1. **Baseline replication**: Train S1 (6 cascades, 60 epochs, 6000 samples/epoch) with reported hyperparameters; confirm validation PSNR ≥26.44 and SSIM ≥0.755 to verify implementation fidelity.
2. **Prompt modality ablation**: Train models with only text prompts vs. only learnable spatial prompts; evaluate on a held-out vendor/field-strength to quantify each modality’s cross-domain generalization role.
3. **Zero-shot vendor test**: Hold out one vendor entirely during training; test the trained model on that vendor’s data to measure true out-of-distribution robustness.
