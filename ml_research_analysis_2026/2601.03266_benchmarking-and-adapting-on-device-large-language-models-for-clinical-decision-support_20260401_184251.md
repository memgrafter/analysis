# Benchmarking and Adapting On-Device Large Language Models for Clinical Decision Support

## Quick Facts
*   **arXiv ID:** 2601.03266
*   **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.03266
*   **Reference Count:** 40
*   **Primary Result:** Fine-tuning the compact 20B-parameter on-device model on curated reasoning data elevates its diagnostic accuracy to 86.5%, surpassing larger baselines and approaching the frontier 671B GPT-5 (88.9%).

## Executive Summary
This study investigates whether small, on-device large language models can perform competitively in clinical decision support while preserving patient privacy and operating under strict computational constraints. The authors benchmark two compact models, `gpt-oss-20b` and `gpt-oss-120b`, against larger proprietary and open-source models across three clinical tasks: general radiology diagnosis, ophthalmology specialty QA, and simulation of expert clinical judgment. Results show that even in zero-shot settings, the on-device models perform competitively. Crucially, fine-tuning the smaller 20B model on a dataset of structured diagnostic reasoning chains—generated by its larger 120B counterpart—boosts its performance dramatically, demonstrating that targeted domain adaptation can effectively compensate for reduced model scale.

## Method Summary
The research employs a three-part evaluation framework. First, zero-shot performance of `gpt-oss-20b` and `gpt-oss-120b` is benchmarked against models like GPT-5, o4-mini, and DeepSeek-R1 on a held-out test set of 207 Eurorad cases from 2025, plus an ophthalmology QA set and a clinical judgment dataset. Second, the `gpt-oss-20b` model is fine-tuned using Low-Rank Adaptation (LoRA) on 1,895 historical radiology cases. The training data consists of four-step chain-of-thought (CoT) reasoning traces automatically generated by the `gpt-oss-120b` teacher model. Third, for the fine-tuned model, inference uses a diverse group beam search strategy with majority voting across beams to stabilize outputs, while zero-shot evaluations use self-consistency (k=3 repeated inferences).

## Key Results
*   The larger on-device model, `gpt-oss-120b`, achieves a general diagnosis accuracy of 84.1%, matching `o4-mini` and surpassing `DeepSeek-R1` (81.6%) in zero-shot evaluation.
*   Fine-tuning the smaller `gpt-oss-20b` model on curated CoT data increases its diagnostic accuracy to 86.5%, exceeding all larger baseline models (including `gpt-oss-120b` at 77.7% overall) and approaching GPT-5 (88.9%).
*   The specific inference protocol (group beam search with diversity and majority vote) significantly improves stability, with intra-model consistency (ICC) ranging from 0.91 to 0.97 across repeated runs.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1: Architectural Efficiency Enables Competitive Zero-Shot Performance
Compact on-device models achieve strong zero-shot clinical reasoning despite far fewer parameters than frontier models due to architectural optimizations like mixture-of-experts routing and efficient attention patterns. These designs preserve the capacity for medical knowledge retrieval and reasoning while drastically reducing computational demands. The paper's benchmarks confirm that the `gpt-oss-120b` model matches or exceeds the performance of much larger proprietary and open-source models on key tasks, supporting the core assumption that clinical reasoning does not require the full parametric capacity of 671B-parameter models.

### Mechanism 2: Targeted CoT Fine-Tuning Compensates for Scale
Performance gaps between small and large models can be closed via fine-tuning on high-quality, systematically generated chain-of-thought data. The method uses the larger `gpt-oss-120b` as a teacher to create a structured four-step diagnostic reasoning dataset (symptom-finding correlation, differential mapping, systematic elimination, convergence). Applying LoRA to the smaller `gpt-oss-20b` distills this reasoning process effectively. The dramatic accuracy gain (from ~77% to 86.5%) demonstrates that explicit reasoning patterns can be transferred to a smaller, architecturally compatible student model.

### Mechanism 3: Diversity-Enhanced Decoding Stabilizes Outputs
Inference-time decoding strategies mitigate the stochasticity of on-device models. The employed diverse group beam search (13 beams, 13 groups, diversity penalty 0.5) prevents mode collapse and encourages exploration of multiple reasoning trajectories. Final diagnoses are selected via majority vote, which aggregates correct answers from diverse beams. This approach reliably improves accuracy over greedy decoding and ensures high output stability (ICC >0.9), making the system suitable for high-stakes clinical applications where consistency is paramount.

## Foundational Learning
*   **Chain-of-Thought (CoT) Prompting:** The paper's fine-tuning and evaluation rely on a structured multi-step diagnostic framework. Understanding CoT is key to grasping how models are guided to produce human-like logical sequences. *Quick check:* How does a rigid four-step CoT template (e.g., "symptom-finding correlation → differential mapping → elimination → convergence") provide more control over diagnostic reasoning structure than a generic "think step by step" prompt?
*   **Parameter-Efficient Fine-Tuning (PEFT) with LoRA:** The fine-tuning process uses 4-bit quantization and LoRA to adapt a 20B parameter model with limited resources. This is central to the claim of practical on-device adaptability. *Quick check:* Why is it important to apply LoRA adapters to *all* linear layers (including attention Q/K/V/Output and FFN layers) and expert layers in a mixture-of-experts model when teaching clinical reasoning?
*   **Self-Consistency Decoding:** The evaluation protocol uses multiple inferences per case and aggregates results (majority vote or mean) to account for LLM stochasticity. This methodology underpins all reported accuracy and stability metrics. *Quick check:* For a nominal task (multiple-choice QA) versus an ordinal task (clinical judgment rating), which aggregation method (majority vote vs. median/mean) is statistically appropriate and why?

## Architecture Onboarding
Data Curation Pipeline -> Reasoning Data Generation -> Fine-Tuning Engine -> Inference Protocol -> Evaluation Suite

**Critical Path:** The entire pipeline depends on a chain where high-quality reasoning data generation (teacher model output) enables successful LoRA transfer (student learning), which in turn requires a robust decoding strategy (beam search) to generate parseable outputs for valid metric calculation. A failure in reasoning data validation (e.g., incorrect diagnoses in teacher chains) breaks the chain.

**Design Tradeoffs:**
*   **Accuracy vs. Resource:** The 20B model fits on 16GB GPUs but shows specialized domain gaps (e.g., 40% vs 60% in breast imaging) that the 120B model mitigates.
*   **Fine-tuning Cost vs. Gain:** LoRA on the 20B model is memory-efficient (~4-bit) but requires 1,895 curated cases for a ~9% absolute gain. Data curation is the primary bottleneck.
*   **Decoding Diversity vs. Stability:** Group beam search improves accuracy over greedy decoding but increases latency. The diversity penalty balances exploration of reasoning paths against computational cost.
*   **Generality vs. Specialization:** Zero-shot on-device models perform well on general diagnosis but lag in management-heavy specialty QA. Fine-tuning on general radiology data may not transfer to these other domains.

**Failure Signatures:**
1.  **Reasoning Data Contamination:** Teacher model (`gpt-oss-120b`) generates incorrect reasoning that passes validation, leading the student to learn errors.
2.  **Prompt-Output Misalignment:** Model output deviates from the constrained format (e.g., adds extra text), causing regex extractor failures and false negatives.
3.  **Beam Search Collapse:** Inadequate diversity penalty causes all beams to converge on the same incorrect path, nullifying majority vote benefits.
4.  **Distribution Shift:** Model excels on curated Eurorad cases but fails on real-world clinical notes with different narrative structures and terminology.

**First 3 Experiments:**
1.  **Zero-shot Baseline Replication:** Run `gpt-oss-20b` and `gpt-oss-120b` on the 207-case test set using the paper's zero-shot prompts and self-consistency (k=3). Verify accuracy matches reported values (±3%) and that >95% of outputs are successfully parsed.
2.  **Fine-tuning Sanity Check:** Fine-tune `gpt-oss-20b` on a small subset (e.g., 100 cases) of the provided training data. Confirm rapid overfitting on training cases but poor test generalization if data is insufficient. Validate LoRA adapter loading and that the model generates 4-step CoT when prompted.
3.  **Decoding Strategy Ablation:** On a fixed 50-case subset, compare greedy decoding, standard beam search, and the paper's diverse group beam search (13/13/0.5). Measure accuracy, duplicate path rate across beams, and score variance across 3 runs. Expect group beam search to reduce duplicates and improve accuracy by ≥1% over greedy.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
*   **Prospective Real-World Performance:** How do on-device LLMs perform in prospective real-time clinical settings compared to retrospective benchmarks? The study acknowledges reliance on static, curated datasets, which may not capture the noise and workflow pressures of live patient care.
*   **Operational Deployment Challenges:** What are the practical hurdles of deploying on-device LLMs regarding hardware reliability, secure EHR integration, and continuous model monitoring? The paper notes these as significant but unaddressed barriers to adoption.
*   **Data-Scarce Specialties:** How can fine-tuning be achieved in clinical specialties lacking high-quality labeled data? The demonstrated success on radiology may not generalize to low-resource domains, leaving effective strategies (e.g., synthetic data, transfer learning) an open question.

## Limitations
*   **Reproducibility Barriers:** Core claims depend on inaccessible `gpt-oss` model weights/APIs and future (2025) Eurorad data, preventing independent verification.
*   **External Validity:** Performance is demonstrated on retrospective, curated case reports; generalizability to noisy, real-world clinical narratives is unproven.
*   **Teacher Model Dependency:** The fine-tuning gains critically rely on the correctness of the teacher model's (`gpt-oss-120b`) generated reasoning chains, which may contain undetected errors.

## Confidence
*   **Mechanism 1 (Architectural Efficiency):** Medium. Benchmarks are detailed but lack architectural ablation studies or independent validation.
*   **Mechanism 2 (Targeted Fine-Tuning):** Medium-High. Experimental design (LoRA on curated CoT) is sound and results are precise, but contingent on inaccessible data and flawless teacher output.
*   **Mechanism 3 (Decoding Stabilization):** Medium. Stability metrics are provided, but the reported gain hinges on a specific, non-standard beam search configuration whose reproducibility is unclear with other models.
*   **Overall:** Confidence is tempered by significant reproducibility constraints and unaddressed real-world deployment risks.

## Next Checks
1.  **Zero-shot Pipeline Validation:** Use an accessible surrogate model (e.g., Llama-3-8B) with the paper's exact prompts and self-consistency (k=3) on a small public radiology QA dataset. Verify output parsing success rate >95% and a plausible performance scale with model size, validating the evaluation pipeline itself.
2.  **Training Data Sanity Probe:** Manually inspect 50 random reasoning chains from the teacher-generated training set. Quantify the prevalence of clinically incorrect steps or hallucinations. An error rate >5% would critically undermine Mechanism 2.
3.  **Decoding Strategy Replication:** Implement the paper's diverse group beam search (13/13/0.5) and compare it against greedy and standard beam search using an accessible 7B-13B model on a fixed 50-case subset. Verify the reported accuracy improvement (~1-2%) and reduction in duplicate reasoning paths, confirming the specific configuration's benefit.
