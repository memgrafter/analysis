# Thoth: Mid-Training Bridges LLMs to Time Series Understanding

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2603.01042
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.01042
- **Reference count:** 36
- **Primary result:** Thoth-8B achieves 97.3% trend accuracy on ChatTime (vs 16.3% for Qwen3-8B), reduces forecasting SMAPE to 0.522 on Time-MQA (from 0.671), and scores 8.05/10 reasoning on KnoTS.

## Executive Summary
Large language models (LLMs) lack innate understanding of time series data. This paper introduces **mid-training**, an intermediate training stage that endows LLMs with general-purpose time series comprehension while preserving their original language and reasoning abilities. The method uses a constructed corpus called **Book-of-Thoth**, which contains paired time series and text for two tasks: *time-series-to-text* (describing patterns) and *text-to-time-series* (reconstructing series from descriptions). This bidirectional alignment creates a shared semantic space between temporal patterns and language. The resulting models, **Thoth**, built on Qwen3, show dramatic improvements across multiple benchmarks, including ChatTime, Time-MQA, and the new knowledge-intensive KnoTS. Ablation studies confirm that both directions are necessary, with time-series-to-text being particularly impactful. Mixing in general pretraining data mitigates catastrophic forgetting, and scaling experiments demonstrate that performance improves steadily with more training data. Thoth also acts as an effective warm-up: when fine-tuned on only 5% of downstream data, it outperforms the base model significantly.

## Method Summary
The authors take a base LLM (Qwen3, 8B or 30B-A3B-Instruct) and perform **full-parameter mid-training** on a proprietary corpus named **Book-of-Thoth** (26.6M tokens). The corpus is composed of:
- 57.9% time-series-to-text pairs (structured and unstructured descriptions),
- 27.4% text-to-time-series pairs (structured reconstruction),
- 14.7% general pretraining data (C4, No Robots) to prevent forgetting,
- 30.6% textual knowledge about time series (books).

The raw time series are synthetically generated via **KernelSynth**, a Gaussian process-based tool that produces diverse temporal dynamics (trend, seasonality, volatility, outliers). The text annotations are created using GPT-5.2 with carefully crafted prompts. Mid-training uses AdamW, a cosine learning rate schedule (peak 1e-5, warmup 0.1), bfloat16 precision, sequence length 4096, global batch size 32, and runs for 3 epochs (~1600 steps) on 8 NVIDIA A800 GPUs with DeepSpeed ZeRO-3. Evaluation follows an *n-shot in-context learning* protocol on three benchmarks: ChatTime (pattern recognition), Time-MQA (multi-task QA including forecasting, classification, etc.), and KnoTS (knowledge-intensive reasoning/decision-making scored by Gemini-3-Pro-Preview).

## Key Results
- Thoth-8B dramatically outperforms Qwen3-8B on ChatTime (97.3% vs 16.3% trend accuracy; 75.0% vs 22.0% volatility accuracy) and Time-MQA (forecasting SMAPE 0.522 vs 0.671; classification accuracy 64% vs 54%). Thoth-30B-A3B achieves strong results on KnoTS (8.05/10 reasoning, 4.96/10 decision-making), approaching much larger models.
- Ablation studies confirm both time-series-to-text and text-to-time-series tasks are essential; removing TS→Text causes the largest drop, while removing Text→TS also harms performance (especially forecasting and imputation).
- Performance scales monotonically with the amount of training data (10% to 100%), indicating the Book-of-Thoth corpus provides a robust learning signal.
- When fine-tuned on only 5% of downstream data, Thoth maintains superior and more stable gains compared to the base model, demonstrating effective warm-up for specialized tasks.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The success of Thoth rests on three interconnected mechanisms.

**Bidirectional alignment creates a shared semantic space.** The Book-of-Thoth corpus provides paired examples for both time-series-to-text and text-to-time-series generation. This forces the model to learn mappings in both directions, grounding temporal concepts in language and vice versa. The necessity of both directions is evidenced by the ablation study: removing TS→Text leads to catastrophic drops across tasks, while removing Text→TS causes smaller but notable declines, particularly in forecasting and imputation. This bidirectional training likely fosters a deeper, more generalizable understanding of temporal patterns than unidirectional approaches.

**Mixing general data mitigates catastrophic forgetting.** Mid-training interleaves standard text corpora (C4, No Robots) with the time-series corpus, rehearsing the model’s pre-trained language and world knowledge. This prevents the “vertical forgetting” that typically plagues intensive domain-specific fine-tuning. The 14.7% proportion of general data is critical; without it, the model would lose its utility as a general-purpose reasoning engine while trying to acquire time-series skills.

**Synthetic corpus provides scalable, task-agnostic supervision.** KernelSynth generates a massive, diverse set of time series covering rich temporal dynamics, while GPT-5.2 supplies high-quality textual annotations. This combination yields a corpus that teaches the model the “language of time series” itself, rather than memorizing patterns for specific downstream tasks. The steady improvement observed as training data scales from 10% to 100% (Figure 7) confirms that the learning signal remains high-quality and generalizable.

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component map:**  
Qwen3 (base LLM) → Book-of-Thoth corpus (26.6M tokens, mixed: TS→Text, Text→TS, general, TS knowledge) → Full-parameter fine-tuning (DeepSpeed ZeRO-3, AdamW, cosine LR, bf16) → Thoth (mid-trained model) → Evaluation on ChatTime, Time-MQA, KnoTS.

**Critical path:**  
1. **Corpus Construction:** Generate synthetic time series with KernelSynth; create TS→Text annotations via GPT-5.2; reverse pairs to obtain Text→TS; curate general text (C4, No Robots) and TS knowledge text; mix all components.  
2. **Mid-Training:** Load base Qwen3; train on Book-of-Thoth mixture for 3 epochs with specified hyperparameters; save checkpoints.  
3. **Evaluation:** For each benchmark, format inputs with fixed n-shot examples; generate responses; compute metrics (accuracy, SMAPE, or Gemini-3-Pro-Preview scores).  
4. **Ablation/Scaling:** Repeat training with modified subsets (remove components, vary % data) to isolate contributions and measure scaling.

**Design tradeoffs:**  
- **Synthetic vs. Real Data:** Synthetic data provides scale and clean annotations but may lack the complexity and noise of real-world series; the paper prioritizes generality over realism.  
- **Full-parameter vs. PEFT:** Full-parameter tuning is computationally expensive but likely necessary for deep bidirectional alignment; parameter-efficient methods (e.g., LoRA) are not explored.  
- **Task Breadth vs. Depth:** The corpus aims for broad coverage of temporal patterns, potentially at the cost of state-of-the-art performance on any single narrow task.

**Failure signatures:**  
1. Catastrophic forgetting (drop in general language abilities, e.g., on MMLU).  
2. Directional imbalance (excellent TS→Text but poor Text→TS, leading to weak forecasting/imputation).  
3. Poor scaling (performance plateaus or degrades with larger data).  
4. Benchmark overfitting (excels on templated QA but fails on creative KnoTS tasks).

**First 3 experiments:**  
1. **Ablation Study:** Train models with one corpus component removed (TS→Text, Text→TS, General) and compare performance to full Thoth-8B.  
2. **Data Scaling:** Train on 10%, 20%, 50%, 75%, 100% of Book-of-Thoth and plot aggregate ChatTime accuracy to verify learning signal scales.  
3. **Downstream Warm-up Evaluation:** Fine-tune both Qwen3-8B and Thoth-8B on only 5% of Time-MQA/ChatTime downstream data; compare performance to demonstrate Thoth's warm-up advantage.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- Should the training objective for text-to-time series be based on alignment with textual semantics rather than standard next-token prediction loss? (Section 7)
- Can reinforcement learning be applied in post-training to avoid undesirable capability trade-offs in multi-task SFT? (Section 7)
- How does the effectiveness of text-to-time series generation scale with model size and data volume, and can it eventually match or surpass the effectiveness of time-series-to-text? (Section 6.4)
- How well does mid-training on Book-of-Thoth’s synthetic time series transfer to real-world, multivariate time series with complex observational noise and missing data? (Inferred)
- Does the mid-trained model’s time series understanding rely on spurious correlations from the synthetic data generation process rather than learning robust temporal concepts? (Inferred)

## Limitations
- **General knowledge retention not measured:** The paper does not evaluate Thoth on standard general NLP benchmarks (e.g., MMLU), leaving the claim of mitigated catastrophic forgetting unverified.
- **Synthetic-to-real gap unstudied:** No analysis of distributional shift between the synthetic Book-of-Thoth and real-world time series, raising doubts about generalizability to raw, noisy data.
- **Specialist vs. generalist trade-off unexplored:** It is unclear whether Thoth’s general time series understanding comes at the cost of underperforming compared to a specialist model trained on a single downstream task.

## Confidence
- **Mid-training improves time-series benchmarks:** High
- **Bidirectional alignment is necessary:** High
- **Preservation of general LLM capabilities:** Low
- **Generalizable “time series language” learned:** Medium
