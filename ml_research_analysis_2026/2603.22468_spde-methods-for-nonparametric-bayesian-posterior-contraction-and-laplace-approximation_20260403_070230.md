# SPDE Methods for Nonparametric Bayesian Posterior Contraction and Laplace Approximation

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2603.22468
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.22468
- **Reference count:** 40
- **Primary result:** Extends diffusion-based Bayesian analysis to infinite-dimensional Hilbert spaces, establishing posterior contraction rates (O(1/√n) under strong concavity) and a finite-sample Laplace approximation bound, illustrated for linear Gaussian inverse problems.

## Executive Summary
This paper develops a framework for Bayesian inference in infinite-dimensional Hilbert spaces by representing the posterior distribution as the invariant measure of a Langevin stochastic partial differential equation (SPDE). The authors establish general conditions (Lipschitz gradients, monotone drift, curvature, measure equivalence) under which they prove posterior contraction rates: in the strongly concave case, the rate is O(1/√n) in Hilbert norm; in the weakly concave case, the rate is characterized by a fixed-point equation. Additionally, they derive a finite-sample bound on the Kullback-Leibler divergence between the posterior and a Laplace (Gaussian) approximation, showing that the error decays as n^{−1+4α}+n^{−1+2α} where α is the MAP convergence rate. Theoretical results are illustrated in the conjugate linear Gaussian inverse problem, where all quantities are explicit. The work bridges diffusion-based sampling, nonparametric statistics, and asymptotic approximations, providing a rigorous foundation for using SPDEs in Bayesian analysis.

## Method Summary
The core idea is to encode the posterior through an SPDE of the form \(d\theta_t = -\frac{1}{n} Q \nabla V(\theta_t) dt + Q \nabla F_n(\theta_t) dt + \sqrt{\frac{2}{n}} Q^{1/2} dW_t\), where \(\theta\) is the parameter in a separable Hilbert space \(H\), \(V\) is a smooth prior potential, \(F_n\) is the scaled log-likelihood, and \(W\) is a cylindrical Brownian motion. Under assumptions ensuring well-posedness (A.1, A.2) and a Lyapunov-type condition (B), the posterior is the unique invariant measure. Contraction rates are derived by analyzing the distance between a typical SPDE trajectory and the truth \(\theta^*\) using Girsanov transforms and concentration inequalities. For strong concavity (C.1-C.3), the distance satisfies a recursive inequality yielding O(1/√n). For weak concavity (W.1-W.4), a fixed-point approach gives a model-dependent rate. The Laplace approximation result uses a perturbation argument around the maximum a posteriori (MAP) estimator, with the error governed by the MAP convergence rate α. The linear Gaussian case validates all assumptions concretely because the posterior is Gaussian and the information operator A = G^*Γ^{-1}G is bounded and self-adjoint.

## Key Results
- **Strong/weak concavity contraction:** For strongly concave log-posterior (C.1-C.3), the posterior contracts at O(1/√n) in the Hilbert norm (Theorem 3.1). Under weaker conditions (W.1-W.4), the rate is characterized by a fixed-point equation (Theorem 3.2).
- **Laplace approximation error:** For a Gaussian approximation centered at the MAP, the KL divergence to the posterior is bounded by \(H(n,\alpha,\delta) = O(n^{-1+4\alpha} + n^{-1+2\alpha})\) uniformly in a high-probability set, where α is the MAP convergence rate (Theorem 4.1).
- **Linear Gaussian example:** For a conjugate Gaussian model (prior N(0,Q), linear forward operator G, Gaussian noise), all constants become explicit: μ=c, L1=‖QA‖_{op}, L2=0, B=1, ε1=0, ε2(n,δ)=\((1/\sqrt{n})(\sqrt{\operatorname{tr}(QAQ)} + \sqrt{2\|QAQ\|_{op} \log(1/δ)})\) (Corollary 5.1).

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The mechanism hinges on interpreting Bayesian updating as a stochastic dynamical system that, when properly designed, converges to the posterior distribution. The Langevin SPDE is a continuous-time analogue of the unadjusted Langevin algorithm, and its invariant measure coincides with the posterior under the specified drift and diffusion coefficients. The contraction analysis leverages the convexity/concavity structure: under strong concavity, the negative log-posterior behaves like a strongly convex function, ensuring rapid mixing and small deviations; under weak concavity, the geometry is more subtle and rates are captured by a fixed-point equation reflecting the interplay between bias and variance. The Laplace approximation relies on the fact that near the MAP, the log-posterior is approximately quadratic; the error bound ties the KL divergence to the speed at which the MAP converges (α) and to the accuracy of local quadratic approximations (ε2). The linear Gaussian case validates all assumptions concretely because the posterior is Gaussian and the information operator A = G^*Γ^{-1}G is bounded and self-adjoint.

| **Langevin SPDE** | Provides the infinite-dimensional stochastic dynamics whose invariant measure is the posterior. | Verify that the drift \(Q\nabla F_n - \frac{1}{n}Q\nabla V\) and diffusion \(\sqrt{2/n} Q^{1/2}\) satisfy conditions A.1, A.2 for well-posedness. |
| **Posterior contraction** | Measures how quickly the posterior concentrates around the true parameter as data size n grows. | Check if the radius \(R_n\) satisfies \(\Pi(\|\theta - \theta^*\|_H \ge R_n \mid X_{1:n}) \to 0\) in probability. |
| **Concavity conditions** | Strong concavity (C.1-C.3) yields parametric rates; weak concavity (W.1-W.4) allows model-dependent rates. | Compute the smallest eigenvalue of the information operator \(QA\) (or its effective condition number after truncation). |
| **Laplace approximation** | Gives a Gaussian proxy to the posterior with controlled KL error. | Approximate the posterior by \(\mathcal{N}(\hat\theta_n, (A_n)^{-1})\) where \(A_n\) is the Hessian at the MAP. |
| **Measure equivalence (Feldman-Hájek)** | Ensures that prior and posterior Gaussian measures are mutually absolutely continuous, necessary for KL bounds. | Verify \(\sum_m (\lambda_m \mu_m)^2 < \infty\) where \(\lambda_m, \mu_m\) are eigenvalues of prior and posterior covariance operators. |
| **Hilbert space inference** | The infinite-dimensional setting accommodates nonparametric models (e.g., function-valued parameters). | Use an orthonormal basis of \(H\) (e.g., eigenfunctions of Q) to truncate and compute posterior samples. |

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component map** (plain text):
Model Setup (H, Y, G, Γ, Q) -> Analytical Posterior (for Gaussian case) -> Theorem 3.1 Strong Contraction Test -> Theorem 3.2 Weak Contraction Test -> Theorem 4.1 KL Bound Test. Optional: SPDE Discretization (Euler-Maruyama) -> Empirical Moments Comparison.

**Critical path**:
1. Verify structural assumptions: L.1 (Q A ≥ c I), L.2 (Q A Q trace-class), curvature (C.1-C.3 or W.1-W.4), measure equivalence (F-H.1, F-H.2).
2. For conjugate Gaussian model, compute exact posterior mean bθ(n) and covariance Σ_n.
3. Determine which contraction theorem applies: compute spectrum of Q A to test strong concavity; if not, set up fixed-point equation for weak concavity.
4. Compute contraction radius R from equality (6) or fixed-point solution.
5. For Laplace approximation, evaluate constants A (Hessian bound), ε2 (empirical process), and α (MAP rate). With Gaussian model α=1/2, A=0, ε2 from Corollary 5.1.
6. Check that H(n,α,δ) (Eq. 22) decays as expected.
7. Optionally, implement SPDE with basis truncation and Euler-Maruyama, compare mean/covariance to analytic posterior.

**Design tradeoffs**:
- Strong concavity gives simple O(1/√n) rates but may not hold for rough priors or non-identifiable models; weak concavity covers more cases but requires solving a fixed-point inequality and may yield slower rates.
- The Laplace approximation assumes α ∈ (1/4, 1/2]; if the true α is smaller, the bound may not vanish, limiting practical use.
- Truncation dimension d in SPDE simulations trades off computational cost vs. fidelity; too small d biases posterior moments, too large d increases noise and step size constraints.

**Failure signatures**:
- **Non-coercive Q A**: smallest eigenvalue of Q A → 0 as d→∞ → strong concavity fails; switch to weak concavity analysis.
- **Feldman-Hájek singularity**: \(\sum (\lambda_m \mu_m)^2 = \infty\) implies posterior and prior are singular → KL infinite, bound vacuous; adjust prior or model.
- **α mismatch**: Estimated α from simulations differs from assumed α in Theorem 4.1 → H(n,α,δ) may not decay; need to recompute α.
- **SPDE numerical instability**: Euler-Maruyama blows up if Δt too large or d too small; monitor energy \(\|\theta_t\|_H\) and reduce Δt until moments stabilize.
- **Underestimated B**: For non-quadratic V, constant B from (C.3) may be larger; bound \(\langle Q\nabla V(\theta),\theta-\theta^*\rangle\) via Lipschitz constants and \(\|\theta-\theta^*\|\).

**First experiments** (exactly three):
1. **Linear Gaussian case:** Implement the explicit Gaussian posterior (compute bθ(n), Σ_n) and verify that the contraction radius R behaves like O(1/√n) by plotting posterior mass outside a ball of radius R vs. n.
2. **MAP rate estimation:** Run repeated simulations for the linear Gaussian model with increasing n, estimate the MAP convergence rate α via log-log regression of \(\|bθ(n)-\theta^*\|_H\) against n, and confirm it is ≈ 1/2.
3. **SPDE discretization study:** Truncate to dimension d (e.g., d=10,50,200), simulate the Euler-Maruyama scheme for the SPDE with step size Δt, and compare the empirical mean and covariance to the analytic posterior; study bias and variance as functions of d and Δt.

## Limitations
- Implicit constants (c, c₁, c₂) in the theorems may be loose, making the rates only asymptotic and hard to use for finite-n planning.
- The MAP convergence rate α is not given a priori; it must be derived model‑specifically, and mis-specification leads to vacuous Laplace bounds.
- Weak concavity conditions involve differential inequalities and a fixed-point equation, which are rarely verifiable in practice; the theorems may have limited reach beyond strongly concave settings.
