# Offline Constrained RLHF with Multiple Preference Oracles

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv:** 2604.00200
- **URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.00200
- **References:** 38
- **Result:** A dual-only offline constrained RLHF algorithm with theoretical finite-sample guarantees and near-zero constraint violation on PKU‑SafeRLHF.

## Executive Summary
This paper addresses offline constrained reinforcement learning from human feedback (RLHF) with two preference oracles: maximizing a target reward (e.g., safety) while respecting a minimum expected reward from a second oracle (e.g., helpfulness). The method casts the constrained objective as a KL‑regularized Lagrangian, whose primal optimizer takes a Gibbs (Boltzmann) policy form. This reduction yields a convex dual problem over a single Lagrange multiplier. A dual‑only projected gradient descent algorithm uses maximum likelihood reward estimates from pairwise comparisons. The authors prove the first finite‑sample guarantee for this setting, separating statistical error (O(√d/N)) and optimization error (O(1/√T)). Empirically, on the PKU‑SafeRLHF dataset, the learned policy achieves near‑zero test constraint violation and improves target reward. The framework also extends to multiple constraints and f‑divergences.

## Method Summary
Given an offline dataset of pairwise preferences from two oracles, the approach first learns linear reward models $\hat{\theta}_1, \hat{\theta}_2$ via regularized maximum likelihood (Bradley‑Terry). A reference policy $\pi_0$ (constructed from Alpaca‑7B token log‑likelihoods) supplies full support and KL‑regularization. The constrained objective $\max_\pi \mathbb{E}_\pi[r_1] \;\text{s.t.}\; \mathbb{E}_\pi[r_2] \ge J_{\min}$ with penalty $\eta D_{\mathrm{KL}}(\pi\|\pi_0)$ admits a closed‑form Gibbs primal: $\pi_\lambda(a|x) \propto \pi_0(a|x) \exp((\langle \hat{\theta}_1 + \lambda \hat{\theta}_2, \phi(x,a)\rangle)/\eta)$. Under Slater’s condition, strong duality holds and the dual function $g(\lambda)$ is convex. The algorithm directly optimizes $\lambda$ via projected gradient descent: compute empirical dual gradient $\hat{g}'(\lambda) = \mathbb{E}_{\pi_\lambda}[\langle \hat{\theta}_2,\phi\rangle] - J_{\min}$, update $\lambda \gets \operatorname{Proj}_{[0,R]}(\lambda - \alpha \hat{g}'(\lambda))$ with $\alpha = \eta/B^2$, and average after $T$ steps to obtain $\bar{\lambda}$. The final policy is $\pi_{\bar{\lambda}}$. Finite‑sample bounds combine MLE error $\beta_N = O(\sqrt{d/N})$ and optimization error $O(1/\sqrt{T})$.

## Key Results
- **Theoretical guarantee:** First finite‑sample bound for offline constrained RLHF with multiple oracles; error decomposes into statistical $O(\sqrt{d/N})$ and optimization $O(1/\sqrt{T})$ terms.
- **Empirical success:** On PKU‑SafeRLHF, test constraint violation = 0.0000 and target (safety) reward improved relative to the reference policy.
- **Algorithmic contribution:** A dual‑only projected gradient method that avoids explicit primal projection and works directly with MLE reward estimates.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
1. **Convex dual reduction**: KL‑regularization induces a Gibbs primal. With Slater’s condition, the constrained problem is equivalent to minimizing a convex dual $g(\lambda)$; convexity follows from the log‑partition function of the Gibbs distribution.
2. **Statistical error propagation**: The MLE error bound $\|\hat{\theta}_k - \theta_k^*\|_{\Sigma_N,\mathrm{reg}} \le \beta_N = O(\sqrt{d/N})$ controls deviation between true and empirical dual values/gradients, linking finite‑sample statistics to dual optimization.
3. **Dual‑only optimization**: Projected gradient descent on $\lambda$ with step size $\alpha = \eta/B^2$ (matching the Lipschitz constant of $g'(\lambda)$) converges in $T$ iterations. The averaged $\bar{\lambda}$ yields a policy that (with high probability) satisfies the constraint and attains near‑optimal utility, inheriting the error decomposition.

## Foundational Learning
- **KL‑regularized RL**: Adds $\eta D_{\mathrm{KL}}(\pi\|\pi_0)$ to keep the learned policy within the offline data support and ensures strict concavity, yielding a closed‑form Gibbs policy.
  - *Quick check*: Why does KL‑regularization yield a Gibbs policy? (Answer: First‑order optimality gives $\pi \propto \pi_0 \exp(r/\eta)$.)
- **Lagrangian duality for constrained MDPs**: Transforms the constrained problem into an unconstrained dual over $\lambda$; under Slater’s condition and appropriate convexity, strong duality holds.
  - *Quick check*: Under what conditions does strong duality hold? (Answer: Slater’s condition plus convexity in primal and concavity in Lagrangian; achieved here via KL‑regularization.)
- **Maximum likelihood from pairwise comparisons**: Bradley‑Terry model $\sigma(r_k(x,a)-r_k(x,a'))$ defines the likelihood; regularized MLE provides consistent reward estimates under identifiability.
  - *Quick check*: What role does the covariance matrix $\Sigma_N$ play in the MLE bound? (Answer: It encodes coverage of feature differences; its minimum eigenvalue governs error scaling.)
- **Gibbs policy and partition function**: The optimal policy under the regularized objective is $\pi_\lambda(a|x) \propto \pi_0(a|x) \exp(\langle \hat{\theta}_1+\lambda\hat{\theta}_2,\phi\rangle/\eta)$. The log‑partition function $\log \sum_a \pi_0 \exp(\ldots)$ is convex in $\lambda$.
  - *Quick check*: How does temperature $\eta$ influence the policy? (Answer: Larger $\eta$ yields behavior closer to $\pi_0$; smaller $\eta$ makes the policy more deterministic.)

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map**: Reward Estimation → Dual Optimizer → Policy Construction
  - *Reward Estimation*: Fits $\hat{\theta}_1,\hat{\theta}_2$ by maximizing the regularized Bradley‑Terry log‑likelihood on pairwise preference data.
  - *Dual Optimizer*: Maintains $\lambda_t$, constructs the Gibbs policy $\pi_{\lambda_t}$, computes empirical dual gradient $\hat{g}'(\lambda_t) = \mathbb{E}_{\pi_{\lambda_t}}[\langle \hat{\theta}_2,\phi\rangle] - J_{\min}$, and performs a projected gradient step $\lambda_{t+1} = \operatorname{Proj}_{[0,R]}(\lambda_t - \alpha \hat{g}'(\lambda_t))$.
  - *Policy Construction*: After $T$ iterations, outputs $\bar{\lambda} = \frac{1}{T}\sum_{t=0}^{T-1}\lambda_t$ and defines $\bar{\pi}(a|x) \propto \pi_0(a|x) \exp((\langle \hat{\theta}_1+\bar{\lambda}\hat{\theta}_2,\phi\rangle)/\eta)$.
- **Critical path**: Accurate reward estimation ($\hat{\theta}$) bounds downstream performance through $\beta_N$; dual optimization must converge ($T$ iterations) to obtain a $\bar{\lambda}$ close to the true $\lambda^*$; the final policy is then deterministic given $\bar{\lambda}$.
- **Design tradeoffs**:
  - Statistical vs. optimization error: total error $\approx \beta_N + B^2R^2/(\eta T)$; $T$ should be chosen so optimization error matches $\beta_N$.
  - Step size: Fixed $\alpha = \eta/B^2$ is theoretically sound but conservative; adaptive schedules accelerate convergence but lack guarantees.
  - Projection radius $R$: Must upper‑bound the true $\lambda^*$; too small yields suboptimality, too large slows convergence.
  - Multiple constraints: Extends to vector $\lambda \in \mathbb{R}^m_+$ with vector gradient and orthant projection; error scales with $O(m)$.
- **Failure signatures**:
  - Persistent constraint violation: $\beta_N$ too large (insufficient data/poor coverage) or $T$ too small (dual did not converge).
  - Degenerate policy (near‑uniform or near‑deterministic with low reward): poorly estimated $\hat{\theta}_2$ or $\bar{\lambda}$ explosion due to excessive $R$.
  - MLE divergence: ill‑conditioned $\Sigma_N$ (small $\lambda_{\min}$) leads to high variance.
  - Numerical instability: $\exp(\langle \hat{\theta},\phi\rangle/\eta)$ may overflow for large norms; use log‑sum‑exp.
- **First three experiments**:
  1. Synthetic validation: Generate data with known linear rewards; vary $N$ (samples) and $T$ (dual steps) to confirm $O(\sqrt{d/N})$ and $O(1/\sqrt{T})$ scaling.
  2. Ablation on step size: Compare fixed $\alpha = \eta/B^2$ vs. the adaptive scheme used in the paper; measure convergence speed and final constraint violation.
  3. PKU‑SafeRLHF replication: Process the dataset with Sentence‑BERT features and Alpaca‑7B reference policy; report training and test metrics (safety $E[r_1]$, helpfulness $E[r_2]$, violation) and compare to $\pi_0$ and greedy policy.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
1. *Joint MLE for correlated oracles*: The current analysis treats oracles separately; joint estimation could reduce variance but requires new error bounds accounting for cross‑oracle dependence.
2. *Theoretical justification of adaptive step size*: The adaptive rule used in experiments accelerates convergence but deviates from the fixed‑step analysis; its impact on dual convergence and constraint violation remains to be proven.
3. *Extension to nonlinear reward functions*: Linear assumption is limiting; nonlinear models introduce representation error and non‑convexity, necessitating new theoretical tools for finite‑sample guarantees and Gibbs‑like policy structure.
4. *Relaxing full coverage assumption*: Assumption that $\pi_0$ has support over all actions may not hold in practice; partial coverage would require new bounds incorporating visitation probabilities.

## Limitations
- Strong theoretical assumptions (Slater’s condition, linear rewards, full support of $\pi_0$) may not hold in realistic settings, limiting guaranteed performance.
- Implementation ambiguities: unclear mapping of dataset labels (“safer”, “better”) to target/constraint oracles; incomplete specification of adaptive step‑size schedule; undocumented Alpaca‑7B decoding parameters for reference policy construction.
- Empirical evaluation is restricted to a single dataset (PKU‑SafeRLHF); reported near‑zero violation may be sensitive to choices and lacks broader generalization.

## Confidence
- **High**: The reduction to a convex dual via KL‑regularized Lagrangian and the resulting Gibbs primal are theoretically sound under standard assumptions.
- **Medium**: The finite‑sample guarantee (novel separation of errors) is theoretically interesting but relies on strong assumptions; empirical results appear promising despite unresolved implementation details.
- **Low**: Extensions to multiple constraints and f‑divergences are claimed but not empirically validated; practical utility remains uncertain.

## Next Checks
1. *Oracle mapping validation*: Train separate models swapping “safer” and “better” labels to determine the correct mapping by comparing resulting metrics with those reported in the paper.
2. *Step‑size sensitivity*: Implement both fixed $\alpha = \eta/B^2$ and the adaptive schedule (once fully specified) and compare convergence speed, stability, and final constraint violation.
3. *Reference policy robustness*: Replace the Alpaca‑based $\pi_0$ with alternatives (e.g., uniform over responses or empirical data distribution) to evaluate sensitivity of coverage, constraint satisfaction, and target reward.
