# MetaOthello: A Controlled Study of Multiple World Models in Transformers

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2602.23164
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.23164
- Reference count: 40
- Primary result: A single transformer trained on multiple Othello variants converges on a mostly shared board-state representation that transfers causally across games, with a layered routing mechanism that economizes by sharing computations where rules align and specializing only on conflicts.

## Executive Summary
This paper introduces MetaOthello, a controlled suite of Othello game variants designed to study how a single transformer organizes multiple conflicting "world models." By training small GPTs on mixed data from variants with different rules or tokenizations, and analyzing them with linear probes and causal interventions, the authors show that transformers do not isolate sub-models. Instead, they learn a common board-state representation that is functionally interchangeable across games. For isomorphic variants with scrambled tokens, representations align via a single orthogonal rotation. When rules partially overlap, the network employs a layered routing strategy: early layers encode game-agnostic state, a middle layer identifies the game, and later layers apply variant-specific computations only where necessary. Performance remains high across all settings (α-score >0.98).

## Method Summary
The authors created synthetic Othello game sequences for four variants: Classic, NoMidFlip, DelFlank, and Iago (which scrambles token mappings). Pure datasets contained 20M sequences per game; mixed datasets were balanced 50/50 mixtures (40M total). Models were 8-layer decoder-only transformers (d_model=512, 8 heads, context=59, vocab=66) trained with AdamW (LR 5e-5, batch 4096) for 250 epochs. Analysis centered on training linear probes per layer to decode board state (3-class: mine/yours/empty) and game identity. Representational similarity was measured via cosine similarity of probe weights, with Procrustes alignment to account for rotations. Causal interventions added probe-derived steering vectors (scaling γ=5) to residual streams to test functional interchangeability. For Iago, a global orthogonal transformation Ω was learned to align Classic and Iago residual streams.

## Key Results
- Transformers learn a shared board-state representation that transfers causally across variants; cross-probe interventions on ambiguous sequences are nearly as effective as matched probes (Figure 3).
- For token-scrambled Iago, per-layer probe weights align via a single orthogonal rotation (Procrustes-aligned cosine similarity ≈0.98), indicating abstract structure invariance (Figure 2).
- When rules partially overlap (e.g., Classic vs. NoMidFlip), early layers remain game-agnostic, a middle layer (e.g., layer 5) identifies game identity (>90% probe accuracy), and later layers diverge in proportion to rule conflict (R² up to 0.95 with tile conflict probability; Figures 5, 6).

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- **Claim**: Transformers trained on heterogeneous game data do not partition into isolated sub-models but converge on a mostly shared board-state representation that transfers causally across variants.
- **Mechanism**: The model learns a common linear subspace for board states sufficient for predicting moves in any trained variant. Interventions using probes from one variant effectively alter predictions in another, indicating functional interchangeability.
- **Core assumption**: Linear probes accurately capture the model’s internal representation of board state (i.e., the representation is linearly decodable).
- **Evidence anchors**:
  - [abstract] "transformers do not isolate sub-models but converge on a mostly shared board-state representation that transfers causally across variants. Cross-probe interventions [...] are nearly as effective as matched probes."
  - [section 5.2] Cross-probe intervention error rates for Classic vs. NoMidFlip/DelFlank are similar to correct-probe interventions (Figure 3).
  - [corpus] Weak direct support; neighbor papers focus on single-game Othello world models, not multi-variant alignment.
- **Break condition**: If cross-probe intervention efficacy drops significantly below matched probes (<80% relative effectiveness), the shared representation hypothesis fails.

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim**: For isomorphic games with scrambled tokenization (Iago), board-state representations are equivalent up to a single orthogonal rotation that generalizes across layers.
- **Mechanism**: The model abstracts away from surface token mappings, learning an invariant geometry for board states. This is revealed by high Procrustes-aligned cosine similarity (~0.98) between Classic and Iago probe weights after aligning for token permutation.
- **Core assumption**: The orthogonal transformation accounts for all representational differences between token-scrambled variants; residual differences are noise.
- **Evidence anchors**:
  - [abstract] "For isomorphic games with scrambled tokens (Iago), representations are equivalent up to a single orthogonal rotation (layer-wise cosine similarity ≈0.98 after Procrustes alignment)."
  - [section 5.3] Raw similarity ≈0.03 (random baseline), but Procrustes-aligned similarity reaches 0.98. Residual stream alignment via a global Ω recovers near-baseline Iago performance (Figure 4).
  - [corpus] Weak; neighbors do not test token-scrambling invariance.
- **Break condition**: If Procrustes alignment fails to significantly exceed random baseline (e.g., aligned similarity <0.8), the invariance claim does not hold.

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim**: When rules partially overlap, early layers maintain game-agnostic states, a middle layer identifies game identity, and later layers specialize, economizing by sharing representations where rules align and diverging only on conflicts.
- **Mechanism**: The network routes processing: initial layers encode board state common to all variants; a pivot layer (e.g., Layer 5) encodes game identity; final layers apply variant-specific computations. Divergence in probe weights correlates with probability of rule conflict between variants.
- **Core assumption**: Linear game-identity probes accurately decode the network’s internal context about which game is being played.
- **Evidence anchors**:
  - [abstract] "early layers maintain game-agnostic representations while a middle layer identifies game identity, and later layers specialize."
  - [section 5.4–5.5] For Classic–NoMidFlip, game-identity probes reach ~90% accuracy only in layers 5–8. Layer 5 intervention shifts output toward NoMidFlip (Figure 5c). Probe dissimilarity correlates with tile conflict probability (R² up to 0.95 at layer 8; Appendix Figure 9).
  - [section 5.6] For Classic–DelFlank, early-layer (1–2) steering improves DelFlank board probe accuracy, while Layer 5 steering does not (Figure 6), indicating different routing.
  - [corpus] Partial support from "Directional Routing in Transformers" (neighbor) on learned suppression directions, but not on game-specific routing.
- **Break condition**: If no single layer shows significantly higher game-identity probe accuracy than others, or if steering at the hypothesized pivot layer does not shift outputs, the layered routing mechanism is unsupported.

## Foundational Learning
- **Concept**: Linear probing
  - *Why needed here*: All representational analyses rely on training linear probes to decode board states ("mine/yours/empty") or game identity from residual streams. Assumes these linear directions capture the network's internal state.
  - *Quick check question*: If a linear probe achieves >95% accuracy on held-out data, does that prove the representation is linear, or could a nonlinear decoder perform better?
- **Concept**: Causal intervention via activation steering
  - *Why needed here*: To test whether probe directions have functional causal effects (not just correlation). Adding probe vectors to activations should steer model predictions if the representation is causally involved.
  - *Quick check question*: If steering with a cross-variant probe changes predictions, does that prove the representations are shared, or could the probe vector be an approximate steering direction for both?
- **Concept**: Orthogonal Procrustes alignment
  - *Why needed here*: To compare probe weight matrices across variants while accounting for possible rotations (e.g., due to scrambled tokenization). Finds the orthogonal transformation that best aligns two sets of vectors.
  - *Quick check question*: Does high Procrustes-aligned similarity imply the underlying representations are identical up to rotation, or could they be different subspaces that happen to align under this metric?

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map**: Token sequence -> 8-layer Transformer (d_model=512, 8 heads, context=59) -> Next-move probabilities (66-token distribution) + Linear probes per layer (board state, game identity)
- **Critical path**: 1) Generate MetaOthello datasets (pure/mixed). 2) Train GPTs (40M sequences, 250 epochs). 3) Train linear probes on 100k held-out activations (per layer). 4) Compute probe weight similarities (cosine, Procrustes-aligned). 5) Perform cross-probe interventions (add probe vectors, γ=5) and measure error. 6) For ambiguous sequences, analyze game-identity probe accuracy and steering effects (Δµ).
- **Design tradeoffs**:
  - *Scale vs. interpretability*: Small models (8L/512d) enable full probing but may not scale to real foundation models' complexity.
  - *Linear probes vs. nonlinear circuits*: Probes assume linear decodability; nonlinear mechanisms (e.g., sparse autoencoders) might reveal more.
  - *Mixed dataset ratio*: 50/50 mixtures are studied; imbalanced mixtures could yield different routing.
- **Failure signatures**:
  - Probe accuracy <90% on any variant → representation not properly learned.
  - Cross-probe intervention error ≈ null baseline → representations not causally shared.
  - Procrustes-aligned similarity ≈ random baseline (0.68) → no syntax invariance.
  - No layer with >85% game-identity probe accuracy → no identifiable routing layer.
- **First 3 experiments**:
  1. **Validate probe fidelity**: For each model (pure and mixed), train board-state probes per layer. Confirm >95% tile accuracy on test sequences (Appendix Figure 8).
  2. **Cross-probe causal test**: On mixed model, pick ambiguous sequences (valid in both games). Apply cross-variant probe steering (e.g., Classic probe on NoMidFlip data). Measure reduction in prediction error relative to correct-probe steering. Expect >70% relative effectiveness (Figure 3).
  3. **Iago rotation check**: For Classic–Iago mixed model, compute per-layer Procrustes alignment between Classic and Iago probe weights. Expect aligned cosine similarity >0.95 (Figure 2). If not, syntax invariance fails.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- **Open Question 1**: How does the model simultaneously maintain a shared board-state representation across games while still discriminating between them for ambiguous sequences, and what nonlinear mechanisms enable this?  
  *Basis in paper*: [explicit] The conclusion states: "The tension between a shared global representation and the need for local precision—suggests that linear analysis alone cannot fully explain the model’s behavior, leaving an opening for future work on nonlinear mechanisms."  
  *Why unresolved*: Linear probes and interventions show cross-variant causal interchangeability, yet the model reliably distinguishes games during ambiguous sequences. The underlying computations that preserve both properties remain unclear.  
  *What evidence would resolve it*: Detailed circuit-level analysis (e.g., attention pattern attribution, MLP activation probing) to identify nonlinear components that encode game identity or combine game-specific information, possibly by decomposing the residual stream into linearly shared and nonlinear divergent subspaces.

- **Open Question 2**: How do findings scale to larger models, imbalanced mixture ratios, and many-way game mixtures?  
  *Basis in paper*: [explicit] Limitations section: "We only test 50/50 mixtures, one 8-layer (dmodel = 512) architecture, and pairwise game mixtures, so the effects of imbalanced mixtures, scale, and many-way arbitration remain open."  
  *Why unresolved*: The current controlled study isolates a narrow regime; scaling to more realistic settings may change representation geometry (e.g., increased capacity could lead to more independent sub-models or different routing).  
  *What evidence would resolve it*: Train models with varying sizes (e.g., dmodel up to 2048, more layers), different mixture ratios (e.g., 90/10), and three or more game variants; apply the same linear probe alignment and intervention metrics to see if shared representation persists or bifurcates.

- **Open Question 3**: What circuit-level mechanisms implement the layerwise routing where early layers track shared state, a middle layer identifies game identity, and later layers specialize?  
  *Basis in paper*: [inferred] The paper identifies that "early layers maintain game-agnostic representations while a middle layer identifies game identity, and later layers specialize" (Results). This suggests a hierarchical routing, but they note "linear analysis alone cannot fully explain" and that nonlinear mechanisms are needed.  
  *Why unresolved*: The study uses linear probes and whole-layer interventions; it does not isolate which attention heads or MLP layers perform game identification or how information flows across layers.  
  *What evidence would resolve it*: Use techniques like activation patching, head ablation, and path analysis to map the precise attention patterns and MLP computations responsible for encoding game identity and directing downstream processing. A sparse autoencoder decomposition could reveal interpretable features for game identity.

- **Open Question 4**: In the Iago experiment, why do orthogonally scrambled tokenizations lead to representations that align via a single orthogonal rotation across all layers? Does this imply a universal transformation or specific inductive biases?  
  *Basis in paper*: [explicit] They report: "For isomorphic games with token remapping, representations are equivalent up to a single orthogonal rotation that generalizes across layers—demonstrating the model learns abstract structure independent of surface tokens." And later: "This observation alone does not determine how such invariance is implemented internally."  
  *Why unresolved*: The analysis finds high similarity after Procrustes alignment but does not explain the mechanism: is there a single rotation that aligns the entire residual stream due to weight tying, or do different layers learn different rotations that happen to be consistent? This is not explained.  
  *What evidence would resolve it*: Test if the same orthogonal transformation (learned on one layer) transfers to other layers when applied to token embeddings vs. residual streams; examine weight matrices for signs of structured transformations. Could also train models with different random seeds to see if rotation is consistent across instantiations, and analyze how the model maps tokens to abstract positions (e.g., via probing the embedding layer).

## Limitations
- Small model scale (8-layer, d_model=512) and exclusive focus on 50/50 pairwise mixtures limit generalizability to larger, more complex, or many-way settings.
- Heavy reliance on linear probes assumes decodability; nonlinear mechanisms might explain residual ambiguities not captured by linear analysis.
- Key implementation details unreported: exact Iago token permutation, α-score computation for mixed games, and intervention scaling factor optimization.

## Confidence
- **Shared representation (Mechanism 1)**: High. Strong evidence from cross-probe interventions nearly matching matched probes and high probe fidelity (>95% tile accuracy).
- **Iago invariance (Mechanism 2)**: High. Procrustes-aligned similarity ~0.98 far exceeds random baseline (~0.68) and generalizes across layers.
- **Layered routing (Mechanism 3)**: Medium. Game-identity probe peaks ~90% and rule-conflict correlation (R² up to 0.95) are suggestive, but DelFlank early-layer effects complicate a simple pivot-layer narrative; nonlinear mechanisms likely required.

## Next Checks
1. **Scale study**: Train larger transformers (e.g., 12-layer, 1024d) on mixed variants to test if shared representation persists or bifurcates with increased capacity.
2. **Nonlinear probing**: Use sparse autoencoders or nonlinear decoders to verify board-state information is not misrepresented by linear probes and to search for nonlinear game-identity features.
3. **Permutation robustness**: Generate multiple random Iago scrambles and confirm Procrustes-aligned similarity >0.95 across permutations, testing invariance consistency.
