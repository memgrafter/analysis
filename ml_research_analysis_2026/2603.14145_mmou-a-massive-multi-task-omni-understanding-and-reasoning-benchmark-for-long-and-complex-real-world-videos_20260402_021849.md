# MMOU: A Massive Multi-Task Omni Understanding and Reasoning Benchmark for Long and Complex Real-World Videos

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2603.14145
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.14145
- Reference count: 34
- Primary result: Best model (Gemini 2.5 Pro) achieves 64.2% accuracy; human performance is 84.3%.

## Executive Summary
Current multimodal large language models (MLLMs) are typically evaluated on isolated modalities or short videos, lacking assessment of joint audio-visual-textual reasoning over long, complex real-world videos. To address this gap, the authors introduce MMOU, a large-scale benchmark comprising 15,000 multiple-choice questions paired with 9,038 long videos (average duration 711.6 seconds) spanning 13 skill categories that require cross-modal and temporal integration. The questions are manually annotated by experts, featuring hard distractors and temporal diversity. Evaluations of over 20 models reveal substantial performance gaps: the best closed-source model (Gemini 2.5 Pro) obtains only 64.2% accuracy, while the strongest open-source model (MiniCPM-o) reaches 46.8%, compared to human performance of 84.3%. Models particularly struggle with basic skills such as temporal reasoning and counting, and performance drops sharply for evidence appearing later in videos. Open-ended evaluation using GPT-5 as a judge yields even lower scores (e.g., Gemini 2.5 Pro overall 3.90/5), indicating deficiencies in correctness and completeness. These findings underscore the significant challenges still present in long-form omni-modal understanding.

## Method Summary
The core method introduces MMOU, a benchmark with 15,000 multiple-choice questions and 9,038 long videos (average 711.6 seconds). Videos cover diverse real-world scenarios and are manually annotated by experts to ensure high-quality, temporally diverse questions with hard distractors. The benchmark evaluates 13 skills that require cross-modal and temporal reasoning, such as linking audio events to visual scenes and counting occurrences over time. This design provides a rigorous test for MLLMs on long-form, integrated understanding.

## Key Results
- Gemini 2.5 Pro (closed-source) and MiniCPM-o (open-source) achieve 64.2% and 46.8% accuracy respectively, compared to 84.3% for humans.
- All models struggle with basic skills like temporal reasoning and counting; accuracy declines notably for evidence occurring later in videos.
- Open-ended evaluation using GPT-5 as a judge yields lower scores (Gemini 2.5 Pro: 3.90/5), highlighting deficiencies in correctness and completeness.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The MMOU benchmark effectively isolates the challenges of long-form omni-modal understanding by requiring models to integrate information across audio, visual, and textual streams over extended temporal contexts. Expert annotation ensures high-quality questions with hard distractors and temporal diversity, making the evaluation rigorous and targeted at specific skills. This design exposes fundamental weaknesses in current MLLMs, particularly in temporal reasoning and counting, and shows that performance degrades with longer reliance on distant evidence. The benchmark thus serves as a precise diagnostic tool for future model development.

## Foundational Learning
- **Multimodal Integration**: Why needed: Fuse audio, visual, and textual cues to form a unified representation for reasoning. Quick check: Can the model correctly answer questions that require correlating a sound with a visual event?
- **Temporal Reasoning**: Why needed: Understand event sequences and temporal relations over long videos. Quick check: Can the model determine the order of actions or duration between events?
- **Long-Context Modeling**: Why needed: Process extended inputs (average 711 s) without forgetting earlier information. Quick check: Does accuracy drop when relevant evidence appears later in the video?
- **Distractor Robustness**: Why needed: Ignore misleading or irrelevant information in multiple-choice options. Quick check: Are model errors concentrated on questions with hard distractors?
- **Expert Annotation Quality**: Why needed: Ensure benchmark reliability and validity. Quick check: Would multiple annotators agree on question-answer correctness?

## Architecture Onboarding
Component map: Video Encoder -> Visual Features -> Temporal Aggregator -> Multimodal Fusion <- Audio Encoder -> Audio Features; Text Encoder -> Text Features -> Multimodal Fusion -> Reasoning Module -> Answer Classifier.

Critical path: The longest dependency chain starts from video frame extraction through the video encoder, temporal aggregation, multimodal fusion, reasoning, and classification. Audio and text encoding run in parallel and converge at fusion. The video path dominates computational cost due to high frame count and spatiotemporal modeling.

Design tradeoffs: Key tradeoffs include: (1) Frame sampling rate vs. temporal resolution, (2) Modality weighting to avoid dominance, (3) Parameter sharing between encoders and fusion to reduce size, (4) Handcrafted vs. end‑to‑end learned features, (5) Transformer‑based temporal modeling (memory‑intensive) vs. pooling (less expressive).

Failure signatures: Typical failures include: (1) Poor performance on temporal ordering questions, indicating weak temporal reasoning; (2) Large accuracy drop when evidence appears late, showing limited context retention; (3) High error on counting tasks; (4) Over‑reliance on textual clues, suggesting insufficient multimodal fusion; (5) Inconsistent performance across skill categories.

First experiments:
1. Baseline implementation: Use frozen CLIP for video (frame‑level), frozen VGGish for audio, and a small LLM for text, with mean pooling and a linear classifier; evaluate on a 10% data subset to establish a performance floor.
2. Modality ablation: Train the baseline with all three modalities, then remove audio, then remove vision, to quantify each modality's contribution to overall accuracy.
3. Length sensitivity test: For a balanced sample of questions, create shortened videos (first 50%, last 50%) and compare model accuracy against full‑length videos to measure the impact of temporal distance.
