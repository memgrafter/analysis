# Bridging Domains through Subspace-Aware Model Merging
## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2603.05768
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.05768
- Reference count: 40
- One-line primary result: SCORE achieves average accuracy gains of 0.74 percentage points (ViT-B-32) and 0.58 p.p. (ViT-L-14) over the best competing merging methods across eight domain generalization benchmarks, and outperforms logit ensembles by 1.12–1.90 p.p.

## Executive Summary
Model merging is a promising approach to combine fine-tuned models from different distribution shifts into a single model that generalizes to unseen domains. However, naïve averaging of model weights often suffers from destructive interference due to overlapping subspaces captured by different domain-specific fine-tunings. This paper investigates why domain shifts cause more severe conflicts than multi-task settings, introducing the Subspace Alignment Ratio (SAR) to quantify overlap. To address this, the authors propose SCORE, a merging method that constructs a shared orthogonal basis from the leading singular vectors of all domain-specific task matrices, projects each matrix into this basis, and prunes extreme off-diagonal entries that represent harmful couplings. Extensive experiments on eight domain generalization benchmarks using three CLIP model scales show that SCORE consistently outperforms seven state-of-the-art merging methods and logit ensemble baselines, yielding accuracy improvements of up to 0.74 percentage points and demonstrating effective conflict mitigation.

## Method Summary
SCORE operates on task vectors Δ_d = θ_d − θ_pre extracted from fine-tuned CLIP image encoders. For each layer, it computes SVD of each Δ_d, retaining the top k = ⌈rank(Δ_d)/D⌉ singular vectors (approximately 1/D of the rank) to form left and right subspaces. These subspaces are concatenated across domains into U* and V*, and a second SVD yields orthogonal bases U_⊥ and V_⊥ that best approximate all domain subspaces in the Frobenius sense. Each Δ_d is then re-expressed in this shared basis: Δ'_d = U_⊥ᵀ Δ_d V_⊥. A trimming operator is applied: all diagonal entries are kept, while off-diagonals within τ = 1.96 standard deviations of the off-diagonal mean are retained; others are zeroed. The trimmed projections are summed across domains to obtain Σ_score, which is reconstructed into a merged delta: Δ_merged = U_⊥ Σ_score V_⊥ᵀ. The final model is θ_merge = θ_pre + Δ_merged. No validation data is used; the method is applied post-hoc after standard fine-tuning on each domain (full fine-tuning, AdamW, lr=1e-5, 10–20 epochs).

## Key Results
- SCORE outperforms seven existing merging methods (Task Arithmetic, TIES, MagMax, PCB, TSV, ISO-C, ISO-CTS) on eight domain generalization benchmarks, achieving average gains of 0.74 percentage points (ViT-B/32) and 0.58 p.p. (ViT-L/14) over the best competitor.
- It surpasses logit ensemble baselines by 1.12–1.90 percentage points.
- Performance gains are consistent across multiple CLIP model scales (ViT-B/32, ViT-B/16, ViT-L/14) and diverse datasets (PACS, DomainNet, ImageNet-R, NICO++, OfficeHome, TerraIncognita, FedISIC, RetinaDomains).

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1: Higher subspace overlap in domain shifts
**Claim:** Domain shifts induce significantly stronger subspace overlap between fine-tuned models than multi-task shifts, creating conflicts during merging.  
**Mechanism:** SVD on task matrices extracts dominant singular directions; Subspace Alignment Ratio (SAR) quantifies pairwise overlap. In domain generalization (DG), models share label space but learn domain-specific features, causing higher SAR than in multi-task learning (MTL) where tasks are semantically distinct.  
**Evidence:** [abstract] states domain shifts cause stronger subspace conflicts; [section 3.2] Figure 2 shows DG SAR values (0.60–0.70) consistently higher than MTL (0.41–0.97 with many lower); [corpus] notes SAR is used in prior work as a conflict proxy.  
**Break condition:** If SAR between domain models is already low (orthogonal subspaces), SCORE’s pruning may discard useful shared directions, potentially underperforming simple averaging.

### Mechanism 2: Shared orthogonal basis construction
**Claim:** Concatenating leading singular vectors and orthogonalizing yields a basis that best approximates all domain subspaces.  
**Mechanism:** For each layer, take top‑k left/right singular vectors from each Δ_d, concatenate into U* and V*, then compute SVD to obtain U_⊥ and V_⊥. This basis minimizes Frobenius distance to all domain subspaces, enabling a common frame where diagonal entries indicate agreement and off‑diagonals indicate coupling.  
**Evidence:** [abstract] describes basis construction; [section 4, Algorithm 1] steps 5–9 detail the process; [corpus] cites MoORE for SVD‑based conflict resolution in MTL, supporting plausibility.  
**Break condition:** If domain subspaces are too divergent, the shared basis may poorly represent some domains, leading to weak diagonal entries and loss of domain‑specific information.

### Mechanism 3: Off‑diagonal trimming for conflict mitigation
**Claim:** Pruning extreme off‑diagonal elements in the projected matrices reduces destructive interference while preserving beneficial shared variability.  
**Mechanism:** After projecting Δ_d into the shared basis (Δ'_d = U_⊥ᵀ Δ_d V_⊥), the trim function keeps all diagonal entries and off‑diagonals within τ=1.96 standard deviations of the off‑diagonal mean; other off‑diagonals are zeroed. Summing trimmed projections across domains yields Σ_score, which is reconstructed. This removes noisy or harmful couplings while retaining moderate cross‑talk.  
**Evidence:** [section 4] equation and Figure 3 illustrate trimming; [section 5.2, Table 3] ablation: full matrix (no trim) catastrophically fails (~7.5% accuracy), diagonal‑only baseline lower, trimmed gains +1.53–2.59 p.p. over diagonal.  
**Break condition:** Fixed τ may be suboptimal; excessive trimming could discard useful coordination signals, especially if moderate off‑diagonals encode beneficial co‑adaptations.

## Foundational Learning
- **Singular Value Decomposition (SVD)**: Decomposes matrices into singular vectors/directions and values, revealing dominant subspaces. SVD is used to extract subspaces of weight updates, measure alignment via SAR, and construct orthogonal bases for merging. *Quick check:* What do the left and right singular vectors represent in the SVD of a layer's weight update matrix?
- **Domain Generalization vs. Multi‑Task Learning**: DG merges models fine-tuned on different distributions of the same task; MTL merges models for different tasks. The shared label space in DG leads to higher subspace overlap and thus stronger conflict, explaining why MTL‑oriented merging methods often fail on DG. *Quick check:* Why would models fine‑uned on different domains of the same classification task have more overlapping subspaces than models fine-tuned on entirely different tasks (e.g., digit vs. landscape classification)?
- **Task Vectors and Model Merging**: A task vector is the difference between fine‑tuned and pre‑trained weights (Δθ). Merging operates on these deltas (e.g., averaging, TIES). SCORE modifies the summation by aligning subspaces and trimming conflicts. *Quick check:* What is a "task vector," and why is subtracting the pre‑trained weights a common practice in model merging?

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component map** (plain text flow):
θ_pre → compute Δ_d for each domain → SVD per Δ_d (U_d, V_d) → retain top‑k vectors → concatenate U_d → U* and V* → SVD(U*) → U_⊥ (shared basis); similarly V_⊥ → for each Δ_d: Δ'_d = U_⊥ᵀ Δ_d V_⊥ → apply trim → Σ_score = Σ_d trim(Δ'_d) → reconstruct Δ_merged = U_⊥ Σ_score V_⊥ᵀ → θ_merge = θ_pre + Δ_merged

**Critical path**: SVD extraction → shared orthogonal basis construction → projection into shared basis → off‑diagonal trimming → reconstruction. Failures in orthogonalization or trimming directly degrade the merged weights.

**Design tradeoffs**:
- Orthogonalization cost: SVD on concatenated U* adds computational overhead but ensures orthogonality, trading off merge time for accuracy.
- Trimming threshold τ: Fixed at 1.96 (95% confidence) may not be optimal across all layers/datasets; too high/low harms performance.
- Top‑k selection: Using 1/D of rank is heuristic; larger k captures more domain specifics but increases basis size and risk of overfitting conflicts.

**Failure signatures**:
- Low SAR between domains: If subspaces are already orthogonal, SCORE’s aggressive pruning may discard useful shared directions, causing underperformance versus simple averaging.
- Unrepresentative shared basis: When projection scores ∥U_⊥ᵀ Δ_d V_⊥∥ are low, diagonal entries are weak and off‑diagonals dominate, indicating basis failure and likely poor merging.
- Full matrix ablation: Merging without trimming produces near‑zero accuracy (Table 3), confirming catastrophic interference from unmitigated off‑diagonals.

**First 3 experiments**:
1. **SAR validation**: Compute pairwise SAR for a DG dataset (e.g., DomainNet) and a multi‑task set (e.g., eight tasks). Confirm DG shows higher average SAR; otherwise, the core premise may be violated.
2. **Ablation of `trim`**: On PACS with ViT‑B/32, compare four merges: (a) diagonal‑only, (b) off‑diagonal‑only, (c) full matrix (no trim), (d) SCORE’s trimmed. Expect (d) > (a) > (b) >> (c). If (c) is not catastrophically bad, conflicts may be low.
3. **Baseline comparison**: On one DG dataset (leave‑one‑domain‑out), compare SCORE against at least three baselines (TIES, TSV, Task Arithmetic). SCORE should rank top‑2. If underperforming, verify SVD rank k, τ, and domain heterogeneity.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- **How does model merging compare to training from scratch on domain mixtures for compositional generalization?** The conclusion suggests using merging to study compositional abilities instead of retraining on domain subsets, but no direct comparison exists. Evidence would involve comparing merged models to models trained on combined source domains on held‑out DG, with computational efficiency metrics.
- **Do models fine‑tuned with different domain generalization losses merge better due to complementary subspaces?** All experiments use standard fine‑tuning; the effect of diverse DG losses (e.g., IRM, DANN) on subspace conflict is unknown. Evidence: merge models trained with various DG methods and measure generalization versus merging standard fine‑tuned models.
- **Can SCORE be effectively applied to non‑vision domains like NLP and generative models?** SCORE is only evaluated on image classification; weight structures in other models may differ. Evidence: apply SCORE to language models (e.g., BERT) fine‑tuned on different text domains or diffusion models fine‑tuned on different styles, and evaluate domain generalization.
- **How can model merging handle models with different architectures or pretrained seeds?** Parameter‑wise merging currently requires same architecture and seed. Practical scenarios often involve heterogeneous models. Evidence: develop alignment techniques (e.g., weight permutation, optimal transport) to map parameters across architectures, then apply SCORE.
- **What is the theoretical relationship between Subspace Alignment Ratio and generalization performance?** SAR is introduced and higher values observed in DG, but the causal link is not formalized. Evidence: derive generalization bounds involving SAR, or conduct controlled experiments isolating SAR effects.

## Limitations
- Implementation details for 1D parameters (e.g., LayerNorm, biases) and exact SVD truncation threshold are unclear, hindering exact reproduction.
- Evaluation is restricted to CLIP vision transformers; generalizability to other architectures (e.g., ResNet, LSTMs) or generative models remains unknown.
- The fixed trimming threshold τ=1.96 may not be optimal across all layers/datasets, and repeated SVD computations introduce non‑trivial computational overhead.

## Confidence
- Domain shifts induce stronger subspace conflicts than multi‑task shifts, measurable via SAR: **High** (empirically validated across eight DG benchmarks).
- Constructing a shared orthogonal basis from concatenated singular vectors aligns domain subspaces: **Medium** (intuitive PCA‑based approach but not independently validated).
- Pruning extreme off‑diagonal elements mitigates destructive interference: **High** (ablation shows catastrophic failure without trimming and consistent gains over diagonal‑only).

## Next Checks
1. Compute pairwise SAR across a broader set of DG vs. MTL dataset pairs (e.g., PACS vs. Taskonomy) to test the generality of the conflict hypothesis.
2. Perform layer‑wise and dataset‑wise search for the optimal τ using a small validation holdout, to assess whether adaptive thresholds improve robustness.
3. Replicate SCORE on non‑CLIP architectures (e.g., ResNet‑50, T5) and on parameter‑efficient fine‑tuning methods (LoRA, adapters) to evaluate broader applicability.
