# DiTS: Multimodal Diffusion Transformers Are Time Series Forecasters

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2602.06597
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.06597
- **Reference count:** 38
- **Primary result:** DiTS achieves state-of-the-art probabilistic forecasting on covariate-aware benchmarks, including lowest Weighted Quantile Loss (0.0702) and Mean Absolute Scaled Error (0.6014) on the FEV leaderboard subset, and reduces average MSE by over 10% on Electricity Price Forecasting (EPF) compared to prior deterministic models.

## Executive Summary
DiTS reformulates the Diffusion Transformer (DiT) for probabilistic multivariate time series forecasting with exogenous covariates. It treats endogenous and exogenous variables as distinct modalities, inspired by multimodal video generation. The architecture uses a dual-stream Transformer block that decouples temporal dynamics (Time Attention) and cross-variate interactions (Variate Attention) via a learnable low-rank decomposition, enabling efficient modeling. Conditioned on exogenous information via adaptive modulation (MM-DiT style) and joint attention, DiTS employs conditional flow matching (rectified flow) for stable training and fast sampling. Experiments on covariate-aware benchmarks (FEV, EPF) show state-of-the-art performance, outperforming foundation models like Chronos-2 and Moirai-2.0.

## Method Summary
DiTS introduces a dual-stream Transformer architecture for diffusion-based probabilistic forecasting. The model processes two separate streams—endogenous target and exogenous covariates—through patch embeddings. A global conditioning vector combines a pooled exogenous embedding and a timestep embedding, which is projected to per-layer modulation parameters (α, β, γ) for adaptive normalization. Each DiTS block applies: (1) Time Attention: shared self-attention over patches independently in each stream, capturing intra-variate temporal patterns; (2) Variate Attention: joint attention with independent Q/K/V projections per stream, attending over concatenated tokens to model inter-variate dependencies; (3) a shared feed-forward network. The forward diffusion process uses linear interpolation (rectified flow), and training minimizes a velocity prediction loss (v-loss). Sampling uses simple Euler integration. This design explicitly factorizes the multivariate attention matrix into low-rank temporal and cross-variate components, reducing computational cost while preserving modality-specific information.

## Key Results
- On the FEV leaderboard subset, DiTS achieves the lowest Weighted Quantile Loss (0.0702) and Mean Absolute Scaled Error (0.6014), surpassing foundation models.
- On Electricity Price Forecasting (EPF) with known future covariates, DiTS reduces average MSE by over 10% compared to the best prior deterministic model across horizons H=24 and H=360.
- Ablation studies confirm that the combination of joint attention and adaptive conditioning (MM-DiT style) outperforms simpler schemes (AdaLN-only, cross-attention-only).

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1: Dual-Stream Orthogonal Factorization
DiTS separates intra-variate temporal dynamics and inter-variate cross-variate interactions through a dual-stream attention mechanism. Time Attention processes each stream (endogenous and exogenous) independently over the temporal (patch) dimension using shared self-attention, capturing autoregressive patterns within each variate. Variate Attention then performs joint attention over concatenated stream tokens with independent Q/K/V projections, modeling cross-variate relationships while respecting modality heterogeneity. This factorization approximates a low-rank decomposition of the full time-variate attention matrix, avoiding the quadratic cost of flattening both dimensions. The core assumption is that multivariate dependencies can be decomposed into orthogonal temporal and cross-variate components without significant information loss. Evidence includes the architecture description and ablation results where DiTS outperforms flattened full-attention models (e.g., Timer-XL) and channel-independent variants.

### Mechanism 2: MM-DiT-Style Adaptive Conditioning
DiTS achieves fine-grained exogenous control by combining joint attention with per-layer adaptive modulation (AdaLN). A global conditioning embedding \(Z_y\) (from exogenous variates and diffusion timestep) is projected via an MLP into layer-specific modulation parameters {α, β, γ} for the Time Attention, Variate Attention, and FFN modules. Joint attention enables token-level interaction between endogenous and exogenous streams, while AdaLN modulates the input distribution of each sub-layer, aligning the endogenous stream's activations with the conditioning context. This dual injection provides both global alignment and local feature fusion. The ablation in Figure 11 shows that combining joint attention and AdaLN yields lower MSE than using either alone.

### Mechanism 3: Flow Matching with Rectified Paths
DiTS uses conditional flow matching (rectified flow) instead of standard diffusion. The forward process is a linear interpolation: \(x_t = (1-t)x_0 + t\epsilon\), defining a straight-line probability path with an analytic velocity field. Training minimizes the v-loss: \(\|v_\theta(x_t, t, c) - (\epsilon - x_0)\|^2\). This avoids the complexity of variational lower bounds and provides deterministic paths that improve training stability. Sampling uses simple Euler integration: \(x_{t-\Delta t} = x_t - v_\theta(x_t, t, c)\Delta t\). The linear path enables high-quality generation with few inference steps (e.g., 5 steps on EPF), as shown in the Log-Normal scheduling experiment (Figure 12). The core assumption is that straight-line transport suffices for time series distributions.

## Architecture Onboarding
### Component Map
Inputs: endogenous series \(x_0\) and exogenous \(X_{1:C}\) with timestep \(t\) → Separate PatchEmbed layers produce tokens \(Z_x^0\) and \(Z_c^0\) → Compute conditioning \(Z_y = e_{cov} + e_{time}\) → For each DiTS block (layer): MLP_mod projects \(Z_y\) to modulation parameters → Time Attention (shared MSAtime applied separately to \(Z_x\) and \(Z_c\)) → Variate Attention (joint attention on concatenated streams, split back) → Shared FFN → pass updated \(Z_x, Z_c\) to next layer → Final \(Z_x\) projected to velocity field \(v_\theta\) → Euler integration for sampling.

### Critical Path
1. Tokenization: Apply patch embeddings to endogenous and exogenous streams.
2. Conditioning: Construct \(Z_y\) from averaged exogenous tokens and timestep embedding.
3. For each DiTS block:
   - Compute α,β,γ for Time, Variate, FFN from \(Z_y\).
   - Apply Time Attention (shared) to each stream with modulation.
   - Apply Variate Attention (joint) to the two streams with modulation.
   - Apply shared FFN with modulation.
4. Output: Linear projection from final endogenous tokens to velocity prediction.
5. Sampling: Use Euler steps to integrate velocity and generate forecast.

### Design Tradeoffs
- **Dual-stream vs. single-stream:** Dual-stream provides explicit decoupling but adds complexity; single-stream (e.g., LDT) is simpler but may underutilize exogenous information.
- **Joint vs. cross attention:** Joint attention (bidirectional stream interaction) enables richer conditioning than cross-attention (unidirectional from covariate to target), but may increase computational cost.
- **Shared vs. separate parameters:** Sharing Time Attention and FFN across streams reduces overfitting and exploits common temporal patterns, while separate projections in Variate Attention respect modality heterogeneity.
- **Flow matching vs. diffusion:** Rectified flow offers stable training and fast sampling (few steps) but may be less expressive for highly multi-modal distributions compared to Gaussian diffusion.

### Failure Signatures
1. **Poor exogenous utilization:** Minimal performance gap between models with/without covariates indicates ineffective conditioning (e.g., modulation or joint attention failing).
2. **Training instability:** Diverging loss or NaNs suggest unsuitable noise schedule, learning rate, or re-normalization issues.
3. **Insufficient cross-variate capture:** Failure to outperform channel-independent models (PatchTST) on strongly correlated data suggests Variate Attention is not contributing (e.g., α_V near zero).
4. **High variance in probabilistic metrics:** Overly wide or narrow prediction intervals indicate poor uncertainty calibration; may need more inference steps or flow matching adjustments.
5. **Data leakage:** Using future statistics during normalization inflates performance; must ensure strict historical-only normalization.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
### Open Question 1
Under what data characteristics (e.g., number of exogenous variates, correlation structure, predictive strength) does the modality decoupling in DiTS provide measurable benefit over unified stream or alternative modality definitions? The paper does not systematically vary exogenous complexity to probe boundaries.

### Open Question 2
How robust is DiTS's reliance on the "low-rank property" of multivariate dependencies when applied to time series with high-rank or complex non-linear inter-variate relationships? The assumption is stated but not validated; effective rank analysis or synthetic experiments are needed.

### Open Question 3
What are the comparative advantages and trade-offs of rectified flow matching versus standard diffusion models (e.g., DDPM) for probabilistic time series forecasting with covariates? The paper cites stability and efficiency but provides no direct ablation against other diffusion formulations.

### Open Question 4
Can more expressive conditioning mechanisms beyond AdaLN and joint attention (e.g., cross-attention at every layer, hierarchical fusion, learned query-based conditioning) further enhance DiTS's ability to incorporate exogenous information? The ablation tested only simplistic alternatives; more sophisticated schemes remain unexplored.

## Limitations
- **Noising scope ambiguity:** It is unclear whether the diffusion noising applies only to the future endogenous segment or the entire series; this affects training dynamics and must be clarified.
- **Hyperparameter gaps:** Exact values for attention heads, dropout, FFN activation, whether Time Attention is truly weight-shared, and training details (optimizer betas, weight decay, LR schedule, early stopping) are unspecified.
- **Normalization leakage risk:** Exogenous normalization protocol (per-sample vs. per-variate, inclusion of future statistics) is undefined; improper handling could cause data leakage and overestimated performance.
