# The Magic Correlations: Understanding Knowledge Transfer from Pretraining to Supervised Fine-Tuning

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2602.11217
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.11217
- Reference count: 40
- Primary result: Knowledge transfer from pretraining to SFT varies by capability category, model scale, and data mixture; larger models preserve accuracy rankings better but lose confidence transfer, and educational filtering has scale-dependent effects on NLI.

## Executive Summary
The paper studies how pretraining data mixtures and model size affect knowledge transfer to supervised fine-tuning (SFT). Across nine data mixtures and two scales (240M, 1B), they evaluate 20 benchmarks in four categories using correlation metrics. Key findings include: larger models maintain accuracy rankings across mixtures (0.59 vs 0.49 correlation) but weaken confidence transfer (0.41 vs 0.66); transfer is high for Science and Commonsense but low for Semantic; scaling changes intra-category dynamics from competition to synergy for Science; calibration patterns differ across categories; and FineWeb-Edu filtering improves Science accuracy but yields non-monotonic NLI effects depending on scale. The work highlights that proxy evaluations may not generalize and raises open questions about underlying mechanisms.

## Method Summary
They trained decoder-only transformers (240M, 1B) on nine pretraining data mixtures built from three web sources (RefinedWeb, FineWeb-Edu, DCLM) at three proportions (65% web, 45% web, 25% web) and two code sources (StarCoder, Stack-v2). Pretraining used AdamW with cosine learning rate for 200k steps, processing 12B tokens for 240M and 52B for 1B. All models were then fine-tuned for 5 epochs on the fixed Tulu-v2-mix dataset with a lower learning rate. Evaluation used 20 multiple-choice benchmarks grouped into Science, Commonsense, Semantic, and NLI. For each model, they recorded accuracy and average confidence (probability of selected answer). They computed cross-stage Pearson correlations for accuracy (`r_stage_acc`) and confidence (`r_stage_conf`), intra-category pairwise coherence metrics (`r_PT_intra`, `r_SFT_intra`, `r_cross_intra`), and alignment between accuracy and confidence (`r_align`). These metrics quantified how pretraining performance predicted SFT performance across mixtures and scales.

## Key Results
- Larger models preserve accuracy rankings better (mean PT→SFT accuracy correlation 0.59 vs 0.49) but lose confidence transfer (0.41 vs 0.66).
- Transfer reliability is category-dependent: Science and Commonsense show high accuracy correlation (>0.5), Semantic shows weak transfer; confidence persists strongly for reasoning tasks at smaller scale.
- Scaling shifts intra-category dynamics from competition to synergy for Science, while confidence coherence degrades with scale except for NLI.
- Calibration varies: Science is well-aligned (performance-confidence r~0.8), Commonsense and Semantic are miscalibrated (r negative); alignment patterns persist except NLI reorganizes (PT-SFT alignment r≈-0.4).
- Educational filtering (FineWeb-Edu) improves Science accuracy but causes scale-dependent NLI effects: at 240M it boosts NLI accuracy (+5.0pp) yet degrades alignment (Δr=-0.80), while at 1B it reduces accuracy (-4.4pp) and alignment improves slightly.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The paper demonstrates that correlation protocols effectively capture knowledge transfer by aggregating performance across diverse data mixtures and scales. This approach works because it controls for architectural and training hyperparameters, isolating the effects of data composition and model size. The observed patterns—such as stronger accuracy transfer for Science and larger models, but weaker confidence transfer—suggest that different capabilities rely on distinct representational properties that are differentially preserved during SFT. The study does not experimentally pinpoint neural mechanisms; instead it provides a descriptive account of how transfer reliability varies, leaving causal explanations for future work.

## Foundational Learning
- **Knowledge Transfer**: The phenomenon where learning from one task or dataset (pretraining) improves performance on another (fine-tuning). *Why needed*: Central concept the paper investigates. *Quick check*: Do models with higher pretraining accuracy tend to have higher SFT accuracy for the same benchmark?
- **Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT)**: Additional supervised training on a target dataset to adapt a pretrained model to specific tasks. *Why needed*: The second stage of the transfer process studied. *Quick check*: Does SFT improve performance on downstream benchmarks compared to pretrained-only models?
- **Pearson Correlation (r)**: A measure of linear association between two variables, ranging from -1 to 1. *Why needed*: Primary metric to quantify transfer (e.g., `r_stage_acc`). *Quick check*: `r = cov(X,Y) / (σ_X σ_Y)`; positive r indicates that better pretraining performance predicts better SFT performance.
- **Calibration**: The degree to which a model's predicted confidence matches its actual accuracy. *Why needed*: Confidence transfer is a key outcome; misalignment indicates over- or under-confidence. *Quick check*: Compute `r_align` as correlation between accuracy and confidence across models; positive values indicate good calibration.
- **Scale Effects**: How increasing model size affects learning dynamics and transfer. *Why needed*: The paper compares 240M and 1B parameter models to assess scaling trends. *Quick check*: Does the correlation between pretraining and SFT accuracy change when moving from 240M to 1B?
- **Data Mixture**: The composition of pretraining corpora (proportions of web, code, curated data). *Why needed*: Main independent variable in the study. *Quick check*: How do different mixture ratios (P0, P1, P2) influence transfer metrics per category?

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component Map**: Pretraining Data Mixtures -> Pretrained Model (decoder-only Transformer) -> Supervised Fine-Tuning (Tulu-v2-mix) -> Evaluation Suite (20 multiple-choice benchmarks).
- **Critical Path**:
  1. Define nine pretraining mixtures with specific web/code/curated proportions.
  2. Pretrain two model scales (240M, 1B) for fixed token budgets (12B tokens for 240M, 52B for 1B) using AdamW and cosine LR.
  3. Fine-tune each pretrained checkpoint on Tulu-v2-mix for 5 epochs with a lower learning rate.
  4. Evaluate all models on the 20 benchmarks, recording accuracy and confidence.
  5. Compute correlation metrics (`r_stage_acc`, `r_stage_conf`, intra-category coherence, `r_align`) to analyze transfer.
- **Design Tradeoffs**:
  - *Scale*: Larger models improve accuracy transfer but reduce confidence coherence, indicating a trade-off between performance preservation and calibration.
  - *Data Mixture*: Adjusting proportions of web vs. code vs. curated data yields category-specific effects; e.g., FineWeb-Edu boosts Science but harms NLI alignment at small scale.
  - *Evaluation*: Confidence calculation (e.g., sum of log-probs vs. token probability) can affect alignment metrics; consistent scoring is crucial.
- **Failure Signatures**:
  - Near-zero or negative `r_stage_acc` for benchmarks like WiC, WSC, MultiRC, and MNLI (at 240M) signals failed accuracy transfer.
  - Negative `r_align` for Commonsense and Semantic categories indicates systemic miscalibration.
  - Non-monotonic NLI accuracy with FineWeb-Edu: positive gain at 240M (+5.0pp) but negative at 1B (-4.4pp) suggests scale-dependent interactions.
- **First Experiments**:
  1. Replicate the correlation analysis using alternative confidence scoring (e.g., per-token probability) to test robustness of transfer patterns.
  2. Ablate components of the Tulu-v2-mix SFT dataset to identify which tasks drive category-specific transfer.
  3. Vary the pretraining token budget (e.g., 6B tokens) to check if correlation trends hold with less data.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
1. Why do educational content filtering (FineWeb-Edu) effects on NLI accuracy and calibration reverse between 240M and 1B scales? The paper documents the reversal but does not explain whether it stems from representational capacity, optimization dynamics, or data distribution interactions.
2. What neural mechanisms explain the inverse scaling dynamics between accuracy and confidence transfer, where larger models strengthen accuracy correlation but weaken confidence correlation? The paper observes the pattern but does not hypothesize or test mechanistic causes.
3. How generalizable are the observed transfer patterns to domains beyond the evaluated benchmarks, such as long-context reasoning, code generation, or safety? The study focuses on 20 benchmarks in four categories; it is unknown whether the category-specific reliability or inverse scaling of confidence transfer holds for other capabilities.
4. What causes the shift from intra-category competition to synergy with model scale in Science benchmarks, and does this reflect fundamental changes in representational structure? The paper describes the transition but does not investigate whether it arises from unified feature spaces, optimization changes, or saturation of task-specific representations.

## Limitations
- The exact composition and prompting of the SFT dataset (Tulu-v2-mix) are not fully described, hindering precise replication.
- Evaluation protocol details (number of few-shot examples, temperature, confidence aggregation method) are omitted, which may affect confidence-based metrics.
- Tokenizer training data and algorithm, as well as hardware/software environment, are unspecified, limiting faithful reproduction.
