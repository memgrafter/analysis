# Reasoning Beyond Literal: Cross-style Multimodal Reasoning for Figurative Language Understanding

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID**: 2601.17197
- **Source URL**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.17197
- **Reference count**: 15
- **Primary result**: A three-step framework (CoT distillation, SFT, RLVR) applied to a small VLM (Qwen2.5-VL-3B) yields state-of-the-art multimodal figurative language understanding with ~3-8% accuracy gains and strong cross-style transfer, outperforming much larger models.

## Executive Summary
This paper tackles the challenge of multimodal figurative language understanding—including sarcasm, humor, offense, and metaphor—by proposing a three-step framework: distilling chain-of-thought (CoT) reasoning traces from a large teacher model, fine-tuning a smaller student model on these traces, and refining it with reinforcement learning using verifiable rewards. The approach achieves substantial accuracy improvements (3-8% over baselines) across all four styles and demonstrates effective cross-style transfer, particularly between sarcasm and humor. Notably, a single lightweight model (Qwen2.5-VL-3B) trained jointly on all styles surpasses much larger open- and closed-source models (e.g., Gemini 2.5 Flash, LLaMA-90B-Vision) on several benchmarks while providing transparent, inspectable reasoning traces.

## Method Summary
The method comprises three stages. First, a teacher VLM (LLaMA3.2-90B-Vision-Instruct) generates step-by-step CoT reasoning traces for each style using a five-step schema; these traces are filtered to ensure validity and alignment with ground-truth labels. Second, a student VLM (Qwen2.5-VL-3B-Instruct) is supervised fine-tuned (SFT) on the filtered CoTs, learning to produce structured reasoning before the final prediction. Third, the SFT model is further refined via reinforcement learning with verifiable rewards (RLVR) using the GRPO algorithm, which optimizes for both label correctness and format adherence while regularizing against the reference policy via a KL penalty. For a generalized model, the CoT data from all styles are concatenated before joint SFT and RLVR. The pipeline is evaluated on standard datasets: MMSD2.0 (sarcasm), Memotion (humor/offense), and MultiMET (metaphor).

## Key Results
- Incorporating explicit reasoning traces improves accuracy by ~3-8% over baselines across sarcasm, humor, offense, and metaphor.
- Strong cross-style transfer is achieved, especially from sarcasm to humor and vice versa, where reasoning learned from one style boosts performance on another.
- A single lightweight model (Qwen2.5-VL-3B) trained jointly on all styles outperforms much larger open- and closed-source models on sarcasm, humor, and offense metrics, while also producing transparent reasoning traces.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The paper demonstrates three key mechanisms:

1. **Claim**: Incorporating explicit chain-of-thought (CoT) reasoning traces significantly improves multimodal figurative language understanding accuracy.
   - **Mechanism**: Distill step-by-step reasoning traces from a large teacher VLM (LLaMA3.2-90B-Vision-Instruct) via zero-shot prompting, filter for validity, then perform supervised fine-tuning (SFT) of a smaller student model (Qwen2.5-VL-3B) on these traces. This teaches the student to emulate structured reasoning before final prediction.
   - **Core assumption**: The teacher model's generated CoT captures correct and generalizable reasoning patterns that transfer to the student; the filtering step ensures only high-quality traces are used.
   - **Evidence anchors**: 
     - abstract: "incorporating explicit reasoning traces substantially improves accuracy (by ~3-8% over baselines)"
     - Table 2 (page 5) shows SFT on distilled CoTs yields ~1.9–4.5% absolute accuracy gains over SFT on binary labels across sarcasm, humor, offense, metaphor.
   - **Break condition**: If the teacher's CoT contains systematic biases or if the student's capacity is insufficient to learn the reasoning patterns, the improvement may diminish or vanish.

2. **Claim**: Reasoning capabilities acquired for one figurative style transfer to other styles, especially semantically related ones (sarcasm ↔ humor), enhancing performance without additional CoT distillation for the target style.
   - **Mechanism**: After SFT on CoTs from a source style, the model undergoes reinforcement learning with verifiable rewards (RLVR) on a target style's data. The reasoning skills learned during SFT provide a foundation that RLVR adapts to the new style.
   - **Core assumption**: Shared reasoning processes (e.g., incongruity detection, intent inference) exist across different figurative styles, allowing positive transfer.
   - **Evidence anchors**: 
     - abstract: "reasoning learned in one style can transfer to others—especially between related styles like sarcasm and humor."
     - Table 3 (page 6) shows cross-style SFT→GRPO results; e.g., SFT on sarcasm + GRPO on humor yields 78.91% accuracy vs. GRPO-only 65.85%. Figure 3 visualizes gains.
   - **Break condition**: Transfer weakens or becomes negative when styles are conceptually distant (e.g., metaphor to offense) or when the source style's reasoning patterns are too specialized.

3. **Claim**: Joint training across multiple figurative styles produces a single lightweight VLM that generalizes robustly and outperforms much larger open- and closed-source models on several benchmarks.
   - **Mechanism**: Distill CoTs from all styles combined, then perform SFT followed by RLVR on the unified dataset. This exposes the model to diverse reasoning patterns, forcing it to learn a generalized reasoning mechanism applicable across styles.
   - **Core assumption**: The diversity of styles provides complementary training signals that boost overall reasoning rather than causing interference.
   - **Evidence anchors**:
     - abstract: "training jointly across styles yields a generalized reasoning VLM that outperforms much larger open- and closed-source models."
     - Table 4 (page 7) shows the combined SFT→GRPO model (Qwen2.5-VL-3B) achieving 90.23% sarcasm, 80.71% humor, 61.49% offense, 69.14% metaphor, surpassing LLaMA90B-Vision and Gemini 2.5 Flash on most styles.
   - **Break condition**: Joint training may degrade performance on individual styles if style-specific nuances are lost, especially when training data is limited (as seen in Table 5 for offense and metaphor with fixed 5k budget).

## Foundational Learning
- **Concept**: Chain-of-Thought (CoT) Reasoning
  - **Why needed here**: The framework hinges on distilling and training on structured reasoning traces; understanding CoT explains the performance boost.
  - **Quick check question**: What are the five steps in the CoT prompt for sarcasm detection? (Answer: Image description, caption interpretation, mismatch detection, intent inference, final label.)

- **Concept**: Reinforcement Learning with Verifiable Rewards (RLVR)
  - **Why needed here**: RLVR refines the SFT model using binary correctness rewards, aligning reasoning with accurate predictions without human feedback.
  - **Quick check question**: How does RLVR differ from standard RLHF, and why is verifiable reward suitable for this task? (Answer: RLVR uses deterministic ground-truth labels as reward instead of a learned reward model; suitable because labels are objective.)

- **Concept**: Cross-modal Incongruity Detection
  - **Why needed here**: Figurative meanings often arise from discrepancies between visual and textual content; this is a core reasoning step in all styles.
  - **Quick check question**: In the sarcasm example "when it says shipping will be 3-7 days and it comes in 3", what is the mismatch? (Answer: The text says shipping will take 3-7 days, but the image shows a package arriving in 3 days, implying it arrived faster than stated, suggesting sarcasm.)

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map**:
  Teacher VLM (LLaMA3.2-90B-Vision-Instruct) -> Filtering -> Student VLM (Qwen2.5-VL-3B-Instruct) -> SFT module -> RLVR module (GRPO) -> Final model.
  For the unified model: Combine all style data before SFT and RLVR.

- **Critical path**:
  1. Distill CoTs from teacher for each style (separately or combined).
  2. Filter CoTs to obtain a reasoning dataset.
  3. SFT student on the reasoning dataset to produce an SFT model.
  4. Apply RLVR on target style(s) data using SFT model as reference to produce the final model.
  5. For a generalized model: concatenate all styles' filtered CoTs before steps 3–4.

- **Design tradeoffs**:
  - Student size vs. performance: Using a 3B parameter model reduces compute but may limit reasoning capacity compared to larger students.
  - CoT filtering strictness: Tight filtering ensures quality but may discard useful imperfect traces; looser filtering risks noise.
  - RLVR reward design: Relying on binary label correctness may not reward nuanced reasoning quality and could lead to reward hacking if format enforcement is too rigid.
  - Joint training vs. style-specific: Combined data improves cross-style transfer but may cause catastrophic interference on styles with limited data.

- **Failure signatures**:
  - CoT generation: Teacher produces low-quality or hallucinated reasoning; filtering removes too many samples (<10% retention) → insufficient training data.
  - SFT phase: Student fails to learn CoT structure (e.g., missing `<think>` tags) → RLVR unstable.
  - RLVR phase: Model overfits to format reward, ignoring accuracy; or KL penalty β mis‑tuned causing divergence.
  - Cross-style: Negative transfer when training on one style degrades performance on another (e.g., metaphor→sarcasm gains negative).

- **First 3 experiments**:
  1. Replicate Table 2: Ablation over training stages (Zero-shot CoT, SFT binary, SFT-CoT, GRPO-only, SFT→GRPO) to verify incremental gains of each component.
  2. Run cross-style transfer: For each source style, SFT on its CoTs then GRPO on each target style; reproduce Table 3 and Figure 3 to confirm transfer patterns.
  3. Compare style-specific vs. combined with fixed budget: Sample 5k per style vs. 1.25k each combined, train SFT→GRPO, evaluate on each test set; reproduce Table 5 to assess data scale vs. diversity.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- How does the architecture and scale of the teacher model influence the quality of distilled reasoning traces and the student's final performance and generalization?
- Do the multimodal reasoning capabilities learned by the model generalize to out-of-distribution datasets for each figurative style?
- Does adopting a style-specific chain-of-thought schema (instead of a uniform five-step template) improve multimodal figurative reasoning performance?
- Can more nuanced, style-adaptive reward functions in the RLVR stage enhance performance on subjective styles (offense, metaphor) where binary accuracy is an imperfect supervision signal?

## Limitations
- The KL penalty coefficient β in the GRPO objective is not specified, which is critical for RLVR stability.
- Exact filtering rules for teacher CoTs (beyond regex completeness) are undisclosed, hindering full reproducibility.
- Distributed training details (batch size, gradient accumulation) and other GRPO hyperparameters are missing.

## Confidence
- **Mechanism 1 (CoT distillation improves accuracy)**: High confidence. Consistent absolute gains of 1.9–4.5% across all styles in Table 2.
- **Mechanism 2 (Cross-style transfer)**: Medium confidence. Positive transfer shown between sarcasm and humor, but break condition suggests limited transfer for distant styles; landscape under-explored.
- **Mechanism 3 (Joint training yields a lightweight generalist)**: Medium confidence. The 3B model surpasses larger VLMs on most styles, yet performance on offense and metaphor drops with limited fixed budget (Table 5), indicating data imbalance sensitivity.

## Next Checks
- **KL penalty sweep**: Systematically vary β (e.g., 0.01, 0.1, 0.5) during GRPO and track validation accuracy/KL divergence to identify the regime that reproduces the reported gains.
- **Human audit of teacher CoTs**: Annotate a random sample of filtered CoTs per style for reasoning errors, hallucination, and bias; correlate teacher CoT quality with student performance to test the assumed transferability.
- **Comprehensive cross‑style transfer matrix**: For each source style, apply SFT followed by GRPO on every other target style (including “distant” pairs like metaphor→offense) to empirically map the transfer landscape and validate the claim that semantically related styles benefit most.
