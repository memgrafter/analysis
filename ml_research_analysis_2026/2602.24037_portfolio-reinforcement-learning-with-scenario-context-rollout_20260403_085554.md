# Portfolio Reinforcement Learning with Scenario-Context Rollout

## Quick Facts
- **ArXiv ID:** 2602.24037
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.24037
- **Reference count:** 40
- **Primary result:** Sharpe ratio improved by up to 76% and maximum drawdown reduced by up to 53% compared to classic and RL baselines, with significantly lower turnover.

## Executive Summary
The paper tackles portfolio rebalancing under market regime shifts by proposing macro‑conditioned scenario‑context rollout (SCR). SCR generates plausible next‑day return scenarios for the current regime by retrieving similar historical macro events from a ShockLedger. A key challenge is the reward‑transition mismatch that arises when scenario‑based rewards are combined with historical tape transitions in temporal‑difference learning, which destabilizes critic training. The authors resolve this by augmenting the critic’s bootstrap target with a counterfactual continuation derived from the scenario mean returns, and blending it with the tape‑based target via a mixing coefficient. In out‑of‑sample tests across 31 U.S. equity and ETF universes, the full method (SCR‑PPO‑Full) achieves substantial risk‑adjusted return improvements and lower turnover relative to strong baselines.

## Method Summary
The method integrates Scenario‑Context Rollout (SCR) with Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO). SCR maintains a library of historical (return, regime descriptor ψ) pairs; at each decision step it retrieves k nearest‑neighbor descriptors, forms a uniform mixture of their returns as the conditional distribution, scales by a stress severity multiplier gₜ, and samples S scenarios. The policy network πθ takes the state ϕₜ (including market features and aggregated scenario statistics) and outputs an unconstrained action, which is projected to feasible weights wₜ. The reward rₜ is computed as the scenario‑averaged excess return minus a risk penalty and a turnover regularization. To train the critic, the authors identify a mismatch: the TD bootstrap uses the realized next state while the reward is based on scenarios. They construct a counterfactual next state by applying the same state update to the scenario mean return, and then blend the tape‑based and counterfactual TD targets using a mixing coefficient β_cf. This yields an augmented target that mitigates the fixed‑point bias of the hybrid Bellman operator. The policy is updated with PPO‑Clip and Generalized Advantage Estimation derived from these augmented targets.

## Key Results
- Sharpe ratio improved by up to 76% relative to classic and RL baselines.
- Maximum drawdown reduced by up to 53%.
- Significantly lower turnover compared to baselines.
- Ablation study confirms the full method (SCR‑PPO‑Full) outperforms variants lacking either the counterfactual continuation or scenario‑based rewards.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1: Scenario‑Context Rollout (SCR) Generates Regime‑Conditioned Returns
- **Claim:** SCR generates plausible next‑day multivariate return scenarios under stress events without data leakage.
- **Mechanism:** A regime descriptor ψₜ (computed from macro shocks, channel activations χₜ, and exposure summaries, excluding portfolio history) indexes a stored library of historical macro scenarios. For the current ψₜ, SCR retrieves the k nearest‑neighbor descriptors from past dates u < t and forms the conditional distribution P_scen(·|ψₜ) as a uniform mixture over their associated return vectors. A regime‑context multiplier gₜ (derived from a stress severity score) gates the scenario returns to attenuate risk‑taking during stress.
- **Core assumption:** Historical macro‑scenario return vectors, when paired with a similar regime descriptor, are valid counterfactual samples for the current decision; i.e., the joint distribution of returns given regime is stationary enough for retrieval to be meaningful.
- **Evidence anchors:**  
  - [abstract] “We propose macro‑conditioned scenario‑context rollout (SCR) that generates plausible next‑day multivariate return scenarios under stress events.”  
  - [section 2.1] Full specification of regime embedding, ShockLedger, kNN retrieval, and gₜ computation.  
- **Break condition:** A novel regime with no historically similar ψₜ forces retrieval from unrelated episodes, yielding economically implausible scenarios; an inaccurate gₜ (e.g., severity baseline quantile outdated) mis‑scales risk exposure.

### Mechanism 2: Counterfactual Continuation Aligns TD Targets
- **Claim:** Constructing a counterfactual next‑state via the scenario mean ¯r_scenₜ₊₁ aligns the TD bootstrap target with scenario‑based rewards, mitigating the reward–transition mismatch.
- **Mechanism:** After sampling S scenarios {Rₜ₊₁ˢ} ∼ P_scen(·|ψₜ), compute the Monte Carlo mean ¯r_scenₜ₊₁. Apply the same state‑update function Upd (used for the realized tape) to this mean: ϕ_cfₜ₊₁ = Upd(ϕₜ, wₜ, ¯r_scenₜ₊₁). This state represents the expected continuation under the scenario distribution while using only information available at time t (leak‑safe).
- **Core assumption:** The value function V is sufficiently smooth in the outcome‑updated memory component of ϕ such that E[V(Upd(ϕ,w,R))] ≈ V(Upd(ϕ,w,E[R])); Lipschitz continuity in that component justifies using the mean as a proxy for the full expectation.
- **Evidence anchors:**  
  - [section 2.2] Eq. (12) defines ϕ_cfₜ₊₁ = Upd(ϕₜ, wₜ, ¯r_scenₜ₊₁).  
  - [section 3] Theorem 3.5 derives a Wasserstein‑form bound on the one‑step MSE when mixing Y^r and Y^c, explicitly featuring the scenario variance σ²_scen(ψ).  
- **Break condition:** Scenario returns are multi‑modal or have heavy tails, making the mean a poor representative; Upd may be highly nonlinear in the return vector, violating the smoothness assumption and introducing bias.

### Mechanism 3: Mixed Bootstrap Target Stabilizes Critic
- **Claim:** Augmenting the critic’s bootstrap target as Y^B_cf = (1−β_cf)·Y^r + β_cf·Y^c stabilizes learning by interpolating between the tape‑based target (low variance, high bias from mismatch) and the counterfactual target (higher variance, lower bias), thereby reducing the fixed‑point bias of the hybrid Bellman operator.
- **Mechanism:** The one‑step TD target is a linear combination of (i) Y^r = rₜ + δV(ϕₜ₊₁) using the realized continuation and (ii) Y^c = rₜ + δV(ϕ_cfₜ₊₁) using the counterfactual continuation. The mixing weight β_cf ∈ [0,1] balances the error terms: squared bias from the mismatch (Wasserstein distance Δ²(ϕ,ψ)) and squared variance from scenario approximation (σ²_scen(ψ)).
- **Core assumption:** The critic’s target mixing can be treated as a static convex combination per time step; the fixed‑point bias of the hybrid operator is proportional to the Wasserstein mismatch and can be reduced by moving toward the scenario‑consistent operator.
- **Evidence anchors:**  
  - [section 2.2] Eq. (13) defines Y^B_cf with mixing coefficient β_cf.  
  - [section 3] Lemma 3.1 isolates the mismatch purely in the continuation term; Proposition 3.2 bounds the operator deviation; Corollary 3.6 derives the optimal mixing weight β⋆ = A/(A+B) that minimizes the bound.  
  - [section 4.3] Ablation results (SCR‑PPO‑NoCF → SCR‑PPO‑Full) show reduced critic residual (Resid‑AUC 0.087 → 0.075) and improved risk‑adjusted returns.  
- **Break condition:** Estimated A (mismatch) or B (scenario variance) is inaccurate, leading to a suboptimal β_cf; extreme values (β_cf = 0 or 1) revert to the biased high‑variance or high‑bias regimes, respectively.

**Concept: Offline Reinforcement Learning & Distribution Shift**

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component map:**  
Historical Tape Buffer (ϕₜ, rₜ₊₁, Upd) -> Policy Network (πθ) -> Projection (Πₜ) -> wₜ -> SCR Module (ψₜ) -> Scenarios & gₜ -> Reward Calculator (rₜ, ¯r) -> State Update (Upd) -> Next States (ϕₜ₊₁, ϕ_cfₜ₊₁) -> Critic Network (Vω)

**Critical path (per training epoch):**  
1. Traverse historical dates t = 0 … T−1 in chronological order.  
2. Build state ϕₜ from available data up to t.  
3. Sample action aₜ ∼ πθ(·|ϕₜ); compute wₜ = Πₜ(aₜ).  
4. Call SCR: obtain P_scen(·|ψₜ), gₜ, and sample S scenarios.  
5. Compute scenario‑averaged reward rₜ and mean ¯r_scenₜ₊₁.  
6. Read realized rₜ₊₁ from tape; compute ϕₜ₊₁ = Upd(ϕₜ, wₜ, rₜ₊₁).  
7. Form counterfactual: ϕ_cfₜ₊₁ = Upd(ϕₜ, wₜ, ¯r_scenₜ₊₁).  
8. Assemble augmented TD target Y^B_cf (using current Vω).  
9. Store (ϕₜ, Y^B_cf) for critic update and compute GAE advantages for actor update.  
10. After full pass, update Vω (MSE regression) and πθ (PPO‑Clip with advantages).

**Design tradeoffs:**  
- **β_cf (mixing weight):** Low β preserves tape continuity but retains mismatch bias; high β reduces bias but amplifies variance from scenario approximation.  
- **S (scenario samples):** Larger S reduces Monte Carlo noise in rₜ and ¯r at the cost of memory and compute.  
- **k (neighbors in SCR):** Small k yields more regime‑specific scenarios but may be sparsely populated; large k increases noise.  
- **λᵨ (risk penalty):** Controls tail‑risk sensitivity; must be calibrated to avoid over‑penalization that distorts the scenario distribution.

**Failure signatures:**  
- Sustained high Bellman residual (resid_l2) during critic training indicates divergence or unresolved mismatch.  
- Growing cumulative gap between scenario‑scored return ∑rₜ and realized tape return ∑realizedₜ suggests misalignment of value estimates.  
- Excessive turnover (ℓ₁ weight change) may signal insufficient regularization or mis‑scaled gₜ.  
- ShockLedger activation shows many novel channels; could indicate regime detector threshold too strict or true structural breaks.

**First 3 experiments:**  
1. **Ablation study:** Run PPO(Historical), SCR‑PPO‑RewardOnly, SCR‑PPO‑NoCF, SCR‑PPO‑Full. Compare Sharpe, MaxDD, Resid‑AUC, and Gap_final.  
2. **β_cf sensitivity sweep:** Train with β_cf ∈ {0, 0.25, 0.5, 0.75, 1} on a validation universe; select value that balances Sharpe and critic stability (low Resid‑AUC).  
3. **Scenario‑to‑real validation:** Plot cumulative average daily return under scenario scoring (∑rₜ) versus cumulative realized return (∑realizedₜ) on test dates; a well‑tuned β_cf should keep these trajectories close (small Gap_final).

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
1. **Adaptive β_cf estimation:** Can the counterfactual mixing coefficient β_cf be adaptively estimated during training to optimize the bias‑variance trade‑off across different market regimes?  
   - *Basis in paper:* The analysis derives an optimal β formula (Eq. 22) that depends on mismatch A and scenario variance B, but the paper uses a fixed β_cf=0.5 in all experiments (Sec. 4.4) and notes the need for a trade‑off.  
   - *Why unresolved:* The true mismatch and variance are unknown and likely non‑stationary, so a fixed β may be suboptimal in some conditions.  
   - *What evidence would resolve it:* An online estimation method (e.g., using TD errors for A and rolling scenario dispersion for B) that adjusts β; empirical comparison of adaptive vs fixed β across all 31 universes, showing improved Sharpe/Calmar and lower residual error.

2. **Cross‑market generalisation:** Does the SCR framework remain effective in non‑US equity markets or alternative asset classes (e.g., crypto, FX) where regime dynamics and data properties differ?  
   - *Basis in paper:* The experiments are conducted exclusively on 31 U.S. equity and ETF universes (Sec. 4.1), and the authors do not claim cross‑market validity.  
   - *Why unresolved:* The scenario retrieval and regime detection rely on patterns present in U.S. equities; other markets may have different tail dependencies, liquidity profiles, or structural breaks that could degrade performance.  
   - *What evidence would resolve it:* Apply the same SCR pipeline to historical datasets from at least three distinct non‑US markets (e.g., European equities, crypto assets, foreign exchange) and compare risk‑adjusted returns and drawdowns against strong baselines; analyse retrieval quality metrics (e.g., nearest‑neighbor similarity) to confirm scenario relevance.

3. **Sensitivity to k (neighbors):** How sensitive is SCR to the hyperparameter k (number of nearest neighbors) in scenario retrieval, and can we learn an optimal data‑driven selection?  
   - *Basis in paper:* The method uses a k‑nearest neighbor retrieval (Eq. 6) but the paper does not discuss the choice of k or provide sensitivity results; this is a key design decision.  
   - *Why unresolved:* k controls the bias‑variance of the scenario distribution—small k yields high variance but regime‑specific scenarios, large k increases bias by mixing distant regimes. An optimal k may vary over time and across universes.  
   - *What evidence would resolve it:* Perform an ablation over a range of k (e.g., 1, 3, 5, 10, 20, 50) on all 31 universes, reporting performance metrics and scenario diversity statistics; possibly develop a validation‑based heuristic (e.g., maximize scenario‑real alignment) to select k online.

4. **Extension to off‑policy RL:** Can the counterfactual continuation augmentation be combined with off‑policy RL algorithms (e.g., DDPG, SAC) without destabilizing learning?  
   - *Basis in paper:* The authors implement SCR with PPO (an on‑policy algorithm) only; the mismatch analysis is general but the empirical validation does not cover off‑policy methods, which are more prone to bootstrapping errors.  
   - *Why unresolved:* Off‑policy critics often suffer from overestimation bias; adding scenario‑based rewards and counterfactual targets could exacerbate this, requiring careful adaptation (e.g., clipping, twin critics).  
   - *What evidence would resolve it:* Implement the SCR pipeline with SAC and DDPG, incorporating the same augmented target (or its variants), and measure training stability (critic loss, TD error) and out‑of‑sample performance relative to PPO and to off‑policy baselines without augmentation.

## Limitations
- **Scenario stationarity:** SCR assumes historical macro scenarios are reusable for new regimes; structural market shifts can break k‑nearest‑neighbour relevance.
- **Counterfactual mean approximation:** Using the scenario mean for state update relies on smoothness of the value function in return space; heavy‑tailed or multimodal scenario distributions may introduce bias.
- **Optimal β mixing:** Optimal β⋆ = A/(A+B) requires accurate estimation of mismatch A and scenario variance B; noisy estimates could yield suboptimal mixing.
