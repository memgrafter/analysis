# Measuring Inclusion in Interaction: Inclusion Analytics for Human-AI Collaborative Learning

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2602.09269
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.09269
- **Reference count:** 31
- **Primary result:** A discourse-based framework quantifies dynamic inclusion in human-AI collaboration via participation equity, affective climate, and epistemic equity metrics, showing greater participation imbalance in human-AI teams vs. human-only and higher epistemic uptake by humans over AI.

## Executive Summary
This paper tackles the challenge of measuring inclusion dynamically in human-AI collaborative problem solving, moving beyond static demographic surveys or post-hoc self-reports. It introduces *inclusion analytics*, a framework that operationalizes three dimensions of inclusion—participation equity, affective climate, and epistemic equity—directly from discourse. Using both simulated conversations (with controlled balance/imbalance) and empirical human-AI team data from the NASA Moon Survival Task, the metrics are evaluated. Results demonstrate that participation equity reliably distinguishes balanced from imbalanced teams and reveals greater imbalance in human-AI teams compared to human-only teams. For epistemic equity, humans show significantly higher idea uptake and endorsement than AI teammates. However, the affective climate metric (politeness uptake) fails to detect a human-AI difference in real data, and the epistemic metric faces challenges from task-driven semantic similarity and generic backchannels. The work shows promise for process-oriented inclusion measurement but highlights the need for refinement to capture nuanced, context-sensitive dynamics in human-AI interaction.

## Method Summary
The framework computes three inclusion metrics per speaker from conversational transcripts:

1.  **Participation Equity:** Uses the Inequality of Participation (IP) index derived from turn or word counts. IP = (1/n) * Σ|Eᵢ - Oᵢ| / (1 - 1/n), where n is speaker count, Eᵢ is the expected cumulative proportion (i/n), and Oᵢ the observed cumulative proportion under perfect equality (IP=0) to maximum imbalance (IP→1).
2.  **Affective Climate (Politeness Uptake):** For each utterance, a 11-dimensional politeness rate vector is created via regex patterns. Politeness uptake for an utterance is the mean cosine similarity between its politeness vector and the vectors of the next K=4 utterances by *other* speakers. Speaker-level scores aggregate these.
3.  **Epistemic Equity (Idea Uptake):** Combines two sub-metrics.
    *   *Adjusted Semantic Uptake:* Uses sentence-BERT embeddings. For utterance *i*, compute mean cosine similarity *Uᵢ* to next K=4 utterances by others. A baseline μ<sub>null,i</sub> is estimated via N=100 Monte Carlo samples of K random utterances from the same conversation, excluding a temporal window around *i* (radius δ unreported). Adjusted score Aᵢ = Uᵢ - μ<sub>null,i</sub>.
    *   *Endorsement Uptake:* Uses regex to detect endorsement markers. For utterance *i*, compute weighted sum Eᵢ over next K=3 turns by others, with exponential decay λ=0.7, excluding self-endorsements.

Metrics are aggregated per speaker. Evaluation compares balanced vs. imbalanced simulated teams (Mann-Whitney U on team-level IP; speaker-level means for others) and humans vs. AI in empirical treatment teams.

## Key Results
-   **Participation Equity:** Successfully distinguished balanced (IP=0.09) vs. imbalanced (IP=0.45) simulated teams (p<.005). In empirical human-AI teams, word-count IP was higher (more imbalanced) in human-AI teams (IP=0.64) vs. human-only teams (IP=0.39) (p<.005).
-   **Affective Climate (Politeness Uptake):** Behaved as expected in simulation (balanced > imbalanced) but showed no significant difference between humans and AI in empirical human-AI teams (p=.15).
-   **Epistemic Equity:** In empirical data, humans had significantly higher adjusted semantic uptake (p<.005) and endorsement uptake (p<.005) than AI teammates. Simulation results for epistemic equity were inconclusive due to task-driven similarity and generic backchannels inflating scores.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The framework operationalizes inclusion as observable, process-oriented properties of interaction rather than post-hoc or demographic proxies. **Participation equity** quantifies turn-taking balance, a fundamental structural feature of collaboration. **Affective climate** via politeness uptake aims to capture the reciprocation of social signals, reflecting mutual respect. **Epistemic equity** via idea uptake attempts to measure whether contributions are substantively engaged with (semantic integration) and explicitly validated (endorsement). By focusing on the *discourse process*, these metrics can, in principle, capture inclusion dynamics in real-time and across different group compositions, including mixed human-AI teams. The use of aggregated, speaker-level scores allows for comparison of individual experiences within a shared task.

## Foundational Learning
*   **Inequality of Participation (IP) Index:** A normalized measure of cumulative share deviation from perfect equality. *Why needed:* Provides a single, interpretable number summarizing participation balance, sensitive to the "long tail" of dominant speakers. *Quick check:* On a 3-person team with word counts [50, 50, 0], IP should be high (~0.67); on [34, 33, 33], IP should be low (~0.02).
*   **Politeness Marker Regex:** A set of lexical patterns for 11 politeness categories (e.g., gratitude, apology). *Why needed:* Offers a simple, explainable way to operationalize affective signals without complex NLP. *Quick check:* "Thank you" should match gratitude; "sorry" should match apology.
*   **Sentence-BERT Embeddings:** Pre-trained model (e.g., `all-MiniLM-L6-v2`) to generate dense vector representations of utterance meaning. *Why needed:* Enables computation of semantic similarity beyond lexical overlap, capturing paraphrased idea uptake. *Quick check:* Similar sentences like "We need water" and "H₂O is essential" should have high cosine similarity (>0.7).
*   **Monte Carlo Baseline for Adjusted Uptake:** Random sampling of temporally proximate but causally unrelated utterances to estimate task-driven similarity. *Why needed:* Aims to subtract the "background" semantic similarity inherent to discussing the same constrained problem. *Quick check:* For a random utterance, its adjusted uptake should center near zero if baseline is correctly estimated.
*   **Exponential Decay for Endorsement:** Weights nearby follow-up turns more heavily (λ=0.7). *Why needed:* Reflects that immediate responses are more likely to be direct reactions to the initiating utterance. *Quick check:* An endorsement in the very next turn contributes weight 0.7; in two turns, 0.49.

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component Map:**
Input Transcript -> Utterance Segmentation & Speaker ID -> Feature Extraction (Tokenization, Embedding, Regex Matching) -> Per-Utterance Metric Calculation (IP precursor, Politeness Uptake, Semantic/Endorsement Uptake) -> Speaker-Level Aggregation -> Team/Group Statistics -> Hypothesis Testing

**Critical Path:** The core computational pipeline flows from raw text to per-utterance scores (especially for politeness and epistemic uptake) and then aggregates to the speaker level for comparative analysis. The IP calculation is a simpler parallel path on aggregated counts.

**Design Tradeoffs:**
*   **Simplicity vs. Nuance:** Regex-based politeness is transparent but misses contextual/sarcastic uses; sentence-BERT captures semantics but struggles with negation and task-specific jargon.
*   **Generality vs. Task-Specificity:** The baseline subtraction aims for generalizability but may not fully account for task-specific repetition patterns (e.g., listing items).
*   **Real-time Potential vs. Batch Processing:** Metrics are computable sequentially (supporting real-time use) but require a look-ahead buffer (K turns) and, for adjusted uptake, random baseline sampling which is computationally heavier.

**Failure Signatures:**
*   **Politeness Uptake:** Near-zero averages across all speakers may indicate regex is too strict for informal chat or that the interaction is purely task-focused with minimal affective language.
*   **Adjusted Semantic Uptake:** Consistently high adjusted scores for all utterances suggest the baseline subtraction (δ, N) is insufficient to capture task-driven similarity; near-zero scores for all may indicate over-subtraction.
*   **Endorsement Uptake:** Extreme values (near 0 or K) for most speakers suggest regex patterns are either never or always matching, likely due to poor adaptation to conversational style.

**First 3 Experiments:**
1.  **Replicate Participation Equity:** Compute IP for a provided synthetic team transcript (e.g., 3 speakers, 100 turns) with known word counts. Validate against manual calculation of the formula.
2.  **Validate Politeness Regex:** Run the regex on a 100-utterance sample from the NASA Moon Survival dataset. Perform a quick manual annotation check: for any utterance with a regex match, verify if a human would classify it as containing that politeness category.
3.  **δ Sensitivity for Adjusted Uptake:** On a subset of empirical human-AI conversations, compute adjusted semantic uptake for a range of δ values (e.g., 2, 4, 8, 16). Plot the distribution of adjusted scores and the human-AI effect size. Identify if the p<.005 result is robust to reasonable δ changes.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
1.  **How can epistemic equity metrics be refined to distinguish genuine idea uptake from semantic similarity arising solely from task constraints, negations, or repetitive backchannels?** The current adjusted semantic uptake subtracts a random baseline but does not specifically account for negation or repetitive agreement that does not reflect genuine idea integration.
2.  **Why does politeness uptake fail to detect significant differences between human and AI teammates in real human-AI teams despite showing expected patterns in simulations, and how can it be optimized for informal, task-focused chat interactions?** The bag-of-words approach may be too simplistic for brief, goal-oriented conversations where politeness markers are sparse, and asymmetric interaction patterns may distort uptake calculations.
3.  **To what extent are the inclusion analytics metrics generalizable across diverse collaborative problem-solving tasks, cultural contexts, and interaction durations beyond the NASA Moon Survival Task and short chat-based experiments?** The framework has only been tested on one specific survival task; different tasks may have inherently different discourse structures and participation norms.
4.  **Can advanced natural language processing techniques (e.g., contextual embeddings, discourse-aware models) improve the sensitivity of inclusion analytics in capturing subtle, implicit forms of epistemic and affective marginalization that current surface-level methods miss?** Current reliance on simple bag-of-words and average sentence embeddings may overlook nuances like sarcasm, indirect disagreement, or contextualized idea value.

## Limitations
-   **Parameter Ambiguity:** Critical parameters are unreported or ambiguous, notably the exclusion radius δ for the adjusted idea uptake baseline and exact utterance segmentation/tokenization rules, limiting faithful reproduction.
-   **Metric Validity Concerns:** Politeness uptake showed no significant human-AI difference in real data (p=0.15). Simulation for epistemic equity was inconclusive due to task-driven similarity and backchannels inflating scores, questioning robustness.
-   **Contextual Specificity:** Evaluation is limited to a single task (NASA Moon Survival) and brief, anonymized chat. Generalizability to other task types, cultural settings, or longer-term collaboration is unestablished.

## Confidence
-   **Participation Equity:** **Medium**. The IP formula is explicit and simulation/empirical results are directionally consistent, but implementation pitfalls (small-n normalization) and unvalidated preprocessing could affect conclusions.
-   **Affective Climate (Politeness Uptake):** **Low**. Simulation worked, but the metric failed to detect a real human-AI difference. Regex coverage for casual chat is likely incomplete, undermining validity for real-world, informal CPS.
-   **Epistemic Equity (Idea Uptake):** **Medium**. Empirical differences were significant, but results critically depend on unreported δ and baseline subtraction method, which simulation suggests may be insufficient for removing task-driven noise.

## Next Checks
1.  **δ Sensitivity Analysis:** Systematically vary the exclusion radius δ (e.g., 2–8 utterances) in adjusted idea uptake calculations on the empirical human-AI dataset to assess the stability of the observed human-AI difference and propose a robust default.
2.  **Regex Validation via Human Annotation:** Sample 200 utterances from NASA Moon Survival chats. Have annotators label the presence/absence of the 11 politeness categories and compute agreement with the paper's regex to quantify over/under-matching and identify needed pattern refinements.
3.  **Simulated Data Pipeline Reproduction:** Recreate the simulation by crafting explicit prompts for a modern LLM (e.g., GPT-4o) to generate balanced and imbalanced teams for each metric's condition. Test whether all three metrics consistently separate the conditions across multiple random seeds, replicating the paper's simulation benchmarks.
