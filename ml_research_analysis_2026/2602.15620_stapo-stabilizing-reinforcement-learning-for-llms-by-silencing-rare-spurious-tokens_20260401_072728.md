# STAPO: Stabilizing Reinforcement Learning for LLMs by Silencing Rare Spurious Tokens

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2602.15620
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.15620
- Reference count: 40
- Primary result: STAPO stabilizes RL training for LLM reasoning and achieves average gains of 7.13% (greedy) and 3.69% (JustRL) over strong baselines across three model scales and six math benchmarks.

## Executive Summary
Reinforcement learning (RL) fine-tuning for large language models (LLMs) in reasoning tasks often suffers from instability and late-stage collapse. Existing regularization techniques like entropy bonuses are insufficient to prevent disruption from a small set of spurious tokens. STAPO addresses this by identifying and silencing these rare tokens, which have low probability and low local entropy but yield positive advantages. By masking their gradients during policy updates, STAPO prevents their disproportionate influence while preserving exploration. Evaluated on Qwen models (1.7B, 8B, 14B) across six mathematical reasoning benchmarks, STAPO maintains stable training entropy and delivers consistent accuracy improvements over GRPO, 20-Entropy, and JustRL.

## Method Summary
STAPO introduces a two-stage mechanism: first, it detects spurious tokens using fixed thresholds on token probability (τp) and entropy (τh). Tokens falling below both thresholds are considered spurious. Second, within a group-based policy optimization framework (similar to GRPO), STAPO masks the gradient contributions of these detected tokens, preventing them from receiving full reward credit. This selective silencing reduces the amplification of noisy updates that lead to training collapse. The method requires no additional reward modeling and operates solely at the token level during RL fine-tuning.

## Key Results
- STAPO achieves average accuracy gains of 7.13% under greedy decoding (ρ_T=1.0, top-p=1.0) and 3.69% under JustRL settings (ρ_T=0.7, top-p=0.9) compared to baselines.
- The approach stabilizes policy entropy throughout training, avoiding the late-stage collapse observed in standard RL methods.
- Results are consistent across model scales (1.7B, 8B, 14B) and six mathematical reasoning benchmarks (AIME24, AIME25, AMC23, MATH500, Minerva, OlympiadBench).

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The core mechanism stems from the analysis of token-level policy gradients: gradients are inversely proportional to token probability and local entropy. Spurious tokens—those with extremely low probability and low entropy—contribute negligible likelihood yet receive full reward when present in correct responses. This creates a harsh, high-magnitude gradient update that destabilizes the policy. By masking these tokens, STAPO removes their update, allowing the optimizer to focus on more informative tokens and maintain a stable exploration-exploitation balance. The group-based aggregation further smooths updates across multiple responses.

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component Map**: Response Sampler -> Token Statistics Extractor (prob, entropy, advantage) -> Spurious Token Detector (τp, τh) -> Gradient Mask Applier -> Group-based Optimizer -> Policy Update

**Critical Path**: For each training iteration, sample multiple responses per prompt. For each token, compute probability, entropy, and advantage. Identify spurious tokens (low prob & low entropy). Zero out their gradient contributions before aggregating across the response group and applying the update.

**Design Tradeoffs**: 
- Fixed thresholds (τp=0.002, τh bottom 20%) are simple but may not generalize across datasets or model scales. Adaptive thresholds could improve robustness.
- Masking may inadvertently suppress useful rare tokens (e.g., mathematical symbols), but their low probability and entropy suggest they are not critical.
- Computational overhead is minimal (threshold checks), but storing per-token statistics increases memory slightly.

**Failure Signatures**: 
- Training entropy dropping sharply or oscillating indicates overmasking or poor threshold calibration.
- Performance gain missing on small models (e.g., 1.7B) suggests scale-dependent effects; need to relax thresholds.
- If spurious token ratio deviates significantly from ~0.01%, threshold adjustments are required.

**Three First Experiments**:
1. **Ablation of Masking**: Train STAPO with masking disabled while keeping other components identical to measure impact on stability and accuracy.
2. **Threshold Sensitivity Sweep**: Vary τp (e.g., {0.001, 0.002, 0.005}) and τh quantile (e.g., {10%, 20%, 30%) across Qwen models to assess robustness and optimal ranges.
3. **Cross-Domain Transfer**: Apply STAPO to a non-math task (e.g., code generation on HumanEval) using the same thresholds to evaluate generalization.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
### Open Question 1
- Question: Does STAPO generalize to non-mathematical reasoning domains, such as code generation or open-ended question answering, while maintaining stability and performance gains?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The authors explicitly state in the conclusion that "our current experiments focus primarily on mathematical reasoning tasks" and that they "have yet to evaluate the proposed method in other domains, such as code generation."
- Why unresolved: The method is only evaluated on six math benchmarks; its effectiveness in other tasks with different reward structures and token distributions remains unknown. Spurious tokens may manifest differently in code or open-ended tasks.
- What evidence would resolve it: Replicate the STAPO training setup on standard code generation benchmarks (e.g., HumanEval, MBPP) using code execution-based rewards. Compare against GRPO, JustRL, and 20-Entropy in terms of pass@k, training entropy stability, and late-stage collapse frequency. Success would show consistent masking benefits (∼0.01% spurious tokens) and stability improvements across domains.

### Open Question 2
- Question: How do token-level properties (probability, entropy, advantage) differ between correct and incorrect reasoning trajectories, and can strategically masking spurious tokens in incorrect responses improve learning efficiency or robustness?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The authors note that "the current analysis focuses primarily on tokens within correct responses" and that they plan to "analyze tokens from incorrect responses" in future work.
- Why unresolved: The definition and detection of spurious tokens are based solely on correct responses. Tokens in incorrect trajectories may exhibit different patterns (e.g., high-entropy errors) that also destabilize training, but this is unexamined.
- What evidence would resolve it: During RL training, log token statistics (probability, entropy, advantage) separately for correct and incorrect responses across model scales. Cluster tokens to identify if distinct spurious patterns exist in incorrect chains (e.g., persistently high-entropy errors). Design masking strategies for these tokens and evaluate whether combined masking (correct + incorrect spurious) further stabilizes training or improves final accuracy.

### Open Question 3
- Question: Why does masking low-entropy, low-probability tokens (the core STAPO mechanism) cause catastrophic performance collapse in smaller models (1.7B) but yield gains in larger models (8B, 14B), as hinted by the ablation study?
- Basis in paper: [inferred] Figure 5 shows that masking "Low Ent & Low Prob" tokens significantly harms 1.7B models while helping 8B/14B. The paper offers no mechanistic explanation, attributing it to "scale-dependent divergence."
- Why unresolved: This scale sensitivity is critical for applying STAPO to smaller models, but the underlying cause—whether due to differences in pretraining distribution, capacity for exploration, or gradient norm scaling—remains speculative.
- What evidence would resolve it: Perform a controlled analysis measuring gradient norm distributions, policy entropy trajectories, and spurious token ratios for each scale when applying STAPO's mask. Use probing tasks to compare how models of different sizes utilize low-entropy tokens (e.g., are they essential for basic reasoning in small models?). Correlate masking impact with model uncertainty calibration or representation similarity.

### Open Question 4
- Question: Can adaptive thresholding for probability (τp) and entropy (τh) replace manual tuning and improve STAPO's robustness across diverse tasks and model architectures?
- Basis in paper: [inferred] The authors use fixed τp = 0.002 and a dynamic entropy quantile τh = 20% (mask bottom 80% low-entropy tokens). They note that a quantile-based τp would prune too many tokens, but no adaptive scheme is proposed.
- Why unresolved: Thresholds are manually set based on Qwen models and math tasks. Their optimality may not transfer to other models (e.g., Llama, Mistral) or domains, limiting STAPO's applicability without costly hyperparameter search.
- What evidence would resolve it: Design adaptive thresholds that adjust during training, e.g., based on rolling statistics of token gradient norms or entropy percentiles. Evaluate STAPO with adaptive thresholds across multiple model families and benchmarks, comparing stability (entropy variance) and performance against fixed-threshold STAPO and baselines. Success would show stable gains without per-dataset tuning.

## Limitations
- The detection of spurious tokens relies on fixed probability and entropy thresholds, which may require per-dataset tuning and lack theoretical derivation.
- The causal link between spurious tokens and instability is based on correlation; a controlled experiment isolating their effect is missing.
- Evaluation is limited to six mathematical reasoning benchmarks; generalization to other domains (e.g., code generation, dialogue) is untested.

## Confidence
- Problem characterization (rare spurious tokens cause collapse): **Medium**
- STAPO's effectiveness (stabilized training + accuracy gains): **High**
- Generalizability beyond math reasoning: **Low**
