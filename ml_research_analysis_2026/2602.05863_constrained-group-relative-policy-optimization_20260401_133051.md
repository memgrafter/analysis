# Constrained Group Relative Policy Optimization
## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2602.05863
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.05863
- **Reference count:** 40
- **Primary result:** Constrained GRPO with scalarized advantages achieves the highest Extended PDM Score on NAVSIM-v2 (0.774 Stage 1, 0.516 Stage 2) while faithfully satisfying behavioral constraints.

## Executive Summary
This paper introduces Constrained Group Relative Policy Optimization (C-GRPO), which extends the critic-free Group Relative Policy Optimization algorithm to handle constrained Markov decision processes via Lagrangian relaxation. The core insight is that naively scalarizing reward and cost components before GRPO's within-group standardization implicitly distorts the intended Lagrange multiplier trade-offs due to varying scales and correlations. The proposed solution, scalarizing advantages—standardizing each component first and then combining with multipliers—preserves multiplier semantics. Empirical evaluation on a gridworld and the NAVSIM-v2 autonomous driving benchmark demonstrates that scalarized advantages respects constraint thresholds and achieves superior task performance compared to scalarized rewards and unconstrained baselines.

## Method Summary
Constrained GRPO adapts GRPO to enforce behavioral constraints by incorporating indicator cost functions and Lagrange multipliers. For each group of trajectories, the algorithm computes per-component (reward and each cost) within-group means and standard deviations. Two scalarization strategies are considered: (1) Scalarized Rewards, which first combines raw components using multipliers and then standardizes the scalar; (2) Scalarized Advantages, which first standardizes each component and then combines them with the multipliers. The latter preserves the intended multiplier effect. The policy is updated using the GRPO objective (clipped surrogate with KL penalty) based on the computed advantages. Multipliers are updated via gradient descent on the constraint violation errors (estimated from the batch) and projected to non-negativity. This approach avoids a learned value function and directly optimizes for constraint satisfaction through the Lagrangian dynamics.

## Key Results
- In a gridworld, scalarized advantages achieved higher goal rates with stable constraint tracking toward thresholds, while scalarized rewards crushed constraints regardless of multiplier values.
- On NAVSIM-v2, Constrained GRPO with scalarized advantages attained the highest Extended PDM Score (0.774 Stage 1, 0.516 Stage 2), outperforming scalarized rewards and unconstrained baselines.
- The method demonstrated faithful constraint adaptation through Lagrangian dynamics, adjusting violations toward specified thresholds.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1
- **Claim:** Naive scalarization of reward and cost terms before GRPO's within-group standardization implicitly distorts the intended Lagrangian trade-offs by reweighting objectives based on component variances and correlations.
- **Mechanism:** When a scalarized reward \( R_S = \lambda^\top x \) (where \( x \) stacks reward and cost components) is standardized within a group, the resulting advantage \( A_{\text{ScRew}} = \frac{\lambda^\top (x - \mu)}{\sigma_{R_S}} \) expands to \( \sum_{j} (\lambda_j \sigma_j / \sigma_{R_S}) Z_j \). The effective weight on each standardized component \( Z_j \) is \( \lambda_j \sigma_j / \sigma_{R_S} \), which depends on the component's within-group standard deviation \( \sigma_j \) and the full covariance structure via \( \sigma_{R_S} = \sqrt{\lambda^\top \Sigma \lambda} \). This means differences in scale and correlation between components alter the relative influence prescribed by \( \lambda \).
- **Core assumption:** The group statistics (means, variances, covariances) of reward and cost components differ, which is typical in practice.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract] "naively scalarizing reward and cost terms before GRPO's within-group standardization implicitly reweights objectives based on component variances and correlations, corrupting the intended Lagrangian trade-offs."
  - [section 4.2, Theorem 4.1] Formal derivation of the expansion \( A_{\text{ScRew}} = (D\lambda)^\top Z / \sigma_{R_S} \) and the resulting effective weights \( \lambda_j \sigma_j / \sigma_{R_S} \).
  - [corpus] Neighbors like "GDPO: Group reward-Decoupled Normalization Policy Optimization" address similar multi-reward normalization issues, indicating a recognized problem space.
- **Break condition:** If all component variances are equal and components are uncorrelated, \( \sigma_{R_S} \propto \|\lambda\|_2 \) and effective weights become proportional to \( \lambda_j \), preserving intended ratios. In practice, this condition rarely holds.

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim:** Scalarizing advantages—standardizing each reward/cost component within the group first, then combining with Lagrange multipliers—preserves the semantics of the multipliers, enabling faithful constraint enforcement.
- **Mechanism:** Compute per-component standardized advantages \( Z_j = (x_j - \mu_j)/\sigma_j \). The final advantage is \( A_{\text{ScAdv}} = \lambda_R Z_R - \sum_{k=1}^K \lambda_k Z_{C_k} \). The coefficient of each \( Z_j \) is exactly \( \lambda_j \), invariant to within-group scales and correlations. Thus, multiplier updates directly and transparently influence the relative importance of objectives.
- **Core assumption:** The normalization of each component is done using its own within-group statistics, and the policy optimization reacts linearly to the combined advantage signal.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract] "The proposed solution—scalarizing advantages (standardizing each component first, then combining with multipliers)—preserves multiplier semantics."
  - [section 4.2] Definition of \( A_{\text{ScAdv}} \) and contrast with scalarized rewards.
  - [section 5, gridworld] Scalarized advantages achieved higher goal rates and stable constraint tracking toward thresholds, while scalarized rewards crushed constraints.
  - [section 6, NAVSIM] Constrained GRPO with scalarized advantages attained highest EPDMS (0.774 Stage 1, 0.516 Stage 2) and showed responsive multiplier-driven constraint adaptation.
- **Break condition:** If the per-component standardization is performed using incorrect group statistics (e.g., across groups instead of within-group), the preservation property fails. Also, extreme outlier values could distort standardization.

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim:** Using indicator cost functions with Lagrangian relaxation enables direct specification of desired behavior rates (e.g., "touch lava at most 1% of the time") without manual reward weighting.
- **Mechanism:** Constraints \( C_k(s,a) = \mathbb{I}[\text{violation}] \) map directly to a violation rate \( J_{C_k}(\pi) \). A threshold \( \tilde{d}_k \) specifies the maximum allowed rate. The Lagrangian \( \mathcal{L}(\pi, \lambda) = J_R(\pi) - \sum_{k} \lambda_k (J_{C_k}(\pi) - \tilde{d}_k) \) transforms the constrained problem into an unconstrained one with a composite reward \( R_c = \lambda_R R - \sum_k \lambda_k C_k \). Multipliers \( \lambda_k \) adapt: if violation rate \( J_{C_k} > \tilde{d}_k \), \( \lambda_k \) increases, penalizing the behavior more.
- **Core assumption:** The indicator cost provides a clear, binary signal for violation, and the empirical violation rate from a batch of experiences is a good estimate of \( J_{C_k}(\pi) \).
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract] "Constraints are specified via indicator cost functions, enabling direct optimization of violation rates through a Lagrangian relaxation."
  - [section 4.1] Formal definition of indicator costs and threshold interpretation: "requiring an agent to touch lava at most 1% of the time corresponds to setting the behaviour threshold \( \tilde{d}_{\text{lava}} = 0.01 \)."
  - [section 6] In NAVSIM, penalty terms (NC, DAC, DDC) are treated as soft constraints with target thresholds ~0.99, yielding better EPDMS than optimizing the multiplicative EPDMS directly.
  - [corpus] Neighbors like "Proactive Constrained Policy Optimization with Preemptive Penalty" and "Safe RL" works also use Lagrangian methods, supporting this as a standard approach for constraints.
- **Break condition:** If the cost function is not a true indicator or is too sparse/noisy, the empirical violation rate may not reflect the true constraint satisfaction, leading to unstable multiplier updates.

## Foundational Learning
- **Concept: Constrained Markov Decision Process (CMDP)**
  - Why needed here: The entire framework extends standard RL to settings with behavioral constraints. Understanding the CMDP formulation—maximizing reward subject to cost thresholds—is prerequisite to grasping the Lagrangian relaxation approach.
  - Quick check question: *How does the feasible set \( \Pi_C \) in a CMDP differ from the unconstrained policy space, and what role do multipliers play in transforming the constrained objective?*
- **Concept: Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO)**
  - Why needed here: GRPO is the base critic-free algorithm being extended. Its core innovation—within-group standardization of returns for advantage estimation—directly interacts with multi-objective scalarization.
  - Quick check question: *In GRPO, how is the advantage \( A_i \) computed from a group of \( G \) trajectory returns \( R(\tau_i) \), and why does this remove the need for a learned value function?*
- **Concept: Lagrangian Methods for Constrained Optimization**
  - Why needed here: The paper uses a min-max Lagrangian to handle constraints. Understanding multiplier updates \( \nabla_{\lambda_k} \mathcal{L} = -(J_{C_k} - \tilde{d}_k) \) and normalization (e.g., Roy et al.'s softmax) is key to predicting constraint dynamics.
  - Quick check question: *What is the effect of increasing a multiplier \( \lambda_k \) on the composite reward \( R_c \), and under what condition will \( \lambda_k \) be decreased during training?*

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map:** Policy Network → Group Rollout Buffer → Per-Component Statistic Module → Advantage Calculator (ScalarizedRewards or ScalarizedAdvantages) → Lagrangian Multiplier Module → PPO/GRPO Update
- **Critical path:**
  1. Sample group of trajectories.
  2. Compute reward/cost for each trajectory.
  3. Compute per-component group stats \( (\mu_j, \sigma_j) \) and standardized components \( Z_j \).
  4. Combine \( Z_j \) with current \( \lambda \) to get final advantages.
  5. Perform policy gradient update (e.g., GRPO).
  6. Compute empirical violation rates \( J_{C_k} \) from the same batch.
  7. Update \( \lambda \) via gradient step on \( -(J_{C_k} - \tilde{d}_k) \).
  8. Repeat.
- **Design tradeoffs:**
  - **Scalarization strategy:** ScalarizedAdvantages preserves multiplier semantics but requires careful per-component standardization. ScalarizedRewards is simpler but risks implicit reweighting if variances/correlations differ.
  - **Constraint threshold setting:** Tight thresholds may require many updates to satisfy; loose thresholds may not enforce behavior strongly. Curriculum over thresholds is an open direction.
  - **Multiplier normalization:** Using a softmax over {reward, constraints} ensures one objective can dominate but may limit fine-grained control if many constraints are active.
- **Failure signatures:**
  - **Constraints consistently violated despite high multipliers:** Indicates ScalarizedRewards distortion; effective weights on cost terms are suppressed by high variance in reward term.
  - **Multipliers oscillate wildly:** Could be due to too large multiplier learning rate, noisy cost estimates, or group size too small for reliable statistics.
  - **Policy collapses to zero cost but performance drops:** Suggests thresholds are too strict or reward signal too weak; multiplier may have over-penalized costs.
- **First 3 experiments:**
  1. **Toy gridworld (single constraint):** Verify that ScalarizedAdvantages allows constraint rate to approach threshold while maximizing reward, whereas ScalarizedRewards crushes the constraint regardless of multiplier.
  2. **Toy gridworld (multiple constraints):** Check interaction between constraints; ScalarizedRewards may fixate on one high-variance constraint, ignoring others. ScalarizedAdvantages should show adaptive reallocation of multiplier emphasis.
  3. **NAVSIM Stage 1:** Validate transfer to complex driving. Check that constrained models (especially ScalarizedAdvantages) achieve higher EPDMS than unconstrained baselines and that penalty term satisfaction rates track thresholds.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
### Open Question 1
**Question:** How can curriculum learning mechanisms be integrated with Constrained GRPO to dynamically adjust constraint thresholds during training, and does this improve both exploration and the ability to satisfy stricter safety constraints?
**Basis in paper:** The conclusion states: "An interesting direction for future work is to couple Constrained GRPO with curriculum-style mechanisms that adapt constraint thresholds over training, potentially improving exploration and enabling satisfaction of stricter constraints."
**Why unresolved:** The current framework uses fixed thresholds; dynamic adjustment may affect the stability of Lagrangian updates and the policy’s ability to learn feasible solutions without getting stuck in local optima.
**What evidence would resolve it:** Experimental evaluations where thresholds start loose and gradually tighten (e.g., linearly or based on performance), measuring metrics like constraint violation rates, task reward, and exploration (e.g., state visitation diversity) compared to fixed thresholds.

### Open Question 2
**Question:** To what extent does the group size in GRPO influence the magnitude of implicit reweighting in scalarized rewards and the stability of Lagrangian multiplier updates in Constrained GRPO?
**Basis in paper:** Theorem 4.1 shows that distortion depends on within-group variance and covariance; group size affects the accuracy of these statistics, but the paper does not analyze this sensitivity (they use fixed group sizes of 8 and 16).
**Why unresolved:** Group size is a key hyperparameter affecting sample efficiency and variance; understanding its interaction with constraint enforcement could guide optimal configuration in practice.
**What evidence would resolve it:** Ablation studies varying group size (e.g., 4, 8, 16, 32) on gridworld and/or NAVSIM, tracking effective weights (as defined in the paper), multiplier dynamics, and final constraint satisfaction rates.

### Open Question 3
**Question:** Can Constrained GRPO be extended to handle continuous cost functions (e.g., energy consumption) rather than binary indicator costs, and does advantage scalarization preserve its benefits in such settings?
**Basis in paper:** The framework is built on indicator cost functions for direct optimization of violation rates; the theoretical distortion analysis (Theorem 4.1) and empirical validation focus on binary constraints. Continuous costs introduce different statistical properties (e.g., non-zero means, non-constant variance) that may alter the distortion effect.
**Why unresolved:** Real-world constraints are often continuous (e.g., minimizing wear-and-tear, energy usage); extending the method would increase its applicability without compromising constraint enforcement.
**What evidence would resolve it:** Modify the gridworld or NAVSIM experiment to include continuous costs (e.g., distance traveled, time steps) and compare scalarized rewards vs scalarized advantages, measuring how well Lagrangian multipliers track desired trade-offs and whether constraints are satisfied at specified thresholds.

## Limitations
- NAVSIM implementation details (VLM architecture, WAT reward definition) are undisclosed, hindering full replication.
- The framework assumes binary indicator cost functions; extending to continuous costs is untested and may introduce challenges.
- Analysis is limited to GRPO; it is unclear whether the scalarization distortion affects other policy gradient methods or how general the solution is.

## Confidence
- **Distortion theory (Theorem 4.1):** High. Mathematical proof is solid; gridworld experiments confirm.
- **Multiplier semantics preserved:** High. \( A_{\text{ScAdv}} = \lambda \cdot Z \) gives coefficients exactly equal to \( \lambda \) by construction.
- **Empirical gains (NAVSIM):** Medium. Gridworld evidence strong, but NAVSIM results lack full reproducibility and cross-benchmark validation.
