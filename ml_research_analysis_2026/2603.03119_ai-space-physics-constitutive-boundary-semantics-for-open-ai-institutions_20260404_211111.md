# AI Space Physics: Constitutive boundary semantics for open AI institutions

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2603.03119
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.03119
- **Reference count:** 34
- **Primary result:** Introduces AI Space Physics—a constitutive transition semantics that classifies all boundary-relevant effects (including expansion without immediate external commits) as FIRST or SECOND-order, mandating atomic witness anchoring and replayable reconstruction to enforce strong governability.

## Executive Summary
Persistent AI institutions face a governance blind spot: latent authority can grow through expansion (e.g., adding tools or connectivity) without producing immediate external commits, evading traditional oversight. This paper proposes AI Space Physics, a formal boundary semantics that classifies every transition's effects. It mandates that all boundary-relevant effects—whether first-order external state changes or second-order structural/policy expansions—must be mediated by a non-bypassable membrane, witnessed atomically (Decide→Anchor→Effect), and stored in a replayable ledger. This shifts governance control from internal interpretability to enforceable invariants over externally effective transitions and their audit trails.

## Method Summary
The work is theoretical, providing a formal framework with definitions, constitutive laws (P-1 family), and operational corollaries (e.g., H-1). No training data or empirical deployment is used. Validation is intended via model-checking artifacts (TLA+ specifications referenced but not provided). A minimal viable reproduction would encode the state model, effect predicates (ExternalEffect, StructuralExpand, PolicyExpand_H), and invariants in TLA+, then test scenarios (e.g., tool installation without external call) to verify that expansions trigger mediation and atomic witness discipline.

## Key Results
- **Law P-1 family:** All ExternalEffect transitions require (a) non-bypassable membrane mediation, (b) atomic Decide→Anchor→Effect commitment, and (c) deterministic replay from witness.
- **Effect taxonomy:** FIRST (immediate external commit), SECOND_T (structural expansion), SECOND_P (policy-induced reach expansion).
- **Corollary H-1:** Risk-weighted expansion pressure must not exceed review capacity; formalizes ΔX_risk ≤ k ΔObsCap.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1: Expansion is a boundary effect
- **Claim:** Authority-surface expansion (e.g., adding tools or connectivity) is governance-relevant even without immediate external commits.
- **Mechanism:** Effect tags classify transitions: `FIRST` for immediate external state changes, `SECOND_T` for structural expansion (capabilities, topology, tools), `SECOND_P` for policy-broadening that enlarges admissible reach. `SECOND_T/SECOND_P` transitions are boundary-relevant and require membrane adjudication.
- **Core assumption:** Governance must control latent authority growth, not just observable actions; expansion changes future external influence potential.
- **Evidence anchors:** [abstract] "Expansion transitions are governance-relevant without immediate external delta." [section 1.1] "A system may produce no immediate external commit... and still increase its expected external influence... by adding tools, privileges, runtime instances, or connectivity."
- **Break condition:** If `SECOND_T/SECOND_P` transitions are not routed through the membrane or lack witness records, latent authority can grow outside governance.

### Mechanism 2: Atomic witness discipline prevents split-phase bypass
- **Claim:** Boundary effects require atomic `Decide → Anchor → Effect` semantics to prevent split-phase bypass.
- **Mechanism:** For any `ExternalEffect` transition, the membrane issues a decision (`ALLOW/REJECT/QUARANTINE`), a witness record binds that decision to the action, and the effect commitment occurs in the same indivisible state transition. No externally effective transition may precede durable witness anchoring.
- **Core assumption:** Witness integrity is safety-critical; retrospective logging is insufficient for enforceable governance.
- **Evidence anchors:** [section 4.3] "Law P-1b (Atomic Adjudication-to-Effect)... witness anchoring must be execution-atomic with externally effective transition commitment." [section 2.5] "Atomicity requires the triple (Decide, Anchor, EffectCommit) as a single transition... no execution prefix may expose an externally effective transition while its witness anchor is absent."
- **Break condition:** If effect commitment can occur in a separate transition step before witness anchoring, witness discipline degrades into after-the-fact logging, enabling undetected boundary crossings.

### Mechanism 3: Horizon-relative policy expansion is a first-class boundary event
- **Claim:** Authority growth is horizon-relative; policy-induced reach expansion (`SECOND_P`) is a first-class boundary event.
- **Mechanism:** An admissibility profile (`A_adm`) declares strategy class `Π_adm` and horizon `H`. `PolicyExpand_H` holds when core state is unchanged but `Reach_H` strictly increases under the new profile. This is a `SECOND_P` effect, requiring adjudication.
- **Core assumption:** Governability requires bounding and observing the evolution of the admissible future authority envelope, not just current state.
- **Evidence anchors:** [section 2.4] "PolicyExpand_H(𝑠→𝑠′)∷ CoreEq(𝑠,𝑠′) ∧ Reach_H(𝑠′; A_adm,𝑡+1) ⊋ Reach_H(𝑠; A_adm,𝑡)." [section 1.2] "We explicitly separate second-order effects into structural expansion (SECOND_T) and policy broadening (SECOND_P)."
- **Break condition:** If policy version changes that enlarge `Reach_H` are not detected and witnessed, the institution's admissible authority surface can expand without deliberate governance consent.

  - Why needed here: Provides a typed taxonomy for all boundary-relevant transitions, closing the blind spot where expansion without commit appears as internal churn.
  - Quick check question: "Is a policy version change that tightens constraints a `SECOND_P` event? Explain the `Reach_H` comparison condition."

  - Why needed here: Ensures tamper-evident, non-equivocating records that make boundary effects auditable and replay-adjudicable.

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map:**
  - Membrane -> Witness Ledger
  - Membrane -> State Projections (S_ext, S_topo, A_adm)
  - Effect Tagger -> Membrane
  - Replay Engine <- Witness Ledger
- **Critical path:**
  1. Detect candidate transition (s, act, s') (e.g., tool install).
  2. Compute TagSet → if ExternalEffect true, flag for mediation.
  3. Membrane evaluates M(...) → produces d.
  4. Atomic transition executes: (decide, anchor(w,d,act), effectCommit) as one step.
  5. Witness w stored with policy_version and ctx_abs.
  6. Later, Replay reconstructs d from w.
- **Design tradeoffs:**
  - Channel completeness (SC1) vs. implementation overhead: Enumerating all C boundary channels is necessary but may require deep system introspection.
  - Atomicity vs. performance: Grouping decide/anchor/effect into one transition may limit concurrency; requires transactional guarantees.
  - Replay determinism vs. expressiveness: Replay only guarantees decision-class reconstruction under fixed policy_version and ctx_abs, not full state replay.
- **Failure signatures:**
  - **Bypass:** ExternalEffect transition occurs without ThroughMembrane (P-1a violation). Monitor for state changes in caps/tools/topology not preceded by a membrane decision.
  - **Split-phase effect:** Effect commit step observable before any witness anchoring for the same action (P-1b violation). Audit logs for EffectCommit events lacking prior Anchor in the same transition.
  - **Replay mismatch:** Replay(policy_version, ctx_abs, w) ≠ d bound in w (P-1c violation). Periodically test witness replay against archived decisions.
  - **SECOND_P misclassification:** PolicyExpand_H false negative because Reach_H comparison used different A_adm profiles or unnormalized trace spaces (SC5 violation). Ensure consistent A_ref and canonical trace encoding for expansion diagnostics.
- **First 3 experiments:**
  1. **Tagging validation:** Seed initial state s0 with no payment tool. Execute action that installs the tool but does not call it. Assert TagSet contains SECOND_T and not FIRST. Verify π_ext(s1)=π_ext(s0) and tools(s1)≠tools(s0).
  2. **Atomicity under concurrency:** Attempt concurrent execution of two actions each causing ExternalEffect. Use a model checker (e.g., TLA+) to verify no interleaving produces a global state where an effect is committed without its corresponding witness anchor in the same atomic step.
  3. **H-1 operationalization:** Instrument a deployment to compute X_risk(t)=̂μ_risk_H(s_t; A_adm,t, L) and ObsCap(t). Simulate expansion pressure (e.g., rapid SECOND_T events) and measure backlog B_t in a review queue. Verify ΔX_risk(t) > k ΔObsCap(t) correlates with B_{t+1} > B_t.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- **Open Question 1:** What systematic method ensures complete enumeration of boundary-relevant channels (SC1) for any given AI institution deployment? Basis: SC1 conditions model completeness on channel set C covering all effect paths; omission invalidates claims. Why unresolved: Real-world systems have heterogeneous, dynamic interfaces (APIs, tools, shared resources), making exhaustive identification non-trivial and error-prone without automated support. Evidence to resolve: A formally defined enumeration procedure or verification tool that, given an institution specification, outputs a channel set and proof that all external-effect paths are captured within the model.
- **Open Question 2:** How can architectures be designed or verified to guarantee non-bypassability (SC2) for all boundary-relevant transitions? Basis: SC2 requires all such transitions to pass through membrane mediation; bypass violates P-1a and breaks strong governability. Why unresolved: Side channels (e.g., covert timing, resource exhaustion, indirect influence through shared state) may bypass explicit mediation points, and architectural patterns to seal all paths are not fully characterized. Evidence to resolve: Formal proofs or model-checking artifacts demonstrating that for a specific architecture, every transition with ExternalEffect(act,s,s') is in T_mem (through membrane).
- **Open Question 3:** What witness infrastructure design achieves tamper evidence and cross-organizational verifiability (SC4) without prohibitive overhead? Basis: SC4 mandates append-only, tamper-evident records; suggests cryptographic commitments and SCITT-style services for multi-party contexts. Why unresolved: Practical deployment across trust boundaries involves trade-offs in performance, standardization, and cryptographic agility; no blueprint exists for large-scale, low-latency witness systems. Evidence to resolve: Deployed protocols with benchmarks showing integrity guarantees (e.g., append-only property, non-repudiation) under realistic load and cross-admin domain scenarios.
- **Open Question 4:** How can policy-declared reach approximations be normalized (SC5) to ensure SECOND_P classification is representation-invariant? Basis: SC5 requires canonical trace space or fixed normalization for PolicyExpand_H comparisons to avoid representation-dependent expansion claims. Why unresolved: Admissibility profiles (A_adm) may encode policy versions and strategy classes in different formats, risking inconsistent detection of reach expansion when policies evolve. Evidence to resolve: A standard ontology and normalization function for reach set descriptions, with proofs that inclusion comparisons under different encodings yield consistent SECOND_P classification within declared approximation profiles.
- **Open Question 5:** How does AI Space Physics extend to multi-core settings with interacting heterogeneous AI runtimes under shared policies? Basis: Section 7.5 identifies multi-core dynamics (e.g., non-commutative composition, coalition SECOND_T cascades) as out of scope for current boundary-centric model. Why unresolved: Current laws and effect tagging assume a single institution; interactions between multiple AI cores may create emergent expansion pathways not captured by P-1 family or SECOND_T/P definitions. Evidence to resolve: An extended formal model defining transition semantics for networks of AI Spaces, plus case studies demonstrating how authority-surface growth propagates across cores and requires joint adjudication.

## Limitations
- The witness ledger structure and atomic transition encoding are under-specified; referenced model-checking artifacts are inaccessible, blocking replication.
- Instantiating the admissibility profile `A_adm` and risk-weight function lacks concrete guidance, leaving `SECOND_P` classification ambiguous.
- The framework remains purely theoretical; no empirical evaluation demonstrates feasibility or performance in real systems.

## Confidence
- **Expansion relevance (SECOND_T/SECOND_P):** Medium. The semantics are well-defined but lack empirical support; corpus signals are weak.
- **Atomic witness discipline:** Medium. Formal laws are clear, yet implementation challenges (e.g., transactional guarantees) are not addressed.
- **Horizon-relative growth and H-1 corollary:** Low. Concepts are highly abstract and no concrete metrics or validation are provided.
