# Crisis-Bench: Benchmarking Strategic Ambiguity and Reputation Management in Large Language Models

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2601.05570  
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.05570  
- **Reference Count:** 40  
- **Primary Result:** Models that balance strategic ambiguity and information withholding achieve higher simulated stock prices than radically transparent or non-engaging models, demonstrating an "alignment tax" for rigid honesty.

## Executive Summary
Crisis-Bench introduces a benchmark to evaluate LLMs in corporate crisis communication, where strategic ambiguity—not pure transparency—is often professionally necessary. It uses a 7-day multi-agent POMDP simulation with 80 synthetic crisis storylines across 8 industries, enforcing strict information asymmetry between a model’s private knowledge and public statements. An Adjudicator-Market Loop translates public sentiment (via VADER/DistilBERT) into a simulated stock price. Results show a clear performance dichotomy: models like GPT-5.1 and Qwen3-235b-a22b optimize for long-term stock value by selectively disclosing information and controlling costs, while radically transparent (Deepseek-v3.2) or refuse-to-engage models (Claude-4.5) suffer financially. Performance scales with model size, suggesting that advanced reasoning helps navigate the trade-off between trust and strategic opacity.

## Method Summary
The benchmark frames a corporate crisis as a partially observable Markov decision process (POMDP) spanning 7 simulated days. Each crisis storyline provides the model with private knowledge (e.g., internal investigation details) not available to the public. The model must generate daily public statements, balancing transparency, legal risk, and sentiment impact. An “Adjudicator” (using VADER and DistilBERT) scores the public sentiment from these statements, which feeds into a “Market Loop” that updates a simulated stock price. The evaluation metric is the final stock price after 7 days, reflecting the economic consequence of the communication strategy.

## Key Results
- **Performance dichotomy:** Models employing strategic ambiguity (GPT-5.1: 68.442, Qwen3-235b-a22b) significantly outperform radically transparent (Deepseek-v3.2) or refusal-based (Claude-4.5) approaches.
- **Scaling with capability:** Larger models generally achieve higher stock prices, indicating that strategic ambiguity requires advanced reasoning.
- **Alignment tax:** Rigidly honest or maximally transparent models exacerbate crisis severity and incur financial penalties, revealing a tension between alignment ideals and professional utility.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
Strategic ambiguity works because real-world crises involve imperfect information, legal constraints, and evolving public perception. Withholding or framing information can prevent panic, manage shareholder expectations, and buy time for internal resolution, whereas premature full disclosure may amplify liability or uncontrollable narrative shifts. The benchmark forces models to explicitly weigh these trade-offs, rewarding those that simulate a nuanced, multi-day communication strategy rather than a one-off honesty principle.

## Foundational Learning
- **POMDP (Partially Observable Markov Decision Process):** Models sequential decision-making under hidden state (private knowledge). *Why needed:* Crises evolve with unseen factors; models must act on incomplete information. *Quick check:* Does the framework account for belief updates over time?
- **Information Asymmetry:** Private vs. public knowledge states. *Why needed:* Mimics real crisis dynamics where companies know more than the public. *Quick check:* Is the asymmetry gap calibrated to realistic scenarios?
- **Sentiment-to-Market Mapping:** Translating textual sentiment to stock movement. *Why needed:* Captures economic impact of public perception. *Quick check:* Is the mapping linear/logarithmic? Is it validated on real crisis data?
- **Multi-Agent Simulation:** Interaction between corporate agent and public/market. *Why needed:* Reflects that communication affects a dynamic ecosystem. *Quick check:* Are market reactions autoregressive or one-off?
- **Cost-Control Mechanism:** Models manage “costs” (e.g., legal, reputational) per statement. *Why needed:* Real crises involve resource constraints. *Quick check:* How are costs quantified and updated?
- **Strategic Ambiguity vs. Transparency Spectrum:** Defining the behavioral space. *Why needed:* To move beyond binary honest/dishonest evaluations. *Quick check:* Are ambiguous statements operationally defined (e.g., hedging, omission)?

## Architecture Onboarding
Component map: Private Knowledge -> Model Response (Daily) -> Public Sentiment Scoring (VADER/DistilBERT) -> Stock Price Update -> Feedback (Next Day State)  
Critical path: Information asymmetry dictates model’s private facts → model generates statement → sentiment analysis yields public reaction → stock price adjusts → new private facts (if any) emerge.  
Design tradeoffs: Realism (complex storylines) vs. computational cost; sentiment model simplicity (VADER) vs. nuance (large transformer); fixed 7-day horizon vs. open-ended crises.  
Failure signatures: 1) Stock price collapse from overly transparent statements triggering panic/lawsuits. 2) Stock price stagnation/decline from refusal to engage causing perceived negligence. 3) Volatile swings from inconsistent messaging.  
First three experiments:  
1. **Vary information asymmetry degree:** Increase/decrease the gulf between private and public knowledge to see if strategic ambiguity advantage holds across levels.  
2. **Swap sentiment adjudicator:** Replace VADER/DistilBERT with a large finetuned sentiment model (e.g., FinBERT) to test sensitivity of stock mapping.  
3. **Model size ablation:** Test mid-tier models (e.g., Llama3-70B, Mistral-Large) to confirm if strategic ambiguity performance scales monotonically with parameters.

## Limitations
- **Simulation realism:** The 7-day POMDP with 80 synthetic storylines may not capture the full complexity of real corporate crises or information dynamics.
- **Sentiment-stock mapping:** Use of VADER/DistilBERT to directly drive stock prices lacks empirical grounding in actual market reactions during crises.
- **Model generalizability:** Findings are based on only four top-tier models; it is unclear if the “alignment tax” and strategic ambiguity advantage apply to other architectures or mid-tier LLMs.

## Confidence
- **Benchmark existence & design:** High (explicitly defined in paper).  
- **Performance dichotomy (transparency vs. ambiguity):** Medium (results shown but without statistical tests or variance estimates).  
- **Scaling with model size & alignment tax:** Medium (correlation observed, but causality and confounding uncontrolled).  
- **Real-world transferability:** Low (no validation on historical crises or expert assessment).

## Next Checks
1. **Reproducibility audit:** Re-run the exact codebase, data splits, and seeds to verify reported stock scores (e.g., GPT-5.1: 68.442) within a 2% margin.
2. **External crisis validation:** Apply Crisis-Bench to 10 historical corporate crises (e.g., BP Deepwater Horizon, United Airlines Flight 3411) using public records to compare model decisions vs. actual outcomes.
3. **Model breadth expansion:** Test 5+ additional mid-tier LLMs (e.g., Llama4-70B, Mistral-Large) to confirm if strategic ambiguity advantage persists beyond the evaluated top-tier models.
