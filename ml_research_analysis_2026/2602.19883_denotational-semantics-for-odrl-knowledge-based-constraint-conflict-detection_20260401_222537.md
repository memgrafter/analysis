# Denotational Semantics for ODRL: Knowledge-Based Constraint Conflict Detection

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2602.19883  
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.19883  
- **Reference count:** 17  
- **Primary result:** Conflict detection reduces to provable emptiness of denotation intersections under incomplete knowledge, yielding three-valued verdicts (Conflict, Compatible, Unknown).

## Executive Summary
This paper introduces a formal denotational semantics for ODRL (Open Digital Rights Language) constraint conflict detection grounded in knowledge bases (KBs). By mapping each ODRL constraint to a set of satisfying KB concepts (its denotation), conflict detection becomes a set-intersection problem with three possible outcomes: Conflict (disjoint), Compatible (overlapping), or Unknown (indeterminate due to incomplete KB axioms or ungrounded values). The framework supports all ODRL composition operators (and, or, xone) and semantic domains (taxonomic, mereological, nominal), and extends to cross-dataspace interoperability via order-preserving KB alignments that preserve conflicts and gracefully degrade unmapped concepts to Unknown. The approach is decidable (EPR encoding), sound under incomplete knowledge, and validated on 154 benchmarks with agreement between Vampire and Z3.

## Method Summary
The task is to implement constraint-level conflict detection for ODRL policies using a knowledge graph as semantic grounding. Inputs consist of pairs of ODRL constraints (e.g., `purpose: commercial` vs. `purpose: nonCommercial`) and a KB defined by a finite set of concepts, a partial order (≤) and a disjointness relation (⊥⊥). The objective is to compute a verdict (Conflict, Compatible, Unknown) for each constraint pair, ensuring soundness: Conflict only when no model satisfies both simultaneously. The method proceeds in three stages: (1) parse constraints and ground operands via the KB’s valuation function γ; (2) compute denotations—sets of KB concepts satisfying each constraint—using bidirectional rules derived from the KB’s order and disjointness; (3) combine per-operand verdicts via conservative intersection and composition tables for ∧/∨/xone. For cross-KB checks, an injective order-preserving alignment α maps constraints from a source KB to a target KB before denotation computation. All queries are encoded in decidable EPR (effectively propositional with bounded ∃) and solved by first-order (Vampire) and SMT (Z3) provers, with verdicts mapped from prover responses: `unsat` → Conflict, `sat` → Compatible, `CounterSatisfiable` → Unknown.

## Key Results
- Conflict detection reduces to set intersection of denotations, yielding sound three-valued verdicts under incomplete knowledge.
- Cross-dataspace alignments preserve conflicts and degrade unmapped concepts to Unknown—never creating false conflicts.
- Exclusive composition (`xone`) requires explicit disjointness axioms in the KB; without them, open-world semantics forces an Unknown verdict even when exactly one branch appears Compatible.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1: Denotational reduction to intersection
- **Claim:** Conflict detection reduces to provable emptiness of denotation intersections under incomplete knowledge.
- **Mechanism:** Each ODRL constraint is mapped to a *denotation*—the set of KB concepts satisfying it. A conflict exists iff the intersection of two constraint denotations is provably empty. Unknown arises when any denotation is indeterminate (⊤) due to ungrounded values or insufficient KB axioms.
- **Core assumptions:** (1) KBs are finite, correctly relate concepts via ≤ and ⊥⊥, and may be incomplete but not erroneous; (2) operand independence allows per-operand analysis.
- **Evidence anchors:** Abstract and Section 2.1 (Definitions 3–5) formalize denotation calculation, conservative intersection, and verdict determination.
- **Break condition:** KB finiteness or downward-closure of ⊥⊥ fails, making denotation calculation undecidable.

### Mechanism 2: Cross-dataspace alignment
- **Claim:** Interoperability is achieved via order-preserving KB alignments that preserve conflicts and degrade unmapped concepts to Unknown.
- **Mechanism:** An alignment α is an injective partial function between concept spaces Cᴬ↦Cᴮ that preserves ≤ and ⊥⊥ mappings. It maps constraints from KB A to KB B. Witness completeness (↓g⊆dom(α) for grounded g) prevents false conflicts from witness loss.
- **Core assumption:** Alignments can be constructed that respect the semantic structure (order and disjointness) of both KBs.
- **Evidence anchors:** Abstract and Section 2.2 (Definition 8, Proposition 2) formalize alignment properties and guarantees.
- **Break condition:** Alignment fails witness completeness (Example 1) or KBs have no shared semantic grounding (empty alignment).

### Mechanism 3: Exclusive composition requires strong axioms
- **Claim:** Exclusive composition (`xone`) requires strictly stronger KB axioms (negative/disjointness) than ∧/∨, as open-world semantics blocks exclusivity without provable non-overlap.
- **Mechanism:** The `xone` verdict demands exactly one per-operand pair is Compatible and all others Conflict. Under open-world, a branch may be Compatible but not provably exclusive from others unless disjointness axioms (⊥⊥) or structural impossibility are present.
- **Core assumption:** KBs follow open-world semantics; absence of negative axioms leaves exclusivity unprovable.
- **Evidence anchors:** Abstract and Section 3.3 (Table 6) show asymmetric verdicts for symmetric `xone` queries due to missing disjointness axioms.
- **Break condition:** KB provides no disjointness axioms or other means to prove non-overlap of non-selected branches.

## Foundational Learning
**Concept: Denotational Semantics**  
- *Why needed:* Provides the mathematical foundation mapping ODRL constraints to sets of satisfying concepts, enabling set-theoretic conflict detection.  
- *Quick check:* How does the denotation of `isAnyOf{v1,v2}` differ from `isAllOf{v1,v2}` in a taxonomic KB?

**Concept: Three-Valued Logic (Kleene)**  
- *Why needed:* Models epistemic incompleteness (Unknown) from missing KB information, with sound and monotonic propagation through ∧/∨/xone.  
- *Quick check:* Why does `Compatible ∨ Unknown = Compatible` but `Compatible ∧ Unknown = Unknown`?

**Concept: Knowledge Base Structure (≤, ⊥⊥)**  
- *Why needed:* The ordered set with disjointness defines the semantic domain (taxonomic, mereological, nominal) and governs all denotation calculations.  
- *Quick check:* In a nominal KB (≤ is identity), what does `isA` effectively become?

## Architecture Onboarding
Component map:  
Constraint Parser -> KB Interface -> Denotation Engine -> Verdict Composer -> (Alignment Module for cross-KB) -> Prover Backend

Critical path: Parse -> Ground -> Denote -> Intersect -> Compose. Alignment step inserts between Ground and Denote for cross-KB queries.

Design tradeoffs:  
- *Decidability vs. expressiveness:* Sticking to EPR (bounded ∃ quantifiers) ensures termination but prohibits direct `xone` encoding (requires ∃ under ¬).  
- *Completeness vs. safety:* Using ⊤ for ungrounded values yields sound Unknown but may miss conflicts solvable with more KB facts (runtime soundness still holds).  
- *Alignment generality:* Order-preserving injections handle many real KB pairs but require manual construction of α.

Failure signatures:  
- Prover returns `Unknown` (CounterSatisfiable in SMT) due to missing KB axioms (e.g., no ⊥⊥ for `xone`).  
- Alignment degrades verdicts to `Unknown` across all operands due to unmapped concepts.  
- Violation of witness completeness (witness loss) fabricates `Conflict` (Example 1); detected by checking ↓g ⊆ dom(α).

First 3 experiments:  
1. **Single-KB validation:** Pick GeoNames (GEO000) and run all 90 single-operator benchmarks (Sec 3.2). Verify Vampire/Z3 agree on all verdicts.  
2. **Alignment test:** Use GeoNames↔ISO3166 pair (GEO000/GEO001). Map a `isPartOf` constraint referencing `bavaria` to ISO3166; verify verdict degrades to `Unknown` due to unmapped `bavaria`.  
3. **xone stress test:** With DPV KB, test `xone(commercial, nonCommercial)` under two requests (ODRL085/086). Observe `Compatible` vs. `Unknown` based on presence of explicit disjointness axiom.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- **How can the framework be extended to cover all 14 KB-dependent ODRL operands?** The current evaluation focuses on a subset; remaining operands (e.g., `industry`, `recipient`, `deliveryChannel`) lack empirical validation and may introduce new semantic challenges.  
- **What is the formal integration path from constraint-level conflict detection to rule-level policy analysis?** The framework stops at constraint pairs; ODRL rules involve constraints, duties, and refinements whose interactions are not modeled.  
- **How can a standardized, discoverable KB registry be designed to automate cross-dataspace alignments?** Alignments are currently manually constructed; a registry with metadata and discovery protocols is needed.  
- **Under open-world semantics, what algorithmic approaches can automatically strengthen a KB with the negative axioms required to resolve `xone` compositions?** KBs like DPV lack explicit disjointness, causing `xone` to default to `Unknown`; no method is provided to derive such axioms.

## Limitations
- Benchmark suite availability is uncertain (arXiv preprint ahead of publication), impeding immediate reproduction.  
- The mapping from benchmark IDs to specific KB files and alignment parameters is not fully documented in the paper.  
- The framework assumes knowledge bases are finite, correctly structured, and error-free, which may not hold for real-world noisy KBs; `xone` often yields `Unknown` without explicit disjointness axioms.

## Confidence
- **Core denotational conflict detection:** High (formal mechanization in Isabelle/HOL, proven soundness, 154/154 benchmark agreement).  
- **Cross-dataspace alignment preservation:** Medium (theoretical guarantees solid, but empirical validation limited to few examples).  
- **Exclusive composition axiom requirement:** High (clear open-world analysis and empirical confirmation via Table 6).

## Next Checks
1. *Alignment witness completeness audit:* For all cross-dataspace benchmarks, programmatically verify that every grounded value’s image lies in the alignment domain (`↓g ⊆ dom(α)`).  
2. *Ablation of disjointness axioms for `xone`:* Remove disjointness axioms from a KB (e.g., DPV) and re-run `xone` queries; confirm all verdicts degrade to `Unknown`. Then restore a disjointness axiom and verify recovery.  
3. *Scalability to larger policies:* Generate synthetic ODRL policies with ≥10 operands and nested `and`/`or`/`xone`. Encode in EPR and measure Vampire/Z3 agreement and completion within a reasonable timeout (e.g., 5 min).
