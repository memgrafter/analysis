# Guideline-grounded retrieval-augmented generation for ophthalmic clinical decision support

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2603.21925
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.21925
- **Reference count:** 35
- **Primary result:** On HealthBench hard subset, Oph-Guid-RAG improved overall score by 30.0% (0.2969 to 0.3861) over GPT-5.2 and achieved accuracy 0.6576, a 10.4% gain over GPT-5.2 and 24.4% over GPT-5.4.

## Executive Summary
Clinical LLMs are prone to hallucination and rely on outdated knowledge, while standard retrieval-augmented generation (RAG) struggles with the complex layouts of clinical guidelines. This paper introduces Oph-Guid-RAG, a system for ophthalmic decision support that uses page-level visual retrieval to preserve tables, flowcharts, and layout information from guideline PDFs. The pipeline includes query decomposition, a router to decide between retrieval-based and direct answering, query rewriting, multimodal retrieval of page images using ColQwen2.5, LLM-based reranking to filter relevant evidence, and generation with a vision-language model. On the HealthBench hard subset of ophthalmology questions, Oph-Guid-RAG raises the overall score by 30.0% compared to GPT-5.2 (0.2969 → 0.3861) and achieves an accuracy of 0.6576, representing a 10.4% improvement over GPT-5.2 and 24.4% over GPT-5.4. Ablation studies confirm that reranking and routing are critical for hard cases, though they reduce answer completeness, revealing a precision-completeness trade-off.

## Method Summary
Oph-Guid-RAG processes a corpus of 305 ophthalmology guideline PDFs (Medlive) by converting each page into a high-resolution image (720 DPI, resized and padded to 5390×7940 pixels). These images are encoded using the ColQwen2.5 multimodal encoder and indexed with FAISS for similarity search. For a user query, the system first uses a Planner LLM to decompose complex questions into up to three subquestions. A Router then classifies each subquestion as either requiring external evidence (RAG) or answerable from the model's knowledge (DIRECT). Subquestions routed to RAG are rewritten to optimize retrieval, then the top-k page images are retrieved. A separate GPT-5.2 filter evaluates each retrieved page's relevance to both the original query and the subquestion; pages deemed irrelevant are discarded, and if none remain the subquestion falls back to DIRECT. The generation stage uses a vision-language model (GPT-5.2) to answer RAG subquestions given the text and relevant page images, while DIRECT subquestions are answered text-only. Finally, subanswers are synthesized into a coherent response, citing the source page URLs, and the full decision trace is logged. This design intentionally favors precision by filtering out uncertain evidence, at the cost of answer completeness.

## Key Results
- Overall score improved by 30.0% on the HealthBench hard subset compared to GPT-5.2.
- Accuracy reached 0.6576, representing a 10.4% improvement over GPT-5.2 (0.5956) and a 24.4% improvement over GPT-5.4 (0.5287).
- Ablation studies confirm reranking and routing are critical for performance on difficult cases, though completeness is reduced, indicating a precision-completeness trade-off.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1: Page-level visual retrieval preserves layout
**Claim:** Page-level visual retrieval preserves clinically significant layout (tables, flowcharts) and avoids OCR brittleness, leading to more accurate evidence retrieval.  
**Mechanism:** Ingest guideline PDFs as high-resolution page images; a multimodal encoder (ColQwen2.5) matches textual query directly against visual features of these images, capturing spatial relationships and tabular structures.  
**Core assumption:** Clinically relevant information in guidelines is often presented in structured visual formats that are degraded/lost during text extraction.  
**Evidence:** Abstract; Section 3.3 (PDF to images without OCR, preserving layout).  
**Break condition:** If guideline content is mostly plain prose, the overhead and potential noise of visual retrieval may not justify benefits over a simpler text-based approach.

### Mechanism 2: LLM-based reranking filter improves precision
**Claim:** An LLM-based reranking filter after initial visual retrieval significantly improves precision by removing semantically mismatched pages, critical for stable performance on hard cases.  
**Mechanism:** Initial visual retrieval (ColQwen2.5+FAISS) yields top-k candidate page images. A separate LLM (GPT-5.2) judges each candidate's relevance to both the original user query and the focused subquestion; only pages passing this filter are used as evidence.  
**Core assumption:** Initial similarity search in the multimodal embedding space has a non-trivial false positive rate, and an LLM can effectively re-rank based on nuanced clinical semantics.  
**Evidence:** Abstract; Section 3.5 (filtering step); Section 5.4 (ablation on hard subset: removing rerank drops overall score by 0.1044 and accuracy by 0.2115).  
**Break condition:** If the cost/latency of an LLM call per retrieved candidate is prohibitive, or if the initial retriever's precision is already exceptionally high for the specific corpus and query distribution.

### Mechanism 3: Controllable routing reduces noise
**Claim:** Controllable routing (RAG vs. DIRECT pathway) reduces noise by avoiding retrieval for queries where it is unlikely to help, improving context awareness and preventing hallucination from irrelevant evidence.  
**Mechanism:** Planner decomposes complex queries; Router decides for each subquestion whether to invoke the RAG pathway (retrieve & ground) or the DIRECT pathway (answer from model's parametric knowledge). This prevents forcing all queries through a retrieval step that might introduce off-topic evidence.  
**Core assumption:** Not all clinical questions require or benefit from external evidence retrieval; some are answerable from pre-trained knowledge or require speculative reasoning.  
**Evidence:** Abstract; Section 3.4 (Router decides); Section 5.4 (ablation: removing router increases context awareness but decreases accuracy).  
**Break condition:** The router miscalibrates, frequently misrouting evidence-intensive queries to DIRECT or speculative queries to RAG.

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component map:**  
Planner -> Router -> Query Rewriter -> Visual Retriever -> Relevance Filter -> Fallback Trigger -> Generator -> Final Synthesis -> Trace Logger

**Critical path (RAG example):**  
User Query -> Planner -> (SubQ1, SubQ2) -> Router (SubQ1=RAG) -> Query Rewriter -> ColQwen2.5 encode -> FAISS search (top-k pages) -> GPT-5.2 filter -> Filtered Pages -> VLM (SubQ1 + Pages) -> SubAnswer1 -> (repeat for other RAG subquestions) -> Final Synthesis -> Output + URLs + trace.json

**Design tradeoffs:**
- Precision vs. Completeness: Reranking and filtering boost accuracy but can discard partially useful evidence, lowering completeness.
- Cost/Latency vs. Quality: Each LLM call (Router, Rewriter, Filter, Generator) adds latency and expense; up to 4+ calls per complex query.
- Evidence Fidelity vs. Usability: Full page images preserve layout but consume large context windows and require a VLM-capable generator.

**Failure signatures:**
- High accuracy, very low completeness on hard subset: system found precise page but failed to synthesize complete answer; likely Generator or Synthesis issue.
- Low accuracy after removing reranking: filter allows too many low-relevance pages, poisoning context.
- All queries routed to DIRECT: router over-triggering DIRECT path; system behaves like a bare LLM.
- Trace shows "no valid evidence" for many RAG-routed subquestions: retrieval index poor or filter too strict.

**First 3 experiments:**
1. **Ablation: Disable Reranking** – run pipeline without the GPT-5.2 filter; expect significant accuracy drop on hard subset.
2. **Ablation: Force All Queries to RAG** – bypass Router; expect increased context awareness but decreased accuracy, validating selective retrieval.
3. **Error Analysis on Hard Subset Traces** – manually inspect 20 hard cases where accuracy=1 but completeness=0 to identify failure source (retrieval, aggregation, or synthesis).

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
1. **Evidence aggregation techniques**  
   *Basis in paper:* The conclusion and limitations state that improved evidence aggregation is needed to address the precision-completeness trade-off.  
   *Why unresolved:* Current system prioritizes precision via strict filtering, leading to low completeness (e.g., 0.0483 on hard subset), possibly omitting relevant details.  
   *What evidence would resolve:* Controlled experiments comparing aggregation methods on HealthBench hard subset, measuring changes in completeness and accuracy, e.g., multi-page context windows or relevance-weighted evidence fusion.

2. **Routing mechanism errors**  
   *Basis in paper:* Limitations note the routing scheme can be unreliable, sometimes adding extraneous retrieval or omitting required evidence.  
   *Why unresolved:* Unreliable routing leads to noisy retrieval (hurting accuracy) or missed evidence (reducing grounding), as seen in ablations.  
   *What evidence would resolve:* Development and evaluation of more robust routing classifiers (e.g., fine-tuned small models, confidence-calibrated LLM decisions) with metrics on routing accuracy and downstream impact on hard-case performance.

3. **Dynamic guideline updates and variations**  
   *Basis in paper:* Limitations highlight reliance on static corpus coverage, lacking updates and regional variations.  
   *Why unresolved:* Static offline indexing cannot adapt to new guidelines or local protocols, risking outdated recommendations in real-world deployment.  
   *What evidence would resolve:* Prototyping incremental update mechanisms (e.g., delta indexing of changed pages, metadata-based versioning) and evaluating performance on a corpus with simulated temporal or regional updates, measuring robustness to obsolete content.

## Limitations
- Uncertain identity of "GPT-5.2/5.3/5.4" models; using public proxies (e.g., GPT-4o) may not match reported scores (Low confidence).
- Missing implementation details: exact prompts, routing thresholds, reranking methodology, and retrieval parameters (Medium confidence).
- Evaluation limited to a specific Medlive ophthalmology guideline corpus and HealthBench subset; generalizability is unclear (Medium-Low confidence).
