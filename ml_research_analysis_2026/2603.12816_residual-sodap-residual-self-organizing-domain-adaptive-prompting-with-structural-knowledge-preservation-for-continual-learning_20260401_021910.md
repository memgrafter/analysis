# Residual SODAP: Residual Self-Organizing Domain-Adaptive Prompting with Structural Knowledge Preservation for Continual Learning

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID**: 2603.12816
- **Source URL**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.12816
- **Reference count**: 40
- **Primary result**: A domain-incremental learning method without task IDs or stored data achieves SOTA average accuracy and forgetting on multiple benchmarks.

## Executive Summary
This paper tackles catastrophic forgetting in domain-incremental learning (DIL) under a challenging setup: no access to past task data and no task identifiers. It introduces Residual SODAP, a framework that combines several innovations—sparse residual prompt selection, data-free pseudo-feature replay, prompt-usage-based drift detection, and uncertainty-aware loss balancing—to adapt to new domains while preserving previously learned classifier knowledge. The method operates by maintaining and dynamically expanding a pool of prompts inserted into a frozen Vision Transformer backbone, using these prompts to steer representations for new domains. Classifier knowledge is preserved via distillation from a frozen teacher head, with synthetic features generated from stored class-wise statistics. Demonstrated on three DIL benchmarks, the approach sets new state-of-the-art results in average accuracy and average forgetting.

## Method Summary
Residual SODAP integrates four core components. First, an α-entmax-based sparse router selects a small subset of prompts per layer from a split pool (frozen and active), combining them residually to adapt representations while retaining stable features from prior domains. Second, a data-free knowledge preservation module stores diagonal Gaussian statistics (mean, variance) for each class after a domain stage and uses them to sample pseudo-features for distillation, aligning the current classifier with a frozen copy of the previous one. Third, a Prompt-Usage-Based Drift Detection (PUDD) module monitors the entropy and set overlap of selection weights; significant drift triggers controlled expansion of the active prompt set. Fourth, an uncertainty-weighting mechanism (based on homoscedastic uncertainty) dynamically balances the multiple training losses (classification, real/pseudo distillation, prompt diversity/norm) to stabilize optimization across domains.

## Key Results
- On the DomainNet (DR) benchmark, Residual SODAP achieves AvgACC of 0.850 and AvgF of 0.047.
- On the Skin Cancer dataset, it achieves AvgACC of 0.760 and AvgF of 0.031.
- On CORe50, it achieves AvgACC of 0.995 and AvgF of 0.003.
- These results represent state-of-the-art performance for the tested domain-incremental learning protocols.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1: Residual Sparse Prompt Selection
- **Claim**: The α-entmax-based residual prompt selection mechanism aims to adapt representations to new domains while preserving prior knowledge by sparsifying prompt activations and combining frozen and active prompts residually.
- **Mechanism**: At each Transformer layer, the CLS token is enhanced via a memory bank read and a bottleneck adapter. The enhanced query is compared to prompt keys using cosine similarity; α-entmax (α=1.5) converts similarities to sparse weights, zeroing low-relevance prompts. The prompt pool is split into frozen (F) and active (A) subsets. Separate α-entmax routing computes representations p_F and p_A; they are combined as p_out = p_F + λ_r p_A (λ_r=0.1) and injected back into the CLS token. This design retains stable features from F (prior domains) while allowing A to provide residuals for adaptation.
- **Core assumption**: Sparse selection (via α-entmax) avoids noise from irrelevant prompts, and the residual combination ensures that frozen prompts act as a stable base that does not interfere with adaptation.
- **Evidence anchors**: [abstract] "α-entmax sparse prompt selection with residual aggregation"; [section 3.1] Specifically, 3.1.2 defines α-entmax and 3.1.3 describes residual combination and partitioning; [corpus] Neighbor papers (e.g., FM-LoRA) investigate meta-prompting for CL, indicating community interest; the α-entmax sparsity approach itself is referenced from Peters et al. [27].
- **Break condition**: If the frozen set F becomes too large or mismatched to the current domain, residual updates may be insufficient. Extreme domain shifts might require more than residuals. Fixed α and λ_r might be suboptimal for some domains.

### Mechanism 2: Statistical Knowledge Preservation via Pseudo-Feature Replay
- **Claim**: Mitigates forgetting at the classifier level without access to past data by storing class-wise feature statistics and using them to generate synthetic features for distillation.
- **Mechanism**: After completing a stage, the current classifier head h_t is duplicated and frozen as a teacher. Class-wise feature means μ_c and diagonal variances σ²_c are estimated online on the current stage's features using Welford's algorithm. At the start of the next stage, these statistics and the teacher are loaded. A student head h_s (initialized from the teacher) is trained jointly with the current classification loss. Two distillation losses are computed: L_real (on real features of the current batch) and L_pseudo (on pseudo features sampled from N(μ_c, diag(σ²_c)) for uniformly selected classes). Both use KL divergence with temperature T=2. The teacher remains frozen; gradients update only the student and prompts.
- **Core assumption**: Class-conditional feature distributions are well approximated by diagonal Gaussians, and distilling from a frozen teacher using such pseudo samples sufficiently preserves decision boundaries.
- **Evidence anchors**: [abstract] "data-free distillation with pseudo-feature replay"; [section 3.2] Details in 3.2.1 (statistics collection) and 3.2.2 (distillation losses); [corpus] Data-free distillation techniques (e.g., DeepInversion [46]) are cited; neighbor CP-Prompt mentions class-wise knowledge alignment, but the exact pseudo-feature replay via diagonal Gaussians appears novel.
- **Break condition**: If feature distributions are multi-modal, highly correlated, or non-Gaussian, the diagonal Gaussian assumption yields poor pseudo samples. As domain shifts accumulate, stored statistics may become outdated, causing negative transfer. Assumes frozen backbone feature space remains stable.

### Mechanism 3: PUDD and Uncertainty-Aware Balancing
- **Claim**: Prompt-Usage-Based Drift Detection (PUDD) coupled with uncertainty-aware multi-loss balancing enables dynamic model adaptation and stable optimization under sequential domain shifts.
- **Mechanism**: PUDD tracks two signals from batch-averaged α-entmax selection weights: (i) entropy H_t, and (ii) IoU between current usage set S_t (weights > τ_s) and a reference set from a sliding window W. The drift score D_t = α·|H_t - ¯H_t|/σ_H + β·(1/max(IoU, η)-1) combines them; after processing a new domain's data, the average drift ¯D determines pool expansion size E = clamp(⌊|A|·¯D/D_max⌋, E_min, E_max). Existing active prompts move to frozen set F, and E new prompts are added to A. Simultaneously, uncertainty weighting (Kendall et al.) assigns each loss term L_i a weight w_i = exp(-s_i), where s_i is a learned log-variance. The total loss L_total = Σ_i (e^{-s_i} L_i + s_i) optimizes both model parameters and {s_i} to balance contributions dynamically.
- **Core assumption**: Domain shifts cause measurable changes in prompt selection patterns, and the homoscedastic uncertainty model appropriately captures the relative reliability of different loss terms.
- **Evidence anchors**: [abstract] "prompt-usage-based drift detection, and uncertainty-aware multi-loss balancing"; [section 3.3] PUDD design and expansion; [section 3.4] uncertainty weighting equations; [corpus] Drift detection is widely used (e.g., ADWIN). Uncertainty weighting for multi-loss balancing appears in multi-task learning (Kendall et al. 2018) and is cited ([13]).
- **Break condition**: PUDD may miss gradual/subtle shifts if entropy and set changes are within noise, causing no expansion and forgetting. Temporary fluctuations could trigger unnecessary expansions if sliding window/thresholds are mis-set. Uncertainty weighting might converge to extreme weights (ignoring some losses) if initial s_i or loss scales are poor.

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map**: Backbone (frozen ViT) with per-layer prompts -> Query Enhancer (memory bank + adapter) -> α-entmax router (sparse selection) -> Residual Combiner (F + A) -> CLS token. Parallel: Classifier Heads (Teacher frozen, Student trainable) <-> Statistics Bank (class μ, σ²) -> Pseudo-feature sampler. PUDD module monitors selection weights and triggers prompt pool expansion. Uncertainty weighting learns log-variances per loss term. Loss aggregator sums weighted losses.
- **Critical path**: For training stage k: forward pass with prompts -> student head logits -> compute CE loss on current data. Load teacher head & class stats -> compute L_real (on real features) and L_pseudo (on sampled pseudo features) using KL divergence. Compute L_div and L_norm on active prompts. Apply uncertainty weights to all losses -> total loss -> backprop updates active prompts, student head, uncertainty params. PUDD monitors batch selection weights. After stage k, freeze teacher head & save class stats. On next domain data, PUDD computes drift ¯D -> sets expansion size E -> moves some active prompts to frozen F and adds E new prompts to A.
- **Design tradeoffs**: 
  - Accuracy vs. forgetting: Larger prompt pools and less sparse selection may improve adaptation but risk forgetting; residual scaling λ_r controls trade-off.
  - Capacity efficiency: Expansions add parameters; clustering prompts or using memory bank size M affect memory/compute.
  - Distillation fidelity: Gaussian pseudo-features are cheap but may not capture complex distributions; more expressive models increase overhead.
  - Automatic balancing: Uncertainty weighting reduces manual tuning but may need careful initialization and clamping to avoid instability.
- **Failure signatures**:
  - High forgetting despite good current accuracy → check pseudo-replay quality and L_real weight.
  - Poor adaptation to new domain → check prompt pool size, α-entmax sparsity, residual scale.
  - Rapid growth of prompt pool → PUDD drift threshold too low; reduce θ or D_max.
  - Unbalanced loss weights (some near zero) → initial s_i or learning rates may be off; adjust clamp bounds.
  - Training instability (loss spikes) → reduce learning rate or increase λ_r gradually.
- **First 3 experiments**:
  1. Baseline replication: Implement OS-Prompt [14] and run on a 2-stage DIL stream (e.g., APTOS→DDR) to obtain reference AvgACC/AvgF.
  2. Sparse selection ablation: Replace α-entmax with softmax in the full model; compare selection entropy (measure sparsity) and forgetting to isolate the impact of sparsity.
  3. Pseudo-replay ablation: Disable pseudo feature generation (K=0) and train full model; evaluate change in AvgF and visualize classifier stability via cross-composition (as in Fig. 1).

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
### Open Question 1
- **Question**: How can Residual SODAP be extended to class-incremental learning (CIL) scenarios where the label space evolves over time, rather than fixed classes as assumed in DIL?
- **Basis in paper**: [explicit] The conclusion explicitly states: "As future work, we will extend Residual SODAP to class-incremental scenarios with evolving label spaces..."
- **Why unresolved**: The current method relies on a fixed classifier head and class-wise statistics for pseudo-replay, which becomes invalid when new classes emerge. Adapting the framework to handle expanding output spaces and unseen-class statistics is non-trivial and unaddressed.
- **What evidence would resolve it**: Experiments on standard CIL benchmarks (e.g., Split CIFAR-100, CORe50 with class splits) showing that Residual SODAP's components—prompt selection, drift detection, and pseudo-replay—can be modified to accommodate new classes without catastrophic forgetting, with metrics comparable to state-of-the-art CIL methods.

### Open Question 2
- **Question**: Can prompt pool expansion in PUDD be made adaptive under strict computational and memory budgets, rather than relying on a fixed maximum capacity and heuristic expansion scale?
- **Basis in paper**: [explicit] The conclusion mentions developing "adaptive expansion schedules and capacity control under compute and memory constraints."
- **Why unresolved**: The current drift-proportional expansion (Eq. 20) uses heuristically set constants (D_max, E_min, E_max) without a theoretical model linking drift scores to optimal capacity usage. This may lead to inefficiency in long streams or resource-constrained settings.
- **What evidence would resolve it**: Theoretical analysis or empirical validation showing a predictable relationship between domain shift magnitude (as measured by PUDD) and the marginal benefit of added prompts, enabling dynamic capacity adjustment that maintains performance while bounding resource growth.

### Open Question 3
- **Question**: Does using class-conditional Gaussian statistics for pseudo-feature replay sufficiently capture the true feature distribution, or does the diagonal covariance assumption limit effectiveness for complex, multi-modal features?
- **Basis in paper**: [explicit] The conclusion proposes "designing class-conditional statistical pseudo replay" and improving it for "long-tailed, noisy medical labels."
- **Why unresolved**: The method approximates each class's feature distribution as a diagonal Gaussian (Eq. 13), which cannot model multi-modal or correlated features. This simplification may degrade replay quality, especially for fine-grained medical distinctions or imbalanced data.
- **What evidence would resolve it**: Ablation studies comparing diagonal Gaussian, full-covariance Gaussian (if memory allows), and non-parametric (e.g., mixture models) approximations on datasets with known feature multi-modality, measuring impact on AvgF and calibration error.

### Open Question 4
- **Question**: Is the core assumption of PUDD—that domain shifts induce detectable changes in prompt usage patterns—always valid, particularly for subtle or gradual domain shifts that may not alter selection behavior significantly?
- **Basis in paper**: [inferred] PUDD detects drift by monitoring entropy and usage set shifts in prompt selection weights (Sec. 3.3), assuming domain changes alter the optimal prompt combination. However, the paper does not validate this assumption across shift types.
- **Why unresolved**: If a new domain is similar to previous ones, prompt usage might not change enough to trigger detection, leading to insufficient expansion and forgetting. Conversely, intra-domain variability could cause false alarms. The sensitivity and robustness of PUDD to shift magnitude are not theoretically analyzed.
- **What evidence would resolve it**: Controlled experiments with synthetic or real datasets where domain shift degree (e.g., via style transfer intensity) is varied, measuring PUDD's detection recall/precision and correlating drift scores with actual performance drops. Failure cases where drift is undetected should be examined.

### Open Question 5
- **Question**: Does the homoscedastic uncertainty weighting (Sec. 3.4) adequately handle correlated uncertainties between loss terms (e.g., L_real and L_pseudo) during sequential domain learning, or could it lead to suboptimal loss balancing?
- **Basis in paper**: [inferred] The method assumes each loss L_i has independent homoscedastic uncertainty (scalar variance σ_i^2), as in Kendall et al. [13]. However, in DIL, losses may become correlated due to shared representation changes, an issue not discussed.
- **Why unresolved**: If losses are correlated, independent scalar uncertainties may misrepresent their relative reliability, causing the weighting to over- or under-emphasize certain terms at different stages. The paper shows dynamic weighting (Fig. 4) but does not analyze loss correlations.
- **What evidence would resolve it**: Analysis of loss correlations (e.g., via covariance matrices) across training stages, and ablation comparing homoscedastic weighting to a heteroscedastic or multi-task formulation that models dependencies. Performance impact on AvgF/ACC when correlations are artificially induced would test robustness.

## Limitations
- Pseudo‑feature replay assumes diagonal Gaussian class distributions; this may be violated under complex or multi‑modal feature shifts.
- Fixed hyperparameters (α=1.5 for entmax sparsity, λ_r=0.1 for residual scaling) might not generalize across all domain‑incremental streams.
- PUDD could miss gradual or subtle domain drift, leading to insufficient prompt expansion and forgetting.
- Uncertainty‑based loss weighting may converge to degenerate values, causing some objectives to be ignored.
- The prompt pool may grow unbounded over long streams, raising memory and computational concerns.
