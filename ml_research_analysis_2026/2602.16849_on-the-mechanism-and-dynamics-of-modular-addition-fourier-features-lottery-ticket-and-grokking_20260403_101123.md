# On the Mechanism and Dynamics of Modular Addition: Fourier Features, Lottery Ticket, and Grokking

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID**: 2602.16849
- **Source URL**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.16849
- **Reference count**: 40
- **Primary result**: The paper explains feature learning and grokking in two-layer neural networks solving modular addition via Fourier analysis, revealing a lottery ticket mechanism where neurons compete to represent single frequencies and weight decay drives sparsity and phase alignment for generalization.

## Executive Summary
This paper provides a mechanistic explanation for how two-layer neural networks learn to perform modular addition, a task known to exhibit "grokking" — delayed generalization. By analyzing the training dynamics in the frequency domain via the Discrete Fourier Transform, the authors show that individual neurons specialize to single Fourier frequencies whose phases align such that the neuron's output peaks at the correct sum. The network then approximates the true modular addition indicator function through a majority-voting mechanism across diversified neurons. Grokking unfolds in three distinct stages: initial memorization, followed by two generalization phases driven by weight decay, which prunes non-dominant frequencies and refines phase alignment. The work connects the lottery ticket hypothesis (within-neuron frequency competition) with geometric feature learning and grokking dynamics.

## Method Summary
The study uses a two-layer MLP with one-hot inputs for \(x, y \in \mathbb{Z}_p\), a hidden layer of \(M\) ReLU neurons, and a linear output layer, trained with cross-entropy loss. The core method tracks the Discrete Fourier Transform (DFT) of each neuron's first-layer weights \(\theta_m\) and output-layer weights \(\xi_m\) over time, extracting per-frequency magnitudes (\(\alpha_m^k, \beta_m^k\)) and phases (\(\phi_m^k, \psi_m^k\)). Key analysis shows that under small initialization, each neuron's weights concentrate on a single frequency, and the phase difference \(2\phi_m^k - \psi_m^k\) aligns near zero. Training dynamics are compared between full-data training (no generalization gap) and a train-test split with weight decay (which induces grokking). Metrics include loss, accuracy, Inverse Participation Ratio (IPR) for sparsity, and the phase alignment metric \(|\sin(D_m)|\).

## Key Results
- **Mechanistic**: Overparameterized neurons develop single-frequency Fourier features with aligned phases; the network uses frequency diversification and phase symmetry across neurons to perform majority-voting, canceling noise and approximating the correct indicator function.
- **Dynamic**: The single-frequency pattern is preserved throughout training; phase alignment occurs rapidly; non-dominant frequencies remain small and are later pruned.
- **Grokking**: The process has three stages: (I) memorization (train loss \(\approx\) 0, test loss rises), (II) first generalization phase where weight decay increases sparsity (IPR) and aligns phases, causing test loss to drop, and (III) slow convergence to perfect generalization as weight decay dominates.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1: Single-Frequency Emergence & Phase Alignment
- **Claim**: Each neuron learns a single-frequency Fourier feature, and its output phase aligns with twice the input phase (\(2\phi_m - \psi_m \approx 0\)).
- **Mechanism**: Under small initialization scale \(\kappa_0\) and overparameterization, gradient flow decouples neurons and frequencies. The ODEs for each neuron show that one frequency dominates due to initial magnitude and small phase misalignment, while others stay negligible; the phase difference \(2\phi_m^k - \psi_m^k\) quickly converges to zero.
- **Core assumptions**: Small \(\kappa_0\), large \(M\), activation with even-order components (ReLU or quadratic).
- **Evidence anchors**: [abstract], §3.1 (Observations 1,2), §5 (Theorems 5.2,5.3).
- **Break condition**: If initialization is not small, decoupling fails; if \(M\) is too small, the single-frequency pattern may not be preserved.

### Mechanism 2: Majority-Voting via Diversification
- **Claim**: The network approximates the modular addition indicator by summing diversified neurons, where noise cancels and the correct peak is amplified.
- **Mechanism**: Neurons sharing a frequency exhibit uniform phase symmetry; their noisy individual outputs cancel in summation, while the correct signal (with aligned phases) adds constructively. Full diversification — equal frequency coverage, uniform phases within groups, comparable magnitudes — ensures a robust logit margin at the true sum.
- **Core assumption**: Full diversification (Definition 4.1): (i) every frequency represented equally, (ii) phases uniform within frequency groups, (iii) magnitudes comparable.
- **Evidence anchors**: [abstract], §4 (Proposition 4.2), §3.1 (Observation 3).
- **Break condition**: Missing frequencies or clustered phases cause error peaks at false positions (e.g., \(2x, 2y\)).

### Mechanism 3: Three-Stage Grokking
- **Claim**: Grokking proceeds via memorization then two generalization phases driven by weight decay.
- **Mechanism**: Loss minimization first drives norm growth and memorization (Stage I). Weight decay then competes: it prunes non-dominant frequencies (increasing IPR/sparsity) and aligns phases, causing test loss to drop (Stage II). Finally, weight decay dominates, slowly converging to perfect generalization (Stage III).
- **Core assumption**: Weight decay, train-test split with rare examples, and the model must first reach a perturbed Fourier solution.
- **Evidence anchors**: [abstract], §3.3 and §A.1.
- **Break condition**: Removing weight decay eliminates grokking; without rare examples, delayed generalization may not occur.

## Foundational Learning
- **Discrete Fourier Transform (DFT)**: Used to transform weight vectors into frequency domain, revealing single-frequency structure, phase relationships, and sparsity. *Quick check*: If \(\theta_m[j] = \cos(\omega_k j + \phi_k)\), what are its DFT coefficients for frequency \(k\)?
- **Lottery Ticket Hypothesis**: Explains frequency dominance within a neuron as a competition where the winning frequency is determined by initial magnitude and phase misalignment. *Quick check*: Within a neuron, what two initialization properties determine the winning frequency?
- **Grokking Phenomenon**: The delayed generalization pattern where test performance improves long after interpolation. *Quick check*: What role does weight decay play in the transition from memorization to generalization?
- **Inverse Participation Ratio (IPR)**: A sparsity measure; higher IPR indicates concentration on fewer frequencies. *Quick check*: How does IPR change as weight decay prunes non-dominant frequencies?
- **Phase Alignment**: The condition \(2\phi - \psi \approx 0\) that makes a neuron's output peak at the correct sum. *Quick check*: Why does phase alignment amplify the correct signal in the network sum?

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map**: One-hot input (x, y) -> First-layer weights θ_m (M neurons) -> ReLU activation -> Output weights ξ_m -> Linear sum -> Logits (p classes) -> Softmax -> Cross-entropy loss.
- **Critical path**: 1) One-hot input encodes integers. 2) First-layer weights learn Fourier features (single-frequency cosine waves). 3) Phase alignment (\(2\phi \approx \psi\)) makes neuron output peak at correct sum. 4) Sum across diversified neurons cancels noise via symmetry. 5) Softmax selects correct class.
- **Design tradeoffs**: 
  - Width \(M\): larger \(M\) promotes diversification and stable majority-voting but risks quadratic instability.
  - Initialization scale \(\kappa_0\): small \(\kappa_0\) ensures decoupling; too small slows training.
  - Activation: ReLU preferred (robust, even-order); quadratic may cause imbalanced neuron growth.
  - Weight decay: critical for grokking; controls sparsity and phase refinement.
- **Failure signatures**:
  - Poor generalization if \(M\) too small (incomplete frequency coverage).
  - No grokking without weight decay.
  - Persistent test error if phases fail to align (e.g., large initial misalignment).
- **First 3 experiments**:
  1. Train on full dataset with small \(\kappa_0\); verify single-frequency pattern via DFT heatmaps of \(\theta_m, \xi_m\).
  2. Introduce train-test split (e.g., 75/25) + weight decay; track train/test loss, IPR, and \(|\sin(D_m)|\) to witness grokking stages.
  3. Ablate: (a) reduce width \(M\); (b) use non-uniform phase initialization; (c) replace ReLU with odd activation – observe degradation in generalization or dynamics.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- Can the lottery ticket mechanism and phase alignment be proved for standard large-scale initialization and stochastic optimizers (e.g., SGD, Adam) instead of the small \(\kappa_0\) and gradient flow assumed?
- Is there a complete theoretical explanation for the training dynamics of two-layer ReLU networks that derives the single-frequency pattern and broken phase symmetry from random multi-frequency initialization?
- How does weight decay quantitatively induce the inverse participation ratio (IPR) to increase and produce the two-phase generalization (stages II–III) in grokking?

## Limitations
- DFT basis normalization and sign conventions are not fully specified, risking incorrect magnitude/phase extraction.
- Grokking hyperparameters (train-test split ratio, weight decay strength, total steps) are loosely described; optimal values may be task-specific.
- The threshold for "sufficiently small" initialization \(\kappa_0\) is not analytically derived; empirical thresholds may vary with activation and width.

## Confidence
- **Mechanism 1** (single-frequency emergence & phase alignment): **High** (backed by theorems and consistent empirical observations under small \(\kappa_0\)).
- **Mechanism 2** (majority-voting via diversification): **Medium** (relies on strong diversification assumptions that may break with limited width or non-uniform initialization).
- **Mechanism 3** (three-stage grokking): **Medium** (empirical evidence strong, but interplay of weight decay and loss minimization not fully formalized).

## Next Checks
