# SCOPE: Semantic Coreset with Orthogonal Projection Embeddings for Federated learning

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID**: 2603.12976
- **Source URL**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.12976
- **Reference count**: 40
- **Primary result**: SCOPE enables communication-efficient coreset selection for federated learning on non-IID scientific data without local training, achieving up to 512× bandwidth reduction and matching or exceeding full-dataset accuracy.

## Executive Summary
SCOPE tackles the challenge of federated learning under extreme data heterogeneity (non-IID, long-tailed scientific datasets) by allowing clients to select compact, globally representative coresets without any local training. It leverages zero-shot vision-language embeddings to compute per-sample utility metrics, aggregates only scalar statistics across clients to form a global class consensus, and applies a two-stage local filter that removes anomalies and prunes redundancy while preserving rare classes. This approach drastically reduces uplink communication (up to 512×), accelerates training (7.72× wall-clock speedup), and lowers computational costs, while maintaining or improving model accuracy compared to using the full distributed dataset.

## Method Summary
SCOPE operates in three phases. First, each client uses a pre-trained vision-language model (MobileCLIP-S2) to compute three zero-shot metrics for every local sample: Representation Score (alignment with true class prototype), Diversity Score (magnitude of orthogonal residual), and Boundary Proximity (similarity to incorrect class prototypes). Second, clients upload class-wise means and variances of these metrics to the server, which aggregates them via weighted averages and the Law of Total Variance to produce global statistics (µ, σ) and class rarity weights (W_c). Only these scalars are transmitted, ensuring communication efficiency. Third, each client standardizes its local metrics using the global statistics and applies a two-stage filter: a Consensus Filter prunes samples with high anomaly score (AS = Z_Sneg − Z_RS); a Dynamic Balancing Filter targets classes with high Local Pruning Priority (LPP = f_c / W_c) and within them prunes samples with high redundancy score (R = Z_RS − Z_Sneg − Z_DS). The resulting coresets are used in standard federated averaging.

## Key Results
- Achieves up to 512× uplink bandwidth reduction and 7.72× wall-clock speedup.
- Matches or exceeds full-dataset federated training accuracy (e.g., +0.85% on CIFAR-10 with imbalance ratio 2).
- Significantly outperforms state-of-the-art coreset methods under extreme imbalance, with robust convergence and effective long-tail preservation.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1: Zero-shot scoring without local training
SCOPE computes per-sample utility metrics (Representation Score, Diversity Score, Boundary Proximity) using a pre-trained vision-language model (MobileCLIP-S2) without any local training. The metrics are derived from geometric properties in the shared embedding space: RS measures alignment with the true class prototype, DS captures orthogonal residual magnitude, and S_neg indicates proximity to incorrect class boundaries. The core assumption is that these geometric relationships predict sample utility even in scientific domains. Evidence includes the paper's equations and experimental success, though direct support from the corpus is weak. Potential break condition: if the VLM lacks semantic grounding for specialized classes, the scores may not correlate with utility.

### Mechanism 2: Global consensus via scalar aggregation
To preserve privacy and reduce communication, clients compute class-centered means and variances of the three metrics locally and send only these scalars to the server. The server aggregates them using weighted averages and the Law of Total Variance to form global mean (µ) and variance (σ) per class, and also computes a rarity weight (W_c) inversely proportional to class frequency. This scalar summary is assumed to adequately represent the global distribution. Evidence is from the paper's aggregation equations. Break condition: under extreme local skew (e.g., single-class clients), local variances may be degenerate, leading to biased global estimates.

### Mechanism 3: Two-stage local filtering with global awareness
Guided by global statistics, each client applies two filters: (1) Consensus Filter removes semantic anomalies by standardizing metrics and computing Anomaly Score (AS = Z_Sneg − Z_RS), pruning the top p_l fraction; (2) Dynamic Balancing Filter prunes redundant samples in globally overrepresented classes. It computes a Local Pruning Priority (LPP = f_c / W_c) and targets classes where LPP exceeds threshold β; within those classes, it computes Redundancy Score (R = Z_RS − Z_Sneg − Z_DS) and prunes the top p_f fraction. The assumption is that this globally guided pruning preserves tail classes. Evidence includes the paper's filter descriptions and experiments showing tail preservation. Break condition: if β is mis-set, tail classes may be over-pruned or head classes under-pruned.

## Foundational Learning
- **Vision-Language Zero-Shot Embeddings**: Used to compute semantic scores without task-specific training by leveraging pre-trained models like CLIP that align images and text. *Why needed*: Enables training-free utility estimation. *Quick check*: How does using natural-language prompts for class labels improve semantic alignment over raw class tokens, and why might this fail for niche scientific domains?
- **Global Heterogeneity Decomposition via Law of Total Variance**: Allows accurate estimation of global metric distributions from locally skewed non-IID data by separating within-client and between-client variations without transmitting raw data. *Why needed*: To form a global consensus from heterogeneous clients. *Quick check*: Why is simple averaging of local means insufficient for global variance estimation in federated settings with extreme label skew?
- **Geometric Coreset Scoring with Redundancy and Boundary Awareness**: Scores samples based on representation fidelity, residual diversity, and decision boundary ambiguity in embedding space to select a representative subset. *Why needed*: To maintain performance while reducing communication and computation. *Quick check*: What is the trade-off between relying solely on representation score (RS) versus incorporating diversity (DS) and boundary (S_neg) scores for long-tail preservation?

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component map**:
Client-side: MobileCLIP-S2 scorer (image encoder, text prototype generator from prompts), metric calculators (RS, DS, S_neg), local profile generator (class-centered means/variances), two-stage filter (Consensus Filter with AS, Balancing Filter with R and LPP). Server-side: Global profile aggregator (rarity weight calculator, mean/variance combiner using Law of Total Variance), policy broadcaster. Training pipeline: Federated averaging with pruned coresets; no changes to base FL algorithm.

**Critical path**:
1. Clients extract scalars for all local samples using VLM (Section 3.3).
2. Clients compute class-centered stats and send to server (Algorithm 1, Phase 1).
3. Server aggregates into global policy (µ, σ, W_c) and broadcasts (Phase 2).
4. Clients apply two-stage filter using global policy to produce coresets (Phase 3).
5. Standard FL training with coresets; repeat across rounds if dynamic updating needed.

**Design tradeoffs**:
- Communication vs. accuracy: Using scalars vs. full embeddings saves bandwidth but loses granular information; scalar stats are empirically sufficient for consensus.
- Generality vs. domain fit: MobileCLIP-S2 is general-purpose; may underperform on highly specialized scientific data without fine-tuning, but relative geometric structure often suffices.
- Pruning aggressiveness: p_l controls noise removal, p_f controls redundancy reduction; β balances class targeting. Must tune based on imbalance ratio and dataset size.

**Failure signatures**:
- Low accuracy at high pruning: Indicates poor score calibration or global consensus failure; check if global variance is well-estimated and if VLM embeddings are semantically aligned.
- Tail class degradation: Suggests β too high or LPP miscalculation; inspect class-wise pruning rates to see if rare classes are being pruned.
- High communication despite scalars: If class count C is very large (e.g., fine-grained datasets), scalar payload may still be heavy; consider grouping classes or using top-k aggregation.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
**Open Question 1**: How can we extract data utility metrics at the edge without local training?
- *Basis*: RQ1 in the introduction.
- *Unresolved*: SCOPE's reliance on a fixed VLM (MobileCLIP) and its geometric assumptions may not hold for all scientific domains; the method's sensitivity to VLM choice and domain shift is not fully explored.
- *Resolution evidence*: A comprehensive evaluation across a diverse set of scientific datasets using a wide range of VLMs (including domain-specific models) and a theoretical analysis of the conditions under which the VLM embedding space preserves class semantics for scoring.

**Open Question 2**: How can we share global distributions without transmitting heavy, privacy-compromised embeddings?
- *Basis*: RQ2 in the introduction.
- *Unresolved*: While scalar sharing reduces bandwidth, the privacy implications are not formally quantified; repeated sharing of these statistics could enable reconstruction attacks or leakage of private data properties.
- *Resolution evidence*: A formal privacy analysis (e.g., demonstrating differential privacy bounds) of the aggregation protocol, combined with empirical studies measuring reconstruction risk from the shared scalars.

**Open Question 3**: How can clients safely prune data without exacerbating global class imbalance?
- *Basis*: RQ3 in the introduction.
- *Unresolved*: The global profile construction relies on noisy scalar aggregates from heterogeneous clients; under extreme non-IID conditions or low participation, the profile may misrepresent true class rarity, causing tail class degradation. Additionally, the redundancy score's correlation with actual data redundancy across domains is not theoretically justified.
- *Resolution evidence*: Robustness experiments with highly skewed Dirichlet partitions and very few participating clients, and a theoretical bound linking the redundancy score to preservation of tail class performance.

## Limitations
- VLM embeddings may be semantically misaligned for specialized scientific domains, leading to poor utility score calibration.
- Scalar aggregation can produce biased global statistics under extreme client skew (e.g., single-class clients).
- Two-stage filter hyperparameters and ambiguous LPP computation may compromise tail class preservation.
