# Hyperparameter Trajectory Inference with Conditional Lagrangian Optimal Transport

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2603.01771
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.01771
- **Reference count:** 40
- **Primary result:** The proposed full method (GθG+Û) consistently outperforms alternatives in diverse tasks (e.g., cancer therapy surrogate reward 102.5 vs 83.6, reacher reward –6.09 vs –6.16) and reduces computational workload dramatically.

## Executive Summary
Neural network performance often depends on hyperparameters (e.g., regularization strengths, reward weights) that are fixed during design but changing them later requires costly retraining. This paper introduces Hyperparameter Trajectory Inference (HTI), which learns a conditional dynamics model from a few samples of a network at different hyperparameter values and constructs a surrogate distribution for any intermediate or new hyperparameter. HTI uses conditional Lagrangian optimal transport (CLOT) with a learned neural metric and a density-based potential to encode inductive biases (least action, dense traversal). Experiments on reinforcement learning, quantile regression, and dropout regularization show consistent improvements over baselines and large reductions in training cost.

## Method Summary
HTI aims to learn a continuous mapping from a hyperparameter λ to the conditional output distribution p(y|x,λ) given observed marginals at a few anchor λ values. It models the transport between marginals as geodesics under a learned Lagrangian L(q, ˙q|x) = ½ ˙qᵀ G(q|x) ˙q – Û(q|x). The kinetic term uses a condition-dependent Riemannian metric GθG (a neural network producing a positive‑definite matrix via Givens rotations and eigenvalues with a fixed sum “budget”), while the potential term Û is a kernel density estimate that encourages paths to traverse dense regions of the data manifold. Both the metric and the optimal transport maps/geodesics are trained jointly in an alternating min‑max procedure: the inner loop solves a conditional optimal transport dual (maximizing a Kantorovich potential while refining the transport map and spline parameters via L‑BFGS), and the outer loop updates the metric to minimize the induced transport cost. At inference, given a new λ, the method selects the enclosing interval, samples from the lower anchor marginal, applies the learned transport map, and evaluates a spline generator at the normalized time to obtain a surrogate output.

## Key Results
- Cancer therapy RL: Average reward 102.5 (full method) vs 83.6 (best baseline), workload reduced from 38 to 11 GPU hours.
- Reacher control: Average reward –6.09 vs –6.16.
- Quantile regression: MSE 0.608 vs 0.620.
- Dropout regularization: Wasserstein distance 0.079 vs 0.060 (Û‑only best).
- Ablations confirm both the neural metric (least‑action bias) and the density potential (dense‑traversal bias) contribute to performance.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- **Claim:** Jointly learning a conditional Lagrangian and the associated optimal transport maps/geodesics enables accurate inference of conditional probability paths across hyperparameters.
- **Mechanism:** The Lagrangian `L=K−Û` with `K=½ ˙qᵀ G(q|x) ˙q` defines geodesics as least‑action curves. Neural networks approximate the metric `GθG`, Kantorovich potentials `gθg,k`, transport maps `TθT,k`, and a spline generator `SθS`. Training alternates: inner maximization fits observed marginals via the dual conditional OT objective; outer minimization adjusts the metric to reduce the associated transport cost. The density‑based `Û` is pre‑computed and fixed.
- **Core assumption:** Hyperparameter‑induced distributions are connected by smooth geodesics in a condition‑dependent Riemannian geometry.
- **Evidence anchors:** [abstract] "jointly learning the Lagrangian function"; [section 4.2] min‑max Algorithm 1; [corpus] extension of neural OT with Lagrangian costs (Pooladian et al., 2024).
- **Break condition:** True dynamics are chaotic or discontinuous; metric collapse (eigenvalues → 0).

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim:** The potential term `Û(q|x)=α log(ˆp(q|x)+ε)` biases geodesics toward dense regions of the conditional data manifold, improving generalization.
- **Mechanism:** `Û` is high where the kernel density estimate `ˆp` is high, raising the Lagrangian cost for low‑density areas, so least‑action paths naturally avoid them.
- **Core assumption:** The conditional output distribution concentrates on a low‑dimensional manifold; traversing high‑density regions yields plausible intermediates.
- **Evidence anchors:** [abstract] "inductive biases based on the manifold hypothesis"; [section 4.1] `Û` definition via Nadaraya‑Watson estimator.
- **Break condition:** Poor kernel bandwidth selection; if data distribution is uniformly multimodal, the bias may force paths through irrelevant regions.

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim:** A neural metric parametrization using learned eigenvalues and Givens rotations provides a flexible, positive‑definite, condition‑dependent geometry without degenerate solutions.
- **Mechanism:** `GθG = RθG EθG RθGᵀ` with `RθG` built from Givens rotations and `EθG` diagonal positive entries summing to a fixed eigenvalue budget.
- **Core assumption:** The optimal transport geodesics can be captured by a smoothly varying Riemannian metric; the budget prevents vanishing eigenvalues while allowing anisotropy.
- **Evidence anchors:** [section 4.3] parametrization details; [section 5.5] ablation showing extension to higher dimensions; [corpus] builds on Pooladian et al. (2024).
- **Break condition:** True metric requires more expressive coupling than rotations; budget set improperly causing optimization instability.

## Foundational Learning

- **Optimal Transport & Semi‑Dual Formulation**  
  *Why needed:* HTI uses conditional OT to define deterministic maps between marginal distributions. The semi‑dual reformulation reduces the problem to optimizing a single potential, enabling neural approximation via the c‑transform.  
  *Quick check:* "What is the c‑transform, and how does it turn the constrained Kantorovich dual into an unconstrained objective over one potential?"

- **Lagrangian Mechanics & Geodesics**  
  *Why needed:* The path‑dependent cost is a Lagrangian `L = K − Û`; geodesics (least‑action curves) under it encode the bias that observed dynamics are optimal in a physically meaningful sense.  
  *Quick check:* "How does the kinetic term `½ ˙qᵀ G(q) ˙q` affect the geodesic shape compared to a Euclidean cost `‖y₀−y₁‖²`?"

- **Amortized Neural Approximators for OT Maps**  
  *Why needed:* Solving OT per query is expensive; amortization trains networks to directly predict transport maps, geodesic parameters, and potentials, scaling to high dimensions.  
  *Quick check:* "In the alternating training, what does the inner maximization achieve versus the outer minimization? How does this relate to the least‑action principle?"

## Architecture Onboarding

### Component map
Û estimator (kernel density) -> GθG (neural metric) -> {gθg,k, TθT,k} (per‑interval potential & map), SθS (spline generator) -> Algorithm 2 (inference)

### Critical path
1. Pre‑process: Compute Û on all observed (y,x) with chosen bandwidths.
2. Initialize θG, {θg,k, θT,k}, θS.
3. Outer loop (e.g., 2001 iterations):
   - Inner loop per interval k (e.g., 0→5):
     * Repeat N_inner times (e.g., 10):
       - Compute warm‑start TθT_k(y) and refine via L‑BFGS (T_c); compute gc.
       - Update θ_T to minimize MSE(TθT, T_c).
       - Update θ_g to maximize dual loss `L_dual`.
       - Update θ_S to minimize spline action `L_path`.
     * After inner loop, compute summed transport costs and update θ_G to minimize `L_metric`.
4. Inference: For target λ, find enclosing interval; sample y_k ~ μ_t_k(·|x); map to y_{k+1}=TθT_k(y_k); get spline parameters φ=SθS(y_k,y_{k+1},x); evaluate spline at normalized time s*.

### Design tradeoffs
- **Metric parametrization:** Learned eigenvalues + Givens rotations (expressive, non‑degenerate) vs. fixed metric (simpler). Budget constraint needed to prevent collapse.
- **Density potential Û:** Non‑parametric data‑driven bias; sensitive to bandwidths h_x, h_y and weight α. Trade‑off between dense traversal and metric influence.
- **Interval‑wise networks:** Simpler implementation but number grows with anchor count. Could be shared by conditioning on both times instead.
- **Amortization:** Avoids per‑query L‑BFGS at inference but introduces approximation error; L‑BFGS during training mitigates but adds overhead.

### Failure signatures
- **Degenerate metric:** Eigenvalues collapse → kinetic vanishes. Monitor EθG values.
- **Inaccurate Û:** Bandwidth mis‑specification leads to over/under‑smoothed density. Inspect p̂ range.
- **Training instability:** Alternating min‑max oscillates. Balance learning rates (η_G vs. η_other) and inner steps.
- **Poor generalization:** If true distributions are not geodesics under learned Lagrangian (chaotic/shifted), surrogates at unobserved λ may be unrealistic. Validate on withheld anchors.

### First 3 experiments
1. **Synthetic semicircles** (Sec. 5.1): 2D controlled geometry; ablations separate least‑action (Kθ) and dense‑traversal (Û) contributions.
2. **Cancer therapy RL** (Sec. 5.2.1): Real‑world; learns policy dynamics from three anchor reward weights; surrogate beats baselines in reward and NK cell preservation.
3. **Reacher continuous control** (Sec. 5.2.2): Validates on different environment with torque penalty weight λ_c; full method highest reward.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- **Multiple hyperparameters:** How to extend HTI to simultaneously vary multiple continuous hyperparameters? Naive dimensionality reduction (e.g., Hilbert curves) has limitations.
- **Chaotic dynamics:** How does HTI perform when hyperparameter‑induced dynamics are chaotic or highly non‑smooth? The least‑action and dense‑traversal biases may not hold.
- **Theoretical guarantees:** What are the sample complexity and error bounds for CLOT‑based HTI? The paper is empirical; no convergence or robustness analysis provided.

## Limitations
- **Implementation gaps:** Critical hyperparameters (N_inner, batch size, FiLM exact form, L‑BFGS tolerance, kernel ε) are underspecified, hindering replication.
- **Density estimator:** Kernel density Û scales poorly with dimension and is sensitive to bandwidth choices; may degrade on high‑dimensional tasks.
- **Model assumption:** Assumes hyperparameter‑induced dynamics follow least‑action geodesics; this may fail with discontinuities or chaotic shifts.

## Confidence
- **CLOT learning infers accurate conditional paths:** Medium. Promising results but depends on delicate hyperparameters and training stability.
- **Dense‑traversal bias (Û) improves generalization:** Medium. Ablations show benefit; sensitivity to bandwidth and α under‑explored.
- **Neural metric parametrization yields flexible, non‑degenerate geometry:** Medium‑High. Eigen‑budget prevents collapse; expressiveness vs. full PD matrices unclear.
- **Overall HTI beats baselines:** Medium. Consistent gains across tasks but comparison set limited; scalability to many anchors untested.

## Next Checks
1. **Hyperparameter sensitivity sweep:** Systematically vary h_x, h_y, α, eigenvalue budget on all reported tasks to quantify robustness and failure thresholds.
2. **Stress‑test on high dimensions and discontinuities:** Apply HTI to larger networks (e.g., image classifiers) and hyperparameters causing discrete mode switches (e.g., dropout rate near 0 vs 1) to probe geodesic assumption.
3. **Ablate density and metric design:** Replace kernel Û with a normalizing‑flow density estimator and compare against an end‑to‑end learned unconstrained metric to isolate each inductive bias’s contribution.
