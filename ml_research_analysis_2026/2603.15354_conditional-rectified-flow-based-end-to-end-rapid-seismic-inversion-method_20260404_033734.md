# Conditional Rectified Flow-based End-to-End Rapid Seismic Inversion Method
## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2603.15354
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.15354
- Reference count: 31
- Primary result: CRF-FWI achieves comparable fidelity to diffusion models but with 50-100x fewer sampling steps (4-5 NFEs vs. 500-1000), enabling rapid seismic inversion.

## Executive Summary
Seismic full-waveform inversion (FWI) is computationally intensive and sensitive to initial models. This paper introduces Conditional Rectified Flow (CRF) for end-to-end seismic inversion, combining a multi-scale seismic encoder with layer-wise conditional injection via MLP-based fusion. On the OpenFWI benchmark, CRF-FWI outperforms InversionNet in accuracy (e.g., SSIM 0.778 vs. 0.658 on CurveFaultB) and matches diffusion models in fidelity while requiring only 4-5 sampling steps—dramatically reducing computation. Zero-shot tests on the Marmousi model demonstrate that CRF-generated initial velocity models are of high quality, alleviating the initialization problem in traditional FWI.

## Method Summary
The method frames velocity model inversion as a conditional generative task. A multi-scale encoder processes observed seismic data into hierarchical feature representations. These features are fused with the iterative reverse process of a rectified flow model via MLP-based conditioning at each layer. Rectified flow, which bridges data and noise via a deterministic ODE, enables high-quality generation with very few function evaluations (NFEs). The entire pipeline is trained end-to-end, learning to map seismic waveforms directly to plausible velocity models.

## Key Results
- On OpenFWI, CRF-FWI achieves SSIM 0.778 on CurveFaultB, surpassing InversionNet's 0.658.
- Sampling efficiency: only 4-5 NFEs versus 500-1000 for diffusion models, a 50-100× acceleration.
- Zero-shot application to Marmousi produces high-quality initial velocity models, showing promise for reducing FWI's dependence on good starting models.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
Rectified flow provides a deterministic mapping between noise and data distributions, requiring few steps to sample. The multi-scale encoder captures both local and global seismic patterns, while layer-wise conditional injection ensures that the seismic information guides the generation at every resolution. The MLP-based fusion adaptively combines features, maintaining efficiency and conditioning strength. Together, these design choices yield fast, accurate inversion without the stochastic sampling burden of diffusion models.

## Foundational Learning
- **Rectified Flow**: A generative modeling technique that learns an ODE mapping noise to data, enabling fast deterministic sampling. *Why needed*: Replaces slow iterative diffusion with few-step generation. *Quick check*: Verify that the flow model's vector field is trained with a regression loss between paired noise and data.
- **Conditional Generation**: The model generates velocity conditioned on seismic data. *Why needed*: Enables direct mapping from observations to solution. *Quick check*: Ensure conditioning signals are properly integrated at multiple layers.
- **Multi-Scale Encoding**: Processing inputs at multiple resolutions to capture features of different scopes. *Why needed*: Seismic data contains both fine-scale reflectors and large-scale velocity trends. *Quick check*: Confirm encoder outputs feature maps at several scales and that they are used.
- **Layer-wise Conditional Injection**: Injecting conditioning information at each layer of the generative model. *Why needed*: Provides persistent guidance throughout the generation process. *Quick check*: Check that each decoder/flow layer receives conditioning via MLP fusion.
- **MLP-based Fusion**: Using multi-layer perceptrons to combine conditioning features with latent representations. *Why needed*: Offers flexible, non-linear integration without heavy overhead. *Quick check*: Inspect that fusion modules are lightweight and trained end-to-end.
- **End-to-End Training**: Optimizing the entire pipeline jointly. *Why needed*: Aligns the encoder and flow model for optimal performance. *Quick check*: Confirm no separate pre-training stages; loss computed on final output.

## Architecture Onboarding
### Component Map
Seismic Data -> Multi-Scale Encoder -> Feature Pyramid -> MLP Fusion (per layer) -> Rectified Flow (UNet-like) -> Velocity Model

### Critical Path
1. Input seismic data passes through the multi-scale encoder to produce features at different resolutions.
2. For each iteration (or layer) of the rectified flow's reverse process, the corresponding scale's features are fused via an MLP into the flow's hidden state.
3. The rectified flow ODE is integrated over a few steps, starting from noise and gradually transformed into a velocity model under the guidance of the seismic features.

### Design Tradeoffs
- **Speed vs. Fidelity**: Rectified flow reduces steps dramatically but may slightly lag behind high-step diffusion models in fine detail quality.
- **Complexity vs. Efficiency**: Multi-scale encoder and per-layer fusion increase model capacity and memory, but remain far cheaper than iterating PDE solves in traditional FWI.
- **Generality vs. Overfitting**: Data-driven training on OpenFWI may not generalize to all geological settings; zero-shot tests show some transferability but robustness is uncertain.

### Failure Signatures
- Poor reconstruction when seismic data is noisy or has acquisition geometry not seen during training.
- Generated velocity models with unrealistic artifacts (e.g., sharp discontinuities, smoothed layers) if conditioning fails or flow training is unstable.
- Degeneration of performance on models with complex fault systems if the encoder fails to capture multi-scale structures.

### First Experiments
1. **Baseline Benchmarking**: Train CRF-FWI on OpenFWI training split, evaluate SSIM, FID on test splits; compare to InversionNet and diffusion baselines.
2. **Sampling Efficiency Sweep**: Measure output quality (e.g., MSE, SSIM) as a function of NFEs (1 to 10 steps) to confirm the 4-5 step sweet spot.
3. **Zero-Shot Generalization**: Apply the trained model to the Marmousi dataset without retraining; compute forward modeling error and structural similarity against true model to assess initial model quality.

## Limitations
- Performance is validated mainly on the OpenFWI benchmark; generalization to diverse, noisy field data remains uncertain.
- Comparison to diffusion models is limited to InversionNet and unspecified baselines; modern diffusion architectures might yield different fidelity trade-offs.
- Claims of 50-100× acceleration rely on NFEs; actual wall-clock speedup depends on hardware and implementation details and needs empirical verification.

## Confidence
- **CRF reduces NFEs dramatically vs. diffusion**: High
- **CRF-FWI outperforms InversionNet on OpenFWI**: Medium
- **Zero-shot Marmousi generates high-quality initial models**: Low
