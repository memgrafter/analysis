# Periodic Regularized Q-Learning
## Quick Facts
- **ArXiv ID:** 2602.03301  
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.03301  
- **Reference count:** 40  
- **Primary result:** PRQ combines a regularized projection operator with a two‑loop structure to achieve finite‑time convergence guarantees for Q‑learning with linear function approximation under both i.i.d. and Markovian sampling, resolving the instability of the deadly triad.

## Executive Summary
The paper tackles the instability of Q‑learning when combined with linear function approximation—a core instance of the “deadly triad.” It introduces Periodic Regularized Q‑Learning (PRQ), which uses a regularized projection to make the projected Bellman operator a contraction and a two‑loop algorithm: an inner loop solves a strongly‑convex regularized subproblem via stochastic gradient descent (SGD) with fixed target parameters, while an outer loop periodically updates the targets. Theoretical analysis yields explicit finite‑time convergence bounds for both i.i.d. and Markovian data. Experiments on a crafted MDP confirm that both regularization and periodic updates are essential: regularized Q‑learning alone diverges, while PRQ converges stably. This work unifies and strengthens prior results on regularized Q‑learning, providing a theoretically grounded and general approach.

## Method Summary
PRQ operates in a linear function approximation setting: Q(s,a) ≈ ϕ(s,a)⊤θ with feature matrix Φ. The core is the regularized projected Bellman equation (RP‑BE): Φθ = Γη T Φθ, where Γη = Φ(Φ⊤DΦ+ηI)⁻¹Φ⊤D is a regularized projection (weighted by D) and T is the Bellman operator. The algorithm implements a two‑loop structure:
- **Inner loop:** For a fixed target θₜ (the previous outer iterate), perform K steps of SGD on the loss  
  Lη(θ, θₜ) = ½‖Γ(R+γPΠ_Φθₜ Φθₜ) – Φθ‖²_D + (η/2)‖θ‖²,  
  which is strongly convex and smooth due to η>0 and positive definite Φ⊤DΦ. This yields an approximate solution θₜ,K.
- **Outer loop:** Periodically set θₜ₊₁,₀ ← θₜ,K, i.e., the current inner iterate becomes the next target. This decouples the max‑operator’s non‑expansiveness from stochastic updates, avoiding the double‑sampling problem.
The method is extended to model‑free settings with i.i.d. samples from a state‑action distribution d or with Markovian trajectories under a behavior policy β. Key hyperparameters: regularization η, inner steps K, SGD step size α, and number of outer iterations T.

## Key Results
- PRQ converges finitely to the unique RP‑BE solution θ*_η with explicit sample complexity bounds.
- Under i.i.d. sampling, the total samples needed to achieve E[‖Φ(θ – θ*_η)‖_∞²] ≤ ε is  
  O( l_η ‖θ*_η‖² / (ε μ_η³ (1 – γ‖Γ_η‖_∞)³) ), where μ_η and l_η depend on Φ⊤DΦ+ηI.
- Under Markovian sampling, an extra factor of the hitting time τ_max appears in the complexity.
- Experiments on a synthetic MDP demonstrate that both regularization and periodic updates are indispensable: regularized Q‑learning (RegQ) diverges even with η>0, while PRQ converges stably.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The stability of PRQ arises from three synergistic mechanisms:

1. **Contraction via regularized projection.** Adding ηI to the projection matrix yields Γη, which shrinks the spectral norm. For sufficiently large η, γ‖Γ_η‖∞ < 1 holds, making ΓηT a contraction. This guarantees linear convergence of the ideal RP‑VI update to a unique fixed point θ*_η, even when the unregularized projected Bellman equation has no solution. Evidence: Lemma 3.2 (contraction condition), Lemma 4.1 (RP‑VI convergence). Break condition: if η is too small, γ‖Γ_η‖∞ ≥ 1 and RP‑VI may diverge.

2. **Inner‑loop SGD with strong convexity.** For each outer iteration, the inner loop approximately minimizes a strongly‑convex regularized subproblem Lη(θ, θₜ) with fixed target θₜ. The strong convexity (μ_η) and smoothness (l_η) of Lη, induced by η>0 and positive definite Φ⊤DΦ, enable a finite‑time analysis of SGD. The inner loop error decays as K increases and is controlled by the step size α. Evidence: Lemma F.1 (properties of Lη), Lemma G.1 (one‑step descent), Proposition 6.1 (loss gap → parameter error). Break condition: insufficient inner steps (small K) cause residual error that accumulates over outer iterations.

3. **Periodic target updates to avoid double sampling.** The outer loop periodically copies the current inner iterate to the target slot: θₜ₊₁,₀ ← θₜ,K. This decouples the max‑operator’s non‑expansiveness from the stochastic gradient updates, avoiding the double‑sampling problem that plagues naive Q‑learning. The overall recursion resembles a noisy RP‑VI with a small perturbation from inner‑loop inaccuracies. Evidence: Section 4 (RP‑VI as ideal), Section 5 (algorithm). Break condition: using the same parameter for current and target (as in RegQ) causes oscillations or divergence even with regularization, as confirmed experimentally.

## Foundational Learning
- **Concept: Strong convexity/smoothness of the inner‑loop objective Lη(θ, θ′).**  
  *Why needed:* These properties enable a finite‑time convergence analysis for SGD; they provide a gradient–loss gap and bound the effect of step size. The condition numbers involving μ_η and l_η directly appear in the sample‑complexity bounds.  
  *Quick check:* If Φ⊤DΦ were singular but η>0, what happens to μ_η and stability? μ_η = λ_min(Φ⊤DΦ)+η remains positive, preserving strong convexity and thus stability.

- **Concept: Contraction of the regularized projected Bellman operator ΓηT.**  
  *Why needed:* Contraction is the key theoretical guarantee that RP‑VI (and thus PRQ with accurate inner loop) converges linearly. It circumvents the non‑contraction issue of the standard projected Bellman operator.  
  *Quick check:* How does increasing η affect ‖Γ_η‖∞ and the fixed point bias? Larger η reduces ‖Γ_η‖∞ (strengthening contraction) but increases the bias of θ*_η away from the true optimal Q*.

- **Concept: Stochastic approximation with Markovian noise.**  
  *Why needed:* In practice, data comes from a single trajectory, leading to temporally dependent samples. The analysis must account for this Markovian dependence, typically via Poisson’s equation and mixing time.  
  *Quick check:* Why does the Markovian sample complexity include an extra τ_max factor compared to i.i.d.? Because the effective sample size is reduced by the hitting time (mixing time), introducing an additional dependence on the Markov chain’s mixing properties.

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component map:**  
Inner loop (SGD on Lη) → Outer loop (periodic reset) → Target update. Regularizer η influences projection operator Γ_η. Sampling module provides transitions either i.i.d. from d or from a Markovian trajectory with behavior policy β.

**Critical path:**
1. Initialize θ₀ arbitrarily.
2. For each outer iteration t:
   - Set inner target θₜ,₀ = θₜ₋₁,K.
   - For k = 0 to K−1:
     * Sample transition (s,a,s′,r) according to sampling mode.
     * Compute gradient g(θₜ,k, θₜ,₀; o) as ∇_θ Lη evaluated at the sample.
     * Update θₜ,k+1 = θₜ,k + α·( –g(θₜ,k, θₜ,₀; o) ).
   - Set θₜ₊₁,₀ ← θₜ,K.
3. After T outer iterations, output θ_{T,K}.

**Design tradeoffs:**
- Regularization η: Larger η guarantees contraction (γ‖Γ_η‖∞<1) but increases bias of θ*_η. Smaller η reduces bias but risks non‑contraction.
- Inner loop length K: Larger K reduces inner approximation error but increases samples per outer step; K must scale as O(1/ε) for final ε‑accuracy.
- SGD step size α: Must satisfy α ≤ min{ 3μ_η/(l_η g_{1,η}), 2/μ_η } (Theorem 6.4); too large causes SGD divergence.

**Failure signatures:**
- **RegQ (no periodic reset):** Parameter oscillations and eventual divergence even with η>0.
- **PRQ with too small K:** Inner loop under‑solved; error accumulates, leading to slow convergence or instability.
- **η too small:** Contraction condition violated (γ‖Γ_η‖∞ ≥ 1); RP‑VI diverges → PRQ diverges.
- **α too large:** SGD in inner loop diverges; monitor loss Lη during inner iterations—if it increases, α is likely too high.

**First 3 experiments (from Section 7):**
1. **Model‑based RP‑VI vs. semi‑gradient RegQ** (Figure 2): On a crafted MDP where the projected Bellman equation has no solution, RP‑VI (PRQ’s ideal update) converges to the RP‑BE solution, while RegQ oscillates indefinitely.
2. **i.i.d. sample‑based PRQ vs. RegQ** (Figure 3): With samples drawn i.i.d. from a state‑action distribution d, PRQ stabilizes to a small neighborhood of the solution; RegQ exhibits large periodic swings and fails to converge.
3. **Markovian sample‑based PRQ vs. RegQ** (Figure 4): Using a single trajectory under behavior policy β, PRQ converges (as predicted by Theorem 6.5), whereas RegQ diverges or wanders erratically.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
1. **Extension to nonlinear function approximation?** The paper focuses on linear features; open whether PRQ’s two‑loop structure can be analyzed for deep networks. Current deep RL analyses rely on restrictive assumptions. A theoretical or empirical demonstration on benchmarks like Atari would be needed.
2. **Approximation error of the RP‑BE solution?** The paper does not bound the difference between the regularized solution θ*_η and the true optimal Q*. Understanding the bias‑regularization trade‑off is crucial for assessing practical performance.
3. **Necessity of the contraction condition?** The analysis requires γ‖Γ_η‖∞ < 1. It is unclear if this condition is necessary or if weaker conditions (e.g., spectral radius <1) suffice. Relaxing it could broaden applicability.
4. **Practical benefits on standard benchmarks?** Experiments are limited to a tiny hand‑crafted MDP. Whether PRQ yields improved sample efficiency or stability on common RL benchmarks (e.g., Atari) compared to methods like DQN with target networks remains unknown.

## Limitations
- **Missing experimental details:** Key hyperparameters (α, K, T), initialization, sampling distribution d, and the weighting matrix D are not specified, preventing exact reproduction.
- **Limited empirical scope:** Evaluation uses only one synthetic MDP (|S|=|A|=2) designed to break RegQ; generalizability to larger or more realistic tasks is not demonstrated.
- **Theoretical bounds may be loose:** Sample complexity depends on problem‑dependent constants (e.g., ‖θ*_η‖, μ_η⁻³) that could be large; no empirical validation of the scaling with ε or γ is provided.

## Confidence
- **Contraction mechanism and two‑loop structure:** High (anchored in Lemmas 3.2, 4.1 and clear algorithmic design).
- **Empirical validation (PRQ converges, RegQ diverges):** Medium (results shown but reproducibility hindered by missing details; sensitive to hyperparameters).
- **Necessity of periodic updates:** High (ablation in Section 7 shows RegQ divergence even with regularization on the tested MDP).
