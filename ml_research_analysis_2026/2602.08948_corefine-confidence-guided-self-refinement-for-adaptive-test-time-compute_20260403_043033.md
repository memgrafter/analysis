# CoRefine: Confidence-Guided Self-Refinement for Adaptive Test-Time Compute

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID**: 2602.08948  
- **Source URL**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.08948  
- **Reference count**: 40  
- **Primary result**: Matches or exceeds Majority@512 accuracy while using ~190× fewer tokens via a lightweight confidence-guided controller.

## Executive Summary
CoRefine introduces a novel approach to reduce the exorbitant token costs of test-time scaling in LLM reasoning. Instead of generating hundreds of parallel traces, it uses a tiny (~211K parameter) controller to interpret token-level confidence dynamics from a frozen LLM and make adaptive decisions: halt, rethink, or switch approaches. This controller drives a sequential refinement loop, leveraging compacted history and targeted prompts to guide the LLM toward correct answers with far fewer tokens. Across multiple math benchmarks and models, CoRefine achieves comparable accuracy to massive parallel sampling at a fraction of the computational cost, while also reducing wall-clock time.

## Method Summary
CoRefine operates by first generating a reasoning trace with token logprobs from a frozen LLM. A confidence extractor computes per-token negative average log-probability of top-k tokens, then aggressively downsamples to 16 bins. A lightweight 3-layer Conv1D controller maps this fixed-length confidence trace to one of three actions: **HALT**, **RETHINK** (review recent reasoning), or **ALTERNATIVE** (try a new approach). The controller is trained on oracle-labeled trajectories—labels derived from the outcome of subsequent refinement iterations (e.g., if the same approach later succeeds, label is RETHINK). At inference, the controller’s decision triggers a synthesis prompt that includes the original problem, compacted history, and action-specific instructions. The loop repeats until a HALT or max iterations. A hybrid tree variant adds parallel warmup and branching for improved robustness on harder problems.

## Key Results
- CoRefine matches or exceeds Majority@512 accuracy while using ~190× fewer tokens (e.g., 0.38M vs 35.5M on AIME24 with DeepSeek-8B).
- The controller achieves 92.6% precision when confidently halting and enables early stopping on 92.5% of problems.
- Wall-clock time reduced by up to 63% at matched compute budgets versus parallel sampling.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1: Confidence as a Control Signal, Not a Correctness Estimator
Token-level confidence dynamics, even when poorly calibrated, provide a reliable control signal for deciding *when* and *how* to refine, not *whether* an answer is correct. The system extracts a downsampled confidence trace (16 bins) from the LLM’s logprobs during generation. A lightweight neural controller maps this temporal pattern to an action. The key insight is that patterns like late-phase divergence, early overconfidence, or mid-trace dips correlate with answer correctness and error type, enabling adaptive compute allocation without requiring perfectly calibrated probabilities.  
*Evidence*: Correct traces maintain higher late-phase confidence (Δ=1.2–1.5 logits) despite early overconfidence, while incorrect traces exhibit flat or declining trajectories. Model-specific patterns (e.g., DeepSeek’s terminal spike vs. Qwen3’s global descent) motivate a learned controller over handcrafted rules.

### Mechanism 2: Targeted Refinement via Controller-Guided Synthesis Prompts
The controller’s decisions enable targeted self-correction by providing context-specific guidance (RETHINK vs. ALTERNATIVE) and compacted history, which is more effective than generic “rethink” prompts or fixed iteration counts. Based on the confidence trace, the synthesis prompt combines the original problem, compacted summaries of previous attempts, and action-specific instructions. RETHINK prompts provide a truncated recent trace for careful review; ALTERNATIVE prompts explicitly demand a new approach. This directs the LLM’s refinement effort where it is most needed, avoiding wasted iterations on already-correct traces or futile corrections of fundamentally flawed approaches.  
*Evidence*: CoRefine averages ~2.7 iterations per problem vs. 20 for Majority@20, achieving similar or better accuracy. The controller’s action distribution (HALT 45.3%, ALTERNATIVE 34.6%, RETHINK 20.1%) shows active strategy selection.

### Mechanism 3: Adaptive Compute Allocation via Early Stopping
The controller learns problem-specific stopping criteria from confidence patterns, enabling early termination on confident, consistent answers while allocating more exploration to difficult problems. The HALT decision is triggered when the confidence trace indicates stability and answer consistency, learned from oracle labels that encode the *actual outcome* of refinement. A step penalty in the training loss encourages early halting when appropriate.  
*Evidence*: The controller achieves a 92.5% early stopping rate; early-stopped problems achieve 83.8% accuracy. HALT rates are higher on easier benchmarks (BRUMO25 66%, AIME24 64%) and lower on harder ones (HMMT25 48%), demonstrating adaptive allocation.

## Foundational Learning
- **Concept: Token-Level Confidence Extraction**  
  *Why needed*: The entire control signal is derived from per-token negative log-probability of top-k tokens. Understanding this extraction (Equation 1) is fundamental to interpreting the controller’s input.  
  *Quick check*: If a model’s token distribution is very peaked (high confidence) early in a wrong answer, how might this “early overconfidence” pattern influence a RETHINK vs. ALTERNATIVE decision?

- **Concept: Downsampling as Low-Pass Filtering**  
  *Why needed*: Raw confidence traces (5k–20k tokens) are noisy. Aggressive average pooling to L=16 bins smooths high-frequency noise while preserving macro-dynamics (early overconfidence, late divergence). This counter-intuitive design improves robustness.  
  *Quick check*: Why would downsampling to 16 bins improve controller accuracy versus using 256 bins or the full trace?

- **Concept: Oracle Label Generation for Sequential Data**  
  *Why needed*: The controller is trained via supervised learning, but labels are not directly available. Labels must be derived from the *outcome* of refinement trajectories—a form of counterfactual labeling.  
  *Quick check*: For an incorrect trace from a parallel sampling run (no iteration history), how do the authors generate an oracle label? What domain knowledge do their heuristics encode?

## Architecture Onboarding

### Component Map
Frozen LLM → Confidence Extractor → Controller (πθ) → Action → Synthesis Prompt Builder → LLM (next iteration) → ... → HALT/Aggregation

### Critical Path
1. LLM generates reasoning trace `y_t` and token logprobs.
2. Confidence Extractor computes downsample `ϕ_t ∈ R¹⁶`.
3. Controller argmax(action) from `ϕ_t`.
4. If action = HALT, return answer; else build synthesis prompt with compacted history and iterate.

### Design Tradeoffs
- **Controller Complexity vs. Performance**: Simpler configuration (raw confidence, L=16, ~211K params) achieves 83–84% val accuracy; adding regional statistics yields <1% gain for +30% params.
- **Sequential vs. Tree**: Pure sequential is simplest and most token-efficient. Tree adds parallel warmup + branching for robustness on hard problems, using slightly more tokens but often higher accuracy.
- **Conservatism**: The controller is “safety-first” (zero false HALTs in case studies), meaning it sometimes over-refines correct answers but almost never stops on wrong ones—a direct result of training on oracle correctness labels.
- **Prompt Engineering**: The two-phase prompt for BixBench (NEUTRAL→AGGRESSIVE) is critical to maintain training distribution fidelity while breaking refusal loops.

### Failure Signatures
- **False HALT**: Controller stops on an incorrect answer (rare, ~5% in analysis, 0% in case studies). Mitigated by Tree’s branching.
- **Excessive Iteration**: Controller fails to HALT, hits max iterations (e.g., on oscillating confidence). Addressed by max iteration cap.
- **Answer Extraction Failure**: Complex `\boxed{}` parsing errors can create false inconsistency signals.
- **Domain Shift Failure**: A controller trained on math may fail on biology if confidence patterns differ, though cross-task generalization shows robustness. BixBench required a 4-class controller with tailored labeling for “REFUSE”.

### First 3 Experiments
1. **Controller Validation Accuracy**: On held-out model-specific traces, confirm the controller predicts oracle actions at >83%. If lower, check confidence extraction and label generation.
2. **Halting Precision on “Confident” Problems**: Run CoRefine-Tree with small warmup (K=4) on 50–100 problems. For problems where ≥50% of tree nodes vote HALT, compute accuracy. Should be ~92%+.
3. **Token vs. Accuracy Pareto**: Compare CoRefine (max iters=20) against Majority@512 and Majority@20 on target benchmarks. CoRefine should dominate: higher accuracy than Majority@20 at lower tokens, and comparable accuracy to Majority@512 at far fewer tokens.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
1. **Cross-domain generalization**: Can the controller generalize beyond math (e.g., to code or scientific reasoning) without task-specific retraining? The paper’s evaluation is confined to math benchmarks; confidence patterns in other domains may differ substantially.
2. **Calibration dependence**: To what extent does performance depend on the underlying model’s confidence calibration? The paper notes better calibration may improve robustness but does not analyze sensitivity or propose calibration methods.
3. **Oracle-free training**: Can we eliminate the need for oracle-labeled trajectories by learning via reinforcement or weak supervision? This would expand applicability to domains without ground-truth verification.

## Limitations
- Oracle labeling heuristics for single-iteration traces are underspecified (no quantitative thresholds for confidence trends/volatility).
- Synthesis prompt compaction algorithm is vague (exact token truncation and summary extraction not detailed).
- Controller may default to HALT due to class imbalance (esp. Qwen3-32B’s 84% HALT labels), potentially reducing refinement.
