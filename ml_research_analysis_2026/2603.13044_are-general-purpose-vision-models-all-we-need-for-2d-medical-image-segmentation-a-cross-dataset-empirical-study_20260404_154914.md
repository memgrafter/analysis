# Are General-Purpose Vision Models All We Need for 2D Medical Image Segmentation? A Cross-Dataset Empirical Study

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2603.13044
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.13044
- Reference count: 30
- Primary result: General-purpose vision models (GP-VMs) consistently achieve higher segmentation accuracy than specialized medical segmentation architectures (SMAs) across three heterogeneous 2D medical datasets.

## Executive Summary
This paper presents a systematic empirical comparison between general-purpose vision models (GP-VMs) and specialized medical segmentation architectures (SMAs) for 2D medical image segmentation. Evaluating eleven models on dermoscopy, endoscopy, and echocardiography datasets under a unified training and evaluation protocol, the study finds that GP-VMs, benefiting from large-scale natural image pretraining and advanced architectural designs, generally outperform SMAs. The top GP-VM (VW-MiT) achieved 89.7% mean Dice on the challenging NeoPolyp dataset, surpassing the best SMA (SwinUMamba, 88.9%). Grad-CAM analysis suggests GP-VMs produce clinically plausible attention maps. The findings challenge the necessity of domain-specific architectural constraints for 2D segmentation and advocate for evaluating GP-VMs as a strong baseline before developing new specialized models.

## Method Summary
The study conducts a controlled benchmark across three 2D medical datasets: ISIC’18 (dermoscopy, 3565 images), NeoPolyp (endoscopy, 945 images), and CAMUS (echocardiography, 1996 images). After filtering duplicates and near-duplicates via perceptual hashing, data is split using random image-level folds for ISIC and NeoPolyp, and patient-aware folds for CAMUS. All models are trained under a unified protocol: ImageNet-pretrained encoders (except U-KAN trained from scratch), AdamW optimizer with a Reflected Exponential (REX) scheduler, dataset-specific losses, 512×512 input resolution (224×224 for HiFormer/MISSFormer), and early stopping at 150 epochs. Hyperparameters (learning rate from {1e-4, 5e-5, 1e-5}) are selected per model–dataset pair via validation mIoU over 100 epochs. Final evaluation uses five-fold cross-validation, reporting micro-averaged mDSC, mIoU, mRec, and mPrec without background. Explainability is assessed via Grad-CAM (M3d-CAM) on the 50 worst cases per fold.

## Key Results
- GP-VMs hold the top performance positions on all three datasets; the leading GP-VM (VW-MiT) outperforms the best SMA (SwinUMamba) by 0.8 percentage points on NeoPolyp (89.7% vs. 88.9% mDSC).
- Performance gaps between top GP-VMs and SMAs are modest (~1–2%) on ISIC’18 and CAMUS, but GP-VMs still achieve the highest scores (e.g., VW-MiT 91.7% mDSC on ISIC’18).
- Grad-CAM visualizations indicate that GP-VMs generate clinically relevant attention maps without domain-specific architectural inductive biases, focusing on lesion boundaries and anatomical structures.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1: Transfer from Natural Image Pretraining
- **Claim:** Large-scale natural image pretraining provides robust, transferable feature representations that generalize well to diverse 2D medical segmentation tasks.
- **Mechanism:** GP-VMs are pretrained on massive datasets like ImageNet, learning rich, multi-scale visual features (edges, textures, shapes) that are largely modality-agnostic. Fine-tuning these features on medical data requires less architectural specialization because the foundational representations are already highly discriminative.
- **Core assumption:** Low-to-mid-level visual features (e.g., edges, corners, textures) are shared between natural and medical images, allowing effective transfer learning despite domain shifts in color, contrast, and semantics.
- **Evidence anchors:** The paper notes GP-VMs benefit from extensive optimization and validation on millions of images. All GP-VMs use ImageNet-pretrained encoders. The empirical superiority of GP-VMs, even on grayscale echocardiography (CAMUS), supports the assumption. A break condition would be if a medical task relies on ultra-high-frequency patterns or artifacts absent in natural images (e.g., specific ultrasound speckle).

### Mechanism 2: Superior Multi-Scale Context Modeling
- **Claim:** Modern, flexible GP-VM architectures (e.g., transformer-based, hierarchical) inherently model multi-scale context and long-range dependencies more effectively than many SMAs constrained by U-Net-style skip-connections.
- **Mechanism:** Designs like SegFormer (transformer encoder + lightweight decoder) or VWFormer (varying window attention) explicitly aggregate context at multiple scales without rigid encoder-decoder symmetry, potentially capturing both fine boundaries and global structure more efficiently than some SMAs that rely on fixed skip connections.
- **Core assumption:** Architectural innovations from the general vision community (e.g., hierarchical transformers, attention mechanisms) are fundamentally superior for dense prediction, regardless of domain, when trained sufficiently.
- **Evidence anchors:** Top GP-VMs (VW-MiT, TransNeXt) consistently outperform SMAs, including SwinUMamba which also uses hierarchical transformers. Grad-CAM shows GP-VMs focus on clinically relevant regions, suggesting effective context modeling. A break condition could be on tasks with extreme class imbalance or tiny structures where rigid U-Net skip connections might force better feature reuse.

### Mechanism 3: Resource Asymmetry in Optimization and Engineering
- **Claim:** GP-VMs receive orders of magnitude more engineering effort (optimization, ablation, scaling) than most SMAs, leading to better convergence and generalization under the same training budget.
- **Mechanism:** GP-VMs are products of large-scale, competitive vision research with meticulous hyperparameter tuning, stable training recipes, and efficient designs. SMAs, often from smaller labs, may have less polished implementations or suboptimal default hyperparameters, creating an unfair comparison even with a unified protocol.
- **Core assumption:** The performance gap is not purely architectural but stems from differences in implementation maturity and optimization heritage.
- **Evidence anchors:** The abstract states GP-VMs benefit from extensive optimization and resources rarely available for MIS. The notes on deviations for some SMA implementations (e.g., U-KAN trained from scratch, MISSFormer/HiFormer at lower resolution) imply less optimized baselines. Neighbor work highlights automation challenges in MIS, suggesting SMA configurations are less standardized. If all models were retrained with exhaustive, equally thorough hyperparameter sweeps, the GP-VM advantage might diminish.

## Foundational Learning
- **Concept:** Transfer Learning & Domain Shift
  - **Why needed:** Understanding how features learned on natural images (ImageNet) apply to medical images with different statistics (grayscale, low contrast, artifacts) is central to interpreting why GP-VMs work.
  - **Quick check:** If a model pretrained on photos can segment an ultrasound, what visual primitives (edges, shapes) are invariant across modalities?
- **Concept:** Hierarchical Multi-Scale Representation
  - **Why needed:** Medical structures vary hugely in size (e.g., tiny polyp vs. entire heart chamber). GP-VMs often use hierarchical backbones that naturally build scale-invariant features, crucial for handling this variability.
  - **Quick check:** How does a transformer's patch hierarchy compare to a U-Net's encoder-decoder feature pyramid for capturing both a 5px pixel and a 200px organ?
- **Concept:** Standardized Empirical Evaluation
  - **Why needed:** The paper's core contribution is a controlled benchmark. Understanding threats like data leakage (patient-aware splits for CAMUS), augmentation consistency, and validation strategy (5-fold CV) is key to trusting the results.
  - **Quick check:** Why is patient-level splitting mandatory for CAMUS but not for ISIC, and how would leakage invalidate a comparison?

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map:** Encoder (ImageNet-pretrained backbone, e.g., MiT-B3, ConvNeXt-S) -> Decoder (Lightweight segmentation head, e.g., UPerHead or model-specific decoder) -> Training Harness (Unified protocol: AdamW + REX scheduler, dataset-specific augmentations, 512x512 input, 150 epochs with early stopping)
- **Critical path:**
  1. Load ImageNet-pretrained encoder weights.
  2. Attach task-specific decoder (UPerHead or native).
  3. Train end-to-end with medical data using the paper's exact augmentation/hyperparameters.
  4. Evaluate with 5-fold CV, no test-time augmentation.
- **Design tradeoffs:**
  - Encoder capacity vs. Medical data size: Larger GP-VMs may overfit on smaller datasets (NeoPolyp) but benefit on larger ones (ISIC). Consider freezing early encoder layers.
  - Decoder simplicity vs. performance: UPerHead is generic but may underfit complex boundaries vs. SMA-specific decoders with deep supervision.
  - Pretraining source: ImageNet pretraining helps but may introduce natural image biases (e.g., color reliance). For grayscale (CAMUS), RGB conversion or channel replication may be suboptimal.
- **Failure signatures:**
  - Class C1 failure on NeoPolyp: All models struggle here (e.g., SU-Mamba 59.2% vs VW-MiT 66.1%). If C1 DSC <60%, attention may be on irrelevant textures.
  - Edge blurring: GP-VMs may produce smoother boundaries than SMAs on high-frequency tasks (e.g., skin lesions). Compare prediction smoothness to U-Net.
  - Modality mismatch: If trained only on RGB dermoscopy, expect collapse on grayscale echocardiography without adaptation.
- **First 3 experiments:**
  1. Baseline replication: Train VW-MiT on ISIC’18 with the paper's protocol. Reproduce mDSC ~91.7% (±0.5%). If lower, debug augmentation or learning rate schedule.
  2. Grad-CAM sanity check: For 5 worst CAMUS cases, generate attention maps. Does the model focus on atrium/ventricle walls? If attention leaks to borders/noise, decoder may be poorly configured.
  3. Ablation of pretraining: Train the same VW-MiT from scratch on NeoPolyp. Expect >10% absolute DSC drop, confirming pretraining necessity. If drop is small, your dataset may be large enough to learn from scratch.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- **Open Question 1:** How do GP-VMs and SMAs generalize out-of-distribution (OOD) to related but held-out medical datasets from the same domain (e.g., other endoscopy benchmarks alongside NeoPolyp)?
  - **Basis:** Future work section explicitly states intent to "systematically assess OOD generalization by reserving related datasets for evaluation where appropriate (e.g., Kvasir-SEG alongside NeoPolyp)."
  - **Why unresolved:** The study used distinct datasets for cross-dataset evaluation but did not test generalization within a domain using separate test sets. Performance on the training distribution may not reflect robustness to domain shifts common in clinical practice.
- **Open Question 2:** Do the performance trends hold for 3D medical image segmentation or in extremely low-data regimes (e.g., <50 annotated volumes/images)?
  - **Basis:** The "Threats to validity" state findings "may not generalize to other 2D MIS tasks, 3D imaging settings, or extremely low-data regimes." The study only evaluated 2D datasets with hundreds to thousands of samples.
  - **Why unresolved:** The paper’s scope is explicitly 2D, and all selected datasets have moderate size. 3D segmentation introduces volumetric context and memory constraints; low-data regimes may exploit architectural inductive biases in SMAs more effectively.
- **Open Question 3:** For which specific clinical scenarios or data characteristics (e.g., ultra-small targets, severe class imbalance, unique artifacts) do specialized SMAs provide a statistically significant advantage over GP-VMs?
  - **Basis:** The discussion notes SMAs "may still be advantageous for certain tasks, data regimes, or deployment scenarios" but does not identify or test these conditions. Class-wise results show all models struggle on NeoPolyp C1, but it is unclear if SMAs have niche strengths.
  - **Why unresolved:** The study aggregates performance over entire datasets and classes. It does not perform a Failure Mode Analysis to correlate specific image-level or pixel-level properties (e.g., target size <10px, contrast-to-noise ratio) with model-specific accuracy differences.
- **Open Question 4:** Are the Grad-CAM attention maps from GP-VMs clinically more accurate or useful for expert interpretation compared to those from SMAs, and can this be quantified?
  - **Basis:** The paper qualitatively states GP-VMs "produce clinically plausible attention maps" but provides no quantitative comparison or clinical validation. Visualizations are limited to 50 worst cases per fold and lack expert assessment.
  - **Why unresolved:** Explainability in medical contexts requires clinical relevance, not just visual plausibility. Without numerical metrics (e.g., alignment with ground truth saliency) or clinician studies, the claim of superior XAI for GP-VMs remains anecdotal.

## Limitations
- Major methodological details (exact augmentation pipelines, perceptual hash thresholds, fold splits) are only available in the GitHub repository, hindering full replication.
- Implementation deviations for some SMAs (e.g., resolution changes, training from scratch) may create an uneven comparison, and the potential impact of unresolved hyperparameter tuning is unknown.
- The study's datasets, while heterogeneous, are limited to three modalities and moderate sizes; generalizability to rarer imaging types (e.g., microscopy, CT) or extremely small structures remains untested.

## Confidence
- **Primary claim (GP-VMs outperform SMAs):** Medium. Strong within-study evidence (unified protocol, 5-fold CV) but confidence reduced by unreproduced details and potential resource asymmetry.
- **Mechanism 1 (natural-pretraining transfer):** Medium. Supported by implied from-scratch ablation but lacks fine-grained analysis of which features transfer across modalities.
- **Mechanism 2 (architectural superiority):** Low-Medium. GP-VMs include top performers, but SwinUMamba (SMA) uses similar hierarchical transformers; the gap may be tuning/implementation, not architecture alone.
- **Mechanism 3 (resource asymmetry):** Medium. Acknowledged in text but not isolated experimentally—no equalized hyperparameter sweeps reported.
