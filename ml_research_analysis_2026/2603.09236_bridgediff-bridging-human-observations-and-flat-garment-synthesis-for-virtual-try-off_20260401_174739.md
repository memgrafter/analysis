# BridgeDiff: Bridging Human Observations and Flat-Garment Synthesis for Virtual Try-Off

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2603.09236
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.09236
- **Reference count:** 40
- **Primary result:** A diffusion-based framework with a Garment Condition Bridge Module and Flat Structure Constraint Module achieves state-of-the-art flat garment synthesis from dressed-person photos, improving occluded region continuity and structural stability.

## Executive Summary
Virtual try-off (VTOFF) seeks to recover a flat garment image from a photo of a person wearing it. Existing methods treat this as direct image translation, struggling with occlusions and the distribution shift between on-body and flat layouts. BridgeDiff introduces a two-stage diffusion framework. First, a Garment Condition Bridge Module (GCBM) extracts robust garment-cue representations from partial observations. Second, a Flat Structure Constraint Module (FSCM) injects explicit flat-structure priors during denoising. Experiments on DressCode and VITON-HD show significant gains in both quantitative metrics and qualitative consistency, particularly in occluded regions.

## Method Summary
BridgeDiff employs a two-stage training pipeline built on latent diffusion. Stage 1 trains the GCBM: a MetaFormer transformer aggregates encoded features from a warped garment view (using a cloth-agnostic mask), the model image, and a noisy version of the target flat garment to produce denoised garment cues \(F_c\). Stage 2 fine-tunes a Stable Diffusion-based denoising UNet. A separate model UNet extracts intermediate features \(M_o\) from the model image and its text description \(T_m\), which are masked to focus on the garment region (\(M'_o\)). The FSCM fuses the GCBM’s cues \(F_c\) with a flat-structure text prompt \(T_{f\,lat}\) via Flat-Constraint Attention, producing structural guidance \(Z_{f\,c}\). This guidance is injected into specific cross-attention layers of the denoising UNet alongside appearance text \(T_c\), decoupling structural and appearance conditioning. The model is trained with the DREAM objective and partial parameter freezing to preserve prior knowledge.

## Key Results
- BridgeDiff sets new state-of-the-art on DressCode (full test): FID 10.92, KID 3.86, PSNR 16.69, SSIM 80.23, LPIPS 23.10, DISTS 20.82.
- On VITON-HD, it achieves FID 9.08, KID 1.53, PSNR 15.00, SSIM 77.42, LPIPS 24.38, DISTS 18.69.
- Qualitative improvements show markedly better continuity in occluded regions and more stable garment geometry.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1: Garment Condition Bridge Module (GCBM)
- **Claim:** GCBM builds a garment-cue representation that captures global appearance and semantic identity, enabling robust inference of continuous details under partial visibility.
- **Mechanism:** GCBM uses a shared image encoder (OpenCLIP) to extract patch tokens from the warped garment \(X_w\), model image \(X_m\), and a noisy version of the flat garment. Learnable positional embeddings are added, and the concatenated sequence is processed by a MetaFormer transformer. The output tokens corresponding to the noisy branch are the cleaned garment cues \(F_c\).
- **Core assumption:** Explicitly modeling and denoising garment cues bridges the distribution gap between on-body observations (often partially occluded) and the canonical flat layout.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract] "Garment Condition Bridge Module (GCBM) builds garment-cue representation that captures global appearance and semantic identity, enabling robust inference of continuous details under partial visibility."
  - [section 3.1] Formal definition of GCBM architecture, loss function (Eq. 1), and training schedule.
  - [corpus] Prior VTOFF methods (e.g., TryOffDiff, MGT) lack an explicit intermediate garment-cue representation, opting for direct translation, which struggles with occlusions.

### Mechanism 2: Flat Structure Constraint Module (FSCM)
- **Claim:** FSCM injects explicit flat-garment structural priors via Flat-Constraint Attention at selected denoising stages, stabilizing garment geometry beyond text-only conditioning.
- **Mechanism:** The FSCM takes the garment cues \(F_c\) from GCBM (projected to the diffusion dimension) and the flat-structure text embedding \(T_{f\,lat}\). It applies a cross-attention operation (FC-Attention) where queries are from \(F'_c\) and keys/values from \(T_{f\,lat}\), producing structural guidance \(Z_{f\,c}\). \(Z_{f\,c}\) is injected into the cross-attention layers of the denoising UNet at a specific early downsampling stage (Down 0), alongside the appearance text \(T_c\).
- **Core assumption:** Directly providing a structural prior during diffusion prevents the model from relying solely on text, which may be ambiguous, and stabilizes the global garment layout.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract] "Flat Structure Constraint Module (FSCM) injects explicit flat-garment structural priors via Flat-Constraint Attention (FC-Attention) at selected denoising stages, improving structural stability beyond text-only conditioning."
  - [section 3.2] Detailed FC-Attention formulation, injection strategy, and hyperparameter \(\gamma\).
  - [corpus] Unified frameworks like Any2AnyTryOn or Voost rely predominantly on text prompts for structure, which can lead to inconsistent geometry.

## Foundational Learning
### Concept 1: Latent Diffusion Models (LDMs)
- **Why needed here:** BridgeDiff extends Stable Diffusion, operating in a compressed VAE latent space. Understanding the forward/reverse diffusion process, noise prediction, and the efficiency gains from latent-space processing is fundamental.

- **Why needed here:** The denoising UNet conditions on multiple signals (text, garment cues) via cross-attention. Comprehending how external feature sequences modulate the noise prediction is key.
- **Quick check:** In a diffusion model’s UNet, how does a cross-attention layer allow a sequence of text or image tokens to influence the feature maps of a noisy latent, and what are the roles of Query, Key, and Value?

### Concept 3: Garment Representation and the On-Body to Flat Gap
- **Why needed here:** VTOFF must reconstruct an unworn garment from a worn observation, facing severe occlusions and a fundamental layout shift. Recognizing these specific challenges explains BridgeDiff’s dual-cue design.
- **Quick check:** Why is virtual try-off considered more challenging than virtual try-on? What specific issues arise from regions that are occluded by the body or self-occluded (e.g., underarms, crotch)?

## Architecture Onboarding

### Component map
Inputs (Model image \(X_m\), mask \(M_c\), texts \(T_m, T_c, T_{f\,lat}\)) -> GCBM (Image Encoder + MetaFormer) -> Garment Cues \(F_c\) -> Model UNet -> \(M'_o\) + Denoising UNet (with FSCM inserted at Down 0) -> Output Latent -> VAE Decoder -> Flat garment image.

### Critical path
1. Preprocess: Compute warped garment \(X_w = X_m \otimes M_c\) and generate textual prompts.
2. Encode images (\(X_w, X_m, X_c\)) with a frozen CLIP image encoder; encode texts (\(T_c, T_{f\,lat}\)) with text encoders.
3. GCBM forward pass (with added noise to \(X_c\) encoding) yields garment cues \(F_c\).
4. Model UNet forward pass (at timestep \(t=0\)) on \(X_m\) and \(T_m\) gives intermediate features \(M_o\); apply downsampled \(M_c\) to obtain \(M'_o\).
5. FSCM fuses \(F_c\) and \(T_{f\,lat}\) into \(Z_{f\,c}\).
6. Initialize random noise \(z_T\). For each diffusion timestep \(t\):
   - Concatenate \(M'_o\) to \(z_t\).
   - Pass through denoising UNet. In the Down 0 cross-attention layer, condition on both \(T_c\) and \(Z_{f\,c}\).
   - Predict noise \(\epsilon_\theta\) and update \(z_{t-1}\) via DDIM step.
7. Decode final \(z_0\) with VAE to produce the flat garment image.

### Design tradeoffs
- **Two-stage training:** Decouples learning of garment cues (GCBM) from diffusion fine-tuning. Benefits: stable convergence, targeted updates. Cost: increased pipeline complexity and storage of intermediate checkpoints.
- **FSCM insertion stage:** Inserting FC-Attention only at the earliest downsampling layer (Down 0) provides structural priors without over-constraining later, higher-resolution stages. Inserting at all layers or too late degrades performance.
- **Parameter freezing:** Freezing most of the denoising UNet preserves Stable Diffusion’s strong generative prior and reduces memory, but limits adaptability; only projections in hybrid self-attention, cross-attention (Down 0), and FSCM are tuned.

### Failure signatures
- **Occlusion artifacts:** Without GCBM, inpainted regions in occluded areas appear discontinuous or texture-fragmented.
- **Structural distortion:** Missing or misconfigured FSCM leads to uncontrolled wrinkling, incorrect hem lines, or distorted silhouettes.
- **Semantic drift:** Removing either \(T_c\) (appearance) or \(T_m\) (model guidance) can yield garments with wrong colors, patterns, or cut.
- **Extreme cases:** For heavily occluded garments or non-standard poses, both GCBM and FSCM may fail to infer coherent details, as noted in the paper’s limitations.

### First 3 experiments
1. **Main quantitative evaluation:** Compare BridgeDiff against SOTA VTOFF methods (e.g., TryOffDiff, MGT, LaDI-VTON) on DressCode and VITON-HD using FID, KID, PSNR, SSIM, LPIPS, DISTS. BridgeDiff achieves superior scores across all metrics.
2. **Ablation study:** Systematically remove GCBM and FSCM components (e.g., w/o cues, w/o FSCM, w/ single condition) to demonstrate their individual and combined contributions. Metrics drop significantly without either module.
3. **FSCM insertion analysis:** Test insertion at various UNet layers (Down0, Down1, Mid, Up1–3, All). Down 0 yields the best results, confirming the importance of early structural guidance.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
### Open Question 1
- **Question:** What architectural or data-driven improvements are necessary for BridgeDiff to reliably handle severe occlusions and extreme poses in virtual try-off?
- **Basis in paper:** [explicit] "Nevertheless, the method can still be imprecise or incomplete under severe occlusions or extreme poses. In future work, we plan to train on larger datasets to further improve the model’s ability to infer visually continuous garment regions." (Conclusion)
- **Why unresolved:** Current performance degrades under these challenging conditions; scaling data alone may not resolve fundamental representation gaps between on-body and flat layouts.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Systematic evaluation on a benchmark with extreme poses/occlusions, comparing data scaling, architectural changes (e.g., stronger priors), and external shape guidance.

### Open Question 2
- **Question:** How sensitive is the garment cue representation from GCBM to the accuracy of the cloth-agnostic mask used to extract the warped garment representation?
- **Basis in paper:** [inferred] GCBM applies a cloth-agnostic mask to the model image to obtain the warped garment representation \(X_w\) (Section 3.1). The paper does not analyze robustness to mask errors.
- **Why unresolved:** Mask inaccuracies are common in real-world scenarios and could propagate corrupted cues into the generation process.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Introduce controlled mask degradations (e.g., boundary noise, region omissions) and measure performance drop; compare with robust mask refinement or adaptive masking strategies.

### Open Question 3
- **Question:** Does the quality of the MLLM-generated textual descriptions significantly impact the final synthesis quality, and can simpler, more efficient alternatives achieve comparable results?
- **Basis in paper:** [inferred] The pipeline uses multiple MLLMs to generate structured garment attributes and descriptions (Appendix E). No analysis of description quality impact or alternatives is provided.
- **Why unresolved:** Errors in descriptions could misguide both GCBM and FSCM; the computational cost is high and may limit scalability.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Correlate description accuracy (against ground-truth annotations) with image metrics; replace MLLM pipeline with template-based approaches and compare performance and efficiency.

### Open Question 4
- **Question:** Is the two-stage training pipeline (first GCBM, then diffusion with FSCM) necessary, or could end-to-end joint training yield better or comparable results?
- **Basis in paper:** [inferred] The framework is trained in two stages, with most denoising UNet parameters frozen during second stage (Section 4.2 and Appendix B). No justification or comparison with end-to-end training is given.
- **Why unresolved:** End-to-end training might improve gradient flow and feature consistency but risks instability or catastrophic forgetting; the trade-off remains unexplored.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Develop an end-to-end variant, compare performance, training stability, convergence speed, and memory consumption with the proposed two-stage approach.

## Limitations
- **Mask and text pipeline opacity:** Exact specification for obtaining cloth-agnostic masks (ground-truth vs. predicted segmentation, specific model/thresholds) and the full MLLM prompt/consensus rules for generating \(T_c\) and \(T_{f\,lat}\) are not provided, hindering faithful reproduction.
- **Generalization to extreme conditions:** Performance degrades under severe occlusions or extreme poses, as admitted by the authors.
- **Complex training procedure:** The two-stage pipeline with partial freezing increases computational overhead and implementation complexity.

## Confidence
- **GCBM effectiveness:** **High** (supported by ablations, mechanism, and clear occluded-region improvements).
- **FSCM structural stabilization:** **High** (validated via insertion analysis and ablation studies).
- **Overall SOTA performance on benchmark datasets:** **High** (consistent metric superiority on DressCode and VITON-HD).
- **Robustness to mask/text variations:** **Low** (no analysis provided; unknown sensitivity).
- **Generalization to unseen extreme poses/occlusions:** **Medium** (admitted limitation; performance likely drops but not quantitatively benchmarked).

## Next Checks
1. **Mask reproducibility study:** Use a fixed, publicly available segmentation model (e.g., CIHP-trained U‑Net) to generate cloth-agnostic masks for all samples; compare performance against using dataset ground-truth masks to quantify sensitivity.
2. **Extreme occlusion benchmark:** Curate a test subset with heavy accessories/limb crossings from DressCode or VITON-HD; evaluate BridgeDiff and a GCBM‑ablated version to measure robustness degradation under severe occlusion.
3. **Text description robustness:** Replace the MLLM-generated \(T_c\) with human-annotated attributes (if available) or a simple template-based approach; compare FID/KID to assess the impact of description quality and computational cost.
