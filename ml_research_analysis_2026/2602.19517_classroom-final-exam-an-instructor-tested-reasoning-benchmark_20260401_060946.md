# Classroom Final Exam: An Instructor-Tested Reasoning Benchmark

## Quick Facts
- **ArXiv ID:** 2602.19517
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.19517
- **Reference count:** 40
- **Primary result:** Frontier models achieve at most 59.69% accuracy on CFE-Bench, with significant gaps in intermediate state maintenance and inefficiencies in reasoning length.

## Executive Summary
CFE-Bench is a new benchmark designed to evaluate multi-step reasoning in STEM domains using authentic university problems. It addresses saturation in existing benchmarks by introducing a variable-based evaluation protocol that reduces false positives and a diagnostic framework that decomposes solutions into reasoning units. Evaluation of frontier models shows they struggle with compositional reasoning, achieving at most 59.69% accuracy, and exhibit inefficiencies such as longer reasoning chains. The benchmark provides a realistic testbed for reasoning research.

## Method Summary
The authors curated 449 authentic university exam problems, each with instructor-verified solutions. They defined explicit answer variables and implemented a string-to-string (S2S) evaluation where a judge model extracts and verifies variable values from model outputs. Solutions are decomposed into atomic reasoning units via a semi-automatic process (model-proposed splits reviewed by experts). This enables diagnostic experiments like unit execution tests and prefix conditioning to isolate failure modes.

## Key Results
- Top model (Gemini-3.1-pro-preview) achieves 59.69% question accuracy on the combined benchmark.
- Models show high atomic step accuracy (~0.8–0.9) but a 5–7 point gap between variable and question accuracy, indicating difficulty in maintaining intermediate states.
- Model-generated reasoning flows are 14–18% longer than instructor solutions, correlating with higher error accumulation.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
**Claim:** Frontier models can often execute individual reasoning steps correctly, but fail to reliably derive and maintain accurate intermediate states during end-to-end generation.  
**Mechanism:** A gap exists between atomic step competence and compositional state preservation; models lose track of intermediate values across multi-step derivations, causing error accumulation.  
**Evidence:** Unit execution accuracy is high (~0.8–0.9) across most step indices; providing unit answers as prefix yields large final-answer gains; a consistent 5–7 point gap exists between variable and question accuracy.  
**Break condition:** If unit execution accuracy drops significantly or prefix injection yields negligible improvement, the mechanism would be weakened.

### Mechanism 2
**Claim:** Models generate longer reasoning flows than instructors, indicating inefficiency and increased error opportunities.  
**Mechanism:** Models tend to insert redundant or excess steps, which not only waste computation but also lengthen the dependency chain, raising the probability of intermediate mistakes.  
**Evidence:** Responses are 14–18% longer than ground truth; this length inflation correlates with higher error accumulation.  
**Break condition:** If models generate similar or shorter chains while maintaining or improving accuracy, the inefficiency hypothesis would be challenged.

### Mechanism 3
**Claim:** Final answer correctness is highly sensitive to specific intermediate values; supplying a single critical intermediate can boost performance nearly as much as providing a full prefix of sub-questions.  
**Mechanism:** Certain intermediates act as pivotal checkpoints; failure to derive them correctly often dooms the solution. When given, these values unlock downstream reasoning.  
**Evidence:** Single-unit injection yields meaningful gains over questions-only; the performance gap is largest at mid-range unit indices where transformations are error‑prone.  
**Break condition:** If injection of a single intermediate answer yields negligible improvement compared to a full prefix, the criticality of individual intermediates would be less supported.

## Foundational Learning
- **Benchmark Saturation**: Existing benchmarks become too easy for frontier models, leading to misleading progress signals.  
  *Quick check:* What are the dangers of relying on a saturated benchmark to steer AI research?
- **Variable-Based Evaluation**: Structured verification avoids false positives from long-form matching by extracting specific answer variables.  
  *Quick check:* Why might direct string (or semantic) matching of a model’s full solution against a reference answer overestimate model capability?
- **Reasoning Decomposition**: Decomposing solutions into atomic units enables diagnosis of where models fail (atomic vs. compositional).  
  *Quick check:* What challenges arise when automatically splitting a complex solution into logically independent, verifiable steps?

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map:** Data Collection → Expert Annotation → Reasoning Flow Decomposition → Evaluation Pipeline → Diagnostic Engine; Model Inference Interface → Evaluation Pipeline & Diagnostic Engine.
- **Critical path:**
  1. Curate raw problem set (source acquisition, initial filtering).
  2. Expert review → finalize problems + variable annotations.
  3. Reasoning flow decomposition (semi‑automatic + human verification).
  4. Implement extraction/verification prompts and validate judge consistency.
  5. Run baseline model evaluations and diagnostic experiments.
- **Design tradeoffs:**
  • S2S vs. L2L – S2S yields reliable metrics but requires costly manual annotation per question; L2L is automatic but prone to false positives.
  • Decomposition automation – Fully automatic unit splitting scales but risks errors; the two‑stage (model then human) approach balances scale and fidelity.
  • Multimodal filtering – Deciding whether visual input is necessary relies on expert judgment; a simpler rule‑based filter may miss subtle dependencies.
  • Judge model choice – GPT‑mini provides good agreement; cheaper but weaker judges could introduce noise.
- **Failure signatures:**
  • Low inter‑annotator agreement on variable definitions or reasoning steps.
  • Discrepancy between S2S scores and expert correctness labels.
  • Unexpected ceiling effects (e.g., all models scoring >90%) suggesting residual saturation.
  • High variance in model outputs under low temperature, indicating instability.
  • For multimodal items, performance near text‑only baseline may signal poor vision integration.
- **First 3 experiments:**
  1. **Baseline replication** – Run a state‑of‑the‑art model (e.g., Gemini‑3.1‑pro‑preview) on the text‑only split using the S2S protocol to confirm reported Question Accuracy (~65%).
  2. **Unit execution test** – Choose a failed question, extract its reasoning flow, and measure accuracy on each sub‑question when the sub‑question is explicitly provided; expect high success on most units.
  3. **Single‑unit injection** – For a few solved examples, replace a mid‑chain unit’s answer with the ground‑truth value and observe final‑answer accuracy; expect significant improvement over uninjected baseline.

## Limitations
- **Evaluation leakage risk:** The judge model (GPT-4o-mini) is itself a frontier model; its consistency and potential bias toward similar outputs are major unknowns.
- **Decomposition validity:** The semi‑automatic reasoning unit splitting may introduce annotator bias or miss logical dependencies, impacting diagnostic metrics.
- **Corpus saturation:** Even current top models achieve ~60%, suggesting the benchmark may still be partially saturated long-term.

## Confidence
- Atomic vs. Compositional Gap: **High**
- Reasoning Length Inflation: **Medium**
- Critical Intermediates: **Medium**

## Next Checks
1. **Judge Sensitivity Test:** Re-run core evaluation using multiple weaker judge models (e.g., smaller open-source models) to measure variance in Question Accuracy.
2. **Decomposition Audit:** Randomly sample 50 failed problems; have experts re‑decompose independently and compute agreement with the paper’s splits.
3. **Ablation on Multimodal Items:** Isolate the multimodal subset; evaluate all models with text‑only input and then with vision to test necessity and integration quality.
