# Causality $\neq$ Invariance: Function and Concept Vectors in LLMs

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2602.22424  
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.22424  
- **Reference count:** 40  
- **Primary result:** Function Vectors (FVs) are format‑dependent causal drivers of in‑context learning, while Concept Vectors (CVs) encode abstract, format‑invariant representations; the two rely on largely disjoint sets of attention heads.

## Executive Summary
This paper tests whether large language models encode abstract relational concepts (e.g., antonym, capital‑city) in a way that is invariant to input format (open‑ended English, open‑ended French, multiple‑choice). Using activation patching and representational similarity analysis, the authors identify two distinct types of vector representations. Function Vectors (FVs) are constructed from attention heads that causally influence in‑context learning (ICL) performance but are highly format‑specific—FVs for the same concept from different formats are nearly orthogonal and steer models most effectively when extraction and application formats match. Concept Vectors (CVs) are built from heads whose representations cluster by concept across all formats; CVs generalize robustly across languages and question types but exert weaker direct effects. Crucially, the heads comprising FVs and CVs show minimal overlap, indicating a mechanistic dissociation between causal task execution and invariant concept encoding.

## Method Summary
The study creates 21 datasets (7 relational concepts × 3 input formats) with ~50 prompts each. For each attention head in Llama 3.1 and Qwen 2.5 models, two metrics are computed: (1) Average Indirect Effect (AIE) via activation patching, which measures the head’s causal contribution to ICL outputs by swapping activations between clean and corrupted prompts; (2) Concept‑RSA, which correlates the head’s representational similarity matrix across all prompts with a binary design matrix marking same‑concept pairs. FVs are formed by summing outputs of the top‑K heads by AIE; CVs by summing outputs of the top‑K heads by Concept‑RSA. Steering experiments add these vectors to the residual stream in an AmbiguousICL task (where two concepts conflict), measuring changes in target token probability ($\Delta P$) and distribution KL divergence between in‑distribution (matching format) and out‑of‑distribution (cross‑format) conditions.

## Key Results
- **Format‑dependence of FVs:** FVs extracted from different formats (e.g., English open‑ended vs. French open‑ended) are nearly orthogonal; steering with an FV is strongest when the extraction and application formats match, and often injects format‑specific tokens (e.g., French words or multiple‑choice brackets).
- **Invariance of CVs:** CVs cluster by concept regardless of format; they steer more consistently across languages and question types, avoid format artifacts, and exhibit lower KL divergence between in‑ and out‑of‑distribution conditions compared to FVs.
- **Mechanistic dissociation:** The sets of attention heads that compose FVs and CVs are largely disjoint (≤1 overlap for $K \leq 20$), despite both being concentrated in middle‑to‑high layers, indicating separate neural mechanisms for causal ICL and abstract concept representation.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1: Function Vectors as Format‑Mixed Causal Drivers
**Claim:** FVs causally drive ICL performance but encode both the relational concept and the input format.  
**Mechanism:** Activation patching identifies a small set of attention heads with high Average Indirect Effect (AIE). Summing their last‑token outputs across prompts yields a compact vector (the FV). When added to the residual stream during a conflicting task (AmbiguousICL), FVs steer the model toward the target concept, but the effect is strongest when the extraction and application formats match.  
**Core assumption:** Causal mediation via activation patching localizes components that directly contribute to output decisions; linear summation of head outputs forms a format‑specific task representation.  
**Evidence anchors:** FVs are nearly orthogonal across formats; format‑RSA scores are high; steering introduces format artifacts (e.g., French tokens, MC brackets).  
**Break condition:** If FVs were fully abstract, their similarity matrices would cluster by concept independent of format, and steering would generalize without injecting surface tokens.

### Mechanism 2: Concept Vectors as Invariant Representations
**Claim:** CVs encode relational concepts at an abstract level, stable across input formats, but have weaker direct causal effects on ICL performance.  
**Mechanism:** Representational similarity analysis (RSA) selects attention heads whose output representations correlate with a binary design matrix marking same‑concept pairs across all formats. Summing these heads’ activations yields CVs. In AmbiguousICL steering, CVs produce smaller but more consistent probability boosts for the target concept across formats, avoiding format‑specific artifacts.  
**Core assumption:** RSA on representational similarity matrices isolates heads that organize information by concept rather than by low‑level surface form; the resulting vector acts as a format‑independent “pointer” to the concept.  
**Evidence anchors:** CVs show high Concept‑RSA and low format‑RSA; CVs cluster by concept in similarity matrices; steering yields lower KL divergence across formats; CVs do not inject format‑specific tokens.  
**Break condition:** If CVs mixed format, their RSA concept scores would be low and format scores high; steering would produce language/format artifacts akin to FVs.

### Mechanism 3: Mechanistic Dissociation of Causal and Invariant Representations
**Claim:** The attention heads that compose FVs (causal) and CVs (invariant) are largely disjoint, despite arising in similar layers, indicating that abstract concept encoding and ICL performance are mediated by distinct neural mechanisms.  
**Mechanism:** Two independent head‑selection pipelines: (1) rank by AIE → top‑K heads → FV; (2) rank by Concept‑RSA → top‑K heads → CV. Overlap analysis shows near‑zero intersection for small $K$, minimal even for large $K$. Layer‑averaged AIE/RSA profiles are correlated but the heads differ.  
**Core assumption:** Distinct head sets imply functional separation: FVs are needed to instantiate a task in a specific format, while CVs modulate existing abstract signals. Orthogonality of FVs across formats further suggests format‑specific basins rather than a single global representation.  
**Evidence anchors:** ≤1 overlap for $K \leq 20$ across models; Figure 5 shows AIE/RSA scores per layer are correlated but heads differ; cross‑format activation patching recovers FV heads, not CV heads.  
**Break condition:** Significant overlap in top‑$K$ heads would suggest a unified mechanism; similar RSA/AIE profiles for the same heads would contradict separation.

## Foundational Learning

- **In‑Context Learning (ICL):** Paradigm where models use few‑shot demonstrations to perform tasks without weight updates. Why needed: The paper studies ICL tasks as the behavioral paradigm where FVs and CVs are localized and tested. Quick check: How does ICL differ from fine‑tuning in terms of weight updates?
- **Activation Patching (Causal Mediation Analysis):** Method to quantify a component’s causal effect by swapping its activations between clean and corrupted prompts. Why needed: Primary method to identify FV heads via Average Indirect Effect (AIE). Quick check: What does the causal indirect effect (CIE) measure, and how is it computed?
- **Representational Similarity Analysis (RSA):** Technique that computes similarity of neural representations (e.g., via cosine) and correlates these matrices with a design matrix. Why needed: Selects CV heads by testing whether head representations cluster by concept across formats. Quick check: How does the RSA design matrix differ for concept invariance versus format invariance?
- **Linear Representation Hypothesis:** Assumption that concepts are encoded linearly in the residual stream, enabling vector arithmetic (e.g., “king” – “man” + “woman” ≈ “queen”). Why needed: Underlies the vector summation and steering interventions used for FVs and CVs. Quick check: What would non‑linear encoding imply for the validity of FV/CV manipulation?

## Architecture Onboarding

### Component map
Attention heads (a_ℓj) -> Residual stream (h_ℓ) at layer ℓ -> Model output.  
Patching site: last token of a middle layer (e.g., layer 20+).  
Extraction prompts: ~50 prompts per concept‑format; mean activation across these forms FV/CV.  
Steering prompt: AmbiguousICL task; vector added to residual at chosen layer.

### Critical path
1. **Data preparation:** Build 21 datasets (7 concepts × 3 formats) with ~50 prompts each; ensure same concept across formats.
2. **Head scoring:** For each head, compute AIE (activation patching) and Concept‑RSA (representational similarity). Requires clean/corrupted prompt pairs and representational similarity matrices over all prompts.
3. **Vector formation:** Select top‑K heads by AIE (FVs) or Concept‑RSA (CVs); compute mean activation over extraction prompts; sum head outputs to get FV/CV per format.
4. **Steering:** In AmbiguousICL, add α·v to residual at chosen layer; measure change in target token probability (ΔP) and output distribution (KL).
5. **Analysis:** Compare similarity matrices (cosine) for FVs/CVs; check format/concept clustering; compute RSA scores; assess artifact injection (foreign tokens, brackets).

### Design tradeoffs
- **K selection:** Small $K$ (1–5) yields sparse, high‑signal vectors but risks missing important heads; large $K$ (20–100) adds noise.
- **Extraction format choice:** In‑distribution (OE‑ENG) vs. out‑of‑distribution (OE‑FR, MC) determines generalization profile; FVs need matched formats, CVs work OOD.
- **Intervention layer:** Effects peak in higher layers; early layers may have insufficient signal, last layers too close to output.
- **Task design:** AmbiguousICL requires interleaved concepts; simpler tasks may not reveal dissociation; must ensure concept present but not dominant.
- **Model families:** Results hold across Llama/Qwen scales, but smaller models show weaker effects (e.g., Llama 8B, Qwen 7B); scaling may affect separation.

### Failure signatures
- **Sparse AIE:** Histogram of AIE scores peaks at zero with long tail. If no heads show positive AIE, patching setup likely flawed (e.g., corrupted prompts not disrupting task).
- **FV format mixing:** High format‑RSA for FVs and orthogonal FV matrices across formats are expected; if absent, format variance in extraction may be insufficient.
- **CV weak steering:** CVs show small ΔP in‑distribution and near‑zero in zero‑shot; normal, but failure if CVs also fail OOD.
- **Head overlap not minimal:** If top‑$K$ AIE and RSA heads overlap substantially, rankings may be underpowered or model‑specific; increase $K$ or check normalization.
- **Cross‑format AP inconsistency:** Should recover same FV heads across source–target formats; if not, patching implementation may be flawed (e.g., mismatched token positions).

### First 3 experiments
1. **Replicate FV/CV separation:** Compute AIE and Concept‑RSA for a subset of heads (e.g., top 50 by each metric). Verify minimal overlap (hypergeometric test) and similar layer profiles. Use Llama 3.1 70B for clear effect.
2. **Test format invariance via similarity matrices:** Build FVs and CVs for one concept (e.g., Antonym) across all three formats. Compute cosine similarity matrix over all prompts. Expect FVs: strong diagonal blocks by format; CVs: blocks by concept. Quantify with RSA concept vs. format scores.
3. **Steering in AmbiguousICL:** Extract ID (OE‑ENG) and OOD (OE‑FR, MC) vectors for a concept. Inject at optimal layer (from layer sweep) with best α/K. Measure ΔP for target token and KL divergence between ID/OOD effects. Also check for injected artifacts (French tokens, brackets) in top‑Δ tokens.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

1. **Interaction between CVs and FVs during ICL:** How do invariant Concept Vectors and format‑dependent Function Vectors dynamically interact to produce coherent task performance? The paper suggests hypotheses (CVs detect tasks while FVs execute them; or they operate as independent backup circuits) but dual‑intervention experiments and layer‑wise probing are needed to resolve this.
2. **Existence of concept‑specific invariant heads:** The global CV head selection targets heads invariant across all seven concepts; this may miss heads that are invariant for only a subset of concepts. Per‑concept RSA analyses and concept‑specific steering tests could reveal such specializations.
3. **Training dynamics of FV and CV heads:** How do these two sets of heads emerge and specialize during model training? Tracking AIE and Concept‑RSA scores across training checkpoints would show whether they co‑evolve, diverge early, or appear at different stages.
4. **Geometric relationship between FV and CV subspaces:** The paper proposes that task representations partition into distinct abstract (CV) and format‑specific (FV) subspaces based on orthogonality and invariance. However, the subspaces’ geometric relationship (e.g., independence, overlap, or dependence) remains untested. Subspace analysis (e.g., rank, overlap) and cross‑format interventions with format information ablated could clarify.

## Limitations
- Hyperparameter choices ($K$, $\alpha$) are model‑specific and incompletely reported, especially for smaller models, risking replication issues.
- AmbiguousICL construction (exemplar count, interleaving pattern) is underspecified, potentially affecting reproducibility.
- Statistical testing for head overlap lacks explicit thresholds and multiple‑testing correction details.
- Concept Vectors exhibit only weak steering effects, leaving open whether they are non‑causal or would show stronger effects with alternative intervention points/timings.
- Invariance tested on 7 concepts across only 3 formats; generalizability to broader tasks, non‑Indo‑European languages, or more complex relations is unknown.

## Confidence
- **FV format‑dependence & causality:** Medium (strong evidence from orthogonality and artifact injection but hinges on corrupted prompt design).
- **CV invariance:** Medium (RSA clustering supports invariance, yet causal role is minimal; design‑matrix choices could bias head selection).
- **Disjoint head sets (FV vs. CV):** High (robust non‑overlap across models, layers, and cross‑format activation patching).

## Next Checks
1. **Full hyperparameter sweep:** For each model (Llama 8B/70B, Qwen 7B/72B), vary $K$ (1–100) and $\alpha$ (1–100) during extraction and steering; report effect stability and identify optimal regimes.
2. **Alternative AmbiguousICL replication:** Replace concept pairs and shot counts with new configurations; verify whether CVs maintain cross‑format generalization and FVs remain format‑bound.
3. **Stricter RSA ablation:** Construct design matrices that remove format variance (e.g., via residualization) before computing concept RSA; test if selected CV heads retain invariance and whether any previously‑CV heads become format‑sensitive.
