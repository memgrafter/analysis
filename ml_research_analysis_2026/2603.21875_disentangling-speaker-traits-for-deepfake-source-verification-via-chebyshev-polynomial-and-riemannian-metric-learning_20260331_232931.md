# Disentangling Speaker Traits for Deepfake Source Verification via Chebyshev Polynomial and Riemannian Metric Learning

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2603.21875
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.21875
- **Reference count:** 0
- **Primary result:** RiemannSD-AAM with ResNet34 achieves EERs of 4.08% (unseen-source same-speaker) and 7.13% (unseen-source different-speaker) with AUCs up to 0.988 on the MLAAD benchmark.

## Executive Summary
This study addresses the challenge of speaker trait entanglement in speech deepfake source verification. The authors propose a speaker-disentangled metric learning (SDML) framework with two novel loss functions: ChebySD-AAM, which uses Chebyshev polynomials to stabilize gradient optimization and adds a speaker margin term, and RiemannSD-AAM, which projects embeddings into hyperbolic space via Riemannian distances to reduce speaker influence. Evaluated on the MLAAD benchmark across four protocols that separate seen/unseen sources and same/different speakers (via pseudo-labels), both losses consistently outperform the AAM-Softmax baseline with multiple encoders. The best results are obtained with ResNet34 and RiemannSD-AAM. t-SNE visualizations confirm effective disentanglement, showing tightly clustered source classes and dispersed speaker patterns.

## Method Summary
The SDML framework adopts a dual-branch architecture. A trainable source encoder (e.g., ResNet34) processes 3-second audio segments (16 kHz, 80-dim filterbanks) to produce source embeddings. Simultaneously, a frozen pre-trained speaker verification model (ReDimNet-B6) extracts a speaker embedding for the same input. Two loss extensions of AAM-Softmax are introduced. **ChebySD-AAM** replaces the cosine-based angular margin with a Chebyshev polynomial approximation to avoid gradient explosion near decision boundaries and adds a penalty term to non-target logits that is proportional to the absolute cosine similarity between source and speaker embeddings. **RiemannSD-AAM** maps source embeddings and class prototypes into the Poincaré ball (hyperbolic space) using an exponential map, employs hyperbolic distance instead of cosine similarity, and adds a penalty based on the hyperbolic distance between source and speaker embeddings. Both losses aim to suppress speaker-related information while learning source-discriminative features.

## Key Results
- With ResNet34, RiemannSD-AAM achieves EERs of 4.08% (unseen-source same-speaker) and 7.13% (unseen-source different-speaker), with AUC up to 0.988.
- Both ChebySD-AAM and RiemannSD-AAM consistently outperform the AAM-Softmax baseline across all four evaluation protocols and multiple encoder architectures.
- t-SNE visualizations reveal distinct source clusters and dispersed speaker patterns, demonstrating effective speaker-source disentanglement.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1
- **Claim:** The ChebySD-AAM loss uses Chebyshev polynomial approximation to stabilize gradient optimization during speaker-source disentanglement.
- **Mechanism:** It replaces the `cos(arccos(x) + m)` term in standard AAM-Softmax with a Chebyshev polynomial series `F_cheb(x, m)`. This avoids the unbounded gradient near ±1 caused by the derivative of `arccos`, while still enforcing an angular margin. A speaker-adaptive margin `M_spk` (based on the absolute cosine similarity between source and frozen speaker embeddings) is added to non-target logits to penalize alignment.
- **Core assumption:** Gradient instability at decision boundaries hampers effective learning of disentangled features; a stable polynomial approximation can provide consistent corrective signals, especially for hard examples, while the speaker margin explicitly suppresses speaker information.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract] "...Chebyshev polynomials for stable gradient optimization and a speaker margin term..."
  - [section 3.2] "ChebySD-AAM extends ChebyAAM by including additional speaker margin terms... This helps mitigating both the gradient explosion problem and providing a stronger corrective signal for hard examples."
  - [corpus] Neighboring papers show general work on disentanglement and robustness but do not directly anchor this specific polynomial mechanism for source verification.
- **Break condition:** If the speaker similarity threshold (`τ`) is poorly set, the penalty `M_spk` may be inactive for many samples (false negatives) or overly punitive (damaging source discriminability), leading to suboptimal disentanglement.

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim:** RiemannSD-AAM projects embeddings into hyperbolic (Poincaré) space to leverage Riemannian distances, better modeling hierarchical source distributions and reducing speaker dominance.
- **Mechanism:** Source embeddings and class prototypes are mapped to the Poincaré ball. The loss uses hyperbolic distance `d_H` instead of cosine similarity. A hyperbolic speaker margin `M_H` (based on `γ - d_H` between source and frozen speaker embeddings) penalizes when the source embedding is too close to the speaker embedding in this curved space.
- **Core assumption:** Synthesis source distributions have a tree-like hierarchical structure better captured by hyperbolic geometry than Euclidean space, and hyperbolic distances naturally increase separation between embeddings carrying different source information, even if they share speaker traits.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract] "...projects embeddings into hyperbolic space via Riemannian distances to reduce speaker influence."
  - [section 3.3] "synthesis source distributions can be assumed to possess tree-like hierarchical structure... Hence, we hypothesize that leveraging... Riemannian geometry... can better disentangle speaker traits."
  - [section 5.3] t-SNE visualization shows "distinct source clusters and dispersed speaker patterns" for RiemannSD-AAM, supporting the disentanglement effect.
  - [corpus] Neighbor papers (e.g., "Curved worlds, clear boundaries") explore hyperbolic spaces for deepfake detection, providing indirect support for the geometric assumption, but not specifically for source verification disentanglement.
- **Break condition:** If the curvature parameter `c` is too low (nearly Euclidean), the hierarchical modeling benefit is lost; if too high, the optimization becomes numerically unstable and the embedding space may fail to properly separate source classes.

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim:** The dual-branch SDML framework architecture—with a trainable source encoder and a frozen, pre-trained speaker verification branch—enables explicit speaker supervision without contaminating source learning.
- **Mechanism:** For each input utterance, a source encoder (e.g., ResNet34) learns source-discriminative embeddings. Simultaneously, a fixed speaker model (ReDimNet-B6) extracts a speaker embedding. The loss functions (ChebySD-AAM or RiemannSD-AAM) use the speaker embedding only to compute the penalty term (`M_spk` or `M_H`), which is added to the logits. The speaker branch is not updated.
- **Core assumption:** A high-quality, general-purpose speaker embedding can serve as a reliable proxy for speaker-related information present in the input. Keeping it frozen prevents catastrophic forgetting of speaker traits and ensures the penalty term is consistent and stable.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [section 3] "SDML adopts a dual-branch architecture... a frozen speaker verification model (ReDimNet-B6) extracts the corresponding speaker embedding... To suppress speaker-related factors during the optimization of `f_src`, we propose two novel loss functions."
  - [section 4.3] "a frozen speaker verification model (ReDimNet-B6)" is used.
  - [corpus] Weak. Neighbors focus on disentanglement techniques but not this specific dual-branch, frozen supervisee architecture for source verification.
- **Break condition:** If the frozen speaker model is poorly matched to the dataset (e.g., language or acoustic condition mismatch), its embeddings may not accurately capture the relevant speaker traits in the deepfake audio, leading to an ineffective or misleading penalty signal.

## Foundational Learning
- **Concept: Angular Margin Losses (AAM-Softmax)**
  - **Why needed here:** The paper's proposed losses are extensions of AAM-Softmax. Understanding the baseline—which adds an additive angular margin `m` in cosine space to tighten class clusters—is essential to grasp what the Chebyshev and Riemannian modifications change.
  - **Quick check question:** What is the purpose of the scale factor `s` and margin `m` in AAM-Softmax, and why might the `arccos` derivative cause problems?
- **Concept: Metric Learning for Verification**
  - **Why needed here:** The core task is a *verification* problem (same vs. different source), solved by learning an embedding space where same-source pairs are closer than different-source pairs. The losses directly implement metric learning objectives.
  - **Quick check question:** How does a loss like AAM-Softmax create intra-class compactness and inter-class separation, and why is this suitable for open-set verification?
- **Concept: Hyperbolic Geometry & Poincaré Ball**
  - **Why needed here:** RiemannSD-AAM fundamentally relies on hyperbolic distance. Knowing that the Poincaré ball is a model of hyperbolic space with a specific distance metric `d_H` and exponential/log map operations is necessary to understand the projection and loss formulation.
  - **Quick check question:** What property of hyperbolic space makes it suitable for representing hierarchical or tree-like data, and how does the curvature parameter `c` affect this?

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map:**
  1.  Input: 3-second audio segment (16kHz) → 80-dim filterbank front-end.
  2.  Source Branch (Trainable): Encoder (e.g., ResNet34) → Source embedding `f_src` (L2-normalized).
  3.  Speaker Branch (Frozen): Pre-trained ReDimNet-B6 → Speaker embedding `f_spk` (L2-normalized).
  4.  Projection (RiemannSD only): Exponential map + projection to Poincaré ball for `f_src`, class prototypes `W_src_yi`, and `f_spk`.
  5.  Loss Calculation: Compute target/non-target logits using either `cosθ` (ChebySD) or `d_H` (RiemannSD). Compute speaker penalty `M_spk` or `M_H` from similarity/distance between `f_src` and `f_spk`. Add penalty to non-target logits. Calculate cross-entropy loss.
  6.  Output: Trained source encoder parameters; frozen speaker model unchanged.
- **Critical path:** The most fragile and performance-critical path is the **loss gradient flow from the speaker penalty term back into the source encoder**. If `λ` is too high, the source encoder may be forced to ignore source discriminative information to simply minimize the speaker penalty. If `λ` is too low, the penalty has no effect. The Chebyshev polynomial degree `K` or curvature `c` must also be tuned to ensure stable, informative gradients.
- **Design tradeoffs:**
  - **ChebySD-AAM vs. RiemannSD-AAM:** Chebyshev offers simpler, Euclidean-based optimization with stabilized gradients. Riemannian introduces non-Euclidean geometry, which may better capture hierarchical source structure but adds complexity (curvature tuning) and potential numerical instability.
  - **Frozen Speaker Model:** Guarantees consistent speaker signal but limits adaptation. A trainable speaker branch could learn task-specific speaker cues but risks the speaker model dominating or collapsing.
  - **Dual-Branch Overhead:** Speaker branch inference adds compute cost (~ReDimNet-B6) during training. Not used at inference for source verification.
- **Failure signatures:**
  - **No disentanglement:** t-SNE shows clusters for *both* source and speaker IDs. Performance on unseen-source/different-speaker trials (P-IV) is poor (high EER).
  - **Catastrophic source forgetting:** Performance on seen-source trials (P-I, P-II) degrades significantly compared to baseline, indicating `λ` is too high and source discriminability is destroyed.
  - **Optimization instability:** Training loss diverges or becomes NaN, especially with RiemannSD-AAM, suggesting curvature `c` is too high or learning rate too large for hyperbolic optimization.
  - **Poor generalization:** Large performance gap between seen-source (P-I, P-II) and unseen-source (P-III, P-IV) protocols, indicating the model has not learned robust, disentangled source features.
- **First 3 experiments:**
  1.  **Replicate Baseline & Ablation:** Train AAM-Softmax baseline and the two proposed losses (ChebySD, RiemannSD) with a single encoder (e.g., ResNet34) on the full training set. Evaluate on all four protocols (P-I to P-IV) to confirm the consistent improvement reported in Table 2. This validates the implementation and the disentanglement effect on unseen-source scenarios.
  2.  **Parameter Sensitivity Sweep:** For each loss, systematically vary the key hyperparameter (`K` for ChebySD, `c` for RiemannSD) while keeping `λ` fixed at a moderate value (e.g., 1). Plot EER on P-III and P-IV. Identify the region where performance stabilizes or peaks (as in Table 4), confirming the existence of a functional operating range.
  3.  **Visualization Check:** After training RiemannSD-AAM, extract embeddings for a subset of the evaluation set. Produce t-SNE plots colored by (a) source ID and (b) pseudo-speaker ID. Verify the pattern: tight, separated clusters by source and dispersed, overlapping points by speaker (Fig. 2). If speaker clusters also form, the disentanglement has failed.

## Limitations
- The exact preprocessing and feature extraction pipeline for the frozen ReDimNet-B6 speaker model is unspecified, creating a key dependency for reproducing the `f_spk_i` embeddings.
- Pseudo-speaker trial label generation (cosine threshold τ=0.5) lacks procedural details, risking protocol inconsistencies.
- Chebyshev polynomial coefficients and hyperbolic projection implementation are not provided in full, requiring extrapolation from citations [15,17].
- Theoretical break conditions for hyperparameters (τ, λ, c, K) are not empirically mapped, leaving optimal ranges for unseen datasets unclear.
- The dual-branch design's computational overhead during training is unreported.

## Confidence
- Reported EER/AUC metrics on MLAAD protocols: **High**
- Mechanistic claims (gradient stability from Chebyshev approximation, hyperbolic space modeling hierarchy): **Medium**
- Assumption that the tree-like source structure is universally best captured by hyperbolic geometry: **Low**
