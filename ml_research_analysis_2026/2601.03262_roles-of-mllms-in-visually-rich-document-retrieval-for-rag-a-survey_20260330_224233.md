# Roles of MLLMs in Visually Rich Document Retrieval for RAG: A Survey

## Quick Facts
- **ArXiv ID:** 2601.03262
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.03262
- **Reference Count:** 40
- **Primary Result:** MLLMs enable RAG over visually rich documents through three distinct roles—captioning, multimodal embedding, or end-to-end encoding—with multimodal embedders achieving +27.4 points average gain on MMEB and end-to-end methods improving MRR@10 by up to 7.7 points.

## Executive Summary
This survey investigates the application of Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs) to retrieval-augmented generation (RAG) over visually rich documents (VRDs) such as PDFs, slides, and forms. VRDs present unique challenges due to layout-dependent semantics, OCR inaccuracies, and complex visual elements like tables and charts. The literature is systematically organized around three functional roles MLLMs play: Modality-Unifying Captioners, which convert non-text content into textual descriptions for conventional text-based indexing; Multimodal Embedders, which map heterogeneous inputs (text, layout, images) into a shared vector space to enable cross-modal retrieval; and End-to-End Representers, which directly encode entire document pages as embeddings, bypassing explicit OCR and layout parsing. The survey analyzes these approaches across key dimensions including retrieval granularity, information fidelity, computational latency, storage overhead, and compatibility with downstream tasks like reranking and grounding. Empirical results show MLLM-based multimodal embedders significantly outperform traditional baselines, while end-to-end methods offer robustness at higher computational cost. The work concludes by highlighting critical trade-offs between precision and efficiency and outlining future research directions such as adaptive retrieval and model efficiency.

## Method Summary
The survey synthesizes research on RAG systems for VRDs, where the core task is retrieving relevant document segments (e.g., passages, pages, regions) to augment generative QA or summarization. Inputs include page images, OCR-extracted text, layout coordinates, and isolated visual elements like figures and tables. Primary objectives are maximizing retrieval quality (metrics like MRR@10, accuracy on VQA benchmarks) while managing practical constraints like latency and storage. Methodologically, existing work is categorized by the role of the MLLM: (1) **Captioning**: MLLMs generate natural language descriptions of visual or structured elements, allowing standard text indexers and retrievers to be used; (2) **Multimodal Embedding**: MLLMs produce unified vector representations for query-document pairs across modalities, requiring specialized vector databases and similarity search; (3) **End-to-End Encoding**: MLLMs directly embed full page images or multi-page contexts, eliminating intermediate parsing but increasing embedding dimensionality and cost. Reproduction involves selecting a document corpus, preprocessing into modality-specific streams, adopting one of the three MLLM strategies (potentially involving fine-tuning), indexing, and evaluating on standard VRD benchmarks.

## Key Results
- MLLM-based multimodal embedders achieve an average improvement of +27.4 points on the MMEB benchmark compared to traditional baselines.
- End-to-end representers improve retrieval MRR@10 scores by up to 7.7 points on VQA-style datasets over non-MLLM methods.
- Each MLLM role presents distinct trade-offs: captioners sacrifice visual fidelity for efficiency; embedders balance accuracy and cost; end-to-end methods maximize fidelity but incur high latency and storage.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
MLLMs address core VRD retrieval challenges by leveraging their inherent multimodal understanding. As captioners, they translate layout and visual patterns into descriptive text, making non-textual information accessible to mature text-based retrieval infrastructure. As multimodal embedders, they jointly encode disparate inputs into a coherent latent space, directly modeling cross-modal similarity without explicit translation. As end-to-end representers, they treat the page as a holistic image, preserving spatial relationships and visual cues that are irretrievably lost during OCR or element extraction. This bypasses error-prone preprocessing cascades, making retrieval more robust to poor-quality scans, complex layouts, or unconventional formatting. The mechanism's effectiveness stems from MLLMs' pre-training on large-scale multimodal data, which imbues them with generalized knowledge of document semantics, visual-text alignment, and logical structure.

## Foundational Learning
- **Visually Rich Documents (VRDs)**: Documents where meaning arises from the interplay of text, layout, and non-textual visual elements (e.g., figures, tables). *Why needed*: Traditional text-centric models fail on VRDs. *Quick check*: Identify a document where removing layout changes the meaning (e.g., a table or flowchart).
- **OCR Brittleness**: Optical Character Recognition's sensitivity to font, quality, and language, leading to errors that propagate through retrieval. *Why needed*: OCR errors corrupt text indexes. *Quick check*: Run a standard OCR engine on a low-quality scanned page; observe missing characters or misrecognitions.
- **Layout-Dependent Semantics**: Information meaning that depends on spatial arrangement (e.g., a two-column article, a form with labeled fields). *Why needed*: Linearizing text destroys structural context. *Quick check*: Compare the meaning of text read in reading order vs. as a flat string from a PDF with multiple columns.
- **Modality Unification**: Converting diverse data types (image, text, coordinates) into a single representation. *Why needed*: Enables a single retrieval mechanism. *Quick check*: Describe a chart in words; does the description capture its key message?
- **Shared Embedding Space**: A vector space where semantically similar items (across modalities) are proximal. *Why needed*: Allows cross-modal search (text query → image result). *Quick check*: Use a multimodal model (e.g., CLIP) to embed a textual query and an image; verify their vectors have high cosine similarity.
- **Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG)**: A paradigm where a retrieval system provides external knowledge to a generator. *Why needed*: Grounds LLMs in factual, up-to-date documents. *Quick check*: Query an LLM with and without provided context; note hallucination reduction with context.

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component Map:**  
Input Document -> Preprocessing (OCR / Layout Parsing / Image Extraction) -> [Branch: Modality-Unifying Captioner (MLLM -> Text Description -> Text Index) | Multimodal Embedder (MLLM -> Joint Embedding -> Vector Index) | End-to-End Representer (MLLM -> Page Embedding -> Vector Index)] -> Index Storage -> Retrieval Engine (Text Search / Vector Similarity) -> Reranker (Optional) -> Context -> Generator (LLM) -> Output

**Critical Path:** The end-to-end latency and accuracy are dominated by the MLLM processing step (captioning, embedding, or encoding). The choice of role determines the entire downstream pipeline (text-based vs. vector-based retrieval) and its associated tooling (e.g., Elasticsearch vs. FAISS).

**Design Tradeoffs:**  
- *Accuracy vs. Fidelity*: Captioners lose fine-grained visual details; end-to-end preserve them but may embed irrelevant background noise.  
- *Latency vs. Storage*: Multimodal embedders use high-dimensional vectors (high storage, moderate latency); captioners use compact text (low storage, low latency) but risk semantic loss.  
- *Compatibility vs. Control*: Captioners integrate seamlessly with existing text IR systems; end-to-end methods require custom vector infrastructure and offer less interpretability.

**Failure Signatures:**  
- Drastic retrieval quality drop on documents with complex visuals (infographics, multi-page diagrams) when using captioners.  
- Excessive memory footprint and query latency beyond SLA when using high-dimensional page embeddings at scale.  
- Inconsistent cross-modal matches (e.g., text query retrieving irrelevant pages with similar visual style) in poorly calibrated multimodal embedders.

**First Three Experiments:**  
1. **Baseline Comparison**: Implement all three MLLM roles (using a fixed model like GPT-4V or LLaVA) on a standard VRD dataset (e.g., DocVQA). Measure retrieval accuracy (ANLS, MRR) and end-to-end latency per query.  
2. **Modality Ablation**: For the multimodal embedder role, train a model with systematically removed input streams (text-only, layout-only, visual-only). Quantify each modality's contribution to retrieval performance on a held-out set.  
3. **Generalization Probe**: Take the best performing model from Exp. 1 and test it on a fundamentally different VRD type (e.g., scientific paper posters or handwritten forms) without fine-tuning. Measure performance delta to assess robustness beyond training distribution.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- **Adaptive Retrieval Strategies**: How can RAG systems for VRDs dynamically select or combine MLLM roles (captioning, embedding, end-to-end) based on document characteristics, query type, and latency/storage constraints? Unknown: feasibility of runtime role switching.
- **Efficiency Improvements**: What model compression, quantization, or hardware-aware techniques can reduce the computational and storage overhead of end-to-end MLLM encoding while preserving retrieval fidelity? Assumption: current costs limit scalability.
- **Systematic Trade-off Optimization**: How can the precision-efficiency trade-off be formally modeled and optimized across the pipeline (preprocessing, indexing, retrieval) for specific application scenarios? Unknown: lack of standardized cross-study benchmarks.

## Limitations
- Survey-based synthesis without primary experimental validation; reliance on reported numbers from diverse studies with inconsistent protocols.
- Exact model architectures, hyperparameters, and training data compositions are often undisclosed, hindering reproduction.
- Benchmarks like MMEB and VQA may not capture the full diversity and complexity of real-world VRDs (e.g., multi-page legal contracts, mixed Reality documents).

## Confidence
- **Role Taxonomy** (Captioner, Embedder, Representer): High – derived from systematic literature analysis.
- **Quantified Gains** (e.g., +27.4 on MMEB, +7.7 MRR@10): Medium – aggregated from multiple studies with varying evaluation rigor.
- **Trade-off Analyses** (precision vs. cost): Low – largely qualitative, lacking controlled cross-study comparisons.
- **Future Directions** (adaptive retrieval, miniaturization): Low – speculative, not empirically validated.
