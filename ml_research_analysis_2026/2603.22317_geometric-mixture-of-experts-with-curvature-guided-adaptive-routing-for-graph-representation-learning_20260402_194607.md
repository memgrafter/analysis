# Geometric Mixture-of-Experts with Curvature-Guided Adaptive Routing for Graph Representation Learning

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID**: 2603.22317
- **Source URL**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.22317
- **Reference count**: 40
- **Primary result**: Achieves state‑of‑the‑art node classification on six benchmarks, with up to +1.12% improvement on CiteSeer and +0.84% on WikiCS over the strongest baselines.

## Executive Summary
Graph-structured data often exhibit heterogeneous topologies that cannot be optimally captured by a single Riemannian manifold. This paper introduces GeoMoE, a mixture‑of‑Experts framework that dynamically combines Euclidean, hyperbolic, and spherical Graph Neural Network experts. The core innovation lies in using Ollivier‑Ricci Curvature (ORC) as an intrinsic geometric prior: a gating network’s outputs are aligned with ORC values via a KL‑divergence loss, and a curvature‑aware contrastive objective further disentangles representations. GeoMoE attains state‑of‑the‑art performance on six node‑classification benchmark datasets, demonstrating consistent gains and interpretable expert routing.

## Method Summary
GeoMoE employs three parallel GNN experts operating in Euclidean (GCN), hyperbolic (HGAT), and spherical (SphGCN) spaces. For each node, a graph‑aware MLP gating network produces a normalized weight vector ω over the experts. The final node representation is a weighted sum of the expert outputs. The model is trained with a composite loss: a standard cross‑entropy classification loss (L_task), an alignment loss L_align that aligns ω with target probabilities derived from ORC values (via a curvature threshold θ and smoothing factor η), and a contrastive loss L_contr that pulls together representations of nodes with similar ORC while pushing apart hard negatives (the K‑2 most cosine‑similar nodes from the rest of the graph). The total loss L_total = α L_task + β L_align + γ L_contr is optimized end‑to‑end. ORC is precomputed offline for all nodes, making the approach agnostic to the specific curvature computation method.

## Key Results
- GeoMoE achieves state‑of‑the‑art accuracy on six node‑classification benchmarks, surpassing strong mixed‑curvature baselines.
- Notable absolute gains: +1.12% on CiteSeer and +0.84% on WikiCS.
- Ablation studies confirm the necessity of both L_align and L_contr; removing either leads to significant performance drops, and gating weights exhibit clear curvature‑aligned patterns.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The fundamental challenge is that real‑world graphs often contain subregions whose geometry is best described by different constant‑curvature manifolds (e.g., hyperbolic for tree‑like hierarchies, spherical for cyclic structures, Euclidean for grid‑like parts). A single‑geometry GNN cannot capture this heterogeneity. GeoMoE’s mixture‑of‑experts design allows the model to select the most appropriate geometric representation on a per‑node basis. The Ollivier‑Ricci Curvature provides a quantifiable, topology‑driven signal: negative ORC suggests hyperbolic suitability, positive ORC suggests spherical, and near‑zero suggests Euclidean. The alignment loss (L_align) explicitly encourages the gating weights to follow this signal, preventing arbitrary routing. The contrastive loss (L_contr) further improves the quality of the fused embeddings by encouraging nodes with similar curvature to cluster together in representation space, while hard negatives sharpen decision boundaries. Together, these mechanisms yield representations that both respect the underlying geometry and remain discriminative for downstream tasks.

## Foundational Learning
- **Ollivier‑Ricci Curvature (ORC)**  
  *Why needed*: Provides a scalar measure of local graph curvature that theoretically correlates with the optimal Riemannian manifold (negative → hyperbolic, positive → spherical, zero → Euclidean).  
  *Quick check*: Compute average ORC on a known hierarchical graph (should be negative) and on a community graph (should be near zero or positive).

- **Mixture‑of‑Experts (MoE)**  
  *Why needed*: Enables adaptive combination of specialized models (experts) to handle diverse patterns within the same data.  
  *Quick check*: Verify that per‑node gating weights are not uniform; at least two experts receive non‑negligible weights across the dataset.

- **Geometric Deep Learning (Manifold‑specific GNNs)**  
  *Why needed*: Each constant‑curvature space induces different notions of distance and similarity, which better capture corresponding graph structures.  
  *Quick check*: Compare Euclidean vs. hyperbolic embeddings of a small tree: hyperbolic should reflect rank‑based distances more naturally.

- **Contrastive Learning**  
  *Why needed*: Maximizes agreement between differently augmented (or here, differently projected) views of the same node while spreading apart dissimilar nodes, improving representation robustness.  
  *Quick check*: Ensure positive pairs (e.g., a node’s fused embedding vs. its expert embeddings) have higher similarity than a random negative pair.

- **Gating Alignment Loss (KL‑divergence)**  
  *Why needed*: Directly ties expert selection to the ORC prior, making routing decisions interpretable and geometry‑grounded.  
  *Quick check*: Pearson correlation between learned gating weights for each expert and node ORC should match theoretical sign (e.g., ω_spherical positively correlated with ORC).

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component map** (plain text flow):
Graph (A, X) -> Precompute ORC (κ) -> Three Experts (Euclidean GCN, hyperbolic HGAT, spherical SphGCN) produce node embeddings h_E, h_H, h_S -> Gating Network (GCN layer + MLP) uses graph structure and features to output gating weights ω (Softmax) -> Fused embedding h_fused = Σ ω_m * h_m -> Task classifier -> Supervised loss L_task. Additionally, L_align computed from ω and κ (via threshold θ and smoothing η) and L_contr computed from h_fused (positive pairs based on similar κ, hard negatives from most similar non‑positive nodes). Total loss L_total = α L_task + β L_align + γ L_contr used for back‑propagation.

**Critical path**: The essential sequence is: (1) compute expert embeddings in parallel; (2) compute gating weights conditioned on the graph; (3) fuse embeddings; (4) compute all three losses; (5) back‑propagate total loss to update experts, gating network, and (implicitly) the fusion weights. The ORC precomputation is a prerequisite but not part of the dynamic graph.

**Design tradeoffs**:
- *Fixed expert set vs. flexible geometry*: Using three predefined constant‑curvature manifolds simplifies implementation but may miss other geometries (e.g., mixed manifolds). Adding more experts increases capacity and computation but risks overfitting and routing collapse.
- *ORC as ground truth*: ORC provides an interpretable prior, but its computation can be expensive or approximate, potentially propagating noise. A fully learned routing signal would avoid this but loses geometric grounding.
- *Multi‑task loss weighting*: Balances task performance and geometric regularization. Poor weighting (e.g., β too low) leads to gating collapse; too high may compromise task accuracy. Requires careful tuning.
- *Hard negative mining*: Selecting top‑(K‑2) similar nodes as negatives improves discriminability but incurs O(N^2) similarity computations if done naively; approximations may degrade quality.

**Failure signatures**:
- *Gating collapse*: One expert’s average weight ω_m approaches 1 for nearly all nodes, others near 0. Diagnosed by monitoring mean ω_m across epochs.
- *Misaligned routing*: Low correlation between ω (especially for spherical/hyperbolic experts) and ORC, violating Theorem 4.2. Suggests β insufficient or θ/η mis‑set.
- *Contrastive‑induced degradation*: Accuracy drops when L_contr is added, possibly due to false negatives from dense similarity mining. May manifest as unstable training or plateauing at low performance.
- *Poor generalization on non‑Euclidean graphs*: If hyperbolic or spherical experts are under‑utilized on datasets known to contain hierarchical or cyclic structures, routing may be stuck in Euclidean mode, indicating inadequate regularization.

**First experiments** (to validate or troubleshoot the implementation):
1. **Hyperparameter sweep**: Systematically vary gating MLP depth, loss weights (α, β, γ), curvature threshold θ, and smoothing η on a subset of datasets (e.g., CiteSeer, WikiCS) to recover reported performance. Identify sensitive parameters and typical ranges.
2. **Hard negative mining comparison**: Train two models – one using fused embeddings h_fused to mine hard negatives (as implied by the paper) and another using the positive expert’s embedding (the expert matched to node’s ORC sign) – and compare classification accuracy and gating‑curvature alignment.
3. **ORC robustness check**: Compute ORC using both exact linear programming and the scalable neighborhood‑overlap approximation; measure correlation between resulting κ and the learned ω. Additionally, add controlled Gaussian noise to κ values and observe the impact on alignment (ω–κ correlation) and task accuracy.

## Limitations
- Critical hyperparameters (gating MLP architecture, loss weights, θ, η, τ_c, K) are unspecified, hindering exact replication.
- The hard negative mining procedure is ambiguous regarding whether fused or positive expert embeddings are used for similarity search.
- ORC precomputation method (exact vs. approximate) is not detailed, which could affect curvature values and thus routing.
- No public code is available, increasing reproducibility barriers.
