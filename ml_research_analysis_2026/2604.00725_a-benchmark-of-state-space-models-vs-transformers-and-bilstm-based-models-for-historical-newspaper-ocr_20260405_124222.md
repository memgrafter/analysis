# A Benchmark of State-Space Models vs. Transformers and BiLSTM-based Models for Historical Newspaper OCR

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2604.00725
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.00725
- **Reference count:** 36
- **Primary result:** Mamba-AR matches DAN's line-level accuracy (1.83% CER) with a 2.9× speedup, and demonstrates linear memory scaling versus quadratic growth.

## Executive Summary
This paper tackles the quadratic computational cost of Transformer-based OCR for long historical newspaper sequences by introducing the first OCR architecture using state-space models (specifically Mamba). The proposed model combines a shared CNN visual encoder with bidirectional and autoregressive Mamba decoders. Benchmarking on a new, high-quality BnL newspaper corpus shows all neural models achieve low error rates (~2% CER at the line level), but significant differences in efficiency. Mamba provides the best accuracy-speed trade-off for long sequences, scaling linearly in memory and outperforming Transformer baselines in inference time while maintaining comparable accuracy.

## Method Summary
The study evaluates end-to-end OCR models on a newly introduced BnL Historical Newspaper Corpus, which includes manually verified gold-standard lines (Antiqua/Fraktur) and severely degraded paragraphs. Architectures share a 5-layer CNN encoder that extracts visual features flattened into a sequence. The core innovation is a bidirectional Mamba connector that processes this sequence in both directions to provide global context with linear complexity. Three decoding paradigms are implemented with Mamba: CTC (non-autoregressive), Autoregressive (AR), and Non-Autoregressive (NAR). These are benchmarked against Transformer models (DAN-AR, VAN-CTC, DANIEL-AR) and a BiLSTM system (PERO-OCR). Training uses AdamW with extensive data augmentation and, for Mamba/DAN, initial pre-training on 1M synthetic lines derived from French Wikipedia. Evaluation metrics include Character Error Rate (CER), Word Error Rate (WER), inference latency, throughput, and peak memory usage across varying sequence lengths.

## Key Results
- Mamba-AR matches DAN's line-level accuracy on Antiqua (1.83% CER) while being 2.9× faster (53.3 ms vs 156.5 ms).
- For paragraphs (~600 chars), Mamba-AR achieves 6.07% CER vs. DAN's 5.24% but is 2.05× faster (195.6 ms vs 401.2 ms).
- Memory scaling confirms theory: Mamba-AR's peak memory grows only 1.26× for 1000-character sequences, while DAN's grows 2.30× (quadratic).

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1
**Claim:** Mamba-based OCR models achieve comparable accuracy to Transformer baselines with significantly reduced computational overhead for long sequences due to their linear-time complexity.
**Mechanism:** Mamba employs selective state-space models (SSMs) where the state transition parameters (B, C, Δ) are input-dependent. This allows content-aware, dynamic information filtering. Unlike Transformers' O(n²) self-attention via pairwise interactions, Mamba's hardware-aware parallel scan operates in O(n) time through discretized continuous-time SSM equations. This enables efficient long-range dependency modeling crucial for OCR without explicit attention matrices.
**Evidence anchors:**
- Abstract: "computational efficiency differentiates them... Mamba-AR matches DAN’s accuracy... with 2.9x faster inference... Memory scaling confirms theoretical linear growth: Mamba-AR’s peak memory increases 1.26x for 1000-character sequences versus DAN’s 2.30x quadratic growth."
- Section 3.2 details the selective SSM formulation; Section 5.3 (Table 7, Figure 3) empirically demonstrates linear vs. quadratic memory scaling.
- BnL paragraph dataset includes sequences up to 1000 characters, providing a realistic stress test.
**Break condition:** For very short sequences (<50 chars), Mamba's state management overhead may negate its efficiency advantage, making faster CTC models preferable.

### Mechanism 2
**Claim:** A shared CNN visual encoder combined with a bidirectional Mamba connector effectively captures both local glyph features and global document context, enabling strong performance across diverse historical scripts without script-specific tuning.
**Mechanism:** The CNN encoder extracts low-level visual patterns (strokes, shapes). The subsequent bidirectional Mamba layer processes the flattened feature sequence forward and backward, merging complementary context. This provides a global representation akin to a Transformer encoder but with linear cost, allowing the decoder to focus on sequential generation without deep cross-attention stacks.
**Evidence anchors:**
- Abstract: "combining a CNN encoder with bidirectional and autoregressive Mamba decoders" and "all neural models achieve low error rates (~2% CER)" on both Antiqua and Fraktur.
- Section 3.3 describes the shared encoder and bidirectional connector; Section 5.1 shows Mamba-AR and DAN both achieve ~1.83% CER on Antiqua lines.
- BnL includes both Antiqua and Fraktur typefaces, testing generalizability.
**Break condition:** For extremely complex layouts (multi-column, overlapping text, illustrations), a pure sequence model may struggle; a dedicated layout module or 2D SSM may be necessary.

### Mechanism 3
**Claim:** The choice of decoding paradigm (CTC, AR, NAR) is the primary differentiator for production deployment, with accuracy gaps marginal (~1% CER) compared to order-of-magnitude speed differences.
**Mechanism:** CTC models assume conditional independence between tokens, enabling a single forward pass and greedy decoding for high throughput. AR models generate tokens sequentially, enabling rich language modeling but incurring latency proportional to sequence length. NAR models predict all tokens in parallel with static queries, offering a middle ground. The paper shows for historical text, the CTC vs. AR accuracy trade-off is small, making speed the deciding factor.
**Evidence anchors:**
- Abstract: "all neural models achieve low error rates (~2% CER), making computational efficiency the main differentiator" and latency comparisons: VAN (6.19 ms) vs DAN (156.5 ms).
- Section 3.1 defines the paradigms; Section 5.1/5.2 tables show accuracy and latency for each. Paragraph results (Table 6) highlight VAN's 23x speedup over DAN with only a 1.18% CER penalty.
- Paragraph dataset's average length (~600 chars) exposes latency differences between AR and non-AR models.
**Break condition:** For text with extreme long-range dependencies (hyphenation across lines, multi-paragraph coherence), CTC's independence assumption may cause more errors, making AR necessary despite speed cost.

## Foundational Learning
- **Concept: State-Space Models (SSMs) and Selective SSMs**
  - *Why needed:* Understand Mamba's O(n) time complexity and input-dependent parameterization for dynamic memory, contrasting with fixed SSMs and quadratic Transformers.
  - *Quick check:* How do input-dependent B, C, Δ parameters in Mamba enable selective information retention, and why is this crucial for OCR compared to a non-selective SSM?

- **Concept: OCR Decoding Paradigms (CTC, Autoregressive, Non-Autoregressive)**
  - *Why needed:* The paper's core finding is that paradigm choice drives efficiency; understanding the alignment vs. generation trade-off and factorization of P(y|I) is essential.
  - *Quick check:* What are the fundamental differences in how CTC, AR, and NAR models factorize P(y|I), and what are the practical implications for latency and memory on a 1000-character paragraph?

- **Concept: Historical Document Degradation and Tokenization**
  - *Why needed:* The paper uses character-level tokenization and tests on degraded multi-script data; knowing why subword (BPE) fails on Fraktur informs vocabulary design for heritage texts.
  - *Quick check:* Why might a BPE tokenizer pre-trained on modern text perform poorly on 19th-century Fraktur, and what does this imply about vocabulary design for historical OCR?

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map:**
  Input image → CNN encoder (5 conv layers) → flattened feature sequence → Bidirectional Mamba connector (Norm+GELU+Linear+FwdMamba+BwdMamba(flip)+FFN) → Decoder head (CTC / AR / NAR) → Linear projection → Character sequence.

- **Critical path:** The bidirectional Mamba connector is the key innovation replacing deep Transformer encoder stacks, providing global context in linear time before decoding.

- **Design tradeoffs:**
  - *Accuracy vs. Speed:* AR models (Mamba-AR, DAN) lead in accuracy but are 5-25× slower than CTC (VAN, Mamba-CTC). NAR offers a compromise.
  - *Tokenization:* Character-level is robust for historical scripts; BPE (DANIEL) risks vocabulary mismatch on Fraktur.
  - *Sequence Length:* For paragraphs (>500 chars), Mamba's linear scaling makes it feasible; Transformer-based AR memory explodes.
  - *Pre-training:* Synthetic pre-training on Wikipedia lines is crucial for Mamba/DAN on limited BnL data.

- **Failure signatures:**
  - **High CER on Fraktur:** Check tokenization (if using BPE) or insufficient Fraktur examples in training.
  - **Poor paragraph accuracy:** Mamba-CTC/NAR lack multi-line layout modeling; use AR variant or explicit line segmentation.
  - **Excessive memory growth:** If memory scales closer to DAN's 2.30× than Mamba's 1.26×, suspect implementation error—ensure Mamba uses selective SSM with state caching, not naive attention.
  - **Training instability:** Mamba may require careful LR schedule (AdamW, 1e-4) and synthetic augmentations. Watch for overfitting on small BnL samples.
  - **ROVER label noise:** Verify test set is gold-standard, not pseudo-labels.

- **First 3 experiments:**
  1. Replicate line-level Mamba-AR vs. DAN benchmark on BnL Antiqua test: verify Mamba-AR CER ≤1.83% with ≤55 ms latency (A100), and active bidirectional connector.
  2. Memory scaling validation: Run inference on synthetic sequences (100, 300, 600, 1000 chars). Plot peak memory; Mamba-AR should show near-linear slope (<1.3× growth from 100→1000) vs. DAN's quadratic.
  3. Cross-script robustness: Evaluate fine-tuned Mamba-AR and DAN on Fraktur test set. If CER >3.5%, augment training with Fraktur-specific degradations and compare to VAN's CTC performance.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
1. How can SSMs be enhanced with explicit layout modeling to perform accurate paragraph-level historical OCR, given current Mamba-CTC/NAR are unsuitable for multi-line text?
2. What tokenization strategy (character vs. subword) optimally balances vocabulary coverage, sequence length, and accuracy for historical newspaper OCR across diverse scripts and degradation?
3. Why do large pre-trained VLMs (TrOCR, Gemini) underperform specialized OCR models on degraded historical newspapers despite massive pre-training on modern documents?

## Limitations
- Synthetic pre-training pipeline (1M French Wikipedia lines) is underspecified, hindering exact replication.
- Implementation details for the bidirectional Mamba connector (weight sharing, FFN dimensions) are not fully disclosed.
- The new BnL corpus lacks external validation for broader newspaper collections.
- Character-level tokenization's coverage of rare historical glyphs is assumed; BPE's failure on Fraktur is noted but not systematically explored.
- Decoding-paradigm trade-offs may not hold for highly ambiguous or multi-script documents.
