# Collective Knowledge Graph Completion with Mutual Knowledge Distillation

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2305.15895
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.15895
- Reference count: 10
- Primary result: CKGC-CKD achieves 14.0% improvement in Hit@1, 6.5% in Hit@10, and 5.5% in MRR over state-of-the-art models on multilingual KGs

## Executive Summary
This paper addresses the challenge of incomplete knowledge graphs (KGs) by proposing a method for multi-KG completion that leverages collective knowledge across complementary KGs. The approach, CKGC-CKD, employs relation-aware graph convolutional network encoders on both individual KGs and a fused KG, treating seed alignments as edges for message propagation. A novel mutual knowledge distillation mechanism enables bidirectional knowledge transfer between individual KG models and the global fused KG model. Experimental results on multilingual datasets demonstrate significant performance improvements, particularly benefiting lower-resource languages through effective knowledge transfer.

## Method Summary
The method employs CompGCN encoders trained on individual KGs and a fused KG where seed alignments are treated as edges. The fused KG model uses an augmented message function that removes the composition operator for seed alignment edges, using standard message passing instead. Mutual knowledge distillation is implemented by having individual and fused models take turns as teacher and student, with the student learning to mimic the teacher's posterior predictions using KL divergence. Final predictions are generated through an ensemble of both model types, combining their strengths in capturing local and global semantic relationships.

## Key Results
- CKGC-CKD achieves 14.0% improvement in Hit@1 over state-of-the-art models
- CKGC-CKD achieves 6.5% improvement in Hit@10 over state-of-the-art models
- CKGC-CKD achieves 5.5% improvement in MRR over state-of-the-art models

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: Augmented CompGCN encoder on fused KG enables cross-KG message propagation via seed alignments
- Mechanism: Seed alignments treated as edges with standard message passing (composition operator removed) to avoid incorrect compositional semantics
- Core assumption: Seed alignments are reliable enough to act as knowledge transfer bridges
- Evidence anchors: Abstract mentions seed alignments as edges; section 3.2 describes augmented message function
- Break condition: Sparse or noisy seed alignments prevent effective knowledge transfer

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: Mutual knowledge distillation enables complementary feature learning between local and global models
- Mechanism: Teacher-student alternation with KL divergence loss on posterior predictions
- Core assumption: Individual and fused models capture different data aspects
- Evidence anchors: Abstract mentions mutual knowledge distillation; section 3.3 describes distillation process
- Break condition: Large performance gaps between models reduce distillation effectiveness

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: Ensemble predictions outperform individual models by leveraging complementary strengths
- Mechanism: Simple additive combination of individual KG and fused KG model scores
- Core assumption: Models capture complementary information
- Evidence anchors: Abstract mentions ensemble generation; section 3.5 describes ensemble mechanism
- Break condition: Highly correlated models or consistent performance gaps reduce ensemble benefits

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map: Individual KG CompGCN models -> Fused KG augmented CompGCN model -> Mutual knowledge distillation -> Ensemble predictions

- Critical path: 1) Encode entities/relations in individual KGs using CompGCN, 2) Encode entities/relations in fused KG using augmented CompGCN, 3) Perform mutual knowledge distillation, 4) Combine predictions via ensemble

- Design tradeoffs: Seed alignments enable transfer but may introduce noise; mutual distillation adds computation; ensemble assumes complementary information capture

- Failure signatures: Poor performance on low-resource languages; significant performance gaps between models; high variance across KGs

- First 3 experiments: 1) Train without mutual distillation to assess augmented CompGCN, 2) Enable mutual distillation and compare performance, 3) Test different ensemble strategies (weighted sum, majority vote)

## Limitations

- Method effectiveness heavily depends on quality and coverage of seed alignments between KGs
- Computational overhead from training multiple models plus mutual distillation may limit scalability
- Ensemble approach assumes complementary information capture which may not hold if models learn similar patterns

## Confidence

- High confidence in augmented CompGCN with seed alignment edges (follows established GCN principles)
- Medium confidence in mutual knowledge distillation effectiveness (implementation details unspecified)
- Medium confidence in ensemble benefits (additive combination may not be optimal)
