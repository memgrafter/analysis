# Off-Policy Evaluation for Action-Dependent Non-Stationary Environments

## Executive Summary
This paper addresses off-policy evaluation in sequential decision-making environments with non-stationarity (active, passive, or hybrid). The key insight is leveraging a higher-order stationarity assumption on performance changes combined with double counterfactual reasoning and importance-weighted instrument-variable regression. This enables OPEN to infer how a policy's past performances change due to non-stationarity and forecast future performance without requiring full model estimation. Empirical results on four non-stationary domains show OPEN consistently outperforms prior methods.

## Method Summary
OPEN addresses off-policy evaluation under non-stationarity by first estimating past performance of a target policy using importance sampling, then applying instrument-variable regression to denoise these estimates, and finally using importance-weighted regression to learn the trend in performance changes. The method makes two key assumptions: performance changes follow a higher-order stationary pattern, and sufficient historical data exists to estimate past performances. OPEN uses double counterfactual reasoning to handle both active and passive non-stationarity, making it applicable across different non-stationarity types.

## Key Results
- OPEN achieves lower absolute bias (1.8 vs 3.2) and mean squared error compared to prior methods on the Diabetes domain
- The method outperforms Fourier-based approaches like Pro-WLS across all four non-stationary test domains
- OPEN maintains performance advantages across varying non-stationarity rates, from low to high change frequencies

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- **Claim:** OPEN can infer how non-stationarity changes policy performance by using double counterfactual reasoning and importance-weighted instrument-variable regression.
- **Mechanism:** The method first uses importance sampling to estimate past performance of a target policy, then uses importance-weighted regression to predict future performance by modeling the structural changes in performance over time.
- **Core assumption:** Performance changes follow a higher-order stationarity assumption where the way changes happen is fixed even if the outcomes change over time.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract]: "OPEN... uses a double application of counterfactual reasoning and a novel importance-weighted instrument-variable regression to obtain both a lower bias and a lower variance estimate of the structure in the changes of a policy's past performances."
  - [section]: "OPEN primarily relies upon two key insights: (a) For active/hybrid non-stationarity... OPEN makes uses counterfactual reasoning twice... (b) Despite reduction to a more familiar auto-regression problem, in this setting naive least-squares based estimates of parameters for auto-regression suffers from high variance..."
- **Break condition:** If the performance changes don't follow any fixed structure or if the past performance is not informative about future changes, the method would fail.

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim:** The instrument-variable regression reduces bias from noisy importance sampling estimates.
- **Mechanism:** Uses past performance estimates as instrument variables to "denoise" the input before performing regression, avoiding the bias that would occur from directly regressing on noisy importance sampling estimates.
- **Core assumption:** Past performance estimates are correlated with current performance but uncorrelated with the noise in the importance sampling estimates.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [section]: "Instrument variables Z represent some side-information and were originally used in the causal literature to mitigate any bias resulting due to spurious correlation... For mitigating bias in our setting, IVs can intuitively be considered as some side-information to 'denoise' the input variable before performing regression."
- **Break condition:** If the instrument variable is not sufficiently correlated with the target or if the noise in the estimates is correlated with the instrument variable, the method would fail.

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim:** Importance-weighted regression reduces variance compared to standard importance sampling.
- **Mechanism:** Instead of importance weighting the target, the method importance weights the squared error in the regression, proportional to how likely that error would be if the target policy was used to collect the data.
- **Core assumption:** Importance weighting the squared error provides a more stable estimate than importance weighting the target directly.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [section]: "The instrument variable technique helped in mitigating bias. To mitigate variance, we draw inspiration from the reformulation of weighted-importance sampling presented for the stationary setting by Mahmood et al. [2014]..."
- **Break condition:** If the importance weights become too extreme (very large or very small), the variance reduction may not be effective.

## Foundational Learning

- **Concept:** Importance sampling for off-policy evaluation
  - Why needed here: The method relies on importance sampling to estimate how a policy would have performed in past episodes when a different policy was actually used to collect data.
  - Quick check question: How does importance sampling correct for the mismatch between the target policy and the behavior policy?

- **Concept:** Counterfactual reasoning
  - Why needed here: The method uses counterfactual reasoning twice - first to estimate past performance, then to predict how performance would change if the target policy was executed instead of the behavior policy.
  - Quick check question: What are the two counterfactual questions that OPEN answers?

- **Concept:** Instrument-variable regression
  - Why needed here: This technique is used to reduce bias from noisy importance sampling estimates by using past performance as an instrument variable.
  - Quick check question: How does instrument-variable regression differ from standard regression when dealing with noisy input variables?

## Architecture Onboarding

- **Component map:** Data collection -> First counterfactual stage (importance sampling) -> Instrument variable stage (regression) -> Second counterfactual stage (importance-weighted regression) -> Forecasting stage (autoregressive)
- **Critical path:**
  1. Collect data using behavior policies
  2. Estimate past performance of target policy using importance sampling
  3. Use instrument-variable regression to denoise past performance estimates
  4. Perform importance-weighted regression to learn performance trend
  5. Forecast future performance using autoregressive model
- **Design tradeoffs:**
  - More past terms for autoregression (larger p) provides more flexibility but increases variance
  - Higher number of Fourier terms in baseline method reduces bias but increases variance
  - Importance weighting reduces variance but can introduce instability if weights are extreme
- **Failure signatures:**
  - High variance in performance estimates indicates importance sampling issues
  - Poor correlation between instrument variables and targets indicates breakdown of IV assumptions
  - Systematic bias in predictions indicates model misspecification
- **First 3 experiments:**
  1. Implement basic importance sampling estimator and test on stationary environment to verify correctness
  2. Add first counterfactual stage and test on passive non-stationary environment
  3. Add instrument-variable regression stage and test on active non-stationary environment

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- Question: How does OPEN's performance degrade when the assumption of higher-order stationarity is violated, particularly in environments with sudden, discontinuous changes?
- Basis in paper: [inferred] The paper mentions that Assumption 1 can be invalid in cases where performance trends are not predictable from past data, such as sudden motor failure in a robot system.
- Why unresolved: The paper provides qualitative examples of when the assumption might fail but does not quantify the degradation in performance or test robustness to such violations.
- What evidence would resolve it: Empirical results showing OPEN's bias and variance on domains with controlled discontinuities or sudden shifts in performance trends, compared to baseline methods.

### Open Question 2
- Question: Can the instrument variable regression in OPEN be extended to handle non-linear functions of past performances (e.g., using RNNs or LSTMs) while maintaining theoretical guarantees?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper mentions that Remark 4 suggests leveraging non-linear functions like RNNs and LSTMs using deep instrument variable methods, but theoretical guarantees for this extension are not provided.
- Why unresolved: The current theoretical results only cover linear functions, and extending to non-linear models would require new proofs for consistency and variance bounds.
- What evidence would resolve it: Empirical results using non-linear models in OPEN, alongside theoretical analysis proving consistency under appropriate assumptions for the non-linear case.

### Open Question 3
- Question: How sensitive is OPEN to the choice of instrument variables, and can alternative IVs (beyond lags of past performances) improve performance in certain domains?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper discusses using statistics based on past performances as IVs and mentions that other choices of instrument variables are viable (Remark 2), but does not extensively explore alternatives.
- Why unresolved: The paper only uses one specific IV (lagged performance estimates) and does not compare against other potential IVs or analyze sensitivity to IV choice.
- What evidence would resolve it: Comparative experiments testing OPEN with different IVs (e.g., state visitation frequencies, policy entropy) across multiple domains, measuring impact on bias and variance.

### Open Question 4
- Question: Can OPEN be extended to provide confidence intervals for its predictions, addressing the variance concerns raised in the limitations section?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper explicitly states that OPEN only provides point estimates and acknowledges this as a limitation for safety-critical settings where confidence intervals are important.
- Why unresolved: The paper does not propose or test any method for obtaining confidence intervals, and the high variance from importance sampling complicates traditional approaches.
- What evidence would resolve it: Development and empirical validation of a method to compute confidence intervals for OPEN's predictions, possibly using bootstrap techniques or a doubly-robust extension.

## Limitations
- Relies on higher-order stationarity assumption that structural changes in performance follow a fixed pattern, which may not hold in all real-world scenarios
- Effectiveness depends critically on having sufficient historical data to estimate past performances and learn the change patterns
- Theoretical guarantees assume certain smoothness conditions that may not always be satisfied in practice

## Confidence

- Mechanism 2 (Instrument-variable regression): High - Well-established technique with clear theoretical justification provided
- Mechanism 3 (Importance-weighted regression): Medium - The variance reduction claims are supported but depend on specific data conditions
