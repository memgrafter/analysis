# Stochastic Marginal Likelihood Gradients using Neural Tangent Kernels

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2306.03968
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.03968
- Reference count: 40
- Primary result: Introduces stochastic lower bounds to linearized Laplace marginal likelihood, enabling scalable gradient-based hyperparameter optimization in deep learning

## Executive Summary
This work addresses the computational bottleneck in hyperparameter optimization for deep learning by developing stochastic lower bounds to the linearized Laplace approximation of the marginal likelihood. The key innovation leverages the neural tangent kernel (NTK) dual form to partition computations across data batches, enabling unbiased stochastic gradient estimation without requiring full-dataset passes. By combining structured parametric approximations like KFAC with careful NTK partitioning strategies, the method achieves up to 25× speedups while maintaining or improving hyperparameter optimization quality compared to full-batch approaches.

## Method Summary
The method uses NTK-based lower bounds to enable stochastic gradient-based hyperparameter optimization in deep learning. It partitions the NTK matrix across data batches and outputs, allowing computation of the marginal likelihood and its gradients on small subsets rather than the full dataset. Structured approximations like KFAC are employed to make the log-determinant computation tractable. The approach maintains theoretical guarantees through the lower bound property while significantly reducing computational complexity from O(N^3C^3) to O(N^3 + CN^2) for NTK computation and O(C) for determinant computation.

## Key Results
- Achieves up to 25× speedups on CIFAR-10/100 datasets while maintaining or improving hyperparameter optimization performance
- Enables previously intractable hyperparameter learning on TinyImagenet with ResNet-50 architecture
- Demonstrates compatibility with structured approximations like block-diagonal Gauss-Newton and KFAC
- Shows that output-wise partitioning provides a good tradeoff between computational efficiency and bound tightness

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
Lower bounds on the linearized Laplace marginal likelihood enable unbiased stochastic estimation and gradients, overcoming the full-dataset requirement. By partitioning the NTK into blocks corresponding to subsets of data and outputs, the log-determinant term becomes separable across these blocks, allowing computation on small batches rather than the full dataset. Core assumption: The NTK has a block structure where each block corresponds to a subset of input-output pairs, and the determinant of the block-diagonal approximation is a lower bound to the true determinant.

### Mechanism 2
Structured parametric approximations like KFAC are themselves lower bounds to the full linearized Laplace, justifying their use in practice. Theorem 1 shows that block-diagonal approximations of the GGN/Hessian result in lower bounds to the linearized Laplace marginal likelihood, with refinements of the partition leading to tighter bounds. Core assumption: The Hessian/GGN can be partitioned into blocks, and the determinant of the block-diagonal approximation is a lower bound to the full determinant.

### Mechanism 3
Output-wise partitioning of the NTK provides a good tradeoff between computational efficiency and bound tightness, enabling scalable hyperparameter optimization. By partitioning the NTK by outputs, each block corresponds to a single output, reducing computational complexity from O(N^3C^3) to O(N^3) for NTK computation and O(C) for determinant computation. Core assumption: The correlation structure of the NTK is such that outputs are mostly independent, and partitioning by outputs does not significantly increase the bound slack.

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map: Data partitioning -> NTK computation -> Log-determinant computation -> Hyperparameter optimization
- Critical path: Compute NTK for a batch → Compute log-determinant of block-diagonal NTK → Estimate marginal likelihood → Compute gradients w.r.t. hyperparameters → Update hyperparameters via SGD
- Design tradeoffs:
  - Batch size vs. bound tightness: Smaller batches lead to faster computation but looser bounds
  - Output-wise vs. data-wise partitioning: Output-wise partitioning is more efficient but may be less accurate if outputs are correlated
  - Structured approximations (e.g., KFAC) vs. full NTK: Structured approximations are faster but may be less accurate
- Failure signatures:
  - Poor hyperparameter optimization performance despite fast computation: Indicates that the bound is too loose due to coarse partitioning or inappropriate structured approximation
  - Out-of-memory errors: Indicates that the batch size or structured approximation is too large for the available memory
- First 3 experiments:
  1. Verify that the NTK computation is correct by comparing to a known implementation on a small dataset
  2. Test the effect of batch size on the bound tightness and hyperparameter optimization performance on a toy problem
  3. Compare the performance of output-wise vs. data-wise partitioning on a small-scale hyperparameter optimization task

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
How can we improve partitioning strategies for the neural tangent kernel (NTK) to make the lower bounds tighter and more efficient? The paper discusses various partitioning strategies but suggests that developing methods to track (anti-)correlation between inputs in the NTK to group them optimally during training could be interesting. Experimental results comparing different partitioning strategies, particularly those that track (anti-)correlation between inputs, would help determine the most effective approach.

### Open Question 2
Can the proposed stochastic marginal likelihood estimators be effectively applied to even larger datasets and more complex models beyond TinyImagenet and ResNet-50? While the paper demonstrates scalability on TinyImagenet, it does not explore performance on larger datasets or more complex models. Experiments applying the estimators to larger datasets (e.g., ImageNet) and more complex models (e.g., Vision Transformers) would help determine their scalability and effectiveness in more challenging scenarios.

### Open Question 3
How do the proposed stochastic marginal likelihood estimators compare to other hyperparameter optimization methods, such as Bayesian optimization or random search, in terms of performance and computational efficiency? The paper focuses on performance compared to full-batch KFAC estimators but does not directly compare them to other hyperparameter optimization methods. Experiments comparing the proposed estimators to other methods (e.g., Bayesian optimization, random search) on various tasks and datasets would help determine their relative performance and computational efficiency.

## Limitations
- Effectiveness depends critically on the choice of partitioning strategy, with output-wise partitioning potentially producing loose bounds when outputs are strongly correlated
- Inherits limitations from linearized Laplace approximations, including sensitivity to posterior covariance choice and potential poor approximation quality for highly non-linear models
- Practical speedup claims depend heavily on implementation details and hardware configurations

## Confidence

- **High confidence**: Theoretical derivation of NTK-based lower bounds and their compatibility with structured approximations (Theorems 1-2)
- **Medium confidence**: Practical speedup claims and memory efficiency improvements, as these depend heavily on implementation details and hardware configurations
- **Low confidence**: Generalizability to very large-scale architectures and datasets beyond those tested, particularly for extremely deep networks or high-resolution image tasks

## Next Checks

2. **Structured approximation comparison**: Benchmark the performance of different structured approximations (diagonal, block-diagonal, KFAC) against the full NTK on small-scale problems to quantify the trade-off between computational efficiency and bound accuracy.

3. **Scaling behavior analysis**: Measure the computational complexity and memory usage as a function of dataset size, model depth, and number of outputs to identify practical limits and guide resource allocation for larger-scale applications.
