# i-Code V2: An Autoregressive Generation Framework over Vision, Language, and Speech Data

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2305.12311
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.12311
- Reference count: 27
- Key outcome: First autoregressive generation framework capable of producing natural language from any combination of vision, language, and speech data

## Executive Summary
i-Code V2 presents the first autoregressive generation framework capable of producing natural language from any combination of vision, language, and speech data. The model leverages large-scale single- and dual-modality datasets for pretraining using a novel cross-modality text completion framework. By integrating state-of-the-art single-modality encoders with a modality-fusing encoder and an autoregressive decoder, i-Code V2 achieves state-of-the-art performance on 3 tasks and competitive results on 4 others across 7 multimodal tasks.

## Method Summary
i-Code V2 is an autoregressive generation framework that integrates frozen single-modality encoders (WavLM Large for speech, OmniVL for vision) with a joint multimodal encoder and autoregressive decoder. The model uses a sequence-to-sequence training objective that converts diverse multimodal tasks into text completion problems. During pretraining, Exponentially Smoothed Weighting (ESW) sampling balances contributions across datasets of different sizes, and only the joint encoder and decoder parameters are updated while single-modality encoders remain frozen.

## Key Results
- Achieved new state-of-the-art performance on 3 tasks: MSMO, Image Chat, and UR-FUNNY
- Matched or outperformed state-of-the-art single- and dual-modality baselines on 7 multimodal tasks
- Demonstrated effectiveness of integrative multimodal pretraining for both discriminative and generative multimodal tasks

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: i-Code V2's cross-modality text completion framework enables generative capabilities across all three modalities by unifying diverse tasks into sequence-to-sequence problems.
- Mechanism: By converting modality-specific objectives into text completion tasks, the model learns to generate natural language from any combination of vision, language, and speech inputs through a shared autoregressive decoder.
- Core assumption: A single text completion framework can effectively handle diverse multimodal tasks that traditionally require separate architectures.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract]: "The whole framework is pretrained end-to-end on a large collection of dual- and single-modality datasets using a novel text completion objective that can be generalized across arbitrary combinations of modalities."
  - [section]: "Using a sequence-to-sequence training objective, as opposed to the modality-specific objectives and task heads common in the multimodal AI space, can be flexibly applied to arbitrary combinations of modalities while simplifying the training and inference process."
- Break condition: The framework fails when tasks require generation of non-text modalities or when task-specific nuances cannot be adequately captured through text completion.

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: Freezing single-modality encoders during pretraining improves efficiency while maintaining strong performance through transfer learning.
- Mechanism: State-of-the-art pretrained vision and speech encoders are kept frozen during multimodal pretraining, with only the joint encoder and decoder parameters being updated, allowing efficient use of pretrained knowledge.
- Core assumption: High-quality pretrained single-modality representations remain effective when combined in a multimodal setting without fine-tuning.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [section]: "To expedite the pretraining process, we freeze the speech and vision encoders during pretraining, only updating the parameters of the Vision-Language-Speech encoder and the language decoder."
- Break condition: The approach fails when the frozen encoders' representations are insufficient for capturing task-specific multimodal relationships.

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: Exponentially Smoothed Weighting (ESW) sampling balances pretraining across datasets of vastly different sizes.
- Mechanism: ESW exponentiates dataset size ratios (with factor S<1) to prevent domination by large datasets while still allowing their contributions.
- Core assumption: Without ESW, large datasets would overwhelm smaller but potentially more valuable datasets during pretraining.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [section]: "We select the pretraining dataset from the candidate pool using 'Exponentially Smoothed Weighting (ESW)' sampling... Assume the size ratio of the datasetA in the overall training datasets is P (A). We exponentiate the ratio by the factor S < 1 then we sample datasets according the re-normalized exponential ratio p(A)S/∑A p(A)s."
- Break condition: The approach fails when S is poorly chosen, either making sampling too uniform or too skewed toward large datasets.

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map: Single-modality encoder outputs → Projection layers → Joint encoder → Decoder cross-attention → Language generation

- Critical path: Single-modality encoder outputs → Projection layers → Joint encoder → Decoder cross-attention → Language generation

- Design tradeoffs:
  - Freezing single-modality encoders: Improves efficiency but limits modality-specific adaptation
  - Text completion framework: Simplifies pretraining but may not capture all task nuances
  - ESW sampling: Balances dataset contributions but requires careful hyperparameter tuning

- Failure signatures:
  - Poor cross-modal attention weights
  - Degraded performance on tasks requiring fine-grained modality-specific understanding
  - Mode collapse in generated outputs
