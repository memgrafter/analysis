# Multi-Dimensional Evaluation of Text Summarization with In-Context Learning

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2306.01200
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.01200
- Reference count: 10
- This paper investigates using large language models with in-context learning for multi-dimensional evaluation of text summarization without requiring training data.

## Executive Summary
This paper introduces ICE (In-Context Evaluation), a framework that uses few-shot prompting with large language models to evaluate text summarization across multiple dimensions including relevance, consistency, fluency, and coherence. The approach leverages GPT-3's in-context learning capabilities by providing a small number of human-annotated examples as prompts, eliminating the need for model training or synthetic data generation. Experiments demonstrate that ICE achieves state-of-the-art correlations with human judgments for relevance and factual consistency evaluation, outperforming existing fine-tuned evaluation metrics while maintaining robustness to the choice of in-context examples.

## Method Summary
ICE uses GPT-3's in-context learning capabilities by providing prompts that include 4 human-annotated examples of (summary, score) pairs for each evaluation dimension. The framework constructs dimension-specific prompts - for consistency it includes source text and summary, for relevance it includes reference summary, and for fluency and coherence it uses only the summary. The method employs uniform sampling to select examples that represent the true score distribution, and uses a temperature of 0 to obtain deterministic responses from GPT-3. Scores are extracted by parsing the numeric string responses, and performance is evaluated using Spearman and Kendall-Tau correlations against human judgments on the SummEval dataset.

## Key Results
- ICE achieves state-of-the-art correlations with human judgments for relevance and factual consistency evaluation
- ICE performance is robust to the choice of in-context examples and improves slightly with more examples (up to 4)
- ICE better aligns with human preferences than existing metrics when evaluating zero-shot summaries from GPT-3

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- Question: How does ICE perform on text generation tasks beyond summarization, such as dialogue or machine translation?
- Basis in paper: [inferred] The paper states that ICE is extensible to new evaluation dimensions without requiring large amounts of human judgments or new synthetic data. It also mentions that the analysis is limited to summarization due to monetary constraints.
- Why unresolved: The paper only evaluates ICE on the task of text summarization, leaving its performance on other NLG tasks unexplored.
- What evidence would resolve it: Conducting experiments using ICE to evaluate generated text on tasks like dialogue, machine translation, or question answering, and comparing its performance to existing evaluation metrics on those tasks.

### Open Question 2
- Question: What is the impact of increasing the number of in-context examples beyond 4 on ICE's performance?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper mentions that the default number of in-context examples is 4, which is the largest that fits within the context window of GPT-3. It also states that performance improves slightly with more examples.
- Why unresolved: The paper does not explore the effect of using more than 4 in-context examples due to the context window limitation of GPT-3.
- What evidence would resolve it: Experimenting with larger context window models like GPT-3-175B or GPT-4, and evaluating ICE's performance with varying numbers of in-context examples (e.g., 8, 16, 32) to determine if there is a point of diminishing returns.

### Open Question 3
- Question: How does the choice of LLM (e.g., GPT-3 vs. BLOOM) affect ICE's performance?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper mentions that ICE relies on an LLM and uses GPT-3 for experiments, but also notes that it can use open-source models like BLOOM.
- Why unresolved: The paper only uses GPT-3 for experiments and does not compare its performance with other LLMs.
- What evidence would resolve it: Conducting experiments using ICE with different LLMs (e.g., GPT-3, BLOOM, OPT) and comparing their performance on the same evaluation tasks to determine if the choice of LLM impacts ICE's effectiveness.

## Limitations
- The evaluation is based on a relatively small dataset (100 articles, 1600 summaries), limiting generalizability
- The approach shows sensitivity to prompt design and example selection, though robustness is demonstrated
- The study focuses on GPT-3 specifically, without exploring whether results generalize to other LLMs or smaller models

- **Medium Confidence**: ICE performance is robust to choice of in-context examples and improves with more examples (up to 4)
