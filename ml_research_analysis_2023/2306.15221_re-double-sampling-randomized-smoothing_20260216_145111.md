# [Re] Double Sampling Randomized Smoothing

## Executive Summary
The Double Sampling Randomized Smoothing (DSRS) framework was introduced to address limitations in existing randomized smoothing methods for certifying robustness of neural networks against adversarial perturbations. DSRS uses an additional smoothing distribution to compute tighter robustness bounds, certifying radii of order Ω(√d) under the l2 norm. The method was evaluated on MNIST and CIFAR-10 datasets using ResNet-110 models, showing consistently larger certified radii compared to standard Neyman-Pearson certification across multiple training methods.

## Method Summary
DSRS introduces a double sampling approach where predictions are sampled under two different distributions P and Q, leveraging joint statistical information to compute more accurate certification bounds. The method supports both standard Gaussian and generalized Gaussian distributions, with the latter controlled by a k hyperparameter that determines the shape parameter. The framework was evaluated across multiple training methods including Gaussian augmentation, consistency regularization, SmoothMix, and adversarial training techniques like PGD and interpolated training.

## Key Results
- DSRS certifies radii of order Ω(√d) under l2 norm, breaking curse of dimensionality limitations
- Consistently larger certified radii compared to Neyman-Pearson certification across MNIST and CIFAR-10 datasets
- Generalized Gaussian distributions with specific k parameters provide optimal certification performance
- High computational demands with experiments requiring 10-65 hours depending on sample sizes

## Limitations
- High computational expense requiring 10-65 hours for experiments with varying sample sizes
- Benefits vary across datasets and training methods, not uniformly dramatic improvements
- Theoretical Ω(√d) bounds may not fully capture practical constraints when scaling to very high-dimensional inputs
- Optimal k parameter configuration appears sensitive and requires careful tuning for each scenario
