# Adversarial Training of Self-supervised Monocular Depth Estimation against Physical-World Attacks

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2301.13487
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2301.13487
- Reference count: 23
- Primary result: Achieves 0.25m average depth error against adversarial objects (vs 6.08m baseline)

## Executive Summary
This paper proposes a self-supervised adversarial training method to defend monocular depth estimation (MDE) models against physical-world adversarial attacks. The approach generates synthetic training data through view synthesis, eliminating the need for expensive real-world data collection. By applying L0-norm-bounded perturbations to synthesized object images and minimizing view reconstruction error, the method achieves significant robustness improvements against various digital and physical attacks while maintaining benign performance. The technique is evaluated on KITTI dataset using Monodepth2 and DepthHints architectures.

## Method Summary
The method addresses physical-world adversarial attacks on MDE by generating synthetic training data that mimics camera-object configurations without requiring real-world data collection. It uses view synthesis to create stereo image pairs from background scenes and synthesized 2D objects, then applies L0-norm-bounded perturbations to generate adversarial views. The model is trained self-supervised by minimizing the reconstruction error between synthesized views, eliminating the need for ground-truth depth labels. This approach effectively simulates physical attacks during training, enabling the model to learn robust depth estimation in the presence of adversarial objects.

## Key Results
- Reduces average depth estimation error of adversarial objects from 6.08m to 0.25m
- Maintains minimal degradation in benign performance (0.82m to 0.87m error increase)
- Outperforms supervised learning and contrastive learning baselines against various attacks
- Achieves significant robustness improvements across multiple evaluation metrics (ABSE, RMSE, ABSR, SQR, δ)

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The method works by exposing the MDE model to realistic adversarial scenarios during training through synthetic data generation. By using view synthesis to create stereo pairs with perturbed objects, the model learns to estimate depth correctly even when faced with adversarial patterns. The L0-norm-bounded perturbations effectively simulate the discrete nature of physical attacks (such as stickers or objects placed in the scene), while the self-supervised training eliminates the need for ground-truth depth labels. This approach allows the model to develop robustness against attacks without requiring extensive real-world data collection.

## Foundational Learning

**Monocular Depth Estimation**: Task of predicting depth maps from single images; needed because MDE is fundamental for autonomous systems and robotics applications.

**Self-supervised Learning**: Training without explicit labels by using supervisory signals from data itself; needed because ground-truth depth is expensive to obtain in real-world scenarios.

**View Synthesis**: Generating novel views by warping images using known camera poses; needed to create synthetic training data that mimics physical-world configurations.

**Adversarial Attacks**: Perturbations designed to fool ML models; needed because MDE models are vulnerable to physical-world attacks that can cause significant safety issues.

**L0-norm**: Count of non-zero elements; needed to simulate discrete physical perturbations (like stickers) rather than continuous digital noise.

**Photometric Consistency**: Ensuring pixel intensities match across different views; needed as the self-supervised signal for training MDE models.

## Architecture Onboarding

**Component Map**: Stereo images -> View synthesis -> L0 perturbation -> Adversarial training -> Robust MDE model

**Critical Path**: Synthesized object images + background scenes -> Camera pose transformation -> View synthesis (It, Is) -> L0 perturbation -> Reconstruction loss minimization

**Design Tradeoffs**: Synthetic data vs real-world collection (cost vs realism), L0 vs L∞ perturbations (physical realism vs mathematical convenience), self-supervised vs supervised training (label-free vs potentially higher performance)

**Failure Signatures**: Poor view synthesis quality (unrealistic training data), ineffective perturbations (weak adversarial examples), overfitting to synthetic patterns (lack of generalization)

**First Experiments**:
1. Validate view synthesis quality by comparing synthesized stereo pairs to ground-truth stereo pairs
2. Test L0 perturbation generation by verifying constraint satisfaction and perturbation magnitude
3. Evaluate reconstruction error convergence during training to ensure effective learning

## Limitations

- Synthetic data generation may not fully capture real-world attack complexity and variability
- Evaluation primarily on KITTI dataset may limit generalizability to other scenarios
- L0-norm formulation may not perfectly represent discrete physical attack nature
- Method effectiveness against complex multi-object or dynamic environment attacks remains unproven

## Confidence

High confidence: The self-supervised adversarial training approach is novel and addresses a significant problem, with clear experimental improvements in robustness against various attacks while maintaining benign performance.

Medium confidence: The synthetic training data generation process is plausible but may have limitations in capturing all real-world attack scenarios.

Low confidence: Generalizability of results to other datasets and real-world deployment scenarios beyond KITTI remains uncertain.
