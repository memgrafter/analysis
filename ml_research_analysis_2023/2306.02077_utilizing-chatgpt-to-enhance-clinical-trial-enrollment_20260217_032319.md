# Utilizing ChatGPT to Enhance Clinical Trial Enrollment

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2306.02077
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.02077
- Reference count: 40
- This study proposes using ChatGPT to improve clinical trial enrollment by extracting patient information from unstructured clinical notes and generating search queries.

## Executive Summary
This paper explores the use of ChatGPT to enhance clinical trial enrollment by converting unstructured clinical notes into effective search queries. The approach uses ChatGPT as an intermediary to extract relevant patient information and generate queries that outperform traditional methods and human-generated queries on benchmark collections. The study demonstrates significant improvements in retrieval performance across multiple metrics, highlighting the potential of large language models to address the challenge of matching patients to appropriate clinical trials.

## Method Summary
The study employs ChatGPT (gpt-3.5-turbo) to process unstructured clinical notes and generate search queries for clinical trial retrieval. The method involves using various prompts with domain-specific roles to extract patient information, optionally combining with a negation detection model (ClinicalBERT) to remove irrelevant negated entities. Generated queries are then used with BM25 retrieval and RM3 query expansion on a collection of ~376k clinical trials from ClinicalTrials.gov, evaluated against TREC 2021 and 2022 Clinical Trials benchmark collections.

## Key Results
- ChatGPT-generated queries achieved higher retrieval performance than both BM25 baseline and human-generated queries across multiple metrics
- Domain-specific system roles in prompts significantly improved query generation compared to generic roles
- Combining ChatGPT with negation detection further enhanced retrieval precision by removing irrelevant negated entities

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map: Clinical note → ChatGPT (with domain-specific prompt) → Extracted keywords/entities → (Optional: Negation detection) → Search engine (BM25) → Clinical trial ranking
- Critical path: The most time-sensitive component is the ChatGPT interaction, as each query requires a separate API call. The negation detection step adds processing time but improves quality.
- Design tradeoffs: Using ChatGPT provides flexibility and reduces need for multiple specialized models, but introduces API costs and potential variability. The negation detection model adds complexity but improves precision.
- Failure signatures: Poor retrieval performance may indicate: 1) ChatGPT not extracting relevant entities, 2) Prompt not providing sufficient context, 3) Negation detection missing relevant conditions, 4) Query expansion introducing topic drift.

  3. Evaluate whether combining ChatGPT with negation detection improves precision compared to ChatGPT alone

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- Question: Can ChatGPT's query generation performance be improved by providing it with previously generated examples or demonstrations?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper experimented with a few-shot approach using four randomly selected queries from a human-generated collection, but found no improvement over zero-shot approaches.
- Why unresolved: The paper only tested with a small number of randomly selected examples. It's unclear if a more carefully curated set of demonstrations, or a different ordering of examples, would yield better results.
- What evidence would resolve it: Experiments comparing different numbers and orderings of demonstration examples, using ChatGPT to generate queries for clinical trials.

### Open Question 2
- Question: How does ChatGPT's performance in clinical trial query generation compare to other large language models (LLMs) trained on biomedical or clinical data?
- Basis in paper: [inferred] The paper compares ChatGPT to BM25 and KeyBERT baselines, but does not directly compare it to other LLMs like BioBERT or GatorTron.
- Why unresolved: The paper does not provide a direct comparison to other LLMs, making it difficult to assess ChatGPT's relative performance.
- What evidence would resolve it: Experiments comparing ChatGPT's query generation performance to other LLMs, using the same benchmark collections and evaluation metrics.

### Open Question 3
- Question: What are the optimal prompt engineering techniques for guiding ChatGPT to generate high-quality queries for clinical trial retrieval?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper explores different prompt engineering approaches, including setting the system's role and providing task descriptions, but does not systematically investigate the impact of different prompt formats or techniques.
- Why unresolved: The paper only tests a limited number of prompt engineering approaches. It's unclear what the optimal techniques are for guiding ChatGPT to generate high-quality queries.
- What evidence would resolve it: Experiments systematically varying prompt formats, techniques, and parameters to identify the optimal approach for generating high-quality clinical trial queries.

## Limitations

- The study relies on proprietary ChatGPT API rather than open models, limiting reproducibility and accessibility
- Limited ablation studies prevent understanding which components drive performance gains
- No qualitative analysis of generated queries to understand failure modes or edge cases

## Confidence

- Retrieval performance claims (High confidence): The evaluation metrics and benchmark comparisons are clearly specified, and the performance differences are substantial and consistent across multiple metrics.
- Prompt engineering effectiveness (Medium confidence): The study shows domain-specific prompts outperform generic ones, but the analysis is limited to a small set of prompt variations without systematic exploration.
- Negation detection benefits (Low confidence): While the approach is conceptually sound, the paper lacks direct comparison between ChatGPT with and without negation detection, making it difficult to isolate this component's contribution.
