# Bayesian Models of Functional Connectomics and Behavior

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2301.06182
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2301.06182
- Reference count: 25
- Primary result: SVSS model outperforms BLR and ridge regression in predicting clinical severity from rs-fMRI data while identifying interpretable subnetworks

## Executive Summary
This paper addresses the challenge of predicting clinical severity from resting-state functional MRI (rs-fMRI) data, particularly in the context of limited sample sizes common in clinical studies. The authors propose a Bayesian approach that combines representation learning with predictive modeling. They first use dictionary learning to decompose rs-fMRI correlation matrices into group-averaged basis networks and patient-specific coefficients. Two Bayesian regression methods are then applied to map these coefficients to clinical scores: a standard Bayesian Linear Regression (BLR) and a Stochastic Search Variable Selection (SVSS) model. The SVSS approach outperforms BLR in prediction accuracy and identifies subnetworks most relevant to clinical outcomes. The authors also present a fully Bayesian joint model that simultaneously learns the dictionary representation and regression parameters, though this model faces computational and convergence challenges. Preliminary results on an autism dataset show that SVSS achieves lower prediction error than classical ridge regression and provides interpretable subnetworks associated with autism symptoms.

## Method Summary
The proposed method consists of two main components: dictionary learning for representation and Bayesian regression for prediction. First, rs-fMRI correlation matrices are decomposed using group-wise sparse dictionary learning, where the group-average matrix Y is approximated as AD, with A being patient-specific coefficients and D being the learned dictionary of basis networks. This decomposition extracts subject-specific deviations from the group average. Two Bayesian regression approaches are then applied: standard Bayesian Linear Regression (BLR) and Stochastic Search Variable Selection (SVSS). BLR uses conjugate priors and Gaussian likelihood to obtain closed-form posterior distributions, while SVSS employs spike-and-slab priors to perform variable selection by assigning each coefficient a probability of being included in the model. The authors also propose a fully Bayesian joint model that learns the dictionary representation and regression parameters simultaneously using MCMC sampling, though this approach faces computational challenges and convergence issues.

## Key Results
- SVSS model achieves lower prediction error than standard BLR and ridge regression on autism dataset
- SVSS identifies specific subnetworks most relevant to clinical outcomes, providing interpretability
- The fully Bayesian joint model faces computational challenges and slow convergence despite theoretical advantages

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The approach works by leveraging the hierarchical structure of brain connectivity data while addressing the small sample size problem common in clinical neuroimaging studies. Dictionary learning decomposes complex correlation matrices into interpretable basis networks plus patient-specific coefficients, effectively reducing dimensionality while preserving subject-level variation. The SVSS regression then performs principled variable selection through spike-and-slab priors, identifying which basis networks are most predictive of clinical outcomes while providing uncertainty quantification through posterior distributions. This combination allows the model to extract meaningful patterns from limited data while avoiding overfitting through Bayesian regularization.

- Quick check: Verify that the learned dictionary captures known functional brain networks

**Spike-and-Slab Priors**
- Why needed: To perform variable selection in regression while maintaining Bayesian uncertainty quantification
- Quick check: Confirm that posterior inclusion probabilities align with cross-validation performance

**Conjugate Priors in BLR**
- Why needed: To obtain closed-form posterior distributions for efficient computation
- Quick check: Verify that posterior means and variances are correctly computed from the analytical solution

## Architecture Onboarding

**Component Map**
Dictionary Learning -> Patient Coefficients -> Bayesian Regression (BLR/SVSS) -> Clinical Prediction

**Critical Path**
The most critical path is from dictionary learning to SVSS regression, as the quality of the learned basis networks directly impacts the regression performance and interpretability of the results.

**Design Tradeoffs**
The authors tradeoff computational efficiency for interpretability by using dictionary learning instead of direct regression on correlation matrices. While this adds computational overhead, it provides meaningful subnetworks that can be clinically interpreted. The SVSS model trades additional computational complexity for superior prediction accuracy and variable selection compared to standard BLR.

**Failure Signatures**
- Poor dictionary learning quality manifests as uninformative basis networks that don't capture functional connectivity patterns
- SVSS convergence issues appear as multimodal posterior distributions or extremely slow mixing of MCMC chains
- Overfitting occurs when cross-validation performance degrades despite good training performance

**First Experiments**
1. Validate dictionary learning by checking if learned basis networks match known functional brain networks
2. Compare SVSS posterior inclusion probabilities with feature importance from ridge regression
3. Test sensitivity of results to different choices of dictionary learning regularization parameters

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
The authors identify several open questions regarding the computational feasibility of the fully Bayesian joint model, the stability of dictionary learning parameters across different cross-validation folds, and the generalizability of the approach to larger datasets with more subjects.

## Limitations
- Computational complexity and convergence challenges for the fully Bayesian joint model
- Dictionary learning results depend heavily on choice of regularization parameters
- Limited validation on small sample sizes (N=42 in autism dataset) raises questions about generalizability

## Confidence

**High Confidence**: The superiority of SVSS over standard BLR in prediction accuracy and interpretability is well-supported by the presented results

**Medium Confidence**: The comparison with ridge regression on the autism dataset, given the limited sample size (N=42) and lack of cross-validation details

**Low Confidence**: The computational feasibility claims for the fully Bayesian joint model due to reported convergence issues
