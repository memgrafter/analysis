# Model Extraction Attacks Against Reinforcement Learning Based Controllers

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2304.13090
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2304.13090
- Authors: 
- Reference count: 29
- One-line primary result: Proposed two-phase algorithm extracts DNN controllers from input-output observations with bounded error, achieving 4.24-6.13 shortlisted policies in inverted pendulum and mountain car tasks

## Executive Summary
This paper introduces model extraction attacks targeting reinforcement learning-based controllers in cyber-physical systems. The attacker aims to reconstruct an unknown deep neural network controller by observing system inputs and outputs while leveraging side-channel information about the reward function. The authors propose a two-phase algorithm combining multi-kernel regression for offline candidate generation with online input-output observation for final policy selection. Theoretical analysis provides guarantees on the bounded error between the original and extracted controllers, while numerical experiments demonstrate effectiveness on standard RL benchmarks.

## Method Summary
The proposed attack operates in two phases. First, an offline phase identifies candidate function estimators by exploiting side-channel information about the reward function using multi-kernel regression to capture the policy landscape. Second, an online phase collects input-output observations from the target system and uses these to shortlist candidate policies that best match the observed behavior. The algorithm guarantees that the final shortlisted policies maintain bounded error relative to the unknown DNN controller, with the error threshold set by the attacker.

## Key Results
- Algorithm achieves 4.24 shortlisted policies for inverted pendulum task and 6.13 for mountain car task
- All shortlisted policies maintain error below user-provided threshold
- Theoretical analysis guarantees bounded error between unknown DNN and estimated policies
- Two-phase approach effectively combines reward function side-channel information with input-output observations

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The attack exploits the fundamental challenge in RL-based control systems: the controller's learned policy is often proprietary and must be protected, but its behavior is observable through system interactions. By combining knowledge of the reward function (a common side-channel in CPS) with input-output observations, the attacker can reconstruct approximate models of the controller. The multi-kernel regression in the offline phase captures the relationship between system states and actions under different reward structures, while the online phase filters these candidates based on actual system behavior.

- **Side-channel analysis**: Recognizing how indirect information (reward functions) can be exploited for model extraction

## Architecture Onboarding

Component map: Reward function side-channel -> Multi-kernel regression -> Candidate policy pool -> Input-output observations -> Policy shortlisting -> Extracted DNN model

Critical path: The algorithm's effectiveness depends on the quality of reward function side-channel information and the quantity of input-output observations. More precise reward information and diverse observations lead to better candidate generation and shorter final lists.

Design tradeoffs: The attack balances between offline computation (multi-kernel regression) and online observation requirements. More extensive offline computation can reduce online observation needs, while more observations can compensate for less precise reward information.

Failure signatures: Poor attack performance manifests as large candidate pools that fail to converge to a small set of high-quality matches, or final policies with error exceeding the threshold. This typically indicates insufficient reward information or inadequate observation coverage.

Three first experiments:
1. Test algorithm with synthetic reward functions of varying precision to quantify impact on candidate pool size
2. Vary observation quantities to measure convergence behavior of the shortlisting process
3. Compare performance against random policy selection to validate effectiveness

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
### Open Question 1
- Question: How does the number of shortlisted policies change with the size and coverage of the input-output data?
- Basis in paper: The paper mentions that the reduction in the number of shortlisted policies depends on the quality and quantity of the collected data.
- Why unresolved: The paper only provides numerical results for specific scenarios with a fixed number of observations.
- What evidence would resolve it: Conducting experiments with varying amounts of input-output data and analyzing how the number of shortlisted policies changes would provide insights into the algorithm's sensitivity to data quantity and coverage.

### Open Question 2
- Question: How does the choice of the kernel function affect the performance of the multi-kernel regression algorithm?
- Basis in paper: The paper mentions using a symmetric positive semidefinite basis function (kernel) to measure the similarity between parameters, but does not explore the impact of different kernel choices.
- Why unresolved: The paper uses a specific kernel function but does not compare its performance with other kernel functions or discuss the impact of kernel choice on the algorithm's effectiveness.
- What evidence would resolve it: Conducting experiments with different kernel functions and comparing their performance in terms of the accuracy of the estimated policies would help determine the impact of kernel choice on the algorithm's effectiveness.

### Open Question 3
- Question: How does the algorithm's performance scale with the complexity of the DNN controller?
- Basis in paper: The paper demonstrates the algorithm's effectiveness on two tasks with different DNN architectures, but does not explore how the algorithm's performance changes with the size or depth of the DNN.
- Why unresolved: The paper only provides results for DNN controllers with a fixed number of layers and neurons, but does not discuss how the algorithm's performance would change with more complex DNN architectures.
- What evidence would resolve it: Conducting experiments with DNN controllers of varying sizes and depths and analyzing the algorithm's performance in terms of the accuracy of the estimated policies and the computational resources required would help determine the algorithm's scalability.

## Limitations
- Theoretical guarantees rely on assumptions about reward function side-channel information that may not hold in all CPS
- Performance metrics lack comparison against baseline extraction methods or alternative approaches
- Validation limited to two specific RL environments (inverted pendulum, mountain car)
- Scalability to more complex DNN architectures and real-world CPS not demonstrated

## Confidence
- High: Theoretical error bounds are formally proven and well-defined
- Medium: Methodology is sound but validation is limited to specific environments
- Low: Practical implications for real-world CPS deployment remain uncertain
