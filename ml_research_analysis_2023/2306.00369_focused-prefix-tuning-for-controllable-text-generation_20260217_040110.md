# Focused Prefix Tuning for Controllable Text Generation

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2306.00369
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.00369
- Reference count: 10
- Key outcome: Focused Prefix Tuning (FPT) improves controllable text generation by separately encoding explicit and implicit attributes using specific and general prefixes, achieving higher control accuracy and better fluency than baseline methods.

## Executive Summary
Focused Prefix Tuning (FPT) addresses attribute transfer in controllable text generation by introducing specific and general prefixes to separately encode explicit and implicit attributes. FPT trains a specific prefix on the annotated attribute subset and a general prefix on the full dataset, then combines their logits via subtraction at inference time to suppress implicit attribute influence. Experiments on single-attribute control tasks show FPT achieves higher control accuracy and better text fluency than vanilla prefix tuning, contrastive prefix tuning, and DExperts, with relevance scores above 80% and lower bias. In multi-attribute control, FPT matches the state-of-the-art Distributional Lens in relevance while offering greater flexibility to add new attributes without retraining existing prefixes.

## Method Summary
FPT extends prefix tuning by introducing a general prefix trained on the full dataset to capture implicit attributes, while the specific prefix focuses on explicit attributes from the annotated subset. During inference, the model combines logits from both prefixes using a scaled subtraction operation to suppress implicit attribute influence while preserving explicit attribute control. The method employs top-p filtering before logit manipulation to maintain fluency, and uses a scaling hyperparameter α to balance the relative contributions of each prefix.

## Key Results
- FPT achieves higher control accuracy and better text fluency than vanilla prefix tuning, contrastive prefix tuning, and DExperts on single-attribute control tasks
- FPT maintains relevance scores above 80% while reducing bias compared to baseline methods
- In multi-attribute control, FPT matches Distributional Lens in relevance while offering greater flexibility for adding new attributes

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: FPT reduces attribute transfer by encoding implicit attributes with a general prefix and suppressing their influence via logit subtraction
- Mechanism: The general prefix is trained on the full dataset, capturing dataset-level tendencies (implicit attributes). During inference, the logits from the specific prefix (explicit attribute) are adjusted by subtracting a scaled version of the general prefix logits, reducing the probability of words associated with implicit attributes
- Core assumption: Implicit attributes are encoded primarily in the general prefix logits and can be effectively suppressed by subtraction without harming explicit attribute control
- Evidence anchors: [abstract] "FPT uses specific and general prefixes to encode the explicit and implicit attributes, respectively. FPT combines the control power of the two prefixes via logits manipulation at inference time."
- Break condition: If the general prefix fails to capture implicit attributes or the subtraction over-suppresses desired words, attribute control quality degrades

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: Training specific and general prefixes separately allows independent optimization of explicit and implicit attribute representations
- Mechanism: The specific prefix is trained only on the annotated subset (Dattr=val) to focus on the explicit attribute. The general prefix is trained on the entire dataset (D) to capture implicit attributes. This separation enables the model to isolate and later subtract implicit influences
- Core assumption: Separate training prevents interference between explicit and implicit attribute learning, ensuring each prefix encodes its target attribute effectively
- Evidence anchors: [section 3.2] "The specific prefix is the same as the prefix in vanilla prefix tuning, so we still use Equation 1 to update its parameters. To update the general prefix's parameters, we optimize the following objective: ..."
- Break condition: If the general prefix overfits to the full dataset and fails to isolate implicit attributes, or if the specific prefix underfits due to limited annotated data

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: Logit manipulation via scaled subtraction maintains fluency while improving controllability
- Mechanism: After obtaining logits from both prefixes, the specific prefix logits are scaled by α and the general prefix logits by (α-1). This ratio preserves relative importance while suppressing implicit attribute influence. Top-p filtering is applied before manipulation to ensure fluency
- Core assumption: The ratio α/(α-1) can be tuned to balance fluency and control, and the subtraction operation does not destabilize the probability distribution
- Evidence anchors: [section 3.3] "Following Liu et al. (2021), we respectively set α and α − 1 as the weight of zattr=val and zgenl t to make the ratio of logits after the logits manipulation equal to 1."
- Break condition: If α is poorly chosen, fluency may degrade (too high α) or control may weaken (too low α)

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map:
  - Specific prefix: Encodes explicit attribute, trained on annotated subset
  - General prefix: Encodes implicit attributes, trained on full dataset
  - Logit manipulation layer: Combines and scales logits during inference
  - Top-p filtering: Ensures fluency before logit adjustment

- Critical path:
  1. Train specific prefix on Dattr=val
  2. Train general prefix on D
  3. At inference, generate logits from both prefixes
  4. Apply top-p filtering to specific prefix logits
  5. Combine logits via scaled subtraction
  6. Sample from adjusted distribution

- Design tradeoffs:
  - Training overhead: Two prefixes instead of one, but each is smaller than full model
  - Inference cost: Two forward passes per step, increasing latency
  - Flexibility: Can add new attributes without retraining existing prefixes

- Failure signatures:
  - High bias scores: General prefix not capturing implicit attributes well
  - Low relevance: Specific prefix underfitting or logit subtraction too aggressive
  - Poor fluency: Top-p filtering too restrictive or α poorly tuned

- First 3 experiments:
  1. Ablation: Remove general prefix, compare attribute relevance and bias
  2. Ablation: Remove specific prefix, compare fluency and implicit attribute suppression
  3. Ablation: Remove logit subtraction, compare control accuracy vs vanilla prefix tuning

## Limitations

- The core mechanism of logit subtraction to suppress implicit attributes lacks direct empirical validation and ablation analysis
- The choice of scaling hyperparameter α is not systematically studied, leaving uncertainty about its sensitivity and robustness
- The evaluation scope is limited, focusing on single-attribute control tasks and comparing primarily against baseline methods without detailed multi-attribute comparisons or human evaluation

## Confidence

- **High Confidence**: FPT achieves higher control accuracy and better text fluency than vanilla prefix tuning, contrastive prefix tuning, and DExperts on single-attribute control tasks
- **Medium Confidence**: FPT matches Distributional Lens in relevance while offering greater flexibility for multi-attribute control
- **Low Confidence**: Logit subtraction effectively suppresses implicit attributes without harming explicit attribute control

## Next Checks

1. **Ablation Study of Logit Subtraction**: Remove the logit subtraction step and compare the resulting model's attribute relevance, fluency, and bias to vanilla prefix tuning to directly test whether the subtraction mechanism is responsible for FPT's improvements

2. **Hyperparameter Sensitivity Analysis**: Systematically vary the scaling hyperparameter α across a range of values (e.g., 0.5 to 2.0) and measure the impact on control accuracy, fluency, and bias to reveal whether FPT is robust to hyperparameter choice

3. **Failure Mode Analysis**: Intentionally train the general prefix on a corrupted or biased dataset (e.g., with artificially introduced implicit attributes) and measure whether FPT can still suppress these attributes effectively to test the robustness of the mechanism and reveal potential failure modes
