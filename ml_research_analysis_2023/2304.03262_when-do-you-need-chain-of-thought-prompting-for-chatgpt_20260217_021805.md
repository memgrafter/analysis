# When do you need Chain-of-Thought Prompting for ChatGPT?

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID**: 2304.03262
- **Source URL**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2304.03262
- **Reference count**: 10
- **Key outcome**: CoT prompting is no longer universally beneficial for ChatGPT, with arithmetic tasks showing degraded performance with CoT while symbolic tasks still benefit, indicating potential overfitting and pretraining data leakage.

## Executive Summary
This paper investigates whether Chain-of-Thought (CoT) prompting remains effective for ChatGPT compared to earlier LLMs like GPT-3. The study tests ChatGPT's zero-shot reasoning performance on multiple tasks under three prompting settings: standard, trigger words, and CoT. Results reveal that ChatGPT performs strongly on arithmetic tasks without CoT prompting, even showing slight performance drops with CoT, suggesting it has memorized arithmetic-related instructions from pre-training. In contrast, CoT still boosts performance on symbolic and other reasoning tasks. This discrepancy indicates potential overfitting to specific instructions and possible data leakage from the pretraining set, concluding that CoT is no longer universally beneficial for ChatGPT.

## Method Summary
The study evaluates ChatGPT's performance on multiple reasoning tasks including arithmetic (GSM8K, MultiArith, AddSub, AQuA, SVAMP), commonsense (CSQA, StrategyQA), and symbolic reasoning (Last Letter, Coin-flip, Date, Object) using three prompting strategies: standard zero-shot, zero-shot with trigger words, and zero-shot with CoT. The researchers compare accuracy across these settings to determine the effectiveness of CoT prompting for ChatGPT. They also examine potential overfitting and instruction memorization by analyzing the model's reasoning capabilities across different task types.

## Key Results
- ChatGPT achieves superior zero-shot arithmetic performance without CoT prompting, suggesting memorization of arithmetic instructions from pre-training
- CoT prompting still improves performance on symbolic and other reasoning tasks, indicating these were not memorized during training
- The effectiveness of CoT varies by task type, revealing potential pretraining recipe leakage that allows inference about training data composition

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- **Claim**: ChatGPT's superior zero-shot arithmetic performance is due to memorization of arithmetic instructions from pre-training.
- **Mechanism**: ChatGPT was exposed to arithmetic reasoning datasets during pre-training with Chain-of-Thought (CoT) instructions. When encountering similar problems, it recalls the memorized solution patterns without needing explicit CoT prompts.
- **Core assumption**: ChatGPT's training data included specific arithmetic datasets with CoT-style instructions.
- **Evidence anchors**:
  - [abstract] "ChatGPT usually achieves the best performance and can generate CoT even without being instructed to do so... it is plausible that ChatGPT has already been trained on these tasks with CoT and thus memorized the instruction"
  - [section 4.2] "ChatGPT commits to memory the instruction for arithmetic tasks in the pre-training stage and autonomously generates the reasoning step when it identifies that the question pertains to arithmetic"
  - [corpus] Weak evidence - no direct corpus evidence of specific arithmetic datasets used in ChatGPT training
- **Break condition**: If arithmetic tasks are presented in novel formats or domains ChatGPT hasn't seen, it would require CoT prompting like other models.

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim**: CoT prompting is still necessary for symbolic and other reasoning tasks because these were not included in ChatGPT's pre-training with CoT instructions.
- **Mechanism**: For tasks where ChatGPT hasn't been pre-trained with CoT instructions, it needs explicit prompting to generate intermediate reasoning steps, similar to GPT-3's behavior.
- **Core assumption**: Symbolic and other reasoning tasks were not part of ChatGPT's pre-training corpus with CoT instructions.
- **Evidence anchors**:
  - [abstract] "CoT is no longer effective for certain tasks such as arithmetic reasoning while still keeping effective on other reasoning tasks"
  - [section 4.1] "For symbolic reasoning and other reasoning tasks, the consistent improvement suggests that CoT prompting is still required for ChatGPT to achieve significant performance boosts"
  - [corpus] Weak evidence - no direct corpus evidence about which reasoning tasks were included in training
- **Break condition**: If symbolic reasoning tasks are presented with instructions ChatGPT has memorized, it might perform well without CoT prompting.

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim**: The effectiveness of CoT prompting reveals potential pretraining recipe leakage, allowing inference about what datasets and instructions were used in training.
- **Mechanism**: By observing which tasks benefit from CoT prompting and which don't, researchers can infer whether specific datasets and instructions were included in the pretraining corpus.
- **Core assumption**: The pretraining process included specific datasets with or without CoT instructions, and this can be reverse-engineered through prompting experiments.
- **Evidence anchors**:
  - [abstract] "it indicates possible leakage of the pretraining recipe, e.g., one can verify whether a dataset and instruction were used in training ChatGPT"
  - [section 2.3] "Dataset Inference aims to verify if Di ∈ C or Ii ∈ C" and "we investigate more realistic data leakage settings that on which datasets ChatGPT are pre-trained"
  - [corpus] Weak evidence - no direct corpus evidence about pretraining recipe leakage methods
- **Break condition**: If pretraining data is sufficiently diverse and instructions are not task-specific, this leakage detection method would become unreliable.

## Architecture Onboarding

- **Component map**: Input (question/prompt) -> Processing (language model inference) -> Output (answer with or without reasoning steps)
- **Critical path**: 
  1. Receive input question
  2. Check against memorized instruction patterns
  3. If memorized pattern matches, generate answer directly
  4. If no match, generate reasoning steps (CoT) if prompted
  5. Output final answer
- **Design tradeoffs**: Memory efficiency vs. flexibility: Memorizing specific instructions improves performance on those tasks but reduces adaptability; Pretraining data selection: Including more diverse tasks with CoT might reduce memorization benefits; Instruction generalization: Broader instructions might be less effective than specific ones
- **Failure signatures**: Arithmetic tasks failing without CoT prompting (would indicate no memorization occurred); Symbolic tasks succeeding without CoT prompting (would indicate unexpected generalization); Inconsistent performance across similar tasks (might indicate partial memorization)
- **First 3 experiments**:
  1. Test arithmetic tasks with modified phrasing to see if memorization is pattern-based
  2. Test symbolic tasks with CoT instructions to confirm if they benefit from explicit prompting
  3. Test a mix of arithmetic and symbolic tasks to identify the boundary between memorized and non-memorized instructions

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- **Question**: What specific datasets or instructions in ChatGPT's pretraining could be inferred to cause the memorization effect for arithmetic reasoning tasks?
- **Basis in paper**: [explicit] The paper discusses dataset inference attacks and mentions that ChatGPT may have been trained on datasets like GSM-8K and MATH, leading to memorized instructions for arithmetic tasks.
- **Why unresolved**: The exact datasets and instructions used in ChatGPT's pretraining are not publicly disclosed, making it difficult to pinpoint which specific data led to the memorization effect.
- **What evidence would resolve it**: A detailed analysis of ChatGPT's pretraining corpus or an independent audit of the datasets used would clarify which specific data led to the memorization effect.

### Open Question 2
- **Question**: How does the memorization of instructions in ChatGPT affect its performance on other types of reasoning tasks beyond arithmetic?
- **Basis in paper**: [inferred] The paper notes that ChatGPT shows strong zero-shot performance on arithmetic tasks without CoT prompting, suggesting a memorization effect, but it still benefits from CoT prompting for symbolic and other reasoning tasks.
- **Why unresolved**: The paper does not provide a comprehensive analysis of how instruction memorization impacts performance across a broader range of tasks.
- **What evidence would resolve it**: Systematic testing of ChatGPT's performance on a diverse set of tasks with and without specific instructions would reveal the extent of memorization effects.

### Open Question 3
- **Question**: Can the dataset inference attack method be extended to identify other types of data leakage or overfitting in large language models?
- **Basis in paper**: [explicit] The paper introduces dataset inference attacks as a method to infer whether specific datasets were used in pretraining, highlighting its potential for detecting data leakage.
- **Why unresolved**: The paper focuses on arithmetic tasks and does not explore the applicability of this method to other domains or types of data leakage.
- **What evidence would resolve it**: Applying the dataset inference attack method to various domains and types of data would demonstrate its broader applicability and limitations.

## Limitations

- The central claim about ChatGPT memorizing arithmetic instructions rests on indirect evidence rather than direct verification of the pretraining corpus
- The proposed mechanism (memorization of CoT instructions) is plausible but not definitively proven, as the authors acknowledge they cannot directly access or confirm the training data composition
- The claim about "pretraining recipe leakage" remains speculative, with broader implications for security and intellectual property not fully substantiated

## Confidence

- **High Confidence**: The empirical observation that ChatGPT performs arithmetic tasks well without CoT prompting, while CoT remains beneficial for symbolic reasoning tasks
- **Medium Confidence**: The interpretation that this performance pattern indicates instruction memorization rather than genuine reasoning capability
- **Low Confidence**: The claim about "pretraining recipe leakage" - while the observation is valid, the broader implications for security and intellectual property remain speculative
