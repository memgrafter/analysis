# Improving Long-Horizon Imitation Through Instruction Prediction

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2306.12554
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.12554
- Reference count: 21
- Primary result: Instruction modeling improves long-horizon imitation learning performance, especially in data-limited settings.

## Executive Summary
This work proposes using language instruction prediction as an auxiliary loss for imitation learning to improve long-horizon planning in data-limited settings. The method trains transformer-based policies with an additional language decoder that predicts step-by-step instructions during training. Experiments on BabyAI and Crafting benchmarks show that instruction modeling significantly improves performance compared to baselines, particularly for complex tasks and with limited demonstrations. In BabyAI, success rates reach 70% with 50k demos using instruction modeling versus 40% without.

## Method Summary
The method extends standard behavior cloning by adding a language decoder to transformer-based policies. During training, the model learns to predict step-by-step instructions in addition to actions. The encoder produces latent representations from observations, and the decoder uses cross-attention to predict instructions conditioned only on past observations up to the instruction start time. The training objective combines action prediction loss with instruction prediction loss, weighted by a coefficient λ. The approach handles both Markovian (fully observed) and non-Markovian (partially observed) settings with appropriate model architectures.

## Key Results
- Instruction modeling significantly improves success rates on complex BabyAI tasks, reaching 70% with 50k demonstrations versus 40% without instruction modeling
- Benefits are most pronounced in data-limited regimes and for combinatorially complex tasks
- The method scales well with instruction annotations, showing 20% improvement with just 50% of demonstrations annotated with instructions
- Forward prediction baselines and contrastive learning show weaker performance, indicating the importance of grounded language supervision

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: Language instruction prediction forces the model to learn temporally extended, abstract representations that generalize better to unseen tasks.
- Mechanism: By predicting step-by-step instructions, the model must understand the high-level plan rather than just memorizing action sequences. This abstraction operates at a level where generalization is easier than low-level action prediction.
- Core assumption: Abstract linguistic representations capture the essential structure of tasks better than raw action sequences, especially for complex, long-horizon tasks.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "inspired by recent advances in transformer-based models, we train agents with an instruction prediction loss that encourages learning temporally extended representations that operate at a high level of abstraction"
  - [section] "Teaching agents to output language instructions for completing tasks has two concrete benefits. First, it forces them to learn at a higher level of abstraction where generalization is easier."
  - [corpus] Weak evidence - corpus doesn't directly address this mechanism
- Break condition: If tasks can be solved by simple, short-horizon reasoning without needing abstract planning, the abstraction benefit disappears.

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: Instruction prediction helps combat compounding errors by forcing the model to consider future steps during training.
- Mechanism: When predicting instructions, the model must understand what actions are needed in the future, not just the immediate next action. This forward-looking behavior helps prevent the accumulation of small errors that compound over long horizons.
- Core assumption: Models that explicitly reason about future steps during training will be more robust to small errors during execution.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "difficulties in such settings are exacerbated in low data regimes where over-fitting stifles generalization and compounding errors hurt accuracy"
  - [section] "by outputting multi-step instructions agents explicitly consider the future"
  - [corpus] Weak evidence - corpus doesn't directly address this mechanism
- Break condition: If the environment is fully Markovian with short task horizons, future consideration may not provide significant benefit.

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: Language instructions provide grounded information that is more useful than predicting future actions alone.
- Mechanism: Predicting language instructions requires understanding the semantic meaning of actions in context, which provides richer training signal than just predicting the next action. The language decoder must understand why actions are taken, not just what actions to take.
- Core assumption: Grounded language provides more meaningful supervision signal than ungrounded action prediction.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [section] "we see that the forward prediction used in the forward baseline hardly contributes to performance, indicating that grounded instructions do more than just combat compounding errors"
  - [section] "Instead of predicting instructions, we use the decoder to predict future actions. This baseline demonstrates the importance of using grounded information"
  - [corpus] Weak evidence - corpus doesn't directly address this mechanism
- Break condition: If actions can be perfectly predicted from observations alone without needing semantic understanding, the grounding benefit may be minimal.

  - Why needed here: The method handles both settings differently, with sequence models for POMDPs and single-step models for fully observed environments

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map: Transformer encoder → latent representations → action predictor + language decoder
- Critical path: Encoder produces latents → action predictor outputs actions → language decoder predicts instructions conditioned only on past latents up to instruction start time
- Design tradeoffs: Using transformers provides good inductive bias for combinatorial planning but increases parameter count vs. CNNs. The cross-attention masking adds complexity but prevents train-test distribution mismatch.
- Failure signatures: Poor instruction prediction accuracy, especially when instructions can be easily predicted from goals alone. Performance degradation when cross-attention masking is removed. Saturation of benefits with sufficient demonstrations.
- First 3 experiments:
  1. Implement the basic transformer architecture without instruction prediction and verify it achieves reasonable baseline performance
  2. Add instruction prediction loss and verify it improves performance on complex tasks while maintaining or slightly degrading on simple tasks
  3. Test with partial instruction annotations (e.g., 50% of demos labeled) to verify the mixed-data training capability

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- Question: How does the effectiveness of instruction prediction scale with the number of instruction annotations per demonstration?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper mentions that one is better off collecting 12.5k language annotations than collecting an additional 25k demonstrations in BabyAI environments. It also states that the method can easily be applied to mixed datasets that have only some instruction labels.
- Why unresolved: The paper does not provide detailed experiments on varying the number of instruction annotations per demonstration, only testing with 0%, 50%, and 100% of demonstrations annotated with language instructions.
- What evidence would resolve it: Experiments varying the number of instruction annotations per demonstration (e.g., 1, 2, 5, 10 instructions per demonstration) and measuring performance impact would provide insights into the scaling behavior.

### Open Question 2
- Question: How does the performance of instruction prediction compare to other auxiliary objectives in the long-horizon planning domain?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper compares instruction modeling to forward prediction and contrastive learning (ATC), but does not explore a wide range of other auxiliary objectives.
- Why unresolved: The paper focuses primarily on instruction modeling and a few specific baselines, without a comprehensive comparison to other potential auxiliary objectives that could aid in long-horizon planning.
- What evidence would resolve it: A systematic comparison of instruction prediction against various other auxiliary objectives (e.g., reward prediction, state reconstruction, inverse dynamics) in long-horizon planning environments would provide insights into its relative effectiveness.

### Open Question 3
- Question: How does the performance of instruction prediction vary with the complexity and structure of the language instructions?
- Basis in paper: [inferred] The paper mentions that tasks in both environments do not communicate all required steps to agents, and that instructions offer less training signal for combinatorially simple tasks. It also shows that instruction prediction accuracy varies across environments.
- Why unresolved: The paper does not provide a detailed analysis of how the performance of instruction prediction varies with the complexity, length, or structure of the language instructions.
- What evidence would resolve it: Experiments varying the complexity, length, and structure of the language instructions (e.g., using different instruction formats, adding noise, or using instructions from different domains) and measuring their impact on performance would provide insights into the robustness of instruction prediction.

## Limitations
- Limited to grid-world environments and a single household manipulation task, raising questions about scalability to continuous control or real-world robotics
- Effectiveness heavily depends on access to quality instruction annotations, though the paper shows some robustness to partial labeling
- The benefits are most pronounced in data-limited settings, with diminishing returns as demonstration quantity increases

## Confidence
- **High confidence**: The core empirical finding that instruction prediction improves imitation learning performance in data-limited settings is well-supported by the experiments
- **Medium confidence**: The proposed mechanisms (abstraction, future consideration, grounded language) are plausible explanations for the improvements, but the evidence is largely correlational rather than causal
- **Low confidence**: The claims about instruction prediction being particularly useful when tasks cannot be predicted from goals alone rely on limited evidence from the Crafting environment

## Next Checks
1. **Cross-domain generalization test**: Implement the method on a continuous control benchmark like Meta-World or a real robot manipulation task to verify the approach transfers beyond grid-world environments
2. **Ablation of individual mechanisms**: Design experiments that isolate the three proposed mechanisms by creating controlled variations where only one mechanism is active
3. **Scaling analysis with instruction quality**: Systematically vary the quality and relevance of instruction annotations by using automatically generated instructions of different granularities and measuring the trade-off between instruction quality and learning performance
