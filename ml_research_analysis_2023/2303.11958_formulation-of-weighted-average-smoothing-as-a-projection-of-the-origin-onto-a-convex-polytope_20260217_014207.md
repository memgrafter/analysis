# Formulation of Weighted Average Smoothing as a Projection of the Origin onto a Convex Polytope

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2303.11958
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.11958
- Reference count: 40
- Primary result: Optimal weights for weighted moving average smoothing can be found by projecting the origin onto a convex polytope defined by the autocorrelation matrix

## Executive Summary
This paper addresses the fundamental problem of finding optimal weight windows for weighted moving average smoothing under squared loss. The authors demonstrate that optimal weight windows are symmetrical around their center and study tapered weight windows that decrease in weight away from the center. The key innovation is reformulating the least squares problem as a projection of the origin onto a convex polytope, which provides both theoretical insight and computational advantages for finding optimal weights.

## Method Summary
The authors formulate the weighted moving average smoothing problem as a quadratic program, then transform it into a projection problem onto a convex polytope. They leverage the symmetry of the autocorrelation matrix to constrain the weight space to symmetric windows, then further restrict to tapered weights that decrease away from the center. The optimal weights are obtained by finding the minimum norm point in this constrained space, which can be computed using Wolfe's method for quadratic programming.

## Key Results
- Optimal weight windows for smoothing are symmetrical around their center
- Tapering weight windows that decrease away from the center are studied
- The weighted average smoothing problem can be reformulated as a projection of the origin onto a convex polytope
- Analytical solutions exist for best windows under certain input data conditions

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: The optimal weight window for weighted moving average smoothing is symmetric around its center

- Core assumption: The autocorrelation matrix is symmetric and positive semi-definite

- Break condition: If the autocorrelation matrix is not symmetric (e.g., non-stationary input), the symmetry assumption fails

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: The weighted moving average smoothing problem can be reformulated as a projection of the origin onto a convex polytope
- Mechanism: By expressing the optimization in terms of symmetric weights and enforcing constraints, the problem reduces to finding the minimum norm point in a convex polytope defined by the columns of a matrix derived from the autocorrelation matrix
- Core assumption: The weight space is a convex polytope and the objective is convex
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "We formulate the corresponding least squares problem as a quadratic program and finally as a projection of the origin onto a convex polytope."
  - [section III-D] "Theorem 1. The problem in Lemma 4 is equivalent to minimize ∥x∥2 subject to x ∈ A where A is the convex polytope defined by the columns of A, where A is the square-root of ˜Q, i.e., AA = ˜Q."
- Break condition: If the weight constraints are not convex or the objective is not quadratic, the polytope projection formulation is invalid

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: Tapering weight windows that decrease away from the center are optimal for smoothing
- Mechanism: The tapering constraint ensures that closer neighbors have more influence on the smoothed value, which aligns with the underlying assumption that nearby data points are more closely related
- Core assumption: The relationship between data points decreases with distance
- Evidence anchors:
  - [section I] "We study the class of tapered weight windows, which decrease in weight as they move away from the center."
  - [section II-B] "we constrain our weight vectors to have tapering weights, i.e., it should be nonincreasing away from the center."
- Break condition: If the data has periodic or repeating patterns where distant points are more related, tapering may not be optimal

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- Question: How can we efficiently solve the quadratic programming problem in Lemma 3 when the solution lies on the boundary of the unit simplex?
- Basis in paper: The paper mentions that if pλ∗ is not non-negative, additional constraints need to be enforced using Lagrange multipliers, but solving this becomes non-trivial
- Why unresolved: The paper states that solving the Lagrangian with inequality constraints is not straightforward and suggests reducing to the projection problem, but does not provide a complete solution method
- What evidence would resolve it: A detailed algorithm or method for solving the constrained quadratic program when the solution is on the boundary, with numerical examples demonstrating its effectiveness

### Open Question 2
- Question: Can the optimal weight window design problem be extended to non-stationary signals where the autocorrelation structure changes over time?
- Basis in paper: The paper assumes a fixed autocorrelation matrix R for the entire signal, which implies stationarity. There is no discussion of time-varying correlations
- Why unresolved: The current formulation relies on a single autocorrelation matrix, making it unsuitable for non-stationary signals without modification
- What evidence would resolve it: A theoretical extension of the formulation to handle time-varying autocorrelations, along with experimental results on non-stationary datasets

### Open Question 3
- Question: What is the computational complexity of the projection method described in Theorem 1, and how does it compare to other optimization approaches for large window sizes?
- Basis in paper: The paper mentions that the problem reduces to finding the minimum norm point in a convex polytope and references Wolfe's method, but does not analyze computational complexity
- Why unresolved: The paper does not provide complexity analysis or benchmarks comparing the projection method to other approaches like interior point methods or gradient descent
- What evidence would resolve it: A detailed complexity analysis of the projection method, along with empirical runtime comparisons across different optimization techniques for various window sizes

## Limitations
- The method assumes stationary input signals with symmetric autocorrelation properties
- The tapering constraint may not be optimal for signals with periodic or repeating patterns
- The computational complexity of the projection method for large window sizes is not analyzed
