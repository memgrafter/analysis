# Adversarial Multi-task Learning for End-to-end Metaphor Detection

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID**: 2305.16638
- **Source URL**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.16638
- **Reference count**: 21
- **Primary result**: AdMul achieves 79.98 average F1 score, outperforming previous state-of-the-art by 2.33 points

## Executive Summary
This paper addresses the challenge of limited training data in metaphor detection by proposing a novel Adversarial Multi-task Learning (AdMul) framework. The approach transfers knowledge from a related task, basic sense discrimination (BSD), to metaphor detection by leveraging abundant word sense disambiguation data. Through adversarial training, the model aligns feature distributions between the two tasks in a shared representation space, enabling learning of task-invariant features. The framework also exploits multi-mode structures for fine-grained alignment, resulting in significant performance improvements across four public datasets.

## Method Summary
The AdMul framework introduces a multi-task learning approach where metaphor detection is trained alongside a constructed basic sense discrimination task. The BSD task is derived from word sense disambiguation data, providing abundant training examples. The model employs adversarial training to align the feature distributions of both tasks in a shared representation space, forcing the model to learn task-invariant features. Additionally, the framework utilizes the multi-mode structures of both tasks to achieve fine-grained alignment, allowing the model to capture nuanced differences between literal and metaphorical language while leveraging shared linguistic patterns.

## Key Results
- AdMul achieves 79.98 average F1 score across four public metaphor detection datasets
- Performance improves by 2.33 points over previous state-of-the-art methods
- Zero-shot transfer results surpass fine-tuned baseline models
- Framework demonstrates effective knowledge transfer from BSD to metaphor detection

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The success of AdMul stems from its ability to transfer knowledge from the abundant BSD task to the data-scarce metaphor detection task. By aligning feature distributions through adversarial training, the model learns representations that are invariant to task-specific variations while preserving semantic distinctions. The multi-mode structure exploitation allows the model to capture fine-grained differences between literal and metaphorical expressions, leveraging shared linguistic patterns while distinguishing task-specific features.

## Limitations
- Semantic alignment between BSD and metaphor detection remains questionable without thorough validation
- Adversarial training mechanism lacks detailed analysis of feature distribution alignment
- Evaluation scope limited to four public datasets without domain generalization testing
- Zero-shot transfer results presented without comparison to alternative transfer learning approaches

## Confidence

**High confidence**: Reported F1 scores and performance improvements over baselines are verifiable from experimental results.

**Medium confidence**: Effectiveness of the multi-task learning framework itself, due to insufficient ablation studies isolating individual component contributions.

**Low confidence**: Benefits of adversarial training for feature alignment, due to inadequate analysis of the adversarial component's actual impact.

## Next Checks
