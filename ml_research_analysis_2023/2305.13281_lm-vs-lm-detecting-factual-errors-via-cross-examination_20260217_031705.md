# LM vs LM: Detecting Factual Errors via Cross Examination
## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2305.13281
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.13281
- Reference count: 40
- Primary result: LMVLM detects factual errors by cross-examining LMs, achieving >70% recall with >80% precision

## Executive Summary
This paper introduces LMVLM, a novel approach for detecting factual errors in language model generation through cross-examination between two LMs. The method prompts one LM to question another about a claim, aiming to expose inconsistencies that indicate factual errors. Experiments demonstrate that this approach significantly outperforms baseline methods across multiple benchmarks and LM variants including ChatGPT, GPT-3, and LLAMA, with over 70% of incorrect claims detected at >80% precision.

## Method Summary
LMVLM employs a multi-turn dialogue where one LM acts as examiner questioning another as examinee about a factual claim. The examiner uses various reasoning strategies to probe the examinee's knowledge, with responses analyzed for inconsistencies or uncertainty that suggest factual errors. The approach leverages the inherent capabilities of modern LMs to engage in coherent dialogue while applying targeted questioning strategies designed to reveal knowledge gaps or contradictions.

## Key Results
- Achieves over 70% recall in detecting incorrect factual claims
- Maintains precision above 80% across multiple benchmarks
- Outperforms baseline methods significantly when tested with ChatGPT, GPT-3, and LLAMA models

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The cross-examination approach works by exploiting the conversational nature of modern LMs and their ability to reason about knowledge consistency. When one LM questions another, inconsistencies or evasions in responses often reveal uncertainty about factual accuracy. The multi-turn dialogue allows for probing depth and context that single-pass evaluation cannot achieve, making it particularly effective at uncovering subtle errors that might pass undetected in standard fact-checking approaches.

## Foundational Learning
- **Cross-examination dialogue**: Two LMs engage in structured conversation where one questions another's knowledge claims. Why needed: Creates interactive environment for knowledge verification. Quick check: Can the examiner maintain coherent follow-up questions?
- **Reasoning strategy prompt engineering**: Crafting prompts that guide the examiner to use specific questioning approaches. Why needed: Directs the dialogue toward effective error detection. Quick check: Do different strategies yield different detection rates?
- **Consistency analysis**: Evaluating responses for logical contradictions or uncertainty indicators. Why needed: Identifies factual errors through internal contradictions. Quick check: Can the system distinguish between genuine uncertainty and deliberate evasion?

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component map**: Examiner LM -> Dialogue Manager -> Consistency Analyzer -> Error Detector
**Critical path**: Claim input → Examiner prompt generation → Multi-turn dialogue → Response analysis → Error classification
**Design tradeoffs**: Multi-turn dialogue provides depth but increases computational cost; complex reasoning strategies improve detection but may reduce reproducibility
**Failure signatures**: Both LMs sharing misconceptions, examiner failing to probe deeply enough, dialogue drift from factual topic
**First experiments**: 1) Test single-turn vs multi-turn dialogue effectiveness, 2) Compare different examiner reasoning strategies, 3) Evaluate performance across claim complexity levels

## Limitations
- Scalability concerns for longer or more complex factual claims where multi-turn dialogue becomes unwieldy
- Heavy reliance on prompt engineering raises reproducibility questions across different research settings
- Evaluation focuses on error detection rather than correction or explanation, limiting practical utility

## Confidence
- LMVLM detecting "over 70% of incorrect claims with >80% precision" - **Medium** confidence
- LMs being able to "critique" other LMs - **Low** confidence

## Next Checks
1. **Generalization test**: Apply LMVLM to claims from domains not represented in the training data, particularly scientific or technical claims requiring specialized knowledge
2. **Ablation study**: Systematically remove or modify examiner reasoning strategies to quantify their individual contributions and identify the minimum viable prompt structure
3. **Human evaluation**: Conduct blind tests where human fact-checkers evaluate the same claims with and without LMVLM assistance to measure practical utility
