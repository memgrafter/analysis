# Parallel development of social preferences in fish and machines

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2305.11137
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.11137
- Reference count: 1
- Key outcome: Social preferences emerge in artificial fish trained with curiosity-driven learning and early in-group exposure, mirroring zebrafish behavior.

## Executive Summary
This paper investigates whether social preferences in animals can emerge from simple learning mechanisms by training artificial neural networks ("artificial fish") in virtual environments. Using curiosity-driven learning and reinforcement learning, the agents develop preferences for in-group members and self-segregate, similar to natural fish behavior. The findings suggest that social grouping can emerge from three ingredients: reinforcement learning, intrinsic motivation, and early social experiences with in-group members. This approach bridges high-dimensional sensory inputs and social preferences, providing a foundation for reverse engineering animal-like social behavior with image-computable models.

## Method Summary
The study uses Unity 3D to simulate virtual fish tanks where artificial fish agents are trained using deep reinforcement learning (PPO) combined with curiosity-driven learning (ICM). Agents have forward-facing cameras (64×64 pixels) and learn from raw sensory inputs to navigate and interact with fish of different colors (orange/blue groups). Training involves 1M steps with intrinsic reward only, followed by testing in 2AFC and self-segregation tasks across 1,000 trials each. The agents are raised in virtual cups with in-group members during a sensitive learning window, then tested for social preferences.

## Key Results
- Artificial fish develop preferences for in-group members over out-group members in 2AFC tasks.
- Artificial fish spontaneously self-segregate with their in-group, mirroring natural behavior.
- Social preferences emerge only when curiosity-driven learning is active during early training with in-group exposure.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: Social preferences emerge from curiosity-driven learning combined with reinforcement learning in embodied agents.
- Mechanism: Curiosity-driven learning rewards agents for unpredictable experiences, which during early social interactions means learning about in-group members. This leads to a preference for familiar groupmates. Reinforcement learning then reinforces these exploratory actions that yield high curiosity rewards.
- Core assumption: In-group members are more unpredictable than static or simple environmental features during early learning, making them natural targets for curiosity-driven exploration.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "curiosity-driven learning can drive the development of complex behaviors through self-supervised learning objectives... Curiosity promotes learning by motivating agents to seek out informative experiences."
  - [section] "we found that curiosity-driven learning motivates agents to learn about groupmates, leading to the development of collective behavior (Lee, Wood, & Wood, 2021) and social grouping."
  - [corpus] No direct corpus support found; corpus neighbors focus on IoT fish farms, electro-sensing, freshness classification, or human-machine sociology rather than curiosity-driven emergence of social grouping.
- Break condition: If in-group members become fully predictable early, curiosity-driven learning no longer motivates learning about them, halting the formation of social preferences.

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: Early social experiences within a specific group create lasting social preferences.
- Mechanism: Agents trained with only in-group members during a sensitive learning window develop a preference for those members. The limited exposure prevents dilution of preference and establishes a stable social template.
- Core assumption: There exists a sensitive learning window (like filial imprinting) during which social preferences can be established and become resistant to later change.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "These social preferences emerged when artificial fish were reared in similar visual (and social) environments as real fish."
  - [section] "curiosity-driven learning must be subject to a learning window to produce social grouping; if learning remains permanently 'active,' agents will continue to seek unpredictable experiences and will likely not develop a preference for in-group members."
  - [corpus] No direct corpus support; corpus neighbors do not discuss sensitive periods or learning windows for social preference formation.
- Break condition: If the learning window is extended indefinitely, agents may keep seeking novelty and never settle into stable social preferences.

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: Embodied interaction in realistic 3D environments is necessary for learning social preferences.
- Mechanism: Pixels-to-actions learning requires agents to process raw sensory input and act in continuous space, mirroring how real animals learn. This embodiment grounds learning in spatial and social dynamics that static models cannot capture.
- Core assumption: High-dimensional sensory inputs processed through embodied navigation are required to develop realistic social behaviors, not just abstract reward signals.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "This approach lays a foundation for reverse engineering animal-like social behavior with image-computable models, bridging the divide between high-dimensional sensory inputs and social preferences."
  - [section] "Due to its flexibility and power, Unity is an ideal testbed for AI simulation... We used 'pixels-to-actions' artificial fish that learn from raw sensory inputs and perform actions in 3D environments."
  - [corpus] Weak support; corpus neighbors discuss robotics and biohybrid systems but not specifically the role of 3D embodied learning for social preference emergence.
- Break condition: If agents are trained in abstract, low-dimensional state spaces, they may fail to develop the same emergent social preferences.

## Foundational Learning

- Concept: Deep reinforcement learning
  - Why needed here: Provides a computational framework for learning adaptive behaviors from high-dimensional sensory inputs in continuous action spaces.
  - Quick check question: Does the agent learn to map raw visual pixels to actions that maximize long-term reward in a 3D environment?

- Concept: Intrinsic motivation (curiosity-driven learning)
  - Why needed here: Drives exploration of informative experiences without external supervision, enabling agents to learn about social partners through novelty-seeking.
  - Quick check question: Does the agent assign higher intrinsic reward to interactions with in-group members when they are more unpredictable than the environment?

- Concept: Sensitive learning windows
  - Why needed here: Constrains the period during which curiosity-driven learning can shape social preferences, ensuring stable preference formation.
  - Quick check question: Does the agent develop stable in-group preferences when curiosity-driven learning is active only during early training, not throughout?

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map:
  Unity 3D environment -> Artificial fish with camera -> CNN encoder -> MLP policy -> Curiosity module (ICM) -> PPO updates

- Critical path:
  1. Spawn fish in virtual cup with in-group members
  2. Fish observes raw visual input -> CNN encodes features
  3. ICM module computes surprise -> intrinsic reward
  4. PPO updates policy to maximize cumulative intrinsic reward
  5. After training, freeze weights and test in 2AFC or self-segregation tasks

- Design tradeoffs:
  - High-resolution vision (64×64) vs. computational cost
  - Intrinsic reward strength (ICM) vs. exploration vs. exploitation balance
  - Training window length vs. preference stability
  - Number of in-group vs. out-group fish in training environment

- Failure signatures:
  - No preference in 2AFC: curiosity module not driving exploration of social features
  - Preference for out-group: training data lacks sufficient in-group exposure or window is too short
  - No self-segregation: policy not learning spatial navigation or social grouping dynamics

- First 3 experiments:
  1. Vary ICM intrinsic reward strength to find threshold for social preference emergence
  2. Change group size ratio (more in-group vs. out-group members) during training to test exposure effects
  3. Extend vs. truncate training window to test sensitive period necessity

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- Question: Do the same domain-general learning mechanisms (reinforcement learning and curiosity-driven learning) apply to other social behaviors beyond grouping, such as leadership, foraging, and navigation?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper mentions these topics as future directions for extending the approach.
- Why unresolved: The current study only tested social preferences and self-segregation. Other social behaviors may require different mechanisms or environmental factors.
- What evidence would resolve it: Replicating the study design with artificial agents learning leadership, foraging, and navigation behaviors in similar environments.

### Open Question 2
- Question: What specific experiences during the training phase lead to individual differences in social preferences and personality traits among artificial agents?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper notes individual differences emerged despite identical learning algorithms and environments, speculating these arose from different experiences.
- Why unresolved: The study did not track or analyze the specific experiences of each artificial agent during training.
- What evidence would resolve it: Detailed logging of each agent's experiences during training, correlating specific experiences with personality traits.

### Open Question 3
- Question: Are there critical periods for developing social preferences similar to the sensitive periods observed in natural filial imprinting?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper speculates about learning windows in nature and their importance for social development.
- Why unresolved: The current study used a fixed training duration for all agents without exploring the effects of different learning window durations.
- What evidence would resolve it: Experiments varying the duration and timing of training periods to identify optimal learning windows for social preference development.

## Limitations
- The findings are based on a single simulation framework and lack real-world biological validation.
- The necessity of embodied 3D learning for social preference emergence is not empirically tested against non-embodied alternatives.
- The existence and boundaries of sensitive learning windows are asserted but not systematically explored.

## Confidence
- Curiosity-driven learning mechanism: Medium
- Sensitive learning window: Low
- Embodied 3D learning necessity: Low

## Next Checks
1. **Ablation of Embodied Learning**: Replicate the core results in a non-embodied, abstract state-space version of the task to test whether 3D embodiment is truly necessary for social preference emergence.

2. **Learning Window Robustness**: Systemmatically vary the duration and onset of curiosity-driven learning to empirically confirm the existence and boundaries of a sensitive period for social preference formation.

3. **Cross-Species Transfer**: Test whether the same training protocol produces similar social preferences in artificial agents modeled after different species (e.g., fruit flies, mice) to assess the generality of the mechanism beyond fish.
