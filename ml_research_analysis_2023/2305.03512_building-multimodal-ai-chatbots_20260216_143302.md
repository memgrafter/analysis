# Building Multimodal AI Chatbots

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2305.03512
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.03512
- Reference count: 0
- This work develops a multimodal chatbot that understands both images and text to carry out engaging conversations with humans.

## Executive Summary
This paper presents a multimodal chatbot system that can understand and respond to both text and images in open-domain conversations. The system uses a two-stage approach: first retrieving relevant images from a database given the dialogue history, then generating responses conditioned on both the dialogue and the retrieved image. The authors develop and evaluate this system on PhotoChat, an open-domain dialogue dataset containing shared photos, achieving promising results in both automatic and human evaluations.

## Method Summary
The multimodal chatbot consists of two transformer-based models: an image retriever that selects relevant photos from a database using a dual-encoder architecture (ViT for images, BERT for text) with contrastive loss, and a response generator that produces replies using a multimodal encoder-decoder architecture (ViT encoder + GPT-2/DialoGPT decoder) with cross-attention. The models are trained on PhotoChat dataset with automatic metrics including Recall@1/5/10, MRR for retrieval, and perplexity, BLEU, and diversity scores for generation.

## Key Results
- Image retriever achieves Recall@1/5/10 of 0.1/0.3/0.4 and MRR of 0.2 when ranking 1,000 images
- Response generator attains lower perplexity (16.9), higher BLEU scores (0.13/0.03), and higher diversity than baseline
- Complete multimodal chatbot system achieves higher image-groundedness (4.3) and engagingness (4.3) in human evaluation

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- **Claim**: Dual-encoder architecture with ViT and BERT improves image-text retrieval performance over single-modality baselines.
- **Mechanism**: ViT encodes images into patch-level embeddings, BERT encodes dialogue history into contextual token embeddings; cosine similarity in joint space ranks images. Contrastive loss pushes matched pairs closer and mismatched pairs apart.
- **Core assumption**: Joint image-text representation space can be learned such that similarity correlates with relevance.
- **Evidence anchors**:
  - [abstract]: "The proposed image retriever outperforms existing baselines VSE++ and SCAN with Recall@1/5/10 of 0.1/0.3/0.4 and MRR of 0.2"
  - [section]: "the four models surpass two baseline models VSE++ and SCAN in Recall@5/10 with maximum difference of +0.06/0.09 and +0.04/0.06"
  - [corpus]: Weak. Corpus shows related works focus on knowledge-grounded or task-oriented retrieval, not image-grounded dialogue. No direct comparison to ViT+BERT retrieval.
- **Break condition**: If image-text alignment is poor (e.g., diverse dialogue topics unrelated to images), contrastive loss fails to create meaningful representations.

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim**: Multimodal response generator achieves lower perplexity and higher BLEU scores than unimodal generators.
- **Mechanism**: ViT encoder produces image embeddings, GPT-2/DialoGPT decoder attends to both text and image via cross-attention; this joint conditioning yields better next-token predictions.
- **Core assumption**: Image information reduces uncertainty in token prediction compared to text-only conditioning.
- **Evidence anchors**:
  - [abstract]: "The proposed response generator also surpasses the baseline Divter with PPL of 16.9, BLEU-1/2 of 0.13/0.03"
  - [section]: "the four multimodal response generators have much lower PPL than the two unimodal response generators by an average margin of -10.8"
  - [corpus]: Weak. Corpus contains multimodal retrieval papers but not multimodal response generation with image-text fusion in dialogue.
- **Break condition**: If image embeddings are noisy or irrelevant, cross-attention adds confusion, increasing perplexity.

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim**: Human evaluation shows multimodal chatbot achieves higher image-groundedness and engagingness than variants without image understanding.
- **Mechanism**: Image-groundedness improves because response generator conditioned on retrieved image avoids contradicting image content; engagingness improves because shared images increase user interest.
- **Core assumption**: Users perceive consistency with images as a positive quality signal.
- **Evidence anchors**:
  - [abstract]: "the complete multimodal chatbot system receives higher image-groundedness of 4.3 and engagingness of 4.3"
  - [section]: "ViT+BERT+ViT+DialoGPT achieves the highest engagingness of 4.3, with margins of +0.1 and +0.6"
  - [corpus]: Weak. Corpus focuses on retrieval and task-oriented dialogue; no human evaluation data for image-groundedness in multimodal dialogue.
- **Break condition**: If images are not well-matched or user preferences for image sharing vary, perceived benefits diminish.

## Foundational Learning

- **Concept**: Transformer attention mechanism
  - **Why needed here**: ViT uses self-attention over image patches; BERT uses bidirectional self-attention over tokens; GPT-2 uses masked self-attention plus cross-attention to image embeddings.
  - **Quick check question**: In ViT, what does the [class] token embedding represent after the final block?
- **Concept**: Contrastive learning
  - **Why needed here**: Image retriever learns via pulling matched image-dialogue pairs together and pushing mismatched pairs apart in embedding space.
  - **Quick check question**: What is the loss function used to enforce similarity between positive image-text pairs?
- **Concept**: Cross-entropy for sequence generation
  - **Why needed here**: Response generator maximizes probability of correct next token given dialogue history and image; perplexity is exponential of average cross-entropy.
  - **Quick check question**: How is perplexity computed from cross-entropy loss over a validation set?

## Architecture Onboarding

- **Component map**: User message → image retriever → response generator → reply+image → UI
- **Critical path**: User message → image retriever → response generator → reply+image → UI
- **Design tradeoffs**:
  - Image database fixed vs. dynamic retrieval: fixed enables precomputed embeddings but limits coverage
  - Single vs. multiple image inputs: single simplifies model but ignores multi-image dialogue history
  - Greedy vs. stochastic sampling: greedy deterministic for eval, nucleus sampling for diversity in deployment
- **Failure signatures**:
  - High perplexity but low BLEU: model predicts reasonable tokens but not matching gold
  - Low image-groundedness: response generator ignores or contradicts retrieved image
  - Slow response time: image encoding done at inference instead of precomputed
- **First 3 experiments**:
  1. Train ViT-base+BERT-base retriever on small subset, evaluate Recall@1/5, check embeddings manually
  2. Train GPT2-medium generator on text-only subset, evaluate PPL/BLEU on validation
  3. Combine best retriever and generator, deploy to UI, collect a few human eval sessions

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- Question: How can we develop a more effective out-of-distribution (OOD) detection mechanism for image retrieval in multimodal chatbots?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper mentions that the current threshold-based approach for deciding when to retrieve images is vague by nature and may be sensitive to human evaluation results. It also notes that a previous work on photo-sharing intent classification achieved only 58.9 F1 score.
- Why unresolved: The paper highlights the need for a more sophisticated OOD detection algorithm but does not provide a specific solution. The current threshold-based approach lacks precision and may not generalize well to diverse conversation contexts.
- What evidence would resolve it: Developing and evaluating a novel OOD detection algorithm specifically designed for multimodal dialogue systems, potentially leveraging techniques like MC dropout or other uncertainty estimation methods. Demonstrating improved human evaluation results compared to the current threshold-based approach would provide strong evidence for the effectiveness of the new method.

### Open Question 2
- Question: How can we design a multimodal response generator that can process and understand multiple images simultaneously in a conversation?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper states that the current multimodal response generator is limited to processing only one image at a time due to the design of the vision-text dual encoder. It suggests that a future work could be implementing response generators that can accept multiple image inputs while semantically distinguishing them.
- Why unresolved: The paper identifies this as a limitation but does not propose a specific solution. Handling multiple images in a conversation is a complex task that requires advanced multimodal understanding and context tracking.
- What evidence would resolve it: Proposing and evaluating an architecture for a multimodal response generator that can effectively process and reason about multiple images in a conversation. Demonstrating improved performance on tasks that require understanding multiple images, such as answering questions about specific images or generating coherent responses that refer to multiple images, would provide evidence for the effectiveness of the new approach.

### Open Question 3
- Question: How can we enable users to share images with the chatbot and incorporate them into the dialogue context?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper mentions that the current web interface does not allow users to send photos and that PhotoChat only contains dialogues where one speaker shares images. It suggests that a future work would be building a chatbot system that understands messages and photos from an undefined number of speakers.
- Why unresolved: The paper acknowledges this as a limitation but does not propose a specific solution. Enabling user image sharing would require modifications to the data collection process, model architecture, and evaluation setup.
- What evidence would resolve it: Collecting a new dataset that includes user image sharing and developing a chatbot system that can handle user-provided images. Demonstrating the ability to understand and respond to user-shared images, as well as evaluating the system's performance on tasks that require image understanding, would provide evidence for the effectiveness of the new approach.

## Limitations

- Image retrieval performance is relatively low with Recall@1=0.1, meaning 90% of relevant images are not retrieved
- System architecture is limited to single-image retrieval rather than considering multi-image dialogue history
- Exact preprocessing steps and implementation details for threshold-based image selection are not fully specified

## Confidence

**High confidence**: The core mechanism of using dual encoders (ViT+BERT) for image-text retrieval and cross-attention models for multimodal response generation is technically sound and well-established in the literature.

**Medium confidence**: The human evaluation results showing higher image-groundedness and engagingness are promising but based on a relatively small sample (12 evaluators × 150 sessions).

**Low confidence**: The exact preprocessing steps for handling data quality issues and the specific implementation details for the threshold-based image selection mechanism are not fully specified, making exact reproduction challenging.

## Next Checks

1. **Ablation study on image retrieval quality**: Systematically test the impact of varying image retrieval accuracy (e.g., using oracle retrieval vs. learned retriever) on response quality to quantify how much downstream performance depends on retrieval performance.

2. **Cross-dataset generalization test**: Evaluate the trained models on a held-out test set from a different domain or a different portion of the Open Images Dataset to assess whether the learned representations generalize beyond the training distribution.

3. **User study with longer dialogues**: Conduct a new human evaluation with extended conversation lengths (10+ turns) to assess whether the single-image retrieval approach maintains coherence and engagingness in sustained interactions, or whether context degradation becomes apparent.
