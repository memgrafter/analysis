# Japanese Lexical Complexity for Non-Native Readers: A New Dataset

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2306.17399

- Authors: 
- Reference count: 17
- Key outcome: First dataset for Japanese lexical complexity prediction with L1-specific annotations for Chinese/Korean and other readers

## Executive Summary
This paper introduces JaLeCoN, the first dataset for Japanese lexical complexity prediction (LCP) specifically designed for non-native readers. The dataset provides separate complexity scores based on the annotators' first language backgrounds (Chinese/Korean vs. others), addressing the need for personalized complexity assessment in language learning applications. The authors demonstrate the effectiveness of a BERT-based system for Japanese LCP, showing that considering the readers' L1 background improves model performance and inter-annotator agreement.

## Method Summary
The JaLeCoN dataset was created by annotating single words and multi-word expressions from News and Government genres in Japanese. Annotators from Chinese/Korean and other language backgrounds rated word complexity on a 5-point scale. A BERT-based model was fine-tuned using Japanese pre-trained weights from Tohoku University, with input sequences constructed from word context and isolated words. The model was evaluated using cross-validation with R2 and MAE metrics, comparing performance across L1 groups.

## Key Results
- JaLeCoN is the first Japanese LCP dataset with L1-specific annotations
- BERT-based model achieves R2 of 0.4351 for CK and 0.6142 for non-CK annotators
- IAA improves from 0.19 to 0.32 (CK) and 0.31 (non-CK) when separating annotators by L1 background
- MWEs show higher mean complexity than single words for both L1 groups

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map: Data preprocessing -> Annotation pipeline -> Model architecture -> Evaluation
- Critical path: 1. Segment input text into words (SUWs and MWEs) -> 2. Format input for BERT with word context and isolated word -> 3. Fine-tune BERT on complexity scores -> 4. Evaluate model performance using cross-validation
- Design tradeoffs:
  - Dense vs. sparse annotation: Dense annotation captures more words but leads to class imbalance
  - L1 group separation: Improves agreement but reduces annotator pool size
  - BERT input format: Including both context and isolated word may improve performance but increases input complexity
- Failure signatures:
  - High MAE in higher complexity tiers suggests insufficient training examples
  - Low R2 values indicate poor model generalization or data quality issues
  - High variance in complexity scores across annotators suggests annotation inconsistency
- First 3 experiments:
  1. Compare BERT performance with and without L1 group separation to quantify the impact of personalized complexity prediction.
  2. Evaluate different input formats for BERT (context-only vs. context+isolated word) to determine optimal feature representation.
  3. Test the effect of different annotation density settings (dense vs. sparse) on model performance and data distribution.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- Question: Does incorporating the L1 background of annotators improve the predictive performance of lexical complexity models for Japanese?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper presents JaLeCoN, a dataset that provides separate complexity scores based on Chinese/Korean (CK) and non-Chinese/Korean (non-CK) annotators' L1 backgrounds, and demonstrates the effectiveness of a BERT-based system for Japanese LCP with consideration of L1 background.
- Why unresolved: The paper shows the effectiveness of the BERT-based system but does not explicitly compare the performance of models trained with and without L1 background consideration.
- What evidence would resolve it: Conduct experiments comparing the performance of LCP models trained with and without L1 background information and evaluate the impact on predictive accuracy.

### Open Question 2
- Question: How do the complexity scores of multi-word expressions (MWEs) differ from those of single words in Japanese lexical complexity prediction?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper mentions that MWEs have higher mean complexity than single words for both L1 groups and are more frequent in the Government genre.
- Why unresolved: The paper does not provide a detailed analysis of the complexity scores of MWEs compared to single words or investigate the factors contributing to the higher complexity of MWEs.
- What evidence would resolve it: Conduct a comprehensive analysis comparing the complexity scores of MWEs and single words, considering factors such as word origin, Chinese character presence, and annotator L1 background.

### Open Question 3
- Question: What is the impact of the sparse annotation setting on the distribution of complexity scores in Japanese lexical complexity prediction?
- Basis in paper: [inferred] The paper mentions that the dense annotation setting results in an uneven distribution of complexity scores, with easy words greatly outnumbering difficult words.
- Why unresolved: The paper does not explore the potential benefits of using a sparse annotation setting to achieve a more balanced distribution of complexity scores across different difficulty levels.
- What evidence would resolve it: Create a new LCP dataset using sparse annotation, where target words are selected based on frequency bands to achieve a balanced distribution of complexity scores, and compare the performance of LCP models trained on this dataset with those trained on JaLeCoN.

## Limitations

- Moderate inter-annotator agreement (0.32 for CK, 0.31 for non-CK) suggests inherent subjectivity in complexity judgments
- Dataset limited to News and Government genres, restricting generalizability to other text types
- Small dataset size (1,587 words) may be insufficient for robust model training, especially given class imbalance from dense annotation

## Confidence

High confidence in dataset creation methodology and observation that L1 background affects complexity perception. Medium confidence in BERT model effectiveness and L1-specific annotation impact on model performance.
