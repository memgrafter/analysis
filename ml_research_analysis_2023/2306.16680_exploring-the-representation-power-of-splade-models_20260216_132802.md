# Exploring the Representation Power of SPLADE Models

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID**: 2306.16680
- **Source URL**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.16680
- **Reference count**: 32
- **Key outcome**: SPLADEv2 can effectively encode useful ranking signals even when vocabulary is constrained to uninformative terms like stopwords or random words

## Executive Summary
This paper investigates the representation power of SPLADE models by training them with controlled vocabularies consisting of stopwords, random words, low-frequency terms, and latent tokens. The authors hypothesize that SPLADE may encode additional ranking signals into common postings lists even when using vocabulary terms not traditionally useful for ranking. Through systematic experiments on the MS MARCO passage corpus, they demonstrate that SPLADEv2 can maintain strong retrieval performance even with highly constrained vocabularies, suggesting it functions more like a dense retriever where lexical matching serves to activate learned representations for relevance. The study provides valuable insights into SPLADE's capabilities and suggests new perspectives on how learned sparse retrieval models operate.

## Method Summary
The study retrains SPLADEv2 with controlled vocabularies on the MS MARCO-v1 passage corpus (8.8M passages) and evaluates performance on the MS MARCO dev set (6,980 queries) and TREC DL2019/2020 collections. The authors create vocabularies consisting of stopwords, random tokens, low-frequency tokens, and latent tokens, then train SPLADEv2 models with each vocabulary type. They compare these models against BM25 baselines, dense retrievers, and the original SPLADEv2 using metrics including Recall@1000, RR@10, and NDCG@10. The experiments systematically explore how different vocabulary compositions and sizes affect retrieval effectiveness.

## Key Results
- SPLADEv2 maintains strong retrieval performance even when vocabulary is limited to 150 stopwords, outperforming BM25 baseline on dev set with RR@10
- Increasing vocabulary size from 150 to 768 tokens results in large and often significant improvements in effectiveness
- SPLADEv2 can encode useful ranking signals using latent tokens that don't represent real terms, suggesting semantic information is captured beyond lexical matching

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: SPLADEv2 can encode useful ranking signals even with vocabularies of uninformative terms like stopwords
- Mechanism: Uses BERT MLM head to predict term importance scores, with sparsity regularization learning to assign high scores to tokens distinguishing relevant from non-relevant documents
- Core assumption: Model can learn to use any tokens as basis for encoding semantic relevance signals
- Evidence: Even with 150 stopwords, SPLADEv2 outperforms BM25 baseline on dev set with RR@10
- Break condition: Vocabulary too small or insufficient training data

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: Increasing vocabulary size improves effectiveness regardless of specific terms
- Mechanism: Larger vocabulary provides more space to model term interactions and semantic relationships
- Core assumption: Model's ability to learn effective representations scales with vocabulary size
- Evidence: Large improvements observed when increasing from 150 to 768 tokens across multiple experiments
- Break condition: Overfitting or additional tokens don't provide meaningful information

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: SPLADEv2 functions as a dense retriever with lexical matching activating learned representations
- Mechanism: Maps documents/queries into high-dimensional space capturing semantic similarity, not relying solely on exact lexical matching
- Core assumption: Model encodes semantic information in term importance scores for relevance ranking
- Evidence: Effectiveness maintained with non-semantic vocabularies suggests learned semantic encoding
- Break condition: Significant performance degradation with constrained non-semantic vocabularies

## Architecture Onboarding

- **Component map**: Input text -> BERT backbone -> MLM head -> Term importance scores -> Sparsity regularization -> Inverted index
- **Critical path**: 1) Text tokenized through BERT for contextual embeddings 2) Embeddings mapped to term importance scores via MLM head 3) Scores combined with sparsity regularization 4) Sparse representation stored in inverted index 5) Queries represented similarly for scoring
- **Design tradeoffs**: Sparsity vs effectiveness (efficiency vs semantic information), vocabulary size (nuance vs computational cost), training data diversity (representation quality)
- **Failure signatures**: Poor retrieval performance (training issues/vocabulary selection), overly dense representations (ineffective sparsity regularization), inconsistent results across collections (overfitting/sensitivity)
- **First 3 experiments**: 1) Compare SPLADEv2 with/without sparsity regularization 2) Vary vocabulary size and composition 3) Compare SPLADEv2 to traditional sparse and dense models

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- Question: How does vocabulary size affect SPLADE models' representation power and effectiveness?
- Basis: Paper explores only 150 and 768 token sizes, leaving relationship unclear
- Why unresolved: Limited exploration of size range
- Resolution: Systematic experiments varying vocabulary sizes across wider range with effectiveness measurements

### Open Question 2
- Question: Can SPLADE effectively encode semantic information using arbitrary/latent tokens, or are there limits?
- Basis: Shows effectiveness with non-semantic vocabularies but extent unexplored
- Why unresolved: Experiments limited to specific non-semantic types
- Resolution: Tests with various non-semantic vocabularies including extreme cases

### Open Question 3
- Question: How does SPLADE with constrained vocabularies compare to dense retrievers?
- Basis: Limited comparison to single dense retriever suggests performance gaps
- Why unresolved: Single comparison doesn't explain contributing factors
- Resolution: Systematic comparisons across multiple dense retrievers analyzing strengths/weaknesses

## Limitations
- Study relies heavily on MS MARCO dataset without external validation for generalizability
- Lack of direct corpus evidence supporting proposed mechanisms, all rely on weak corpus signals
- No ablation studies examining specific contributions of vocabulary composition versus size

## Confidence

**High Confidence**: SPLADEv2 performance improvements with increased vocabulary size (150→768 tokens), supported by quantitative measurements and statistical significance testing across multiple experiments.

**Medium Confidence**: SPLADE can be viewed as dense retriever with lexical matching activating learned representations, though limited direct corpus evidence and need for cross-dataset validation reduce confidence.

**Low Confidence**: Specific mechanisms by which SPLADE encodes semantic signals using uninformative tokens, as evidence is largely indirect without rigorous ablation studies.

## Next Checks

1. **Cross-dataset Validation**: Replicate controlled vocabulary experiments across TREC Robust, GOV2, and domain-specific collections to assess generalizability beyond MS MARCO.

2. **Mechanism Dissection Study**: Conduct systematic ablation varying (a) vocabulary composition with fixed size, (b) vocabulary size with fixed composition, and (c) both simultaneously to isolate relative contributions.

3. **Semantic Content Analysis**: Implement semantic similarity analysis comparing document representations from different vocabulary types to provide direct evidence for semantic encoding with uninformative tokens.
