# When and Why Momentum Accelerates SGD:An Empirical Study

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2306.09000
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.09000
- Reference count: 40
- Primary result: Momentum accelerates SGD when effective learning rates are large by preventing abrupt sharpening of the directional Hessian

## Executive Summary
This paper investigates the conditions under which momentum accelerates stochastic gradient descent (SGD) in deep learning. The authors introduce the concept of effective learning rates, which unify the influence of momentum coefficient and batch size over the learning rate. Through extensive experiments, they demonstrate that momentum acceleration occurs when effective learning rates exceed a certain threshold. The key finding is that momentum prevents abrupt sharpening—a sudden jump in the directional Hessian along the update direction—which causes oscillations and slower convergence in standard SGD.

## Method Summary
The authors conduct experiments using CIFAR10 dataset with VGG13-BN network, comparing SGD and SGD with momentum (SGDM) across different batch sizes (256, 512, 1024) and momentum coefficients (0, 0.5, 0.9). They calculate effective learning rates using the formula ηef = η/(1-µ) * k/b and analyze training dynamics by computing directional Hessian along update directions. The experiments focus on observing the "align-and-deviate" pattern between SGD and SGDM, where the two optimizers perform similarly at low effective learning rates but diverge as rates increase.

## Key Results
- Momentum accelerates SGD only when effective learning rates exceed a threshold (around 0.05-0.1)
- The align-and-deviate pattern consistently appears across different batch sizes and momentum coefficients
- Abrupt sharpening of directional Hessian occurs at the same moment SGD begins to deviate from SGDM and converge slower
- Reducing batch size has a similar effect to adding momentum by preventing abrupt sharpening

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: Momentum accelerates SGD when effective learning rates are large
- Mechanism: When effective learning rates exceed a threshold, momentum prevents abrupt sharpening of the directional Hessian, maintaining stable convergence
- Core assumption: Abrupt sharpening occurs when the directional Hessian along the update direction suddenly increases, causing oscillations and slower convergence
- Evidence anchors: [abstract] "when ηef surpasses a certain threshold, SGDM begins to perform better than SGD"; [section] consistent align-and-deviate pattern observation

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: Reducing batch size has similar effect to adding momentum through preventing abrupt sharpening
- Mechanism: Smaller batch sizes introduce stochastic noise that prevents the directional Hessian from reaching the critical threshold where abrupt sharpening occurs
- Core assumption: Both momentum and stochastic noise can prevent alignment of update directions with maximum eigenvalue eigenspace of Hessian
- Evidence anchors: [abstract] "momentum acceleration is closely related to 'abrupt sharpening'"; [section] reducing batch size has similar effect as adding momentum

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: The align-and-deviate pattern emerges from the relationship between directional Hessian magnitude and convergence stability
- Mechanism: Before deviation, directional Hessian remains small, keeping SGD and SGDM aligned; after deviation, abrupt sharpening causes oscillations in GD that GDM avoids through momentum
- Core assumption: Directional Hessian staying around 0 indicates stable convergence, while sudden jumps indicate instability
- Evidence anchors: [abstract] "Momentum defers abrupt sharpening, thereby accelerating training process"; [section] misalignment between SGD and SGDM happens at same moment as abrupt sharpening

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- Question: What specific mechanisms within momentum cause the delay of abrupt sharpening, and how do they quantitatively relate to the momentum coefficient?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper states that momentum defers abrupt sharpening and thus accelerates training, but does not detail the specific mechanisms or provide quantitative relationships.
- Why unresolved: The paper establishes a correlation between momentum and the delay of abrupt sharpening but lacks a deep mechanistic explanation or quantitative analysis of how the momentum coefficient affects this delay.
- What evidence would resolve it: Experimental data showing the relationship between different momentum coefficients and the timing of abrupt sharpening, along with a theoretical model explaining the underlying mechanisms.

### Open Question 2
- Question: How does the phenomenon of abrupt sharpening interact with other known phenomena in optimization, such as the sharpness of the loss landscape or the concept of the Edge of Stability?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper connects abrupt sharpening to the Edge of Stability but does not explore interactions with other optimization phenomena.
- Why unresolved: While the paper establishes a link between abrupt sharpening and Edge of Stability, it does not explore how abrupt sharpening interacts with other aspects of the loss landscape or optimization dynamics.
- What evidence would resolve it: Comprehensive studies comparing abrupt sharpening with other phenomena like loss landscape sharpness, incorporating a variety of optimization algorithms and network architectures.

### Open Question 3
- Question: Does the align-and-deviate pattern observed in SGD and SGDM extend to other optimization algorithms, such as Adam or RMSprop, and under what conditions?
- Basis in paper: [inferred] The paper focuses on SGD and SGDM and does not investigate other optimizers, leaving open the question of whether similar patterns exist elsewhere.
- Why unresolved: The study is limited to SGD and SGDM, and does not provide insights into the behavior of other popular optimizers like Adam or RMSprop.
- What evidence would resolve it: Empirical studies applying the same framework to other optimizers, identifying whether and how the align-and-deviate pattern manifests across different optimization algorithms.

## Limitations
- Findings are limited to specific architectures (VGG13-BN) and datasets (CIFAR10), limiting generalizability
- The connection between momentum and abrupt sharpening is correlational rather than definitively causal
- Hessian estimation accuracy may introduce noise in measurements of directional Hessian behavior

## Confidence
- Effective learning rate framework and align-and-deviate pattern: High confidence
- Connection to abrupt sharpening mechanism: Medium confidence
- Generalizability to other architectures and datasets: Medium confidence
