# Performance-optimized deep neural networks are evolving into worse models of inferotemporal visual cortex

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2306.03779
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.03779
- Reference count: 40
- Key outcome: ImageNet-trained DNNs become progressively worse at predicting IT cortex responses as their ImageNet accuracy increases

## Executive Summary
This paper investigates the surprising inverse relationship between deep neural network (DNN) accuracy on ImageNet and their ability to predict neural responses in primate inferotemporal (IT) cortex. The authors find that as DNNs improve on ImageNet, they become progressively worse at predicting IT responses across three independent experiments and various architectures including CNNs, vision transformers, and self-supervised models. They propose that this trade-off occurs because ImageNet-trained DNNs learn different visual features than those encoded by IT cortex. To address this issue, they introduce the neural harmonizer, a training routine that aligns DNN representations with human perception. Harmonized DNNs significantly improve their ability to predict IT responses while maintaining ImageNet accuracy, suggesting that incorporating human behavioral data is crucial for building more accurate models of the visual cortex.

## Method Summary
The study evaluates 135 different DNNs trained on ImageNet for their ability to predict neural responses in primate IT cortex using Brain-Score, which measures prediction accuracy via partial least squares regression. The authors analyze spatially-resolved neural recordings from two monkeys viewing natural images to map feature selectivity. They introduce the neural harmonizer, which adds an additional loss term during training to align DNN gradients with human feature importance maps derived from ClickMe experiments. Feature extraction and visualization is performed using CRAFT (Concept Recursive Activation Factorization). The methodology involves comparing prediction accuracy and feature explanations between baseline and harmonized models.

## Key Results
- DNNs trained on ImageNet show progressively worse prediction of IT cortex responses as their ImageNet accuracy increases
- The accuracy-prediction trade-off persists across CNNs, vision transformers, and self-supervised models
- The neural harmonizer significantly improves DNN prediction of IT responses while maintaining ImageNet accuracy
- Spatially-resolved neural recordings reveal that ImageNet-trained DNNs rely on different visual features than those encoded by IT cortex

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: ImageNet-trained DNNs improve object recognition accuracy but diverge from the feature representations used by IT cortex.
- Mechanism: As DNNs scale and optimize for ImageNet performance, they rely increasingly on features that differ from those encoded by primate IT cortex, leading to reduced neural prediction accuracy.
- Core assumption: The visual features that optimize for ImageNet accuracy are not aligned with the biological features that drive IT neuron responses.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract]: "DNNs have become progressively worse models of IT as their accuracy has increased on ImageNet."
  - [section]: "DNNs trained on ImageNet learn to rely on different visual features than those encoded by IT and that this problem worsens as their accuracy increases."
- Break condition: If the ImageNet dataset or training objective is fundamentally restructured to align with biological vision constraints, the divergence may be mitigated.

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: The neural harmonizer aligns DNN feature representations with human perception, improving their ability to predict IT responses.
- Mechanism: By incorporating human feature importance maps into the training process, the neural harmonizer forces DNNs to prioritize visual features that are more consistent with human perception, thereby improving their alignment with IT neuron responses.
- Core assumption: Human perception and IT neuron responses are sufficiently aligned that improving DNN alignment with human perception will also improve alignment with IT responses.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract]: "We successfully resolved this issue with the neural harmonizer... Harmonized DNNs significantly improve their ability to predict IT responses while maintaining ImageNet accuracy."
  - [section]: "Training DNNs with the neural harmonizer on ImageNet involves an additional loss to standard cross-entropy for object recognition... This extra loss forces a model's gradients to appear as similar as possible to feature importance maps derived from human behavioral experiments."
- Break condition: If human perception and IT neuron responses diverge significantly on certain visual features, the neural harmonizer may not fully resolve the trade-off.

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: Spatially-resolved neural recordings reveal the mismatch between DNN and IT feature selectivity.
- Mechanism: By mapping neural responses across different image regions, the study identifies which features drive IT neuron activity, allowing comparison with DNN feature selectivity to reveal misalignment.
- Core assumption: Spatial mapping of neural activity provides a more detailed and accurate representation of feature selectivity than aggregate neural responses.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [section]: "The recordings also provided a coarse estimate of which image features drove neuronal activity... These neuronal activity maps reveal that DNNs trained on ImageNet learn to rely on different visual features than those encoded by IT."
  - [corpus]: Limited direct evidence; the mechanism is inferred from the experimental design and results.
- Break condition: If the spatial mapping technique introduces artifacts or biases, the conclusions about feature selectivity mismatch may be invalid.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- Question: Why do deep neural networks (DNNs) trained on ImageNet rely on different visual features than those encoded by the inferior temporal (IT) cortex of primates?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The authors state that DNNs trained on ImageNet learn to rely on different visual features than those encoded by IT, and this mismatch worsens as their accuracy increases.
- Why unresolved: The paper does not provide a definitive explanation for this feature mismatch. It only presents evidence that the mismatch exists and persists across various DNN architectures and training methods.
- What evidence would resolve it: Further research is needed to understand the underlying reasons for this feature mismatch. This could involve investigating the differences in the data used to train DNNs versus the visual experiences of primates, or examining the architectural differences between DNNs and the primate visual cortex.

### Open Question 2
- Question: How can the neural harmonizer be improved to better align DNNs with primate visual processing?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The authors demonstrate that the neural harmonizer significantly improves DNNs' ability to predict IT responses, but they also acknowledge that harmonized DNNs still only explain 50-60% of the variance in neuronal activity.
- Why unresolved: While the neural harmonizer shows promise, it is not yet a complete solution. The authors suggest that more work is needed to expand the ClickMe feature importance dataset and potentially develop new alignment methods.
- What evidence would resolve it: Further research could focus on expanding the ClickMe dataset to cover more images, exploring alternative methods for aligning DNNs with primate vision, or investigating the limitations of the current neural harmonizer approach.

### Open Question 3
- Question: How do the findings of this study impact the use of DNNs in understanding and modeling primate visual processing?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The authors suggest that their findings indicate a need for a revised approach to using DNNs for modeling the visual cortex, incorporating human behavioral data and potentially other biological constraints.
- Why unresolved: The paper does not provide specific guidelines or recommendations for how to revise the current approach to using DNNs in visual neuroscience research.
- What evidence would resolve it: Further research is needed to develop new training methods, data collection strategies, and evaluation metrics that better align DNNs with primate visual processing. This could involve interdisciplinary collaborations between computer scientists, neuroscientists, and cognitive psychologists.

## Limitations
- The study focuses on a limited set of DNN architectures and does not explore the full range of possible model variations or training regimes that could potentially resolve the accuracy-prediction trade-off
- The neural harmonizer relies on human feature importance maps, but it is unclear how representative these maps are of the full diversity of human perception and whether they fully capture the complexity of IT feature selectivity
- The spatial resolution of the neural recordings may not be sufficient to capture fine-grained feature selectivity, potentially limiting the accuracy of the comparisons between DNN and IT feature representations

## Confidence

High confidence in the observation of a trade-off between ImageNet accuracy and IT neural prediction accuracy, supported by multiple experiments and independent data sets
Medium confidence in the mechanism explaining the trade-off, as it relies on the assumption that ImageNet-optimized features differ from IT features, which may not hold for all DNN architectures or training regimes
Medium confidence in the effectiveness of the neural harmonizer, as its performance depends on the quality and representativeness of the human feature importance maps used for alignment
