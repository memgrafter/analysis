# Temporal Data Meets LLM -- Explainable Financial Time Series Forecasting

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2306.11025
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.11025
- Reference count: 40
- Primary result: LLM-based approach achieves up to 66.5% binary precision and 35.2% bin precision on NASDAQ-100 stock forecasting

## Executive Summary
This paper introduces a novel approach to financial time series forecasting using Large Language Models (LLMs) to address challenges of cross-sequence reasoning, multi-modal data integration, and explainability. The method combines zero-shot/few-shot inference with GPT-4 and instruction-based fine-tuning with Open LLaMA, augmented by Chain-of-Thoughts reasoning. Experiments on NASDAQ-100 stocks show that the proposed LLM approach outperforms baselines including ARMA-GARCH and gradient-boosting models, achieving up to 66.5% binary precision and 35.2% bin precision with GPT-4 few-shot and COT. The fine-tuned Open LLaMA model demonstrates reasonable performance at 64.7% binary precision. The approach also generates human-readable explanations with strong ROUGE scores, demonstrating LLMs' potential for explainable financial forecasting.

## Method Summary
The approach uses LLMs (GPT-4 and fine-tuned Open LLaMA 13B) to forecast weekly/monthly stock returns by reasoning over multimodal financial data including price time series, news, and company metadata. The method employs zero-shot/few-shot inference with carefully structured prompts, instruction-based fine-tuning, and Chain-of-Thoughts reasoning to improve forecasting performance and generate explanations. Data preprocessing involves normalizing price series, binning percentage changes, and generating company profiles and news summaries. The model predicts stock movement direction and magnitude, providing human-readable explanations for its predictions.

## Key Results
- GPT-4 few-shot with COT achieves 66.5% binary precision and 35.2% bin precision
- Open LLaMA fine-tuned model achieves 64.7% binary precision
- Chain-of-Thoughts improves performance by several percentage points
- ROUGE scores demonstrate high-quality human-readable explanations
- LLM approach outperforms ARMA-GARCH and gradient-boosting baselines

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: LLMs can effectively reason over heterogeneous multimodal financial data by leveraging their inherent knowledge and reasoning capabilities.
- Mechanism: LLMs combine numerical time series, textual news, and company metadata into a unified reasoning framework using instruction-based prompting. The Chain-of-Thoughts technique enhances this by forcing explicit step-by-step reasoning that captures cross-modal dependencies.
- Core assumption: The LLM's pre-training on diverse internet data provides sufficient domain knowledge to understand financial concepts and temporal patterns without extensive domain-specific training.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "LLMs can make a well-thought decision by reasoning over information from both textual news and price time series"
  - [section] "LLMs have demonstrated outstanding reasoning and inference capability over multi-modal data"
  - [corpus] Weak - related papers focus on multimodal time series but don't specifically address financial domain reasoning
- Break condition: The LLM lacks sufficient domain-specific knowledge for complex financial instruments or market structures not well-represented in pre-training data.

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: Fine-tuning with instruction-based examples enables publicly available LLMs to perform financial forecasting tasks effectively.
- Mechanism: By compressing historical data into structured prompts and fine-tuning on 30K+ examples, Open LLaMA learns to generate forecasts and explanations that match the performance of larger models like GPT-4.
- Core assumption: The compressed prompt format preserves essential information while being tractable for smaller models, and the fine-tuning process effectively teaches the model to follow instructions.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [section] "Open LLaMA 13B model's zero-shot inference... typically tends to replicate portions of the prompt... Therefore, it is incapable of properly handling instruction-based prompts... without undergoing a process of fine-tuning"
  - [section] "Once fine-tuned, the Open LLaMA 13B model demonstrates a much more satisfactory comprehension of the instruction"
  - [corpus] Weak - related papers mention instruction tuning but not specifically for financial time series
- Break condition: The compression process loses critical temporal relationships or the model overfits to the fine-tuning examples without generalizing to new market conditions.

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: Chain-of-Thoughts prompting consistently improves forecasting performance by enabling more thorough reasoning.
- Mechanism: Adding explicit reasoning instructions forces the model to consider multiple factors and their relationships before making a prediction, capturing insights that would be missed in direct prediction.
- Core assumption: The model's reasoning capabilities are sufficiently developed that explicit step-by-step thinking improves output quality rather than just adding noise.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [section] "the technique of Chain of Thoughts (COT) [38, 64], which has been found to enhance the effectiveness of LLMs in other research studies"
  - [section] "we find LLMs can make a well-thought decision by reasoning over information from both textual news and price time series"
  - [section] "this notably improved the performance by a few points (see Section 4.2)"
- Break condition: The reasoning steps become overly verbose without adding substantive value, or the model's reasoning process introduces bias or incorrect assumptions.

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map:
  - Yahoo Finance downloader -> normalization -> binning -> metadata extraction
  - Company profiles -> news summarization -> example selection -> prompt assembly
  - GPT-4/Open LLaMA processing -> prediction generation -> explanation extraction
  - Binary/bin precision calculation -> MSE computation -> ROUGE score evaluation
  - ARMA-GARCH, LightGBM, heuristic models

- Critical path: Data preprocessing -> prompt construction -> LLM inference -> result validation
- Design tradeoffs:
  - Model size vs. performance: GPT-4 vs. Open LLaMA (13B vs. larger)
  - Prompt length vs. information retention: Full context vs. compressed summaries
  - Granularity vs. predictability: More bins vs. stable predictions
- Failure signatures:
  - Model consistently predicts extreme bins (U5+/D5+) indicating overconfidence
  - Explanations contain hallucinations or irrelevant information
  - Performance degrades significantly on out-of-distribution market conditions
- First 3 experiments:
  1. Run zero-shot inference on a single stock with simple prompt structure to validate basic functionality
  2. Add Chain-of-Thoughts instruction and measure performance improvement
  3. Implement few-shot learning with similar stock examples and compare to zero-shot results

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- Question: How do LLM-based financial forecasting models perform on longer time horizons (e.g., quarterly or annual predictions) compared to shorter time horizons (weekly/monthly)?
- Basis in paper: [inferred] The paper focuses on weekly/monthly forecasting but mentions quarterly/yearly as potential future work.
- Why unresolved: The experiments only evaluated weekly and monthly predictions using 4-week months, leaving longer horizons unexplored.
- What evidence would resolve it: Experiments comparing LLM performance on quarterly and annual forecasts versus weekly/monthly results, with metrics like precision, MSE, and explanation quality.

### Open Question 2
- Question: What is the relative importance of news sentiment versus historical price patterns in LLM-based financial forecasting?
- Basis in paper: [inferred] The methodology combines price data with news data, but ablation studies isolating their contributions are not provided.
- Why unresolved: The paper integrates both data sources but doesn't quantify their individual contributions to model performance.
- What evidence would resolve it: Controlled experiments removing news data or price data to measure performance changes, and analysis of feature importance from the gradient-boosting baseline.

### Open Question 3
- Question: How does LLM-based forecasting performance vary across different market sectors or stock characteristics?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper mentions NASDAQ-100 stocks but doesn't analyze performance by sector or stock characteristics.
- Why unresolved: The results are aggregated across all NASDAQ-100 stocks without breaking down by sector, market cap, or volatility.
- What evidence would resolve it: Performance analysis stratified by sector (technology, healthcare, financials, etc.) and stock characteristics (market cap quartiles, historical volatility tiers).

## Limitations
- Performance metrics may have hindsight bias in example selection and limited generalizability beyond NASDAQ-100 dataset
- Reliance on GPT-4 for preprocessing creates dependency chain where errors can propagate through forecasting pipeline
- Compression of complex temporal relationships into fixed-length prompts may lead to information loss over longer forecasting horizons

## Confidence

- **High Confidence**: The fundamental premise that LLMs can process multimodal financial data and generate explanations, supported by consistent performance improvements with Chain-of-Thoughts prompting and clear evidence of superior results compared to traditional baselines.
- **Medium Confidence**: The effectiveness of instruction-based fine-tuning for achieving comparable performance with smaller models like Open LLaMA, though specific training methodology introduces some uncertainty about reproducibility.
- **Low Confidence**: The long-term reliability and robustness of the approach under diverse market conditions and across different stock exchanges or asset classes not represented in the NASDAQ-100 dataset.

## Next Checks

1. **Out-of-Distribution Market Testing**: Evaluate the model's performance on stocks from different market indices (e.g., S&P 500, FTSE 100) and during historically volatile periods (2008 financial crisis, COVID-19 market crash) to assess robustness to varying market conditions and economic cycles.

2. **Ablation Study on Prompt Components**: Systematically remove or modify individual prompt elements (news summaries, company profiles, historical examples) to quantify their contribution to final performance and identify potential redundancy or overfitting in the current prompt structure.

3. **Real-Time Deployment Simulation**: Implement a rolling window forecasting system that makes predictions on future data points without access to actual outcomes, measuring performance degradation over time and identifying any temporal drift in model accuracy that would not be captured by static historical testing.
