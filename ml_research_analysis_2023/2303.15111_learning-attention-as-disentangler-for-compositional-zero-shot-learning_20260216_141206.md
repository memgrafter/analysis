# Learning Attention as Disentangler for Compositional Zero-shot Learning

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2303.15111
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.15111
- Reference count: 40
- Primary result: ADE achieves new state-of-the-art on three CZSL benchmarks, increasing AUC by up to 3.9 and HM by up to 2.2% on Clothing16K.

## Executive Summary
This paper addresses compositional zero-shot learning (CZSL) by disentangling attribute and object concepts using cross-attention mechanisms. The key innovation is employing cross-attention between concept-sharing image pairs to learn disentangled concept embeddings, combined with an EMD-based regularization at the attention level. The method significantly outperforms previous approaches on three benchmark datasets under both closed- and open-world settings.

## Method Summary
ADE uses frozen ViT-B-16 features and three attention disentanglers: composition self-attention, attribute cross-attention, and object cross-attention. These modules project images into concept-exclusive subspaces by leveraging cross-attention between paired images sharing the same attribute or object. An EMD regularization term enforces concept exclusivity at the attention level. During inference, predictions are improved by combining attribute, object, and composition probabilities with a tunable coefficient β.

## Key Results
- Achieves new state-of-the-art on Clothing16K, UT-Zappos50K, and C-GQA datasets
- Increases AUC by up to 3.9% and HM by up to 2.2% on Clothing16K
- Demonstrates consistent improvements under both closed-world and open-world CZSL settings

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Cross-attention between concept-sharing image pairs disentangles attribute and object features into separate subspaces
- The cross-attention module uses one image as query and another sharing the same concept as key-value, producing outputs exclusive to the shared concept
- Core assumption: Vision transformers encode richer multi-head global information than CNNs, making it possible to project different concepts into distinct subspaces
- Evidence anchor: [abstract] "we propose to exploit cross-attentions as compositional disentanglers to learn disentangled concept embeddings"

### Mechanism 2
- The adapted EMD regularization enforces disentanglers to learn the concept of interest rather than the opposite concept
- EMD is computed at the attention level using [CLS]-to-patch logits as supplies and patch-to-patch logits as moving costs
- Core assumption: Attention maps contain meaningful feature similarity information that can be quantified by EMD to enforce concept exclusivity
- Evidence anchor: [abstract] "we employ a regularization at the attention level. Specifically, we adapt the earth mover's distance (EMD) as a feature similarity metric"

### Mechanism 3
- Combining attribute, object, and composition probabilities during inference improves unseen generalization
- The final prediction score is a weighted sum: p(c) + β · p(a) · p(o), where β is tuned on validation data
- Core assumption: Attribute and object predictions learned during training are accurate enough to improve composition prediction when combined multiplicatively
- Evidence anchor: [abstract] "benefiting from concept disentanglement, we improve the inference process and tune the prediction score by combining multiple concept probabilities"

  - Why needed here: The method relies on cross-attention between token embeddings to disentangle concepts; understanding multi-head self-attention is essential to follow the design
  - Quick check question: How does cross-attention differ from self-attention in terms of input roles and output semantics?

  - Why needed here: The regularization term is adapted from EMD; knowing its formulation as a minimal-cost flow problem explains why it can enforce similarity constraints at the attention level
  - Quick check question: In the adapted EMD, what do the supplies, demands, and moving costs correspond to in the cross-attention context?

  - Why needed here: The paper reports AUC, harmonic mean, and calibration-based metrics; understanding the generalized ZSL protocol is needed to interpret results
  - Quick check question: Why does the calibration term γ help overcome the bias toward seen compositions in CZSL?

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map: Paired images -> Frozen ViT-B-16 backbone -> Cross-attention disentanglers -> MLP embedders -> Word embeddings -> EMD regularization -> Loss computation

- Critical path:
  1. Encode paired images → tokens
  2. Apply three attention modules → concept-exclusive features
  3. Embed features → word embedding space
  4. Compute cross-entropy losses + EMD regularization
  5. Backpropagate to update embedders and embeddings only

- Design tradeoffs:
  - Using frozen ViT preserves rich pre-trained features but limits end-to-end adaptation
  - Cross-attention increases computation vs. self-attention but provides explicit concept disentanglement
  - EMD regularization adds complexity but enforces stronger concept exclusivity compared to cosine/euclidean distances

- Failure signatures:
  - High cross-entropy loss but low EMD regularization → attention not capturing concept similarity
  - Low cross-entropy loss but poor unseen accuracy → overfit to seen compositions; check β tuning and calibration term
  - EMD regularization dominates loss → attention maps become noisy; reduce regularization weight

- First 3 experiments:
  1. Ablation: Remove EMD regularization and compare AUC on Clothing16K; expect drop in unseen accuracy
  2. Backbone swap: Replace ViT-B-16 with ResNet18 and retrain; expect performance drop confirming ViT advantage
  3. Inference β sweep: Test β=0.0, 0.5, 1.0, ∞ on validation set; confirm that tuned β outperforms fixed β=1.0

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

- How does the proposed ADE method perform when applied to datasets with a larger number of attributes and objects compared to the datasets used in the paper?
- How does the choice of backbone architecture (e.g., ViT vs. ResNet) impact the performance of the ADE method, and is there a specific backbone that is optimal for different types of datasets?
- How does the proposed ADE method handle cases where attribute and object concepts are highly entangled in spatial features, and can it effectively disentangle such concepts?

## Limitations

- Weak empirical support for the core mechanisms, particularly the claim that cross-attention outperforms traditional spatial attention
- No ablation showing that EMD regularization performs better than simpler distance metrics like cosine or Euclidean
- Inference score fusion (β tuning) is validated only on the Clothing16K dataset
- Use of frozen ViT features limits end-to-end adaptation
- No reported uncertainty estimates or variance across runs

## Confidence

- High: The overall framework of using cross-attention for concept disentanglement is technically sound and well-motivated by the literature
- Medium: The adaptation of EMD to attention maps is novel, but lacks direct empirical justification compared to simpler alternatives
- Low: The claim that ViT attention is inherently superior for disentanglement is weakly supported and may be dataset-dependent

## Next Checks

1. Run an ablation study comparing EMD regularization to cosine and Euclidean distance baselines on all three datasets to verify that EMD provides a measurable improvement
2. Perform a backbone swap experiment (e.g., ViT-B-16 → ResNet50) to confirm that the performance gain is not solely due to the frozen transformer features
3. Report variance across 3-5 training runs for each dataset to assess result stability and the robustness of the β tuning procedure
