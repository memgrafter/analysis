# Stance Prediction and Analysis of Twitter data : A case study of Ghana 2020 Presidential Elections

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2306.14203
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.14203
- Reference count: 24
- Primary result: Logistic Regression achieved 71.13% accuracy on three-class stance classification of Ghanaian election tweets

## Executive Summary
This study conducted stance detection analysis on Twitter data from the 2020 Ghana presidential election to understand public opinions on the two main candidates. The authors collected 99,356 tweets, manually annotated 3,090 into three classes (Against, Neutral, Support), and performed preprocessing. They evaluated two lexicon-based approaches (VADER and TextBlob) and five supervised machine learning approaches (SVM, LR, MNB, SGD, RF) using accuracy, precision, recall, and F1-score. Logistic Regression achieved the best performance with 71.13% accuracy on the three-class classification task. The authors then used Logistic Regression to classify all 99,356 tweets and analyzed the results. Key findings include that Nana Addo Dankwa Akufo-Addo received more tweets overall, with 60% being neutral, while John Dramani Mahama received more negative tweets (58%).

## Method Summary
The authors collected 99,356 tweets related to the 2020 Ghana presidential election and manually annotated 3,090 tweets into three classes: Against, Neutral, and Support. They performed standard preprocessing steps including tokenization, stopword removal, and lemmatization. The study evaluated two lexicon-based approaches (VADER and TextBlob) and five supervised machine learning approaches (SVM, Logistic Regression, Multinomial Naive Bayes, Stochastic Gradient Descent, and Random Forest). Models were assessed using accuracy, precision, recall, and F1-score. Logistic Regression was selected as the best-performing model and used to classify all 99,356 tweets. The analysis revealed differences in tweet volumes and sentiment distributions between the two main candidates.

## Key Results
- Nana Addo Dankwa Akufo-Addo received more tweets overall than John Dramani Mahama
- 60% of tweets about Akufo-Addo were neutral in stance
- 58% of tweets about Mahama were negative in stance

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The study demonstrates that supervised machine learning approaches, particularly Logistic Regression, can effectively classify political stance in Twitter data when trained on manually annotated examples. The model leverages patterns in word usage, context, and linguistic features to distinguish between supportive, neutral, and opposing stances toward political candidates.

## Foundational Learning
- Stance Detection: Classification of text as supporting, opposing, or neutral toward a target entity; needed to understand public opinion dynamics in social media.
- Lexicon-based vs. Supervised Learning: Comparison of rule-based sentiment analysis against machine learning approaches; needed to establish baseline performance and determine optimal methodology.
- Political Discourse Analysis: Examination of public sentiment in electoral contexts; needed to provide insights into voter preferences and campaign effectiveness.

## Architecture Onboarding
- Component Map: Data Collection -> Preprocessing -> Manual Annotation -> Model Training -> Classification -> Analysis
- Critical Path: The most time-consuming and critical step is manual annotation of training data, which directly impacts model performance.
- Design Tradeoffs: Limited annotated data vs. large unlabeled corpus; general-purpose lexicons vs. domain-specific sentiment analysis.
- Failure Signatures: Misclassification of tweets containing sarcasm, code-switching, or culturally specific references.
- First Experiments:
  1. Evaluate inter-annotator agreement on a subset of manually labeled data
  2. Perform error analysis on misclassified tweets to identify systematic weaknesses
  3. Compare performance of different preprocessing strategies on the Ghanaian Twitter dataset

## Limitations
- Small manually annotated dataset (3,090 tweets) compared to total corpus (99,356 tweets) raises generalizability concerns
- Moderate 71.13% accuracy suggests challenges in accurately distinguishing stance categories
- General-purpose lexicons may not capture unique linguistic features of Ghanaian Twitter discourse
