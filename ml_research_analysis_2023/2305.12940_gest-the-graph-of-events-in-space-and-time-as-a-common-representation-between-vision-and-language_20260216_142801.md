# GEST: the Graph of Events in Space and Time as a Common Representation between Vision and Language

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2305.12940
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.12940
- Reference count: 25
- GEST provides a unified graph-based representation for vision and language, improving semantic similarity and content generation.

## Executive Summary
GEST (Graph of Events in Space and Time) is a novel representation for stories as graphs of events connected by temporal, spatial, and logical relations. It enables structured, explainable comparison and generation of both textual and visual narratives. Experiments show GEST-based similarity metrics outperform traditional text metrics and enhance state-of-the-art transformer-based metrics like BLEURT when combined linearly. For text generation, models using GEST as input produce more coherent narratives than those using only atomic event descriptions, especially when ordering events temporally.

## Method Summary
GEST represents stories as graphs where nodes are events (extracted from text or video) and edges encode temporal, spatial, and logical relations. Graph matching algorithms (Spectral Matching, Neural Graph Matching) compare these structured representations to measure semantic similarity. Sequence-to-sequence models (e.g., BART) are trained to convert between text and GEST, enabling both similarity assessment and content generation. The approach is validated on bAbI and Videos-to-Paragraphs datasets, demonstrating GEST's ability to capture richer semantic structure and improve generation quality.

## Key Results
- GEST-based similarity metrics outperform traditional metrics (BLEU, METEOR, ROUGE) and enhance BLEURT when combined linearly.
- Models trained on GEST input generate more coherent narratives than those using only atomic event descriptions.
- GEST effectively captures richer semantic structure, improving generation quality especially with limited training data.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- GEST provides a more interpretable similarity space by explicitly modeling events and their relations as graphs.
- Mechanism: Graph matching compares structured representations directly, bypassing opaque n-gram or embedding spaces.
- Core assumption: Events and their relations in space-time are the most natural way to represent and compare stories.
- Evidence anchors: GEST allows explainable similarity measurement through graph matching; instead of projecting texts into n-gram or Transformer space, we propose the space of events in space-time.
- Break condition: If event extraction or relation inference is noisy, graph representation becomes unreliable.

### Mechanism 2
- GEST boosts performance of heavily trained metrics like BLEURT when combined linearly.
- Mechanism: Text is converted to GEST, GEST similarity is computed, then linearly combined with the original metric's score.
- Core assumption: GEST captures complementary information to learned metrics, especially for paraphrasing or style differences.
- Evidence anchors: GEST can boost a state-of-the-art, strongly trained metric even when combined in the simplest, linear way.
- Break condition: If GEST generation model is poor, combined metric inherits and amplifies those errors.

### Mechanism 3
- GEST enables better text generation by explicitly encoding temporal and logical relations between events.
- Mechanism: Models trained on GEST input generate more coherent narratives because graph structure preserves ordering and causal links.
- Core assumption: Temporal and logical relations between events are crucial for coherent story generation.
- Evidence anchors: Results validate GEST is suited for text generation and provides better representation than plain textual descriptions of atomic events.
- Break condition: If dataset lacks rich temporal relations, advantage of GEST over flat sequences diminishes.

- **Dependency parsing and semantic role labeling**: Needed to extract events, entities, locations, and temporal relations from raw text into GEST nodes and edges. Quick check: Can you identify the main verb, subject, and object in "John picked up the football"?
- **Graph matching and spectral methods**: Needed to compute similarity between two GESTs by comparing node and edge embeddings. Quick check: What does the principal eigenvector of an affinity matrix represent in graph matching?
- **Sequence-to-sequence modeling with Transformers**: Needed to learn mappings between text and GEST for both similarity and generation tasks. Quick check: Why is a pre-trained BART or GPT model useful for text↔graph conversion?

## Architecture Onboarding

- **Component map**: Text preprocessing → Dependency parser → Event extractor → GEST builder; GEST → Graph matcher → Similarity score; Text → GEST encoder → GEST → GEST decoder → Text; Hybrid: Text → BLEURT → Text → GEST encoder → GEST matcher → Combined score
- **Critical path**: For similarity: Text → GEST → Graph matching → Score; For generation: GEST → Decoder → Text
- **Design tradeoffs**: Handcrafted rules ensure interpretability but limit scalability; graph matching is exact but computationally heavier than embedding-based similarity; linear combination with BLEURT is simple but may miss nonlinear synergies.
- **Failure signatures**: Low BLEURT + high GEST similarity → Possible paraphrase or style variation not captured by BLEURT; High BLEURT + low GEST similarity → Semantic mismatch in events or relations; Generated text with scrambled event order → GEST temporal edges not well learned.
- **First 3 experiments**: 1) Verify GEST extraction on bAbI: check each sentence yields one event node with correct entities, location, and inferred context; 2) Test graph matching: compare two identical GESTs (score ~1) and two random GESTs (score ~0); 3) Ablation on generation: train S2T vs G2T models on Videos-to-Paragraphs and measure coherence and ordering metrics.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- Question: Can GEST representations be automatically generated with high accuracy for complex, real-world stories beyond bAbI and Videos-to-Paragraphs?
- Basis: The paper acknowledges that building GEST from text currently requires handcrafted rules and human intervention, particularly for inferring implicit information like locations and timeframes.
- Why unresolved: Current methods rely on simplified datasets and manual intervention, suggesting the approach may not scale to more complex narratives with ambiguous or implicit information.
- What evidence would resolve it: A large-scale study applying GEST to diverse, real-world text corpora with automated parsing achieving high accuracy (>90%) on inferred information and minimal human correction.

### Open Question 2
- Question: How does the performance of GEST-based metrics compare to state-of-the-art metrics when trained on comparable amounts of data?
- Basis: The paper shows GEST metrics outperform traditional metrics but are outperformed by BLEURT, which was trained on 1.8 million samples.
- Why unresolved: The comparison is inherently unfair due to the massive data disparity. It's unclear if GEST's performance advantage stems from its representation or simply from having more training data.
- What evidence would resolve it: A controlled experiment training GEST metrics on the same scale of data as BLEURT and comparing their performance on the same benchmarks.

### Open Question 3
- Question: Can GEST representations effectively model and generate abstract events and complex narratives beyond simple temporal and spatial relations?
- Basis: The paper mentions that GEST can theoretically represent events at different scales and levels of abstraction, but experiments focus on simple events with temporal relations.
- Why unresolved: Current experiments only demonstrate GEST's effectiveness on simple, structured narratives. It's unclear if the representation can handle the complexity and ambiguity of real-world stories involving abstract concepts, motivations, and complex causal relationships.
- What evidence would resolve it: Successful application of GEST to generate or evaluate narratives involving abstract concepts (e.g., political revolutions, emotional journeys, philosophical arguments) with human evaluation confirming semantic coherence and relevance.

## Limitations
- GEST extraction currently relies on handcrafted rules and human intervention, limiting scalability to complex narratives.
- The approach has only been validated on simplified datasets (bAbI, Videos-to-Paragraphs) and may not generalize to diverse, real-world data.
- Linear combination with BLEURT may not capture the full potential of combining structured graph representations with learned metrics.

## Confidence
- **High confidence**: GEST provides a structured, interpretable representation for stories as graphs of events with explicit relations (temporal, spatial, logical).
- **Medium confidence**: GEST improves semantic similarity measurement over traditional n-gram metrics and can enhance transformer-based metrics like BLEURT when combined linearly.
- **Low confidence**: GEST consistently enables better text generation across diverse domains and dataset sizes, and that the handcrafted extraction rules scale effectively to real-world data.

## Next Checks
1. **Cross-dataset validation**: Evaluate GEST-based similarity and generation on a diverse, real-world video description dataset (e.g., MSR-VTT or VATEX) to test generalizability.
2. **Scalability test**: Apply GEST extraction to a noisy, multi-sentence narrative dataset and assess the quality and coherence of the resulting graphs.
3. **Nonlinear combination**: Experiment with nonlinear fusion methods (e.g., neural networks) to combine GEST and BLEURT scores, potentially improving upon the linear baseline.
