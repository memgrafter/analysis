# Structural Explanations for Graph Neural Networks using HSIC

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2302.02139
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2302.02139
- Authors: 
- Reference count: 38
- Primary result: Proposed HSIC-based method achieves higher fidelity and sparsity scores than baselines on MUTAG chemical graph classification

## Executive Summary
This paper introduces a model-agnostic method for explaining Graph Neural Networks (GNNs) by identifying important nodes, edges, and subgraphs using the Hilbert-Schmidt Independence Criterion (HSIC). The approach extends GraphLIME by incorporating group and fused regularization into HSIC Lasso, enabling detection of significant substructures in graphs through kernel-based nonlinear dependency measures. The method is evaluated on synthetic and real-world datasets, demonstrating improved explanation quality compared to existing approaches.

## Method Summary
The method uses HSIC Lasso with group and fused regularization to select important graph structures. It perturbs graphs to create auxiliary datasets, then measures nonlinear dependencies between these perturbations and GNN predictions using kernel-based similarity measures. Group lasso identifies important substructures by grouping related nodes, while fused lasso automatically clusters connected important nodes. The approach is model-agnostic and works without requiring access to the internal workings of the GNN.

## Key Results
- Outperforms existing GNN explanation methods on fidelity and sparsity metrics
- Accurately identifies crucial structures in synthetic wheel and cycle graphs
- Achieves higher performance than baselines on MUTAG chemical graph classification task
- Successfully handles both node-level and subgraph-level explanations

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: HSIC Lasso can detect node importance by measuring nonlinear dependencies between node features and GNN predictions.
- Mechanism: HSIC Lasso uses kernel-based methods to capture nonlinear dependencies between perturbed graph structures and their predictions, allowing identification of significant nodes or edges through regularization.
- Core assumption: The dependency between graph structure and GNN predictions can be captured through kernel-based similarity measures.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract]: "detect significant structures in graphs using the Hilbert-Schmidt independence criterion (HSIC), which captures the nonlinear dependency between two variables through kernels"
  - [section]: "we employ HSIC Lasso to solve the node selection problem using the perturbation of graphs"
  - [corpus]: Weak evidence - no directly relevant corpus neighbors
- Break condition: If the relationship between graph structure and predictions is not captured by the kernel functions used, the method will fail to identify important structures.

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: Group regularization enables detection of important subgraphs rather than individual nodes.
- Mechanism: By grouping nodes into overlapping sets and applying group lasso regularization, the method can identify significant substructures that contribute to predictions.
- Core assumption: Important structural features in graphs exist as groups of related nodes rather than isolated individual nodes.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract]: "extend the GraphLIME method for node explanation with a group lasso and a fused lasso-based node explanation method"
  - [section]: "we use the group and fused regularization. The group and fused regularization with GraphLIME enables the interpretation of GNNs in substructure units"
  - [corpus]: Weak evidence - no directly relevant corpus neighbors
- Break condition: If important structures cannot be meaningfully grouped, or if overlapping groups cause interference, the method may fail to identify correct substructures.

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: Fused regularization automatically clusters important nodes without predefined groups.
- Mechanism: Generalized fused lasso encourages similar coefficients for connected nodes, automatically identifying clusters of important nodes in large graphs.
- Core assumption: Important nodes in predictions tend to be connected or form clusters in the graph structure.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract]: "we use the group and fused regularization. The group and fused regularization with GraphLIME enables the interpretation of GNNs in substructure units"
  - [section]: "HSIC lasso with a generalized fused regularization term is able to automatically cluster important nodes or edges"
  - [corpus]: Weak evidence - no directly relevant corpus neighbors
- Break condition: If important nodes are scattered and not connected, fused regularization may fail to identify them correctly.

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map: Graph perturbation module -> HSIC Lasso solver -> Regularization selection (L1, Group, Fused) -> Prediction model (black box) -> Evaluation metrics
- Critical path: Perturb graph -> Generate auxiliary dataset -> Compute Gram matrices -> Solve HSIC Lasso optimization -> Extract important nodes/edges -> Validate results
- Design tradeoffs: The method trades computational complexity (O(n²P) space) for model-agnostic interpretability, versus gradient-based methods that require model access but are faster

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

- **Open Question 1**: How can the proposed method be extended to handle out-of-distribution (OOD) problems that arise from structural perturbations in the graph?
  - Basis in paper: [explicit] The authors discuss the OOD problem in Section 6, stating that structural perturbations can generate OOD samples, causing misleading explanations.
  - Why unresolved: The paper suggests using feature perturbations or calibrating the distribution used in HSIC lasso as potential solutions, but these are not fully explored or validated.
  - What evidence would resolve it: Experiments comparing the proposed method with feature perturbations and calibrated distributions on various graph datasets would provide insights into the effectiveness of these approaches.

- **Open Question 2**: How does the choice of kernel function (e.g., Gaussian vs. Delta) impact the performance of the proposed method in identifying important structures in graphs?
  - Basis in paper: [explicit] The authors mention using different kernel functions for different tasks, such as Gaussian for continuous node features and Delta for binary features indicating node existence.
  - Why unresolved: The paper does not provide a comprehensive analysis of how different kernel functions affect the method's performance across various graph tasks.
  - What evidence would resolve it: A systematic study comparing the performance of the proposed method using different kernel functions on a range of graph datasets would clarify the impact of kernel choice.

- **Open Question 3**: Can the proposed method be adapted to handle dynamic graphs where the importance of nodes or edges changes over time?
  - Basis in paper: [explicit] The authors briefly mention the potential for explaining dynamic graphs in the related work section, but do not explore this in their experiments.
  - Why unresolved: The current method focuses on static graphs, and adapting it to dynamic graphs would require addressing the temporal aspect of node and edge importance.
  - What evidence would resolve it: Developing and testing an extension of the proposed method for dynamic graphs, and comparing its performance to existing methods for explaining GNNs on dynamic graphs, would demonstrate its effectiveness in this setting.

## Limitations

- Hyperparameter ranges for regularization parameters are not provided
- Method's scalability to very large graphs is not addressed
