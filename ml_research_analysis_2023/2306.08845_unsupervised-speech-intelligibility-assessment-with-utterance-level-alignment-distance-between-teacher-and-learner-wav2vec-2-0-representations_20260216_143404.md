# Unsupervised speech intelligibility assessment with utterance level alignment distance between teacher and learner Wav2Vec-2.0 representations

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2306.08845
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.08845
- Reference count: 0
- Classification accuracy of 96.58% using cosine distance on the VoisTUTOR dataset

## Executive Summary
This paper introduces an unsupervised approach for automatic speech intelligibility detection using alignment distance computed via dynamic time warping (DTW) between Wav2Vec-2.0 representations of teacher and learner speech. The method calculates alignment distances using three measures—mean absolute error, mean squared error, and cosine distance—and uses these as a metric to differentiate between intelligible and non-intelligible speech. Experiments on the VoisTUTOR dataset yield classification accuracies of 90.37%, 92.57%, and 96.58% respectively for the three distance measures, outperforming baseline methods. The highest accuracy of 96.58% is achieved using cosine distance, which also shows the best performance across different phoneme categories and phone lengths.

## Method Summary
The proposed method employs Wav2Vec-2.0 representations of teacher and learner speech utterances, computing alignment distances using dynamic time warping (DTW). Three distance metrics—mean absolute error (MAE), mean squared error (MSE), and cosine distance—are evaluated to measure the dissimilarity between the representations. The cosine distance measure achieves the highest classification accuracy of 96.58% on the VoisTUTOR dataset, demonstrating superior performance in detecting speech intelligibility without requiring labeled data.

## Key Results
- Cosine distance achieves 96.58% classification accuracy on the VoisTUTOR dataset
- MAE and MSE achieve classification accuracies of 90.37% and 92.57% respectively
- Cosine distance outperforms baseline methods and shows best performance across phoneme categories and phone lengths

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The method leverages Wav2Vec-2.0 representations to capture phonetic and linguistic features of speech, enabling robust comparison between teacher and learner utterances. Dynamic time warping aligns these representations temporally, allowing precise measurement of dissimilarity through alignment distances. The cosine distance metric is particularly effective as it captures angular differences in high-dimensional feature space, making it sensitive to subtle phonetic variations that indicate intelligibility.

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component Map:** Wav2Vec-2.0 -> DTW -> Distance Metric (MAE/MSE/Cosine)
**Critical Path:** Teacher speech → Wav2Vec-2.0 → DTW alignment → Distance calculation → Intelligibility classification
**Design Tradeoffs:** 
- Wav2Vec-2.0 representations provide rich features but may be computationally intensive
- DTW ensures temporal alignment but can be sensitive to noise and utterance length
- Cosine distance captures subtle phonetic differences but may require careful normalization
**Failure Signatures:** 
- Poor performance on non-standard speech conditions (noise, accents)
- Reduced accuracy for short utterances due to alignment challenges
- Sensitivity to Wav2Vec-2.0 model limitations in capturing specific phonetic nuances
