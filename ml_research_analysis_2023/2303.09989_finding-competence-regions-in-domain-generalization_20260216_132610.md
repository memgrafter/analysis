# Finding Competence Regions in Domain Generalization

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2303.09989
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.09989
- Reference count: 40
- Primary result: Proposes framework to identify competence regions using incompetence scores for reliable predictions under mild distribution shifts

## Executive Summary
This paper introduces a framework for identifying competence regions in domain generalization, where model predictions can be trusted even under mild distribution shifts. The core approach uses incompetence scores derived from out-of-distribution detection methods to set thresholds for accepting or rejecting predictions. Experiments demonstrate that accuracy within these competence regions is higher than on the full dataset, validating the concept. However, the incompetence scores are not yet accurate enough to consistently recover in-distribution accuracy across all tested domains. Notably, models specifically optimized for domain generalization do not outperform naive baselines within competence regions, suggesting current techniques may not be fully effective.

## Method Summary
The proposed framework identifies competence regions by computing incompetence scores for each sample using out-of-distribution detection methods. A threshold is set on these scores, where samples with low scores are accepted as trustworthy predictions and high-score samples are rejected. This creates a competence region where the model's performance is expected to be reliable. The method leverages the intuition that samples similar to training data (low incompetence) will have more accurate predictions, while those far from the training distribution (high incompetence) should be rejected. Experiments evaluate this approach across multiple domain generalization benchmarks to assess the existence and quality of competence regions.

## Key Results
- Competence regions exist where model accuracy is higher than on full dataset
- Incompetence scores can identify these regions, though not yet precisely enough to recover in-distribution accuracy
- Domain generalization models do not outperform naive baselines within competence regions
- The framework provides a way to reject uncertain predictions while maintaining higher accuracy on accepted samples

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The mechanism works by leveraging the relationship between sample similarity to training data and prediction reliability. Out-of-distribution detection methods naturally quantify how far a sample deviates from the training distribution. By thresholding these incompetence scores, the framework creates a region where samples are close enough to training data that predictions are more likely to be correct. This is particularly valuable in domain generalization where distribution shifts occur, as it allows the model to recognize when it's operating in familiar territory versus when predictions become unreliable.

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component map**: Input data -> Incompetence score computation (OOD detector) -> Threshold application -> Accepted/rejected samples -> Performance evaluation

**Critical path**: Data → OOD detector → Incompetence scores → Thresholding → Competence region evaluation

**Design tradeoffs**: The framework trades coverage (fraction of samples accepted) for reliability (accuracy within accepted region). Higher thresholds mean fewer samples but potentially higher accuracy, while lower thresholds accept more samples at the cost of reliability.

**Failure signatures**: Incompetence scores fail to correlate well with actual accuracy; domain generalization techniques don't improve performance in competence regions; thresholds that are too high reject too many samples; thresholds that are too low admit unreliable predictions.

## Limitations
- Incompetence scores not accurate enough to consistently recover in-distribution accuracy across all domains
- Domain generalization models optimized for generalization do not outperform naive baselines within competence regions
- The framework requires effective OOD detection, which remains challenging in practice

## Confidence

- Low: Claim that current domain generalization techniques do not outperform baselines within competence regions needs additional validation across more diverse datasets
