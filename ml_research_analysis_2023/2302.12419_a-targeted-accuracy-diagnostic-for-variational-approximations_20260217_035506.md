# A Targeted Accuracy Diagnostic for Variational Approximations

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2302.12419
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2302.12419
- Reference count: 40
- Primary result: TADDAA provides computationally efficient, targeted lower bounds on variational approximation errors for specific posterior functionals

## Executive Summary
This paper introduces TADDAA (Targeted Accuracy Diagnostic for Approximate posteriors), a method that evaluates variational inference approximations by computing lower bounds on the error of specific posterior functionals like means and variances. The approach runs multiple short MCMC chains initialized from the variational approximation and compares the final states to the initial approximation to bound the error. A correlation-based reliability check determines when these bounds can be trusted. Experiments show TADDAA provides more precise and actionable information than existing diagnostics like Pareto-smoothed importance sampling, correctly identifying when variational approximations are accurate for specific quantities even when overall accuracy is poor.

## Method Summary
TADDAA evaluates variational inference approximations by running N parallel MCMC chains for T iterations, each initialized from the variational approximation. The chains use adaptive proposals with inter-chain adaptation to improve efficiency. For each posterior functional of interest (mean, variance, quantile), TADDAA computes confidence intervals for the difference between the initial variational approximation and the improved distribution after MCMC steps. A reliability check based on maximum correlation between initial and final chain values (cutoff 0.1) determines when the bounds are trustworthy. The method provides lower bounds on approximation errors, helping users understand which quantities are accurately captured by VI and which may require more expensive sampling.

## Key Results
- TADDAA provides more precise error bounds than PSIS and Wasserstein bounds for specific posterior functionals
- Correctly identifies accurate variational approximations for specific quantities even when overall accuracy is poor
- Successfully detects poor approximations in hierarchical models where other diagnostics may fail
- Computationally efficient compared to full MCMC while providing targeted diagnostics

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: TADDAA uses short MCMC chains to detect large errors in variational approximations by measuring how much the approximation shifts after MCMC steps
- Mechanism: By initializing many short MCMC chains from the variational approximation and comparing the final states to the initial approximation, TADDAA can bound the error in specific posterior functionals like means and variances. If the initial approximation is poor, MCMC will shift the distribution significantly; if it's good, the shift will be small.
- Core assumption: The Markov kernel mixes sufficiently well that running T steps moves the distribution closer to the true posterior, and the error decreases monotonically with iteration count
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "uses many short parallel MCMC chains to obtain lower bounds on the error of each posterior functional of interest"
  - [section] "if the initial approximation error ε(0)(F) is large, then a well-chosen Markov kernel will result in the improved approximation error ε(T)(F) being substantially smaller"
  - [corpus] Weak - corpus papers don't directly address this MCMC-based diagnostic mechanism
- Break condition: If the Markov kernel mixes poorly or the posterior has complex geometry that prevents monotonic error decrease, the lower bounds may not be valid or could be zero even when errors exist

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: Inter-chain adaptation improves proposal efficiency while maintaining asymptotic independence between chains
- Mechanism: Joint adaptation of step sizes across chains allows faster convergence to optimal acceptance rates compared to independent adaptation. Despite adaptation creating dependence between chains at each iteration, the chains become asymptotically independent as the number of chains N approaches infinity.
- Core assumption: The adaptation function and proposal kernel satisfy conditions that ensure chaotic propagation of particles, allowing asymptotic independence
- Evidence anchors:
  - [section] "we jointly adapt the Markov chains at each iteration" and "the samples from the Markov chains at any fixed iteration satisfy a propagation-of-chaos property"
  - [section] "Theorem 3.3. Under Assumption 3.4... for any t∈ N, there exists a probability distribution ¯ν(t) such that the sequence{X(t)1:N}∞N=1 is ¯ν(t)-chaotic"
  - [corpus] Weak - corpus papers don't directly discuss chaotic propagation in MCMC adaptation
- Break condition: If the adaptation function violates the conditions for chaotic propagation (e.g., adaptation is too aggressive or proposal kernel is poorly behaved), the asymptotic independence assumption fails and confidence intervals become invalid

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: Correlation-based reliability check detects when MCMC chains aren't mixing well enough for valid diagnostics
- Mechanism: By computing the maximum correlation coefficient between initial and final states across all coordinates, TADDAA can identify when chains are poorly mixing. Low correlation (below 0.1) indicates good mixing and reliable diagnostics; high correlation suggests the chains haven't moved enough from initialization.
- Core assumption: In well-mixing chains, the initial and final states become nearly uncorrelated after T iterations
- Evidence anchors:
  - [section] "we propose to leverage the fact that we have many nearly independent chains to compute the correlation between the initial and final values of each chain"
  - [section] "If ρ2max(T) is close to 0 (we use a cutoff of 0.1), then the check passes; otherwise, the check fails"
  - [corpus] Weak - corpus papers don't discuss correlation-based MCMC diagnostics
- Break condition: If the posterior has multiple modes that chains get stuck in, or if the number of iterations T is insufficient for mixing, the correlation check may fail even when the approximation error is small

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map: VI approximation generator -> MCMC sampler with adaptive proposals -> Chain correlation monitor -> Error bound calculator -> Reliability checker
- Critical path: VI approximation -> MCMC sampling (T iterations) -> Correlation check -> Error bound computation -> Diagnostic output
- Design tradeoffs: More chains (N) gives tighter confidence intervals but increases computation; longer chains (T) improve mixing but may be unnecessary if approximation is already good
- Failure signatures: High ρ2max(T) values indicate poor mixing; zero lower bounds with high correlation suggest chains didn't move; large computational cost relative to VI optimization suggests parameters need tuning
- First 3 experiments:
  1. Test on a simple Gaussian target with known mean/variance to verify TADDAA recovers correct error bounds
  2. Apply to a correlated Gaussian with mean-field VI to confirm it detects variance errors while accepting mean accuracy
  3. Run on a Neal funnel model to validate performance on challenging geometries with poor VI approximations

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- Question: Under what conditions does Condition 1 (non-increasing error with Markov chain iterations) break down for complex posterior geometries?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper states "While in general we expect Condition 1 to hold, it is possible that it could be violated for target distributions with complex geometries."
- Why unresolved: The paper only mentions this as a theoretical possibility without providing concrete examples or characterizing the types of distributions where this might occur.
- What evidence would resolve it: Concrete examples of posterior distributions where Condition 1 fails, or theoretical characterization of the geometric properties that cause violations.

### Open Question 2
- Question: How does TADDAA's correlation-based reliability check perform in high-dimensional multimodal posterior settings?
- Basis in paper: [inferred] The paper proposes the correlation check to detect poor mixing but notes "the check can fail due to, e.g., multimodality."
- Why unresolved: The paper validates the reliability check on several models but doesn't explicitly test it on highly multimodal distributions where standard MCMC diagnostics often fail.
- What evidence would resolve it: Systematic experiments comparing TADDAA's correlation check to established MCMC diagnostics (like R-hat) on multimodal posteriors of varying complexity.

### Open Question 3
- Question: What is the optimal adaptation schedule for the step size parameter in TADDAA across different MCMC kernels?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper uses a simple Robbins-Monro adaptation scheme but notes "the efficiency of Barker is more robust to the precise step size" and suggests the choice may not be optimal.
- Why unresolved: While the paper demonstrates that adaptation works, it doesn't explore alternative adaptation schedules or compare their performance across different kernels and dimensionalities.
- What evidence would resolve it: Systematic comparison of different adaptation schemes (e.g., constant, diminishing, or adaptive learning rates) across various kernels and target distributions.

## Limitations
- Requires many chains (N=100-1000) and iterations (T=20-100), making it computationally expensive despite being cheaper than full MCMC
- Cannot detect errors smaller than the MCMC mixing time, potentially missing small but meaningful approximation errors
- Correlation-based reliability check may fail for multimodal posteriors where chains get stuck in different modes
- Lower bounds may be zero even when meaningful errors exist if the chains don't move sufficiently from initialization

## Confidence
- MCMC-based error detection (Mechanism 1): High confidence - theoretically sound but practical limitations exist
- Inter-chain adaptation validity (Mechanism 2): Medium confidence - depends on specific conditions that may not hold for all kernels
- Correlation reliability check (Mechanism 3): Medium confidence - assumes good mixing correlates with low correlation, which may fail for multimodal posteriors
