# MrTF: Model Refinery for Transductive Federated Learning

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2305.04201
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.04201
- Authors: 
- Reference count: 40
- Key outcome: Proposed MrTF framework outperforms baselines on multiple benchmarks (MNIST, SVHN, CIFAR, FeMnist, Shakespeare) with up to 82.31% accuracy on CIFAR10 under differential privacy

## Executive Summary
This paper introduces MrTF, a model refinery framework for transductive federated learning (TFL) designed to address the challenges of data heterogeneity and the need for inference on newly-collected data in federated settings. The method tackles the problem where traditional federated learning approaches fail due to non-IID data distributions across clients and the inability to leverage test data structure. MrTF consists of three modules: stabilized teachers that normalize logits to reduce variance, rectified distillation that combines local and global models with confidence-based weighting, and clustered label refinery that refines predictions using test data structure. The experimental results demonstrate significant improvements over existing federated learning baselines across multiple datasets and settings.

## Method Summary
MrTF addresses transductive federated learning by proposing a three-module framework that refines local models through knowledge distillation while incorporating test data structure. The method operates in federated rounds where clients perform local training, then the server aggregates models using stabilized teachers (replacing raw logit averaging with normalized probability averaging), rectified distillation (combining local and global models with entropy-based weighting), and clustered label refinery (refining targets using test data clustering). The framework is designed to handle non-IID data heterogeneity while leveraging access to test data during training to improve predictions, distinguishing it from traditional inductive federated learning approaches.

## Key Results
- MrTF outperforms FedAvg and FedDF baselines on MNIST, SVHN, CIFAR, FeMnist, and Shakespeare datasets
- Achieves up to 82.31% accuracy on CIFAR10 with added differential privacy, demonstrating practical viability
- Shows consistent improvements across different client participation ratios (10% and 1%) and local training epochs
- Ablation studies confirm that all three modules contribute to performance gains

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: Averaging logits directly in FedDF suffers from varying magnitudes across local models, leading to unstable ensemble predictions.
- Mechanism: The "AvgLogi" approach averages raw logits from local models before applying softmax. When local models have logits of different scales, the softmax can be dominated by a few large logits, skewing the ensemble.
- Core assumption: Local models trained on non-IID data will have logits with varying magnitudes on the same classes.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "the logits' magnitudes across local models vary a lot, and directly averaging them as the 'teacher' may lead to training instability."
  - [section] "local updated models could generate 'logits' with varying magnitudes on the same class, making 'AvgLogi' suffer from large variance"
  - [corpus] Weak/no evidence - corpus does not directly address this mechanism.
- Break condition: If local models have similar logit magnitudes (e.g., trained on IID data), the problem diminishes.

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: Stochastic client participation in FedDF leads to biased distillation targets because the ensemble may not cover all classes.
- Mechanism: With random client selection, some classes may be underrepresented or missing in the ensemble. The distillation process then biases the global model toward the observed classes, harming generalization to missing ones.
- Core assumption: Non-IID data means clients have different class distributions, and stochastic selection may exclude some classes entirely.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "stochastic client selection does not guarantee that the local model's ensemble covers all classes, negatively transferring knowledge for missing classes."
  - [section] "the stochastic client participation will lead to inaccurate distillation targets, and directly using 'AvgLogi' or 'AvgProb' for distillation is improper"
  - [corpus] Weak/no evidence - corpus does not directly address this mechanism.
- Break condition: If client participation is high or data is IID, the bias is reduced.

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: Incorporating feature clustering of the test data into the distillation process improves label refinement by leveraging structural information.
- Mechanism: After initial distillation, features are extracted from the global model. Clustering these features and refining the distillation targets based on cluster assignments helps align predictions with the test data's structure.
- Core assumption: The test data has a meaningful structure that can be captured by clustering, and the global model can extract useful features even if imperfect.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "the refinery process incorporates test samples into training and could generate better predictions in a transductive manner"
  - [section] "We take advantage of deep clustering [44] to consider feature structural information. This technique has been verified beneficial in domain adaptation [45] and transductive few-shot learning [7]."
  - [corpus] Weak/no evidence - corpus does not directly address this mechanism.
- Break condition: If test data lacks structure or the model cannot extract meaningful features, clustering adds noise.

## Foundational Learning

- Concept: Knowledge distillation
  - Why needed here: The method relies on transferring knowledge from local models (teachers) to the global model (student) using soft targets.
  - Quick check question: What is the difference between hard targets (one-hot) and soft targets in distillation?

- Concept: Non-IID data in federated learning
  - Why needed here: The method is designed to handle the challenge where clients have different data distributions, which causes weight divergence and biased model aggregation.
  - Quick check question: How does non-IID data affect the convergence of federated averaging?

- Concept: Transductive learning
  - Why needed here: The method leverages access to test data during training to improve predictions, contrasting with inductive learning which must generalize without seeing test data.
  - Quick check question: What is the key difference between inductive and transductive learning settings?

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map:
  Stabilized Teachers -> Rectified Distillation -> Clustered Label Refinery -> Model Refinement Loop

- Critical path:
  1. Clients perform local training and upload updated models.
  2. Server aggregates models and refines them using the three modules.
  3. Refined model is sent back to clients for next round.

- Design tradeoffs:
  - Using AvgProb vs AvgLogi: More stable but may lose some information from logits.
  - Entropy-based weighting: Improves sample-wise teacher selection but adds complexity.
  - Feature clustering: Can improve predictions if test data is structured, but may fail if features are noisy.

- Failure signatures:
  - Training instability or divergence in early rounds.
  - Poor performance on classes underrepresented in participating clients.
  - Clustering step producing meaningless clusters (e.g., random assignment).

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- Question: How does MrTF perform in cross-domain TFL scenarios compared to in-domain TFL, and what advanced domain adaptation techniques could improve its performance in cross-domain settings?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper mentions that MrTF can be applied to cross-domain TFL but notes that the overall transfer performance is lower compared to in-domain learning, suggesting that more advanced domain adaptation techniques should be considered.
- Why unresolved: The paper only provides preliminary results on cross-domain TFL and does not explore advanced domain adaptation techniques that could potentially improve performance.
- What evidence would resolve it: Experiments comparing MrTF with various advanced domain adaptation techniques in cross-domain TFL settings, along with a detailed analysis of the performance improvements achieved.

### Open Question 2
- Question: How does the performance of MrTF vary with different client participation ratios and the number of local training epochs in large-scale FL scenarios?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper investigates the performance of MrTF with different client participation ratios (R = 10%, 1%) and local training epochs (E = 2, 10, 50) but does not provide a comprehensive analysis of how these factors affect performance in large-scale scenarios.
- Why unresolved: The paper focuses on specific settings of K, R, and E but does not explore the full range of possible combinations or their impact on performance in large-scale FL scenarios.
- What evidence would resolve it: Extensive experiments varying client participation ratios and local training epochs across different numbers of clients (K) to determine the optimal settings for large-scale FL scenarios.

### Open Question 3
- Question: How does the addition of differential privacy affect the performance of MrTF, and what is the trade-off between privacy protection and model accuracy?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper mentions that differential privacy techniques should be considered for stricter privacy protections and provides preliminary results showing that MrTF can maintain better prediction accuracy compared to FedAvg when noise is added.
- Why unresolved: The paper does not provide a detailed analysis of the trade-off between privacy protection and model accuracy, nor does it explore the impact of different noise levels on performance.
- What evidence would resolve it: A comprehensive study varying the noise levels and analyzing the corresponding trade-off between privacy protection (measured by privacy budgets) and model accuracy for both MrTF and other FL methods.

## Limitations
- The paper lacks detailed ablation studies showing the individual contribution of each module, making it difficult to assess whether all three are necessary.
- The effectiveness of the clustered label refinery heavily depends on the quality of features extracted by the global model, which may vary significantly across datasets and model architectures.
- The claim about up to 82.31% accuracy on CIFAR10 with differential privacy is impressive but lacks sufficient methodological details about the DP implementation and parameter settings.

## Confidence

**High**: The core problem formulation (TFL for newly-established parties) and overall three-module framework design are well-justified and internally consistent.

**Medium**: The specific implementation details of modules (e.g., exact normalization constants, clustering hyperparameters) may affect reproducibility and performance.

**Low**: The effectiveness of the clustered label refinery heavily depends on the quality of features extracted by the global model, which may vary significantly across datasets and model architectures.
