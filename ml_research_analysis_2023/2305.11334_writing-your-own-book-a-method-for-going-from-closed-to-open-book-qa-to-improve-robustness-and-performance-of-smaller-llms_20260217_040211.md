# Writing your own book: A method for going from closed to open book QA to improve robustness and performance of smaller LLMs

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2305.11334
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.11334
- Reference count: 5
- Key outcome: Tree-Search and Self-contextualizing QA methods improve T0 3B model performance on 1475 open-ended questions, with informativeness increasing from 8.4% to 53.3%

## Executive Summary
This paper introduces two novel methods to enhance large language model (LLM) performance on question-answering (QA) tasks: Tree-Search and Self-contextualizing QA. Tree-Search is a sampling technique that extracts diverse information from an LLM by branching at high-entropy positions during decoding. Self-contextualizing QA leverages Tree-Search to enable the model to create its own context using a wide range of information relevant to the prompt, then uses that context to answer the original question in an open-book QA format. Experiments using the T0 3B model show significant improvements in answer informativeness (53.3% vs 8.4% for closed-book), with smaller but still positive gains in accuracy (31.2% vs 12.5%), coherence (29.6% vs 11.9%), and consistency (26.4% vs 14.1%). The methods also improve robustness, with performance positively correlated with tree size.

## Method Summary
The authors propose Tree-Search as a sampling technique designed to extract diverse information from an LLM for a given prompt by branching at high-entropy positions during decoding. Self-contextualizing QA leverages Tree-Search to enable the model to create its own context using a wide range of information relevant to the prompt, then uses that context to answer the original question in an open-book QA format. The approach transforms closed-book QA into open-book QA by having the model generate its own relevant context through the Tree-Search sampling process, which is then used to inform the final answer generation.

## Key Results
- Tree-Search and Self-contextualizing QA improve answer informativeness from 8.4% to 53.3% on 1475 open-ended questions using T0 3B model
- Accuracy increases from 12.5% to 31.2% when using the proposed methods
- Coherence improves from 11.9% to 29.6% and consistency from 14.1% to 26.4% compared to closed-book performance
- Performance shows positive correlation with tree size, suggesting scalability benefits

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The methods work by leveraging the LLM's own capabilities to generate diverse, relevant context through Tree-Search sampling. By branching at high-entropy positions during decoding, the approach captures multiple potential information paths, which are then synthesized into a comprehensive context. This self-generated context effectively transforms the problem from closed-book to open-book QA, allowing the model to draw from a wider range of information when formulating answers. The branching mechanism at high-entropy positions ensures diversity in the generated content, while the tree structure enables systematic exploration of different information pathways relevant to the prompt.

## Architecture Onboarding

**Component Map**
Tree-Search Sampler -> Context Generator -> QA Answer Generator -> Evaluation Metrics

**Critical Path**
The critical path involves: 1) Input prompt processing, 2) Tree-Search sampling with branching at high-entropy positions, 3) Context generation from sampled tree, 4) Answer generation using the self-created context, 5) Evaluation against ground truth

**Design Tradeoffs**
The method trades computational overhead from sampling multiple paths against improved answer quality and robustness. The branching factor and tree depth represent key hyperparameters balancing exploration diversity with computational feasibility.

**Failure Signatures**
Potential failures include: excessive branching leading to computational infeasibility, context generation that is too broad or irrelevant, and hallucination of incorrect information during the self-contextualization phase.

## Limitations
- The experimental setup using T0 3B on 1475 questions raises questions about generalizability to other model families
- The evaluation metrics focus primarily on informativeness, accuracy, coherence, and consistency but do not address potential issues with factual correctness or hallucination rates
- The quality assessment of self-generated context remains unclear - there's no explicit validation that the context created through Tree-Search is factually accurate or relevant
- Computational cost implications of the sampling process are not quantified, which is critical for practical deployment

## Confidence
- Confidence: Medium in the reported performance improvements due to limited validation scope and potential evaluation biases
- Confidence: Low regarding claims about computational efficiency and scalability
- Confidence: Medium for the conceptual framework, pending empirical validation across diverse model architectures
