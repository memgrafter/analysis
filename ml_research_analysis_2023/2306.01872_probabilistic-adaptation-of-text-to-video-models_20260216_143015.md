# Probabilistic Adaptation of Text-to-Video Models

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2306.01872
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.01872
- Reference count: 40
- Primary result: Achieves better FVD and FID scores than both pretrained and small task-specific models using as few as 1.25% of the parameters of the large model

## Executive Summary
This paper introduces Video Adapter, a method to adapt large pretrained text-to-video diffusion models to specialized domains without expensive fine-tuning. The core idea is to treat the pretrained model as a probabilistic prior and combine its score function with that of a small, task-specific video model through score addition during diffusion sampling. This enables generating high-quality videos that preserve the broad knowledge of the large model (temporal consistency, object permanence) while adapting to domain-specific styles like animation, sci-fi, egocentric manipulation, and robotics.

## Method Summary
Video Adapter works by training a small video diffusion model on domain-specific data, then combining its score function with the pretrained model's score during sampling. At each denoising step, the method sums the predictions from both models, scaled by an inverse temperature parameter γ. For classifier-free guidance, it constructs an implicit classifier using the unconditional score of the small model. This probabilistic composition allows the small model to capture task-specific distributions while the pretrained model provides general video priors like temporal consistency and object permanence.

## Key Results
- Achieves better FVD and FID scores than both pretrained and small task-specific models
- Uses as few as 1.25% of the parameters of the large model (0.07B vs 5.6B)
- Successfully adapts to domains including animation, sci-fi, egocentric manipulation, and robotics
- Enables data augmentation for sim-to-real transfer in robotics

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- **Claim:** Video Adapter achieves effective domain adaptation by probabilistically composing the score functions of a large pretrained video diffusion model and a small task-specific model.
- **Mechanism:** The pretrained diffusion model acts as a probabilistic prior via its score function (equivalent to an energy-based model). The task-specific model learns to capture the residual distribution. At each denoising step, their scores are summed to guide sampling toward videos that are both high-likelihood under the task data and consistent with the pretrained prior's general video statistics (temporal coherence, object permanence).
- **Core assumption:** The pretrained model's score function encodes robust, generalizable video statistics that remain useful across domains, and that these can be combined additively in the score space without destroying either component's contribution.
- **Evidence anchors:** [abstract] "leverages the score function of a large pretrained video diffusion model as a probabilistic prior to guide the generation of a task-specific small video model"; [section 3.2] "the denoising prediction is the sum of predictions from both the pretrained and small video diffusions"
- **Break condition:** If the pretrained model's score function is too domain-specific or poorly aligned with the task domain, the additive composition could distort the intended style or dynamics.

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim:** Classifier-free guidance (CFG) can be extended to the probabilistic composition by using the unconditional score of the task model to shape the effective classifier, avoiding collapse to the pretrained style.
- **Mechanism:** Instead of applying CFG to the pretrained model directly (which would have too many modes), the method constructs a product distribution p_product(τ|text) ∝ p_pretrained(τ|text) p_θ(τ|text), then uses the unconditional score p_θ(τ) as the baseline in the CFG formula. This implicitly creates a classifier between the conditioned product distribution and the unconditional task model, and scaling it by α produces low-temperature samples that retain the task style while benefiting from the prior.
- **Core assumption:** The unconditional task model score can serve as a valid baseline for CFG when combined with the product distribution, and the product's density ratio can be stably estimated via this formulation.
- **Evidence anchors:** [section 3.3] "we propose to first construct a new text-conditional distribution ... we can then use the density ratio ... to construct a new implicit classifier"
- **Break condition:** If the unconditional task model has poor coverage or high variance, the CFG baseline could be noisy, leading to unstable guidance.

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim:** Scaling the pretrained score by an inverse temperature γ allows tunable control over the strength of the prior, enabling gradual blending between task-specific style and pretrained knowledge.
- **Mechanism:** The combined denoising prediction is ϵ_combined = ϵ_θ + γ ϵ_pretrained. By varying γ from 0 to a large value, the user can interpolate between pure task model output and output heavily biased toward the pretrained model's general video patterns. This is essential for balancing adaptation fidelity with generalization.
- **Core assumption:** The scale of the pretrained score is appropriate for additive combination and can be linearly weighted without calibration.
- **Evidence anchors:** [section 3.2] "scale the pretrained distribution by an inverse temperature γ ... which corresponds to scaling the denoised prediction"
- **Break condition:** If γ is too large, the output collapses to the pretrained model's generic style; if too small, the prior's benefits (temporal consistency, object permanence) are lost.

## Foundational Learning

- **Concept: Diffusion probabilistic models as energy-based models (EBMs)**
  - Why needed here: Understanding that a diffusion model's denoising function is the score of an EBM allows the insight that scores can be added to combine distributions.
  - Quick check question: What is the relationship between the denoising function ϵ_θ(τ,t) and the gradient of an energy function?
  - Answer: ϵ_θ(τ,t) = ∇_τ E_θ(τ,t), i.e., the denoising function estimates the score of the EBM.

- **Concept: Product of probability distributions and their score-space combination**
  - Why needed here: The key to Video Adapter is that sampling from p₁p₂ corresponds to summing their scores, which is only true because both are expressed as EBMs.
  - Quick check question: If p₁ ∝ e^{-E₁} and p₂ ∝ e^{-E₂}, what is the score of their product?
  - Answer: ∇ log(p₁p₂) = ∇E₁ + ∇E₂.

- **Concept: Classifier-free guidance (CFG) and implicit classifiers**
  - Why needed here: Video Adapter extends CFG beyond direct classifier use by constructing an implicit classifier from the unconditional and conditional scores, enabling low-temperature sampling while preserving the prior.
  - Quick check question: In standard CFG, what does the scalar α control?
  - Answer: It scales the difference between conditional and unconditional scores, increasing the "confidence" of the classifier and sharpening samples.

## Architecture Onboarding

- **Component map:** Pretrained diffusion model (5.6B params) -> Small diffusion model (0.07B-0.14B params) -> Edge encoder (optional) -> CFG wrapper -> Sampling loop
- **Critical path:**
  1. Load frozen pretrained model
  2. Load small model (with/without edge conditioning)
  3. For each timestep t:
     - Compute ϵ_small(τ,t|text) and γ ϵ_pretrained(τ,t|text)
     - Compute unconditional ϵ_small(τ,t)
     - Form implicit classifier: ϵ_cfg = ϵ_small + α (ϵ_small + γ ϵ_pretrained - ϵ_small)
     - Run diffusion step with ϵ_cfg
  4. Return final video

- **Design tradeoffs:**
  - Parameter count vs. adaptation quality: smaller small models save compute but may underfit
  - γ (prior strength): too high → style collapse; too low → loss of pretrained benefits
  - CFG α: too high → noisy samples; too low → insufficient stylization
  - Edge conditioning: enables stylization but adds preprocessing complexity

- **Failure signatures:**
  - Output videos look like generic internet videos → γ too high
  - Output videos have unrealistic dynamics or artifacts → small model underfitting or CFG α too high
  - Output videos are blurry or low quality → small model capacity insufficient or sampling steps inadequate

- **First 3 experiments:**
  1. Train a small model on a simple domain (e.g., animation style) and run Video Adapter with γ=0.5, α=1.0; inspect qualitative blending
  2. Sweep γ from 0 to 2.0; measure FVD/FID on a held-out validation set to find optimal prior strength
  3. Compare CFG α=1.0 vs α=2.0 on the same task; evaluate sample sharpness and consistency

## Limitations
- The practical success depends heavily on the pretrained model's score function being well-behaved and additive, with no ablation studies for misaligned domains
- The novel implicit classifier construction for CFG lacks rigorous validation and comparison to simpler alternatives
- The linear scaling of pretrained scores by temperature γ assumes appropriate magnitude without verification, potentially requiring dataset-specific calibration

## Confidence

- **High confidence:** The basic score addition mechanism (Mechanism 1) - follows directly from established diffusion theory and the mathematical relationship between EBMs and diffusion models
- **Medium confidence:** The classifier-free guidance extension (Mechanism 2) - theoretically motivated but practical implementation details and stability across different tasks are not fully explored
- **Medium confidence:** The temperature scaling approach (Mechanism 3) - linear scaling is a reasonable heuristic, but optimal scaling may require dataset-specific calibration

## Next Checks

1. **Prior misalignment stress test:** Train a small model on a domain completely disjoint from the pretrained model's training data (e.g., medical imaging videos), then run Video Adapter with various γ values. Measure whether the pretrained prior actually helps or hinders adaptation, and identify at what point the prior becomes detrimental.

2. **CFG baseline comparison:** Implement a simpler alternative where only the small model is conditioned and compare qualitative and quantitative results against the proposed implicit classifier approach. This would validate whether the added complexity of the implicit classifier construction is necessary.

3. **Score magnitude analysis:** For each task domain, measure the L2 norm of the pretrained model's score function and compare it to the small model's score. If the ratio varies significantly across domains, this would suggest that the fixed γ scaling assumption is insufficient and that adaptive scaling might be needed.
