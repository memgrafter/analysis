# Calibrating the Rigged Lottery: Making All Tickets Reliable

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2302.09369
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2302.09369
- Reference count: 40
- Primary result: Sparse training method (CigL) reduces ECE by up to 47.8% while maintaining or improving accuracy on multiple datasets and architectures

## Executive Summary
This paper addresses the reliability problem in sparse training, where sparsity constraints exacerbate over-confidence in deep neural networks. The authors introduce CigL, a sparse training method that uses two complementary masks - a deterministic mask for efficient weight selection and a random mask for exploration - to improve both accuracy and calibration. The method is theoretically grounded as a hierarchical variational approximation to a deep Gaussian process and validated across multiple datasets, model architectures, and sparsity levels.

## Method Summary
CigL introduces a dual-mask approach to sparse training that combines deterministic weight selection with stochastic exploration. The deterministic mask efficiently identifies important weights using magnitude and gradient criteria, while the random mask enables better exploration of the weight space under sparsity constraints. The method also incorporates weight and mask averaging to produce a final sparse model with improved calibration. Theoretically, CigL is framed as a hierarchical variational approximation that can better capture complex posterior dependencies in sparse models.

## Key Results
- Reduces Expected Calibration Error (ECE) by up to 47.8% compared to baseline sparse training methods
- Maintains or improves accuracy while achieving better calibration
- Effective across multiple datasets (CIFAR-10, CIFAR-100, TinyImageNet) and model architectures
- Achieves these improvements with only slight increases in computation and storage requirements

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- **Claim**: CigL improves confidence calibration by better exploring the weight space through dual masks
- **Mechanism**: The deterministic mask efficiently searches for important weights using magnitude-based pruning and gradient-guided regrowth, while the random mask adds stochastic exploration, allowing the model to escape spurious local minima and discover better weight values
- **Core assumption**: Sparsity constraints create narrow optimization routes that limit weight space exploration; random masking compensates by injecting exploration while preserving topology found by deterministic pruning
- **Evidence anchors**:
  - [abstract]: "The former efficiently searches and activates important weights by exploiting the magnitude of weights and gradients. While the latter brings better exploration and finds more appropriate weight values by random updates."
  - [section]: "The random mask allows the model to better explore the weight space under sparsity constraints... forcing the model to explore more in other directions of the weight space..."
  - [corpus]: Weak. No direct corpus citations; claim is inferred from method description
- **Break condition**: If the random mask sparsity is set too high (>15%), temporary models become too sparse to explore meaningfully, collapsing exploration gains

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim**: CigL's hierarchical variational approximation improves posterior fit under sparsity constraints
- **Mechanism**: By introducing a two-level hierarchy (mask + weight), CigL expands the variational family, capturing complex posterior dependencies and marginal distributions that single-mask methods cannot express
- **Core assumption**: Sparse training induces stronger correlations between the random mask Z and weights W, requiring hierarchical structure to model them
- **Evidence anchors**:
  - [abstract]: "Theoretically, we prove our method can be viewed as a hierarchical variational approximation of a probabilistic deep Gaussian process."
  - [section]: "the model is more challenging to fully explore the weight space due to sparsity constraint... Our CigL adds a hierarchical structure to the variational distributions so that we have a larger family of distributions..."
  - [corpus]: Weak. Corpus lacks direct variational inference citations; claim is method-theoretical
- **Break condition**: If σ² (variational variance) is too small, the hierarchy collapses to a point estimate, losing the benefits of posterior uncertainty

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim**: Weight & mask averaging aggregates multiple sparse models to improve calibration without increasing inference cost
- **Mechanism**: By collecting {Z(t), W(t)} pairs across epochs and averaging them element-wise, CigL approximates the posterior mean, smoothing out overconfident predictions while retaining sparsity benefits
- **Core assumption**: Multiple sparse models from different weight/mask configurations capture complementary aspects of the posterior, and their average yields better calibration than any single model
- **Evidence anchors**:
  - [abstract]: "Then, with the designed weight & mask averaging procedure to obtain one single sparse model with improved confidence calibration and comparable or even higher accuracy."
  - [section]: "we obtain the single output sparse model by averaging the weights of these temporary sparse models, which can be viewed as a mask-based weighted averaging."
  - [corpus]: Weak. No corpus evidence of mask-weighted averaging; claim is method-specific
- **Break condition**: If the averaging window is too small (T* too late), collected samples are too similar, providing little calibration benefit

## Architecture Onboarding

- **Component map**: Deterministic mask M -> Random mask Z -> Weight updates W -> Averaging buffer -> Final model
- **Critical path**:
  1. Initialize sparse weights W(0), masks M, Z
  2. For each iteration: Sample Z, update W via SGD with masked gradients
  3. Every ΔT: Update M by magnitude/gradient criteria
  4. After T*: Buffer {Z(t), W(t)} at each epoch
  5. Final: Average buffered pairs to produce output model
- **Design tradeoffs**:
  - Mask sparsity vs. exploration: Higher random mask sparsity increases exploration but risks degrading performance of temporary models
  - Averaging window size vs. calibration gain: Longer windows capture more diversity but increase memory
  - Deterministic mask update frequency vs. topology stability: Frequent updates may disrupt convergence; infrequent updates may miss good weights
- **Failure signatures**:
  - Calibration does not improve: Random mask sparsity too low or averaging window too short
  - Accuracy drops sharply: Deterministic mask update rule too aggressive; prune too many good weights
  - Training instability: Random mask p too high; temporary models too sparse
- **First 3 experiments**:
  1. Run CigL vs RigL on CIFAR-10 ResNet-50 at 95% sparsity; compare ECE and accuracy
  2. Sweep random mask sparsity p (5%, 10%, 15%) to find sweet spot for calibration gain
  3. Compare weight & mask averaging vs. only weight averaging to isolate contribution of mask averaging

## Limitations
- Theoretical claims around hierarchical variational approximation lack rigorous derivation in the main text
- Ablation studies for mask averaging and random mask contributions are incomplete
- Random mask exploration mechanism lacks quantitative evidence showing it discovers genuinely better weight configurations

## Confidence

**Limits & Confidence**
The paper presents a novel approach to sparse training with strong empirical calibration improvements, but several limitations warrant attention. The theoretical claims around hierarchical variational approximation lack rigorous derivation in the main text, relying on references that may not fully support the specific adaptation to sparse training. Empirical results show significant ECE reductions (up to 47.8%), but the ablation studies for mask averaging and random mask contributions are incomplete - the paper does not isolate whether weight averaging alone would achieve similar gains. The random mask exploration mechanism, while intuitive, lacks quantitative evidence showing it discovers genuinely better weight configurations rather than just adding noise. Confidence in the mechanism claims is **Medium**: the dual-mask framework is well-defined, but the specific pathways to calibration improvement need more rigorous validation. Confidence in the theoretical framing is **Low**: the hierarchical VI connection is asserted but not fully proven for the sparse setting. Confidence in the empirical results is **High** for the main comparisons, but **Medium** for ablation studies.
