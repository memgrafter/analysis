# FAIRER: Fairness as Decision Rationale Alignment

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2306.15299
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.15299
- Reference count: 40
- Key outcome: Introduces decision rationale alignment for improving DNN fairness, showing significant improvements over existing methods while maintaining accuracy

## Executive Summary
This paper addresses the fairness of deep neural networks by examining how models make predictions rather than just focusing on output parity. The authors identify that existing fairness regularization methods fail to achieve decision rationale alignment, where neurons should respond consistently across different subgroups. They propose a new fairness metric called parameter parity score and introduce a novel task called decision rationale alignment. The gradient-guided parity alignment method encourages gradient-weighted consistency of neurons across subgroups, leading to improved fairness while maintaining high accuracy across three datasets (Adult, CelebA, and Credit).

## Method Summary
The paper introduces decision rationale alignment as a new fairness task, where the goal is to ensure that neurons in a neural network respond consistently across different subgroups. The method uses a gradient-guided approach to relax the objective function and encourage gradient-weighted consistency of neurons. The loss function combines classification loss, fairness regularization, and decision rationale alignment terms. The parameter parity score measures consistency of neuron responses across subgroups by assuming neuron independence. The method is implemented using a 3-layer MLP architecture with specific hyperparameters (learning rate 0.001, batch size 1000, λ in range 0.2-0.6, β = λ/10).

## Key Results
- Gradient-guided parity alignment significantly improves fairness metrics (-DP and -EO) while maintaining high accuracy (Average Precision)
- The proposed method outperforms existing fairness regularization approaches on three benchmark datasets
- Decision rationale alignment addresses the limitation of current fairness methods that fail to ensure consistent neuron behavior across subgroups

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The method works by aligning the decision rationales across subgroups through gradient-weighted consistency of neurons. By incorporating this alignment into the loss function, the model learns to make predictions in a way that is consistent across different subgroups, not just in the final output. This approach addresses the fundamental limitation of existing methods that focus only on output parity without considering how the model arrives at its decisions.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- How does the assumption of neuron independence affect the accuracy of the parameter parity score in capturing the decision rationale?
- Can the decision rationale alignment method be extended to more complex network architectures beyond the layer-wise DNNs?
- How does the decision rationale alignment impact the robustness of the model against adversarial attacks?

## Limitations
- The method is currently limited to layer-wise DNN architectures and may not extend to complex models like ResNet or Transformers
- The parameter parity score has dataset-specific limitations and may not generalize well across different data distributions
- The approach assumes neuron independence, which may not capture complex interactions between neurons in practice
