# Relax, it doesn't matter how you get there: A new self-supervised approach for multi-timescale behavior analysis

## Executive Summary
This paper introduces BAMS, a self-supervised representation learning framework for behavior analysis. BAMS uses two key innovations: a Histogram of Actions (HoA) objective that predicts future action distributions instead of sequences, and a multi-timescale architecture with separate short- and long-term embedding spaces. On a synthetic quadruped robot dataset, BAMS accurately decodes robot type, velocity, and terrain properties. On the MABe 2022 Mouse Triplet Challenge, BAMS achieves state-of-the-art performance, ranking first overall and on all sequence-level tasks, and top-3 on 7 of 9 frame-level tasks. The method effectively captures both local behavioral dynamics and global attributes like strain or time of day.

## Method Summary
BAMS is a self-supervised representation learning framework for behavior analysis that combines two novel components: (1) a Histogram of Actions (HoA) objective that predicts the distribution of future actions rather than their sequence, and (2) a multi-timescale architecture with separate short-term and long-term embedding spaces. The model uses two Temporal Convolutional Network (TCN) encoders with different receptive field sizes to capture behavioral dynamics at different timescales. The HoA objective predicts histograms of future actions using 2-Wasserstein distance, while bootstrapping latent predictive losses enable training without negative samples. The framework is trained end-to-end on unlabeled behavioral data and evaluated using linear probes on downstream tasks.

## Key Results
- BAMS achieves state-of-the-art performance on the MABe 2022 Mouse Triplet Challenge, ranking first overall and on all sequence-level tasks
- The method ranks top-3 on 7 of 9 frame-level tasks in the MABe challenge
- On synthetic quadruped robot data, BAMS accurately decodes robot type, velocity, and terrain properties
- The approach effectively captures both local behavioral dynamics and global attributes like strain or time of day

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: The Histogram of Actions (HoA) objective enables stable long-range predictions by predicting distributions rather than exact sequences.
- Mechanism: By predicting the histogram of future actions over a fixed time window, the model is relieved from the burden of precisely timing each action, which is inherently difficult in complex and unpredictable behavior. This distribution-based prediction reduces the sensitivity to small temporal misalignments and allows the model to focus on the overall behavioral "fingerprint" instead of exact timing.
- Core assumption: Predicting distributions over future actions is more stable and easier to learn than predicting exact action sequences, especially for complex behaviors.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "a novel action-prediction approach that aims to predict the distribution of actions over future timesteps, without modeling exactly when each action is taken"
  - [section] "We propose to predict the distribution of future actions rather than their sequence... By predicting the histogram of future actions over a fixed time window, the model is relieved from the burden of precisely timing each action"
  - [corpus] Weak corpus support; neighbor papers do not directly address distribution-based action prediction.
- Break condition: If the distribution of future actions is highly multimodal or changes rapidly over time, predicting a single histogram may lose important temporal dynamics.

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: The multi-timescale architecture enables the model to separately capture short-term behavioral dynamics and long-term trends without interference.
- Mechanism: Two separate TCN encoders are used—one with a short receptive field for local dynamics and one with a long receptive field for global trends. Each encoder is trained with its own bootstrapping loss on positive views from its respective timescale, preventing the short-term and long-term representations from interfering with each other.
- Core assumption: Behavioral factors that vary over different timescales (e.g., local actions vs. global attributes) are better modeled by separate representations rather than a single shared representation.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "A multi-scale architecture that builds separate latent spaces to accommodate short- and long-term dynamics"
  - [section] "We design an architecture that separates the different timescales by using two TCN encoders... We introduce a new approach to build representations across different scales while preserving the granularity in each"
  - [corpus] Weak corpus support; neighbor papers do not discuss multi-timescale bootstrapping in behavioral representation learning.
- Break condition: If the timescales are not clearly separable or if there is significant overlap in the factors driving behavior at different timescales, separate representations may not be beneficial.

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: Bootstrapping within each timescale using latent predictive losses enables learning without negative samples.
- Mechanism: For each timescale, positive views are selected from nearby time points (short-term) or any point in the same sequence (long-term). A predictor network is trained to map the current embedding to the embedding of the positive view, using a stop-gradient operator to prevent trivial solutions. This allows the model to learn stable representations without the need for negative samples.
- Core assumption: Positive views from the same sequence at different timescales can serve as effective training signals for learning meaningful representations.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "A multi-task representation learning model for behavior that combines two novel components: ... (ii) A multi-scale architecture that builds separate latent spaces to accommodate short- and long-term dynamics"
  - [section] "We draw inspiration from recent work [ 21, 52, 22] that uses latent predictive losses to learn representations without the need for negative examples; this happens by encouraging positive views to be mapped to similar points in the latent space"
  - [corpus] Weak corpus support; neighbor papers do not discuss bootstrapping within timescales using latent predictive losses.
- Break condition: If the sequence is too short or the behavior is too variable, selecting appropriate positive views may become difficult, leading to poor representation learning.

## Foundational Learning

  - Why needed here: TCNs are used as the building blocks for the multi-timescale architecture, as they can capture temporal dependencies in sequential data with varying receptive field sizes.

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map: Input features -> Short-term TCN encoder -> Long-term TCN encoder -> Concatenated embedding -> HoA predictor -> Short-term predictor -> Long-term predictor -> Losses

- Critical path:
  1. Extract features from input data (e.g., keypoint positions)
  2. Pass features through short-term and long-term TCN encoders
  3. Predict future action histograms using HoA predictor
  4. Bootstrap representations using latent predictive losses
  5. Aggregate embeddings and compute losses
  6. Backpropagate and update model parameters

- Design tradeoffs:
  - Separate vs. shared representations: Separate representations allow for better modeling of different timescales but increase model complexity.
  - Receptive field size: Larger receptive fields capture longer-term dependencies but may miss short-term details.
  - Number of prediction frames: More frames provide better long-term predictions but increase computational cost.

- Failure signatures:
  - Poor performance on long-term tasks: May indicate insufficient receptive field size or poor long-term representation learning.
  - Poor performance on short-term tasks: May indicate insufficient receptive field size or poor short-term representation learning.
  - Unstable training: May indicate issues with loss functions or hyperparameters.

- First 3 experiments:
  1. Test HoA objective with a simple TCN on a synthetic dataset with known action distributions.
  2. Test multi-timescale architecture with bootstrapping on a dataset with clear short-term and long-term behavioral factors.
  3. Test the full BAMS model on the MABe Mouse Triplet dataset and compare performance to baseline methods.

## Limitations
- The claim of state-of-the-art performance on MABe 2022 Mouse Triplet Challenge is well-supported by the results, but the evaluation metrics and comparison methods against other methods are not fully specified in the paper.
- The effectiveness of the Histogram of Actions (HoA) objective is demonstrated on synthetic quadruped robot data and the MABe challenge, but its generalization to other behavioral domains is not explicitly validated.
- The multi-timescale architecture's ability to capture both short-term dynamics and long-term trends is theoretically sound, but the paper does not provide direct evidence of how well the model disentangles these factors in practice.
