# Task-oriented Document-Grounded Dialog Systems by HLTPR@RWTH for DSTC9 and DSTC10

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2304.07101
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2304.07101
- Reference count: 40
- Primary result: Achieved state-of-the-art results on DSTC9 and DSTC10 datasets using hierarchical selection, bi-encoders, and noisy channel model for factuality.

## Executive Summary
This paper presents a task-oriented dialog system that grounds responses in external documents. The approach tackles three subtasks: detecting knowledge-seeking turns, selecting relevant documents, and generating responses. The authors propose methods to improve efficiency, such as hierarchical selection and bi-encoder models. They also introduce a noisy channel model to enhance the factuality of generated responses. The system is evaluated on the DSTC9 and DSTC10 datasets, achieving state-of-the-art results on several automatic metrics. The authors also reconsider the automatic evaluation metrics for the generation task, showing that recently proposed factuality metrics have a higher correlation to human judgments.

## Method Summary
The paper proposes a three-stage document-grounded dialog system: knowledge-seeking turn detection using binary classification, document selection using cross-encoder or bi-encoder models, and response generation using BART. The authors introduce hierarchical selection to improve efficiency by breaking document retrieval into domain-entity-document stages, bi-encoder models for precomputing document embeddings, and a noisy channel model to improve factuality by combining response generation with document explanation likelihood. The system is adapted for spoken dialogs using data augmentation and ASR transcript preprocessing.

## Key Results
- Hierarchical selection achieves 24x speedup while improving accuracy over full cross-encoder retrieval
- Bi-encoder models provide additional 100x speedup with comparable retrieval quality
- Factuality metrics (BLEU-1 document overlap, token-level F1 overlap, Q2) show higher correlation with human judgments than traditional metrics

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: Hierarchical Selection reduces computational cost by breaking retrieval into domain-entity-document stages
- Mechanism: Prunes search space at each stage by first identifying relevant domain, then entity, then specific document
- Core assumption: Document relevance is strongly correlated with domain and entity relevance
- Evidence anchors: Abstract claims 24x speedup with better results; section describes the three-stage approach
- Break condition: If document relevance is not correlated with entity/domain, pruning could eliminate correct document early

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: Bi-encoder architecture enables O(1) retrieval after precomputing document embeddings
- Mechanism: Separate encoding of dialog and documents, similarity computed via precomputed embeddings
- Core assumption: Similarity metrics approximate full cross-encoder relevance scores for ranking
- Evidence anchors: Section describes precomputing embeddings and nearest neighbor search; abstract mentions 100x speedup
- Break condition: If embedding space doesn't preserve fine-grained relevance distinctions, nearest neighbor ranking fails

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: Noisy channel model improves factuality by combining response generation with document explanation likelihood
- Mechanism: Weights response probability by both generation likelihood and document explanation likelihood
- Core assumption: Document selection model serves as proxy for document faithfulness when applied in reverse
- Evidence anchors: Abstract mentions factuality improvement; section describes Bayes theorem derivation
- Break condition: If channel model likelihood doesn't correlate with factual accuracy, reranking won't improve factuality

## Foundational Learning

- Concept: Cross-encoder relevance scoring
  - Why needed here: Baseline method for comparing dialog context with documents; understanding its computational cost motivates bi-encoder and hierarchical approaches
  - Quick check question: What is the computational complexity of scoring all documents in a cross-encoder model with N documents and batch size of 1?

- Concept: Contrastive learning for bi-encoders
  - Why needed here: Training bi-encoders requires learning to pull relevant document embeddings close to dialog embedding and push irrelevant ones apart
  - Quick check question: How does NT-Xent loss differ from triplet loss in terms of negative sampling strategy?

- Concept: Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG)
  - Why needed here: Provides way to marginalize over multiple documents during generation, reducing impact of selection errors
  - Quick check question: In RAG, why is it necessary to renormalize selection probabilities when approximating sum over documents with top-n subset?

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map: Detection -> Selection -> Generation
- Critical path: Detection → Selection → Generation. Each step's output feeds the next; errors propagate forward.
- Design tradeoffs:
  - Speed vs. accuracy: Cross-encoders are accurate but slow; bi-encoders are fast but may lose nuance
  - Generalization vs. specialization: Hierarchical selection is more efficient but assumes strong domain-entity-document structure
  - Factuality vs. fluency: Noisy channel model improves factuality but may reduce response naturalness if not tuned
- Failure signatures:
  - Detection: High false negatives → relevant turns missed; high false positives → unnecessary document lookups
  - Selection: Low R@1 → system often picks wrong document; high MRR but low R@1 → correct doc is ranked low
  - Generation: BLEU/METEOR scores high but factuality metrics low → responses are fluent but not grounded
- First 3 experiments:
  1. Train baseline cross-encoder selection model and measure R@1, latency per turn on GTX 1080 Ti
  2. Replace with hierarchical selection; compare R@1 and latency, check if beam search improves recall of near-tie entities
  3. Train bi-encoder with NT-Xent loss; evaluate retrieval accuracy vs cross-encoder and measure speedup

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- Question: Can bi-encoder models achieve performance parity with cross-encoder models for knowledge selection?
- Basis in paper: Authors mention improving bi-encoder training to "reduce the gap between cross-encoder and bi-encoder models"
- Why unresolved: Paper shows improvements but doesn't demonstrate full parity with cross-encoders
- What evidence would resolve it: Head-to-head comparison on same test set with identical training conditions showing comparable performance metrics

### Open Question 2
- Question: How effective are factuality metrics compared to traditional lexical overlap metrics?
- Basis in paper: Authors "reconsider automatic metrics" and show factuality metrics "have higher correlation to human judgments"
- Why unresolved: Paper doesn't establish whether factuality metrics should replace traditional metrics entirely or how they perform across different domains
- What evidence would resolve it: Comprehensive evaluation across multiple dialog domains comparing both metric types' ability to identify high-quality responses

### Open Question 3
- Question: What is optimal approach for adapting document-grounded dialog systems to handle spoken conversations with ASR transcripts?
- Basis in paper: Authors propose "data augmentation techniques" and "style adaptation methods" for noisy ASR transcripts
- Why unresolved: Paper presents several approaches but doesn't determine which combination yields best performance
- What evidence would resolve it: Systematic ablation studies testing different combinations of preprocessing, data augmentation, and style adaptation techniques

## Limitations

- Limited quantitative evidence for bi-encoder retrieval quality compared to cross-encoder baseline
- Noisy channel model's factuality improvements lack empirical validation
- No ablation study on hierarchical selection's accuracy loss from domain/entity pruning

## Confidence

- Hierarchical selection claims: Medium (24x speedup stated but no pruning error analysis)
- Bi-encoder speedup claims: Medium (100x speedup stated but limited retrieval accuracy comparison)
- Factuality model claims: Low (conceptual description but no empirical factuality gains shown)
