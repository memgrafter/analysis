# Pre-Trained Language-Meaning Models for Multilingual Parsing and Generation

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID**: 2306.00124
- **Source URL**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.00124
- **Reference count**: 11
- **Primary result**: Introduces a multilingual pre-trained language-meaning model that achieves state-of-the-art performance on both multilingual DRS parsing and DRS-to-text generation tasks across English, German, Italian, and Dutch.

## Executive Summary
This paper introduces a novel multilingual pre-trained language-meaning model that jointly learns to map natural language texts and Discourse Representation Structures (DRSs) into a universal semantic space. By leveraging the language-neutral nature of DRSs, the approach enables bidirectional alignment between meaning representations and natural language across multiple languages. The model uses mBART-50 as its backbone and employs a two-stage pre-training strategy: basic denoising followed by supervised denoising to bridge the gap between pre-training and downstream tasks. The approach achieves state-of-the-art results on both DRS parsing (text-to-DRS) and DRS-to-text generation tasks, with human evaluation confirming the effectiveness of the model. Cross-lingual transfer learning is adopted to improve performance on non-English languages, and COMET is shown to be a more reliable automatic metric for generation quality assessment.

## Method Summary
The method uses mBART-50 as a backbone and employs a multi-stage training approach. First, basic denoising pre-training is applied to the multilingual corpus to learn robust representations. Then, supervised denoising pre-training is performed using parallel DRS-text pairs to explicitly learn the mapping between meaning representations and natural language. Finally, multilingual fine-tuning is conducted on the combined dataset of all languages, followed by monolingual fine-tuning for each specific language. The model treats DRSs as language-neutral representations, enabling cross-lingual knowledge transfer. During training, DRSs and texts are concatenated with language and DRS prefixes to learn the universal semantic space.

## Key Results
- Achieves state-of-the-art performance on multilingual DRS parsing with significant improvements over baseline models
- Outperforms previous models on DRS-to-text generation tasks across all four languages (English, German, Italian, Dutch)
- Human evaluation confirms COMET has higher correlation with human judgments than BLEU for generation quality assessment
- Cross-lingual transfer learning consistently improves performance on non-English languages

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
Joint modeling of meaning representations and natural language enables bidirectional alignment in a shared semantic space. By concatenating DRSs and text sequences with language and DRS prefixes during pre-training, the model learns to map both representations into the same continuous space, enabling parsing (text→DRS) and generation (DRS→text) with a single model. Core assumption: DRSs are language-neutral and can serve as an interlingua across languages, making the semantic space universal.

### Mechanism 2
Supervised denoising pre-training bridges the gap between self-supervised pre-training and downstream parsing/generation tasks. After basic denoising, the model is trained on parallel DRS-text pairs to explicitly learn the mapping between meaning representations and natural language, aligning pre-training objectives closer to fine-tuning tasks. Core assumption: The gap between denoising pre-training and semantic parsing/generation tasks reduces performance without task-specific alignment.

### Mechanism 3
Multilingual fine-tuning with parallel data enables cross-lingual knowledge transfer for low-resource languages. After pre-training, the model is fine-tuned on multilingual DRS-text pairs, allowing knowledge from high-resource languages (e.g., English) to transfer to lower-resource languages (German, Italian, Dutch). Core assumption: DRSs being language-neutral allows semantic knowledge to transfer across languages during fine-tuning.

## Foundational Learning

- **Sequence-to-sequence modeling with Transformer architecture**: Needed to convert between text and DRS sequences bidirectionally; seq2seq with Transformers provides this capability with attention mechanisms for alignment. Quick check: How does the Transformer decoder attend to the encoder output during text-to-DRS parsing?

- **Pre-training with denoising objectives (masking, reconstruction)**: Needed to learn robust representations of both natural language and DRSs before fine-tuning; denoising forces the model to understand context and fill in missing information. Quick check: What is the difference between basic denoising and supervised denoising in this framework?

- **Evaluation metrics for semantic parsing (Smatch) and generation (BLEU, METEOR, COMET)**: Needed because tasks require different evaluation approaches; Smatch measures semantic overlap for parsing while n-gram and neural metrics assess generation quality. Quick check: Why does COMET show higher correlation with human judgments than BLEU for DRS-to-text generation?

## Architecture Onboarding

- **Component map**: mBART-50 backbone → Basic denoising pre-training → Supervised denoising pre-training → Multilingual fine-tuning → Monolingual fine-tuning → Parsing/Generation
- **Critical path**: Pre-training stages (B-PT → S-PT) → Multilingual fine-tuning (F-FT) → Monolingual fine-tuning (S-FT) → Task-specific inference
- **Design tradeoffs**: Using mBART-50 provides multilingual coverage but may limit vocabulary size for DRS-specific tokens; joint modeling simplifies architecture but requires careful data balancing.
- **Failure signatures**: Ill-formed DRS outputs (cannot convert to graph), semantic mismatches between generated text and DRS meaning, poor cross-lingual transfer for low-resource languages.
- **First 3 experiments**:
  1. Fine-tune mBART-50 directly on English DRS-text pairs (M1 baseline) to establish performance without pre-training.
  2. Add basic denoising pre-training on multilingual data (M2) to test if DRS representation learning improves performance.
  3. Add supervised denoising pre-training (M3) to verify if bridging the pre-training-gap improves results.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
How would the model perform on longer sentences and complex discourse structures beyond the relatively short sentences in the PMB dataset? The authors note that "A large part of the dataset that we used in our experiments are semantic annotations for relatively short sentences" and acknowledge uncertainty about performance on longer sentences or more complex discourse structures.

### Open Question 2
Would incorporating DRS graph structures directly into the model architecture and training process improve performance compared to the current sequential notation approach? The authors mention that "Perhaps a more natural way is to model DRSs as graph structures and let training objectives directly utilize any structural information from DRS" and note this as a potential improvement.

### Open Question 3
What is the impact of using English WordNet synsets as the interlingua for non-English languages, and would a truly multilingual semantic resource improve performance? The authors acknowledge that "Although we say that the DRSs are language-neutral, the concepts in the vocabulary are based on the English WordNet" and note potential limitations when non-English words don't have direct correspondence to appropriate synsets.

## Limitations

- Evaluation relies heavily on the PMB corpus, which has inherent annotation biases and limited coverage of linguistic phenomena
- The effectiveness of supervised denoising pre-training lacks theoretical grounding for why this specific approach bridges the pre-training gap
- Cross-lingual transfer benefits are measured only across four languages, making generalization claims uncertain

## Confidence

- **High Confidence**: The basic effectiveness of denoising pre-training for semantic tasks; the superiority of COMET over BLEU for generation evaluation
- **Medium Confidence**: The effectiveness of supervised denoising pre-training in bridging the pre-training gap; the claim that DRSs are language-neutral
- **Low Confidence**: Generalization of cross-lingual transfer benefits to languages beyond the four studied; the long-term stability of the approach with different corpus sizes and quality levels

## Next Checks

1. **Ablation on Supervised Denoising**: Remove the supervised denoising pre-training stage and measure the performance drop across all languages to quantify the exact contribution of this component beyond basic denoising.

2. **Cross-Lingual Transfer Robustness**: Test the model on an additional language (e.g., French or Spanish) not included in the original training to validate the cross-lingual transfer claims beyond the four languages studied.

3. **DRS Representation Neutrality**: Conduct a linguistic analysis comparing DRS structures across languages for equivalent sentences to verify the language-neutrality assumption, particularly focusing on languages with different syntactic structures (e.g., Japanese or Turkish).
