## Executive Summary
This paper addresses counterfactual outcome prediction in longitudinal data with irregular sampling and time-dependent confounding, focusing on medical applications like tumor growth modeling. The authors propose S4Model, a structured state space model that outperforms TE-CDE, a neural controlled differential equation approach, by 10x better normalized mean squared error while reducing training time by 1.63x. S4Model achieves this through efficient handling of irregularly sampled data without requiring continuity assumptions, making it more stable during training and less sensitive to weight initialization. The method demonstrates strong empirical performance with 10x fewer parameters and guaranteed convergence across various confounding and sampling settings.

## Method Summary
The paper proposes S4Model, a deep structured state space model for counterfactual outcome prediction in longitudinal data. The method processes irregularly sampled sequences using a sequence-to-sequence architecture with 4 layers and latent dimension 256. S4Model decomposes the HiPPO matrix into normal plus low-rank representation, reducing computational complexity from O(N²L) to O(N). The architecture includes linear transformations for treatment and outcome prediction, with optional domain adversarial training to remove bias from time-dependent confounders. The model is trained using Adam optimizer with learning rate 0.0005 for A,B,C parameters and 0.00002 for other parameters, over 50 epochs on synthetic lung tumor growth data generated via PK-PD model and Hawkes process.

## Key Results
- S4Model achieves 10x better normalized mean squared error compared to TE-CDE baseline
- S4Model reduces per-epoch training time by 1.63x while using 10x fewer parameters
- S4Model demonstrates greater stability during training and reduced sensitivity to weight initialization

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

- What specific factors contribute to the performance gap between S4Model and TE-CDE?
  - Basis: The authors state "we need further investigation to understand the factors that affect the performance gap between our model and TE-CDE"
  - Why unresolved: While the authors demonstrate S4Model's superior performance, they do not provide a detailed analysis of why TE-CDE underperforms in various settings.
  - What evidence would resolve it: A comprehensive ablation study comparing the effects of different model components on performance.

- How does S4Model perform on real-world medical datasets compared to synthetic data?
  - Basis: The authors use only simulated data for evaluation, despite mentioning real-world applications in healthcare.
  - Why unresolved: The paper focuses on a simulated lung tumor growth dataset and does not validate the model on actual clinical data.
  - What evidence would resolve it: Application of S4Model to real electronic health record (EHR) or clinical trial data with known outcomes.

- Can S4Model be effectively applied to sparse longitudinal data with limited observations?
  - Basis: The authors mention future work on examining "applicability of the state space model under a sparse data setting"
  - Why unresolved: The current evaluation uses datasets with sufficient observations, and the authors acknowledge this limitation.
  - What evidence would resolve it: Experiments comparing S4Model's performance on datasets with varying levels of sparsity and observation frequency.

## Limitations

- The paper relies on simulated data from a PK-PD tumor growth model, raising questions about external validity to real-world medical applications.
- The performance comparison with TE-CDE lacks implementation details for the baseline, making it difficult to assess the robustness of the 10x improvement claim.
- The method's effectiveness for very sparse longitudinal data remains unexplored, despite being mentioned as future work.

## Confidence

- High Confidence (5/5): Claims about S4Model's computational efficiency (1.63x training speedup, 10x fewer parameters) are well-supported by the structured state space formulation and complexity analysis.
- Medium Confidence (3/5): Claims about counterfactual outcome prediction performance are moderately supported by synthetic experiments but require validation on real-world medical datasets to establish clinical relevance.
- Low Confidence (2/5): Claims about robustness to initialization and training stability are based on limited experimental evidence and require more systematic ablation studies.
