# Mixture-of-Domain-Adapters: Decoupling and Injecting Domain Knowledge to Pre-trained Language Models Memories

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2306.05406
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.05406
- Reference count: 31
- Primary result: MixDA achieves superior performance on in-domain tasks (GLUE), out-of-domain tasks (ChemProt, RCT, IMDB, Amazon), and knowledge-intensive tasks (KILT) with only 0.7% parameter overhead.

## Executive Summary
This paper presents Mixture-of-Domain-Adapters (MixDA), a parameter-efficient method for adapting pre-trained language models to specific domains by injecting domain-specific knowledge into the feed-forward networks (FFNs) of the Transformer architecture. MixDA employs a two-stage adapter-tuning strategy: first tuning domain-adapters on unlabeled data to capture domain-specific knowledge, then tuning task-adapters and a mixture-of-adapters (MoA) gate on labeled data for task-specific adaptation. The approach demonstrates superior performance across various domains and tasks while adding minimal parameters and preserving general knowledge through FFN decoupling.

## Method Summary
MixDA decouples the FFNs of pre-trained language models into frozen original FFNs and domain-specific adapters that learn domain knowledge in parallel. The method uses a two-stage training strategy: Stage 1 trains domain-adapters on unlabeled domain data while freezing all other parameters, and Stage 2 trains task-adapters and an MoA gate on labeled task data. The MoA gate dynamically weights outputs from different domain-adapters and FFNs based on input, enabling flexible knowledge combination. The approach is implemented on RoBERTa-large with domain adapters in specific layers (7 and 11) and achieves efficient adaptation with only 0.7% parameter overhead.

## Key Results
- Achieves superior performance on in-domain tasks (GLUE) compared to standard fine-tuning and adapter baselines
- Demonstrates effectiveness on out-of-domain tasks (ChemProt, RCT, IMDB, Amazon) showing strong generalization
- Shows significant improvements on knowledge-intensive tasks (KILT) by leveraging structured and unstructured domain knowledge
- Maintains parameter efficiency with only 0.7% additional parameters while preserving general knowledge

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: Decoupling FFNs into original and domain-adapter parts enables knowledge preservation and injection without catastrophic forgetting
- Mechanism: The original pre-trained FFNs are frozen and maintain old-domain knowledge while domain-adapters learn new domain-specific knowledge in parallel
- Core assumption: Knowledge is stored in FFNs and can be separated into distinct components for different domains
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "we decouple the feed-forward networks (FFNs) of the Transformer architecture into two parts: the original pre-trained FFNs to maintain the old-domain knowledge and our novel domain-specific adapters to inject domain-specific knowledge in parallel"
  - [section 3.1] "Previous studies (Geva et al., 2021; Cao et al., 2021; Meng et al., 2022) suggest that factual associations are stored in the FFNs of some Transformer layers"
- Break condition: If knowledge is not primarily stored in FFNs or if the parallel structure causes interference between old and new knowledge

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: Two-stage training strategy enables effective domain adaptation while preserving general knowledge
- Mechanism: Stage 1 trains domain-adapters on unlabeled data to capture domain-specific knowledge while keeping FFNs frozen; Stage 2 trains task-adapters and MoA gate on labeled data for task-specific adaptation
- Core assumption: Domain knowledge can be learned separately from task-specific knowledge, and unlabeled data is sufficient for domain knowledge acquisition
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "Our proposed Mixture-of-Domain-Adapters (MixDA) employs a two-stage adapter-tuning strategy that leverages both unlabeled data and labeled data to help the domain adaptation: i) domain-specific adapter on unlabeled data; followed by ii) the task-specific adapter on labeled data"
  - [section 3.1] "In Stage 1, the domain-adapter is trained with the domain-specific and old-domain datasets in one batch. Note that all other parameters are frozen except the domain-adapter at this stage"
- Break condition: If unlabeled data is insufficient for domain knowledge acquisition or if task-specific knowledge interferes with domain knowledge

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: Mixture-of-Adapters gate enables dynamic knowledge fusion and scalability across multiple domains
- Mechanism: MoA gate learns to weight outputs from different domain-adapters and FFNs based on input, allowing flexible knowledge combination and easy addition of new domains
- Core assumption: Different domains have complementary knowledge and the MoA gate can learn optimal combinations for different inputs
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "Then we adopt a mixture-of-adapters gate to fuse the knowledge from different domain adapters dynamically"
  - [section 3.3] "The MoA gate receives the outputs from the attention layer q, the domain-adapter K, and the FFN F. q is first sent into a multi-layer perceptron (MLP): h = MLP(q)"
- Break condition: If the MoA gate cannot learn effective weighting strategies or if combining knowledge from too many domains causes degradation

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map: RoBERTa-large base model → frozen FFN layers + domain-adapters (Stage 1) → MoA gate + task-adapters (Stage 2)
- Critical path: Unlabeled domain data → Stage 1 domain-adapter training → Labeled task data → Stage 2 MoA gate and task-adapter training → Inference
- Design tradeoffs: Decoupling FFNs adds minimal parameters (0.7% overhead) but requires two-stage training vs. single-stage fine-tuning; parallel domain-adapters enable scalability but add complexity in MoA gate
- Failure signatures: Poor performance on in-domain tasks (MoA gate not learning effective combinations); catastrophic forgetting (domain-adapters interfering with FFNs); scalability issues (MoA gate overwhelmed with too many domain-adapters)
- First 3 experiments:
  1. Implement single domain-adapter on biomedical domain and test on ChemProt dataset
  2. Add MoA gate and test parallel domain-adapters on review and biomedical domains
  3. Compare MixDA with Pfeiffer adapter baseline on GLUE tasks using few-shot setting

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- Question: How does the performance of MixDA scale with the size and diversity of the domain-specific knowledge corpus used in Stage 1 training?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper mentions using unstructured knowledge from domains like biomedical, reviews, and GLUE tasks, but does not systematically explore the impact of varying corpus sizes or diversity.
- Why unresolved: The paper focuses on demonstrating effectiveness rather than exploring the limits of scaling domain knowledge.
- What evidence would resolve it: Experiments comparing MixDA performance using different sizes and diversity levels of domain-specific corpora in Stage 1.

### Open Question 2
- Question: What is the optimal placement of domain adapters within the Transformer layers for different types of tasks and domains?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper uses Layers 7 and 11 for domain adapters, but notes this is a design choice without systematic exploration of alternatives.
- Why unresolved: The paper does not investigate how adapter placement affects performance across different domains and tasks.
- What evidence would resolve it: Comparative experiments placing domain adapters in different Transformer layers and measuring impact on various task types.

### Open Question 3
- Question: How does MixDA's performance compare to other parameter-efficient fine-tuning methods when applied to very large language models (e.g., models with >1B parameters)?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper mentions that MixDA is efficient but does not test it on very large models or compare extensively with other PEFT methods at scale.
- Why unresolved: The experiments focus on RoBERTa-large (355M parameters) and do not explore scaling to much larger models.
- What evidence would resolve it: Performance comparison of MixDA versus other PEFT methods on models with >1B parameters across various domains.

## Limitations

- Knowledge storage assumption: The claim that factual associations are primarily stored in FFNs and can be cleanly decoupled remains empirically uncertain and needs more validation.
- Two-stage training sufficiency: The approach assumes unlabeled domain data is sufficient for domain knowledge acquisition, which may not hold for highly specialized domains with limited data.
- MoA gate scalability: Performance degradation with many domain-adapters is not extensively tested, and the gating mechanism's behavior with conflicting domain knowledge is unclear.

## Confidence

- High confidence: MixDA's parameter efficiency (0.7% overhead) and superior performance on standard benchmarks (GLUE, ChemProt, KILT) are well-supported by experimental results.
- Medium confidence: The two-stage training strategy's effectiveness and the MoA gate's dynamic knowledge fusion capabilities are supported but need more extensive ablation studies.
- Low confidence: The fundamental claim about FFNs being the primary knowledge storage location and the clean separability of domain knowledge requires more direct evidence.

## Next Checks

1. **Knowledge localization study**: Perform ablation experiments removing domain-adapters from different Transformer layers to empirically validate FFNs as the primary knowledge storage location.

2. **Cross-domain interference analysis**: Systematically evaluate MixDA's performance when domain-adapters contain conflicting knowledge (e.g., biomedical vs. legal domains) to test the MoA gate's conflict resolution capabilities.

3. **Data efficiency validation**: Compare MixDA's performance with varying amounts of unlabeled domain data to establish the minimum data requirements for effective domain adaptation and identify potential failure points.
