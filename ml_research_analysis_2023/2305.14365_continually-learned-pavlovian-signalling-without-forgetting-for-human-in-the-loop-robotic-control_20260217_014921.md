# Continually Learned Pavlovian Signalling Without Forgetting for Human-in-the-Loop Robotic Control

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2305.14365
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.14365
- Authors: 
- Reference count: 23
- Primary result: Look-ahead state representations can mitigate forgetting in TD prediction learning for robotic feedback, but physical system constraints and human factors remain significant challenges.

## Executive Summary
This paper addresses the problem of Pavlovian signalling for human-in-the-loop robotic control, where an agent learns to provide feedback to a human operator to avoid workspace contact. Previous on-policy methods suffered from forgetting when predictions were no longer experienced, while off-policy approaches failed to provide the expected solution. The study demonstrates that using a look-ahead state representation in TD(0.9) learning can effectively prevent forgetting by updating predictions on future timesteps rather than the current state. However, even with this improvement, human trials still experienced contact events due to system delays and variability in human control.

## Method Summary
The study used a Bento Arm robotic platform with 5 degrees of freedom and conductive workspace edges detected via Arduino analog input. TD learning algorithms (TD(0), TD(0.9), GTD) with tile-coding state representation were implemented to predict contact events and trigger audio feedback. The key innovation was look-ahead TD, where predictions were updated based on future timesteps rather than current states. Automated and human-controlled trials were conducted with 50 back-and-forth motions each, comparing standard TD, off-policy GTD, and look-ahead TD variants to evaluate their effectiveness in preventing workspace contact.

## Key Results
- TD(0.9) learned to avoid workspace contact quickly but still experienced forgetting when predictions were no longer experienced
- Off-policy GTD did not overcome the forgetting problem seen in on-policy methods
- TD(0.9) with 2-bin look-ahead representation performed best, avoiding further contact in automated trials
- Human trials with look-ahead TD still experienced contact events, likely due to system delays and human control variability

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: Look-ahead TD reduces forgetting by updating predictions on a future timestep, avoiding direct exposure to the state that would cause unlearning.
- Mechanism: The agent learns on timestep t but uses the prediction from timestep t+lookahead for signaling. Since the signaling timestep's prediction isn't being updated during learning, it retains the memory of contact boundaries even when the arm avoids them.
- Core assumption: Physical system delays and servo play do not cause the arm to drift into contact before the look-ahead timestep's prediction is used.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "we instead identified beneficial properties of a look-ahead state representation that made existing approaches able to learn (and not forget) predictions"
  - [section] "by using the prediction a specified number of bins ahead of the active bin in the state space, this method should make it impossible for the predictions being used to signal to diminish since they are not physically visited by the system"
- Break condition: If the look-ahead value is too small, the arm drifts into contact before the prediction timestep; if too large, the prediction becomes irrelevant due to accumulated uncertainty.

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: Off-policy GTD fails to prevent forgetting because the reduced overlap between target and behavior policies limits the learning signal for contact boundaries.
- Mechanism: GTD learns about the target policy (desired motion direction) but experiences samples from the behavior policy (actual motion). When the arm successfully avoids contact, the behavior policy produces fewer samples near contact boundaries, weakening the target policy's learning of those states.
- Core assumption: The behavior and target policies are sufficiently different that learning becomes unstable when contact is avoided.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "off-policy learning did not provide the expected solution to the forgetting problem"
  - [section] "when the system motion stops or changes direction the observations do not impact learning about motion in the direction it was previously travelling"
- Break condition: If the behavior and target policies align perfectly, off-policy learning would function as on-policy and show no forgetting.

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: TD(0.9) with standard representation suffers from value decay across repeated motions, causing predictions to drop below the signaling threshold.
- Mechanism: As the arm repeatedly visits the same bins without contact, the temporal-difference error diminishes, causing the weight updates to reduce the predicted value until it no longer triggers the signal.
- Core assumption: The tile-coding representation has sufficient resolution to distinguish between states near and far from contact boundaries.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "TD(0.9) learned to avoid workspace contact quickly but still forgot predictions when they were no longer experienced"
  - [section] "upon close inspection what happens to the predictions is they decay in the bins being visited, but not the ones being used"
- Break condition: If the representation includes features that persist across time (like velocity thresholds) or if the reward function penalizes value decay, forgetting would be reduced.

  - Why needed here: The state space is continuous (joint positions and velocities), requiring discretization for practical learning with limited samples

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map:
  Bento Arm (5-DOF robotic arm with conductive rod) -> Workspace (conductive taped square with Arduino analog input detection) -> Control interface (Xbox 360 thumb-stick for human, motion sequencer for automated) -> Learning module (TD or GTD algorithms with tile-coding representation) -> Signaling system (audio feedback for human, motion control for automated) -> Prediction threshold (400, constant across conditions)

- Critical path:
  1. Arm moves and reports position/velocity to learning module
  2. Learning module updates predictions based on contact or prediction threshold
  3. If threshold exceeded, signaling system activates (audio or motion change)
  4. Arm continues until contact or trial completion

- Design tradeoffs:
  - Fixed threshold vs adaptive threshold: Fixed simplifies comparison but may not suit all users
  - Look-ahead distance: Longer look-ahead prevents forgetting but increases prediction uncertainty
  - Representation granularity: Finer bins distinguish states better but require more samples to learn

- Failure signatures:
  - Gradual increase in contacts over trials (forgetting problem)
  - Predictions dropping below threshold despite proximity to contact
  - Inconsistent arm motion at extremes (servo play)
  - Human participant missing signals due to audio delay

- First 3 experiments:
  1. Compare TD(0) vs TD(0.9) with automated motion to establish baseline forgetting behavior
  2. Implement one-bin look-ahead TD with automated motion to verify mechanism against forgetting
  3. Test two-bin look-ahead TD with human participant to evaluate practical effectiveness under system delays

## Limitations

- The study was conducted on a specific 5-DOF robotic arm with conductive contact detection, limiting generalizability to other platforms
- Human trials still experienced contact events despite the look-ahead mechanism, suggesting system delays and human control variability remain significant challenges
- The paper does not explore alternative off-policy algorithms or investigate the specific failure mode of GTD in depth

## Confidence

- High Confidence: The forgetting problem in standard TD(0.9) learning is well-established through multiple trials and consistent with prior literature on TD prediction learning
- Medium Confidence: The look-ahead mechanism's effectiveness in automated trials, though promising, needs verification across more diverse robotic platforms and control policies
- Low Confidence: The claim that off-policy GTD failed to prevent forgetting due to policy mismatch, as the study did not explore alternative off-policy algorithms or investigate the specific failure mode in depth
