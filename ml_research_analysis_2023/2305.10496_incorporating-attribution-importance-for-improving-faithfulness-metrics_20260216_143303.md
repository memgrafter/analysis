# Incorporating Attribution Importance for Improving Faithfulness Metrics

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2305.10496
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.10496
- Reference count: 23
- Key outcome: Soft erasure yields consistently higher diagnosticity scores (0.529 vs 0.394 for hard-comprehensiveness) across multiple NLP tasks and attribution methods

## Executive Summary
This paper addresses the fundamental challenge of evaluating faithfulness in feature attribution methods for NLP models. Traditional approaches use hard token removal or retention, which can be overly harsh or lenient in measuring attribution quality. The authors propose a soft erasure approach that proportionally masks token embeddings based on their importance scores, providing a more nuanced evaluation framework. The method demonstrates improved diagnosticity scores and better alignment with human judgments across multiple attribution methods and datasets.

## Method Summary
The paper introduces a soft erasure criterion that interpolates between original and zeroed token embeddings based on attribution importance scores. Instead of completely removing or retaining tokens as in hard removal methods, soft erasure uses a linear interpolation where less important tokens are partially masked. This approach is evaluated using two standard faithfulness metrics - comprehensiveness and sufficiency - showing consistently higher diagnosticity scores (0.529 for soft-comprehensiveness vs 0.394 for hard-comprehensiveness) across multiple NLP tasks and attribution methods including LIME, Gradient-based, and Integrated Gradients approaches.

## Key Results
- Soft erasure achieves 0.529 diagnosticity score for comprehensiveness compared to 0.394 for hard removal
- The approach shows consistent improvements across multiple attribution methods (LIME, Gradient, Integrated Gradients)
- Soft erasure demonstrates better alignment with human judgments while maintaining computational efficiency

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The soft erasure approach works by recognizing that hard removal creates artificial discontinuities in model behavior that don't reflect real-world attribution dynamics. By interpolating token embeddings proportionally to their importance scores, the method creates a more gradual degradation of model performance that better captures the true causal relationship between features and predictions. This smoother transition allows for more accurate measurement of attribution faithfulness by avoiding the extreme sensitivity to token presence/absence that characterizes hard removal methods.

## Foundational Learning
- **Token embedding interpolation**: Understanding how to smoothly transition between original and masked embeddings is crucial for soft erasure implementation
- **Faithfulness metrics**: Comprehending comprehensiveness and sufficiency as evaluation frameworks for attribution methods
- **Pairwise correlation analysis**: The evaluation relies on measuring correlations between metric scores and human judgments
- **Attribution method diversity**: Familiarity with LIME, Gradient-based, and Integrated Gradients methods is needed to understand the empirical validation
- **Transformer embedding mechanics**: Understanding how token embeddings interact in transformer architectures helps contextualize the soft erasure approach

## Limitations
- Empirical validation limited to three attribution methods and four datasets, potentially limiting generalizability
- Assumes linear interpolation between embeddings may not capture complex non-linear interactions in transformer models
- Computational overhead compared to hard removal is not quantified
- Stability of correlation metrics across random seeds or model checkpoints is unexamined

## Confidence
High confidence: The core methodological contribution of soft erasure and its mathematical formulation is well-grounded and internally consistent.

Medium confidence: The empirical improvements in diagnosticity scores across the tested attribution methods and datasets are robust within the experimental setup, but may not generalize to all NLP tasks or model architectures.

Low confidence: The claim that soft erasure "better reflects the actual model behavior" relies on correlation with human judgments without establishing ground truth causal relationships in model internals.

## Next Checks
1. Test soft erasure across additional attribution methods including attention-based approaches and test time intervention techniques to verify the claimed robustness.

2. Conduct ablation studies varying the interpolation function (e.g., exponential, sigmoid) rather than linear interpolation to determine optimal soft erasure formulation.

3. Measure computational overhead and runtime performance compared to hard erasure across different model scales (BERT, RoBERTa, larger transformers) to assess practical deployment viability.
