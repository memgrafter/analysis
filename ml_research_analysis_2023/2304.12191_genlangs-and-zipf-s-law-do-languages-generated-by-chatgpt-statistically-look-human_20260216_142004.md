# "Genlangs" and Zipf's Law: Do languages generated by ChatGPT statistically look human?

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2304.12191
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2304.12191
- Reference count: 0
- Three genlangs (Voxphera, Vivenzia, Lumivoxa) were generated and analyzed for their word frequency distributions

## Executive Summary
This study investigates whether languages generated by ChatGPT (genlangs) follow Zipf's law, a statistical pattern observed in human languages where word frequency is inversely proportional to rank. Three genlangs were created and their word frequency distributions analyzed. The results show that genlangs adhere closely to Zipf's law, with correlation coefficients ranging from -0.925 to -0.967, similar to natural and artificial human languages. The combined genlangs' Zipfian curve closely resembled that of a human-authored English novel, suggesting that genlangs statistically look human.

## Method Summary
The researchers generated three fictional languages (Voxphera, Vivenzia, and Lumivoxa) using ChatGPT. They then analyzed the word frequency distributions of these genlangs and compared them to Zipf's law, a statistical pattern observed in human languages. The study calculated correlation coefficients between the observed word frequencies and the expected Zipfian distribution. Additionally, they compared the combined genlangs' word frequency distribution to that of a human-authored English novel to assess similarity.

## Key Results
- Genlangs (Voxphera, Vivenzia, and Lumivoxa) adhere closely to Zipf's law, with correlation coefficients ranging from -0.925 to -0.967
- The combined genlangs' Zipfian curve closely resembles that of a human-authored English novel
- The results suggest that genlangs statistically look human, supporting the hypothesis that they reflect the statistical properties of human languages

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The mechanism behind genlangs adhering to Zipf's law likely stems from the statistical patterns present in the training data used to develop ChatGPT. As a language model trained on a vast corpus of human-generated text, ChatGPT has learned and internalized the statistical properties of natural languages, including the Zipfian distribution of word frequencies. When generating new languages, the model applies these learned patterns, resulting in genlangs that exhibit similar statistical properties to human languages.

## Limitations
- The study is based on a small sample of three generated languages, which may not be representative of ChatGPT's broader language generation capabilities
- The analysis focuses solely on word frequency distributions and Zipf's law, potentially overlooking other important linguistic features
- The comparison with a single human-authored English novel may not adequately represent the diversity of natural language patterns

## Confidence
- High confidence in the adherence of genlangs to Zipf's law based on the observed correlations (-0.925 to -0.967) and similarity to human language patterns
- Medium confidence in the generalizability of results due to the limited sample size and scope of analysis
- Low confidence in the ability to definitively conclude that genlangs "statistically look human" based solely on Zipf's law adherence
