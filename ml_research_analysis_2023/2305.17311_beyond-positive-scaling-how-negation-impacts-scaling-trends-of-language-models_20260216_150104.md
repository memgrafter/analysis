# Beyond Positive Scaling: How Negation Impacts Scaling Trends of Language Models

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2305.17311
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.17311
- **Reference count:** 31
- **Key outcome:** Language models exhibit inverse, U-shaped, or positive scaling on negation tasks depending on prompting method and model family, explained by task decomposition into question answering and negation understanding subtasks.

## Executive Summary
This paper introduces NeQA, a dataset of questions with negation designed to study how language models handle negation. The authors demonstrate that language models exhibit varying scaling trends—inverse, U-shaped, or positive—depending on the prompting method and model family used. They decompose the task into two subtasks: question answering and negation understanding, finding that the first scales linearly while the second scales sigmoidally with an emergent transition point. This decomposition provides a mechanistic explanation for the observed scaling trends and offers insights into the strengths and weaknesses of different language models when processing negation.

## Method Summary
The authors constructed the NeQA dataset by applying rule-based negation transformations to existing QA datasets (NegatedLAMA and OBQA), resulting in 1,718 questions. They evaluated multiple model families (GPT-3, GPT-3 Text Series, Cohere, Jurassic) using three prompting methods (zero-shot, zero-shot with hint, few-shot with chain-of-thought). The evaluation measured accuracy on multiple-choice questions with two answer choices. Task decomposition analysis was performed by separating the original non-negated questions (Task 1) from the negation understanding component (Task 2), and their scaling behaviors were composed to explain the final observed trends.

## Key Results
- Language models show inverse, U-shaped, or positive scaling on negation tasks depending on prompting and model family
- Task decomposition reveals Task 1 (question answering) scales linearly positive while Task 2 (negation understanding) scales sigmoidally with an emergent transition point
- Stronger prompts and larger model families shift the negation understanding transition point earlier, changing the overall scaling shape
- The composition of the two subtask scaling trends accurately predicts the final scaling behavior observed in NeQA

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
Language models exhibit inverse, U-shaped, or positive scaling on negation tasks depending on prompting method and model family. The scaling trend is determined by the composition of two subtasks—question answering and negation understanding. Task 1 scales linearly positive, Task 2 scales sigmoidally with an emergent transition point, and their composition yields the final scaling trend. The performance of the composed task can be modeled as a product of the two subtask performances: `t(x) = t1(x) * s2(x) + (1 - t1(x)) * (1 - s2(x))`, where `s2(x)` is the negation understanding signal. Break condition: If the subtasks are not independent (e.g., shared representations that interfere), the composition formula fails and the scaling trend cannot be predicted by multiplying subtask curves.

### Mechanism 2
Stronger prompts or larger model families shift the transition point of negation understanding earlier, changing the scaling shape. Prompt strength and model capacity control when the sigmoid transition in Task 2 occurs. Earlier transition points move the composed task from inverse to U-shape to positive scaling. Break condition: If the transition point is not smooth or is multimodal, the simple earlier/later narrative breaks down.

### Mechanism 3
Task decomposition provides a generalizable way to understand complex scaling trends in multi-component tasks. Any task that can be decomposed into subtasks with different scaling behaviors can have its overall scaling trend predicted by composing the subtask trends. Break condition: If the task cannot be cleanly decomposed or if subtask interactions are non-linear, the decomposition fails to predict the overall scaling trend.

## Limitations
- Task decomposition analysis relies on a simplified multiplicative model of subtask interactions that may not hold for all complex tasks
- Dataset construction through rule-based negation transformations may introduce artifacts that bias observed scaling trends
- Analysis focuses on multiple-choice questions with binary answers, which may not fully capture negation processing nuances in more open-ended contexts

## Confidence

**High Confidence:** The observation that different prompting methods and model families exhibit varying scaling trends (inverse, U-shaped, positive) on the NeQA dataset is well-supported by the experimental results.

**Medium Confidence:** The proposed task decomposition into question answering and negation understanding as an explanatory mechanism is plausible but depends on the assumption of subtask independence.

**Low Confidence:** The generalizability of the task decomposition framework to other complex tasks beyond NeQA remains speculative.

## Next Checks
