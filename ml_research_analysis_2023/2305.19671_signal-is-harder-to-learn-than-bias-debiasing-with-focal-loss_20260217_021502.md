# Signal Is Harder To Learn Than Bias: Debiasing with Focal Loss

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2305.19671
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.19671
- Reference count: 24
- Key result: SiH achieves 71.63% accuracy on Colored MNIST with 0.5% bias-conflicting samples, outperforming second-best method at 63.98%

## Executive Summary
This paper tackles spurious correlations in deep learning by proposing Signal is Harder (SiH), a variational autoencoder-based method that trains biased and unbiased classifiers simultaneously. The approach leverages the assumption that signal is harder to learn than bias, using focal loss to identify and upweight bias-conflicting samples for the unbiased classifier. The method also includes a VAE component for visualizing bias by perturbing latent bias embeddings and reconstructing debiased images.

## Method Summary
SiH uses a variational autoencoder architecture with separate signal and bias encoders, a shared decoder, and two classifiers. The biased classifier is trained with generalized cross-entropy loss on bias-aligned samples, while the unbiased classifier uses focal loss-based reweighting to focus on bias-conflicting samples that the biased classifier struggles with. During inference, latent adversarial perturbations inspired by DeepFool are applied to bias embeddings to visualize and remove bias in reconstructed images.

## Key Results
- On Colored MNIST with 0.5% bias-conflicting samples, SiH achieves 71.63% accuracy vs 63.98% for second-best method
- SiH matches or improves upon state-of-the-art debiasing methods across various bias-conflicting percentages (0.5% to 20%)
- Bias visualization approach produces fewer artifacts than previous methods while maintaining conservative bias removal

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: Focal loss upweights bias-conflicting samples by measuring difficulty the biased classifier has in predicting them
- Mechanism: Unbiased classifier trained with weighting factor derived from (1 - predicted probability of biased classifier)^q
- Core assumption: Bias-conflicting samples are harder for biased classifier to predict correctly than bias-aligned samples
- Evidence: Abstract states focal loss identifies and upweights bias-conflicting samples; section 3 describes using loss to emphasize samples biased classifier struggles with

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: Training VAE alongside classifiers enables visualization of bias through latent space perturbations
- Mechanism: Bias embeddings perturbed at inference to remove bias information, then passed through decoder for debiased reconstructions
- Core assumption: VAE can learn disentangled signal and bias representations that can be independently manipulated
- Evidence: Abstract mentions VAE allows visualizing bias through perturbing latent bias embeddings; section 3 proposes visualization approach by perturbing bias at inference

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: "Signal is harder to learn than bias" assumption enables effective separation of samples for unbiased classifier training
- Mechanism: Biased classifier learns from bias-aligned samples while unbiased classifier focuses on harder signal through focal loss weighting
- Core assumption: Spurious correlations (bias) are more easily learnable patterns than underlying signal attributes
- Evidence: Abstract mentions using focal loss to identify bias-conflicting samples; section 1 states assumption that malignant bias attributes are easier to learn than underlying signal

## Foundational Learning

- Concept: Focal Loss for addressing class imbalance
  - Why needed here: Focal loss upweights hard-to-classify samples, which in this context are bias-conflicting samples that biased classifier struggles with
  - Quick check question: What happens to weight of sample that biased classifier already predicts with high confidence when using focal loss?

- Concept: Variational Autoencoder for disentangled representation learning
  - Why needed here: VAE learns separate signal and bias embeddings that can be independently manipulated for visualization purposes
  - Quick check question: How does KL divergence term in VAE loss encourage learning of meaningful latent representations?

- Concept: Adversarial perturbations for minimal input modification
  - Why needed here: DeepFool-inspired perturbations remove bias from embeddings with minimal changes to avoid creating artifacts in reconstructions
  - Quick check question: What norm is typically used to measure size of adversarial perturbations in DeepFool?

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map:
  - Signal encoder (Es) -> Signal classifier (Cs) -> Signal embeddings (zs)
  - Bias encoder (Eb) -> Bias classifier (Cb) -> Bias embeddings (zb)
  - Decoder (D) takes concatenated [zs; zb] to reconstruct input
  - Biased classifier trained with GCE loss on zb
  - Unbiased classifier trained with focal-loss-weighted CE on zs

- Critical path:
  1. Input -> Es + Eb -> zs + zb -> Cs + Cb -> Predictions
  2. zs + zb -> D -> Reconstruction
  3. During training: weighted CE losses for Cs and Cb

- Design tradeoffs:
  - Using focal loss vs. RDS weighting: focal loss is simpler but requires choosing q hyperparameter
  - VAE reconstruction weight (100x) vs. classification performance: higher weight improves visualization but may slow convergence
  - Separate encoders vs. shared encoder: separate allows better disentanglement but increases parameters

- Failure signatures:
  - Poor reconstruction quality suggests VAE not learning useful representations
  - Similar performance to vanilla model indicates reweighting not effective
  - Artifacts in bias visualizations indicate signal-bias entanglement
  - High variance across runs suggests instability in focal loss weighting

- First 3 experiments:
  1. Train with only signal encoder and classifier on Colored MNIST to establish baseline
  2. Add bias encoder and biased classifier with GCE loss, verify bias alignment
  3. Add focal loss weighting to unbiased classifier, measure improvement over GCE-only approach

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- Question: How does proposed reweighting scheme based on focal loss compare to RDS weighting scheme in terms of theoretical guarantees and robustness to different types of biases?
- Basis: Authors compare SiH (focal loss) to SiH RDS (RDS weighting) on Colored MNIST and Corrupted CIFAR-10, showing SiH performs better on average, but lack theoretical analysis of why focal loss is more effective or how it behaves under different bias conditions
- Why unresolved: Paper presents empirical evidence but lacks theoretical explanation for superiority of focal loss over RDS, does not explore conditions under which one method might outperform other or how each method handles different types of biases
- What evidence would resolve it: Theoretical analysis comparing two reweighting schemes under various bias scenarios, including proofs of convergence or robustness guarantees; experiments testing methods on wider range of datasets with different types of biases

### Open Question 2
- Question: How effective is proposed bias visualization method in identifying unknown biases in real-world datasets, and what are its limitations?
- Basis: Authors propose using latent adversarial perturbations to visualize biases by perturbing bias embeddings and reconstructing debiased images, claim method is more conservative than previous approaches avoiding artifacts, but do not provide thorough evaluation of effectiveness in identifying unknown biases in real-world datasets
- Why unresolved: Paper only demonstrates bias visualization method on synthetic datasets (Colored MNIST and Corrupted CIFAR-10) and does not explore performance on real-world datasets with complex and potentially unknown biases; authors do not discuss limitations such as sensitivity to quality of disentangled representations or applicability to different types of biases
- What evidence would resolve it: Experiments applying bias visualization method to real-world datasets with known biases (e.g., ImageNet) to assess ability to identify and visualize these biases accurately; discussion of method's limitations and potential failure cases

### Open Question 3
- Question: How does proposed method scale to more complex datasets and different modalities, such as text or audio data?
- Basis: Authors acknowledge efficacy of SiH on more challenging datasets and other modalities requires further investigation; suggest using more expressive generative models like GANs or diffusion models for future work
- Why unresolved: Paper only evaluates method on simple image datasets (Colored MNIST and Corrupted CIFAR-10) and does not explore performance on more complex datasets or other data modalities; authors do not provide insights into challenges or potential modifications required to adapt method to different data types
- What evidence would resolve it: Experiments applying SiH to more complex image datasets (e.g., ImageNet) and other modalities (e.g., text or audio) to assess scalability and effectiveness; discussion of challenges and potential modifications required for adapting method to different data types

## Limitations

- The foundational assumption that "signal is harder to learn than bias" lacks empirical validation beyond synthetic datasets
- VAE disentanglement approach may not scale well to high-dimensional data or scenarios where signal and bias are deeply intertwined
- Method's effectiveness on real-world datasets with subtle or complex biases remains unverified

## Confidence

- Signal is Harder assumption: Medium - Supported by synthetic experiments but unverified on real-world data
- Focal loss effectiveness: High - Well-established technique in class imbalance literature, though application here is novel
- VAE visualization utility: Medium - Demonstrated on synthetic data but artifacts and disentanglement quality remain concerns
