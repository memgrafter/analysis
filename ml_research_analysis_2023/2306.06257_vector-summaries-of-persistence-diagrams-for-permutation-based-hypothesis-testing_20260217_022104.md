# Vector Summaries of Persistence Diagrams for Permutation-based Hypothesis Testing

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2306.06257
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.06257
- Reference count: 40
- Primary result: Vectorized Betti function achieves competitive hypothesis testing performance with significantly lower computational cost than Wasserstein distances

## Executive Summary
This paper addresses the computational challenges of permutation-based hypothesis testing for persistence diagrams (PDs) when using Wasserstein distances. The authors propose using Lp distances between vector summaries of PDs, specifically introducing a new integration-based vectorization method for the Betti function. They prove stability results for this vectorization with respect to Wasserstein distance and introduce a strong mixing procedure for permuting group labels to increase test power. Through extensive experiments on synthetic and real data, they demonstrate that the vectorized Betti function (VAB) achieves competitive results compared to the baseline Wasserstein distance method while significantly reducing computational cost.

## Method Summary
The paper proposes a permutation-based hypothesis testing framework that replaces Wasserstein distances between persistence diagrams with Lp distances between vector summaries. The key innovation is a new integration-based vectorization method for the Betti function, which averages the function over intervals formed by neighboring scale points rather than evaluating it at grid points. This reduces computational complexity while maintaining topological information. The authors prove stability results showing that small perturbations in PDs lead to proportionally small changes in the Betti function and its vectorization. They also introduce a strong mixing procedure for permuting group labels that maximizes between-group distances to increase test power. The method is evaluated across synthetic benchmarks (circle vs. ellipse, point cloud classification) and real data (graph classification, breast cancer, credit scoring), demonstrating competitive performance with significantly lower computational cost than Wasserstein-based approaches.

## Key Results
- VAB vectorization reduces computational cost by 2-3 orders of magnitude compared to Wasserstein distances while maintaining competitive test power
- Strong mixing procedure consistently increases test power across all experiments, with the optimal number of exchanged diagrams being approximately 1-3 depending on group sizes
- VAB outperforms or matches persistence landscapes and persistence images in most test scenarios while being computationally cheaper
- On real data (graph classification, breast cancer, credit scoring), VAB with strong mixing achieves comparable or better performance than Wasserstein distance baselines

- Core assumption: The integrated average over intervals provides sufficient representation of the Betti function's behavior for hypothesis testing purposes.

- Claim: The strong mixing procedure for permuting group labels increases the power of the hypothesis test by maximizing between-group distances.

- Core assumption: When the null hypothesis is false, permutations that maximize between-group distances will produce test statistics that are more likely to exceed the observed value.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- Question: How do the stability bounds for the Betti function and VAB compare in practice to the theoretical bounds proven in the paper, especially for different weight functions w?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper derives stability results for the Betti function and VAB with respect to the Wasserstein distance, showing bounds involving the infinity norm of w and its gradient.
- Why unresolved: The paper proves theoretical stability bounds but does not provide empirical validation of these bounds across different weight functions and datasets.
- What evidence would resolve it: Experiments measuring the actual Wasserstein distance between PDs versus the L1/L2 distance between their Betti functions or VAB vectors for various weight functions and datasets.

### Open Question 2
- Question: How does the strong mixing procedure for permuting group labels affect the type I error rate in practice, and can this inflation be mitigated while maintaining power gains?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper introduces a strong mixing procedure for permuting group labels and observes it consistently increases test power, but also notes it causes minor inflation of the type-I error rate.
- Why unresolved: The paper demonstrates the power increase but does not systematically study the trade-off between power gains and type-I error inflation, nor does it propose methods to mitigate the inflation.
- What evidence would resolve it: Controlled experiments varying the strength of mixing and measuring both power and type-I error, plus analysis of alternative mixing strategies that might preserve power while controlling type-I error.

### Open Question 3
- Question: How sensitive is the hypothesis testing procedure to the choice of filtration type (Vietoris-Rips, alpha complex, lower-star) when applied to different data types?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper applies the testing procedure using various filtration types (Vietoris-Rips, alpha, lower-star) on different synthetic and real datasets, observing varying performance.
- Why unresolved: While the paper shows different filtrations work better for different data types, it doesn't provide systematic guidelines for choosing the optimal filtration or study the sensitivity of results to filtration choice.
- What evidence would resolve it: Comparative studies across diverse data types measuring test performance using different filtrations, plus analysis of how data characteristics (dimension, noise level, intrinsic dimension) correlate with optimal filtration choice.

## Limitations
- The stability results for VAB rely on specific conditions for the weight function w that may not hold in all applications
- The strong mixing procedure can cause minor inflation of the type-I error rate, though this is typically small in practice
- Performance on high-dimensional data or densely distributed PDs may degrade compared to simpler cases
