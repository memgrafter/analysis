# Curvature-Sensitive Predictive Coding with Approximate Laplace Monte Carlo

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2303.04976
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.04976
- **Reference count:** 40
- **Key outcome:** LMC ELBO with block-diagonal Hessian approximation improves sample quality and log-likelihoods in predictive coding models.

## Executive Summary
This work introduces a Laplace Monte Carlo (LMC) evidence lower bound (ELBO) for predictive coding (PC) in deep generative models, addressing issues of poor sample quality and marginal likelihoods in standard PC training. The key insight is that the standard PC objective neglects a Hessian-based curvature term, which would regularize sharpness and prevent overconfidence in the posterior. The LMC ELBO uses samples from a Laplace-optimal Gaussian posterior and achieves higher log-likelihoods and more diverse samples than standard PC. To address computational challenges, a block-diagonal Hessian approximation is proposed, reducing memory complexity from O(N²) to O(max(n²L, nF·nO)) while maintaining PSD guarantees. A hybrid model combining Dirac and Laplace posteriors further reduces memory usage. Experiments on image datasets show consistent improvements in BPD scores and sample diversity. The method implicitly regularizes sharpness, acting as an Occam's razor and preventing divergence. This bridges PC with modern variational inference, offering a practical, scalable approach to training hierarchical latent variable models.

## Method Summary
The method combines predictive coding with Laplace Monte Carlo (LMC) to incorporate curvature information into variational inference for deep generative models. Standard PC uses a Dirac delta posterior, neglecting Hessian curvature that would regularize sharpness. LMC instead samples from a Laplace-optimal Gaussian posterior with inverse-Hessian covariance, implicitly regularizing sharpness. To make this scalable, a block-diagonal Hessian approximation is proposed, reducing memory complexity while maintaining positive semi-definiteness. A combined model uses PC for lower layers and LMC for higher layers to balance quality and efficiency. Training involves MAP inference, Hessian computation (full or approximate), sampling from the Laplace posterior, and ELBO-based parameter updates.

## Key Results
- LMC consistently outperforms standard PC across multiple image datasets (MNIST, CIFAR-10, SVHN, CelebA) in terms of bits per dimension (BPD) scores and sample diversity
- The block-diagonal Hessian approximation (ALMC) reduces memory complexity from O(N²) to O(max(n²L, nF·nO)) while maintaining positive semi-definiteness
- LMC implicitly regularizes sharpness of the loss landscape, acting as an Occam's razor and preventing training divergence
- Combined models using PC for lower layers and ALMC for higher layers achieve good reconstruction quality with lower memory usage

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: Excluding the Hessian curvature term from the PC objective leads to overfitting in the posterior and increased sharpness of the loss landscape, causing training instability.
- Mechanism: The standard PC objective uses a Dirac delta posterior, ignoring the second-order curvature information. This omission removes regularization on the sharpness of the log joint density, allowing the posterior to become overconfident and the loss landscape to become too sharp, increasing the risk of gradient divergence.
- Core assumption: The sharpness of the loss landscape (measured by the maximum eigenvalue of the Hessian) correlates with training instability in gradient descent.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [section] "It is unsurprising then that one of the practical difficulties that arise with PC is managing the risk of divergence during the inference procedure as training progresses... This increased risk of divergence, can therefore be elegantly explained as a failure to regularise for the curvature of the log joint due to the exclusion of the log determinant Hessian term."
  - [abstract] "The key insight is that the standard PC objective neglects a Hessian-based curvature term, which would regularize sharpness and prevent overconfidence in the posterior."
  - [corpus] Weak - corpus contains general papers on Laplace and Monte Carlo methods but lacks direct comparison of PC with and without Hessian regularization.
- Break condition: If the Hessian term is included but still leads to divergence, suggesting other factors (like step size or model capacity) dominate.

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: Sampling from a Laplace-optimal Gaussian posterior (with inverse-Hessian covariance) during learning regularizes the model by preventing overconfidence and acts as an Occam's razor.
- Mechanism: By sampling from the Laplace-optimal posterior, the model accounts for uncertainty in latent states. This sampling implicitly penalizes overly sharp log joint densities because sharper densities lead to more concentrated posteriors, which are less representative of true uncertainty. This regularization encourages simpler, more robust models.
- Core assumption: The entropy of the Laplace-optimal posterior is inversely related to the sharpness of the log joint, so sampling from it naturally discourages sharpness.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [section] "Furthermore, in section 5 we demonstrate empirically that despite not optimising with respect to the log determinant Hessian directly, our objective function nonetheless successfully regularises for the sharpness of the probability landscape."
  - [abstract] "The method implicitly regularizes sharpness, acting as an Occam's razor and preventing divergence."
  - [corpus] Weak - no direct corpus evidence; the connection between Laplace sampling and Occam's razor is inferred from the text.
- Break condition: If sampling from the Laplace posterior does not reduce sharpness or improves training stability, the mechanism may be invalid.

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: Using a block-diagonal Hessian approximation reduces memory complexity from O(N²) to O(max(n²L, nF·nO)) while maintaining positive semi-definiteness, enabling scalable curvature-aware learning.
- Mechanism: Full Hessian computation scales quadratically with total latent dimensionality N, which is prohibitive for deep networks. By retaining only within-layer curvature information, the block-diagonal approximation drastically reduces memory usage while preserving PSD guarantees, making it computationally feasible to use curvature information in large models.
- Core assumption: Curvature within a layer is more important than cross-layer curvature for regularization, and the approximation remains effective for learning.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [section] "To improve upon the memory complexity of computing the full Hessian matrix required for the Laplace ELBO objective, we present a novel block diagonal approximation to the Hessian that has lower memory complexity, and is guaranteed positive semi-definite."
  - [abstract] "To address computational challenges, a block-diagonal Hessian approximation is proposed, reducing memory complexity from O(N²) to O(max(n²L, nF·nO)) while maintaining PSD guarantees."
  - [corpus] Weak - no direct corpus evidence; the claim is supported by the paper's derivation and experimental setup.
- Break condition: If the block-diagonal approximation fails to capture essential curvature structure, leading to poor performance despite lower memory usage.

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map:
  Generative model -> MAP inference -> Hessian computation -> Laplace sampling -> ELBO objective -> Parameter updates

- Critical path:
  1. Define generative model architecture
  2. For each batch, perform inference to find MAP estimates
  3. Compute Hessian (full or approximate)
  4. Sample from Laplace posterior
  5. Compute ELBO objective (PC, LMC, or ALMC)
  6. Update model parameters via SGD

- Design tradeoffs:
  - Full Hessian vs. block-diagonal: Accuracy vs. memory/computation
  - Combined model vs. pure LMC/ALMC: Training stability and reconstruction quality vs. full curvature awareness
  - Number of Monte Carlo samples (K): Variance of gradient estimates vs. computation

- Failure signatures:
  - Non-PSD Hessians: Often early in training; may require fallback to identity matrix
  - Divergence during inference: Suggests sharpness regularization is insufficient or step size too large
  - Poor sample quality: May indicate inadequate model capacity or insufficient curvature regularization

- First 3 experiments:
  1. Train a simple 2-layer model on MNIST with PC objective; verify standard performance metrics
  2. Replace PC with LMC (full Hessian) on the same model; measure changes in BPD and sharpness
  3. Switch to ALMC (block-diagonal Hessian) and compare memory usage and performance against LMC

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- Question: Does the curvature-regularizing effect of the Laplace Monte Carlo (LMC) objective generalize to other generative modeling tasks beyond image datasets?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper states "Across (almost) all the configurations and image datasets tested, our LMC and ALMC methods consistently outperformed PC in terms of log marginal likelihood and sample quality." It then speculates that "These results serve as a clear demonstration that the PC objective function as commonly presented is insufficient for producing generative models that capture the diversity of the data generating distribution."
- Why unresolved: The experiments only evaluated on image datasets (MNIST, CIFAR10, SVHN, CelebA). The authors did not test LMC on other data types like text, audio, or structured data.
- What evidence would resolve it: Applying LMC to diverse generative modeling tasks (e.g., text generation, audio synthesis, molecular design) and comparing performance against standard PC and other baselines.

### Open Question 2
- Question: What is the impact of the block-diagonal Hessian approximation on the quality of the variational posterior in very deep networks?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The authors derive a "novel block diagonal approximation to the Hessian that has better memory complexity, and is guaranteed positive semi-definite" and note that "Perhaps unexpectedly, the resultant approximate LMC objective consistently performs better than the LMC objective." However, they do not provide theoretical justification for why the approximation improves performance.
- Why unresolved: The paper provides empirical evidence that ALMC outperforms LMC but does not analyze how the approximation affects the quality of the posterior approximation, especially as network depth increases.
- What evidence would resolve it: Theoretical analysis of how the block-diagonal approximation affects the KL divergence between the true and approximate posterior, or empirical studies comparing performance as a function of network depth.

### Open Question 3
- Question: How sensitive is the LMC/ALMC performance to the choice of K (number of Monte Carlo samples)?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper states "We used K = 20 for our LMC and ALMC objectives" but does not explore how performance scales with K or provide guidance on selecting K.
- Why unresolved: The authors do not report results with different K values or analyze the bias-variance tradeoff of using different numbers of samples.
- What evidence would resolve it: Systematic experiments varying K across a wide range (e.g., 1, 5, 10, 20, 50, 100) to identify the optimal number of samples for different dataset sizes and model architectures.

### Open Question 4
- Question: Can the curvature regularization from LMC/ALMC be achieved through alternative methods that don't require computing the Hessian?
- Basis in paper: [inferred] The authors argue that the benefit of LMC comes from "regularising for the sharpness of the probability landscape" but acknowledge computational challenges with Hessian computation. They don't explore whether simpler regularization methods could achieve similar effects.
- Why unresolved: The paper focuses on the Laplace approximation perspective but doesn't investigate whether other regularization techniques (e.g., spectral normalization, sharpness-aware minimization) could provide similar benefits without the computational overhead.
- What evidence would resolve it: Comparative experiments testing alternative sharpness-regularizing methods against LMC/ALMC on the same tasks, or theoretical analysis of the relationship between Hessian-based and other regularization approaches.

## Limitations
- The method's effectiveness is demonstrated primarily on image datasets; generalization to other data types remains unproven
- The block-diagonal Hessian approximation's quality for very deep networks (e.g., >10 layers) has not been thoroughly evaluated
- Computational overhead of Hessian computation, even with approximation, may limit scalability to extremely large models (>100M parameters)

## Confidence
- **High Confidence:** The mathematical derivation of the LMC ELBO and its relationship to the standard PC objective is rigorous and well-supported by the variational inference literature
- **Medium Confidence:** The empirical improvements in BPD scores and sample quality are demonstrated across multiple datasets, but the magnitude of improvement varies significantly between datasets
- **Low Confidence:** The scalability claims for the block-diagonal approximation, while theoretically justified, lack extensive validation on very large models
