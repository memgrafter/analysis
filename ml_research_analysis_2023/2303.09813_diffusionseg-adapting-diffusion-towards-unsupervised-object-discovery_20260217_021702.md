# DiffusionSeg: Adapting Diffusion Towards Unsupervised Object Discovery

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2303.09813
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.09813
- Reference count: 40
- Key outcome: New state-of-the-art results in unsupervised object discovery with 12.5%, 9.6%, and 12.1% IoU improvements on three benchmarks

## Executive Summary
This paper presents DiffusionSeg, a novel synthesis-exploitation framework that adapts pre-trained diffusion models for unsupervised object discovery tasks including saliency segmentation and object localization. The method addresses two key challenges: the structural gap between generative and discriminative models, and the lack of labeled data in unsupervised settings. The framework consists of two stages - a synthesis stage that generates synthetic image-mask pairs using a pre-trained text-to-image diffusion model (Stable Diffusion) and AttentionCut method, followed by an exploitation stage that bridges the structural gap using diffusion inversion to map input images back to diffusion features for training a lightweight decoder.

## Method Summary
DiffusionSeg is a two-stage synthesis-exploitation framework. In the synthesis stage, it generates synthetic image-mask pairs using a pre-trained text-to-image diffusion model (Stable Diffusion) combined with a training-free AttentionCut method that leverages cross- and self-attention maps to create masks. The exploitation stage bridges the structural gap between generative and discriminative models by using diffusion inversion to map input images back to their corresponding diffusion features, which are then used to train a lightweight decoder on the synthetic data. This approach enables unsupervised object discovery without requiring labeled training data.

## Key Results
- Achieves new state-of-the-art performance on six standard benchmarks (ECSSD, DUTS, DUT-OMRON for segmentation; VOC07, VOC12, COCO20K for detection)
- Improves IoU by 12.5%, 9.6%, and 12.1% for saliency segmentation on three benchmarks compared to AttentionCut
- Achieves 75.2%, 78.3%, and 63.1% CorLoc on VOC07, VOC12, and COCO20K respectively for object localization
- Outperforms existing GAN-based approaches on the CUB dataset

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: The diffusion inversion technique enables diffusion models to be used for discriminative tasks by mapping input images back to their corresponding diffusion features.
- Mechanism: The method uses a modified version of DDIM (Denoising Diffusion Implicit Models) that combines the forward/reverse process with conditional denoising UNet to create a deterministic mapping from image to noise under a given conditioning text.
- Core assumption: The modified DDIM process can create a deterministic forward/reverse mapping that preserves enough information to reconstruct discriminative features.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [section]: "We combine inversion technique with diffusion models, to deterministically map the given image back to diffusion features."
  - [abstract]: "In the second exploitation stage, to bridge the structural gap, we use the inversion technique, to map the given image back to diffusion features."
  - [corpus]: Weak - no direct corpus evidence about diffusion inversion for discriminative tasks
- Break condition: If the deterministic mapping loses too much information or cannot be computed efficiently for high-resolution images.

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: The AttentionCut method generates high-quality masks by leveraging cross-attention and self-attention maps from the diffusion model.
- Mechanism: Cross-attention maps indicate locality between conditioning text and image, while self-attention maps indicate semantic similarity between pixels. These are combined with objectness and inner coherence terms to create a unified energy function for mask generation.
- Core assumption: Cross-attention maps provide reasonable objectness estimates, and self-attention maps capture semantic coherence sufficient for accurate segmentation.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [section]: "Cross-attention Ac indicates locality between the conditioning text and noisy image, thus Ac can coarsely describe objectness. Self-attention As inside one image indicates pairwise semantic similarity between pixels, thus As could roughly describe coherence."
  - [abstract]: "We propose a novel training-free AttentionCut to obtain masks in the first synthesis stage."
  - [corpus]: Weak - no direct corpus evidence about using diffusion attention maps for mask generation
- Break condition: If attention maps from diffusion models do not correlate well with object boundaries or semantic regions.

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: The synthetic data generation capability of diffusion models solves the labeled data scarcity problem in unsupervised settings.
- Mechanism: The method uses a pre-trained text-to-image diffusion model to generate abundant synthetic image-mask pairs, where masks are obtained through the AttentionCut method, providing sufficient training data for downstream tasks.
- Core assumption: Synthetic images generated by diffusion models are realistic enough and their corresponding masks are accurate enough to serve as effective training data.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [section]: "We synthesize abundant images, and propose a novel training-free AttentionCut to obtain masks in the first synthesis stage."
  - [abstract]: "To alleviate data insufficiency, we synthesize abundant images, and propose a novel training-free AttentionCut to obtain masks in the first synthesis stage."
  - [corpus]: Weak - no direct corpus evidence about using diffusion-generated data for unsupervised object discovery
- Break condition: If the synthetic data distribution significantly differs from real-world data or the masks are not accurate enough.

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map:
  Pre-trained Stable Diffusion model (frozen) -> Diffusion inversion module (modified DDIM) -> AttentionCut mask generation module -> Segment decoder (lightweight FCN) -> CLIP model for image classification

- Critical path:
  1. Generate synthetic data using pre-trained diffusion model and AttentionCut
  2. Perform diffusion inversion to extract features from input images
  3. Train segment decoder on synthetic data using extracted features
  4. Evaluate on downstream tasks

- Design tradeoffs:
  - Using frozen pre-trained models vs. fine-tuning for better task-specific performance
  - Quality vs. quantity of synthetic data (more data may be less realistic)
  - Complexity of AttentionCut vs. simpler mask generation methods

- Failure signatures:
  - Poor segmentation results indicating issues with either the synthetic data quality or the diffusion inversion
  - High computational cost suggesting the diffusion inversion is not efficient enough
  - Overfitting to synthetic data indicating distribution mismatch

- First 3 experiments:
  1. Generate a small set of synthetic images and manually verify mask quality using AttentionCut
  2. Test diffusion inversion on a few sample images and visualize the reconstructed features
  3. Train the segment decoder on a small synthetic dataset and evaluate on a held-out validation set

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- Question: How does the CLIP-classifiable prior perform on object discovery tasks for categories outside the ImageNet dataset?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The authors mention using ImageNet categories for CLIP classification and acknowledge that combining semantically similar classes is necessary. They also note that DiffusionSeg is robust to the absence of this prior, unlike AttentionCut.
- Why unresolved: The paper focuses on ImageNet categories and does not explore the effectiveness of the CLIP-classifiable prior on categories beyond this dataset. The robustness of DiffusionSeg without the prior is mentioned, but its performance on out-of-distribution categories is not evaluated.
- What evidence would resolve it: Conducting experiments on datasets with categories not present in ImageNet, such as specialized or niche datasets, would provide evidence of the CLIP-classifiable prior's effectiveness and the generalization capability of DiffusionSeg.

### Open Question 2
- Question: What is the impact of varying the number of synthetic image-mask pairs on the performance of DiffusionSeg?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The authors discuss the generation of a large synthetic dataset and mention that increasing the scale of synthetic data improves performance, as shown in Figure 6. However, they do not explore the impact of varying the number of pairs within a specific range.
- Why unresolved: While the paper demonstrates that more synthetic data leads to better performance, it does not investigate the optimal number of image-mask pairs needed for effective training or the point of diminishing returns.
- What evidence would resolve it: Conducting experiments with different sizes of synthetic datasets, such as 10k, 20k, 50k, and 100k pairs, and measuring the performance of DiffusionSeg on each would provide insights into the relationship between dataset size and model performance.

### Open Question 3
- Question: How does DiffusionSeg compare to other generative models, such as GANs, in terms of stability and robustness during training?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The authors mention that diffusion models have advantages over GANs in terms of sample quality, diversity, stability, and robustness. However, they do not provide a direct comparison of training stability and robustness between DiffusionSeg and GAN-based methods.
- Why unresolved: While the paper highlights the advantages of diffusion models over GANs, it does not empirically compare the training stability and robustness of DiffusionSeg with GAN-based approaches on object discovery tasks.
- What evidence would resolve it: Training both DiffusionSeg and a comparable GAN-based method on the same object discovery task and monitoring metrics such as training loss, convergence speed, and sensitivity to hyperparameters would provide evidence of their relative stability and robustness.

## Limitations
- The method relies heavily on the quality of pre-trained diffusion models and their attention mechanisms, which may not generalize well to all object types or domains
- Diffusion inversion may not preserve all discriminative information needed for complex object discovery tasks, particularly for objects with intricate textures or subtle boundaries
- Performance is contingent on the availability of high-quality pre-trained diffusion models and CLIP models, which may not be accessible in all scenarios

## Confidence
- High Confidence: The overall framework design and experimental methodology are well-established. The reported improvements over state-of-the-art methods are substantial and consistent across multiple benchmarks.
- Medium Confidence: The diffusion inversion technique for discriminative tasks, while theoretically plausible, lacks extensive empirical validation beyond the presented results. The quality of synthetic data generated for training is assumed to be sufficient but could vary based on implementation details.
- Medium Confidence: The AttentionCut method's effectiveness relies on the assumption that diffusion model attention maps accurately capture objectness and semantic coherence, which may not hold for all object categories or complex scenes.
