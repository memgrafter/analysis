# One-sided Matrix Completion from Two Observations Per Row

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2306.04049
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.04049
- Reference count: 40
- One-sided matrix completion from two observations per row enables recovery of right singular vectors

## Executive Summary
This paper addresses the challenge of recovering right singular vectors of a low-rank matrix when only two entries per row are observed. Traditional matrix completion methods fail in this setting, but the authors propose an algorithm that imputes the missing entries of X^T X and then factors the result. The approach leverages the randomness in observation patterns to rule out matrices that are too correlated with the observation pattern, enabling recovery even with minimal observations.

## Method Summary
The algorithm imputes missing entries of X^T X by leveraging the structure imposed by having exactly two observations per row. By constructing an estimate of the Gram matrix X^T X and then factoring it, the method recovers the right singular vectors of the original matrix X. The approach is proven to succeed with high probability when the number of rows is at least Ω(r^2 d log d), where r is the rank and d is the number of columns.

## Key Results
- Algorithm recovers X^T X in additive Frobenius error with Ω(r^2 d log d) rows
- Outperforms standard matrix completion and direct factorization methods
- Shows strong performance on both synthetic data and real-world low-coverage genome sequencing data

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The key insight is that having exactly two observations per row, combined with random observation locations across rows, creates sufficient structure to enable recovery. The algorithm exploits this by imputing X^T X, which captures the necessary relationships between columns. The randomness in which columns are observed prevents overly correlated matrices from being valid solutions, while the fixed two-per-row constraint provides enough information to reconstruct the right singular space.

## Architecture Onboarding
Component map: Data → Observation pattern → Imputation → X^T X estimation → Factorization → Right singular vectors

Critical path: For each row, observe 2 entries → impute row into complete matrix → compute X^T X → eigendecomposition → extract right singular vectors

Design tradeoffs: Fixed 2 observations per row vs. adaptive sampling - fixed provides theoretical guarantees but may be suboptimal in practice

Failure signatures: Poor performance when rows are correlated or observation pattern is non-random; degradation when rank is overestimated

## Limitations
- Independence assumption between rows may not hold in structured data
- Requirement for Ω(r^2 d log d) rows may be prohibitive for very large-scale problems
- Performance when rank r is unknown or mis-specified remains unclear

## Confidence
- Theoretical guarantees: High confidence in mathematical derivation, Medium confidence in practical applicability
- Empirical results: Medium confidence - improvements shown but limited evaluation domains
- Scalability claims: Low confidence without larger dataset verification
