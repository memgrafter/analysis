# LaMPP: Language Models as Probabilistic Priors for Perception and Action

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID**: 2302.02801
- **Source URL**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2302.02801
- **Reference count**: 16
- **Primary result**: LAMPP improves performance on rare, out-of-distribution, and structurally novel inputs across perception and control tasks

## Executive Summary
LAMPP introduces a framework for using language models as probabilistic priors in non-linguistic perception and control tasks. The approach casts labeling and decision-making as inference in probabilistic graphical models where LMs parameterize prior distributions over labels, decisions, and parameters. This allows principled integration of uncertain observations with background knowledge. The framework is applied to semantic segmentation, household navigation, and video action recognition tasks, demonstrating consistent improvements on rare categories and out-of-distribution examples.

## Method Summary
LAMPP integrates language models into probabilistic graphical models as priors over labels, decisions, and parameters. The framework formulates perception and control tasks as probabilistic inference problems where language model outputs define prior distributions. These priors are then combined with task-specific likelihood functions and observations through Bayesian inference. The approach is task-agnostic but requires domain-specific likelihood modeling and inference procedures. For each application, the method leverages pretrained models (Mask2Former for segmentation, navigation policies, and action recognition networks) and conditions their outputs on LM-derived priors.

## Key Results
- Semantic segmentation: Mean IoU improves from 47.8 to 48.3 on SUN RGB-D, with shower curtain IoU increasing by +18.9
- Household navigation: Success rate increases from 52.7% to 66.5%
- Video action recognition: Zero-shot recall improves from 44.4% to 45.7%

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
LAMPP works by leveraging language models' broad knowledge to provide structured priors that capture background information and common sense reasoning. The probabilistic formulation allows principled uncertainty quantification and enables the system to leverage LM knowledge even when observations are ambiguous or rare. By treating LMs as sources of prior distributions rather than direct predictors, the framework maintains task-specific optimization while benefiting from LM knowledge. The approach is particularly effective for rare categories because LM priors can provide information that is absent from training data.

## Foundational Learning

**Probabilistic Graphical Models**
- Why needed: Provides mathematical framework for combining uncertain observations with prior knowledge
- Quick check: Can identify conditional independence relationships and perform belief propagation

**Bayesian Inference**
- Why needed: Enables principled combination of prior distributions (from LMs) with likelihood functions
- Quick check: Can compute posterior distributions given priors and observations

**Language Model Knowledge Distillation**
- Why needed: Allows transfer of LM's broad knowledge into structured priors for specific tasks
- Quick check: Can extract meaningful priors from LM outputs that improve downstream task performance

## Architecture Onboarding

**Component Map**: Observation Data -> Likelihood Model -> Probabilistic Fusion Layer -> Prior LM Distribution -> Inference Engine -> Task Output

**Critical Path**: Raw observations are processed by task-specific models to produce likelihoods, which are combined with LM-derived priors through probabilistic inference to produce final outputs

**Design Tradeoffs**: The approach trades computational overhead (LM queries) for improved generalization on rare categories, with the balance depending on the frequency of rare events in the target domain

**Failure Signatures**: Poor performance when LM priors are incorrect or contradictory to observations, computational bottlenecks from frequent LM queries, and degradation when task-specific likelihood models are weak

## Limitations
- Empirical improvements are modest on main metrics (0.5% IoU gain) despite larger gains on rare categories
- Significant computational overhead from language model queries, particularly problematic for real-time applications
- Framework implementations appear tailored to specific tasks rather than truly generalizable across domains

## Confidence
- Core theoretical framework: **High**

- Practical significance of improvements: **Medium**
- Scalability to real-world applications: **Low**
