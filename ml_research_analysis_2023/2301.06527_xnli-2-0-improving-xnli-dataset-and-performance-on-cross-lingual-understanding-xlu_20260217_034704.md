# XNLI 2.0: Improving XNLI dataset and performance on Cross Lingual Understanding (XLU)

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2301.06527

## Executive Summary
This paper presents XNLI 2.0, an improved version of the XNLI cross-lingual natural language inference dataset. The authors re-translate the MNLI training set into 14 languages using Google Translate, creating a new dataset that addresses translation quality issues in the original XNLI. Through experiments with XLM-RoBERTa models trained on each of the 15 languages, they demonstrate that the XNLI 2.0 dataset yields approximately 3% higher accuracy compared to the original dataset, and that high-resource languages like German can outperform English in cross-lingual transfer tasks.

## Method Summary
The authors improve the XNLI dataset by using Google Translate to freshly translate the MNLI dataset into 14 languages, creating XNLI 2.0. They then train separate XLM-RoBERTa models on each of the 15 languages (including English) and evaluate performance on both the original XNLI test set and the newly translated XNLI 2.0 test set. The evaluation focuses on comparing average accuracy improvements and analyzing which high-resource languages transfer best to low-resource languages like Swahili and Urdu.

## Key Results
- Models trained on XNLI 2.0 achieved approximately 76% average accuracy versus 73% on the original XNLI test set
- High-resource languages like German, Greek, and Bulgarian outperformed English in cross-lingual transfer to low-resource languages
- For Swahili, Thai (70.7%), Greek (70.8%), and German (70.4%) all outperformed English (68.7%) on cross-lingual inference

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: Improved translation quality directly increases model accuracy
- Mechanism: Google Translate quality improved significantly since XNLI's original creation, leading to better-aligned semantic representations between source and target languages in the training data
- Core assumption: Translation quality is a primary bottleneck for cross-lingual model performance
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "We have used Google Translate to freshly translate the MNLI dataset to 14 different language training datasets"
  - [section] "As we look deeper into every machine-translated train set, we find that the current cloud translation systems, such as Google Translate, perform at a level far better than that of three years ago"
  - [corpus] Corpus shows multiple related works on translation artifacts, suggesting this is a recognized concern

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: High-resource languages can outperform English for cross-lingual transfer
- Mechanism: Training on certain high-resource languages (German, Greek, Bulgarian) provides better linguistic coverage or semantic representation for transfer to low-resource languages than English
- Core assumption: English is not universally optimal as the source language for cross-lingual transfer
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "models trained on high-resource languages like German can outperform English-trained models on cross-lingual inference"
  - [section] "In the case of Swahili, three languages perform the best and have almost similar accuracy. Thai (70.7%), Greek (70.8%), and German (70.4%) outperform English (68.7%)"
  - [corpus] Corpus includes works on cross-lingual transferability suggesting this is an active research area

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: Separate models per language enable better cross-lingual understanding than single-source training
- Mechanism: Training separate XLM-RoBERTa models on each of the 15 languages captures language-specific nuances that are lost when using English-only training data
- Core assumption: Language-specific training provides better representations than cross-lingual training from a single source
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "We also perform experiments by training models in all 15 languages and analyzing their performance"
  - [section] "Most of the models until now have been trained only in English and evaluated in other languages. However, in this paper, we will try to focus on performing cross-lingual transfer learning by training separate models on each of the 15 languages"
  - [corpus] Corpus includes related work on multilingual models, supporting the premise that separate language models are viable

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map: XLM-RoBERTa (base) model → fine-tuned on MNLI dataset → evaluated on XNLI 2.0 test set → accuracy metrics computed per language pair
- Critical path: Dataset translation quality → model training per language → evaluation on translated test sets → accuracy comparison
- Design tradeoffs: Training separate models per language increases computational cost but may capture language-specific nuances better than single multilingual model
- Failure signatures: Low accuracy improvements despite better translation quality, or specific language pairs showing unexpectedly poor performance
- First 3 experiments:
  1. Train English model on original XNLI dataset and evaluate on both original and XNLI 2.0 test sets to establish baseline improvement
  2. Train German model and compare its performance on Swahili and Urdu test sets against English-trained model
  3. Select one high-resource language (e.g., Greek) and evaluate its performance across all 15 target languages to identify patterns in cross-lingual transfer effectiveness

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- Question: How would XNLI 2.0 perform when used with larger multilingual models like XLM-RoBERTa large instead of the base version?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The authors mention they could not replicate the original XLM-R large model and used their own training parameters, suggesting potential improvements with larger models
- Why unresolved: The paper only reports results using XLM-RoBERTa base, not the larger variant, leaving the performance gains from bigger models unknown
- What evidence would resolve it: Training and evaluating XLM-RoBERTa large on XNLI 2.0 and comparing results to both the base model and the original XNLI dataset

### Open Question 2
- Question: Does XNLI 2.0 show similar improvements in cross-lingual transfer for other NLP tasks like question answering or named entity recognition?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The authors suggest in the conclusion that cross-lingual transfer learning could be significant for other tasks like QA and NER
- Why unresolved: The paper only evaluates improvements on natural language inference, not other downstream tasks
- What evidence would resolve it: Applying XNLI 2.0 training methodology to other multilingual datasets for different NLP tasks and measuring performance improvements

### Open Question 3
- Question: How do XNLI 2.0 results compare when using translation models other than Google Translate, such as newer models like DeepL or GPT-based translators?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The authors chose Google Translate because it performs significantly better than three years ago, but don't compare other modern translation services
- Why unresolved: The paper only evaluates one translation service, potentially missing better alternatives that could further improve results
- What evidence would resolve it: Re-translating the MNLI dataset using multiple modern translation services and comparing resulting model performance on the same benchmark tasks

## Limitations

- Lack of detailed hyperparameter specifications (learning rate, batch size, sequence length) makes exact reproduction challenging
- Only evaluates Google Translate without comparing against other translation systems or human-translated data
- Doesn't address potential bias from using the same translation system for both training and evaluation data

## Confidence

**High Confidence Claims:**
- The XNLI 2.0 dataset achieves approximately 3% higher average accuracy compared to the original XNLI dataset
- Certain high-resource languages (German, Greek, Bulgarian) outperform English in cross-lingual transfer to low-resource languages
- Translation quality improvements in Google Translate over the past three years are significant enough to impact model performance

**Medium Confidence Claims:**
- The improvement in accuracy is primarily due to improved translation quality rather than other factors
- The specific ranking of languages (Thai, Greek, German performing best for Swahili) generalizes beyond the tested conditions
- Separate models per language are more effective than single multilingual models for cross-lingual understanding

**Low Confidence Claims:**
- The methodology would generalize to other cross-lingual tasks beyond NLI
- The observed improvements would persist with different model architectures or translation systems
- The specific percentage improvements (3% overall, 70.7% for specific language pairs) are precisely reproducible

## Next Checks

1. **Translation Quality Validation**: Manually inspect 100 randomly sampled premise-hypothesis pairs from XNLI 2.0 across 3 language pairs (high-resource to low-resource, high-resource to high-resource, low-resource to low-resource) to verify semantic alignment and label consistency. Compare against original XNLI translations to quantify improvement quality.

2. **Hyperparameter Sensitivity Analysis**: Reproduce the German-to-Swahili transfer experiment across three different learning rates (1e-5, 2e-5, 5e-5) and two batch sizes (16, 32) to determine if the observed 70.4% accuracy is robust or sensitive to training configuration.

3. **Translation System Comparison**: Repeat the English-to-Swahili experiment using both Google Translate and another commercial translation service (e.g., Microsoft Translator) to determine if the accuracy improvements are specific to Google Translate or represent a more general translation quality effect.
