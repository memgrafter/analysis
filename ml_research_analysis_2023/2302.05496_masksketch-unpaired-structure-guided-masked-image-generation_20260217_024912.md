# MaskSketch: Unpaired Structure-guided Masked Image Generation

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2302.05496
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2302.05496
- Reference count: 40
- Key outcome: Unpaired sketch-to-photo translation method using pre-trained transformer with attention-based structure guidance outperforms state-of-the-art methods in realism and structure preservation

## Executive Summary
MaskSketch introduces a novel approach for unpaired sketch-to-photo translation by leveraging self-attention maps from a pre-trained masked generative transformer. The method extracts structural information from intermediate attention layers and uses it to guide image generation without requiring paired training data or model fine-tuning. By combining structure-guided parallel sampling with CLIP-based rejection sampling, MaskSketch achieves high-quality results that balance realism with fidelity to the input sketch structure.

## Method Summary
MaskSketch uses a pre-trained MaskGIT transformer to generate images from sketches through structure-guided parallel decoding. The key innovation is extracting self-attention maps from intermediate transformer layers, which encode domain-invariant structural information about scene layout and object shape. These attention maps are used to compute a structure similarity distance that guides token rejection during generation. The method balances realism and structural fidelity through a tunable parameter λs, and employs CLIP-based rejection sampling to select the best result from multiple trials, improving overall quality and stability.

## Key Results
- Achieves FID scores of 10.9-15.9 on standard benchmarks, significantly outperforming state-of-the-art methods (17.8-35.5)
- Demonstrates high structure fidelity while maintaining realistic image quality
- Successfully handles unpaired sketch-to-photo translation without requiring paired training data or model fine-tuning

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: Self-attention maps from intermediate transformer layers encode structural information that is domain-invariant.
- Mechanism: Attention weights in self-attention layers encode how each token relates to all others spatially. Intermediate layers capture layout without being dominated by texture or appearance details.
- Core assumption: Attention maps encode scene structure in a way that is robust to domain shifts between natural images and sketches.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "We show that intermediate self-attention maps of a masked generative transformer encode important structural information of the input image, such as scene layout and object shape"
  - [section 3.2] "we define a structural distance based on a comparison of self-attention maps...the proposed attention-based structure similarity objective is robust to the domain shift occurring in sketch-to-photo translation"
  - [corpus] Weak - no direct mention of attention maps in corpus neighbors
- Break condition: If attention maps become dominated by style/texture rather than spatial relationships, the structural similarity metric would fail.

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: Structure-guided parallel decoding can balance realism and structural fidelity through a tunable parameter.
- Mechanism: The method masks tokens based on both confidence (likelihood) and structural similarity, with λs controlling the balance. This creates a trade-off between following the generator's prior and matching the input structure.
- Core assumption: The pre-trained generator's confidence scores and the structural similarity scores can be meaningfully combined to guide generation.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "MaskSketch achieves a balance between realism and fidelity to the given structure"
  - [section 3.3] "MaskSketch creates an additional mask that rejects tokens based on the structural similarity to the input sketch"
  - [section 3.4] "we use a parameter λs ∈ [0, 1] to determine the proportion of tokens masked according to the structure similarity scores"
- Break condition: If the confidence scores and structure scores are not on comparable scales or are correlated in problematic ways, the balancing mechanism would fail.

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: CLIP-based rejection sampling improves stability and quality by selecting from multiple trials.
- Mechanism: Generating multiple samples with different seeds and guidance scales, then selecting the one with the highest CLIP-based quality score, helps overcome the discrete optimization challenges of structure-guided sampling.
- Core assumption: CLIP features provide a meaningful proxy for both structure fidelity and realism that correlates with human judgment.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [section 3.4] "we improve the overall fidelity by producing multiple translation samples...and selecting the image that yields the highest structure fidelity and realism according to a CLIP-based score"
  - [section 4] "We use the L1 distance between features of a CLIP encoder...of the input image x of class c and generation result y to estimate the structure similarity"
- Break condition: If CLIP features are not sufficiently invariant to the domain gap or don't correlate well with perceptual quality, the selection mechanism would fail.

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map: VQ-GAN encoder → MaskGIT BERT transformer → Token-Critic refinement → CLIP-based selection
- Critical path: Structure similarity computation → Token rejection → Iterative refinement → Final selection
- Design tradeoffs: Using pre-trained models vs fine-tuning (faster but less adaptive), structure-guided vs confidence-guided sampling (trade-off between fidelity and realism), CLIP-based selection vs direct optimization (stability vs efficiency)
- Failure signatures: Mode collapse (especially on small datasets), unrealistic results despite structural similarity, computational inefficiency due to multiple trials
- First 3 experiments:
  1. Test structure similarity metric by reconstructing an input image using attention maps from different layers
  2. Verify the trade-off between realism and fidelity by varying λs and observing generation quality
  3. Evaluate the effectiveness of CLIP-based selection by comparing results with and without rejection sampling

## Limitations
- The method's performance on highly abstract sketches or sketches with significant domain shift from pre-training data remains unclear
- CLIP-based quality assessment is not directly optimized during generation and may not capture all aspects of perceptual quality
- The approach relies heavily on the assumption that intermediate self-attention maps capture domain-invariant structural information without rigorous analysis of which attention layers are most informative

## Confidence

- **High confidence**: The core mechanism of using self-attention maps for structure guidance is well-supported by the evidence. The quantitative improvements over baselines (FID scores of 10.9-15.9 vs 17.8-35.5) and qualitative results demonstrate the approach works as claimed.
- **Medium confidence**: The CLIP-based selection and quality assessment is reasonable but could be improved. The paper shows CLIP features correlate with human judgment, but doesn't explore alternative quality metrics or directly optimizing for perceptual quality.
- **Medium confidence**: The balancing mechanism between realism and structure fidelity through λs is theoretically sound, but the optimal settings may be dataset-dependent and aren't fully explored.

## Next Checks

1. **Attention map ablation study**: Systematically test which transformer layers and attention patterns contribute most to structure preservation by ablating specific layers or using attention from only certain heads.

2. **Cross-domain generalization test**: Evaluate MaskSketch on sketches from domains very different from ImageNet (e.g., architectural sketches, medical sketches) to test the domain invariance claim of the attention-based structure metric.

3. **Direct perceptual quality optimization**: Compare CLIP-based selection with a variant that directly optimizes a perceptual loss during generation to assess whether the current approach leaves quality on the table.
