# Graph2topic: an opensource topic modeling framework based on sentence embedding and community detection

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2304.06653
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2304.06653
- Reference count: 40
- G2T outperforms baseline models in topic coherence, diversity, interpretability, and coverage across eight datasets.

## Executive Summary
Graph2topic (G2T) is an opensource topic modeling framework that combines pretrained language models with community detection algorithms. The framework addresses key limitations of traditional topic modeling methods by automatically determining the number of topics and providing probabilistic distributions of words in topics and topics in documents. G2T integrates four main modules: document representation using sentence embeddings, semantic graph construction, topic community detection, and topic representation using a variant of TFIDF.

## Method Summary
G2T operates through a four-stage process: First, documents are encoded into semantic embeddings using sentence-level embedding methods like SimCSE. Second, a semantic graph is constructed by calculating cosine similarities between document embeddings and pruning to retain only the top 95% most significant edges. Third, community detection algorithms (both overlapping and non-overlapping) identify topic communities in the graph. Finally, a TFIDF-based method scores and selects representative words for each topic while penalizing words that appear across multiple topics. The framework is designed to work with various community detection algorithms and can handle both cases where documents belong to single or multiple topics.

## Key Results
- G2T achieves higher NPMI and CV coherence scores compared to baseline topic modeling methods across eight diverse datasets
- The framework automatically determines the number of topics, eliminating the need for manual specification
- G2T provides probabilistic distributions of both words in topics and topics in documents, enhancing interpretability and downstream applicability

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
Sentence-level embeddings capture more integrated contextual information than word or token embeddings, leading to better topics. Document-level semantics are more relevant to topic discovery than individual word semantics.

### Mechanism 2
Community detection on semantic graphs automatically determines the number of topics and handles multi-topic documents. Similar documents cluster into communities that correspond to coherent topics, and overlapping communities allow a document to belong to multiple topics.

### Mechanism 3
TFIDF-based word selection within topic clusters yields interpretable and coherent topic representations. Important topic words should be frequent within a topic and rare across other topics.

## Foundational Learning

- Concept: Sentence embeddings vs. word embeddings
  - Why needed here: G2T relies on high-quality semantic document representations
  - Quick check question: Why might averaging BERT token embeddings perform worse than sentence-BERT embeddings for topic modeling?

- Concept: Community detection in networks
  - Why needed here: The core innovation is mapping documents to a graph and detecting topic communities
  - Quick check question: What is the difference between LPA and CPM in terms of handling document-topic overlap?

- Concept: TFIDF and its variants
  - Why needed here: Topic word selection in G2T uses a modified TFIDF
  - Quick check question: How does penalizing words that appear in multiple clusters improve topic distinctiveness?

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map: Sentence Embedding Model -> Semantic Graph Construction -> Community Detection -> TFIDF-based Topic Representation -> Output
- Critical path: Embed → Graph → Community → TFIDF → Output. Each stage depends on the previous one.
- Design tradeoffs: Using full embeddings vs. reduced dimensions balances quality and speed; overlapping vs. non-overlapping communities trade interpretability vs. coverage.
- Failure signatures: (1) Too many small topics → graph pruning threshold too high; (2) Uninterpretable topics → TFIDF parameters off; (3) Single-topic assumption violated → need overlapping community detection.
- First 3 experiments:
  1. Run G2T on a small dataset (e.g., 20NG) with default settings; inspect topic-word lists for coherence.
  2. Vary the graph pruning percentage P (e.g., 80%, 90%, 95%) and observe topic count and coherence changes.
  3. Swap community detection method (LPA vs. CPM) and compare coverage and interpretability scores.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
How does the performance of G2T compare to other topic modeling methods when applied to longer documents that exceed the 512 token limit of most pretrained language models? The paper mentions truncation issues but lacks empirical results.

### Open Question 2
How does the choice of community detection algorithm impact the quality and interpretability of topics generated by G2T in different domains or with different types of documents? The paper compares algorithms but lacks comprehensive domain analysis.

### Open Question 3
How does the performance of G2T in downstream NLP tasks, such as text classification and recommendation, compare to other topic modeling methods? The paper mentions validation is needed but provides no empirical results.

## Limitations

- Performance heavily depends on quality of sentence embeddings and community detection algorithms
- May struggle with very short documents or highly specialized domains where pretrained models miss domain-specific semantics
- Computational complexity of community detection on large graphs is not thoroughly addressed

## Confidence

**High confidence** in combining sentence embeddings with community detection improving topic coherence and interpretability.

**Medium confidence** in automatic topic number determination, lacking extensive ablation studies on parameter sensitivity.

**Low confidence** in framework's scalability to very large document collections.
