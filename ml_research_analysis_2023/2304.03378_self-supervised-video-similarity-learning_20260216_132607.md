# Self-Supervised Video Similarity Learning

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID**: 2304.03378
- **Source URL**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2304.03378
- **Authors**: 
- **Reference count**: 40
- **Key outcome**: S2VS achieves state-of-the-art performance on video retrieval and detection tasks without labeled data, outperforming supervised methods on VCDB, FIVR, and EVVE benchmarks.

## Executive Summary
This paper introduces S2VS, a self-supervised video similarity learning approach that addresses multiple video retrieval and detection tasks without requiring labeled data. The method employs instance-discrimination via task-tailored augmentations and a combination of InfoNCE loss with a novel loss that jointly optimizes self-similarity and hard-negative similarity. By training a single universal model, S2VS achieves state-of-the-art performance on benchmark tasks ranging from video copy detection to incident and event retrieval, outperforming methods that rely on labeled data. The approach is evaluated on VCDB, FIVR, and EVVE datasets, demonstrating significant improvements in both retrieval (mAP) and detection (μAP) metrics. The code and pretrained models are publicly available.

## Method Summary
S2VS uses a ViSiL architecture with a fixed ResNet50 backbone pre-trained on ImageNet, combined with a trainable representation network using dot-attention for region weighting. The method applies weak and strong augmentations to create positive pairs from the same video and negative pairs from different videos. Training uses a combined loss function: InfoNCE for contrastive learning plus Lsshn for jointly optimizing self-similarity and hardest-negative similarity. The approach is trained on DnS-100K dataset for 30K iterations with a batch size of 64, AdamW optimizer, and learning rate 5e-5.

## Key Results
- S2VS outperforms state-of-the-art supervised methods on video copy detection (VCDB), video incident retrieval (FIVR), and video event retrieval (EVVE) tasks
- Achieves significant improvements in both retrieval (mAP) and detection (μAP) metrics across all benchmark datasets
- Single universal model trained without labels performs better than specialized supervised models for different video similarity tasks

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- **Claim**: Instance-discrimination with task-tailored augmentations enables the model to learn video similarity without labels by treating each video as its own class.
- **Mechanism**: The approach applies weak and strong augmentations to each video, creating positive pairs (different views of the same video) and negative pairs (views of different videos). The InfoNCE loss then encourages the model to pull positive pairs closer in the similarity space while pushing negatives apart.
- **Core assumption**: Different transformations of the same video preserve semantic content relevant to similarity tasks (copies, incidents, events), while transformations of different videos produce dissimilar representations.
- **Evidence anchors**:
  - [abstract]: "This is achieved by learning via instance-discrimination with task-tailored augmentations and the widely used InfoNCE loss"
  - [section]: "We perform training with self-supervision in the spirit of instance-discrimination, i.e., two augmented videos originating from the same original video are considered as positive to each other, or negative otherwise."
- **Break condition**: If augmentations destroy temporal or spatial structures critical for the similarity task (e.g., temporal shuffle drops all temporal context), the model fails to learn meaningful similarity.

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim**: The Lsshn loss jointly optimizing self-similarity and hardest-negative similarity improves performance over InfoNCE alone by regularizing the similarity matrix more effectively.
- **Mechanism**: While InfoNCE operates per positive pair, Lsshn maximizes the self-similarity (diagonal of the similarity matrix) and minimizes the hardest negative similarity (most confusing negative for each video). This spreads representations more evenly in the similarity space.
- **Core assumption**: Self-similarity should be high and hardest negatives should be pushed down for effective similarity learning, which InfoNCE alone doesn't explicitly enforce.
- **Evidence anchors**:
  - [abstract]: "the widely used InfoNCE loss together with an additional loss operating jointly on self-similarity and hard-negative similarity"
  - [section]: "the newly proposed Lsshn that maximizes self-similarity and minimizes the hardest negative similarity of each video in the batch"
- **Break condition**: If λ (weight of Lsshn) is too high, the network focuses excessively on Lsshn at the expense of InfoNCE, degrading performance.

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim**: The ViSiL architecture with temporal matching CNN effectively captures fine-grained spatio-temporal structures in video similarity when trained with SSL.
- **Mechanism**: The architecture computes region-level features, spatial matching via Chamfer similarity, and learns temporal patterns through a CNN on similarity matrices. This design is particularly suited for capturing the fine-grained structures needed for copy detection, incident retrieval, and event retrieval.
- **Core assumption**: The temporal CNN can learn meaningful patterns from similarity matrices even when trained with SSL rather than supervised labels.
- **Evidence anchors**:
  - [abstract]: "We adopt the ViSiL [44] architecture for video similarity, which needs labeled video datasets for its development in prior works [44, 45], but we train it in a self-supervised way"
  - [section]: "We adopt the ViSiL variant proposed in DnS [45], namely the fine-grained attention student, as our similarity network architecture"
- **Break condition**: If the temporal CNN cannot learn useful patterns from SSL training signals alone, the architecture would underperform compared to supervised training.

## Foundational Learning

- **Instance discrimination**: Understanding how contrastive learning works by treating each instance as its own class.
  - Why needed here: The core training objective relies on instance discrimination to create positive/negative pairs without labels.
  - Quick check question: How does instance discrimination differ from class-conditional contrastive learning?

- **InfoNCE loss**: Understanding the contrastive loss formulation and its temperature parameter.
  - Why needed here: InfoNCE is the primary loss function for the SSL training, and temperature τ is a critical hyperparameter.
  - Quick check question: What happens to the learned representations as the temperature τ approaches 0 or infinity?

- **Temporal matching with CNNs**: Understanding how CNNs can learn patterns from similarity matrices.
  - Why needed here: The temporal matching function uses a CNN to process similarity matrices, which is key to the architecture's effectiveness.
  - Quick check question: Why use a CNN on similarity matrices rather than directly on frame features for temporal modeling?

## Architecture Onboarding

- **Component map**: Input video -> Backbone (ResNet50) -> Representation network (dot-attention) -> Spatial matching (Chamfer similarity) -> Temporal matching (4-layer CNN) -> Final similarity score -> Loss computation (InfoNCE + Lsshn)

- **Critical path**: Input video → Backbone → Representation network → Spatial matching → Temporal matching → Final similarity score → Loss computation

- **Design tradeoffs**: Fixed backbone vs. trainable representation network (faster training but less adaptation), hand-crafted spatial matching vs. learned (more interpretable but potentially less flexible), combination of InfoNCE and Lsshn (better performance but more complex optimization).

- **Failure signatures**: 
  - High self-similarity but poor discrimination between different videos indicates InfoNCE is not working properly.
  - Very low self-similarity indicates Lsshn is not working properly.
  - Similar performance to ViSiLf baseline suggests SSL training is not effective.

- **First 3 experiments**:
  1. Train with only weak augmentations and InfoNCE to establish baseline SSL performance.
  2. Add strong augmentations incrementally (global → frame → temporal → video-in-video) to measure impact on performance.
  3. Train with InfoNCE only vs. InfoNCE + Lsshn to verify the contribution of the additional loss.

## Limitations

- The Lsshn loss function's exact formulation and sensitivity to hyperparameters are not fully specified, making it difficult to assess robustness
- Evaluation focuses on benchmark datasets without exploring real-world deployment scenarios or robustness to domain shifts
- Reliance on pre-trained ImageNet features may limit generalization to videos with very different visual characteristics than natural scenes

## Confidence

- **High confidence**: The core mechanism of instance-discrimination with task-tailored augmentations (Mechanism 1) is well-established in SSL literature and the paper provides sufficient implementation details for reproducibility.
- **Medium confidence**: The effectiveness of the combined InfoNCE + Lsshn loss (Mechanism 2) is demonstrated empirically, but the theoretical justification for why this specific combination outperforms other alternatives is limited.
- **Medium confidence**: The ViSiL architecture's effectiveness when trained with SSL (Mechanism 3) is shown through competitive results, but the ablation study for the temporal matching component is incomplete.

## Next Checks

1. **Ablation study on augmentation types**: Systematically disable each augmentation type (global, frame, temporal, video-in-video) and measure performance degradation to quantify their individual contributions.

2. **Hyperparameter sensitivity analysis**: Vary the λ weight for Lsshn and temperature τ for InfoNCE across a wider range to identify optimal values and assess stability.

3. **Cross-dataset generalization test**: Evaluate the model on datasets from different domains (e.g., surveillance, sports, medical videos) to assess how well the SSL approach generalizes beyond the training distribution.
