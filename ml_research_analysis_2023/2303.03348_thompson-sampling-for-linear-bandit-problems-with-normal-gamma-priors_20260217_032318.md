# Thompson Sampling for Linear Bandit Problems with Normal-Gamma Priors

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2303.03348
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.03348
- Reference count: 21
- Primary result: Bayesian regret bound of order sqrt(KT W_0(T/K^(1-2ε))) where W_0 is Lambert's principal W-function

## Executive Summary
This paper presents a Bayesian regret analysis for Thompson sampling in stochastic linear bandit problems with finitely many independent arms. The authors consider a setting where rewards are sampled from normal distributions with unknown variance, using multivariate normal-gamma priors to represent environmental uncertainty. The key theoretical contribution is establishing that normal-gamma priors are conjugate to the reward model and deriving a Bayesian regret bound that scales as sqrt(KT W_0(T/K^(1-2ε))), where W_0 is Lambert's W-function. This bound is sharper than the standard sqrt(KT log T) bound for bounded rewards, as the Lambert function grows more slowly than log T.

## Method Summary
The paper analyzes Thompson sampling for linear bandit problems where each arm's reward follows a normal distribution with unknown mean and variance. The authors use multivariate normal-gamma priors to represent uncertainty over both the linear parameter vectors and the variance parameters. By showing that these priors form a conjugate prior to the reward model, they can analytically compute posterior distributions and implement Thompson sampling efficiently. The regret analysis relies on establishing concentration properties of the posterior distributions under the condition that the 5/2-moment of the variance distribution exists.

## Key Results
- Normal-gamma priors are conjugate to the normal distribution with unknown mean and variance
- Bayesian regret bound of order sqrt(KT W_0(T/K^(1-2ε))) where ε ∈ (0,1)
- The Lambert W-function based bound improves upon the standard sqrt(KT log T) bound
- Empirical comparison shows performance differences between normal-gamma TS and Gaussian TS under misspecification

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The paper's approach works because normal-gamma distributions form a conjugate prior for normal distributions with unknown mean and variance. This conjugacy allows for tractable posterior updates and efficient Thompson sampling implementation. The linear structure of rewards enables dimensionality reduction in the posterior computations, while the finite arm setting simplifies the regret analysis. The existence of the 5/2-moment for the variance distribution ensures sufficient concentration properties for the Bayesian regret analysis.
