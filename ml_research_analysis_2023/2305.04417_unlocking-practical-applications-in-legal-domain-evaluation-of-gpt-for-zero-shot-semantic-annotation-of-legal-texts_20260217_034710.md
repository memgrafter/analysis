# Unlocking Practical Applications in Legal Domain: Evaluation of GPT for Zero-Shot Semantic Annotation of Legal Texts

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2305.04417
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.04417
- Reference count: 40
- Key result: GPT-3.5 achieves F1 scores of .73 (court opinions), .86 (contracts), and .54 (statutes) in zero-shot legal text annotation

## Executive Summary
This study evaluates GPT-3.5's capability for zero-shot semantic annotation of legal texts across three distinct domains: court opinions, contracts, and statutory/regulatory provisions. The model is provided with concise one-sentence definitions of semantic types and asked to classify short text snippets without any task-specific training. Results demonstrate strong performance, particularly for contracts (F1 = .86) and court opinions (F1 = .73), significantly outperforming a Jaccard similarity baseline. While performance is lower than supervised models trained on large legal datasets, GPT-3.5 remains competitive when limited training data is available, suggesting valuable applications in legal AI workflows like contract review and case analysis.

## Method Summary
The study employs a zero-shot learning approach where GPT-3.5 is given concise definitions of semantic types and asked to classify short legal text snippets across three domains: court opinions, contracts, and statutory/regulatory provisions. The evaluation uses F1 scores to measure performance against a Jaccard similarity baseline. The study tests the model's ability to understand and categorize legal concepts without any task-specific training, demonstrating that GPT-3.5 can effectively annotate legal texts using only general language understanding capabilities.

## Key Results
- GPT-3.5 achieves F1 = .73 for court opinions, .86 for contracts, and .54 for statutes/regulations
- Performance significantly outperforms Jaccard similarity baseline across all domains
- Zero-shot approach remains competitive with supervised models when training data is limited

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
GPT-3.5's strong performance in zero-shot legal text annotation stems from its extensive pre-training on diverse web text, including legal documents. The model's ability to understand context, extract relevant information, and apply semantic labels without task-specific training demonstrates transfer learning capabilities from general language understanding to specialized legal domains. The concise one-sentence definitions provide sufficient context for the model to accurately categorize legal text snippets, leveraging its pattern recognition and reasoning abilities developed during pre-training.

## Limitations
- Limited to three legal domains with small sample sizes (N=100 court opinions, N=100 contracts, N=48 statutory provisions)
- Performance gap remains between zero-shot and supervised approaches (F1 differences of 0.11-0.19)
- Jaccard similarity baseline may not represent state-of-the-art traditional methods
