# The eBible Corpus: Data and Bible Translation for Low-Resource Languages

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2304.09919
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2304.09919
- Reference count: 22
- Primary result: eBible corpus contains 1,009 Bible translations in 833 languages across 75 language families, enabling benchmark tasks for low-resource machine translation

## Executive Summary
This paper introduces the eBible corpus, a comprehensive dataset of Bible translations designed to address the scarcity of parallel data for low-resource languages in machine translation. The corpus contains 1,009 Bible translations across 833 languages and 75 language families, making it one of the largest collections for extremely low-resource language pairs. The authors establish benchmark tasks based on realistic translation workflows and evaluate multiple models, including fine-tuned NLLB, achieving BLEU scores of 35.1 for Austronesian languages and 31.6 for Trans-New Guinea languages. This work aims to spur innovation in neural machine translation for languages with extremely limited available data.

## Method Summary
The eBible corpus was constructed by collecting Bible translations from various sources, resulting in 1,009 translations across 833 languages spanning 75 language families. The authors designed several benchmark tasks motivated by realistic translation workflows, likely involving different levels of data availability and translation directions. They evaluated multiple models including fine-tuned NLLB on these benchmarks, using BLEU as the primary evaluation metric. The work focuses specifically on extremely low-resource settings where traditional MT approaches struggle due to data scarcity.

## Key Results
- eBible corpus contains 1,009 Bible translations in 833 languages across 75 language families
- Fine-tuned NLLB achieves BLEU scores of 35.1 on Austronesian languages and 31.6 on Trans-New Guinea languages
- The benchmarks address realistic translation workflows for low-resource languages
- Corpus addresses extreme data scarcity challenges in low-resource machine translation

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
Bible translations provide rich, parallel text resources across hundreds of languages, many of which have limited digital content. The structured nature of religious texts, with consistent themes and vocabulary across translations, creates natural parallel corpora. By leveraging this existing resource, the authors create training data where none previously existed for many language pairs. The consistent translation patterns in religious texts also make them particularly suitable for training translation models, as they often involve similar linguistic constructions and concepts across languages.

## Architecture Onboarding

**Component Map**
Raw Bible texts -> Preprocessing pipeline -> Parallel corpus construction -> Benchmark task creation -> Model training and evaluation

**Critical Path**
The critical path involves transforming raw Bible translations into aligned parallel corpora, then using these to train and evaluate translation models on benchmark tasks.

**Design Tradeoffs**
The authors trade domain specificity (Bible texts) for data availability, accepting that results may not generalize to other domains. They also balance between creating comprehensive benchmarks and managing computational costs of evaluation across 833 languages.

**Failure Signatures**
Potential failures include poor alignment quality leading to noisy training data, benchmark tasks that don't reflect real-world translation needs, and BLEU scores that don't correlate with actual translation quality for low-resource languages.

## Limitations
- Benchmark tasks may not fully capture real-world translation quality despite being motivated by realistic workflows
- Automatic BLEU evaluation may not accurately reflect translation quality for low-resource languages without human validation
- Religious text domain may limit generalizability to other translation scenarios
- Paper lacks detailed linguistic analysis of morphological complexity across included language families

- Translation quality claims: Medium
