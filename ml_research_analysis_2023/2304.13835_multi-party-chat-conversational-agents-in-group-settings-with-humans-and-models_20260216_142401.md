# Multi-Party Chat: Conversational Agents in Group Settings with Humans and Models

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2304.13835
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2304.13835
- Reference count: 24
- MultiLIGHT dataset contains 10,917 three-party fantasy role-play conversations

## Executive Summary
This paper addresses the critical gap in conversational AI by extending dialogue systems from pairwise to multi-party settings. The authors propose and evaluate methods for conversational agents to handle two key challenges: deciding when to speak (turn-taking) and producing coherent utterances that account for multiple participants. To enable this research, they collect a new dataset called MultiLIGHT consisting of 10,917 three-party conversations where humans role-play fantasy characters. The paper demonstrates that training models on this multi-party data significantly improves their performance in group settings compared to models trained on traditional two-party dialogue data.

## Method Summary
The authors tackle multi-party conversational AI by focusing on turn-taking and utterance coherence in group settings. They create the MultiLIGHT dataset through Amazon Mechanical Turk, where three workers role-play assigned fantasy characters in structured conversations. Four model architectures are proposed to handle multi-party dialogue: Silence OR utterance model, Speaker AND utterance model, Speaker only model, and Utterance only model. These models are trained and evaluated on their ability to both decide when to speak and generate contextually appropriate responses. Human evaluations assess model performance across five dimensions: consistency, engagingness, identity, lack of contradictions, and sensibility.

## Key Results
- MultiLIGHT-trained models significantly outperform existing state-of-the-art models in multi-party settings
- The Speaker AND utterance model achieves the best performance in both turn-taking and utterance coherence
- Human evaluations confirm that MultiLIGHT-trained models score higher on consistency, engagingness, identity, lack of contradictions, and sensibility
- Training on multi-party dialogue data provides substantial improvements over two-party trained models

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The paper's approach works because it directly addresses the fundamental differences between two-party and multi-party dialogue. In group settings, agents must track multiple conversation threads, manage complex turn-taking dynamics, and ground responses in the context of multiple speakers simultaneously. By collecting data that captures these dynamics and training models specifically on this data, the agents learn to handle the increased complexity of multi-party interactions. The Speaker AND utterance model's superior performance suggests that jointly modeling speaker selection and response generation is more effective than treating these tasks separately.

## Architecture Onboarding

Component Map:
Data Collection -> Dataset Preprocessing -> Model Training -> Evaluation -> Human Assessment

Critical Path:
1. MultiLIGHT dataset creation through role-play conversations
2. Four model architectures development and training
3. Automated evaluation of turn-taking and utterance coherence
4. Human evaluation across five quality dimensions
5. Comparative analysis against baseline models

Design Tradeoffs:
- Controlled role-play setting ensures data quality but may limit ecological validity
- Fantasy theme provides engaging scenarios but may not generalize to all domains
- Joint speaker-response modeling improves performance but increases computational complexity
- Human evaluation provides qualitative insights but introduces subjectivity

Failure Signatures:
- Poor turn-taking resulting in conversational overlap or awkward silences
- Contextually inappropriate responses that ignore multiple speakers
- Identity confusion where responses don't match assigned character roles
- Contradictions between generated responses and conversation history
- Generic responses that fail to engage with specific conversation content

First Experiments:
1. Evaluate baseline two-party trained models on MultiLIGHT data to establish performance gap
2. Compare the four proposed model architectures on a held-out validation set
3. Conduct ablation studies removing role-play elements to assess their impact on model performance

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
The paper acknowledges that role-playing constraints may affect the naturalness of the collected data and that findings may not generalize to other conversational domains. The authors note that the controlled fantasy setting, while useful for data collection, limits the applicability of results to real-world multi-party conversations in different contexts.

## Limitations
- Role-play methodology may not capture authentic multi-party conversation dynamics
- Limited comparison to other multi-party trained models may overstate relative improvements
- Fantasy setting focus restricts generalizability to other conversational domains
- Human evaluation methodology lacks detailed protocol description for assessing potential biases
