# What does the Failure to Reason with "Respectently" in Zero/Few-Shot Settings Tell Us about Language Models?

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2305.19597
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.19597
- Reference count: 22
- Models fail to reason with implicit "respectively" constructions, relying on explicit lexical cues rather than semantic understanding

## Executive Summary
This paper investigates how language models handle "respectively" constructions in natural language inference tasks, revealing significant limitations in their ability to reason with implicit respective readings. The authors create two datasets—WikiResNLI (synthetic) and NatResNLI (natural)—to evaluate model performance across explicit and implicit usage scenarios. Experiments demonstrate that while models achieve near-perfect accuracy on explicit cases in zero-shot settings, they struggle substantially with implicit constructions requiring common sense reasoning and fail to generalize from synthetic to natural data. Even large generative models like LLaMA, FLAN-T5, and GPT-JT show limited capability, indicating that current models rely on shallow lexical-syntactic heuristics rather than genuine semantic-pragmatic understanding of coordinate structures.

## Method Summary
The study evaluates transformer-based models (RoBERTa, ALBERT, DeBERTa) on two newly created datasets: WikiResNLI with synthetically generated premise-hypothesis pairs containing "respectively" constructions, and NatResNLI with naturally occurring examples. Models are fine-tuned on various NLI corpora (MNLI, SNLI, ANLI, FEVER-NLI, LingNLI, WANLI) and tested in zero-shot and few-shot settings. The evaluation examines performance differences between explicit and implicit respective readings, as well as generalization from synthetic to natural data. Fine-grained analysis categorizes contradiction types (1S1O, 1S2O, 2S1O) to understand specific failure patterns.

## Key Results
- Models achieve near-perfect accuracy on explicit entailment pairs in zero-shot settings but struggle with implicit constructions
- Performance drops significantly when generalizing from synthetic WikiResNLI to natural NatResNLI data
- Models fail to capture semantic-pragmatic meaning, relying instead on lexical-syntactic heuristics
- Fine-tuning on WikiResNLI improves respective reading performance without impacting other NLI tasks

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- **Claim**: Language models fail to generalize respective readings because they rely on explicit lexical cues rather than semantic-pragmatic understanding.
- **Mechanism**: The model learns surface patterns (e.g., the word "respectively") instead of the underlying one-to-one bijective mapping between coordinate sets.
- **Core assumption**: Models are not explicitly trained to understand cross-serial dependencies and rely on shallow heuristics.
- **Evidence anchors**:
  - [abstract]: "While few-shot learning is easy in the presence of explicit cues, longer training is required when the reading is evoked implicitly"
  - [section 4]: "models cannot fully correctly reason with respective readings... The best model, DeBERTa, only achieves 35% accuracy if fine-tuned with MNLI"
- **Break condition**: If the training data includes explicit lexical markers for respective readings, the model can achieve near-perfect accuracy on explicit cases but fails on implicit ones.

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim**: Generalization from synthetic to natural data is limited because natural sentences contain more complex linguistic structures.
- **Mechanism**: Synthetic data has controlled structures (e.g., simple coordinate pairs), while natural data includes relative clauses, coreferences, and inverted sentences that models haven't learned to handle.
- **Core assumption**: The model's learned representations are too narrow to handle linguistic variation beyond the training distribution.
- **Evidence anchors**:
  - [section 3.2]: "NatResNLI's sentences have more complicated linguistic constructions than WikiResNLI, such as relative clauses, e.g., sentence 7(a), implicit coreferences in sentence 7(b), and inverted sentences in sentence 7(c)"
  - [section 4]: "scores on NatResNLI are almost always lower than on WikiResNLI due to domain drift"
- **Break condition**: If training data includes diverse linguistic constructions similar to natural occurrences, the model might generalize better.

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim**: Models don't effectively learn abstract respective reading relations because they lack common sense and world knowledge.
- **Mechanism**: The model treats each instance as isolated rather than understanding the underlying semantic relationship that requires background knowledge.
- **Core assumption**: Models are not explicitly taught to use common sense reasoning for understanding coordinate structures.
- **Evidence anchors**:
  - [section 4]: "Interestingly, the models are able to surpass 95% after 32 shots, while pre-assigned labels only have 90% match... models are able to align with humans for respective reading reasoning"
  - [section 4]: "The top-5 frequent properties are: twinned administrative bodies, took place, are capitals of, buried in, and family names. Knowledge about relative location... and knowledge about humans... thus seem to play an important role"
- **Break condition**: If the model is explicitly trained with common sense knowledge or world knowledge integration, it might better understand implicit respective readings.

## Foundational Learning

- **Concept**: Cross-serial dependencies in coordinate structures
  - Why needed here: Understanding how elements in one coordinate pair map to elements in another is fundamental to respective readings
  - Quick check question: Given "John and Mary visited Paris and London respectively," which person visited which city?

- **Concept**: Distinction between explicit and implicit linguistic markers
  - Why needed here: Models must learn to recognize respective readings even when the word "respectively" is absent
  - Quick check question: Is "John and Mary are husband and wife" a respective reading? Why or why not?

- **Concept**: Bijective mapping and mutual exclusivity in semantic relations
  - Why needed here: Respective readings require one-to-one correspondence between sets, often with mutually exclusive properties
  - Quick check question: Why can't "John and Mary ate a falafel and a tortilla" be interpreted as a respective reading?

- **Design tradeoffs**:
  - Controlled synthetic data vs. natural linguistic complexity
  - Explicit lexical markers vs. implicit semantic understanding
  - Zero-shot learning vs. few-shot generalization
  - Model size and training data vs. reasoning capability

- **Failure signatures**:
  - Perfect performance on explicit cases but chance-level on implicit
  - Strong in-domain learning but poor cross-dataset generalization
  - Inability to handle 1S2O and 2S1O contradiction types
  - Performance degradation with complex linguistic structures

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- Question: What specific linguistic constructions beyond 1S1O, 1S2O, and 2S1O are most challenging for models to reason about with "respectively" constructions?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper notes that their datasets only cover "one sentence in the premise" and "do not exhaust all possible and complicated realizations of respective readings" in the limitations section.
- Why unresolved: The paper only evaluates three basic coordination structures and doesn't explore more complex constructions like nested coordinations or longer serial relationships.
- What evidence would resolve it: Testing models on datasets with more complex coordination patterns (e.g., three or more conjuncts, nested structures, or mixed distributive-collective readings) would reveal which specific constructions remain challenging.

### Open Question 2
- Question: How do different training objectives (e.g., MLM vs. causal language modeling) affect models' ability to reason with respective readings?
- Basis in paper: [inferred] The paper focuses on NLI models but doesn't compare how different pretraining objectives might impact performance on these constructions.
- Why unresolved: The experiments only use models trained with standard NLI objectives, leaving open whether different pretraining approaches could improve performance.
- What evidence would resolve it: Comparing models with different pretraining objectives (masked language modeling, causal language modeling, contrastive objectives) on the same respective reading tasks would show which training approaches are most effective.

### Open Question 3
- Question: What is the relationship between a model's general language understanding ability and its specific ability to reason with respective readings?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper notes that large generative models like LLaMA, FLAN-T5, and GPT-JT "still face substantial challenges in reasoning with respective readings" despite making "significant strides in natural language understanding."
- Why unresolved: The paper shows these models struggle with respective readings but doesn't analyze whether this difficulty correlates with other aspects of language understanding or represents a distinct failure mode.
- What evidence would resolve it: Correlating models' performance on respective readings with their performance on other NLU tasks (e.g., question answering, commonsense reasoning benchmarks) would reveal whether this is a general reasoning deficit or specific to coordinate structures.

## Limitations

- Synthetic data may not fully capture real-world linguistic complexity, potentially overestimating model capabilities
- Manual creation of natural data introduces potential subjectivity and may lack sufficient diversity
- Evaluation focuses on accuracy metrics without deeper analysis of model reasoning processes
- Study examines only a specific subset of NLI tasks, limiting generalizability to broader language understanding

## Confidence

**High Confidence:**
- Models struggle with implicit respective readings despite performing well on explicit cases
- Generalization from synthetic to natural data is significantly limited
- Fine-tuning on respective-specific data improves performance on respective readings without impacting other NLI tasks

**Medium Confidence:**
- Models rely on lexical-syntactic heuristics rather than semantic-pragmatic understanding
- Performance differences between model architectures are meaningful
- The 90% human agreement threshold for respective readings is appropriate

**Low Confidence:**
- Claims about the specific role of common sense knowledge in respective reading comprehension
- Generalizability of findings to other linguistic constructions beyond "respectively"
- The extent to which current limitations reflect fundamental model architecture constraints versus training data issues

## Next Checks

1. **Cross-linguistic validation**: Test whether the same patterns hold across multiple languages by creating respective reading datasets in languages with different syntactic structures (e.g., German, Japanese, Arabic). This would validate whether the observed limitations are universal or language-specific.

2. **Ablation study on linguistic complexity**: Systematically vary the complexity of constructions in synthetic data (adding relative clauses, coreferences, inversions) to identify the precise threshold where model performance degrades. This would clarify whether models fail due to overall complexity or specific linguistic features.

3. **Interpretability analysis**: Apply attention visualization and feature importance techniques to examine whether models are actually learning the semantic-pragmatic mapping or simply memorizing surface patterns. This would distinguish between genuine understanding and pattern matching.
