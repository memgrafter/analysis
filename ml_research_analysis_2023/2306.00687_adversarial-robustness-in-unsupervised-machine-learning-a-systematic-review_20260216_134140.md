# Adversarial Robustness in Unsupervised Machine Learning: A Systematic Review

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2306.00687
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.00687
- Reference count: 40
- Primary result: Systematic review of 86 papers reveals privacy attacks dominate UL research while defenses remain limited

## Executive Summary
This systematic literature review examines adversarial robustness in unsupervised machine learning, identifying 86 relevant papers that explore attacks and defenses across GANs, autoencoders, and clustering algorithms. The research reveals a significant imbalance where privacy attacks, particularly membership inference against GANs, dominate the landscape while defensive measures remain limited except for differential privacy. Adversarial examples primarily target autoencoders and super-resolution models, poisoning attacks affect most UL types, and most attacks rely on distance metrics for perturbation control. The study proposes a novel model of attack attributes to guide future research and highlights critical challenges in developing effective defensive measures that don't compromise model performance.

## Method Summary
The review employed systematic literature review methodology following Keele et al. guidelines, collecting papers from IEEE Xplore, ACM Digital Library, arXiv, and Scopus databases. Papers were filtered based on inclusion/exclusion criteria and quality assessment questions, then analyzed using thematic analysis. The final corpus of 86 papers was coded and categorized based on attack methods, target models, and attacker knowledge scenarios. Data extraction focused on identifying attacks, defenses, and challenges related to adversarial robustness in unsupervised machine learning.

## Key Results
- Privacy attacks, especially membership inference against GANs, dominate the research landscape
- Defensive measures remain limited except for differential privacy
- Adversarial examples primarily target autoencoders and super-resolution models
- Poisoning attacks affect most UL types with success rates dependent on attacker knowledge
- Most attacks rely on distance metrics for perturbation control

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: Adversarial attacks on UL systems can be categorized by attack method, target model, and attacker knowledge level, with each combination having distinct success patterns
- Mechanism: The systematic review identifies 86 papers and categorizes attacks into methods (adversarial examples, poisoning, membership inference, etc.), target models (GAN, autoencoder, clustering), and attacker knowledge (white/grey/black box)
- Core assumption: The categorization accurately reflects real-world attack patterns and success rates
- Evidence anchors: "The review reveals that privacy attacks, particularly membership inference against GANs, dominate the research landscape"; "For autoencoders, the main source of attacks stems from adversarial examples"
- Break condition: If real-world attack data significantly diverges from the paper's categorization or if new attack methods emerge that don't fit existing categories

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: Defensive measures for UL systems are significantly less developed than for supervised learning, with differential privacy being the primary exception
- Mechanism: The review finds that most UL technologies lack effective defensive measures, except for differential privacy which is widely studied and applied
- Core assumption: The scarcity of defensive research is accurately captured and reflects the actual state of the field
- Evidence anchors: "defensive measures remain limited except for differential privacy"; "there is a noticeable lack of defensive methods used in UL, except for differential privacy"
- Break condition: If new defensive methods are developed and proven effective for UL systems that weren't captured in this review

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: The effectiveness of attacks and defenses varies significantly based on the attacker's knowledge level about the target system
- Mechanism: The review systematically analyzes how attack success rates change with different knowledge scenarios (white box, grey box, black box)
- Core assumption: The knowledge level classification and its impact on attack effectiveness is valid across different UL technologies
- Evidence anchors: "An adversary targeting autoencoders also often relies on distance metrics to determine the difference between clean and malicious data"; "In a physical system, knowledge of the AutoEncoder enables the adversary to perform a general attack"
- Break condition: If empirical evidence shows that knowledge level has minimal impact on attack effectiveness in real-world scenarios

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- Question: How effective are defenses against adversarial examples in autoencoders beyond the limited approaches mentioned?
- Basis in paper: The paper notes that defensive measures for adversarial examples in autoencoders are "not very well researched"
- Why unresolved: The review found limited research on autoencoder-specific defenses against adversarial examples, despite their prevalence
- What evidence would resolve it: Empirical studies comparing multiple defense strategies specifically designed for autoencoders against various adversarial attack types

### Open Question 2
- Question: What are the fundamental differences in adversarial vulnerability between clustering algorithms that use different distance metrics?
- Basis in paper: The paper states that "how the distance is calculated impacts the vulnerabilities and adversarial robustness of clustering"
- Why unresolved: The review found speculation about metric-specific vulnerabilities but no systematic analysis of how different distance metrics affect clustering robustness
- What evidence would resolve it: Comparative analysis of multiple clustering algorithms with different distance metrics under identical adversarial attack scenarios

### Open Question 3
- Question: How does model diversity in GANs affect membership inference attack success across different privacy budgets?
- Basis in paper: The paper discusses that "when the GAN produces diverse but non-generalized samples, the model is vulnerable to membership inference"
- Why unresolved: The relationship between diversity, generalization, and privacy budget effectiveness in GANs was identified as a research gap
- What evidence would resolve it: Controlled experiments varying GAN architecture diversity and generalization while measuring membership inference success under different differential privacy implementations

## Limitations
- Limited to 86 papers identified through systematic review methodology, potentially missing emerging research
- Focus on specific UL technologies (GANs, autoencoders, clustering) may limit generalizability to other UL methods
- Reliance on paper abstracts and full-text analysis may miss nuanced details about attack and defense effectiveness

## Confidence

**High Confidence:** The categorization of attack types and their prevalence across different UL technologies, supported by systematic analysis of 86 papers

**Medium Confidence:** The effectiveness of differential privacy as the primary defense mechanism, though specific quantitative performance metrics are limited

**Low Confidence:** The generalizability of findings to all UL systems and the real-world effectiveness of proposed defenses, as most studies use synthetic datasets
