# Backward Compatibility During Data Updates by Weight Interpolation

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID**: 2301.10546
- **Source URL**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2301.10546
- **Reference count**: 40
- **Primary result**: BCWI reduces negative flips by up to three times without sacrificing accuracy

## Executive Summary
This paper addresses the problem of regression bugs in machine learning model updates, specifically focusing on negative flips where instances correctly classified by the old model become misclassified by the updated model. The authors propose Backward Compatible Weight Interpolation (BCWI), which interpolates between the weights of the old and new models to reduce negative flips while maintaining improved accuracy. The method is straightforward, incurs no inference cost, and allows tuning without retraining.

## Method Summary
The paper proposes interpolating between the weights of old and new models to reduce negative flips during model updates. BCWI uses a parameter α to control the interpolation, where θ_interpolated = αθ_old + (1-α)θ_new. The method is evaluated on three datasets (MASSIVE, Banking77, AG News) across two update scenarios: adding i.i.d. data and adding new classes. The authors also introduce FisherBCWI, which uses the Fisher information matrix for importance weighting, and SoupBCWI, which employs soup ensembles of new models to further reduce negative flips.

## Key Results
- BCWI reduces negative flips by up to three times compared to baseline methods
- Accuracy is maintained or improved while reducing negative flips
- The method is effective across different datasets and update scenarios
- No inference cost is incurred during deployment
- Tuning is performed without retraining

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: Interpolating model weights from old and new models reduces negative flips while maintaining accuracy.
- Mechanism: The new model, initialized from the old model, stays in the same loss basin, making weight interpolation smooth and avoiding sharp accuracy drops.
- Core assumption: The new model lies within the same local minimum as the old model when fine-tuned from it.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract]: "BCWI describes the interpolation between the weights of the old and new model and we show in extensive experiments that it reduces negative flips without sacrificing the improved accuracy of the new model."
  - [section 7.3]: "Because the new model is initialized by the old model... it stays within the same basin."
  - [corpus]: Weak; no direct neighbor paper supports this mechanism.
- Break condition: If the new model converges to a different basin due to training instability or data shift, interpolation may cross a high-loss barrier and degrade accuracy.

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: Using the Fisher information matrix as importance weighting focuses interpolation on parameters critical for old model predictions.
- Mechanism: Diagonal Fisher matrix elements weight each parameter, prioritizing weights important for the old model while allowing new model parameters to dominate where beneficial.
- Core assumption: Fisher information accurately reflects parameter importance for preserving old model predictions.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract]: "We further introduce FisherBCWI which uses the Fisher information matrix as importance weighting..."
  - [section 4.1]: "The importance of each weight can be quantified by the diagonal of the empirical Fisher information matrix..."
  - [corpus]: Weak; no direct neighbor paper supports this mechanism.
- Break condition: If Fisher estimates are inaccurate (e.g., small sample size), importance weighting may misalign and hurt both accuracy and flip reduction.

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: Soup ensembles of new models reduce negative flips without increasing inference cost.
- Mechanism: Averaging weights of multiple independently fine-tuned models smooths predictions and reduces variance in classification decisions.
- Core assumption: Multiple independent fine-tunings sample similar but slightly different minima, and averaging them reduces outlier predictions that cause flips.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract]: "SoupBCWI which employs soup ensembles... to further reduce negative flips."
  - [section 4.2]: "A soup ensemble is formed by averaging the weights of multiple models... We find that the soup ensemble of new models is reducing negative flips."
  - [corpus]: Weak; no direct neighbor paper supports this mechanism.
- Break condition: If models in the ensemble converge to very different minima, averaging may produce suboptimal predictions and increase flips.

## Foundational Learning

- Concept: Catastrophic forgetting and its distinction from negative flips.
  - Why needed here: The paper distinguishes between accuracy loss (forgetting) and negative flips; understanding both is critical to frame the problem correctly.
  - Quick check question: If a model's accuracy on old data stays the same but it misclassifies some old instances, is this catastrophic forgetting?
- Concept: Fisher information matrix as a measure of parameter importance.
  - Why needed here: FisherBCWI uses diagonal Fisher entries to weight parameters during interpolation.
  - Quick check question: In a binary classification, which parameters would have higher Fisher values—those affecting confident correct predictions or uncertain ones?
- Concept: Ensemble methods and weight averaging in deep learning.
  - Why needed here: SoupBCWI averages weights of multiple models; understanding how this differs from output averaging is key.
  - Quick check question: Does averaging model weights always produce the same predictions as averaging their output probabilities?

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map: Old model weights (θ_old) -> New model weights (θ_new) -> Fisher diagonal (F_old) for FisherBCWI -> Soup ensemble weights (average of M θ_new) -> Interpolation parameter α (tuned post-training)
- Critical path:
  1. Train old model on old data.
  2. Train new model on updated data (initialized from old).
  3. Compute Fisher diagonal if using FisherBCWI.
  4. Form soup ensemble if using SoupBCWI.
  5. Interpolate weights with α tuned on dev set.
  6. Evaluate on test set.
- Design tradeoffs:
  - Interpolation vs. retraining: Interpolation avoids additional training but may be less optimal than retraining with regularization.
  - Fisher weighting vs. uniform: Fisher weighting may better preserve old predictions but requires extra computation.
  - Soup ensemble size vs. benefit: More models reduce flips but increase training time.
- Failure signatures:
  - Large α → high negative flips, low accuracy.
  - Small α → low accuracy, high negative flips.
  - FisherBCWI with poor Fisher estimate → erratic behavior.
- First 3 experiments:
  1. Run BCWI with α=0.5 on MASSIVE AD scenario and record accuracy/NFR.
  2. Compare BCWI vs. Mixout baseline on Banking77 AC scenario.
  3. Test SoupBCWI-4 vs. SoupBCWI-2 on AG News AD scenario.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

The paper does not explicitly call out open questions but several important ones emerge from the work:

### Open Question 1
- Question: How does BCWI's negative flip rate reduction scale with the size of the regression set, particularly for extremely large datasets?
- Basis in paper: [inferred] The paper demonstrates effectiveness on datasets with thousands of instances, but does not explore performance on datasets with millions of instances.
- Why unresolved: The experimental datasets used (MASSIVE, Banking77, AG News) are relatively small compared to industrial-scale datasets. The paper does not investigate whether the observed three-fold reduction in negative flips scales linearly or experiences diminishing returns.
- What evidence would resolve it: Testing BCWI on regression sets with 10x or 100x more instances than used in the paper, measuring whether the reduction in negative flips remains proportional to the observed results.

### Open Question 2
- Question: What is the relationship between interpolation parameter α and the effective learning rate during fine-tuning, and how does this affect convergence?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper notes that BCWI is largely insensitive to hyperparameter selection, but does not analyze the relationship between α and learning rate.
- Why unresolved: The paper treats α as a post-training parameter but does not explore whether certain α values might implicitly regularize the model in ways similar to adjusting learning rate, or whether there are optimal α-learning rate combinations.
- What evidence would resolve it: Systematic experiments varying both α and learning rate simultaneously to identify if certain combinations produce better results than others, and whether α acts as a form of implicit learning rate regularization.

### Open Question 3
- Question: Does BCWI's effectiveness depend on the similarity between the old and new data distributions, and how does it perform under significant distribution shift?
- Basis in paper: [inferred] The paper assumes i.i.d. data addition and does not test scenarios with significant distribution shift between old and new data.
- Why unresolved: All experiments use data splits from the same source, so the paper does not address whether BCWI remains effective when the new data has different characteristics (e.g., different user demographics, time periods, or domains).
- What evidence would resolve it: Testing BCWI on datasets where the new data comes from a different source or time period than the old data, measuring whether the negative flip reduction remains consistent or degrades with increasing distributional divergence.

## Limitations
- The assumption that new models stay in the same loss basin as old models is not empirically validated
- Fisher information matrix estimation quality and its impact on performance is not analyzed
- Effectiveness under significant distribution shift between old and new data is not tested
- The paper does not explore the relationship between α and learning rate

## Confidence

- **High confidence**: BCWI reduces negative flips compared to baselines in the reported experiments
- **Medium confidence**: FisherBCWI provides consistent improvement over BCWI (but only shown on one dataset)
- **Low confidence**: Mechanism explanations (loss basin hypothesis, Fisher importance weighting, ensemble smoothing) are plausible but lack direct empirical validation
