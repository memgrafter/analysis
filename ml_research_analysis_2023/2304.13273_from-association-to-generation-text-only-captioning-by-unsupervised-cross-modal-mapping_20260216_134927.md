# From Association to Generation: Text-only Captioning by Unsupervised Cross-modal Mapping

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID**: 2304.13273
- **Source URL**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2304.13273
- **Reference count**: 15
- **Key outcome**: Knight achieves state-of-the-art zero-shot image and video captioning by retrieving k-nearest-neighbor captions from CLIP embedding space and generating captions using a language decoder trained only on text features.

## Executive Summary
This paper introduces Knight, a zero-shot captioning method that leverages CLIP's cross-modal embedding space to generate captions without paired image-caption training data. The key innovation is using CLIP similarity to retrieve relevant captions from a corpus, then training a text-only decoder on these retrieved captions. Knight achieves state-of-the-art performance on MS-COCO, Flickr30k, MSR-VTT, and MSVD datasets, demonstrating strong generalization capabilities while avoiding the need for costly image-text pairing during training.

## Method Summary
Knight uses CLIP to encode images/videos and retrieve the k-nearest-neighbor captions from a text corpus based on embedding similarity. A GPT-2 large decoder is then trained using autoregressive loss on the CLIP text features of these retrieved captions. For video captioning, Knight averages CLIP similarity scores across keyframes to retrieve relevant captions for each frame. The method requires no paired image-caption data during training, making it truly unsupervised while achieving strong performance on standard captioning benchmarks.

## Key Results
- Knight achieves 64.0 BLEU-1, 22.6 BLEU-4, 24.0 METEOR, 48.0 ROUGE-L, 56.3 CIDEr, and 16.3 SPICE on MS-COCO for image captioning
- For video captioning on MSR-VTT, Knight achieves 72.6 BLEU-1, 25.4 BLEU-4, 28.0 METEOR, 50.7 ROUGE-L, 31.9 CIDEr, and 8.5 SPICE
- Knight demonstrates significant advantage in cross-domain generalization compared to supervised methods

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: Knight achieves cross-modal mapping by retrieving k-nearest-neighbor captions in CLIP embedding space, thus avoiding direct image-to-text generation across the modality gap.
- Mechanism: Instead of training a decoder to operate in both modalities, Knight maps image features to text features using CLIP's similarity metric and retrieves the most similar captions from the training corpus. The decoder is trained and applied only in the text modality, eliminating the need to bridge the modality gap.
- Core assumption: CLIP encodes images and texts into two separate subspaces with a significant gap; directly training a decoder across these modalities fails due to misalignment.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "CLIP is hard to apply to generation-based tasks. This is due to the lack of decoder architecture and pre-training tasks for generation... previous works have created generation capacity for CLIP through additional language models, a modality gap between the CLIP representations of different modalities and the inability of CLIP to model the offset of this gap, which fails the concept to transfer across modalities."
  - [section] "Although it is possible to learn the pattern between two modalities with a large amount of data, [Ramesh et al., 2022] demonstrates that the overhead of this process is huge. Nevertheless, we note that the effective cross-modal interaction of CLIP is under the association of paired data... Although the modality gap makes it hard to achieve cross-modal generation directly, it can be achieved by association indirectly."
  - [corpus] "Improving Cross-modal Alignment with Synthetic Pairs for Text-only Image Captioning" discusses cross-modal alignment, suggesting Knight's retrieval-based mapping is a relevant approach.
- Break condition: If CLIP's embedding space does not encode images and texts into sufficiently separable subspaces, or if the similarity metric fails to retrieve semantically relevant captions, the mapping would fail.

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: Knight's training process is text-only and unsupervised, requiring only a corpus of captions without paired image-caption data.
- Mechanism: During training, Knight selects captions from the corpus, retrieves their k-nearest neighbors using CLIP similarity, and trains the decoder autoregressively on the text features of these captions. No images or videos are used in training.
- Core assumption: CLIP's pre-trained knowledge is sufficient to establish meaningful associations between captions, enabling the decoder to learn caption generation without paired image data.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "With text-only unsupervised training, Knight achieves state-of-the-art performance in zero-shot methods for image captioning and video captioning."
  - [section] "In the training phase, we first select captions from the corpus for training and use the CLIP similarity to retrieve the k-nearest-neighbor captions that are most similar to the training captions. Then, we use the CLIP features of the training captions to train the decoder by an autoregression loss."
  - [corpus] "Text-Only Training for Image Captioning with Retrieval Augmentation and Modality Gap Correction" aligns with Knight's text-only training approach.
- Break condition: If the corpus lacks diversity or representativeness, or if CLIP's similarity metric fails to retrieve meaningful neighbors, the unsupervised training would not generalize well.

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: Knight can be extended to video captioning by averaging CLIP similarity scores across keyframes and retrieving k-nearest-neighbor captions for each frame.
- Mechanism: For video captioning, Knight extracts keyframes, computes CLIP similarity for each keyframe to the caption corpus, retrieves k-nearest neighbors for each frame, averages the retrieved captions, and feeds them into the decoder.
- Core assumption: The decoder can model temporal relationships between frames by reasoning over the averaged information from k-nearest-neighbor captions across keyframes.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "For video captioning, we average the retrieved results for each keyframe to achieve multi-frame input for the video."
  - [section] "Compared with the image, video is a set of multiple frames. We first extract the keyframes of the inference video... For each keyframe, we get the Si...   - [corpus] "Zero-shot video captioning with evolving pseudo-tokens" suggests Knight's approach is relevant to video captioning.
- Break condition: If averaging across keyframes loses important temporal or semantic information, or if the decoder cannot model frame relationships effectively, video captioning quality would degrade.

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map: CLIP (ResNet50x64 encoder, 1024-dim embeddings) -> 3-layer MLP (1024→1280 dim mapping) -> GPT-2 large decoder (1280-dim embeddings, 36 layers, 20 heads) -> generated captions
- Critical path: Inference image -> CLIP encoding -> k-nearest-neighbor retrieval -> MLP mapping -> GPT-2 decoding -> generated caption
- Design tradeoffs: Text-only training avoids costly image-text pairing but requires a large, diverse caption corpus; k-nearest-neighbor retrieval limits generation to corpus content but ensures semantic relevance
- Failure signatures: Poor caption quality indicates issues with CLIP's embedding space, corpus diversity, or decoder training; high computational cost suggests inefficient retrieval or decoding
- First 3 experiments:
  1. Vary k (e.g., 1, 5, 10) and measure caption quality to find optimal retrieval breadth
  2. Reduce training corpus size (e.g., 10%, 50%, 100%) to test data efficiency
  3. Compare Knight's zero-shot performance against supervised baselines on cross-domain transfer

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

- How does the choice of k (number of nearest neighbors) impact the trade-off between generation diversity and quality in Knight?
- How does Knight perform on datasets with significantly different domain characteristics compared to the training corpus?
- Can Knight be extended to handle other generation-based tasks beyond image and video captioning, such as visual question answering or image generation from text?

## Limitations

- The method is fundamentally constrained by the diversity and quality of the caption corpus - it cannot generate captions describing concepts or entities absent from training data
- Performance depends heavily on CLIP's ability to encode meaningful semantic relationships in its embedding space
- The k-nearest-neighbor approach may struggle with rare or novel concepts not well-represented in the training corpus

## Confidence

- **High confidence**: The core mechanism of using CLIP similarity for k-NN retrieval and text-only training is clearly specified and theoretically sound
- **Medium confidence**: The reported benchmark performance, as it depends on implementation details not fully specified (MLP architecture, hyperparameters)
- **Low confidence**: Generalization to domains outside the evaluated datasets (COCO, Flickr30k, MSR-VTT, MSVD)
