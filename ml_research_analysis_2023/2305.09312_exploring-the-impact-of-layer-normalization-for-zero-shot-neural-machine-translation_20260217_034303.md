# Exploring the Impact of Layer Normalization for Zero-shot Neural Machine Translation

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2305.09312
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.09312
- Reference count: 40
- This paper demonstrates that PostNorm consistently outperforms PreNorm by up to 12.3 BLEU points for zero-shot translation while maintaining comparable supervised performance.

## Executive Summary
This paper investigates how the positioning of Layer Normalization (LayerNorm) - before (PreNorm) or after (PostNorm) residual connections - affects zero-shot neural machine translation (ZST) performance. Through systematic experiments across three multilingual datasets covering 54 zero-shot directions, the authors demonstrate that PostNorm consistently outperforms PreNorm by up to 12.3 BLEU points for ZST while maintaining comparable performance on supervised directions. The study reveals that PreNorm's placement of LayerNorm before residual connections leads to higher off-target rates and reduced generalizability for unseen language pairs. Analysis using layer-wise language recognition shows that PostNorm produces hidden states more independent of source language and more target-language dependent, contributing to better ZST performance. The authors conclude that PostNorm should be considered the default setting for ZST systems, challenging the current convention of using PreNorm in most ZST frameworks.

## Method Summary
The paper compares PreNorm (LayerNorm before residual connections) versus PostNorm (LayerNorm after residual connections) in Transformer architectures for zero-shot translation. Experiments use three multilingual datasets: OPUS (30 ZST directions, 12M sentences, 7 languages), IWSLT (6 ZST directions, 1.38M sentences, 4 languages), and Europarl (12 ZST directions, 15.78M sentences, 5 languages). Models are trained with different language tag settings (S-ENC-T-DEC, T-ENC) and residual connection configurations. The study employs layer-wise language recognition analysis to track source/target language dependence in encoder hidden states and includes Swap-PreNorm variants to validate structural hypotheses. Performance is measured using BLEU scores and off-target rates across 3 random seeds per configuration.

## Key Results
- PostNorm outperforms PreNorm by up to 12.3 BLEU points for zero-shot translation while maintaining comparable supervised performance
- PreNorm shows higher off-target rates and is more sensitive to language tag settings than PostNorm
- PostNorm encoder hidden states are less dependent on source language and more target-language dependent, contributing to better ZST performance
- The order of LayerNorm and self-attention/feed-forward network in PreNorm is identified as the main factor affecting ZST performance

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: PostNorm's positioning of LayerNorm after residual connections reduces source language dependence in encoder hidden states, improving ZST generalizability.
- Mechanism: In PostNorm, normalization occurs after information has flowed through self-attention and FFN layers, allowing the model to better capture target-relevant features while reducing source language imprint on encoder representations.
- Core assumption: Source language information in encoder states negatively impacts zero-shot translation performance by creating spurious correlations that don't generalize to unseen language pairs.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "PostNorm produces hidden states more independent of source language and more target-language dependent, contributing to better ZST performance"
  - [section] "the encoder hidden states of PostNorm are less dependent on the source language than PreNorm (L6 of Pre/Post-Src of 3 lower sub-figures)"
  - [corpus] "Impact of Layer Norm on Memorization and Generalization in Transformers" (weak evidence, indirect connection)
- Break condition: If source language independence doesn't correlate with ZST performance, or if target language dependence becomes harmful for certain language pairs.

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: PreNorm's placement of LayerNorm before residual connections creates shallow sub-networks that limit deep feature extraction needed for ZST.
- Mechanism: PreNorm's "unraveled view" shows multiple shallow sub-networks (embedding→layer norm→attention→layer norm paths) that bypass deep feature extraction, while PostNorm forces all information through residual connections, enabling better hierarchical representation learning.
- Core assumption: Deep feature extraction through residual connections is critical for zero-shot translation performance, and shallow paths are insufficient.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "we reveal structural flaws in PreNorm for ZST" and "the order of LayerNorm and self-attention/feed-forward network in PreNorm is the main factor affecting its ZST performance"
  - [section] "PreNorm includes shallow sub-networks, such as the embedding layer output fed through encoder layers without any operation except for the final LayerNorm (red path in Fig. 1), but PostNorm does not"
  - [corpus] "Pre-RMSNorm and Pre-CRMSNorm Transformers: Equivalent and Efficient Pre-LN Transformers" (weak evidence, indirect connection)
- Break condition: If Swap-PreNorm experiments don't validate the structural flaw hypothesis, or if shallow sub-networks prove beneficial for certain language pairs.

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: PostNorm is less sensitive to language tag settings than PreNorm, reducing off-target translation errors in ZST.
- Mechanism: PostNorm's normalization after residual connections creates more robust language-agnostic representations that are less influenced by encoder-side language tags, while PreNorm's normalization before residual connections amplifies tag effects, leading to higher off-target rates.
- Core assumption: Language tag sensitivity directly correlates with off-target translation rates, which negatively impacts ZST performance.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "PreNorm is more sensitive to language tag settings than PostNorm, negatively impacting ZST performance"
  - [section] "For PreNorm and PostNorm with the 'T-ENC' language tag (#3, #4, #7, and #8), they have similar off-target rates" vs "For PreNorm and PostNorm with the 'S-ENC-T-DEC' language tag (#1, #2, #5, and #6), the off-target rates show a more considerable discrepancy"
  - [corpus] No direct corpus evidence found (explicitly noted)
- Break condition: If language tag settings don't significantly impact off-target rates, or if PostNorm's reduced sensitivity doesn't translate to better ZST performance.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- Question: How does the positioning of LayerNorm affect the stability and convergence speed of zero-shot translation training compared to supervised directions?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper mentions that PreNorm is known to stabilize Transformer training and that PostNorm is the original Transformer setting, but does not systematically compare convergence behavior between the two settings specifically for ZST.
- Why unresolved: The experiments focus on final BLEU scores rather than training dynamics, and convergence characteristics may differ between supervised and zero-shot directions.
- What evidence would resolve it: Training curves showing loss/accuracy progression over epochs for both PreNorm and PostNorm across supervised and zero-shot directions would reveal whether one setting converges faster or more stably.

### Open Question 2
- Question: Does the number of layers in the Transformer model affect the relative performance of PreNorm versus PostNorm for zero-shot translation?
- Basis in paper: [inferred] The paper uses Transformer-base and Transformer-big models but does not systematically vary the number of layers to study its interaction with LayerNorm positioning.
- Why unresolved: The analysis focuses on different LayerNorm positions and language tag settings but does not isolate the effect of model depth, which could interact with LayerNorm positioning.
- What evidence would resolve it: Experiments comparing PreNorm and PostNorm across models with varying numbers of layers (e.g., 4, 6, 8, 12 layers) would show whether the observed performance differences scale with model depth.

### Open Question 3
- Question: How would applying LayerNorm positioning analysis to multilingual pre-trained models like mBART affect zero-shot translation performance after fine-tuning?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The limitations section explicitly mentions that exploration of LayerNorm settings in pre-trained models like mBART for fine-tuning ZST has not been addressed.
- Why unresolved: The current study only examines models trained from scratch, while pre-trained models are increasingly common in NMT practice.
- What evidence would resolve it: Fine-tuning experiments with mBART or similar models using different LayerNorm settings for the fine-tuning stage would reveal whether the observed PreNorm/PostNorm differences persist or change in pre-trained settings.

## Limitations
- The study's findings are primarily correlational, with causal mechanisms inferred rather than experimentally validated
- Experiments focus on specific language families (primarily Indo-European) and may not generalize to low-resource or distant language pairs
- The architectural variation of using 8 encoder layers for Europarl vs 6 for other datasets could influence normalization effects

## Confidence

- **High Confidence**: PostNorm consistently outperforms PreNorm in ZST BLEU scores (12.3 points improvement), and this effect is robust across three datasets and multiple language tag configurations.
- **Medium Confidence**: The mechanism of source language independence in encoder states contributing to ZST performance, supported by LLR analysis showing reduced source language dependence in PostNorm.
- **Low Confidence**: The structural flaw hypothesis regarding shallow sub-networks in PreNorm, as this is inferred from architectural analysis rather than directly measured performance impacts.

## Next Checks
1. **Causal Mechanism Validation**: Conduct ablation studies where source language information is explicitly removed from encoder states (via masking or disentanglement) to measure direct impact on ZST performance, confirming whether source language independence is truly causal rather than correlational.
2. **Cross-Domain Generalization**: Test PostNorm vs PreNorm on additional language families including low-resource pairs, distant language families (e.g., Japanese-English), and morphologically rich languages to verify the robustness of findings beyond Indo-European languages.
3. **Training Dynamics Analysis**: Track and compare the evolution of encoder hidden states throughout training for both normalization settings using language similarity metrics, identifying whether PostNorm's advantages emerge early in training or develop gradually over time.
