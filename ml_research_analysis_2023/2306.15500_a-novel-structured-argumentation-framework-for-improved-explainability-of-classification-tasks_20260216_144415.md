# A novel structured argumentation framework for improved explainability of classification tasks

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2306.15500
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.15500
- Reference count: 23
- A novel structured argumentation framework that enhances decision trees with boolean logic operators to improve model understandability

## Executive Summary
This paper introduces the extended argumentative decision graph (xADG), a novel structured argumentation framework that enhances traditional argumentative decision graphs by allowing arguments to incorporate boolean logic operators and multiple premises. The xADG framework enables more concise argumentation graphs, potentially improving user understandability. The study proposes a methodology for constructing xADGs from decision trees and evaluates their performance across classification tasks of varying complexity. Results demonstrate that xADGs achieve strong balanced accuracy while reducing the average number of supports needed to reach conclusions. The framework outperforms existing techniques in terms of predictive capacity and model size, producing plausibly understandable models that maintain equivalent inferential capability to their source decision trees.

## Method Summary
The method involves constructing xADGs from decision trees using a three-step process: (1) building a well-formed ADG from the decision tree's inferential paths, (2) simplifying the ADG by removing redundant paths and arguments, and (3) applying modifications (m1, m2, m3) to merge arguments with same conclusions and incorporate boolean logic operators. The approach uses UCI machine learning repository datasets (Car Evaluation, Adult, Bank Marketing, Myocardial infarction complications) and decision trees built using CART algorithm with maximum depths chosen based on balanced accuracy analysis. xADGs are constructed by applying the WellBuiltArguments function to merge arguments and remove attacks from the initial ADG.

## Key Results
- xADGs achieve strong balanced accuracy while reducing the average number of supports needed to reach conclusions
- The framework outperforms existing techniques in terms of predictive capacity and model size
- xADGs maintain equivalent inferential capability to their source decision trees while producing more concise models

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: xADG improves model understandability by reducing the number of arguments and attacks while maintaining predictive accuracy.
- Mechanism: By allowing arguments to use boolean logic operators and multiple supports, xADG can merge or eliminate redundant arguments that would require separate nodes in a traditional decision tree. This consolidation reduces the overall model size without sacrificing inferential capability.
- Core assumption: A smaller model with fewer distinct components is inherently easier for humans to understand and reason about.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "The xADG framework enables more concise argumentation graphs, potentially improving user understandability."
  - [section] "The expectation is that a smaller model will be easier for human reasoners to understand and extend."
  - [corpus] Weak evidence - no direct citations found in corpus neighbors about understandability metrics.
- Break condition: If merging arguments leads to complex nested boolean expressions that exceed human cognitive capacity to parse, the model may become less understandable despite having fewer components.

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: xADG maintains equivalent predictive accuracy to decision trees while offering a more interpretable structure.
- Mechanism: The xADG framework preserves the inferential paths of the source decision tree through strategic argument modifications (m1, m2, m3) that maintain logical equivalence. The boolean logic operators allow multiple decision paths to be represented within single arguments.
- Core assumption: Logical equivalence between decision tree paths and boolean argument structures can be preserved during the transformation process.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "Results demonstrate that xADGs achieve strong balanced accuracy while reducing the average number of supports needed to reach conclusions."
  - [section] "The remaining of this section details the subsequent steps: the simplification of ADGs and possible modifications to derive xADGs."
  - [corpus] Weak evidence - no direct citations found in corpus neighbors about accuracy preservation during argumentation transformation.
- Break condition: If the boolean logic expressions become too complex or if the transformation introduces subtle logical errors, the predictive accuracy may degrade.

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: xADG enables easier knowledge discovery and refinement compared to traditional decision trees.
- Mechanism: The argumentative structure allows domain experts to directly examine and modify the logical relationships between features, rather than working through abstract tree structures. The attack relationships explicitly show contradictions that can guide knowledge refinement.
- Core assumption: Human experts can more effectively engage with argumentative structures than with decision tree nodes when refining domain knowledge.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "The framework outperforms existing techniques in terms of predictive capacity and model size, producing plausibly understandable models that maintain equivalent inferential capability to their source decision trees."
  - [section] "Hence, it can be argued that it is more adequate for knowledge discovery, acquisition and refinement."
  - [corpus] Weak evidence - no direct citations found in corpus neighbors about knowledge refinement in argumentative frameworks.
- Break condition: If the argumentative structure becomes too complex or if the attack relationships are not intuitive to domain experts, the framework may not facilitate easier knowledge refinement.

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map:
  - xADG (top level): Argumentation framework with arguments and attacks
  - Arguments: Internal structure with boolean logic operators (AND, OR) and multiple supports
  - Supports: Feature-value predicates that form the premises of arguments
  - Attacks: Relationships between arguments representing contradictions
  - Decision Tree: Source model that provides inferential paths for xADG construction
  - Algorithms: Construction (Algorithm 1), simplification (Algorithm 2), and modification (m1, m2, m3) procedures

- Critical path: Decision Tree → Well-formed ADG → Simplified ADG → xADG
  - Each step transforms the model while preserving inferential capability
  - Modifications m1, m2, m3 are applied iteratively to optimize model size

- Design tradeoffs:
  - Model size vs. complexity of boolean expressions
  - Number of supports per argument vs. readability
  - Attack relationships vs. explicit contradiction representation
  - Preservation of predictive accuracy vs. model simplification

- Failure signatures:
  - Degraded predictive accuracy after transformation
  - Boolean expressions becoming too complex to interpret
  - Excessive number of supports per argument (>5)
  - Loss of attack relationships that were crucial for understanding contradictions

- First 3 experiments:
  1. Implement Algorithm 1 to convert a simple decision tree (depth 2-3) to a well-formed ADG, verify the number of arguments and attacks
  2. Apply Algorithm 2 to remove redundant paths from the ADG, measure reduction in size
  3. Apply modification m2 to merge arguments with same conclusions, track changes in number of supports and attacks

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- Question: What is the impact of using xADG modifications that slightly reduce predictive capacity but significantly improve understandability?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper suggests that modifications that slightly reduce predictive capacity might allow significant gains in understandability, but this has not been explored.
- Why unresolved: The paper focused on maintaining equivalent inferential capability to the source decision trees while improving model size and structure. It did not explore the trade-off between slight reductions in predictive accuracy and potential gains in understandability.
- What evidence would resolve it: Empirical studies comparing xADGs with and without modifications that reduce predictive capacity, measuring both accuracy and user understandability through human subject testing.

### Open Question 2
- Question: How do xADGs perform in terms of understandability when built from massive decision trees with a larger number of supports?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper mentions that user studies are needed to evaluate the understandability of xADGs with a larger number of supports, especially when derived from massive decision trees.
- Why unresolved: The current evaluation focused on classification problems of different sizes but did not extensively explore the upper bounds of xADG complexity or conduct user studies to assess understandability.
- What evidence would resolve it: User studies comparing the understandability of xADGs derived from decision trees of varying depths and complexities, measuring factors like time to comprehend, accuracy of interpretation, and user preference.

### Open Question 3
- Question: How can human knowledge be effectively incorporated into xADGs by domain experts?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper suggests that xADGs allow knowledge to be more easily incorporated through the addition of arguments and attacks, and proposes testing this in various contexts as future work.
- Why unresolved: The paper demonstrated the potential for incorporating human knowledge but did not provide a methodology or evaluate the effectiveness of this approach in practice.
- What evidence would resolve it: Case studies or experiments where domain experts actively contribute to the construction or refinement of xADGs, measuring the impact on model performance and the ease of knowledge integration compared to traditional methods.
