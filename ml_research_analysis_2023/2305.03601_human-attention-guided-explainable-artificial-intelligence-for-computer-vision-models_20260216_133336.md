# Human Attention-Guided Explainable Artificial Intelligence for Computer Vision Models

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2305.03601
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.03601
- Authors: 
- Reference count: 40
- Key outcome: HAG-XAI outperforms existing methods for object detection models by simultaneously enhancing plausibility and faithfulness through learning from human attention data

## Executive Summary
This study addresses the limited faithfulness and plausibility of current saliency-based explainable AI (XAI) methods for object detection models. While these methods work well for image classification, they fail to capture meaningful features used by object detectors. To address this, the authors propose Human Attention-Guided XAI (HAG-XAI), which learns from human attention data to optimally combine explanatory information from the model. HAG-XAI uses trainable activation functions and smoothing kernels to maximize the similarity of the generated XAI saliency maps to human attention maps. For object detection models, HAG-XAI simultaneously enhances both plausibility and faithfulness, outperforming existing methods. For image classification models, it enhances plausibility at the expense of faithfulness. The learned functions are model-specific and generalize well to other databases.

## Method Summary
The method involves training object detection models (Yolo-v5s and Faster-RCNN) on the BDD-100K dataset, collecting human attention data through eye-tracking during vehicle detection tasks, and implementing the HAG-XAI method to optimize the combination of activation maps and gradient maps using trainable activation functions and smoothing kernels. The approach aims to maximize the similarity between generated XAI saliency maps and human attention maps while preserving model faithfulness.

## Key Results
- HAG-XAI simultaneously enhances plausibility and faithfulness for object detection models, outperforming traditional XAI methods
- For image classification models, HAG-XAI enhances plausibility at the expense of faithfulness
- The learned HAG-XAI parameters are model-specific and generalize well to other databases, as demonstrated by cross-database experimental results on MS-COCO

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- **Claim**: Human attention maps have higher faithfulness than traditional XAI saliency maps for object detection, but not for image classification.
- **Mechanism**: In object detection, the features humans attend to are similar to those used by the model for decision-making, making human attention maps more faithful explanations. For image classification, humans use different features than the model, so human attention maps have lower faithfulness.
- **Core assumption**: Human attention during visual tasks reflects the features most diagnostic for successful task performance.

- **Break condition**: If human attention does not reflect task-relevant diagnostic features, or if AI models use fundamentally different feature representations than humans.

- **Mechanism**: The learnable parameters in HAG-XAI adjust how gradient and activation maps are combined to better match human attention patterns while preserving the model's feature use patterns.
- **Core assumption**: The combination of gradient and activation maps can be optimized to better reflect both human attention and model decision-making processes.
- **Evidence anchors**:

## Architecture Onboarding

- **Component map**: Raw gradients and activations → learnable activation scaling → gradient smoothing → activation-output combination → final smoothing → normalized saliency map
- **Critical path**: Raw gradients and activations → learnable activation functions (ϕα+ α− and ϕβ+ β−) → gradient smoothing (Gvα Aα and Gvγ Aγ) → activation-output combination → final smoothing → area-based normalization function (¯µ) → final saliency map
- **Design tradeoffs**: The method trades computational complexity (8 learnable parameters) for improved explanation quality. The smoothing operations help with plausibility but could potentially obscure important details.
- **Failure signatures**: If plausibility improves but faithfulness degrades, the model may be overfitting to human attention. If both metrics are low, the learnable parameters may not be converging properly or the smoothing may be too aggressive.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- Question: Does HAG-XAI generalize to object detection tasks beyond the five vehicle categories tested in BDD-100K and MS-COCO?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The authors tested generalization to MS-COCO with 80 categories but note they only examined five common categories in their results. They state "Future work may examine this possibility" for broader generalization.
- Why unresolved: The current study only evaluated generalization on a subset of common categories, not the full range of object types.
- What evidence would resolve it: Testing HAG-XAI on all 80 MS-COCO categories or other diverse object detection datasets with various object types would demonstrate full generalization capability.

### Open Question 2
- Question: How does HAG-XAI perform when applied to object detection models with different architectural designs compared to Yolo-v5s and Faster-RCNN?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The authors tested HAG-XAI on two specific architectures (one-stage and two-stage) and state "using a model with a cognitively-plausible design may allow the method to generate more human-like attention maps."
- Why unresolved: The study only examined two architectures, leaving open whether the learned functions transfer to other architectural designs.
- What evidence would resolve it: Applying HAG-XAI to various object detection architectures (e.g., RetinaNet, EfficientDet, CenterNet) and comparing performance would reveal architectural dependencies.

### Open Question 3
- Question: Can incorporating non-human-like features improve the faithfulness of HAG-XAI explanations without sacrificing plausibility?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The authors note "Since we found in Study 1 that more human-like explanations can lead to more faithful explanations, Study 2 mainly considered the features matching human attention. However, some non-human-like features may also contribute to faithful explanations."
- Why unresolved: The study focused exclusively on human-attention-guided features, leaving the potential role of non-human-like features unexplored.
- What evidence would resolve it: Developing hybrid approaches that combine human-attention-guided and non-human-like features, then testing faithfulness and plausibility trade-offs, would address this question.

## Limitations
- The method relies on human attention data collected from a specific task (vehicle detection), which may not generalize to other object detection tasks or broader visual understanding scenarios
- The proposed method introduces additional learnable parameters that increase computational complexity and may lead to overfitting to human attention patterns at the expense of model faithfulness
- The study does not provide extensive ablation studies to isolate the impact of individual components (activation functions vs. smoothing kernels) on the final performance

## Confidence
- **High confidence**: The claim that HAG-XAI improves plausibility for both object detection and image classification tasks is well-supported by the experimental results presented.
- **Medium confidence**: The assertion that HAG-XAI simultaneously enhances plausibility and faithfulness for object detection models, while trading off faithfulness for plausibility in image classification, is supported but requires further validation on diverse datasets and tasks.
- **Low confidence**: The generalizability of the learned parameters to other databases and tasks, as well as the robustness of the method to different model architectures, needs further investigation.
