# The economic trade-offs of large language models: A case study

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2306.07402
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.07402
- Reference count: 32
- Cost savings of $60,000 annually using distilled GPT-2 model for customer service

## Executive Summary
This paper presents a cost framework for evaluating large language models in customer service applications, analyzing trade-offs between model customization strategies and their economic impact. The authors compare prompt engineering, fine-tuning, and knowledge distillation across GPT-2, GPT-3, and Cohere models using real agent feedback to measure usability. They find that model usability strongly impacts cost savings, with the most usable responses outweighing higher inference costs. The study estimates an annual cost savings of $60,000 using a distilled GPT-2 model for their case study brand, and demonstrates that low perplexity correlates with higher usability rates, enabling extrapolation to additional models.

## Method Summary
The authors analyze economic trade-offs by preparing brand-specific and general conversational datasets, implementing model customization strategies including prompt engineering for GPT-3 and Cohere, fine-tuning GPT-2 and GPT-3, and distilling GPT-2 models. They evaluate model outputs using real agent feedback on usability (use/edit/ignore), calculate perplexity, and compute Expected Net Cost Savings (ENCS) for each configuration. The analysis focuses on a single retail brand's customer service data, with 100,059 training conversations and 287 test conversations from 2022.

## Key Results
- Model usability (probability of use, edit, or ignore) directly impacts cost savings more than inference cost differences
- Lower perplexity correlates with higher usability scores, enabling extrapolation to untested models
- Knowledge distillation significantly reduces inference cost with minimal impact on usability, cutting costs by nearly half

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: Model usability directly impacts cost savings more than inference cost differences
- Mechanism: When agents frequently use or edit model responses, time saved outweighs computational cost. ENCS formula captures this by multiplying usage probability by labor savings and subtracting generation cost
- Core assumption: Agents consistently apply saved time to productive work, and response quality directly correlates with willingness to use or edit
- Evidence anchors: Abstract states usability can make up for large inference cost differences; ENCS definition provided in section 3; weak corpus support
- Break condition: If model quality degrades to point where agents rarely use or edit responses, time savings drop below inference costs

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: Lower perplexity correlates with higher usability scores, enabling extrapolation to untested models
- Mechanism: Perplexity measures how well model predicts next token; lower perplexity suggests more coherent responses. Study finds statistically significant linear relationship between perplexity and probability of use/edit/ignore
- Core assumption: Relationship between perplexity and usability is stable across different model configurations and brands
- Evidence anchors: Section 4.4.2 states low perplexity correlates with probability of use; Section J shows significance (p < 0.05 for P(edit), p < 0.001 for P(use) and P(ignore)); weak corpus support
- Break condition: If linear relationship doesn't hold for new model or brand, extrapolation becomes unreliable

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: Knowledge distillation significantly reduces inference cost with minimal impact on usability
- Mechanism: Distilling fine-tuned GPT-2 model halves parameters and reduces latency, cutting inference cost per response by nearly half while maintaining similar usability scores
- Core assumption: Distilled model retains enough original model's learned patterns to generate useful responses
- Evidence anchors: Section 4.3.3 states distillation reduces latency and cost by almost half; Section I shows distilled model is 1.7x faster and 25x cheaper; weak corpus support
- Break condition: If distilled model's quality drops below usability threshold, cost savings negated by reduced agent productivity

## Foundational Learning

- Concept: Expected Net Cost Savings (ENCS) calculation
  - Why needed here: ENCS quantifies trade-off between model usability and inference cost, enabling data-driven decisions about which model to deploy
  - Quick check question: If model's responses are used 70% of time, save 25 seconds per accepted response, and cost $0.002 to generate, what is ENCS per message assuming $10/hour agent rate?

- Concept: Perplexity as proxy for response quality
  - Why needed here: Perplexity provides computationally cheap way to estimate usability across many models without manual annotation
  - Quick check question: If Model A has perplexity 4.0 and Model B has perplexity 8.0, and linear model predicts P(use) = 0.6 - 0.05*perplexity, which model is expected to have higher usability?

- Concept: Knowledge distillation process
  - Why needed here: Distillation creates smaller, faster models suitable for production while preserving most of original model's capabilities
  - Quick check question: If distilled model has 81M parameters versus 117M for original, what percentage reduction in parameters does this represent?

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map: Data ingestion → Model training (fine-tuning/distillation/prompt engineering) → Inference service (GPU cluster) → Agent annotation interface → Cost calculation pipeline
- Critical path: Data preprocessing → Model adaptation strategy selection → Inference deployment → Agent feedback collection → ENCS calculation and decision making
- Design tradeoffs: In-house models offer control and data privacy but require infrastructure investment; third-party APIs reduce upfront cost but increase per-inference expense and limit customization
- Failure signatures: High ignore rates indicate poor model quality; low throughput suggests GPU underprovisioning; unexpected cost spikes may indicate inefficient model serving or increased traffic
- First 3 experiments:
  1. Compare perplexity vs. usability for small set of fine-tuned models to validate linear relationship
  2. Measure inference latency and cost on target GPU hardware for distilled vs. full-sized models
  3. Conduct A/B testing with agents using different model configurations to measure real-world ENCS

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- Question: How does relationship between perplexity and usability generalize to other domains beyond customer service?
- Basis in paper: [inferred] Paper extrapolates usability scores from perplexity for additional models but acknowledges this is pilot analysis based on single brand and limited data
- Why unresolved: Linear models showing significance between perplexity and usability were trained on small dataset (861 generated responses) from single brand. Paper explicitly states additional data needed to determine generalizability
- What evidence would resolve it: Larger study across multiple domains and brands, with more extensive human evaluation data to validate if perplexity-usability relationship holds consistently

### Open Question 2
- Question: What is upper bound of cost savings possible with excellent LLM for agent assistance?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] Paper mentions previous work on vigilance tasks suggests there may be upper bound to cost savings as agents become less vigilant when model performs well
- Why unresolved: Paper acknowledges this as limitation but doesn't quantify or investigate upper bound. Noted as factor that could impact maximum achievable cost savings
- What evidence would resolve it: Empirical studies measuring agent performance and cost savings as LLM quality improves, specifically looking for point of diminishing returns where human oversight becomes less critical

### Open Question 3
- Question: How do real-world agent incentives and metrics impact effectiveness of LLM suggestions?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] Paper acknowledges existing agent metrics like handle time targets could incentivize different behavior than observed in their study, and agents in real situations might accept responses at higher rates
- Why unresolved: Study compensated agents at flat rate with no bonuses/penalties for judgment decisions, but real-world agent compensation structures often include performance metrics that could alter behavior
- What evidence would resolve it: Controlled study comparing agent behavior and suggestion acceptance rates under different incentive structures, particularly including metrics that directly impact agent compensation

## Limitations
- Single brand focus limits generalizability of cost-benefit findings across different domains and industries
- Reliance on agent feedback for usability scoring may introduce subjective bias and doesn't capture potential long-term impacts on customer satisfaction
- Study doesn't account for potential model failures or edge cases that could significantly impact usability and cost calculations

## Confidence
- High Confidence: Core finding that model usability directly impacts cost savings more than inference cost differences is well-supported by ENCS framework and agent feedback data
- Medium Confidence: Extrapolation of usability scores based on perplexity correlation is reasonable but should be validated across different domains and model families
- Low Confidence: Exact generalizability of cost-benefit analysis to other brands or industries remains uncertain without additional validation

## Next Checks
1. Cross-domain validation: Test perplexity-usability correlation and ENCS framework on customer service data from multiple brands and industries to assess generalizability
2. A/B testing in production: Implement most promising models (distilled GPT-2 and high-usability configurations) in live customer service environment for at least one month to measure real-world cost savings and agent productivity impacts
3. Edge case analysis: Systematically test model responses to challenging or ambiguous customer queries to identify failure modes and their impact on usability and cost calculations
