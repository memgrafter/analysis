# KG-BERTScore: Incorporating Knowledge Graph into BERTScore for Reference-Free Machine Translation Evaluation

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID**: 2301.12699
- **Source URL**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2301.12699
- **Reference count**: 32
- **Primary result**: KG-BERTScore achieves 0.830 average Pearson correlation with human judgments for language pairs into English, outperforming state-of-the-art reference-free metrics.

## Executive Summary
This paper addresses the challenge of reference-free machine translation evaluation, which is more difficult than reference-based evaluation. The proposed KG-BERTScore metric incorporates multilingual knowledge graphs into BERTScore by combining word embeddings from pre-trained multilingual models with bilingual named entity matching scores. Experiments on the WMT19 QE as a metric without references shared task show that KG-BERTScore achieves higher correlation with human judgments than current state-of-the-art reference-free metrics, with an average Pearson correlation of 0.830 for language pairs into English. The study also analyzes the impact of different pre-trained multilingual models and weighting parameters on KG-BERTScore performance.

## Method Summary
The paper proposes KG-BERTScore, which extends BERTScore by incorporating knowledge graph information through bilingual named entity matching. The method combines contextual word embeddings from pre-trained multilingual models with entity matching scores derived from multilingual knowledge graphs. The approach uses a weighted combination of BERTScore similarity and named entity matching similarity to create a composite evaluation metric. The weighting parameters are tuned to optimize correlation with human judgments on reference-free translation quality assessment tasks.

## Key Results
- KG-BERTScore achieves an average Pearson correlation of 0.830 with human judgments for language pairs into English on the WMT19 dataset
- The proposed metric outperforms current state-of-the-art reference-free evaluation metrics in correlation with human assessments
- Analysis shows the impact of different pre-trained multilingual models and weighting parameters on overall performance

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The integration of knowledge graph information addresses a key limitation of semantic similarity metrics by providing cross-lingual entity alignment that captures factual correctness beyond semantic meaning. Named entity matching supplements contextual embeddings by explicitly identifying when entities (people, organizations, locations) are correctly translated between languages. This dual approach - combining semantic similarity from BERTScore with entity-level matching from knowledge graphs - creates a more comprehensive evaluation that better aligns with human judgment of translation quality, particularly for named entities which are critical for translation accuracy.

## Foundational Learning
- **BERTScore**: Uses contextual embeddings from BERT to compute similarity between candidate and reference translations
  - Why needed: Provides robust semantic similarity measurement beyond exact string matching
  - Quick check: Verify BERT embeddings capture semantic similarity across languages

- **Reference-free MT evaluation**: Evaluating translation quality without reference translations
  - Why needed: Reference translations are expensive to obtain and may not represent all valid translations
  - Quick check: Ensure metric correlates with human judgments on diverse translation outputs

- **Knowledge graphs**: Structured representations of entities and their relationships across languages
  - Why needed: Provides cross-lingual entity alignment information that complements semantic similarity
  - Quick check: Verify named entity matching improves correlation with human judgments

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map**: Input text -> Multilingual BERT embeddings -> Named entity extraction -> Entity matching scores -> Weighted combination -> KG-BERTScore output
- **Critical path**: The most time-consuming step is generating multilingual BERT embeddings for both candidate and reference texts
- **Design tradeoffs**: Tradeoff between computational complexity (BERT inference) and evaluation quality; knowledge graph integration adds complexity but improves correlation
- **Failure signatures**: Poor performance on languages with limited knowledge graph coverage; degradation when entity matching fails due to ambiguous entities
- **3 first experiments**:
  1. Compare KG-BERTScore vs baseline BERTScore on WMT19 dataset
  2. Test different multilingual model combinations (e.g., mBERT vs XLM-R)
  3. Evaluate impact of different weighting parameters on correlation with human judgments

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- How well KG-BERTScore generalizes to language pairs beyond those into English remains an open question requiring further investigation
- The optimal weighting parameters for combining BERTScore and named entity matching scores may vary across different language families and translation domains
- The computational efficiency of KG-BERTScore compared to baseline metrics and its scalability for large-scale evaluation tasks needs further analysis

## Limitations
- The study primarily focuses on language pairs into English, limiting generalizability to other translation directions
- Evaluation relies on a single dataset (WMT19 QE as a metric without references shared task), potentially missing broader evaluation scenarios
- The specific contribution of knowledge graph integration versus baseline BERTScore is not thoroughly validated through ablation studies

## Confidence
- **High Confidence**: The experimental methodology using Pearson correlation with human judgments is sound and the reported results are statistically valid for the tested conditions
- **Medium Confidence**: The claim that KG-BERTScore outperforms all state-of-the-art reference-free metrics should be interpreted with caution due to limited dataset and translation direction scope
- **Low Confidence**: The analysis of weighting parameters' impact lacks depth, making it difficult to assess robustness across different parameter settings
