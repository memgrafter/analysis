# The Power of Typed Affine Decision Structures: A Case Study

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2304.14888
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2304.14888
- Reference count: 22
- Primary result: TADS provide precise diagnostics on robustness errors with PCA-based simplification enabling verification of medium-sized problems

## Executive Summary
This paper introduces Typed Affine Decision Structures (TADS) as a novel white-box representation for neural networks that enables formal verification of robustness properties. The key innovation is Precondition Projection, which restricts TADS to specific input regions of interest, combined with PCA for dimensionality reduction. TADS offer unique advantages in providing detailed error explanations and complete characterizations of violating inputs that current verification tools cannot deliver. While TADS alone don't scale efficiently to large networks, the PCA-based approach enables practical verification with acceptable accuracy trade-offs.

## Method Summary
The method transforms piece-wise linear neural networks (PLNNs) into TADS, which are decision DAGs with algebraic properties enabling formal verification. Precondition Projection restricts the TADS to specific input regions (like ε-balls around inputs), while PCA reduces dimensionality for scalability. The approach involves transforming the network to TADS, applying precondition projection, performing PCA-based dimensionality reduction if needed, verifying robustness in reduced space, and lifting results back to the original space. The system achieves 74% accuracy on 6-dimensional PCA versus 91% for full MNIST classification while providing precise diagnostics on robustness errors.

## Key Results
- TADS provide semantic-preserving transformation from PLNNs to decision DAGs enabling formal verification
- Precondition projection enables local verification by restricting TADS to input regions of interest
- PCA-based simplification achieves 74% accuracy on 6-dimensional MNIST vs 91% for full classification
- TADS uniquely characterize complete sets of violating inputs and identify closest adversarial examples

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: TADS provides semantic-preserving transformation from PLNNs to decision DAGs
- Mechanism: The transformation θ maps neural network layers into TADS nodes while preserving compositionality and algebraic structure
- Core assumption: Piecewise affine functions can be represented exactly as TADS without loss of information
- Evidence anchors: [section 2.5] "Lemma 2.17 (Trinity: PLNNs, TADS, and PAFs): There exists a semantics preserving transformation θ : N → Θ"
- Break condition: If the neural network contains non-piecewise-affine components or if the transformation introduces semantic drift

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: Precondition projection restricts TADS to specific input regions
- Mechanism: Polyhedral preconditions are encoded as TADS inequalities and composed with the original TADS
- Core assumption: Polyhedral regions can be accurately encoded as TADS decision predicates
- Evidence anchors: [section 4.1] "Given a TADS t representing a piece-wise affine function f : Rⁿ → Rᵐ we are interested in the behavior of f on a given (small) polyhedron S ⊆ Rⁿ"
- Break condition: If the precondition region is non-convex or if the projection eliminates critical paths needed for verification

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: PCA-based dimensionality reduction enables scalable verification
- Mechanism: PCA identifies principal components, projects inputs into lower-dimensional space, and verification results are lifted back using neighborhood preservation
- Core assumption: Data distribution along principal components captures essential structure for robustness verification
- Evidence anchors: [section 5.2] "Restricting the search for adversarial examples to S via a PCA-based transformation drastically reduces the computational load"
- Break condition: If adversarial examples exist primarily along non-principal component directions or if PCA reconstruction introduces significant approximation error

  - Why needed here: TADS represents neural networks as PAFs, and robustness verification relies on understanding how PAFs behave in different regions

  - Why needed here: TADS algebraic properties depend on affine functions forming a vector space under addition and scalar multiplication
  - Quick check question: Show that the composition of two affine functions is also affine

  - Why needed here: PCA is the dimensionality reduction technique that makes TADS verification scalable for high-dimensional problems

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map: PLNN → TADS transformation (θ) → Precondition projection (t‖S) → PCA encoding/decoding (ρₖ, θₖ) → TADS composition and verification → Argmax extension for classification

- Critical path: 1) Transform PLNN to TADS using θ, 2) Apply precondition projection for the region of interest, 3) If scalable, verify directly; if not, apply PCA dimensionality reduction, 4) Perform verification in reduced space, 5) Lift results back to original space

- Design tradeoffs:
  - Accuracy vs scalability: Higher dimensional PCA preserves more information but reduces verification efficiency
  - Exact vs approximate verification: Full TADS gives exact results but doesn't scale; PCA gives approximate results but scales
  - Global vs local explanation: TADS provides global explanations but precondition projection focuses on local properties

- Failure signatures:
  - TADS explosion: Exponential growth in nodes when transformation doesn't terminate
  - PCA information loss: Adversarial examples exist in directions orthogonal to principal components
  - Neighborhood distortion: PCA reconstruction doesn't preserve the ε-ball structure

- First 3 experiments:
  1. Verify a 2-layer ReLU network on a synthetic 2D dataset without PCA to understand basic TADS behavior
  2. Apply precondition projection to a single MNIST digit and visualize the resulting TADS
  3. Test PCA-based verification on MNIST with varying numbers of principal components (k=2,4,6) and measure accuracy vs scalability tradeoff

## Limitations
- TADS-based verification faces scalability challenges for large networks due to exponential growth in decision DAG size
- PCA-based simplification introduces approximation error that may miss adversarial examples in non-principal component directions
- The approach is currently limited to PLNNs with ReLU-like activations and needs extension to other architectures

## Confidence
- TADS transformation correctness: Medium - Theoretical foundation is sound but practical implementation details matter significantly
- Precondition projection effectiveness: High - Well-established technique in verification literature
- PCA-based scalability claims: Medium - Demonstrated on MNIST but needs validation on more complex datasets
- Robustness characterization utility: High - Novel capability with clear practical value for debugging
