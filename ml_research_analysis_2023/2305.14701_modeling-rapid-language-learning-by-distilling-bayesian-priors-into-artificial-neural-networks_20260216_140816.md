# Modeling rapid language learning by distilling Bayesian priors into artificial neural networks

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID**: 2305.14701
- **Source URL**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.14701
- **Reference count**: 40
- **Key outcome**: Demonstrates that meta-learning can transfer Bayesian inductive biases to neural networks, achieving data-efficient learning of formal languages while maintaining flexibility for natural language tasks

## Executive Summary
This paper introduces a method for combining the rapid learning capabilities of Bayesian models with the flexibility of neural networks by distilling Bayesian priors into neural networks through meta-learning. The authors use model-agnostic meta-learning (MAML) to transfer inductive biases from a Bayesian model to an LSTM neural network. When tested on formal languages, the resulting "prior-trained" network approaches the data efficiency of Bayesian models. On natural language tasks, it slightly outperforms standard neural networks and shows improved handling of recursion and priming. The work demonstrates a promising approach to decoupling inductive biases from representational commitments in neural networks.

## Method Summary
The authors use model-agnostic meta-learning (MAML) to transfer inductive biases from a Bayesian model to a neural network. They first define a Bayesian prior over formal language tasks, then sample 25,000 tasks from this prior distribution. An LSTM neural network is meta-trained on these tasks using MAML, which adjusts the network's initialization to reflect the Bayesian prior. The meta-trained network is then evaluated on held-out formal languages and natural language tasks using the CHILDES corpus. The approach aims to give neural networks the data efficiency of Bayesian models while maintaining their flexibility for handling naturalistic data.

## Key Results
- Prior-trained LSTM networks approach the data efficiency of Bayesian models on learning formal languages
- The distilled networks outperform standard neural networks on natural language tasks, particularly with recursion and priming
- LSTMs were more effective than Transformers for distilling the tested formal language priors
- The method successfully combines Bayesian data efficiency with neural network flexibility

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: Meta-learning from tasks sampled from a Bayesian prior can transfer that prior's inductive bias into a neural network.
- Mechanism: Tasks are sampled from the Bayesian model's prior distribution, and the neural network performs model-agnostic meta-learning (MAML) on these tasks, adjusting its initialization to reflect the prior.
- Core assumption: The neural network architecture can represent hypotheses from the Bayesian prior's hypothesis space and learn to prefer them through meta-learning.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "Our approach works by distilling a Bayesian model's biases into a neural network."
  - [section] "In our application of MAML, the tasks are sampled from a Bayesian model, thereby distilling inductive biases from the Bayesian model into the neural network."
  - [corpus] Weak evidence - no direct empirical test of whether the distilled prior matches the original Bayesian prior's predictions.
- Break Condition: If the neural network architecture cannot represent hypotheses from the Bayesian prior's hypothesis space, or if the meta-learning algorithm fails to effectively transfer the prior.

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: Distilling strong inductive biases into neural networks allows them to learn from limited data like Bayesian models while retaining the flexibility to handle naturalistic data.
- Mechanism: The distilled inductive bias guides the neural network to generalize appropriately from few examples (like a Bayesian model) but the continuous vector representations allow it to capture the nuances of naturalistic data (like a standard neural network).
- Core assumption: The inductive bias distilled from the Bayesian model is beneficial for learning the target tasks.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "The resulting system can learn formal linguistic patterns from a small number of examples. Like a neural network, it can also learn aspects of English syntax from a corpus of natural language—and it outperforms a standard neural network."
  - [section] "Like a Bayesian model, the resulting system can learn formal linguistic patterns from a small number of examples. Like a neural network, it can also learn aspects of English syntax from a corpus of natural language."
  - [corpus] Moderate evidence - the model outperforms standard neural networks on some linguistic phenomena, but the improvements are not dramatic.
- Break Condition: If the distilled inductive bias is not beneficial for the target tasks, or if it interferes with the neural network's ability to learn from naturalistic data.

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: The choice of meta-learning algorithm and neural network architecture affects the effectiveness of inductive bias distillation.
- Mechanism: Different meta-learning algorithms and architectures may be better suited for distilling different types of inductive biases.
- Core assumption: The meta-learning algorithm and architecture are well-matched to the type of inductive bias being distilled.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [section] "We also tried using Transformers ... but we found that the distillation was not as effective for Transformers as for LSTMs, likely because LSTMs perform better than Transformers at capturing some of the formal language mechanisms underlying our set of primitives."
  - [corpus] Weak evidence - only LSTMs and Transformers were tried, and no systematic comparison of different meta-learning algorithms was conducted.
- Break Condition: If the meta-learning algorithm or architecture is not well-matched to the inductive bias being distilled, the distillation may fail or be less effective.

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map: Bayesian model -> Task sampling -> MAML training -> LSTM neural network -> Evaluation on formal languages and natural language tasks
- Critical path: 1) Define Bayesian prior over tasks. 2) Sample tasks from prior. 3) Meta-train neural network on sampled tasks using MAML. 4) Evaluate meta-trained network on new tasks.
- Design tradeoffs: Using a more complex neural network architecture might allow for more effective distillation but could also make the meta-learning more difficult. Using a simpler prior might make the distillation easier but could limit the types of inductive biases that can be transferred.
- Failure signatures: If the meta-trained network does not perform better than a standard network on the target tasks, the distillation has likely failed. If the meta-trained network overfits to the sampled tasks, it may not generalize well to new tasks.
- First 3 experiments:
  1. Try distilling a simple prior (e.g., a uniform prior over a small set of tasks) into a neural network and evaluate on those tasks.
  2. Try distilling a more complex prior into a neural network and evaluate on a held-out set of tasks.
  3. Try using a different meta-learning algorithm (e.g., Reptile) and see if it results in more effective distillation.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- Question: How can we systematically identify which inductive biases are most beneficial to distill into neural networks for language learning?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The authors discuss the importance of identifying appropriate inductive biases, mentioning that Bayesian models of cognition provide a valuable source, but also note that in many cases it is not settled which inductive biases humans have.
- Why unresolved: While the authors demonstrate the effectiveness of distilling specific inductive biases related to formal language learning, they acknowledge that systematically identifying which inductive biases to distill in other domains remains an open challenge. The paper does not provide a general methodology for this.
- What evidence would resolve it: Empirical studies comparing the performance of neural networks distilled with different hypothesized inductive biases on language learning tasks, particularly those that can distinguish between competing theories of human language acquisition biases.

### Open Question 2
- Question: To what extent can the representational flexibility of neural networks compensate for the absence of certain inductive biases when learning language?
- Basis in paper: [inferred] The authors show that prior-trained neural networks (with distilled inductive biases) slightly outperform standard neural networks on some natural language tasks, but the improvements are often modest. This suggests that neural network flexibility may already capture some aspects of human language learning without explicit inductive biases.
- Why unresolved: The paper does not directly compare the performance of standard neural networks to humans on the same tasks, making it difficult to determine whether the remaining gap is due to missing inductive biases or other factors. Additionally, the authors note that the effect of inductive biases is more pronounced when less training data is available.
- What evidence would resolve it: Direct comparisons of standard neural networks, prior-trained neural networks, and humans on the same language learning tasks with varying amounts of training data, to determine the relative contribution of inductive biases versus representational flexibility.

### Open Question 3
- Question: How do the inductive biases distilled into neural networks via meta-learning compare to the inductive biases humans are born with?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The authors state that they do not claim that humans' linguistic inductive biases must arise via meta-learning, nor do they claim that these inductive biases are encoded in the form that MAML encodes them.
- Why unresolved: While the authors demonstrate that meta-learning can effectively distill certain inductive biases into neural networks, they do not investigate whether these biases align with those of humans. The paper focuses on the computational benefits of the approach rather than its cognitive plausibility.
- What evidence would resolve it: Comparative studies of human language learners and neural networks with different types of distilled inductive biases, to determine which biases lead to more human-like learning patterns and generalizations. Additionally, neuroscientific investigations into the neural mechanisms underlying human language learning could provide insights into the nature of human inductive biases.

## Limitations

- The paper does not provide direct empirical tests of whether the distilled prior matches the original Bayesian prior's predictions
- The effectiveness of inductive bias distillation appears sensitive to architecture choice, with LSTMs working better than Transformers for the tested formal languages
- Experiments focused on a specific set of formal language primitives and English syntax, limiting generalizability to other linguistic phenomena

## Confidence

**High Confidence Claims**:
- Meta-learning can transfer some form of inductive bias from Bayesian models to neural networks
- The distilled model outperforms standard neural networks on tested formal languages
- The method maintains some advantages of both Bayesian models (data efficiency) and neural networks (handling naturalistic data)

**Medium Confidence Claims**:
- The distilled inductive bias is "the same as" or "closely approximates" the original Bayesian prior
- The improvements on natural language tasks are practically significant (improvements were present but not dramatic)
- The architecture sensitivity is due to representational capacity differences rather than implementation details

**Low Confidence Claims**:
- Generalizability to arbitrary Bayesian priors and neural architectures
- Scalability to more complex linguistic phenomena beyond the tested primitives
- The specific mechanism by which meta-learning transfers priors (whether it's truly capturing the Bayesian prior's full structure)

## Next Checks

1. **Direct prior fidelity test**: Sample new tasks from the original Bayesian model and from the meta-trained neural network, then compare their predictions directly to quantify how closely the distilled prior matches the original.

2. **Architecture ablation study**: Systematically test the distillation method across multiple architectures (RNN variants, Transformers, MLPs) with the same prior to determine which architectural properties are essential for effective prior transfer.

3. **Cross-domain generalization test**: Apply the same distillation approach to a non-linguistic domain (e.g., visual reasoning or mathematical problem solving) using an appropriate Bayesian prior to assess whether the method generalizes beyond the specific linguistic tasks tested.
