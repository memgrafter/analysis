# Phonetic and Prosody-aware Self-supervised Learning Approach for Non-native Fluency Scoring

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID**: 2305.11438
- **Source URL**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.11438
- **Reference count**: 0
- **Primary result**: Self-supervised pre-training on phone and duration reconstruction improves non-native fluency scoring (PCC: 0.835 on Speechocean762, 0.833 on ByteRead)

## Executive Summary
This paper tackles the challenge of limited labeled data for non-native fluency scoring by introducing a self-supervised learning (SSL) approach that leverages both phonetic and prosodic information. The method involves pre-training a model to reconstruct masked phone and duration information from unlabeled speech and text data, followed by fine-tuning on human-annotated fluency scores. Experimental results on Mandarin datasets show significant improvements over baseline systems, with the BLSTM-pre architecture achieving Pearson correlation coefficients of 0.835 and 0.833 on Speechocean762 and ByteRead datasets respectively. Ablation studies confirm the effectiveness of incorporating both phonetic and prosodic components during pre-training.

## Method Summary
The proposed approach addresses data scarcity in non-native fluency scoring through a two-stage process. First, a self-supervised pre-training stage uses unlabeled speech and text data to train the model to reconstruct masked phone and duration information, capturing both phonetic and prosodic patterns. The pre-trained model is then fine-tuned on human-annotated fluency scores. The system employs BLSTM architecture for pre-training, with experimental validation showing superior performance compared to baseline SSL approaches like HuBERT on Mandarin datasets.

## Key Results
- BLSTM-pre system achieves PCC of 0.835 on Speechocean762 dataset
- BLSTM-pre system achieves PCC of 0.833 on ByteRead dataset
- Outperforms baseline SSL-based approaches on both datasets
- Ablation studies confirm effectiveness of both phonetic and prosodic pre-training components

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The approach works by leveraging self-supervised pre-training to capture rich phonetic and prosodic representations from unlabeled data, which are then transferred to the fluency scoring task through fine-tuning. By reconstructing masked phone and duration information, the model learns robust representations of both segmental (phonetic) and suprasegmental (prosodic) features that are crucial for assessing non-native speech fluency.

## Architecture Onboarding

**Component map**: Speech/text data -> Pre-training (phone/duration reconstruction) -> Fine-tuning (fluency scoring) -> Fluency score

**Critical path**: Pre-training with phone and duration reconstruction objectives is the critical path that enables downstream fluency scoring performance

**Design tradeoffs**: BLSTM vs Transformer architectures for pre-training; phonetic vs prosodic pre-training objectives; phone vs character-level representations

**Failure signatures**: Poor reconstruction accuracy during pre-training, low correlation between predicted and human fluency scores, degradation when removing phonetic or prosodic components
