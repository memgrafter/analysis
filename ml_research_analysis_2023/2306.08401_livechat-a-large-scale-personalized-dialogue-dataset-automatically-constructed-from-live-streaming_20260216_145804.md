# LiveChat: A Large-Scale Personalized Dialogue Dataset Automatically Constructed from Live Streaming

## Executive Summary
This paper introduces LiveChat, a large-scale personalized dialogue dataset automatically constructed from live streaming videos. The dataset contains 1.33 million real-life Chinese dialogues with detailed persona profiles for each of the 351 streamers. To build the dataset, the authors propose an automatic dialogue-constructing method that processes live videos and uses a reply-to-whom matching technique to extract valid dialogue sessions. Experiments on two benchmark tasks - response modeling and addressee recognition - show that leveraging persona profiles and larger average sessions per persona improves model performance. The authors also benchmark the transferability of advanced generation-based models on LiveChat, finding that pre-trained dialogue models perform poorly on this video-sourced data, while large language models exhibit richer informativeness but worse relevance under few-shot settings.

## Method Summary
The authors propose an automatic dialogue-constructing method that processes live streaming videos to extract dialogue sessions. The method involves three key steps: 1) Transcribing live videos using an Automatic Speech Recognition (ASR) tool to obtain spoken language segments; 2) Identifying streamer replies using a text matching method based on their profile information; and 3) Extracting valid dialogue sessions using a reply-to-whom matching technique. The dataset contains detailed persona profiles for each of the 351 streamers, including basic profiles (age, gender, location, character, skill, live streaming time, audience number) and text profiles (sentences describing personal habits or characteristics). The authors conduct experiments on two benchmark tasks - response modeling and addressee recognition - using both retrieval-based and generation-based models, and evaluate their performance using metrics such as Recall@1, Recall@2, Mean Reciprocal Rank (MRR), BLEU-n, and ROUGE-n.

## Key Results
- The LiveChat dataset contains 1.33 million real-life Chinese dialogues with detailed persona profiles for 351 streamers.
- Incorporating persona profiles and larger average sessions per persona improves model performance on response modeling and addressee recognition tasks.
- Pre-trained dialogue models perform poorly on the video-sourced LiveChat data, while large language models exhibit richer informativeness but worse relevance under few-shot settings.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The proposed method works by leveraging the unique characteristics of live streaming platforms, where streamers interact with their audience in real-time. By automatically constructing dialogue sessions from live videos and incorporating detailed persona profiles, the dataset captures the nuances of personalized dialogues. The use of reply-to-whom matching helps to accurately identify the addressee of each streamer response, which is crucial for training effective addressee recognition models. Additionally, the inclusion of both basic and text profiles provides a comprehensive representation of each streamer's persona, allowing models to learn personalized response styles and content.

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component Map**: Live videos -> ASR transcription -> Text matching (streamer identification) -> Reply-to-whom matching (dialogue session construction) -> Persona profile extraction -> Model training and evaluation.

**Critical Path**: The critical path involves the successful extraction of dialogue sessions using the reply-to-whom matching technique and the incorporation of accurate persona profiles. Any errors or noise in these steps can significantly impact the quality of the dataset and the performance of the trained models.

**Design Tradeoffs**: The authors trade off between the complexity of the automatic dialogue-constructing method and the quality of the resulting dataset. While the method is designed to be fully automatic, some noise and errors may be introduced due to the limitations of ASR transcription and text matching techniques.

**Failure Signatures**: Common failure modes include poor ASR transcription quality, inaccurate streamer identification, and incorrect reply-to-whom matching. These failures can lead to noisy dialogue sessions and inaccurate persona profiles, which in turn can degrade model performance.

## Limitations
- The dataset's reliance on Chinese-language live streaming content may limit its applicability to other languages and cultural contexts.
- The ASR transcription quality and video processing pipeline details are not fully specified, which could impact dataset quality.
- The generalizability of the findings to other streaming platforms remains uncertain without additional validation studies.
