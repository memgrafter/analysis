# Graph-Level Embedding for Time-Evolving Graphs

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2306.01012
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.01012
- Reference count: 29
- Primary result: Proposes a novel method for graph-level embedding in dynamic networks using multilayer graphs and temporal backtracking random walks; outperforms baselines on temporal graph similarity ranking tasks.

## Executive Summary
This paper introduces a novel method for generating graph-level embeddings in dynamic or temporal networks. The approach constructs a multilayer graph from temporal snapshots and employs a modified random walk with temporal backtracking to capture both structural and temporal dependencies. The resulting node contexts are used to train a document-level language model (doc2vec), producing embeddings that capture the evolution of the graph over time. The method is evaluated on five publicly available datasets for temporal graph similarity ranking, demonstrating superior performance compared to existing baselines.

## Method Summary
The method constructs a multilayer graph from temporal graph snapshots, where each layer represents a snapshot and inter-layer edges connect corresponding nodes across time. A temporal backtracking random walk is applied to generate node contexts, balancing structural exploration within layers and temporal exploration across layers via a stay probability α. These contexts are treated as sentences and aggregated by snapshot into documents, which are then used to train a doc2vec model to produce graph-level embeddings.

## Key Results
- Outperforms static graph-level embedding methods, temporal node-level embedding methods, and the state-of-the-art tdGraphEmbed method.
- Achieves higher precision at 10 and 20 (p@10, p@20) and better Spearman’s and Kendall’s rank correlation coefficients on five datasets.
- Demonstrates effectiveness in capturing both structural and temporal information in dynamic networks.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: Multilayer graph construction preserves both structural and temporal dependencies.
- Mechanism: Each layer represents a snapshot; inter-layer edges connect corresponding nodes across time, enabling random walks to move vertically (across time) and horizontally (within layer).
- Core assumption: Structural patterns in earlier snapshots are relevant to later ones and can be preserved via explicit edges.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract]: "Our approach involves constructing a multilayer graph and using a modified random walk with temporal backtracking to generate temporal contexts for the graph's nodes."
  - [section]: "Each layer M_t, t = 0, 1, ..., |T|, is constructed by the nodes of G and the edges of snapshot G_t. We build inter-layer edges between each pair of M_t and M_{t-1} by directly connecting the corresponding nodes from t to t-1."
- Break condition: If inter-layer edges are removed, the model loses temporal dependency tracking and degrades to static embedding.

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: Temporal backtracking random walk with stay probability α balances structural vs temporal context sampling.
- Mechanism: During each walk step, the walker stays in the current layer with probability α to gather structural info, or moves to the previous layer with probability 1-α to capture historical info.
- Core assumption: Staying in the current layer more often than moving backward yields better balance between local structure and temporal history.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [section]: "We define the stay constant α such that the probability of staying in the current layer is α, and the probability of going to the previous layer is 1-α."
  - [abstract]: "Our approach involves constructing a multilayer graph and using a modified random walk with temporal backtracking to generate temporal contexts for the graph's nodes."
- Break condition: If α is set too low, temporal context dominates and structural locality is lost; if too high, temporal context is under-sampled.

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: Treating temporal contexts as sentences and using doc2vec produces graph-level embeddings that preserve snapshot identity.
- Mechanism: Each node's walk path in a snapshot becomes a "sentence"; all sentences from a snapshot form a "document" tagged by timestamp. doc2vec learns a paragraph vector for each snapshot, which serves as the graph-level embedding.
- Core assumption: The bag-of-contexts representation captures sufficient information to distinguish snapshots without explicit ordering.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract]: "We then train a 'document-level' language model on these contexts to generate graph-level embeddings."
  - [section]: "We represent the context of each node in a G_t snapshot as a sentence. These sentences are concatenated to create a document that represents the snapshot."
- Break condition: If sentence ordering were enforced, the model would incorrectly assume temporal dependence within snapshots, which does not exist.

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map: Multilayer graph builder -> Temporal backtracking random walker -> Sentence aggregator -> Doc2vec trainer -> Embedding retriever
- Critical path: Multilayer graph → Random walk sampling → Sentence aggregation → doc2vec training → Embedding output
- Design tradeoffs:
  - Memory vs. fidelity: Larger multilayer graphs capture more temporal detail but consume more memory.
  - α tuning: Higher α preserves more structure, lower α captures more temporal evolution.
  - Walk length and count: Longer walks may overfit to local structure; more walks increase runtime.
- Failure signatures:
  - All embeddings look similar → likely α too high, or walk length too short.
  - Model overfits to noise → too many walks or walk length too long relative to snapshot size.
  - Extremely slow training → doc2vec on too many sentences; consider subsampling or reducing walk count.
- First 3 experiments:
  1. Validate multilayer graph construction: ensure edges exist both intra- and inter-layer as expected.
  2. Test walk sampling: sample a few walks and verify transition probabilities match the formula.
  3. Train doc2vec on a small synthetic dataset and confirm paragraph vectors differ across snapshots.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- Question: How does the choice of the stay constant 𝛼 affect the quality of graph-level embeddings in terms of capturing both structural and temporal information?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper mentions that the stay constant 𝛼 is set to be larger than 0.5 and states that "This ensures that the influence of older timestamps decays smoothly as the probability of entering previous layers decreases exponentially." However, it does not provide a detailed analysis of how different values of 𝛼 impact the embedding quality.
- Why unresolved: The paper only provides one specific value for 𝛼 and does not explore the sensitivity of the model to this parameter or compare embeddings generated with different 𝛼 values.
- What evidence would resolve it: Conducting experiments with various 𝛼 values and comparing the resulting embeddings' performance on downstream tasks would provide insights into the optimal choice of 𝛼.

### Open Question 2
- Question: Can the proposed temporal backtracking random walk method be extended to handle continuous-time dynamic graphs instead of discrete-time snapshots?
- Basis in paper: [inferred] The current method operates on discrete-time snapshots of the dynamic graph, as evidenced by the problem definition and the construction of the multilayer graph. The paper does not discuss handling continuous-time dynamics.
- Why unresolved: The paper focuses on discrete-time snapshots and does not explore the possibility of extending the method to continuous-time dynamic graphs.
- What evidence would resolve it: Developing and evaluating an extension of the temporal backtracking random walk method for continuous-time dynamic graphs, along with comparing its performance to the discrete-time approach, would address this question.

## Limitations
- Lack of detailed descriptions of the datasets and baseline methods, particularly tdGraphEmbed.
- Absence of a thorough ablation study on the random walk parameters and their impact on embedding quality.
- Evaluation limited to temporal graph similarity ranking, which may not generalize to other tasks.

## Confidence
- High confidence: The multilayer graph construction and temporal backtracking mechanism are clearly described and logically sound.
- Medium confidence: The use of doc2vec for graph-level embedding is appropriate, but the assumption that sentence ordering is irrelevant may not hold in all cases.
- Low confidence: The exact impact of the random walk parameters (p, q, α) on the final embeddings is not fully explored, and the evaluation metrics may not capture all aspects of embedding quality.
