# Weakly-Supervised Questions for Zero-Shot Relation Extraction

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2301.09640
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2301.09640
- Authors: 
- Reference count: 25
- Key outcome: Achieves 65.4 F1 on RE-QA, close to 66.1 with gold templates, outperforming state-of-the-art ZRE baselines on FewRel and WikiZSL datasets.

## Executive Summary
This paper addresses zero-shot relation extraction (ZRE) by generating natural language questions for unseen relations using a pre-trained question generation model, then using a QA model to generate tail entities. The key innovation is a training objective that combines off-policy sampling with marginal maximum likelihood optimization to avoid ungrammatical questions and improve semantic relevance. The method achieves competitive performance without requiring manually created gold question templates, demonstrating that QA models can match or exceed specialized ZRE models.

## Method Summary
The method uses pre-trained T5 models for question generation and answer generation, fine-tuned on QA datasets (SQuAD, NarrativeQA, RACE, BoolQ, DropQ). The core training objective combines marginal maximum likelihood (MML) optimization with off-policy sampling to avoid training collapse. During training, questions are sampled from a fixed pre-trained search module rather than the question generator being trained, with importance weighting to correct gradient estimates. Relation descriptions from Wikidata are appended to relation keywords to improve question quality. The model is evaluated on RE-QA, FewRel, and WikiZSL datasets using F1 score for tail entity extraction.

## Key Results
- Achieves 65.4 F1 on RE-QA, close to 66.1 with gold templates
- Outperforms state-of-the-art ZRE baselines on FewRel and WikiZSL datasets
- Demonstrates effective transfer of QA models to ZRE without gold question templates

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: Off-policy sampling avoids ungrammatical spurious questions that arise from directly sampling from the question generator during training.
- Mechanism: During training, questions are sampled from a fixed pre-trained search module S(q) that approximates the question posterior P(q|c,e1,r,e2). This is distinct from the question generator PθQ being trained. The gradient updates are corrected using importance weighting φ(q)/S(q) to account for the sampling distribution.
- Core assumption: The fixed search module S(q) produces grammatically correct and semantically relevant questions while the question generator PθQ is still being trained.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract]: "We propose a new learning objective that combines off-policy sampling and MML optimization."
  - [section 3.4]: "We apply an off-policy sampling technique (Levine et al., 2020; Pang and He, 2020) and use a fixed separate sampling module to sample questions during training."
  - [corpus]: Weak evidence - no explicit corpus statistics on question quality or grammar scores.
- Break condition: If the search module S(q) becomes outdated relative to PθQ's improvements, or if importance weights become unstable due to large variance in φ(q)/S(q).

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: Dual training of question and answer generators with marginal maximum likelihood (MML) optimization enables effective transfer of QA models to zero-shot relation extraction without gold templates.
- Mechanism: The model jointly optimizes PθQ (question generator) and PθA (answer generator) by marginalizing over latent questions q. The gradient for the answer module uses samples from PθQ, while the question module uses off-policy samples from S(q) with importance weighting.
- Core assumption: The latent question space is sufficiently expressive to capture the semantics of unseen relations, and the MML objective provides stable gradients for both modules.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract]: "Our technique can successfully translate relation descriptions into relevant questions, which are then leveraged to generate the correct tail entity."
  - [section 3.3]: "With MML training, the objective is approximated with numerical summation using questions sampled from the question module."
  - [corpus]: Moderate evidence - performance gains (65.4 F1) compared to baselines without gold templates.
- Break condition: If the question posterior PθQ becomes too peaked or degenerate, or if the answer module overfits to specific question patterns.

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: Appending relation descriptions to relation keywords provides additional context that improves question generation quality and semantic relevance.
- Mechanism: During training, the relation description from Wikidata is concatenated with the relation keyword r before being fed to both the question generator PθQ and the sampling module S(q). This augmented input helps disambiguate relations with similar keywords.
- Core assumption: Relation descriptions contain disambiguating information that is not present in the short relation keywords alone.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [section 4.1]: "For all the datasets, if possible, we retrieve the relation description from the wikidata and append it to the relation keyword r in the question generator PθQ or the sampling module S(q)."
  - [corpus]: Weak evidence - no ablation studies provided on the impact of relation descriptions.
- Break condition: If relation descriptions are unavailable, noisy, or introduce ambiguity rather than resolving it.

## Foundational Learning

- Concept: Marginal Maximum Likelihood (MML) optimization for latent variable models
  - Why needed here: The true question q is unobserved, requiring marginalization over possible questions to learn the joint distribution P(e2,q|c,e1,r).
  - Quick check question: How does MML differ from standard maximum likelihood when dealing with latent variables?

- Concept: Off-policy reinforcement learning and importance weighting
  - Why needed here: Prevents training collapse by using a fixed search policy for sampling while training a separate policy, with importance weights correcting the gradient estimates.
  - Quick check question: What is the purpose of the φ(q)/S(q) term in the gradient updates?

- Concept: Sequence-to-sequence question generation with pre-trained transformers
  - Why needed here: The question generator must produce natural language questions conditioned on context, head entity, and relation, requiring strong language modeling capabilities.
  - Quick check question: Why is pre-training on QA corpora beneficial for the question generation task?

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map: Pre-trained T5 models → Question generator PθQ → Search module S(q) → Answer generator PθA → Tail entity output
- Critical path: Input context → Entity/relation extraction → Relation description retrieval → Question generation (PθQ) → Question sampling (S(q)) → Answer generation (PθA) → Tail entity output
- Design tradeoffs:
  - Using off-policy sampling adds complexity but prevents ungrammatical questions
  - MML optimization requires sampling multiple questions per example, increasing compute
  - Pre-training on multiple QA datasets provides generalization but may introduce task-specific biases
- Failure signatures:
  - Low F1 scores with high variance across folds suggests question generation issues
  - Perplexity spikes in generated questions indicate grammatical degradation
  - Performance drops when relation descriptions are unavailable
- First 3 experiments:
  1. Compare OffMML-G vs MML-G on RE-QA with and without relation descriptions to measure their impact
  2. Measure perplexity of generated questions during training to detect grammatical degradation
  3. Ablation study: use only relation keywords vs keywords + descriptions in the question generator

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- Question: How does the OffMML-G training objective perform compared to other ZRE methods on document-level entity-relation extraction?
- Basis in paper: [inferred] The paper mentions that future work could deploy the technique on document-level entity-relation extraction, but does not provide any experimental results.
- Why unresolved: The paper only evaluates the method on sentence-level RE datasets (RE-QA, FewRel, and WikiZSL). Document-level RE involves more complex contexts and potentially longer dependencies, which may require additional modeling considerations.
- What evidence would resolve it: Experiments applying the OffMML-G method to document-level RE datasets, comparing performance with other document-level ZRE methods.

- Question: What is the impact of using different question generation models (e.g., T5-large instead of T5-small) on the performance of the proposed method?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper states that it uses T5-small models in all experiments and mentions that further gains can be achieved by switching to T5-large models.
- Why unresolved: The paper does not explore the performance difference between different model sizes for the question generation component.
- What evidence would resolve it: Experiments using different sizes of T5 models for question generation, comparing performance metrics and computational costs.

- Question: How does the proposed method handle ambiguous relation keywords that can lead to multiple valid questions?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper discusses this issue, noting that omitting the tail entity in the question generator while using context can result in multiple plausible questions for ambiguous relations.
- Why unresolved: While the paper introduces a solution (appending relation descriptions and using a search module that includes the tail entity), it doesn't thoroughly evaluate how well this resolves the ambiguity problem in practice.
- What evidence would resolve it: Detailed analysis of generated questions for ambiguous relations, including human evaluation of question relevance and correctness, and comparison with baseline methods on ambiguous cases.

## Limitations
- Limited empirical evidence for the stability of the fixed search module S(q) throughout training
- Sparse ablation studies, particularly regarding the impact of relation descriptions
- Significant computational overhead from sampling multiple questions per training example without thorough efficiency analysis
- Small performance gap (0.7 F1) between gold templates and proposed method suggests room for improvement

## Confidence
- **High confidence**: The core methodology of using QA models for ZRE without gold templates is sound and well-validated across multiple datasets (FewRel, WikiZSL, RE-QA).
- **Medium confidence**: The specific implementation of off-policy sampling with importance weighting provides measurable benefits, though the exact magnitude depends on implementation details not fully specified.
- **Low confidence**: The claim that relation descriptions significantly improve performance lacks empirical validation through ablation studies.

## Next Checks
1. **Ablation study**: Compare OffMML-G performance with and without relation descriptions on RE-QA to quantify their impact on question quality and downstream F1 scores.
2. **Sampling efficiency analysis**: Measure the relationship between number of samples collected per training example (currently 8) and model performance to identify the point of diminishing returns.
3. **Stability monitoring**: Track the KL divergence between S(q) and PθQ(q|c,e1,r) throughout training to empirically verify that the search module remains a valid sampling distribution as the question generator improves.
