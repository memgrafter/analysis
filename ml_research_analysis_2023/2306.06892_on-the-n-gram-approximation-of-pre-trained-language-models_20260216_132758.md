# On the N-gram Approximation of Pre-trained Language Models

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2306.06892
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.06892

## Executive Summary
This paper addresses the problem of using large pre-trained language models (PLMs) like GPT-2 for language modeling in automatic speech recognition (ASR), specifically focusing on low-resource scenarios. The core idea is to approximate GPT-2 into an n-gram model using two methods: text sampling-based conversion and probability-based conversion. Additionally, the authors introduce a vocabulary-restricted decoding method for random sampling. The primary results show that interpolating with a large sampled corpus improves test perplexity over a baseline trigram by 15%, and the vocabulary-restricted decoding method further improves this by 5% in domain-specific settings. The study evaluates the effects of domain difficulty and data size on the usability of generated text across eight domain-specific corpora.

## Method Summary
The paper proposes two methods to approximate GPT-2 into an n-gram model: text sampling-based approximation (SBA) and probability-based approximation (PBA). For SBA, GPT-2 is fine-tuned on the training data, then used to generate a large synthetic corpus (100x the training size) using top-p sampling. This generated text is used to build an n-gram model (RS-KN3) which is interpolated with a baseline Kneser-Ney trigram (KN3). For PBA, the paper directly converts GPT-2 probabilities to n-gram probabilities, but finds this method less effective due to probability sharing between n-gram orders. The authors also introduce a vocabulary-restricted decoding method (VR-KN3) that constrains the BPE token pool during generation to only those present in the training vocabulary, improving domain specificity.

## Key Results
- Interpolating with a large sampled corpus improves test perplexity over a baseline trigram by 15%.
- Vocabulary-restricted decoding further improves perplexity by 5% in domain-specific settings.
- Probability-based approximation is less effective than sampling-based approximation due to GPT-2's tendency to overestimate lower-order n-gram probabilities.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: Large-scale text sampling from a fine-tuned GPT-2 can generate domain-specific text that improves n-gram language model perplexity when interpolated with the baseline.
- Mechanism: The PLM's pre-training on large general corpora provides it with strong language modeling capabilities. Fine-tuning adapts these capabilities to the target domain, and generating many synthetic sentences from the fine-tuned model provides additional training data for the n-gram model, effectively augmenting the original training corpus.
- Core assumption: The generated text preserves domain characteristics and linguistic structure sufficiently to benefit the n-gram model.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract]: "interpolating with a large sampled corpus improves test perplexity over a baseline trigram by 15%"
  - [section]: "GPT-2 is fine-tuned with a learning rate of 5e-5... Text generation uses top-p [20] sampling with p=0.95 and a temperature of 1.0. Unless specified otherwise, we generate 100 times the training data for all SBA experiments."
  - [corpus]: Weak evidence; corpus neighbors show related work but no direct perplexity comparison for this exact sampling method.
- Break condition: If the generated text drifts significantly from the target domain or introduces noise that degrades the n-gram model's performance.

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: Vocabulary-restricted decoding during text generation reduces out-of-vocabulary (OOV) tokens and improves domain specificity of the generated text.
- Mechanism: By restricting the BPE token pool during sampling to only those present in the training vocabulary, the generated text is forced to use domain-relevant vocabulary, reducing the introduction of irrelevant or domain-drifting tokens.
- Core assumption: The training set vocabulary is sufficiently representative of the domain to allow meaningful text generation without introducing significant artifacts.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [section]: "We expect that in domain-specific scenarios, constraining the generation vocabulary will suppress tokens and force GPT-2 to model the words in the training set better."
  - [section]: "In relative terms, we see that RS-KN3 under-performs the baseline considerably (+40% ppl) with ATCO2... On the other hand, VR-KN3 performs strongly here with 20% improvement over KN3."
  - [corpus]: Weak evidence; corpus neighbors don't discuss vocabulary restriction specifically.
- Break condition: If the vocabulary restriction is too severe, preventing the model from generating grammatically correct or meaningful sentences.

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: Probability-based approximation (PBA) of GPT-2 to n-gram probabilities is less effective than sampling-based approximation due to GPT-2's tendency to overestimate lower-order n-gram probabilities.
- Mechanism: PBA directly assigns n-gram probabilities from the PLM, but the subword nature of GPT-2 and probability sharing between n-gram orders leads to inaccurate probability assignments, especially for lower-order n-grams.
- Core assumption: The probability sharing and subword granularity of GPT-2 create systematic biases when converting to word-level n-gram probabilities.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [section]: "GPT-2 overestimates lower-order n-gram probabilities during PBA because of probability sharing between higher and lower-order n-grams."
  - [section]: "PBA incurs significantly higher deterioration in comparison... This is consistent with previously reported results using PBA [8, 11], but more drastic with GPT-2."
  - [corpus]: Weak evidence; corpus neighbors show related PLM approximation work but not specific PBA vs SBA comparison for GPT-2.
- Break condition: If the probability conversion method is modified to account for the subword granularity and probability sharing issues.

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map:
  - GPT-2 124M model (huggingface) -> Fine-tuning pipeline -> Text generation module -> N-gram model builder -> Vocabulary builder -> Evaluation pipeline

- Critical path:
  1. Fine-tune GPT-2 on training data
  2. Generate large synthetic corpus using fine-tuned GPT-2
  3. Build n-gram models (baseline, sampled, interpolated)
  4. Evaluate perplexity and OOV rate on test set

- Design tradeoffs:
  - Sampling vs. Probability-based approximation: Sampling is more effective but requires generating a large corpus; PBA is direct but less accurate for GPT-2.
  - Vocabulary restriction: Improves domain specificity but may limit grammatical diversity.
  - Interpolation weights: Need to be tuned for optimal performance; too much weight on sampled data can introduce noise.

- Failure signatures:
  - High OOV rate in generated text: Indicates vocabulary restriction is too severe or generated text is not domain-relevant.
  - Perplexity degradation with interpolation: Suggests generated text is introducing noise or domain drift.
  - Slow convergence during fine-tuning: May indicate insufficient training data or inappropriate hyperparameters.

- First 3 experiments:
  1. Fine-tune GPT-2 on a small domain-specific corpus and generate a small synthetic corpus (e.g., 10x training data) to verify the fine-tuning and generation process.
  2. Build a baseline n-gram model and an interpolated model using the generated corpus, and compare perplexities on a held-out development set.
  3. Implement vocabulary-restricted decoding and compare perplexity and OOV rate with unrestricted sampling on a small domain-specific dataset.

## Limitations
- The computational cost of generating large-scale synthetic corpora (100x the training data) may not be practical for very low-resource scenarios.
- The paper does not thoroughly explore the impact of different interpolation weights between the baseline and sampled models.
- The vocabulary-restricted decoding method may introduce artifacts or grammatical errors if the training vocabulary is too limited.

## Confidence
- **High Confidence**: The core finding that sampling-based approximation (SBA) outperforms probability-based approximation (PBA) for GPT-2 is well-supported by the experimental results across multiple domains.
- **Medium Confidence**: The claim that interpolating a large sampled corpus improves test perplexity over a baseline trigram by 15% is supported by the data, but the exact magnitude of improvement may vary depending on the specific dataset and fine-tuning procedure.
- **Low Confidence**: The assertion that GPT-2 "overestimates lower-order n-gram probabilities" during PBA is stated but not rigorously proven; the evidence is primarily observational from the perplexity degradation.
