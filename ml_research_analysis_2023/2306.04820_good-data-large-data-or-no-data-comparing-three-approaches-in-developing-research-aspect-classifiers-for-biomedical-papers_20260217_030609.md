# Good Data, Large Data, or No Data? Comparing Three Approaches in Developing Research Aspect Classifiers for Biomedical Papers

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID**: 2306.04820
- **Source URL**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.04820
- **Reference count**: 19
- **Primary result**: Task-aligned data is critical; PubMed 200K and LLMs do not outperform CODA-19 fine-tuned model

## Executive Summary
This study investigates whether large automatically curated datasets or large language models can replace task-specific training data for research aspect classification in biomedical papers. The authors compare three approaches: training on a large biomedical dataset (PubMed 200K RCT), using large language models (GPT-4), and fine-tuning on a task-aligned dataset (CODA-19). Results show that neither the large dataset nor the LLMs outperform the SciBERT model fine-tuned specifically on CODA-19, demonstrating the importance of having dedicated, task-aligned training data for specialized classification tasks.

## Method Summary
The paper compares three approaches for research aspect classification: (1) fine-tuning SciBERT on PubMed 200K RCT and CODA-19 separately and using mixing strategies, (2) zero/few-shot prompting with GPT-4, and (3) fine-tuning SciBERT directly on CODA-19. Position encoding is incorporated by adding normalized sentence position tokens to each sentence. The models are evaluated on CODA-19's test set using accuracy and macro F1 scores. The study tests whether large-scale biomedical data or powerful LLMs can substitute for task-specific training data.

## Key Results
- PubMed 200K RCT does not improve CODA-19 classification performance regardless of training approach used
- GPT-4 performs well in zero-shot and few-shot settings but does not outperform SciBERT fine-tuned on CODA-19
- Position encoding improves performance over bi-ANN+CRF baseline and helps capture sentence order information
- SciBERT fine-tuned on CODA-19 with position encoding achieves the best results (accuracy: 0.698, macro F1: 0.709)

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- **Claim**: Task-aligned data remains critical even with large-scale pretraining or LLMs.
- **Mechanism**: Models pre-trained on general or scientific corpora (SciBERT, GPT-4) still rely heavily on the distribution of their training data. When the target task (CODA-19) has different label semantics and class distributions than the training corpus, fine-tuning on mismatched data does not transfer well.
- **Core assumption**: Pre-training on biomedical/scientific corpora reduces but does not eliminate the need for in-domain task data.
- **Evidence anchors**:
  - [abstract] "using the PubMed 200K RCT dataset does not improve performance for the CODA-19 task"
  - [section 4.2] "SciBERTPubMed+Pos does not show any improvement over the performance on the CODA-19 test set"

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim**: Position encoding can substitute for explicit inter-sentence modeling in sequential classification tasks.
- **Mechanism**: By normalizing sentence positions and prepending them as tokens, the model can learn positional dependencies without requiring CRF or other structured prediction layers.
- **Core assumption**: The relative order of sentences within abstracts is informative for aspect classification.
- **Evidence anchors**:
  - [section 3.4] "we incorporate position information by simply adding a position encoding to the beginning of each sentence"
  - [section 4.1] "SciBERTPubMed+Pos outperforms the bi-ANN+CRF model"

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim**: Fine-tuning large language models on task-specific data yields better performance than zero/few-shot prompting.
- **Mechanism**: Even powerful LLMs like GPT-4, when used in zero-shot or few-shot mode, cannot match the performance of models fine-tuned on the exact task distribution because their internal representations are not adapted to the task's specific patterns.
- **Core assumption**: In-context learning via prompting is insufficient for complex domain-specific classification tasks.
- **Evidence anchors**:
  - [abstract] "GPT-4 performs well in both zero-shot and few-shot settings, it does not outperform the SciBERT model fine-tuned on the CODA-19 dataset"
  - [section 4.3] "SciBERTCODA+Pos remains the best-performing model with an accuracy of 0.698 and a macro F1 score of 0.709"

## Foundational Learning

- **Concept**: Dataset alignment and label mapping
  - Why needed here: CODA-19 and PubMed 200K use different label sets; direct application without mapping leads to incorrect training signals.
  - Quick check question: If PubMed's "Methods" maps to CODA-19's "Method", what should "Objective" map to?

- **Concept**: Position encoding as feature injection
  - Why needed here: Abstracts have implicit sentence order that informs aspect roles; models need explicit positional features to capture this.
  - Quick check question: How is the position value normalized in the paper?

- **Concept**: Fine-tuning vs. prompting trade-offs
  - Why needed here: Choosing between adapting a model to a task (fine-tuning) or relying on in-context learning (prompting) impacts performance and resource use.
  - Quick check question: Which approach performed better on CODA-19: zero-shot GPT-4 or SciBERT fine-tuned on CODA-19?

## Architecture Onboarding

- **Component map**: SciBERT backbone → Position encoding layer → Classification head → Loss function (cross-entropy). For LLMs, input prompt → Model → Output parsing → Label mapping.
- **Critical path**: Data preprocessing (label mapping, position encoding) → Model training/fine-tuning → Evaluation on CODA-19 test set.
- **Design tradeoffs**: Using position encoding avoids complex CRF layers but may not capture all sequential dependencies. Fine-tuning on mismatched datasets may introduce noise but can provide regularization.
- **Failure signatures**: Poor performance on minority labels (e.g., Purpose in CODA-19) suggests data imbalance or misalignment. Consistently low recall in zero-shot settings suggests prompt design or model capacity limits.
- **First 3 experiments**:
  1. Fine-tune SciBERT on CODA-19 with and without position encoding to confirm the impact.
  2. Train on PubMed 200K with position encoding and map labels to CODA-19 to test transfer.
  3. Evaluate GPT-4 zero-shot and few-shot on a sampled subset of CODA-19 to benchmark LLM performance.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- Question: Would using domain-adaptive pretraining on PubMed 200K RCT data before fine-tuning on CODA-19 improve performance?
- Basis in paper: [inferred] The authors mention that domain-adaptive pretraining is always helpful (Gururangan et al., 2020) and hypothesize that two-staged mixing strategy with classification objective may not be the best way for adapting the model to a specific domain.
- Why unresolved: The paper only tested fine-tuning SciBERT on PubMed 200K and then fine-tuning on CODA-19, but did not explore domain-adaptive pretraining as a separate approach.
- What evidence would resolve it: Training results comparing domain-adaptive pretraining on PubMed 200K followed by fine-tuning on CODA-19 against the current approaches.

### Open Question 2
- Question: Would incorporating the context of the entire abstract (rather than just position encoding) improve the performance of research aspect classification?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The authors mention in the conclusion that they will explore methods for the model to consider the context and predict all sentences within a single abstract at once.
- Why unresolved: The current study only used position encoding as a simple way to incorporate sentence position, but did not explore more sophisticated context-aware approaches.
- What evidence would resolve it: Performance comparison between the current position-encoding approach and a context-aware approach that considers the entire abstract.

### Open Question 3
- Question: How would the performance of LLMs change if they were fine-tuned on a large biomedical corpus before being used for few-shot or zero-shot learning on CODA-19?
- Basis in paper: [inferred] The authors found that GPT-4, which was not specifically fine-tuned on biomedical data, performed well but not better than SciBERT fine-tuned on CODA-19. This suggests that domain-specific fine-tuning might further improve LLM performance.
- Why unresolved: The study only evaluated LLMs in their pre-trained state without any task-specific or domain-specific fine-tuning.
- What evidence would resolve it: Performance comparison between the current LLM results and LLM results after fine-tuning on a large biomedical corpus.

## Limitations

- Dataset alignment uncertainty: The exact label mapping strategy between PubMed 200K and CODA-19 remains underspecified, making it unclear whether poor transfer stems from fundamental dataset mismatch or suboptimal alignment.
- LLM version variability: The study uses GPT-4 without specifying access method, and results may not generalize to other versions or deployment methods.
- Position encoding generalization: The benefit of position encoding may not extend to longer documents or different structural patterns where sequential dependencies are more complex.

## Confidence

- **High Confidence**: The core finding that task-aligned data remains critical for specialized classification tasks. Well-supported by direct experimental comparisons.
- **Medium Confidence**: The conclusion that zero/few-shot prompting with GPT-4 underperforms fine-tuned models. Results are clear but may shift with newer LLM versions or improved prompt engineering.
- **Low Confidence**: The assertion that position encoding universally substitutes for CRF layers. This appears dataset-specific and may not hold for documents with more complex structural dependencies.

## Next Checks

1. **Label Mapping Verification**: Conduct ablation studies testing different label mapping strategies between PubMed 200K and CODA-19 to isolate whether poor transfer stems from fundamental dataset mismatch versus alignment methodology.

2. **LLM Version Benchmarking**: Repeat the GPT-4 experiments using multiple model versions and deployment methods to establish robustness of the prompting versus fine-tuning comparison.

3. **Cross-Dataset Generalization**: Apply the position encoding approach to a different sequential classification task (e.g., legal document section classification) to test whether the CRF-substitution benefit extends beyond biomedical abstracts.
