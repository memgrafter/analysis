# New Metrics to Encourage Innovation and Diversity in Information Retrieval Approaches

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2301.08062
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2301.08062
- Reference count: 0
- The paper proposes new IR evaluation metrics that reward systems for retrieving relevant documents missed by other systems, aiming to promote diversity in approaches.

## Executive Summary
This paper introduces a new class of evaluation metrics for information retrieval that incentivize innovation by rewarding systems for finding relevant documents that other systems miss. The metrics modify classic Precision@K and Average Precision to incorporate a linear interpolation parameter α that balances standard performance versus innovation reward based on document rarity. Experiments over four TREC collections demonstrate that these metrics change system rankings compared to traditional metrics while providing higher discriminative power and evaluation stability.

## Method Summary
The method introduces two new metrics—P@KRareness and APRareness—that incorporate document rarity into standard IR metrics. Document rarity R(d) is calculated as 1 - Sd/S, where Sd is the number of systems retrieving document d and S is the total number of systems. The metrics use a linear interpolation parameter α to balance between standard performance (1-α)Rel(di) and rarity-weighted performance αRel(di)R(di). This creates a mixture model where α=0 reproduces the original metrics, while α=1 focuses entirely on rare document retrieval.

## Key Results
- The proposed metrics change system rankings compared to existing metrics, as measured by decreasing Kendall's τ values as α increases
- P@KRareness and APRareness achieve higher discriminative power than baseline metrics, with more significantly different system pairs identified via Tukey's HSD tests
- The metrics demonstrate higher evaluation stability across topic sampling compared to standard metrics, with stability scores ranging from 0.5-0.8 versus 0.3-0.6 for baselines

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: The linear interpolation parameter α controls the balance between standard IR performance and innovation reward.
- Mechanism: By modifying Precision@K and Average Precision metrics to include a term weighting document rarity (R(d) = 1 - Sd/S), systems are incentivized to retrieve documents that other systems miss. The mixture model (1-α)Rel(di) + αRel(di)R(di) allows smooth transition from standard to innovation-focused evaluation.
- Core assumption: Document rarity (how many other systems retrieve a document) is a valid proxy for methodological innovation.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "Whereas traditional IR metrics focus on user experience, our two 'innovation' metrics instead reward exploration of more divergent, higher-risk ranking methods that find relevant documents missed by most other systems."
  - [section] "A simple mixture-weight parameter controls the relative weight placed on such rarity, and setting this to zero reverts to the original metric."
- Break condition: If document rarity does not correlate with methodological differences, the incentive structure fails to promote innovation.

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: The metrics achieve higher discriminative power by creating larger score gaps between systems.
- Mechanism: By rewarding rare relevant documents, systems that find unique relevant documents receive higher scores than they would under standard metrics. This creates more significant differences between system pairs when measured with statistical tests like Tukey's HSD.
- Core assumption: Greater score variation leads to better differentiation between system approaches.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [section] "Table 1 shows the number of significantly different pairs for baseline and our proposed metrics when we use 95% and 99% significance thresholds. We observe that our metrics have higher discriminative power than baselines."
  - [section] "Increasing α tends to increase discriminative power across test collections."
- Break condition: If the score distribution becomes too compressed or if most systems achieve similar scores regardless of rarity, discriminative power decreases.

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: The metrics provide higher evaluation stability by reducing sensitivity to topic sampling.
- Mechanism: By incorporating rarity, the metrics smooth out performance variations across different topic sets. The stability experiment samples half the topics and measures how consistently systems are ranked across trials.
- Core assumption: Averaging over rarity-weighted scores reduces variance compared to standard metrics.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [section] "We take the average stability scores of all pairs as the overall metric stability... Results for baselines vs. proposed metrics are shown in Table 2. P@100Rareness and APRareness yield a higher stability score in all cases vs. their classic counterparts."
- Break condition: If topic sampling introduces systematic bias that is amplified by the rarity component, stability could decrease.

## Limitations
- The metrics assume binary relevance judgments, limiting applicability to datasets with graded relevance
- The relationship between document rarity and true methodological innovation is not empirically validated
- The paper does not explore how different test collection properties affect metric performance

## Confidence
- **High confidence**: The mathematical formulation of P@KRareness and APRareness is correct and the metrics revert to standard versions when α=0
- **Medium confidence**: The empirical findings regarding improved discriminative power and stability are robust, though dependent on the specific TREC collections used
- **Medium confidence**: The interpretation that these metrics will incentivize innovation in practice requires further validation through actual participation in evaluation campaigns
