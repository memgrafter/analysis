# Semi-supervised Clustering with Two Types of Background Knowledge: Fusing Pairwise Constraints and Monotonicity Constraints

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2302.14060
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2302.14060
- Reference count: 40
- This study introduces the first method for combining two types of background knowledge—pairwise constraints and monotonicity constraints—in clustering

## Executive Summary
This paper presents the first method to combine pairwise constraints and monotonicity constraints in semi-supervised clustering. The proposed Pairwise Constrained K-Means - Monotonic (PCKM-Mono) algorithm uses an EM optimization scheme with a hybrid objective function that balances monotonic distance metrics and pairwise constraint penalties. Experiments on 12 benchmark datasets and a real-world case study demonstrate that PCKM-Mono outperforms purely monotonic and purely constrained clustering methods while achieving competitive results for constraint satisfaction and monotonicity.

## Method Summary
PCKM-Mono extends pairwise constrained K-means clustering by integrating monotonicity constraints through a hybrid objective function. The method uses an EM optimization scheme where the E-step assigns instances to clusters minimizing a combined cost of monotonic distance and constraint violation penalties, and the M-step updates centroids as averages of assigned instances. The monotonic distance metric is based on preference decomposition, and pairwise constraints (Must-link and Cannot-link) are incorporated as penalty terms in the objective function.

## Key Results
- PCKM-Mono outperforms purely monotonic and purely constrained clustering methods on 12 benchmark datasets in terms of Adjusted Rand Index
- The method achieves competitive results for constraint satisfaction (Unsat measure) and monotonicity (Non-Monotonic Index)
- Statistical analysis confirms PCKM-Mono's superiority for general clustering quality over baselines
- Real-world case study on Shanghai Ranking of World Universities validates the method's practical applicability

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: The hybrid objective function enables joint optimization of monotonicity and pairwise constraints, balancing both through weighted terms.
- Mechanism: The cost function combines a monotonic distance metric (based on preference) with penalty terms for pairwise constraint violations. This hybrid structure ensures that both types of constraints influence the clustering outcome simultaneously during EM optimization.
- Core assumption: The monotonic distance metric and constraint penalty terms can be meaningfully combined into a single scalar objective without destroying the individual semantic meaning of each component.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "An expectation-minimization (EM) optimization scheme is proposed to optimize a hybrid objective function which fuses both monotonicity and pairwise constraints."
  - [section] "The cost function of the proposed PCKM-Mono algorithm combines two main elements: a monotonic distance measure (proposed in [18]) and a pairwise constraint-based penalty term."
- Break condition: If the weight balance between monotonic and pairwise terms is inappropriate, the optimization may favor one constraint type too heavily, degrading performance on the other.

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: The EM optimization scheme with the hybrid objective allows the method to converge to a local optimum that satisfies both monotonicity and pairwise constraints reasonably well.
- Mechanism: In the E-step, instances are assigned to clusters that minimize the hybrid cost, balancing proximity under the monotonic metric and constraint satisfaction. The M-step updates centroids using standard averaging. Iteration refines the partition.
- Core assumption: The monotonic distance metric (based on preference decomposition) is continuous and differentiable enough for EM convergence.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "This objective function can be optimized with an EM optimization scheme."
  - [section] "An expectation-minimization (EM) optimization scheme is used, along with a hybrid objective function which takes into account both pairwise constraints and monotonicity constraints."
- Break condition: If the monotonic distance metric produces flat regions or discontinuities, EM may fail to converge or converge to poor local optima.

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: By combining monotonicity and pairwise constraints, the method can exploit complementary information, improving overall clustering quality beyond what either type alone achieves.
- Mechanism: Monotonicity constraints provide global ordering information across instances, while pairwise constraints provide local relationship information. Their combination allows the algorithm to leverage both, correcting errors that would arise from using only one type.
- Core assumption: The dataset contains sufficient structure such that monotonicity and pairwise constraints are compatible and complementary rather than contradictory.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "Experiments on 12 benchmark datasets... show that PCKM-Mono outperforms purely monotonic and purely constrained clustering methods..."
  - [section] "By examining the results, it seems obvious that the proposed algorithm, PCKM-Mono, is able to find a balance between constraint satisfaction and the monotonicity of the output partition."
- Break condition: If monotonicity and pairwise constraints are highly contradictory on a dataset, the hybrid method may produce inferior results compared to using only the more reliable constraint type.

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map:
  - Input: Dataset X with n instances and u features, pairwise constraint sets C= (Must-link) and C≠ (Cannot-link), number of clusters K
  - Core: EM optimization loop with hybrid objective function (monotonic distance + constraint penalties)
  - Output: Partition C of K non-overlapping clusters
  - Key subroutines: Distance calculation using preference-based metric, constraint penalty evaluation, centroid update

- Critical path:
  1. Initialize centroids randomly
  2. E-step: Assign each instance to cluster minimizing hybrid cost
  3. M-step: Update centroids as average of assigned instances
  4. Check convergence (centroid shift < threshold)
  5. Repeat until converged or max iterations reached

- Design tradeoffs:
  - Using a hybrid objective balances two constraint types but requires careful weight tuning
  - EM provides a simple optimization framework but may converge to local optima
  - The monotonic distance metric is computationally more expensive than Euclidean distance but provides ordering information

- Failure signatures:
  - Poor ARI scores despite high constraint satisfaction may indicate the monotonic distance metric is not well-suited to the data
  - High NMI but poor ARI may indicate the method is overly enforcing monotonicity at the expense of true cluster structure
  - Non-convergence may indicate issues with the monotonic distance metric or inappropriate initialization

- First 3 experiments:
  1. Run PCKM-Mono on a small monotonic dataset with no constraints to verify it produces reasonable clusters
  2. Run with only pairwise constraints to verify constraint satisfaction mechanism works
  3. Run with only monotonicity constraints to verify the monotonic distance metric produces ordered clusters

## Limitations

- The hybrid objective function requires careful weight tuning between monotonic and pairwise terms, which is not fully specified in the paper
- Performance on datasets where monotonicity and pairwise constraints are contradictory is not well-characterized
- The paper lacks implementation details for the preference-based distance metric and constraint generation procedure
