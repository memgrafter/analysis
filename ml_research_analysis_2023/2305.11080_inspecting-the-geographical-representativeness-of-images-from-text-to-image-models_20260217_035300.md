# Inspecting the Geographical Representativeness of Images from Text-to-Image Models

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2305.11080
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.11080
- Reference count: 40
- Primary result: Text-to-image models DALL·E 2 and Stable Diffusion exhibit geographical bias, with images reflecting US and Indian surroundings more than other countries.

## Executive Summary
This study investigates geographical representativeness in images generated by text-to-image models DALL·E 2 and Stable Diffusion. Through a crowdsourced study with participants from 27 countries, the research finds that these models show significant geographical bias, with the US and India receiving the highest representation scores while many other countries score below 3 out of 5. The study reveals that specifying country names in prompts improves geographical representativeness by 1.44 points for DALL·E 2 and 0.75 for Stable Diffusion on average. Interestingly, the research also finds that realism scores correlate with geographical representativeness, suggesting that more realistic images are perceived as more geographically representative.

## Method Summary
The study uses 10 common nouns (city, beach, house, festival, road, dress, flag, park, wedding, kitchen) and evaluates images generated for 27 countries using both DALL·E 2 and Stable Diffusion. A crowdsourced study with 540 participants from these countries rates 80 images each (4 per noun: 2 from each model) on geographical representativeness and realism using 1-5 Likert scales. The study compares images generated with and without country names in prompts to assess the impact of geographical context specification.

## Key Results
- Geographical representativeness scores average 2.39/5 across 27 countries for underspecified prompts, with US scoring highest at 3.35 and India second at 3.23
- Specifying country names increases average scores to 3.49/5, with DALL·E 2 outperforming Stable Diffusion by about 0.6 points
- Realism scores correlate with geographical representativeness (Pearson correlation of 0.62 for Stable Diffusion)
- Automatic evaluation methods using CLIP similarity and nearest neighbors proved inadequate for assessing geographical representativeness

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: Specifying country names in text prompts increases geographical representativeness of generated images.
- Mechanism: Adding country context provides explicit geographical cues that guide the model to generate images reflecting local artifacts and surroundings.
- Core assumption: The model's training data contains sufficient geographical examples for the specified countries to influence generation.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "Specifying the country names in the input increases the representativeness by 1.44 points on average for DALL·E 2 and 0.75 for Stable Diffusion"
  - [section 3.3] "we observe that for each nation, mentioning its name in the prompt increases the average GR score for that country as compared to the under-speciﬁed case"
  - [corpus] Weak - related papers discuss geographical disparities but don't directly support the prompt specification mechanism
- Break condition: If training data lacks sufficient geographical examples for the specified country, or if the model ignores contextual information in prompts.

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: Geographical representativeness correlates with image realism scores.
- Mechanism: Participants' perception of geographical representativeness is influenced by how realistic the generated images appear, with more realistic images being judged as more representative of their surroundings.
- Core assumption: Realism and geographical representativeness are perceived as related qualities by human evaluators.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [section 3.4] "we ﬁnd that geographical representativeness and realism scores are correlated, with a Pearson correlation of 0.62 for Stable Diffusion"
  - [section 3.4] "participants, on average, indicate that the realism inﬂuenced their ratings on geographical representativeness to a moderate extent"
  - [corpus] Weak - related papers don't directly address the relationship between realism and geographical representativeness
- Break condition: If participants consciously separate realism from geographical appropriateness in their evaluations, or if unrealistic images are still recognized as geographically appropriate.

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: DALL·E 2 generates more geographically representative images than Stable Diffusion when country names are specified.
- Mechanism: DALL·E 2's training process or architectural design better incorporates geographical context from country-specific prompts.
- Core assumption: The architectural or training differences between DALL·E 2 and Stable Diffusion result in different sensitivities to geographical context.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [section 3.5] "for country-speciﬁc prompts, the geographical representativeness of images generated through DALL·E 2 are higher than those from Stable Diffusion by about 0.6 points"
  - [section 3.5] "we ﬁnd that (i) for country-speciﬁc prompts, the geographical representativeness of images generated through DALL·E 2 are higher than those from Stable Diffusion"
  - [corpus] Weak - related papers don't compare these specific models' geographical representation capabilities
- Break condition: If the difference is due to random variation or if the country-specific prompts don't actually provide meaningful geographical context.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- Question: Do internet access disparities explain the geographical bias in text-to-image models?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper notes that internet access is not uniformly distributed across the globe and that crowdsourcing platforms have few participants from developing countries
- Why unresolved: The study was limited to 27 countries due to participant availability, leaving out many developing nations
- What evidence would resolve it: A study with participants from a wider range of developing countries would reveal if the geographical bias persists across all nations or is concentrated in specific regions

### Open Question 2
- Question: Can automatic evaluation methods be improved to accurately assess geographical representativeness?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper explores two approaches (CLIP-based similarity and nearest neighbors) and finds both inadequate for evaluating geographical representativeness
- Why unresolved: The current automatic methods fail to capture the nuances of geographical representation that human evaluators can identify
- What evidence would resolve it: Development of new feature extractors or evaluation metrics that better capture geographical features and align more closely with human judgments

### Open Question 3
- Question: What is the relationship between the realism of generated images and their geographical representativeness?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper finds a correlation between photo-realism scores and geographical representativeness ratings, but the relationship is not fully understood
- Why unresolved: While correlation is established, the causal relationship and mechanisms are unclear
- What evidence would resolve it: Controlled experiments manipulating image realism while keeping geographical content constant to isolate the effect of realism on perceived representativeness

## Limitations

- Limited geographic coverage with only 27 countries represented, missing large regions of Africa and Asia
- Potential cultural bias in ratings as participants may internalize "Western" standards for common nouns
- Automatic evaluation methods (CLIP similarity and nearest neighbors) failed to correlate with human ratings, limiting scalability

## Confidence

- Medium: Geographical bias exists in text-to-image models (supported by quantitative results but limited by potential cultural bias in ratings)
- Medium: Country name specification improves geographical representativeness (mechanistically sound but dependent on training data coverage)
- Low: CLIP similarity and nearest neighbor methods are inadequate for geographical representativeness (based on correlation analysis but not exhaustively tested with alternative approaches)

## Next Checks

1. **Cross-cultural validation study**: Replicate the study with participants from the same cultural background as the target country (e.g., have Indians rate Indian images) to determine if geographical representativeness scores improve when cultural context is shared between raters and target images.

2. **Prompt engineering experiment**: Systematically test variations in prompt formulation (e.g., "house in Tokyo" vs "Japanese house" vs "house surrounded by Japanese architecture") to determine the optimal way to elicit geographically appropriate responses from both models.

3. **Training data analysis**: Analyze the training data distribution of both DALL·E 2 and Stable Diffusion to quantify the geographical representation of different regions, correlating this with the observed generation biases to determine if the model outputs reflect training data imbalances.
