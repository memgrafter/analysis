# Multi-source Education Knowledge Graph Construction and Fusion for College Curricula

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2305.04567
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.04567
- Reference count: 14
- Primary result: Automated framework for constructing and fusing large-scale knowledge graphs from heterogeneous educational resources in Electronic Information discipline

## Executive Summary
This paper presents an automated framework for constructing and fusing knowledge graphs from heterogeneous educational resources, specifically targeting college curricula in the Electronic Information discipline. The approach leverages natural language processing and machine learning techniques to extract knowledge units from textbooks, slides, and syllabi, then fuses them into a unified graph database containing over 60,000 entities and 80,000 triples. Data cleaning using a language error correction model ensures high-quality knowledge concepts. The resulting knowledge graph enables analysis of course correlations, identification of popular knowledge concepts, and discovery of interdisciplinary relationships, offering a scalable solution for enhancing learning efficiency and providing comprehensive educational insights.

## Method Summary
The framework extracts knowledge concepts using named entity recognition and regular expressions from multi-source educational materials, then cleans the data using a language error correction model. The extracted knowledge is structured into a four-level hierarchy (KnowledgeUnit, KnowledgeChapter, KnowledgeBlock, KnowledgePoint) and imported into a Neo4j graph database. Cross-course knowledge graph fusion is performed through entity name matching and attribute union to identify equivalent concepts and create mapping relationships. The system analyzes course correlations by calculating the proportion of equivalence relation edges between courses relative to total knowledge concepts.

## Key Results
- Successfully constructed knowledge graphs from heterogeneous educational resources containing over 60k entities and 80k triples
- Demonstrated ability to identify course correlations and interdisciplinary relationships through graph fusion
- Showed potential for discovering popular knowledge concepts and analyzing curriculum structure

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: Knowledge graph construction improves learning efficiency by visualizing complex relationships between curriculum concepts
- Mechanism: The framework extracts structured entities from heterogeneous educational resources and fuses them into a unified graph database, enabling students to explore relationships and dependencies
- Core assumption: Students benefit from visual representations of curriculum structure and can more efficiently identify core concepts when relationships are explicitly mapped
- Evidence anchors: [abstract] "Our objective is to enhance the learning efficiency of students and to explore new educational paradigms enabled by AI"; [section III.C] "It can provide powerful data support for teachers to achieve the corresponding teaching objectives and highlight the core knowledge concepts to improve teaching quality"

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: Data cleaning and error correction significantly improve knowledge graph quality and fusion accuracy
- Mechanism: The language error correction model identifies and corrects syntax errors, misspellings, and mixed language content in extracted entities, ensuring high-quality knowledge concepts for accurate graph fusion
- Core assumption: Cleaner data leads to more accurate entity matching and better fusion results, which in turn provides more reliable insights about course relationships
- Evidence anchors: [section II.B] "Data cleaning is an momentous step to ensure the accuracy of entities, their attributes and the effect of graph fusion because uncleaned data may lead to wrong decisions and unreliable data mining"; [section I] "We utilize language error correction model enabled by deep learning for data cleaning"

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: Cross-course knowledge graph fusion reveals interdisciplinary relationships and supports curriculum optimization
- Mechanism: The framework identifies equivalent knowledge concepts across different courses through entity name matching and attribute union, then creates mapping relationships to visualize course correlations and intersections
- Core assumption: Knowledge concepts appear across multiple courses, and identifying these overlaps provides valuable insights for curriculum design and student learning pathways
- Evidence anchors: [section II.C] "We carry out knowledge fusion to alleviate the redundant knowledge nodes and improve the data quality for our knowledge graph"; [section III.A] "We judge the degree of correlation between any two courses by calculating the proportion of the number of equivalence relation edges between two courses in the total knowledge concepts"

## Limitations

- The framework's effectiveness depends heavily on the quality of input educational materials and the sophistication of the language error correction model, neither of which are fully specified
- The approach assumes that knowledge concepts can be reliably extracted through pattern matching and named entity recognition, but complex or domain-specific terminology may be missed or incorrectly categorized
- The claims about discovering interdisciplinary relationships and analyzing course correlations through graph fusion lack sufficient empirical validation

## Confidence

- **High Confidence**: The basic architecture of extracting hierarchical knowledge units from educational materials and storing them in a graph database is technically sound and well-established
- **Medium Confidence**: The data cleaning approach using language error correction models is plausible but depends on the specific implementation details not provided
- **Low Confidence**: The claims about discovering interdisciplinary relationships and analyzing course correlations through graph fusion lack sufficient empirical validation
