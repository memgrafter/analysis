# Evaluation of the syllables pronunciation quality in speech rehabilitation through the solution of the classification problem

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2301.10585
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2301.10585
- Reference count: 24
- This paper proposes using LSTM classification to assess pronunciation quality in post-surgical speech rehabilitation, achieving high accuracy on patient-specific models but poor generalization across patients.

## Executive Summary
This paper addresses the challenge of objectively assessing pronunciation quality in speech rehabilitation following oncological surgery of speech-forming organs. The authors propose a novel approach that treats pre- and post-surgical speech recordings as two distinct classes and uses LSTM neural networks to classify syllables during rehabilitation. The classification score serves as a quantitative metric for pronunciation quality, offering a more objective alternative to traditional expert-based assessments. The methodology shows strong performance when applied to individual patients or single genders, with accuracy reaching 0.99 on training data and 0.95 on test data. However, the approach struggles with cross-patient generalization, achieving only chance-level performance (0.52) when trained on combined patient data. The proposed metric demonstrates strong correlation (r=0.86) with expert GOST ratings, validating its effectiveness for individual patient assessment.

## Method Summary
The authors employ LSTM neural networks to classify syllables recorded during speech rehabilitation, treating pre-surgical and immediate post-surgical recordings as two distinct classes. The classification score is used as a quantitative metric for pronunciation quality. The approach is tested on 7 patients over 10 months, with 10 repetitions of each syllable. Models are trained both on individual patients and on all patients combined, with additional experiments using subsets of syllables containing problematic phonemes. The resulting metric is validated against expert GOST ratings from two speech specialists.

## Key Results
- Patient-specific and gender-specific models achieve high accuracy (up to 0.99 training, 0.95 test)
- Combined patient training results in near-chance performance (0.52 accuracy)
- Subset of syllables with problematic phonemes improves accuracy over full set
- Strong correlation (r=0.86) between proposed metric and expert GOST ratings

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The LSTM neural network effectively captures temporal dependencies in speech patterns, allowing it to distinguish between pre-surgical and post-surgical pronunciation characteristics. By training on individual patient data, the model learns patient-specific baseline patterns and tracks improvement over time. The classification score reflects how closely current pronunciation matches the pre-surgical baseline, providing a quantitative measure of rehabilitation progress.

- Limited sample size (7 patients, 2 experts)
- Phoneme selection criteria not fully justified
