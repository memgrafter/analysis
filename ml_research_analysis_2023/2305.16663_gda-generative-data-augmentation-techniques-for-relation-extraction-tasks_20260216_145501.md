# GDA: Generative Data Augmentation Techniques for Relation Extraction Tasks

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2305.16663
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.16663
- Reference count: 32
- Primary result: 2.0% F1 improvement in low-resource relation extraction settings

## Executive Summary
This paper introduces GDA (Generative Data Augmentation), a novel technique specifically designed for relation extraction tasks. GDA employs a dual-module architecture that generates semantically consistent and syntactically diverse augmented sentences. The method leverages multi-task learning to create coherent and diverse examples that preserve the original relation structures. Under low-resource conditions across three datasets, GDA demonstrates a 2.0% F1 improvement compared to baselines without augmentation.

## Method Summary
GDA utilizes a multi-task learning framework with two complementary modules designed to preserve both semantic consistency and syntax structures in augmented sentences. The approach generates semantically consistent, coherent, and diverse augmented sentences specifically tailored for relation extraction tasks. The dual-module architecture works together to maintain the integrity of relation structures while introducing syntactic diversity in the generated examples.

## Key Results
- Achieves 2.0% F1 improvement in low-resource relation extraction settings
- Demonstrates effectiveness across three different datasets
- Outperforms no-augmentation baselines under resource-constrained conditions

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The dual-module architecture of GDA preserves both semantic consistency and syntax structures through complementary mechanisms. The multi-task learning framework enables the model to generate augmented sentences that maintain the original relation's meaning while introducing syntactic diversity. This targeted approach to augmentation is particularly effective for relation extraction tasks where preserving the semantic relationship between entities is crucial while still providing sufficient diversity in training examples.

## Architecture Onboarding

Component map: Input sentences -> Semantic module -> Syntax module -> Augmented sentences -> RE model training

Critical path: The semantic module ensures relation preservation while the syntax module introduces structural variation, both feeding into the augmented sentence generation.

Design tradeoffs: The dual-module approach balances semantic preservation against syntactic diversity, with potential tension between maintaining exact relation structures and introducing sufficient variation for generalization.

Failure signatures: Generated sentences that lose the original relation semantics or become syntactically incoherent would indicate module imbalance or training issues.
