# Recipes for Sequential Pre-training of Multilingual Encoder and Seq2seq Models

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2306.08756
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.08756
- Reference count: 25
- Primary result: Two sequential training recipes reduce total compute cost by 27% compared to training each model from scratch while matching task performance

## Executive Summary
This paper explores efficient recipes for pre-training both a multilingual encoder and a seq2seq model by reusing one model's weights for the other. The authors propose two sequential training recipes that reduce total compute cost by 27% compared to training each model from scratch. The first recipe involves extracting the encoder from a seq2seq model and continuing MLM training, but this approach underperforms a from-scratch encoder, especially on sequence labeling tasks. The second recipe involves warm-starting seq2seq training with a pre-trained encoder, then unfreezing the encoder partway through training. This two-stage approach matches the performance of training each model from scratch while using 27% less total compute.

## Method Summary
The method involves two sequential pre-training recipes. Recipe 1: Train a seq2seq model from scratch, extract the encoder, and continue MLM pre-training. Recipe 2: Pre-train an encoder using MLM, initialize a seq2seq model with this encoder, train with the encoder frozen, then unfreeze the encoder partway through training. Both approaches use the same multilingual corpus (Wikipedia and mC4 in 12 languages) and are evaluated on XNLI, mATIS++, WikiANN, UDPOS, mTOP, and XSUM tasks. The key innovation is finding that unfreezing the encoder during seq2seq training allows matching from-scratch performance with reduced compute.

## Key Results
- Sequential pre-training reduces total compute cost by 27% compared to training each model from scratch
- Recipe 1 (seq2seq-then-MLM) underperforms from-scratch encoder, especially on sequence labeling tasks
- Recipe 2 (MLM-then-seq2seq with encoder unfreezing) matches from-scratch seq2seq performance
- Encoders from seq2seq pre-training underperform MLM encoders on sequence labeling tasks by 3.2-17.3 points on mATIS++ SL

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: Pre-training a seq2seq model with masked de-noising (replacing spans with mask tokens) produces a better encoder than simply dropping tokens.
- Mechanism: The explicit mask token in the input sequence provides a clear signal to the model about where tokens are missing, making the reconstruction task easier and leading to better encoder representations.
- Core assumption: Adding mask tokens reduces the complexity of the de-noising task without sacrificing the quality of learned representations.
- Evidence anchors: Weak. No direct corpus evidence supporting this mechanism; the claim is based on model design intuition.

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: Unfrozen encoder during seq2seq pre-training enables better adaptation of the encoder to the de-noising objective compared to keeping it frozen.
- Mechanism: Allowing the encoder to adapt during seq2seq training enables it to learn representations that are more suitable for the reconstruction task, improving generation quality.
- Core assumption: The encoder's representations learned via MLM are not optimal for the seq2seq de-noising task, and unfreezing allows beneficial adaptation.
- Evidence anchors: Weak. No direct corpus evidence; claim based on experimental results.

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: MLM pre-training is more effective than seq2seq pre-training for sequence labeling tasks that use token-level representations directly.
- Mechanism: MLM trains the model to predict individual masked tokens based on their context, which is more aligned with the requirements of sequence labeling tasks that classify each token.
- Core assumption: Sequence labeling tasks benefit more from token-level understanding than from sequence-level reconstruction.
- Evidence anchors: Weak. No direct corpus evidence; claim based on experimental results across tasks.

## Foundational Learning

- Concept: Masked Language Modeling (MLM)
  - Why needed here: MLM is the pre-training objective used to train the encoder-only model and to initialize the encoder for the two-stage seq2seq training. Understanding MLM is crucial for grasping how the encoder learns representations.
  - Quick check question: In MLM, what percentage of tokens are typically masked, and what are the three possible ways a masked token can be replaced during training?

- Concept: Sequence-to-Sequence (seq2seq) Pre-training
  - Why needed here: Seq2seq models are used for both the from-scratch training and the two-stage training recipe. Understanding the de-noising objective and the architecture differences is essential.
  - Quick check question: What is the difference between the de-noising objectives when using span dropping vs. span masking in seq2seq pre-training?

- Concept: Cross-Lingual Zero-Shot Learning
  - Why needed here: The models are pre-trained on multilingual data and evaluated on zero-shot cross-lingual transfer. Understanding this concept is important for interpreting the experimental results.
  - Quick check question: In zero-shot cross-lingual evaluation, how is the model fine-tuned and tested across different languages?

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map: MLM pre-training (encoder) -> Seq2seq pre-training (encoder + decoder) -> Fine-tuning on downstream tasks
- Critical path: MLM pre-training (encoder) → Seq2seq pre-training (encoder + decoder) → Fine-tuning on downstream tasks
- Design tradeoffs:
  - Encoder frozen vs. unfrozen during seq2seq pre-training: Frozen saves compute but may hurt generation quality; unfrozen improves generation but increases compute cost
  - Decoder size: Smaller decoder reduces compute but may limit the model's ability to learn complex reconstruction tasks
  - Masking vs. dropping in de-noising: Masking provides explicit signal but may make task too easy; dropping is more challenging but may lead to better representations
- Failure signatures:
  - Poor sequence labeling performance: Likely due to using seq2seq-pretrained encoder instead of MLM-pretrained encoder
  - Subpar generation quality: Encoder may be frozen for too long during seq2seq pre-training or decoder may be too small
  - High compute cost: Using from-scratch training for both encoder and seq2seq instead of sequential pre-training recipe
- First 3 experiments:
  1. Compare MLM-pretrained encoder vs. seq2seq-pretrained encoder on a sequence labeling task like UDPOS POS tagging to confirm the performance gap.
  2. Train a two-stage seq2seq model with encoder frozen for the entire pre-training vs. encoder unfrozen partway through to measure the impact on generation quality.
  3. Vary the decoder size (1, 2, 12 layers) in the seq2seq pre-training to find the optimal tradeoff between compute cost and performance.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- Question: Does attention fusion provide any consistent benefit across different tasks or model sizes?
- Basis in paper: The paper states that attention fusion during cross-attention in the two-stage seq2seq model does not provide a clear benefit compared to standard cross-attention.
- Why unresolved: The paper only tests attention fusion in one specific configuration (2stage-bart-12e12d-attn-f) and finds no clear improvement. It's unclear whether this approach might be beneficial in other settings.
- What evidence would resolve it: Systematic testing of attention fusion across different model sizes, tasks, and training configurations to determine if there are any scenarios where it provides consistent benefits.

### Open Question 2
- Question: Why does MLM pre-training produce better encoders for sequence labeling tasks compared to seq2seq de-noising?
- Basis in paper: The paper shows that encoders from seq2seq models significantly underperform MLM encoders on sequence labeling tasks (e.g., 3.2-17.3 point gap on mATIS++ SL).
- Why unresolved: The paper notes this gap exists but does not investigate the underlying reasons for this difference in encoder quality between training objectives.
- What evidence would resolve it: Analysis of what linguistic properties each pre-training objective captures, examination of intermediate representations, or controlled experiments varying masking strategies in seq2seq training.

### Open Question 3
- Question: What is the optimal timing for unfreezing the encoder during the two-stage seq2seq pre-training?
- Basis in paper: The paper shows that unfreezing the encoder partway through training (after 200k updates) produces good results, but doesn't explore other unfreezing schedules.
- Why unresolved: The paper only tests one unfreezing schedule and doesn't investigate whether earlier or later unfreezing, or gradual unfreezing, might be more effective.
- What evidence would resolve it: Systematic exploration of different unfreezing schedules (e.g., unfreezing after 50k, 100k, 300k updates) and gradual vs. abrupt unfreezing to identify optimal timing for different tasks.

## Limitations

- Experimental validation is primarily empirical rather than theoretical, with key mechanisms explained through design intuition
- Performance improvements, while measurable, are relatively modest (27% compute reduction with matching performance)
- Study focuses on multilingual settings but evaluates on a limited set of tasks and languages, raising questions about generalizability
- Paper doesn't explore whether these sequential pre-training recipes transfer to monolingual settings or different model scales

## Confidence

**High Confidence**: The empirical observation that seq2seq pre-training with frozen encoder followed by unfreezing matches from-scratch performance with 27% compute reduction. The experimental results across multiple tasks and the comparison to baseline approaches provide strong evidence for this claim.

**Medium Confidence**: The proposed mechanisms explaining why sequential pre-training works (explicit mask tokens making reconstruction easier, encoder adaptation benefits). While the paper provides experimental evidence, the explanations are based on design intuition rather than rigorous theoretical analysis or ablation studies that isolate specific mechanisms.

**Low Confidence**: The claim that MLM pre-training is particularly effective for sequence labeling tasks due to token-level understanding. This conclusion is drawn from aggregate performance across tasks but lacks systematic investigation of the underlying reasons or exploration of alternative explanations.

## Next Checks

1. **Mechanism isolation experiment**: Design an ablation study that systematically varies (a) whether mask tokens are used vs. dropping spans, (b) whether the encoder is frozen or unfrozen during seq2seq pre-training, and (c) the decoder size. This would isolate which components contribute most to the performance gains and test the proposed mechanisms more rigorously.

2. **Downstream task dependency analysis**: Conduct a more detailed analysis of which downstream tasks benefit from MLM-pretrained vs. seq2seq-pretrained encoders. Include a wider variety of task types (e.g., document-level classification, generative tasks beyond summarization) and analyze the relationship between task characteristics and optimal pre-training strategy.

3. **Compute-efficiency frontier exploration**: Systematically explore the tradeoff curve between compute cost and task performance by varying the number of updates in each stage of sequential pre-training. Determine whether the 27% reduction is a local optimum or if different staging strategies could achieve better efficiency-performance tradeoffs.
