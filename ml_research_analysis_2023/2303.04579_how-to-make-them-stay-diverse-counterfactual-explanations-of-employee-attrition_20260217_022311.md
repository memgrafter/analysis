# "How to make them stay?" -- Diverse Counterfactual Explanations of Employee Attrition

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2303.04579
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.04579
- Reference count: 4
- Primary result: Proposed method generates diverse counterfactual explanations for employee attrition cases, identifying actionable recommendations for HR departments

## Executive Summary
This paper addresses employee attrition prediction by proposing a method for generating diverse counterfactual explanations from historical data. The approach uses multiple attrition cases to identify optimal interventions that organizations can implement to reduce attrition probability. The method is applied to the IBM employee attrition dataset, demonstrating how diverse recommendations can be generated based on different combinations of factors such as salary increases, promotion timing, and job satisfaction improvements.

## Method Summary
The paper proposes an iterative method for computing a set of highly diverse counterfactual changes. Diversity is defined by the number of overlapping features between explanations - highly diverse explanations should use and change completely different features. The approach trains binary classifiers on historical employee data and then generates counterfactual explanations that identify actionable changes to prevent attrition. The method focuses on providing recommendations that are both diverse and actionable for HR departments to implement.

## Key Results
- Proposed technique identified three actionable recommendations: increase Percent Salary Hike by 40%, reduce Years Since Last Promotion by 5 years, or increase Percent Salary Hike by 20% and Job Satisfaction by 50%
- Recommendations are consistent with existing literature on employee retention factors
- Method demonstrates ability to generate diverse counterfactual explanations based on multiple attrition cases
- Applied successfully to IBM employee attrition dataset with binary classifier models

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The approach works by leveraging counterfactual explanations to identify specific, actionable changes that would prevent attrition in individual cases. By generating diverse explanations that change different feature combinations, the method provides organizations with multiple intervention strategies rather than a single solution. The diversity metric ensures that recommendations cover different aspects of employee experience, from compensation to career development to job satisfaction.

## Foundational Learning
- Counterfactual explanations: Alternative scenarios that would have led to different outcomes - needed for identifying specific intervention points, check by validating explanations are both actionable and lead to desired outcome
- Diversity in recommendations: Ensuring explanations change different feature combinations - needed to provide multiple intervention strategies, check by measuring overlap between feature changes
- Binary classification for attrition prediction: Using historical data to predict attrition probability - needed as foundation for generating counterfactuals, check by measuring classifier accuracy on validation set

## Architecture Onboarding
Component map: Historical data -> Binary classifier -> Counterfactual generator -> Diverse recommendations
Critical path: Data preprocessing -> Model training -> Explanation generation -> Diversity filtering -> Final recommendations
Design tradeoffs: Diversity vs. actionability - more diverse recommendations may be less immediately implementable; computational cost vs. explanation quality
Failure signatures: Overfitting to historical data, recommendations not aligned with business constraints, explanations too complex to implement
First experiments: 1) Test diversity metric on synthetic data with known feature overlaps, 2) Compare recommendation diversity against baseline random selection, 3) Validate explanation actionability through HR expert review
