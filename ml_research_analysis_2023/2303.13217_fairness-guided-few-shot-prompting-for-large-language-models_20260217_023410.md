# Fairness-guided Few-shot Prompting for Large Language Models

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2303.13217
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.13217
- Reference count: 29
- One-line primary result: G-fair-Prompting consistently outperforms state-of-the-art methods with improvements of over 10% on tasks like TREC

## Executive Summary
This paper addresses the instability of in-context learning (ICL) in large language models caused by variations in demonstration selection, order, and prompt formats. The authors propose optimizing prompts by minimizing predictive bias, measured via entropy of the model's output on content-free inputs. Two search strategies are introduced: T-fair-Prompting (top-k selection) and G-fair-Prompting (greedy search), both showing significant improvements over baseline methods across multiple tasks and models including BLOOM and LLaMA.

## Method Summary
The method optimizes few-shot prompts by minimizing predictive bias measured through entropy of the model's output on content-free inputs. T-fair-Prompting selects the top-k fairest individual demonstrations based on their entropy scores, while G-fair-Prompting uses greedy search to iteratively construct prompts that maximize fairness. The approach eliminates the need for a development set by using the fairness metric as a proxy for task accuracy, enabling efficient prompt optimization without additional labeled data.

## Key Results
- G-fair-Prompting consistently outperforms T-fair-Prompting and state-of-the-art methods like diversity-guided and similarity-guided approaches
- Improvements of over 10% are observed on dataset TREC
- Fairness metric strongly correlates with task accuracy, enabling efficient prompt optimization without requiring a development set

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- **Claim**: Entropy of the model's output on content-free inputs correlates with task accuracy, enabling prompt optimization without a development set.
- **Mechanism**: The entropy of the predictive distribution on a content-free input serves as a proxy for predictive bias. Lower entropy (higher bias) indicates the model is making more confident but potentially incorrect predictions, while higher entropy suggests the model is more uncertain and less biased.
- **Core assumption**: The predictive bias measured on a content-free input is representative of the model's bias on actual task inputs.
- **Evidence anchors**:
  - [abstract]: "they propose to optimize prompts by minimizing predictive bias, measured via entropy of the model's output on content-free inputs."
  - [section 3.3]: "We employ entropy as a measure of predictive bias...we employ the uniformity of the predictive distribution to characterize the bias of a given prompt."
- **Break condition**: If the content-free input does not adequately represent the semantic space of the task, the entropy measure may not correlate with actual task performance.

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim**: Greedy search for prompt construction outperforms simple selection of top-k fairest demonstrations.
- **Mechanism**: Greedy search iteratively selects the demonstration that maximally improves fairness at each step, considering both local and global bias reduction. This approach balances individual demonstration fairness with overall prompt fairness.
- **Core assumption**: The optimal prompt is not simply the concatenation of the fairest individual demonstrations, but requires consideration of their combined effect.
- **Evidence anchors**:
  - [section 4.2]: "G-fair-Prompting follows the standard procedure of greedy search...the chosen demonstration is the one that allows the updated prompts to achieve the highest fairness score."
  - [section 5.2]: "G-fair-Prompting consistently outperforms T-fair-Prompting...improvements of over 10% are observed on dataset TREC."
- **Break condition**: If the greedy search gets stuck in a local optimum that does not generalize well to the task, it may not find the globally optimal prompt.

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim**: Fairness-guided prompting is more effective than calibration methods for improving ICL performance.
- **Mechanism**: By selecting fairer prompts directly, the model's predictions are inherently less biased, reducing the need for post-hoc calibration. Fairer prompts lead to more uniform predictive distributions, which correlate with better task performance.
- **Core assumption**: Fairer prompts lead to better calibration inherently, making additional calibration steps less necessary or even detrimental.
- **Evidence anchors**:
  - [section 5.2]: "G-fair-Prompting can outperform random selection with calibration...post calibration could negatively affect the model's performance in many scenarios."
  - [section 3.3]: "we have discovered that, even without calibration, the prompt selected by our method can outperform a randomly selected prompt with calibration."
- **Break condition**: If the fairness metric does not adequately capture all relevant aspects of bias for the task, fairness-guided prompting may not lead to optimal performance even without calibration.

## Foundational Learning

- **Concept**: Entropy as a measure of uncertainty
  - **Why needed here**: Entropy is used to quantify the uniformity of the model's predictive distribution on content-free inputs, serving as a proxy for predictive bias.
  - **Quick check question**: If a model outputs a uniform distribution over classes for a content-free input, what is the entropy value?

- **Concept**: Greedy algorithms and local optima
  - **Why needed here**: G-fair-Prompting uses a greedy search strategy to iteratively improve prompt fairness, which may get stuck in local optima.
  - **Quick check question**: In what scenario might a greedy algorithm fail to find the globally optimal solution?

- **Concept**: Correlation vs. causation
  - **Why needed here**: The paper establishes a correlation between fairness and task accuracy, but this does not necessarily imply that improving fairness will always improve accuracy.
  - **Quick check question**: If two variables are correlated, does this mean that changing one will cause a change in the other?

## Architecture Onboarding

- **Component map**:
  Input: Training set S = {(xi, yi)}N i, pretrained LLM M, transformation template Γ(·), context-free input η
  → Fairness evaluation: Entropy calculation for individual demonstrations and prompts
  → Search strategies: T-fair-Prompting (top-k selection) and G-fair-Prompting (greedy search)
  → Output: Optimized prompt ρ

- **Critical path**:
  1. Compute fairness scores for individual demonstrations
  2. Apply T-fair-Prompting or G-fair-Prompting to construct optimized prompt
  3. Evaluate performance of optimized prompt on test set

- **Design tradeoffs**:
  - T-fair-Prompting is faster (O(N)) but may be suboptimal due to its purely local perspective
  - G-fair-Prompting is slower (O(N^2)) but considers both local and global bias reduction
  - Using fairness as a proxy for performance eliminates the need for a development set but relies on the assumption that fairness correlates with accuracy

- **Failure signatures**:
  - If fairness scores do not correlate with task accuracy, the optimization process may not improve performance
  - If the greedy search gets stuck in a local optimum, G-fair-Prompting may not find the best prompt
  - If the content-free input does not adequately represent the task domain, the fairness metric may be misleading

- **First 3 experiments**:
  1. Verify that entropy on content-free inputs correlates with task accuracy for a simple dataset and model
  2. Compare performance of T-fair-Prompting vs. random selection for different values of k
  3. Evaluate the effectiveness of G-fair-Prompting vs. T-fair-Prompting on a more complex dataset

## Limitations
- Weak corpus support with minimal citations for related work, suggesting potential novelty or literature review gaps
- Critical assumption that entropy on content-free inputs correlates with task accuracy lacks extensive external validation
- Greedy search approach risks getting stuck in local optima without systematic analysis of when this occurs

## Confidence

- **High Confidence**: The experimental results showing improved performance over baselines (random selection, diversity-guided, similarity-guided) are well-supported by the data presented. The improvements of 10%+ on TREC and consistent outperformance across models and tasks provide strong empirical evidence for the effectiveness of the approach.

- **Medium Confidence**: The correlation between fairness (entropy) and accuracy is demonstrated within the paper's experimental framework but lacks external validation. The mechanism connecting predictive bias measurement to task performance is logically sound but not extensively tested across different domains or model architectures.

- **Low Confidence**: The broader impact and generalizability of the approach beyond the specific datasets and models tested remains uncertain. The absence of related work citations and the weak corpus signals suggest either methodological isolation or potential gaps in literature review.

## Next Checks

1. **Cross-Domain Robustness Test**: Validate whether the entropy-accuracy correlation holds across diverse task domains (e.g., reasoning, generation, summarization) beyond text classification. This would test the fundamental assumption underlying the fairness metric.

2. **Alternative Optimization Strategy Comparison**: Compare G-fair-Prompting against other optimization approaches (e.g., beam search, evolutionary algorithms, or gradient-based methods) to assess whether greedy search is optimal or merely sufficient for the task.

3. **Transferability Analysis**: Test whether prompts optimized for one model (e.g., BLOOM) transfer effectively to other model architectures (e.g., GPT, Claude) or whether fairness optimization is model-specific, which would have significant practical implications for deployment.
