# Abstractive Text Summarization Using the BRIO Training Paradigm

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID**: 2305.13696
- **Source URL**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.13696
- **Reference count**: 5
- **Primary result**: BRIO-trained models outperform existing abstractive summarization models on CNNDM and VieSum datasets, especially for Vietnamese.

## Executive Summary
This paper introduces the BRIO training paradigm to improve abstractive summarization by reducing model dependence on reference summaries. BRIO generates multiple diverse candidate summaries per document, evaluates them with ROUGE-based quality scores, and trains both a generator (MLE loss) and an evaluator (contrastive loss) to push the model toward producing summaries that score high on quality. The approach is tested on English (CNNDM) and Vietnamese (VieSum) datasets, showing state-of-the-art performance, particularly for Vietnamese summarization.

## Method Summary
The method involves fine-tuning pre-trained language models (BARTpho, ViT5) on target datasets (CNNDM, VieSum), then applying the BRIO paradigm. BRIO generates N=6 diverse candidate summaries per document using diverse beam search, ranks them by average ROUGE against reference, and trains a generator (MLE) and evaluator (contrastive loss) jointly. Optionally, the process is looped by using BRIO-generated summaries as pseudo-references in subsequent training rounds.

## Key Results
- BRIO-trained models outperform all existing abstractive summarization models on both CNNDM and VieSum datasets.
- Fine-tuning backbones before BRIO yields higher ROUGE scores than training BRIO directly on standard backbones.
- Looping BRIO training improves ROUGE scores initially, but gains diminish after 2-3 iterations.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: BRIO improves summarization by reducing dependence on reference summaries through contrastive learning between candidate summaries and references.
- Mechanism: BRIO generates multiple candidate summaries (candsums) using diverse beam search, evaluates them with ROUGE-based quality scores, and trains both a generator (MLE loss) and an evaluator (contrastive loss) to push the model toward producing summaries that score high on quality without over-relying on the exact reference.
- Core assumption: That generating diverse candidates and ranking them via ROUGE scores provides a better training signal than single MLE on the reference, and that the evaluator can learn to prefer candidates closer to human-like summaries.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "The BRIO training paradigm assumes a non-deterministic distribution to reduce the model's dependence on reference summaries, and improve model performance during inference."
  - [section 4.3] "BRIO assigns probability mass to output summary candidates based on their quality using contrastive learning."
  - [corpus] Weak - the corpus contains only papers with very low citation counts; no direct empirical support for BRIO's effectiveness here.
- Break condition: If the contrastive evaluator overfits to the ROUGE metric rather than human quality, or if candidate generation fails to produce diverse enough outputs, the benefit diminishes.

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: Fine-tuning the backbone models before applying BRIO yields higher ROUGE scores than training BRIO directly on standard backbones.
- Mechanism: Pre-training the base model (e.g., BARTpho, ViT5) on the target domain (VieSum) aligns the model's representations with the data distribution, so that when BRIO contrastive learning is applied, the candidates are of higher intrinsic quality and the contrastive loss is more effective.
- Core assumption: Domain-specific fine-tuning improves the backbone's ability to generate plausible candidates, which in turn makes the BRIO contrastive signal more informative.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [section 4.2] "To improve the quality of summaries created, we fine-tune the backbone models using the Trainer provided by Hugging Face."
  - [section 4.3] "In our work, the fine-tuned backbone abstractive summarization models, presented in the previous section, are used to produce N=6 candsums..."
  - [corpus] Weak - no corpus evidence of prior work combining fine-tuning and BRIO; claim is novel to this paper.
- Break condition: If fine-tuning causes overfitting to training data, candidate diversity may drop, weakening BRIO's contrastive signal.

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: Looping BRIO training (using candsums from the BRIO-trained model as new training signals) can further improve ROUGE scores, though gains diminish after a few iterations.
- Mechanism: After initial BRIO training, the model's generated candsums are used as pseudo-references in a second BRIO round, reinforcing patterns the model has learned while maintaining diversity via diverse beam search.
- Core assumption: The model's own outputs after BRIO are of sufficient quality to serve as effective training signals in subsequent rounds, and diversity is preserved to avoid collapse.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [section 4.4] "As suggested by Liu et al. (2022a), we perform loop processing, using the candsums created by the abstractive summarization models trained with BRIO to train the models."
  - [section 4.4] "However, after several iterations of looping, the ROUGE scores seem to change very little. Especially, BARTpho and ViT5 almost reach the highest ROUGE scores with 2 iterations."
  - [corpus] Weak - no prior work cited showing diminishing returns after BRIO-loop; this is an empirical observation from the paper.
- Break condition: If model outputs converge too quickly or diversity drops, further loops yield negligible improvement or degrade quality.

## Foundational Learning

- Concept: **Contrastive learning in sequence generation**
  - Why needed here: BRIO's core improvement comes from training an evaluator to rank candidate summaries; understanding how contrastive objectives differ from standard MLE is essential to grasp why BRIO reduces reference dependence.
  - Quick check question: In contrastive learning for summarization, what is the role of the "negative" examples, and how are they constructed in BRIO?

- Concept: **Diverse beam search and candidate generation**
  - Why needed here: BRIO depends on generating multiple diverse candidate summaries per document; knowing how diverse beam search trades off diversity against quality helps explain the choice of N=6 candidates in this work.
  - Quick check question: How does the diversity penalty parameter in diverse beam search affect the overlap between generated candidates?

- Concept: **Domain adaptation via fine-tuning**
  - Why needed here: The paper shows that fine-tuning backbones on VieSum before BRIO improves results; understanding why domain alignment matters is key to replicating or extending the approach.
  - Quick check question: What metric or signal indicates that a backbone model is well-aligned with the target summarization domain?

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map:
  - Raw articles → extract content/summaries → clean → split into train/val/test
  - BARTpho, ViT5 (base versions) → fine-tuned on VieSum
  - BRIO module: Generator (fine-tuned backbone) → diverse beam search → N candsums → evaluator → contrastive loss
  - Looping: Optionally re-run BRIO with candsums from previous BRIO round
  - Evaluation: ROUGE-1/2/L scores on held-out test set

- Critical path:
  1. Fine-tune backbone on target corpus (e.g., VieSum)
  2. Generate N diverse candidates per document
  3. Rank candidates by average ROUGE against reference
  4. Train generator (MLE) and evaluator (contrastive) jointly
  5. Optionally loop: use BRIO outputs as new pseudo-references

- Design tradeoffs:
  - Number of candidates N: Higher N → more diversity but higher compute; too low → insufficient signal
  - Beam search diversity penalty: Higher penalty → more diverse but potentially lower-quality candidates
  - Fine-tuning vs. BRIO-first: Fine-tuning first aligns model, but increases total training time

- Failure signatures:
  - ROUGE scores plateau or degrade after BRIO training → possible evaluator overfitting or lack of candidate diversity
  - Extremely low candidate diversity → likely diversity penalty too low or model collapsed during fine-tuning
  - Long training times with minimal gains → candidate generation or contrastive loss not providing useful signal

- First 3 experiments:
  1. Fine-tune BARTpho/ViT5 on VieSum for 1 epoch; verify ROUGE improvement over baseline
  2. Apply BRIO with N=6 candidates; measure ROUGE gain and inspect candidate diversity (e.g., distinct n-grams)
  3. Run BRIO-loop once; compare ROUGE before/after loop and check for signs of collapse

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- Question: How does the performance of abstractive summarization models trained with BRIO compare when using different numbers of candsums (candidate summaries)?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper mentions that Liu et al. (2022a) use BART1024-length model to create 16 candsums, while this study uses 6 candsums for each document using diverse beam search.
- Why unresolved: The paper does not provide a systematic comparison of model performance using different numbers of candsums.
- What evidence would resolve it: Conduct experiments with varying numbers of candsums and evaluate the impact on model performance.

### Open Question 2
- Question: What is the impact of using different evaluation metrics for abstractive summarization models trained with BRIO?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper states that using ROUGE metric for evaluating the abstractive summarization models trained with BRIO seems unfair because these models could produce summaries which are independent on the reference summaries.
- Why unresolved: The paper does not explore alternative evaluation metrics for models trained with BRIO.
- What evidence would resolve it: Experiment with alternative evaluation metrics and compare their effectiveness in assessing model performance.

### Open Question 3
- Question: How does the performance of abstractive summarization models trained with BRIO vary across different languages and datasets?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper presents experiments on English (CNNDM) and Vietnamese (VieSum) datasets, but does not provide a comprehensive analysis of performance across different languages and datasets.
- Why unresolved: The paper does not investigate the generalizability of BRIO-trained models across diverse languages and datasets.
- What evidence would resolve it: Conduct experiments on multiple languages and datasets to assess the performance and generalizability of BRIO-trained models.

## Limitations

- The paper lacks explicit detail on the contrastive loss function and candidate quality scoring in BRIO, which are critical for faithful reproduction.
- The strong results on Vietnamese summarization are not benchmarked against prior work, raising questions about novelty and robustness.
- The diminishing returns after 2-3 BRIO-loop iterations are observed but not deeply analyzed—why exactly gains plateau is not fully explained.

## Confidence

- **High confidence** in the core BRIO mechanism (contrastive ranking + MLE training) as described in the abstract and method section.
- **Medium confidence** in the effectiveness of fine-tuning before BRIO, since this is a logical extension but not benchmarked against alternatives (e.g., BRIO without fine-tuning on same hardware).
- **Low confidence** in the long-term stability of BRIO-looping gains, due to minimal analysis of why improvements plateau and no ablation on candidate diversity across loops.

## Next Checks

1. Re-implement BRIO with explicit logging of candidate diversity metrics (distinct n-grams, ROUGE overlap) and contrastive loss values to verify the training signal is as intended.
2. Conduct an ablation study comparing ROUGE scores when fine-tuning is applied before vs. after BRIO, on the same hardware, to isolate the effect of the fine-tuning step.
3. Run BRIO-loop for 5+ iterations and track candidate diversity and ROUGE trends to confirm whether the plateau is due to model convergence or diversity collapse.
