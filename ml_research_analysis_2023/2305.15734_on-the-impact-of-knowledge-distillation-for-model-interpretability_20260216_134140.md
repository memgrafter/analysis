# On the Impact of Knowledge Distillation for Model Interpretability
## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2305.15734
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.15734
- Reference count: 40
- Primary result: Knowledge distillation improves model interpretability by transferring class-similarity information from teacher to student models

## Executive Summary
This paper demonstrates that knowledge distillation (KD) enhances model interpretability beyond just performance improvements. The authors define interpretability through the lens of concept detectors identified via network dissection, measuring the object-centricity of activation maps. They attribute interpretability gains to the transfer of class-similarity information from teacher to student models through logit distillation. Experiments show that KD increases concept detectors compared to models trained from scratch or with label smoothing, across various KD methods, architectures, and interpretability measures.

## Method Summary
The authors train ResNet-18 models using various approaches: from scratch, with label smoothing, and via knowledge distillation using ResNet-34 as teacher. They evaluate interpretability through concept detector counts using network dissection on the Broden dataset, alongside accuracy metrics, entropy within semantically similar classes, five-band-scores, DiffROAR scores, and loss gradients. Multiple KD variants are tested including vanilla KD, attention transfer, factor transfer, contrastive representation distillation, self-supervision KD, and self-KD. Temperature (T) and alpha (α) hyperparameters are varied to analyze their impact on interpretability.

## Key Results
- KD models show increased concept detector counts compared to models trained from scratch or with label smoothing
- Various interpretability metrics (five-band-scores, DiffROAR, loss gradients) improve with KD across different methods
- Self-knowledge distillation significantly improves interpretability by reducing learning capacity gaps between teacher and student
- Qualitative visualizations confirm more object-centric and human-aligned feature representations in KD models

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1
- Claim: Knowledge distillation improves model interpretability by transferring class-similarity information from teacher to student models.
- Mechanism: During logit distillation, the teacher model's output distribution contains higher probabilities for semantically similar classes. The student model learns to mimic this distribution, resulting in more object-centric activation maps that better align with human-interpretable concepts.
- Core assumption: The teacher model's class-similarity information is valuable for improving the student model's feature representation quality.
- Evidence anchors: [abstract] "We attributed the improvement in interpretability to the class-similarity information transferred from the teacher to student models via logit distillation."
- Break condition: If the teacher model's class-similarity information is noisy or misleading, or if the student model cannot effectively learn from the teacher's distribution.

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: Knowledge distillation improves interpretability across various interpretability measures beyond just concept detectors.
- Mechanism: KD enhances multiple interpretability metrics including five-band-scores, DiffROAR, and loss gradients by producing more discriminative feature representations that better align with task-relevant features.
- Core assumption: Different interpretability measures capture complementary aspects of model interpretability that can all be improved through KD.
- Evidence anchors: [abstract] "Various quantitative and qualitative experimental results support that KD improves model interpretability across KD methods, notions, and datasets."
- Break condition: If different interpretability measures conflict or if improvements in one metric come at the expense of others.

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: Self-knowledge distillation can significantly improve interpretability by reducing the gap between teacher and student learning capacities.
- Mechanism: When teacher and student have similar architectures, the student can more effectively understand and internalize the class-similarity information without the complexity mismatch that might occur with larger teacher models.
- Core assumption: Learning capacity gaps between teacher and student models hinder effective knowledge transfer for interpretability.
- Evidence anchors: [section 3.3] "Cho & Hariharan (2019) argued that if there was a gap between the learning capacities of the teacher and student models, the student model might not effectively understand the content."
- Break condition: If self-KD leads to overfitting or if the reduced model capacity limits the ability to capture complex patterns.

  - Why needed here: Understanding logit distillation, feature distillation, and other KD variants is crucial for comprehending how different types of knowledge transfer affect interpretability.
  - Quick check question: What is the key difference between logit distillation and feature distillation in terms of the information transferred?

  - Why needed here: The paper uses multiple interpretability measures (concept detectors, five-band-scores, DiffROAR, loss gradients) requiring understanding of what each metric captures.

## Architecture Onboarding
- Component map: Teacher model (ResNet-34) → KD process → Student model (ResNet-18) → Interpretability evaluation pipeline
- Critical path: Teacher training → Student training with KD → Activation map extraction → Concept detector identification → Interpretability quantification
- Design tradeoffs: Using larger teacher models provides more knowledge but may create learning capacity gaps; using self-KD avoids capacity issues but may limit knowledge richness.
- Failure signatures: Poor interpretability despite successful KD could indicate noisy class-similarity information in the teacher or ineffective student learning.
- First 3 experiments:
  1. Train ResNet-34 teacher on ImageNet, extract class probabilities for semantically similar classes to verify class-similarity information exists
  2. Implement basic logit distillation from teacher to student, measure change in concept detector count
  3. Compare interpretability metrics (concept detectors, DiffROAR) between student trained with and without KD

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
### Open Question 1
- Question: Does knowledge distillation improve interpretability beyond the specific metrics (concept detectors, five-band-scores, DiffROAR, loss gradients) tested in this paper, particularly for other types of interpretability measures?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The authors note that they "demonstrate that KD enhances model interpretability not only in specific domains (vision), but also across diverse domains (NLP)" and that they measured interpretability "regardless of the KD method and how the interpretability measurement was defined." However, they do not exhaustively test all possible interpretability measures.
- Why unresolved: The paper focuses on a limited set of interpretability measures. Other measures like SHAP values, LIME explanations, or causal interpretability measures were not explored.
- What evidence would resolve it: Experiments testing KD's impact on a broader range of interpretability measures across different model types and tasks would clarify whether the interpretability benefits generalize beyond the measures studied.

### Open Question 2
- Question: What is the precise mechanism by which class-similarity information transferred via logit distillation improves model interpretability?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The authors state that "class-similarity information transferred from the teacher to student models" improves interpretability, but they do not provide a detailed mechanistic explanation of how this transfer process works at the feature level.
- Why unresolved: While the paper demonstrates correlation between class-similarity information and interpretability, it does not explain the underlying neural mechanisms that cause this improvement.
- What evidence would resolve it: Detailed analysis of intermediate layer activations, feature space geometry comparisons, or ablation studies isolating different components of the distillation process could reveal the mechanistic pathway.

### Open Question 3
- Question: Does the interpretability improvement from KD transfer to downstream tasks when student models are fine-tuned on new datasets?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The authors state that "KD models by large models could be used more reliably in various fields" but do not test whether interpretability improvements persist after fine-tuning.
- Why unresolved: The paper only evaluates interpretability on the original training data and Broden dataset. It's unclear whether these benefits remain when models are adapted to new tasks.
- What evidence would resolve it: Experiments fine-tuning KD and non-KD models on various downstream tasks and measuring interpretability before and after fine-tuning would determine if benefits persist.

## Limitations
- The analysis relies heavily on network dissection, which may not capture all aspects of model interpretability
- The Broden dataset used for concept detector evaluation has limited semantic diversity compared to real-world applications
- The paper doesn't thoroughly investigate the relationship between model accuracy improvements and interpretability gains

## Confidence
- High Confidence: The claim that KD improves concept detector count and object-centricity of activation maps is well-supported by quantitative metrics and qualitative visualizations across multiple KD methods and architectures.
- Medium Confidence: The mechanism of class-similarity transfer improving interpretability is plausible but relies on indirect evidence through entropy measurements rather than direct visualization of transferred information.
- Medium Confidence: The claim that self-KD specifically improves interpretability due to reduced learning capacity gaps is supported by experiments but could benefit from more extensive ablation studies.

## Next Checks
1. Conduct ablation studies isolating the effect of class-similarity information from other KD benefits by training models with random teacher distributions versus semantically informed distributions.
2. Test interpretability improvements on out-of-distribution data to verify that learned concept detectors generalize beyond the training distribution.
3. Implement additional interpretability metrics not based on network dissection (such as SHAP values or integrated gradients) to cross-validate the improvement claims across different evaluation paradigms.
