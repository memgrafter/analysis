# A Unified Framework of Policy Learning for Contextual Bandit with Confounding Bias and Missing Observations

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2303.11187
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.11187
- Reference count: 40
- Key outcome: Proposed CAP algorithm achieves fast statistical rates for offline policy learning with unobserved confounders and missing observations

## Executive Summary
This paper addresses the challenge of learning optimal policies from observational data in contextual bandits when both unobserved confounders and missing observations are present. The authors propose a novel Causal-Adjusted Pessimistic (CAP) algorithm that leverages integral equation systems and pessimism to achieve fast statistical rates. The framework unifies various scenarios including instrumental variables and proxy variables, providing a general solution to confounded offline contextual bandits with missing data.

## Method Summary
The CAP algorithm solves an integral equation system to identify the conditional average treatment effect (CATE) despite unobserved confounders and missing data. It constructs a confidence set using a minimax estimator and applies pessimistic policy optimization to guard against spurious correlations. The method achieves fast statistical rates by leveraging the structure of side observations (IVs or PVs) to compensate for both confounding and missingness, without requiring uniform coverage of the action space.

## Key Results
- CAP achieves fast statistical rates of rOp(T^{-1/2}) sub-optimality for contextual bandits with side observations
- The algorithm unifies treatment of instrumental variables and proxy variables in a single framework
- Under mild assumptions on data distribution, CAP provably learns near-optimal policies from confounded offline data with missing observations

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: CAP solves a novel integral equation system to recover CATE despite unobserved confounders and missing data
- Mechanism: The IES couples two conditional moment equations that leverage side observations to compensate for both confounding and missingness
- Core assumption: Completeness assumptions on IVs or PVs ensure the IES has a unique solution
- Evidence anchors: Theorem 4.2 shows IES uniquely determines CATE under completeness conditions
- Break condition: If completeness fails, IES has no solution and CATE recovery fails

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: Uncertainty quantification via confidence sets enables pessimism to guard against spurious correlations
- Mechanism: Unconditional moment loss approximates RMSE; confidence set contains true CATE with high probability
- Core assumption: Test function class Θ is compatible and star-shaped
- Evidence anchors: Theorem 6.4 establishes confidence set properties with proper threshold
- Break condition: If compatibility constant is large, confidence set fails to contain true CATE

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: Fast statistical rates achievable by combining IES-based estimation with pessimism
- Mechanism: Sub-optimality bound depends on distribution shift ratios bk that only involve optimal policy
- Core assumption: Distribution shift ratios bk are bounded
- Evidence anchors: Theorem 6.5 shows rOp(T^{-1/2}) rate under bounded bk
- Break condition: If missingness eliminates all data for some action, bk → ∞ breaking fast rate

## Foundational Learning

- Concept: Integral equations and completeness conditions
  - Why needed here: IES identifies CATE by exploiting completeness of IVs/PCs; without it, no solution exists
  - Quick check question: Given IVs Z complete over (X,A), does the IES in Theorem 4.2 uniquely determine g*?

- Concept: Minimax estimation and Fenchel duality
  - Why needed here: Transforms conditional moment restrictions into unconditional ones for empirical loss minimization

  - Why needed here: Guards against overestimation bias when optimizing with estimated CATE

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map: Data preprocessing -> IES solver -> Confidence set builder -> Pessimistic optimizer -> Evaluation
- Critical path: 1) Parse observational dataset, handle missingness 2) Construct IES based on IV/Proxy structure 3) Estimate bridge functions via minimax loss 4) Build confidence set with proper eD 5) Apply pessimism to select policy 6) Bound sub-optimality
- Design tradeoffs: Hypothesis class H size vs. realizability εH; Dual class Θ size vs. compatibility εΘ; Threshold eD vs. coverage
- Failure signatures: High εH or εΘ → poor CATE estimation; Unbounded bk → missingness invalidates fast rate; η not ~ Op(1/√T) → slow statistical rate
- First 3 experiments: 1) Synthetic IV case with full coverage 2) Missing context at random 3) Proxy variable with missing outcome proxy

## Limitations
- Performance critically depends on completeness assumptions for instrumental/proxy variables
- Fast statistical rates require bounded distribution shift ratios that may not hold in practice
- Implementation details for constructing bridge functions and confidence sets are underspecified

## Confidence

**High Confidence**: IES-based CATE identification is theoretically sound when completeness conditions are met; pessimism follows established RL principles

**Medium Confidence**: Fast rates rely on specific distribution shift conditions that may not generalize; novel combination needs more empirical validation

**Low Confidence**: Practical implementation details for finite samples are underspecified, making direct reproduction challenging
