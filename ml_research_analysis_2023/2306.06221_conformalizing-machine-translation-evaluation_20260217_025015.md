# Conformalizing Machine Translation Evaluation

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2306.06221
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.06221
- Reference count: 13
- Primary result: Conformal prediction can correct confidence intervals to achieve desired coverage in MT evaluation

## Executive Summary
This paper addresses the critical problem of unreliable confidence intervals in machine translation evaluation, where existing uncertainty estimation methods often underestimate model uncertainty and produce misleading predictions. The authors propose using conformal prediction, a distribution-free method, to obtain confidence intervals with theoretical guarantees on coverage. They demonstrate that conformal prediction can "correct" the confidence intervals of previous methods to achieve the desired coverage level.

The paper also highlights biases in estimated confidence intervals across different language pairs and translation quality levels, and shows how conditional conformal prediction techniques can address these imbalances by obtaining calibration subsets for each data subgroup, leading to equalized coverage.

## Method Summary
The authors apply conformal prediction to machine translation evaluation metrics to provide distribution-free confidence intervals with theoretical coverage guarantees. They demonstrate how conformal methods can adjust existing uncertainty estimates from previous approaches to achieve the desired coverage level. The paper introduces conditional conformal prediction techniques that address subgroup imbalances by creating separate calibration sets for different language pairs and translation quality levels, ensuring equalized coverage across diverse evaluation scenarios.

## Key Results
- Conformal prediction can correct confidence intervals of previous methods to achieve desired coverage
- Estimated confidence intervals show biases across different language pairs and translation quality levels
- Conditional conformal prediction techniques address subgroup calibration issues by creating separate calibration subsets
- Coverage equalization achieved across diverse language pairs and quality levels using conditional approaches

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
Conformal prediction provides distribution-free confidence intervals by using calibration data to determine prediction regions that achieve the desired coverage level. The method works by computing nonconformity scores on a held-out calibration set and using these scores to construct intervals that contain the true value with guaranteed probability, regardless of the underlying data distribution.

## Foundational Learning
- Conformal prediction: Distribution-free method for constructing prediction sets with guaranteed coverage
  - Why needed: Traditional uncertainty quantification methods make strong distributional assumptions that often fail in practice
  - Quick check: Verify theoretical coverage guarantees hold empirically across different data distributions

- Nonconformity scores: Measures of how different a new example is from the calibration set
  - Why needed: Core mechanism for determining which predictions fall within the confidence interval
  - Quick check: Ensure score computation is consistent across different subgroups

- Conditional conformal prediction: Extension that creates separate calibration sets for subgroups
  - Why needed: Addresses coverage imbalances across different language pairs and quality levels
  - Quick check: Verify equalized coverage across all subgroups after conditioning

## Architecture Onboarding

Component map: Calibration data -> Nonconformity score computation -> Prediction interval construction -> Coverage verification

Critical path: The core workflow involves setting aside calibration data, computing nonconformity scores, determining prediction intervals based on desired coverage level, and validating coverage across subgroups.

Design tradeoffs: The method requires setting aside calibration data, which reduces the amount of data available for training. However, this trade-off is necessary to achieve theoretical coverage guarantees. Conditional approaches improve subgroup calibration but increase computational overhead and require sufficient samples in each subgroup.

Failure signatures: Coverage may drop below theoretical guarantees if calibration sets are too small or if the nonconformity score distribution differs significantly between calibration and test data. Subgroup imbalances can lead to poor calibration for minority groups.

First experiments:
1. Verify coverage guarantees on synthetic data with known distributions
2. Compare coverage across different language pairs with varying sample sizes
3. Test conditional vs. unconditional approaches on subgroup-imbalanced datasets

## Limitations
- Requires setting aside calibration data, which may be problematic with limited datasets
- Focuses on specific metrics and language pairs, limiting generalizability across all MT evaluation scenarios
- Computational overhead of conformal methods compared to traditional approaches not thoroughly examined

## Confidence

High confidence: Conformal prediction provides distribution-free confidence intervals with theoretical coverage guarantees, supported by established theoretical foundations and experimental validation.

Medium confidence: Conditional conformal prediction addresses subgroup calibration issues, demonstrated through specific language pairs and quality levels examined in experiments.

Low confidence: Practical superiority of conformal methods over existing uncertainty quantification approaches in real-world MT evaluation lacks sufficient empirical comparison and analysis of computational trade-offs.
