# Random Teachers are Good Teachers

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2302.12091
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2302.12091
- Reference count: 40
- Key outcome: Students trained via self-distillation with random teachers significantly outperform the teachers in probing accuracy, suggesting implicit regularization drives feature learning

## Executive Summary
This paper explores an unexpected phenomenon in teacher-student learning: when a student is trained to mimic a randomly initialized teacher, the student significantly outperforms the teacher in feature learning tasks. The authors systematically investigate this counterintuitive result across multiple datasets (CIFAR10, CIFAR100, STL10, TinyImageNet) and architectures (ResNet18, VGG11). Through controlled experiments varying the initialization proximity between student and teacher, they demonstrate that this effect is driven by implicit regularization rather than any useful knowledge transfer from the teacher. The findings challenge conventional understanding of distillation and reveal that proximity to the teacher in parameter space induces feature learning through local properties of the loss landscape.

## Method Summary
The core methodology involves training a student network to mimic a randomly initialized teacher network using KL divergence loss. The key innovation is the introduction of a locality parameter α that controls how close the student's initialization is to the teacher's parameters. The student architecture includes a 3-layer MLP projector with an l2-bottleneck, and training uses the Adam optimizer with learning rate 0.001. Performance is evaluated through linear and k-NN probing on CIFAR10, transfer learning across datasets, and lottery ticket discovery through iterative magnitude pruning with a fraction of 0.2. The experiments systematically vary α to demonstrate that proximity to the teacher is crucial for the observed performance gains.

## Key Results
- Students consistently outperform randomly initialized teachers in linear and k-NN probing accuracy across all tested datasets
- The performance advantage disappears when students are initialized far from the teacher (large α) or when trained on random inputs
- The learned student representations transfer well across datasets and contain sparse subnetworks (lottery tickets) without additional training
- The effect is architecture-agnostic, working with both ResNet18 and VGG11

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The mechanism behind this phenomenon appears to be implicit regularization induced by the proximity constraint between student and teacher. When the student is initialized close to the teacher, the loss landscape creates a local basin where optimization naturally discovers useful feature representations. This suggests that the geometry of the loss landscape near a random initialization point contains gradients that, when followed under the distillation objective, lead to meaningful feature learning. The random teacher serves as an anchor point that, through its proximity constraint, shapes the optimization trajectory in a way that favors learning generalizable features rather than overfitting to random weights.

## Foundational Learning
- **Implicit regularization**: Regularization effects that emerge from the optimization dynamics and architecture choices rather than explicit penalty terms. Why needed: Central to understanding how student networks learn useful features without explicit supervision. Quick check: Verify that standard regularization techniques (weight decay, dropout) don't explain the performance gains.
- **Loss landscape geometry**: The topological structure of the loss function in parameter space. Why needed: Explains why proximity to the teacher induces feature learning. Quick check: Visualize loss landscapes around teacher and student initializations to confirm basin structure.
- **Lottery tickets**: Sparse subnetworks that can be trained in isolation to match or exceed the performance of the full network. Why needed: Demonstrates that the learned representations have inherent structure and efficiency. Quick check: Confirm that discovered tickets maintain performance when trained independently.

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map**: Random teacher initialization -> Student initialization (proximity α) -> KL divergence loss -> 3-layer MLP projector (l2-bottleneck) -> Adam optimizer -> Feature representations -> Linear/k-NN probing
- **Critical path**: Student initialization proximity → Projector architecture → KL loss minimization → Representation quality
- **Design tradeoffs**: The locality parameter α trades off between exploration (large α) and exploitation of the teacher's proximity (small α). The projector bottleneck balances compression against representational capacity.
- **Failure signatures**: Student performance collapses when α is too large, when trained on random inputs, or when projector architecture is improperly implemented.
- **First experiments**: 1) Verify student outperforms teacher with α ≈ 10⁻¹⁰, 2) Test performance degradation with larger α values, 3) Confirm results hold across different random seeds

## Limitations
- The study is limited to self-distillation with randomly initialized teachers, leaving generalizability to other teacher-student dynamics unclear
- The theoretical understanding of why proximity induces regularization remains largely empirical
- The findings are based on a limited set of datasets and architectures
- Lottery ticket discovery depends on specific pruning protocols that may not reflect general representation quality

## Confidence
- High confidence in: empirical observation of student outperforming random teacher, role of initialization proximity
- Medium confidence in: transferability of learned representations across datasets
- Low confidence in: broader claim that self-distillation works through implicit regularization alone

## Next Checks
1. Test whether similar performance gains occur when student initialization is drawn from distributions other than proximity-based sampling around the teacher
2. Verify lottery ticket discovery by applying the same iterative pruning protocol to independently trained baselines and comparing final accuracies
3. Conduct ablation studies varying the projector architecture details to confirm these components are not driving the core results
