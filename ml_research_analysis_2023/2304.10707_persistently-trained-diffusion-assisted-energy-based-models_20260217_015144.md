# Persistently Trained, Diffusion-assisted Energy-based Models

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2304.10707
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2304.10707
- Reference count: 40
- Key outcome: DA-EBMs achieve superior OOD detection (AUROC up to 0.98) and realistic post-training image generation on MNIST-type datasets, overcoming local mixing in persistent EBM training.

## Executive Summary
Persistent training with MCMC sampling can achieve stable long-run samples from real data for energy-based models (EBMs), but typically fails to generate realistic post-training samples from random noise and suffers from out-of-distribution (OOD) reversal. This paper proposes diffusion-assisted EBMs (DA-EBMs) that jointly model the original data and diffusion data at multiple noise levels. By incorporating an enhanced sampling algorithm (MALA within Gibbs mixture sampling), DA-EBMs overcome local mixing and learn globally aligned energy functions. On MNIST-type datasets, DA-EBMs achieve superior OOD detection and realistic post-training image generation, unlike previous methods.

## Method Summary
DA-EBMs extend traditional EBMs by introducing diffusion data through a forward diffusion process and learning a joint EBM over both the original data and diffusion data at multiple noise levels. The method uses persistent training with a replay buffer and an enhanced sampling algorithm called MALA within Gibbs mixture sampling (MGMS) that alternates between Markov moves (sampling from conditional distributions using MALA) and global jumps (exact sampling of time labels). This enables the sampler to explore both the original distribution and auxiliary diffusion distributions effectively, overcoming local mixing issues.

## Key Results
- Achieves superior OOD detection (AUROC up to 0.98) on MNIST-type datasets
- Generates realistic post-training image samples from random noise
- First persistently trained EBM that simultaneously achieves long-run stability, post-training sampling, and proper density estimation for image data

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: Persistent training with local mixing MCMC can learn only locally meaningful energy functions, not globally aligned ones.
- Mechanism: When MCMC sampling suffers from local mixing (cannot traverse low-density barriers between modes), the learned energy function only matches the data distribution locally within each mode, leading to globally misaligned energy landscapes.
- Core assumption: The MCMC sampler used during persistent training enables only local mixing for multimodal distributions.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "persistent training using an MCMC sampler which suffers local mixing may only learn local energy functions, which are locally meaningful but globally misaligned"
  - [section] "Eq. (5) dictates the network parameter θ̂ such that the transition kernel Kθ̂ leaves the replay-buffer distribution ˆq invariant. Eq. (6) induces moment matching between ˆp and ˆq such that the replay-buffer distribution ˆq resembles the real data distribution ˆp."

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: Diffusion-assisted EBMs overcome local mixing by introducing auxiliary distributions that bridge different modes.
- Mechanism: By modeling both original data and diffusion data at multiple noise levels simultaneously, the joint EBM creates connected auxiliary distributions that enable the MCMC sampler to traverse between modes, leading to globally aligned energy functions.
- Core assumption: Diffusion data at multiple noise levels can effectively "fill low-density regions" and create bridges between modes.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "motivated by this understanding and inspired by recent success of score-based diffusion modeling, we propose to introduce diffusion data through a forward diffusion process and learn a joint EBM"
  - [section] "diffusion data created with multiple noise levels can also be used as auxiliary distributions to bridge different modes in multimodal data or 'fill low-density regions'"

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: The MGMS algorithm enables proper sampling from the joint EBM, leading to globally aligned energy functions and post-training generation capability.
- Mechanism: MGMS alternates between Markov moves (sampling from conditional distributions using MALA) and global jumps (exact sampling of time labels), allowing the sampler to explore both the original distribution and auxiliary diffusion distributions effectively.
- Core assumption: The conditional distributions pθ(x|t) and pθ(t|x) can be properly sampled using MALA and exact sampling respectively.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "incorporating an enhanced sampling algorithm (MALA within Gibbs mixture sampling)"
  - [section] "The two operations are called, respectively, Markov move and global jump, which draws ˜t1 given ˜x1 independently of ˜t0. These two operations illustrate how MGMS may achieve proper sampling from a multimodal distribution"

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map:
  - Energy network -> Parameterizes Uθ(x,t) for joint EBM
  - Replay buffer -> Stores synthetic data for persistent training
  - MGMS sampler -> Performs enhanced sampling using MALA and Gibbs sampling
  - Diffusion scheduler -> Generates diffusion data at multiple noise levels
  - Training loop -> Combines sampling and parameter updates

- Critical path:
  1. Generate diffusion data from real data
  2. Initialize persistent training with replay buffer
  3. Sample using MGMS (alternating MALA and global jumps)
  4. Update parameters using gradients from real and synthetic data
  5. Repeat until convergence

- Design tradeoffs:
  - Persistent vs noise/hybrid initialization: Persistent enables long-run stability but suffers from local mixing; noise/hybrid enables post-training generation but not long-run stability
  - Number of diffusion time steps: More steps provide better bridges but increase computational cost
  - MALA step size: Larger steps increase mixing but may reduce acceptance rates

- Failure signatures:
  - Energy landscape has large ranges with almost invisible modes (indicates local mixing problem)
  - Post-training samples fail to generate realistic images (indicates poor global alignment)
  - OOD detection shows reversal (higher likelihoods for OOD than in-distribution data)

- First 3 experiments:
  1. Train EBM with persistent initialization on 1D Gaussian mixture - observe local but misaligned energy function
  2. Implement MGMS on simple multimodal distribution - verify it can traverse between modes
  3. Train DA-EBM on MNIST with different numbers of diffusion steps - observe how post-training generation quality changes

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- Question: Can the proposed diffusion-assisted EBM (DA-EBM) method be scaled to more complex image datasets and larger models?
- Basis in paper: [inferred] The paper mentions that the post-training sampling of DA-EBM is more costly than other methods and that further investigation is needed to scale it to more complex images and larger models.
- Why unresolved: The paper only demonstrates the effectiveness of DA-EBM on MNIST-type datasets and acknowledges the need for further research to apply it to more complex scenarios.
- What evidence would resolve it: Successful application of DA-EBM to larger and more complex image datasets (e.g., CIFAR-10, ImageNet) with comparable or improved performance in terms of long-run stability, post-training sampling, and OOD detection.

### Open Question 2
- Question: How does the choice of diffusion scheduling affect the performance of DA-EBM?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper mentions that the cumulative-sum diffusion scheduling used in the experiments is not particularly optimized and can possibly be improved for better training performance.
- Why unresolved: The paper does not explore different diffusion scheduling strategies or provide a systematic analysis of their impact on DA-EBM's performance.
- What evidence would resolve it: Comparative studies of different diffusion scheduling strategies (e.g., linear, quadratic, logarithmic) and their effects on DA-EBM's performance metrics (e.g., OOD detection, image generation quality) on various datasets.

### Open Question 3
- Question: Can the local mixing issue in persistent EBM training be addressed without introducing diffusion data?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper identifies local mixing as a key challenge in persistent EBM training and proposes diffusion data as a solution. However, it does not explore alternative approaches to address this issue.
- Why unresolved: The paper focuses on the diffusion-assisted approach and does not investigate other potential solutions to the local mixing problem in persistent EBM training.
- What evidence would resolve it: Development and evaluation of alternative methods to address local mixing in persistent EBM training, such as improved sampling algorithms, modified energy functions, or novel training strategies, and their comparison with the diffusion-assisted approach in terms of performance and efficiency.

## Limitations
- Evaluation limited to relatively simple datasets (MNIST-type)
- No comparison to recent diffusion-based generative models on complex image datasets
- Limited ablation studies on the impact of different diffusion schedules or network architectures

## Confidence

- High confidence: The core mechanism of using diffusion data to bridge modes and enable global mixing is theoretically sound
- Medium confidence: The empirical results on MNIST/FashionMNIST demonstrate the proposed approach works as claimed
- Low confidence: Whether this approach will scale effectively to complex natural image distributions
