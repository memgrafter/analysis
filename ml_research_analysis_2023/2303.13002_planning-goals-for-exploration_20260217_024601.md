# Planning Goals for Exploration

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2303.13002
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.13002
- Reference count: 40
- Key outcome: PEG solves 3-block stacking, a classic open problem in robot RL, while other methods fail to make meaningful progress

## Executive Summary
This paper addresses unsupervised exploration in goal-conditioned reinforcement learning, where an agent must learn to achieve diverse tasks in unknown environments without task-specific rewards during training. The proposed method, PEG (Planning Exploratory Goals), directly optimizes goal selection to maximize exploration value using a learned world model and sampling-based planning algorithms. Experiments demonstrate that PEG outperforms existing goal-directed exploration methods and successfully achieves 3-block stacking, a classic open problem in robot RL that other methods fail to solve.

## Method Summary
PEG combines goal-conditioned RL with planning-based goal selection to enable efficient exploration. The method learns a world model to predict state transitions and policy behavior, then uses this model to optimize goal selection via sampling-based planning algorithms like MPPI. PEG operates in two phases: a Go-phase where the goal-conditioned policy attempts to reach the optimized goal, followed by an Explore-phase where an intrinsic motivation-driven exploration policy further explores from the reached state. The goal optimization maximizes the expected exploration value of the terminal state, accounting for both reachability and downstream exploration potential.

## Key Results
- PEG achieves significantly higher exploration coverage than baselines (LEXA, MEGA, SkewFit, Plan2Explore) across multiple simulated robotics environments
- PEG successfully solves 3-block stacking, a classic open problem in robot RL that other methods cannot meaningfully progress on
- The method demonstrates strong performance in challenging 6-DoF robotics tasks including Point Maze, Walker, and Ant Maze environments

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
Optimizing goal selection directly for exploration value enables more efficient exploration because it accounts for both reachability and downstream exploration potential. PEG optimizes goals to maximize the expected exploration value of terminal states reached by the current policy, then launches an exploration policy from those states. This two-phase approach leverages the goal-conditioned policy's ability to reach promising areas, then uses an exploration policy to thoroughly explore from there.

### Mechanism 2
Using sampling-based planning algorithms for goal optimization is computationally tractable because the goal space is lower-dimensional than action sequences. PEG adapts sampling-based planners like MPPI, typically used for low-level action planning, to optimize over goal commands. The lower-dimensional search space enables easy optimization independent of task horizon.

### Mechanism 3
The Go-Explore mechanism (Go-phase followed by Explore-phase) is critical because it separates efficient goal-reaching from thorough exploration, avoiding the pitfall of rare states that are dead ends. After the goal-conditioned policy reaches a state via the Go-phase, the exploration policy conducts undirected exploration from that state during the Explore-phase, ensuring that even if the goal state is novel, the agent can still explore effectively from there.

## Foundational Learning

- **Goal-Conditioned Reinforcement Learning**: PEG operates within the GCRL framework where policies are conditioned on goal commands and must learn to achieve diverse goals. Why needed: The method requires understanding how goal-conditioned policies can be trained and evaluated. - **World Models and Model-Based RL**: PEG relies on a learned world model to predict policy behavior and optimize goals. Why needed: Understanding how world models are trained and used is critical for implementing PEG. Quick check: How does a variational recurrent state space model (VRSSM) differ from a standard forward dynamics model?

- **Intrinsic Motivation and Exploration Rewards**: PEG uses an exploration policy trained with intrinsic motivation during the Explore-phase. Why needed: The method depends on effective exploration strategies for thorough environment coverage. Quick check: What is the difference between count-based exploration bonuses and prediction error-based exploration rewards?

## Architecture Onboarding

- **Component map**: World model (VRSSM) -> Goal-conditioned policy (πG) -> Exploration policy (πE) -> Goal optimization module (MPPI/CEM) -> Exploration value function (VE)

- **Critical path**: 1) Collect data with current policies, 2) Update world model with new data, 3) Update πG and πE in imagination using the world model, 4) Optimize goals using MPPI to maximize expected exploration value, 5) Execute Go-Explore episodes with optimized goals

- **Design tradeoffs**: Model-based vs. model-free (PEG uses MBRL for sample efficiency but requires more computation), Goal space restriction (PEG samples from full state space for generality vs. restricting to achievable states), Exploration policy choice (Plan2Explore disagreement vs. other intrinsic motivation methods)

- **Failure signatures**: Poor exploration (goal optimization consistently selects dead ends), High variance in performance (world model predictions are unreliable), Slow learning (goal optimization is too conservative)

- **First 3 experiments**: 1) Verify world model can predict goal-conditioned policy behavior on held-out data, 2) Test goal optimization in Point Maze with known optimal goals, 3) Compare PEG's exploration coverage vs. baselines in Walker environment

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

- **Open Question 1**: How would PEG perform with model-free RL approaches instead of model-based RL? The authors note that PEG is currently implemented on top of a model-based RL backbone and mention that a model-free version could be computationally and conceptually simpler.

- **Open Question 2**: What is the impact of the goal optimization algorithm on PEG's performance? The authors mention using MPPI but note that other optimizers like CEM could work well, providing only brief comparison in the appendix.

- **Open Question 3**: How does PEG scale to environments with very large or continuous goal spaces? The authors mention that complex environments may permit extremely large goal spaces where purely unsupervised exploration approaches like PEG are unlikely to succeed within reasonable time.

## Limitations

- PEG's effectiveness in environments with significantly higher-dimensional observations or more complex dynamics is untested
- The computational overhead of running MPPI optimization for goal selection during training is not thoroughly characterized
- The claim that PEG's approach is fundamentally superior to simply targeting rare states for exploration lacks rigorous ablation studies

## Confidence

- **High Confidence**: The core mechanism of using world models to predict exploration value and optimize goal selection is well-supported by experimental results across multiple environments
- **Medium Confidence**: The claim that PEG solves 3-block stacking, a "classic open problem," is supported by results but lacks comparison to the full landscape of prior work on this specific task
- **Low Confidence**: The assertion that PEG's approach is fundamentally superior to simply targeting rare states for exploration lacks rigorous ablation studies isolating the impact of the exploration value optimization

## Next Checks

1. Test PEG in environments with higher-dimensional goal spaces (e.g., 20+ dimensional goals) to measure the scalability limits of the sampling-based optimization

2. Conduct systematic experiments with degraded world model accuracy to quantify the method's robustness to model uncertainty

3. Perform an ablation study comparing PEG against a simpler baseline that optimizes goals based only on novelty/reachability metrics, without the full exploration value prediction
