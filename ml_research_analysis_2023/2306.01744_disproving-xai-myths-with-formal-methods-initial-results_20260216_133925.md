# Disproving XAI Myths with Formal Methods -- Initial Results

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2306.01744
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.01744
- Reference count: 40
- Key outcome: The paper investigates and disproves three widely held myths in eXplainable AI (XAI): (1) Intrinsic interpretability, (2) Model-agnostic explanations, and (3) Shapley values for explainability

## Executive Summary
This paper rigorously disproves three widely held myths in eXplainable AI using formal methods. The authors demonstrate that decision trees can contain significant redundancy in their explanations, model-agnostic explainability methods like Anchors can produce insufficient explanations, and Shapley values can assign higher importance to irrelevant features than relevant ones. The work employs logic-based reasoning, SAT solving, and computational complexity analysis to validate these refutations, highlighting the need for formal guarantees in XAI to ensure trustworthy explanations.

## Method Summary
The paper employs formal methods including SAT solving, model counting, and computational complexity analysis to rigorously test XAI interpretability claims. For each myth, the authors formalize the claim as a logical or computational property and then attempt to verify or falsify it. The methodology involves computing abductive explanations (AXp) and contrastive explanations (CXp) for boolean classifiers, comparing path-based explanations from decision trees with formal minimal sufficient explanations, validating model-agnostic explanations using SAT solvers, and computing Shapley values to assess feature importance.

## Key Results
- Decision tree paths contain significant redundancy compared to formal abductive explanations
- Model-agnostic explainability methods (e.g., Anchors) fail to produce sufficient explanations when tested against formal criteria
- Shapley values can assign higher importance scores to irrelevant features than to relevant ones
