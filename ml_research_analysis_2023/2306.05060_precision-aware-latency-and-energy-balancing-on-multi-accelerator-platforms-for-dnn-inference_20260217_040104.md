# Precision-aware Latency and Energy Balancing on Multi-Accelerator Platforms for DNN Inference

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2306.05060
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.05060
- Reference count: 26
- Primary result: ODiMO reduces energy by up to 33% and latency by up to 31% with minimal accuracy loss across CIFAR-10, Tiny-ImageNet, and VWW datasets.

## Executive Summary
ODiMO is a hardware-aware DNN mapping tool that performs fine-grain layer partitioning across heterogeneous accelerators to minimize energy/latency while maintaining accuracy. It uses a differentiable, channel-wise mixed-precision search method to assign each layer's channels to different accelerators based on their quantization support, optimizing the trade-off between accuracy and inference cost during training. Experiments show significant energy and latency reductions compared to manual mappings, with minimal accuracy drops. The method is hardware-agnostic and produces rich Pareto-fronts in accuracy vs energy/latency space.

## Method Summary
ODiMO employs a differentiable search strategy to optimize DNN layer partitioning across heterogeneous accelerators. The method uses channel-wise mixed-precision assignments, where each layer's channels can be mapped to different accelerators based on their quantization capabilities. During training, the approach jointly optimizes both the neural network parameters and the layer-to-accelerator assignments through a differentiable relaxation. The search process considers hardware constraints and characteristics, producing mappings that balance accuracy, energy consumption, and latency. The tool generates Pareto-optimal solutions, allowing designers to select configurations based on their specific requirements.

## Key Results
- Energy reduction up to 33% and latency reduction up to 31% compared to manual mappings
- Minimal accuracy degradation (-0.53% on CIFAR-10, -0.32% on Tiny-ImageNet)
- On DIANA accelerator, achieved 33% energy savings for 0.53% accuracy loss
- 37.63% accuracy improvement over cost-only baseline with only 1.12× higher energy

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
ODiMO's effectiveness stems from its ability to perform fine-grained channel-level partitioning across heterogeneous accelerators while jointly optimizing both network parameters and hardware mappings. The differentiable search allows gradient-based optimization of discrete layer-to-accelerator assignments through continuous relaxation, enabling efficient exploration of the vast design space. By considering quantization support of different accelerators, the method can leverage the strengths of each hardware unit while maintaining accuracy through intelligent channel distribution.

## Foundational Learning
- Hardware cost modeling: Essential for predicting energy and latency of different mappings; quick check: verify model accuracy against real hardware measurements
- Differentiable architecture search: Enables gradient-based optimization of discrete assignments; quick check: confirm convergence behavior and stability
- Channel-wise mixed-precision: Allows per-channel quantization flexibility; quick check: measure impact on accuracy vs hardware efficiency
- Heterogeneous accelerator coordination: Critical for managing communication and synchronization; quick check: analyze overhead from cross-accelerator transfers
- Pareto optimization: Balances multiple objectives effectively; quick check: validate diversity of generated solutions

## Architecture Onboarding
- Component map: Input DNN -> Channel partitioning module -> Accelerator assignment module -> Hardware cost estimation -> Joint optimization loop -> Output: Mapped DNN
- Critical path: Forward pass computation → Channel partitioning decision → Accelerator mapping → Cost estimation → Backward pass update
- Design tradeoffs: Fine-grained partitioning increases flexibility but adds communication overhead; mixed-precision improves accuracy but complicates hardware design
- Failure signatures: Poor mappings show high cross-accelerator communication; quantization mismatches cause accuracy drops; suboptimal partitions lead to load imbalance
- First experiments: (1) Test basic mapping on single-layer network with two accelerators, (2) Validate quantization support detection, (3) Verify Pareto front generation on simple model

## Limitations
- Limited comparison against other state-of-the-art NAS-based multi-accelerator mapping approaches
- Evaluation restricted to three small datasets (CIFAR-10, Tiny-ImageNet, VWW) and five models
- Absence of real hardware deployment results to validate model predictions
- Scalability to larger models and datasets remains unverified
