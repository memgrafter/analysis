# Counterfactual Evaluation of Peer-Review Assignment Policies

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2305.17339
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.17339
- Reference count: 40
- Key outcome: Higher weights on text similarity improve review quality; randomization reduces quality only marginally

## Executive Summary
This paper evaluates peer-review assignment algorithms using counterfactual analysis of fraud-prevention randomization. The authors leverage natural assignment randomness in two venues (TPDP and AAAI) to compare counterfactual assignment policies against actual policies. Standard off-policy evaluation methods fail due to positivity violations, requiring the introduction of partial identification methods based on monotonicity and Lipschitz smoothness assumptions.

## Method Summary
The authors use off-policy evaluation to compare counterfactual assignment policies to the actual policy used in peer-review. They exploit randomness in fraud-prevention assignment policies to create counterfactual scenarios. Due to positivity violations in standard methods, they develop partial identification approaches under monotonicity and Lipschitz smoothness assumptions on the covariate-outcome mapping.

## Key Results
- Higher weights on text similarity improve review quality
- Randomization reduces review quality only marginally
- Standard off-policy methods fail due to positivity violations, necessitating partial identification methods

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The paper leverages randomization in fraud-prevention assignment policies as a natural experiment to estimate causal effects of different assignment algorithms. By assuming monotonicity and Lipschitz smoothness in the relationship between covariates and outcomes, the authors can bound counterfactual review quality even when standard identification assumptions fail.

## Foundational Learning
- **Positivity violation**: Occurs when some treatments have zero probability under the policy, preventing standard off-policy evaluation. Quick check: Verify treatment probabilities across all covariate strata.
- **Monotonicity assumption**: Requires that the outcome function doesn't decrease with certain inputs. Quick check: Test for violations in observed data patterns.
- **Lipschitz smoothness**: Bounds how rapidly the outcome can change with respect to covariates. Quick check: Calculate empirical gradient bounds from data.

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map**: Data collection -> Assignment policy evaluation -> Partial identification method -> Quality bounds
- **Critical path**: Assignment randomness detection → Covariate-outcome relationship modeling → Identification bounds calculation → Quality comparison
- **Design tradeoffs**: Partial identification provides bounds vs. point estimates; handles positivity violations but sacrifices precision
- **Failure signatures**: Violated monotonicity assumptions lead to incorrect bounds; violated smoothness assumptions produce overly wide or narrow bounds
- **First experiments**: 1) Test monotonicity assumption on historical assignment data, 2) Validate smoothness bounds using simulated data, 3) Compare partial identification bounds with traditional methods on synthetic datasets

## Limitations
- Results depend heavily on monotonicity and Lipschitz smoothness assumptions
- Analysis limited to only two venues with different scales
- TPDP dataset small (95 papers), raising statistical power concerns
- Partial identification provides bounds rather than precise estimates
