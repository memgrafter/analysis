# Concurrent ischemic lesion age estimation and segmentation of CT brain using a Transformer-based network

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2306.12242
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.12242
- Reference count: 40
- Primary result: Transformer-based MTL approach achieves AUC of 0.933 for classifying lesion ages ≤4.5 hours

## Executive Summary
This study proposes a transformer-based network for concurrent ischemic lesion age estimation and segmentation in CT brain imaging. The method leverages gated positional self-attention and CT-specific data augmentation to capture long-range spatial dependencies while maintaining data efficiency. By jointly optimizing lesion age estimation and segmentation through multi-task learning, the model predicts age via probability density functions, enabling uncertainty quantification. Evaluated on 776 clinical CT images, it outperforms single-task baselines and task-specific state-of-the-art methods for age classification, while also providing competitive segmentation performance.

## Method Summary
The method employs a transformer-based network with gated positional self-attention to jointly estimate ischemic lesion age and segment lesions in CT brain imaging. The architecture uses a ResNeXt-50 backbone with pyramid pooling module, followed by a transformer encoder-decoder with GPSA layers. It incorporates CT-specific data augmentation including intensity transforms and slice thickness interpolation. Age estimation is performed using quantile regression with split normal distributions to capture uncertainty. The model is trained end-to-end with random loss weighting from a Dirichlet distribution to balance multi-task learning objectives.

## Key Results
- Achieves AUC of 0.933 for classifying lesion ages ≤4.5 hours
- Dice similarity coefficient of 38.2% for lesion segmentation
- Outperforms single-task baselines and task-specific state-of-the-art methods
- Demonstrates improved data efficiency through GPSA compared to standard transformers

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: Multi-task learning with age estimation improves segmentation accuracy.
- Mechanism: Joint optimization of complementary tasks forces the network to learn shared representations that encode both spatial lesion structure and temporal dynamics, improving generalization.
- Core assumption: Age estimation and segmentation are inherently complementary tasks; learning one benefits the other.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "These two tasks, lesion segmentation and age estimation, appear to be inherently complementary..."
  - [section] "As the appearance of an ischemic lesion is highly dynamic, a perfect segmentation model would need to recognize lesions for all time points and implicitly understand the lesion age."

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: Gated positional self-attention improves data efficiency.
- Mechanism: GPSA modules modulate attention based on position versus content, incorporating useful CNN-like inductive biases while maintaining Transformer flexibility, enabling effective training with limited medical data.
- Core assumption: Standard Transformers struggle with small datasets due to lack of inductive biases; GPSA mitigates this.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "By utilizing gated positional self-attention and CT-specific data augmentation, the proposed method can capture long-range spatial dependencies while maintaining its ability to be trained from scratch under low-data regimes..."
  - [section] "To improve the data efficiency of the model we replace the multi-head attention layers in the encoder with GPSA layers."

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: Quantile loss with probability density estimation improves age prediction reliability.
- Mechanism: Predicting multiple quantiles and combining them via split normal distributions captures uncertainty and reduces over-reliance on single point estimates, leading to more robust age predictions.
- Core assumption: Lesion age estimates follow a normal-like distribution; capturing this via quantiles improves overall accuracy.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [section] "To enable the uncertainty of lesion age estimates to be quantized, we used quantile loss for the lesion age loss La. We predict three quantiles, assuming that estimates for lesion age are normally distributed..."
  - [section] "At inference time, we combine lesion age estimates if their associated predicted segmentation masks are connected in 3D. Given a set of K lesion age quantile predictions..."

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map:
  CNN backbone (ResNeXt) -> pyramid pooling module (PPM) -> Transformer encoder with GPSA -> Transformer decoder -> MLP heads (lesion classification, bounding box, age estimation) -> segmentation head (U-Net style)

- Critical path:
  1. Input CT slice -> CNN backbone + PPM -> feature embedding
  2. Feature embedding + positional encoding -> Transformer encoder-decoder -> object queries
  3. Object queries -> MLP heads -> lesion/bounding box/age predictions
  4. Object queries + encoder output -> segmentation head -> masks

- Design tradeoffs:
  - 2D slices vs 3D volumes: 2D is more data-efficient but may miss volumetric context
  - Transformer vs CNN: Transformers capture long-range dependencies but require more data; GPSA mitigates this
  - Single-task vs MTL: MTL may improve both tasks but risks negative transfer if tasks are not complementary

- Failure signatures:
  - Segmentation head fails: Masks poorly aligned or noisy
  - Age estimation fails: High MAE/RMSE, poor AUC for classification
  - Transformer underfits: GPSA gating not learning; revert to multi-head attention
  - Overfitting: Excessive augmentation or insufficient regularization

- First 3 experiments:
  1. Train with only segmentation head (ablation): Verify MTL benefit
  2. Replace GPSA with standard multi-head attention: Test data efficiency claim
  3. Use single quantile (mean only) vs three quantiles: Validate uncertainty modeling benefit

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- Question: How would the model's performance change if 3D inputs and 3D segmentation heads were used instead of 2D slices?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The authors acknowledge this as a potential limitation, stating "Only one experienced scan reader was used to annotate the images" and discuss the 2D nature of the current model. They suggest future work could extend the method to 3D.
- Why unresolved: The paper only evaluates a 2D model despite acknowledging potential benefits of 3D processing for capturing volumetric relationships.
- What evidence would resolve it: A direct comparison of the 2D model against a 3D version using the same dataset and evaluation metrics.

### Open Question 2
- Question: How robust is the model to variations in CT scanner manufacturers and protocols?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The authors note that all CT images came from Siemens scanners and reference studies showing significant variation between manufacturers.
- Why unresolved: The dataset used was homogeneous in terms of scanner manufacturer, limiting generalizability claims.
- What evidence would resolve it: Testing the model on datasets from multiple scanner manufacturers and with different acquisition protocols, comparing performance across these variations.

### Open Question 3
- Question: What is the optimal number of quantiles to predict for lesion age estimation, and how does this choice affect uncertainty quantification?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The authors chose to predict three quantiles assuming normal distribution, but note this is a design choice that could be explored further.
- Why unresolved: The paper does not explore alternative numbers of quantiles or different distributional assumptions.
- What evidence would resolve it: Systematic comparison of models predicting different numbers of quantiles (e.g., 2, 3, 5) on the same dataset, evaluating both estimation accuracy and quality of uncertainty quantification.

## Limitations

- Moderate sample size (776 patients) may limit generalizability to broader clinical populations
- Segmentation performance (Dice of 38.2%) indicates room for improvement compared to established benchmarks
- Dataset homogeneity (all Siemens scanners) limits assessment of model robustness to scanner variations

## Confidence

- Multi-task learning benefits: Medium confidence - ablation studies show improvement but could be strengthened
- Transformer architecture claims: High confidence - clear specifications and consistent performance gains
- Uncertainty quantification approach: Medium confidence - split normal assumption may not fully capture true lesion age distribution
