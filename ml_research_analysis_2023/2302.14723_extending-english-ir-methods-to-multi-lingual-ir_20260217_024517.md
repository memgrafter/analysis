# Extending English IR methods to multi-lingual IR

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2302.14723
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2302.14723
- Reference count: 18
- Primary result: Won WSDM CUP 2023 MIRACL challenge with hybrid multilingual IR approach achieving 80.4 nDCG@10 on known languages and 84.6 on surprise languages

## Executive Summary
This paper presents the winning submission to the WSDM CUP 2023 MIRACL challenge, which required multilingual information retrieval across 16 languages. The core innovation was extending English IR methods to multilingual settings through a hybrid approach combining document translation, multilingual sparse and dense retrievers, and rerankers. The team developed the first SPLADE model capable of working in over 10 languages and the first multilingual RankT5 model. Their approach achieved first place in both known and surprise languages tracks, demonstrating that translation-based methods combined with multilingual models can effectively handle diverse linguistic challenges.

## Method Summary
The approach combined document translation to English using NLLB-200 with multilingual SPLADE and Contriever models, plus multilingual RankT5 rerankers. The strategy involved creating diverse first-stage runs using BM25, translated SPLADE, multilingual SPLADE variants, mContriever, and mColBERT, then applying multiple reranking techniques. Key innovations included training multilingual SPLADE from scratch with a custom 120k-token tokenizer based on MIRACL statistics, and developing multilingual RankT5 models. The hybrid ensemble approach achieved state-of-the-art performance across both known and surprise language sets.

## Key Results
- Achieved first place in WSDM CUP 2023 MIRACL challenge
- 80.4 nDCG@10 on development set and 73.3 on test set for known languages
- 84.6 average nDCG@10 on development set and 85.8 on test set for surprise languages
- Ensemble of 4 rerankers improved hybrid performance from 80.4 to 81.0 nDCG@10

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: Translating non-English corpora to English allows leveraging highly optimized English IR models without architectural changes
- Mechanism: NLLB-200 translates 16 languages into English, then existing English SPLADE, Contriever, and RankT5 models are applied directly, avoiding the need to train multilingual versions from scratch
- Core assumption: Translation quality is high enough that downstream IR performance in English approximates what would be achieved in the original language
- Evidence anchors: [abstract] "document translation to English using NLLB-200"; [section 2.2] "The next step was to translate the corpora into English. This is the simplest way of working in any language, as English translators are pretty strong"
- Break condition: If translation errors accumulate in ways that IR models misinterpret, or if domain-specific terminology is lost, performance would degrade sharply

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: Training multilingual SPLADE models from scratch on MIRACL outperforms translated-English SPLADE and monolingual models
- Mechanism: Tokenizer built from 120k tokens extracted from MIRACL statistics; MLM+FLOPS pretraining on 15 non-English corpora; fine-tuning on mMARCO then MIRACL
- Core assumption: Joint multilingual vocabulary captures cross-lingual semantics better than disjoint monolingual tokenizers, and enough pretraining data exists to learn robust representations
- Evidence anchors: [section 2.4] "We went back and recreated a tokenizer from scratch with just 120k tokens, but based on the statistics of the 15 non-english MIRACL datasets"; [section 2.4] "mSPLADE-sTok is not only faster to run, but it is also much more stable and effective compared to mSPLADE-small and mSPLADE-large"
- Break condition: If tokenizer size is too small to cover language-specific morphology, or if pretraining epochs are insufficient, cross-lingual effectiveness collapses

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: Combining diverse first-stage models with late reranking yields multiplicative gains in nDCG@10
- Mechanism: Ensemble of BM25, translated SPLADE, multilingual SPLADE variants, mContriever, and mColBERT forms a high-recall initial ranking; then multiple RankT5-based rerankers refine top-K results
- Core assumption: First-stage models capture orthogonal signal strengths; rerankers can learn to discriminate between close-scoring documents that the initial ranker cannot separate
- Evidence anchors: [abstract] "Key innovations included training the first SPLADE model capable of working in over 10 languages, and training the first multilingual RankT5"; [section 3.2] "none of our rerankers outperformed the first stage by more than 1.5 nDCG@10 and that adding these 4 rerankers improves our hybrid from 80.4 to 81.0"
- Break condition: If reranking introduces noise or overfitting to dev set, gains vanish on test data; if initial recall is already near-perfect, further improvement is negligible

## Foundational Learning

- Concept: Dense vs. sparse retrieval tradeoffs
  - Why needed here: mContriever and mColBERT (dense) complement BM25 and SPLADE (sparse); understanding their recall/efficiency profiles guides hybrid design
  - Quick check question: What is the recall@100 difference between mContriever and BM25 in the dev set, and what does that imply about complementarities?

- Concept: Cross-lingual representation learning
  - Why needed here: Training multilingual SPLADE requires mapping tokens from multiple languages into a shared semantic space; failure modes include token ambiguity and vocabulary coverage gaps
  - Quick check question: How does the 120k-token tokenizer size compare to XLM-R's 250k+ tokens, and why might smaller be better in this context?

- Concept: Reranker negative sampling strategies
  - Why needed here: RankT5 models are trained on negatives from the best model's top-1k; understanding this hardness schedule is critical to reproduce effectiveness
  - Quick check question: If you change the negative source from top-1k to random, how would you expect nDCG@10 to change and why?

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map: NLLB-200 translation → English SPLADE (fine-tuned) → Multilingual SPLADE (sTok) → mContriever/mColBERT → BM25 baseline → RankT5 rerankers (mono, mT0, BloomZ variants) → Ranx ensemble
- Critical path: Corpus translation → first-stage retrieval → reranking → final ensemble scoring
- Design tradeoffs: Translation quality vs. compute cost (large NLLB for small corpora, distilled for large); tokenizer size vs. memory use (120k vs 240k tokens); number of rerankers vs. diminishing returns
- Failure signatures: Recall@20 plateauing early despite adding more models indicates saturation; reranking gains < 1 nDCG@10 suggest overfitting or poor negative sampling
- First 3 experiments:
  1. Translate a small corpus (e.g., Swedish) to English, run BM25 + T-SPLADE, compare recall@100 to monolingual baseline
  2. Train mSPLADE-sTok on 5 languages only, evaluate nDCG@10 vs translated SPLADE on dev set
  3. Fine-tune RankT5 on top-1k negatives from mContriever, measure improvement over first-stage hybrid

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- Question: Does the use of multilingual SPLADE models provide significant improvements over translated SPLADE models for non-English languages in MIRACL?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper notes that monolingual SPLADE did not generalize well to MIRACL, and while multilingual SPLADE showed improvement, the authors were not satisfied and further developed a tokenizer from scratch
- Why unresolved: The paper indicates that multilingual SPLADE models were not fully optimized and could potentially be improved with better hyperparameter tuning and longer pretraining
- What evidence would resolve it: A more comprehensive study comparing multilingual SPLADE models with different tokenizers and pretraining strategies against translated SPLADE models on the MIRACL dataset

### Open Question 2
- Question: How much does the effectiveness of retrieval models in MIRACL depend on the quality and comprehensiveness of the dataset's labeling?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper discusses the potential issue of false negatives in the MIRACL dataset due to the labeling process, which might not fully capture the effectiveness of the retrieval models
- Why unresolved: The analysis in the paper is preliminary and focuses on a limited set of queries, suggesting that a more thorough investigation is needed to understand the impact of labeling on model performance
- What evidence would resolve it: A detailed analysis of the labeling quality across all queries in MIRACL, including a comparison of the documents retrieved by different models against the labeled data to identify potential false negatives

### Open Question 3
- Question: What is the impact of using rerankers in the MIRACL dataset, and how can their training be optimized for better performance?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper notes that rerankers did not significantly improve the results, and the authors suggest that better training methods for rerankers might be needed
- Why unresolved: The paper indicates that the rerankers used were not fully optimized, and there is a lack of understanding of how to effectively train rerankers for the MIRACL dataset
- What evidence would resolve it: Experiments with different reranking models and training strategies, including the use of more diverse and challenging negative samples, to determine the optimal approach for reranking in MIRACL

## Limitations

- The 128-token truncation limit for translated queries could cause significant information loss for longer queries in morphologically rich languages
- The 120k-token multilingual SPLADE tokenizer is notably smaller than XLM-R's 250k+ tokens, raising questions about coverage for languages with large vocabularies
- The unusually high 85.8 nDCG@10 on surprise languages compared to 73.3 on known languages seems counterintuitive and suggests potential evaluation artifacts

## Confidence

**High Confidence:** The hybrid approach of combining translated English models with multilingual models, the use of NLLB-200 for translation, and the general methodology of ensemble reranking are well-documented and reproducible.

**Medium Confidence:** The effectiveness of the 120k-token multilingual SPLADE tokenizer and the specific training procedures for multilingual models are partially described but lack critical implementation details.

**Low Confidence:** The unusually high performance on surprise languages, the claimed effectiveness of mSPLADE-sTok versus mSPLADE-large, and the precise negative sampling strategy for RankT5 training are not sufficiently explained or validated.

## Next Checks

1. **Translation Quality Impact:** Measure the impact of NLLB-200's 128-token truncation on query effectiveness by comparing nDCG@10 when using full-length vs. truncated queries for languages with known long query patterns (e.g., German compound words).

2. **Tokenizer Coverage Analysis:** Compare the coverage and OOV rates of the 120k-token mSPLADE tokenizer against XLM-R's tokenizer on the MIRACL development set, and measure the correlation between OOV rates and performance degradation across languages.

3. **Negative Sampling Ablation:** Re-train the RankT5 rerankers using different negative sampling strategies (random vs. top-1k vs. hybrid) and measure the variance in nDCG@10 improvement across the ensemble to quantify the contribution of the proposed hardness schedule.
