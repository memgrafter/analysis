# Heavy-Tailed Regularization of Weight Matrices in Deep Neural Networks

## Executive Summary
This paper proposes heavy-tailed regularization for deep neural networks, motivated by the observation that heavy-tailed spectra of weight matrices correlate with better generalization. The authors introduce four regularization methods: Weighted Alpha and Stable Rank penalties, and Bayesian priors using Power-law and Fréchet distributions. These methods encourage heavier tails in the weight matrices' spectra. Experiments on KMNIST and CIFAR10 datasets with FC3, LeNet5, and ResNet18 architectures show that heavy-tailed regularization outperforms vanilla training, weight decay, and spectral norm regularization in terms of test accuracy. Specifically, Stable Rank with appropriate parameters achieved the best performance across different models and datasets.

## Method Summary
The paper introduces four heavy-tailed regularization methods to improve generalization in deep neural networks. These include Weighted Alpha and Stable Rank penalties, and Bayesian priors using Power-law and Fréchet distributions. The methods are designed to encourage heavier tails in the weight matrices' spectra. Models are trained using mini-batch SGD with learning rate decay, comparing against baseline methods like vanilla training, weight decay, and spectral norm regularization. The approach is tested on FC3, LeNet5, and ResNet18 architectures with KMNIST and CIFAR10 datasets.

## Key Results
- Heavy-tailed regularization methods outperform vanilla training, weight decay, and spectral norm regularization on KMNIST and CIFAR10 datasets
- Stable Rank regularization with appropriate parameters achieved the best performance across different models and datasets
- Weighted Alpha regularization with decay or lower threshold variants showed improved results compared to its basic form

## Limitations

- Limited empirical scope: Experiments are restricted to two datasets (KMNIST, CIFAR10) and three architectures (FC3, LeNet5, ResNet18).
- Statistical validation concerns: The paper lacks statistical significance testing between different regularization methods.
- Hyperparameter sensitivity: The paper reports optimal hyperparameter choices but doesn't thoroughly explore the sensitivity of results to these choices.
