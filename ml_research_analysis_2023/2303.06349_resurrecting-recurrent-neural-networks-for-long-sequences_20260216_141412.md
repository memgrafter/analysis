# Resurrecting Recurrent Neural Networks for Long Sequences

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2303.06349
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.06349
- Reference count: 40
- One-line primary result: Linear RNNs with careful initialization can match deep SSM performance on long-range tasks

## Executive Summary
This paper investigates whether deep state-space models' (SSMs) impressive performance on long-range reasoning tasks can be matched by carefully designed recurrent neural networks (RNNs). Through systematic analysis of RNN components, the authors identify that linear recurrences interleaved with position-wise nonlinearities, combined with proper initialization and normalization, can achieve performance matching deep SSMs like S4/S4D/S5 on all Long Range Arena (LRA) tasks. The resulting Linear Recurrent Unit (LRU) demonstrates that simple RNN architectures, when properly parameterized, can effectively model long-range dependencies without the complexity of deep SSMs.

## Method Summary
The method involves modifying standard RNNs through three key changes: linearizing the recurrence (removing nonlinearities), using complex diagonal recurrence matrices for computational efficiency, and implementing stable exponential parameterization with normalization. The architecture consists of a 6-layer deep network with residual connections, where each layer contains a Linear Recurrent Unit followed by a GLU/MLP mixing layer and skip connection. The LRU uses complex-valued diagonal matrices parameterized in exponential form to ensure stability, with eigenvalues initialized uniformly on a ring in the complex plane and normalized to prevent activation blow-up during forward passes.

## Key Results
- LRU matches S4/S4D/S5 performance across all six LRA tasks (sCIFAR, ListOps, Text, Retrieval, PathFinder, PathX)
- Linear recurrence with position-wise nonlinearities outperforms standard RNNs with nonlinear recurrences
- Complex diagonal parameterization enables parallel training while preserving expressive power
- Stable exponential initialization with normalization enables learning long-range dependencies

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- **Claim:** Removing nonlinearities from the recurrence improves performance when interleaved with MLP blocks.
- **Mechanism:** Position-wise nonlinearities introduce spectral leakage, transferring signal information to higher frequencies, enabling complex nonlinear dynamics without recurrence nonlinearities.
- **Core assumption:** Deep architecture with alternating linear RNN layers and nonlinear MLP/GLU blocks can approximate highly nonlinear sequence-to-sequence maps.
- **Evidence anchors:** Abstract mentions "substantial boost in test accuracy" from removing recurrence nonlinearities; section confirms improved accuracy on most LRA tasks.
- **Break condition:** Position-wise layers cannot effectively introduce spectral leakage or depth is insufficient.

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim:** Complex diagonal recurrent matrices enable efficient training and inference without losing expressivity.
- **Mechanism:** Diagonalization allows parallel scans during training and element-wise exponentiation during inference while preserving eigenvalue spectrum.
- **Core assumption:** Eigenvalue spectrum is the critical factor for performance, not specific parameterization.
- **Evidence anchors:** Abstract mentions diagonalization for speed without performance loss; section confirms training speedup for deep linear RNNs.
- **Break condition:** Eigenvalue spectrum cannot be preserved during diagonalization or parallelization benefits are negated.

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim:** Stable exponential parameterization with normalization enables learning long-range dependencies.
- **Mechanism:** Exponential parameterization decouples magnitude and phase for easier optimization; γ normalization prevents forward-pass blow-up with eigenvalues near unit circle.
- **Core assumption:** Proper initialization distribution and normalization are more critical than specific HiPPO theory for long-range performance.
- **Evidence anchors:** Abstract mentions "better parameterizations and initializations" improving long-range modeling; section confirms stable parameterization improves performance.
- **Break condition:** Optimization landscape becomes too flat or normalization cannot prevent numerical instability.

## Architecture Onboarding

- **Component map:** Input encoder → Linear Recurrent Unit (LRU) → GLU/MLP mixing layer → Skip connection → Normalization → Output projection
- **Critical path:** Forward pass through sequence with parallel scan, followed by position-wise GLU activation and skip connection
- **Design tradeoffs:**
  - Linear vs nonlinear recurrence: Linear recurrences with position-wise nonlinearities provide better performance but require careful initialization
  - Dense vs diagonal matrices: Diagonal matrices enable parallelization but require eigenvalue preservation during diagonalization
  - Magnitude vs phase initialization: Near-unit-circle initialization helps long-range dependencies but requires normalization to prevent blow-up
- **Failure signatures:**
  - Vanishing gradients: Eigenvalues too far from unit circle
  - Exploding activations: Eigenvalues too close to unit circle without normalization
  - Poor optimization: Non-axis-aligned loss landscape due to standard parameterization
- **First 3 experiments:**
  1. Implement linear recurrence (remove nonlinearity) and verify performance improvement on ListOps
  2. Diagonalize the recurrence matrix and measure training speedup on sCIFAR
  3. Add stable exponential parameterization and γ normalization, test on PathFinder task

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

- **Open Question 1:** How does the choice of normalization strategy (e.g., batch normalization vs. layer normalization) affect the performance and stability of the Linear Recurrent Unit on long-range reasoning tasks?
- **Open Question 2:** What is the impact of using a smaller phase initialization range for the eigenvalues on tasks other than PathX, such as sCIFAR or ListOps?
- **Open Question 3:** How does the performance of the Linear Recurrent Unit scale with increasing sequence lengths beyond those used in the Long Range Arena benchmark?
- **Open Question 4:** What is the effect of using different activation functions in the mixing layer (e.g., GLU vs. ReLU) on the performance of the Linear Recurrent Unit?

## Limitations

- Experimental validation limited to LRA benchmark, may not capture all aspects of long-range sequence modeling
- Hyperparameter tuning details sparse, making reproducibility challenging without extensive optimization
- Weak direct evidence for proposed mechanisms, with most supporting references being general works on SSMs and RNNs

## Confidence

**High confidence:** Core claim that linear recurrences interleaved with position-wise nonlinearities can match deep SSM performance on LRA tasks.

**Medium confidence:** Mechanism explaining why removing nonlinearities from recurrence improves performance through spectral leakage.

**Low confidence:** Claim that diagonalization preserves the critical eigenvalue spectrum for performance.

## Next Checks
