# Comparative study on Judgment Text Classification for Transformer Based Models

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2306.01739
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.01739
- Reference count: 0
- Primary result: XLNet with GELU activation achieves highest accuracy of 85% for legal judgment classification

## Executive Summary
This study evaluates six transformer-based models (BERT, ALBERT, RoBERTa, XLNet, ELECTRA, FNet) for predicting winners in legal judgments using text classification. The models are trained on 200 judgment documents with four activation functions. XLNet with GELU activation achieves the highest accuracy of 85%, while FNet performs the worst. All models show confidence levels up to 99% in predictions. Validation loss increases beyond a certain threshold due to limited training data. The results demonstrate the effectiveness of transformer models in legal text classification, with XLNet-GELU emerging as the best performer. Future work includes expanding the dataset and developing a searchable web interface for legal document retrieval.

## Method Summary
The study trains six transformer models on 200 judgment documents from Indian Kanoon, using four activation functions (ReLU, GELU, SiLU, GELU-New) per model. Models are trained for 100 epochs using Adam optimizer with attention dropout (0.3 for most models, 0.1 for SiLU). Text is tokenized with a 512 token limit and split 80/20 for training/validation. The objective is to classify judgments as petitioner or respondent wins based on the verdict statement. Performance is evaluated using accuracy, training loss, and validation loss metrics.

## Key Results
- XLNet with GELU activation achieves highest accuracy of 85%
- FNet performs worst among the six transformer models tested
- Validation loss increases beyond threshold due to limited training data
- All models show confidence levels up to 99% in predictions
