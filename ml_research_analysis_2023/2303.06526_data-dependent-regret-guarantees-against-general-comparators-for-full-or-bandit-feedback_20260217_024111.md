# Data Dependent Regret Guarantees Against General Comparators for Full or Bandit Feedback

## Executive Summary
This paper introduces a novel algorithmic framework for adversarial online learning that achieves data-dependent regret guarantees against arbitrary comparator sequences. The key innovation is the use of equivalence classes to implicitly represent and update weights for all possible expert selection sequences, allowing the algorithm to compete effectively against complex comparator classes without prior knowledge of loss sequences. The framework provides regret bounds that are scale-invariant, translation-invariant, and data-dependent, with performance characterized by competition complexity W. The approach works for both full expert feedback and bandit feedback settings, achieving O(sqrt(WT)) and O(sqrt(MWT)) regret bounds respectively.

## Method Summary
The paper proposes a new algorithmic framework based on equivalence classes that implicitly maintains weights for all possible expert selection sequences. By grouping equivalent sequences together, the algorithm can efficiently update and compete against complex comparator classes. The method uses a dynamic weight updating scheme that adapts to the observed losses while maintaining theoretical guarantees. The framework is designed to work in both full feedback and bandit feedback settings, with regret bounds that depend on the competition complexity W rather than the number of experts or time horizon directly.

## Key Results
- Achieves O(sqrt(WT)) regret in full feedback setting, where W is competition complexity
- Achieves O(sqrt(MWT)) regret in bandit feedback setting with M experts
- Regret bounds are data-dependent, scale-invariant, and translation-invariant
- Framework works against arbitrary comparator sequences without prior knowledge of loss sequences

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The algorithm works by implicitly representing and updating weights for all possible expert selection sequences through equivalence classes. This allows it to maintain low regret against complex comparator classes by efficiently grouping together sequences that have similar performance characteristics. The equivalence class structure enables the algorithm to adapt to the observed data while maintaining theoretical guarantees, as it can focus computational resources on the most relevant sequences rather than tracking all possible combinations explicitly.

### Component Map

### Critical Path
The critical path involves forming equivalence classes from observed expert sequences, updating weights within these classes based on observed losses, and using these weights to make predictions while maintaining low regret guarantees.

### Design Tradeoffs
- Computational complexity vs. regret performance: Maintaining all equivalence classes vs. approximation methods
- Memory requirements vs. adaptivity: Explicit storage vs. implicit representation
- Theoretical guarantees vs. practical implementation: Tight bounds vs. computational tractability

### Failure Signatures
- Poor performance when competition complexity W is very large relative to T
- Computational intractability with large numbers of experts or time steps
- Suboptimal regret when equivalence classes are poorly formed or maintained
