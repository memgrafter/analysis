# GOHSP: A Unified Framework of Graph and Optimization-based Heterogeneous Structured Pruning for Vision Transformer

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2301.05345
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2301.05345
- Reference count: 9
- Achieves 40% parameter reduction on CIFAR-10 and 30-50% sparsity on ImageNet with improved accuracy and runtime efficiency

## Executive Summary
GOHSP introduces a unified framework that combines graph-based ranking with optimization-based heterogeneous structured pruning for Vision Transformers. The method first identifies important attention heads using a Markov chain-based ranking algorithm, then employs a soft-pruning approach integrated into optimization to achieve structured sparsity patterns. This approach achieves significant compression performance while maintaining or improving model accuracy compared to existing structured pruning methods.

## Method Summary
The GOHSP framework operates in two main stages: graph-based head ranking and optimization-based pruning. First, it computes inter-head similarity matrices from training data and constructs a Markov chain where the stationary distribution determines attention head importance scores. These scores guide the creation of a soft-pruning mask that is incorporated into an optimization problem using Douglas-Rachford splitting. The method enforces both head-level and column-level sparsity through group L0-norm constraints while maintaining model accuracy through careful optimization of the remaining parameters.

## Key Results
- Achieves 40% parameter reduction on CIFAR-10 with maintained or improved accuracy
- Reaches 30-50% sparsity on ImageNet while outperforming existing structured pruning methods
- Demonstrates runtime efficiency improvements through heterogeneous structured sparsity patterns

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: Graph-based head ranking uses inter-head correlation modeled as a Markov chain to determine attention head importance
- Mechanism: The method constructs a graph where nodes represent attention heads and edge weights represent cosine similarity between head outputs. By treating this as a Markov chain and computing the stationary distribution, each head receives an importance score based on how likely a random walker stays in that state
- Core assumption: The stationary distribution of a Markov chain built on inter-head similarity accurately reflects head importance for model performance
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "We first develop a graph-based ranking for measuring the importance of attention heads"
  - [section] "Once the importance score for each state is obtained via calculating the stationary distribution, the corresponding attention heads can be ranked"
  - [corpus] Weak evidence - no corpus papers directly discuss Markov chain-based attention head ranking
- Break condition: If head similarity doesn't correlate with functional importance, or if the Markov chain assumptions (irreducibility, aperiodicity) don't hold

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: Soft-pruning mask integrates importance scores into optimization to guide heterogeneous structured sparsity
- Mechanism: Instead of directly removing low-importance heads, the method creates a binary mask that is incorporated into the optimization objective as a quadratic penalty term. This allows the model to gradually adjust weights while respecting the desired sparsity patterns
- Core assumption: Using importance scores as soft guidance during optimization is more effective than immediate hard pruning
- Evidence anchors:
  - [section] "Such binary mask serves as the guideline for the next-stage optimization, and it is essentially integrated into Eq. 1 as follows"
  - [section] "the ranking-only 'soft' pruning strategy...can provide more flexibility and possibility for the next-stage optimization procedure to identify better structured sparse models"
  - [corpus] Weak evidence - limited corpus discussion of soft-pruning masks in ViT contexts
- Break condition: If the soft-pruning mask doesn't effectively guide the optimization, or if the penalty term causes optimization instability

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: Douglas-Rachford splitting method solves the multi-constraint structured pruning problem by decomposing it into simpler subproblems
- Mechanism: The method introduces auxiliary variables and indicator functions to transform the original problem into an equivalent form, then uses Douglas-Rachford splitting to iteratively update primal and dual variables while maintaining sparsity constraints
- Core assumption: The complex structured pruning problem can be effectively decomposed and solved through alternating updates of different variable sets
- Evidence anchors:
  - [section] "we adopt 'soft-pruning' strategy via optimizing the entire ViT models towards the desired structured sparse formats"
  - [section] "Douglas—Rachford splitting method...can be a suitable optimization solution for such types of problem"
  - [corpus] Weak evidence - limited corpus discussion of Douglas-Rachford splitting for ViT pruning specifically
- Break condition: If the decomposition introduces significant approximation error, or if convergence is too slow for practical use

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map:
  Graph-based ranking module -> Soft-pruning mask generator -> Optimization engine -> Fine-tuning module

- Critical path:
  1. Compute inter-head similarity matrix from training data
  2. Build Markov chain and calculate stationary distribution
  3. Generate soft-pruning mask based on importance scores
  4. Run optimization with Douglas-Rachford splitting
  5. Apply hard pruning and fine-tune

- Design tradeoffs:
  - Soft vs hard pruning: Soft pruning provides more flexibility but requires more complex optimization
  - Batch size for ranking: Larger batches provide better estimates but increase computation
  - Penalty parameter ρ: Higher values speed up sparsity but may hurt accuracy

- Failure signatures:
  - Convergence issues in optimization (oscillating or diverging losses)
  - Poor ranking results (inconsistent importance scores across different batch sizes)
  - Accuracy degradation after pruning (indicating loss of critical heads/columns)

- First 3 experiments:
  1. Verify head ranking consistency: Run ranking with different batch sizes and compare importance score rankings
  2. Test soft vs hard pruning: Implement both approaches and compare accuracy at same sparsity levels
  3. Sensitivity analysis: Vary penalty parameter ρ and observe trade-off between convergence speed and final accuracy

## Limitations
- The graph-based ranking mechanism relies heavily on theoretical assumptions with limited empirical validation in the ViT domain
- The soft-pruning approach introduces additional hyperparameters whose sensitivity and optimal values across different architectures remain unclear
- The evaluation is limited to specific ViT variants and benchmark datasets, requiring further validation for generalization

## Confidence
**Medium Confidence** - The core mechanism of using graph-based ranking for attention head importance is conceptually sound but lacks extensive empirical validation specific to ViT architectures.

**Medium Confidence** - The soft-pruning optimization approach is theoretically justified, but its practical advantages over traditional hard pruning methods need more comprehensive comparison.

**Medium Confidence** - The reported compression and accuracy improvements are significant, but generalization to other transformer architectures and real-world applications remains to be demonstrated.
