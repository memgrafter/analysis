# Is ChatGPT better than Human Annotators? Potential and Limitations of ChatGPT in Explaining Implicit Hate Speech

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2302.07736
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2302.07736
- Reference count: 22
- Primary result: ChatGPT correctly identifies 80% of implicit hateful tweets with explanations rated clearer than human-generated ones

## Executive Summary
This study evaluates ChatGPT's capability to detect and explain implicit hate speech compared to human annotators. Using the LatentHatred dataset of 795 tweets, the authors find that ChatGPT achieves 80% accuracy in identifying implicit hate speech while generating explanations that human evaluators perceive as clearer than those produced by human annotators. The research reveals a concerning pattern where ChatGPT's judgments align more closely with lay evaluators than human annotators in cases of disagreement, suggesting potential reinforcement of incorrect perceptions despite overall strong performance.

## Method Summary
The authors collected 795 tweets from the LatentHatred dataset and used ChatGPT (January 9, 2023 version) to classify each tweet as hateful or not hateful while generating one-sentence explanations (NLEs). Each tweet received three responses from ChatGPT. The study then conducted Amazon Mechanical Turk user studies with experienced workers to evaluate ChatGPT's classification accuracy and the quality of its generated NLEs compared to human-generated NLEs. Quality was assessed through Clarity and Informativeness scores, while classification accuracy was measured against human annotations.

## Key Results
- ChatGPT correctly identifies 80% of implicit hateful tweets
- ChatGPT-generated NLEs are perceived as clearer than human-generated NLEs
- In 20% of cases where ChatGPT disagrees with human annotations, lay evaluators align with ChatGPT's judgment

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
ChatGPT's transformer-based architecture enables it to capture nuanced contextual patterns in language that distinguish implicit hate speech from benign content. The model's ability to generate coherent explanations stems from its pretraining on diverse web text, allowing it to articulate reasoning patterns. However, the alignment between ChatGPT's judgments and lay evaluator perceptions in disagreement cases suggests the model may be reinforcing common but potentially incorrect interpretations of implicit hate speech.

## Foundational Learning

## Limitations
- Results limited to English language and specific dataset, reducing generalizability
- No official ChatGPT API available at time of study, creating potential reproducibility issues
- Human evaluators were experienced MTurk workers, which may not represent broader population perspectives

## Confidence
High for classification accuracy and explanation quality metrics based on established dataset and controlled user studies
Medium for comparative human evaluator alignment results due to specific demographic of experienced MTurk workers
Low for broader implications about ChatGPT's role in hate speech detection given limited scope and lack of cross-validation

## Next Checks
1. Test ChatGPT's performance on explicit hate speech and mixed implicit/explicit datasets to establish relative strengths and weaknesses
2. Conduct the same evaluation with a more diverse set of human annotators and evaluators across different cultural contexts
3. Compare ChatGPT's performance against specialized hate speech detection models trained specifically on implicit hate speech to quantify its advantages and limitations
