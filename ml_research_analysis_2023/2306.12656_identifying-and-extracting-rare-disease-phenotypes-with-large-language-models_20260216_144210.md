# Identifying and Extracting Rare Disease Phenotypes with Large Language Models

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2306.12656
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.12656
- Reference count: 14
- Primary result: Fine-tuning BioClinicalBERT achieved F1-score of 0.689, outperforming ChatGPT's zero-shot (F1 0.472) and few-shot (F1 0.591) performance on rare disease NER

## Executive Summary
This paper addresses the challenge of extracting rare disease phenotypes from unstructured text using natural language processing models. The authors compare traditional fine-tuning of BioClinicalBERT with prompt learning using ChatGPT in zero- and few-shot settings. They evaluate performance on a high-quality annotated corpus of rare disease texts, finding that while fine-tuning generally performs better, ChatGPT achieves similar or higher accuracy for certain entity types (rare diseases and signs) in few-shot settings with minimal labeled data.

## Method Summary
The study compares two approaches for named entity recognition (NER) in rare disease texts: fine-tuning BioClinicalBERT on a training set (80% of RareDis corpus) and prompt learning with ChatGPT using zero-shot and few-shot settings. The RareDis corpus contains 832 annotated texts with 4,065 rare diseases, 1,814 diseases, 316 symptoms, and 3,317 signs. Performance is evaluated using precision, recall, and F1-score under both exact and relaxed matching settings on a test set (10% of data).

## Key Results
- Fine-tuning BioClinicalBERT achieved F1-score of 0.689, outperforming ChatGPT's zero-shot (0.472) and few-shot (0.591) performance
- ChatGPT achieved similar or higher accuracy than fine-tuned models for rare diseases and signs in the one-shot setting
- Simple sentence-based prompts performed similarly or better than structured list prompts while requiring fewer tokens

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: Zero-shot and few-shot prompt learning reduces annotation burden for rare disease NER
- Mechanism: ChatGPT can extract entities directly from text without any labeled examples (zero-shot) or with just one or few examples (few-shot), bypassing the need for large annotated corpora
- Core assumption: The model's pre-training on vast text data enables it to generalize to new tasks with minimal supervision
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "This capability, which provides opportunities to significantly reduce the manual burden of annotation without sacrificing model performance, is especially attractive for NER in the context of rare diseases."
  - [section] "Overall, while fine-tuning a pre-trained biomedical language model led to better performance, prompt learning with ChatGPT achieved similar or higher accuracy for some entities (i.e., rare diseases and signs) with a single example, demonstrating its potential for out-of-the-box NER in the few-shot setting."
- Break condition: If the model lacks relevant pre-training data or if the task requires highly specialized knowledge not captured in its training corpus, performance will degrade

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: Entity type overlap in rare disease texts complicates NER and affects model performance
- Mechanism: Rare diseases, diseases, signs, and symptoms have overlapping semantics, making it difficult for models to distinguish between them. ChatGPT's performance varies depending on how well the prompt differentiates these entities
- Core assumption: The model can leverage subtle differences in prompt definitions to improve entity classification accuracy
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "Unlike corpora with distinct entity types, e.g., {person, location, organization } or {problem, test, treatment}, RareDis consists of entities with considerable semantic overlap."
  - [section] "Because the only difference between the prompts for these entities was the task guidance, i.e., specifying symptoms as problems that cannot be measured, whereas signs can be measured, this finding suggests that ChatGPT is sensitive to even small variations in the prompt."
- Break condition: If prompt definitions are not precise or if the model cannot resolve semantic ambiguities, entity classification errors will increase

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: Prompt format affects ChatGPT's NER performance and computational cost
- Mechanism: Simple sentence-based prompts perform similarly or better than structured list prompts and require fewer tokens, reducing computational cost
- Core assumption: ChatGPT is more receptive to conversational, less structured prompts
- Evidence anchors:
  - [section] "Compared to prompts written as a structured list, simple sentences generally achieved similar or better performance, suggesting that ChatGPT may be more receptive to conversational prompts. Moreover, simple sentences required fewer tokens and were preferred over structured lists from a cost perspective."
- Break condition: If the task complexity increases beyond ChatGPT's ability to parse simple prompts, structured formats may become necessary despite higher costs

## Limitations
- Small and potentially biased corpus (832 texts from single source) limits generalizability
- Semantic overlap between entity types presents inherent challenges not fully resolved by prompt engineering
- Results limited to English texts and one rare disease dataset

## Confidence
- Medium: The comparison between fine-tuned BioClinicalBERT and ChatGPT is supported by the study, but generalizability to other datasets and entity types requires validation
