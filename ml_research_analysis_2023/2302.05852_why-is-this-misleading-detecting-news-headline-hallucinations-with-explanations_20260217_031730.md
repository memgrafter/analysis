# "Why is this misleading?": Detecting News Headline Hallucinations with Explanations

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2302.05852
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2302.05852
- Reference count: 40
- Key outcome: ExHalder framework achieves 84.46% accuracy and 67.16% recall on news headline hallucination detection with explanation generation capabilities

## Executive Summary
This paper introduces ExHalder, a novel framework for detecting hallucinations in automatically generated news headlines. The system addresses a critical challenge in automated news generation where headlines may misrepresent or be unsupported by their underlying articles. ExHalder leverages knowledge transfer from natural language inference datasets and incorporates explanation generation to improve detection accuracy, particularly in scenarios with limited training data. The framework demonstrates state-of-the-art performance across seven datasets while providing human-readable justifications for its predictions.

## Method Summary
ExHalder employs a knowledge transfer approach from natural language inference (NLI) datasets to detect news headline hallucinations. The framework uses a BERT-based architecture fine-tuned on NLI data, then adapts to the hallucination detection task through additional training on news headline-article pairs. A key innovation is the integration of explanation generation capabilities, where the model produces natural language justifications for its predictions. The system operates by encoding both headlines and articles, computing semantic similarity, and determining whether the headline is supported by the article content. The explanation module generates rationales by identifying key evidence spans and constructing coherent textual explanations.

## Key Results
- Achieves 84.46% accuracy and 67.16% recall on the NewsHeadEx dataset for hallucination detection
- Demonstrates state-of-the-art performance across seven benchmark datasets
- Shows significant improvement in low-resource settings compared to baseline approaches
- Generates high-quality human-readable explanations that align with model predictions

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The framework's effectiveness stems from its dual approach: leveraging pre-trained language understanding from NLI tasks and incorporating explanation generation. By transferring knowledge from NLI datasets, ExHalder benefits from pre-learned semantic relationships and inference patterns that are transferable to the hallucination detection task. The explanation generation component serves a dual purpose - it provides interpretability while also reinforcing the model's understanding of what constitutes hallucinated content through explicit reasoning about evidence.

## Foundational Learning
- **Natural Language Inference**: Understanding semantic relationships between text pairs (premise-hypothesis) - needed for knowledge transfer from established NLI datasets; quick check: ability to classify entailment, contradiction, and neutral relationships
- **Semantic Similarity Metrics**: Computing semantic alignment between headlines and articles - needed for determining content consistency; quick check: cosine similarity between contextualized embeddings
- **Explanation Generation**: Producing human-readable justifications for predictions - needed for interpretability and model transparency; quick check: coherence and relevance of generated explanations
- **Fine-tuning Strategies**: Adapting pre-trained models to specialized tasks - needed for efficient use of limited training data; quick check: performance improvement over baseline models
- **Low-resource Learning**: Effective model performance with limited training examples - needed for practical deployment scenarios; quick check: performance degradation as training data decreases

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component Map**: BERT Encoder -> Semantic Similarity Module -> Classification Head -> Explanation Generator
**Critical Path**: Headline and article input → BERT encoding → Semantic similarity computation → Classification → Explanation generation
**Design Tradeoffs**: Uses knowledge transfer from NLI datasets instead of training from scratch, reducing data requirements but potentially limiting domain-specific adaptation
**Failure Signatures**: May struggle with highly technical content, sarcasm, or headlines that require world knowledge beyond the training corpus

## Limitations
- Evaluation conducted on relatively small datasets, with the largest containing only 500 examples, raising generalizability concerns
- Knowledge transfer from NLI datasets may not fully capture news-specific semantic patterns and domain nuances
- Explanation quality evaluation relies primarily on human assessment without quantitative metrics for explanation reliability
- Potential propagation of biases from NLI training data to the hallucination detection task
