# Generalized Disparate Impact for Configurable Fairness Solutions in ML

## Executive Summary
This paper addresses the need for diverse fairness metrics for continuous protected attributes in machine learning, identifying limitations in the current Hirschfeld-Gebelein-Renyi (HGR) indicator. The authors introduce the Generalized Disparate Impact (GeDI) family of indicators, which measure dependency based on how well user-specified functions of the protected attribute approximate the target variable. GeDI indicators complement HGR semantics, are fully interpretable, robust for finite samples, and configurable to suit specific applications. The approach allows for fine-grained constraints to permit certain types of dependence while forbidding others selectively.

## Method Summary
The paper proposes a novel family of fairness indicators called Generalized Disparate Impact (GeDI) that can complement the existing HGR indicator. GeDI measures the dependency between a continuous protected attribute and a target variable by assessing how well user-specified functions of the protected attribute can approximate the target. This approach allows for more flexible and configurable fairness constraints, enabling practitioners to permit certain types of dependence while forbidding others. The authors demonstrate that GeDI indicators correlate with HGR but have different semantics, offering a valid alternative for fairness metrics in machine learning applications.

## Key Results
- GeDI indicators measure dependency based on function approximation of target variables from protected attributes
- GeDI complements HGR semantics while being fully interpretable and robust for finite samples
- The approach allows fine-grained constraints to permit or forbid specific types of dependence
- Experiments show GeDI can effectively enforce fairness constraints in machine learning models

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
GeDI works by quantifying the dependency between continuous protected attributes and target variables through function approximation. Unlike traditional measures that provide a single dependency score, GeDI allows users to specify which aspects of the dependency are important for their application. By measuring how well functions of the protected attribute can approximate the target variable, GeDI captures different semantic notions of fairness that may be more appropriate for specific use cases. This mechanism provides a more nuanced and configurable approach to fairness constraints compared to existing methods.

## Architecture Onboarding

**Component Map:**
Data -> GeDI Indicator -> Fairness Constraint -> ML Model

**Critical Path:**
The critical path involves computing the GeDI indicator from the data, applying it as a fairness constraint during model training, and evaluating the resulting model's fairness-accuracy tradeoff.

**Design Tradeoffs:**
- Flexibility vs. interpretability: More complex function families may capture nuanced dependencies but reduce interpretability
- Computational cost vs. precision: More sophisticated approximations may be more accurate but computationally expensive
- Generality vs. specificity: Broad function families may apply to many domains but may not capture domain-specific fairness notions

**Failure Signatures:**
- If GeDI indicators show high variance across different function families, it may indicate unstable fairness constraints
- Poor correlation between GeDI and actual fairness outcomes suggests the need for different function specifications
- Computational bottlenecks during GeDI calculation may limit scalability to large datasets
