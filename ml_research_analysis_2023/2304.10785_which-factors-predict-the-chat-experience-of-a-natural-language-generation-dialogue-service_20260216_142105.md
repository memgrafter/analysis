# Which Factors Predict the Chat Experience of a Natural Language Generation Dialogue Service?

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2304.10785
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2304.10785
- Reference count: 23
- Key outcome: PLS-SEM model achieved R² of 0.541 for predicting chat experience in NLG dialogue systems

## Executive Summary
This study proposes a conceptual model to predict user chat experience in natural language generation dialogue systems. Using PLS-SEM with 120 participants interacting with 20 personality-configured chat agents, the research identifies key predictors of chat experience including users' favorability scores, coherence, sentiment, and similarity in dialogue. The findings suggest that adaptive dialog systems can leverage these factors to infer user experience and optimize interactions through prompt engineering.

## Method Summary
The study employed Partial Least Squares Structural Equation Modeling to analyze relationships between latent variables affecting chat experience. Participants from Taiwan interacted with 20 chat agents configured with different Big Five personality traits through the LINE messaging platform. The NLG model (GPT-3 Davinci) generated responses based on personality prompts. Quality metrics including coherence (Microsoft/DialogRPT-human-vs-rand), sentiment (distilbert-base-uncased-finetuned-sst-2-english), and similarity (sentence-BERT) were calculated. User feedback was collected through questionnaires, and analysis was performed using SmartPLS 4 software.

## Key Results
- The PLS-SEM model achieved an R² of 0.541 for predicting chat experience
- Favorability score showed the strongest predictive power (path coefficient = 0.641, p < 0.001)
- Users preferred agents with Extroversion, Openness, Conscientiousness, Agreeableness, and Non-Neuroticism personalities
- Coherence, sentiment, and similarity in dialogue were positive predictors of chat experience

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- Question: How does the effectiveness of the proposed model vary across different languages or cultural contexts?
- Basis in paper: [inferred] The paper only evaluated the model with participants in Taiwan and using English prompts for a Chinese-speaking population. It would be valuable to test the model's generalizability across different languages and cultures.
- Why unresolved: The study's sample was limited to Taiwanese participants, and the prompts were in English. Cultural and linguistic differences could impact how users perceive chatbot interactions and the factors that predict chat experience.
- What evidence would resolve it: Replicating the study with participants from different cultural and linguistic backgrounds, using prompts in their native languages, and comparing the model's performance across these groups.

### Open Question 2
- Question: How does the length and depth of conversation affect the predictive power of the model?
- Basis in paper: [inferred] The study limited conversations to 10 turns per round. It's unclear how the model would perform with longer or more in-depth conversations.
- Why unresolved: The study's design constrained the conversation length, which may not reflect real-world chatbot interactions. Longer conversations could reveal additional factors or change the relative importance of existing ones.
- What evidence would resolve it: Conducting experiments with varying conversation lengths and depths, and analyzing how the model's predictive power and the significance of different factors change.

### Open Question 3
- Question: How do individual differences in user characteristics (e.g., personality, attachment style, prior experience with chatbots) moderate the relationships in the proposed model?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper collected data on users' attachment style but did not explore how individual differences might moderate the model's relationships.
- Why unresolved: The study focused on the overall relationships between factors and chat experience but did not investigate how these relationships might differ based on user characteristics.
- What evidence would resolve it: Conducting a study that includes a broader range of user characteristics, such as personality traits, prior chatbot experience, and demographic information, and analyzing how these factors moderate the relationships in the model.

## Limitations
- Limited corpus evidence with only 1 related paper found
- Use of translated questionnaires and English NLG for Chinese-speaking participants
- Sample size of 120 participants limits generalizability across different cultural contexts
