# Deep Attention-Based Alignment Network for Melody Generation from Incomplete Lyrics

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2301.10015
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2301.10015
- Reference count: 20
- Key outcome: Proposed model generates meaningful lyrics and melodies from seed keywords, with ASW/CSWP embeddings outperforming others in BLEU scores and subjective evaluation.

## Executive Summary
This paper introduces a deep attention-based alignment network for automatic melody generation from incomplete lyrics. The method employs an encoder-decoder RNN with attention to first predict full lyrics from seed keywords, then generate corresponding melodies. The model leverages syllable-level skip-gram embeddings, particularly ASW and CSWP representations, to capture semantic and phonetic relationships in lyrics. Experiments demonstrate competitive performance in generating coherent lyrics and melodies, validated through both quantitative BLEU scores and subjective human evaluation.

## Method Summary
The approach consists of two stages: first, a lyrics predictor LSTM generates full lyrics from seed keywords using syllable-level skip-gram embeddings; second, an attention-based encoder-decoder RNN generates melody attributes (pitch, duration, rest) conditioned on the predicted lyrics. The encoder processes the generated lyrics to produce hidden states, while the attention mechanism aligns these with the decoder states at each time step. Three separate decoders independently generate each musical attribute, optimized via cross-entropy loss. The model is trained on a parsed English lyrics-melody dataset with embeddings learned via skip-gram models.

## Key Results
- The attention-based alignment enables effective syllable-to-note mapping, capturing rhythmic and melodic correspondence.
- ASW and CSWP syllable-level skip-gram embeddings outperform other representations in both BLEU scores and subjective evaluation.
- The encoder-decoder RNN architecture with separate attribute decoders produces coherent lyrics and melodies with appropriate musical attributes.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: The attention-based alignment enables syllable-to-note mapping that captures rhythmic and melodic correspondence between lyrics and melody.
- Mechanism: At each decoder time step, the attention model computes a context vector as a weighted sum of encoder hidden states, where weights are learned alignment scores between the current decoder state and each encoder state. This allows the model to focus on the relevant syllable when generating each note or rest.
- Core assumption: The alignment between lyrics and melody is monotonic and local, meaning each syllable primarily influences the notes sung with it.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "The attention mechanism is exploited to align the predicted lyrics with the melody during the lyrics-to-melody generation."
  - [section III-B] "The attention vector ct is used to align the input lyrics with the melody by computing alignment model..."
- Break condition: If the melody contains long notes spanning multiple syllables or complex rhythmic patterns that violate local alignment assumptions, the attention mechanism may fail to capture proper correlations.

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: Syllable-level skip-gram embeddings (especially ASW and CSWP) capture semantic and phonetic relationships in lyrics that improve melody generation quality.
- Mechanism: Skip-gram models learn dense vector representations by predicting surrounding context tokens. ASW combines syllable and word embeddings additively, while CSWP concatenates syllable, word, and projected syllable embeddings. These rich representations encode both syllable-level and word-level information.
- Core assumption: The semantic and phonetic content of lyrics influences melodic contour and rhythm, and this relationship can be captured in embedding space.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [section III-A] "We train skip-gram models to obtain the embedding vectors of lyrics at syllable and word levels..."
  - [section IV-C] "The results for each embedding representation are shown in Fig. 3. We can observe that ASW and CSWP lyrics embeddings in each music attribute generation outperform other representations."
- Break condition: If the relationship between lyrics semantics/phonetics and melody is not learnable from the training data, or if the embedding dimensions are insufficient to capture relevant patterns, performance will degrade.

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: The encoder-decoder RNN architecture with separate attribute decoders enables generation of coherent lyrics and corresponding melodies with appropriate pitch, duration, and rest patterns.
- Mechanism: The encoder processes predicted lyrics sequentially to produce hidden states. Three separate decoders (for pitch, duration, and rest) each use the context vector and decoder state to generate their respective attributes. This modular approach allows specialized modeling of each musical dimension.
- Core assumption: Pitch, duration, and rest attributes can be modeled independently given the lyrical context, and the joint distribution can be factorized appropriately.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [section III] "Lyrics to melody generator is modeled as a conditional distribution model over melody sequence on a lyrics sequence..."
  - [section III-B] "The independent decoder for each attribute receives the lyric context vector Ct and decoder state to generate the pitch, duration, and rest attributes for each syllable."
- Break condition: If the attributes are not conditionally independent given the lyrics, or if correlations between attributes are crucial for musical coherence, the factorization may be inadequate.

## Foundational Learning

- Concept: Encoder-decoder architecture with attention
  - Why needed here: To handle variable-length input lyrics and generate corresponding variable-length melodies while maintaining alignment between syllables and notes
  - Quick check question: How does the attention mechanism help the decoder focus on relevant parts of the input sequence at each generation step?

- Concept: Skip-gram word embeddings
  - Why needed here: To convert discrete lyrical tokens into dense continuous representations that capture semantic and phonetic relationships
  - Quick check question: What is the difference between continuous bag-of-words and skip-gram models in word2vec?

  - Why needed here: To provide quantitative measure of how well generated melodies match ground truth melodies in terms of pitch, duration, and rest attributes
  - Quick check question: What does a higher BLEU score indicate about the quality of generated melodies compared to ground truth?

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map:
  - Seed lyrics → Lyrics Predictor → Lyric Embeddings → Lyrics Encoder → Attention → Melody Decoders → Generated melody
- Critical path: Seed lyrics → Lyrics Predictor → Lyric Embeddings → Lyrics Encoder → Attention → Melody Decoders → Generated melody
- Design tradeoffs:
  - Using separate decoders for each attribute allows specialized modeling but ignores correlations between attributes
  - ASW and CSWP embeddings provide richer representations but increase dimensionality and computational cost
  - Attention mechanism adds complexity but improves alignment modeling
- Failure signatures:
  - BLEU scores significantly lower than reported values
  - Subjective evaluation ratings consistently below 3/5
  - Generated melodies with unrealistic pitch ranges or durations
  - Attention weights showing no clear pattern or focus
- First 3 experiments:
  1. Train with only pitch decoder to isolate the effect of attention on pitch generation
  2. Compare ASW vs CSWP embeddings using the same architecture to validate embedding impact
  3. Remove attention mechanism to test if it's essential for alignment quality

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- Question: How does the choice of syllable-level skip-gram embedding (ASW vs CSWP) quantitatively impact the quality of generated melodies beyond BLEU scores?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper shows ASW performs better than CSWP in subjective evaluations and BLEU scores, but does not explore deeper quantitative analysis.
- Why unresolved: BLEU scores provide a limited view; other metrics like F1 score, precision, or recall for individual attributes (pitch, duration, rest) are not explored.
- What evidence would resolve it: Additional experiments using comprehensive evaluation metrics (e.g., F1, precision, recall) for each attribute and comparing ASW vs CSWP embeddings.

### Open Question 2
- Question: What is the impact of incorporating prior musical knowledge (e.g., harmonic rules, genre-specific constraints) on the quality of generated melodies?
- Basis in paper: [inferred] The authors mention the potential to improve the model by injecting prior musical knowledge, indicating a gap in the current approach.
- Why unresolved: The model relies solely on learned representations without explicitly encoding musical theory or genre-specific rules.
- What evidence would resolve it: Experiments comparing the proposed model with variations that incorporate musical theory or genre constraints, and evaluating the impact on melody quality.

### Open Question 3
- Question: How does the proposed model perform on diverse musical genres beyond pop music?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The dataset used is described as a large-scale paired lyrics-melody dataset for English songs, but the genre distribution is not specified.
- Why unresolved: The model's generalizability to different musical styles and genres is not explored.
- What evidence would resolve it: Testing the model on datasets from various genres (e.g., classical, jazz, rock) and evaluating the quality of generated melodies in each genre.

### Open Question 4
- Question: How does the length and complexity of seed lyrics affect the quality of generated full-length lyrics and melodies?
- Basis in paper: [inferred] The model is designed to handle incomplete lyrics, but the impact of varying lengths and complexities of seed lyrics is not explored.
- Why unresolved: The relationship between seed lyrics' characteristics and the quality of generated content is not investigated.
- What evidence would resolve it: Experiments varying the length and complexity of seed lyrics and evaluating the corresponding quality of generated lyrics and melodies.

## Limitations

- Data availability and reproducibility concerns due to reliance on a parsed dataset without direct access or detailed preprocessing steps.
- Limited evaluation scope with subjective evaluation on only 11 participants and no statistical validation of results.
- Potential generalizability issues as the model is trained and evaluated only on English pop songs without validation on other languages or genres.

## Confidence

- **High Confidence**: The technical implementation of the attention-based encoder-decoder architecture is well-described and follows established patterns in sequence-to-sequence modeling.
- **Medium Confidence**: The reported performance improvements with ASW and CSWP embeddings are supported by experimental results, but the relative importance of each embedding component is not systematically analyzed.
- **Low Confidence**: Claims about the model's ability to generate "meaningful lyrics" and "melodies that convey appropriate emotions" are based on subjective evaluation without detailed analysis of what constitutes meaningful or emotionally appropriate content.

## Next Checks

1. **Data Quality Verification**: Reconstruct the syllable-level parsing process and verify that the lyrics-melody alignment is accurate, particularly for complex rhythmic patterns where one note spans multiple syllables or vice versa.

2. **Embedding Ablation Study**: Systematically remove or modify components of the ASW and CSWP embeddings (e.g., remove word components, change projection dimensions) to quantify their individual contributions to performance.

3. **Cross-Genre Evaluation**: Test the trained model on melodies from different musical genres or languages to assess generalization beyond the training corpus and identify potential limitations of the attention alignment mechanism.
