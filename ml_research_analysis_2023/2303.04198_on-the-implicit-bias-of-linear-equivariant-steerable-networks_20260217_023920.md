# On the Implicit Bias of Linear Equivariant Steerable Networks

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2303.04198
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.04198
- Reference count: 40
- One-line primary result: Linear equivariant steerable networks converge in direction to the unique group-invariant max-margin classifier under gradient flow.

## Executive Summary
This paper investigates the implicit bias of gradient flow on linear equivariant steerable networks for group-invariant binary classification. The authors prove that the parameterized predictor converges in direction to the unique group-invariant classifier with maximum margin determined by the input group action. Under a unitary assumption on the input representation, they establish the equivalence between steerable networks and data augmentation. The paper demonstrates improved margin and generalization bounds of steerable networks over non-invariant counterparts, providing theoretical justification for the effectiveness of equivariant architectures in learning invariant functions.

## Method Summary
The paper studies the behavior of linear equivariant steerable networks under gradient flow (infinitesimal gradient descent) on the empirical risk with exponential loss. The key idea is to leverage the equivariance property of the network to show that the directional limit of the learned predictor is proportional to the max-margin SVM solution of the transformed data set. Under a unitary assumption on the input representation, the authors prove the equivalence between steerable networks and data augmentation, where the augmented data set is obtained by applying the group action to the original data. The improved margin and generalization bounds are derived by comparing the optimization landscape of the steerable network to that of a fully-connected network.

## Key Results
- The directional limit of the parameterized predictor under gradient flow is proportional to the max-margin SVM solution of the transformed data set.
- Under unitary input actions, the directional limit is proportional to the max-margin SVM solution of the original data set, establishing the equivalence between steerable networks and data augmentation.
- The margin of the steerable network on the augmented data set is always larger than that of a fully-connected network, leading to improved generalization bounds.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The mechanism behind the results relies on the equivariance property of the network and the gradient flow dynamics. The equivariance ensures that the network output transforms consistently under the group action, which in turn implies that the gradient of the loss also transforms accordingly. This allows the authors to relate the optimization landscape of the steerable network to that of the transformed data set. Under the unitary input representation assumption, the group action is orthogonal, which simplifies the analysis and enables the equivalence between steerable networks and data augmentation.

## Limitations
- The unitary input representation assumption is critical for establishing the equivalence between steerable networks and data augmentation, but its practical relevance and prevalence in real-world data are unclear.
- The analysis focuses on linear steerable networks, and it is uncertain whether the insights extend to nonlinear cases or deeper architectures.
- The assumption of infinitesimal gradient descent (gradient flow) may not accurately reflect practical training dynamics with finite step sizes.

## Confidence
- Directional convergence result (Theorem 3.1): High
