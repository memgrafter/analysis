# Challenges facing the explainability of age prediction models: case study for two modalities

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2303.06640
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.06640
- Reference count: 8
- Primary result: Published benchmark models for age prediction from EEG signals (MAE: 7.93 and 7.23 years) and lung X-ray images (XGBoost/CatBoost regressors) to advance XAI research for these modalities

## Executive Summary
This paper addresses the challenge of explaining AI models for age prediction in healthcare applications, focusing on two specific modalities: EEG signals and lung X-ray images. The authors developed and shared benchmark models for these modalities, including Linear Regression and Multi-Layer Perceptron models for EEG data, achieving Mean Absolute Errors of 7.93 and 7.23 years respectively, and XGBoost and CatBoost models for X-ray data. By making these models publicly available, the research aims to facilitate the development of new explainable AI techniques for age prediction in healthcare settings where traditional explanation methods are not suitable for these specific data types.

## Method Summary
The authors created benchmark models for age prediction from two challenging modalities. For EEG data, they extracted 1,653 features from coherence matrices and frequency bands across 19 electrodes, then trained Linear Regression and Multi-Layer Perceptron models. For lung X-ray images, they employed a Siamese network with EfficientNet-B0 backbone to generate 512-dimensional embeddings, followed by XGBoost and CatBoost regressors. The models were trained on datasets containing 20,365 patients (ages 16-70) for EEG and an unspecified number for X-ray images.

## Key Results
- EEG Linear Regression model achieved Mean Absolute Error of 7.93 years
- EEG Multi-Layer Perceptron model achieved Mean Absolute Error of 7.23 years
- Siamese network successfully generated meaningful embeddings for X-ray images despite limited labeled examples
- Both modalities present unique challenges for explainable AI techniques

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: Linear models are interpretable by design, making them baseline candidates for age prediction.
- Mechanism: The paper explicitly states that models "vary in structure, ranging from linear regression models, which are interpretable by design, to tree based boosted models and multi-layer neural networks."
- Core assumption: The inherent interpretability of linear models provides sufficient explanation for healthcare stakeholders.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "the following sections summarise the data on which these models were trained" and mention linear regression alongside more complex models
  - [section] "two machine learning models were fitted... a Linear Regression model and a Multi-Layer Perceptron"
  - [corpus] No direct corpus evidence supporting this mechanism
- Break condition: If linear model performance is insufficient for clinical utility, interpretability advantage may be outweighed by need for better accuracy

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: Siamese networks enable few-shot learning for X-ray embeddings despite limited labeled examples.
- Mechanism: The paper states "we decided to use one of the few-shot learning methods, namely siamese networks. Few-shot learning helps to deal with a problem when a few examples in each class are present."
- Core assumption: Siamese networks can effectively create meaningful embeddings for age prediction from X-ray data.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "images of children and adults should not be mixed in one class in the standard classification task. However, embeddings are representations of images, so we decided to use one of the few-shot learning methods"
  - [section] "We trained the Siamese network with 2,158 images for 20 epochs and a learning rate 0.0005"
  - [corpus] Weak corpus evidence - only one related paper mentions few-shot learning but not in context of X-rays
- Break condition: If the embeddings don't capture age-relevant features, subsequent regression models will fail regardless of architecture

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: Different modalities require different XAI approaches due to data structure differences.
- Mechanism: The paper emphasizes "the most popular methods of explainable AI are agnostic to the structure of the model but they are not agnostic to the modality of the data" and highlights challenges with "multidimensional series in the EEG signals and grayscale X-ray images."
- Core assumption: Standard XAI methods designed for tabular or image data cannot be directly applied to EEG signals or grayscale X-rays.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "If they are designed for tabular, image, or text data, it is difficult to transfer them to EEG signals or grayscale images"
  - [section] "we created and shared a collection of benchmark models for two modalities that currently do not have representations suitable for explanations"
  - [corpus] [corpus] Weak corpus evidence - no related papers directly address XAI modality challenges for age prediction
- Break condition: If new XAI techniques emerge that can handle these modalities, this mechanism becomes less relevant

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map:
  - EEG pipeline: Raw EEG → preprocessing → 1,653 features → Linear Regression/MLP → age prediction
  - X-ray pipeline: Raw X-ray images → Siamese network → 512-dimensional embeddings → XGBoost/CatBoost → age prediction
  - Shared components: Data preprocessing, model training, validation, benchmarking repository

- Critical path: Data preprocessing → model training → validation → explanation method development
  - For EEG: Feature extraction from raw signals is most critical
  - For X-ray: Siamese network training and embedding quality is most critical

- Design tradeoffs:
  - EEG: Simpler models (linear regression) offer interpretability but lower accuracy vs complex models (MLP)
  - X-ray: Siamese network adds complexity but enables few-shot learning with limited labeled data
  - General: Model performance vs explainability tradeoff, especially for healthcare applications

- Failure signatures:
  - EEG: Poor feature engineering leads to both accuracy and explainability issues
  - X-ray: Poor Siamese network training leads to meaningless embeddings regardless of regression model choice
  - Both: Overfitting on training data reduces generalizability for clinical use

## Limitations
- Limited scope to only two specific modalities without broader validation across other medical imaging or biosignal types
- No comparative analysis with state-of-the-art age prediction models from other domains or modalities
- Missing ablation studies to quantify the contribution of individual feature sets or model components
- Limited discussion of clinical validation beyond technical performance metrics

## Confidence
