# Robust low-rank training via approximate orthonormal constraints

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2306.01485
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.01485
- Reference count: 40
- Primary result: Proposed method achieves 50-80% compression while maintaining or improving accuracy and robust accuracy compared to full models and other low-rank methods

## Executive Summary
This paper addresses the challenge of training low-rank neural networks that are both computationally efficient and adversarially robust. The authors observe that standard low-rank training methods lead to exploding condition numbers, resulting in poor adversarial robustness. To solve this, they propose a novel training algorithm that maintains the network's weights on the low-rank matrix manifold while enforcing approximate orthonormal constraints to keep the condition number bounded. The method achieves significant compression (50-80%) while improving or maintaining both accuracy and robust accuracy compared to full models and other low-rank methods.

## Method Summary
The proposed method trains low-rank neural networks by maintaining weights on the low-rank matrix manifold while enforcing approximate orthonormal constraints. The algorithm uses projected gradient flows to update the low-rank factors U, S, and V while projecting onto the Stiefel manifold (for orthonormal columns) and a singular value constraint set Σs(ε) (to bound singular values). This approach ensures the condition number remains bounded throughout training, addressing the instability that typically occurs with standard low-rank training methods.

## Key Results
- Achieves 50-80% compression on MNIST and CIFAR10 datasets
- Maintains or improves accuracy compared to full models and other low-rank methods
- Improves robust accuracy by 1-2% on CIFAR10 while reducing model size by 50-80%
- Provides theoretical guarantees through approximation theorems

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: Low-rank weight matrices with exploding singular values lead to large condition numbers, which cause loss of adversarial robustness
- Mechanism: Standard low-rank training compensates for missing parameters by increasing the magnitude of remaining singular values, inflating the condition number cond(W) = σmax/σmin
- Core assumption: Adversarial robustness is directly correlated with condition number across different architectures and datasets
- Evidence anchors: Abstract and main text observations, weak evidence in corpus papers
- Break condition: If correlation between condition number and robustness breaks down for certain activation functions or architectures

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: Bounding singular values to a narrow band around constant s keeps condition number bounded and improves robustness
- Mechanism: Constrains low-rank factor S to lie within set Σs(ε) where all singular values are within [s-ε, s+ε], ensuring cond2(W) ≤ 1 + τ
- Core assumption: Projection of S onto Σs(ε) can be computed efficiently without significantly harming optimization
- Evidence anchors: Abstract and main text claims, no direct evidence in corpus papers
- Break condition: If projection becomes too restrictive and prevents effective learning

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: Low-rank training with bounded singular values approximates ideal full-rank model if high-performing low-rank sub-network exists
- Mechanism: Approximation theorem shows proposed algorithm computes good approximation when optimal low-rank trajectory exists
- Core assumption: High-performing low-rank sub-network with bounded singular values exists for given architecture and dataset
- Evidence anchors: Abstract and main text approximation theorem, weak evidence in corpus papers
- Break condition: If no high-performing low-rank sub-network with bounded singular values exists

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map:
  - Low-rank factors: U (n×r), S (r×r), V (m×r)
  - Gradients: ∇U L, ∇S L, ∇V L
  - Projections: Stiefel manifold (orthonormal columns), Σs(ε) (bounded singular values)
  - Optimization steps: SGD with momentum or Adam for each factor
  - Compression ratio: α = 1 - r(n+m+r)/(nm)

- Critical path:
  1. Initialize U, S, V with appropriate ranks
  2. Compute gradients ∇U L, ∇S L, ∇V L
  3. Update U, V using projected gradient flows
  4. Project U, V onto Stiefel manifold
  5. Update S using gradient flow
  6. Project S onto Σs(ε)
  7. Repeat for all layers and iterations

- Design tradeoffs:
  - Rank selection: Higher rank improves accuracy but reduces compression
  - Conditioning tolerance τ: Lower τ improves robustness but may harm optimization
  - Initialization: Poor initialization can lead to stiff optimization paths

- Failure signatures:
  - Exploding/vanishing gradients during training
  - Condition numbers of layers growing beyond tolerance
  - Accuracy degradation compared to baseline
  - Training instability or divergence

- First 3 experiments:
  1. Implement Algorithm 1 on a simple 2-layer MLP with synthetic data, varying rank r and conditioning tolerance τ. Monitor condition numbers and accuracy.
  2. Compare proposed method with vanilla low-rank training on LeNet5 for MNIST, using 50% compression. Measure accuracy, robust accuracy, and condition numbers.
  3. Test sensitivity to initialization by starting with ill-conditioned weight matrices. Compare convergence and final performance with well-conditioned initialization.

## Limitations
- Theoretical link between condition number and adversarial robustness remains relatively underexplored
- Method's performance on larger, more complex architectures (e.g., ResNets) is not demonstrated
- Robustness gains on CIFAR10 are modest (1-2% absolute)
- Requires careful tuning of conditioning tolerance τ and rank selection

## Confidence

### Confidence Labels
- **High Confidence**: Low-rank training algorithm implementation and convergence properties are well-defined and supported by gradient flow analysis
- **Medium Confidence**: Empirical results on MNIST and CIFAR10 are reproducible and show consistent trends, but theoretical grounding of condition number-robustness link is less rigorous
- **Low Confidence**: Approximation theorem assumes existence of high-performing low-rank sub-network with bounded singular values, but no empirical evidence verifies this assumption
