# Decomposing Complex Queries for Tip-of-the-tongue Retrieval

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2305.15053
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.15053
- Reference count: 14
- Key outcome: Query decomposition improves gold book recall by up to 7% relative at Recall@5 over strong baselines

## Executive Summary
This paper addresses the challenge of tip-of-the-tongue (TOT) retrieval, where users forget specific details about items they're seeking but can describe contextual information. The key insight is that TOT queries often contain clues about document elements beyond just main text, such as book covers or publication dates. The proposed MOREL approach decomposes complex queries into sub-queries targeting specific document fields, routes them to specialized retrievers, and ensembles the results. Experiments on a new dataset of 14,441 real-world book queries demonstrate significant improvements in retrieval accuracy compared to traditional single-retriever approaches.

## Method Summary
The MOREL framework addresses TOT retrieval by first decomposing complex queries into sub-queries using GPT-3.5, with each sub-query targeting a specific document field (title, description, publication date, or cover image). These sub-queries are then routed to specialized retrievers: Contriever for text fields, CLIP for cover images, and a symbolic function for dates. The retrieved results from each specialized retriever are combined through weighted ensemble scoring. The approach is evaluated on the newly introduced WhatsThatBook dataset, which contains 14,441 query-book pairs collected from the GoodReads TOT forum, with each book represented by title, description, publication year, and cover image.

## Key Results
- Query decomposition improves gold book recall by up to 7% relative at Recall@5 over Contriever baseline
- Specialized retrievers for different document fields outperform general-purpose retrievers
- Ensemble of specialized retrievers provides further gains over individual specialized retrievers
- MOREL demonstrates strong performance across different recall metrics (R@1, R@5, R@10)

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: Decomposing a complex query into sub-queries allows routing to specialized retrievers that are better suited for each query aspect.
- Mechanism: Long TOT queries contain multiple facets (e.g., plot, cover description, personal context). By extracting sub-queries focused on individual facets, each can be matched against the most relevant document field (text, image, metadata) using the optimal retrieval method for that modality.
- Core assumption: The facets in a TOT query can be reliably identified and mapped to specific document fields.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract]: "by decomposing the query into individual clues, routing those as sub-queries to specialized retrievers"
  - [section]: "MOREL decomposes complex queries into subqueries routed to specific retrieval experts"
  - [corpus]: Weak - no direct citations, but related works on query decomposition and multi-modal retrieval exist (e.g., ReasonIR, Query-Aware GNNs).
- Break condition: If the query decomposer fails to identify relevant facets or maps them incorrectly, the routing will be ineffective.

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: Using specialized retrievers for different modalities (text, image, metadata) improves matching accuracy compared to a single retriever.
- Mechanism: Different document fields have different optimal matching strategies. Text fields benefit from semantic matching (e.g., Contriever), images from visual-text matching (e.g., CLIP), and metadata from rule-based or symbolic matching. Specialized retrievers can leverage these strategies.
- Core assumption: The document fields contain sufficient information in their respective modalities to enable accurate matching.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract]: "incorporating query decompositions into retrievers can improve gold book recall up to 7% relative again for Recall@5"
  - [section]: "incorporating query decomposition into retrievers can improve gold book recall up to 7% relative again for Recall@5"
  - [corpus]: Weak - no direct citations, but the use of CLIP and specialized retrievers is a common approach in multi-modal retrieval.
- Break condition: If the document fields are sparse or lack information in their respective modalities, the specialized retrievers will have limited effectiveness.

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: Ensemble of results from specialized retrievers improves overall recall compared to any single retriever.
- Mechanism: Different retrievers capture different aspects of relevance. An ensemble combines their strengths, capturing a broader range of query-document matches than any single retriever alone.
- Core assumption: The specialized retrievers provide complementary information and their results can be meaningfully combined.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract]: "and ensembling the results"
  - [section]: "combining retrieved results" and "Incorporating all dates, covers, and titles together provides further gains"
  - [corpus]: Weak - no direct citations, but ensemble methods are a standard approach in information retrieval.
- Break condition: If the specialized retrievers are highly correlated or their results are difficult to combine effectively, the ensemble may not provide significant improvement.

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map:
  Query -> Query Decomposer -> Sub-queries -> Specialized Retrievers -> Scores -> Ensemble -> Ranked Documents

- Critical path:
  Query -> Query Decomposer -> Sub-queries -> Specialized Retrievers -> Scores -> Ensemble -> Ranked Documents

- Design tradeoffs:
  - Query decomposition accuracy vs. complexity: More complex decomposition may improve accuracy but increase processing time.
  - Retriever specialization vs. generalization: Specialized retrievers may be more accurate but less flexible.
  - Ensemble method complexity vs. effectiveness: More complex ensemble methods may improve results but increase computational cost.

- Failure signatures:
  - Low recall: Query decomposer may not be accurately identifying facets, or specialized retrievers may not be effective.
  - High computational cost: Query decomposition or ensemble methods may be too complex.
  - Poor ranking: Ensemble method may not be effectively combining scores.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- Question: How do different query decomposition strategies (e.g., using different LLMs, prompt templates, or in-context examples) impact retrieval performance for TOT queries?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper describes using GPT 3.5 for query decomposition with specific prompt templates and in-context examples, but does not explore the impact of varying these choices.
- Why unresolved: The paper presents a single query decomposition approach without comparing it to alternatives or conducting ablation studies on the decomposition process itself.
- What evidence would resolve it: Experiments comparing retrieval performance using different LLMs (e.g., GPT-4, Claude), prompt templates, numbers of in-context examples, or alternative decomposition methods (e.g., rule-based, supervised) would provide insights into the impact of these choices.

### Open Question 2
- Question: Can the query decomposition framework be extended to handle queries that target multiple document fields simultaneously, rather than just routing clues to individual experts?
- Basis in paper: [inferred] The current framework decomposes queries into clues for individual fields and routes them to specialized retrievers. However, TOT queries often contain information relevant to multiple fields, and combining these clues could potentially improve retrieval.
- Why unresolved: The paper focuses on decomposing queries into single-field clues and combining retrieval results, but does not explore methods for handling multi-field queries or joint modeling of field interactions.
- What evidence would resolve it: Experiments comparing the current decomposition approach to methods that jointly model multiple fields (e.g., using a single retriever that attends to all fields, or a hierarchical decomposition strategy) would demonstrate the potential benefits of handling multi-field queries.

### Open Question 3
- Question: How does the effectiveness of query decomposition for TOT retrieval vary across different domains or types of items beyond books (e.g., movies, music, products)?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper introduces the WhatsThatBook dataset and demonstrates the effectiveness of query decomposition for book retrieval, but does not evaluate the approach on other domains or item types.
- Why unresolved: While the framework is designed to be modular and adaptable to different retrievers, its generalizability to other domains or item types remains unexplored.
- What evidence would resolve it: Experiments applying the query decomposition framework to TOT retrieval datasets for other domains (e.g., movies, music, products) and comparing performance to domain-specific baselines would demonstrate the approach's generalizability.

## Limitations
- Dataset limitation: Results are based on a single dataset from GoodReads TOT forum, which may not generalize to other TOT scenarios
- Implementation uncertainty: Key implementation details like ensemble weights and specific GPT-3.5 prompt examples are unspecified
- Limited baseline comparison: Does not compare against more recent specialized retrieval approaches that could provide stronger baselines

## Confidence
- Medium confidence: The paper demonstrates significant improvements in TOT retrieval through query decomposition, but several aspects remain uncertain including query decomposition accuracy metrics, unspecified ensemble weights, and limited dataset diversity.
