# On the Stepwise Nature of Self-Supervised Learning

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2303.15438
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.15438
- Reference count: 40
- One-line primary result: Self-supervised learning methods learn high-dimensional embeddings one dimension at a time in discrete, well-separated steps

## Executive Summary
This paper presents a theoretical and empirical study of the training dynamics of self-supervised learning (SSL) methods with joint embedding networks. The key finding is that SSL methods learn their high-dimensional embeddings one dimension at a time in a series of discrete, well-separated steps. This stepwise learning behavior is first identified in a linearized model of Barlow Twins, where exact solutions show that each embedding dimension is learned sequentially by following the top eigenmodes of a contrastive kernel. The same stepwise phenomenon is then observed in experiments with deep ResNets trained using Barlow Twins, SimCLR, and VICReg losses, both with small and standard initialization.

## Method Summary
The paper develops a theoretical framework for understanding SSL training dynamics by first analyzing a linear model with Barlow Twins loss, then kernelizing the solution for general kernel machines. Experiments are conducted on CIFAR-10 and STL-10 datasets using ResNet-50 encoders with various SSL losses. The authors track loss and eigenvalues of the cross-covariance matrix during training, comparing predictions from initial and empirical neural tangent kernels.

## Key Results
- Linear Barlow Twins model exhibits exact stepwise learning by following top eigenmodes of contrastive kernel
- Same stepwise phenomenon observed in deep ResNets with Barlow Twins, SimCLR, and VICReg losses
- Final embeddings governed by top eigenmodes of kernel-like matrix, analogous to kernel PCA
- Stepwise learning persists with both small and standard initialization schemes

## Limitations

- The stepwise learning phenomenon relies heavily on the infinite-width NTK regime, where training dynamics become linear.
- The theory assumes full-batch gradient flow, whereas practical implementations use mini-batches and discrete optimization.
- Experiments show the stepwise pattern persists in deep ResNets, but the magnitude of eigenvalues is larger and the steps less clean than predicted.

## Confidence

- Linear model stepwise dynamics: **High** - Exact solutions are derived and validated
- Kernelized solution generalization: **Medium** - Relies on NTK assumptions that hold asymptotically
- Deep network experiments: **Medium** - Shows similar patterns but with quantitative deviations from theory
- Stepwise as generic SSL mechanism: **Low-Medium** - Compelling evidence but not proven universal
