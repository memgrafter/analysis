# Natural Language Generation for Advertising: A Survey

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2306.12719
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.12719
- Reference count: 16
- Primary result: Comprehensive survey of AdNLG research over the past decade, identifying abstractive neural approaches with RL optimization as dominant trends

## Executive Summary
This survey provides a comprehensive review of Natural Language Generation for online advertising (AdNLG) over the past decade, systematically categorizing approaches from template-based methods to advanced abstractive neural models. The survey identifies abstractive approaches using neural networks as the dominant paradigm, with reinforcement learning being widely employed to directly optimize advertising performance metrics like click-through rates. Key challenges identified include the need for standardized evaluation methods, improving diversity in generated ads, enhancing faithfulness to avoid hallucinations, incorporating multimodal information, and developing benchmark datasets. Despite significant industry involvement, the field lacks public benchmarks and standardized error analysis, limiting replicability and new research.

## Method Summary
The survey systematically reviews AdNLG research by categorizing approaches into template-based, extractive, and abstractive methods. It analyzes over 30 papers from the past decade, focusing on model architectures, optimization strategies (particularly reinforcement learning), evaluation metrics, and identified challenges. The methodology involves comprehensive literature review, categorization of research trends, and identification of key problems and potential solutions. The survey emphasizes the shift from rule-based template methods to data-driven neural approaches, particularly abstractive models that generate novel ad text rather than extracting from sources.

## Key Results
- Abstractive neural approaches dominate AdNLG research, with many studies employing reinforcement learning to optimize advertising performance metrics
- Key evaluation aspects include advertising performance, diversity, faithfulness, fluency, and relevance
- Major challenges include lack of standardized evaluation methods, improving diversity, enhancing faithfulness, incorporating multimodal information, and developing benchmark datasets

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: Abstractive neural approaches dominate AdNLG due to their flexibility in capturing meaning rather than surface forms.
- Mechanism: Abstractive methods leverage encoder-decoder architectures (e.g., BART, GPT-2) to generate novel ad texts that may differ in wording from the input while preserving the intended message.
- Core assumption: The semantic content of landing pages and user queries can be mapped to persuasive ad text without requiring exact phrase matching.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "This survey entails a review of the research trends on this topic over the past decade, from template-based to extractive and abstractive approaches using neural networks."
  - [section 4] "With the recent success of neural networks in a wide variety of NLG tasks, abstractive approaches using neural language generation models have been more actively studied..."
- Break condition: When input-output semantic alignment is critical (e.g., legal compliance), surface-level extraction may be preferred over abstractive generation.

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: Reinforcement learning directly optimizes advertising performance metrics like CTR by treating ad generation as a sequential decision process.
- Mechanism: RL agents generate ad text sequences and receive rewards based on predicted CTR from a trained CTR model, enabling end-to-end optimization beyond token-level losses.
- Core assumption: A differentiable or surrogate reward signal (predicted CTR) can guide generation toward higher-performing ads.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "Additionally, key challenges and directions revealed through the survey, including metric optimization..."
  - [section 4.1] "Reinforcement learning (RL) has been widely used as a representative approach to directly optimize advertising performance metrics."
- Break condition: If reward signal is sparse or noisy (e.g., CTR predictions are unreliable), RL optimization may diverge or produce low-quality outputs.

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: Controlled generation methods enable content diversity by conditioning on aspect labels or syntactic tags.
- Mechanism: Models accept additional control codes (e.g., aspect categories like "Product" or POS tags) to steer generation toward different selling points or grammatical structures.
- Core assumption: The model has learned distinct representations for different control signals during training.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [section 4.2] "Golobokov et al. (2022) and Chai et al. (2022) proposed essentially the same controllable ad text generation model that can highlight different selling points..."
  - [section 4.2] "Regarding form diversity, Jin et al. (2022) proposed a model designed to generate slogans using syntactic control."
- Break condition: If control codes are not well-aligned with training data distribution, generated outputs may be incoherent or irrelevant.

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map:
  - Input encoder (e.g., landing page text, user query, multimodal features)
  - Control module (optional: aspect classifiers, POS taggers)
  - Sequence decoder (e.g., Transformer-based model like BART)
  - Reward estimator (CTR prediction model for RL)
  - Diversity/metric enforcement module (optional)

- Critical path: Input → Encoder → Decoder → Output ad text → Reward estimator → Policy update (if RL)

- Design tradeoffs:
  - Template-based: high precision, low diversity vs. abstractive: high diversity, potential hallucinations
  - RL optimization: direct metric alignment vs. training instability
  - Controllable generation: diversity vs. complexity in training and inference

- Failure signatures:
  - Low diversity: high Self-BLEU, repetitive outputs
  - Hallucinations: unfaithful claims not present in input
  - Poor relevance: low keyword coverage, mismatched selling points
  - Training instability: RL reward collapse, mode collapse

- First 3 experiments:

  2. Add a simple RL fine-tuning loop using a CTR prediction model as reward; compare CTR-optimized vs. MLE baselines.
  3. Integrate a controllable generation module (e.g., aspect control); measure diversity and relevance improvements.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- Question: What is the actual impact of optimizing advertising performance metrics (like CTR) on the effectiveness of generated ads?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper states that the effectiveness of optimizing advertising performance metrics has not been well studied in the literature, with only one ablation study reported.
- Why unresolved: Most studies use conventional evaluation criteria that don't necessarily correspond to advertising performance metrics. There's a lack of error analysis to understand what components contribute to improvements.
- What evidence would resolve it: A comprehensive study comparing the performance of models optimized for advertising metrics versus those optimized for traditional NLG metrics, including detailed error analysis and real-world A/B testing results.

### Open Question 2
- Question: How can we develop more robust methods for measuring the faithfulness of generated ad text that account for lexical variability like synonyms and abbreviations?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper notes that existing faithfulness approaches rely on surface matching of words and phrases, which can't handle different wording. It suggests NLI-based methods as a potential direction.
- Why unresolved: Current methods judge faithfulness based on exact phrase matching between input and generated text, failing to account for semantic equivalence expressed through different words.
- What evidence would resolve it: Development and evaluation of faithfulness metrics that can handle lexical variability, with experiments showing improved correlation with human judgments compared to surface-matching approaches.

### Open Question 3
- Question: How can multimodal information (images, videos, layout) from landing pages be effectively incorporated into AdNLG models to generate more informative and attractive ad texts?
- Basis in paper: [inferred] The paper mentions that most studies treat AdNLG as text-to-text generation and don't use multimodal information from landing pages, despite its potential to help models understand content better.
- Why unresolved: The field has paid insufficient attention to multimodality compared to other NLG tasks, and there's limited exploration of how visual and textual information can be combined for ad generation.
- What evidence would resolve it: Development of multimodal AdNLG models that incorporate visual features and layout information, with experimental results showing improvements in ad attractiveness and relevance compared to text-only approaches.

## Limitations
- Heavy reliance on proprietary in-house data from major ad platforms, creating significant barriers to reproducibility and independent validation
- Lack of standardized evaluation protocols and public benchmark datasets, making it difficult to compare different approaches fairly across studies
- Limited peer-reviewed publications with complete methodological details due to significant industry involvement

## Confidence
- Confidence in abstractive neural methods dominating the field: High
- Confidence in RL effectiveness for CTR optimization: Medium
- Confidence in proposed solutions for diversity and faithfulness challenges: Low
