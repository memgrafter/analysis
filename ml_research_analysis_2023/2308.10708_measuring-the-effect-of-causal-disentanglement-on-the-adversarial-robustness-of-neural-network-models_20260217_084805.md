# Measuring the Effect of Causal Disentanglement on the Adversarial Robustness of Neural Network Models

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2308.10708
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2308.10708
- Authors: 
- Reference count: 10
- This paper presents the first quantitative study of causal disentanglement in causal neural networks and its impact on adversarial robustness.

## Executive Summary
This paper investigates how causal disentanglement in neural networks affects adversarial robustness. The authors implement four state-of-the-art causal neural network models with a common ResNet18 backbone and measure their causal disentanglement using content/style disentanglement metrics adapted from computer vision. Through comprehensive experiments on three benchmark datasets and seven adversarial attack configurations, they find strong evidence that decorrelation between causal and confounder signals improves robustness, while high pixel-level information in the confounder signal degrades it.

## Method Summary
The authors implement four causal neural network models (CAMA, CaaM, CausalAdv, DICE) with a shared ResNet18 backbone architecture. They train these models on MNIST, CIFAR10, and CIFAR100 datasets while recording model checkpoints and validation metrics throughout training. For each trained model, they compute five disentanglement metrics including Distance Correlation (DC) between causal/confounder signals and Information over Bias (IoB) for signal information content. The models are then evaluated under seven different white-box adversarial attack configurations to measure classification accuracy degradation. Correlation analysis is performed between the disentanglement metrics and both clean and adversarial accuracy to identify relationships between causal disentanglement and robustness.

## Key Results
- Strong positive association (r=0.820, p=0.001) between decorrelation of causal and confounder signals and adversarial robustness
- Moderate negative association (r=-0.597, p=0.040) between pixel-level information content of confounder signal and adversarial robustness
- High distance correlation between causal signal and input signals associated with higher classification accuracy on both clean and adversarially perturbed data

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: Causal disentanglement improves adversarial robustness by decorrelating causal and confounder signals
- Mechanism: When a model learns to separate causal features from spurious confounder features, adversarial attacks targeting the confounder signal become less effective as the decorrelation reduces reliance on spurious correlations
- Core assumption: Adversarial attacks primarily exploit spurious correlations between confounders and labels, not causal relationships
- Evidence anchors: [abstract] "causal disentanglement should make models more robust against such attacks", [section 2] "adversarial attacks exploit spurious or non-causal correlations"

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: High pixel-level information content in the confounder signal degrades adversarial robustness
- Mechanism: If the confounder signal retains too much low-level pixel information, it provides attackers with more opportunities to craft perturbations that exploit these details
- Core assumption: Low-level pixel information in the confounder signal is more vulnerable to adversarial perturbations than higher-level semantic information
- Evidence anchors: [abstract] "moderate negative association between the pixel-level information content of the confounder signal and adversarial robustness (r=-0.597, p=0.040)", [section 5] "high pixel-level information in the confounder signal s in terms of 1 − BoI(X, S) is moderately associated with relative adversarial performance degradation"

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: Causal signal informativeness (distance correlation with input) is positively associated with both clean and adversarial accuracy
- Mechanism: A causal signal that is highly correlated with the input image captures more relevant features for classification, making the model's task easier
- Core assumption: The causal signal is used directly for classification, so its informativeness is crucial for accurate predictions
- Evidence anchors: [section 5] "high distance correlation between the causal signal and the input signals is associated with higher classification accuracy", [section 5] "PCC between M2 and a model's clean test classification accuracy ac is r = 0.741"

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map: Input → Separation → Intervention → Classification
- Critical path: The separation mechanism must cleanly split the signal, the intervention mechanism must approximate the backdoor adjustment effectively, and the classification must rely primarily on the causal signal
- Design tradeoffs: Balancing causal/confounder separation vs. information preservation, choosing between pixel-level vs. semantic-level confounder signal content, deciding on the complexity of the intervention mechanism
- Failure signatures: High correlation between causal and confounder signals (DC(C,S) close to 1), low classification accuracy on clean data, high performance drop under adversarial attacks, overfitting during training

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- Question: Does the decorrelation of causal and confounder signals have a causal effect on adversarial robustness, or is it merely correlated with other factors that drive robustness?
- Basis in paper: The paper finds a strong positive association between decorrelation of causal and confounder signals and adversarial robustness, but acknowledges this is correlational
- Why unresolved: The study only measures association, not causation; other unmeasured factors could drive both decorrelation and robustness
- What evidence would resolve it: Experiments that actively manipulate decorrelation levels and measure the resulting changes in robustness would establish causality

### Open Question 2
- Question: Is there an optimal level of confounder signal informativeness for maximizing adversarial robustness, or does this depend on the specific task and dataset?
- Basis in paper: The paper finds a moderate negative association between confounder signal pixel-level information and robustness, but notes this contradicts some previous findings about the benefits of informative style signals
- Why unresolved: The study only measures associations across models, not within a single model varying confounder informativeness; different tasks may have different optimal confounder properties
- What evidence would resolve it: Controlled experiments varying the information content of confounder signals within a single model architecture across multiple tasks and datasets

### Open Question 3
- Question: How do the findings about causal disentanglement and robustness generalize to more complex real-world datasets and modern neural architectures beyond ResNet18?
- Basis in paper: The study is limited to three relatively simple benchmark datasets and a common ResNet18 backbone
- Why unresolved: The results may not scale to more complex, high-dimensional data or different architectural choices that could affect disentanglement
- What evidence would resolve it: Replicating the study with diverse datasets of varying complexity and modern architectures like Vision Transformers or more advanced causal neural networks

## Limitations
- Reliance on simplified datasets (MNTP, CIFAR10, CIFAR100) which may not capture real-world causal disentanglement complexity
- Assumes a binary causal/confounder signal structure which may oversimplify more complex causal scenarios
- Decorrelation metrics measure statistical independence but may not capture all aspects of true causal disentanglement

## Confidence
- High confidence in the positive correlation between causal/confounder decorrelation and adversarial robustness (r=0.820, p=0.001)
- Medium confidence in the negative association between confounder pixel information and robustness (r=-0.597, p=0.040)
- Medium confidence in the causal disentanglement mechanism explanation, pending validation on more complex datasets

## Next Checks
1. Test the observed relationships on more complex datasets like ImageNet or domain-specific datasets where causal relationships are less obvious
2. Evaluate whether the observed effects hold under different adversarial attack strategies, particularly those that might target causal features rather than confounders
3. Conduct ablation studies to isolate whether the decorrelation effect or the specific architectural choices drive the robustness improvements
