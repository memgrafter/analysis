# Tailoring Language Generation Models under Total Variation Distance

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2302.13344
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2302.13344
- Reference count: 40
- Key outcome: Introduces TaiLr, a training objective using total variation distance to reduce text degeneration in language generation by downweighting low-quality training samples

## Executive Summary
This paper addresses the critical problem of text degeneration in neural language generation, where models systematically overestimate the probability of corrupted text sequences during autoregressive decoding. The authors propose TaiLr, a novel training objective that leverages total variation distance (TVD) to downweight low-quality training samples and reduce overestimation. By introducing a token-level weighting factor based on the model's probability and a tunable hyperparameter γ to balance bias-variance tradeoff, TaiLr improves generation quality across multiple tasks while alleviating the overestimation of degenerated sequences without sacrificing diversity.

## Method Summary
TaiLr is a training objective that modifies the standard maximum likelihood estimation by incorporating total variation distance (TVD) to downweight low-quality training samples. The key innovation is a token-level weighting factor that depends on the model's probability and a hyperparameter γ that interpolates between one-hot and model distributions. This creates a γ-mixture proxy distribution that balances bias and variance in estimating TVD. The objective is optimized using an upper bound on sequence-level TVD with token-level factorization, allowing efficient training of autoregressive models while maintaining TVD's robustness to outliers.

## Key Results
- TaiLr reduces overestimation of degenerated sequences in synthetic experiments with LSTM models on COCO image captions
- Achieves 3.5 BLEU improvement on IWSLT14 German-English translation while maintaining diversity
- Improves long text generation quality on WritingPrompts dataset with better perplexity and distinct-n scores

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
The total variation distance (TVD) reduces overestimation of degenerated sequences by preventing the model from placing probability mass where the data distribution has zero probability. TVD measures the absolute difference between two distributions averaging at each point. When the model overestimates a sequence (assigns it higher probability than the real data), the TVD gradient becomes zero, stopping further increase in that region.

### Mechanism 2
The TaiLr objective balances the bias-variance tradeoff in estimating TVD by introducing a tunable hyperparameter γ that interpolates between one-hot and model distributions. TaiLr uses a γ-mixture proxy distribution that combines the one-hot distribution (unbiased but high variance) with the model distribution (biased but low variance). As training progresses, γ can be adjusted to reduce variance early on and bias later.

### Mechanism 3
The token-level factorization of TVD allows autoregressive language models to be trained with position-wise weighting of log-likelihood. By developing an upper bound on sequence-level TVD with token-level factorization, the loss can be computed at each position given the prefix, maintaining the efficient training structure of MLE while incorporating TVD's robustness.

- Component map: Autoregressive model (Transformer/LSTM) -> TaiLr objective with γ-parameterized proxy distribution -> Weighted log-likelihood loss -> Backpropagation

- Critical path:

  3. Backpropagate gradients through the weighted log-likelihood
  4. Tune γ and bm based on validation performance
  5. Evaluate on test set with standard generation metrics

- Design tradeoffs:
  - Pros: Reduces overestimation of degenerated sequences, improves generation quality, maintains diversity
  - Cons: Requires tuning of γ and bm, may be sensitive to dataset entropy
  - Alternatives: Loss truncation (binary sequence-level weights), Unlikelihood training (penalizes specific patterns), GOLD (off-policy RL with importance weights)

- Failure signatures:
  - High variance in early training stages (γ too close to 1)
  - Slow convergence or underfitting (γ too small)
  - Overly short generations (loss truncation baseline)
  - Low diversity and high repetition (GOLD baseline)

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

The paper does not explicitly call out specific open questions, but based on the discussion, several important questions remain:

- How does TaiLr perform on generation tasks with different data characteristics, such as low-resource languages, highly specialized domains, or noisy data?
- How does the TaiLr objective compare to other recently proposed objectives that aim to address the text degeneration problem, such as nucleus sampling or top-k sampling?
- How does the TaiLr objective perform on other natural language generation tasks beyond machine translation, text summarization, and long text generation, such as dialogue generation, question answering, or code generation?

## Limitations

- The effectiveness of TVD relies heavily on the assumption that corrupted sequences have zero probability under the real data distribution, which may not hold for all real-world datasets
- The method requires tuning of hyperparameters γ and bm, with optimal values varying significantly across different tasks and datasets
- Computational overhead from the proxy distribution and weighting computations could become significant for very large models or extensive hyperparameter tuning

## Confidence

**High Confidence**: The core theoretical derivation of the TVD upper bound and its token-level factorization appears sound, with well-established mathematical framework for connecting TVD to autoregressive training objective.

**Medium Confidence**: Empirical results show consistent improvements across tasks, but magnitude of gains varies considerably (3.5 BLEU on IWSLT14 vs more modest gains on Gigaword), suggesting method may be more beneficial for certain types of generation tasks.

**Low Confidence**: Comparison with alternative methods like Unlikelihood training and GOLD could be more comprehensive, without investigating scenarios where these methods might be preferable or how they compare in terms of training stability.
