# Provably Efficient Model-Free Algorithms for Non-stationary CMDPs

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2303.05733
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.05733
- Reference count: 40
- Primary result: First model-free, simulator-free RL algorithms with sublinear regret and zero constraint violation for non-stationary CMDPs

## Executive Summary
This paper addresses the challenge of reinforcement learning in episodic non-stationary constrained Markov Decision Processes (CMDPs), where both the reward and constraint functions can change over time. The authors propose novel model-free algorithms that achieve sublinear regret and zero constraint violation without requiring a simulator or prior knowledge of the non-stationarity. The key innovation is a restart strategy that divides the learning process into frames, combined with an optimism bonus term to handle the changing environment. The algorithms are analyzed in both tabular and linear function approximation settings, providing theoretical guarantees on their performance.

## Method Summary
The proposed approach introduces a restart strategy that partitions the total episodes into frames, allowing the algorithm to adapt to changes in the environment. Within each frame, the algorithm maintains an optimistic estimate of the value function, incorporating an additional bonus term to account for non-stationarity. This bonus term is derived from the variation budget, which quantifies the extent of changes in the MDP over time. The algorithm updates its estimates at the beginning of each frame based on the observed data, ensuring that the regret and constraint violation remain bounded. In the linear function approximation setting, the algorithm leverages the structure of the feature representation to achieve better sample complexity.

## Key Results
- Achieves regret bound of O(H^4 S^{1/2} A^{1/2} B^{1/3} K^{4/5}) in tabular setting with zero constraint violation
- Achieves regret bound of O(K^{3/4} H^{9/4} d^{5/4} B^{1/4}) in linear function approximation setting with zero constraint violation
- Proposes a general framework for addressing non-stationarity without requiring prior knowledge of the variation budget

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The restart strategy allows the algorithm to adapt to changes in the environment by periodically updating its estimates. The optimism bonus term counteracts the non-stationarity by encouraging exploration in regions of the state-action space that may have changed. This combination ensures that the algorithm maintains a balance between exploration and exploitation, leading to sublinear regret and zero constraint violation.

- Optimism in the face of uncertainty: A principle in RL that encourages exploration by maintaining optimistic estimates of the value function. Needed to ensure that the algorithm explores regions of the state-action space that may have changed due to non-stationarity. Quick check: Verify that the optimism bonus term is properly scaled with the variation budget.
- Linear function approximation: A method for representing value functions using a linear combination of features. Needed to handle large or continuous state spaces. Quick check: Verify that the feature representation satisfies the necessary assumptions for the regret bound.

## Architecture Onboarding
Component map: Frame initialization -> Optimism bonus calculation -> Value function update -> Policy execution
Critical path: The algorithm alternates between executing the current policy and updating the value function estimates at the beginning of each frame.
Design tradeoffs: The restart strategy balances the need for adaptation with the computational cost of frequent updates. The optimism bonus term trades off exploration with the risk of overestimating the value function.
Failure signatures: If the variation budget is underestimated, the algorithm may fail to adapt to significant changes in the environment. If the optimism bonus term is not properly scaled, the algorithm may either explore too much or too little.
First experiments: 1) Verify that the algorithm achieves sublinear regret and zero constraint violation in a simple non-stationary CMDP. 2) Test the sensitivity of the algorithm to different variation budgets. 3) Compare the performance of the algorithm with and without the optimism bonus term.

## Limitations
- The algorithms assume access to the variation budget, which may not be available in practice.
- The theoretical bounds do not provide insights into the empirical performance of the algorithms on real-world datasets or complex simulated environments.
- The framework for addressing non-stationarity is abstract and lacks specific implementation details.
