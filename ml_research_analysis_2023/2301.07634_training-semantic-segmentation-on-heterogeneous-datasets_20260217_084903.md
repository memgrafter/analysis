# Training Semantic Segmentation on Heterogeneous Datasets

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2301.07634
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2301.07634
- Authors: [Redacted]
- Reference count: 40
- Key outcome: Introduces HTSS framework enabling semantic segmentation training on heterogeneous datasets with different label spaces and supervision types, achieving improved performance on seen datasets, better generalization to unseen datasets, and increased knowledgeability across hundreds of semantic classes.

## Executive Summary
This paper addresses the challenge of training semantic segmentation models on heterogeneous datasets with conflicting label spaces and mixed supervision types (pixel-level, bounding boxes, image tags). The proposed Heterogeneous Training of Semantic Segmentation (HTSS) framework introduces semantic atoms as fine-grained semantic primitives that enable consolidation of diverse label spaces into a unified taxonomy. By transforming weak annotations into per-pixel pseudo-labels and training a single model across all datasets simultaneously, HTSS achieves significant performance improvements on seen datasets, demonstrates strong generalization to unseen datasets, and substantially increases the number of recognizable semantic concepts.

## Method Summary
The HTSS framework trains a single semantic segmentation model across multiple heterogeneous datasets by first constructing a unified taxonomy of semantic atoms that resolves label space conflicts. Weak annotations (bounding boxes, image tags) are converted to per-pixel pseudo-labels through specialized generators, then refined during training. The model uses a single backbone (ResNet-50 with PSP module) and classifier over the unified semantic atom space, trained with a combination of cross-entropy losses for strong labels and conditional cross-entropy for weak labels. This approach enables simultaneous training on datasets with disjoint or conflicting label spaces while maintaining inference capability over the entire semantic space.

## Key Results
- HTSS outperforms single-dataset baselines on seen datasets (Cityscapes, Vistas, IDD) by 2-5% mIoU
- Demonstrates strong zero-shot generalization to unseen datasets (WildDash, KITTI) with performance gains of 1-4% mIoU
- Increases knowledgeability metric from 100 to 172 recognizable semantic concepts with sufficient segmentation quality
- Achieves 9× increase in training data volume through dataset aggregation

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1: Dataset Aggregation
HTSS improves segmentation performance on seen datasets by aggregating more training examples across multiple heterogeneous datasets. By combining pixel-labeled datasets with conflicting label spaces into a unified taxonomy, the model gains access to 9× more images and resolves semantic conflicts via semantic atoms, allowing joint training on a richer set of examples. Core assumption: The union of datasets does not introduce label space conflicts that degrade performance. Break condition: If label space conflicts are not properly resolved, the model may become confused and performance may degrade.

### Mechanism 2: Domain Diversity for Generalization
HTSS improves generalization to unseen datasets by increasing dataset diversity and semantic coverage. Training on multiple datasets with different domains exposes the model to varied visual contexts, enabling better zero-shot transfer to unseen datasets. Core assumption: Diversity in training data leads to better generalization. Break condition: If the added datasets introduce domain shifts that are too large, the model may overfit to source domains and perform poorly on target domains.

### Mechanism 3: Weak Supervision Integration
HTSS increases the number of recognizable semantic concepts by incorporating fine-grained classes from weakly-labeled datasets. By transforming weak annotations into per-pixel pseudo-labels and combining them with strong labels, the model learns to segment finer semantic concepts that are absent in pixel-labeled datasets. Core assumption: Weak supervision can be effectively converted to strong supervision without losing spatial accuracy. Break condition: If pseudo-label generation introduces too much noise, the model may learn incorrect boundaries and fail to recognize fine-grained concepts.

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map: Input images -> Backbone (ResNet-50) -> PSP feature extractor -> Classifier over semantic atoms -> Output segmentation map
- Critical path: 1. Load and preprocess heterogeneous datasets 2. Generate unified taxonomy of semantic atoms 3. Convert weak annotations to pseudo-labels 4. Train single-backbone FCN with mixed supervision 5. Evaluate on seen, unseen, and knowledgeability metrics
- Design tradeoffs:
  - Memory vs. batch size: Larger batch sizes improve optimization but require smaller image sizes due to GPU memory constraints
  - Semantic granularity vs. label space conflicts: Finer-grained classes increase knowledgeability but may introduce more conflicts that need resolution
  - Weak supervision quality vs. training stability: High-quality pseudo-labels improve performance, but noisy pseudo-labels may degrade it
- Failure signatures:
  - Performance degradation on seen datasets: Likely due to unresolved label space conflicts or poor pseudo-label quality
  - Poor generalization to unseen datasets: Likely due to insufficient domain diversity in training data
  - Low knowledgeability: Likely due to ineffective integration of weakly-labeled datasets or overly conservative pseudo-label refinement
- First 3 experiments:
  1. Train HTSS on Cityscapes + Vistas + IDD with conflicting label spaces and evaluate on seen and unseen datasets
  2. Train HTSS on Cityscapes (pixel) + ECP (bbox) and evaluate on underrepresented classes (e.g., vulnerable road users)
  3. Train HTSS on Cityscapes + Cityscapes Traffic Signs + MTS and evaluate on traffic sign subclasses

## Limitations

- Semantic atom construction methodology is partially specified and relies on external lexical databases without providing reproducible mapping procedures
- Performance improvements on seen datasets are well-supported, but generalization claims to unseen datasets show more variable results with limited statistical significance testing
- Knowledgeability metric, while conceptually sound, lacks comparison against established semantic richness metrics

## Confidence
