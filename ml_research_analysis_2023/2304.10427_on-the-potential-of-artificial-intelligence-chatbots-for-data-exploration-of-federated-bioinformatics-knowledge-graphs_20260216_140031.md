# On the Potential of Artificial Intelligence Chatbots for Data Exploration of Federated Bioinformatics Knowledge Graphs

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2304.10427
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2304.10427
- Reference count: 13

## Executive Summary
This paper explores the potential of ChatGPT in facilitating data access to federated knowledge graphs, particularly in the field of bioinformatics. The authors present examples of how ChatGPT can be used to summarize dataset contents, explain queries, and generate federated queries across public knowledge graphs based on natural language questions. The results show that ChatGPT can provide clear and understandable summaries of dataset contents, and can explain both single dataset and federated queries with a high degree of accuracy. However, the authors also highlight the limitations of ChatGPT, including the problem of hallucinations and the difficulty of debugging answers. Despite these limitations, the authors argue that ChatGPT can support domain experts in exploring the available data, and suggest that further research is needed to improve the technology and address its limitations.

## Method Summary
The paper uses ChatGPT (GPT-4, March 14th free research version) without fine-tuning to explore its potential in generating and explaining SPARQL queries for federated knowledge graphs in bioinformatics. The method involves testing ChatGPT's ability to summarize dataset contents, explain existing SPARQL queries, and generate new queries from natural language questions using public bioinformatics knowledge graphs (UniProt, OMA, Bgee). The authors evaluate ChatGPT's performance based on the accuracy and executability of the generated queries and the quality of the explanations provided.

## Key Results
- ChatGPT can generate high-level dataset summaries that are clear and understandable to end users.
- ChatGPT can explain existing SPARQL queries with reasonable accuracy.
- ChatGPT can generate SPARQL queries from natural language questions, even for federated queries, though not always directly executable.

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map: User question -> ChatGPT -> SPARQL query generation -> SPARQL endpoints (UniProt, OMA, Bgee) -> Query execution -> Result presentation
- Critical path: User question → ChatGPT processing → SPARQL query generation → Query execution → Result presentation. The most critical step is accurate query generation from natural language.
- Design tradeoffs: ChatGPT offers flexibility in understanding natural language but lacks precision with identifiers and may generate suboptimal queries. Traditional QA systems are more precise but less flexible in handling natural language.
- Failure signatures: Common failures include hallucinations in explanations, incorrect SPARQL endpoint URLs, wrong identifier mappings, and queries that don't execute due to syntax errors or semantic mismatches.
- First 3 experiments:
  1. Test ChatGPT's ability to summarize a well-documented dataset like UniProt using a simple natural language prompt.
  2. Provide ChatGPT with a basic SPARQL query and ask it to explain the query's purpose and structure.
  3. Ask ChatGPT to generate a simple SPARQL query from a natural language question about a single dataset, then test the query's executability.

## Limitations
- The evaluation is limited to a small set of hand-crafted examples without systematic benchmarking.
- The paper does not address robustness to diverse user inputs, error recovery, or reproducibility of results.
- Hallucinations and identifier mismatches are acknowledged as major failure modes, yet the authors do not propose concrete mitigation strategies.

## Confidence
- **High** - ChatGPT can generate high-level dataset summaries when the underlying data is well-documented.
- **Medium** - ChatGPT can explain SPARQL queries with reasonable accuracy, but results may vary with query complexity.
- **Medium** - ChatGPT can produce executable SPARQL queries from natural language, though performance drops on federated queries and complex reasoning tasks.
- **Low** - The ability to debug or verify ChatGPT-generated queries is limited, making the system unsuitable for critical data exploration without manual oversight.
