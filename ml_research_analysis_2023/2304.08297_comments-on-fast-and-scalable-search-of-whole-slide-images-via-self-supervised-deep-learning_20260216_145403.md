# Comments on 'Fast and scalable search of whole-slide images via self-supervised deep learning'

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2304.08297
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2304.08297

## Executive Summary
This commentary critically examines the SISH method presented in "Fast and scalable search of whole-slide images via self-supervised deep learning." The authors argue that SISH represents an incremental modification of the previously published Yottixel approach, lacking proper attribution and transparent reporting. The commentary highlights issues with citation practices, methodological comparisons, and evaluation metrics, emphasizing the need for rigorous standards in advancing medical image search research.

## Method Summary
The commentary analyzes the SISH method, which employs mosaic generation and MinMax binarization techniques for whole-slide image search. The method uses autoencoder-based embeddings and self-supervised learning concepts, but the authors argue these are not truly novel approaches. The commentary questions the fairness of comparing SISH's autoencoder embeddings with Yottixel's pre-trained DenseNet features, and criticizes the use of macro-averaging for evaluation metrics in an unbalanced dataset.

## Key Results
- SISH uses Yottixel's mosaic generation concept without adequate acknowledgment
- MinMax binarization employed without citing original works where the technique was introduced
- Self-supervised image search terminology is questioned as a misnomer
- Unfair comparison between autoencoder embeddings and pre-trained DenseNet features
- Macro-averaging in unbalanced datasets deviates from standard top-k evaluation practices

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The commentary does not provide a mechanism explanation for why SISH works, as its focus is on critiquing the method's originality and presentation rather than explaining its functionality.
