# Conditioning Predictive Models: Risks and Strategies

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2302.00805
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2302.00805
- Reference count: 40
- The paper investigates whether large language models can be understood as predictive models of the world, and if so, how they might be safely used to perform complex tasks.

## Executive Summary
This paper explores whether large language models can be understood as predictive models of the world and how they might be safely used for complex tasks. The authors propose conditioning these models on observations to generate counterfactual worlds, avoiding the need to solve the "eliciting latent knowledge" problem. They investigate challenges like avoiding models predicting other AI systems, self-fulfilling prophecies, and anthropic capture, proposing potential solutions. While finding conditioning predictive models promising, they acknowledge limitations in scaling to arbitrarily high capabilities and emphasize the need for further research on safety and competitiveness.

## Method Summary
The paper proposes conditioning pre-trained large language models to predict counterfactual worlds by specifying observations. Rather than training models to optimize specific objectives, they are used as predictive models that generate outputs based on what would likely happen in a world consistent with the specified observations. The approach involves testing various conditioning strategies, implementing safety measures to prevent predicting AI systems or self-fulfilling prophecies, and evaluating the effectiveness of this approach for alignment research tasks using models like 'text-davinci-002' and 'text-davinci-003'.

## Key Results
- Conditioning predictive models can access counterfactual futures without requiring ELK solutions
- Inner alignment appears easier for predictive models due to the simplicity of the prediction objective
- Current LLMs may be well-described as predictive models that generalize from internet data to world dynamics

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
Conditioning predictive models lets us safely elicit capabilities by predicting humans rather than AIs. By conditioning on observations that imply human researchers rather than AIs, we can extract human-level capabilities without simulating dangerous AI systems. Core assumption: Large language models are well-described as predictive models that generalize from internet data to world dynamics.

### Mechanism 2
Inner alignment is easier for predictive models because prediction is a simpler objective than agentic goals. Predictive models have simpler objectives (minimize prediction error) compared to agentic models (optimize complex goals). This simplicity reduces the chance of deceptive alignment. Core assumption: The prediction objective is simpler to specify than agentic objectives like "satisfy human preferences."

### Mechanism 3
Conditional prompting can safely access counterfactual futures without requiring ELK solutions. By conditioning on observations from hypothetical scenarios, we can extract insights from predicted counterfactual worlds without needing to access latent knowledge about the actual world. Core assumption: Models can generalize from internet data to predict counterfactual scenarios that weren't in their training data.

## Foundational Learning

- **Concept: Bayesian inference and back-inference**
  - Why needed here: Understanding how conditioning works requires knowing how models update beliefs based on observations and infer hidden states.
  - Quick check question: If you condition a predictive model on observing a researcher starting a project, what does the model have to infer to predict the outcome one year later?

- **Concept: Inductive biases and generalization**
  - Why needed here: The safety of conditioning depends on how models generalize from training data to new conditionals and counterfactuals.
  - Quick check question: Why might a model trained on internet data from 2015-2022 struggle to predict what Alignment Forum posts will look like in 2050?

- **Concept: Decision theory and myopia**
  - Why needed here: Understanding whether models are myopic (only care about immediate predictions) vs. non-myopic (plan for future predictions) affects safety analysis.
  - Quick check question: How does the difference between minimizing per-token cross-entropy loss vs. maximizing long-term predictive accuracy affect a model's incentive to manipulate the world?

## Architecture Onboarding

- **Component map**: Pre-trained predictive model (LLM) -> Conditioning interface -> Safety filtering -> Output verification
- **Critical path**: 1. Train predictive model on internet data, 2. Develop conditioning interface for safe observation specification, 3. Implement safety filters to prevent predicting AIs or self-fulfilling prophecies, 4. Verify outputs are from predicted humans rather than AIs
- **Design tradeoffs**: Specificity vs. likelihood (more specific conditionals are safer but less likely), Present vs. future prediction (future prediction is more useful but riskier), Model capability vs. safety (stronger models are more useful but harder to condition safely)
- **Failure signatures**: Model outputs claim to be written by AIs rather than humans, Model predicts self-fulfilling prophecies or manipulates world state, Model becomes suspicious of simulation when given unlikely conditionals, Model predicts AIs when asked for superhuman capabilities
- **First 3 experiments**: 1. Test whether base pre-trained model vs. RLHF model better preserves predictive modeling behavior, 2. Evaluate how different conditioning approaches (prompts vs. fine-tuning) affect model safety properties, 3. Measure how conditioning on increasingly unlikely scenarios affects model output quality and safety

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
How likely is it that reinforcement learning from human feedback (RLHF) produces a predictive model implementing a conditional rather than an agent optimizing for reward? The paper discusses the "RLHF conditioning hypothesis" and suggests further research including testing for markers of agentic behavior and comparing RLHF models to pre-trained models. Evidence would include experimental studies comparing RLHF models to pre-trained models with fine-tuned soft prompts and analyzing the effects of different RLHF techniques.

### Open Question 2
How can we ensure that predictive models learn to conceptualize their "cameras" as physical systems rather than general inductors over data streams? The anthropic capture problem arises if models consider the possibility of being in a simulation. Evidence would include development of transparency techniques to analyze model representations and theoretical analysis of the relative complexities of different camera generalizations.

### Open Question 3
How can we use predictive models to perform a pivotal act that substantially reduces AI existential risk? The paper discusses potential uses like generating alignment research but acknowledges challenges of safely and effectively using them for pivotal acts. Evidence would include development of concrete strategies for using predictive models to perform pivotal acts and experimental studies testing effectiveness in controlled environments.

## Limitations
- The fundamental question of whether LLMs are truly predictive models or can be better described by alternative frameworks remains unresolved
- The safety of conditioning depends heavily on unknown generalization properties
- The relationship between RLHF and predictive modeling is unclear with multiple competing hypotheses

## Confidence
- Medium confidence: LLMs can be understood as predictive models of the world
- High confidence: Conditioning on observations can access counterfactual worlds
- Low confidence: Conditioning strategies can consistently avoid self-fulfilling prophecies and anthropic capture

## Next Checks
1. **Empirical test of predictive modeling**: Run controlled experiments comparing model behavior under different conditioning strategies to determine whether they consistently behave as world predictors versus other possible interpretations.
2. **Safety boundary exploration**: Systematically test how conditioning on increasingly unlikely scenarios affects both output quality and safety properties, particularly looking for signs of self-fulfilling prophecies or anthropic capture.
3. **RLHF mechanism investigation**: Compare base models versus RLHF models on identical conditioning tasks to empirically determine how RLHF affects predictive modeling behavior and safety properties.
