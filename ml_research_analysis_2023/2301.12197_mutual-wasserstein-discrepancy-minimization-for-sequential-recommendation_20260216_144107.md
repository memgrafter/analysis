# Mutual Wasserstein Discrepancy Minimization for Sequential Recommendation

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2301.12197
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2301.12197
- Reference count: 40
- Primary result: Achieves 2.24%-20.10% improvements over state-of-the-art baselines on four benchmark datasets

## Executive Summary
This paper introduces MStein, a self-supervised learning framework for sequential recommendation that addresses key limitations in existing contrastive learning methods. The core innovation is using Wasserstein Discrepancy Measurement (WDM) instead of KL-divergence for mutual information estimation between augmented sequences. MStein leverages the 2-Wasserstein distance, which is symmetric, more robust to small batch sizes, and can model uncertainty in stochastic augmentation processes. Extensive experiments on four Amazon datasets demonstrate significant performance improvements across multiple metrics including Recall@N, NDCG@N, and MRR.

## Method Summary
MStein builds upon STOSA's stochastic embeddings to model user-item interactions as distributions rather than deterministic vectors. The framework uses Wasserstein self-attention to compute item similarities via 2-Wasserstein distance, then applies a novel WDM loss that maximizes mutual information between augmented sequence views. Data augmentation generates perturbed views through techniques like random masking and cropping, while the WDM loss computes symmetric distance measurements that are more stable and efficient than KL-divergence alternatives. The model jointly optimizes recommendation accuracy and self-supervised learning objectives through a weighted combination of the two loss functions.

## Key Results
- Achieves 20.10% improvement in Recall@20 on Amazon Beauty dataset compared to strongest baseline
- Demonstrates 2.24%-7.31% improvements across all metrics on multiple benchmark datasets
- Shows superior performance with smaller batch sizes (32-512) compared to KL-divergence methods requiring larger batches
- Exhibits robustness against perturbations with only minor performance degradation under noise injection

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
The Wasserstein Discrepancy Measurement addresses KL-divergence's asymmetry problem by using symmetric 2-Wasserstein distance. Unlike KL-divergence which requires computing both DKL(p(x_a)||p(x_b)) and DKL(p(x_b)||p(x_a)) for symmetry, WDM naturally provides symmetric measurements between augmented sequence distributions. This is crucial for contrastive learning where we need to maximize similarity between views from the same user.

### Mechanism 2
WDM improves training stability through Lipschitz continuity approximation. The Wasserstein self-attention module and WDM loss together create approximately Lipschitz continuous functions that are more robust to perturbations and provide stable gradients during training. This leads to better handling of noisy interactions and improved generalization.

### Mechanism 3
WDM enables exact optimization of alignment and uniformity properties critical for contrastive learning. The WDM loss decomposes into alignment (minimizing distances between positive pairs) and uniformity (maximizing distances between negative pairs) components, exactly optimizing these properties rather than asymptotically. This provides better theoretical grounding for the contrastive objective.

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map:
  STOSA Encoder -> Stochastic embeddings (mean and covariance)
  Data Augmentation -> Two perturbed views of sequence
  WDM Loss -> Compute Wasserstein distances between augmented pairs
  Backpropagation -> Update model parameters
  Inference -> Compute distances to all items -> Generate recommendations

- Critical path:
  1. Input sequence → STOSA Encoder → Stochastic embeddings
  2. Data augmentation → Two perturbed views of the same sequence
  3. WDM Loss → Compute Wasserstein distances between augmented pairs
  4. Backpropagation → Update model parameters
  5. Inference → Compute distances to all items → Generate recommendations

- Design tradeoffs:
  - Stochastic vs deterministic embeddings: Stochastic embeddings model uncertainty but increase computational complexity
  - Batch size: Smaller batch sizes work better with WDM but may require more training epochs
  - Augmentation strength: Stronger augmentations provide more diverse views but risk breaking sequential correlations

- Failure signatures:
  - Training instability: Large gradient fluctuations or NaN losses
  - Poor performance: Similar performance to baseline methods or worse than deterministic approaches
  - Overfitting: Excellent validation performance but poor test performance

- First 3 experiments:
  1. Ablation study: Compare MStein with and without WDM loss to isolate its contribution
  2. Batch size sensitivity: Evaluate performance across different batch sizes to validate WDM's efficiency
  3. Robustness test: Introduce varying levels of noise to sequences and measure performance degradation

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- Question: How does MStein's performance scale with even larger batch sizes beyond those tested?
- Basis in paper: The paper shows sensitivity analysis to batch sizes but doesn't test extremely large batch sizes
- Why unresolved: The experiments only tested batch sizes up to 1024, leaving the question of how MStein would perform with industrial-scale batch sizes
- What evidence would resolve it: Additional experiments with batch sizes in the 2048-16384 range, comparing MStein's performance and training efficiency against other methods

### Open Question 2
- Question: How does MStein perform on datasets with different sequential patterns, such as those with longer or more irregular time gaps between interactions?
- Basis in paper: The paper uses Amazon review datasets which may have relatively regular interaction patterns, but doesn't test on datasets with varied temporal characteristics
- Why unresolved: The current experiments focus on timestamp-ordered interactions from e-commerce, which may not represent all sequential recommendation scenarios
- What evidence would resolve it: Experiments on datasets like music streaming (with daily/weekly patterns) or news recommendations (with rapidly changing content), comparing MStein's ability to capture different temporal dynamics

### Open Question 3
- Question: What is the computational overhead of MStein's Wasserstein distance calculations compared to standard contrastive learning methods?
- Basis in paper: The paper mentions theoretical advantages but doesn't provide runtime or memory usage comparisons
- Why unresolved: The paper focuses on recommendation quality metrics but doesn't report training/inference time or memory requirements, which are crucial for practical deployment
- What evidence would resolve it: Detailed runtime benchmarks comparing MStein's training time per epoch, inference latency, and memory consumption against baseline methods on the same hardware

## Limitations

- The theoretical advantages of WDM over KL-divergence lack extensive mathematical justification beyond empirical demonstrations
- The exact formulation of alignment and uniformity decomposition from WDM loss remains somewhat abstract with limited mathematical derivation
- The choice of β hyperparameter and its impact on different datasets is not thoroughly explored

## Confidence

- High confidence in the empirical performance claims (20.10% improvement over baselines) based on extensive experiments across four datasets
- Medium confidence in the theoretical advantages of WDM over KL-divergence, as the mathematical proofs are incomplete
- Low confidence in the generalizability of results to non-Amazon datasets and other recommendation domains
