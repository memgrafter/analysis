# Evaluating and Improving the Coreference Capabilities of Machine Translation Models

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2302.08464
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2302.08464
- Reference count: 14
- MT models consistently underperform state-of-the-art monolingual coreference resolvers on coreference tasks

## Executive Summary
This paper addresses the critical gap in evaluating and improving coreference resolution capabilities of machine translation (MT) models. The authors develop an automatic evaluation methodology that derives coreference clusters from MT output by analyzing target-language morphological gender agreement, eliminating the need for target-language annotations. They evaluate commercial and open-source MT systems across six target languages and three benchmarks, finding consistent underperformance compared to monolingual coreference resolvers. To improve performance, they fine-tune MT models on augmented data requiring coreference resolution, either implicitly through sentences with non-singleton clusters or explicitly with inline coreference markers, achieving significant improvements in target-side consistency while maintaining translation quality.

## Method Summary
The paper proposes a two-pronged approach to address coreference in MT. First, it develops an evaluation methodology that maps target-language morphological gender agreement back to source-side coreference clusters, enabling automatic assessment of MT models' implicit coreference resolution abilities without requiring target-language annotations. Second, it fine-tunes MT models on augmented data specifically designed to require coreference resolution. The implicit approach uses sentences with non-singleton coreference clusters, while the explicit approach adds inline coreference markers to source sentences. Both approaches are evaluated on three benchmarks (Wino-X, WinoMT, BUG) across six target languages (Arabic, German, Spanish, Hebrew, Russian, French).

## Key Results
- MT models consistently underperform state-of-the-art monolingual coreference resolvers on target-side consistency metrics
- Fine-tuning on coreference-requiring data significantly improves MT models' implicit coreference resolution abilities
- Explicit coreference markers in source sentences provide stronger signal than implicit exposure alone
- Improvements in coreference performance are achieved without degrading overall translation quality

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: MT models can be evaluated for coreference resolution by mapping target-language morphological gender agreement back to source-side coreference clusters
- Mechanism: The paper observes that in many languages, grammatical gender agreement between pronouns and their antecedents is mandatory. By analyzing target-language gender morphology, we can infer which source entity the pronoun refers to, effectively reconstructing source-side coreference clusters without target-language annotations
- Core assumption: Target-language gender agreement reliably reflects source-side coreference decisions made by the MT model
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "develops an evaluation methodology that derives coreference clusters from MT output and evaluates them without requiring annotations in the target language"
  - [section] "we formulate a new metric quantifying the ability of the MT model to implicitly resolve source-side coreference (henceforth, Target-side Consistency), defined as the proportion of instances in which the morphological gender of an entity matches that of its referring pronoun in the target language T"
  - [corpus] Weak - only 5 related papers found, none directly supporting this specific mechanism
- Break condition: If target language doesn't encode grammatical gender, or if gender agreement doesn't reliably indicate coreference (e.g., in pro-drop languages where pronouns can be omitted)

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: Fine-tuning MT models on data requiring explicit coreference resolution improves their implicit coreference capabilities
- Mechanism: The paper proposes two data augmentation approaches: (1) Coref data with all sentences having non-singleton clusters, and (2) Gender data subset with gendered pronouns. By exposing MT models to more instances requiring coreference decisions during fine-tuning, the models learn to better handle coreference implicitly
- Core assumption: MT models can learn coreference resolution implicitly from increased exposure to coreference-requiring instances
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "fine-tune MT models on augmented data requiring coreference resolution, either implicitly (using sentences with non-singleton clusters) or explicitly (adding inline coreference markers)"
  - [section] "Our implicit approach consists of fine-tuning MT models on texts that specifically require many coreference decisions, thus exposing the model to more implicit coreference signal"
  - [corpus] Weak - related papers discuss coreference resolution but not specifically MT fine-tuning approaches
- Break condition: If the fine-tuning data is too noisy or doesn't contain sufficient coreference-requiring instances, or if the model capacity is insufficient to learn from the augmented data

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: Adding explicit coreference markers to source sentences during fine-tuning provides stronger signal than implicit exposure alone
- Mechanism: The paper adds inline coreference markers (e.g., "<ENT1> suitcase </ENT1> because <ENT1> it </ENT1>") to source sentences during fine-tuning. This explicit signal helps the MT model learn the coreference relationships more directly than implicit exposure to coreference-requiring instances
- Core assumption: Explicit coreference markers provide clearer learning signal than implicit exposure to coreference-requiring instances
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "Our explicit approach further enriches source sentences with predicted coreference markers"
  - [section] "Second, we use the non-singleton clusters from the coreference model to add inline coreference markers in the source sentences"
  - [corpus] Weak - no direct corpus evidence supporting this specific mechanism
- Break condition: If the coreference markers are incorrectly predicted or if the model becomes overly reliant on the markers rather than learning general coreference patterns

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map: Source sentence → Coreference resolver → Data augmentation → MT model → Target translation → Word alignment → Morphological analysis → Coreference cluster inference → Consistency scoring
- Critical path: For evaluation - source sentence → MT translation → word alignment → morphological analysis → coreference cluster inference → consistency scoring. For improvement - source sentence → coreference resolution → data augmentation → MT fine-tuning → improved translations
- Design tradeoffs: The evaluation methodology trades off precision (requiring accurate alignment and morphological analysis) for flexibility (not requiring target-language annotations). The fine-tuning approach trades off model size/complexity for improved coreference performance
- Failure signatures: Poor target-side consistency scores, high alignment error rates, morphological analysis failures, or coreference marker prediction errors

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- Question: What specific linguistic features beyond grammatical gender could be used to improve the coreference evaluation methodology for MT models?
- Basis in paper: [inferred] The paper discusses limitations of using only gender agreement and mentions that future work could expand to include "more semantic and syntactic constraints that the translation needs to follow (e.g., singular/plural agreement)" and "number and person agreement phenomena."
- Why unresolved: The current methodology focuses solely on gender agreement between coreferring mentions. While this provides a controlled evaluation, it may miss important aspects of coreference resolution that involve other linguistic features.
- What evidence would resolve it: Developing and testing evaluation metrics that incorporate additional linguistic features like number, person, semantic roles, and syntactic dependencies. Comparing performance of MT models on these extended metrics versus the current gender-only approach.

### Open Question 2
- Question: How does the performance gap between MT models and monolingual coreference resolvers vary across different language families and linguistic typologies?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper shows consistent underperformance of MT models compared to coreference resolvers, with some variation across languages (e.g., German performs better than Russian). It mentions that "these languages form a diverse set with respect to how they encode grammatical gender" and "belong to four families."
- Why unresolved: While the paper demonstrates a general performance gap, it doesn't systematically analyze how this gap varies across different language families or specific linguistic properties (e.g., pro-drop languages vs. non-pro-drop languages, languages with different gender systems).
- What evidence would resolve it: Conducting a systematic analysis of coreference performance across language families, correlating MT model performance with specific linguistic properties. This could reveal which language characteristics most challenge MT coreference capabilities.

### Open Question 3
- Question: What is the relationship between explicit coreference supervision in fine-tuning data and the resulting improvements in MT coreference performance?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper experiments with two fine-tuning approaches - one using all sentences with non-singleton clusters and another using only sentences with gendered pronouns. It finds that "WinoMT results are better when fine-tuning on Gender data" while "Wino-X's like sentences with the pronoun 'it' appear only in Coref data."
- Why unresolved: The paper shows that both approaches improve performance, but doesn't fully explore the relationship between the specificity of coreference supervision (implicit vs. explicit markers) and the magnitude of improvement across different types of coreference phenomena.
- What evidence would resolve it: Designing experiments that systematically vary the amount and specificity of coreference supervision in fine-tuning data, then measuring the resulting improvements across different coreference phenomena (e.g., gendered pronouns, neutral pronouns, event coreference).

## Limitations

- The evaluation methodology relies on the assumption that target-language morphological gender agreement reliably indicates coreference relationships, which may not hold in all languages or contexts
- The fine-tuning approach's effectiveness depends on the quality and representativeness of the augmented data, which is generated using coreference resolvers that may have their own limitations
- The methodology may miss important aspects of coreference resolution that involve linguistic features beyond grammatical gender

## Confidence

**High Confidence**: The claim that MT models underperform state-of-the-art monolingual coreference resolvers in target-side consistency is supported by extensive empirical evaluation across multiple benchmarks and languages.

**Medium Confidence**: The claim that fine-tuning MT models on coreference-requiring data improves their implicit coreference capabilities is supported by experimental results, but the magnitude of improvement varies significantly across datasets and models.

**Medium Confidence**: The claim that explicit coreference markers provide stronger signal than implicit exposure alone is supported by comparative results, but the difference is not substantial in all cases.

## Next Checks

1. **Cross-linguistic validation**: Test the evaluation methodology on additional target languages with different gender systems (e.g., languages with three genders, or no grammatical gender) to assess the generalizability of the approach.

2. **Human evaluation**: Conduct human annotation studies to verify the accuracy of the target-side consistency metric, particularly for cases where the automatic gender analysis might be ambiguous or incorrect.

3. **Ablation study on fine-tuning data**: Systematically vary the proportion of coreference-requiring sentences in the fine-tuning data to determine the optimal balance between general translation quality and coreference performance.
