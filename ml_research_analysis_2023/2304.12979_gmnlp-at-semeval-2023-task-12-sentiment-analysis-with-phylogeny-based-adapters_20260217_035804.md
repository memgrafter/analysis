# GMNLP at SemEval-2023 Task 12: Sentiment Analysis with Phylogeny-Based Adapters

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2304.12979
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2304.12979
- Reference count: 12
- This paper describes GMU's sentiment analysis system for the SemEval-2023 shared task AfriSenti-SemEval, focusing on sentiment classification for 12 African languages.

## Executive Summary
This paper presents GMU's sentiment analysis system for the SemEval-2023 shared task AfriSenti-SemEval, which focuses on sentiment classification across 12 African languages. The system leverages AfroXLMR-large, a multilingual language model pre-trained on African languages, and introduces data augmentation techniques including dictionary-based and machine translation-based methods. Phylogeny-based adapter tuning is employed to create multiple models that share knowledge across related languages, which are then ensembled for final predictions. The system achieves the best F1-score on the Amharic track, outperforming the second-best system by 6.2 points, and ranks 5th overall among 10 systems participating in all 15 tracks.

## Method Summary
The system fine-tunes AfroXLMR-large with task-adapters and creates phylogeny-based adapter stacks organized by language family, genus, and language levels. Data augmentation is performed using dictionary-based translations from PanLex and MT-based translations from Stanford Sentiment Treebank. Multiple adapter configurations are trained with MLM, and the best models are selected and ensembled via majority voting for final predictions on the test set.

## Key Results
- Achieved best F1-score on the Amharic track, outperforming the second-best system by 6.2 points
- Ranked 5th overall among 10 systems participating in all 15 tracks
- Demonstrated effectiveness of phylogeny-based adapters for knowledge sharing across related African languages

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: Phylogeny-based adapters improve sentiment classification by enabling knowledge transfer across linguistically related languages.
- Mechanism: Adapter layers are inserted between each layer of the pre-trained language model and are organized according to a phylogenetic tree that reflects the evolutionary relationships among African languages. This structure allows related languages to share learned representations and adapt more effectively.
- Core assumption: Languages that share a common family or genus have similar linguistic structures, making knowledge transfer beneficial.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract]: "Alongside finetuning, we perform phylogeny-based adapter-tuning to create several models and ensemble the best models for the final submission."
  - [section 4.4]: "Phylogeny-based adapters (Faisal and Anastasopoulos, 2022), similar to the hierarchical ones of Chronopoulou et al. (2022) enable knowledge sharing across related languages from the phylogeny-informed tree hierarchy."
  - [corpus]: Weak - the corpus does not contain direct evidence about the phylogenetic adapter mechanism's effectiveness, only mentions related systems.
- Break condition: If the phylogenetic tree structure does not reflect actual linguistic similarities, or if languages are too dissimilar even within the same family, the knowledge transfer may degrade performance.

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: Data augmentation using dictionary-based and machine translation methods increases model robustness to code-mixing and morphological variations.
- Mechanism: Synthetic training examples are generated by replacing English words with their translations from a bilingual dictionary (dictionary-based) or by translating entire sentences from English SST (MT-based). This expands the training set and introduces variations that mimic real-world language mixing.
- Core assumption: Exposure to augmented, albeit noisy, data helps the model generalize better to unseen or low-resource language patterns.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract]: "We also introduce augmented training data along with original training data."
  - [section 4.3]: "To improve the robustness of our language systems to variations in language, we utilize data augmentation techniques to increase the amount of available training data."
  - [corpus]: Weak - related papers mention data augmentation but do not specifically address dictionary or MT-based methods for African languages.
- Break condition: If the augmented data is too noisy or ungrammatical, it could confuse the model and lead to worse generalization rather than improvement.

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: Ensemble of multiple phylogeny-based models reduces variance and improves final sentiment classification accuracy.
- Mechanism: Multiple models are trained with different adapter configurations (e.g., task-adapter only, genus+lang+task-adapter, family+genus+lang+task-adapter). The best models are selected and combined via majority voting to produce the final prediction.
- Core assumption: Different adapter configurations capture complementary aspects of language structure, and combining them mitigates individual model weaknesses.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract]: "Phylogeny-based adapter tuning is employed to create multiple models that share knowledge across related languages, which are then ensembled for final predictions."
  - [section 5.4]: "We benchmark the different combinations... We adapter-tune the MLM using the Clean dataset... After the fine-tuning, we will have four adapter components to use in four stack combinations to get our final model."
  - [corpus]: Weak - no direct evidence in the corpus about the ensemble strategy's impact on African language sentiment tasks.
- Break condition: If the models in the ensemble are too similar or if the selection process does not capture diverse strengths, the ensemble may not outperform the best single model.

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map:
  Pre-trained AfroXLMR-large model -> Adapter layers organized by phylogenetic tree (family, genus, language levels) -> Data augmentation pipeline (dictionary-based, MT-based) -> Ensemble module for combining multiple models

- Critical path:
  1. Load pre-trained model and prepare datasets (original + augmented)
  2. Train adapters using phylogeny-based stacking
  3. Evaluate and select best adapter configurations
  4. Ensemble selected models for final predictions

- Design tradeoffs:
  - Using more adapter levels increases knowledge sharing but also model complexity and training time
  - More aggressive data augmentation can help but risks introducing noise
  - Ensemble size vs. computational cost at inference

- Failure signatures:
  - Degradation in performance when using certain adapter combinations (indicates poor phylogenetic alignment)
  - Worse results after data augmentation (suggests noisy synthetic data)
  - High variance across runs (suggests instability in adapter training)

- First 3 experiments:
  1. Compare full fine-tuning vs. adapter tuning on a high-resource language (e.g., Hausa) to confirm efficiency gains
  2. Train models with and without data augmentation on a low-resource language (e.g., Amharic) to measure augmentation impact
  3. Test different adapter hierarchies (genus-only vs. family+genus) on a related language pair (e.g., Algerian Arabic and Moroccan Darija) to validate phylogeny-based sharing

## Limitations

- Dataset Coverage: The system was evaluated on 12 African languages, but only achieved top performance on the Amharic track, suggesting the approach may not be universally effective across all language families.
- Reproducibility Challenges: Key technical details are underspecified, including exact language ID tokens and specific adapter training hyperparameters.
- Augmentation Quality: The paper reports using dictionary-based and MT-based data augmentation but does not evaluate the quality or linguistic validity of the generated synthetic data.

## Confidence

**High Confidence**: The core methodology of using adapter tuning with phylogenetically organized language hierarchies is technically sound and well-established in the literature. The implementation details for standard fine-tuning and adapter training are clearly specified.

**Medium Confidence**: The effectiveness of the phylogeny-based adapter approach is supported by results on Amharic (best F1-score) but lacks comprehensive validation across the full set of 15 tracks. The benefits of data augmentation are claimed but not rigorously evaluated.

**Low Confidence**: The quality and impact of the specific data augmentation techniques (dictionary-based and MT-based) are not independently validated. The selection process for the final ensemble model is not transparently documented.
