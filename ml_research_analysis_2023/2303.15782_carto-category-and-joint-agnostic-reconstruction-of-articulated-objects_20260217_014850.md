# CARTO: Category and Joint Agnostic Reconstruction of ARTiculated Objects

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2303.15782
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.15782
- Authors: 
- Reference count: 40
- Key outcome: CARTO is a single-shot approach for reconstructing articulated objects from stereo RGB observations. It learns a joint category- and joint-agnostic decoder for geometry and articulation, enabling detection, 6D pose estimation, and reconstruction of multiple objects in one forward pass. The method introduces a physically grounded regularization term to disentangle shape and joint codes in the latent space. Trained on synthetic data, CARTO transfers to real-world scenarios and outperforms a two-stage baseline by 20.4% absolute mAP 3D IOU50 on novel instances, while running at 1 Hz for up to eight objects on a Titan XP GPU.

## Executive Summary
CARTO introduces a novel single-shot approach for reconstructing articulated objects from stereo RGB observations. The method learns a joint category- and joint-agnostic decoder for geometry and articulation, enabling detection, 6D pose estimation, and reconstruction of multiple objects in one forward pass. By disentangling shape and joint codes in the latent space and introducing a physically grounded regularization term, CARTO achieves strong performance on both synthetic and real-world data while running at 1 Hz for up to eight objects on a Titan XP GPU.

## Method Summary
CARTO is a single-shot approach for reconstructing articulated objects from stereo RGB observations. It learns a joint category- and joint-agnostic decoder for geometry and articulation, enabling detection, 6D pose estimation, and reconstruction of multiple objects in one forward pass. The method introduces a physically grounded regularization term to disentangle shape and joint codes in the latent space. Trained on synthetic data, CARTO transfers to real-world scenarios and outperforms a two-stage baseline by 20.4% absolute mAP 3D IOU50 on novel instances, while running at 1 Hz for up to eight objects on a Titan XP GPU.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: A joint-agnostic decoder can reconstruct articulated objects across multiple categories without needing per-category retraining.
- Mechanism: The method disentangles shape and articulation by splitting the latent code into shape code $z_s$ and joint code $z_j$. This separation allows the decoder to use the same weights for all categories while the joint code encodes articulation-specific information.
- Core assumption: Articulated objects from different categories can be represented by a shared geometric decoder when the articulation state is encoded separately.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "learn a single geometry and articulation decoder for multiple object categories"
  - [section 3.2] "split the latent code in two separate codes: a shape and a joint code"
  - [corpus] Weak - corpus papers focus on category-level or single-category articulation estimation, suggesting this joint-agnostic approach is novel.
- Break condition: If the shape code and joint code entanglement is insufficient, the decoder cannot generalize across categories.

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: Physical regularization in joint code space improves articulation state prediction accuracy.
- Mechanism: A similarity regularization term is introduced to enforce that latent joint codes have the same similarity relationships as their corresponding real joint states (both type and state). This creates a structured joint code space.
- Core assumption: Articulated objects with similar articulation states (joint type and state) should have similar joint codes.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [section 3.3.1] "impose structure among our learned joint codes by proposing a physically grounded regularization term"
  - [section 3.3.1] "minimizing the L1-norm between both similarity measurements"
  - [corpus] Weak - corpus papers do not mention joint code regularization, indicating this is a novel contribution.
- Break condition: If the regularization strength is not properly tuned, the joint code space may become too constrained or too dispersed.

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: Single-shot inference from stereo RGB observations enables fast and accurate articulated object reconstruction.
- Mechanism: The encoder predicts a heatmap of importance values, dense pixel maps of poses, and latent codes (shape and joint) in one forward pass. Non-maximum suppression extracts object centers, and the decoder reconstructs objects in their canonical frame.
- Core assumption: The encoder can predict all necessary information (poses, codes) from a single stereo image without iterative optimization.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "Combined with our stereo image encoder we infer the 3D shape, 6D pose, size, joint type, and the joint state of multiple unknown objects in a single forward pass"
  - [section 3.1] "predict an importance scalar ψ... predict a dense pixel map of canonical 6D poses... also predict a joint code z_j"
  - [section 4.3] "Inference time is fast and can run on a NVIDIA TITAN XP GPU at 1 HZ for eight or less objects present"
- Break condition: If the encoder fails to accurately predict poses or latent codes, the single-shot approach will produce incorrect reconstructions.

## Foundational Learning

  - Why needed here: They allow continuous, differentiable surface representation for articulated objects, enabling efficient reconstruction and articulation state prediction.

  - Why needed here: Separating shape and articulation allows the model to generalize across categories and articulation states without retraining.
  - Quick check question: What would happen if shape and joint codes were not disentangled? How would this affect reconstruction accuracy across categories?

  - Why needed here: The encoder needs to extract 3D spatial information and object features from stereo images to predict poses and latent codes.
  - Quick check question: Why is stereo input preferred over monocular input for this task? What are the limitations of learned stereo depth methods?

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map: Stereo image -> Encoder (SimNet-based with CenterSnap extensions) -> Heatmap, depth map, pose map, shape code map, joint code map, segmentation mask, 3D bounding boxes -> Decoder (geometry decoder, joint decoder) -> Reconstructed objects with 6D poses and articulation states

- Critical path:
  1. Stereo image → encoder → latent codes and poses
  2. Latent codes → decoder → reconstructed object in canonical frame
  3. Poses → transform object to camera frame
  4. Output: Reconstructed objects with 6D poses and articulation states

- Design tradeoffs:
  - Single-shot vs. two-stage: Single-shot is faster but requires more complex encoder; two-stage is slower but can use separate specialized models
  - Joint-agnostic vs. category-specific: Joint-agnostic generalizes across categories but may sacrifice some category-specific accuracy
  - SDF representation vs. other implicit representations: SDFs provide accurate surface reconstruction but may be slower than occupancy networks

- Failure signatures:
  - Poor detection: Low importance heatmap peaks or incorrect pose predictions
  - Reconstruction artifacts: Incorrect geometry or articulation due to poor latent code prediction or decoder errors
  - Slow inference: Encoder or decoder bottlenecks, especially with many objects

- First 3 experiments:
  1. Train decoder only on a single category and compare to category-agnostic decoder
  2. Evaluate the effect of joint code regularization by training with and without it
  3. Test the full pipeline on synthetic data with ground truth segmentation vs. predicted segmentation

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- Question: How can CARTO be extended to handle objects with multiple joints?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] "Currently CARTO is only trained on objects with a single joint. To extend CARTO to objects with an arbitrary number of joints, we must be able to calculate pairwise similarity between two object states."
- Why unresolved: The paper identifies the need for pairwise similarity calculation but does not provide a solution or methodology for implementing this for multi-joint objects.
- What evidence would resolve it: A proposed method or algorithm that successfully calculates pairwise similarities for multi-joint objects and demonstrates improved performance over single-joint models.

### Open Question 2
- Question: How can CARTO be adapted to handle categorically different objects not seen during training?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] "While CARTO is able to generalize to unseen instances, it still relies on a learned shape prior. Using test time adaption techniques such as done by [24] helps mitigating this issues, but is not sufficient to deal with categorically different objects."
- Why unresolved: The paper acknowledges the limitation of relying on a learned shape prior and the insufficiency of test time adaptation for categorically different objects, but does not provide a solution.
- What evidence would resolve it: A successful application of CARTO to categorically different objects with improved performance over current methods, or a novel approach that overcomes the limitations of relying on a learned shape prior.

### Open Question 3
- Question: What is the impact of using joint optimization of pose, scale, and codes on CARTO's performance and execution time?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] "Additionally, while the single-forward pass is fast, jointly optimizing for pose, scale, codes like done in [8, 18] could further improve results with the cost of added execution time."
- Why unresolved: The paper suggests that joint optimization could improve results but notes the trade-off with execution time, without providing empirical evidence or a detailed analysis.
- What evidence would resolve it: Experimental results comparing the performance and execution time of CARTO with and without joint optimization of pose, scale, and codes.

## Limitations

- The paper relies on synthetic training data and limited real-world evaluation, raising questions about real-world generalization.
- The method is currently limited to objects with a single joint, requiring further development to handle multi-joint objects.
- CARTO still relies on a learned shape prior, which may limit its ability to handle categorically different objects not seen during training.

## Confidence

- **High Confidence**: Single-shot inference capability and basic reconstruction quality (1 Hz inference speed, 20.4% mAP IOU50 improvement over baseline)
- **Medium Confidence**: Joint code disentanglement and physical regularization effectiveness (novel mechanism but limited ablation studies)
- **Low Confidence**: Real-world generalization claims (minimal real data validation and no comparison to category-specific approaches)
