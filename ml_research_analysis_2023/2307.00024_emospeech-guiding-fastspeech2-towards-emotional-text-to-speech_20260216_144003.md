# EmoSpeech: Guiding FastSpeech2 Towards Emotional Text to Speech

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2307.00024
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2307.00024

- Reference count: 0

## Executive Summary
EmoSpeech extends FastSpeech2 for emotional text-to-speech synthesis by incorporating emotion conditioning embeddings, Conditional Layer Normalization, Conditional Cross-Attention, and eGeMAPS predictors. The model also employs adversarial training to enhance speech quality. Evaluation on the Emotional Speech Database shows that EmoSpeech produces more natural and expressive speech compared to the baseline, with higher MOS scores and improved emotion recognition accuracy. The Conditional Cross-Attention mechanism allows the model to emphasize different parts of the sentence based on the emotion, contributing to more realistic emotional prosody.

## Method Summary
EmoSpeech builds upon FastSpeech2 by adding several emotion-aware components. Conditional Layer Normalization (CLN) replaces standard LayerNorm in both encoder and decoder, conditioning normalization parameters on concatenated speaker-emotion embeddings. Conditional Cross-Attention (CCA) uses emotion-specific keys and values to reweight encoder outputs, enabling emotion-dependent token emphasis. An eGeMAPS predictor estimates acoustic features from text to guide emotional prosody generation. The model is trained with reconstruction loss, adversarial loss (using a JCU discriminator), feature matching loss, and eGeMAPS MSE loss. A separate iSTFTNet vocoder is trained on the same dataset for waveform synthesis.

## Key Results
- EmoSpeech achieves MOS of 4.37 ± 0.62, outperforming Expressive FastSpeech2 (3.74 ± 0.65 MOS)
- Higher emotion recognition accuracy compared to baseline
- Conditional Cross-Attention enables emotion-specific emphasis on different sentence parts
- Adversarial training with JCU discriminator improves naturalness without sacrificing emotional expressiveness

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: EmoSpeech outperforms Expressive FastSpeech2 in both MOS and emotion recognition accuracy.
- Mechanism: Conditional Cross-Attention (CCA) reweights tokens according to emotion, allowing the model to emphasize different parts of the sentence based on the emotion type.
- Core assumption: Attention weights can capture emotion-dependent intonation patterns in the input text.
- Evidence anchors:

  - [corpus] Weak signal: no direct citation of MOS or accuracy in related papers; assumption relies on internal EmoSpeech evaluation.
- Break condition: If emotion-dependent attention weights do not correlate with human perception of emotional emphasis in speech.

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: Conditional Layer Normalization (CLN) improves emotional expressiveness by conditioning layer normalization on emotion embeddings.
- Mechanism: CLN replaces traditional LayerNorm in both encoder and decoder, scaling and biasing based on the concatenated speaker-emotion embedding.
- Core assumption: Normalizing with emotion-specific parameters preserves emotional prosody while stabilizing training.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [section 3.4] "Following the early success of AdaSpeech4 [21] in applying the Conditional Layer Norm (CLN) for zero-shot speech synthesis, we adopt CLN for emotional speech synthesis."
  - [abstract] "The proposed model outperforms an existing implementation of FastSpeech2 extended for Emotional Speech Synthesis... regarding MOS and emotion recognition accuracy."
  - [corpus] Weak signal: related works cite zero-shot multi-speaker setups, but not explicit emotional TTS gains.
- Break condition: If CLN causes overfitting or loss of emotional variability in synthesized speech.

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: Adversarial training with JCU discriminator mitigates quality degradation in multi-speaker, multi-emotion setups.
- Mechanism: Joint conditional-unconditional discriminator optimizes both feature matching and adversarial objectives during training.
- Core assumption: Adversarial loss forces the model to produce mel spectrograms indistinguishable from real data, improving naturalness.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [section 3.6] "To boost the quality of generated speech, we utilize the same technique as in [22] and apply adversarial training for EmoSpeech."

- Break condition: If adversarial training introduces artifacts that degrade perceived emotional authenticity.

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map: Text → Encoder (with CLN + CCA) → Variance Adapter (with EMP) → Decoder (with CLN + CCA) → Mel spectrogram → Vocoder → Waveform
- Critical path: Text → Encoder (with CLN + CCA) → Variance Adapter (with EMP) → Decoder (with CLN + CCA) → Mel spectrogram → Vocoder → Waveform
- Design tradeoffs:
  - Adding CLN increases model size by ~15% but improves emotional expressiveness; inference speed is maintained.
  - CCA introduces per-layer attention weights; this adds complexity but allows emotion-specific token emphasis.
  - Adversarial training improves naturalness but may slightly smooth intonation peaks, reducing emotion classification accuracy.
- Failure signatures:
  - Emotion classification accuracy drops sharply → adversarial training may be oversmoothing.
  - MOS score stagnates or degrades → CLN or CCA may be misconfigured or over-regularized.
  - Inference latency increases → unnecessary repetition of conditioning operations or inefficient attention implementation.
- First 3 experiments:
  1. Replace LayerNorm with CLN in encoder only; evaluate MOS and emotion accuracy.
  2. Add Conditional Cross-Attention to encoder; compare attention weight distributions for different emotions.
  3. Train with adversarial loss; measure trade-off between naturalness (MOS) and emotion recognition accuracy.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- Question: How does the choice of eGeMAPS features impact the perceived emotional expressiveness and naturalness of synthesized speech in EmoSpeech?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The authors conducted an ablation study to determine the optimal number of eGeMAPS features for prediction, finding that predicting two features yielded the best results according to human annotators.
- Why unresolved: The study focused on a limited set of features (two features were selected) and a specific dataset (ESD). It is unclear whether these findings generalize to other emotional speech datasets or if a different combination of eGeMAPS features might be more effective for certain emotions or speaker characteristics.
- What evidence would resolve it: A comprehensive study evaluating the impact of various combinations of eGeMAPS features on emotional expressiveness and naturalness across multiple emotional speech datasets and speaker profiles.

### Open Question 2
- Question: How does the Conditional Cross-Attention (CCA) mechanism in EmoSpeech compare to other methods for handling the uneven distribution of emotions across a sentence in terms of naturalness and emotional expressiveness?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The authors claim that the CCA mechanism allows EmoSpeech to emphasize different parts of the sentence depending on the emotion, leading to more natural and expressive speech synthesis. However, they do not provide a direct comparison with other methods.
- Why unresolved: The paper does not compare the CCA mechanism to other approaches for handling emotion distribution, such as using separate emotion embeddings for different parts of the sentence or employing attention mechanisms that are specifically designed for emotional speech synthesis.
- What evidence would resolve it: A comparative study evaluating the naturalness and emotional expressiveness of speech synthesized using the CCA mechanism, alternative methods for handling emotion distribution, and a baseline model without any emotion distribution handling.

### Open Question 3
- Question: How does the performance of EmoSpeech vary across different languages and emotional speech datasets?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The authors evaluated EmoSpeech on the English subset of the Emotional Speech Database (ESD) and reported high MOS scores and emotion recognition accuracy. However, the paper does not explore the model's performance on other languages or emotional speech datasets.
- Why unresolved: The study focused on a single language (English) and a specific emotional speech dataset (ESD). It is unclear whether EmoSpeech's performance generalizes to other languages with different emotional speech characteristics or to datasets with different emotional categories or speaker demographics.
- What evidence would resolve it: A cross-lingual and cross-dataset evaluation of EmoSpeech, comparing its performance on multiple languages and emotional speech datasets with varying emotional categories and speaker demographics.

## Limitations
- MOS improvement of 0.63 has large confidence interval (±0.62) suggesting substantial inter-rater variability
- Results based on single dataset (ESD) with limited emotional diversity (5 emotions, 10 speakers)
- Emotion recognition accuracy metric depends on unspecified classifier architecture

## Confidence
- **High confidence**: Architectural extensions (CLN, CCA, eGeMAPS predictor) are technically sound and follow established patterns
- **Medium confidence**: MOS improvement claim is credible but large confidence interval reduces certainty about practical significance
- **Low confidence**: Emotion recognition accuracy metric and its improvement are difficult to evaluate without knowing classifier details
