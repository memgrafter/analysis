# Joint Repetition Suppression and Content Moderation of Large Language Models

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2304.10611
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2304.10611
- Authors: 
- Reference count: 14
- One-line primary result: Unlikelihood-based training achieves significant reductions in token and n-gram repetition while improving content moderation over baseline post-hoc filtering approaches

## Executive Summary
This paper addresses two key limitations of large language models: repetitive text generation and offensive content production. The authors propose a novel training framework based on unlikelihood objectives that combines token-level and sequence-level losses with a new "block loss" term to discourage offensive phrases. Unlike previous post-hoc filtering approaches that can disrupt coherence, this method trains the model to avoid undesirable content from the start. Evaluated on bullet-point-to-paragraph generation tasks, the approach achieves substantial reductions in repetition while maintaining strong overall performance.

## Method Summary
The authors introduce a training framework that uses unlikelihood objectives to jointly suppress repetitive text and offensive content in LLMs. The method combines token-level unlikelihood (penalizing exact token repetitions), sequence-level unlikelihood (targeting duplicate n-grams), and a novel block loss term (discouraging offensive phrases from a predefined blocklist). These components work together with repetition penalties applied during training, inference, and post-processing. The approach is implemented on top of a pretrained BART model and evaluated using standard language modeling metrics alongside specialized repetition and content moderation measures.

## Key Results
- Significant reduction in both token repetition and n-gram repetition compared to baseline BART models
- Improved content moderation performance over post-hoc filtering approaches like SentenceBERT
- Minimal impact on overall model performance as measured by perplexity, accuracy, ROUGE, and BERTScore
- Effective handling of offensive content through block loss integration during training

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: Unlikelihood training discourages the model from generating specific tokens or sequences that have appeared before or are deemed inappropriate.
- Mechanism: The unlikelihood loss adds a penalty for tokens in the negative candidate set, which includes previously generated tokens or offensive phrases. This creates a disincentive for the model to repeat or generate such content.
- Core assumption: The model can effectively learn to avoid certain tokens/sequences when they are associated with higher loss during training.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract]: "we apply non-exact repetition suppression using token and sequence level unlikelihood loss"
  - [section]: "In unlikelihood training objective, higher loss applies to a set of tokens called negative candidates, and thus discourages the model from generating them."
  - [corpus]: Weak evidence; neighboring papers discuss content moderation but not unlikelihood training specifically.

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: The block loss term extends unlikelihood training to explicitly handle offensive phrases by treating them as negative candidates.
- Mechanism: Block loss defines offensive phrases as negative candidates, so the model is penalized for generating them. This allows the model to avoid offensive content from the start, rather than filtering it out after generation.
- Core assumption: Offensive phrases can be effectively identified and collected in advance to form the blocklist.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract]: "we further explore the framework of unlikelihood training objective in order to jointly endow the model with abilities to avoid generating offensive words and phrases from the beginning."
  - [section]: "Based on this idea, we further generalize the scope of negative candidates and define a new loss term called 'block loss'. In block loss, the negative candidates are defined as blocklist phrases 𝐵𝑛 that we have collected in advance."
  - [corpus]: Weak evidence; neighboring papers discuss content moderation but not block loss specifically.

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: Combining token-level and sequence-level unlikelihood objectives addresses both exact and non-exact repetitions.
- Mechanism: Token-level unlikelihood targets exact token repetitions, while sequence-level unlikelihood targets duplicate n-grams. This combination ensures both fine-grained and broader repetition suppression.
- Core assumption: The model can learn to suppress both token-level and sequence-level repetitions simultaneously without conflicting objectives.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract]: "we introduce a novel training framework based on unlikelihood objectives. They combine token-level and sequence-level unlikelihood losses"
  - [section]: "Token-level unlikelihood objective defines negative candidates as previous tokens within targets... Sequence-level unlikelihood objective follows the same principle, but it defines negative candidates as previous duplicate n-grams within predicted sequence."
  - [corpus]: Weak evidence; neighboring papers discuss repetition but not the combination of token-level and sequence-level unlikelihood.

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map:
  Pretrained BART model -> Token-level unlikelihood loss -> Sequence-level unlikelihood loss -> Block loss -> Repetition penalty -> Rule-based filters

- Critical path:
  1. Pretrain BART model with standard likelihood loss.
  2. Fine-tune with token-level unlikelihood loss to suppress token repetitions.
  3. Further fine-tune with sequence-level unlikelihood loss to suppress n-gram repetitions.
  4. Apply block loss to handle offensive phrases.
  5. Use repetition penalties and rule-based filters during inference and post-processing.

- Design tradeoffs:
  - Unlikelihood training may slow down convergence compared to standard likelihood training.
  - Block loss requires a comprehensive blocklist, which may be difficult to maintain.
  - Repetition penalties and rule-based filters may introduce additional complexity during inference.

- Failure signatures:
  - Model still generates repetitive or offensive content despite unlikelihood training.
  - Model becomes overly conservative and fails to generate diverse or creative content.
  - Unlikelihood training objectives conflict with each other, leading to unstable training.

- First 3 experiments:
  1. Compare token-level unlikelihood loss vs. standard likelihood loss on a small dataset to measure repetition suppression.
  2. Add sequence-level unlikelihood loss to the token-level unlikelihood training and evaluate n-gram repetition reduction.
  3. Introduce block loss with a small blocklist and measure offensive content reduction while monitoring overall model performance.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

The paper does not explicitly call out open questions, but the evaluation suggests several areas for further investigation, particularly regarding the scalability of block loss to larger vocabularies and the trade-offs between repetition suppression and semantic coherence in low-resource data regimes.

## Limitations

- The blocklist approach for offensive content moderation may struggle with context-dependent toxicity and emerging harmful phrases not captured in the predefined list
- The computational overhead of unlikelihood training objectives and repetition penalties during inference could impact practical deployment efficiency
- The evaluation relies on synthetic bullet-point-to-paragraph generation rather than real-world deployment scenarios

## Confidence

**High Confidence:** The core mechanism of unlikelihood training for repetition suppression is well-established in the literature and the paper provides clear implementation details. The improvements in repetition metrics over standard BART training are substantial and reproducible.

**Medium Confidence:** The integration of content moderation through block loss shows promise, but the evaluation relies on rule-based filters that may not capture nuanced offensive content. The claim that unlikelihood training outperforms post-hoc filtering for content moderation needs validation on more diverse and realistic datasets.

**Low Confidence:** The assertion that joint training for repetition and content moderation doesn't compromise overall model performance requires more extensive validation. The paper reports minimal impact on perplexity and generation quality metrics, but doesn't explore potential degradation in other important capabilities.

## Next Checks

1. **Ablation Study on Blocklist Quality:** Systematically evaluate model performance with varying blocklist sizes and compositions (e.g., broad vs. narrow, context-dependent vs. context-independent phrases) to quantify the sensitivity of content moderation effectiveness to blocklist quality and completeness.

2. **Real-world Deployment Simulation:** Test the trained models on actual user-generated content from social media or forum datasets to assess performance on naturally occurring repetitions and offensive content, rather than synthetic bullet-point generation tasks.

3. **Long-range Generation Analysis:** Evaluate model performance on longer generation tasks (multiple paragraphs or extended dialogues) to determine if the repetition suppression benefits degrade over extended sequences and whether content moderation remains effective in longer contexts.
