# Boosting Theory-of-Mind Performance in Large Language Models via Prompting

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID**: 2304.11490
- **Source URL**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2304.11490
- **Reference count**: 40
- **Key outcome**: Appropriate prompting substantially enhances LLM theory-of-mind reasoning, with GPT-4 reaching near-human accuracy (80%) in zero-shot settings and surpassing human performance (100%) with prompting.

## Executive Summary
This study investigates whether in-context learning prompts can improve large language models' theory-of-mind (ToM) reasoning capabilities. Testing four GPT models across 16 ToM and 16 control scenarios, the research demonstrates that prompting strategies—particularly step-by-step and chain-of-thought approaches—significantly boost model performance. While GPT-4 already achieves near-human accuracy without prompting, all RLHF-trained models show substantial improvements with appropriate prompting, suggesting that context-dependent cognitive capacities can be effectively enhanced through prompt engineering.

## Method Summary
The study evaluated four GPT models (Davinci-2, Davinci-3, GPT-3.5-Turbo, GPT-4) on theory-of-mind reasoning tasks using four prompting strategies: zero-shot, step-by-step thinking, two-shot chain-of-thought reasoning, and combined approaches. Models were tested on 16 ToM scenarios and 16 control scenarios designed to isolate specific reasoning elements. Performance was measured as the percentage of correctly answered questions, with comparisons made to human benchmark performance. The prompting strategies were systematically varied to determine which approaches most effectively enhanced ToM reasoning capabilities.

## Key Results
- GPT-4 achieved 80% accuracy in zero-shot settings and 100% with prompting, surpassing human performance
- All RLHF-trained models (excluding Davinci-2) improved significantly with prompting, reaching >80% accuracy
- Performance gains were not due to copying reasoning steps, as similar improvements occurred with non-ToM examples
- Prompting strategies substantially enhanced LLM ToM reasoning across multiple model variants

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
Assumption: Prompting works by activating specific cognitive processes that align with theory-of-mind reasoning requirements. The step-by-step and chain-of-thought prompts likely help models decompose complex social reasoning into manageable sub-tasks, making explicit the mental state tracking and perspective-taking that ToM demands. RLHF training may have implicitly encoded ToM capabilities that are only triggered when prompted appropriately, suggesting these models possess latent reasoning abilities that emerge under specific contextual conditions.

## Limitations
- Limited task diversity with only 16 ToM and 16 control scenarios, potentially missing complex naturalistic reasoning
- Single human benchmark without multiple raters limits generalizability of human-LLM comparisons
- Does not explore ceiling effects or diminishing returns with more complex ToM scenarios

## Confidence
- **Prompting improves ToM performance**: High confidence (consistent improvements across models and strategies)
- **GPT-4 surpassing human performance**: Medium confidence (human baseline methodology not fully specified)
- **RLHF models benefiting from prompting**: Medium confidence (does not distinguish genuine reasoning from pattern matching)

## Next Checks
1. **Expanded Task Set**: Validate findings using 50-100 diverse ToM scenarios including naturalistic social reasoning contexts
2. **Human Performance Benchmarking**: Conduct direct human comparisons using same prompts and experimental conditions with multiple raters
