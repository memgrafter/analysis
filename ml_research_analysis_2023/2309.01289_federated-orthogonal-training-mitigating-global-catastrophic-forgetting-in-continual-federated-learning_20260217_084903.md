# Federated Orthogonal Training: Mitigating Global Catastrophic Forgetting in Continual Federated Learning

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2309.01289
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2309.01289
- Reference count: 40
- Key outcome: Proposed FOT framework achieves up to 15% higher average accuracy and 27% lower forgetting compared to state-of-the-art methods in Continual Federated Learning

## Executive Summary
This paper addresses the challenge of Global Catastrophic Forgetting in Continual Federated Learning (CFL), where a global model must learn new tasks over time without forgetting previous ones while preserving privacy. The authors propose a novel Federated Orthogonal Training (FOT) framework that extracts the global principal subspace of each layer for old tasks and modifies new task updates to be orthogonal to these subspaces, reducing interference between tasks. The framework includes a Global Principal Subspace Extraction (GPSE) method that preserves privacy through secure aggregation. Experiments on multiple benchmarks demonstrate that FOT significantly outperforms existing state-of-the-art methods in terms of accuracy and forgetting reduction.

## Method Summary
The proposed Federated Orthogonal Training (FOT) framework consists of two main components: Global Principal Subspace Extraction (GPSE) and orthogonal training. GPSE is a privacy-preserving method that extracts the principal subspace of each layer for old tasks using secure aggregation across clients. The orthogonal training component then modifies the gradient updates for new tasks to be orthogonal to the extracted principal subspaces, minimizing interference with previously learned knowledge. This approach allows the global model to learn new tasks incrementally without forgetting previously acquired knowledge, while maintaining privacy through the use of secure aggregation techniques.

## Key Results
- FOT achieves up to 15% higher average accuracy compared to state-of-the-art methods
- FOT reduces forgetting by up to 27% compared to existing approaches
- The proposed method requires minimal computation and communication overhead

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The effectiveness of FOT lies in its ability to maintain a low-dimensional representation of previously learned tasks (the principal subspaces) and enforce orthogonality between new task updates and these subspaces. By projecting new updates onto the orthogonal complement of the principal subspaces, FOT ensures that learning new tasks does not interfere with the knowledge acquired for old tasks. This orthogonalization process effectively separates the parameter space for different tasks, preventing catastrophic forgetting while allowing for efficient learning of new tasks. The use of secure aggregation in GPSE further ensures that this process can be performed without compromising the privacy of individual clients' data.

## Foundational Learning
- **Principal Component Analysis (PCA)**: Used to extract the principal subspace of each layer for old tasks, capturing the most important features learned during previous tasks. Quick check: Verify that the extracted subspaces capture sufficient variance to represent the old tasks effectively.
- **Orthogonal Projection**: Ensures that new task updates do not interfere with the principal subspaces of old tasks by projecting updates onto the orthogonal complement. Quick check: Confirm that the orthogonality constraint is properly enforced during training.
- **Secure Aggregation**: Allows for privacy-preserving computation of the principal subspaces across multiple clients without revealing individual client data. Quick check: Validate that the secure aggregation mechanism maintains privacy while accurately computing the global principal subspaces.
- **Continual Learning**: The ability to learn new tasks incrementally without forgetting previously acquired knowledge, which is the primary challenge addressed by FOT. Quick check: Assess the model's performance on both new and old tasks to ensure minimal forgetting.

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component Map**: GPSE -> Orthogonal Training -> Global Model Update
- **Critical Path**: Extract principal subspaces (GPSE) → Orthogonalize new task updates → Update global model
- **Design Tradeoffs**: Balancing the dimensionality of principal subspaces (memory) vs. accuracy; computational overhead of orthogonalization vs. forgetting reduction
- **Failure Signatures**: Increased forgetting if principal subspaces are not accurately extracted; reduced accuracy if orthogonalization is too restrictive

## Limitations
- The computational complexity of the Global Principal Subspace Extraction (GPSE) method is not thoroughly analyzed, which could be a concern for resource-constrained edge devices
- The secure aggregation mechanism for preserving privacy is mentioned but not thoroughly explained or evaluated in terms of its effectiveness and efficiency
- Experiments are conducted on standard benchmarks, but real-world applicability and performance in practical federated learning scenarios remain to be validated

## Confidence
- High confidence in the mathematical formulation and the orthogonal training approach
