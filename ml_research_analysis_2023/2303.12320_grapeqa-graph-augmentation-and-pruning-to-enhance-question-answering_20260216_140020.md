# GrapeQA: GRaph Augmentation and Pruning to Enhance Question-Answering

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2303.12320
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.12320
- Reference count: 12
- Primary result: New state-of-the-art accuracy on OpenBookQA (90%), CommonsenseQA (74.05%), and MedQA-USMLE (39.51%)

## Executive Summary
GrapeQA enhances commonsense question-answering by addressing limitations in information flow between question-answer pairs and knowledge graphs. The method introduces two key improvements: Prominent Entities for Graph Augmentation (PEGA) adds noun chunk nodes from the QA pair to the working graph, while Context-Aware Node Pruning (CANP) removes less relevant nodes to simplify reasoning. These enhancements significantly improve performance over QA-GNN, achieving new state-of-the-art results on three datasets with the largest gains observed on OpenBookQA.

## Method Summary
GrapeQA improves graph-based question-answering by augmenting the working graph with noun chunks extracted from the question-answer pair (PEGA) and pruning less relevant nodes (CANP). The method uses language model embeddings to represent noun chunks as new nodes in the graph, connected to both the original knowledge graph nodes and to each other. CANP then clusters and removes the least relevant extra nodes based on their similarity to answer entity nodes, reducing graph complexity while maintaining essential information for reasoning.

## Key Results
- Achieves 90% accuracy on OpenBookQA, a 22.2% improvement over QA-GNN
- Sets new state-of-the-art at 74.05% on CommonsenseQA (+0.65% over QA-GNN)
- Improves MedQA-USMLE to 39.51% accuracy (+1.5% over QA-GNN)
- PEGA+CANP combination yields the largest gains, particularly on OpenBookQA

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
Adding noun chunk nodes from the QA pair to the working graph improves information flow between the QA context and the knowledge graph. PEGA extracts noun phrases from the question-answer pair and represents them as nodes in the working graph, connected to both the original KG nodes and to each other. This provides more direct connections between the QA context and the KG, allowing better information exchange. The core assumption is that noun chunks from the QA pair contain relevant semantic information that should be directly connected to the KG nodes for better reasoning.

### Mechanism 2
Removing less relevant nodes from the working graph simplifies graph reasoning and improves performance. CANP clusters Extra nodes with Answer entity nodes based on relevance scores, then removes the least relevant cluster. This reduces the graph size and focuses reasoning on more relevant information. The core assumption is that some Extra nodes (2-hop neighbors from the KG) are less relevant to the QA pair than others, and removing them won't hurt performance.

### Mechanism 3
The combination of graph augmentation and pruning works synergistically, with each technique addressing different limitations of the working graph. PEGA adds relevant information that was missing from the WG, while CANP removes noise that was introduced. Together they create a more focused graph that's neither too sparse (missing information) nor too dense (containing noise). The core assumption is that the WG typically has both missing relevant information AND irrelevant nodes, and addressing both issues simultaneously provides benefits greater than either alone.

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map: Noun chunk extraction (spaCy/NLTK) -> LM encoding (RoBERTa-large or SapBERT) -> Graph augmentation (PEGA) -> Relevance scoring (for CANP) -> Graph pruning (CANP) -> GNN reasoning -> Classification head

- Critical path: QA context → noun chunk extraction → LM encoding → graph augmentation → relevance scoring → graph pruning → GNN reasoning → classification

- Design tradeoffs:
  - Using noun chunks vs. other types of QA context augmentation
  - Simple average pooling vs. more sophisticated noun chunk representation methods
  - Removing entire clusters vs. individual nodes in CANP
  - Fixed vs. adaptive number of GNN layers

- Failure signatures:
  - Performance drops when noun chunk extraction misses key entities
  - Over-pruning that removes important information
  - GNN over-fitting to the augmented structure
  - Relevance scoring becoming a bottleneck in training

- First 3 experiments:
  1. Run baseline QA-GNN to establish reference performance
  2. Add PEGA only (no pruning) to test augmentation impact
  3. Add CANP only (no augmentation) to test pruning impact

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
What is the optimal number of GNN layers for GrapeQA across different datasets? The paper shows that 5 layers work well for most settings, but CSQA with PEGA-only shows better results with 4 layers. This remains unresolved because the paper only tests a limited range of layer numbers (4, 5, 6) and the optimal number may vary depending on the dataset and model configuration. A comprehensive ablation study testing a wider range of GNN layer numbers (e.g., 2, 3, 4, 5, 6, 7) on all three datasets with both PEGA+CANP and PEGA-only models would resolve this.

### Open Question 2
How does the performance of GrapeQA change with different noun chunk extraction methods? The paper compares spaCy and NLTK noun chunk extraction methods against a baseline of randomly adding 20% of QA words, showing significant performance differences. This remains unresolved because the paper only tests three noun chunk extraction methods and the impact of other extraction methods or variations in parameters is unknown. Testing additional noun chunk extraction methods (e.g., Stanford CoreNLP, custom rule-based approaches) and varying parameters like minimum/maximum chunk size on all three datasets would resolve this.

### Open Question 3
What is the impact of CANP on datasets with different WG node distributions? The paper notes that CANP is not necessary for MedQA due to fewer extra nodes, but it improves performance on OBQA and CSQA. This remains unresolved because the paper only examines one dataset where CANP is not beneficial, but the threshold for when CANP becomes useful is unclear. Analyzing the relationship between the number of extra nodes in WG and CANP's effectiveness across a range of datasets with varying WG sizes, or creating synthetic datasets with controlled WG node distributions would resolve this.

### Open Question 4
How does the choice of knowledge graph affect GrapeQA's performance? The paper uses ConceptNet for OBQA and CSQA, but a different graph for MedQA, suggesting that the choice of KG may impact performance. This remains unresolved because the paper only tests two KGs (ConceptNet and a domain-specific graph for MedQA) and does not explore how different KGs or KG structures might affect results. Testing GrapeQA on the same datasets using different KGs (e.g., WordNet, Wikidata, domain-specific graphs) and comparing performance across different KG structures and sizes would resolve this.

## Limitations
- The method's reliance on noun chunk extraction may miss domain-specific terminology or compound concepts critical for accurate reasoning
- Reported improvements, particularly the 22.2% gain on OpenBookQA, may not generalize to other question-answering tasks or knowledge graph structures
- The pruning mechanism's effectiveness depends heavily on the quality of relevance scoring, which is not fully explained

## Confidence

**High Confidence**: The basic mechanism of adding noun chunk nodes to enhance information flow between QA context and knowledge graph is theoretically sound and well-supported by the implementation details provided.

**Medium Confidence**: The pruning mechanism's effectiveness depends heavily on the quality of relevance scoring. While the concept is sound, the paper provides limited details about how relevance is computed and whether this scoring generalizes across different types of questions and knowledge domains.

**Low Confidence**: The synergistic effect claim (that PEGA and CANP together provide benefits greater than either alone) is based on results from a single dataset. The mechanism for why these two approaches complement each other rather than potentially conflicting is not fully explained.
