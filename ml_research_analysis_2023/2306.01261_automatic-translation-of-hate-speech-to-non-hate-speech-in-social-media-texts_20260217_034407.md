# Automatic Translation of Hate Speech to Non-hate Speech in Social Media Texts

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2306.01261
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.01261
- Reference count: 40
- Primary result: mBART model achieves highest mean sentence BLEU score of 0.236 and median score of 0.167 for hate speech translation task

## Executive Summary
This study introduces a novel task of translating hate speech into non-hate speech while preserving semantic meaning in Spanish social media texts. The authors propose using sequence-to-sequence models, including RNN, T5, mBART, and Marian, to transform harmful content into acceptable alternatives. A new dataset is constructed with multiple non-hate translations per hate speech example, and models are evaluated using BLEU-based metrics across 4-fold cross-validation. Results show that mBART outperforms other approaches, demonstrating the potential of multilingual pre-training for this task. The work establishes baseline performance and highlights the need for improved models to address online hate speech.

## Method Summary
The approach involves fine-tuning sequence-to-sequence models on a Spanish dataset containing hate speech paired with multiple manually-created non-hate translations. The dataset is built from IberEval 2018 hate speech corpus with additional manual translations requiring three non-hate variants per hate speech text. Four baseline models are evaluated: RNN, T5-small, mBART-large, and Marian. Models are trained using 4-fold cross-validation on the 1,680 hate speech texts with 5,208 total translations. Performance is measured using sentence-level BLEU scores, including mean, median, and variance across folds.

## Key Results
- mBART model achieved highest performance with mean sentence BLEU score of 0.236 and median of 0.167
- RNN baseline showed limitations with name preservation errors in translations
- Dataset contains 1,680 hate speech texts with 5,208 non-hate translations (average 3.1 per text)
- 4-fold cross-validation used due to dataset size considerations

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: mBART's multilingual pre-training with denoising objective captures semantic and syntactic nuances better than simpler models
- Mechanism: mBART's pre-training on multiple languages using masking and permutation tasks exposes it to diverse linguistic structures, enabling better generalization for in-language rewriting tasks
- Core assumption: Multilingual denoising pre-training provides better semantic preservation than monolingual or simpler architectures
- Evidence anchors: mBART achieved highest BLEU scores; pre-trained using BART objective on multiple languages; related papers suggest trend toward multilingual approaches
- Break condition: If task requires deep cultural/context-specific understanding not captured in pre-training data

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: Multiple non-hate variants per hate speech sample enable better evaluation of paraphrasing ability
- Mechanism: Requiring three translations per input captures multiple valid rephrasing approaches, allowing evaluation across diverse outputs
- Core assumption: Multiple references improve BLEU evaluation robustness and reflect real-world paraphrasing variability
- Evidence anchors: Dataset designed with three examples per text; average 3.1 translations per text; novel design choice not seen in related work
- Break condition: High annotator disagreement or unclear semantic boundaries may introduce evaluation noise

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: K-fold cross-validation on low-resource dataset improves model reliability
- Mechanism: 4-fold split ensures each sample used for both training and testing, reducing variance from limited data
- Core assumption: Low-resource tasks benefit more from cross-validation than simple train/test split
- Evidence anchors: Dataset explicitly considered low-resource; uniform fold sizes; tailored methodological choice
- Break condition: Too small dataset may still lead to high variance despite cross-validation

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map: Input text → Tokenizer → Encoder (RNN, T5, mBART, or Marian) → Decoder → Output text → BLEU evaluation
- Critical path: Tokenizer → Encoder → Decoder → Output generation
- Design tradeoffs: RNN simpler/faster but less accurate; T5 flexible/task-agnostic; mBART leverages multilingual pre-training; Marian optimized for high-quality translation but less flexible
- Failure signatures: Low BLEU scores indicate poor semantic preservation; high variance suggests overfitting; name replacement errors indicate vocabulary limitations

  1. Train and evaluate RNN baseline to establish performance floor

## Limitations
- Dataset reliability concerns due to lack of inter-annotator agreement scores and quality control documentation
- BLEU metric validity questioned as it may not fully capture meaning preservation in rewriting tasks
- Generalizability limited to Spanish language and specific cultural context

## Confidence

**High Confidence**: mBART achieving highest BLEU scores (0.236 mean, 0.167 median) is supported by experimental results and methodologically sound.

**Medium Confidence**: Multilingual pre-training advantage is reasonable but lacks ablation studies to isolate effect from model size differences.

**Low Confidence**: Claims about effectively combating hate speech spread are speculative without real-world deployment evidence or impact studies.
