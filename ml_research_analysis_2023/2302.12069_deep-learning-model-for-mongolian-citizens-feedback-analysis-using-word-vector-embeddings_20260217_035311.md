# Deep learning model for Mongolian Citizens Feedback Analysis using Word Vector Embeddings

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID**: 2302.12069
- **Source URL**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2302.12069
- **Reference count**: 2
- **Primary result**: BiLSTM with custom FeedbackWord2Vec embeddings achieved 80.1% accuracy for government agency prediction and 82.7% for emotional type prediction in Mongolian feedback classification.

## Executive Summary
This study addresses the challenge of classifying citizen feedback in Mongolian, a low-resource language with limited NLP tools. The research applies deep learning models with word embeddings to categorize feedback by responsible government agency and emotional type. Using approximately 1,300 feedback items, the study compares FastText embeddings against a custom FeedbackWord2Vec model across CNN and BiLSTM architectures. The results demonstrate that deep learning approaches can effectively automate feedback categorization in Mongolian, with the BiLSTM architecture combined with the custom embeddings showing the best performance at 80.1% and 82.7% accuracy for the two classification tasks.

## Method Summary
The methodology employs a structured approach to Mongolian feedback classification through three main stages. First, preprocessing involves tokenization, punctuation removal, and converting text to lowercase to standardize the input data. Second, word embeddings are generated using both the FastText pre-trained Mongolian model and a custom FeedbackWord2Vec model trained specifically on the feedback dataset to capture domain-specific language patterns. Third, neural network architectures process these embeddings: CNN models apply convolutional layers to detect local patterns in the feedback text, while BiLSTM models use bidirectional recurrent layers to capture contextual dependencies from both directions. The models are trained and evaluated using accuracy metrics on the government agency prediction and emotional type classification tasks.

## Key Results
- BiLSTM with FeedbackWord2Vec embeddings achieved 80.1% accuracy for government agency prediction
- BiLSTM with FeedbackWord2Vec embeddings achieved 82.7% accuracy for emotional type classification
- Custom FeedbackWord2Vec embeddings outperformed pre-trained FastText embeddings in both tasks

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The success of this approach stems from combining domain-specific word embeddings with architectures capable of capturing both local and contextual patterns in text. The FeedbackWord2Vec embeddings are trained specifically on the feedback corpus, allowing them to capture the unique vocabulary and semantic relationships present in citizen-government communication. This domain adaptation is crucial for low-resource languages where general-purpose embeddings may not adequately represent specialized terminology or communication patterns. The BiLSTM architecture's ability to process text bidirectionally enables it to understand context from both preceding and following words, which is particularly valuable for capturing the nuanced language often present in feedback where sentiment and intent depend heavily on full contextual understanding.

## Foundational Learning

**Word Embeddings**: Dense vector representations that capture semantic relationships between words
- Why needed: Convert discrete text tokens into continuous numerical representations that neural networks can process
- Quick check: Embeddings should place semantically similar words (like "happy" and "satisfied") closer in vector space

**CNN for Text**: Convolutional neural networks apply filters to detect local patterns and n-gram features
- Why needed: Identify important local phrases and patterns that might indicate specific agencies or emotional states
- Quick check: Filter outputs should highlight key phrases like "poor service" or "excellent response"

**BiLSTM Architecture**: Bidirectional Long Short-Term Memory networks process sequences in both forward and backward directions
- Why needed: Capture long-range dependencies and contextual information that depends on both preceding and following words
- Quick check: Model should correctly interpret context-dependent phrases like "not bad" versus "bad service"

**Domain-Specific Embeddings**: Word vectors trained on specific corpora rather than general text
- Why needed: Capture specialized vocabulary and usage patterns unique to government feedback communications
- Quick check: Domain-specific embeddings should better represent government-related terminology than general embeddings

**Preprocessing for Low-Resource Languages**: Standardization techniques like tokenization and normalization
- Why needed: Compensate for limited training data by ensuring consistent representation of similar linguistic patterns
- Quick check: Similar feedback should have consistent token representations after preprocessing

## Architecture Onboarding

**Component Map**: Raw feedback text -> Preprocessing (tokenization, cleaning) -> Word Embeddings (FastText/FeedbackWord2Vec) -> Neural Network (CNN/BiLSTM) -> Classification Output

**Critical Path**: The essential processing flow involves text preprocessing feeding into word embeddings, which then provide input to the neural network layers, ultimately producing the classification predictions. The embedding quality and model architecture selection represent the most critical decisions affecting performance.

**Design Tradeoffs**: The study balances between using pre-trained embeddings (which provide broader linguistic knowledge but may lack domain specificity) versus custom embeddings (which capture domain patterns but require sufficient training data). Similarly, CNN models offer faster training and good local pattern detection, while BiLSTM models provide better contextual understanding at the cost of increased computational complexity and training time.

**Failure Signatures**: Models may fail when encountering feedback containing sarcasm, complex compound words, or domain-specific terminology not well-represented in the embeddings. Performance degradation is likely when feedback contains mixed languages, informal expressions, or references to agencies not well-represented in the training data.
