# S-REINFORCE: A Neuro-Symbolic Policy Gradient Approach for Interpretable Reinforcement Learning

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2305.07367
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.07367
- Reference count: 7
- Primary result: S-REINFORCE combines neural networks and symbolic regression to generate interpretable policies while maintaining strong performance across various RL environments.

## Executive Summary
S-REINFORCE introduces a neuro-symbolic reinforcement learning algorithm that produces both high-performing and interpretable policies by combining neural network policy gradient learning with periodic symbolic regression. The approach alternates between training a neural network to learn numerical policies and fitting symbolic expressions to capture interpretable policy forms. By using importance sampling with the symbolic policy, S-REINFORCE reduces variance in policy gradient estimates while maintaining interpretability. The method has been validated across multiple environments with both discrete and continuous action spaces.

## Method Summary
S-REINFORCE trains a neural network using standard policy gradient methods while periodically fitting a symbolic regressor to the network's output. During SR fitting intervals, the symbolic policy is used for importance sampling to reduce gradient variance. The SR is updated at regular intervals rather than continuously, balancing interpretability gains with computational efficiency. The importance sampling correction uses the symbolic policy when it closely approximates the neural network policy, otherwise reverting to standard Monte Carlo sampling.

## Key Results
- Achieves competitive or superior performance compared to standard policy gradient methods across tested environments
- Generates interpretable symbolic policy expressions that relate states to action probabilities
- Demonstrates effectiveness on both discrete (Cartpole, Acrobot) and continuous (Lunar Lander) action space environments
- Shows improved reward performance when importance sampling is applied with accurate symbolic policies

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: S-REINFORCE improves policy interpretability by periodically transferring knowledge from a neural network (NN) to a symbolic regressor (SR) during training.
- Mechanism: The NN learns a numerical policy through standard policy gradient updates, while the SR fits to the NN's output at regular intervals to capture interpretable symbolic expressions. This reduces the need for expensive symbolic regression throughout all training episodes.
- Core assumption: The NN's learned policy converges to a stable representation that can be effectively captured by symbolic regression at intermediate points.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "The NN component learns to generate a numerical probability distribution over the possible actions using a policy gradient, while the SR component captures the functional form that relates the associated states with the action probabilities."
  - [section] "The periodic knowledge transfer from NN to SR alleviates the cost of symbolic regression throughout all training episodes."
- Break condition: If the NN's policy changes too rapidly or chaotically between SR fitting intervals, the symbolic expressions will be inaccurate or misleading.

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: Importance sampling using the symbolic policy reduces variance in policy gradient estimates compared to Monte Carlo sampling alone.
- Mechanism: When the symbolic policy approximates the NN policy well, using it for action sampling through importance sampling corrects for sampling error and reduces variance in the gradient estimate.
- Core assumption: The symbolic policy remains close enough to the NN policy to provide a valid importance sampling correction without introducing high variance.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "The SR-generated policy expressions are then utilized through importance sampling to improve the rewards received during the learning process."
  - [section] "When actions are sampled from ˜πsym, which is close to πD, then this sampling correction eliminates variance in the action selection."
- Break condition: If the symbolic policy diverges significantly from the NN policy, importance sampling will introduce high variance and degrade performance.

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: Concurrent training of NN and SR provides a cost-effective balance between interpretability and computational efficiency.
- Mechanism: The NN is trained exhaustively throughout all episodes, while the SR is fitted only at regular intervals, avoiding the exponential computational cost of symbolic regression at every step.
- Core assumption: The SR can effectively capture the policy function from data collected during limited intervals without needing continuous updates.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "The periodic knowledge transfer from NN to SR alleviates the cost of symbolic regression throughout all training episodes."
  - [section] "Along with training the NN, the SR is started fitting at episode Etf, and thereafter, the fitting is continued at regular intervals of Eδ episodes."
- Break condition: If the SR requires more frequent updates to accurately track the policy, the computational savings will be diminished or eliminated.

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map:
  - Neural Network (NN) -> Policy gradient module -> Action selection
  - Data collection -> Symbolic Regressor (SR) -> Importance sampling controller
  - SR -> Symbolic policy expressions -> Policy gradient module

- Critical path:
  1. Collect trajectory using current policy
  2. Compute rewards-to-go Gt for each transition
  3. If outside SR fitting interval: Train NN with Monte Carlo loss
  4. If within SR fitting interval: Train NN with importance sampling loss
  5. At SR fitting intervals: Fit SR to NN outputs from collected data
  6. Use fitted symbolic policy for importance sampling until next fitting

- Design tradeoffs:
  - SR fitting frequency vs. computational cost: More frequent fitting improves symbolic policy accuracy but increases computational overhead
  - SR basis function complexity vs. interpretability: More complex basis functions can capture better policies but may reduce interpretability
  - Importance sampling activation threshold vs. performance stability: Earlier activation may help learning but risks high variance if symbolic policy is inaccurate

- Failure signatures:
  - Sudden drops in reward after SR fitting intervals indicate symbolic policy is diverging from NN policy
  - Persistent low rewards suggest SR cannot capture the NN policy effectively
  - High variance in gradient estimates indicates importance sampling is introducing noise rather than reducing it
  - Computational bottlenecks at SR fitting intervals suggest the symbolic regression is too expensive for the chosen frequency

- First 3 experiments:
  1. Cartpole-v0 environment with basic SR fitting every 10 episodes to verify the framework works on a simple control task
  2. Acrobot-v1 environment to test the approach on a higher-dimensional action space with discrete actions
  3. LunarLanderContinuous-v2 environment to validate performance on a continuous action space requiring Gaussian policy representation

## Limitations
- Limited empirical validation across a small set of benchmark environments without comprehensive comparison to state-of-the-art methods
- Computational complexity of symbolic regression fitting may become prohibitive for high-dimensional state spaces
- Dependence on periodic SR fitting introduces potential policy discontinuity and requires careful tuning of fitting intervals

## Confidence
- Medium: The core mechanism of alternating between NN training and SR fitting is well-described, but empirical validation is limited to a small set of environments.
- Low: Claims about variance reduction through importance sampling lack rigorous theoretical backing or ablation studies demonstrating the effect size.
- Medium: The interpretability benefits are demonstrated qualitatively through symbolic expressions, but quantitative measures of interpretability are absent.

## Next Checks
1. Ablation Study on SR Frequency: Systematically vary the SR fitting interval (Eδ) to identify the optimal balance between computational cost and policy accuracy, measuring both reward performance and symbolic expression quality.

2. Importance Sampling Variance Analysis: Implement variance tracking for policy gradient estimates with and without importance sampling across training episodes to empirically verify the claimed variance reduction benefits.

3. Scalability Test: Evaluate S-REINFORCE on environments with significantly higher dimensional state and action spaces (e.g., robotic control tasks from OpenAI Gym) to assess computational feasibility and policy quality degradation patterns.
