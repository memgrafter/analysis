# Improving physics-informed neural networks with meta-learned optimization

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2303.07127
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.07127
- Authors: 
- Reference count: 33
- One-line primary result: Meta-learned optimizers substantially reduce training loss and improve accuracy of physics-informed neural networks compared to standard optimizers like Adam.

## Executive Summary
This paper introduces a meta-learning approach to optimize physics-informed neural networks (PINNs) for solving differential equations. The authors propose training a learnable optimizer, implemented as a multi-layer perceptron, on a distribution of differential equation tasks using an evolutionary strategy. This meta-learned optimizer is then used to train PINNs on various differential equations, resulting in reduced training loss and improved accuracy compared to standard optimizers like Adam. The meta-learned optimizer also exhibits transfer learning abilities, allowing it to be successfully deployed on different differential equations.

## Method Summary
The method involves training a meta-learned optimizer, implemented as a multi-layer perceptron with three output heads (step size, bias, and weight update), using a persistent evolutionary strategy on a set of differential equation tasks. The meta-learned optimizer is then used to train physics-informed neural networks on specific differential equations, with the goal of reducing training loss and improving solution accuracy. The approach is evaluated on several differential equations, including the linear advection equation, Poisson's equation, the Korteweg-de Vries equation, and Burgers' equation, and compared against the standard Adam optimizer.

## Key Results
- Meta-learned optimizers substantially reduce training loss compared to Adam optimizer for physics-informed neural networks.
- Meta-learned optimizers improve the accuracy of solutions to differential equations, as measured by point-wise error against analytical or high-resolution reference solutions.
- Meta-learned optimizers exhibit transfer learning abilities, allowing them to be successfully deployed on different differential equations not seen during meta-training.

  5. Use the trained optimizer to train the physics-informed neural network on a specific task

- Design tradeoffs:
  - Size of the meta-learned optimizer: Larger optimizers may be more expressive but also more computationally expensive to meta-train
  - Diversity of the meta-training task distribution: More diverse tasks may lead to better generalization but also make meta-training more challenging
  - Hyperparameters of the meta-training process: Different choices may lead to different levels of performance and computational cost

- Failure signatures:
  - Meta-learned optimizer does not improve over standard optimizers: The meta-training process may not have converged or the task distribution may not be representative
  - Meta-learned optimizer overfits to the meta-training tasks: The optimizer may not generalize well to new tasks, indicating a need for more diverse meta-training tasks or regularization
  - Meta-learned optimizer leads to unstable training: The optimizer may be too aggressive or the learning rate may be too high, requiring tuning of the optimizer architecture or training process

- What evidence would resolve it: Systematic studies investigating the transfer learning capabilities of meta-learned optimizers across a diverse set of differential equation classes, including both related and unrelated equations.

## Limitations
- Limited scope of evaluation: The paper only considers a small set of differential equations to demonstrate the effectiveness of the meta-learned optimizer.
- Lack of comparison to other meta-learning approaches: The paper only compares the meta-learned optimizer to standard optimizers like Adam.
- Potential overfitting: The meta-learned optimizer is trained on a specific set of tasks and then applied to new tasks, which may lead to overfitting.

## Confidence
