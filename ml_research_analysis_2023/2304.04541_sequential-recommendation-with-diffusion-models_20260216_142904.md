# Sequential Recommendation with Diffusion Models

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2304.04541
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2304.04541
- Reference count: 40
- Primary result: State-of-the-art performance on three benchmark datasets, achieving 16.27% improvement over DuoRec on Beauty dataset NDCG@5

## Executive Summary
This paper introduces DiffRec, a novel diffusion model-based approach for sequential recommendation that addresses key limitations of existing VAE- and GAN-based methods. The authors propose a carefully designed transition process that maps discrete items to hidden representations in the forward process and converts hidden representations back to item probability distributions in the reverse process. By introducing a selective noising strategy that only perturbs the target item rather than the entire sequence, DiffRec achieves superior performance on benchmark datasets while maintaining computational efficiency.

## Method Summary
DiffRec adapts diffusion models to sequential recommendation by designing transitions between discrete items and hidden representations. The forward process progressively adds noise to discrete items, converting them to hidden representations, while the reverse process denoises to reconstruct item probability distributions. The key innovation is a selective noising strategy that only perturbs the target item, making the approach more suitable for sequential recommendation tasks. The model also incorporates efficient training and inference strategies to reduce computational cost while maintaining state-of-the-art performance.

## Key Results
- Achieves state-of-the-art performance on Beauty, Toys, and ML-1M datasets
- Outperforms SASRec, BERT4Rec, STOSA, ACVAE, and CL4SRec by significant margins
- Demonstrates 16.27% improvement over DuoRec on Beauty dataset NDCG@5
- Shows superior performance across multiple evaluation metrics including Recall@K and NDCG@K

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The diffusion model framework provides a probabilistic approach to modeling the sequential recommendation task. By treating the item prediction as a denoising problem, DiffRec can capture complex sequential patterns and long-range dependencies more effectively than traditional autoregressive models. The selective noising strategy ensures that the model focuses on learning meaningful item transitions rather than being distracted by noise in the entire sequence context.

## Architecture Onboarding

**Component Map:** Input Sequence -> Forward Process (Noising) -> Hidden Representation -> Reverse Process (Denoising) -> Item Probability Distribution

**Critical Path:** The denoising network in the reverse process is the critical component, as it must accurately reconstruct item probabilities from increasingly noisy inputs.

**Design Tradeoffs:** Selective noising vs. full sequence noising - selective noising reduces computational complexity but may miss some contextual information; diffusion steps vs. model capacity - more steps improve reconstruction but increase computational cost.

**Failure Signatures:** Poor performance on long sequences may indicate insufficient denoising steps or inadequate model capacity; degraded performance on sparse datasets may suggest the need for better item representation learning.

**First Experiments:** 1) Ablation study on number of diffusion steps, 2) Comparison of selective vs. full noising strategies, 3) Evaluation on datasets with varying sequence lengths
