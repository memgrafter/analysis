# Putting Natural in Natural Language Processing

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2305.04572
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.04572
- Authors: 
- Reference count: 7
- Key outcome: Argues NLP has focused on written language at the expense of spoken language, which is the primary human communication modality

## Executive Summary
This paper makes the case that Natural Language Processing has been dominated by written language processing due to historical convenience, despite spoken language being the primary mode of human communication. The author argues that spoken language contains richer information including paralinguistic features like emotional state, intonation, and rhythm that are lost in writing. Recent advances in deep learning have created convergence between speech processing and NLP methods, providing an opportunity to unify these fields and develop more natural language models.

## Method Summary
The paper does not present a specific method or training procedure for reproduction. Instead, it analyzes the current state of NLP research through publication patterns in the ACL anthology, discusses the challenges and opportunities of processing spoken language, and advocates for a shift in focus within the NLP community towards spoken language processing. The paper highlights the convergence of methods in speech processing and NLP due to the adoption of deep learning, particularly transformer architectures and self-supervised learning approaches.

## Key Results
- NLP has overwhelmingly focused on written rather than spoken language due to historical convenience
- Spoken language contains richer information than written language, including paralinguistic features
- Recent deep learning advances have created methodological convergence between speech processing and NLP

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
Written language processing in NLP has dominated the field due to historical convenience and efficiency, creating an implicit bias against spoken language research. The ubiquity and efficiency of written text as a symbolic representation of language made it the default focus for NLP researchers, leading to specialized methods and benchmarks designed for written text that do not transfer well to spoken language.

### Mechanism 2
Spoken language contains richer information than written language, including paralinguistic features that improve model performance. Spoken language carries additional information like speaker identity, emotional state, intonation, rhythm, and visual cues that are lost in written transcription, providing more signal for models to learn from.

### Mechanism 3
Convergence of methods between speech processing and NLP creates opportunity for unification and advancement. Both fields now use similar deep learning architectures (transformers) and self-supervised learning approaches, reducing technical barriers to integration and enabling cross-pollination of ideas and methods.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
How can NLP models be developed to process spoken language directly, without relying on transcriptions, and what would be the performance impact compared to text-based models? Current NLP models are predominantly text-based, and developing models that can process raw audio signals directly presents significant technical challenges.

### Open Question 2
What is the impact of incorporating paralinguistic information (speaker identity, emotional state, intonation, rhythm) on the performance of NLP models for tasks such as sentiment analysis, dialogue systems, and speaker recognition? Current NLP benchmarks and evaluation metrics are largely designed for text, and there is limited research on how to effectively utilize paralinguistic information in NLP models.

### Open Question 3
How can NLP models be made more data-efficient by learning from spoken language, similar to how human children acquire language through exposure to speech and grounded perception? Most current NLP models are trained on large amounts of text data, and there is limited research on how to leverage the self-supervised learning potential of spoken language and its grounding in perception.

## Limitations

- The paper lacks quantitative data on the relative performance of speech-based vs text-based models on comparable tasks
- The assertion that spoken language is "richer" and therefore should lead to better models remains largely theoretical without systematic ablation studies
- The proposed unification of speech processing and NLP lacks concrete implementation guidelines or evaluation frameworks

## Confidence

**High Confidence**: The historical dominance of written language in NLP is well-documented and the methodological convergence between speech processing and NLP through transformers is clearly established.

**Medium Confidence**: The claim that spoken language contains richer information than written language is supported by linguistic theory but requires empirical validation through systematic model comparisons.

**Low Confidence**: The assertion that focusing on spoken language will automatically lead to better models and more human-like language processing lacks direct experimental evidence in the paper.
