# Can Membership Inferencing be Refuted?

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2303.03648
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.03648
- Reference count: 40
- This paper shows that model owners can refute membership inference claims by constructing proofs-of-repudiation that demonstrate a model could have been trained without the target data point

## Executive Summary
This paper investigates the practical reliability of membership inference (MI) attacks by demonstrating that model owners can effectively neutralize MI claims through a novel refutation mechanism. The authors introduce proofs-of-repudiation (PoRs) - forged training logs that show a model could have been trained without the target data point while maintaining functional equivalence. Their empirical results show successful PoR generation for 98.8% of MNIST and 87.8% of CIFAR-10 training data points, challenging the practical effectiveness of MI attacks as a privacy metric.

## Method Summary
The authors propose a novel approach where model owners can refute membership inference attacks by constructing proofs-of-repudiation (PoRs). The core mechanism involves generating forged training logs that demonstrate a model could have been trained without the target data point while maintaining parameter similarity. The algorithm strategically replaces mini-batches containing the target point with alternative mini-batches that produce nearly identical gradient updates. To improve computational efficiency, the method reuses forged mini-batches across multiple training points, achieving three orders of magnitude speedup. The approach also handles practical training techniques like data augmentation and momentum through careful tracking and replication of update rules.

## Key Results
- Successfully constructed valid PoRs for 98.8% of MNIST training data points
- Achieved 87.8% success rate for CIFAR-10 training data points
- PoRs effectively neutralize MI attacks even from state-of-the-art methods
- Computational overhead reduced by three orders of magnitude through mini-batch reuse

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: Model owners can refute membership inference claims by constructing proofs-of-repudiation (PoRs) that demonstrate a model could have been trained without the target data point.
- Mechanism: The model owner generates a forged training log showing the model's training trajectory on a dataset that excludes the target data point. This forged log is constructed by replacing mini-batches containing the target point with alternative mini-batches that produce nearly identical gradient updates.
- Core assumption: Two models with parameters differing by at most ε' are functionally equivalent for membership inference attacks.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "model owners can refute MI claims by constructing proofs-of-repudiation (PoRs) demonstrating that a model could have been trained without the target data point"
  - [section II] "The PoR enables the model owner to plausibly deny the MI prediction and present a counter claim that x∗ is in fact not a member"
  - [corpus] Weak - related papers discuss MI attacks but don't address repudiation mechanisms
- Break condition: If the target data point is an outlier or out-of-distribution, or if the adversary knows all but one data point in the training set

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: Forging training logs is computationally feasible through strategic reuse of forged mini-batches across multiple training points.
- Mechanism: The algorithm splits the training dataset into κ subsets for each iteration, generates forged mini-batches for one subset, and reuses them for all data points in that subset. This reduces computational overhead by three orders of magnitude compared to naive approaches.
- Core assumption: The forged mini-batches can approximate the original gradients sufficiently well while excluding the target data point.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [section III-A] "To reduce the overhead of expensive gradient computation, we propose to re-use forged mini-batches across PoRs"
  - [section III-A] "The total number of gradient computation in our algorithm is κ · µ · τ + n · τ /λ"
  - [corpus] Weak - related work focuses on MI attacks themselves rather than forging mechanisms
- Break condition: If the data augmentation or SGD modifications cannot be properly accounted for in the forging process

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: PoRs can be generated efficiently even with practical training techniques like data augmentation and modified SGD.
- Mechanism: The algorithm extends support for random horizontal flip augmentation by storing transformation information, and accommodates momentum, weight decay, and learning rate scheduling by using the same update rules when reconstructing forged models.
- Core assumption: The modified training techniques can be reversed or simulated when generating forged logs.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [section III-A] "We extend our algorithm to support these techniques as follows" (momentum, weight decay, learning rate scheduling)
  - [section III-A] "We store a binary flag along with each sample index to indicate whether the corresponding sample is flipped"
  - [corpus] Weak - related papers discuss MI attacks but not handling of practical training modifications
- Break condition: If the training technique creates dependencies that cannot be replicated without the original data point

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map:
  Original model training pipeline -> PoR generation algorithm -> MI attack evaluation framework -> Dataset preprocessing and augmentation handling -> Forged mini-batch storage and retrieval system

- Critical path:
  1. Train original model with logging of all training details
  2. Generate forged mini-batches for each training point
  3. Construct PoR from forged mini-batches
  4. Verify PoR by reproducing training trajectory
  5. Evaluate against MI attacks to confirm refutation

- Design tradeoffs:
  - Computational efficiency vs quality of forging (λ parameter)
  - Storage requirements for forged mini-batches vs on-the-fly reconstruction
  - Support for complex training techniques vs simplicity of implementation
  - Proof generation time vs verification time

- Failure signatures:
  - High ℓ2 distance between original and forged models indicates poor forging quality
  - Significant difference in MI attack predictions between models suggests insufficient equivalence
  - Inability to generate PoR for certain data points indicates potential outliers
  - High variance in membership score differences reveals model sensitivity issues

- First 3 experiments:
  1. Generate PoRs for a small subset of MNIST data points using standard LeNet5 to verify basic functionality
  2. Test PoR generation with data augmentation enabled to validate augmentation handling
  3. Evaluate PoR quality against different MI attack types to assess attack agnosticism

## Limitations
- Success rate drops to 0% when adversaries know all but one data point in the training set
- Assumes adversaries cannot distinguish between legitimate and forged training trajectories
- May not hold for highly sensitive models or data outliers where parameter distance constraints are violated

## Confidence
- Central claim about neutralizing MI attacks: High confidence
- Computational feasibility of forging training logs: Medium confidence
- Success rate claims: High confidence for evaluated scenarios
