# Dictionary-based Phrase-level Prompting of Large Language Models for Machine Translation

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2302.07856
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2302.07856
- Reference count: 8
- We propose a novel approach, DiPMT, for incorporating dictionary information into prompting-based machine translation. This method enables fine-grained phrase-level control by providing possible translations for input words in the prompts. Our experiments demonstrate significant improvements in translation quality for both low-resource and out-of-domain settings. Specifically, DiPMT achieves an average improvement of 0.9 BLEU points for low-resource languages and up to 13 BLEU points for out-of-domain translation. Furthermore, our analysis reveals that DiPMT provides a substantial level of controllability over the final translation, with a significant gap compared to baseline methods.

## Executive Summary
This paper introduces Dictionary-based Phrase-level Prompting (DiPMT), a novel approach for incorporating dictionary information into prompting-based machine translation. DiPMT enhances large language models' translation capabilities by providing possible translations for input words directly in the prompts, enabling fine-grained control over the output. The method shows significant improvements in translation quality for both low-resource languages and out-of-domain scenarios, with BLEU score improvements of up to 13 points. The approach demonstrates that dictionary-based prompting can effectively guide large language models without requiring explicit training, offering a promising direction for improving translation quality in challenging settings.

## Method Summary
DiPMT incorporates dictionary information into prompts by appending hints about possible translations for specific source words. The method uses a few-shot prompting approach with OPT-175B or Bloom-176B models, where dictionary translations are added to the prompt in the format "In this context, the word X means A; the word Y means B, C, D." The approach excludes the 500 most frequent words from hinting and provides up to 3 dictionary translations per word. For experiments, the method uses Flores-101 for low-resource languages and various out-of-domain datasets, with dictionaries either from Conneau et al. (2017) or automatically extracted using Shi et al. (2021) method.

## Key Results
- DiPMT achieves an average improvement of 0.9 BLEU points for low-resource languages
- Up to 13 BLEU points improvement for out-of-domain translation tasks
- The model performs better than baseline when word type coverage exceeds 20% for most language pairs
- Providing gold hints (matching reference translations) improves BLEU scores by an additional 1.1 points on average

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: Dictionary hints in prompts improve rare word translation by providing explicit lexical options.
- Mechanism: The LLM is prompted with possible translations for specific input words, which guides the model's generation without enforcing hard constraints.
- Core assumption: The LLM's attention mechanism can integrate the provided dictionary hints during generation.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "Our experiments demonstrate significant improvements in translation quality for both low-resource and out-of-domain settings."
  - [section] "DIPMT uses prompting-based translation and incorporates dictionary information into the prompt directly."
- Break condition: If the LLM's context window is insufficient to include both the dictionary hints and the source sentence meaningfully, or if the hints are not relevant to the context.

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: Word type coverage in the dictionary correlates with translation quality improvement.
- Mechanism: Higher coverage of source word types in the dictionary leads to more opportunities for the model to use hints, thereby improving translation accuracy.
- Core assumption: The LLM can effectively utilize hints for words it has dictionary entries for, and these hints are relevant to the translation task.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [section] "The model performs better than the baseline when the word type coverage is above a certain threshold (20% for Ind→Eng and 5−10% for other pairs)."
  - [corpus] "Dictionary-based prompting methods can enhance translation on them, but most methods use all the available dictionaries, which could be expensive."
- Break condition: If the dictionary contains too many irrelevant translations or if the model overfits to the hints provided, leading to degradation in performance.

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: Providing gold hints (those that match the reference translation) leads to higher BLEU scores than providing all available dictionary translations.
- Mechanism: By constraining the hints to only those that are correct, the model is guided more precisely towards the correct translation.
- Core assumption: The dictionary contains accurate translations that can be matched to the reference.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [section] "Table 6 presents the results. It shows that if DIPMT has access to the oracle translations, it can improve the BLEU score by another a 1.1 points on average for OPT."
- Break condition: If the dictionary does not contain the correct translations or if the model ignores the hints even when they are correct.

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map:
  - Input sentence -> Dictionary lookup -> Prompt construction -> LLM generation -> Output translation
- Critical path:
  - Input sentence → Dictionary lookup → Prompt construction → LLM generation → Output translation
- Design tradeoffs:
  - Providing more hints can improve accuracy but may also introduce noise or cause the model to ignore the hints.
  - Using a smaller context window limits the number of hints that can be provided.
- Failure signatures:
  - Degradation in translation quality when irrelevant hints are provided.
  - No improvement over baseline when dictionary coverage is too low.

  1. Test DIPMT with a small dictionary to observe the minimum coverage needed for improvement.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- Question: How does DIPMT perform when extended to incorporate phrase-level translations instead of word-level translations?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper states "Extending DIPMT to incorporate phrase-level translations should be trivial, and we leave this exploration to future work."
- Why unresolved: The current implementation is limited to word-level translations, and the performance of phrase-level extensions has not been evaluated.
- What evidence would resolve it: Experimental results comparing phrase-level DIPMT with the current word-level implementation across various language pairs and domains.

### Open Question 2
- Question: What is the optimal method for selecting which words to provide dictionary hints for in the prompts?
- Basis in paper: [inferred] The paper uses a heuristic of excluding the 500 most frequent words, but this may not be optimal for all language pairs or domains.
- Why unresolved: The current approach is based on a simple frequency threshold, which may not capture the most challenging or beneficial words for translation in all contexts.
- What evidence would resolve it: Comparative analysis of different word selection strategies (e.g., based on difficulty, ambiguity, or domain-specific importance) and their impact on translation quality.

### Open Question 3
- Question: How does DIPMT's performance scale with larger language models and more comprehensive dictionaries?
- Basis in paper: [inferred] The experiments are conducted with specific model sizes (OPT-175B and Bloom-176B) and dictionary coverage rates, but the scalability of performance improvements is not explored.
- Why unresolved: The relationship between model size, dictionary coverage, and translation quality improvements is not fully characterized, particularly for models larger than those tested.
- What evidence would resolve it: Systematic experiments varying model sizes and dictionary coverage rates to determine the point of diminishing returns for DIPMT's effectiveness.

## Limitations

- Dictionary Quality and Relevance: The paper relies on dictionaries that are either human-annotated or automatically extracted, but the exact quality and relevance of these dictionaries for out-of-domain data remain uncertain.
- Context Window Constraints: The paper does not explicitly discuss the impact of context window limitations on the effectiveness of dictionary hints.
- Model Compatibility: The performance of the method across different model architectures and sizes is not thoroughly explored.

## Confidence

**High Confidence**:
- The mechanism by which dictionary hints improve translation quality for rare words is well-supported by the experimental results showing BLEU score improvements, particularly for low-resource and out-of-domain settings.
- The correlation between dictionary coverage and translation quality improvement is clearly demonstrated, with a threshold effect observed at around 20% coverage for most language pairs.

**Medium Confidence**:
- The claim that providing gold hints leads to higher BLEU scores than providing all available dictionary translations is supported by the data, but the extent of improvement may vary depending on the quality and coverage of the dictionary.
- The assertion that the method provides a substantial level of controllability over the final translation is plausible given the observed differences in hint usage, but the exact degree of controllability may depend on specific implementation details and model behavior.

**Low Confidence**:
- The potential for model overfitting to hints or the impact of irrelevant hints on performance is acknowledged but not deeply explored, leaving some uncertainty about the robustness of the method across different datasets and languages.
