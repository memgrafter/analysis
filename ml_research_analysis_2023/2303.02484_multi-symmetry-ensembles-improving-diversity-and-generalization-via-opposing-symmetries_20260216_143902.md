# Multi-Symmetry Ensembles: Improving Diversity and Generalization via Opposing Symmetries

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2303.02484
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.02484
- Reference count: 19
- Primary result: Multi-Symmetry Ensembles achieve up to 0.9% accuracy improvement over naive ensembles by capturing opposing invariant and equivariant hypotheses

## Executive Summary
This paper introduces Multi-Symmetry Ensembles (MSE), a framework that improves ensemble diversity and generalization by explicitly capturing opposing hypotheses along symmetry axes. Unlike traditional deep ensembles that rely on stochastic perturbations, MSE leverages contrastive representation learning to create models with fundamentally different symmetry properties (invariant vs equivariant to transformations). The authors demonstrate that ensembles constructed from models with opposing hypotheses outperform naive ensembles of a single hypothesis type across multiple datasets, with improved uncertainty quantification and transfer learning performance.

## Method Summary
The MSE framework involves pre-training models using contrastive learning with invariance and equivariance constraints, fine-tuning each model for classification, and then constructing ensembles using greedy or random selection strategies. The key innovation is using opposing symmetry constraints during pre-training to create models that capture fundamentally different hypotheses about the data, rather than relying solely on stochastic variations.

## Key Results
- MSE achieves up to 0.9% improvement in classification accuracy compared to naive ensembles
- Ensembles with opposing hypotheses show better uncertainty quantification (lower NLL, higher area under uncertainty curve)
- Including lower-accuracy models from opposing hypotheses can improve ensemble performance more than adding higher-accuracy models from the leading hypothesis
- Transfer learning performance improves when ensembles contain multiple symmetry-based hypotheses

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: Multi-Symmetry Ensembles capture opposing hypotheses along symmetry axes to improve ensemble diversity beyond stochastic perturbations
- Mechanism: Contrastive learning creates models separately invariant and equivariant to transformations, capturing conflicting hypotheses that traditional deep ensembles miss
- Core assumption: Large, diverse datasets contain multiple and conflicting dominant hypotheses requiring exploration beyond a single hypothesis class
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "We leverage recent advances in contrastive representation learning to create models that separately capture opposing hypotheses of invariant and equivariant functional classes"
  - [section] "While deep ensembles are effective at capturing different solutions around one hypothesis, Multi-Symmetry Ensembles can learn diverse solutions around inherently opposing hypotheses"
  - [corpus] Weak evidence - related papers focus on ensemble diversity but not specifically on symmetry-based opposing hypotheses
- Break condition: If the dataset is small or homogeneous, one hypothesis may dominate uniformly

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: Including lower-accuracy models from opposing hypotheses can improve ensemble performance more than adding higher-accuracy models from the leading hypothesis
- Mechanism: Greedy ensemble selection alternates between equivariant and invariant models, suggesting diversity along symmetry axes is more valuable than pure accuracy
- Core assumption: Error inconsistency between models from opposing hypotheses drives ensemble performance
- Evidence anchors:
  - [section] "Ensembles constructed only from a single hypothesis very quickly give marginal ensembling gains from adding more members"
  - [section] "the outcome of the greedy search produces the following sequence of models: [Eq, Inv, Eq, Eq, Inv, Eq, Inv]"
  - [corpus] Moderate evidence - related papers discuss diversity but not specifically the benefit of including lower-accuracy opposing hypotheses
- Break condition: If models from opposing hypotheses have very high error correlation

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: Multi-Symmetry Ensembles improve uncertainty quantification and transfer learning performance by capturing multiple hypotheses
- Mechanism: Models with different symmetry properties better represent uncertainty around each hypothesis, improving NLL and transfer performance
- Core assumption: Different downstream tasks encompass different sets of hypotheses, so ensembles with multiple symmetry-based hypotheses generalize better
- Evidence anchors:
  - [section] "Multi-Symmetry Ensembles of opposing hypotheses (Eq + Inv) performs slightly better in terms of NLL when compared to ensembles with members only sampled from a single hypothesis (Eq)"
  - [section] "after transfer learning on iNaturalist-1K, for example, the invariant model switches to become the dominant hypothesis, outperforming the equivariant model by 1.2%"
  - [corpus] Weak evidence - related papers discuss transfer learning but not specifically symmetry-based ensembles
- Break condition: If transfer tasks are very similar to pre-training dataset

## Foundational Learning

- Concept: Invariance vs Equivariance
  - Why needed here: The paper relies on understanding that invariance means outputs don't change when inputs are transformed, while equivariance means outputs transform according to input transformations
  - Quick check question: What's the difference between a model that's rotation invariant vs rotation equivariant when applied to a rotated image?

- Concept: Contrastive Representation Learning
  - Why needed here: The framework uses contrastive learning to create invariant and equivariant models by pulling together views of the same image while pushing away views of different images
  - Quick check question: How does the InfoNCE loss encourage invariance to the base transformations in contrastive learning?

- Concept: Ensemble Diversity Metrics
  - Why needed here: The paper evaluates diversity using error inconsistency, variance of predictions, and KL-divergence between model predictions
  - Quick check question: Why is error inconsistency a good measure of ensemble diversity, and how is it calculated?

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map:
  Backbone encoder (ResNet-50) -> Projector network -> Prediction network (for equivariant models) -> Linear classifier (fine-tuning) -> Ensemble combination (averaging)

- Critical path:
  1. Pre-train contrastive learners with invariant/equivariant constraints
  2. Fine-tune each pre-trained model into a classifier
  3. Apply greedy selection to choose diverse ensemble members
  4. Combine predictions via averaging

- Design tradeoffs:
  - Using stronger backbones vs simpler models for faster experimentation
  - Linear probing vs fine-tuning for converting to classifiers
  - Random vs greedy ensemble selection strategies
  - Number of transformations to explore (rotation vs additional symmetries)

- Failure signatures:
  - Low diversity metrics despite using opposing hypotheses (suggests transformations aren't creating meaningful differences)
  - Greedy selection choosing only models from one hypothesis (suggests dataset doesn't benefit from opposing hypotheses)
  - Poor transfer performance on datasets with high hypothesis imbalance

- First 3 experiments:
  1. Verify that rotation invariant and equivariant models have significantly different per-class accuracies on ImageNet
  2. Test greedy ensemble selection on a small dataset to see if it alternates between invariant and equivariant models
  3. Compare NLL uncertainty quantification between ensembles with and without opposing hypotheses

## Limitations
- The approach relies heavily on large, diverse datasets like ImageNet with multiple conflicting hypotheses
- Evidence base is moderately strong for ImageNet but weak for transfer datasets across only six tested domains
- Contrastive learning hyperparameters and specific implementation details for prediction loss remain unspecified

## Confidence
- High confidence in the core claim that ensembles with opposing hypotheses outperform single-hypothesis ensembles
- Medium confidence in the greedy selection strategy's ability to identify optimal ensemble members
- Medium confidence in the uncertainty quantification improvements across transfer tasks

## Next Checks
1. Replicate the greedy ensemble selection on a small synthetic dataset where hypothesis conflict is artificially induced to verify the alternating pattern
2. Conduct ablation studies on the contrastive learning hyperparameters (temperature, learning rate) to determine their impact on ensemble diversity
3. Test MSE performance on a dataset with known hypothesis imbalance (like imbalanced CIFAR variants) to validate robustness when opposing hypotheses are less clear
