# Stackelberg Games for Learning Emergent Behaviors During Competitive Autocurricula

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2305.03735
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.03735
- Reference count: 35
- Asymmetric MARL environments often converge to low-quality equilibria; ST-MADDPG leverages Stackelberg game theory to improve leader payoff and emergent strategy sophistication.

## Executive Summary
This paper introduces ST-MADDPG, a novel multi-agent reinforcement learning algorithm designed to address the challenges of asymmetric competitive environments where one agent typically dominates, leading to poor strategic co-evolution. By framing the problem as a Stackelberg game—where one agent (leader) anticipates and responds to the other (follower)—ST-MADDPG enables the leader to achieve better equilibrium payoffs and fosters the emergence of more sophisticated behaviors. The method is evaluated across three asymmetric robotics environments, demonstrating clear performance gains and strategic novelty compared to standard MADDPG.

## Method Summary
ST-MADDPG builds on MADDPG by incorporating Stackelberg game theory into the learning process. The algorithm designates one agent as the leader and the other as the follower, with the leader optimizing its policy while anticipating the follower's best response. This hierarchical interaction is implemented by modifying the loss functions and training sequences so that the leader’s updates consider the follower’s optimal reaction, encouraging more robust and strategic behaviors. The approach is tested in asymmetric competitive robotics scenarios, including cartpole, hopper, and fencing tasks.

## Key Results
- ST-MADDPG outperforms MADDPG in asymmetric competitive robotics tasks, particularly where one agent has a clear disadvantage.
- In the Hopper with adversarial disturbances, ST-MADDPG-trained agents show significant robustness improvements under both adversarial and random disturbances.
- In the Fencing Game, some ST-MADDPG agents develop advanced strategies, such as manipulating opponent joint configurations.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The Stackelberg formulation gives the leader agent an advantage by forcing it to anticipate and counteract the follower's best response. This hierarchical setup prevents the follower from exploiting the leader, leading to higher equilibrium payoffs and more complex, adaptive strategies. By shifting from a symmetric to an asymmetric game structure, ST-MADDPG promotes richer co-evolutionary dynamics.

## Foundational Learning
- **Multi-Agent Reinforcement Learning (MARL)**: Understanding cooperative and competitive dynamics between multiple agents in shared environments.
  - Why needed: Asymmetric tasks require specialized handling to avoid degenerate equilibria.
  - Quick check: MARL algorithms should converge to meaningful, non-trivial equilibria in competitive settings.
- **Stackelberg Games**: Leader-follower strategic frameworks where the leader moves first and the follower responds optimally.
  - Why needed: Provides a principled way to structure asymmetric agent interactions for better payoffs.
  - Quick check: The leader should achieve higher payoffs than in symmetric games.
- **Deep Deterministic Policy Gradient (DDPG)**: Actor-critic algorithm for continuous control in reinforcement learning.
  - Why needed: ST-MADDPG extends MADDPG (a multi-agent extension of DDPG) to incorporate leader-follower dynamics.
  - Quick check: Agents should learn stable, continuous control policies.
- **Asymmetric Competitive Environments**: Scenarios where agents have different strengths or roles, leading to imbalanced equilibria.
  - Why needed: These are common in real-world robotics and game settings.
  - Quick check: Standard MARL should struggle to produce high-quality strategies in these settings.
- **Adversarial Disturbances**: External perturbations designed to destabilize agent performance.
  - Why needed: Tests robustness and adaptability of learned policies.
  - Quick check: Policies should maintain performance under varied, unpredictable disturbances.
- **Emergent Behaviors**: Complex strategies that arise from the interaction of simple rules and learning processes.
  - Why needed: Indicates sophistication and adaptability of the learned policies.
  - Quick check: Behaviors should go beyond naive exploitation and show strategic depth.

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component Map**: ST-MADDPG modifies MADDPG by adding leader/follower designation and modifying loss functions to incorporate follower’s best response.
- **Critical Path**: Agent initialization → Leader/follower assignment → Iterative policy updates (leader anticipates follower) → Policy evaluation and strategy refinement.
- **Design Tradeoffs**: Leader advantage improves payoff but may reduce diversity of emergent strategies; requires careful role assignment.
- **Failure Signatures**: If roles are misassigned, follower may dominate; if hyperparameters are off, convergence may stall or oscillate.
- **First 3 Experiments**:
  1. Run ST-MADDPG vs MADDPG in symmetric tasks to confirm no degradation in balanced scenarios.
  2. Vary leader/follower assignments in asymmetric tasks to test sensitivity and robustness.
  3. Analyze policy trajectories in the Fencing Game to identify and verify qualitatively novel strategies.

## Limitations
- Limited ablation studies leave sensitivity to leader/follower assignment and hyperparameters unclear.
- Claims of "sophisticated strategies" are primarily anecdotal; deeper qualitative analysis is needed.
- Applicability to cooperative asymmetric tasks has not been explored.

## Confidence
- ST-MADDPG outperforms MADDPG in asymmetric competitive tasks: High
- Leader advantage leads to higher equilibrium payoffs: Medium
- Learned behaviors are qualitatively more sophisticated: Low

## Next Checks
1. Conduct ablation studies varying leader/follower roles and initialization to test sensitivity.
2. Perform qualitative behavioral analysis (e.g., trajectory visualization, action diversity) to substantiate claims of strategic sophistication.
3. Test ST-MADDPG in asymmetric cooperative settings to assess broader applicability beyond competitive games.
