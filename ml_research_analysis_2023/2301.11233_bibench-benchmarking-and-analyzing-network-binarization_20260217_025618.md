# BiBench: Benchmarking and Analyzing Network Binarization

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2301.11233
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2301.11233
- Reference count: 40
- Provides comprehensive benchmark for network binarization across diverse tasks, architectures, and hardware

## Executive Summary
BiBench provides a comprehensive benchmark for network binarization, addressing challenges in evaluating accuracy and efficiency across diverse tasks, architectures, and hardware. The study evaluates 8 binarization algorithms on 9 datasets, 13 architectures, 2 deployment libraries, and 14 hardware platforms. Key findings include significant performance variation across modalities and tasks, challenges with Transformer architectures, robust corruption resistance in binarized networks, and limitations in hardware inference support despite theoretical efficiency gains.

## Method Summary
The study benchmarks 8 network binarization algorithms across 9 datasets including ImageNet, Cityscapes, BDD100K, and IMDB, using 13 different architectures ranging from CNNs to Transformers. Two deployment libraries (ncnn and TensorRT) and 14 hardware platforms are evaluated for inference performance. The benchmarking framework measures accuracy, robustness to synthetic noise, and training/inference efficiency, providing standardized evaluation protocols for comparing binarization methods.

## Key Results
- Binarized operators significantly impact performance, with accuracy varying greatly across modalities and tasks
- Transformers are challenging for binarization, while CNNs and MLPs perform better
- Binarized networks show robust corruption resistance
- Theoretical complexity is similar across algorithms, but hardware inference is limited by deployment libraries

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
Network binarization reduces computational complexity by constraining weights and activations to {-1, +1}, enabling efficient bitwise operations. The study demonstrates that while theoretical complexity reduction is substantial, practical performance depends heavily on implementation quality through deployment libraries and hardware support. Soft gradient approximation methods and channel-wise scaling factors emerge as critical components for maintaining accuracy during the binarization process.

## Foundational Learning
- **Binary neural networks**: Networks with weights and activations constrained to {-1, +1}, enabling bitwise operations instead of floating-point arithmetic. Needed for understanding the fundamental premise of binarization and its computational advantages.
- **Soft gradient approximation**: Methods that approximate gradients during backpropagation to enable training of discrete networks. Quick check: Verify that straight-through estimators or other gradient approximation methods are being used during training.
- **Channel-wise scaling factors**: Per-channel scaling applied to binary weights to recover accuracy lost during binarization. Quick check: Confirm scaling factors are learned or tuned for each channel rather than using global scaling.

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map**: Data → Model (CNN/Transformer/MLP) → Binarization Algorithm → Deployment Library → Hardware Platform
- **Critical path**: Binarization Algorithm selection → Architecture choice → Deployment Library optimization
- **Design tradeoffs**: Accuracy vs. efficiency tradeoff in binarization, with Transformers suffering more than CNNs
- **Failure signatures**: Poor performance on complex architectures like Transformers, limited hardware support
- **First experiments**: 1) Compare accuracy across different binarization algorithms on CNNs, 2) Evaluate robustness to synthetic noise across architectures, 3) Measure inference time differences between deployment libraries

None

## Limitations
- Focus on binary networks without considering ternary or multi-bit quantization alternatives
- Potential biases from using specific deployment libraries (ncnn and TensorRT)
- Absence of latency measurements on actual edge hardware platforms

## Confidence
- Empirical results comparing binarization algorithms: **High**
- Corruption resistance findings: **Medium** (based on synthetic noise experiments)
- Edge device performance claims: **Low** (limited hardware testing)

## Next Checks
