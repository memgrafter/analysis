# On the Joint Interaction of Models, Data, and Features

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2306.04793
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.04793
- Reference count: 40
- Key outcome: A new framework for understanding feature learning in deep neural networks through an "interaction tensor" approach, explaining Generalization Disagreement Equality (GDE) without calibration assumptions.

## Executive Summary
This paper introduces a novel framework for understanding feature learning in deep neural networks by defining features as principal components of last-layer activations and constructing an "interaction tensor" that captures how features are distributed across data points and models. Through empirical analysis on CIFAR-10, the authors observe that feature frequencies follow a long-tailed distribution and that high-confidence data points tend to have fewer features. Based on these observations, they propose a combinatorial model with dominant and rare features that explains the Generalization Disagreement Equality (GDE) phenomenon without requiring calibration assumptions. The framework is validated through numerical simulations and experiments including interventions like merging classes and partitioning data.

## Method Summary
The method involves training multiple ResNet18 models on CIFAR-10 with different data subsets, extracting last-layer activations, and applying PCA to obtain feature vectors. Features are clustered across models using correlation thresholds, and an interaction tensor is constructed to capture feature presence in data points and models. The framework analyzes feature frequencies and their relationship to model confidence, then derives closed-form expressions for accuracy and agreement under a combinatorial model where features are either dominant (high frequency) or rare (low frequency). The model assumes data points contain either a small number of dominant features or a large number of rare features, and models learn features proportionally to their frequency.

## Key Results
- Feature frequencies in deep networks follow a long-tailed distribution
- Models with higher confidence tend to have learned fewer features
- The number of models with a feature is positively correlated with its frequency in the data
- The combinatorial framework explains GDE without requiring calibration assumptions
- Interventions like merging classes and partitioning data validate the framework's predictive power

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: Features are defined as principal components of last-layer activations, enabling meaningful clustering across models and data points.
- Mechanism: PCA reduces high-dimensional activation vectors to orthogonal subspaces (features). The interaction tensor captures whether a feature is present in both a model and a data point. Clustering uses correlation thresholds to identify shared features across models.
- Core assumption: The principal components of last-layer activations represent the minimal set of features models use for classification, and these features are stable enough to cluster across models.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] defines features as "principal components of last-layer activations"
  - [section 3.1] describes projecting representations to Rk and interpreting principal components as features
  - [corpus] neighbors show tensor-based modeling is a valid approach for capturing interactions
- Break condition: If model activations are highly non-linear or the principal components don't capture discriminative information, the feature definition fails.

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: The interaction tensor's structure reveals that feature frequencies follow a long-tailed distribution, and high-confidence data points have fewer features.
- Mechanism: The tensor is summed over models to count feature frequencies, revealing the distribution. Summing over features and computing confidence allows analysis of the relationship between confidence and feature count.
- Core assumption: The distribution of features in the interaction tensor accurately reflects the true distribution of features in the data.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] states "feature frequencies follow a long-tailed distribution"
  - [section 4] describes using the interaction tensor to compute feature frequencies and their relationship to confidence
  - [corpus] neighbors include tensor-based modeling approaches, validating the tensor methodology
- Break condition: If the thresholding procedure for feature presence is too strict/loose, or if the model ensemble is not representative, the distribution may be skewed.

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: The combinatorial framework with dominant and rare features explains the Generalization Disagreement Equality (GDE) without requiring calibration assumptions.
- Mechanism: The framework models features as either dominant (high frequency) or rare (low frequency), and data points as containing either a small number of dominant features or a large number of rare features. Models learn features proportionally to their frequency. Analytical expressions for accuracy and agreement are derived.
- Core assumption: The simplification of features into two types (dominant and rare) and data points into two types (dominant and rare) captures the essential behavior observed in the interaction tensor.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] states the framework explains "the recently discovered Generalization Disagreement Equality (GDE) that allows for estimating the generalization error with only unlabeled data"
  - [section 5] describes the combinatorial model and derives closed-form expressions for accuracy and agreement
  - [corpus] neighbors show tensor-based modeling is a valid approach for capturing interactions
- Break condition: If the actual feature distribution is not well-approximated by two types, or if the relationship between feature frequency and learning probability is more complex, the framework may not hold.

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map:
  Data preprocessing -> PCA on activations -> Feature clustering -> Interaction tensor construction -> Framework analysis -> Experiments

- Critical path:
  1. Load pre-trained ResNet18 models and extract last-layer activations
  2. Apply PCA to activations to obtain feature vectors
  3. Compute correlation matrices between features across models
  4. Apply greedy clustering algorithm to identify shared features
  5. Construct interaction tensor by thresholding feature presence
  6. Analyze feature frequencies and their relationship to confidence
  7. Implement and analyze the combinatorial framework

- Design tradeoffs:
  - PCA vs. other dimensionality reduction methods: PCA is chosen for its efficiency and ability to capture linear dependencies, but other methods might capture non-linear relationships.
  - Number of principal components: More components capture more information but increase computational cost and risk redundancy.
  - Clustering threshold: Higher thresholds result in fewer, more distinct clusters, while lower thresholds result in more, less distinct clusters.

- Failure signatures:
  - Feature clustering produces too many or too few clusters: The threshold might be too low or too high.
  - The interaction tensor is mostly zeros: The threshold for feature presence might be too high.
  - The combinatorial framework doesn't fit the data: The simplification of features into two types might be too coarse.

- First 3 experiments:
  1. Vary the number of principal components used in PCA and observe the effect on feature clustering and the interaction tensor.
  2. Vary the correlation threshold used in clustering and observe the effect on the number and quality of feature clusters.
  3. Test the combinatorial framework with different values of the parameters (pd, c, td, tr, nd, nr) and observe the effect on accuracy and agreement.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- Question: How does the agreement function ζ depend on the hypothesis distribution and data point x in the general case?
- Basis in paper: [inferred] The paper states that ζ is a function of the hypothesis distribution and a data point x in the most general case, but assumes it is independent of x for simplicity.
- Why unresolved: The paper does not provide an explicit form for ζ in the general case or explore how it might vary with different hypothesis distributions and data points.
- What evidence would resolve it: Empirical studies comparing the agreement between different pairs of models on various data points and hypothesis distributions could provide insights into the form of ζ.

### Open Question 2
- Question: How do features evolve over the course of training, and what factors influence their frequency distribution?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper shows that the feature frequency distribution changes over the course of training, with tail features decreasing in frequency and head features increasing initially before stabilizing.
- Why unresolved: The paper does not investigate the mechanisms behind this evolution or identify the specific factors that influence the final frequency distribution of features.
- What evidence would resolve it: Longitudinal studies tracking the feature frequency distribution of individual features throughout training and correlating these changes with factors such as model architecture, optimization algorithm, and data properties could shed light on the feature evolution process.

### Open Question 3
- Question: How does the model's capacity affect its ability to learn and utilize features, and what is the relationship between capacity and the number of dominant and rare features learned?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper shows that increasing model capacity (c) leads to an increase in both accuracy and agreement, with both metrics saturating at high capacities.
- Why unresolved: The paper does not explore the relationship between capacity and the specific number of dominant and rare features learned by the model, or how this relationship varies with different data distributions.
- What evidence would resolve it: Experiments varying model capacity while keeping other factors constant and measuring the number of dominant and rare features learned could reveal the relationship between capacity and feature learning. Comparing these results across different data distributions could provide further insights.

## Limitations

- The framework's reliance on PCA to define features may miss non-linear or task-irrelevant features
- The simplification of features into two types (dominant and rare) may not capture the full complexity of real-world feature distributions
- The framework's applicability to datasets and model architectures beyond CIFAR-10 and ResNet18 remains uncertain

## Confidence

**High Confidence**: The empirical observations about feature frequencies following a long-tailed distribution and the correlation between feature count and model confidence. The construction of the interaction tensor and the basic methodology for feature clustering are well-defined and reproducible.

**Medium Confidence**: The combinatorial framework's ability to explain GDE without calibration assumptions. While the analytical expressions are derived, the framework's simplifications may limit its applicability to real-world scenarios.

**Low Confidence**: The generalizability of the framework to other datasets and model architectures beyond CIFAR-10 and ResNet18. The paper's focus on a specific setup raises questions about how the framework would perform in different contexts.
