# Understanding the Benefits of Image Augmentations

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2306.06254
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.06254
- **Reference count:** 23
- **Key outcome:** This study uses CKA to analyze how augmentations affect different layers of residual networks, comparing architectures with varying depths and initialization schemes (scratch vs. ImageNet fine-tuning).

## Executive Summary
This study investigates how different image augmentations affect various layers of residual networks by using Centered Kernel Alignment (CKA) to measure representation similarity. The research compares models trained from scratch versus those fine-tuned from pre-trained weights, revealing that deeper layers of fine-tuned ResNets are more significantly impacted by augmentations than early layers. The study also finds that augmentations combining two images (mixup, cutmix) affect networks more than single-image augmentations, with model depth playing a crucial role in determining these impact patterns. These findings have important implications for continual learning and layer freezing strategies.

## Method Summary
The study trains multiple ResNet variants (ResNet-32, ResNet-56, ResNet-18, ResNet-50) on CIFAR-10 and Tiny-ImageNet-200 datasets with various augmentations including horizontal flips, random crops, cutout, mixup, cutmix, and AutoAugment. Two baseline models without augmentations are trained for each architecture, and CKA similarity is computed between corresponding layers of these baselines. The percent decrease in CKA between augmented and baseline models serves as the primary metric for measuring augmentation impact on different layers. The study compares fine-tuned models (initialized with ImageNet weights) against models trained from scratch to understand initialization effects.

## Key Results
- Deeper layers of fine-tuned ResNets show greater CKA changes from augmentations than early layers
- Mixup and cutmix augmentations produce significantly larger CKA changes than single-image augmentations
- Model depth influences augmentation impact patterns, with deeper networks showing more specialized layer responses

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: Deeper layers of fine-tuned ResNets receive more impact from augmentations than early layers because early layers have already learned low-level, universal features during pre-training, while deeper layers must adapt to dataset-specific high-level features.
- Mechanism: Pre-trained weights initialize early layers with robust low-level feature detectors (edges, textures) that transfer across tasks. When fine-tuning, augmentations primarily affect the deeper layers that must specialize to the new dataset's semantic categories.
- Core assumption: The layers of a ResNet have distinct roles, with early layers capturing general visual primitives and deeper layers capturing dataset-specific semantics.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "Deeper layers of ResNets initialized with ImageNet-1K weights and fine-tuned receive more impact from the augmentations than early layers."
  - [section] "Given that the first layers of CNNs are known to learn low, pixel-level features that are more universal than the deeper layers, the early layers of ResNet-18 and ResNet-50 likely already learned strong, universal representations when loaded with the pre-trained ImageNet-1K weights."

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: Augmentations combining two images (mixup, cutmix) affect networks more than single-image augmentations because they fundamentally alter the relationship between input features and output labels, requiring deeper network adaptations.
- Mechanism: Single-image augmentations preserve the original label while transforming the image, maintaining the input-label relationship. Mixup and cutmix create new synthetic examples with interpolated labels, forcing the network to learn more complex decision boundaries and affecting deeper feature representations.
- Core assumption: The magnitude of CKA change correlates with how fundamentally the augmentation alters the input-label relationship.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "networks trained with augmentation that use information from two images affect the learned weights significantly more than augmentations that operate on a single image."
  - [section] "mixup and cutmix clearly affect the convolutional layers more than the other augmentation. From Table 1, the greatest average percent decreases in CKA similarity are 15.63 for cutmix on ResNet-56 and 16.91 for mixup on ResNet-18."

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: Model depth influences augmentation impact patterns because deeper networks have more layers that can specialize to dataset-specific features when trained from scratch.
- Mechanism: Shallow networks have fewer layers to distribute the learning burden, causing augmentations to affect both early and late layers. Deeper networks can compartmentalize feature learning, with early layers focusing on general features and deeper layers on specific ones, creating distinct impact patterns.
- Core assumption: Network depth provides architectural capacity for specialized layer roles during training.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "The number of layers in a convolutional neural network plays a significant role in how they are affected by augmentations."
  - [section] "The pattern does not hold consistently for the smaller residual networks trained on CIFAR-10. Augmentations such as AutoAugment, cutmix, and solarizing considerably affect the first half of the layers."

## Architecture Onboarding

- **Component map**:
  CKA computation module -> Model training pipeline -> Augmentation library -> Data loaders -> Experiment tracking

- **Critical path**:
  1. Initialize two ResNet models with same architecture but different random seeds (none1, none2)
  2. Train ten more models with different augmentations
  3. Compute CKA between corresponding layers of none1 and none2 (baseline)
  4. Compute CKA between each augmented model and both none models
  5. Calculate percent decrease in CKA as measure of augmentation impact

- **Design tradeoffs**:
  - Using CKA vs. other similarity metrics (CCA, distance correlation) trades computational efficiency for interpretability
  - Training from scratch vs. fine-tuning trades generalization capability against dataset specificity
  - Studying only ResNets vs. including Vision Transformers limits architectural generalizability

- **Failure signatures**:
  - No difference in CKA patterns between fine-tuned and scratch models suggests initialization doesn't matter
  - All augmentations showing similar CKA impacts suggests the study can't distinguish augmentation effects
  - CKA scores remaining constant across all layers suggests augmentations don't affect learned representations

- **First 3 experiments**:
  1. Train two ResNet-32 models on CIFAR-10 with no augmentations, compute baseline CKA matrix
  2. Train ResNet-32 with horizontal flips only, compute CKA against both baseline models, calculate impact
  3. Repeat with random crops, compare impact pattern to horizontal flips to identify which layers are most affected

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

- **Open Question 1**: How do the patterns of layer impact from augmentations differ across different model architectures beyond ResNets (e.g., Vision Transformers, EfficientNets)?
  - Basis in paper: [inferred] The paper notes this is a limitation and only studies CNNs with residual connections, suggesting this is an open area for investigation.
  - Why unresolved: The study is limited to ResNet architectures due to computational constraints, leaving the question of how these findings generalize to other architectures unanswered.
  - What evidence would resolve it: Comparative studies measuring CKA across layers of different architectures (ViTs, EfficientNets, etc.) trained with the same augmentations and datasets would provide direct evidence of architectural differences in layer impact patterns.

- **Open Question 2**: Is there a causal relationship between the magnitude of CKA change in a layer and the importance of that layer's features for task performance?
  - Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper discusses how some augmentations produce high accuracy with low CKA changes and vice versa, questioning the meaning of CKA differences.
  - Why unresolved: The paper shows that CKA changes don't consistently correlate with accuracy improvements, but doesn't establish whether layers with larger CKA changes are more critical for performance.
  - What evidence would resolve it: Ablation studies where specific layers are frozen during training with different augmentations, measuring the performance drop relative to the magnitude of CKA change, would establish causality.

- **Open Question 3**: How do augmentation effects on layer representations vary across different datasets and real-world data distributions?
  - Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper acknowledges using only CIFAR-10 and Tiny-Imagenet-200 and suggests more datasets and real-world data should be tested.
  - Why unresolved: The study's limited scope to two datasets means the findings may not generalize to other data distributions or real-world scenarios.
  - What evidence would resolve it: Replicating the CKA analysis across multiple diverse datasets (medical imaging, satellite imagery, real-world photography) would reveal whether augmentation patterns are dataset-dependent or more universal.

## Limitations
- The study only examines ResNet architectures, limiting generalizability to other network types like Vision Transformers
- CKA similarity may not capture all aspects of how augmentations affect learning dynamics
- The computational cost of CKA calculations restricted the study to comparing only two baseline models versus augmented variants

## Confidence

**High Confidence:**
- Early layers of fine-tuned ResNets show less CKA change than deeper layers
- Mixup and cutmix create greater CKA changes than single-image augmentations
- Model depth influences augmentation impact patterns

**Medium Confidence:**
- Greater CKA changes correlate with improved accuracy
- Fine-tuned models show different augmentation patterns than scratch-trained models
- All augmentations follow consistent layer-wise impact patterns across datasets

**Low Confidence:**
- CKA changes directly measure augmentation "effectiveness"
- Findings generalize to non-ResNet architectures
- Layer-wise CKA patterns predict optimal freezing strategies for transfer learning

## Next Checks
1. **Architectural Generalization Test**: Replicate the CKA analysis on Vision Transformer models trained with the same augmentations to determine if layer-wise impact patterns hold across architectures.

2. **Temporal CKA Analysis**: Track CKA similarity changes throughout training epochs rather than just at convergence to identify when augmentations exert their maximum influence on different layers.

3. **Correlation Validation**: Systematically measure the relationship between CKA change magnitude and downstream task performance across multiple datasets to verify whether higher CKA changes consistently predict better generalization.
