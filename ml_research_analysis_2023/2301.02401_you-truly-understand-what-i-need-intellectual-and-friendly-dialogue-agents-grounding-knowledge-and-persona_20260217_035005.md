# You Truly Understand What I Need: Intellectual and Friendly Dialogue Agents grounding Knowledge and Persona

## Executive Summary
This paper proposes INFO, a conversational model that grounds external knowledge and persona information to generate less hallucinated and more engaging responses. The model employs poly-encoder-based candidate scoring for both knowledge and persona grounding tasks, and uses a retrieval-augmented generator with a knowledge-persona enhanced query to produce informative and engaging dialogue. Experiments on the FoCus dataset show that INFO achieves state-of-the-art performance in both grounding and generation tasks, outperforming baselines like GPT-2 and BART in metrics such as BLEU, ROUGE, and BERTScore. Human evaluation confirms INFO's responses are less hallucinated and more engaging compared to baselines. Qualitative analysis further demonstrates INFO's effectiveness in grounding and generation.

## Method Summary
INFO is a conversational model that employs poly-encoder-based candidate scoring for both knowledge and persona grounding tasks. It uses a retrieval-augmented generator with a knowledge-persona enhanced query to produce informative and engaging dialogue. The model is trained in a multi-tasking manner with specific loss functions for grounding and generation. The knowledge selector and persona selector are based on poly-encoders, which capture richer interactions between dialogue context and candidate sources. The RAG-Token generator marginalizes over retrieved documents when predicting each token, ensuring factual consistency with the source.

## Key Results
- INFO achieves state-of-the-art performance in both grounding and generation tasks on the FoCus dataset
- INFO outperforms baselines like GPT-2 and BART in automatic metrics such as BLEU, ROUGE, and BERTScore
- Human evaluation confirms INFO's responses are less hallucinated and more engaging compared to baselines

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: Poly-encoder based candidate scoring improves grounding accuracy by capturing richer interactions between dialogue context and candidate sources than bi-encoder or cross-encoder.
- Mechanism: The poly-encoder computes multiple context codes (M=16) to attend over all tokens in the input, creating a global dialogue feature Udial that aggregates diverse aspects of the context. This feature is then used to score each candidate via dot product, selecting the highest-scoring knowledge and persona sentences.
- Core assumption: Multiple context codes enable the model to capture diverse semantic aspects of the dialogue context, leading to better matching with relevant knowledge/persona candidates.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [section] "We employ poly-encoder (Humeau et al., 2019) as knowledge selector and persona selector to exploit its enhanced capability of capturing relevance between candidate set and context"
  - [table 3] "Poly-encoder 53.29 31.46 58.26 42.35 53.06 92.29 80.87 39.56 99.22" (outperforms bi-encoder and cross-encoder on generation metrics)
- Break condition: If the number of context codes M is too small, the model loses its ability to capture diverse aspects, reverting to bi-encoder performance.

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: Retrieval-augmented generation with knowledge-persona enhanced query (KPEQ) reduces hallucination by directly accessing relevant external documents during generation.
- Mechanism: After selecting knowledge and persona candidates, they are combined with dialogue history into KPEQ, which is fed to a DPR retriever to fetch top-K relevant paragraphs. The RAG-Token generator then marginalizes over these documents when predicting each token, ensuring factual consistency with retrieved sources.
- Core assumption: The retriever can find documents relevant to the KPEQ that contain the ground truth knowledge, and the generator can effectively use this information to avoid contradicting the source.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "our model generates the utterance with lesser hallucination and more engagingness utilizing retrieval augmented generation with knowledge-persona enhanced query"
  - [table 5] "INFO 26.36 7.40 15.48 12.18 14.32 53.14" (outperforms TF-IDF and DPR on BERTScore, indicating better factual consistency)
- Break condition: If the retriever fails to find relevant documents, the generator has no external knowledge to ground its responses, leading to hallucination.

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: The persona level indicator enables active use of persona by dynamically selecting the number of persona sentences to ground, improving engagingness.
- Mechanism: A binary cross-entropy classifier predicts how many persona sentences (0, 1, or 2+) should be used based on the [CLS] token of the input. The top-N persona candidates are then selected according to this prediction, allowing the model to use persona more actively rather than passively confirming existing persona.
- Core assumption: The persona level indicator can accurately predict when persona should be used to make responses more engaging, and the poly-encoder can handle variable numbers of persona sentences.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [section] "Since multiple personas can be chosen to generate the responses, consideration of one or more persona sentences are needed"
  - [table 3] "INFO 53.29 31.46 58.26 42.35 53.06 92.29 80.87 39.56 99.22" (F1 score of 80.87 indicates active persona use, while bi-encoder and cross-encoder have lower F1 scores)
- Break condition: If the persona level indicator predicts incorrectly (e.g., always 0), the model will fail to use persona when it should, reducing engagingness.

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map:
  - Input construction: Dialogue history + knowledge snippet → U
  - Poly-encoder candidate scoring: U → grounding scores for knowledge and persona candidates
  - Persona level indicator: U → number of persona sentences to use
  - Knowledge-persona enhanced query (KPEQ): U + predicted knowledge + predicted persona → query for retriever
  - Dense Passage Retriever (DPR): KPEQ → top-K relevant paragraphs
  - RAG-Token generator: KPEQ + retrieved paragraphs → final response
  - Loss functions: Knowledge grounding loss (LKG), persona grounding loss (LPG), generation loss (LS)

- Critical path: Input construction → Poly-encoder scoring → KPEQ construction → DPR retrieval → RAG-Token generation
- Design tradeoffs:
  - Poly-encoder vs. bi-encoder: Better accuracy vs. faster inference
  - RAG-Token vs. RAG-Sequence: Better handling of multiple documents vs. simpler architecture
  - Number of context codes (M): More codes → better accuracy vs. higher computation cost
- Failure signatures:
  - Low grounding accuracy: Poly-encoder not capturing relevant features, or retriever not finding relevant documents
  - High hallucination: Retriever failing to find relevant documents, or generator not properly using retrieved information
  - Low engagingness: Persona level indicator predicting incorrectly, or model not actively using persona
- First 3 experiments:
  1. Ablation study: Remove poly-encoder and use bi-encoder, measure impact on grounding and generation metrics
  2. Retriever comparison: Replace DPR with TF-IDF, measure impact on BERTScore and hallucination
  3. Loss weighting: Vary λKG:λPG:λS ratio, measure impact on grounding accuracy vs. generation quality tradeoff

## Limitations

- The model relies heavily on the quality of the FoCus dataset, which may contain biases or limitations in its knowledge sources and persona annotations
- The computational cost of the poly-encoder architecture (16 context codes) may make it less suitable for real-time applications
- The reliance on external knowledge retrieval introduces additional failure points if the DPR retriever fails to find relevant documents

## Confidence

*High Confidence Claims:*
- The poly-encoder architecture improves grounding accuracy compared to simpler approaches (supported by Table 3 showing superior F1 scores)
- Retrieval-augmented generation reduces hallucination by accessing external knowledge sources (supported by Table 5 showing better BERTScore)
- The multi-task training approach with weighted losses effectively balances grounding and generation objectives (supported by the overall performance metrics)

*Medium Confidence Claims:*
- The persona level indicator successfully improves engagingness by dynamically selecting persona usage (indirectly supported by F1 scores, but not directly measured)
- The specific loss weighting ratio (1:1:5) is optimal for the task (based on ablation studies, but not extensively explored)
- The combination of knowledge and persona grounding is superior to approaches using only one type of grounding (compared to baselines, but no direct ablation study shown)

*Low Confidence Claims:*
- The model's performance on out-of-domain knowledge (not present in FoCus dataset)
- The model's ability to handle very long dialogues or multiple knowledge sources per turn
- The robustness of the persona level indicator across different types of persona information
