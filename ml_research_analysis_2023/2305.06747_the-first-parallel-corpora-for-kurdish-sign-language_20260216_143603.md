# The First Parallel Corpora for Kurdish Sign Language

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2305.06747
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.06747
- Reference count: 1
- Primary result: First parallel corpus between Kurdish and Kurdish Sign Language (KuSL) with 500 sentence pairs

## Executive Summary
This work presents the first parallel corpus between Kurdish and Kurdish Sign Language (KuSL), containing 500 sentence pairs derived from Kurdish language textbooks. The corpus includes sign glosses and HamNoSys representations for each sign, enabling the training of a Moses statistical machine translation engine. The system was evaluated using BLEU score (53.8%) and assessed for understandability by Deaf participants. The high BLEU score is attributed to structural similarities between Kurdish and KuSL, as well as the use of contextually relevant training data from educational materials.

## Method Summary
The researchers developed a parallel corpus by selecting 500 sentences from Kurdish language textbooks and recording corresponding sign glosses and HamNoSys representations. These sentence pairs were used to train a Moses statistical machine translation engine for translating between Kurdish and KuSL. The evaluation involved both automatic BLEU scoring and qualitative assessment by Deaf participants who rated the understandability of translated sentences. The corpus and related resources will be made publicly available under a CC BY-NC-SA 4.0 license on Kurdish-BLARK.

## Key Results
- First parallel corpus created for Kurdish Sign Language with 500 sentence pairs
- BLEU score of 53.8% achieved for translation accuracy
- Deaf participants confirmed strong understandability for trained sentences, with limitations for mixed and novel constructions

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The translation system works effectively because Kurdish and Kurdish Sign Language share structural similarities that facilitate statistical machine translation. The use of textbook-derived sentences ensures contextual relevance and consistency in the training data. HamNoSys representations provide a standardized notation system that captures essential phonological features of signs, enabling systematic processing by the translation engine.

## Foundational Learning

- **HamNoSys Representations**: Why needed - Standardize sign notation for computational processing. Quick check - Confirm all signs are represented using consistent HamNoSys conventions.

- **Statistical Machine Translation**: Why needed - Enable translation without explicit rule-based systems. Quick check - Test translation performance with varying amounts of training data.

## Architecture Onboarding

**Component Map**
Kurdish Sentences -> Sign Gloss Recording -> HamNoSys Annotation -> Moses MT Training -> Translation Output

**Critical Path**
Sentence selection and recording → Gloss annotation → HamNoSys encoding → Model training → Evaluation

**Design Tradeoffs**
The choice of statistical over neural machine translation prioritizes interpretability and resource efficiency given the small corpus size, though at the cost of potential accuracy limitations.

**Failure Signatures**
- BLEU score degradation with out-of-domain sentences
- Reduced understandability for mixed or novel constructions
- Limited vocabulary coverage due to small corpus size

**3 First Experiments**
1. Evaluate translation quality on sentences containing previously unseen vocabulary
2. Test model performance when mixing textbook and conversational sentence structures
3. Measure BLEU score changes when expanding corpus size incrementally

## Limitations
- Corpus contains only 500 sentence pairs, limiting vocabulary and structural coverage
- Evaluation relies on subjective understandability rather than standardized comprehension metrics
- High BLEU score may be inflated due to structural similarities rather than genuine translation quality
- Limited testing scope raises questions about real-world utility for untrained sentences

## Confidence
- **High Confidence**: Corpus development methodology and technical implementation are sound
- **Medium Confidence**: BLEU score and understandability results are internally consistent but lack external validation
- **Low Confidence**: Claims about practical utility for real-world communication are not sufficiently supported

## Next Checks
1. Conduct systematic comprehension testing with Deaf participants using novel sentences not present in training data
2. Expand corpus size to 2,000-3,000 sentence pairs and evaluate scalability of translation quality
3. Compare performance against baseline models trained on scrambled or randomly paired data to validate meaningful translation
