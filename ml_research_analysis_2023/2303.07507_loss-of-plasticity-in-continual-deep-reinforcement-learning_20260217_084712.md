# Loss of Plasticity in Continual Deep Reinforcement Learning

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2303.07507
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.07507
- Authors:
- Reference count: 11
- Primary result: CReLU activation functions mitigate loss of plasticity in Rainbow DQN when continually trained on sequences of Atari games

## Executive Summary
This paper demonstrates that Rainbow, a state-of-the-art deep reinforcement learning agent, suffers from loss of plasticity when continually trained on a sequence of Atari 2600 games. The agent's performance deteriorates over successive visits to the same game, and the analysis shows that the activation footprint of the network becomes sparser, contributing to diminishing gradients. A simple mitigation strategy, using concatenated ReLU (CReLU) activation functions, is proposed and shown to be effective in facilitating continual learning. The CReLU variant of Rainbow maintains or improves performance over successive visits, and the analysis of internal statistics provides evidence of improved plasticity. The results highlight the importance of addressing loss of plasticity in continual deep reinforcement learning.

## Method Summary
The study evaluates Rainbow DQN on the Sequential Arcade Learning Environment (S-ALE), where agents are trained on sequences of Atari games without resetting weights or replay buffers between visits. The baseline Rainbow uses standard ReLU activations, while the proposed variant replaces ReLU with CReLU in all layers except the first. Performance is measured through online training performance across multiple visits to the same game, with analysis of internal statistics including activation sparsity (ℓ0 norms), gradient norms, and weight changes.

## Key Results
- Rainbow suffers from loss of plasticity, showing performance degradation over successive visits to the same game
- Activation sparsity increases in value and advantage networks, contributing to diminishing gradients
- CReLU effectively mitigates loss of plasticity by maintaining higher activation norms and gradient flow
- CReLU improves or maintains performance across all tested games while standard Rainbow degrades

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: CReLU prevents activation collapse by ensuring at least one of two outputs is non-zero for each input signal, except when input is exactly zero.
- Mechanism: CReLU takes input x and concatenates [ReLU(x), ReLU(-x)], guaranteeing that for every non-zero input, at least one of the two outputs is positive. This maintains higher ℓ0 norms of activations across layers compared to standard ReLU.
- Core assumption: Standard ReLU activation causes diminishing activation patterns over successive game visits, leading to vanishing gradients and loss of plasticity.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract]: "Our analysis shows that the activation footprint of the network becomes sparser, contributing to the diminishing gradients."
  - [section 5]: "CReLU outputs twice the number of input signals and ensures that, for every input signal, one of the two output signal is nonzero, except when the input signal is exactly zero."
  - [corpus]: "Maintaining Plasticity in Deep Continual Learning" discusses maintaining activation diversity as key to preserving plasticity.
- Break condition: If input values are exactly zero (due to 32-bit floating point precision), both outputs can be zero, potentially breaking the guarantee.

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: CReLU maintains gradient flow by preserving non-zero activations in value and advantage networks, preventing gradient collapse.
- Mechanism: By keeping more units active (higher ℓ0 activation norms), CReLU ensures more gradients flow backward through the network during training, allowing weight updates to continue effectively.
- Core assumption: Gradient collapse is caused by sparse activations in ReLU networks, where inactive units contribute nothing to weight updates via backpropagation.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [section 4.3]: "If a ReLU is zero, then it does not contribute to the change in its incoming weights, hindering adaptation."
  - [section 5]: "Figure 8c shows that the relative norm of the gradients stays large (as we would expect if activations did not collapse)."
  - [corpus]: "Deep Reinforcement Learning with Plasticity Injection" discusses gradient flow as critical for maintaining learning capacity.
- Break condition: If the network architecture or training dynamics override the activation benefits, gradient collapse could still occur.

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: CReLU mitigates catastrophic interference by maintaining network plasticity, allowing better adaptation to new game distributions while preserving ability to learn.
- Mechanism: By preventing activation collapse and gradient vanishing, CReLU helps the network maintain its capacity to adapt to new task distributions without completely forgetting previous knowledge.
- Core assumption: Loss of plasticity and catastrophic interference are distinct but related phenomena that both need to be addressed for effective continual learning.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [section 6]: "CReLU does not address this issue and we see no reason to expect as such" (regarding catastrophic forgetting).
  - [section 5]: "Rainbow-CReLU—unlike Rainbow-ReLU—continues to maintain or improve its performance on successive visits in all five games."
  - [corpus]: "Mitigating Plasticity Loss in Continual Reinforcement Learning by Reducing Churn" discusses churn reduction as a way to maintain plasticity.
- Break condition: If the task similarity is too low or the distribution shift is too large, CReLU alone may not prevent interference between tasks.

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map:
  Input: Stacked frames (4 most recent Atari frames)
  -> Convolution network: Shared layers processing visual input
  -> Value network: Estimates expected return from state
  -> Advantage network: Estimates relative advantage of actions
  -> Output: Q-values for each possible action
  Key change: Replace ReLU with CReLU in all layers except the first

- Critical path:
  1. Observe state (stacked frames)
  2. Process through convolution network with CReLU activations
  3. Split to value and advantage streams
  4. Compute Q-values
  5. Select action using epsilon-greedy policy
  6. Store transition in replay buffer
  7. Sample minibatch and compute gradients
  8. Update weights using backpropagation

- Design tradeoffs:
  - CReLU doubles activations but keeps parameters constant in most layers
  - May increase computational cost due to more activations
  - Could potentially increase overfitting risk due to larger effective network capacity
  - Simple to implement but may not address catastrophic forgetting

- Failure signatures:
  - Performance degradation over successive game visits
  - Decreasing ℓ0 norms of activations in value and advantage networks
  - Vanishing gradient norms (ℓ0 and ℓ1)
  - Weight changes becoming negligible relative to first visit
  - Training loss increasing while performance plateaus or declines

- First 3 experiments:
  1. Compare Rainbow-ReLU vs Rainbow-CReLU on a single game (no continual learning) to verify CReLU doesn't inherently improve performance
  2. Test Rainbow-CReLU with invariant output dimension (fewer parameters) to isolate activation benefits from capacity changes
  3. Run ablation study with CReLU only in specific layers (e.g., just value/advantage networks) to identify which layers most benefit from the activation change

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- Question: How do different architectural modifications beyond CReLU (e.g., random re-initialization, utility-based pruning) compare in mitigating loss of plasticity in continual deep RL?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The authors mention these as promising directions for future work, noting that CReLU is not the only solution.
- Why unresolved: The paper only evaluates CReLU as a mitigation strategy. While effective, it's unclear if other architectural modifications could be more effective or address different aspects of the problem.
- What evidence would resolve it: Systematic comparison of various architectural modifications (random re-initialization, utility-based pruning, etc.) against CReLU and a baseline (Rainbow with ReLU) in the S-ALE setup, measuring performance across multiple visits and analyzing activation/gradient statistics.

### Open Question 2
- Question: What is the relationship between the degree of similarity between tasks (games/modes) and the severity of loss of plasticity?
- Basis in paper: [inferred] The authors observe loss of plasticity in Breakout (varying game modes with different dynamics) but not in Freeway or Space Invaders. This suggests a potential link between task similarity and plasticity loss.
- Why unresolved: The paper doesn't provide a quantitative measure of task similarity or systematically vary it to establish a clear relationship with plasticity loss.
- What evidence would resolve it: Controlled experiments varying task similarity (e.g., using different subsets of games/modes with known similarities) and measuring the rate of activation collapse and performance degradation across visits.

### Open Question 3
- Question: Can continual deep RL agents be designed to both mitigate loss of plasticity and address catastrophic forgetting simultaneously?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The authors conclude that CReLU mitigates loss of plasticity but doesn't address catastrophic forgetting, which remains a challenge for continual learning.
- Why unresolved: The paper focuses on loss of plasticity as the primary phenomenon and only briefly mentions catastrophic forgetting as a separate challenge that needs to be addressed.
- What evidence would resolve it: Development and evaluation of a continual deep RL agent that shows both maintained plasticity (as measured by CReLU) and retained knowledge across multiple visits to the same task (as measured by performance recovery without relearning from scratch).

## Limitations

- CReLU does not address catastrophic forgetting, which remains a separate challenge for continual learning
- The analysis focuses on activation sparsity and gradient flow but doesn't definitively establish causation with performance degradation
- Limited evaluation to Atari 2600 games, which may not generalize to other domains or more diverse task sequences

## Confidence

- Medium-High: The quantitative metrics for activation sparsity and gradient flow provide strong evidence for CReLU's effectiveness
- Medium: While correlation between activation collapse and performance is clear, causation is not definitively established
- Medium: The paper clearly articulates its limitation regarding catastrophic forgetting, but the relationship between plasticity loss and interference could be explored further
