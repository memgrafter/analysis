# CFA: Class-wise Calibrated Fair Adversarial Training

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2303.14460
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.14460
- Reference count: 40
- This paper addresses the problem of class-wise fairness in adversarial training of deep neural networks.

## Executive Summary
This paper addresses the problem of class-wise fairness in adversarial training of deep neural networks. While adversarial training improves overall robustness, it often leads to disparate robustness across different classes. The authors propose a Class-wise Calibrated Fair Adversarial (CFA) training framework to customize training configurations for each class automatically. The core idea is to dynamically adjust the perturbation margin, regularization, and weight averaging based on the difficulty of each class, measured by training accuracy. Theoretical analysis and empirical experiments on CIFAR-10 and Tiny-ImageNet datasets demonstrate that CFA significantly improves both overall robustness and fairness compared to state-of-the-art methods.

## Method Summary
CFA introduces three key components to address class-wise fairness in adversarial training. First, it calibrates perturbation margins per class based on training accuracy, allocating stronger attacks to easier classes and weaker attacks to harder classes. Second, it adjusts regularization weights per class, reducing the emphasis on robustness for harder classes to maintain clean accuracy. Third, it implements fairness-aware weight averaging that only includes checkpoints where worst-class robustness exceeds a threshold, stabilizing the final model's fairness. The method is evaluated on CIFAR-10 and Tiny-ImageNet with PreActResNet-18, showing significant improvements in both overall robust accuracy and worst-class robust accuracy.

## Key Results
- CFA achieves 50.1% average robust accuracy on CIFAR-10 with PreActResNet-18, outperforming baselines by 2-4%
- Worst-class robust accuracy improves to 26.5% compared to 23.0% with standard AT and 25.2% with TRADES
- The method demonstrates both theoretical guarantees and empirical improvements in class-wise fairness across multiple datasets

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: Class-wise calibrated perturbation margins improve robustness by allocating stronger attacks to easier classes and weaker attacks to harder classes
- Mechanism: The method uses training accuracy as a proxy for class difficulty. Classes with higher training accuracy (easier classes) receive larger perturbation margins, while classes with lower training accuracy (harder classes) receive smaller margins. This prevents over-regularization of already difficult classes while still providing adequate adversarial training to easier classes.
- Core assumption: Training accuracy is a reliable proxy for class difficulty and correlates with optimal perturbation margin selection
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "The core idea is to dynamically adjust the perturbation margin, regularization, and weight averaging based on the difficulty of each class, measured by training accuracy"
  - [section 3.1] "we propose to leverage the class-wise training accuracy as the measurement of difficulty"
  - [corpus] Weak - no direct corpus evidence supporting this specific mechanism
- Break condition: If training accuracy becomes a poor proxy for difficulty (e.g., due to class imbalance or early stopping effects)

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: Class-wise calibrated regularization improves fairness by reducing the regularization weight for harder classes
- Mechanism: The method adjusts the TRADES regularization parameter β based on class difficulty. Harder classes (with lower training accuracy) receive smaller β values, reducing the emphasis on robustness at the expense of clean accuracy. This allows harder classes to maintain better clean accuracy while still gaining some robustness.
- Core assumption: The trade-off between clean accuracy and robustness can be managed independently per class
- Evidence anchors:
  - [section 3.2] "for hard classes (e.g., classes 2, 3, 4), improving β can only obtain limited robustness improvement but drop clean accuracy significantly"
  - [section 4.2] "For the hard classes which have lower βy tend to bias higher weight 1/(1+βy) on its natural loss L(θ;x,y)"
  - [corpus] Weak - no direct corpus evidence supporting this specific mechanism
- Break condition: If the clean accuracy/robustness trade-off is class-specific rather than following a universal pattern

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: Fairness-aware weight averaging stabilizes the worst-class robustness during training
- Mechanism: The method modifies exponential moving average (EMA) to only include checkpoints where the worst-class robustness exceeds a threshold. This eliminates unstable or unfair checkpoints from the final model, preventing degradation of the worst-class performance.
- Core assumption: The worst-class robustness fluctuates significantly during training and can be stabilized by selective checkpoint averaging
- Evidence anchors:
  - [section 3.3] "the worst class robustness fluctuates significantly" and "simply selecting the checkpoint with the best overall robustness like the previous method [19] may result in poor robust fairness"
  - [section 4.3] "we consider eliminating the unfair checkpoints out in the weight averaging process"
  - [corpus] Weak - no direct corpus evidence supporting this specific mechanism
- Break condition: If the worst-class robustness is inherently unstable and cannot be effectively smoothed by checkpoint selection

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map: CFA framework consists of CCM (Class-wise Calibrated Margin) -> CCR (Class-wise Calibrated Regularization) -> FAWA (Fairness Aware Weight Averaging)
- Critical path: During each training iteration, the model processes a batch, computes class-wise training accuracy, updates per-class perturbation margins and regularization weights based on this accuracy, and optionally updates the weighted average model if the worst-class robustness exceeds the threshold.
- Design tradeoffs: The framework trades off computational complexity (maintaining per-class parameters) for improved fairness, and requires careful tuning of base perturbation budget and regularization budget parameters to achieve optimal performance.
- Failure signatures: Poor performance may manifest as either (1) failure to improve worst-class robustness despite overall accuracy gains, or (2) significant degradation in clean accuracy or overall robustness due to overly aggressive per-class customization.
- First 3 experiments:
  1. Implement CCM only with AT baseline to verify that class-wise perturbation margins improve worst-class robustness
  2. Add CCR to TRADES baseline to verify that class-wise regularization improves both overall and worst-class robustness
  3. Add FAWA to verify that selective checkpoint averaging stabilizes worst-class performance without degrading overall accuracy

## Limitations
- The method's performance on more complex datasets or with different model architectures remains untested
- The reliance on training accuracy as a proxy for class difficulty may not hold for all scenarios, particularly in cases of severe class imbalance
- The computational overhead of maintaining per-class parameters may limit scalability to large datasets with many classes

## Confidence

- Mechanism 1 (Class-wise perturbation margins): Medium - Theoretical justification exists but empirical validation across diverse scenarios is limited
- Mechanism 2 (Class-wise regularization): Medium - The trade-off analysis is sound but may not generalize to all architectures
- Mechanism 3 (Fairness-aware weight averaging): Medium - The stability improvement is demonstrated but the threshold selection criteria need further validation
