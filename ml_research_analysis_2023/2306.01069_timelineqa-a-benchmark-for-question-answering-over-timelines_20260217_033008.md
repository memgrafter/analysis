# TimelineQA: A Benchmark for Question Answering over Timelines

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2306.01069
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.01069
- Reference count: 17
- Primary result: Extractive QA systems outperform retrieval-augmented generative QA for atomic queries over lifelog data

## Executive Summary
This paper introduces TimelineQA, a benchmark designed to evaluate question answering over lifelogs - private databases containing a person's experiences inferred from digital data like photos, maps, and health apps. The authors generate lifelogs for imaginary people with different personas and include questions over these lifelogs. Through extensive experiments, they demonstrate that extractive QA systems significantly outperform retrieval-augmented generative QA systems for atomic queries, while table-based QA techniques achieve the best results for complex multi-hop queries involving aggregates when the ground truth episode set is available.

## Method Summary
The authors created the TimelineQA benchmark by generating lifelog episodes for imaginary people with diverse personas, covering 25 categories from major life events to daily activities. They evaluated three types of QA systems: extractive QA (span prediction), retrieval-augmented generative QA (RAG), and table-based QA (Tapex). Models were tested using zero-shot, fine-tuned, and oracle retrievers to assess performance across different retrieval scenarios. The evaluation focused on both atomic queries (where answers are spans within episodes) and multi-hop queries involving aggregates like counting or averaging.

## Key Results
- Extractive QA systems significantly outperform RAG systems for atomic queries on lifelog data
- Fine-tuning dense retrievers on lifelog episodes substantially improves QA accuracy
- Table-based QA (Tapex) achieves state-of-the-art performance for multi-hop queries with aggregates when ground truth episodes are available

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: Extractive QA systems outperform retrieval-augmented generative QA systems for atomic queries over lifelog data
- Mechanism: Lifelog episodes use templated, structured text where answers are always spans within single episodes, allowing extractive models to directly locate and return these spans without generative noise
- Core assumption: Answers to atomic queries are always contiguous spans within episode text requiring no complex reasoning
- Evidence anchors: Abstract states extractive QA outperforms RAG for atomic queries; section 5.2 confirms this is expected given benchmark construction
- Break condition: If answers require synthesis across multiple pieces of information or are non-contiguous, extractive methods would fail

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: Multi-hop queries with aggregates require table-based QA when ground truth episode set is available
- Mechanism: Aggregations and relational reasoning map naturally to SQL-like operations that table QA models can execute over structured episode records
- Core assumption: Episode sets can be identified and structured into relational tables with clear schemas
- Evidence anchors: Abstract shows Tapex achieves best results for multi-hop queries with aggregates; section 5.3 describes Tapex's SQL-like reasoning
- Break condition: If episode sets are incomplete, ambiguous, or schema mapping fails, table QA cannot perform correctly

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: Dense retrievers fine-tuned on lifelog episodes significantly improve QA accuracy
- Mechanism: Episodes contain domain-specific language not present in general pretraining; fine-tuning aligns retriever embeddings with episodic vocabulary and semantics
- Core assumption: Lifelog episodes follow consistent patterns and fine-tuning data is representative of test distribution
- Evidence anchors: Section 5.2 shows significant boost in performance for both extractive and RAG setups after fine-tuning
- Break condition: If fine-tuning data is too small or unrepresentative, zero-shot retrieval could be equally effective

## Foundational Learning

- Concept: Dense passage retrieval (DPR)
  - Why needed here: Lifelog episodes are too numerous for memory; efficient similarity search over embeddings is required for retrieval-augmented and table-based QA
  - Quick check question: How does DPR encode episodes and queries into the same vector space for similarity search?

- Concept: Span-based extractive QA
  - Why needed here: Atomic lifelog queries always have answers that are spans within single episodes; extractive models can directly predict these spans without generation
  - Quick check question: What is the difference between extractive and generative QA models in terms of output space?

- Concept: Table QA with SQL-like reasoning
  - Why needed here: Multi-hop queries with aggregates map naturally to SQL operations; table QA models can learn to execute these operations over structured episode records
 - Quick check question: How does a table QA model convert a natural language question into a structured operation over table rows?

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map: Lifelog generator -> Dense retriever (zero-shot/fine-tuned/oracle) -> QA reader (extractive/RAG/table) -> Evaluation pipeline
- Critical path: 1) Generate lifelog episodes 2) Index episodes with dense retriever 3) Retrieve top-k episodes for query 4) Feed to QA reader 5) Output answer and compute accuracy
- Design tradeoffs: Templated vs. natural language episodes (templated ensures ground truth but limits diversity); retriever type (zero-shot general but weak, fine-tuned strong but requires data, oracle perfect but unrealistic); QA model (extractive accurate for spans but cannot generate, RAG can generate but noisier, table QA best for aggregates but needs structured input)
- Failure signatures: Low accuracy with extractive QA (answers not spans or require multi-hop reasoning); low accuracy with RAG (retrieval fails or reader cannot generate correct spans); low accuracy with table QA (evidence set incomplete, schema mapping fails, or question requires non-aggregate reasoning)
- First 3 experiments: 1) Evaluate extractive QA vs. RAG on atomic queries with zero-shot retriever 2) Fine-tune dense retriever on lifelog episodes and re-evaluate both QA models 3) Evaluate table QA model on multi-hop queries with oracle retriever

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- Question: How can QA systems effectively handle the linguistic variation between user queries and the language used in lifelog descriptions?
- Basis in paper: Inferred
- Why unresolved: The paper acknowledges users may use different terms or refer to more aggregate concepts than what's explicitly mentioned in lifelog (e.g., asking about "utilities" when lifelog contains individual utility bills)
- What evidence would resolve it: Developing and evaluating techniques that bridge the gap between query language and lifelog descriptions, such as semantic matching or paraphrasing

### Open Question 2
- Question: How can QA systems effectively handle multi-modal reasoning where answers require analyzing both text and visual content in lifelogs?
- Basis in paper: Inferred
- Why unresolved: The paper mentions some queries may require multi-modal reasoning (e.g., identifying a bar from a photo) but the current benchmark is limited to text-based queries
- What evidence would resolve it: Creating a benchmark with diverse multimedia content and developing QA systems that can effectively reason over both text and images

### Open Question 3
- Question: How can QA systems effectively handle temporal reasoning such as answering questions about event durations, sequences, or time between episodes?
- Basis in paper: Inferred
- Why unresolved: The paper highlights that many lifelog queries are temporal in nature and require sophisticated reasoning about time, which current QA algorithms struggle with
- What evidence would resolve it: Developing and evaluating QA systems that can effectively reason about temporal relationships, durations, and sequences in lifelogs

## Limitations

- The benchmark uses synthetic lifelog data rather than real-world lifelogs, which may not capture the full complexity and diversity of actual lifelog data
- Evaluation is limited to specific atomic and multi-hop queries, leaving unclear how models would perform on more diverse and open-ended questions
- The paper does not explore potential biases and ethical considerations that may arise when working with lifelog data, such as privacy concerns

## Confidence

- High confidence: Extractive QA outperforms RAG for atomic queries (well-supported by experimental results and benchmark construction)
- Medium confidence: Table-based QA is best for multi-hop queries with aggregates (supported by experiments but assumes ground truth episode set availability)
- Low confidence: Fine-tuning dense retrievers significantly improves QA accuracy (based on specific experimental setup, may not generalize)
