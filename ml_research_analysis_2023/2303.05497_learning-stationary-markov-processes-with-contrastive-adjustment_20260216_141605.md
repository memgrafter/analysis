# Learning Stationary Markov Processes with Contrastive Adjustment

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2303.05497
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.05497
- Reference count: 40
- Primary result: Introduces contrastive adjustment algorithm for learning Markov transition kernels with desired stationary distributions, showing competitive FID scores on image synthesis tasks

## Executive Summary
This paper presents a novel approach to learning Markov transition kernels whose stationary distributions match target data distributions. The method, called contrastive adjustment, iteratively adjusts transition probabilities based on Monte Carlo samples to enforce detailed balance. A specific transition model called the noise kernel is proposed, parameterized as a denoising neural network, which can trade mixing speed for sample fidelity by modeling data across multiple noise levels. Experiments demonstrate that this approach produces diverse, high-quality samples and achieves competitive performance on CIFAR-10, LSUN Church, and CelebA-HQ datasets.

## Method Summary
The contrastive adjustment algorithm learns Markov transition kernels by iteratively refining transition probabilities to ensure the stationary distribution matches the data distribution. The core idea involves sampling from the current kernel, measuring the discrepancy between the empirical distribution of samples and the target data distribution, and adjusting transition probabilities accordingly. The noise kernel model represents data at multiple noise levels and is parameterized as a denoising neural network. During training, the kernel learns to denoise samples at various noise scales, enabling the model to capture multi-scale structure in the data. The learned Markov chain supports local exploration of the data manifold, making it suitable for applications like variant generation and inpainting.

## Key Results
- Achieves competitive Fréchet Inception Distances compared to state-of-the-art generative models on CIFAR-10, LSUN Church, and CelebA-HQ datasets
- Noise kernels trained with contrastive adjustment produce diverse, high-quality samples
- The learned Markov chain enables local exploration of the data manifold, suitable for human-in-the-loop design processes

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The method works by enforcing detailed balance through iterative adjustment of transition probabilities. By sampling from the current Markov chain and comparing the resulting distribution to the target data distribution, the algorithm identifies regions where transitions need to be strengthened or weakened. The noise kernel parameterization allows the model to capture multi-scale structure by learning denoising operations at various noise levels. This multi-scale representation enables the model to generate samples that are both diverse and faithful to the target distribution.

## Architecture Onboarding

**Component Map**
Noise Kernel (Denoising NN) -> Contrastive Adjustment Algorithm -> Markov Chain Sampler -> Sample Evaluation -> Transition Probability Update

**Critical Path**
The denoising neural network generates transition probabilities, which are refined by the contrastive adjustment algorithm to ensure detailed balance, producing samples that approximate the target data distribution.

**Design Tradeoffs**
The noise kernel must balance expressiveness with computational efficiency. Higher-dimensional noise representations can capture more complex data structures but increase computational cost and training difficulty. The contrastive adjustment algorithm trades iteration count for accuracy in enforcing detailed balance.

**Failure Signatures**
- Poor sample quality may indicate inadequate noise level coverage
- Slow convergence suggests the transition probabilities are not being adjusted sufficiently
- Mode collapse could indicate the contrastive adjustment is overemphasizing certain regions
