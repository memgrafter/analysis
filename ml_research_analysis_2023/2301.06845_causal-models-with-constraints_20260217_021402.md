# Causal Models with Constraints

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2301.06845
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2301.06845
- Reference count: 15
- The paper introduces causal models with constraints, which extend standard causal models to allow non-causal relationships between variables, such as mathematical equations.

## Executive Summary
This paper presents an extension to causal models that incorporates non-causal constraints, allowing for mathematical relationships between variables such as LDL + HDL = TOT. The authors introduce a new disconnection operation that enables interventions on related variables while preserving logical consistency. This framework captures situations where variables have part-whole or unit conversion relationships that cannot be represented in standard causal models. The paper provides a sound and complete axiomatization for the extended language and demonstrates its usefulness through examples involving cholesterol levels, unit conversions, and ambiguous interventions.

## Method Summary
The authors extend standard causal models by incorporating non-causal constraints through a disjunction operator that allows for multiple causal explanations of observed data. The key innovation is the disconnection operation, which removes causal equations for selected variables while preserving non-causal constraints. This enables interventions on related variables while maintaining logical consistency. The framework uses structural equations to represent both causal and non-causal relationships, with the non-causal constraints taking precedence during interventions. The authors provide a formal axiomatization that is both sound and complete for the extended language.

## Key Results
- Introduction of causal models with constraints that extend standard causal models to handle non-causal relationships
- Definition of a new disconnection operation that enables interventions on related variables while preserving logical consistency
- Sound and complete axiomatization for the extended language
- Demonstration of the framework's usefulness through examples involving cholesterol levels, unit conversions, and ambiguous interventions

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The framework works by explicitly modeling both causal and non-causal relationships between variables through structural equations. The disconnection operation allows for selective removal of causal dependencies while maintaining non-causal constraints, ensuring logical consistency during interventions. This dual representation enables modeling of complex relationships like part-whole or unit conversion relationships that standard causal models cannot capture. The sound and complete axiomatization guarantees that reasoning within the framework is both valid and comprehensive.

## Foundational Learning

1. **Structural Equations**: Mathematical representations of variable relationships
   - Why needed: To formalize both causal and non-causal dependencies between variables
   - Quick check: Can represent both LDL + HDL = TOT and LDL = f(HDL, U) relationships

2. **Disconnection Operation**: A mechanism to remove causal equations while preserving constraints
   - Why needed: To enable interventions on related variables without violating logical consistency
   - Quick check: After disconnection, can intervene on HDL while maintaining LDL + HDL = TOT

3. **Non-causal Constraints**: Mathematical or logical relationships that aren't causal
   - Why needed: To model part-whole, unit conversion, or definitional relationships
   - Quick check: TOT must always equal LDL + HDL regardless of interventions

4. **Sound and Complete Axiomatization**: Formal proof that the reasoning system is valid and comprehensive
   - Why needed: To ensure the framework's logical correctness and completeness
   - Quick check: All valid inferences can be derived and no invalid inferences can be proven

## Architecture Onboarding

Component Map: Variables -> Structural Equations -> Disconnection Operation -> Axiomatization

Critical Path: The framework's core functionality flows from defining variables and their relationships through structural equations, applying the disconnection operation for interventions, and validating reasoning through the axiomatization.

Design Tradeoffs: The framework prioritizes logical consistency and comprehensive modeling over computational efficiency. By maintaining both causal and non-causal relationships explicitly, it ensures correctness but may require more computational resources than standard causal models.

Failure Signatures: The framework may fail when:
- Non-causal constraints are inconsistent with each other
- Disconnection operations violate the underlying mathematical relationships
- Interventions create impossible scenarios that violate non-causal constraints
