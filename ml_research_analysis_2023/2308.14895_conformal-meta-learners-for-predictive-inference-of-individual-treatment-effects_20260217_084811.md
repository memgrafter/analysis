# Conformal Meta-learners for Predictive Inference of Individual Treatment Effects

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID**: 2308.14895
- **Source URL**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2308.14895
- **Reference count**: 40
- **Primary result**: Conformal meta-learners provide valid predictive intervals for ITEs by leveraging stochastic dominance of pseudo-outcome conformity scores over oracle scores.

## Executive Summary
This paper develops conformal meta-learners for predictive inference of individual treatment effects (ITEs) in causal inference. The key innovation is applying conformal prediction on top of CATE meta-learners to obtain valid predictive intervals for ITEs. The authors focus on a class of meta-learners based on two-stage pseudo-outcome regression, and develop a stochastic ordering framework to study their validity. They prove that commonly used CATE meta-learners, such as the doubly-robust learner, satisfy a model- and distribution-free stochastic dominance condition, making their conformal inferences valid for relevant levels of target coverage. Numerical experiments show that conformal meta-learners provide valid intervals with competitive efficiency while retaining the favorable point estimation properties of CATE meta-learners.

## Method Summary
The method applies standard conformal prediction to CATE meta-learners that use two-stage pseudo-outcome regression. First, nuisance parameters (propensity score, potential outcomes) are estimated on a training split. Second, pseudo-outcomes are constructed and used to train a CATE model. Third, conformal prediction is applied on held-out pseudo-outcomes to generate prediction intervals. The key theoretical insight is that if the pseudo-outcome conformity scores stochastically dominate oracle scores evaluated on unobserved ITEs, the resulting intervals achieve valid coverage. The authors prove that IPW- and DR-learners satisfy this stochastic dominance condition, while X-learner may under-cover.

## Key Results
- Conformal meta-learners provide marginally valid inference when pseudo-outcome conformity scores stochastically dominate oracle scores.
- IPW- and DR-learners guarantee stronger FOSD than required, enabling coverage for all levels of target coverage.
- Numerical experiments show conformal meta-learners provide valid intervals with competitive efficiency while retaining favorable point estimation properties.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- **Claim**: Conformal meta-learners provide valid predictive intervals for ITEs by leveraging stochastic dominance of pseudo-outcome conformity scores over oracle scores.
- **Mechanism**: The meta-learner constructs pseudo-outcomes that depend only on observed variables, then applies standard conformal prediction on these pseudo-outcomes. If the pseudo-outcome conformity scores Vφ stochastically dominate the oracle scores V*, the resulting intervals achieve valid coverage.
- **Core assumption**: The pseudo-outcome transformation preserves a stochastic ordering such that Vφ ⪰(1) V*, Vφ ⪯(2) V*, or Vφ ⪰mcx V*.
- **Evidence anchors**:
  - [abstract] "We show that inference with conformal meta-learners is marginally valid if their (pseudo outcome) conformity scores stochastically dominate oracle conformity scores evaluated on the unobserved ITEs."
  - [section 4] "We show that inference with conformal meta-learners is valid if their conformity scores satisfy certain stochastic ordering conditions with respect to 'oracle' conformity scores evaluated on unobserved ITEs."
  - [corpus] Weak evidence - corpus neighbors discuss related conformal causal inference methods but do not directly confirm the stochastic dominance claim.
- **Break condition**: The stochastic dominance condition fails, e.g., when using X-learner with absolute residual scores, or when propensity score is misspecified/unknown.

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim**: The two-stage pseudo-outcome regression framework enables model-agnostic, distribution-free valid inference by decoupling nuisance estimation from conformal calibration.
- **Mechanism**: Stage 1 estimates nuisance parameters (propensity score, potential outcomes). Stage 2 constructs pseudo-outcomes and trains a CATE model. Stage 3 applies conformal prediction on held-out pseudo-outcomes, which share the same covariate distribution as test data, thus addressing covariate shift.
- **Core assumption**: The propensity score is known, and the pseudo-outcome transformation yields valid proxy targets for ITEs.
- **Evidence anchors**:
  - [section 3.1] "Conformal meta-learners address (1) because the distribution of covariates associated with pseudo-outcomes is the same for training and testing data, and they address (2) since the calibration step is decoupled from model architecture."
  - [section 3.2] "Given the dataset D = {(Xi, Wi, Yi)}i, we create three mutually-exclusive subsets: Dφ, Dt and Dc."
- **Break condition**: If the propensity score is unknown or misspecified, the model- and distribution-free guarantee is lost.

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim**: IPW- and DR-learners guarantee stronger FOSD than required, enabling coverage for all levels of target coverage.
- **Mechanism**: Both IPW and DR pseudo-outcomes are constructed so that E[eYφ | X = x] = τ(x), which ensures that their conformity scores dominate oracle scores in the first-order stochastic dominance sense.
- **Core assumption**: The pseudo-outcome transformation is unbiased for CATE and the conformity score function is absolute residual.
- **Evidence anchors**:
  - [section 4] "We prove that commonly used CATE meta-learners, such as the doubly-robust learner, satisfy a model- and distribution-free stochastic (or convex) dominance condition, making their conformal inferences valid for practically-relevant levels of target coverage."
  - [section 4, Theorem 2] "For any distribution P(X, W, Y(0), Y(1)), CATE estimate bτ, and nuisance estimate bφ, the IPW- and the DR-learners satisfy Vφ ⪰mcx V*."
- **Break condition**: If the conformity score is not absolute residual (e.g., signed distance), the stochastic dominance may not hold, and coverage is not guaranteed.

## Architecture Onboarding

- **Component map**:
  - Nuisance estimator (π, µ0, µ1) -> Pseudo-outcome generator f(Z, bφ) -> CATE regressor bτ(X) -> Conformity scorer V(X, eYφ; bτ) -> Quantile calculator QVφ(1−α) -> Interval constructor [bτ(x) ± QVφ(1−α)]

- **Critical path**:
  1. Split data into Dφ, Dt, Dc
  2. Estimate nuisance parameters on Dφ
  3. Generate pseudo-outcomes and train bτ on Dt
  4. Compute conformity scores on Dc
  5. Calculate quantile and form intervals

- **Design tradeoffs**:
  - Known vs. estimated propensity score: Known yields stronger guarantees but limits applicability.
  - Choice of pseudo-outcome: IPW/DR provide unbiased CATE targets; X-learner does not.
  - Conformity score: Absolute residual enables FOSD; signed distance may not.

- **Failure signatures**:
  - Undercoverage → Stochastic dominance not satisfied (e.g., X-learner with absolute residual)
  - Overly wide intervals → Strong dominance causing conservative intervals
  - Poor point estimation → Suboptimal CATE model or pseudo-outcome generation

- **First 3 experiments**:
  1. **Synthetic test (Setup A - no effect)**: Verify that all learners achieve target coverage; X-learner should under-cover.
  2. **Synthetic test (Setup B - heterogeneous effects)**: Compare efficiency and RMSE across learners; DR-learner expected to balance accuracy and coverage.
  3. **Semi-synthetic (IHDP/NLSM)**: Assess real-world performance; evaluate how CDF gap between Vφ and V* affects interval width and coverage.

## Limitations

- **Known propensity score assumption**: The paper assumes known propensity scores, which limits applicability to real-world scenarios where propensity scores must be estimated.
- **Efficiency degradation with strong dominance**: When CDF of conformity scores has very strong dominance over oracle CDF, intervals become overly conservative and less efficient.
- **Limited comparison to alternatives**: The paper does not compare conformal meta-learners to Bayesian methods or other approaches for predictive inference of ITEs.

## Confidence

- **High confidence**: The mechanism that conformal meta-learners provide valid intervals when pseudo-outcome conformity scores stochastically dominate oracle scores. This is directly proven in Section 4 with Theorems 1 and 2.
- **Medium confidence**: The claim that IPW- and DR-learners guarantee stronger FOSD than required, enabling coverage for all levels of target coverage. While the paper provides theoretical justification, the empirical evidence in semi-synthetic datasets shows variable performance.
- **Low confidence**: The assumption that known propensity scores are available in practice. This is a significant limitation for real-world applications, and the impact of estimated propensity scores on validity is not addressed.

## Next Checks

1. **Test unknown propensity scores**: Implement a version of the conformal meta-learner where the propensity score is estimated (e.g., using logistic regression) rather than known, and evaluate how this affects coverage and interval width across the three meta-learners.

2. **Vary conformity score functions**: Replace the absolute residual conformity score with signed distance and alternative functions, then measure changes in coverage, efficiency, and stochastic dominance relationships between Vφ and V*.

3. **Stress test stochastic dominance**: Generate synthetic data with controlled CDF gaps between Vφ and V*, then systematically evaluate how varying degrees of dominance affect interval coverage, width, and point estimation accuracy.
