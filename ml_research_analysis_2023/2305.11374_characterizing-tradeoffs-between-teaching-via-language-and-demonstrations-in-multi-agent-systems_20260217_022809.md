# Characterizing tradeoffs between teaching via language and demonstrations in multi-agent systems

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2305.11374
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.11374
- Reference count: 3
- Primary result: Language teaching enables better generalization to unseen scenarios than demonstrations, particularly for complex tasks

## Executive Summary
This paper investigates the effectiveness of teaching via language versus demonstrations using multi-agent systems in a grounded communication task. The authors train neural network agents to teach students to select optimal objects based on messages or demonstrations, varying task difficulty and teacher competence. They find that demonstration is more effective for simpler tasks, while language becomes more effective as task difficulty increases due to its ability to generalize to unseen scenarios. When teacher competence is limited, language maintains some effectiveness while demonstrations fail to enable generalization.

## Method Summary
The paper uses a teacher-student framework where neural networks learn to communicate in a signaling bandits task. Teachers receive world states (combinations of objects with color and shape features) and must communicate information to help students select the highest utility object. Two teaching modalities are compared: language (discrete token sequences) and demonstration (showing examples). Both teacher and student use MLPs for state encoding and GRUs for sequence processing, trained jointly via gradient descent with Gumbel-Softmax for differentiable sampling. Task difficulty is varied by the number of possible feature values (n=3 to 6), and teacher competence is manipulated by varying training data percentage (5% to 80% of world states).

## Key Results
- Demonstration teaching is more effective than language for simple tasks (n=3 feature values)
- Language teaching becomes more effective as task difficulty increases (n=4 to 6)
- Language maintains effectiveness with limited teacher competence while demonstrations fail to generalize
- Language conveys higher-level abstractions about world states, such as relative utilities of features

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: Demonstration is more effective than language for simpler tasks because the teacher can directly show the correct action without needing to generalize to unseen scenarios.
- Mechanism: In simple tasks, the mapping between actions and rewards is straightforward, so the teacher can directly demonstrate the correct action. The student can learn this mapping without needing to generalize beyond the demonstrated examples.
- Core assumption: The task has a simple, deterministic mapping between actions and rewards that can be captured in a few demonstrations.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract]: "teaching by demonstration is more effective in the simplest settings"
  - [section]: "When the task is relatively easy, demonstration agents outperform language agents"
  - [corpus]: Weak evidence - the corpus neighbors focus on robot demonstration rather than this theoretical distinction
- Break condition: As task complexity increases, the number of possible demonstrations becomes combinatorially large, making it difficult for demonstration agents to cover all scenarios.

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: Language becomes more effective as task difficulty increases because it allows for generalization to unseen scenarios through abstract communication.
- Mechanism: In complex tasks, language allows the teacher to communicate abstract relationships between features and rewards, which the student can use to generalize to new scenarios not seen during training.
- Core assumption: Language can convey abstract relationships between features and rewards that are not tied to specific examples.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract]: "language is more effective as task difficulty increases, due to its ability to generalize more effectively to unseen scenarios"
  - [section]: "The language agent no longer exhibits overfitting: train and validation performance is similar"
  - [corpus]: Weak evidence - corpus doesn't directly address the generalization capability of language vs. demonstrations
- Break condition: If the language system cannot effectively learn to represent abstract relationships, it may fail to generalize even in complex tasks.

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: Language is more robust to teacher competence limitations because it can convey generalizations even with limited data.
- Mechanism: Even with limited training data, language can convey general principles about the task structure, while demonstration requires seeing many specific examples to capture the full space of possibilities.
- Core assumption: The teacher can learn to extract and communicate general principles from limited data, even if they cannot demonstrate every possible scenario.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract]: "language maintains some effectiveness while demonstrations fail to enable generalization"
  - [section]: "language maintains the ability to convey generalized knowledge about a world state even when agents have limited access to novel data"
  - [corpus]: Weak evidence - corpus doesn't directly address the robustness of language vs. demonstrations with limited data
- Break condition: If the teacher cannot learn to extract general principles from limited data, language may also fail to be robust.

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map: Teacher (Tgame + Tutt) -> Message/Demonstration -> Student (Sgame) -> Action -> Reward
- Critical path: Teacher generates message/demonstration → Student receives and decodes → Student selects action → Reward is received by both
- Design tradeoffs:
  - Language agents have a fixed vocabulary but can combine tokens in many ways
  - Demonstration agents have a potentially infinite vocabulary (all possible demonstrations) but are limited by the number they can show
  - Both use Gumbel-Softmax for differentiable sampling during training
- Failure signatures:
  - Language agents: Overfitting to training data (train performance much higher than validation)
  - Demonstration agents: Poor performance when task complexity increases or data is limited
  - Both: Random chance performance indicates failure to learn any meaningful communication protocol
- First 3 experiments:
  1. Vary channel capacity for both modalities and measure performance
  2. Vary task difficulty (number of possible values per feature) and measure which modality performs better
  3. Vary amount of training data for teacher and measure robustness of each modality

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- Question: Under what specific conditions does demonstration outperform language for teaching complex tasks, and how does this threshold vary with the number of features or possible feature values in the world state?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper shows that demonstration outperforms language for the simplest setting (n=3), but language becomes more effective as task difficulty increases. The authors note that as the number of possible demonstrations becomes combinatorially large as the task scales up, whereas the quantity of abstractions that might be conveyed increases at a slower rate.
- Why unresolved: The paper only tests up to n=6 feature values and doesn't systematically explore the exact threshold where language overtakes demonstration performance. The authors speculate about combinatorial explosion but don't provide a precise mathematical model of this relationship.
- What evidence would resolve it: Systematic experiments varying n from 3 to 10+ while tracking the exact point where language performance surpasses demonstration performance, combined with a formal analysis of the relationship between feature space size and abstraction capacity.

### Open Question 2
- Question: How does the effectiveness of language vs demonstration teaching change when the student has different prior knowledge or learning capacities, and what is the interaction between teacher competence and student competence?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper varies teacher competence by limiting training data but doesn't vary student capacity. They mention that language is more robust to teachers with low competence, with demonstration agents failing to learn in limited-data settings.
- Why unresolved: The experiments only vary the amount of training data for teachers, not student learning capacity. The paper doesn't explore how different student architectures or prior knowledge might interact with the language/demonstration tradeoff.
- What evidence would resolve it: Experiments with varying student architectures (e.g., different model sizes, architectures with different inductive biases) and/or students with different amounts of prior knowledge, to see how the effectiveness of language vs demonstration teaching changes.

### Open Question 3
- Question: What specific types of abstractions can language convey that demonstrations cannot, and how does this relate to the structure of the world state and reward function?
- Basis in paper: [inferred] The analysis shows that language conveys relative utilities of features rather than exact quantities, and the authors suggest this is a form of abstraction. However, they don't systematically characterize what other types of abstractions might be possible.
- Why unresolved: The paper only analyzes one aspect of the abstractions conveyed by language (relative feature utilities) and doesn't explore the full space of possible abstractions or how they relate to the structure of the task.
- What evidence would resolve it: Detailed analysis of the messages produced by language agents across many different task structures, combined with experiments that systematically vary the structure of the world state and reward function to see which types of abstractions are most useful in different contexts.

## Limitations

- The experimental task uses a simplified signaling bandits framework that may not capture the full complexity of real-world teaching scenarios
- The discrete token space for language represents a constrained communication channel that differs from open-ended human language
- The analysis of what language conveys is based on qualitative interpretation rather than explicit interpretability methods

## Confidence

- **High Confidence:** The core empirical finding that demonstration outperforms language for simple tasks while language becomes more effective for complex tasks is well-supported by the experimental results
- **Medium Confidence:** The claim that language is more robust to teacher competence limitations is supported but could benefit from additional experiments with more diverse competence constraints
- **Medium Confidence:** The interpretation that language conveys higher-level abstractions about feature utilities is reasonable given the evidence but relies on indirect analysis

## Next Checks

1. **Generalization to New Feature Types:** Test whether the observed tradeoff pattern holds when extending the task to include additional feature types beyond color and shape, or when using continuous rather than discrete features

2. **Teacher Error Analysis:** Systematically vary the amount and type of noise in teacher demonstrations or language to better understand the robustness boundaries of each modality under different competence limitations

3. **Interpretable Representation Analysis:** Apply activation maximization or other interpretability techniques to the teacher's utterance representations to directly visualize what abstract features are being encoded and how they relate to the underlying world state utilities
