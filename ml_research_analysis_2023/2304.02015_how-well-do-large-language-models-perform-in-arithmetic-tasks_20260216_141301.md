# How well do Large Language Models perform in Arithmetic tasks?

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2304.02015
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2304.02015
- Reference count: 12
- Models tested on MATH 401 dataset show GPT-4 and ChatGPT outperform others significantly in arithmetic tasks

## Executive Summary
This paper introduces MATH 401, a novel dataset of 401 arithmetic expressions designed to evaluate the arithmetic abilities of large language models (LLMs). The dataset tests various operations including basic arithmetic, exponentiation, trigonometry, and logarithms with integers, decimals, and irrational numbers. The authors systematically evaluate leading LLMs including GPT-4, ChatGPT, InstructGPT, Galactica, and LLaMA, revealing substantial performance gaps between models. Beyond benchmarking, the paper analyzes multiple factors affecting arithmetic performance including tokenization strategies, instruction fine-tuning, chain-of-thought prompting, and scaling laws, providing insights into the fundamental arithmetic capabilities of current LLMs.

## Method Summary
The authors created MATH 401, a dataset containing 401 arithmetic expressions with diverse operators and number types. They tested multiple state-of-the-art LLMs including GPT-4, ChatGPT, InstructGPT, Galactica, and LLaMA on this dataset. The evaluation used three metrics: accuracy (exact match), relative error, and non-number ratio (responses that don't contain numbers). The authors conducted systematic experiments analyzing how factors like tokenization strategy, instruction fine-tuning, prompt engineering, and model scale affect arithmetic performance. They also examined interpolation versus extrapolation capabilities by testing numbers within and beyond typical pre-training ranges.

## Key Results
- GPT-4 and ChatGPT outperform other models significantly across all arithmetic operations and number types
- Models with instruction fine-tuning and RLHF show marked improvement in arithmetic accuracy compared to base models
- Tokenization strategy (whole numbers vs. digit-split) substantially impacts performance, with whole-number preservation favoring better results
- Chain-of-thought prompting with "Calculate:" automatically generates step-by-step reasoning for complex expressions, though standard COT prompts don't improve performance

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1: Tokenization as a fundamental arithmetic signal
- Splitting numbers into individual digits (Galactica/LLaMA) versus preserving whole numbers (ChatGPT/GPT-4) alters the frequency distribution of tokens in pre-training, impacting the model's ability to learn number relationships.
- Core assumption: The pre-training corpus contains arithmetic expressions and their token-level frequency influences downstream arithmetic ability.
- Evidence anchors:
  - Number tokens with more digits (e.g. 23) apparently appear less than single digit token (e.g. 2 and 3).
  - Splitting numbers into individual tokens neglects all number tokens with more digits and makes all single digit tokens (mainly 0∼9) appear in the pre-training corpus in the same order of magnitude.

### Mechanism 2: Instruction fine-tuning and reinforcement learning as arithmetic enablers
- Models with instruction fine-tuning and RLHF (like ChatGPT and GPT-4) perform significantly better at arithmetic than models without these stages.
- Core assumption: Instruction tuning datasets contain labeled arithmetic examples and RLHF feedback is arithmetic-specific.
- Evidence anchors:
  - Comparing Opt-30B with Opt-Iml-Max-30B... Bloom with BloomZ... code-davinci-002 with text-davinci-002... show that instruction tuning can boost the performance in all metrics.
  - Text-davinci-003 (RLHF) outperforms text-davinci-002 (SFT) in arithmetic tasks which shows RLHF is important for building arithmetic ability.

### Mechanism 3: Chain-of-thought generation as implicit arithmetic reasoning
- Models can implicitly generate step-by-step reasoning when prompted with "Calculate:", improving performance on complex arithmetic expressions.
- Core assumption: The model has learned latent decomposition patterns during pre-training that can be activated by specific prompts.
- Evidence anchors:
  - Surprisingly, we find that COT does not improve the performance of any group even in group 14 with long arithmetic expressions.
  - Using 'Calculate:' as the prompt can automatically generate chain-of-thoughts for long arithmetic expressions and generate answers directly for easy questions.

## Foundational Learning

- Concept: Arithmetic expression tokenization
  - Why needed here: Understanding how numbers are tokenized determines whether models see "123" as one token or three separate digits, affecting arithmetic learning.
  - Quick check question: If a model tokenizes "123.45" as "1 2 3 . 4 5", what happens to the frequency of the token "23" versus "2" and "3"?

- Concept: Instruction fine-tuning mechanics
  - Why needed here: Instruction tuning transforms general language models into task-specific calculators by providing labeled arithmetic examples.
  - Quick check question: How does the presence of "Calculate:" in the prompt alter the model's internal processing path compared to no prompt?

- Concept: Reinforcement learning from human feedback (RLHF)
  - Why needed here: RLHF refines the model's arithmetic outputs by rewarding correct answers and penalizing errors based on human preferences.
  - Quick check question: What distinguishes RLHF from standard supervised fine-tuning in terms of how it shapes arithmetic behavior?

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map: Tokenizer → Pre-training → Instruction Tuning → RLHF → Inference Prompting
- Critical path: Tokenizer → Pre-training (arithmetic corpus frequency) → Instruction Tuning (arithmetic examples) → RLHF (arithmetic reward shaping) → Prompt selection (activation of arithmetic reasoning)
- Design tradeoffs: Digit-level tokenization improves frequency balance but loses whole-number semantic meaning; instruction tuning improves accuracy but requires labeled data; RLHF improves behavior but needs human feedback.
- Failure signatures: Low accuracy on large numbers → tokenization issue; correct on easy queries but wrong on hard → extrapolation problem; wrong on decimal operations → instruction data gap.
- First 3 experiments:
  1. Compare tokenization strategies (whole vs digit-split) on a fixed arithmetic dataset to isolate tokenization effects.
  2. Test instruction-tuned vs non-instruction-tuned versions on the same arithmetic tasks to measure fine-tuning impact.
  3. Vary prompt formats ("Calculate:", "\begin{equation}", no prompt) to determine optimal activation method for arithmetic reasoning.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- Question: What is the minimum model size required to achieve reliable arithmetic abilities across all operations tested in MATH 401?
- Basis in paper: The paper notes that "30B may be enough for arithmetic ability" and observes that models over 30B do not improve significantly compared to 30B models, except for ChatGPT which performs exceptionally well despite potentially being smaller than 175B.
- Why unresolved: The paper does not systematically test smaller model sizes or conduct a detailed scaling analysis to determine the precise threshold where arithmetic abilities emerge and stabilize.
- What evidence would resolve it: Systematic evaluation of models at various sizes (e.g., 1B, 3B, 7B, 13B, 30B) on MATH 401 to identify the point where performance plateaus across all arithmetic operations.

### Open Question 2
- Question: How does the tokenization strategy (character-level vs. word-level vs. subword) affect arithmetic performance, particularly for large numbers and decimals?
- Basis in paper: The paper observes that "Galactica and LLaMA split numbers into individual tokens" and discusses how this affects pre-training term frequencies, but does not conduct a controlled experiment comparing different tokenization strategies.
- Why unresolved: The paper only analyzes existing models with different tokenization schemes but does not implement a controlled study where the same model architecture is trained with different tokenization approaches.
- What evidence would resolve it: Training multiple versions of the same model architecture with different tokenization strategies (character, word, subword) and evaluating them on MATH 401 to isolate the effect of tokenization on arithmetic performance.

### Open Question 3
- Question: Does chain-of-thought reasoning improve arithmetic performance when properly implemented, and what are the optimal prompts for this task?
- Basis in paper: The paper tests zero-shot COT with the prompt "Let us solve this equation step by step" and finds it does not improve performance, but notes that the "Calculate:" prompt automatically generates chain-of-thought for long expressions.
- Why unresolved: The paper only tests one COT prompt and does not explore alternative prompting strategies or fine-tuning approaches that might better elicit step-by-step reasoning for arithmetic tasks.
- What evidence would resolve it: Systematic testing of various COT prompts (both zero-shot and few-shot), different prompt formats, and potentially fine-tuning models specifically on arithmetic with explicit reasoning steps to determine optimal approaches.

## Limitations

- Tokenization impact analysis is correlational rather than causal, lacking controlled experiments to definitively prove tokenization is the primary driver of performance differences
- RLHF specificity is unclear - improvements may come from general response quality refinement rather than arithmetic-specific feedback
- Interpolation/extrapolation distinction lacks granular analysis across difficulty levels and number magnitudes

## Confidence

**High Confidence**: The comparative ranking of models (GPT-4 > ChatGPT > other models) across arithmetic tasks is well-supported by empirical results. The observation that instruction-tuned models generally outperform base models is also strongly evidenced.

**Medium Confidence**: The mechanisms proposed for why tokenization affects performance (frequency distribution changes) and why instruction tuning helps (explicit arithmetic training) are plausible but not definitively proven. The evidence supports these as contributing factors but doesn't establish them as primary drivers.

**Low Confidence**: The claim that "Calculate:" prompts automatically trigger chain-of-thought reasoning is weakly supported. The paper acknowledges COT doesn't improve performance, yet simultaneously claims this prompt generates implicit reasoning chains - these claims appear contradictory without further explanation.

## Next Checks

1. **Tokenization Ablation Study**: Create controlled experiments where the same model architecture is tested with different tokenization strategies (whole numbers vs digit-split) on identical arithmetic datasets. Measure performance differences while controlling for pre-training data and instruction tuning to isolate tokenization effects.

2. **RLHF Arithmetic Specificity Test**: Compare arithmetic performance between models with general RLHF versus models with RLHF specifically focused on mathematical correctness. This would determine whether arithmetic improvements come from general response quality refinement or arithmetic-specific feedback.

3. **Number Range Performance Analysis**: Conduct detailed analysis of model performance across specific number ranges (single-digit, double-digit, triple-digit, etc.) and operations. This would clarify whether performance degradation occurs gradually with number size or exhibits threshold effects, providing insight into whether issues stem from tokenization, extrapolation limitations, or computational capacity.
