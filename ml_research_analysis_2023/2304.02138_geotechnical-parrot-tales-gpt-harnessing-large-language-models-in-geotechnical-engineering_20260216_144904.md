# Geotechnical Parrot Tales (GPT): Harnessing Large Language Models in geotechnical engineering

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2304.02138
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2304.02138
- Reference count: 1
- Key outcome: The paper proposes prompt engineering techniques to improve the reliability of GPT models in geotechnical engineering by mitigating hallucinations and enabling accurate soil classification, database queries, and complex analyses.

## Executive Summary
This paper addresses the critical challenge of hallucinations in large language models (LLMs) like GPT when applied to geotechnical engineering tasks. The authors propose a comprehensive set of prompt engineering techniques to enhance model performance and reliability. Key methods include developing context-specific search engines, creating unified interfaces for complex engineering tasks, and employing chain-of-thought prompting for reasoning. The primary results demonstrate that with proper prompt engineering, GPT can accurately classify soils, generate correct API requests for database queries, and perform complex geotechnical analyses while ensuring deterministic answers and preventing hallucinations.

## Method Summary
The authors propose a multi-faceted approach to improve GPT reliability in geotechnical engineering. They develop context-specific search engines by converting textual knowledge (such as DIGGS schema) into context vectors using OpenAI's text embedding model. A unified interface is created using natural language to handle complex geotechnical tasks, with the GroundMotion Database schema provided as context. The GPT model is restricted to specific actions (question, reason, code) through an Actor framework. Chain-of-thought prompting is employed to enable GPT to classify fine-grained soils based on liquid and plastic limits by providing examples of input-output pairs. These techniques collectively aim to mitigate hallucinations and improve the accuracy and reliability of GPT in geotechnical applications.

## Key Results
- GPT can accurately classify soils using chain-of-thought prompting with input-output examples
- The model generates correct API requests for database queries when provided with schema context
- Context-specific search engines ensure deterministic answers and prevent hallucinations in complex geotechnical analyses

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The paper's approach works by providing GPT with structured context and constraints that guide its responses. By converting domain-specific knowledge into vector embeddings, the model can access relevant information without hallucinating. The unified interface with restricted actions prevents the model from wandering into unsupported operations. Chain-of-thought prompting breaks down complex reasoning into manageable steps, mimicking how human experts approach geotechnical problems. The temperature setting is carefully controlled to balance between natural language generation and scientific accuracy.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- Question: How can we ensure consistent and reliable feedback from humans in the loop when training LLMs for specialized technical domains?
- Basis in paper: The paper mentions that incorporating humans in the loop can introduce new biases and they might lack the necessary expertise for specific tasks, resulting in less accurate feedback.
- Why unresolved: The paper highlights the issue but does not provide a solution or methodology for ensuring consistent and reliable human feedback.
- What evidence would resolve it: Research studies demonstrating effective methods for training experts to provide consistent feedback, or experiments showing improved LLM performance with a reliable human feedback system.

### Open Question 2
- Question: What are the long-term effects of using chain-of-thought prompting on the reasoning abilities of LLMs in geotechnical engineering applications?
- Basis in paper: The paper discusses the use of chain-of-thought prompting to elicit reasoning in LLMs and its potential to facilitate knowledge discovery.
- Why unresolved: The paper presents the concept and shows initial results but does not explore the long-term impact of this approach on LLM reasoning abilities.
- What evidence would resolve it: Longitudinal studies tracking the performance of LLMs using chain-of-thought prompting over extended periods, comparing it with other prompting techniques.

### Open Question 3
- Question: How can we effectively balance the trade-off between the temperature setting and the accuracy of GPT responses in geotechnical engineering applications?
- Basis in paper: The paper discusses the temperature setting in GPT and its effect on the naturalness and inventiveness of the generated text, but also mentions that a temperature of zero is required for consistent results in scientific applications.
- Why unresolved: The paper presents the trade-off but does not provide a clear methodology for balancing these competing requirements in practical applications.
- What evidence would resolve it: Experiments demonstrating optimal temperature settings for different types of geotechnical engineering tasks, considering both accuracy and naturalness of responses.
