# An Comparative Analysis of Different Pitch and Metrical Grid Encoding Methods in the Task of Sequential Music Generation

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2301.13383
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2301.13383
- Reference count: 40
- Primary result: Class-octave encoding significantly improves pitch-related metrics compared to MIDI encoding in sequential music generation

## Executive Summary
This study investigates how different encoding methods for pitch and metrical information affect the performance of token-based sequential music generation models. The authors compare MIDI number encoding against pitch class-octave decomposition and analyze the effects of grid resolution and complexity on rhythmic quality. Using a small Transformer-XL model on a melody generation task, they find that explicit pitch class-octave encoding significantly improves pitch-related metrics, while finer metrical grids and multiple-token encoding enhance rhythm but increase overfitting risk. The results highlight critical trade-offs between encoding complexity and model generalization, demonstrating that domain-informed feature design can substantially improve performance even with limited model capacity.

## Method Summary
The study uses a 4-layer Transformer-XL model with 32 embedding dimensions, 4 attention heads, and approximately 0.5M parameters to generate melodies from the Wikifonia dataset. The model is trained on sequences encoded using four hyper-parameters: pitch encoding (MIDI vs. class-octave), position complexity (single vs. multiple tokens), position resolution (0, 1, 4, 16, 32, 48, 64), and duration resolution (4, 8, 12, 16). The training procedure involves AdamW optimization with learning rate 2e-4 for approximately 25,000 steps, with random transposition augmentation applied to prevent overfitting. Generated melodies are evaluated using objective metrics including MAI, H(P), H(PC), SC, MSD for pitch-related features, and MD, H(D), GC, EBR for rhythmic features, with distributional similarity measured via overlapped area between generated and true distributions.

## Key Results
- Class-octave encoding significantly outperforms MIDI encoding on all pitch-related metrics
- Higher positional resolution (PR) and duration resolution (DR) improve rhythmic quality but cause early overfitting
- Single-token positional complexity combined with high PR and DR achieves the best approximations of ground truth distributions
- Smaller networks with lower embedding dimensions are more susceptible to overfitting, particularly with single-token positional encoding

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: Class-octave encoding significantly improves pitch-related metrics by explicitly preserving pitch relationships
- Mechanism: Decomposing MIDI pitch numbers into pitch class and octave tokens allows the model to learn shared pitch class patterns across octaves, which are fundamental in music theory
- Core assumption: The model can leverage explicit pitch class-octave structure to improve distributional learning of pitch-related features
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract]: "class-octave encoding significantly outperforms the taken-for-granted MIDI encoding on pitch-related metrics"
  - [section 4.1]: "the class-octave encoding breaks down the pitch feature into two tokens, as the name suggested"

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: Higher positional resolution (PR) and duration resolution (DR) improve rhythm modeling by providing more granular temporal information
- Mechanism: Dense grid encoding captures finer rhythmic details, allowing the model to learn more precise temporal relationships between notes and beats
- Core assumption: The model can effectively utilize additional positional information without overfitting, and the dataset contains sufficient rhythmic variation at these granularities
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract]: "finer grids and multiple-token grids improve the rhythmic quality, but also suffer from over-fitting at early training stage"
  - [section 5.2.2]: "higher PR and DR combined with single-token PC resulted in the best approximations"
  - [corpus]: Weak - related papers focus on poetry generation rather than musical rhythm encoding

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: Single-token positional complexity (PC) combined with high PR and DR creates a trade-off between rhythm quality and training stability
- Mechanism: Single-token encoding (using repetitive position tokens) is more susceptible to exposure bias and overfitting, but can capture rhythm better when combined with high resolution
- Core assumption: The auto-regressive nature of the model and teacher forcing create exposure bias that affects single-token encodings more severely than multiple-token encodings
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract]: "single token approach and the multiple token approach are compared"
  - [section 7.1]: "smaller PR and smaller DR resulted in more consistent grooves. Single tokens should not be learnt too fast"

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map: Data encoding -> Model training -> Sampling -> Metric calculation -> Distribution comparison
- Critical path: Data encoding → Model training → Sampling → Metric calculation → Distribution comparison
- Design tradeoffs:
  - Embedding dimension (32) vs. model capacity: Low dimension risks overfitting but saves parameters
  - Positional resolution (PR) vs. sequence length: Higher PR increases sequence length and computational cost
  - Single-token vs. multiple-token PC: Single-token is more prone to exposure bias but can capture rhythm better with high PR/DR
- Failure signatures:
  - Overfitting: Test loss increases while training loss decreases
  - Exposure bias: Single-token models perform well on training data but poorly on generated samples
  - Poor rhythm quality: Low OA for rhythmic metrics (MD, H(D), GC, EBR)

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- Question: How does the choice between MIDI number encoding and class-octave encoding impact the model's ability to generalize across different musical genres beyond the monophonic melody task studied?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper compares MIDI number encoding against pitch class-octave encoding in a monophonic melody generation task and finds that class-octave encoding significantly outperforms MIDI encoding on pitch-related metrics
- Why unresolved: The study is limited to a specific dataset (Wikifonia) and task (monophonic melody generation). The influence of encoding on genre-specific musical features or polyphonic music remains unexplored
- What evidence would resolve it: Comparative experiments across diverse musical genres and tasks, such as polyphonic music or genre-specific datasets, using both encoding methods to evaluate generalization

### Open Question 2
- Question: What are the long-term effects of using smaller networks and lower embedding dimensions on the model's ability to avoid overfitting in music generation tasks?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper highlights overfitting concerns when using smaller networks (0.5M parameters) and lower embedding dimensions (32) on the melody generation task, especially in low-dimensional embedding spaces
- Why unresolved: The study focuses on short-term training and specific metrics. The long-term impact of these architectural choices on overfitting and model robustness is not fully explored
- What evidence would resolve it: Extended training experiments and analysis of model performance over time, along with ablation studies varying network sizes and embedding dimensions, to identify thresholds for overfitting

### Open Question 3
- Question: How does the interaction between position complexity (PC) and position resolution (PR) influence the model's ability to generate musically coherent rhythmic structures in polyphonic settings?
- Basis in paper: [inferred] The paper discusses the interaction between PC and PR in monophonic melody generation, showing that higher PR and DR combined with single-token PC improve rhythmic quality but lead to overfitting
- Why unresolved: The study is limited to monophonic music and does not address the complexities of polyphonic rhythmic structures, where multiple voices interact
- What evidence would resolve it: Experiments on polyphonic music generation tasks, varying PC and PR, to evaluate their impact on rhythmic coherence and interaction between multiple voices

## Limitations

- Dataset specificity: The study exclusively uses the Wikifonia dataset with 4/4 time signature melodies, limiting generalizability to other musical styles and time signatures
- Model architecture constraints: The experiments use a deliberately small Transformer-XL model (0.5M parameters) to isolate encoding effects, raising questions about scalability to larger models
- Evaluation methodology: The study relies on objective metrics comparing distributional similarities rather than subjective musical quality assessments, potentially missing perceptual aspects of musicality

## Confidence

**High Confidence**: The pitch class-octave encoding advantage is well-supported by clear distributional differences in pitch-related metrics (MAI, H(P), H(PC), SC, MSD). The mechanism—explicit decomposition of pitch relationships—is theoretically sound and empirically validated across multiple experiments.

**Medium Confidence**: The overfitting observations for single-token positional encoding at high resolution are consistent but may be partially attributed to the small model size. The exposure bias mechanism is plausible but not directly measured or isolated from other training dynamics.

**Low Confidence**: The claim that multiple-token encoding "smoothly approaches the ground truth distribution" is based on limited comparisons and doesn't account for potential trade-offs in generation diversity or computational efficiency. The study doesn't explore intermediate resolution settings that might balance quality and generalization.

## Next Checks

1. **Cross-dataset validation**: Replicate the pitch encoding experiments on a different melody dataset (e.g., Bach chorales or Irish folk tunes) to test whether class-octave encoding advantages generalize beyond Wikifonia's pop-style melodies and 4/4 time signature constraint.

2. **Model scaling experiment**: Repeat key experiments with a larger Transformer model (e.g., 2-4M parameters) to determine whether encoding effects persist when the model has greater capacity to learn complex pitch and rhythm relationships from raw MIDI encodings.

3. **Hybrid encoding test**: Implement a mixed approach that uses class-octave encoding for pitch but multiple-token positional encoding at moderate resolution (PR=4, DR=8) to test whether combining the strengths of both encoding strategies can mitigate overfitting while maintaining rhythmic quality.
