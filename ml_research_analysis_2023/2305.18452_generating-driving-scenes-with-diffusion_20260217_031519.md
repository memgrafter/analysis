# Generating Driving Scenes with Diffusion

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2305.18452
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.18452
- Reference count: 32
- Primary result: Diffusion-based method generates realistic driving scenes with regional adaptability

## Executive Summary
This paper introduces a novel approach for generating driving scenes using a diffusion model combined with a variational autoencoder (VAE). The method learns a latent representation of driving scenes through the VAE, which outputs oriented bounding boxes for agents, and then trains a diffusion model to generate new samples from this latent space conditioned on map data. The system is evaluated across four US regions, demonstrating both visual realism and the ability to adapt to regional characteristics. Notably, the model trained on the full dataset performs comparably to region-specific models, indicating strong generalization capabilities.

## Method Summary
The proposed approach combines a VAE and diffusion model for driving scene generation. The VAE learns to encode driving scenes into a latent space while outputting oriented bounding boxes for agents as reconstruction targets. The diffusion model is then trained to denoise these latent embeddings, conditioned on map data inputs. This architecture allows the system to generate new driving scenes by first sampling from the diffusion model in latent space and then decoding through the VAE. The method is trained and evaluated on real-world driving data from four different US regions, with the diffusion model learning to capture both common patterns and regional variations in driving behavior and scene composition.

## Key Results
- Model generates realistic and diverse driving scenes across four US regions
- Full-dataset model performs close to region-specific models, showing strong generalization
- System successfully captures unique regional characteristics in generated scenes

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The approach leverages the VAE's ability to learn compressed latent representations of complex driving scenes, with oriented bounding boxes providing structured geometric information about agents. The diffusion model then learns to denoise and generate new samples in this structured latent space, conditioned on map data that provides contextual information. This two-stage process allows the model to capture both the spatial relationships between agents and the environmental context from maps, resulting in realistic scene generation that respects both agent dynamics and scene geometry.

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component Map**: Raw Scene Data -> VAE Encoder -> Latent Embeddings -> Diffusion Model -> Denoised Latents -> VAE Decoder -> Generated Scene
**Critical Path**: The core pipeline flows from VAE encoding of ground truth scenes to latent representation, through the diffusion model for denoising/generation, and back through VAE decoding to produce final scenes.
**Design Tradeoffs**: Using bounding boxes instead of pixel reconstruction sacrifices fine-grained visual detail for computational efficiency and structured representation; latent space generation trades direct pixel control for more coherent scene composition.
**Failure Signatures**: Unrealistic agent placements suggesting diffusion model struggles with complex multi-agent interactions; missing regional characteristics indicating insufficient conditioning; geometric inconsistencies suggesting VAE reconstruction issues.
**First Experiments**: 1) Generate single-agent scenes to validate basic pipeline functionality, 2) Test map conditioning by generating scenes with varied map inputs, 3) Compare generated scenes against held-out real data using standard metrics.
