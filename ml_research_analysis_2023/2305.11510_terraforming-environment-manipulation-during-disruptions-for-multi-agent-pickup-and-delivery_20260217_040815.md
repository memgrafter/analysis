# Terraforming -- Environment Manipulation during Disruptions for Multi-Agent Pickup and Delivery

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2305.11510
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.11510
- **Reference count:** 9
- **Primary result:** Terraforming RHCR (tRHCR) improves throughput by over 10% and reduces maximum service time by more than 50% compared to standard RHCR in simulated MAPD with disruptions.

## Executive Summary
This work introduces "terraforming MAPD" (tMAPD), a framework for multi-agent pickup and delivery in warehouse settings where agents can dynamically relocate inventory pods to open new pathways during disruptions. The core contribution is tRHCR, which extends the RHCR algorithm to judiciously invoke terraforming only when disruptions (e.g., dropped items, agent malfunctions) are detected. The method uses a simplified tMAPD subproblem to decide which obstacles to move and where, focusing terraforming efforts only when beneficial. Empirically, tRHCR significantly improves throughput and service time in simulation without drastically increasing runtime.

## Method Summary
The method builds on RHCR (Reshuffling High-level Conflict Resolution) by adding a disruption-triggered terraforming mechanism. When a disruption is detected, tRHCR invokes a simplified tMAPD subproblem to decide which nearby obstacles to move and where to place them, creating new pathways for agents. This decision is made only when disruptions are observed, ensuring computational cost is kept in check. The approach leverages the fact that agents in MAPD can manipulate obstacles, enabling dynamic reconfiguration of the environment to mitigate the impact of disruptions.

## Key Results
- tRHCR improves throughput by over 10% compared to standard RHCR.
- Maximum service time is reduced by more than 50% using tRHCR.
- Performance gains are achieved without drastically increasing runtime.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The method works by extending RHCR with a disruption-triggered mechanism that allows agents to dynamically reconfigure the environment. When a disruption occurs, tRHCR solves a simplified tMAPD subproblem to identify which obstacles can be moved to create shortcuts. This targeted terraforming reduces congestion and allows agents to complete tasks more efficiently, especially when disruptions block critical paths.

- **Design tradeoffs:** Computational cost vs. performance gain; disruption detection accuracy vs. false triggers; obstacle relocation complexity vs. path improvement.
- **Failure signatures:** False-positive disruption detection leading to unnecessary terraforming; tMAPD subproblem selecting suboptimal obstacles; increased runtime during peak load.

## Limitations
- Disruption detection assumes binary disruption events, which may not be robust to noisy real-world sensor data.
- Performance gains are shown only in simulation; no physical robot or human-in-the-loop testing is reported.
- The tMAPD subproblem heuristic may not generalize to complex obstacle configurations.
- Computational overhead and decision latency during peak throughput are not evaluated.

## Confidence
- Algorithmic framework and simulation results: **Medium**
- Real-world deployment feasibility: **Low**
- Disruption detection robustness: **Low**
