# A Discerning Several Thousand Judgments: GPT-3 Rates the Article + Adjective + Numeral + Noun Construction

## Quick Facts

## Executive Summary
This study investigates GPT-3's understanding of the rare Article + Adjective + Numeral + Noun (AANN) construction in English. Using a custom prompt, the study elicits acceptability judgments from GPT-3 and compares them to human ratings. The findings show that GPT-3's judgments align broadly with human judgments and generally conform to proposed linguistic constraints for the AANN construction. However, some divergences between GPT-3 and human judgments, as well as from the literature, are observed. Specifically, GPT-3 prefers the adjective order that is dispreferred in the literature (quantitative before qualitative), and it shows different relative ratings for degenerate versions of the construction compared to humans. The study highlights GPT-3's ability to recognize and use the AANN construction, suggesting a significant amount of constructional knowledge, but also indicates areas for further research into its understanding of the construction's meaning and function.

## Method Summary
The study uses GPT-3 (text-davinci-002) to elicit acceptability judgments for the AANN construction. A custom prompt asks GPT-3 to rate sentences as "good" or "bad" based on acceptability. The prompt was validated on the CoLA corpus, achieving 84% accuracy and 0.63 MCC. Templatically generated sentences with varying adjective/noun types and templates were created and run through GPT-3. Human ratings were collected via Amazon Mechanical Turk on a subset of sentences. Mixed-effects regression was used to compare human and GPT-3 ratings across conditions.

## Key Results
- GPT-3 rates the AANN construction as acceptable as the standard construction and rates degenerate versions significantly lower
- GPT-3 shows significant differences in acceptability ratings for different adjective-noun combinations, with stubbornly distributive adjectives (e.g., colors) rated as least acceptable
- GPT-3 significantly prefers the quantitative-before-qualitative adjective order, which is dispreferred in the literature

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- Question: How does the acceptability of the AANN construction vary across different dialects of English?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper acknowledges that English is not a monolith and that the acceptability of the AANN construction may vary across dialects.
- Why unresolved: The study used only US-based raters and did not systematically explore dialectal variation.
- What evidence would resolve it: Conducting the same experiments with raters from different English-speaking regions and comparing the results.

### Open Question 2
- Question: What is the underlying mechanism that allows LLMs to override the heuristic that "a" signals a singular noun in the AANN construction?
- Basis in paper: [inferred] The paper discusses how LLMs must overcome frequency biases and heuristics to master the AANN construction, but does not explore the specific mechanisms.
- Why unresolved: The study focuses on the performance of GPT-3 rather than the internal mechanisms of language models.
- What evidence would resolve it: Using causal intervention techniques to understand how LLMs process the AANN construction and override standard heuristics.

### Open Question 3
- Question: How do contextual factors influence the acceptability of the AANN construction?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper mentions that the AANN construction is sensitive to context, citing examples where acceptability varies based on how easily the noun can be construed as a single unit.
- Why unresolved: Due to the combinatoric nature of the construction and the cost of experiments, the study did not explore larger contextual effects.
- What evidence would resolve it: Testing the AANN construction in varied and rich contexts to determine how acceptability changes.

## Limitations
- The prompt design for eliciting acceptability judgments may not fully capture the nuanced acceptability scales humans use
- The templatic sentence generation may not reflect how humans encounter these constructions in natural contexts
- The divergence in adjective ordering preferences between GPT-3 and literature claims suggests GPT-3 may have learned different constraints than human speakers

## Confidence
- **High confidence**: GPT-3 can distinguish AANN from degenerate constructions (ratings significantly different)
- **Medium confidence**: GPT-3's overall pattern of acceptability judgments broadly aligns with human judgments
- **Low confidence**: GPT-3's specific preference for quantitative-before-qualitative adjective order, given the lack of clear human preferences and weak corpus support
