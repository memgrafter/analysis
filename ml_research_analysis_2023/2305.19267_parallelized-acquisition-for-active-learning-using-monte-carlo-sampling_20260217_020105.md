# Parallelized Acquisition for Active Learning using Monte Carlo Sampling

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2305.19267
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.19267
- Reference count: 40
- Primary result: NORA (Nested sampling Optimization for Ranked Acquisition) achieves efficient parallelization in active learning for Bayesian inference, outperforming sequential optimization on multi-modal likelihoods

## Executive Summary
This paper addresses the challenge of efficient parallelized active learning for Bayesian inference when evaluating the true likelihood function is computationally expensive. The authors propose NORA, a method that uses nested sampling to explore the mean of a Gaussian Process surrogate model and ranks Monte Carlo samples according to their conditioned acquisition function values to generate nearly optimal batches of sampling locations. This approach is more parallelizable than traditional sequential optimization methods and can handle multi-modal likelihoods more effectively. The method is tested on various synthetic and cosmological inference problems, showing comparable accuracy to sequential optimization for simple likelihoods and superior performance for multi-modal cases.

## Method Summary
NORA (Nested sampling Optimization for Ranked Acquisition) is a method for parallelized active learning that uses a Gaussian Process surrogate model to emulate expensive likelihood functions. The key innovation is using nested sampling to explore the GP mean efficiently, then ranking Monte Carlo samples according to their conditioned acquisition function values to generate batches of sampling locations. This approach avoids the computational cost of repeatedly optimizing the acquisition function while still producing near-optimal sampling proposals. The method is particularly effective for multi-modal likelihoods where traditional sequential optimization may get stuck in local optima.

## Key Results
- NORA achieves efficient parallelization by generating nearly-optimal batches using an almost-embarrassingly parallel nested sampler on the GP mean
- For simple likelihoods, NORA achieves comparable accuracy to sequential optimization
- For multi-modal likelihoods, NORA significantly outperforms sequential optimization due to better exploration of the parameter space

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: Nested sampling on the GP mean is much faster than optimizing the acquisition function because it avoids expensive GP standard deviation predictions.
- Mechanism: Nested sampling explores the mean of the GP, which is cheaper to compute via vectorized matrix multiplication, while acquisition function optimization would require many costly standard deviation evaluations.
- Core assumption: The mean of the GP provides a good enough approximation of the acquisition function's structure for effective sampling.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract]: "by generating nearly-optimal batches of candidates using an almost-embarrassingly parallel Nested Sampler on the mean prediction of the GP"
  - [section]: "Predicting the GP mean and standard deviation at multiple points simultaneously is much faster due to the possible use of vectorized matrix multiplication routines, but such vectorization is hard to exploit during optimization."
- Break condition: If the GP mean poorly represents the acquisition function's multimodality, nested sampling may miss important regions.

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: The ranked acquisition pool (RAP) efficiently creates near-optimal batches by conditioning on previously selected candidates.
- Mechanism: New samples are inserted into the pool based on their unconditioned acquisition values, then conditioned to all higher-ranked samples, ensuring the batch approximates sequential conditioning without expensive repeated optimizations.
- Core assumption: Conditioning only lowers acquisition values, so the ranking process converges to a good approximation of the sequential optimum.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract]: "The resulting nearly-sorted Monte Carlo sample is used to generate a batch of candidates ranked according to their sequentially conditioned acquisition function values"
  - [section]: "we develop an algorithm to rank the MC samples according to their acquisition function value conditioned to the rest of the candidates, using Kriging believer"
- Break condition: If the acquisition function is not monotonic in GP uncertainty, conditioning may not always lower values, breaking the ranking logic.

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: NORA outperforms sequential optimization on multi-modal likelihoods because nested sampling better explores the full dynamic range.
- Mechanism: Nested sampling naturally balances exploration and exploitation by sampling from constrained likelihood contours, making it more likely to find distant modes than gradient-based optimizers.
- Core assumption: The multi-modal structure of the acquisition function is well-captured by nested sampling's slice sampling approach.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract]: "Our approach addresses this issue by generating nearly-optimal batches of candidates using an almost-embarrassingly parallel Nested Sampler"
  - [section]: "due to the use of slice sampling to sample from constrained likelihood contours it scales mildly with dimensionality, and due to its cluster identification algorithm it also very good at identifying global maxima even when multiple local maxima are present"
- Break condition: If the likelihood has extremely narrow modes or complex degeneracies, nested sampling may still struggle to resolve them efficiently.

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map:
  Gaussian Process surrogate model -> Nested sampling module (POLYCHORD) -> Ranked acquisition pool (RAP) algorithm -> Training set management -> Parallel execution framework (MPI)

- Critical path:
  1. Fit GP to current training set
  2. Run nested sampling on GP mean
  3. Compute acquisition function at nested samples
  4. Build ranked acquisition pool
  5. Select batch of new sampling locations
  6. Evaluate true likelihood in parallel
  7. Update training set and repeat

- Design tradeoffs:
  - Exploration vs exploitation balance in acquisition
  - Number of Kriging believer steps vs computational cost
  - Nested sampling precision vs runtime
  - Batch size vs quality of candidates

- Failure signatures:
  - Poor GP fit leading to bad acquisition proposals
  - RAP getting stuck in local optima
  - Nested sampling missing important modes
  - MPI parallelization overhead dominating runtime

- First 3 experiments:
  1. Run NORA on a simple 2D Gaussian likelihood and compare convergence to ground truth
  2. Test parallelization scaling by increasing MPI processes on a fixed problem
  3. Apply NORA to a multi-modal synthetic likelihood and compare to sequential optimization

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- Question: How does NORA's performance scale with dimensionality for highly multi-modal posteriors beyond d=4?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The authors note that NORA greatly outperforms sequential optimization for multi-modal likelihoods, but only test up to 4 dimensions in their examples
- Why unresolved: The paper doesn't explore NORA's performance on highly multi-modal problems in higher dimensions (d>4), leaving the scaling behavior unknown
- What evidence would resolve it: Systematic testing of NORA on synthetic multi-modal likelihoods in dimensions d=5, 6, 8, 10 with varying numbers of modes

### Open Question 2
- Question: What is the optimal trade-off between the number of MPI processes and threads per process for NORA across different problem types?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The authors discuss parallelization but note that "the choice of the ratio of MPI processes to threads per process" depends on various factors and "will look at substituting POLYCHORD by a nested sampler that can perform vectorized calls"
- Why unresolved: The paper presents some preliminary results but doesn't provide a systematic study of optimal parallelization configurations for different likelihood evaluation speeds and dimensionalities
- What evidence would resolve it: Benchmark studies testing different MPI/thread configurations across a range of problem types (fast/slow likelihoods, low/high dimensions)

### Open Question 3
- Question: How does NORA's exploration-exploitation balance compare quantitatively to sequential optimization across different likelihood geometries?
- Basis in paper: [inferred] The authors suggest NORA has "less aggressive acquisition" and "more exploratory behaviour" compared to sequential optimization, but don't provide quantitative comparisons
- Why unresolved: While the authors qualitatively describe differences in behavior, they don't quantify the exploration-exploitation trade-off or provide metrics to compare these behaviors systematically
- What evidence would resolve it: Metrics quantifying exploration (e.g., coverage of parameter space, discovery of distant modes) and exploitation (e.g., concentration near modes) for both methods across various test problems

## Limitations
- The accuracy of the GP mean approximation for the acquisition function in high-dimensional spaces is not fully explored
- The scalability of the ranked acquisition pool algorithm to very large batch sizes is unclear
- The method's performance on real-world, noisy data with complex correlations is not demonstrated

## Confidence

- **High Confidence**: The core mechanism of using nested sampling on GP mean for efficient exploration is well-established and supported by evidence
- **Medium Confidence**: The ranked acquisition pool algorithm's effectiveness in creating near-optimal batches is plausible but requires more extensive testing across diverse scenarios
- **Low Confidence**: The method's performance on extremely high-dimensional problems (>10 dimensions) and its ability to handle highly complex, multi-modal likelihoods are not thoroughly validated
