# Are Layout-Infused Language Models Robust to Layout Distribution Shifts? A Case Study with Scientific Documents

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2306.01058
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.01058
- Reference count: 18
- Key outcome: Layout-infused models degrade by up to 20 F1 points under layout distribution shifts, with simple training strategies reducing degradation by over 35% relative F1 but not matching in-distribution performance.

## Executive Summary
This paper investigates whether layout-infused language models remain effective when faced with documents exhibiting unfamiliar layouts from different publishers. The authors introduce a novel experimental framework for testing robustness to layout distribution shifts, using publisher metadata to partition a scientific document dataset into train and test sets with different layout distributions. They find that under layout distribution shifts, model performance degrades by up to 20 F1 points. While simple strategies like increasing training diversity or few-shot adaptation can partially mitigate this drop (reducing it by over 35% relative F1), models fail to match in-distribution performance in any out-of-distribution condition. This highlights the need to consider layout distribution shifts in model evaluation and presents a methodology for conducting such evaluations.

## Method Summary
The paper introduces GROTOAP 2-LDS, a benchmark for evaluating layout distribution shift robustness in scientific documents. Using publisher metadata from the GROTOAP 2 dataset, the authors partition documents into in-distribution (ID) and out-of-distribution (OOD) splits. They evaluate BERT, LayoutLM, LayoutLMv2, LayoutLMv3, and SciBERT on document structure recovery tasks, measuring macro-F1 scores across both ID and OOD conditions. The methodology includes experiments with increased training diversity and few-shot adaptation to assess mitigation strategies.

## Key Results
- Layout-infused models experience up to 20 F1 point performance degradation under layout distribution shifts
- Increasing training diversity reduces degradation by over 35% relative F1, but with diminishing returns
- Few-shot adaptation improves OOD performance, with LayoutLMv2 showing the highest OOD test performance after adaptation
- Models fail to reach in-distribution performance levels in any tested out-of-distribution condition

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: Layout-infused models overfit to specific layout distributions seen during training, causing performance degradation on out-of-distribution layouts.
- Mechanism: Layout-specific components (spatial text position, image embeddings) receive less pretraining data and increase total model parameters, leading to higher variance and overfitting to layout patterns.
- Core assumption: Layout features are more sensitive to distribution shifts than textual features.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract]: "Simple training strategies, such as increasing training diversity, can reduce this degradation by over 35% relative F1; however, models fail to reach in-distribution performance in any tested out-of-distribution conditions."
  - [section]: "We hypothesize that layout-specific components overfit more because they receive less pretraining data compared to text-only components, or because they increase total model parameter count (e.g., LayoutLM, LayoutLMv2, and LayoutLMv3 contain 20-45% more parameters than BERT and SciBERT)."
  - [corpus]: Weak evidence; no direct mention of overfitting in corpus papers.

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: Increasing layout diversity during training improves robustness to layout distribution shifts.
- Mechanism: Exposure to more varied layout patterns during training reduces the model's reliance on specific layout features, improving generalization.
- Core assumption: Model learns to recognize layout patterns rather than memorizing specific configurations.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract]: "Simple training strategies, such as increasing training diversity, can reduce this degradation by over 35% relative F1."
  - [section]: "These results suggest that increasing the diversity of layouts observed during training can improve model robustness, but that this strategy provides diminishing returns as training diversity continues to increase."
  - [corpus]: No direct evidence; this is a novel finding from the paper.

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: Few-shot adaptation allows models to quickly adjust to new layout distributions.
- Mechanism: Fine-tuning on a small number of examples from the target distribution allows the model to adapt its layout-specific components without overfitting.
- Core assumption: Layout patterns have enough shared structure across distributions for few-shot learning to be effective.
- Evidence anchors:

- Critical path: 1. Load pre-trained model 2. Incorporate layout features 3. Train on out-of-distribution layouts 4. Evaluate on held-out test set 5. Perform few-shot adaptation if needed
- Design tradeoffs: Layout-infused models vs text-only models, Training diversity vs overfitting risk, Few-shot adaptation vs full retraining
- Failure signatures: High variance in performance across different layout distributions, Poor performance on layouts not seen during training, Overfitting to specific layout patterns
- First 3 experiments: 1. Train layout-infused model on limited publisher data, test on held-out publisher 2. Increase training diversity by including more publishers, test on same held-out publisher 3. Perform few-shot adaptation on held-out publisher, test again

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- Question: How do layout-infused models perform on document structure recovery when tested on scientific papers from publishers not included in any training set?
- Basis in paper: [inferred] The paper evaluates performance on four held-out publishers (ACTA, BMC, PLOS, RU) but does not test on completely unseen publishers that were never in any training set.
- Why unresolved: The experiments only test out-of-distribution transfer within the GROTOAP 2 dataset's publisher set, leaving the question of generalization to entirely new publishers unanswered.
- What evidence would resolve it: Testing models on scientific papers from publishers completely absent from the GROTOAP 2 dataset or from different document types (e.g., conference proceedings vs. journals) would show whether the observed performance drops are due to layout distribution shifts or publisher-specific characteristics.

### Open Question 2
- Question: Does the performance drop under layout distribution shifts stem primarily from layout changes, text domain shifts, or their interaction?
- Basis in paper: [inferred] The paper notes that performance drops occur for both layout-infused and text-only models, and that categories with largest drops are those characterized by spatial location, but does not isolate the effects of layout vs. text domain shifts.
- Why unresolved: The GROTOAP 2 dataset contains papers from biomedical journals where layout and textual domain are inherently correlated, making it difficult to disentangle the effects of layout shifts from text domain shifts.
- What evidence would resolve it: Synthetic experiments with controlled LaTeX-based manipulations that vary layout while keeping text content constant, or vice versa, would isolate the effects of each type of shift on model performance.

### Open Question 3
- Question: Would larger-scale pretraining of layout-specific components improve model robustness to layout distribution shifts?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The authors hypothesize that layout-specific components overfit more because they receive less pretraining data compared to text-only components, or because they increase total model parameter count.
- Why unresolved: The current experiments use models pretrained on the same datasets as existing work, and do not test whether additional pretraining on more diverse layout data would improve robustness.
- What evidence would resolve it: Training layout-infused models on datasets with significantly more layout diversity during pretraining, then testing on the GROTOAP 2-LDS benchmark, would show whether increased pretraining data improves robustness to layout distribution shifts.

## Limitations
- The framework relies on publisher-based partitioning which may not capture all types of layout distribution shifts
- The study focuses exclusively on scientific documents, limiting generalizability to other document types
- The paper doesn't address potential confounding factors such as variations in document quality, scanning artifacts, or language differences across publishers

## Confidence
- **High Confidence**: Empirical finding that layout-infused models experience performance degradation under layout distribution shifts; observation that increasing training diversity improves robustness with diminishing returns
- **Medium Confidence**: Hypothesis that layout-specific components overfit more due to fewer pretraining examples and increased parameter count
- **Low Confidence**: Claim that few-shot adaptation allows layout-infused models to adapt more quickly to new layout distributions

## Next Checks
1. Cross-Document Type Validation: Replicate the experimental framework using non-scientific documents (forms, invoices, webpages) to test whether layout distribution shift robustness generalizes beyond academic papers

2. Ablation Study on Layout Components: Systematically disable or modify layout-specific components (spatial embeddings, image features) to quantify their individual contributions to both performance gains and vulnerability to distribution shifts

3. Temporal Distribution Shift Analysis: Test model performance on documents published across different time periods to evaluate robustness to temporal layout evolution, which may reveal different failure modes than publisher-based shifts
