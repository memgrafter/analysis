# Evaluating Language Models for Knowledge Base Completion

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2303.11082
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.11082
- Reference count: 27
- Large-scale, unbiased benchmark dataset WD-Known created with 3.9M random Wikidata facts

## Executive Summary
This paper addresses the problem of overestimated language model performance in knowledge base completion by introducing WD-Known, a large-scale unbiased benchmark dataset. The authors systematically evaluate BERT's capabilities for both existing-fact prediction and missing-fact completion through human annotation. They find that previous benchmarks significantly overestimated LM performance, while WD-Known provides more realistic estimates. Despite lower overall performance, LM-based KBC shows strong results for language-related and socio-demographic relations, with simple improvements like vocabulary expansion and prompt engineering enabling significant expansion of Wikidata.

## Method Summary
The authors created WD-Known, a large-scale unbiased benchmark dataset containing 3.9M random facts sampled from Wikidata. They systematically evaluated BERT's knowledge base completion potential through two tasks: predicting existing facts and predicting missing facts with human annotation. The evaluation covered 14 relations with human annotations, comparing performance against existing benchmarks. The study also tested simple improvements like vocabulary expansion and prompt formulation to enhance LM-based KBC performance.

## Key Results
- WD-Known reveals significantly lower LM performance than previous benchmarks, providing more realistic estimates
- LM-based KBC achieves high precision (82-90%) for specific relations with vocabulary expansion: nativeLanguage (+21x), citizenOf (+0.3x), usedLanguage (+2.1x)
- LMs demonstrate strong generalization capabilities for socio-demographic relations

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The effectiveness stems from addressing bias in benchmark datasets and leveraging LMs' inherent understanding of language patterns. By using random sampling from Wikidata, WD-Known eliminates the selection bias present in previous benchmarks that favored easily predictable facts. The success with socio-demographic relations leverages LMs' strong training on natural language patterns related to human characteristics and relationships. Simple improvements like vocabulary expansion work because they directly address the knowledge gaps in LMs' pretraining, while prompt engineering taps into the models' contextual understanding capabilities.

## Foundational Learning

**Knowledge Base Completion (KBC)**: The task of predicting missing facts in knowledge bases, crucial for maintaining and expanding structured knowledge. Needed because knowledge bases are inherently incomplete and manual completion is impractical at scale. Quick check: Can the model predict that Paris is the capital of France given partial knowledge?

**Prompt Engineering**: Designing specific input prompts to guide LMs toward desired outputs. Essential for KBC because it helps LMs understand the context and format of expected answers. Quick check: Does the prompt clearly indicate the relation type and expected answer format?

**Vocabulary Expansion**: Extending the model's understanding of entity names and relations beyond its pretraining. Critical for KBC success because LMs may not know specific entity names not seen during pretraining. Quick check: Are entity names and relations properly formatted for the model to recognize?

## Architecture Onboarding

Component Map: Input Prompt -> BERT Encoder -> Fact Prediction -> Output Verification
Critical Path: Prompt formulation and vocabulary expansion directly impact prediction accuracy
Design Tradeoffs: Random sampling ensures unbiased evaluation but may include harder cases; simple improvements are effective but may not capture full LM potential
Failure Signatures: Poor performance on technical/scientific relations, inconsistent results across different relation types
First Experiments:
1. Test basic fact prediction accuracy on simple relations (e.g., placeOfBirth)
2. Evaluate vocabulary expansion effectiveness for a single relation type
3. Compare prompt engineering variations for the same relation

## Limitations
- Human annotation covers only 14 relations, leaving significant gaps in performance understanding
- Vocabulary expansion success may not generalize across all relation types
- Direct performance comparisons with biased benchmarks may be misleading

## Confidence
High confidence in: 
- Previous benchmarks overestimated LM performance
- WD-Known provides more realistic performance estimates
- Simple vocabulary expansion and prompt engineering work for specific relations

Medium confidence in:
- Generalizability of success factors across relation types
- Exact magnitude of performance improvements
- Long-term stability of reported gains

## Next Checks
1. Expand human annotation coverage to include at least 50% of relations in WD-Known
2. Conduct cross-validation with different LM architectures beyond BERT
3. Implement systematic analysis of annotation consistency with inter-annotator agreement measurement
