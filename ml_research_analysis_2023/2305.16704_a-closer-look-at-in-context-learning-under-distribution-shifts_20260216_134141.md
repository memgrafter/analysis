# A Closer Look at In-Context Learning under Distribution Shifts

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2305.16704
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.16704
- Authors: 
- Reference count: 14
- Key outcome: This work investigates in-context learning (ICL) under distribution shifts by comparing transformers and set-based MLPs on linear regression tasks. The authors derive conditions under which optimal models emulate OLS or ridge regression and propose set-based MLPs as a natural baseline architecture. Experiments show that transformers outperform set-based MLPs on in-distribution tasks and degrade more gracefully under mild distribution shifts, but both architectures fail under severe shifts. ## Executive Summary
This paper investigates in-context learning (ICL) under distribution shifts by comparing transformers and set-based MLPs on linear regression tasks. The authors derive theoretical conditions under which optimal ICL models emulate OLS or ridge regression, and propose set-based MLPs as a natural baseline architecture. Through controlled experiments, they show that while transformers outperform set-based MLPs on in-distribution tasks, both architectures fail under severe distribution shifts, and the ranking of in-distribution ICL performance does not predict out-of-distribution performance.

## Method Summary
The authors generate synthetic linear regression data with varying distribution shifts, where inputs are drawn from different Gaussian distributions at train and test time. They implement set-based MLPs with permutation-invariant functions and transformers from the GPT-2 family, training both architectures on in-distribution data and evaluating performance on both in-distribution and out-of-distribution data. The set-based MLPs use varying depths (4, 5, 10, 17, 26) with ReLU activations and batch norm, while transformers use depths 12 and 16 as specified in Garg et al. (2022).

## Key Results
- Set-based MLPs exhibit ICL but underperform transformers on in-distribution tasks
- Transformers show better resilience to mild distribution shifts compared to set-based MLPs
- Both architectures fail under severe distribution shifts
- In-distribution ICL performance does not predict out-of-distribution performance

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
Set-based MLPs can exhibit in-context learning by leveraging permutation-invariant architectures that approximate OLS/ridge regression under certain conditions. The set-based MLP architecture uses permutation-invariant functions (via summation over transformed inputs and labels) to estimate the underlying linear parameter β. This allows the model to compute predictions without relying on input order, making it naturally suited for ICL tasks.

### Mechanism 2
Transformers degrade more gracefully under mild distribution shifts compared to set-based MLPs due to their attention-based architecture. Transformers use attention mechanisms to weigh the importance of different input-output pairs dynamically, allowing them to adapt to small changes in input distribution while maintaining performance.

### Mechanism 3
The ranking of in-distribution ICL performance does not predict out-of-distribution performance for either architecture. ICL performance depends on the model's ability to learn and apply the underlying algorithm (OLS/ridge) within the training distribution. However, out-of-distribution performance requires additional generalization capabilities that may not correlate with in-distribution abilities.

  - Set-based MLP: Erratic performance under mild distribution shifts, complete failure under severe shifts

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- Question: Why do transformers outperform set-based MLPs in in-context learning under mild distribution shifts?
- Basis in paper: The paper demonstrates that transformers degrade more gracefully than set-based MLPs under mild distribution shifts, but the underlying reason remains unexplored.
- Why unresolved: The paper identifies the empirical observation but does not provide a theoretical explanation for why transformers are more robust to distribution shifts compared to set-based MLPs.
- What evidence would resolve it: A theoretical analysis comparing the inductive biases of transformers and set-based MLPs under distribution shifts, or controlled experiments isolating specific architectural differences that contribute to the observed performance gap.

### Open Question 2
- Question: Can the in-context learning abilities of set-based MLPs be improved to match transformers?
- Basis in paper: The paper shows that set-based MLPs exhibit in-context learning but underperform transformers, even when the architecture is well-suited for the task.
- Why unresolved: The paper does not explore modifications to the set-based MLP architecture or training procedure that could close the performance gap with transformers.
- What evidence would resolve it: Experiments demonstrating improved set-based MLP performance through architectural changes, alternative training objectives, or enhanced regularization techniques that enable better generalization under distribution shifts.

### Open Question 3
- Question: How do distribution shifts beyond those tested affect in-context learning performance?
- Basis in paper: The paper only tests mild and severe distribution shifts in the input space, but does not explore other types of distribution shifts (e.g., label distribution shifts, covariate shifts in the prompt space).
- Why unresolved: The experimental scope is limited to specific input distribution shifts, leaving open questions about how other types of distribution shifts impact in-context learning.
- What evidence would resolve it: Experiments testing in-context learning under various types of distribution shifts (e.g., shifts in label noise, prompt structure, or feature distributions) to characterize the robustness of transformers and set-based MLPs across different scenarios.

### Open Question 4
- Question: Are there algorithmic improvements that could enhance out-of-distribution in-context learning?
- Basis in paper: The paper shows that both transformers and set-based MLPs fail under severe distribution shifts, indicating a need for better OOD-ICL methods.
- Why unresolved: The paper does not propose or test modifications to the learning algorithms that could improve OOD-ICL performance.
- What evidence would resolve it: Development and evaluation of algorithmic techniques (e.g., meta-learning, robust optimization, or uncertainty-aware methods) that enable transformers or set-based MLPs to maintain in-context learning abilities under severe distribution shifts.

## Limitations

- Architecture Specification: The paper references transformer architectures from Garg et al. (2022) but doesn't provide complete architectural details beyond depth, which could affect reproduction fidelity.
- Generalization Boundaries: The theoretical analysis focuses on linear regression specifically, and the extent to which these findings generalize to non-linear regression tasks or classification remains unclear.
- Distribution Shift Characterization: The paper defines mild and severe shifts using simple mean shifts, but real-world distribution shifts often involve more complex transformations.
