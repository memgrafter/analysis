# Growing and Serving Large Open-domain Knowledge Graphs

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2305.09464
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.09464
- Reference count: 24
- One-line primary result: Extends Saga platform with graph embeddings, semantic annotation, and targeted knowledge extraction for large-scale KG construction and serving.

## Executive Summary
This paper presents extensions to the Saga platform for constructing and serving large-scale knowledge graphs (KGs). The authors introduce a graph embedding pipeline to support fact ranking, verification, entity linking, and related entities; a semantic annotation service to link Web documents to KG entities; and a targeted knowledge extraction framework to identify and fill coverage gaps in the KG. The work also discusses adaptations for private on-device personal KG construction, including incremental updates, cross-device sync, and global knowledge enrichment. The approach emphasizes scalability, quality, and integration of open-domain Web content into the KG.

## Method Summary
The authors extend Saga with three main capabilities: (1) a graph embedding pipeline that trains and indexes embeddings of KG entities for use in fact ranking, verification, entity linking, and related entities; (2) a scalable semantic annotation service that links entities in Web documents to the KG, effectively expanding the KG with edges to open-domain Web content; and (3) a targeted knowledge extraction framework that identifies coverage issues in the KG and extracts missing information from the Web to enrich the KG. The paper also discusses adaptations for on-device personal KG construction, including incremental updates, cross-device synchronization, and global knowledge enrichment.

## Key Results
- Graph embeddings trained on filtered KG triples support fact ranking, verification, entity linking, and related entities services.
- Semantic annotation service links billions of Web documents to KG entities, expanding the KG with open-domain edges.
- Targeted knowledge extraction framework identifies and fills coverage gaps in the KG using Web search and extraction models.
- On-device KG construction supports incremental updates, cross-device sync, and global knowledge enrichment for personalization.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: Knowledge graph embeddings enable efficient fact ranking, verification, and entity linking.
- Mechanism: By training embeddings on filtered KG triples, entities and relations are represented as vectors in continuous space. Similarity between vectors supports ranking (e.g., most relevant occupations), verification (fact plausibility), and linking (disambiguating entity mentions).
- Core assumption: Embeddings preserve the semantic structure of the KG and are trained on relevant triples only.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "We present a graph embedding pipeline built on Saga’s Graph Query Engine that trains and indexes embeddings of KG entities. These graph embeddings have many direct use cases, such as fact ranking, fact verification, entity linking..."
  - [section 2] "we represent the entities and predicates of a KG using a continuous vector space such that their inherent structure is preserved"
- Break condition: If embeddings are trained on noisy or irrelevant data, their utility for ranking, verification, and linking degrades.

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: Semantic annotation links Web documents to KG entities, enriching the KG with open-domain edges.
- Mechanism: A scalable service uses mention detection, named entity recognition, and entity linking with contextual reranking. Precomputed entity embeddings allow fast similarity scoring between entity mentions in text and KG entities.
- Core assumption: The entity linking model can disambiguate entities based on context and scale to billions of Web pages.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "We then describe how our platform, including graph embeddings, can be leveraged to create a Semantic Annotation service that links unstructured Web documents to entities in our KG."
  - [section 3.1] "Our extensible semantic annotation service supports various classical and neural-based components for tasks such as mention detection, named and nominal entity recognition and entity linking with contextual reranking."
- Break condition: If the annotation service cannot scale or the contextual reranking fails to disambiguate, linking quality drops.

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: Targeted knowledge extraction from the Web improves KG coverage and freshness.
- Mechanism: Missing/stale facts are identified, relevant Web documents are retrieved via synthesized queries, and extractors (rule-based or neural) harvest candidate facts. A verification model uses evidence signals to select high-quality facts.
- Core assumption: Synthesized search queries retrieve relevant documents and extractors can accurately extract structured facts from semi-structured or unstructured sources.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "Finally, we leverage annotated Web documents to drive Open-domain Knowledge Extraction... This targeted extraction framework identifies important coverage issues in the KG..."
  - [section 4] "we focus on missing and stale facts that matter most and leverage Web search to find relevant documents... We then use the Query Synthesizer to automatically generate search queries to identify relevant documents..."
- Break condition: If the search queries fail to retrieve relevant documents or the verification model cannot distinguish high-quality facts, KG coverage does not improve.

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map: Saga KG platform -> Graph Query Engine -> Embedding Training (GPU cluster) -> Embedding Service (vector index) -> Semantic Annotation Service -> Web Annotation Pipeline -> Open Domain Knowledge Extraction (Query Synthesizer, Extractors, Verification Model)
- Critical path: Entity mentions in Web text -> Semantic annotation with embeddings -> Linked Web documents -> Targeted extraction -> Fact verification -> KG enrichment
- Design tradeoffs: Disk-based vs. in-memory training for scalability; rule-based vs. neural extractors for variety; static vs. dynamic knowledge enrichment for privacy
- Failure signatures: Poor entity linking precision (broken disambiguation); low fact verification recall (missed high-quality facts); annotation latency spikes (bottleneck in similarity computation)
- First 3 experiments:
  1. Train embeddings on a filtered KG view and measure ranking accuracy for a sample of fact ranking queries
  2. Deploy semantic annotation on a small Web corpus and evaluate entity linking precision/recall against ground truth
  3. Run targeted extraction on a set of missing facts and verify that the verification model selects high-quality facts with >90% accuracy

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- Question: How does the trade-off between scalability and quality of knowledge graph embeddings vary across different downstream applications?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper discusses shallow vs. reasoning-based embedding models and their scalability challenges, and mentions different filtering strategies for relevant facts.
- Why unresolved: The paper describes the embedding pipeline but doesn't provide empirical comparisons of different models or filtering strategies across use cases like fact ranking, verification, and entity linking.
- What evidence would resolve it: Comparative studies showing embedding quality metrics (accuracy, recall) versus computational cost for each application scenario.

### Open Question 2
- Question: What is the optimal balance between static global knowledge assets and dynamic enrichment for on-device personalization?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper describes three enrichment paths but doesn't discuss when to use each or how to allocate resources between them.
- Why unresolved: The paper presents the enrichment methods as complementary but doesn't provide guidance on their relative effectiveness or cost-benefit trade-offs.
- What evidence would resolve it: User studies or A/B tests measuring personalization effectiveness against resource usage for each enrichment method.

### Open Question 3
- Question: How can web-scale semantic annotation services maintain freshness while minimizing computational costs?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper discusses challenges of annotating billions of webpages with variety, scale, and rate of change, but doesn't detail freshness maintenance strategies.
- Why unresolved: The paper mentions incremental processing but doesn't quantify how often documents need re-annotation or what triggers re-annotation.
- What evidence would resolve it: Metrics showing annotation accuracy decay over time and cost analysis of different re-annotation scheduling strategies.

## Limitations

- Implementation details for key components (embedding models, annotation architectures, extraction models) are not specified.
- Scalability of semantic annotation at true Web scale (billions of pages) and effectiveness of synthesized search queries for targeted extraction are not empirically demonstrated.
- On-device KG construction approach lacks specifics on incremental updates and synchronization protocols.

## Confidence

- Graph embeddings enabling KG tasks: High confidence (well-established approach with clear mechanism)
- Semantic annotation linking Web content to KG: Medium confidence (scalability and accuracy details missing)
- Targeted extraction improving KG coverage: Medium confidence (effectiveness depends on search and verification components)
- On-device KG construction: Low confidence (minimal technical details provided)
