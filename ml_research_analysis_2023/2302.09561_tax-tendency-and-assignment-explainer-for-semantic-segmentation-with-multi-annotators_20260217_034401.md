# TAX: Tendency-and-Assignment Explainer for Semantic Segmentation with Multi-Annotators

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID**: 2302.09561
- **Source URL**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2302.09561
- **Reference count**: 40
- **Key outcome**: TAX framework provides interpretable semantic segmentation by learning annotator-specific convolution kernels and prototype banks, achieving 85.80% assignment accuracy on PASCAL VOC while maintaining competitive segmentation performance.

## Executive Summary
This paper introduces the Tendency-and-Assignment Explainer (TAX) framework for interpretable semantic segmentation in multi-annotator scenarios. The key insight is that different annotators exhibit distinct labeling tendencies that can be captured through annotator-dependent convolution kernel subsets and visual prototypes. TAX jointly learns these components to provide both accurate segmentation and explanations for "who" contributed to the segmentation and "why" particular assignments were made. The framework demonstrates that interpretability can be achieved without sacrificing segmentation quality, showing comparable performance to state-of-the-art methods while offering trace-able explanations at both annotator and assignment levels.

## Method Summary
TAX addresses multi-annotator semantic segmentation by learning annotator-dependent convolution kernel subsets for each pixel, where each subset captures the labeling tendencies of specific annotators. The framework consists of a tendency-preserving segmentation model that uses these kernel subsets, and an annotator assigner with a prototype bank that provides visual guidance for why particular annotator tendencies are selected. During training, uncertain regions (boundaries) are identified using a pre-trained vanilla segmentation model to create pseudo-annotator masks that guide the learning of annotator assignment. The prototype bank contains local image patterns associated with specific annotator tendencies, allowing the model to explain its decisions by showing the most similar training patches during inference.

## Key Results
- Achieved 85.80% assignment accuracy on PASCAL VOC dataset, demonstrating effective identification of annotator labeling tendencies
- Maintained comparable segmentation performance to state-of-the-art methods while providing interpretable outputs
- Successfully identified visual similarities between input patches and training data through prototype-based explanations
- Demonstrated effectiveness on synthetic datasets (PASCAL VOC, EndoScene) and real-world dataset (LIDC-IDRI)

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: The model learns distinct convolution kernel subsets for each annotator, allowing it to capture individual labeling tendencies.
- Mechanism: During training, each pixel's convolution operation uses the kernel subset corresponding to the annotator mask prediction. The annotator-aware segmentation loss ensures each kernel subset only observes data from its corresponding annotator.
- Core assumption: Labeling tendencies manifest differently at the pixel level, and these differences can be captured by learning separate kernel parameters for each annotator.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract]: "We learn convolution kernel subsets for modeling labeling tendencies of each type of annotation"
  - [section]: "we propose to learn the annotator-dependent convolution kernel set C in these final layers, since labeling tendencies are reflected at finer feature resolutions"
- Break condition: If labeling tendencies don't vary significantly between annotators or if the annotator mask prediction is inaccurate, the kernel subsets may not learn meaningful differences.

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: The prototype bank provides visual guidance that explains why particular annotator labeling tendencies are selected during inference.
- Mechanism: The annotator assigner learns a prototype bank where each prototype represents local image patterns associated with specific annotator tendencies. During inference, the model identifies which prototypes are most similar to input patches, providing a traceable explanation for annotator assignment.
- Core assumption: Local image patterns can be clustered into prototypes that represent the visual characteristics of different annotator labeling tendencies.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract]: "a prototype bank is jointly observed to offer visual guidance for learning the above kernels"
  - [section]: "our model allows to explain why particular annotator labeling tendency is utilized during inference, realizing explainable and traceable segmentation outputs"
- Break condition: If prototypes fail to capture meaningful visual differences between annotators or if the similarity metric doesn't correlate with actual labeling tendencies, the assignment explanations become unreliable.

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: The pseudo-annotator mask guides the learning of the annotator assigner by focusing on uncertain regions where labeling tendencies differ most.
- Mechanism: The model identifies uncertain regions using a pre-trained vanilla segmentation model, then assigns specific annotator tendencies to these regions while using shared tendencies for consensus regions. This targeted approach helps the assigner learn where annotator differences matter most.
- Core assumption: Boundary regions contain the most significant differences between annotator labeling tendencies, making them the most informative for learning annotator assignment.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [section]: "the motivation is that, the boundaries are the most uncertain regions and thus are the major variance of segmentation tendencies"
  - [section]: "we identify uncertain regions of an input image xi by pre-training a vanilla segmentation model"
- Break condition: If uncertain regions don't actually correlate with labeling tendency differences, or if the pre-trained model's uncertainty estimates are poor, the pseudo-annotator mask may provide misleading guidance.

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map: Input image → Annotator assigner G → Annotator mask → Tendency-preserving segmentation model F → Segmentation output. The annotator assigner must be trained first (using pseudo-annotator masks) before the segmentation model can learn its annotator-dependent kernels.

- Critical path: The pipeline begins with the annotator assigner G processing the input image to predict annotator masks, which then guide the tendency-preserving segmentation model F in selecting the appropriate convolution kernel subsets for each pixel. This determines the final segmentation output.

- Design tradeoffs: The model trades some segmentation performance (comparable to baselines rather than exceeding them) for interpretability. The additional complexity of learning annotator-dependent kernels and prototype banks increases training time and model size.

- Failure signatures: Poor annotator mask predictions (low assignment accuracy), segmentation performance similar to or worse than baseline models, or prototypes that don't visually resemble their corresponding training patches.

- First 3 experiments:
  1. Train on a synthetic multi-annotator dataset with clearly distinguishable labeling tendencies (like the morphological operations described) and verify that different kernel subsets are learned.
  2. Test the annotator assigner's ability to correctly identify annotator tendencies on held-out data and visualize the most similar training patches.
  3. Evaluate segmentation performance with and without the annotator-dependent kernels to confirm that the added complexity doesn't significantly degrade accuracy.

## Limitations

- The evaluation relies heavily on synthetic datasets with artificially created annotator differences through morphological operations, raising questions about generalization to real-world annotator disagreements.
- The prototype bank scalability is not addressed, particularly how it handles many annotators or large datasets where the bank may become too small to capture all variations or too large to remain interpretable.
- The computational overhead of learning annotator-dependent kernel subsets and maintaining a prototype bank is not thoroughly analyzed, with no detailed comparison to standard segmentation approaches.

## Confidence

- **High Confidence**: The core mechanism of learning annotator-dependent kernel subsets and the general framework architecture are well-supported by the methodology description and evaluation results.
- **Medium Confidence**: The assignment accuracy results (85.80% on PASCAL VOC) are convincing, but the interpretability claims depend heavily on the quality of the prototype bank and whether the visual guidance actually corresponds to human-understandable reasons for annotator selection.
- **Low Confidence**: The generalizability to real-world multi-annotator scenarios beyond the synthetic morphological operations is not well-established, and the paper doesn't address how the framework handles continuous variations in labeling tendencies rather than discrete categories.

## Next Checks
