# Promise and Limitations of Supervised Optimal Transport-Based Graph Summarization via Information Theoretic Measures

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2305.07138
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.07138
- Reference count: 30
- One-line primary result: Proposes supervised graph summarization using optimal transport with mutual information, showing improved classification accuracy over baselines while proving NP-hardness and theoretical limitations.

## Executive Summary
This paper addresses supervised graph summarization by extending optimal transport (OT)-based compression to incorporate class-label information through mutual information estimates. The authors propose a method that computes an initial node distribution and cost matrix based on mutual information between node attributes, edge indicators, and class labels, then applies OT compression to reduce graph size while preserving class-relevant information. The method is evaluated on MNIST, CIFAR-10, MiniImageNet, NYC taxi data, and synthetic datasets, showing improved classification accuracy and runtime compared to OTC, Graph Coarsening, and baseline approaches. The paper also proves NP-hardness of the mutual information maximization problem and demonstrates that the OT approach fails to satisfy information monotonicity, providing theoretical insights into the method's limitations.

## Method Summary
The supervised optimal transport graph summarization method extends the standard OT compression framework by incorporating mutual information estimates between graph structure and class labels. The algorithm computes an initial probability distribution ρ₀ over nodes using mutual information between node attributes and class labels, then constructs a cost matrix incorporating both KL divergence and mutual information terms. This cost matrix and initial distribution are passed to an OT compression routine along with the complete graph and target vertex count. The resulting compressed graph preserves edges and nodes that are more informative for classification. The method is compared against OTC (unsupervised OT compression), Graph Coarsening, and a baseline with no compression across multiple datasets.

## Key Results
- The proposed method achieves higher classification accuracy than OTC, Graph Coarsening, and baseline on MNIST, CIFAR-10, and NYC taxi datasets
- Runtime performance shows the method is faster than OTC while maintaining or improving accuracy
- The paper proves that maximizing mutual information for supervised summarization is NP-hard, justifying the OT-based approximation approach
- Theoretical analysis shows the OT method fails to satisfy information monotonicity, meaning minimizing transport cost does not always maximize class label information

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- **Claim**: The method outperforms previous approaches by incorporating mutual information estimates between node attributes, edge indicators, and class labels into the optimal transport compression framework.
- **Mechanism**: Mutual information quantifies the dependency between graph structure and class labels. By embedding this into the OT cost function, the method prioritizes preserving edges and nodes that are more informative for classification, leading to better downstream performance.
- **Core assumption**: Mutual information estimates between node features and class labels, as well as between edge indicators and class labels, can be accurately computed from the training data.
- **Evidence anchors**:
  - [abstract] "They empirically show performance improvements over previous works in terms of classification accuracy and time on synthetic and certain real datasets."
  - [section] "We propose a summarization method that incorporates mutual information estimates between random variables associated with sample graphs and class labels into the optimal transport compression framework."
  - [corpus] Weak - no direct mention of mutual information in corpus, but the method is related to optimal transport which is present in corpus papers.
- **Break condition**: If mutual information estimates are inaccurate or biased, the method may prioritize the wrong nodes/edges, leading to worse compression.

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim**: The NP-hardness of the mutual information maximization problem constrains expectations from proposed solutions, making the proposed OT-based approach a reasonable alternative.
- **Mechanism**: Since directly maximizing mutual information is computationally intractable, the OT-based method provides an efficient heuristic that approximates this goal by minimizing transport cost under a constraint on the number of vertices.
- **Core assumption**: The OT-based approach provides a good approximation to the mutual information maximization problem, even though it cannot guarantee optimality.
- **Evidence anchors**:
  - [abstract] "We show an NP-hardness of approximation result for this problem, thereby constraining what one should expect from proposed solutions."
  - [section] "We propose a natural information-theoretic objective function for the task of supervised graph summarization and show that it is NP-hard to optimize, even approximately."
  - [corpus] Weak - no direct mention of NP-hardness, but the corpus papers discuss optimal transport which is related to the method.
- **Break condition**: If the OT-based approach deviates significantly from the optimal solution to the mutual information maximization problem, its performance may suffer.

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim**: The method's limitations, such as the failure to satisfy information monotonicity, provide insights into the inductive biases of different graph summarization approaches.
- **Mechanism**: By understanding the limitations of the OT-based approach, researchers can better design future methods that address these shortcomings and develop a more comprehensive understanding of graph summarization.
- **Core assumption**: Identifying and understanding the limitations of the OT-based approach is valuable for advancing the field of graph summarization.
- **Evidence anchors**:
  - [abstract] "We also theoretically explore the limitations of the optimal transport approach for the supervised summarization problem and we show that it fails to satisfy a certain desirable information monotonicity property."
  - [section] "We then explore the limitations of the optimal transport approach, both theoretically and empirically, toward achieving our third goal stated above."
  - [corpus] Weak - no direct mention of information monotonicity, but the corpus papers discuss limitations of optimal transport methods.
- **Break condition**: If the limitations of the OT-based approach are not well-understood or addressed, future methods may inherit these limitations.

## Architecture Onboarding

- **Component map**: Mutual Information Estimation -> Cost Matrix Construction -> OT Compression -> Classification
- **Critical path**: Estimate mutual information from training data → Compute initial distribution ρ₀ and cost matrix → Apply OT-based summarization to both training and test sets → Train and evaluate classifier on summarized data
- **Design tradeoffs**: The method trades off between preserving informative graph structure (by using mutual information) and computational efficiency (by using OT). It also trades off between local and global information in setting the OT parameters.
- **Failure signatures**: If the method performs worse than baseline, potential failure points include inaccurate mutual information estimates, suboptimal OT parameters, or limitations of the OT approach itself.
- **First 3 experiments**:
  1. Evaluate the method on a simple synthetic dataset with known class-label dependencies to verify that it can identify and preserve informative graph structure.
  2. Compare the method's performance with and without mutual information estimates to quantify the impact of incorporating class-label information.
  3. Test the method on a real dataset with a different graph structure (e.g., social networks) to assess its generalizability beyond the evaluated datasets.

## Limitations
- The method assumes all graphs share a common vertex set, limiting applicability to datasets like chemistry applications where this assumption is violated
- The NP-hardness of mutual information maximization constrains theoretical guarantees, requiring the OT-based approach as an approximation
- The OT approach fails to satisfy information monotonicity, meaning minimizing transport cost does not guarantee maximizing class label information
