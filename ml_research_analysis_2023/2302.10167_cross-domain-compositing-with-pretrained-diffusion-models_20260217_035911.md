# Cross-domain Compositing with Pretrained Diffusion Models

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2302.10167
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2302.10167
- Reference count: 40
- Key outcome: Cross-domain image compositing using iterative inference-time conditioning with masked ILVR technique

## Executive Summary
This paper presents a novel approach for cross-domain image compositing using pretrained diffusion models. The method employs an iterative, inference-time conditioning scheme that infuses injected objects with contextual information from background scenes through localized, controllable changes. By leveraging masked ILVR (Image Local Variance Reduction), the technique blends low-frequency information from reference images with high-frequency information from current step images using smoothed masks. The approach demonstrates effectiveness across various tasks including image blending, object immersion, texture replacement, CG2Real translation, and stylization.

## Method Summary
The proposed method uses pretrained diffusion models with an iterative conditioning scheme that enables cross-domain compositing. At each denoising step, the approach applies a masked ILVR technique where low-frequency components from the reference image are blended with high-frequency components from the current step image using a smoothed mask. This allows for localized modifications while preserving overall image coherence. The conditioning is performed at inference time without requiring additional training or annotations, making the method flexible and efficient. The iterative process ensures that injected objects receive appropriate contextual information from the background scene, resulting in realistic composites.

## Key Results
- Demonstrates successful cross-domain compositing across multiple tasks including image blending, object immersion, and texture replacement
- Shows effective CG2Real translation and stylization capabilities without requiring domain-specific training
- Achieves high-quality results that outperform prior work in qualitative and quantitative comparisons
- Enables data augmentation for downstream tasks like single-view reconstruction

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The method works by leveraging the denoising capabilities of pretrained diffusion models while introducing controlled conditioning at each inference step. The masked ILVR technique specifically allows for selective blending of frequency components, ensuring that injected objects maintain their structural details (high-frequency) while adapting to the background context (low-frequency). This frequency-aware blending, combined with iterative conditioning, enables the model to produce coherent composites that respect both the injected object's characteristics and the background scene's attributes.

## Architecture Onboarding

**Component Map**: Input image -> Frequency decomposition -> Masked ILVR blending -> Denoising diffusion model -> Output image

**Critical Path**: The most critical components are the masked ILVR blending operation and the iterative conditioning loop. The frequency decomposition must accurately separate low and high frequencies, while the mask smoothing directly impacts the quality of the composite. The conditioning scheme must maintain consistency across denoising steps.

**Design Tradeoffs**: The method trades computational efficiency for flexibility - requiring multiple denoising steps versus single-pass methods. The frequency-based approach provides better control than spatial-only methods but adds complexity. Using pretrained models avoids training costs but depends on model quality and generalization.

**Failure Signatures**: Poor frequency separation leading to artifacts, overly aggressive masking causing visible seams, insufficient iterations resulting in incomplete conditioning, and model domain bias causing unrealistic composites in certain style combinations.
