# Span Identification of Epistemic Stance-Taking in Academic Written English

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2306.02038
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.02038
- Reference count: 0
- Primary result: RoBERTa + LSTM model achieves macro-averaged F1 of .7208 for stance-taking span identification

## Executive Summary
This paper presents a novel approach to identifying epistemic stance-taking expressions in academic English using the engagement framework from Appraisal analysis. The authors developed a gold-standard corpus of 4,688 sentences (126,411 tokens) annotated for eight rhetorical stance categories and supplementary discourse elements. The study compares three neural architectures for span identification - spaCy default, RoBERTa + Bi-LSTM, and Dual-RoBERTa - with the RoBERTa + LSTM model achieving the best performance (macro F1 = .7208), slightly outperforming intercoder reliability estimates.

## Method Summary
The study employed three neural architectures for end-to-end span identification: spaCy default span categorizer, RoBERTa + Bi-LSTM, and Dual-RoBERTa. The corpus was split into 5 folds with oversampling of minority classes, and models were trained using Adam optimizer with learning rate scheduling and early stopping. The Dual-RoBERTa architecture combined two transformer embeddings with a linear layer, while the RoBERTa + LSTM model used a token-level LSTM to encode BERT embeddings. All models used a two-stage pipeline with a span enumerator suggesting candidate spans and a span categorizer predicting categories.

## Key Results
- RoBERTa + LSTM achieved macro-averaged F1 of .7208, outperforming both spaCy default and Dual-RoBERTa
- Matthews Correlation Coefficient was .5476 and Cohen's Kappa was .5229, indicating substantial agreement
- The best-performing model slightly outperformed intercoder reliability estimates (F1 = .6629) before adjudication
- The system successfully identified and categorized stance-taking expressions across eight engagement categories

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The system works by leveraging transformer-based architectures to capture the contextual dependencies inherent in epistemic stance-taking expressions. The engagement framework provides a theoretically-grounded categorization system that captures how writers position their propositions in relation to potential readers and alternative viewpoints. The RoBERTa + LSTM architecture is particularly effective because it combines the contextualized embeddings from the transformer with sequential processing that can capture local dependencies within stance expressions.

- **Span Identification**: The task of detecting and categorizing contiguous text spans expressing specific rhetorical functions - needed to grasp the core NLP problem being addressed
- **Transformer-based Architectures**: Neural models using self-attention mechanisms (like RoBERTa) for contextualized embeddings - needed to understand the technical approach to encoding linguistic context
- **Macro-averaged F1 Score**: Evaluation metric that treats all classes equally regardless of frequency - needed to assess model performance across imbalanced stance categories
- **Oversampling**: Technique to balance class distributions by duplicating minority class instances - needed to understand how the study addressed data imbalance

**Component Map**: BAWE/MICUSP/Timed Essays corpus -> 5-fold splits with oversampling -> Span enumerator (suggestions) -> Span categorizer (prediction) -> Evaluation metrics

**Critical Path**: Corpus preparation -> Annotation guideline development -> Model training (5-fold CV) -> Performance evaluation

**Design Tradeoffs**: The study prioritized using minimal context (3-sentence window) over full-document analysis to balance annotation validity with practical constraints, potentially sacrificing some contextual information for scalability.

**Failure Signatures**: Models may underperform due to differences in data preprocessing, tokenization inconsistencies, or span enumeration approach generating different candidate spans than the original implementation.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- How do the three neural architectures differ in capturing linguistic features for different engagement categories, and which patterns does each model capture better?
- How does the minimal context approach (3-sentence window) affect annotation reliability compared to full-document analysis?
- What are the implications of collapsing Concur/Pronounce into PROCLAIM and Endorse/Attribute into ATTRIBUTION for the theoretical framework?

## Limitations
- The system is limited to a single discourse framework (Engagement from Appraisal analysis) and may not generalize to other rhetorical phenomena
- The custom-trained RoBERTa-base-academic3 model is not publicly available, and specific tokenization procedures are not fully documented
- The dataset composition heavily favors certain source corpora (BAWE and MICUSP), potentially introducing domain-specific biases

## Confidence
- **High confidence**: The core methodology for span identification using transformer-based architectures is sound and well-documented
- **Medium confidence**: Reported performance metrics are reliable given the 5-fold cross-validation approach
- **Low confidence**: Exact implementation details for data preprocessing, token-level alignment, and the dual-RoBERTa architecture combination mechanism are insufficient for perfect reproduction
