# Evaluating Post-hoc Interpretability with Intrinsic Interpretability

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2305.03002
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.03002
- Reference count: 11
- Primary result: SmoothGrad and Occlusion methods show statistically higher overlap with ProtoPNet attribution maps compared to Deconvolution and Lime

## Executive Summary
This paper proposes a novel approach to validate post-hoc interpretability methods by comparing them with intrinsic interpretability methods. The authors use ProtoPNet as ground-truth and compare its attribution maps with saliency maps from 8 different post-hoc methods using 10 saliency metrics. The validation is performed on a digital pathology dataset (PatchCamelyon), demonstrating that some post-hoc methods (SmoothGrad and Occlusion) show significantly better overlap with ProtoPNet's attribution maps than others.

## Method Summary
The study trains CNNs (ResNet18, ResNet152, DenseNet101) and ProtoPNets on the PatchCamelyon dataset, then extracts saliency maps using 8 post-hoc methods (Saliency, Deconvolution, GuidedBackprop, SmoothGrad, IntegratedGradients, Occlusion Sensitivity, SHAP, LIME) from the CNNs. ProtoPNet's intrinsic attribution maps are extracted and compared to the saliency maps using 10 adapted saliency metrics. Statistical analysis (Friedman rank test and Nemenyi test) is used to determine significant differences in overlap between methods.

## Key Results
- SmoothGrad and Occlusion methods show statistically significant higher overlap with ProtoPNet attribution maps
- Deconvolution and Lime methods show the least overlap with ProtoPNet attribution maps
- The study validates ProtoPNet as a suitable ground-truth for evaluating post-hoc interpretability methods

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: ProtoPNet's attribution maps can serve as ground truth for validating post-hoc saliency methods because they are inherently tied to the model's learned prototypes.
- Mechanism: ProtoPNet learns prototypes representing key concepts from training data and generates attribution maps highlighting regions similar to these prototypes during inference.
- Core assumption: Attribution maps accurately reflect the model's reasoning and can be considered faithful representations of important regions for classification.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "As intrinsic interpretability methods are considered to be more faithful to the underlying model's behavior Chen et al. (2019) as they do not require an external method after training, it can become a good ground-truth of what regions of the image the saliency maps should focus."
  - [section] "ProtoPNet Chen et al. (2019), a deep learning architecture based on a convolutional neural network, learns the optimal prototypes automatically and makes predictions based on the similarity of the instance to each prototype."

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: Comparing post-hoc saliency maps to ProtoPNet's attribution maps using saliency metrics provides a quantitative measure of the saliency method's faithfulness to the model.
- Mechanism: The study computes 10 different saliency metrics to compare overlap between saliency maps from post-hoc methods and attribution maps from ProtoPNet.
- Core assumption: The saliency metrics used are appropriate for comparing saliency maps and attribution maps, providing meaningful information about faithfulness.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "To evaluate the similarity between saliency map methods and attribution maps we adapted 10 saliency metrics from the saliency model literature"
  - [section] "The metrics used to evaluate saliency models were adapted for evaluating saliency maps and attribution maps Bylinskii et al. (2019)."

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: The experimental results show that some post-hoc saliency methods have higher overlap with ProtoPNet's attribution maps than others, indicating greater faithfulness.
- Mechanism: Statistical analysis (Friedman rank test) is used to determine significant differences in overlap between methods after computing saliency metrics.
- Core assumption: The experimental setup is sound and statistical analysis is appropriate for drawing conclusions about relative faithfulness.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "Overall, SmoothGrad and Occlusion were found to have a statistically bigger overlap with ProtoPNet while Deconvolution and Lime have been found to have the least."
  - [section] "Following the work of Denˇ sar Demsar (2006) with N = 10 (number of metrics) and k = 8 (number of saliency map methods), the 8 methods were compared among themselves for a 5% significance level using the two-tailed Nemenyi test Demsar (2006)."

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map:
  Data preprocessing (normalization, augmentation) -> Model training (CNNs, ProtoPNets) -> Saliency map extraction (8 post-hoc methods) -> Attribution map extraction (ProtoPNet) -> Saliency map evaluation (10 metrics) -> Statistical analysis (Friedman rank test, Nemenyi test)

- Critical path:
  1. Preprocess PatchCamelyon dataset
  2. Train CNNs and ProtoPNets on the dataset
  3. Extract saliency maps from CNNs using post-hoc methods
  4. Extract attribution maps from ProtoPNets
  5. Compute saliency metrics comparing saliency maps to attribution maps
  6. Perform statistical analysis to determine significant differences

- Design tradeoffs:
  - ProtoPNet vs. post-hoc methods: ProtoPNet is intrinsically interpretable but may have lower performance or less fine-grained explanations compared to post-hoc methods
  - Saliency metrics: Different metrics focus on different aspects of similarity/dissimilarity, and some may be more appropriate than others for comparing saliency maps to attribution maps

- Failure signatures:
  - Low performance of CNNs or ProtoPNets on the PatchCamelyon dataset
  - Inconsistent or unexpected saliency maps from post-hoc methods
  - Poor overlap between saliency maps and attribution maps across all metrics
  - Lack of significant differences in overlap between saliency methods

- First 3 experiments:
  1. Train a simple CNN (e.g., ResNet18) on a subset of the PatchCamelyon dataset and visualize its saliency maps using a few post-hoc methods to understand their behavior
  2. Implement a basic version of ProtoPNet with a small number of prototypes and visualize its attribution maps on the same subset of images to understand its behavior
  3. Compute a few saliency metrics (e.g., AUC, NSS) comparing the saliency maps from the CNN to the attribution maps from ProtoPNet on the subset of images to understand the initial overlap

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

- Question: Does the resolution of ProtoPNet's attribution maps significantly impact the overlap with post-hoc saliency methods?
  - Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper notes that ProtoPNet's attribution maps are "soft and smooth" due to small prototype dimensions chosen during hyperparameter optimization
  - Why unresolved: The paper suggests this is a trade-off between performance and fine-grained explanations, but doesn't quantify how much this affects overlap metrics
  - What evidence would resolve it: Experiments varying prototype dimensions and measuring resulting changes in overlap metrics with different saliency methods

- Question: Would using ProtoPNet with fine annotation (IAIA-BL) produce more similar attribution maps to post-hoc methods compared to the base ProtoPNet?
  - Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper mentions IAIA-BL as an extension that produces more fine-grained attribution maps using fine annotations, but states these were not available in their dataset
  - Why unresolved: The paper acknowledges this as future work but doesn't provide empirical comparison
  - What evidence would resolve it: Applying IAIA-BL to the same dataset and comparing its attribution maps with post-hoc methods using the same metrics

- Question: Do saliency maps from intermediate layers correlate better with ProtoPNet's attribution maps than those from the final layer?
  - Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper states "we focused on the saliency maps produced by the last fully-connected layer" and suggests this as future work
  - Why unresolved: The paper only analyzed final-layer saliency maps, leaving layer-wise analysis unexplored
  - What evidence would resolve it: Computing overlap metrics between intermediate-layer saliency maps and ProtoPNet attribution maps across multiple network depths

## Limitations
- Reliance on ProtoPNet's attribution maps as ground truth may be limited if prototypes don't fully capture relevant concepts
- Results may not generalize beyond the single medical imaging dataset (PatchCamelyon) used
- Potential resolution mismatch between ProtoPNet's attribution maps and post-hoc saliency maps could affect overlap metrics

## Confidence
- High confidence: Experimental methodology and statistical analysis are sound
- Medium confidence: ProtoPNet attribution maps can serve as reliable ground truth for validating post-hoc methods
- Medium confidence: Specific ranking of post-hoc methods based on their overlap with ProtoPNet attribution maps
