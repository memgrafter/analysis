# Towards Better Orthogonality Regularization with Disentangled Norm in Training Deep CNNs

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2306.09939
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.09939
- Reference count: 40
- Key outcome: Novel disentangled orthogonality regularization method surpasses previous approaches in near-orthogonality, with relaxation theory enabling enhanced performance in deep networks

## Executive Summary
This paper addresses the challenge of filter orthogonality in deep convolutional neural networks (CNNs) by proposing a novel disentangled orthogonality regularization method. The authors decompose the orthogonality constraint into separate diagonal (norm) and correlation components, allowing more efficient optimization toward strict orthogonality. They also identify that strict orthogonality becomes counterproductive as model depth increases due to transition dimension constraints, and propose a relaxation theory that maintains orthogonality only where it matters most. Extensive experiments on CIFAR-10 and CIFAR-100 demonstrate state-of-the-art performance gains while providing insights into the limitations of strict orthogonality in deep networks.

## Method Summary
The paper introduces a disentangled orthogonality regularization method that separates filter norm variance (diagonal loss) from correlation between filters (correlation loss), optimizing these components independently rather than using a single Frobenius norm term. The method also incorporates a relaxation theory where strict orthogonality is enforced only within an estimated transition dimension - the effective representation space that grows sublinearly with model capacity. This relaxation is applied layer-wise with a control ratio that determines which filter pairs remain strictly orthogonal. The approach is tested on ResNet and WideResNet architectures using SGD with Nesterov Momentum, with training schedules adapted for CIFAR-10 (160 epochs) and CIFAR-100 (200 epochs).

## Key Results
- Disentangled norm regularization achieves superior near-orthogonality compared to Frobenius and SRIP methods
- Strict orthogonality regularization shows diminishing returns as model depth increases
- Relaxed orthogonality regularization recovers performance gains in deeper networks
- State-of-the-art test accuracy improvements on CIFAR-10 and CIFAR-100 benchmarks

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map: Disentangled norm calculator -> Transition dimension estimator -> Relaxation ratio controller -> Regularization loss combiner
- Critical path: 1) Compute Gram matrix from filters 2) Separate diagonal and correlation components 3) Calculate respective losses 4) Estimate transition dimension 5) Apply relaxation to pairs outside transition dimension 6) Combine with task loss
- Design tradeoffs: Strict vs relaxed orthogonality (better near-orthogonality vs performance), computational cost (additional normalization steps), transition dimension estimation (fixed vs adaptive)
- Failure signatures: Performance degradation with depth, training instability with aggressive relaxation, over-regularization leading to underfitting
