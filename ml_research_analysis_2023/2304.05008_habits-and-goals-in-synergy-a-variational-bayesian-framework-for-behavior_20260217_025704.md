# Habits and goals in synergy: a variational Bayesian framework for behavior

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2304.05008
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2304.05008
- **Reference count:** 31
- **Primary result:** Novel framework unifies habitual and goal-directed behaviors using a Bayesian latent variable, enabling skill sharing and zero-shot generalization through predictive coding and active inference.

## Executive Summary
This paper introduces a variational Bayesian framework that unifies habitual and goal-directed behaviors by representing both as distributions of a shared latent variable "intention" z. Habitual behavior emerges from the prior distribution of z, while goal-directed behavior is generated from the posterior conditioned on the goal. The framework leverages predictive coding and active inference to enable zero-shot generalization from habitual to goal-directed behavior without additional training. Experiments on a T-maze navigation task demonstrate that the agent can acquire diverse and effective habitual behaviors while flexibly planning to achieve goals.

## Method Summary
The framework combines variational recurrent neural networks with reinforcement learning to model behavior as samples from a latent intention variable z. A 1-layer GRU processes z to predict observations and actions. For habitual behavior, z is sampled from a prior N(0,I), while for goal-directed behavior, z is inferred through active inference by minimizing free energy. The model is trained using SAC combined with a free energy loss that includes reconstruction, KL-divergence, and action entropy terms. The T-maze environment uses 360° RGB camera observations (16×64 pixels) and continuous 2D motor actions, with the agent learning to randomly escape left or right exits as habitual behavior before performing zero-shot goal-directed planning.

## Key Results
- Agent successfully acquires diverse habitual behaviors, randomly escaping left or right exits in the T-maze
- Zero-shot goal-directed planning achieves desired goals without additional training after habitual behavior acquisition
- Stochasticity in the latent variable z, properly regularized by KL-divergence, enables diverse yet effective motor actions
- Predictive coding with compact latent representation enables efficient active inference for goal-directed planning

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- **Claim:** Introducing a Bayesian latent variable ("intention") enables skill sharing between habitual and goal-directed behaviors by unifying their representation.
- **Mechanism:** Both behaviors are modeled as different distributions of the same latent variable z. Habitual behavior uses the prior distribution (unconditioned), while goal-directed behavior uses the posterior distribution (conditioned on the goal). The shared policy network maps z to actions, enabling reuse of learned motor skills.
- **Core assumption:** The latent intention z captures sufficient information to generate both habitual and goal-directed actions through the same neural pathways.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract] "We incorporate both behaviors in one framework by introducing a Bayesian latent variable called 'intention'."
  - [section 3.1] "The main RNN is a 1-layer gated recurrent unit (GRU) which, at step t, takes z_t as input and predicts the current and subsequent observation."
  - [corpus] Weak - related papers discuss habit transfer but don't address the Bayesian unification mechanism specifically.
- **Break condition:** If z cannot encode sufficient behavioral information, or if the policy network cannot generalize across both distributions, the skill sharing will fail.

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim:** Stochasticity in the latent variable z enables diverse habitual behaviors while maintaining effectiveness through proper regularization.
- **Mechanism:** The prior distribution of z is stochastic (diagonal unit Gaussian), allowing exploration of different behavioral options. The KL-divergence term in the free energy loss regularizes this stochasticity, preventing excessive randomness while maintaining diversity.
- **Core assumption:** The KL-divergence term appropriately balances exploration and exploitation, allowing the agent to discover diverse yet effective behaviors.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [section 3.2.2] "The KL-divergence term DKL[q(zt|ˆx1:t+1)||p(zt)] provides a theoretical foundation to link those two behaviors by bounding their difference."
  - [section 4.2] "It can be seen that a branching of the hq_t representation occurs after the first several steps. This branching is introduced by the randomness of the 2-dimensional latent variable z."
  - [corpus] Weak - related papers discuss stochasticity in behavior but don't address the specific regularization mechanism.
- **Break condition:** If the KL-divergence weighting is too low, behaviors become too random; if too high, diversity is lost.

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim:** Predictive coding with a compact latent representation enables zero-shot generalization from habitual to goal-directed behavior.
- **Mechanism:** The agent learns an internal model that predicts future observations given z. For goal-directed behavior, active inference infers the z values that minimize the free energy with respect to the goal. The compact representation of z makes this search tractable.
- **Core assumption:** The learned predictive model accurately captures the relationship between z and future observations, enabling effective goal-directed planning.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract] "by leveraging the idea of predictive coding, it enables an agent to seamlessly generalize from habitual to goal-directed behavior without requiring additional training."
  - [section 3.3] "We fixed the model weights and bias while treat ((μ_AIf_t, σ_AIf_t), (μ_AIf_t+1, σ_AIf_t+1), · · ·) as trainable variables, and optimize them to minimize the variational free energy loss function w.r.t. the goal."
  - [corpus] Weak - related papers discuss predictive coding but don't address the specific zero-shot generalization mechanism.
- **Break condition:** If the predictive model is inaccurate, or if z cannot represent the necessary information for goal-directed behavior, the generalization will fail.

## Foundational Learning

- **Variational Bayesian Methods**
  - Why needed here: Provides the theoretical foundation for modeling habitual and goal-directed behaviors as different distributions of the same latent variable.
  - Quick check question: How does the KL-divergence term in the variational free energy loss function enable skill sharing between habitual and goal-directed behaviors?

- **Predictive Coding**
  - Why needed here: Enables the agent to learn an internal model that predicts future observations, which is essential for goal-directed planning.
  - Quick check question: How does the compact representation of the latent variable z enable efficient active inference for goal-directed behavior?

- **Reinforcement Learning**
  - Why needed here: Provides the framework for learning effective habitual behaviors through trial-and-error interaction with the environment.
  - Quick check question: How does the policy network in this framework differ from traditional RL policy networks in terms of its input?

## Architecture Onboarding

- **Component map:** CNN encoder → GRU with z → Policy network → Actions → Environment → CNN decoder → Free energy loss
- **Critical path:** z generation → RNN state update → action selection → environment interaction → observation prediction → loss computation
- **Design tradeoffs:**
  - Stochastic vs. deterministic z: Stochasticity enables diversity but requires regularization
  - Prior vs. posterior z: Prior enables habitual behavior, posterior enables goal-directed behavior
  - Compact vs. detailed representation: Compact representation enables efficient planning but may lose information
- **Failure signatures:**
  - Agent gets stuck in repetitive behaviors: KL-divergence term too high
  - Agent fails to reach goals: Predictive model inaccurate or z representation insufficient
  - Training instability: Learning rate or loss function weighting incorrect
- **First 3 experiments:**
  1. Train agent with different KL-divergence weightings to find optimal balance between diversity and effectiveness
  2. Test agent's ability to generalize to new goals not seen during training
  3. Compare performance with and without the predictive coding component

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

- **Open Question 1:** How does the stochasticity in the latent variable z specifically contribute to the emergence of diverse habitual behaviors, and what is the optimal level of stochasticity for balancing exploration and exploitation?
- **Open Question 2:** How does the framework handle situations where the goal is not reachable by the agent's habitual behavior, and what mechanisms are in place to enable the agent to discover and learn new motor skills to achieve such goals?
- **Open Question 3:** How does the framework perform in more complex environments with higher-dimensional observations and a larger number of possible actions, and what are the computational challenges associated with scaling up the framework?

## Limitations
- The framework's performance depends critically on hyperparameter choices (βx, βz, βa) which are not systematically explored
- KL-divergence regularization's effectiveness in balancing diversity and efficiency requires further validation across different task complexities
- The assumption that a single latent variable z can capture all necessary information for both habitual and goal-directed behaviors remains theoretical without extensive ablation studies

## Confidence
- Mechanism 1 (skill sharing via Bayesian latent variable): Medium - supported by experimental results but lacks theoretical guarantees
- Mechanism 2 (stochasticity enabling diversity): Medium - demonstrated in experiments but regularization effects not fully characterized
- Mechanism 3 (zero-shot generalization): Medium - shows promise in controlled experiments but scalability to complex environments untested
