# Parity Calibration

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2305.18655
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.18655
- Reference count: 27
- Key outcome: Parity calibration improves decision-making in sequential regression by calibrating whether next observation will increase or decrease

## Executive Summary
This paper introduces parity calibration for sequential regression problems, addressing the need to forecast whether the next observation will increase or decrease compared to the current one. The authors demonstrate that parity calibration can be expressed as binary calibration and propose using online versions of Platt scaling (MW, IW, and OPS) to achieve it. Through three real-world case studies—COVID-19 forecasting, weather prediction, and nuclear fusion control—the proposed methods significantly improve parity calibration and sharpness over the expert forecaster, with OPS often performing best. The results demonstrate the practical value of parity calibration in decision-making contexts where calibrated probability forecasts are essential.

## Method Summary
The paper addresses parity calibration in sequential regression by first extracting parity probabilities from base distributional forecasts, then applying online calibration methods to improve these probabilities. The core approach transforms the regression problem into a binary classification problem where parity calibration reduces to binary calibration. The authors propose three online variants of Platt scaling: Increasing Window (IW), Moving Window (MW), and Online Platt Scaling (OPS). These methods update calibration parameters as new data arrives, handling the non-stationarity typical of real-world sequential data. The calibrated parity probabilities are then used to make better-informed decisions in domains where knowing the direction of change is crucial.

## Key Results
- OPS consistently achieves the best parity calibration error (PCE) across all three case studies
- Parity calibration significantly improves sharpness metrics while maintaining or improving accuracy
- All online calibration methods (MW, IW, OPS) outperform the baseline expert forecaster in parity calibration
- The methods work across diverse domains: epidemiology, meteorology, and plasma physics

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- **Claim:** Parity calibration is mathematically equivalent to binary calibration when forecasting whether the next observation will increase or decrease compared to the current one.
- **Mechanism:** The paper defines parity calibration for sequential regression problems and shows it reduces to binary calibration by transforming the regression problem into a binary classification problem using the parity outcome eyt = 1 {yt ≤ yt−1}.
- **Core assumption:** The sequential nature of the data can be leveraged to transform a regression problem into a binary classification problem without loss of information.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract] "Parity calibration can be expressed as binary calibration"
  - [section] "Define the parity outcomes as eyt := 1 {yt ≤ yt−1}, and observe that the parity calibration condition (Eq. (2)) is equivalently written as..."
- **Break condition:** If the sequential structure is broken or if the transformation loses information about the original regression problem.

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim:** Online Platt scaling can effectively calibrate parity probabilities in non-stationary sequential data.
- **Mechanism:** The paper proposes using online versions of Platt scaling (Increasing Window, Moving Window, and Online Platt Scaling) to achieve parity calibration by updating calibration parameters as new data arrives.
- **Core assumption:** The data stream exhibits non-stationarity that requires online updating of calibration parameters.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract] "we use an online binary calibration method to achieve parity calibration"
  - [section] "We propose using PS to perform this modification... A natural possibility would be to use an initial part of the test-stream to learn fixed PS parameters once, as described in the previous subsection. However, real-world regression sequences (weather, stocks, etc) have non-stationary shifting behavior across time."
- **Break condition:** If the data stream becomes stationary or if the computational cost of online updating becomes prohibitive.

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim:** Quantile calibration of regression forecasts does not imply parity calibration of derived parity probabilities.
- **Mechanism:** The paper provides synthetic and real-world examples showing that even when a regression forecast is quantile calibrated, the derived parity probabilities can be severely miscalibrated.
- **Core assumption:** Calibration in traditional regression (quantile or distributional) focuses on different aspects than parity calibration.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract] "we show that extracting parity probabilities from a quantile-calibrated regression model can lead to severe miscalibration"
  - [section] "Since calibration is regarded as a desirable quality of distributional forecasts, one may wonder whether a calibrated ˆFt is sufficient for parity calibration... We show that this is not the case with the following examples."
- **Break condition:** If the parity probabilities are derived differently or if additional constraints are imposed on the regression forecast.

## Limitations
- The paper lacks theoretical guarantees for the convergence or optimality of online calibration methods in non-stationary settings
- Performance depends heavily on hyperparameters (uf, ws, γ, D) with limited guidance on selection
- Computational cost may be prohibitive for high-frequency sequential data or real-time applications
