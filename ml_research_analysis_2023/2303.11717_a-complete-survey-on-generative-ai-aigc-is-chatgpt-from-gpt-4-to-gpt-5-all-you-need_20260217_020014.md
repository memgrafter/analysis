# A Complete Survey on Generative AI (AIGC): Is ChatGPT from GPT-4 to GPT-5 All You Need?

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2303.11717
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.11717
- Reference count: 40
- One-line primary result: Comprehensive survey of generative AI covering fundamental techniques, core tasks, and industry applications

## Executive Summary
This survey provides a comprehensive overview of generative AI (AIGC), covering fundamental techniques, core tasks, and industry applications. The paper addresses the growing popularity of AIGC tools like ChatGPT and DALL-E, highlighting the need for a thorough understanding of this rapidly evolving field. Key techniques include transformer architectures, self-supervised pretraining, and generative modeling methods like GANs and diffusion models. The survey categorizes AIGC tasks based on output types, such as text generation (chatbots, machine translation), image generation (restoration, editing, text-to-image), and other modalities like video, 3D content, and speech. Applications span various industries, including education, gaming, media, advertising, and entertainment. The paper also discusses challenges and future outlooks, emphasizing the need for more flexible control, improved fine-tuning, and broader accessibility beyond big tech companies.

## Method Summary
The survey is structured around AIGC tasks, organized by output type (text, image, video, 3D, speech, graph). Each task section covers fundamental techniques, recent advancements, and applications. The authors conducted a comprehensive literature review and synthesis of existing research on generative AI and AIGC, categorizing and organizing the surveyed information based on output types and industry applications. The survey analyzes challenges and future outlooks for generative AI, discussing its potential evolution and societal impact.

## Key Results
- AIGC tasks are categorized by output type (text, image, video, 3D content, speech, graph) with coverage of fundamental techniques and applications
- Transformer architectures, self-supervised pretraining, and generative modeling methods (GANs, diffusion models) are identified as key techniques
- Applications span multiple industries including education, gaming, media, advertising, and entertainment

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: Generative AI's popularity stems from its ability to create high-dimensional content (text, images, etc.) beyond traditional analytical AI's label generation.
- Mechanism: The transition from Web 2.0's User Generated Content (UGC) to Web 3.0's AI-Generated Content (AIGC) addresses the trade-off between volume and quality by leveraging AI's generation power.
- Core assumption: AIGC can generate content at scale with acceptable quality, blurring the line between content consumers and creators.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract]: "In the era of AI transitioning from pure analysis to creation, it is worth noting that ChatGPT... is just a tool out of numerous AIGC tasks."
  - [section]: "The shift towards AIGC has the potential to greatly transform the way data is consumed and produced, giving individuals and organizations more control and flexibility in the content they create and consume."
- Break condition: If AIGC-generated content quality consistently falls below acceptable thresholds for practical applications, or if ethical/legal concerns severely limit adoption.

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: The recent surge in AIGC's popularity is driven by a confluence of factors: advanced models, abundant data, and powerful computing resources.
- Mechanism: Large language models like GPT-3/4 are trained on massive datasets (e.g., CommonCrawl, 570GB after filtering) using powerful GPUs/TPUs, enabling unprecedented generation capabilities.
- Core assumption: The scale of data and compute directly correlates with the quality and diversity of generated content.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [section]: "ChatGPT was built on top of GPT3 [40] partly trained on CommonCrawl dataset, which has 45TB of compressed plaintext before filtering and 570GB after filtering."
  - [section]: "The computing capability of its CUDA has improved from the first CUDA-capable GPU (GeForce 8800) in 2006 to the recent GPU (Hopper) with hundreds of times more computing power."
- Break condition: If data scaling laws plateau or computing costs become prohibitive, limiting further model improvements.

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: The effectiveness of AIGC models relies on self-supervised pretraining to learn rich representations from unlabeled data, followed by fine-tuning for specific tasks.
- Mechanism: Models like BERT (masking) and GPT (autoregressive) pretrain on large corpora, then adapt to downstream tasks like chatbots or image captioning with additional training.
- Core assumption: The learned representations from pretraining generalize well to diverse downstream tasks with minimal task-specific data.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [section]: "BERT [87] (see Figure 7) simplifies it to an end-to-end denoising framework by predicting the masked patches from the unmasked patches."
  - [section]: "GPT-family models [40, 338, 339] also show competitive performance [Without the need for fine-tuning]."
- Break condition: If pretraining becomes too computationally expensive relative to task-specific training, or if fine-tuning fails to adapt models effectively to niche domains.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- Question: Can GPT-5 (or future GPT variants) unify all AIGC tasks for diversified content creation?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper's title and abstract directly pose this question, noting that "Impressed by the capability of ChatGPT, many people are wondering about its limits: can GPT-5 (or other future GPT variants) help ChatGPT unify all AIGC tasks for diversified content creation?"
- Why unresolved: The paper acknowledges this as an open question but does not provide a definitive answer. It suggests that a comprehensive review of existing AIGC tasks is needed to address this question.
- What evidence would resolve it: Empirical studies comparing the performance of GPT-5 (or future variants) across multiple AIGC tasks, demonstrating its ability to handle diverse content creation tasks with comparable or superior performance to specialized models.

### Open Question 2

- Why unresolved: The paper acknowledges this as a challenge but does not provide specific solutions or methods to improve interpretability.
- What evidence would resolve it: Development and validation of interpretability techniques specifically designed for AIGC models, demonstrating their ability to explain model decisions and outputs.

### Open Question 3
- Question: How can AIGC be applied to more specialized domains beyond the ones mentioned in the paper?

- What evidence would resolve it: Case studies or pilot projects demonstrating successful AIGC implementation in specialized domains, along with detailed analysis of challenges and solutions encountered.
