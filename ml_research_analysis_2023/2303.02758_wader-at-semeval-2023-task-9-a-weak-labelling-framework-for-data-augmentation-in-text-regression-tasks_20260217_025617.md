# WADER at SemEval-2023 Task 9: A Weak-labelling framework for Data augmentation in tExt Regression Tasks

## Executive Summary
This paper introduces WADER, a weak-labeling strategy for data augmentation in text regression tasks, specifically addressing data imbalance and scarcity issues. The method employs distribution-based sampling to select underrepresented labels, translates and back-translates text for augmentation, and validates labels using a baseline model. Experiments on the SemEval 2023 Task 9 multilingual intimacy detection dataset show that WADER improves performance over baselines, ranking 32nd overall and outperforming in several languages. XLM-RoBERTa models generally outperformed XLNET in multilingual settings. Moderate difference thresholds in label validation yielded better results, likely due to regularization and increased diversity.

## Method Summary
WADER addresses data scarcity in text regression tasks through a weak-labeling framework that combines distribution-based sampling, translation-based augmentation, and label validation. The method first analyzes label distribution to identify underrepresented labels, then translates and back-translates text to generate augmented data while preserving semantic meaning. A baseline model validates augmented labels by checking that new predictions fall within a specified threshold of the original labels. The approach balances augmentation quality with diversity through tunable parameters controlling label difference thresholds and sampling strategies.

## Key Results
- WADER achieved 32nd place in SemEval 2023 Task 9 multilingual intimacy detection
- XLM-RoBERTa models outperformed XLNET in multilingual settings
- Moderate label difference thresholds (0.2-0.3) yielded optimal results

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
WADER's effectiveness stems from addressing the fundamental challenge of data imbalance in text regression tasks through strategic augmentation. The distribution-based sampling ensures that underrepresented labels receive adequate representation in the training data, preventing model bias toward majority classes. Translation-based augmentation preserves semantic content while introducing linguistic diversity, helping models generalize across different language expressions of similar concepts. The label validation mechanism using baseline models ensures that augmented data maintains reasonable label quality without being identical to original samples, providing regularization benefits. The moderate difference thresholds create a sweet spot between preserving original label meaning and introducing enough variation to prevent overfitting.

## Limitations
- Translation quality variability across languages can introduce noise in augmented data
- Potential overfitting when using augmented data during training, though partially mitigated by label validation
- Method's sensitivity to specific vocabulary and context-dependent terms limits generalizability across domains

## Confidence
High confidence:
- WADER improves performance on the specific SemEval Task 9 dataset compared to baselines
- XLM-RoBERTa outperforms XLNET in multilingual settings
- Moderate difference thresholds yield better results

Medium confidence:
- The approach generalizes to other text regression tasks
- Translation-based augmentation remains effective across diverse language pairs
- The framework handles context-dependent vocabulary well

Low confidence:
- WADER maintains effectiveness with significantly different data distributions
- The method scales efficiently to very large datasets
- Weak-labeling quality remains consistent across all languages in the dataset
