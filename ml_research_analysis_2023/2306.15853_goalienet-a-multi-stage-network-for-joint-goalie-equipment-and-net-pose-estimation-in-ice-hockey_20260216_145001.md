# GoalieNet: A Multi-Stage Network for Joint Goalie, Equipment, and Net Pose Estimation in Ice Hockey

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2306.15853
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.15853
- Authors: 
- Reference count: 9
- Primary result: 84% average accuracy across 29 goalie, equipment, and net keypoints

## Executive Summary
This paper presents GoalieNet, a multi-stage deep neural network designed to jointly estimate the pose of ice hockey goalies, their equipment, and the net. The challenging nature of this task stems from concealed joints under padding, equipment keypoint detection, and net tracking. GoalieNet leverages cross-stage aggregation between adjacent stages and varying kernel sizes to improve localization accuracy. Trained on 29 keypoints covering the goalie, equipment, and net simultaneously, the network exploits spatial relationships between these entities to achieve promising results on NHL benchmark data.

## Method Summary
GoalieNet is a multi-stage deep neural network that jointly estimates the pose of ice hockey goalies, their equipment, and the net. The network uses cross-stage aggregation between adjacent stages and different kernel sizes at various levels to improve localization accuracy. It is trained on 29 keypoints covering the goalie, equipment, and net simultaneously to exploit spatial relationships between these entities. The network outputs heatmaps for each keypoint, allowing for probabilistic detection and handling of occlusion. Evaluation is performed using Euclidean distance between predicted and actual keypoint positions, normalized by bounding box diameter.

## Key Results
- Achieved 84% average accuracy across all 29 keypoints
- 22 out of 29 keypoints detected with more than 80% accuracy
- Stick-related keypoints (22, 23, 24) showed lower accuracy (~50%) due to narrow shape and rapid movement
- Net keypoints (25-29) achieved high accuracy due to relative motionlessness

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: Joint estimation of goalie, equipment, and net improves accuracy by exploiting spatial relationships between keypoints
- Mechanism: The network is trained on all 29 keypoints simultaneously, allowing it to learn the spatial relationships and dependencies between the goalie's body parts, equipment, and net positions. This joint learning approach helps the network make more accurate predictions by considering the contextual information from related keypoints.
- Core assumption: The spatial relationships between goalie, equipment, and net keypoints are consistent enough across different game situations to be learned and exploited by the network
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract]: "We train GoalieNet on all 29 keypoints across the goalie, equipment, and net in a joint fashion, as in practice, the goalie, the equipment, and the net have a strong spatial relationship with each other"
  - [section]: "Experimental results using NHL benchmark data demonstrate that the proposed GoalieNet can achieve an average of 84% accuracy across all keypoints"
- Break condition: If the spatial relationships between keypoints vary significantly across different game situations or if occlusion patterns disrupt these relationships, the joint estimation approach may not provide the expected accuracy benefits.

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: Multi-stage architecture with cross-stage aggregation improves localization accuracy for keypoints
- Mechanism: The GoalieNet architecture uses multiple stages where information is aggregated between adjacent stages. This allows the network to progressively refine its predictions by combining information from different stages, leading to more accurate keypoint localization.
- Core assumption: Information from adjacent stages can be meaningfully combined to improve keypoint localization without introducing noise or conflicting information
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract]: "GoalieNet, a multi-stage deep neural network for jointly estimating the pose of the goalie, their equipment, and the net"
  - [section]: "the cross-stage aggregation is leveraged between adjacent stages, and different kernel sizes are used in various levels to improve localization accuracy"
- Break condition: If the aggregation between stages introduces conflicting information or if the progressive refinement doesn't actually improve localization, the multi-stage approach may not provide benefits and could even degrade performance.

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: Different kernel sizes at various levels improve localization accuracy for keypoints at different scales
- Mechanism: The network uses different kernel sizes in different levels to handle keypoints of varying sizes and scales. Larger kernels can capture broader context for larger objects like the net, while smaller kernels can focus on finer details for smaller, closely-spaced keypoints like those on leg pads.
- Core assumption: Keypoints on different body parts and equipment have sufficiently different scales and contexts that they benefit from different kernel sizes
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract]: "different kernel sizes are used in various levels to improve localization accuracy"
  - [section]: "keypoints on the leg pads are too close together and may require smaller kernels for finer localization"
- Break condition: If the variation in keypoint scale and context is not significant enough to warrant different kernel sizes, or if the fixed kernel sizes don't match the actual requirements of the keypoints, this approach may not improve accuracy.

- Why unresolved: The paper does not provide a direct comparison between the joint estimation approach and separate estimation approaches in terms of accuracy and computational efficiency.
- What evidence would resolve it: A comparative study between joint and separate estimation approaches, evaluating both accuracy and computational efficiency, would provide insights into the benefits and drawbacks of each method.

## Limitations

- Limited evaluation on NHL data with specific camera angles and lighting conditions, raising questions about generalizability
- Low accuracy on stick-related keypoints due to narrow shape and rapid movement
- Implementation details for cross-stage aggregation and kernel size variation are not fully specified
