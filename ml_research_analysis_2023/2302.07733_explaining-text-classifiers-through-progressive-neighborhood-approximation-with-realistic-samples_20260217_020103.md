# Explaining text classifiers through progressive neighborhood approximation with realistic samples

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID**: 2302.07733
- **Source URL**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2302.07733
- **Reference count**: 40
- **Primary result**: XPROA achieves higher fidelity and R2 scores than competitors through two-stage interpolation with counterfactuals

## Executive Summary
This paper introduces XPROA, a local model-agnostic explainer for text classifiers that progressively refines neighborhoods through two-staged interpolation using a generative autoencoder. The method improves semantic and syntactic correctness of neighboring texts by leveraging a generative model with reconstruction ability and locality-preserving property. An alternative method, XPROB, constructs neighborhoods transparently through probability-based editions on prototypes. Experiments on Yelp and Amazon review datasets demonstrate the effectiveness of both methods in terms of correctness, completeness, and compactness of explanations.

## Method Summary
XPROA constructs neighborhoods through two-stage interpolation: first between counterfactual landmarks to explore local space broadly, then between these intermediates and the query point to refine the boundary. The method relies on a generative autoencoder with reconstruction ability and locality-preserving property to ensure semantic coherence. XPROB provides a transparent alternative using probability-based edition on top-k counterfactual prototypes, iteratively editing query words using local n-gram context probabilities. Both methods train a linear surrogate model on the constructed neighborhoods and extract feature attributions as explanations.

## Key Results
- XPROA achieves higher fidelity and R2 scores than competing methods on both datasets
- Both XPROA and XPROB demonstrate strong completeness (confidence drop) and compactness (AOPC) metrics
- XPROB provides stable and interpretable explanations while avoiding reliance on opaque generative models
- Sensitivity analysis shows importance of generator choice and potential risks of opaque models

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- **Claim**: Two-stage interpolation using counterfactuals as landmarks produces more focused and diverse neighborhoods
- **Mechanism**: First stage interpolates between landmark counterfactuals to explore local space broadly; second stage interpolates between these intermediate points and the query point to refine the boundary
- **Core assumption**: The latent space learned by the generative autoencoder preserves locality, so interpolated points remain semantically close
- **Evidence anchors**: [abstract]: "two-stage interpolation using counterfactuals as landmarks"; [section]: "iterative process... closer counterfactuals to x are discovered and exploited"
- **Break condition**: If the generative model fails to preserve locality, interpolated points may drift far from the decision boundary, causing the neighborhood to lose focus

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim**: Reconstruction ability and locality-preserving property are essential for a generative model used in local explanations
- **Mechanism**: The reconstruction ability ensures the model can accurately represent the query point in latent space; the locality-preserving property ensures that nearby latent points correspond to semantically similar texts
- **Core assumption**: Without both properties, the generated neighborhood will not faithfully reflect the local decision boundary
- **Evidence anchors**: [abstract]: "reconstruction ability and locality-preserving property"; [section]: "essential assumption of generators"
- **Break condition**: If either property is violated, the neighborhood construction will fail to represent local behavior accurately

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim**: Probability-based edition on prototypes achieves competitive explanations without relying on an opaque generative model
- **Mechanism**: Selects top-k counterfactual prototypes, then iteratively edits each word of the query into the prototypes using local n-gram context probabilities
- **Core assumption**: Local context in the prototype corpus is sufficient to guide meaningful edits toward the query text
- **Evidence anchors**: [abstract]: "probability-based editions as an alternative to the generator-based solution"; [section]: "probability-based edition finds the best fit for w∗ under the context of the prototype"
- **Break condition**: If the prototype corpus is too small or unrepresentative, edits may produce incoherent or irrelevant texts

## Foundational Learning

- **Concept**: Latent space interpolation for text generation
  - **Why needed here**: Enables smooth manipulation of text semantics without directly editing raw tokens
  - **Quick check question**: If you interpolate between two unrelated latent vectors, what is the expected semantic outcome?

- **Concept**: Feature attribution in linear surrogate models
  - **Why needed here**: Provides interpretable importance scores for words based on the surrogate's weights
  - **Quick check question**: How does the weighting scheme in the surrogate loss function influence which features are deemed important?

- **Concept**: Counterfactual reasoning in local explanations
  - **Why needed here**: Identifies minimal changes needed to flip the prediction, helping to understand the decision boundary
  - **Quick check question**: What distinguishes a factual from a counterfactual in the context of the proposed methods?

## Architecture Onboarding

- **Component map**: Query text → Encoder → Latent vector → Interpolation → Decoder → Synthetic texts → Surrogate training → Explanation extraction; Alternative path: Query text → Prototype selection → Probability-based editing → Synthetic texts → Surrogate training → Explanation extraction

- **Critical path**: For XPROA: Query → Latent encoding → Landmark initialization → Iterative interpolation → Neighbor finalization → Surrogate training → Attribution extraction; For XPROB: Query → Prototype selection → Iterative editing → Neighbor finalization → Surrogate training → Attribution extraction

- **Design tradeoffs**: Using a generative model (XPROA) offers more realistic samples but adds opacity and computational overhead; using probability-based edition (XPROB) is transparent but may generate less semantically coherent texts if the prototype corpus is limited

- **Failure signatures**: Poor reconstruction loss → Inaccurate neighbor focus; Collapsed neighborhoods (identical instances) → Surrogate overfits, attributions unreliable; Large deviations in stability tests → Explanations not robust to small input changes

- **First 3 experiments**: 1) Run both XPROA and XPROB on a simple binary sentiment dataset; compare fidelity scores of surrogates; 2) Perform ablation: remove the second interpolation stage in XPROA; observe change in neighborhood diversity; 3) Test stability: generate 5 similar variants of a query; measure attribution variance across methods

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- **Question**: How do different generative model architectures (e.g., VAE vs. DAAE) specifically impact the quality and reliability of local explanations for text classifiers?
- **Basis in paper**: [explicit] The paper compares DAAE and VAE generative models, showing that DAAE generally performs better due to its reconstruction ability and locality-preserving property, but also notes the potential risks of relying on opaque models
- **Why unresolved**: While the paper demonstrates the performance differences between DAAE and VAE, it doesn't delve into the specific architectural features that contribute to these differences or explore other generative model architectures
- **What evidence would resolve it**: A comprehensive study comparing various generative model architectures (e.g., VAE, DAAE, GAN-based models) on their ability to generate high-quality, reliable explanations for text classifiers, considering factors like reconstruction loss, latent space geometry, and explanation stability

### Open Question 2
- **Question**: How can we develop more transparent and interpretable generative models for use in explainable AI, while maintaining their ability to generate realistic and diverse samples for local explanations?
- **Basis in paper**: [explicit] The paper highlights the opacity of generative models as a concern for explainable AI, and proposes XPROB as an alternative method that constructs neighborhoods transparently through probability-based editions
- **Why unresolved**: The paper doesn't provide a detailed analysis of how to make generative models more transparent or explore alternative approaches that balance interpretability with generation quality
- **What evidence would resolve it**: Research into methods for interpreting the internal workings of generative models, such as visualizing latent space representations or analyzing feature attributions, as well as the development of new generative model architectures designed with interpretability in mind

### Open Question 3
- **Question**: How can we evaluate the effectiveness of local explanation methods for text classifiers in a more comprehensive and standardized way, considering both quantitative and qualitative aspects?
- **Basis in paper**: [explicit] The paper evaluates explanation methods using metrics like fidelity, R2 score, confidence drop, and AOPC, but acknowledges the limitations of these metrics and the lack of ground truth for explanations
- **Why unresolved**: The paper doesn't propose a unified framework for evaluating explanation methods that considers both quantitative and qualitative aspects, and it doesn't address the challenge of establishing ground truth for explanations
- **What evidence would resolve it**: The development of a comprehensive evaluation framework that combines quantitative metrics with human-grounded evaluations, as well as the creation of benchmark datasets with ground truth explanations for text classification tasks

## Limitations
- Reliance on generative autoencoders introduces opacity into the explanation pipeline, potentially undermining trust in the explanations themselves
- Strong performance demonstrated only on binary sentiment tasks; validation on more complex multi-class or nuanced classification problems is lacking
- The effectiveness of probability-based edition depends heavily on the quality and representativeness of the prototype corpus

## Confidence

- **Mechanism 1 (Two-stage interpolation)**: High - well-supported by experimental results and ablation studies
- **Mechanism 2 (Reconstruction and locality properties)**: Medium - theoretically sound but relies heavily on the choice of generative model
- **Mechanism 3 (Probability-based edition)**: Medium-Low - simpler approach but limited evaluation of prototype corpus quality and edge cases
