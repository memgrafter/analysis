# Learning from Visual Observation via Offline Pretrained State-to-Go Transformer

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2306.12860
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.12860
- Authors: 
- Reference count: 40
- One-line primary result: Achieves competitive performance on Atari and Minecraft tasks using only video demonstrations without environmental rewards

## Executive Summary
This paper introduces a two-stage framework for learning from visual observation (LfVO) in Atari and Minecraft environments. The method first pretrains a State-to-Go (STG) Transformer offline using expert demonstrations to predict and differentiate latent transitions, then uses the resulting model to provide intrinsic rewards for downstream reinforcement learning tasks. The approach significantly outperforms baselines in sample efficiency and overall performance, achieving results comparable to policies trained with environmental rewards in some games. This demonstrates the potential of utilizing video-only data for reinforcement learning without requiring online data collection or reward signals.

## Method Summary
The proposed framework consists of two stages: offline pretraining and online reinforcement learning. During pretraining, a State-to-Go (STG) Transformer is trained to predict latent transitions using expert demonstrations. The model includes a feature encoder (CNNs + MLP), a GPT-style STG Transformer with causal attention, a Wasserstein GAN discriminator, and a temporal distance regressor (TDR). The STG Transformer predicts embedding changes rather than absolute embeddings, using residual prediction to focus on transition dynamics. After pretraining, the discriminator provides intrinsic rewards for downstream reinforcement learning tasks where an agent learns solely from these rewards without environmental rewards. The approach is evaluated on Atari and Minecraft tasks, demonstrating significant improvements in sample efficiency and performance.

## Key Results
- STG Transformer achieves 2-10x sample efficiency improvement over baselines across multiple Atari and Minecraft tasks
- In some games, STG achieves performance comparable to policies trained with environmental rewards
- Ablation studies confirm the importance of TDR for temporal alignment and residual prediction for transition modeling

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: The STG Transformer offline pretraining stage provides a robust transition discriminator that generalizes to unseen agent behaviors.
- Mechanism: By training the discriminator offline using Wasserstein distance, the model learns to differentiate expert transitions from non-expert transitions without requiring online data collection.
- Core assumption: The offline expert dataset captures sufficient transition diversity to enable the discriminator to generalize.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "the STG Transformer provides intrinsic rewards for downstream reinforcement learning tasks where an agent learns merely from intrinsic rewards without environmental rewards"
  - [section 3.3] "the WGAN discriminator can clearly distinguish between the state sequences collected under the learning policy and the expert state sequences, without fine-tuning"
  - [corpus] Weak evidence; related work focuses on online adversarial methods, not offline pretraining.
- Break condition: If the offline dataset is too narrow or biased, the discriminator may fail to generalize to novel agent trajectories.

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: The temporal distance regressor (TDR) ensures temporally-aligned embeddings that improve transition prediction quality.
- Mechanism: TDR predicts symlog temporal distances between embeddings, forcing the feature encoder to produce embeddings where temporal proximity is reflected in embedding proximity.
- Core assumption: Temporally-aligned embeddings are necessary for accurate transition prediction in visual observation settings.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [section 3.2] "TDR is responsible for updating the Eξ and Pϕ as an auxiliary component"
  - [section 4.3] "the embeddings learned by STG exhibit remarkable continuity, in stark contrast to the scattered and disjoint embeddings produced by STG-"
  - [corpus] Weak evidence; most related work focuses on contrastive learning or temporal prediction without explicit temporal alignment modules.
- Break condition: If visual observations have non-monotonic temporal relationships (e.g., due to respawns), temporal alignment may not improve prediction.

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: The residual prediction approach (predicting embedding changes rather than absolute embeddings) improves transition prediction quality.
- Mechanism: By predicting the difference between consecutive embeddings and adding it to the current embedding, the model can focus on the transition dynamics rather than absolute positioning in embedding space.
- Core assumption: Transition dynamics are easier to predict than absolute state representations in visual observation settings.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [section 3.2] "we predict the embedding change and combine it with token embeddings in a residual way, which is commonly applied in transition prediction"
  - [section 4.3] Visual comparison shows STG embeddings are more continuous than STG- embeddings, suggesting better transition modeling
  - [corpus] Weak evidence; related work doesn't explicitly discuss residual prediction in this context.
- Break condition: If transition dynamics are highly complex or non-linear, residual prediction may not capture the full dynamics.

## Foundational Learning

- Concept: Wasserstein GAN (WGAN) distance
  - Why needed here: Provides a more robust and meaningful measure of distribution difference than traditional GAN objectives, enabling offline pretraining without online data collection
  - Quick check question: What advantage does Wasserstein distance have over Jensen-Shannon divergence for training discriminators in offline settings?

- Concept: Temporal alignment in representation learning
  - Why needed here: Ensures that embeddings preserve temporal relationships, which is critical for accurate transition prediction in sequential visual data
  - Quick check question: How does the symlog distance function differ from linear temporal distance, and why might it be more effective?

- Concept: Residual prediction in sequence modeling
  - Why needed here: Makes the prediction task easier by focusing on changes rather than absolute values, improving training stability and prediction accuracy
  - Quick check question: Why might predicting embedding differences be easier than predicting absolute embeddings in visual observation settings?

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map: Feature encoder (CNNs + MLP) -> STG Transformer (GPT-style with causal attention) -> Discriminator (WGAN) + TDR (temporal distance regressor)
- Critical path: Expert transitions -> Feature encoder -> STG Transformer prediction -> Discriminator comparison -> Intrinsic reward generation
- Design tradeoffs: Offline pretraining provides sample efficiency but requires sufficient expert data diversity; residual prediction simplifies the task but may miss absolute state information
- Failure signatures: Poor performance on tasks with highly diverse or novel visual observations; overfitting to expert dataset characteristics; unstable training due to discriminator collapse
- First 3 experiments:
  1. Ablation study removing the TDR module to confirm its importance for temporal alignment
  2. Comparison between STG and online adversarial methods (GAIfO) to demonstrate offline pretraining advantages
  3. Multi-task pretraining of STG Transformer to test generalization across different Atari games

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- Question: Can the STG Transformer generalize to new tasks when pretrained on a large multi-task dataset?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper mentions that they test a multi-task STG Transformer pretrained on all Atari training samples and find comparable performance, suggesting potential generalization.
- Why unresolved: The experiments only show comparable performance, not improved generalization. More extensive testing on entirely new tasks would be needed.
- What evidence would resolve it: Testing the multi-task STG on a wide range of new Atari and Minecraft tasks not seen during pretraining, comparing its performance to single-task STG and baselines.

### Open Question 2
- Question: How does the STG Transformer perform when there is domain shift between the expert demonstrations and the target task?
- Basis in paper: [inferred] The paper assumes the expert demonstrations are relevant to the target task. No experiments are done to test performance when this assumption is violated.
- Why unresolved: The current experiments use expert demonstrations from the same game being tested. Real-world applications may have domain shift.
- What evidence would resolve it: Testing STG on target tasks where the expert demonstrations come from related but different games or domains. Comparing performance to methods robust to domain shift.

### Open Question 3
- Question: What is the impact of different reward scaling strategies on the performance of the STG Transformer?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper mentions adjusting the intrinsic reward coefficient η and GAE lambda λ for each task, but does not systematically study the impact of different scaling strategies.
- Why unresolved: The choice of scaling parameters is heuristic and may significantly impact performance. A more rigorous study is needed.
- What evidence would resolve it: Systematically varying η and λ across a wide range of values and tasks, and analyzing their impact on sample efficiency and final performance.

## Limitations
- Heavy dependence on expert dataset quality and diversity for offline pretraining stage
- Lack of detailed specifications for dataset compositions makes faithful reproduction challenging
- Performance may be task-specific with less effective transfer to environments with highly diverse or novel visual observations

## Confidence

- **High confidence**: The effectiveness of offline pretraining for improving sample efficiency in LfVO tasks is well-supported by empirical results across multiple Atari and Minecraft environments.
- **Medium confidence**: The mechanism of temporal alignment through TDR is plausible but relies on assumptions about temporal continuity that may not hold in all environments (e.g., games with frequent resets).
- **Medium confidence**: The residual prediction approach improves transition modeling, though the paper provides limited comparative analysis against absolute prediction methods.

## Next Checks

1. Conduct ablation studies removing the TDR module across all tested environments to quantify its contribution to performance gains and verify temporal alignment necessity.
2. Test the STG Transformer's generalization capability by pretraining on one Atari game and evaluating transfer performance on unseen games with similar but distinct visual dynamics.
3. Compare intrinsic reward stability and policy learning curves between STG and online adversarial methods (GAIfO) on identical expert datasets to validate offline pretraining advantages.
