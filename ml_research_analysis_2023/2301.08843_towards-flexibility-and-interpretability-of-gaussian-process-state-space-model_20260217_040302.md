# Towards Flexibility and Interpretability of Gaussian Process State-Space Model

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2301.08843

## Executive Summary
The paper addresses the limited representation power of Gaussian process state-space models (GPSSMs) due to elementary kernels, hindering their application in complex scenarios. The authors propose the Transformed GPSSM (TGPSSM) that leverages parametric normalizing flows to enrich GP priors, enabling greater flexibility and expressiveness. They develop a scalable variational inference algorithm that offers a flexible and optimal structure for the variational distribution of latent states, benefiting from the sparse GP representation and bijective nature of normalizing flows. A constrained optimization framework is integrated to enhance state-space representation and optimize hyperparameters.

## Method Summary
The proposed Transformed GPSSM (TGPSSM) addresses the limited expressiveness of standard GPSSMs by incorporating parametric normalizing flows to transform the Gaussian process priors. The approach develops a scalable variational inference algorithm that leverages the sparse GP representation and the bijective nature of normalizing flows to achieve a flexible variational distribution structure for latent states. The method also integrates a constrained optimization framework to optimize hyperparameters and enhance state-space representation. The TGPSSM framework aims to provide superior learning and inference performance compared to existing GPSSM approaches while maintaining interpretability through the underlying GP structure.

## Key Results
- TGPSSM outperforms several state-of-the-art methods on synthetic and real datasets
- On the kink-step function dataset, the MSE of the proposed CO-TGPSSM is 0.4702, significantly lower than competing GPSSMs
- The approach demonstrates superior learning and inference performance across benchmark problems

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The proposed TGPSSM leverages parametric normalizing flows to transform the Gaussian process priors, which enriches the expressiveness of the model and enables it to capture more complex patterns in the data. The scalable variational inference algorithm takes advantage of the sparse GP representation and the bijective nature of normalizing flows to achieve a flexible and optimal structure for the variational distribution of latent states. This combination allows the model to better approximate the true posterior distribution while maintaining computational efficiency. The constrained optimization framework further enhances the state-space representation and optimizes hyperparameters, leading to improved performance on benchmark datasets.

## Architecture Onboarding

**Component Map:**
Data -> Normalizing Flow -> Transformed GP Prior -> State-Space Model -> Inference Algorithm -> Optimized Hyperparameters

**Critical Path:**
The critical path involves the transformation of the GP prior through the normalizing flow, followed by the state-space model formulation and the variational inference algorithm. The constrained optimization framework then optimizes the hyperparameters, which directly impacts the performance of the entire system.

**Design Tradeoffs:**
The use of parametric normalizing flows introduces additional flexibility but also increases the complexity of the model and the number of hyperparameters to tune. The choice of flow architecture must balance expressiveness with computational efficiency and numerical stability. The sparse GP representation reduces computational complexity but may sacrifice some modeling accuracy compared to full GPs.

**Failure Signatures:**
- Numerical instability in the normalizing flow transformations
- Poor performance due to suboptimal choice of flow architecture or hyperparameters
- Computational bottlenecks when scaling to large datasets or high-dimensional problems
