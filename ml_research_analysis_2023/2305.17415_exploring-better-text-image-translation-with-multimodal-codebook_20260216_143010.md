# Exploring Better Text Image Translation with Multimodal Codebook

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID**: 2305.17415
- **Source URL**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.17415
- **Reference count**: 6
- **Primary result**: Proposes multimodal codebook model with 4-stage training framework for text image translation, creating OCRMT30K dataset and achieving performance improvements over baselines.

## Executive Summary
This paper addresses the challenge of text image translation (TIT), where embedded source texts in images need to be translated to target languages. The authors tackle two main bottlenecks: the lack of publicly available TIT datasets and error propagation from optical character recognition (OCR) systems. They create the OCRMT30K Chinese-English dataset and propose a TIT model with a multimodal codebook that associates images with relevant texts to provide supplementary translation information. A multi-stage training framework leverages additional bilingual texts, OCR datasets, and the OCRMT30K dataset to train their model. Experiments demonstrate the effectiveness of both the proposed model and training framework.

## Method Summary
The proposed method consists of a TIT model with a multimodal codebook and a four-stage training framework. The multimodal codebook learns shared latent representations that map images and ground-truth texts into the same semantic space, providing supplementary information during translation. The training framework includes: (1) pretraining text encoder and decoder on bilingual corpus, (2) pretraining the multimodal codebook on monolingual data, (3) training image encoder and codebook on OCR data via image-text alignment, and (4) fine-tuning the full model on the OCRMT30K dataset. The model processes OCR-recognized text through a text encoder while extracting visual features from images using a Vision Transformer, with the multimodal codebook providing additional context to the translation decoder.

## Key Results
- Created OCRMT30K dataset with 30K Chinese-English text image translation examples
- Proposed multimodal codebook model that associates images with relevant texts for supplementary translation information
- Achieved performance improvements over text-only baselines and commercial systems
- Demonstrated effectiveness of multi-stage training framework leveraging additional bilingual texts and OCR data

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- **Claim**: Multimodal codebook learns shared latent representations that map images and ground-truth texts into the same semantic space.
- **Mechanism**: During training, the multimodal codebook clusters visual and textual features into shared latent codes. During inference, the input image is quantized into latent codes that capture information related to the ground-truth text, providing supplementary information to the translation decoder.
- **Core assumption**: The multimodal codebook can effectively learn to map semantically related image-text pairs into the same latent codes, even when the OCR-recognized text is incorrect.
- **Evidence anchors**: [abstract] "associate the image with relevant texts, providing useful supplementary information for translation"; [section] "Via Lita, we expect to enable both image and text representations to be quantized into the same latent codes"
- **Break condition**: If the codebook fails to learn meaningful clusters that capture semantic relationships between images and texts, the supplementary information provided during inference will be irrelevant or misleading.

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim**: Multi-stage training framework effectively leverages additional data to improve model performance.
- **Mechanism**: The framework first pretrains text encoder and decoder on large bilingual corpus, then pretrains multimodal codebook on monolingual data, then uses OCR data to train image encoder and codebook via image-text alignment, and finally fine-tunes on TIT dataset. Each stage builds on the previous one, allowing the model to learn progressively more complex relationships.
- **Core assumption**: Each stage of training contributes valuable knowledge that improves the model's ability to handle the TIT task.
- **Evidence anchors**: [abstract] "multi-stage training framework involving text machine translation, image-text alignment, and TIT tasks"; [section] "we propose a multi-stage training framework for our TIT model, which can fully exploit additional bilingual texts and OCR data for model training"
- **Break condition**: If any stage fails to provide meaningful improvements, or if there is catastrophic forgetting between stages, the overall framework may not improve performance.

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim**: Maintaining image-text alignment task during fine-tuning reduces the gap between pretraining and fine-tuning stages.
- **Mechanism**: By keeping the image-text alignment loss (L3) during the final fine-tuning stage, the model maintains the learned ability to associate images with relevant texts while adapting to the specific TIT task.
- **Core assumption**: The image-text alignment task learned in stage 3 is beneficial for the final TIT task, and maintaining it during fine-tuning prevents loss of this knowledge.
- **Evidence anchors**: [abstract] "we maintain the image-text alignment task at this stage to reduce the gap between the third and fourth training stages"; [section] "L3 is still involved, which maintains the training consistency and makes finetuning smoothing"
- **Break condition**: If the image-text alignment task conflicts with the TIT task objectives, or if it causes overfitting to the alignment objective rather than the translation objective.

## Architecture Onboarding

- **Component map**: OCR Text → Text Encoder → Multimodal Codebook ← Image Encoder ← Image → Multimodal Codebook → Text Decoder → Translation Output

- **Critical path**: During inference, the critical path is: Image → Image Encoder → Multimodal Codebook (quantization) → Decoder (with cross-attention to recognized text and latent codes) → Translation output

- **Design tradeoffs**:
  - Discrete vs. continuous latent representations: Discrete codes provide better interpretability and control but may lose some information
  - Shared vs. separate codebooks: Shared codebook reduces complexity but may create interference between modalities
  - Number of latent codes: More codes provide finer granularity but increase computational cost and risk of overfitting

- **Failure signatures**:
  - Poor translation quality despite correct OCR: Indicates multimodal codebook is not providing useful supplementary information
  - Translation quality close to text-only baseline: Suggests image encoder or codebook is not learning meaningful associations
  - Overfitting to training data: May indicate insufficient regularization or too many parameters relative to dataset size

- **First 3 experiments**:
  1. Ablation study removing the multimodal codebook to measure its contribution to performance
  2. Analysis of latent code distributions to verify they capture semantically related image-text pairs
  3. Comparison of different codebook sizes and quantization strategies to find optimal configuration
