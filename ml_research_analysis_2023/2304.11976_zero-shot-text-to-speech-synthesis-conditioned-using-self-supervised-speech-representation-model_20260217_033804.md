# Zero-shot text-to-speech synthesis conditioned using self-supervised speech representation model

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2304.11976
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2304.11976
- Reference count: 0
- Zero-shot TTS method using SSL models improves speaker similarity and rhythm transfer compared to x-vector baselines

## Executive Summary
This paper proposes a zero-shot text-to-speech (TTS) method that uses self-supervised learning (SSL) models like HuBERT and wav2vec 2.0 to extract speaker embeddings, replacing traditional approaches like x-vector or GSTs. The method introduces separate conditioning for acoustic features and phoneme duration prediction to obtain disentangled embeddings between rhythm-based and acoustic-feature-based speaker characteristics. Objective and subjective evaluations show improved naturalness and similarity scores compared to x-vector-based methods, particularly for rhythm transfer tasks.

## Method Summary
The proposed method uses a non-autoregressive TTS model (FastSpeech2) conditioned with speaker embeddings extracted from SSL models. The SSL model (HuBERT or wav2vec 2.0) processes speech representations to obtain embeddings, which are aggregated using weighted-sum of outputs from multiple hidden layers. The embedding module converts these outputs into fixed-length vectors, with separate conditioning for the duration predictor and other predictors. A HiFi-GAN vocoder generates the final speech waveform. The method is trained on a Japanese multi-speaker corpus with 1,083 speakers.

## Key Results
- SSL-based conditioning outperforms x-vector baselines in both naturalness and similarity scores
- Separate conditioning for duration predictor improves rhythm transfer capability
- HuBERT performs better than wav2vec 2.0 for speaker similarity tasks
- LSTM with attention aggregation outperforms average pooling for rhythm transfer

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
SSL models like HuBERT and wav2vec 2.0 extract richer speaker embeddings that better capture rhythm-based speaker characteristics compared to traditional methods like x-vector. These models are trained on large amounts of unlabeled speech data containing diverse speakers and speaking styles, allowing them to learn comprehensive representations of speaker characteristics including rhythm information crucial for speaker similarity.

### Mechanism 2
Separate conditioning of acoustic features and phoneme duration prediction allows for disentangled embeddings that improve rhythm transfer capability. By obtaining separate embedding vectors for the duration predictor and other predictors in the TTS model, the system can capture rhythm-based speaker characteristics independently from acoustic-feature-based ones, enabling generating speech with one speaker's acoustic characteristics and another's rhythm characteristics.

### Mechanism 3
Weighted-sum aggregation of SSL model outputs from multiple layers captures a more comprehensive representation of speaker characteristics than single-layer extraction. By aggregating outputs from each hidden layer of the pre-trained SSL model, the method can capture information at different levels of abstraction, as different layers may contain different types of speaker information.

## Architecture Onboarding

- **Component map**: Text transcript → Text-to-phoneme conversion → SSL Model (HuBERT/wav2vec 2.0) → Embedding Module (weighted-sum + aggregation) → FastSpeech2 (separate conditioning) → Mel-spectrogram → HiFi-GAN → Speech

- **Critical path**: Text → Encoder → SSL Model → Embedding Module → TTS Model → Mel-spectrogram → HiFi-GAN → Speech

- **Design tradeoffs**: 
  - SSL model choice: HuBERT vs. wav2vec 2.0 (HuBERT performed better in experiments)
  - Aggregation method: Average pooling vs. LSTM with attention (LSTM with attention performed better for rhythm transfer)
  - Conditioning strategy: Common vs. separate conditioning (separate conditioning improved rhythm transfer)

- **Failure signatures**:
  - Poor speaker similarity: May indicate insufficient speaker diversity in SSL training data or inadequate embedding extraction
  - Incorrect rhythm: Could suggest issues with separate conditioning or layer weighting in aggregation
  - Low naturalness: Might indicate problems with the TTS model architecture or training process

- **First 3 experiments**:
  1. Implement the basic architecture with a single SSL model (e.g., HuBERT) and common conditioning, using average pooling for aggregation
  2. Add separate conditioning for duration predictor and compare performance on rhythm transfer tasks
  3. Experiment with different SSL models (HuBERT vs. wav2vec 2.0) and aggregation methods (average pooling vs. LSTM with attention) to optimize for both naturalness and similarity

## Limitations

- The paper does not fully specify the SSL model training configuration (masking strategy, pre-training duration, exact fairseq parameters), which may affect reproducibility of the embedding quality
- The exact architecture of the embedding module, particularly the LSTM with attention mechanism for aggregation, lacks sufficient detail for precise replication
- The claim that separate conditioning improves rhythm transfer is supported by experimental results but the mechanism explaining why this specifically works better than common conditioning for rhythm is not fully explored

## Confidence

- **High confidence**: The overall approach of using SSL models for zero-shot TTS conditioning is technically sound and well-supported by existing research
- **Medium confidence**: The claim about SSL models capturing richer speaker characteristics is supported by experimental results but the specific advantage over x-vector is not conclusively proven
- **Medium confidence**: The benefit of separate conditioning for rhythm transfer is demonstrated empirically but the underlying mechanism could be better explained
