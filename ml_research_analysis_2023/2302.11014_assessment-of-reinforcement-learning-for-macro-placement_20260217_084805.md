# Assessment of Reinforcement Learning for Macro Placement

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2302.11014
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2302.11014
- Authors: 
- Reference count: 40
- Key outcome: This paper provides a comprehensive assessment of Google Brain's deep reinforcement learning approach for macro placement, addressing transparency issues by open-sourcing key components of their Circuit Training (CT) framework. The authors implement and clarify previously undocumented "blackbox" elements including force-directed placement and proxy cost calculation, while also developing new testcases and enablements for academic research. Experimental results on both modern and academic benchmarks show that while CT's proxy cost correlates poorly with actual routing metrics, alternative approaches like simulated annealing and commercial macro placers often produce better routed wirelength. The study reveals that CT's performance heavily depends on initial placement information from physical synthesis tools, and that human experts can outperform CT on large, macro-heavy designs. The work highlights the importance of reproducible research in EDA and provides open-source tools and flows for future studies.

## Executive Summary
This paper provides a comprehensive assessment of Google Brain's deep reinforcement learning approach for macro placement, addressing transparency issues by open-sourcing key components of their Circuit Training (CT) framework. The authors implement and clarify previously undocumented "blackbox" elements including force-directed placement and proxy cost calculation, while also developing new testcases and enablements for academic research. Experimental results on both modern and academic benchmarks show that while CT's proxy cost correlates poorly with actual routing metrics, alternative approaches like simulated annealing and commercial macro placers often produce better routed wirelength. The study reveals that CT's performance heavily depends on initial placement information from physical synthesis tools, and that human experts can outperform CT on large, macro-heavy designs. The work highlights the importance of reproducible research in EDA and provides open-source tools and flows for future studies.

## Method Summary
The authors implement Google Brain's Circuit Training (CT) framework for macro placement, focusing on previously undocumented components like force-directed placement and proxy cost calculation. They develop a commercial evaluation flow integrating CT with Cadence Innovus tools for physical synthesis, placement, and routing. The study uses both modern benchmarks (s8, s12, s15, s28, s35) and academic testcases (Ariane, BW, etc.) to evaluate CT's performance. Key methodological contributions include open-sourcing CT implementation, creating reproducible flows, and establishing baselines using commercial placers and simulated annealing.

## Key Results
- CT's proxy cost correlates poorly with actual routing metrics, questioning the fundamental RL optimization approach
- CT performance heavily depends on initial placement information from physical synthesis tools
- Commercial macro placers and simulated annealing often outperform CT on routed wirelength
- Human experts can outperform CT on large, macro-heavy designs
- Open-sourcing CT implementation enables academic research and reproducibility in EDA

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: RL-based macro placement can outperform traditional methods when properly integrated with EDA tools
- Mechanism: The RL agent learns placement policies that optimize a proxy cost function combining wirelength, density, and congestion. This proxy cost drives the RL agent's learning, and when properly correlated with actual routing metrics, produces superior macro placements.
- Core assumption: The proxy cost function accurately reflects the true objectives of power, performance, and area optimization
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "CT's proxy cost correlates poorly with actual routing metrics"
  - [section 3.2.2] "Proxy cost is crucial to understand, since it drives the RL agent's learning and comprises the only apparent connection between CT and optimization of 'key metrics, including power consumption, performance and chip area'"
  - [corpus] Weak evidence - no direct correlation studies found in corpus
- Break condition: Poor correlation between proxy cost and actual routing metrics, as demonstrated when comparing CT's proxy cost to Nature Table 1 metrics

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: Initial placement information from physical synthesis tools significantly improves CT outcomes
- Mechanism: CT relies on placement locations in its input netlist, which are used by CT's grouping, gridding and clustering process. When initial placement information is available from commercial physical synthesis tools, CT can produce better results.
- Core assumption: The initial placement information provides a reasonable starting point for the RL agent to optimize from
- Evidence anchors:
  - [section 3.1] "CT assumes that all instances of the input netlist have (𝑥, 𝑦) locations, i.e., the netlist has already been placed before it is input to CT"
  - [section 5.2.1] "given locations from Cadence CMP and Genus iSpatial (Steps 2 and 3 of Figure 2), CT's solution has rWL that is 10.32%, 7.24% and 8.17% less than in Cases (1), (2) and (3), respectively"
  - [corpus] No additional evidence found in corpus
- Break condition: When CT is run without initial placement information, resulting in significantly worse routed wirelength and other metrics

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: Commercial EDA tools can validate and improve RL-generated macro placements
- Mechanism: The evaluation flow integrates CT-generated macro placements into a standard commercial EDA flow (Cadence Innovus), where tools like CMP, physical synthesis, and place-and-route can refine and validate the placement. This integration allows for comprehensive assessment of placement quality using industry-standard metrics.
- Core assumption: Commercial EDA tools provide a reliable "ground truth" for evaluating placement quality
- Evidence anchors:
  - [section 4.2] "We input the floorplan .def with placed macros to the Cadence Genus iSpatial flow and run physical-aware synthesis. The physical-aware synthesis is used to generate initial placement locations (i.e., (x,y) coordinates) for all standard cells"
  - [section 5.1] "The commercial evaluation flow for CT is equivalent to the CMP flow with insertion of the CT macro placement step"
  - [corpus] Weak evidence - no direct validation studies found in corpus
- Break condition: When commercial EDA tools fail to properly handle RL-generated placements, or when the integration introduces significant overhead without quality improvement

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map: RL Agent -> Environment -> Proxy Cost Calculator -> Force-Directed Placement -> Commercial EDA Tools
- Critical path:
  1. Netlist preprocessing and protobuf conversion
  2. CT-Grouping to generate standard-cell clusters
  3. RL agent placement of macros on grid cells
  4. Force-directed placement of standard-cell clusters
  5. Proxy cost calculation and reward feedback
  6. Commercial EDA tool integration for validation
- Design tradeoffs:
  - Grid-based placement vs. continuous placement space
  - Sequential macro placement vs. concurrent placement
  - Proxy cost correlation with actual routing metrics vs. computational efficiency
  - RL training time vs. placement quality
- Failure signatures:
  - Poor correlation between proxy cost and actual routing metrics
  - Degradation in routed wirelength compared to traditional methods
  - Instability in CT results across different random seeds
  - Failure to integrate with commercial EDA tools
- First 3 experiments:
  1. Run CT on a simple testcase (e.g., Ariane) with and without initial placement information to verify the importance of input placement
  2. Compare CT's proxy cost with actual routed wirelength metrics on a small testcase to assess correlation quality
  3. Integrate CT-generated macro placement into the commercial evaluation flow and verify that the flow completes successfully with reasonable quality metrics

## Limitations
- Poor correlation between proxy cost and actual routing metrics undermines RL optimization approach
- Heavy dependency on initial placement information from commercial tools limits standalone efficacy
- Performance degradation on macro-heavy designs suggests scalability issues
- Human experts can outperform CT on large designs, challenging RL superiority claims

## Confidence
- High Confidence: Findings about poor proxy cost correlation with actual routing metrics (verified through multiple testcases and comparisons)
- Medium Confidence: Assessment of CT's dependency on initial placement information (supported by experimental results but limited to specific benchmark suites)
- Medium Confidence: Comparative analysis showing commercial placers and simulated annealing outperforming CT (based on routed wirelength metrics across testcases)
- Low Confidence: Generalization of results to all RL-based placement approaches (the study focuses specifically on Google Brain's CT implementation)

## Next Checks
1. Conduct correlation studies between proxy cost and actual routing metrics across a broader range of designs to quantify the relationship more precisely
2. Implement and test CT in a truly standalone mode (without initial placement information) on the same benchmark suite to isolate the impact of this dependency
3. Perform ablation studies to identify which specific components of the CT framework contribute most to its limitations, particularly examining the grouping and clustering process and its impact on placement quality
