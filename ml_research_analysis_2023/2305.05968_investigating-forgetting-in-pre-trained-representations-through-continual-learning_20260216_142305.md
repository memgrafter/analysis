# Investigating Forgetting in Pre-Trained Representations Through Continual Learning

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2305.05968
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.05968
- Reference count: 18
- Pre-trained language models experience representation forgetting during continual learning, with both syntactic and semantic knowledge being lost

## Executive Summary
This paper investigates representation forgetting in pre-trained language models during continual learning. The authors design three metrics to measure the evolution of general knowledge: overall generality destruction (GD), syntactic knowledge forgetting (SynF), and semantic knowledge forgetting (SemF). Through extensive experiments on various pre-trained models and task sequences, the study reveals that generality is destructed and both syntactic and semantic knowledge are forgotten through continual learning. The authors also find that training on general linguistic tasks first can mitigate general knowledge forgetting, and hybrid continual learning methods combining rehearsal and regularization achieve more balanced results compared to those only considering rehearsal or regularization.

## Method Summary
The authors propose three metrics (GD, SynF, SemF) to measure knowledge forgetting in pre-trained language models during continual learning. They conduct extensive experiments using BERT and RoBERTa models on controlled task sequences, evaluating the evolution of general knowledge as the models learn new tasks. The study also explores the impact of task ordering (general linguistic tasks first) and compares different continual learning methods (rehearsal-only, regularization-only, and hybrid approaches).

## Key Results
- Generality is destructed in pre-trained language models through continual learning
- Both syntactic and semantic knowledge are forgotten during the learning process
- Training on general linguistic tasks first can mitigate general knowledge forgetting
- Hybrid continual learning methods combining rehearsal and regularization achieve more balanced results compared to those only considering rehearsal or regularization

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The paper demonstrates that pre-trained language models experience representation forgetting during continual learning due to the catastrophic interference between new tasks and previously learned knowledge. The proposed metrics (GD, SynF, SemF) effectively capture the degradation of general knowledge, syntactic understanding, and semantic understanding respectively. The mechanism behind knowledge forgetting involves the model's tendency to adapt its representations to new tasks, which can overwrite or distort previously acquired knowledge.

## Foundational Learning
- Catastrophic forgetting: The tendency of neural networks to rapidly forget previously learned information upon learning new information. Why needed: To understand the core phenomenon being investigated in continual learning scenarios.
- Continual learning: A learning paradigm where models are trained on a sequence of tasks, with the goal of retaining performance on all tasks. Why needed: To contextualize the experimental setup and evaluation methodology.
- Knowledge representation: The way information is encoded in model parameters and activations. Why needed: To analyze how different types of knowledge (general, syntactic, semantic) are affected by continual learning.

## Architecture Onboarding
- Component map: Pre-trained model (BERT/RoBERTa) -> Task sequence -> Evaluation metrics (GD, SynF, SemF)
- Critical path: Pre-trained model initialization -> Task learning sequence -> Knowledge forgetting measurement
- Design tradeoffs: Balancing between retaining old knowledge and learning new tasks, choosing between rehearsal, regularization, or hybrid approaches
- Failure signatures: Rapid degradation of performance on previously learned tasks, decrease in GD, SynF, and SemF scores
- First experiments:
  1. Evaluate GD, SynF, and SemF on a pre-trained model without any additional training
  2. Measure knowledge forgetting when learning a single new task
  3. Compare forgetting rates between models trained on general linguistic tasks first vs. specific tasks first

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- How do the proposed metrics (GD, SynF, SemF) perform on more complex, real-world task sequences?
- Can the findings be generalized to other transformer architectures beyond BERT and RoBERTa?
- What is the optimal combination of rehearsal and regularization in hybrid continual learning methods for different task types?

## Limitations
- The validation of the proposed metrics (GD, SynF, SemF) against established benchmarks is limited
- The experimental setup uses controlled task sequences, which may not fully capture the complexity of real-world continual learning scenarios
- The analysis focuses primarily on BERT and RoBERTa models, potentially limiting generalizability to other transformer architectures

## Confidence
- High confidence: The observation that generality is destructed and both syntactic and semantic knowledge are forgotten during continual learning is well-supported by the experimental results
- Medium confidence: The finding that training on general linguistic tasks first can mitigate knowledge forgetting is supported but could benefit from more extensive ablation studies
- Medium confidence: The claim that hybrid continual learning methods achieve more balanced results is substantiated but may be influenced by specific hyperparameter choices

## Next Checks
1. Validate the proposed metrics (GD, SynF, SemF) against established forgetting metrics in the continual learning literature to ensure they capture the phenomena effectively

3. Conduct a detailed ablation study on the hybrid continual learning methods to isolate the contributions of rehearsal and regularization components and identify optimal combinations for different task types
