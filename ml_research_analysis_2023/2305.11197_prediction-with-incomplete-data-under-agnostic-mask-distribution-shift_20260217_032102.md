# Prediction with Incomplete Data under Agnostic Mask Distribution Shift

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2305.11197
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.11197
- Reference count: 39
- Reduces RMSE gap to optimal by 30%-50% compared to second best method

## Executive Summary
This paper proposes StableMiss, a method for prediction with incomplete data when the missingness pattern distribution may shift between training and testing. The key innovation is a double parameterization approach that learns optimal predictors for each mask pattern while decorrelating features and masks to avoid reliance on training-specific correlations. Under the assumption that the complete feature-label distribution remains invariant, StableMiss achieves significantly better generalization under mask distribution shift compared to existing methods.

## Method Summary
StableMiss addresses prediction with incomplete data under mask distribution shift by combining double parameterization with decorrelation. The method first approximates optimal predictors for each mask pattern using a neural network that maps masks to predictor parameters. Then, it decorrelates features and masks through sample reweighting, minimizing correlations under a reweighted training distribution. This approach avoids the exponential explosion of learning separate predictors for each mask pattern while preventing the model from relying on spurious training-time correlations between features and missingness.

## Key Results
- StableMiss significantly outperforms state-of-the-art methods in terms of RMSE when mask distribution shifts between training and testing
- Reduces the gap to optimal RMSE by 30%-50% compared to the second best method on Gaussian-Mix feature under MAR mask
- Maintains theoretical guarantees under MCAR and MAR missingness patterns when complete feature-label distribution is invariant

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: StableMiss learns optimal predictors for each mask pattern and decorrelates features and mask to avoid intra-mask correlations.
- Mechanism: The method parameterizes a double mapping: first from mask to predictor parameters, then from features and mask to predictions. Decorrelation via sample reweighting removes unwanted correlations that could harm generalization under mask distribution shift.
- Core assumption: The joint distribution of complete features and labels is invariant between training and testing.
- Evidence anchors: [abstract] "We focus on the case where the underlying joint distribution of complete features and label is invariant, but the missing pattern, i.e., mask distribution may shift agnostically between training and testing."
- Break condition: If the complete feature-label joint distribution shifts between training and testing, the method's core assumption fails and generalization breaks.

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: The double parameterization approach avoids exponential explosion while approximating optimal predictors for each mask pattern.
- Mechanism: Instead of learning 2^n separate predictors (one per mask pattern), StableMiss learns a neural network that maps masks to predictor parameters, then applies these parameters to observed features. This reduces complexity from exponential to polynomial in feature dimension.
- Core assumption: The optimal predictor can be parameterized as a function of the mask, i.e., g(xm, m) = gφθ(m)(x⊙m).
- Evidence anchors: [section 4.1] "Since the number of optimal predictors increases exponentially with feature dimension [Morvan et al., 2020], we approximate the optimal predictors jointly using a double parameterization technique"
- Break condition: If the optimal predictor cannot be well-approximated by the parameterized form, the method loses its theoretical guarantee.

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: Sample reweighting decorrelates features and mask to prevent learned parameters from relying on training distribution correlations.
- Mechanism: The method learns sample weights that minimize cross-covariances between features and mask elements, creating a reweighted training distribution where these correlations are reduced. This prevents the model from memorizing training-specific patterns.
- Core assumption: Reducing correlation between features and mask under training distribution helps the learned parameters generalize to shifted mask distributions.
- Evidence anchors: [section 4.2] "We decorrelate by sample reweighting. Specifically, we learn a weighting function w(xm, m) by minimizing the correlation under distribution ̃pw"
- Break condition: If the decorrelation process overfits to training data or removes useful signal, generalization may suffer.

## Foundational Learning

- Concept: Conditional independence in missing data patterns (MCAR vs MAR)
  - Why needed here: The method's theoretical guarantees depend on whether missingness is independent of unobserved values (MCAR) or only depends on observed values (MAR).
  - Quick check question: In MAR setting, can the probability of a feature being missing depend on other observed features?

- Concept: Double parameterization and function approximation
  - Why needed here: The core innovation requires understanding how to parameterize a complex function (optimal predictor for each mask) using a simpler parametric form.
  - Quick check question: Why is learning 2^n separate predictors infeasible for high-dimensional data?

- Concept: Sample reweighting and distribution matching
  - Why needed here: The decorrelation mechanism relies on reweighting samples to create a new effective training distribution.
  - Quick check question: What happens to the effective training distribution when you assign different weights to samples?

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map: Feature encoder -> Mask encoder -> Predictor -> Decorrelation module
- Critical path: Mask → φθ(m) → predictor parameters → features → prediction
- Design tradeoffs: More complex predictor φθ vs simpler but less expressive; stronger decorrelation vs preserving useful correlations; computational cost of decorrelation vs potential gains
- Failure signatures: Poor performance when mask distribution shift is extreme; overfitting to training mask patterns; decorrelation removing useful signal
- First 3 experiments: 1) Train StableMiss on Gaussian feature with MCAR mask, test under same distribution to verify basic functionality; 2) Train on MCAR, test on MAR with same missing rate to test distribution shift handling; 3) Compare with baseline methods on real-world dataset with natural missingness

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- Question: How does the performance of StableMiss scale with the dimensionality of the feature space?
- Basis in paper: [inferred] The paper notes that the number of optimal predictors increases exponentially with feature dimension, which is why StableMiss uses a double parameterization technique. However, the paper does not directly investigate how the performance degrades (or improves) as the number of features increases.
- Why unresolved: The experiments use a fixed feature dimension (n=50) for synthetic data, and the real-world datasets have varying but fixed dimensions. There is no systematic study of how StableMiss's performance changes with increasing dimensionality.
- What evidence would resolve it: A systematic experiment varying the feature dimension in the synthetic datasets, plotting performance metrics against dimension, and comparing against the exponential scaling of the optimal predictors.

### Open Question 2
- Question: How sensitive is StableMiss to the choice of hyper-parameters, particularly the regularization coefficient γ in the decorrelation step?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper mentions that γ is a hyper-parameter coefficient for the regularization term in the decorrelation objective, and it is selected from {0.1, 0.2, 0.5, 1, 2, 5, 10}. However, the paper does not report how sensitive the results are to this choice.
- Why unresolved: The paper does not provide a sensitivity analysis or report the range of performance variation across different γ values.
- What evidence would resolve it: A sensitivity analysis plotting performance metrics against γ values, and possibly a recommendation for the optimal range of γ.

### Open Question 3
- Question: Can StableMiss be extended to handle more complex missingness patterns, such as Missing Not At Random (MNAR)?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper focuses on MCAR and MAR cases, and states "We focus on the Missing Completely At Random (MCAR) case and Missing At Random (MAR) case." It does not discuss MNAR.
- Why unresolved: MNAR is a more general and challenging missingness pattern where the missingness depends on the unobserved values, and the paper does not provide any theoretical or empirical results for this case.
- What evidence would resolve it: An extension of the StableMiss framework to handle MNAR, with theoretical guarantees and empirical results demonstrating its effectiveness.

## Limitations
- Assumes complete feature-label distribution invariance between training and testing, which may not hold in real-world scenarios
- Decorrelation mechanism could potentially remove genuinely predictive feature-mask relationships
- Theoretical framework provides limited guidance for handling Missing Not At Random (MNAR) patterns

## Confidence

High confidence in empirical results showing StableMiss outperforms baselines on synthetic datasets with controlled mask distribution shifts.

Medium confidence in theoretical guarantees, given limited corpus evidence and strong assumptions about distribution invariance.

Medium confidence in decorrelation approach, as the paper provides limited discussion of potential failure modes when decorrelation removes useful signal.

## Next Checks

1. Test StableMiss on synthetic datasets with extreme mask distribution shifts (e.g., from MCAR to highly MAR with complex dependencies) to assess failure boundaries.

2. Conduct ablation study of decorrelation strength by systematically varying the decorrelation objective weight to quantify tradeoff between removing spurious correlations and preserving useful ones.

3. Apply StableMiss to a real-world dataset with natural missingness patterns where complete feature-label distribution invariance is questionable, comparing performance against simpler methods.
