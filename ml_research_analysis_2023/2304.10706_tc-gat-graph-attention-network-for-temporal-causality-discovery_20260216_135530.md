# TC-GAT: Graph Attention Network for Temporal Causality Discovery

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2304.10706
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2304.10706
- Reference count: 40
- Key outcome: TC-GAT achieves Macro-F1 scores of 89.97% on TC-SemEval2010-Task8 and 90.97% on TC-AltLex, outperforming baselines

## Executive Summary
TC-GAT is a novel approach for causality extraction in text that leverages temporal relationships through a graph attention network architecture. The model integrates temporal and causal relations using a knowledge graph adjacency matrix and an equilibrium mechanism to balance their interplay. Experimental results demonstrate significant performance improvements over baseline models like BERT-CRF and C-GCN, with ablation studies confirming the contributions of each component to the overall success.

## Method Summary
TC-GAT employs BERT for contextual encoding, followed by T-GAT and C-GAT layers for temporal and causal attention respectively. The model uses a causal knowledge graph to construct an adjacency matrix for C-GAT, while T-GAT focuses on temporal relationships between events. An equilibrium mechanism balances the outputs of these attention mechanisms to prevent temporal dominance from degrading causal predictions. The model is trained using cross-entropy loss and evaluated on two datasets: TC-SemEval2010-Task8 and TC-AltLex.

## Key Results
- Achieves Macro-F1 of 89.97% on TC-SemEval2010-Task8 dataset
- Achieves Macro-F1 of 90.97% on TC-AltLex dataset
- Outperforms baseline models including BERT-CRF and C-GCN
- Ablation studies show significant performance drops when removing equilibrium mechanism (0.02% on TC-SemEval2010-Task8, 1.89% on TC-AltLex)

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
Temporal attention in T-GAT enables the model to focus on words with temporal significance, improving causal relation extraction. The T-GAT layer uses graph attention to assign higher weights to nodes that have strong temporal relationships. Core assumption: Temporal relationships between events are reliable signals for inferring causality. Evidence: Abstract states TC-GAT "employs a graph attention mechanism to assign weights to the temporal relationships." Break condition: If temporal relationships are noisy or misleading, attention may overweight irrelevant temporal cues.

### Mechanism 2
C-GAT integrates causal knowledge graph adjacency to focus on causally related words, improving prediction accuracy. C-GAT takes contextual features and the causal relationship adjacency matrix to compute attention weights. Core assumption: Causal relationships can be represented as a graph and the adjacency matrix accurately encodes these relations. Evidence: Abstract mentions leveraging "a causal knowledge graph to determine the adjacency matrix." Break condition: If the causal knowledge graph is incomplete or inaccurate, C-GAT may propagate incorrect adjacency information.

### Mechanism 3
The equilibrium mechanism balances the influence of temporal and causal attention, preventing temporal dominance from degrading causal predictions. An attention-based trade-off coefficient is learned to weight the outputs of T-GAT and BERT. Core assumption: Temporal relationships, while informative, can also mislead if overemphasized; balancing them with contextual features yields better causality extraction. Evidence: Abstract states "we implement an equilibrium mechanism to regulate the interplay between temporal and causal relations." Break condition: If the equilibrium mechanism underweights useful temporal cues or overweights noise, causal extraction accuracy will drop.

- Concept: Temporal causal relationships (Before, After, Simultaneous, Include, Be-Include)
  - Why needed here: The model relies on these categories to construct temporal matrices and guide T-GAT attention
  - Quick check question: How would the model handle an event pair that is simultaneous but not causally related?

- Concept: Graph Attention Networks (GAT)
  - Why needed here: GAT is used both for temporal (T-GAT) and causal (C-GAT) attention weighting in the model
  - Quick check question: What is the difference between GAT and GCN, and why is GAT preferred here?

- Concept: BERT contextual embeddings
  - Why needed here: BERT provides word-level contextual features that are combined with T-GAT and C-GAT outputs
  - Quick check question: Why might BERT embeddings be more useful than static embeddings for this task?

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map: Input text → BERT embedding → BiLSTM context features → T-GAT (temporal attention) → C-GAT (causal attention) → Equilibrium mechanism → Output prediction
- Critical path: BERT → BiLSTM → T-GAT/C-GAT → Equilibrium → Classifier; temporal and causal attention paths converge at the equilibrium step
- Design tradeoffs: Using GAT allows dynamic weighting of neighbors but adds computational overhead; equilibrium mechanism adds robustness but requires careful tuning to avoid underfitting either signal
- Failure signatures: Low Macro-F1 on either causal or temporal labels; high variance between P and R values; instability when removing equilibrium
- First 3 experiments:
  1. Train TC-GAT on TC-SemEval2010-Task8 with full model to verify Macro-F1 ~89.97%
  2. Remove the equilibrium mechanism to observe Macro-F1 drop (~0.02% on TC-SemEval2010-Task8, ~1.89% on TC-AltLex)
  3. Replace BERT with static embeddings to confirm performance degradation (Macro-F1 drops ~2.69% on TC-SemEval2010-Task8)

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
How do different equilibrium mechanisms compare in balancing temporal and causal influences, and what is the optimal configuration for various domain corpora? Basis: The paper mentions the equilibrium mechanism balances temporal and causal relationships but does not explore alternative mechanisms or domain-specific optimization. Why unresolved: Only one equilibrium mechanism implemented without comparative analysis. What evidence would resolve it: Systematic experiments comparing multiple equilibrium mechanisms across diverse domain-specific datasets.

### Open Question 2
What is the impact of different temporal relationship classifications on causality extraction performance, and how sensitive is the model to changes in temporal granularity? Basis: The paper uses five temporal categories but does not explore alternative classification schemes. Why unresolved: Authors chose specific temporal classification without exploring whether more granular or different temporal categories would improve or degrade performance. What evidence would resolve it: Comparative experiments using different temporal classification schemes with systematic performance evaluation.

### Open Question 3
How does the TC-GAT model perform on longer documents where temporal and causal relationships span multiple sentences, and what architectural modifications would improve long-range dependency capture? Basis: The paper constrains text length to 50 units and does not address document-level temporal-causal relationships. Why unresolved: Experimental setup limits text length, and model architecture does not explicitly address cross-sentence temporal and causal relationships. What evidence would resolve it: Experiments testing the model on multi-sentence documents with explicit evaluation of cross-sentence temporal and causal relationship extraction.

## Limitations

- Equilibrium mechanism's exact mathematical formulation is unspecified, making it difficult to determine whether performance gains are from the balancing effect or other factors
- Causal knowledge graph structure and adjacency matrix construction process are not detailed, raising questions about reproducibility
- Limited ablation study scope - only equilibrium mechanism was removed in experiments, with no tests on removing individual GAT components

## Confidence

- **High confidence**: BERT's contribution to performance (supported by significant Macro-F1 drop when removed)
- **Medium confidence**: T-GAT's effectiveness in capturing temporal relationships (mechanism described but not independently validated)
- **Medium confidence**: C-GAT's ability to leverage causal adjacency matrices (mechanism described but adjacency matrix details missing)
- **Low confidence**: Equilibrium mechanism's specific impact (only one ablation test performed)

## Next Checks

1. Implement and test alternative balancing mechanisms (e.g., simple averaging, learned gating without temporal attention) to isolate the equilibrium mechanism's contribution
2. Create synthetic datasets with controlled temporal relationships to test whether T-GAT correctly weights temporal vs. non-temporal causal cues
3. Perform ablation studies removing either T-GAT or C-GAT individually to quantify their separate contributions beyond the combined equilibrium effect
