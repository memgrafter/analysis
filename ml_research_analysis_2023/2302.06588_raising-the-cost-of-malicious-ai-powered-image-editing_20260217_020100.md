# Raising the Cost of Malicious AI-Powered Image Editing

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2302.06588
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2302.06588
- Reference count: 40
- Key outcome: Immunization against AI-powered image editing using imperceptible adversarial perturbations that disrupt diffusion models' ability to generate realistic edits

## Executive Summary
This paper presents a method to protect images from malicious AI-powered editing by adding imperceptible adversarial perturbations that disrupt diffusion models' ability to generate realistic edits. The authors propose two attack methods: encoder attack (perturbing the image to make the encoder output a "bad" representation) and diffusion attack (perturbing the image to make the diffusion process generate a target image). Experiments on Stable Diffusion show that immunized images yield unrealistic edits compared to non-immunized ones, with quantitative metrics confirming substantial differences. The diffusion attack proves more effective than the encoder attack.

## Method Summary
The method involves adding imperceptible adversarial perturbations to images to disrupt the operation of targeted diffusion models. Two attack approaches are employed: the encoder attack, which forces the latent diffusion model's encoder to map the input image to a "bad" latent representation, and the diffusion attack, which disrupts the entire diffusion process by making the final generated image resemble a target image. Both attacks use projected gradient descent optimization with ℓ∞ norm constraint of 16/255, step size 2/255, and 200 steps. The effectiveness is evaluated by comparing edits of immunized versus non-immunized images using image quality metrics (FID, PR, SSIM, PSNR, VIFp, FSIM) and CLIP-based image-prompt similarity measurements.

## Key Results
- Immunized images yield unrealistic edits compared to non-immunized images
- Diffusion attack is more effective than encoder attack
- Quantitative metrics (FID, PR, SSIM, PSNR, VIFp, FSIM) confirm substantial differences between edits of immunized and non-immunized images
- Immunization decreases similarity between edited images and textual prompts

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: The encoder attack forces the latent diffusion model's encoder to map the immunized image to a "bad" latent representation.
- Mechanism: The adversarial perturbation δencoder is optimized to minimize the distance between the latent representation of the immunized image and a target latent representation ztarg, which is chosen to correspond to a semantically unrelated image (e.g., a gray image). This forces the LDM to generate images that are semantically unrelated to the original image.
- Core assumption: The LDM's generation process is heavily dependent on the latent representation produced by the encoder.
- Break condition: If the LDM uses multiple encoding stages or if the latent space is more robust to adversarial perturbations, the encoder attack may become less effective.

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: The diffusion attack disrupts the entire diffusion process by making the final generated image resemble a target image, effectively nullifying both the effect of the immunized image and the textual prompt.
- Mechanism: The adversarial perturbation δdiffusion is optimized to minimize the distance between the image generated by the LDM and a target image xtarg. This forces the LDM to generate an image that resembles the target image, regardless of the input image and textual prompt.
- Core assumption: The LDM's generation process can be manipulated by perturbing the input image in a way that affects the entire diffusion process.
- Break condition: If the LDM uses techniques to make the diffusion process more robust to adversarial perturbations, the diffusion attack may become less effective.

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: The immunization process decreases the similarity between the edited images and the textual prompts used to generate them, effectively preventing the LDM from using the prompt to guide the generation.
- Mechanism: By disrupting the diffusion process, the immunization process prevents the LDM from effectively using the textual prompt to guide the generation of the edited image. This results in a decrease in the similarity between the generated images and the textual prompts.
- Core assumption: The LDM's generation process is heavily dependent on the textual prompt.
- Break condition: If the LDM uses techniques to make the prompt conditioning more robust to adversarial perturbations, the immunization process may become less effective at preventing the LDM from using the prompt.

- Component map: Input image → Encoder → Latent representation → Diffusion process → Decoder → Generated image
- Critical path: Input image → Encoder → Latent representation → Diffusion process → Decoder → Generated image
- Design tradeoffs:
  - Tradeoff between the strength of the adversarial perturbation and its perceptibility to the human eye.
  - Tradeoff between the effectiveness of the immunization process and the computational cost of generating the adversarial perturbations.
- Failure signatures:
  - If the adversarial perturbations are too weak, the diffusion models may still be able to generate realistic edits.
  - If the adversarial perturbations are too strong, they may become noticeable to the human eye.

## Limitations
- The immunization effectiveness may degrade when images undergo transformations like cropping, rotation, or compression
- The approach requires cooperation between model developers to ensure forward compatibility of immunization
- Immunizing adversarial perturbations may be ineffective against future versions of diffusion models trained with adversarial robustness

## Confidence

- **High Confidence**: The core observation that immunized images yield unrealistic edits compared to non-immunized ones is well-supported by quantitative metrics (FID, PR, SSIM, PSNR, VIFp, FSIM) and qualitative visual examples. The experimental methodology for comparing immunized vs. non-immunized images is clearly specified and reproducible.

- **Medium Confidence**: The relative effectiveness comparison between encoder and diffusion attacks is supported by the data, but the exact magnitude of improvement may vary depending on the specific images and prompts used. The claim that immunized images prevent LDM from using textual prompts effectively is plausible but requires more rigorous evaluation across diverse prompt-image pairs.

- **Low Confidence**: The assumption that immunization provides "future-proof" protection against models trained after immunization is stated but not empirically validated. The paper doesn't address potential adversarial training techniques that future models might employ to become robust against these attacks.
