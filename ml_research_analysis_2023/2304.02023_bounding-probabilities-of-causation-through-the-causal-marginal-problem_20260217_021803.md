# Bounding probabilities of causation through the causal marginal problem

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2304.02023
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2304.02023
- Authors: 
- Reference count: 40
- Primary result: A method to significantly tighten existing bounds on probabilities of causation by imposing counterfactual consistency between SCMs constructed from independent datasets

## Executive Summary
This paper addresses the fundamental challenge of bounding probabilities of causation in causal inference when dealing with multiple independent datasets examining different treatments on the same outcome variable. The authors introduce the causal marginal problem framework that combines datasets to tighten bounds on counterfactual probabilities by enforcing consistency between Structural Causal Models. They propose a novel information-theoretic approach using conditional mutual information to falsify counterfactual probabilities and a Maximum Entropy-based method to select the "best" SCM when bounds are unsatisfactory.

## Method Summary
The core approach involves constructing response function parameterised SCMs from marginal distributions P(X,Z) and P(Y,Z) where X and Y are different treatments and Z is the common outcome variable. The method enforces counterfactual consistency constraints between these SCMs, which restricts the space of possible SCMs and tightens bounds on counterfactual probabilities. When bounds remain unsatisfactory, a Maximum Entropy optimization selects the entropy-maximizing SCM as a point estimate. The framework also introduces conditional mutual information as a measure to falsify counterfactual probabilities by quantifying information flow through noise variables.

## Key Results
- Symbolic bounds that significantly tighten existing bounds on all Probabilities of Causation (PNS, PN, PS), not just treatment effects
- Demonstration that combining datasets with degenerate conditionals (e.g., high mortality rates under no treatment) can dramatically tighten bounds
- A framework for reasoning with imprecise probabilities using Maximum Entropy when bounds are insufficient for decision-making

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: Combining independent datasets with different treatments but the same outcome tightens bounds on probabilities of causation.
- Mechanism: The causal marginal problem framework enforces counterfactual consistency between SCMs constructed from marginal distributions, restricting the range of possible SCMs and thus narrowing bounds on counterfactual probabilities.
- Core assumption: Each dataset provides information about the counterfactual influence of its treatment, and combining them restricts the space of plausible SCMs.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract]: "we outline how to significantly tighten existing bounds on the probabilities of causation, by imposing counterfactual consistency between SCMs constructed from such independent datasets"
  - [section]: "By comparing the range of λX obtained from P(X,Z ) to the range of λX that satisfies these equations, we may be further able to restrict the range of λX."
  - [corpus]: Weak - no direct corpus evidence for this specific mechanism.
- Break condition: If the datasets are not independent or the marginal distributions are incompatible, the tightening may not occur or may be incorrect.

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: Information-theoretic measures like conditional mutual information can falsify SCMs and provide bounds on counterfactual probabilities.
- Mechanism: By quantifying the information that noise variables (capturing unmeasured causes) provide about the outcome, given the treatment, we can determine whether hypothesized SCMs are consistent with additional datasets.
- Core assumption: The noise variable NZ can be viewed as capturing the influence of remaining causal ancestors, and its information content can be measured.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract]: "we describe a new information theoretic approach on falsification of counterfactual probabilities, using conditional mutual information to quantify counterfactual influence"
  - [section]: "This decomposition defines an interpretable characteristic difference of the SCMs, namely whether or not NZ already provides a significant part of information about Z if X is not known"
  - [corpus]: Weak - no direct corpus evidence for this specific mechanism.
- Break condition: If the information-theoretic bounds are not tight, they only provide necessary conditions and may not fully falsify inconsistent SCMs.

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: Maximum entropy principles can be used to select the "best" SCM when bounds are unsatisfactory, providing point estimates and quantifying evidence strength.
- Mechanism: The entropy of the MaxEnt SCM serves as a measure of uncertainty in the space of plausible SCMs, with lower entropy indicating stronger evidence from the data.
- Core assumption: The maximum entropy distribution is a "good guess" when forced to assume a unique distribution consistent with constraints.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract]: "we provide an additional method of inference when the bounds are unsatisfactory: A maximum entropy based method that defines a metric for the space of plausible SCMs and proposes the entropy maximising SCM for inferring counterfactuals"
  - [section]: "When the entropy of the MaxEnt SCM is small, the space of admissible SCMs is small since the MaxEnt distribution of a large volume is large"
  - [corpus]: Weak - no direct corpus evidence for this specific mechanism.
- Break condition: If the maximum entropy solution is not unique or the entropy reduction is not significant, the point estimate may be unreliable.

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map:
  - Marginal distributions P(X,Z) and P(Y,Z) -> SCM construction -> Response function parameterization -> Counterfactual consistency constraints -> Linear programming optimization -> Symbolic bounds
  - Conditional mutual information calculation -> SCM falsification
  - Maximum entropy optimization -> Point estimates

- Critical path:
  1. Parse input marginals
  2. Construct initial SCMs and parameterise with λX
  3. Apply counterfactual consistency constraints
  4. Solve linear program to tighten bounds
  5. (Optional) Apply MaxEnt for point estimates if bounds unsatisfactory

- Design tradeoffs:
  - Symbolic vs numerical bounds: Symbolic bounds are more interpretable but harder to derive
  - Binary vs continuous variables: Binary case is tractable but limits applicability
  - Tightness vs computational cost: Tighter bounds require more complex constraints and optimization

- Failure signatures:
  - Inconsistent marginals: No feasible solution to linear program
  - Unsatisfactory bounds: λmax - λmin remains large despite constraints
  - MaxEnt instability: Entropy optimisation fails to converge

- First 3 experiments:
  1. Verify symbolic bounds on simple binary case with degenerate conditionals
  2. Test information-theoretic falsification with synthetic data where ground truth is known
  3. Evaluate MaxEnt point estimates against true values in simulation studies

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- Question: Under what conditions does combining multiple marginal datasets with degenerate conditionals provide tight bounds on the Probabilities of Causation?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper mentions that when P(Y,Z) has degenerate conditionals (e.g., P(Z=0|Y=0)=0 or P(Z=0|Y=0)=1), the bounds on PNS can be significantly tightened. However, the conditions under which this occurs and the magnitude of the tightening are not fully characterized.
- Why unresolved: While the paper provides examples and proofs for specific cases, a general theory for when and how much the bounds are tightened is not established.
- What evidence would resolve it: A comprehensive study examining various combinations of marginal datasets with different levels of degeneracy in their conditionals, and the resulting bounds on Probabilities of Causation.

### Open Question 2
- Question: How does the Maximum Entropy approach for selecting the "best" SCM perform in practice compared to other methods, especially when the bounds are not tight enough?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper introduces a Maximum Entropy-based method to select the SCM that maximizes entropy while being consistent with the data. However, the effectiveness of this approach in real-world scenarios is not fully explored.
- Why unresolved: The paper provides theoretical justifications and numerical simulations, but practical applications and comparisons with other methods are lacking.
- What evidence would resolve it: Empirical studies comparing the Maximum Entropy approach to other methods (e.g., Bayesian approaches, frequentist methods) in various real-world scenarios where the bounds are not tight enough.

### Open Question 3
- Question: Can the information-theoretic approach for falsifying SCMs be extended to high-dimensional settings with more than two treatments and continuous variables?
- Basis in paper: [inferred] The paper demonstrates the information-theoretic approach using binary variables and a limited number of treatments. However, the approach is not tested or theoretically justified for high-dimensional settings.
- Why unresolved: The paper does not provide a framework or theoretical guarantees for extending the approach to more complex scenarios.
- What evidence would resolve it: Theoretical analysis and empirical studies demonstrating the effectiveness of the information-theoretic approach in high-dimensional settings with continuous variables and multiple treatments.

## Limitations

- The method currently focuses on binary treatments and outcomes, limiting its immediate applicability to real-world continuous-variable scenarios
- Information-theoretic falsification provides only necessary conditions, not sufficient ones, for rejecting counterfactuals
- Maximum entropy inference may not always yield unique solutions when bounds remain wide
