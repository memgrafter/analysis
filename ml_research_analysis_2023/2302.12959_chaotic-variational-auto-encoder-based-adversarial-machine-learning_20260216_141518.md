# Chaotic Variational Auto encoder-based Adversarial Machine Learning

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2302.12959
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2302.12959
- Reference count: 13
- Key outcome: VAE-Deep-WNN and C-VAE-Deep-WNN variants successfully lower AUC scores during evasion and data-poison attacks on finance/cybersecurity datasets

## Executive Summary
This paper introduces VAE-Deep-WNN and its chaotic variant C-VAE-Deep-WNN for generating adversarial samples that effectively attack machine learning models. The key innovation replaces standard MLP-based VAEs with Wavelet Neural Networks in both encoder and decoder components, while the chaotic variant uses logistic map-generated noise in latent space sampling. Tested on five binary classification datasets across finance and cybersecurity domains, both variants successfully reduced model AUC scores through evasion and data-poison attacks, with VAE-Deep-WNN generally outperforming other variants.

## Method Summary
The method trains Variational Autoencoders with Wavelet Neural Networks replacing standard MLPs in encoder and decoder components. For chaotic variants, logistic map generates noise sequences used in latent space reparameterization. The approach involves preprocessing datasets with Min-Max scaling and SMOTE for imbalance handling, training VAE variants on 70% of data, generating adversarial samples from either test data (evasion) or training data (data-poison), and evaluating attack effectiveness by training LR and DT models on original or adversarial data while measuring AUC score degradation.

## Key Results
- VAE-Deep-WNN and C-VAE-Deep-WNN successfully reduced AUC scores on all tested datasets
- VAE-Deep-WNN generally outperformed C-VAE-Deep-WNN and MLP-based variants
- Data-poison attacks achieved nearly 50% AUC reduction, more effective than evasion attacks
- The proposed models performed better than baseline MLP-based VAE variants

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: VAE-Deep-WNN generates more effective adversarial samples than MLP-based VAEs
- Mechanism: Wavelet Neural Networks use wavelet activation functions with translation and dilation parameters, better approximating complex nonlinear patterns and improving adversarial sample quality
- Core assumption: Wavelet functions are better suited than ReLU/tanh for capturing underlying data distributions in adversarial contexts
- Evidence anchors: [abstract] "WNN is considered computationally efficient in solving image and audio processing, speech recognition, and time-series forecasting." [section 3.2] "Wavelet Neural Networks (WNN), hidden units called wavelons, are primarily based on wave processing having parameters that are used in wavelet analysis." [corpus] Weak evidence - no direct corpus papers addressing wavelet vs MLP performance in adversarial generation

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: Chaotic variants introduce controlled randomness in latent space via logistic map, improving attack effectiveness
- Mechanism: Logistic map generates chaotic noise sequences that replace standard Gaussian noise in the reparameterization step, creating more diverse latent representations
- Core assumption: Chaotic noise is more effective than Gaussian noise for generating diverse adversarial samples
- Evidence anchors: [section 3.4.3] "the noise is the normal distribution between (0,1)... the noise can changed as per requirement... used Logistic map to generate chaotic number for 'ε' in C-VAE" [section 5.5] "C-VAE-Deep-WNN outperformed the VAE, C-VAE-MLP shown it is better at the generation of the adversarial samples than these two variants." [corpus] Weak evidence - corpus lacks papers on chaotic noise in VAEs for adversarial attacks

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: Adversarial sample generation during training (Data-Poison) is more effective than during testing (Evasion) in reducing model performance
- Mechanism: By corrupting the training distribution, the model learns incorrect patterns, leading to fundamental degradation rather than surface-level evasion
- Core assumption: Models are more vulnerable to poisoned training data than to adversarial test samples
- Evidence anchors: [section 5.4] "all of the VAE variants affected AUC with a great percentage which empirically proves that the actual attack happened." [section 5.4] "the percentage drop is almost nearly around 50% which is making the machine learning model from correct prediction to making the model falsely predicting one class to other." [corpus] Weak evidence - corpus does not compare poison vs evasion effectiveness directly

  - Why needed here: VAEs generate synthetic samples by learning data distribution; essential for creating realistic adversarial examples

  - Why needed here: WWNs provide alternative activation functions with translation/dilation parameters that may better model adversarial data patterns
  - Quick check question: How do wavelet functions differ from standard neural network activations in handling nonlinear relationships?

- Chaotic Maps (Logistic Map)
  - Why needed here: Logistic map introduces deterministic yet unpredictable noise sequences in latent space, increasing sample diversity
  - Quick check question: What parameter range of the logistic map produces chaotic behavior?

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map: Input → Encoder (WNN/MLP) → Latent Space (μ, σ, ε) → Decoder (WNN/MLP) → Output → Loss (reconstruction + KL divergence)
- Critical path: Encoder training → Latent space sampling → Decoder reconstruction → Loss computation → Backpropagation with weight/translation/dilation updates
- Design tradeoffs: Wavelet functions offer better function approximation but increase parameter complexity; chaotic noise adds diversity but may reduce stability
- Failure signatures: Vanishing gradients (if wavelet parameters not tuned), unstable training (chaotic noise too extreme), poor reconstruction (latent space misconfiguration)
- First 3 experiments:
  1. Train VAE-MLP on a balanced dataset, measure AUC drop after evasion attack
  2. Replace MLP with WNN in encoder/decoder, repeat evasion attack, compare AUC reduction
  3. Introduce logistic map noise in latent space of WNN variant, perform data-poison attack, measure AUC degradation

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- Question: How does the performance of VAE-Deep-WNN and C-VAE-Deep-WNN compare when applied to time series or image datasets, as opposed to tabular data?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper states that future work will involve applying these models to time series and images, implying that this comparison has not yet been made
- Why unresolved: The study only tested these models on tabular data related to finance and cybersecurity, so their effectiveness on other data types remains unexplored
- What evidence would resolve it: Testing the models on time series and image datasets and comparing their performance to their performance on tabular data would provide the necessary evidence

### Open Question 2
- Question: How do the proposed VAE-Deep-WNN and C-VAE-Deep-WNN models perform against other machine learning models such as Multi-layer Perceptron (MLP), Boosting, and Bagging models in terms of adversarial sample generation?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper mentions that future work will involve performing attacks on other machine learning models, suggesting that this comparison has not been made
- Why unresolved: The study only tested the models on Logistic Regression and Decision Tree models, so their effectiveness against other models remains unexplored
- What evidence would resolve it: Testing the models on MLP, Boosting, and Bagging models and comparing their performance to their performance on Logistic Regression and Decision Tree models would provide the necessary evidence

### Open Question 3
- Question: How does the choice of wavelet function impact the quality of adversarial samples generated by the VAE-Deep-WNN and C-VAE-Deep-WNN models?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper states that the choice of wavelet function significantly impacted the performance of the attacks, with the Morlet wavelet function performing the best
- Why unresolved: While the paper identifies the Morlet wavelet function as the best performer, it does not provide a comprehensive comparison of all wavelet functions or explain why the Morlet function performs better
- What evidence would resolve it: Conducting a detailed analysis of all wavelet functions and comparing their impact on the quality of adversarial samples would provide the necessary evidence

## Limitations

- Evidence supporting wavelet superiority over MLPs in adversarial contexts is weak, with no direct corpus papers addressing this comparison
- Chaotic noise mechanism lacks empirical validation through comparison with standard Gaussian noise approaches
- Study does not establish relative effectiveness of different attack strategies or validate performance across diverse model architectures beyond LR and DT

## Confidence

- Wavelet vs MLP performance: Medium
- Chaotic noise effectiveness: Medium
- Attack success on tested datasets: High
- Generalization to other model types: Low
