# Dynamic Stashing Quantization for Efficient Transformer Training

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2303.05295
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.05295
- Reference count: 34

## Executive Summary
This paper introduces Dynamic Stashing Quantization (DSQ), a novel quantization strategy designed to reduce the computational and memory costs associated with Large Language Model (LLM) training. DSQ focuses on aggressively quantizing intermediate results stored between forward and backward passes, employing a time-adaptive approach that starts with lower precision and gradually increases it during training. The method uses Block Floating Point (BFP) quantization and dynamically adjusts precision levels based on validation loss. DSQ achieves significant reductions in arithmetic and DRAM operations while maintaining accuracy comparable to 32-bit floating-point baselines on tasks like machine translation and fine-tuning.

## Method Summary
Dynamic Stashing Quantization (DSQ) is a quantization strategy that reduces memory operations by aggressively quantizing intermediate results stored between forward and backward passes during training. The method employs a time-adaptive approach, starting with lower precision and gradually increasing it during training to balance accuracy and efficiency. DSQ uses Block Floating Point (BFP) quantization and dynamically adjusts precision levels based on validation loss. The primary results show that DSQ achieves up to a 20.95× reduction in arithmetic operations and a 2.55× reduction in DRAM operations compared to 16-bit fixed-point training, while maintaining comparable accuracy to 32-bit floating-point baselines on tasks like machine translation (IWSLT17 DE-EN) and fine-tuning (GLUE benchmark).

## Key Results
- Achieves up to 20.95× reduction in arithmetic operations compared to 16-bit fixed-point training
- Achieves up to 2.55× reduction in DRAM operations compared to 16-bit fixed-point training
- Maintains comparable accuracy to 32-bit floating-point baselines on IWSLT17 DE-EN machine translation and GLUE fine-tuning tasks

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
DSQ works by aggressively quantizing intermediate results stored between forward and backward passes during training, which are typically stored in high precision. By using a time-adaptive approach that starts with lower precision and gradually increases it during training, DSQ balances accuracy and efficiency. The method employs Block Floating Point (BFP) quantization, which allows for dynamic adjustment of precision levels based on validation loss. This approach reduces the number of memory operations required during training, leading to significant computational savings without compromising model accuracy.
