# Measuring Cross-Lingual Transferability of Multilingual Transformers on Sentence Classification

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2305.08800
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.08800
- Reference count: 40
- Primary result: Introduces IGap, a metric decomposing transfer error into interlingual transfer gap, intralingual generalization gap, and training error, showing better transferability measurement than baseline metrics.

## Executive Summary
This paper introduces IGap, a novel metric for measuring cross-lingual transferability of multilingual Transformers on sentence classification tasks. IGap decomposes transfer error into three interpretable components: interlingual transfer gap (knowledge lost during transfer), intralingual generalization gap (model's ability to generalize within a language), and training error. The metric enables more precise measurement and comparison of transferability across different models, algorithms, and transfer directions. Experimental results demonstrate that IGap outperforms existing metrics in measuring and ranking cross-lingual transferability, and can estimate transferability without requiring end-task data through the use of randomly-generated labels.

## Method Summary
The IGap metric computes cross-lingual transferability by fine-tuning multilingual Transformers on source language data, then evaluating on target language validation sets. The method involves calculating interlingual transfer gap (knowledge lost when transferring from source to target language), intralingual generalization gap (within-language performance gap), and training error. For zero-shot scenarios, randomly-generated labels on parallel sentences provide a data-efficient way to estimate transferability. The metric selects checkpoints based on minimum interlingual transfer gap while controlling for training error, and can be applied to various multilingual models including mBERT, XLM, XLM-R, and InfoXLM across multiple language pairs using datasets like XNLI and PAWS-X.

## Key Results
- IGap outperforms transfer gap and representation-based metrics in measuring and ranking cross-lingual transferability
- Cross-lingual transfer methods implicitly reduce IGap scores, indicating improved transferability
- Better-aligned representations do not guarantee better transferability, challenging common assumptions
- IGap can effectively estimate transferability without end-task data using randomly-generated labels

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: IGap decomposes transfer error into three interpretable components, enabling more precise measurement of cross-lingual transferability than aggregate metrics.
- Mechanism: By separating interlingual transfer gap, intralingual generalization gap, and training error, IGap isolates the component directly related to cross-lingual knowledge loss, filtering out confounding factors like task difficulty and representation quality.
- Core assumption: The translation used for computing interlingual transfer gap is sufficiently accurate to ensure equal task difficulty across languages.
- Evidence anchors: [abstract] "IGap decomposes transfer error into interlingual transfer gap, intralingual generalization gap, and training error, allowing comparison across models, algorithms, and transfer directions." [section 3.2] "Interlingual transfer gap Ginter measures how much knowledge is lost after the cross-lingual transfer."
- Break condition: If translations are poor, Ginter will reflect translation quality rather than true cross-lingual transferability.

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: IGap can estimate transferability without end-task data by using randomly-generated labels on parallel sentences.
- Mechanism: Randomly-generated labels ensure the knowledge to be transferred is unseen by the model while parallel sentences provide the necessary cross-lingual structure for transfer evaluation.
- Core assumption: Randomly-generated labels are sufficiently challenging and meaningful to elicit transferable patterns from the model.
- Evidence anchors: [abstract] "IGap can estimate transferability without end-task data." [section 3.2] "Using randomly-generated labels has two advantages... compared to task-specific data, parallel sentences are typically much easier to obtain for low-resource languages."
- Break condition: If the random labels are too trivial or the parallel data is of low quality, the estimated transferability scores will not correlate with true transferability.

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: IGap outperforms transfer gap and representation-based metrics in measuring and ranking cross-lingual transferability.
- Mechanism: By incorporating training error and focusing on interlingual transfer gap under controlled conditions, IGap provides more stable and consistent measurements across different models and transfer directions compared to metrics that conflate multiple factors.
- Core assumption: Training error can be controlled sufficiently through checkpoint selection to provide meaningful comparisons.
- Evidence anchors: [abstract] "Experimental results show that IGap outperforms baseline metrics for transferability measuring and transfer direction ranking." [section 4.1] "IGAP shows clear curves and consistent results among six different transfer directions."
- Break condition: If the training error control mechanism fails or if the interlingual transfer gap is not properly isolated, IGap's advantage over baseline metrics diminishes.

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map:
  - Metric computation engine -> parallel data processor -> random label generator -> model interface -> evaluation harness

- Critical path:
  1. Fine-tune source language model and save checkpoints
  2. Obtain parallel validation data and translations
  3. Compute interlingual and intralingual gaps for each checkpoint
  4. Select checkpoint with minimum interlingual gap above training error threshold
  5. Calculate final IGap score

- Design tradeoffs:
  - Using human translations vs. machine translations: accuracy vs. scalability
  - Checkpoint frequency vs. computational cost: granularity vs. efficiency
  - Random label complexity vs. transferability signal: challenge level vs. sensitivity

- Failure signatures:
  - Inconsistent IGap scores across similar models: likely issues with parallel data quality
  - Negative IGap scores in zero-shot setting: possible errors in gap calculation or translation alignment
  - High variance in IGap estimates: insufficient checkpoint sampling or unstable fine-tuning

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- Question: Does IGap correlate with downstream task performance in zero-shot cross-lingual transfer?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper shows IGap outperforms transfer gap and representation-based metrics for transferability measuring, but doesn't directly correlate IGap scores with downstream task performance.
- Why unresolved: While the paper demonstrates IGap's superiority as a transferability metric, it doesn't establish a quantitative relationship between IGap scores and actual transfer performance on downstream tasks.
- What evidence would resolve it: Empirical analysis showing the correlation between IGap scores and actual zero-shot transfer accuracy across multiple tasks and language pairs.

### Open Question 2
- Question: How does IGap behave for tasks beyond sentence classification, such as sequence generation or question answering?
- Basis in paper: [inferred] The paper focuses exclusively on sentence classification tasks and explicitly states this limitation.
- Why unresolved: The decomposition of transfer error into interlingual transfer gap, intralingual generalization gap, and training error may not generalize to other task types that have different error structures.
- What evidence would resolve it: Application of IGap to various NLP tasks beyond classification and comparison with existing task-specific transferability metrics.

### Open Question 3
- Question: What is the optimal value of the error tolerance parameter ε in Equation 5 for different model scales and training regimes?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper uses a fixed ε=0.025 for XNLI experiments but doesn't explore how this parameter affects IGap's sensitivity to training error.
- Why unresolved: The choice of ε directly affects which models are included in the minimum calculation and could significantly impact the metric's stability and interpretability.
- What evidence would resolve it: Systematic experiments varying ε across different model sizes, training durations, and task difficulties to determine sensitivity and recommend optimal values.

## Limitations

- IGap's effectiveness heavily depends on the quality of translations used for computing interlingual transfer gaps, potentially conflating translation quality with true cross-lingual transferability.
- The computational cost of evaluating multiple checkpoints to find optimal training error levels may limit practical applicability in resource-constrained settings.
- The metric's generalizability to NLP tasks beyond sentence classification remains unexplored, with decomposition framework potentially needing adaptation for different output structures.

## Confidence

**High Confidence**: The claim that IGap provides a more interpretable decomposition of transfer error than aggregate metrics is well-supported by the theoretical framework and experimental results showing clearer curves and more consistent rankings across different transfer directions.

**Medium Confidence**: The assertion that IGap outperforms representation-based metrics in measuring transferability is supported by the experimental comparisons, though the differences are not dramatic in all cases.

**Low Confidence**: The claim that cross-lingual transfer methods implicitly reduce IGap is based on limited experimental evidence and may depend heavily on specific implementation details of the transfer methods.

## Next Checks

1. **Translation Quality Validation**: Conduct experiments where the same IGap computation is performed using machine-translated data with varying quality levels to quantify sensitivity to translation noise and establish quality thresholds.

2. **Cross-Task Generalization**: Apply IGap to evaluate transferability in a different NLP task domain, such as named entity recognition or text generation, to test whether the decomposition framework generalizes beyond sentence classification.

3. **Zero-Shot Transfer Robustness**: Systematically vary the complexity and randomness of synthetic labels used for transferability estimation in zero-shot settings to determine the optimal balance between label complexity and sensitivity.
