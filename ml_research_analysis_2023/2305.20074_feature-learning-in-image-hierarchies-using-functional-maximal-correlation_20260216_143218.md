## Executive Summary
This paper introduces the Hierarchical Functional Maximal Correlation Algorithm (HFMCA), a method for learning diverse features in self-supervised learning by framing view similarities as dependencies and ensuring contrastivity through orthonormality. HFMCA measures statistical dependence within image hierarchies, from pixels and patches to full images, and aligns with a vanilla CNN topology for approximating orthonormal basis functions. This enables the decomposition of density ratios between neighboring layers of feature maps, providing interpretability by revealing the resemblance between supervision and self-supervision through internal representations.

## Method Summary
HFMCA proposes a novel approach to self-supervised learning by measuring statistical dependence within image hierarchies using functional maximal correlation. The method decomposes density ratios between neighboring layers of feature maps, enabling interpretability through the resemblance between supervision and self-supervision in internal representations. HFMCA aligns with a vanilla CNN topology for approximating orthonormal basis functions, ensuring diverse feature learning through orthonormality and contrastivity.

## Key Results
- HFMCA achieves faster convergence, higher accuracy, and improved stability compared to benchmark models on CIFAR10 and CIFAR100 datasets.
- The method shows robustness across varying augmentation protocols and provides a novel dependence measure for evaluating augmentation quality.
- HFMCA's learned spectrum and basis functions offer insights into the network's internal representations under different supervision types.
