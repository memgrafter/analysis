# Reconstruction Distortion of Learned Image Compression with Imperceptible Perturbations

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2306.01125
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.01125
- Reference count: 18
- Key outcome: Attack on learned image compression models achieves MS-SSIM degradation of up to 0.7572 while maintaining imperceptible perturbations

## Executive Summary
This paper presents an adversarial attack method targeting learned image compression (LIC) models by generating imperceptible perturbations that severely degrade reconstruction quality. The attack exploits human visual insensitivity to high-frequency components by applying a DCT-based Imperceptibility Constraint (IC) that restricts perturbations to high-frequency bands. Through experiments on the Kodak dataset using four different LIC models, the authors demonstrate that their method can achieve significant MS-SSIM degradation (up to 0.7572) while keeping perturbations unnoticeable. The study also reveals interesting findings about varying robustness levels across different LIC models and irregular perturbation patterns that may impact reconstruction quality.

## Method Summary
The proposed attack method generates adversarial examples by maximizing the Frobenius norm-based loss between original images and their reconstructed counterparts from LIC models. The attack introduces imperceptible perturbations through a DCT-based Imperceptibility Constraint (IC) that filters perturbations to preserve high-frequency components while masking low-frequency components using a binary mask. The method employs differential approximation quantization for gradient-based optimization, with the overall loss function combining reconstruction distortion and the IC constraint. The attack is evaluated under white-box assumptions with perturbation budgets of ϵ=32 and ϵ=64 on four different LIC models (Anchor, Hyperprior, Factorized, Joint) trained with varying quality levels on the Kodak dataset.

## Key Results
- Achieves MS-SSIM degradation of up to 0.7572 (decreased from 0.9205 to 0.1633) with perturbation budget ϵ=32
- Maintains imperceptibility with MS-SSIM between adversarial examples and original images at 0.9112 for Anchor model with ϵ=32
- Reveals varying robustness levels across LIC models, with Hyperprior and Joint models showing superior robustness
- Identifies irregular perturbation patterns (e.g., small square patterns) that significantly impact reconstruction quality

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- Question: How can we systematically design and implement defenses against adversarial attacks on learned image compression models that exploit the irregular perturbation patterns observed in this study?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper mentions that adversarial perturbations contain irregular patterns (e.g., small square patterns) that may significantly impact reconstruction quality, suggesting these patterns could be leveraged for detection and defense.
- Why unresolved: The paper only hypothesizes about potential defense mechanisms based on these patterns but does not explore or validate any specific defensive strategies. The irregularity and significance of these patterns need further investigation to develop effective countermeasures.
- What evidence would resolve it: Empirical studies demonstrating that specific detection algorithms can identify these irregular patterns with high accuracy, and validation that incorporating such detection mechanisms into LIC systems effectively prevents reconstruction quality degradation.

### Open Question 2
- Question: What architectural or training modifications to learned image compression models could improve their robustness against adversarial attacks without significantly compromising compression efficiency?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper finds that different LIC models demonstrate varying levels of robustness, with Hyperprior and Joint models appearing more robust, suggesting that model architecture influences vulnerability to attacks.
- Why unresolved: The paper observes differences in robustness across models but does not investigate which architectural features or training strategies contribute to this robustness or how to intentionally design more robust models.
- What evidence would resolve it: Comparative analysis of different LIC architectures identifying specific design elements that enhance robustness, followed by experimental validation showing that models with these elements maintain high compression performance while demonstrating improved resistance to adversarial attacks.

### Open Question 3
- Question: How do adversarial perturbations affect the perceptual quality and utility of reconstructed images for different downstream applications beyond reconstruction quality metrics?
- Basis in paper: [inferred] The paper focuses on reconstruction quality metrics (PSNR and MS-SSIM) but does not explore how adversarial perturbations impact the practical utility of reconstructed images for tasks like object recognition, scene understanding, or medical imaging.
- Why unresolved: The study quantifies reconstruction distortion but does not investigate whether and how these distortions affect the usability of images for specific applications, which could have important practical implications.
- What evidence would resolve it: Experiments evaluating the performance of various computer vision tasks on adversarial examples reconstructed by LIC models, comparing results to original images and establishing whether certain types of perturbations are more disruptive to specific applications.

## Limitations

- The paper lacks psychophysical validation to confirm that high-frequency perturbations are truly imperceptible to human observers
- The attack assumes white-box access to the LIC model, which may not reflect realistic attack scenarios
- The correlation between reconstruction quality and robustness is based on a limited sample of four models without broader statistical analysis

## Confidence

- High confidence: The mathematical formulation of the attack using Frobenius norm and DCT-based IC is well-defined and reproducible
- Medium confidence: The reported degradation metrics (MS-SSIM values, PSNR measurements) are measurable and verifiable through reproduction
- Low confidence: Claims about human imperceptibility of high-frequency perturbations lack psychophysical validation

## Next Checks
