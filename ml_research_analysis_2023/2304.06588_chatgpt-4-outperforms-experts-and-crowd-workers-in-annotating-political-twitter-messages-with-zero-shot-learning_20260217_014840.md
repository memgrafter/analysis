# ChatGPT-4 Outperforms Experts and Crowd Workers in Annotating Political Twitter Messages with Zero-Shot Learning

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2304.06588
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2304.06588
- Reference count: 0
- Key outcome: ChatGPT-4 achieved higher accuracy (~0.75) and reliability than human expert coders and crowd workers in classifying political affiliation of Twitter users

## Executive Summary
This study evaluates ChatGPT-4's performance in classifying the political affiliation of Twitter users based on tweet content, comparing it against expert coders and crowd workers. Using 500 tweets from U.S. politicians during the 2020 election, the LLM demonstrated superior accuracy, higher reliability, and equal or lower bias than human annotators. The model successfully interpreted complex tweets requiring contextual knowledge and inference about author intent, suggesting LLMs could enable large-scale, cost-effective interpretive text analysis in social science research.

## Method Summary
The study collected 500 tweets from U.S. senators during the 2020 election (250 per party) after filtering for length and removing retweets, replies, and URLs. ChatGPT-4 was prompted to classify each tweet as Democrat or Republican based on political context. The model was run five times each at low (0.2) and high (1.0) temperature settings. Performance was compared against ground truth party affiliation using accuracy metrics, Krippendorf's Alpha for reliability, and bias measurement through party guess fractions.

## Key Results
- ChatGPT-4 achieved mean accuracy of approximately 0.75, outperforming both expert coders and crowd workers
- The LLM demonstrated higher intercoder reliability (Krippendorf's Alpha) compared to human annotators
- The model showed equal or lower bias than human classifiers, with balanced party predictions
- ChatGPT-4 successfully interpreted tweets requiring contextual knowledge about U.S. politics and author intent

### Mechanism 1
- **Claim**: ChatGPT-4 outperforms human annotators because it can interpret tweets requiring contextual knowledge and inference about author's intent.
- **Mechanism**: The model's training on massive text corpora allows it to capture contextual information that humans need to explicitly reason about, enabling better interpretation of implicit political signals.
- **Core assumption**: The contextual knowledge embedded in the LLM's training data is sufficient and accurate for political tweet interpretation.
- **Evidence anchors**:

  - [section] "Some require detailed knowledge of US politics, such as the positions of the two parties on particular political issues in Oklahoma"
- **Break condition**: If the model's training data lacks sufficient coverage of current political contexts or contains biased representations that don't align with ground truth.

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim**: LLMs provide higher reliability than human coders due to reduced variability in interpretation.
- **Mechanism**: Human annotators bring individual biases and interpretations that vary between individuals, while LLMs apply consistent reasoning patterns across all inputs.
- **Core assumption**: Consistency in interpretation equals higher reliability for this classification task.
- **Evidence anchors**:
  - [abstract] "ChatGPT-4 has achieves higher accuracy, higher reliability, and equal or lower bias than the human classifiers"
  - [section] "To measure intercoder reliability, we calculate Krippendorf's Alpha. Figure 2 shows the result. We here see that compared to human classiﬁers, the LLM in fact has a much higher reliability"
- **Break condition**: If the consistency of LLM interpretation masks systematic errors or biases that would be caught by human diversity.

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim**: Zero-shot learning capability allows LLMs to perform well without task-specific training.
- **Mechanism**: The model's general language understanding enables it to infer task requirements from instructions alone, eliminating the need for expensive fine-tuning.
- **Core assumption**: General language understanding transfers effectively to specialized political classification tasks.
- **Evidence anchors**:
  - [abstract] "This paper assesses the accuracy, reliability and bias of the Large Language Model (LLM) ChatGPT-4 on the text analysis task of classifying the political affiliation of a Twitter poster based on the content of a tweet"
  - [section] "The model was run with high and low values for temperature... we ran the model 5 times at a low temperature (0.2), and at a high temperature (1.0) to capture variability in responses"
- **Break condition**: If the task requires domain-specific knowledge not captured in general training data, or if instruction-following capabilities degrade with task complexity.

## Limitations

- Dataset size is limited to 500 tweets from a specific time period and political context, potentially limiting generalizability
- Binary Democrat/Republican classification may oversimplify the political spectrum and miss nuanced positions
- Ground truth relies on self-identified party affiliation, which may not capture complex political realities

## Confidence

- **High confidence**: The core finding that ChatGPT-4 achieves higher accuracy than human annotators for this specific classification task on the tested dataset
- **Medium confidence**: The reliability findings (Krippendorf's Alpha comparisons) due to potential differences in annotation protocols between human and LLM coders
- **Low confidence**: The bias assessment conclusions, as the study only examines bias toward one party versus another without considering other forms of bias
