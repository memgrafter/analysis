# Backdoor Attacks and Defenses in Federated Learning: Survey, Challenges and Future Research Directions

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2303.02213
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.02213
- Reference count: 40
- Primary result: Comprehensive survey categorizing FL backdoor attacks and defenses, highlighting challenges and future research directions

## Executive Summary
This survey provides a systematic overview of backdoor attacks and defenses in federated learning (FL). It categorizes FL backdoor attacks into data poisoning and model poisoning techniques, further subdividing them based on characteristics such as semantic vs. artificial backdoors and single-trigger vs. distributed-trigger attacks. The paper reviews state-of-the-art defense strategies, grouping them into pre-aggregation, in-aggregation, and post-aggregation defenses. The survey emphasizes the importance of developing robust defenses against backdoor attacks in FL, considering practical assumptions and physical-world applicability.

## Method Summary
The paper systematically categorizes FL backdoor attacks into data poisoning and model poisoning techniques, further subdividing them based on their characteristics. It reviews and categorizes state-of-the-art defense strategies into pre-aggregation, in-aggregation, and post-aggregation defenses. The survey evaluates the effectiveness of various attacks and defenses using metrics such as attack success rate (ASR), main task accuracy (MTA), true positive rate (TPR), and true negative rate (TNR).

## Key Results
- Backdoor attacks in FL can be categorized into data poisoning and model poisoning, with various subtypes based on attack characteristics
- Defense strategies are classified into pre-aggregation, in-aggregation, and post-aggregation methods
- Existing defenses face challenges in non-IID data distributions and semantic backdoor attacks using out-of-distribution samples
- Future research directions include developing defenses that balance attack mitigation with maintaining main task accuracy and incorporating interpretable techniques for enhanced detection

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- **Claim:** Federated learning backdoor attacks succeed because the aggregation process dilutes the effect of individual malicious updates unless the adversary scales their poisoned updates strategically.
- **Mechanism:** In FL, the server averages model updates from multiple clients. If an adversary sends a poisoned update without scaling, the aggregation step reduces its impact. To overcome this, the adversary scales the poisoned update by a carefully chosen factor to dominate the benign updates and insert the backdoor into the global model.
- **Core assumption:** The adversary has knowledge of global parameters or can estimate them to choose an effective scaling factor.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract] "Backdoor attacks involve the insertion of malicious functionality into a targeted model through poisoned updates from malicious clients."
  - [section] "The most simplistic way to amplify the backdoor effect is to scale the updates from adversarial clients to dominate the updates from benign ones."
  - [corpus] Weak evidence - the corpus does not explicitly mention scaling as a mechanism.
- **Break condition:** If the adversary cannot estimate global parameters accurately or if the number of malicious clients is too small relative to benign clients, the scaled update may still be diluted.

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim:** Federated learning backdoor defenses work by detecting and excluding anomalous model updates from malicious clients before aggregation.
- **Mechanism:** Defenses in the pre-aggregation phase analyze the similarity of model updates from different clients. Updates that deviate significantly from the norm are identified as potential backdoors and excluded from the aggregation process. This prevents the backdoor from being incorporated into the global model.
- **Core assumption:** Malicious model updates are sufficiently different from benign updates to be detected as anomalies.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract] "Defenses in the pre-aggregation process aim to identify and remove (or reduce) the impact of malicious updates before the global update phase happens."
  - [section] "Pre-AD methods are implemented before the server aggregates model updates from clients... These methods first identify adversarial clients as anomalous data in the distribution of local model updates and then exclude them."
  - [corpus] Weak evidence - the corpus does not provide specific details on anomaly detection techniques.
- **Break condition:** If the adversary can make their updates appear similar to benign updates (stealthy attacks) or if the data distribution is highly non-IID, anomaly detection may fail.

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim:** Federated learning backdoor attacks can be effective even with a single malicious client by exploiting the decentralized nature of FL and targeting specific vulnerabilities in the aggregation process.
- **Mechanism:** Instead of relying on multiple malicious clients, a single adversary can launch a backdoor attack by carefully crafting their local model update to exploit weaknesses in the aggregation algorithm or by targeting specific aspects of the global model that are more susceptible to manipulation.
- **Core assumption:** The adversary can identify and exploit specific vulnerabilities in the FL system or aggregation algorithm.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract] "Backdoor attacks have received significant attention in the literature due to their potential to impact real-world deep learning applications. However, they have not been thoroughly studied in the context of FL."
  - [section] "The biggest limitation of these methods is that the updates from benign clients may dilute the backdoor effect."
  - [corpus] Weak evidence - the corpus does not provide specific examples of single-client backdoor attacks.
- **Break condition:** If the FL system has robust defenses that can detect and mitigate attacks from individual clients or if the aggregation algorithm is designed to be resilient to manipulation.

## Architecture Onboarding

- **Component map:**
  FL clients -> Client model training -> Client model update -> Orchestration server -> Aggregation -> Global model update -> Client model update
- **Critical path:**
  Client model training → Client model update → Server aggregation → Global model update → Client model update
- **Design tradeoffs:**
  - Tradeoff between model accuracy and defense effectiveness
  - Tradeoff between communication overhead and defense robustness
  - Tradeoff between privacy preservation and defense capability
- **Failure signatures:**
  - High attack success rate despite defense mechanisms
  - Significant degradation in main task accuracy after defense implementation

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- Question: How effective are backdoor defenses in federated learning against semantic backdoor attacks that use out-of-distribution samples?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper mentions that existing defenses are primarily designed for in-distribution and single-trigger backdoor attacks, and specifically notes that FLAME and DeepSight are more versatile but still have limitations. It also states that semantic backdoor attacks using out-of-distribution samples are not well addressed by current defenses.
- Why unresolved: The paper indicates that most defenses focus on in-distribution attacks and do not thoroughly evaluate their effectiveness against out-of-distribution semantic backdoor attacks, which are a significant threat.
- What evidence would resolve it: Empirical studies comparing the performance of various backdoor defenses against out-of-distribution semantic backdoor attacks in federated learning scenarios would provide evidence to resolve this question.

### Open Question 2
- Question: What is the impact of data heterogeneity (non-IID data distribution) on the effectiveness of backdoor attacks and defenses in federated learning?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper discusses that most defenses are designed for non-IID data distributions but also mentions that the data distribution among participants in FL is often unpredictable. It also notes that the effectiveness of adversarial training in non-IID settings remains uncertain.
- Why unresolved: The paper highlights the uncertainty surrounding the effectiveness of backdoor defenses in non-IID scenarios and the need for further research on the impact of data heterogeneity on both attacks and defenses.
- What evidence would resolve it: Comprehensive studies evaluating the performance of backdoor attacks and defenses under various data heterogeneity scenarios, including different degrees of non-IID distributions, would provide evidence to resolve this question.

### Open Question 3
- Question: How can interpretable techniques be effectively incorporated into federated learning models to enhance backdoor attack detection and defense?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper suggests that interpretable techniques, which have been widely studied in single-party ML models, could be applied to FL to provide transparency into decision-making processes and help detect malicious clients and prevent backdoor attacks. It mentions the use of saliency maps and visualization techniques as potential approaches.
- Why unresolved: While the paper discusses the potential of interpretable techniques in FL, it does not provide concrete methods or evaluate their effectiveness in detecting and defending against backdoor attacks in federated learning scenarios.
- What evidence would resolve it: Research demonstrating the implementation and evaluation of interpretable techniques in FL models, along with their effectiveness in detecting and mitigating backdoor attacks, would provide evidence to resolve this question.

## Limitations

- Many defense strategies are evaluated under simplified assumptions and may not generalize to complex, non-IID data distributions
- Limited discussion of physical-world applicability of backdoor triggers
- Lack of empirical validation across different FL frameworks
