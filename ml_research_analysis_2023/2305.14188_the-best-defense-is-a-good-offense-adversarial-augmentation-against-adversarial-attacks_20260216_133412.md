# The Best Defense is a Good Offense: Adversarial Augmentation against Adversarial Attacks

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2305.14188
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.14188
- **Reference count:** 40
- **Primary result:** Introduces A5, the first framework for certified preemptive defense against adversarial attacks

## Executive Summary
This paper introduces A5 (Adversarial Augmentation Against Adversarial Attacks), a novel framework that takes a proactive approach to defending against adversarial attacks by modifying inputs or physical objects before attacks can occur. Unlike traditional defenses that react after attacks are crafted, A5 uses certified bounds from automatic perturbation analysis tools to create defensive perturbations that guarantee misclassification failure under attack. The method is demonstrated through four recipes: offline robustification, online robustification with a dedicated robustifier network, co-training of robustifier and classifier, and physical object robustification. Extensive experiments show A5 consistently outperforms state-of-the-art certified defenses across multiple datasets.

## Method Summary
A5 works by optimizing defensive perturbations that guarantee any adversarial attack within a specified magnitude will fail to change the correct classification. The framework uses certified bounds (computed via tools like CROWN-IBP) to constrain the optimization of defensive perturbations. Four implementation recipes are presented: A5/O (offline robustification with ground truth), A5/R (online robustification with a dedicated robustifier network), A5/RC (co-training of robustifier and classifier), and A5/P/A5/PC (physical object robustification for optical character recognition). The method is designed to provide protection against any white, grey, or black box attack while maintaining high clean accuracy.

## Key Results
- A5 consistently outperforms state-of-the-art certified defenses across MNIST, CIFAR10, FashionMNIST, and Tinyimagenet
- A5/RC achieves the best overall performance by co-training classifier and robustifier networks
- Physical object robustification (A5/PC) demonstrates 0.73% error and 4.20% certified error on 62 characters for OCR applications
- The method effectively protects against any attack magnitude up to the specified defensive perturbation limit

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1: Certified Preemptive Defense
- **Claim:** Certified preemptive defense works by proactively perturbing inputs to guarantee misclassification failure under attack
- **Mechanism:** A5 uses automatic perturbation analysis tools to compute certified bounds on classifier outputs, then optimizes defensive perturbations to maximize worst-case cross-entropy
- **Core assumption:** Defensive perturbations can be computed to keep inputs in correct classification regions under attack
- **Evidence anchors:** [abstract] "craft a defensive perturbation to guarantee that any attack...will fail"; [section] "minimize the worst case cross entropy"
- **Break condition:** Defensive perturbation exceeds magnitude limit or classifier landscape cannot accommodate both defensive perturbation and attacks

### Mechanism 2: Co-training Benefits
- **Claim:** Co-training the robustifier and classifier achieves better results than using a pre-trained robust classifier
- **Mechanism:** A5/RC jointly trains robustifier and classifier by minimizing average worst-case cross-entropy across training data
- **Core assumption:** Joint optimization leads to better co-adaptation than separate training
- **Evidence anchors:** [section] "A5/RC is equivalent to A5/R, but it leverages the co-adaptation of C and R by joint training"
- **Break condition:** Co-training instability or failure to converge to solution with high clean and certified accuracy

### Mechanism 3: Physical Object Robustification
- **Claim:** Physical objects can be made certifiably robust by optimizing their appearance to withstand adversarial attacks after acquisition
- **Mechanism:** A5/P and A5/PC modify physical appearance w of objects by finding defensive perturbations such that acquired inputs remain correctly classified
- **Core assumption:** Acquisition process is known and can be modeled for optimization
- **Evidence anchors:** [section] "show the application of A5 for the design of certifiably robust physical objects"
- **Break condition:** Variable or unpredictable acquisition process, or physical modifications degrade object function

  - **Why needed here:** A5 builds on concepts from adversarial training but takes a preemptive approach; understanding the trade-offs helps explain why A5 can outperform traditional methods
  - **Quick check question:** Why does adversarial training often result in a trade-off between clean accuracy and certified accuracy?

  - **Why needed here:** Implementing A5 requires understanding how to parameterize defensive perturbations, optimize worst-case cross-entropy, and potentially co-train multiple networks

## Architecture Onboarding

- **Component map:** Pre-trained classifier C -> Automatic perturbation analysis (CROWN-IBP) -> Defensive perturbation optimizer -> Robustified input
- **Critical path:** For A5/RC: 1) Initialize C and R, 2) For each batch: compute defensive perturbation via R, apply to inputs, compute bounds and worst-case cross-entropy, 3) Backpropagate through both C and R, 4) Update parameters, 5) Repeat until convergence
- **Design tradeoffs:** Using pre-trained robust classifier (A5/R) vs. co-training (A5/RC) - co-training achieves better results but requires more careful training; defensive perturbation magnitude (ϵD) vs. image quality degradation; robustifier architecture complexity vs. computation time
- **Failure signatures:** High clean error with low certified error (over-regularization), low clean error with high certified error (insufficient defense), training instability or divergence (especially for A5/RC), defensive perturbations that significantly alter image appearance beyond acceptable limits
- **First 3 experiments:**
  1. Implement A5/O on MNIST with a pre-trained CROWN-IBP classifier, varying ϵD and comparing clean/certified errors to baseline
  2. Implement A5/R on CIFAR10, training a robustifier to defend against attacks with magnitude ϵA, and evaluate against autoattack
  3. Implement A5/RC on FashionMNIST, co-training classifier and robustifier with data augmentation, and compare to A5/R results

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- **Question:** How well does A5 generalize to other classifier architectures beyond the ones tested (MNIST, CIFAR10, FashionMNIST, Tinyimagenet)?
- **Basis in paper:** [explicit] The authors note that their code allows testing on "other datasets" and mention potential integration with "ℓ∞-dist neurons" as future work
- **Why unresolved:** The paper only demonstrates results on four specific datasets and architectures, with limited discussion of generalization across different model types
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Systematic testing of A5 across diverse architectures (CNNs, transformers, different depths/widths) and tasks (object detection, segmentation, NLP) showing consistent performance improvements

### Open Question 2
- **Question:** What is the optimal balance between the initial robustness of classifier C and the effectiveness of A5's robustification?
- **Basis in paper:** [explicit] The authors state "finding the optimal initial robustness of C remains an open question" and observe that co-training works best when C is "initially trained for ϵC_A ≤ ϵA"
- **Why unresolved:** The paper shows that very robust initial classifiers can be detrimental, but doesn't provide clear guidelines on the optimal starting point for different scenarios
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Empirical studies mapping performance across a grid of initial robustness levels and attack magnitudes, identifying sweet spots for different application domains

### Open Question 3
- **Question:** Does A5's robustification generalize to other classifiers like adversarial attacks do, or is it specific to the trained classifier?
- **Basis in paper:** [inferred] The authors ask "we do not know if robustification generalizes to other classifiers as adversarial attacks do" and note this in their conclusion
- **Why unresolved:** All experiments test A5 with a specific classifier, and the framework's protection against attacks from other classifiers is not explored
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Experiments where A5-robustified inputs are tested against multiple different classifiers (including ones trained with different methods) to measure transferability of the robustification

## Limitations
- Reliance on certified bounds from automatic perturbation analysis tools may not scale well to large, complex models
- Defensive perturbation optimization assumes classification landscape can be sufficiently regularized without degrading clean accuracy
- Physical object robustification assumes perfect knowledge of the acquisition process, which may not be realistic in practical scenarios

## Confidence
- **High confidence** in the core mechanism of preemptive defensive perturbations, as this is well-supported by theoretical foundations in certified robustness
- **Medium confidence** in the empirical results, as the paper provides extensive experiments but lacks ablation studies on key hyperparameters
- **Low confidence** in the physical object robustification claims, as these are demonstrated only on a limited OCR example without broader validation

## Next Checks
1. Implement A5 on a dataset with more complex images (e.g., TinyImageNet) to test scalability and verify if certified accuracy improvements hold for higher-dimensional inputs
2. Conduct ablation studies on defensive perturbation magnitude (ϵD) and robustifier architecture to understand the trade-offs between clean accuracy, certified accuracy, and computational cost
3. Test A5 against adaptive white-box attacks that specifically target the defensive perturbation mechanism to verify robustness claims against strong adversaries
