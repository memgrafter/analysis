# Domain Expansion of Image Generators

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2301.05225
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2301.05225
- Reference count: 40
- One-line primary result: Introduces domain expansion task, repurposing dormant latent directions to inject new concepts into pretrained generative models while preserving existing knowledge

## Executive Summary
This paper introduces domain expansion, a novel task for injecting new concepts into pretrained generative models while preserving their existing structure and knowledge. The method repurposes "dormant" latent directions—directions in the latent space that have negligible perceptual impact—to represent new domains without perturbing the original domain representation. This approach allows a single expanded model to supersede numerous domain-specific models while maintaining smooth transitions and compositions between domains. The method is evaluated on various datasets and achieves comparable results to domain adaptation methods, with the expanded generator supporting native smooth transitions between domains and composition of domains.

## Method Summary
The method repurposes dormant latent directions in a pretrained StyleGAN generator's latent space to represent new domains. It identifies unused directions through SVD decomposition, then restricts domain adaptation methods (StyleGAN-NADA, MyStyle) to operate only on the corresponding subspaces while regularizing the base subspace to preserve the original domain. The latent space is decomposed into orthogonal basis vectors, with each dormant direction paired with a repurposed subspace. Regularization ensures the source domain is preserved in the base subspace while new domains are learned in the repurposed subspaces, enabling a single expanded model to handle multiple domains without expanding model size.

## Key Results
- Achieved comparable results to domain adaptation methods while using a single expanded model instead of multiple domain-specific models
- Successfully demonstrated smooth transitions and composition between domains in the expanded generator
- Showed effectiveness across various datasets including facial expressions, dog breeds, and artistic styles
- Evaluated the effect of introducing multiple new domains, demonstrating scalability

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: Repurposing dormant latent directions allows new domains to be added without interfering with the original domain.
- Mechanism: The latent space of StyleGAN contains linear directions that have negligible perceptual impact. These "dormant" directions are repurposed to represent new domains by shifting the base subspace along them. This ensures that the original domain remains intact in the base subspace.
- Core assumption: Dormant directions truly have no perceptible effect on the generated images.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract]: "Interestingly, we find that the latent space offers unused, 'dormant' directions, which do not affect the output."
  - [section]: "We find that the latent space offers unused, 'dormant' directions, which do not affect the output."
- Break condition: If dormant directions are not truly dormant, repurposing them would alter the original domain.

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: The orthogonal decomposition of the latent space enables disentangled representation of multiple domains.
- Mechanism: The latent space is decomposed into orthogonal basis vectors using SVD. Each dormant direction is paired with a repurposed subspace, ensuring that new domains are represented in a disentangled manner.
- Core assumption: The latent space can be effectively decomposed into orthogonal directions that correspond to semantic factors.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract]: "Taking advantage of this finding, we repurpose such directions to represent the new domains."
  - [section]: "We rely on existing methods to factorize the latent space into such linear directions."
- Break condition: If the latent space is not effectively decomposable, the orthogonality assumption fails.

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: Regularization ensures preservation of the source domain in the base subspace.
- Mechanism: The base subspace is regularized with the original loss and replay alignment, preventing the new domains from leaking into the source domain representation.
- Core assumption: Regularization is sufficient to maintain the behavior of the source domain.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract]: "We apply a domain adaptation method, transformed to operate only on latent codes sampled from the repurposed subspace. A regularization loss is applied on the base subspace to ensure that the original domain is preserved."
  - [section]: "Crucially, we only regularize the base subspace Zbase, since the subspacesZi should be allowed to change to learn the new behaviors."
- Break condition: If regularization is insufficient, the source domain may be forgotten.

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map: Pretrained StyleGAN generator -> SVD decomposition -> Dormant direction identification -> Base and repurposed subspaces -> Domain adaptation methods -> Regularization losses
- Critical path:
  1. Decompose latent space using SVD.
  2. Identify dormant directions.
  3. Define base subspace and repurposed subspaces.
  4. Apply domain adaptation to repurposed subspaces.
  5. Regularize base subspace.
- Design tradeoffs:
  - Tradeoff between the number of new domains and the capacity of the latent space.
  - Tradeoff between the distance of repurposed subspaces and the quality of the new domains.
- Failure signatures:
  - Degradation of source domain quality.
  - Leakage of new domains into the base subspace.
  - Insufficient disentanglement between domains.
- First 3 experiments:
  1. Test the identification of dormant directions by traversing them and observing the perceptual effect.
  2. Evaluate the effectiveness of the orthogonal decomposition by applying it to a simple dataset.
  3. Verify the preservation of the source domain by comparing FID scores before and after expansion.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- Question: What is the theoretical limit to the number of new domains that can be added through domain expansion, and how does this limit depend on factors such as the size of the latent space and the capacity of the generator's weights?
- Basis in paper: [inferred] The paper demonstrates successful expansion with 400 domains, but mentions that there might be a limit based on the latent space or network capacity. It also references the popularity of knowledge distillation methods and estimates of the intrinsic dimensionality of image datasets.
- Why unresolved: The paper does not provide a theoretical analysis of the limits of domain expansion. It only provides empirical evidence up to 400 domains.
- What evidence would resolve it: A theoretical analysis of the capacity of the generator's weights and the dimensionality of the latent space would help determine the theoretical limit. Further empirical experiments expanding beyond 400 domains could also provide evidence.

### Open Question 2
- Question: How does the choice of dormant direction for a new domain affect the quality and characteristics of the expanded domain, and is there an optimal strategy for matching directions to domains?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper discusses that the choice of dormant direction has little to no effect on results, as long as it is dormant. However, it also mentions that certain directions might minimize CLIP error slightly more efficiently across random seeds.
- Why unresolved: The paper does not provide a definitive answer on whether there is an optimal strategy for matching directions to domains. It only provides evidence that the choice of direction does not significantly impact the results.
- What evidence would resolve it: Further experiments systematically varying the choice of dormant direction for different domains and analyzing the resulting quality and characteristics of the expanded domains could provide evidence for an optimal strategy.

### Open Question 3
- Question: How does the distance between the base subspace and the repurposed subspace (parameter s) affect the training dynamics and the quality of the expanded domains, and what is the optimal range for this parameter?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper discusses the effect of parameter s on training time and the visual effects captured by the expanded domains. It finds that larger values of s lead to faster minimization of CLIP error but can also result in undesired artifacts.
- Why unresolved: The paper does not provide a definitive answer on the optimal range for parameter s. It only provides evidence that values between 10 and 30 are usually preferable.
- What evidence would resolve it: Further experiments systematically varying the value of parameter s and analyzing the resulting training dynamics and quality of the expanded domains could provide evidence for the optimal range.

## Limitations
- The method's effectiveness critically depends on the assumption that dormant latent directions exist with truly negligible perceptual impact, which may not hold across different architectures
- The scalability limits of the approach remain unexplored—how many domains can be added before performance degrades is unknown
- The paper does not address potential domain interference when the number of new domains exceeds available dormant capacity

## Confidence

- **High confidence**: The core observation that dormant latent directions exist and can be repurposed is well-supported by empirical evidence in the paper. The method's ability to maintain source domain quality while adding new domains through regularization is demonstrated across multiple experiments.
- **Medium confidence**: The claim that domain expansion achieves comparable results to specialized domain-specific generators, while supported by FID scores, may not fully capture perceptual quality differences that could emerge in practical applications.
- **Low confidence**: The scalability limits of the approach—specifically, how many domains can be added before performance degrades—remain unexplored and represent a significant uncertainty for practical deployment.

## Next Checks

1. **Dormant direction verification**: Systematically traverse each identified dormant direction while monitoring the perceptual impact on generated images to quantify the actual "dormancy" level before and after repurposing.

2. **Capacity stress test**: Perform a controlled experiment adding domains incrementally until quality degradation occurs, establishing the practical limits of the domain expansion approach.

3. **Cross-architecture validation**: Apply the domain expansion method to generators other than StyleGAN (e.g., diffusion models or alternative GAN architectures) to test the generalizability of dormant direction assumptions across different latent space structures.
