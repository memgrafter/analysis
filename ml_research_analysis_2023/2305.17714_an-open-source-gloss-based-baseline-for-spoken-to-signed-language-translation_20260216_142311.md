# An Open-Source Gloss-Based Baseline for Spoken to Signed Language Translation

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2305.17714
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.17714
- Reference count: 13
- Implements a text-to-gloss-to-pose-to-video pipeline for sign language translation

## Executive Summary
This paper presents an open-source implementation of a spoken-to-signed language translation system that converts text into sign language videos through a modular pipeline approach. The system translates spoken language text into sign language glosses, converts these glosses into skeletal poses, and generates videos from these poses. The implementation supports three language pairs: German to Swiss German Sign Language, French to French Sign Language of Switzerland, and Italian to Italian Sign Language of Switzerland. The open-source nature of the system enables collaboration and improvements in the field of sign language translation.

## Method Summary
The system employs a four-stage pipeline: text-to-gloss translation, gloss-to-pose conversion, and pose-to-video generation. For text-to-gloss translation, three components are proposed: a lemmatizer to reduce words to their base forms, a rule-based system for word reordering and dropping based on sign language grammar, and a neural machine translation system trained on parallel text-gloss corpora. The gloss-to-pose conversion utilizes lexicon data from three signed languages, extracting skeletal poses from video references. The pose-to-video generation creates final sign language videos from the pose sequences, though the specific implementation details of this stage are not extensively described in the paper.

## Key Results
- Neural machine translation system achieved 13.7% exact match accuracy for German to Swiss German Sign Language glosses
- Rule-based components showed BLEU scores ranging from 0.06 to 0.52 depending on language pair
- System supports three language pairs: German-Swiss German Sign Language, French-French Sign Language of Switzerland, and Italian-Italian Sign Language of Switzerland
- Gloss-to-pose conversion utilizes skeletal poses extracted from lexicon videos for three signed languages

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The pipeline architecture works by decomposing the complex task of sign language translation into manageable subtasks. Each component addresses a specific challenge: lemmatization handles morphological variations in spoken language, rule-based reordering accounts for grammatical differences between spoken and signed languages, and NMT captures more nuanced translation patterns. The modular design allows for independent improvement of each stage while maintaining system functionality.

## Foundational Learning
- **Sign language gloss**: A written representation of signs using spoken language words, why needed for bridging between text and visual signs, quick check: can you identify glosses in a sentence?
- **Neural machine translation**: Deep learning approach for sequence-to-sequence translation, why needed for capturing complex translation patterns, quick check: does your NMT model handle rare words?
- **Skeletal pose extraction**: Process of extracting body joint positions from video, why needed for representing sign movements, quick check: are pose trajectories smooth and natural?
- **Rule-based reordering**: Grammar-specific rules for word order transformation, why needed for sign language syntax differences, quick check: does reordering improve translation quality?

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map**: Text -> [Lemmatizer + Rule-based + NMT] -> Glosses -> [Pose Extractor] -> Poses -> [Video Generator] -> Sign Language Video
- **Critical path**: Text-to-gloss translation is the bottleneck, with NMT component showing lowest accuracy at 13.7%
- **Design tradeoffs**: Modular approach enables flexibility but may propagate errors between stages; open-source implementation prioritizes accessibility over optimized performance
- **Failure signatures**: Low BLEU scores indicate rule-based components struggle with complex grammar; NMT exact match below 15% suggests limited vocabulary coverage
- **First experiments**: 1) Test lemmatizer on morphologically rich words, 2) Evaluate rule-based reordering on simple sentence structures, 3) Measure NMT performance on in-domain versus out-of-domain text

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
The paper does not explicitly call out specific open questions, but the limited evaluation of the gloss-to-pose and pose-to-video components suggests open questions about the naturalness and intelligibility of generated sign language videos.

## Limitations
- NMT system shows only 13.7% exact match accuracy for German to Swiss German Sign Language
- Gloss-to-pose conversion performance metrics are not provided
- Generated videos lack evaluation by deaf signers for naturalness and intelligibility
- System may struggle with out-of-domain text and rare vocabulary items

## Confidence
- Pipeline architecture effectiveness: High
- NMT component performance: Low
- Rule-based component reliability: Medium
- Overall system usability: Medium
- Open-source implementation quality: High
