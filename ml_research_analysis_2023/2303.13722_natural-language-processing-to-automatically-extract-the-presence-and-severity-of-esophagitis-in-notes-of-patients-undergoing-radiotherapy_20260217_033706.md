# Natural language processing to automatically extract the presence and severity of esophagitis in notes of patients undergoing radiotherapy

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2303.13722
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.13722
- Reference count: 40
- Primary result: Fine-tuned PubmedBERT achieved macro-F1 scores of 0.92, 0.82, and 0.74 for identifying esophagitis presence, severe esophagitis, and grade severity, respectively

## Executive Summary
This study developed NLP models to automatically extract esophagitis presence and severity from clinical notes of patients undergoing radiotherapy for lung cancer. The authors fine-tuned statistical and BERT-based models on gold-labeled and silver-labeled datasets to classify esophagitis according to CTCAE guidelines. The best-performing model, fine-tuned PubmedBERT, demonstrated strong performance across three classification tasks, with macro-F1 scores of 0.92 for esophagitis presence, 0.82 for severe esophagitis, and 0.74 for grade severity. The study also showed that model performance improved by selecting informative note sections and incorporating silver-labeled data, and that the models could transfer to esophageal cancer notes without additional fine-tuning.

## Method Summary
The study employed a multi-stage approach to develop NLP models for esophagitis classification. First, clinical notes from patients undergoing radiotherapy were preprocessed and relevant sections were selected. Then, both gold-standard annotations (manually labeled by clinicians) and silver-standard annotations (automatically generated using rule-based methods) were created. Multiple models were fine-tuned on these datasets, including statistical classifiers and BERT-based models like PubmedBERT. The models were evaluated on three tasks: identifying the presence of esophagitis, detecting severe esophagitis, and classifying CTCAE grade severity. Performance was measured using macro-F1 scores, and the best-performing model was validated on a separate dataset of esophageal cancer notes to assess transferability.

## Key Results
- Fine-tuned PubmedBERT achieved macro-F1 scores of 0.92, 0.82, and 0.74 for identifying esophagitis presence, severe esophagitis, and grade severity, respectively
- Model performance improved by selecting informative note sections and incorporating silver-labeled data
- The models demonstrated good transferability to esophageal cancer notes without additional fine-tuning

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The success of the NLP models in extracting esophagitis severity from clinical notes can be attributed to several factors. First, the use of domain-specific pre-trained models like PubmedBERT, which was trained on biomedical literature, provides a strong foundation for understanding medical terminology and context. Second, the incorporation of both gold-standard and silver-standard annotations allows the models to learn from high-quality labeled data while also benefiting from the larger volume of silver-labeled data. Third, the selection of informative note sections helps the models focus on the most relevant information, reducing noise and improving classification accuracy. Finally, the multi-task approach, which addresses different aspects of esophagitis classification, allows the models to capture various levels of severity and clinical significance.

## Architecture Onboarding

Component map: Clinical notes -> Preprocessing -> Silver labeling -> Gold labeling -> Model fine-tuning -> Evaluation -> Transfer validation

Critical path: Clinical notes → Preprocessing → Section selection → Annotation → Fine-tuning → Evaluation

Design tradeoffs: The study chose to use both gold and silver annotations to balance annotation quality with dataset size. They opted for PubmedBERT over general BERT variants to leverage biomedical domain knowledge. Section selection was implemented to reduce noise but may miss relevant information in non-selected sections.

Failure signatures: Poor performance on fine-grained severity classification (grade severity F1=0.74) suggests the model struggles with subtle distinctions. Lower performance on severe esophagitis detection (F1=0.82) indicates challenges with identifying clinically significant cases. The reliance on silver-labeled data introduces potential noise that may affect model reliability.

## Limitations
- Model validation was limited to institutional data from a single health system, potentially limiting generalizability
- The improvement from silver-labeled data introduces uncertainty about annotation quality and potential noise propagation
- Performance differences across tasks suggest the models may struggle with fine-grained severity classification, particularly for CTCAE grade extraction
