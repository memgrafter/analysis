# Evaluation of Regularization-based Continual Learning Approaches: Application to HAR

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2304.13327
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2304.13327
- Reference count: 23
- Key outcome: Three regularization-based Continual Learning methods (LwF, EWC, EWCLwF) show scenario-dependent performance for HAR, with no single method universally superior.

## Executive Summary
This paper evaluates three regularization-based Continual Learning (CL) methods—Learning without Forgetting (LwF), Elastic Weight Consolidation (EWC), and their combination (EWCLwF)—for Human Activity Recognition (HAR) in class-incremental scenarios. The study aims to address catastrophic forgetting when models learn new activity classes over time. Experiments use the UCI HAR dataset with six activities across three learning scenarios: switching between similar and different activities, learning different activities continuously, and learning similar activities continuously. Results demonstrate that method performance depends heavily on the learning scenario, with EWC excelling at activity type switching, LwF superior for similar activities, and EWCLwF best for diverse continuous learning.

## Method Summary
The paper evaluates three regularization-based Continual Learning methods for HAR using the UCI HAR dataset. A CNN model with 196 filters, 1x4 max pooling, and 1024 dense units is pretrained on 10 examples per class. Three class-incremental scenarios are tested: (0) similar activities then different, (1) different activities continuously, and (2) similar activities continuously. The methods compared are LwF (knowledge distillation), EWC (parameter importance via Fisher Information Matrix), and EWCLwF (combined approach). Performance is measured using overall accuracy, task-specific accuracy, and forgetting metrics across six learning rounds.

## Key Results
- EWC performs best when switching between activity types due to its parameter importance estimation
- LwF excels when learning similar activities continuously through knowledge distillation
- EWCLwF combines strengths for diverse activity learning by balancing plasticity and stability
- No single method outperforms others across all learning scenarios

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- **Claim:** EWC outperforms other methods when switching between activity types due to its parameter importance estimation via Fisher Information Matrix.
- **Mechanism:** EWC computes the Fisher Information Matrix (FIM) for each parameter after training on a task, estimating the importance of each parameter to the task. When learning a new task, EWC penalizes changes to parameters deemed important to previous tasks, preserving knowledge while allowing plasticity.
- **Core assumption:** The FIM provides an accurate estimate of parameter importance, and the posterior distribution can be approximated with a Gaussian distribution.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract]: "EWC performs best when switching between activity types"
  - [section]: "EWC estimates the importance of the model parameters with the posterior distribution... It penalizes the model when there is a significant change to those important parameters in the new task."
  - [corpus]: Weak evidence - corpus papers discuss EWC but don't directly support the switching scenario claim.
- **Break condition:** The FIM approximation becomes inaccurate (near-zero gradients), or the assumption of parameter independence breaks down in highly correlated parameter spaces.

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim:** LwF excels when learning similar activities continuously because it directly transfers knowledge from the teacher model using knowledge distillation.
- **Mechanism:** LwF uses the teacher model's output probabilities as soft targets for the student model. The knowledge distillation loss encourages the student to produce similar probability distributions to the teacher, preserving learned representations while adapting to new data.
- **Core assumption:** The teacher model's output probabilities contain meaningful information about the relationships between classes that should be preserved.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract]: "LwF is superior for similar activities"
  - [section]: "LwF uses knowledge distillation (KD) loss as a regularization term, transferring the knowledge from teacher to student model."
  - [corpus]: Weak evidence - corpus papers discuss LwF but don't directly support the similar activities claim.
- **Break condition:** The teacher model becomes outdated or irrelevant, or the new data distribution differs significantly from the teacher's training distribution.

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim:** EWCLwF combines the strengths of EWC and LwF, performing best when learning diverse activities continuously by balancing plasticity and stability.
- **Mechanism:** EWCLwF combines the knowledge distillation loss from LwF with the parameter importance penalty from EWC. This dual regularization approach provides both direct output preservation and parameter-level protection against forgetting.
- **Core assumption:** The combination of knowledge distillation and parameter importance penalties provides complementary regularization benefits.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract]: "EWCLwF excels when learning diverse activities continuously"
  - [section]: "EWCLwF was proposed to combine EWC and LwF. The loss function is denoted in (6):"
  - [corpus]: Weak evidence - corpus papers discuss EWCLwF but don't directly support the diverse activities claim.
- **Break condition:** The combined regularization becomes too strong, preventing adequate learning of new tasks, or the two regularization terms conflict with each other.

- **Design tradeoffs:**
  - LwF vs EWC: LwF provides direct output preservation but may be too rigid; EWC allows more flexibility but relies on accurate parameter importance estimation.
  - EWCLwF complexity: Combining both methods increases implementation complexity but may provide better overall performance.
  - Hyperparameter sensitivity: Each method has multiple hyperparameters (learning rates, regularization strengths) that require careful tuning.

- **Failure signatures:**
  - LwF: If forgetting is high despite using knowledge distillation, the teacher model may be outdated or the temperature scaling may be incorrect.
  - EWC: If performance degrades over time, the Fisher Information Matrix approximation may be breaking down.
  - EWCLwF: If neither LwF nor EWC strengths are apparent, the regularization terms may be conflicting or the hyperparameters may be poorly tuned.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- Question: How can we develop a dynamic continual learning method that automatically adapts to different learning scenarios in HAR applications?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The authors discuss the need for "a dynamic learning technique that can help the model deal with a variety of classes that the model has not seen before" and mention a "meta-learner could adapt and choose which strategy in the portfolio of state-of-the-art CL approaches to use depending on the found clustering."
- Why unresolved: The paper shows that no single regularization method performs best across all scenarios, and while they suggest a meta-learning approach, they don't implement or test such a system.
- What evidence would resolve it: Development and empirical testing of a meta-learner that can accurately detect the learning scenario (similar activities, different activities, or mixed) and dynamically switch between LwF, EWC, or EWCLwF methods, showing improved performance across all scenarios compared to using a single method.

### Open Question 2
- Question: How can continual learning methods be improved for long-term learning scenarios with many incremental classes?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The authors note that "EWC has shown drawbacks when learning new classes incrementally" and "the regularization strength is initially very high and lowered only to decay stability" in long-term scenarios.
- Why unresolved: While the paper demonstrates EWC's limitations over multiple learning rounds, it doesn't propose or test solutions to maintain performance in extended learning scenarios.
- What evidence would resolve it: Implementation of adaptive regularization strength adjustment mechanisms or alternative methods that maintain consistent performance across many incremental learning rounds without catastrophic forgetting.

### Open Question 3
- Question: What is the optimal combination of regularization and replay methods for continual learning in HAR applications?
- Basis in paper: [inferred] The authors suggest "One research avenue to this problem could be the combination of regularization and replay methods which can be more all-purpose since the latter have access to some stored data."
- Why unresolved: The paper only evaluates pure regularization methods and doesn't explore hybrid approaches that combine regularization with replay mechanisms.
- What evidence would resolve it: Empirical comparison of hybrid methods (combining regularization with replay) against pure regularization methods across various HAR learning scenarios, demonstrating superior performance in terms of accuracy and forgetting prevention.

## Limitations
- The study relies heavily on the UCI HAR dataset, which may not generalize to more complex real-world scenarios with higher variability and noise.
- The CNN architecture is specified as derived from a previous study but lacks detailed implementation specifics, creating uncertainty in exact reproduction.
- The three regularization methods are compared under fixed hyperparameters without extensive sensitivity analysis, potentially missing optimal configurations.
