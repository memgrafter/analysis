# Calibration of Quantum Decision Theory: Aversion to Large Losses and Predictability of Probabilistic Choices

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2303.02028
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.02028
- **Reference count:** 40
- **Primary result:** QDT model outperforms logit-CPT in binary risky choice dataset, with attraction factor capturing large loss aversion

## Executive Summary
This paper presents the first calibration of quantum decision theory (QDT) to a binary risky choice dataset. The authors propose a parametric formulation combining stochastic cumulative prospect theory for utility factors with constant absolute risk aversion for attraction factors, allowing separation of loss aversion effects. The calibrated QDT model demonstrates significant performance improvements over traditional logit-CPT models across multiple fit criteria and both aggregate and individual levels. The attraction factor proves particularly effective at capturing aversion to large losses, representing a novel methodological contribution to decision theory modeling.

## Method Summary
The authors developed a parametric analytical formulation of quantum decision theory that combines elements of stochastic cumulative prospect theory (logit-CPT) for the utility factor with constant absolute risk aversion (CARA) for the attraction factor. This formulation allows separation of aversion to extreme losses and transfers it into the QDT attraction factor. The model was calibrated to a binary risky choice dataset using maximum likelihood estimation, with parameter penalization via the Wilks test of nested hypotheses to account for the larger number of QDT parameters. Performance was evaluated using multiple fit criteria and tested through both aggregate and individual-level analyses, including out-of-sample prediction on a second experiment iteration.

## Key Results
- QDT model significantly outperforms logit-CPT at both aggregate and individual levels
- Performance advantage confirmed across all considered fit criteria
- Attraction factor most effective for prospects with large losses
- Out-of-sample predictions (second iteration) show robust QDT superiority

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The mechanism relies on the quantum decision theory's ability to separate cognitive processing stages through its utility and attraction factors. By using logit-CPT for utility (capturing traditional prospect theory effects) and CARA for attraction (modeling quantum interference effects), the model can isolate and capture different aspects of decision-making. The attraction factor specifically captures quantum coherence effects that manifest as aversion to large losses, which traditional models cannot separate from other utility components.

## Foundational Learning
- **Quantum Decision Theory**: Framework modeling decision-making as quantum measurement processes; needed for understanding non-classical interference effects in choices
- **Stochastic Cumulative Prospect Theory**: Probabilistic extension of prospect theory incorporating reference points and probability weighting; needed for capturing utility-based risk preferences
- **Constant Absolute Risk Aversion**: Utility function maintaining constant risk aversion regardless of wealth; needed for tractable attraction factor formulation
- **Wilks Test for Nested Hypotheses**: Statistical test comparing models with different parameter counts; needed for fair comparison between QDT and simpler models
- **Binary Risky Choice Paradigms**: Experimental design presenting choices between probabilistic outcomes; needed for the empirical context of the study
- **Maximum Likelihood Estimation**: Statistical method for parameter fitting; needed for calibrating the parametric QDT formulation

## Architecture Onboarding

**Component Map:** QDT model <- logit-CPT utility factor + CARA attraction factor -> likelihood function -> parameter estimation

**Critical Path:** Prospect description → Utility calculation (logit-CPT) → Attraction calculation (CARA) → Quantum interference → Choice probability → Model fit

**Design Tradeoffs:** Parametric formulation enables analytical tractability but may oversimplify complex decision processes; separation of utility and attraction factors provides interpretability but increases parameter count requiring penalization

**Failure Signatures:** Poor out-of-sample performance would indicate overfitting; failure to separate loss aversion effects would suggest model misspecification; equal performance to logit-CPT would indicate no quantum advantage

**First Experiments:** 1) Compare QDT and logit-CPT on a simple binary choice dataset with varying loss magnitudes; 2) Test sensitivity of attraction factor to different CARA parameter values; 3) Evaluate model performance on choices involving certainty effects
