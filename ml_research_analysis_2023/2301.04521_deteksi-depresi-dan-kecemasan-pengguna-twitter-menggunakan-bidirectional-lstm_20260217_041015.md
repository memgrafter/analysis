# Deteksi Depresi dan Kecemasan Pengguna Twitter Menggunakan Bidirectional LSTM

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2301.04521
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2301.04521
- Reference count: 0
- Primary result: BiLSTM achieved 94.12% accuracy for depression/anxiety detection in Indonesian Twitter data

## Executive Summary
This study proposes a Bidirectional LSTM (BiLSTM) model for detecting depression and anxiety in Indonesian Twitter users. The BiLSTM architecture processes text in both forward and backward directions simultaneously, capturing contextual information that standard LSTM misses by only reading in one direction. The model outperformed traditional machine learning methods and standard LSTM, achieving 94.12% accuracy with 0.9759 precision and 0.8386 recall on a dataset of 2,751 Indonesian tweets.

## Method Summary
The study used a dataset of 2,751 Indonesian tweets annotated for depression/anxiety (1,857 negative, 894 positive). Text preprocessing removed numbers, URLs, mentions, and punctuation while preserving meaning through no stemming, stopword removal, or slang normalization. The Keras tokenizer converted words to integers with zero-padding to 1000 tokens. The BiLSTM model featured 200-dimensional embeddings, sigmoid activation, Adam optimizer with 1e-3 learning rate, batch size 64, L2 regularization, 0.5 dropout, and 25 epochs. Results were compared against k-NN, SVM, Decision Tree, Naïve Bayes, MLP, and standard LSTM baselines.

## Key Results
- BiLSTM achieved highest accuracy of 94.12% compared to 87.06% for standard LSTM
- Precision of 0.9759 and recall of 0.8386 demonstrated strong detection capability
- Outperformed all traditional ML baselines (k-NN, SVM, Decision Tree, Naïve Bayes, MLP)

## Limitations
- Dataset size of 2,751 tweets is relatively small for deep learning applications
- Class imbalance (1,857 vs 894 samples) could affect model generalization
- Only evaluated on Indonesian language data, limiting cross-linguistic generalizability

## Confidence

- High confidence: The superior performance of BiLSTM over standard LSTM (94.12% vs 87.06% accuracy) is well-supported by the results presented in Table 3. The architectural improvements of bidirectional processing are theoretically sound and empirically validated.
- Medium confidence: The claim that BiLSTM better captures long-term dependencies is supported by the gradient vanishing discussion, but the specific impact on mental health detection accuracy could benefit from additional ablation studies.
- Low confidence: The assertion that standard LSTM's one-directional reading is insufficient for this task lacks strong empirical backing beyond the performance comparison, and alternative explanations for the performance gap cannot be ruled out.
