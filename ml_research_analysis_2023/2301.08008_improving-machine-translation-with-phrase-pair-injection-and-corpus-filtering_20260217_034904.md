# Improving Machine Translation with Phrase Pair Injection and Corpus Filtering

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2301.08008
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2301.08008
- Authors: 
- Reference count: 9
- Key outcome: The proposed approach achieves significant improvements in Machine Translation (MT) systems for 3 low-resource language pairs, Hindi-Marathi, English-Marathi, and English-Pashto, and 6 translation directions by up to 2.7 BLEU points, on the FLORES test data.

## Executive Summary
This paper proposes a combination of Phrase Pair Injection (PPI) and LaBSE-based Corpus Filtering to improve the quality and quantity of parallel data extracted from a large pseudo-parallel corpus. The authors extract good quality parallel sentences and phrases from the pseudo-parallel corpus, augment them with the parallel corpus, and train Neural Machine Translation (NMT) models. The proposed approach achieves significant improvements in Machine Translation (MT) systems for 3 low-resource language pairs, Hindi-Marathi, English-Marathi, and English-Pashto, and 6 translation directions by up to 2.7 BLEU points, on the FLORES test data. The results show that the models trained using the extracted phrase pairs and parallel sentences outperform the models trained using the whole pseudo-parallel corpus when augmented with the parallel corpus.

## Method Summary
The paper combines Phrase Pair Injection (PPI) with LaBSE-based Corpus Filtering to improve MT quality. First, LaBSE filters out low-quality parallel sentences from the pseudo-parallel corpus. Then, PPI extracts the longest unique phrase pairs from a PBSMT phrase table, and LaBSE filters these phrase pairs. The filtered parallel sentences and phrase pairs are augmented with the parallel corpus, and Transformer-based NMT models are trained on this augmented data. The approach aims to reduce noise and redundancy in the pseudo-parallel corpus while increasing the quantity of high-quality training data.

## Key Results
- Significant BLEU improvements (up to 2.7 points) for 3 low-resource language pairs and 6 translation directions
- Models trained on extracted phrase pairs and parallel sentences outperform models trained on full pseudo-parallel corpus when augmented with parallel corpus
- chrF scores also show consistent improvements across language pairs

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: Combining Phrase Pair Injection (PPI) with LaBSE-based corpus filtering improves MT quality by extracting both high-quality parallel sentences and phrase pairs from pseudo-parallel corpora.
- Mechanism: The approach first uses LaBSE to filter out low-quality parallel sentences from the pseudo-parallel corpus, then applies PPI to extract the longest unique phrase pairs, and finally applies LaBSE again to filter those phrase pairs. This two-stage filtering ensures both the sentence and phrase-level data are of high quality before being augmented with the parallel corpus for NMT training.
- Core assumption: The quality of pseudo-parallel data can be effectively improved by combining neural-based filtering (LaBSE) with phrase extraction and re-filtering.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "We extract good quality parallel sentences and phrases from the pseudo-parallel corpus... augment them with the parallel corpus, and train Neural Machine Translation (NMT) models."
  - [section 3.2] "We perform the LaBSE-based filtering on these longest unique phrase pairs to remove the poor quality phrase pairs."
  - [corpus] Weak - the paper does not provide direct quantitative evidence that the filtered phrase pairs are of higher quality than unfiltered ones, only that the final BLEU scores improve.
- Break condition: If the pseudo-parallel corpus contains mostly low-quality sentence pairs, the LaBSE filtering threshold may become too restrictive, leading to very small training sets and potentially worse performance than using the full corpus.

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: Using the longest unique phrase pairs from the phrase table reduces redundancy and noise compared to using all extracted phrase pairs.
- Mechanism: The phrase table generated by the PBSMT model contains multiple and repetitive phrase pairs for each sentence pair. By selecting only the longest unique phrase pairs, the approach reduces redundancy and focuses on more informative phrase pairs, which are then further filtered by LaBSE to remove poor quality pairs.
- Core assumption: Longer phrase pairs are more informative and less likely to be noisy than shorter ones, and uniqueness reduces redundancy.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [section 3.2] "As each sentence pair leads to multiple and repetitive phrase pairs in the Phrase Table, we only keep the longest unique phrase pairs from the extracted phrase pairs."
  - [corpus] Weak - the paper does not provide direct evidence that longest unique phrase pairs are less noisy or more informative than all extracted pairs, only that this selection is made.
- Break condition: If the longest unique phrase pairs still contain significant noise or if the PBSMT model generates very few unique pairs, the quality improvement may be minimal.

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: Augmenting the parallel corpus with filtered pseudo-parallel data improves NMT performance more than using the full pseudo-parallel corpus alone.
- Mechanism: By combining the high-quality parallel sentences and phrase pairs extracted from the pseudo-parallel corpus with the existing parallel corpus, the NMT model receives additional training data that is of higher quality than the unfiltered pseudo-parallel corpus, leading to improved translation quality as measured by BLEU and chrF scores.
- Core assumption: The quality gain from the filtered pseudo-parallel data outweighs any potential negative effects of including some lower-quality pairs that pass the filtering thresholds.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "We show that the extracted parallel sentences and phrases significantly improve the performance of the MT systems."
  - [section 5] "We can observe from the results that the models trained using the extracted phrase pairs and parallel sentences outperform the models trained using the whole pseudo-parallel corpus when augmented with the parallel corpus."
  - [corpus] Moderate - the paper shows BLEU score improvements but does not analyze the quality distribution of the filtered vs. unfiltered data.
- Break condition: If the filtering thresholds are set too low, the augmented data may include too much noise, negating the quality benefits and potentially degrading performance.

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map: Pseudo-parallel corpus -> LaBSE model -> PBSMT model -> Phrase table -> NMT model -> BLEU/chrF metrics

- Critical path:
  1. Generate sentence embeddings for pseudo-parallel corpus using LaBSE
  2. Compute cosine similarities and filter parallel sentences
  3. Train PBSMT model on pseudo-parallel corpus
  4. Extract longest unique phrase pairs from phrase table
  5. Apply LaBSE filtering to phrase pairs
  6. Augment parallel corpus with filtered sentences and phrase pairs
  7. Train NMT model on augmented corpus
  8. Evaluate on test data

- Design tradeoffs:
  - Filtering threshold selection: Higher thresholds yield higher quality data but smaller corpus size, potentially leading to underfitting. Lower thresholds include more data but may introduce noise.
  - Phrase pair selection: Using longest unique pairs reduces redundancy but may miss shorter, valid phrase pairs. Including all pairs increases data size but adds noise and redundancy.
  - Computational cost: LaBSE filtering is computationally expensive for large corpora, requiring significant GPU resources and time.

- Failure signatures:
  - Poor BLEU scores despite increased corpus size: Indicates that the filtering thresholds are too low, allowing too much noise into the training data.
  - Very small training corpora after filtering: Indicates that the filtering thresholds are too high, resulting in insufficient training data.
  - NMT model training instability or slow convergence: Could indicate issues with the augmented data, such as inconsistent quality or insufficient diversity.

- First 3 experiments:
  1. Train NMT model on parallel corpus only (baseline) and evaluate BLEU score.
  2. Apply LaBSE filtering to pseudo-parallel corpus with a moderate threshold, augment with parallel corpus, and train NMT model. Evaluate BLEU score and compare to baseline.
  3. Implement PPI to extract longest unique phrase pairs from pseudo-parallel corpus, apply LaBSE filtering to these pairs, augment with parallel corpus and LaBSE-filtered sentences, and train NMT model. Evaluate BLEU score and compare to previous experiments.

- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper mentions using various threshold values in LaBSE Filtering and Phrase Pair Injection and the need to try multiple combinations to obtain good results.
- Why unresolved: The paper does not provide a systematic analysis of how different threshold combinations affect the extracted data and translation performance.
- What evidence would resolve it: A comprehensive study varying the threshold values in both LaBSE filtering and Phrase Pair Injection, and evaluating the resulting translation performance across different language pairs.

### Open Question 2

- Why unresolved: The current study is limited to three specific language pairs, and the generalizability of the approach to other low-resource languages is yet to be demonstrated.
- What evidence would resolve it: Applying the proposed approach to a diverse set of low-resource language pairs and evaluating the translation performance improvements.

### Open Question 3
- Question: What is the computational cost and scalability of the proposed approach, especially when dealing with very large pseudo-parallel corpora?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper mentions that computing sentence embeddings using LaBSE for a huge corpus is computationally expensive.
- Why unresolved: The paper does not provide a detailed analysis of the computational resources required or the scalability of the approach for larger datasets.
- What evidence would resolve it: A thorough evaluation of the computational cost and runtime of the proposed approach on corpora of varying sizes, along with potential optimizations for scalability.

## Limitations
- The approach relies heavily on the quality of the pseudo-parallel corpus and the effectiveness of the LaBSE filtering thresholds, which are not fully detailed for each language pair.
- The paper does not provide direct quantitative evidence that the longest unique phrase pairs are more informative or less noisy than all extracted pairs, only that this selection method is used.
- The computational cost of LaBSE filtering for large corpora is significant and may limit practical applicability.

## Confidence
- High confidence in the overall experimental methodology and the use of established metrics (BLEU/chrF) for evaluation.
- Medium confidence in the effectiveness of combining PPI with LaBSE filtering, as the paper shows BLEU score improvements but does not provide detailed analysis of the quality distribution of filtered vs. unfiltered data.
- Low confidence in the specific threshold values and hyperparameter settings used for LaBSE filtering and PPI extraction, as these are not fully specified for each language pair.
