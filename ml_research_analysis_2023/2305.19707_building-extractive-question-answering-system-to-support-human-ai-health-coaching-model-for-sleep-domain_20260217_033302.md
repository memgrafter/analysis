# Building Extractive Question Answering System to Support Human-AI Health Coaching Model for Sleep Domain

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2305.19707
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.19707
- Reference count: 4
- Primary result: Domain-specific QA system fine-tuned on SleepQA dataset achieves 24% Exact Match, outperforming baseline in human evaluation (40% of cases) but not in automatic metrics.

## Executive Summary
This paper develops a domain-specific extractive QA system for sleep-related health coaching questions. The authors created SleepQA, a dataset of 7,005 passages and 5,000 expert-annotated labels, and fine-tuned PubMedBERT for retrieval and BioBERT for reading. While automatic evaluation showed the system underperforming a baseline (24% vs 30% EM), human evaluation on real-world questions demonstrated the fine-tuned system's superiority in 40% of cases. A data-centric framework improved performance by 17% through passage selection enhancement and question reformulation, though it still didn't exceed the baseline on test labels.

## Method Summary
The authors built a two-stage QA pipeline with PubMedBERT retrieval and BioBERT reader models fine-tuned on the SleepQA dataset. They applied a data-centric framework using passage selection enhancement and question reformulation (paraphrasing, word replacement, back translation) to improve performance. The system was integrated into a human-AI health coaching model and tested in a pilot RCT with 15 participants.

## Key Results
- Fine-tuned system achieved 24% Exact Match on test labels, below the baseline's 30% EM
- Human evaluation showed the fine-tuned system outperformed baseline in 40% of real-world questions
- Data-centric enhancement improved performance by 17% (to 28% EM) through better negative passage selection and question reformulation

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
Fine-tuning domain-specific BERT models on expert-annotated sleep QA pairs improves the system's ability to answer sleep-related factoid questions. Domain-specific pre-training (PubMedBERT) + task-specific fine-tuning (BioBERT) adapts general language representations to sleep medicine vocabulary and QA patterns, increasing precision in extracting answers from relevant passages. Core assumption: Sleep-domain language and question-answer patterns differ sufficiently from general domain that general BERT models underperform without fine-tuning.

### Mechanism 2
Human evaluation on real-world questions can reveal system effectiveness that automatic metrics miss. Real-world questions reflect actual coaching scenarios; human assessors can judge answer usefulness, coherence, and appropriateness in context, whereas EM-based metrics only check exact span matches. Core assumption: The distribution of real-world questions differs from curated test labels, and human relevance judgments capture practical utility better than exact string matching.

### Mechanism 3
Data-centric enhancement methods (passage selection and question reformulation) improve retrieval and reader performance on test labels. Better negative passage selection reduces false positives during retrieval training; question reformulation via paraphrasing/back translation increases model robustness to lexical variation, leading to higher EM scores. Core assumption: Initial dataset has noisy or suboptimal negative examples and questions that are too rigid, limiting generalization; augmenting and refining data addresses these gaps.

## Foundational Learning

- **Domain-specific pre-training (e.g., PubMedBERT)**
  - Why needed here: Sleep medicine contains specialized terminology and context that general BERT models do not capture well; pre-training on biomedical corpora aligns embeddings with domain vocabulary.
  - Quick check question: What is the difference between general BERT and PubMedBERT in terms of training corpus?

- **Extractive QA pipeline (retrieval + reader)**
  - Why needed here: In a human-AI coaching model, the QA system must quickly surface precise evidence from a knowledge base; a two-stage pipeline enables efficient and accurate span extraction.
  - Quick check question: How does the retriever's choice of passages affect the reader's ability to find correct answers?

- **Data-centric framework for dataset improvement**
  - Why needed here: Real-world QA performance depends on training data quality; enhancing negatives and reformulating questions improves model robustness and accuracy on held-out test sets.
  - Quick check question: Why might simple data augmentation fail to improve retrieval if negative passages are poorly chosen?

## Architecture Onboarding

- **Component map**: Knowledge Base (SleepQA) -> PubMedBERT Retriever -> BioBERT Reader -> Human-AI Interface
- **Critical path**: 1. Receive client question → 2. PubMedBERT retrieves candidate passages → 3. BioBERT reader extracts answer span → 4. Human coach reviews/uses answer → 5. Feedback loop for continuous improvement
- **Design tradeoffs**: Retrieval vs Reader accuracy (better retriever reduces reader workload but may miss correct passages); Dataset size vs annotation quality (larger datasets improve coverage but may introduce noise); Human evaluation vs automatic metrics (human judgment captures practical utility but is slower and subjective)
- **Failure signatures**: Low EM on test labels but high human satisfaction (retrieval misses correct passages but reader extracts useful context); High EM on test labels but low human satisfaction (model overfits to synthetic patterns, fails on real questions); Retrieval returns irrelevant passages (negative passage selection too lenient or question reformulation too aggressive)
- **First 3 experiments**: 1. Compare PubMedBERT vs Lucene BM25 retriever on held-out SleepQA subset using EM; 2. Test BioBERT reader with different negative sampling strategies during fine-tuning; 3. Evaluate question reformulation methods (paraphrasing vs back translation) on reader performance

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
How can domain-specific QA systems be further optimized to outperform baseline systems on both automatic and human evaluations? Basis in paper: The authors note that despite enhancements, the system did not outperform the baseline in automatic evaluation, suggesting room for further optimization. Why unresolved: The paper indicates potential but does not provide conclusive methods for achieving superior performance in both evaluation types. What evidence would resolve it: Conducting additional experiments with varied domain-specific datasets and evaluation metrics could provide insights into effective optimization strategies.

### Open Question 2
What specific features of real-world questions contribute to the human evaluation outperforming automatic evaluation? Basis in paper: The human evaluation showed the system excelled in real-world questions, suggesting differences in question complexity or context. Why unresolved: The paper does not detail the characteristics of real-world questions that lead to better human evaluation outcomes. What evidence would resolve it: Analyzing the linguistic and contextual features of real-world questions compared to test labels could clarify the factors influencing evaluation outcomes.

### Open Question 3
How do data quality enhancement methods impact the performance of domain-specific QA systems in healthcare applications? Basis in paper: The paper discusses the introduction of data-centric methods but notes that the enhanced system still did not surpass the baseline. Why unresolved: The effectiveness of data quality enhancement methods remains uncertain, as indicated by the limited improvement in system performance. What evidence would resolve it: Further experimentation with diverse data enhancement techniques and their application in different healthcare domains could elucidate their impact on system performance.

## Limitations

- Performance gap remains between fine-tuned system (24% EM) and baseline (30% EM) on test labels despite data-centric enhancements
- Human evaluation results based on small pilot study with 15 participants may not generalize to broader populations
- Data-centric framework improved performance by 17% but still fell short of baseline, suggesting current enhancement methods have not yet closed the performance gap

## Confidence

- **High Confidence**: SleepQA dataset construction and integration into human-AI health coaching model are well-documented and directly supported by results
- **Medium Confidence**: Effectiveness of domain-specific fine-tuning is supported but tempered by lower automatic evaluation performance compared to baseline
- **Low Confidence**: Data-centric framework's improvement is promising but still below baseline; exact mechanisms and hyperparameters not fully specified
