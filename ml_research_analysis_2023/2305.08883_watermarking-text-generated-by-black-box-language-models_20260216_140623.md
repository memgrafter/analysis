# Watermarking Text Generated by Black-Box Language Models

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2305.08883
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.08883
- Reference count: 40
- Primary result: Novel framework for watermarking black-box LLM outputs without model access, achieving high detection accuracy and robustness against common attacks

## Executive Summary
This paper addresses the challenge of detecting machine-generated text when only black-box LLM APIs are available, where internal model probabilities cannot be accessed. The authors propose a novel watermarking framework that injects detectable patterns into already-generated text by computing context-sensitive binary encodings for words and selectively replacing words with synonyms that represent a target bit value. Detection is achieved via statistical testing of the resulting encoding distribution. Experiments on Chinese and English datasets demonstrate effective watermark injection with high fidelity and robustness against re-translation, polishing, word deletion, and synonym substitution attacks, while maintaining semantic quality.

## Method Summary
The proposed method computes a random binary encoding for each word based on the hash of the word and its preceding word. In non-watermarked text, these encodings follow a Bernoulli distribution with p ≈ 0.5. Watermark injection selectively replaces words with bit-0 encodings using synonyms that have bit-1 encodings, thereby shifting the distribution toward more bit-1 occurrences. Detection uses a statistical test (Z-test) to determine if the observed proportion of bit-1 encodings significantly deviates from 0.5. Context-based synonym generation using BERT preserves semantic quality during watermark injection, while the distributed nature of the watermark provides robustness against common text attacks.

## Key Results
- Effective watermark injection with high detection accuracy on both Chinese and English datasets
- Robustness against re-translation, polishing, word deletion, and synonym substitution attacks
- Maintenance of semantic quality with METEOR scores around 0.9
- No need for access to internal model probabilities or training data

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- **Claim:** The watermark is detectable via statistical testing of the binary encoding distribution.
- **Mechanism:** The method computes a random binary encoding for each word based on the hash of the word and its preceding word. In non-watermarked text, these encodings follow a Bernoulli distribution with p ≈ 0.5. Watermark injection selectively replaces words with bit-0 encodings using synonyms that have bit-1 encodings, thereby shifting the distribution toward more bit-1 occurrences. Detection uses a statistical test (Z-test) to determine if the observed proportion of bit-1 encodings significantly deviates from 0.5.
- **Core assumption:** The hash function produces near-uniform outputs, ensuring that in natural text the encodings are balanced. The synonym generation and selection process preserves semantic quality while biasing toward bit-1 encodings.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract] "The encodings computed for non-watermarked text conform to a Bernoulli distribution, wherein the probability of a word representing bit-1 being approximately 0.5."
  - [section 4.1] "Building on this, we propose to inject the watermark by altering the distribution, raising the proportion of words representing bit-1."
- **Break condition:** If the synonym generation produces candidates that do not carry the expected binary encoding due to context-dependent hash collisions, or if the semantic similarity constraints are too strict and prevent sufficient substitutions, the distribution shift may be insufficient for reliable detection.

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim:** Context-based synonym generation preserves semantic quality during watermark injection.
- **Mechanism:** BERT is used to generate synonym candidates by partially masking the target word and predicting possible replacements. Semantic similarity is evaluated using sentence embeddings, global word embeddings, and contextualized word embeddings. Only candidates meeting similarity thresholds are retained for substitution.
- **Core assumption:** BERT's masked language modeling objective makes it effective at generating semantically appropriate synonyms, and the similarity metrics accurately filter out unsuitable candidates.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [section 2.2] "Since BERT's pre-training task involves predicting masked words within a text, it is well-suited for synonym generation."
  - [section 4.2] "We adopt three metrics to evaluate semantic similarity, namely sentence embedding similarity (S_sent), global word embedding similarity (S_global), and contextualized word embedding similarity (S_context)."
- **Break condition:** If the synonym generation fails to produce candidates with high enough semantic similarity, or if the similarity thresholds are set too low, substitutions may degrade text quality or fail to inject the watermark effectively.

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim:** The watermark is robust against common text attacks as long as the majority of watermark-bearing words are not modified.
- **Mechanism:** The watermark is distributed across many words, each contributing a bit to the overall encoding. Attacks like re-translation, polishing, word deletion, and synonym substitution can remove some watermark-bearing words, but unless a large proportion are altered, the statistical test remains sensitive enough to detect the watermark.
- **Core assumption:** The watermark's distributed nature means that partial removal does not completely destroy the statistical signal, and attacks that preserve semantic quality cannot remove too many watermark-bearing words without degrading the text.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract] "results under re-translation, polishing, word deletion, and synonym substitution attacks reveal that it is arduous for attackers to remove the watermark without compromising the original semantics."
  - [section 5.6] "Our watermark can be effectively preserved as long as the majority of watermark-bearing words are not modified."
- **Break condition:** If an attack modifies a sufficiently large proportion of watermark-bearing words (e.g., synonym substitution with high probability), the statistical distribution may revert to near-random, making detection unreliable.

## Foundational Learning

- **Concept:** Binary encoding function using hash-based randomness
  - **Why needed here:** Provides a way to embed information into text without modifying the original model, using only the text itself as input
  - **Quick check question:** Why does combining the hash of the current word with the previous word improve the randomness of the binary encoding?

- **Concept:** Statistical hypothesis testing (Z-test) for detection
  - **Why needed here:** Enables detection of the watermark without requiring access to model internals, by testing whether the observed binary encoding distribution deviates from random
  - **Quick check question:** What is the null hypothesis in the watermark detection test, and what does rejecting it imply?

- **Concept:** Semantic similarity metrics combining global and contextualized embeddings
  - **Why needed here:** Ensures that synonym substitutions preserve meaning while allowing controlled watermark injection
  - **Quick check question:** How do sentence-level and word-level similarity scores complement each other in selecting appropriate synonyms?

## Architecture Onboarding

- **Component map:** Text → Binary Encoding → POS Filter → Synonym Generation → Semantic Filtering → Watermark Sampling → Injected Text → Detection
- **Critical path:** Text → Binary Encoding → POS Filter → Synonym Generation → Semantic Filtering → Watermark Sampling → Injected Text → Detection
- **Design tradeoffs:**
  - Strict semantic thresholds improve fidelity but weaken watermark strength
  - Parallel processing speeds up injection/detection but increases memory usage
  - Fast detection mode is quicker but less accurate than precise mode
- **Failure signatures:**
  - Low detection accuracy despite watermark injection: likely due to overly strict semantic thresholds or insufficient substitutions
  - Degraded semantic quality: synonym filtering may be too lenient or BERT generation failing
  - High false positives: statistical test parameters (significance level) may be misconfigured
- **First 3 experiments:**
  1. Run watermark injection with default parameters on a small English sample and verify semantic similarity scores
  2. Perform detection in fast mode on watermarked vs. non-watermarked text and check ROC curve
  3. Apply a re-translation attack with 50% probability and measure F1-score and semantic similarity

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

- Question: How does the proposed method's performance scale when applied to significantly longer texts, such as multi-page documents or books?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper mentions that watermark strength increases with text length, but does not explore performance on very long texts.
- Why unresolved: The experiments only tested text lengths up to 300 words/characters, leaving uncertainty about scalability.
- What evidence would resolve it: Experiments testing the method on texts of varying lengths, from short paragraphs to full documents, measuring detection accuracy and computational efficiency.

- Why unresolved: The paper does not explore applications beyond pure text, and no experiments or discussion address multimodal scenarios.
- What evidence would resolve it: Demonstrations of the method applied to watermarking text within images, videos, or other media formats, along with robustness testing against common attacks in those contexts.

- Why unresolved: The authors do not discuss the broader societal impact or potential risks of their method being used for surveillance or censorship.
- What evidence would resolve it: A dedicated analysis of the ethical considerations, including case studies of potential misuse and proposed safeguards or guidelines for responsible deployment.

## Limitations

- Performance may degrade for specialized vocabulary or domain-specific terminology where context-sensitive word meanings differ from general usage
- Robustness claims assume attackers are unaware of the specific encoding function and synonym generation process
- Detection power may be significantly reduced for very short texts or texts with limited substitutable words

## Confidence

**High Confidence:**
- The binary encoding function using word hashes produces near-uniform distributions
- The statistical detection framework using Z-tests is theoretically sound for watermark detection
- The overall architecture connecting encoding, synonym generation, and detection is coherent

**Medium Confidence:**
- Semantic quality preservation claims depend on specific threshold choices that may not generalize
- Robustness against re-translation and polishing attacks assumes these operations don't systematically remove watermark-bearing words
- The distributed watermark nature provides sufficient redundancy for reliable detection

**Low Confidence:**
- Claims about attack resistance without knowing attacker capabilities and knowledge
- Generalization to languages beyond Chinese and English without validation
- Performance in real-world deployment scenarios with variable text lengths and domains
