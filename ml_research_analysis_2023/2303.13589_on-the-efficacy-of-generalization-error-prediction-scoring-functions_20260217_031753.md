# On the Efficacy of Generalization Error Prediction Scoring Functions

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2303.13589
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.13589
- Reference count: 0
- This work rigorously evaluates popular generalization error prediction (GEP) scoring functions under realistic conditions, finding that simple model agreement scores consistently outperform more complex approaches across distribution shifts and data corruptions.

## Executive Summary
This paper rigorously evaluates three popular generalization error prediction (GEP) scoring functions—confidence, local manifold smoothness (LMS), and model agreement (MA)—under realistic conditions including distribution shifts and data corruptions. The study finds that simple model agreement scores consistently outperform more complex approaches like confidence and LMS across all tested scenarios. The research also reveals that ensemble diversity is crucial for GEP performance, particularly when training data is compromised, and introduces a variant that improves diversity through synthetic label noise injection. Notably, the work demonstrates that simplicity bias in deep neural networks can severely degrade GEP effectiveness when simple features no longer correlate with labels in out-of-distribution settings.

## Method Summary
The study evaluates three GEP scoring functions—confidence, local manifold smoothness (LMS), and model agreement (MA)—on CIFAR10 training data and tests their performance on STL10, CIFAR10.1, and CIFAR-10-C datasets. Models are trained using ResNet-18 architecture with 200 epochs, and a thresholding-based GEP mechanism is used with threshold τ optimized on a validation set. The study systematically tests these scoring functions under various conditions including data fidelity modifications (label noise, measurement noise, under-sampling) and distribution shifts. A key contribution is the introduction of a MA variant that improves ensemble diversity through synthetic label noise injection during training.

## Key Results
- Model agreement (MA) scores consistently outperform confidence and LMS scores across all distribution shifts and data corruptions
- Ensemble diversity is crucial for GEP performance, especially when training data is compromised
- Synthetic label noise injection improves MA performance by enhancing ensemble diversity
- Simplicity bias in deep neural networks can severely degrade GEP effectiveness when simple features become uncorrelated with labels in out-of-distribution settings

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: Simple model agreement scores outperform complex scoring functions under distribution shifts and data corruptions.
- Mechanism: Model agreement measures consensus among independently trained models, which inherently captures diversity in learned representations and reduces reliance on spurious correlations.
- Core assumption: Independent model training creates sufficient diversity to detect when predictions are unreliable.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "we find, absent complex mechanisms, that state-of-the-art confidence- and smoothness- based scores fail to outperform simple model-agreement scores"
  - [section 3.1] "Across all target datasets, and corresponding levels of distribution shifts, MA is by far the most effective at predicting generalization"
  - [corpus] Weak - no direct evidence in neighboring papers
- Break condition: When models converge to similar solutions due to strong inductive biases or limited training diversity.

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: Ensemble diversity improves generalization error prediction performance, especially with compromised training data.
- Mechanism: Introducing synthetic label noise during ensemble training forces models to learn different decision boundaries, increasing diversity and improving error detection.
- Core assumption: Synthetic label noise creates meaningful diversity without significantly harming ensemble accuracy.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "we find that model-agreement scores continue to perform well and that ensemble diversity is important for improving its performance"
  - [section 3.2] "we propose a simple variant to MA that is designed to improve the diversity of the ensemble by synthetically corrupting the data using low levels of label noise"
  - [section 3.2] "our variant MA0.02 improves GEP performance on STL (over 50% on CIFAR10, CIFAR10 (MN), CIFAR10 (US))"
  - [corpus] Weak - no direct evidence in neighboring papers
- Break condition: When synthetic noise overwhelms the signal, causing models to learn incorrect patterns.

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: Simplicity bias in deep neural networks degrades generalization error prediction performance when simple features no longer correlate with labels in out-of-distribution settings.
- Mechanism: Deep networks rely on simple, spurious features that may not generalize to new distributions, causing GEP scores to be misleading when these features become uncorrelated with labels.
- Core assumption: Simple features that work well on training data may fail to generalize to out-of-distribution data.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "the research demonstrates that simplicity bias in deep neural networks—where models rely on brittle, spurious features—can severely degrade GEP effectiveness"
  - [section 4] "we demonstrated how reliance on simple features can adversely affect the GEP performance"
  - [section 4] "the gap between GEP's performance on the Corr. and Rand. variants of the target dataset increases"
  - [corpus] Weak - no direct evidence in neighboring papers
- Break condition: When the target distribution maintains the same correlation between simple and complex features as the training distribution.

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map:
  - CIFAR10 training data -> data fidelity modifications -> model training -> ensemble of 10 ResNet-18 models -> scoring functions (confidence, LMS, MA) -> GEP mechanism (thresholding) -> MAE evaluation

- Critical path:
  1. Load and preprocess CIFAR10 training data
  2. Apply data fidelity modifications (label noise, measurement noise, under-sampling)
  3. Train ensemble of models independently
  4. Compute sample-level scores using confidence, LMS, and MA
  5. Apply thresholding GEP mechanism to obtain dataset-level predictions
  6. Calculate MAE against true accuracy on target datasets

- Design tradeoffs:
  - Simplicity vs. accuracy: Simple MA scoring outperforms complex mechanisms
  - Ensemble size vs. diversity: Larger ensembles provide more stable scores but with diminishing returns
  - Synthetic noise level vs. accuracy: More noise increases diversity but may harm ensemble performance

- Failure signatures:
  - MA scores become unreliable when models overfit to training data
  - LMS performance degrades with inappropriate augmentation strategies
  - Confidence scores fail when models become overconfident on out-of-distribution data

- First 3 experiments:
  1. Compare MA, confidence, and LMS scores on clean CIFAR10 → STL10 distribution shift
  2. Evaluate effect of ensemble size (2, 4, 6, 8, 10 models) on MA performance
  3. Test MA0.02 variant with 2% synthetic label noise on corrupted training data

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- Question: Does increasing ensemble diversity through methods other than label noise injection (e.g., adversarial training, different architectures) improve generalization error prediction performance?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper introduces a variant MA0.02 that improves diversity through synthetic label noise injection and shows improved GEP performance.
- Why unresolved: The paper only explores one method of increasing ensemble diversity, leaving open whether other diversity-increasing methods would be equally or more effective.
- What evidence would resolve it: Comparative experiments testing MA variants with different diversity-enhancing techniques (architectural diversity, adversarial training, etc.) under various distribution shifts and data fidelity conditions.

### Open Question 2
- Question: What is the fundamental relationship between simplicity bias and the effectiveness of different scoring functions (confidence, LMS, MA) across various dataset domains and model architectures?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper demonstrates that simplicity bias can adversely affect GEP performance and shows correlation between simplicity bias strength and GEP degradation in a controlled setting.
- Why unresolved: The paper only examines simplicity bias in one synthetic dataset with specific features; it remains unclear how this generalizes to other domains and architectures.
- What evidence would resolve it: Systematic experiments across diverse datasets (text, audio, tabular), model architectures (CNNs, transformers, RNNs), and feature complexity levels to quantify simplicity bias's impact on each scoring function.

### Open Question 3
- Question: Can scoring functions be designed to explicitly detect and account for when models are relying on simple spurious features versus robust features?
- Basis in paper: [inferred] The paper shows that scoring functions perform poorly when simple features break correlation with labels, suggesting a need for detection mechanisms.
- Why unresolved: Current scoring functions don't distinguish between feature types, and the paper only identifies the problem without proposing solutions.
- What evidence would resolve it: Development and evaluation of scoring functions that can identify feature types (simple vs. robust) and demonstrate improved GEP performance when simple features become unreliable.

### Open Question 4
- Question: How does the choice of augmentation strategy affect the effectiveness of LMS scoring under different types of distribution shifts and corruptions?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper notes that LMS performance is sensitive to augmentation strategy choice and that RandAug may not capture properties relevant for real-world shifts.
- Why unresolved: The paper only tests one augmentation strategy (RandAug) and doesn't systematically explore how different strategies affect LMS performance across various shift types.
- What evidence would resolve it: Comparative experiments testing LMS with different augmentation strategies (CutMix, MixUp, AutoAugment, etc.) across diverse corruption types and distribution shifts to identify optimal strategies.

## Limitations

- The study's focus on CIFAR10-to-STL10 distribution shifts represents a narrow slice of real-world distribution shifts, limiting generalizability
- The lack of theoretical grounding for why model agreement outperforms more sophisticated methods remains a gap
- The paper only explores one method of increasing ensemble diversity, leaving open whether other diversity-increasing methods would be equally or more effective

## Confidence

- **High confidence**: Model agreement consistently outperforms confidence and LMS scores across distribution shifts and data corruptions
- **Medium confidence**: Ensemble diversity through synthetic label noise improves GEP performance, though the optimal noise level may be dataset-dependent
- **Medium confidence**: Simplicity bias adversely affects GEP effectiveness, but the relationship between spurious feature correlation and GEP performance needs further exploration
