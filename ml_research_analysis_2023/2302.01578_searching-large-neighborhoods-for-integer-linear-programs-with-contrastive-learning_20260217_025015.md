# Searching Large Neighborhoods for Integer Linear Programs with Contrastive Learning

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID**: 2302.01578
- **Source URL**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2302.01578
- **Reference count**: 40
- **Primary result**: CL-LNS significantly outperforms state-of-the-art ILP solvers and learned heuristics on four NP-hard problems using contrastive learning for large neighborhood search.

## Executive Summary
This paper introduces CL-LNS, a method that uses contrastive learning to train a destroy heuristic for Large Neighborhood Search (LNS) in Integer Linear Programming (ILP). The approach learns to select variables for reoptimization by contrasting positive samples (from the Local Branching expert) with negative samples (random perturbations). Using graph attention networks with richer features than prior work, CL-LNS achieves state-of-the-art performance on four NP-hard problems, outperforming both traditional solvers and learned heuristics across multiple metrics including primal gap, primal integral, survival rate, and best performing rate.

## Method Summary
CL-LNS uses contrastive learning to train a destroy heuristic for LNS in ILPs. It collects positive samples (intermediate solutions from Local Branching) and negative samples (perturbed versions of optimal subsets) to train a graph attention network that predicts variable scores. The policy selects a subset of variables to reoptimize, solving a sub-ILP with the remaining variables fixed. An adaptive neighborhood size mechanism balances exploration and exploitation. The method uses richer features than prior GCN-based approaches and employs InfoNCE contrastive loss for training.

## Key Results
- CL-LNS significantly outperforms state-of-the-art ILP solvers (CPLEX, SCIP) and learned heuristics on minimum vertex cover, maximum independent set, combinatorial auction, and set covering problems
- The method achieves superior performance across multiple metrics including primal gap, primal integral, survival rate, and best performing rate
- CL-LNS generalizes well to test instances twice as large as training instances, demonstrating strong scalability

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: Contrastive learning improves destroy heuristic quality by discriminating between good and bad variable subsets
- Mechanism: Positive samples (near-optimal intermediate solutions from LB) are contrasted with negative samples (perturbed subsets with lower improvement) using contrastive loss, encouraging the model to predict scores that distinguish good from bad subsets
- Core assumption: Positive and negative samples are sufficiently distinct in their objective improvement effects
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract]: "CL-LNS collects positive and negative solution samples from an expert heuristic that is slow to compute and learns a new one with a contrastive loss"
  - [section 4.1]: Defines positive samples as intermediate solutions within αp of optimal improvement, and negative samples as perturbed subsets with improvement below αn times optimal
  - [corpus]: No direct corpus evidence; this is a novel application of contrastive learning to ILP destroy heuristics
- Break condition: If positive and negative samples overlap significantly in quality, the contrastive signal weakens and the model cannot learn to discriminate effectively

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: Using richer features and GAT improves policy representation capacity over prior GCN-based approaches
- Mechanism: Variable node features include both standard ILP features (from Gasse et al. 2019) and additional root-node BnB features (from Khalil et al. 2016), then processed through GAT layers with attention mechanisms
- Core assumption: Additional features contain information useful for identifying good destroy subsets, and GAT can better capture interactions than GCN
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract]: "We use graph attention networks and a richer set of features to further improve its performance"
  - [section 4.2]: Describes richer feature set and GAT architecture replacing GCN
  - [section 5.2, ablation study]: CL-LNS outperforms IL-LNS-GAT-FF (imitation learning with same enhancements) and shows benefits of GAT over GCN
- Break condition: If additional features are noisy or redundant, or if GAT overfits to small training sets, performance may degrade

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: Adaptive neighborhood size improves anytime performance by balancing exploration and exploitation
- Mechanism: Neighborhood size kt starts at k0 and grows multiplicatively (γ>1) when no improvement is found, capped at β·n to keep sub-ILPs tractable
- Core assumption: Small neighborhoods find local improvements quickly, while larger neighborhoods help escape local minima
- Evidence anchors:
  - [section 2.2]: Describes adaptive neighborhood mechanism with parameters γ and β
  - [section 5.1, hyperparameter tuning]: Discusses β tuning and its impact on performance
  - [corpus]: No direct corpus evidence; this is standard LNS practice
- Break condition: If γ is too large, LNS may explore too aggressively and waste time; if β is too small, it may not explore sufficiently

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map:
  Data collection module -> Graph encoder -> Policy head -> LNS engine -> Training loop

- Critical path: Data collection → Graph encoding → Score prediction → Subset selection → Sub-ILP solve → Solution improvement

- Design tradeoffs:
  - Sample collection cost vs. quality: More diverse samples improve learning but increase data collection time
  - Neighborhood size vs. tractability: Larger neighborhoods may find better solutions but make sub-ILPs harder to solve
  - GAT depth vs. overfitting: Deeper networks may overfit on small training sets

- Failure signatures:
  - Poor performance on training set: Likely issues with feature encoding or contrastive loss
  - Good training but poor generalization: Overfitting to training instances or insufficient feature generalization
  - Slow convergence: Sub-ILP solving bottleneck or ineffective destroy heuristics

- First 3 experiments:
  1. Verify data collection produces expected positive/negative sample ratios and quality differences
  2. Test policy network on small ILPs to ensure it learns to rank variables by improvement potential
  3. Run full LNS pipeline on a single instance to validate end-to-end performance and identify bottlenecks

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- The paper does not explicitly call out open questions, but the limitations section suggests areas for future work including extending to mixed integer linear programs and exploring larger negative sample ratios.

## Limitations
- Performance comparisons rely on specific baseline configurations that may not represent all possible solver parameter tunings
- The sample collection process from Local Branching is computationally expensive and alternative expert policies are not explored
- Generalization claims are based on test instances up to twice the size of training instances, but performance on significantly larger problems remains untested

## Confidence
- **High confidence**: Claims about CL-LNS outperforming standard solvers on the four benchmark problems
- **Medium confidence**: Claims about GAT with richer features being superior to GCN
- **Medium confidence**: Claims about contrastive learning providing benefits over imitation learning

## Next Checks
