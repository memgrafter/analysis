# On the causality-preservation capabilities of generative modelling

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID**: 2301.01109
- **Source URL**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2301.01109
- **Reference count**: 6
- **Key outcome**: Generative adversarial networks preserve causal relationships only in simple cases where correlation equals causation, with limitations in complex causal structures.

## Executive Summary
This paper investigates whether generative adversarial networks (GANs) can preserve causal relationships when generating synthetic data. Through systematic evaluation of standard GANs, TimeGAN, and CausalGAN across three progressively complex causal structures, the authors demonstrate that while GANs can replicate joint probability distributions, they often converge to simpler causal models rather than preserving the true underlying causal structure. The research reveals fundamental limitations in using GANs for causal inference, particularly when the true causal structure is more complex than simple correlation.

## Method Summary
The evaluation employs a synthetic data generation approach using known structural causal models (SCMs) with three complexity levels: cross-sectional, time-series, and full structural models. Three GAN variants (standard GAN, TimeGAN, and CausalGAN) are trained on these datasets, and their synthetic outputs are analyzed using OLS regression, autoregressive models, and causal discovery methods (LiNGAM). The parameter estimates from synthetic data are compared against ground truth to assess causal preservation capabilities.

## Key Results
- Standard GANs preserve causal relationships only when correlation equals causation, failing in more complex structural models
- TimeGAN captures temporal dynamics when they exist across all variables but converges to simplified representations when temporal dependencies are limited
- CausalGAN improves causal preservation when the true causal graph is known, but requires complete and correct causal structures
- GANs consistently prefer simpler causal models due to machine learning regularization favoring Occam's Razor

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: GANs preserve causal relationships when correlation equals causation
- Mechanism: Under the assumption that observed correlations directly reflect causal structure, the GAN loss functions optimize for joint probability distribution replication, which coincides with the true causal model
- Core assumption: The underlying causal model is minimal and matches the correlation structure
- Break condition: When the true causal structure requires more complexity than the minimal correlation model, the GAN converges to a simpler representation that preserves general distribution but not causal relationships

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: TimeGAN captures temporal dynamics when they exist across all variables
- Mechanism: TimeGAN combines unsupervised GAN loss with supervised autoregressive loss to capture step-wise conditional distributions, allowing it to model temporal dependencies when they are present in the data structure
- Core assumption: Temporal dependencies exist in all variables, not just a single target variable
- Break condition: When only a subset of variables exhibit temporal dependence, TimeGAN may converge to a simplified representation that ignores the true causal temporal structure

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: CausalGAN preserves causal structure when the true causal graph is known
- Mechanism: By incorporating the known causal graph into the generator architecture, CausalGAN constrains data generation to follow the actual causal relationships rather than just joint probabilities
- Core assumption: The causal graph is correctly specified and complete
- Break condition: When the causal graph is incomplete, incorrect, or requires time-series structure that CausalGAN cannot handle, the preservation of causal relationships fails

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map: Data generation -> GAN training -> Synthetic data generation -> Causal inference -> Result comparison
- Critical path: The evaluation flow is: Generate data with known causal structure → Train GAN on generated data → Generate synthetic data from GAN → Apply causal inference methods to both datasets → Compare results to assess causal preservation
- Design tradeoffs: The paper chooses to use a simplified known causal model for evaluation rather than real-world data, sacrificing real-world complexity for the ability to definitively assess causal preservation capabilities
- Failure signatures: GANs failing to preserve causality when the true causal structure is more complex than the minimal correlation model, TimeGAN converging to simplified temporal representations, and CausalGAN requiring complete and correct causal graphs that are often unavailable

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- Question: Does the preference for simpler models in machine learning (Occam's Razor) inherently limit the ability of GANs to capture complex causal structures in synthetic data?
- Basis in paper: The paper states that "Our results point at the reason being the often-used regularisation in machine learning that builds in a preference for smaller models (as posited in occam's razor) which is not necessarily a valid principle in causality."
- Why unresolved: The paper provides evidence of this limitation but does not fully explore the theoretical underpinnings or potential solutions to this problem.
- What evidence would resolve it: Experiments testing different regularization techniques on GANs, specifically designed to capture complex causal structures, would help determine if Occam's Razor is indeed the limiting factor.

### Open Question 2
- Question: Can incorporating causal knowledge (e.g., through causal discovery algorithms) significantly improve the causal preservation capabilities of GANs, especially in cases with complex causal structures?
- Basis in paper: The paper discusses CausalGAN, which incorporates known causal graphs, and finds that it performs better than standard GANs in maintaining causal relationships, but notes that the necessary assumptions for causal discovery are too restrictive for real-world applications.
- Why unresolved: The paper only tests CausalGAN on a simple causal structure and does not explore the limitations of incorporating causal knowledge in more complex scenarios.
- What evidence would resolve it: Experiments applying CausalGAN or similar methods to synthetic datasets with increasingly complex causal structures would reveal the extent to which causal knowledge can improve GAN performance.

### Open Question 3
- Question: How do privacy-preserving techniques, such as differential privacy, affect the ability of GANs to generate synthetic data that accurately reflects the causal structure of the original data?
- Basis in paper: The paper mentions that privacy guarantees come at the price of replication quality and discusses the use of differential privacy in GANs.
- Why unresolved: The paper does not explore the specific impact of privacy-preserving techniques on causal preservation capabilities.
- What evidence would resolve it: Experiments comparing the causal preservation capabilities of GANs with and without privacy-preserving techniques, using datasets with known causal structures, would provide insights into this trade-off.

## Limitations

- The evaluation relies entirely on synthetic data with known causal structures rather than real-world data, which may not capture practical complexity
- The paper does not explore hybrid approaches that combine GANs with explicit causal modeling techniques
- The assumption that correlation equals causation in simple cases may not hold in real-world scenarios with unobserved confounders

## Confidence

- **High confidence**: GANs preserve causal relationships only in simple cases where correlation equals causation
- **Medium confidence**: TimeGAN captures temporal dynamics when they exist across all variables
- **Medium confidence**: CausalGAN performs better with known causal graphs
