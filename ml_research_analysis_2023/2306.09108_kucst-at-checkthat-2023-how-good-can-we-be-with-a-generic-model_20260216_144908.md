# KUCST at CheckThat 2023: How good can we be with a generic model?

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID**: 2306.09108
- **Source URL**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.09108
- **Reference count**: 18
- **Primary result**: Gradient Boosting achieves weighted F1-score of 0.77 in subjectivity detection (4th out of 11 teams) and 0.56 in political bias detection (3rd out of 5 teams)

## Executive Summary
This paper presents a machine learning approach to detecting subjectivity in news sentences and political bias in news articles, using stylometric features inspired by authorship attribution and profiling. The method applies a range of classifiers—including Gradient Boosting, Logistic Regression, and Random Forest—to features such as Bag-of-Words, TF-IDF, character and POS n-grams, morphological features, and BERT embeddings. In Task 2 (subjectivity detection), Gradient Boosting achieved a weighted F1-score of 0.77 and ranked 4th out of 11 teams. In Task 3A (political bias detection), the same model attained a weighted F1-score of 0.56 and ranked 3rd out of 5 teams, with a Mean Absolute Error of 0.736. Results indicate that character n-grams and Gradient Boosting perform well, while TF-IDF features are less effective. Training time remains low for smaller datasets but increases with corpus size.

## Method Summary
The approach combines stylometric feature engineering with multiple classifiers for detecting subjectivity and political bias. Feature sets include character and POS n-grams, morphological features, Bag-of-Words, TF-IDF, and BERT embeddings. Gradient Boosting, Logistic Regression, and Random Forest classifiers are evaluated. For Task 2, Gradient Boosting with character n-grams achieves highest F1-score of 0.77. For Task 3A, Gradient Boosting again performs best with F1-score of 0.56 and MAE of 0.736. Results show character n-grams are effective while TF-IDF underperforms.

## Key Results
- Gradient Boosting with character n-grams achieves weighted F1-score of 0.77 for subjectivity detection (4th out of 11 teams)
- Same model attains F1-score of 0.56 and MAE of 0.736 for political bias detection (3rd out of 5 teams)
- Character n-grams outperform TF-IDF features in both tasks
- Training time remains efficient for smaller datasets

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The approach leverages stylometric features that capture linguistic patterns associated with subjectivity and bias. Character n-grams effectively model stylistic variations, while Gradient Boosting combines weak learners to improve prediction accuracy. The combination of morphological features and n-grams provides robust representation of writing style, enabling detection of subtle linguistic cues that signal subjectivity and political orientation.

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component Map**: Raw Text -> Feature Extraction (Char n-grams, POS n-grams, Morph features, BoW, TF-IDF, BERT) -> Classifier (Gradient Boosting, LR, RF) -> Prediction

**Critical Path**: Feature extraction (especially character n-grams) -> Gradient Boosting classifier -> weighted F1-score calculation

**Design Tradeoffs**: Character n-grams provide better stylometric representation but may lose semantic information; TF-IDF offers semantic weighting but performs poorly for stylistic tasks; Gradient Boosting balances accuracy and training time

**Failure Signatures**: Poor performance with TF-IDF indicates stylistic features matter more than semantic weighting; low F1-scores suggest feature set may not capture domain-specific bias patterns
