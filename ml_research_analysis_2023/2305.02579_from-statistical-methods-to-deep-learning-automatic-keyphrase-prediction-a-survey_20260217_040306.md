# From Statistical Methods to Deep Learning, Automatic Keyphrase Prediction: A Survey

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2305.02579
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.02579
- Reference count: 39
- Key outcome: Deep learning-based extraction and generation models achieve the best results, with ONE2SET paradigm outperforming others

## Executive Summary
This survey comprehensively reviews 167 studies on automatic keyphrase prediction, covering the evolution from statistical methods to deep learning approaches. The paper provides a detailed taxonomy of extraction and generation models, summarizes common datasets and evaluation metrics, and conducts controlled experiments comparing representative models. The findings demonstrate that supervised deep learning models significantly outperform unsupervised methods, with ONE2SET generation paradigm showing particular effectiveness. The survey also discusses emerging directions including multi-modal keyphrase prediction and the role of large language models like ChatGPT.

## Method Summary
The study systematically reviews literature on keyphrase prediction, categorizing approaches into unsupervised statistical models, unsupervised graph-based models, unsupervised deep learning models, and supervised deep learning models. Experiments compare representative models from each category using identical evaluation metrics (F1@5 and F1@M) across five common datasets: KP20k, Inspec, NUS, Krapivin, SemEval. Models are trained on KP20k training set and evaluated on all datasets. The study also compares generation paradigms (ONE2ONE, ONE2SEQ, ONE2SET) and examines ChatGPT's performance under zero-shot setting.

## Key Results
- Supervised deep learning-based extraction and generation models achieve the best results, confirming the trend from statistical models to deep learning
- ONE2SET generation paradigm outperforms ONE2SEQ and ONE2ONE paradigms
- ChatGPT shows promising zero-shot performance but remains inferior to state-of-the-art supervised models
- Unsupervised statistical extraction models perform worst among all approaches

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- **Claim**: Supervised deep learning models outperform unsupervised ones because they learn task-specific feature representations directly from labeled data rather than relying on hand-crafted heuristics.
- **Mechanism**: Neural encoders (e.g., BERT, SciBERT) map documents into dense semantic spaces; decoders or classifiers then use these representations to predict keyphrases. The supervised loss directly optimizes for the target metric (e.g., F1), enabling end-to-end fine-tuning.
- **Core assumption**: The training data distribution is similar to the test distribution; labeled data captures the keyphrase quality criteria.
- **Evidence anchors**:
  - [abstract]: "deep learning-based extraction and generation models achieve the best results"
  - [section]: "deep learning-based models achieve the best result, echoing the development trend of natural language processing studies from statistical models to deep learning-based models"
- **Break condition**: Large domain shift between training and test sets (e.g., Inspec vs KP20k) degrades performance.

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim**: ONE2SET generation paradigm outperforms ONE2SEQ and ONE2ONE because it models keyphrase generation as a set prediction problem, eliminating the need for ordering and reducing duplication.
- **Mechanism**: The decoder produces multiple keyphrases in parallel, each conditioned on a learnable control code. The Hungarian algorithm aligns predictions with targets during training, ensuring coverage without enforcing sequence order.
- **Core assumption**: Keyphrases are independent enough that parallel generation does not harm quality; the model can learn to generate diverse phrases from control codes.
- **Evidence anchors**:
  - [abstract]: "ONE2SET generation paradigm outperforms other paradigms"
  - [section]: "its decoder utilizes different learnable control codes to generate a set of keyphrases in parallel"
- **Break condition**: If keyphrases have strong semantic dependencies, parallel generation may miss necessary ordering cues.

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim**: ChatGPT under zero-shot setting performs competitively with unsupervised methods because it leverages pre-trained knowledge from large corpora, enabling it to extract keyphrases without task-specific fine-tuning.
- **Mechanism**: The model maps the input document to a semantic representation, then uses its generative capacity to produce candidate keyphrases. Zero-shot usage avoids overfitting to any single dataset.
- **Core assumption**: The pre-training corpus covers domain-relevant language patterns; the model's generative head can produce coherent keyphrases.
- **Evidence anchors**:
  - [abstract]: "Large language models like ChatGPT show promising results but are still inferior to state-of-the-art supervised models"
  - [section]: "ChatGPT outperforms all other unsupervised keyphrase extraction methods in terms of F1@5-score and F1@M-score under the zero-shot setting"
- **Break condition**: When keyphrase definitions are highly domain-specific, pre-training may not generalize well.

## Foundational Learning

- **Concept**: Encoder-decoder architecture for sequence-to-sequence tasks.
  - **Why needed here**: Keyphrase generation requires mapping variable-length documents to variable-length keyphrase outputs; the encoder-decoder framework naturally handles this.
  - **Quick check question**: In a seq2seq model, which component processes the input document and which generates the output keyphrases?
- **Concept**: Set prediction with Hungarian matching.
  - **Why needed here**: ONE2SET needs to align unordered predictions with unordered targets; Hungarian matching provides an optimal one-to-one alignment without imposing order.
  - **Quick check question**: What matching algorithm is used in ONE2SET to align predicted keyphrases with target keyphrases during training?
- **Concept**: Domain adaptation and dataset bias.
  - **Why needed here**: Supervised models degrade when training and test domains differ; understanding this helps diagnose why Inspec performs differently from other datasets.
  - **Quick check question**: Why do supervised extraction models perform worse on Inspec compared to KP20k despite being trained on KP20k?

## Architecture Onboarding

- **Component map**: Document → Encoder (BERT/SciBERT) → Decoder (Transformer with copying mechanism) → Alignment (Hungarian algorithm for ONE2SET) → Final keyphrases
- **Critical path**: Document → Encoder → Decoder (generation) → Alignment/Scoring → Final keyphrases
- **Design tradeoffs**:
  - ONE2SEQ vs ONE2SET: Sequential vs parallel generation; simplicity vs reduced duplication
  - Supervised vs unsupervised: Requires labeled data vs no labels; better performance vs domain robustness
  - Large models vs lightweight: Higher accuracy vs inference speed and resource constraints
- **Failure signatures**:
  - Low F1@5 but high F1@M: Too many predictions or poor ranking
  - High duplication: ONE2SEQ without explicit diversity constraints
  - Domain mismatch: Supervised models failing on out-of-domain datasets
- **First 3 experiments**:
  1. Compare ONE2SEQ vs ONE2SET on KP20k to observe duplication and coverage differences
  2. Test supervised vs unsupervised models on Inspec to quantify domain adaptation effects
  3. Evaluate zero-shot vs few-shot ChatGPT performance across multiple datasets to measure generalization

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- **Question**: How can we effectively leverage external information, such as knowledge bases or pre-trained models, to improve the quality of absent keyphrase predictions?
- **Basis in paper**: [explicit] The paper mentions that researchers explore information beyond input documents to generate better keyphrases, including using similar documents, keyphrases of similar documents, and pre-trained models.
- **Why unresolved**: While the paper discusses various approaches to incorporate external information, it does not provide a comprehensive solution or empirical comparison of different methods.
- **What evidence would resolve it**: A large-scale empirical study comparing the effectiveness of different external information sources and integration methods for absent keyphrase prediction.

### Open Question 2
- **Question**: What is the optimal balance between present and absent keyphrase predictions in a unified keyphrase prediction model?
- **Basis in paper**: [inferred] The paper discusses the different paradigms for keyphrase generation and mentions that dominant studies model the generations of present and absent keyphrases in a unified manner, although their prediction difficulties vary greatly.
- **Why unresolved**: The paper does not provide a definitive answer on how to balance the prediction of present and absent keyphrases, and it suggests that individually modeling their generations might be more reasonable.
- **What evidence would resolve it**: An empirical study comparing the performance of unified models versus models that separately handle present and absent keyphrase predictions.

### Open Question 3
- **Question**: How can we develop more accurate evaluation metrics for keyphrase prediction that consider the semantic similarity between predicted and target keyphrases?
- **Basis in paper**: [explicit] The paper mentions that conventional evaluation metrics mainly focus on the comparison between the surface representations of stemmed phrases, which may not capture the semantic similarity between phrases.
- **Why unresolved**: The paper suggests that evaluation metrics should consider the comparison between semantic representations of phrases and the application effect in downstream tasks, but it does not provide specific metrics or empirical studies.
- **What evidence would resolve it**: A comprehensive study proposing and evaluating new metrics that capture the semantic similarity between predicted and target keyphrases, as well as their impact on downstream tasks.

## Limitations

- The performance comparisons rely on unspecified hyperparameter settings for individual models
- Limited empirical validation of ChatGPT's performance relative to state-of-the-art supervised models
- Evaluation focuses primarily on F1 metrics without exploring keyphrase diversity or semantic coverage
- Domain adaptation effects are observed but not systematically analyzed

## Confidence

- **High confidence**: Supervised deep learning models outperform unsupervised ones (supported by experimental results across multiple datasets)
- **Medium confidence**: ONE2SET paradigm superiority (based on controlled experiments but with potential implementation dependencies)
- **Medium confidence**: ChatGPT's zero-shot performance (limited empirical validation beyond reported comparisons)
