# Simplicity Bias in 1-Hidden Layer Neural Networks
## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2302.00457
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2302.00457
- Reference count: 40
- Primary result: 1-hidden layer neural networks exhibit simplicity bias, depending mainly on low-dimensional subspaces even with complex input features, when data is linearly separable.

## Executive Summary
This paper introduces a formal definition of simplicity bias (SB) in 1-hidden layer neural networks, showing that such networks tend to rely on low-dimensional input projections when data is linearly separable, regardless of the presence of more complex features. Theoretical analysis proves that the learned function primarily depends on the 1-dimensional subspace enabling linear separability. Empirical results on datasets like Imagenette and Waterbirds-Landbirds demonstrate this low-dimensional dependence in practice. Additionally, the authors propose an ensemble approach that trains successive models on unused features, yielding more noise-robust models.

## Method Summary
The authors analyze 1-hidden layer neural networks trained with squared loss on linearly separable data, proving that the learned function depends primarily on a 1-dimensional subspace. They validate this simplicity bias empirically on real datasets by examining input dependence and demonstrate a novel ensemble method that promotes diversity by training on features not utilized by earlier models. The ensemble is shown to improve robustness to Gaussian noise.

## Key Results
- Theoretical proof that 1-hidden layer networks with squared loss on linearly separable data depend mainly on a 1-dimensional subspace.
- Empirical demonstration of low-dimensional input dependence on Imagenette and Waterbirds-Landbirds datasets.
- Ensemble approach that trains on unused features yields models significantly more robust to Gaussian noise.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
Simplicity bias arises because, in the case of linear separability, the optimization process for 1-hidden layer networks with squared loss naturally converges to solutions that primarily use the simplest (1-dimensional) separating feature, even if other more complex features are available. This occurs because the network's capacity is limited, and the loss landscape encourages reliance on the minimal sufficient statistic for separation. The ensemble method further leverages this by explicitly encouraging diversity through orthogonal feature usage, thereby reducing overfitting and improving noise robustness.

- **Low-dimensional subspace dependence**: Central to the definition and detection of simplicity bias; quick check: measure principal components or feature importance to quantify input dependence.

## Limitations
- Theoretical analysis is limited to linearly separable data with a single hidden layer and squared loss, restricting generality.
- Empirical demonstrations do not conclusively rule out alternative explanations for observed low-dimensional dependence.
- The proposed ensemble method lacks systematic comparison to standard ensemble techniques, leaving its relative effectiveness unclear.

## Confidence
- Core theoretical result about linear separability driving low-dimensional dependence is well-supported (High confidence).
- Empirical evidence for simplicity bias on real datasets is suggestive but not definitive (Medium confidence).
- Ensemble approach shows promise but needs more extensive validation across tasks and baselines (Medium confidence).

## Next Checks

3. Conduct ablation studies to isolate the contribution of low-dimensional projections to model robustness, separating from other factors like regularization.
