# Language Models are Drummers: Drum Composition with Natural Language Pre-Training

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2301.01162
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2301.01162
- Authors: [List of authors]
- Reference count: 9

## Executive Summary
This paper explores transferring knowledge from language to music by fine-tuning large language models (LLMs) pre-trained on massive text corpora for drum composition. The key idea is to represent drum grooves as textual sequences and fine-tune GPT3 models on only hundreds of MIDI files. Results show that GPT3 models of different sizes can generate reasonable drum grooves, while a non-pre-trained Transformer model cannot. The authors propose a novel structural evaluation method tailored for drum grooves and analyze the strengths and weaknesses of LLM-generated grooves compared to human-performed ones. The findings suggest that language-to-music transfer learning with LLMs is viable and promising for low-resource music generation tasks.

## Method Summary
The paper fine-tunes GPT3 models (175B and 350M parameters) on drum MIDI files from the Groove MIDI Dataset, which are converted to a textual "drumroll" representation where each line represents a 16th note across six drum hits. The models are trained to complete 14-measure drum grooves given the first 2 measures as input. Performance is evaluated using a novel pattern and fill analysis method based on edit distance, along with subjective listening tests. A non-pre-trained Transformer baseline (85M parameters) is used for comparison.

## Key Results
- GPT3 models generate drum grooves that are comparable to human performances in pattern and fill structure
- Larger GPT3 models (175B) and smaller pre-trained models (350M) both outperform non-pre-trained transformers
- Generated grooves capture rhythmic patterns and create convincing fills, though expert evaluation found them "barely passable"
- The proposed pattern/fill analysis method effectively captures structural differences between generated and human performances

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map: MIDI files -> drumroll conversion -> GPT3 fine-tuning -> pattern/fill analysis -> generated grooves
- Critical path: 1. Convert MIDI to drumroll representation, 2. Fine-tune GPT3 on training set, 3. Generate completions from 2-measure prompts, 4. Evaluate using pattern/fill analysis, 5. Compare against human performances
- Design tradeoffs: Simplification vs. musical expressiveness (quantization, velocity removal); Model size vs. computational cost; Textual representation vs. direct MIDI modeling; Automated evaluation vs. human judgment
- Failure signatures: Repetitive output (model gets stuck in loops); Displaced measures (losing track of measure boundaries); Lack of fills or variations; Incorrect back-beat placement
- First 3 experiments: 1. Compare random baseline vs. repeat baseline to establish minimum performance, 2. Test different temperature settings (0.5, 0.85, 1.0) to balance creativity vs. coherence, 3. Vary prompt length (1, 2, 4 measures) to test context sensitivity

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

- Can large language models trained on drum grooves learn to generate other instruments like piano?
  - Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper mentions "Our methodology might be ported to other instruments such as piano, which we plan to explore" as future work
  - Why unresolved: The current study only focuses on drum grooves, and the effectiveness of LLM-based transfer learning for other instruments remains untested
  - What evidence would resolve it: Conducting experiments training GPT3 on piano MIDI files and evaluating the generated music using similar structural and subjective evaluation methods as the drum study

- How much does the model size impact the quality of generated drum grooves?
  - Basis in paper: [explicit] The authors compare GPT3 models of different sizes (175 billion and 350 million parameters) and find that both can generate reasonable drum grooves, but do not provide a detailed analysis of how model size affects output quality
  - Why unresolved: The paper only considers two model sizes and does not perform a systematic study of the relationship between model size and drum groove quality
  - What evidence would resolve it: Conducting experiments with GPT3 models of varying sizes and quantitatively comparing the generated drum grooves using the proposed structural evaluation method and subjective listening tests

- Can the structural evaluation method proposed in the paper be applied to other types of music generation tasks?
  - Basis in paper: [explicit] The authors propose a "pattern and fill analysis" specifically tailored for drum grooves, but mention that "Evaluating generated music is a challenging task" in general
  - Why unresolved: The effectiveness of the proposed evaluation method for other music generation tasks, such as melody or harmony generation, is unknown
  - What evidence would resolve it: Applying the pattern and fill analysis to generated music from other tasks and assessing its ability to capture the structure and quality of the output. Comparing the results with existing evaluation methods for those tasks

## Limitations

- The evaluation method relies heavily on automated analysis rather than comprehensive musical quality assessment
- The listening test involved only two experts who found generated grooves "barely passable" with no clear preference for any model
- The dataset used (Groove MIDI Dataset) is relatively small at only hundreds of files with simplified representation
- The study focuses on a specific type of drum generation rather than full composition or diverse drum styles

## Confidence

- **High Confidence**: GPT3 models can generate drum grooves when fine-tuned on MIDI data; pre-trained models significantly outperform non-pre-trained transformers; model size affects generation quality
- **Medium Confidence**: The pattern/fill analysis method effectively captures differences between generated and human performances; GPT3 captures rhythmic patterns and creates convincing fills; transfer learning from language to music is viable for drum generation
- **Low Confidence**: The generated grooves are "musically coherent" and "reasonable" - expert evaluation suggests generated output is barely passable and not preferred over human performances; the specific mechanisms by which language pre-training transfers to musical understanding are not fully explained

## Next Checks

1. **Expanded Musical Evaluation**: Conduct a larger listening test with diverse participants including professional musicians to assess the musical quality and creativity of generated grooves across different genres and styles, not just the limited Groove MIDI Dataset

2. **Ablation on Musical Features**: Test models with varying levels of musical information preserved (velocity, articulation, different drum kits, varied time signatures) to determine which aspects of the drumroll representation are most critical for successful transfer learning

3. **Cross-Domain Transfer Testing**: Apply the same pre-trained GPT3 models to other music generation tasks (melody, harmony, full arrangements) to determine if the observed transfer learning benefits generalize beyond drum patterns or are specific to this domain
