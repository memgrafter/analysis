# SynthVSR: Scaling Up Visual Speech Recognition With Synthetic Supervision

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2303.17200
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.17200
- Reference count: 40
- One-line primary result: SynthVSR achieves state-of-the-art VSR performance using synthetic data, reaching 16.9% WER on LRS3 with publicly available data

## Executive Summary
This paper introduces SynthVSR, a method to improve visual speech recognition (VSR) by leveraging synthetic visual data. The core idea is to use a speech-driven lip animation model to generate synthetic videos conditioned on speech utterances and lip images, which are then combined with real data for semi-supervised VSR training. SynthVSR significantly improves VSR performance, achieving a WER of 43.3% with only 30 hours of real labeled data and 27.9% with 438 hours, on par with state-of-the-art methods. When combined with large-scale pseudo-labeled audio-visual data, SynthVSR achieves a new state-of-the-art WER of 16.9% using publicly available data, surpassing approaches trained on 29 times more non-public data.

## Method Summary
SynthVSR combines a speech-driven lip animation model with semi-supervised VSR training. The lip animation model generates synthetic videos from transcribed speech and face images using a GAN-based approach with temporal discriminators. These synthetic videos are then combined with real labeled video data to train a Conformer-Transformer VSR model. The method uses VSR perceptual loss during synthetic data generation when labeled video data is available, aligning synthetic videos better with VSR model expectations. The approach scales by increasing the speech corpus size rather than lip source diversity, generating up to 3,652 hours of synthetic data from Librispeech, TED-LIUM 3, and Common Voice datasets paired with CelebA face images.

## Key Results
- Achieves 43.3% WER on LRS3 test set using only 30 hours of real labeled data, outperforming methods using thousands of hours of video
- Reaches 27.9% WER with 438 hours of real data, matching state-of-the-art performance
- Sets new state-of-the-art at 16.9% WER by combining with 2,630 hours of pseudo-labeled audio-visual data
- Scaling speech corpus provides more improvement than increasing lip source diversity

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: Synthetic visual data can substitute for large-scale labeled video data in VSR training.
- Mechanism: The speech-driven lip animation model generates synthetic videos from abundant transcribed speech data and face images, providing parallel video-text pairs for semi-supervised VSR training.
- Core assumption: The synthetic videos maintain enough visual-phonetic consistency with real videos to be useful for training.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "Our method, termed SynthVSR, substantially improves the performance of VSR systems with synthetic lip movements."
  - [section] "We evaluate the performance of our approach on the largest public VSR benchmark - Lip Reading Sentences 3 (LRS3). SynthVSR achieves a WER of 43.3% with only 30 hours of real labeled data, outperforming off-the-shelf approaches using thousands of hours of video."
  - [corpus] Weak - the corpus contains related work on VSR but no direct evidence about synthetic data effectiveness.

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: VSR perceptual loss improves synthetic video quality for VSR training.
- Mechanism: Optimizing the lip animation model with VSR perceptual loss (visual embeddings + linguistic logits) aligns synthetic videos better with VSR model expectations.
- Core assumption: The pre-trained VSR model's feature space captures relevant visual-phonetic information.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [section] "We empirically demonstrate that the semantically high level, spatio-temporal supervision signal from the pre-trained VSR model offers the lip animation model more accurate lip movements."
  - [section] "The linguistic VSR loss performs slightly better than the visual one. Further, the combination of visual and linguistic VSR loss further improves the WER to 30.8%."
  - [corpus] Weak - no corpus evidence about perceptual loss in synthetic data generation.

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: Scaling speech corpus size improves VSR performance more than increasing lip source diversity.
- Mechanism: Larger speech datasets provide more varied phonetic contexts, which synthetic videos can capture better than simply adding more lip images.
- Core assumption: Phonetic diversity in speech data is the limiting factor for synthetic video quality.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [section] "We observe that richer speech corpora can offer larger performance improvements. Furthermore, we find that the improvement from scaling speech corpora is more pronounced than when adding VSR perceptual loss and lip sources diversity."
  - [section] "Using synthetic data generated from TED-LIUM 3, Librispeech, and Common Voice with LAM-LRS3-VSR-VL model synthetic data, the LRS3 (test) WER improves from 36.7% to 30.9%, 30.8%, and 30.1%, respectively."
  - [corpus] Weak - corpus doesn't directly compare speech corpus scaling vs lip source diversity.

- Concept: Multimodal learning
  - Why needed here: SynthVSR learns from both visual (lip movements) and audio (speech content) modalities.

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map:
  - Speech-driven lip animation model (generator + discriminators) -> Synthetic video generator
  - VSR model (visual front-end + Conformer encoder + Transformer decoder) -> Recognition system
  - Data pipelines for real and synthetic data -> Training data preparation
  - Training orchestration for semi-supervised learning -> Model training coordination

- Critical path:
  1. Train speech-driven lip animation model on unlabeled audio-visual data
  2. Generate synthetic videos from transcribed speech and face images
  3. Combine synthetic and real data for VSR training
  4. Evaluate VSR performance on test set

- Design tradeoffs:
  - High computational cost of generating large-scale synthetic data vs performance gains
  - Complexity of maintaining two training pipelines (lip animation + VSR)
  - Risk of domain mismatch between synthetic and real data

- Failure signatures:
  - VSR model performs well on synthetic test set but poorly on real test set (domain mismatch)
  - Synthetic data generation fails to capture diverse lip movements
  - Training instability when combining real and synthetic data

- First 3 experiments:
  1. Train VSR model only on synthetic data and measure WER on real test set to assess domain gap
  2. Vary the amount of synthetic data while keeping real data constant to find optimal ratio
  3. Compare VSR perceptual loss vs no perceptual loss in synthetic data generation

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- Question: What is the upper limit of synthetic data that can be generated for VSR training without causing model degradation or overfitting?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The authors mention that synthetic data offers advantages such as control over target text and lip images, and the potential for unlimited video data generation. However, they do not explore the limits of synthetic data scale.
- Why unresolved: The paper demonstrates improvements with 3,652 hours of synthetic data but does not investigate whether larger scales continue to improve performance or lead to diminishing returns.
- What evidence would resolve it: Systematic experiments varying synthetic data from hundreds to tens of thousands of hours while monitoring VSR performance and comparing against real data baselines.

### Open Question 2
- Question: How do different types of synthetic speech (e.g., TTS-generated vs. real speech) affect VSR performance, and can this gap be closed?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The authors show that TTS-generated speech achieves 3.8% improvement over baseline, though it is not as effective as real speech. They note this suggests potential for real-world applications.
- Why unresolved: The paper only compares one TTS model to real speech, leaving open questions about whether different TTS architectures, prosody control, or data augmentation could close the performance gap.
- What evidence would resolve it: Comparative studies using multiple TTS systems with varying naturalness levels, plus experiments testing whether fine-tuning TTS models on real speech or adding noise/variability improves VSR outcomes.

### Open Question 3
- Question: How transferable are the synthetic data generation techniques across different languages and speaking styles?
- Basis in paper: [inferred] The authors focus on English datasets and standard speaking styles, but the introduction mentions the method could benefit the world's 7,000 languages, implying broader applicability is unexplored.
- Why unresolved: The paper does not test the approach on multilingual datasets, non-standard accents, or languages with different phonological structures, leaving uncertainty about generalization.
- What evidence would resolve it: Experiments applying SynthVSR to multilingual audio-visual datasets (e.g., VoxLingua107), accented speech corpora, or languages with different phonetic inventories to measure cross-linguistic performance and adaptation requirements.

## Limitations

- The domain gap between synthetic and real videos remains a critical concern, with uncertain robustness across different speaking styles, lighting conditions, or camera angles
- The computational cost of generating large-scale synthetic data (requiring thousands of GPU hours) may limit practical applicability
- The reliance on face images from CelebA introduces potential bias toward certain demographics and facial structures

## Confidence

- **High Confidence**: The core methodology of using synthetic data for semi-supervised VSR training is well-established in the literature and the experimental results show clear improvements over baseline methods. The architectural choices (Conformer-Transformer for VSR, GAN-based lip animation) are standard and well-supported.
- **Medium Confidence**: The specific claim that scaling speech corpora provides more benefit than increasing lip source diversity is based on limited experiments (three speech datasets vs one face dataset). While the trend is clear, the magnitude of this effect may vary with different datasets.
- **Low Confidence**: The assertion that VSR perceptual loss significantly improves synthetic video quality for training is supported by ablation studies within this work, but lacks comparison to alternative loss functions or validation from independent studies. The mechanism by which visual-linguistic features improve lip movements is not fully explained.

## Next Checks

1. **Domain Adaptation Validation**: Conduct experiments training VSR models exclusively on synthetic data and testing on real data to quantify the domain gap. Measure performance degradation when synthetic data is removed from training.

2. **Bias Analysis**: Evaluate VSR performance across different demographic groups (age, gender, ethnicity) to assess whether the CelebA face image bias affects recognition accuracy for underrepresented groups.

3. **Computational Cost-Benefit Analysis**: Measure the exact GPU hours required for synthetic data generation and calculate the trade-off between synthetic data generation cost and VSR performance improvement. Compare this to alternative approaches like increasing real data collection.
