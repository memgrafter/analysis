# Bridging History with AI A Comparative Evaluation of GPT 3.5, GPT4, and GoogleBARD in Predictive Accuracy and Fact Checking

## Quick Facts

## Executive Summary
This study evaluates three large language models (GPT-3.5, GPT-4, and Google-BARD) using a novel "Distance to Reality" (DTR) metric for historical fact-checking and predictive analysis. The models were tasked with predicting outcomes related to historical events, with their responses measured against established facts. Results demonstrated GPT-4's superior performance, achieving the lowest average DTR score of 0.035, followed by GPT-3.5 (0.15) and Google-BARD (0.20). The findings highlight AI's potential in historical studies while emphasizing the need for AI to complement traditional historical inquiry rather than replace it.

## Method Summary
The study employed a novel "Distance to Reality" (DTR) metric to evaluate the performance of three large language models in historical fact-checking and predictive analysis. Historical events were provided to the models, which were then asked to predict related outcomes. These predictions were measured against established historical facts to calculate DTR scores, with lower scores indicating higher accuracy. The methodology focused on comparative analysis across GPT-3.5, GPT-4, and Google-BARD, though specific details about the historical event sample or prompt engineering variations were not provided.

## Key Results
- GPT-4 achieved the lowest average DTR score (0.035), indicating the highest accuracy in historical fact-checking.
- GPT-3.5 followed with an average DTR score of 0.15, showing moderate performance.
- Google-BARD scored the highest average DTR (0.20), reflecting the lowest accuracy among the three models.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The study leverages the advanced contextual understanding and predictive capabilities of large language models to analyze historical events. GPT-4's superior performance is attributed to its ability to grasp complex historical contexts and generate more accurate predictions. The DTR metric quantifies the deviation between model predictions and established historical facts, providing a measurable basis for comparison.

## Limitations
- The DTR metric, while innovative, lacks established validation in historical research contexts, raising questions about its reliability.
- The study does not specify the sample of historical events used, making it difficult to assess representativeness or potential bias.
- No account is taken of variations in model performance based on prompt engineering or contextual framing.
- The focus on predictive accuracy may not fully capture the nuanced interpretive and analytical capabilities required for comprehensive historical inquiry.

## Confidence
- **High Confidence**: GPT-4's superior performance in minimizing DTR scores is consistently supported by the data.
- **Medium Confidence**: The general ranking of models (GPT-4 > GPT-3.5 > Google-BARD) is plausible but requires further validation across diverse datasets.
- **Low Confidence**: The reliability of the DTR metric as a standalone measure for historical accuracy remains uncertain without broader peer review.
