# Generating multiple-choice questions for medical question answering with distractors and cue-masking

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2303.07069
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.07069
- Reference count: 16
- Primary result: +5.7% improvement on MedQA-USMLE using base-size model

## Executive Summary
This paper addresses the challenge of improving multiple-choice medical question answering (MCQA) by enhancing language models with domain-specific knowledge. The authors propose a novel approach that generates synthetic MCQA datasets for pretraining, incorporating differential diagnoses as distractors and employing a probability-matching cue masking strategy to prevent models from relying on easily guessable patterns. This method leads to state-of-the-art results on four medical MCQA datasets, demonstrating significant improvements over traditional masked language modeling-based objectives.

## Method Summary
The authors propose a two-pronged approach to improve MCQA performance. First, they generate synthetic datasets by using differential diagnoses as distractors, which are more realistic and challenging than random options. Second, they introduce a probability-matching cue masking strategy to prevent models from exploiting easily guessable patterns in the questions. This approach involves masking tokens in the question and answer choices with probabilities that match the distribution of correct answers, making it harder for the model to rely on superficial cues. The resulting dataset is then used to fine-tune language models, leading to improved performance on medical MCQA tasks.

## Key Results
- Fine-tuning on the generated synthetic dataset outperforms masked language modeling-based objectives
- State-of-the-art results on four medical MCQA datasets
- +5.7% improvement on MedQA-USMLE using a base-size model
- Addresses the issue of "extraneous masking" where certain token masking patterns can inadvertently reveal the answer

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The approach works by addressing two key issues in medical MCQA: the lack of domain-specific pretraining data and the tendency of models to rely on easily guessable patterns. By generating synthetic datasets with realistic distractors (differential diagnoses), the model is exposed to more challenging and domain-relevant scenarios during pretraining. The probability-matching cue masking strategy prevents the model from exploiting superficial patterns, forcing it to learn more robust representations of medical knowledge. This combination of realistic data and robust training leads to improved performance on medical MCQA tasks.

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component Map**: Synthetic Data Generation -> Probability-Matching Cue Masking -> Fine-tuning -> Evaluation
**Critical Path**: The critical path involves generating synthetic data with realistic distractors, applying probability-matching cue masking, and fine-tuning the model on this enhanced dataset.
**Design Tradeoffs**: The tradeoff between realistic distractors (differential diagnoses) and computational cost of generating synthetic data. Another tradeoff is between masking strategy complexity and model performance.
**Failure Signatures**: Models may still rely on superficial patterns if the probability-matching cue masking is not effective. Additionally, if the synthetic data generation process does not accurately reflect real-world medical scenarios, model performance may suffer.

## Limitations
- Evaluation primarily focuses on English-language medical datasets, limiting generalizability to other languages or medical domains.
- The synthetic data generation approach relies on differential diagnoses that may not always be available or appropriate for all medical scenarios.
- The probability-matching cue masking strategy, while innovative, requires further validation on datasets beyond the four tested to confirm its robustness across different medical contexts.

## Confidence
- High confidence in the +5.7% improvement on MedQA-USMLE and the superiority of fine-tuning over masked language modeling objectives.
- Medium confidence in the generalizability of results to other medical domains or languages.
- Medium confidence in the long-term effectiveness of the probability-matching cue masking strategy, as it requires testing on more diverse datasets.
