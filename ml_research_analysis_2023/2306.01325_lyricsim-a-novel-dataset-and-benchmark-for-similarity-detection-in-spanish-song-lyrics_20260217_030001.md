# LyricSIM: A novel Dataset and Benchmark for Similarity Detection in Spanish Song Lyrics

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2306.01325
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.01325
- Reference count: 31
- Introduces a dataset of 676 annotated Spanish song lyric pairs for semantic similarity detection

## Executive Summary
This paper introduces LyricSIM, a novel dataset and benchmark specifically designed for semantic similarity detection in Spanish song lyrics. The dataset contains 676 high-quality annotated pairs of Spanish song lyrics, rated by 63 native annotators using a six-point semantic differential scale. The authors evaluate the performance of various state-of-the-art monolingual and multilingual language models on this dataset, establishing baseline results. The best-performing model, MarIA large, achieves a combined score of 90.02, followed closely by mDeBERTa with a score of 89.15.

## Method Summary
The authors created LyricSIM by collecting 676 pairs of Spanish song lyrics and having them annotated by 63 native Spanish speakers through Amazon Mechanical Turk. Annotators rated the semantic similarity of lyric pairs on a six-point scale. The dataset was then used to evaluate several state-of-the-art language models, including monolingual models trained specifically on Spanish (like MarIA) and multilingual models (like mDeBERTa and mBERT). Performance was measured using Pearson correlation and accuracy metrics, with results combined into a single score.

## Key Results
- MarIA large achieved the best performance with a combined score of 90.02
- mDeBERTa was the second-best model with a score of 89.15
- Monolingual models trained on Spanish showed advantages over multilingual models for this specific task

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
Assumption: The success of monolingual Spanish models like MarIA suggests that language-specific pretraining provides advantages for capturing the semantic nuances in Spanish lyrics. The task's focus on song lyrics may require understanding of cultural context and poetic devices that are better captured by models trained on domain-specific data. The use of a semantic differential scale for annotation likely provides more nuanced similarity judgments than binary or categorical labels, enabling more precise model training and evaluation.
