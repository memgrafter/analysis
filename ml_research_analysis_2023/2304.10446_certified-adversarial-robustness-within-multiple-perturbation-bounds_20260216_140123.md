# Certified Adversarial Robustness Within Multiple Perturbation Bounds

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2304.10446
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2304.10446
- Reference count: 40
- Key outcome: State-of-the-art certified robustness against both ℓ1 and ℓ2 perturbations on CIFAR-10

## Executive Summary
This work addresses the challenge of improving certified adversarial robustness against multiple perturbation norms simultaneously. The proposed method combines Gaussian and Uniform noise distributions during training and certification, introducing a novel Normal-Uniform noise distribution with negative kurtosis. A similarity regularizer based on KL-divergence aligns training and inference distributions. The approach outperforms existing methods in terms of Average Certified Radius (ACR) across both ℓ1 and ℓ2 norms when compared at fixed clean accuracy levels, demonstrating state-of-the-art performance on CIFAR-10.

## Method Summary
The method trains a base classifier with Normal-Uniform noise augmentation (a mixture distribution with negative kurtosis) and applies a similarity regularizer using KL-divergence between predictions under different noise distributions. During certification, the model is evaluated separately under Gaussian and Uniform smoothing, then a hybrid classifier combines these certificates by selecting the maximum certified radius between the two distributions when they agree on the label, and abstaining otherwise. The approach challenges the conventional wisdom that training and certifying with the same noise level yields optimal results.

## Key Results
- Achieves state-of-the-art ACR across both ℓ1 and ℓ2 norms on CIFAR-10
- Outperforms Gaussian and Uniform smoothing baselines at fixed clean accuracy levels
- Demonstrates that training with Normal-Uniform noise and certifying with different noise levels improves robustness-accuracy trade-off

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: Combining Gaussian and Uniform smoothing certificates produces a stronger hybrid classifier with better ℓ1 and ℓ2 robustness.
- Mechanism: The hybrid classifier uses rules to select the maximum certified radius between the two smoothing distributions when they agree on the label, and abstains otherwise. This leverages the complementary strengths of Gaussian (optimal for ℓ2) and Uniform (optimal for ℓ1) smoothing.
- Core assumption: The base classifier's predictions under both smoothing distributions are sufficiently correlated that abstaining only when they disagree doesn't discard too much data.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "We propose a novel certification scheme, that combines the certificates obtained using different noise distributions to obtain optimal results against multiple perturbation bounds."
  - [section] "We build a hybrid smoothed classifier from these 8 outputs using the rules presented in Table 1 depending on the different possibilities arising from the predictions and their confidence."
- Break condition: If the base classifier's predictions under different smoothing distributions become uncorrelated, the hybrid classifier would abstain too often, reducing coverage.

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: Training with Normal-Uniform noise distribution (negative kurtosis) improves robustness compared to Gaussian or Uniform alone.
- Mechanism: The Normal-Uniform distribution combines the properties of both Gaussian and Uniform noise, exposing the model to a broader range of perturbations during training. The negative kurtosis reduces outlier generation, improving training stability.
- Core assumption: The model can learn to be robust to both ℓ1 and ℓ2 perturbations when trained on a mixture distribution rather than a single distribution.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "We propose a novel training noise distribution along with a regularized training scheme to improve the certification within both ℓ1 and ℓ2 perturbation norms simultaneously."
  - [section] "The use of a platykurtic distribution during training reduces the probability of generating outliers, thereby improving the training stability as shown in Table 4."
- Break condition: If the Normal-Uniform distribution parameters are poorly chosen, the model may not learn effectively from either component distribution.

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: Using different noise magnitudes during training and certification improves performance over using the same noise level.
- Mechanism: Lower noise during certification preserves more clean accuracy while maintaining good robustness, and the similarity regularizer aligns the training and certification distributions even when they differ.
- Core assumption: The robustness-accuracy trade-off is not optimized at the same noise level for training and certification.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "We also empirically invalidate the argument that training and certifying the classifier with the same amount of noise gives the best results."
  - [section] "As shown in Table 3, both clean accuracy and ACR improve when a lower σ is used during certification."
- Break condition: If the difference between training and certification noise is too large, the similarity regularizer may not adequately align the distributions.

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map: Base classifier → Noise augmentation (Normal-Uniform during training, Gaussian/Uniform during certification) → Prediction aggregation (hybrid classifier) → Regularization (similarity KL divergence)
- Critical path: Training → Certification (Gaussian) → Certification (Uniform) → Hybrid aggregation
- Design tradeoffs: Using different training and certification noise magnitudes improves performance but requires careful regularization to align distributions.
- Failure signatures: Low clean accuracy with high ℓ1 ACR but low ℓ2 ACR suggests the hybrid aggregation rules may be too conservative; high clean accuracy with low ACRs suggests insufficient regularization.
- First 3 experiments:
  1. Train a baseline Gaussian smoothed classifier with fixed σ for both training and certification.
  2. Train with Normal-Uniform noise and certify with both Gaussian and Uniform separately to verify individual improvements.
  3. Implement the hybrid aggregation and test on a small subset to verify the certification rules work as expected.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- Question: How does the proposed Normal-Uniform distribution compare to other noise distributions for achieving certified robustness against multiple perturbation norms?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper proposes the Normal-Uniform distribution as a training noise distribution, showing it improves ACR across both ℓ1 and ℓ2 norms compared to Gaussian and Uniform noise distributions alone.
- Why unresolved: While the paper demonstrates superior performance of the Normal-Uniform distribution over Gaussian and Uniform distributions, it does not explore other potential noise distributions that might offer even better results.
- What evidence would resolve it: Comparative experiments evaluating the proposed Normal-Uniform distribution against other noise distributions (e.g., Laplace, Student's t-distribution) for achieving certified robustness against multiple perturbation norms.

### Open Question 2
- Question: What is the impact of the similarity regularizer's hyperparameter β on the trade-off between clean accuracy and certified robustness?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper mentions that the hyperparameter β is tuned to achieve the required level of clean accuracy with optimal ACR, but does not provide a detailed analysis of its impact on the trade-off.
- Why unresolved: The paper does not explore the sensitivity of the model's performance to different values of β, which is crucial for understanding the trade-off between clean accuracy and certified robustness.
- What evidence would resolve it: A comprehensive study analyzing the impact of varying β on the clean accuracy and ACR across different levels of noise magnitude and perturbation norms.

### Open Question 3
- Question: How does the proposed certification method compare to other methods for combining certificates obtained using different noise distributions?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper proposes a novel certification method that combines certificates obtained using Gaussian and Uniform noise distributions, showing improved ACR across both ℓ1 and ℓ2 norms.
- Why unresolved: The paper does not compare the proposed certification method to other potential methods for combining certificates obtained using different noise distributions, leaving the optimality of the proposed method unclear.
- What evidence would resolve it: Comparative experiments evaluating the proposed certification method against other methods for combining certificates obtained using different noise distributions, such as weighted averaging or majority voting.

## Limitations
- Performance depends heavily on careful hyperparameter tuning of Normal-Uniform distribution parameters and similarity regularizer strength
- Hybrid certification strategy complexity requires precise coordination between ℓ1 and ℓ2 certification steps
- Implementation-specific effects may vary across hardware or software environments

## Confidence
- **High confidence**: The theoretical foundation of randomized smoothing and KL divergence-based regularization is well-established in the literature
- **Medium confidence**: The empirical improvements over baselines are demonstrated, but results depend on specific hyperparameter choices
- **Medium confidence**: The hybrid certification strategy's effectiveness relies on correlation between base classifier predictions under different smoothing distributions
