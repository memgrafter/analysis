# Learning to Grow Pretrained Models for Efficient Transformer Training

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2303.00980
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.00980
- Reference count: 40
- Primary result: Saves up to 50% computational cost of training transformers from scratch while outperforming baselines

## Executive Summary
This paper introduces LiGO (Learning to Grow), a method for accelerating transformer training by learning a linear mapping to grow pretrained models. The approach initializes larger transformer parameters as a linear transformation of smaller pretrained models, with the mapping factorized using Kronecker products for width and depth expansion. Evaluated across language and vision transformers, LiGO achieves up to 50% computational savings while consistently outperforming strong baselines that reuse pretrained models.

## Method Summary
LiGO accelerates transformer training by learning a linear growth operator that maps parameters from smaller pretrained models to initialize larger models. The linear mapping is factorized as a composition of width- and depth-expansion operators, each further factorized using Kronecker products to reduce parameter complexity. The growth operator is optimized for a small number of steps (e.g., 100) on a subset of data, then used to initialize the larger model for standard training. This approach enables significant computational savings while maintaining or improving performance compared to training from scratch.

## Key Results
- Saves up to 50% computational cost compared to training from scratch
- Consistently outperforms strong baselines that reuse smaller pretrained models
- Effective across both language (BERT, RoBERTa, GPT2) and vision (DeiT, CaiT) transformers
- Can be combined with other training strategies like layer dropping and staged training

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- **Claim:** The model growth is formulated as learning a linear mapping from the smaller model's parameters to initialize the larger model, enabling reuse of pretrained knowledge.
- **Mechanism:** The linear mapping M transforms the vectorized parameters of the smaller model Θ(small) into those of the larger model Θ(new) via Θ(new) = MΘ(small).
- **Core assumption:** The implicit knowledge captured in smaller models' parameters can be linearly projected to initialize larger models effectively.
- **Break condition:** If the linear mapping fails to capture complex nonlinear relationships between small and large model parameters, performance may degrade.

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim:** Kronecker factorization of width and depth growth operators enables efficient learning of the linear mapping M by reducing parameter complexity.
- **Mechanism:** The operator M is decomposed into LdepthRwidth where each is further factorized using Kronecker products, reducing parameter count from O(D1²D2²L1) to O(L1D1D2 + L1L2).
- **Core assumption:** Kronecker factorization preserves sufficient representational power while drastically reducing learnable parameters.
- **Break condition:** If the Kronecker factorization oversimplifies the growth process, the resulting model may not achieve comparable performance.

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim:** The width- and depth-expansion operators can be learned with only 100 gradient steps, making the approach computationally efficient.
- **Mechanism:** The growth operator M is optimized using SGD for a small number of steps (e.g., 100) on a small amount of data, which has negligible compute cost compared to full training.
- **Core assumption:** A small number of optimization steps is sufficient to learn an effective initialization that accelerates full training.
- **Break condition:** If more gradient steps are required to achieve effective initialization, the computational savings may be reduced.

## Architecture Onboarding

- **Component map:** Growth Operator (M) -> Width Expansion (Rwidth) -> Depth Expansion (Ldepth) -> Training Loop

- **Critical path:**
  1. Define smaller and larger model architectures
  2. Extract vectorized parameters from smaller model
  3. Initialize LiGO operator with Kronecker factorization
  4. Optimize LiGO with 100 SGD steps
  5. Initialize larger model with learned mapping
  6. Continue training as usual

- **Design tradeoffs:**
  - Depth vs width expansion balance: The factorization allows independent control but requires careful parameterization
  - Kronecker factorization vs full matrix: Dramatically reduces parameters but may limit expressiveness
  - Number of optimization steps: 100 steps provides good balance between efficiency and performance

- **Failure signatures:**
  - Model initialization produces NaN or extreme values
  - Training diverges immediately after initialization
  - Final performance significantly below training from scratch baseline
  - Optimization of LiGO fails to converge in 100 steps

- **First 3 experiments:**
  1. Implement LiGO for depth-only expansion (BERT-Small → BERT-Base with same width) and verify against StackBERT baseline
  2. Implement LiGO for width-only expansion (BERT-Base with 512 → 768 dimensions) and compare against Net2Net
  3. Full LiGO implementation on BERT-Small → BERT-Base and measure FLOPs savings and performance relative to training from scratch

## Limitations

- Only evaluated on transformers with full attention mechanisms, not tested on sparse attention or convolutional transformers
- Limited evaluation on downstream fine-tuning tasks (only one GLUE task for BERT and one classification task for DeiT)
- Requires smaller and larger models to have similar architectures, limiting applicability when architectures differ substantially
- Computational savings measured primarily in FLOPs and wall time, with limited investigation of memory savings and practical deployment benefits

## Confidence
