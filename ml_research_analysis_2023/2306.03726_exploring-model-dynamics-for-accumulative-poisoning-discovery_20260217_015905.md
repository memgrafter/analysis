# Exploring Model Dynamics for Accumulative Poisoning Discovery

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2306.03726
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.03726

- Reference count: 40

## Executive Summary
This paper introduces Memorization Discrepancy, a novel measure for detecting accumulative poisoning attacks in real-time data streaming. The method leverages model dynamics by comparing outputs of current and historical models on the same data, revealing distinct patterns between poisoned and clean samples. Based on this measure, the authors propose Discrepancy-aware Sample Correction (DSC), which selectively calibrates potential poison samples using early stopping during adversarial correction. Experiments on CIFAR-10, CIFAR-100, and SVHN datasets demonstrate that DSC effectively defends against accumulative poisoning attacks, maintaining higher accuracy after poison injection and trigger batch exposure compared to baseline methods like standard training, gradient clipping, and adversarial training.

## Method Summary
The method involves tracking model dynamics during training by storing historical model checkpoints. Memorization Discrepancy is calculated as the KL divergence between outputs of current and historical models on the same samples. Samples with high discrepancy are identified as potential poison samples and corrected through adversarial correction with early stopping. The approach works in real-time data streaming scenarios where accumulative poisoning attacks can dynamically update poison samples based on model status.

## Key Results
- DSC maintains significantly higher accuracy than standard training (ST), gradient clipping (GC), and adversarial training (AT) after poison injection
- Memorization Discrepancy effectively distinguishes poison samples from clean samples with increasing backtracking intervals
- The selective correction mechanism prevents over-calibration of clean samples while effectively correcting poison samples

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- **Claim**: Memorization Discrepancy captures model-level changes caused by imperceptible poisoning, making poison samples distinguishable from clean samples.
- **Mechanism**: By comparing the outputs of the current model and a historical model on the same sample, the method detects how poison samples affect model dynamics differently than clean samples.
- **Core assumption**: Poison samples optimized for a specific model stage cause distinct output changes compared to clean samples, especially when the model has learned the clean data distribution.
- **Evidence anchors**:
  - [abstract] "By implicitly transferring the changes in the data manipulation to that in the model outputs, Memorization Discrepancy can discover the imperceptible poison samples based on their distinct dynamics from the clean samples."
  - [section] "Different from the offline poisoning adversaries, the accumulative poisoning attack is allowed to interact with the model status to update its poison samples dynamically in the training process."
  - [corpus] Weak evidence. Related papers focus on data poisoning but not model dynamics detection.
- **Break condition**: If poison samples are optimized to mimic clean sample dynamics across multiple model stages, the discrepancy signal weakens.

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim**: The monotonic increase property of Memorization Discrepancy with backtracking interval enables selective calibration of poison samples.
- **Mechanism**: As the backtracking interval increases, the discrepancy between clean and poison samples grows, allowing the method to identify a discriminative model status where poison samples are most distinguishable.
- **Core assumption**: Model learning dynamics create a measurable difference in how poison and clean samples are processed over time, which can be exploited for detection.
- **Evidence anchors**:
  - [section] "The value of Memorization Discrepancy will increase as the model training... the value of Memorization Discrepancy on poison samples are consistently distinguishable from that on clean ones."
  - [section] "The poison samples can be more distinguishable from clean samples by enlarging the backtracking interval in Memorization Discrepancy to find a highly discriminative status."
  - [corpus] Weak evidence. Related work focuses on poisoning attacks but not on exploiting model dynamics for detection.
- **Break condition**: If the attacker can predict and counteract the model's learning trajectory, the monotonic property may not hold.

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim**: Early stopping in sample correction prevents over-calibration of clean samples while effectively correcting poison samples.
- **Mechanism**: By setting a threshold based on Memorization Discrepancy, the method stops correction when the discrepancy falls below a certain level, avoiding unnecessary modification of clean samples.
- **Core assumption**: Clean samples have lower Memorization Discrepancy values than poison samples, allowing for a threshold that separates them.
- **Evidence anchors**:
  - [section] "Due to the indiscriminate correction, AT over-optimizes the clean samples and leads to much lower accuracy than the pre-trained model. In contrast, our DSC can still achieve comparable performance with ST through the selective correction of Memorization Discrepancy."
  - [section] "The multi-step correction will stop if Memorization Discrepancy is smaller than an adjustable threshold during the pre-defined correction steps. This operation can avoid over-calibration for those clean samples."
  - [corpus] Weak evidence. Related papers discuss poisoning attacks but not selective correction mechanisms.
- **Break condition**: If the threshold is set too low, clean samples might be incorrectly identified as poison and over-corrected.

## Architecture Onboarding

- **Component map**: 
  Historical model storage -> Current model training loop -> Memorization Discrepancy calculation module -> Sample correction module with early stopping -> Threshold adjustment mechanism

- **Critical path**: 
  Sample capture -> Memorization Discrepancy calculation -> Threshold comparison -> Sample correction (if needed) -> Model update

- **Design tradeoffs**:
  - Storage cost vs. detection accuracy (more historical models provide better detection but increase storage)
  - Correction threshold vs. false positive rate (higher thresholds reduce over-correction but may miss some poison samples)
  - Backtracking interval vs. computational cost (larger intervals improve detection but require more computation)

- **Failure signatures**:
  - Consistently low accuracy on clean data (threshold too low)
  - No improvement in accuracy after trigger batch (mechanism not detecting poison samples)
  - High computational cost (inefficient implementation of discrepancy calculation)

- **First 3 experiments**:
  1. Implement Memorization Discrepancy calculation on a simple dataset with known poison samples to verify the discrepancy signal

  3. Evaluate the impact of different backtracking intervals on detection accuracy and computational cost

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- Question: What are the fundamental limits of Memorization Discrepancy's effectiveness across different model architectures and learning tasks?
- Basis in paper: [inferred] The paper shows the phenomenon exists across different backbones (ResNet, VGG-11, SmallCNN) on CIFAR-10, but doesn't establish theoretical bounds on its effectiveness or explore why it works.
- Why unresolved: The paper demonstrates empirical effectiveness but doesn't provide theoretical guarantees about when and why Memorization Discrepancy can distinguish poison from clean samples. The mechanism relies on assumptions about model dynamics that aren't fully proven.
- What evidence would resolve it: Mathematical proofs establishing conditions under which Memorization Discrepancy successfully identifies poison samples, along with empirical studies across diverse model families and tasks showing the boundaries of its effectiveness.

### Open Question 2
- Question: How does Memorization Discrepancy perform against adaptive attackers who are aware of the defense mechanism?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper mentions adaptive attackers in Table 3 and Section C.12, showing that incorporating the Memorization Discrepancy constraint into the poison generation reduces poisoning effectiveness, but doesn't fully explore the arms race between defense and attack.
- Why unresolved: The paper only considers a basic adaptive attacker who incorporates the discrepancy constraint into generation. More sophisticated adaptive strategies that could potentially circumvent the defense remain unexplored.
- What evidence would resolve it: Experiments with increasingly sophisticated adaptive attackers that actively try to minimize Memorization Discrepancy while maintaining poisoning effectiveness, along with defenses that can withstand such attacks.

### Open Question 3
- Question: Can Memorization Discrepancy be effectively extended to offline poisoning scenarios where the defender doesn't have access to the training process?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper acknowledges this limitation in Section D, noting that utilizing Memorization Discrepancy in offline settings "may require more improvement or adjustment" and is left as future work.
- Why unresolved: The current method relies on historical model checkpoints during training, which aren't available in standard offline training scenarios where poisoned data is mixed with clean data from the beginning.
- What evidence would resolve it: Development of techniques that can approximate or simulate the historical model information needed for Memorization Discrepancy in offline settings, or alternative approaches that capture similar model dynamics without requiring sequential access.

### Open Question 4
- Question: What is the relationship between the threshold selection strategy and the trade-off between false positives (incorrectly flagging clean samples) and false negatives (missing poison samples)?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper discusses threshold selection in Section 4.1 and Appendix C.4, showing empirical results with different thresholds but not providing systematic analysis of the trade-off or optimal selection strategies.
- Why unresolved: The paper uses empirical threshold tuning based on clean validation data but doesn't provide theoretical guidance on how to balance the competing objectives of minimizing both types of errors, especially as poisoning strategies evolve.
- What evidence would resolve it: Systematic studies mapping threshold values to false positive/false negative rates across different poisoning scenarios, along with principled methods for threshold selection that optimize the trade-off based on application requirements.

## Limitations
- The method requires access to historical model checkpoints during training, limiting applicability to offline poisoning scenarios
- Effectiveness against adaptive attackers who optimize poison samples to minimize Memorization Discrepancy remains untested
- Computational overhead of storing and comparing multiple historical models could be prohibitive in resource-constrained settings

## Confidence

- **High confidence**: Memorization Discrepancy can distinguish poison from clean samples in controlled experiments
- **Medium confidence**: The monotonic increase property with backtracking interval generalizes across datasets and attack scenarios
- **Low confidence**: DSC's robustness against adaptive poisoning attacks that actively evade discrepancy detection
