# Information Redundancy and Biases in Public Document Information Extraction Benchmarks

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2304.14936
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2304.14936
- Reference count: 40
- Key outcome: Resampling strategies eliminating template overlap cause significant performance drops (10.5% F1 on SROIE, 3.5% on FUNSD) revealing benchmark data leaks

## Executive Summary
This paper identifies significant template overlap between training and testing sets in public document information extraction benchmarks (SROIE and FUNSD), which artificially inflates model performance by enabling memorization of document layouts rather than true understanding. The authors propose and validate resampling strategies that ensure zero template overlap between train and test splits, demonstrating that this adjustment leads to substantial performance drops across various state-of-the-art models. The results show that original benchmarks contained data leaks that compromised their ability to measure true generalization, and the resampled splits provide a more realistic evaluation of model performance on unseen document templates.

## Method Summary
The authors quantify template overlap in SROIE and FUNSD benchmarks by analyzing document similarity metrics, then develop resampling strategies to create train-test splits with zero template overlap. They evaluate multiple state-of-the-art models (BERT, AlBERT, RoBERTa, and multi-modal variants) on both original and resampled splits, comparing performance to measure the impact of eliminating template redundancy. The methodology focuses on ensuring that no document templates appear in both training and testing sets, thereby preventing models from memorizing layout patterns rather than learning generalizable extraction capabilities.

## Key Results
- BERT, AlBERT, and RoBERTa models drop an average of 10.5% F1 score on resampled SROIE compared to original
- Multi-modal models show smaller drops of 7.5% F1 on resampled SROIE and only 0.5% F1 on FUNSD
- FUNSD shows more modest performance degradation (3.5% F1 drop) compared to SROIE
- The performance drops validate that original benchmarks contained significant data leaks through template overlap

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The mechanism works because template overlap in the original benchmarks allowed models to memorize document layouts and visual patterns rather than learning robust information extraction capabilities. When models encounter similar templates in both training and testing phases, they can achieve high performance through pattern matching and layout recognition rather than understanding the semantic content and relationships between entities. By eliminating template overlap through resampling, the evaluation becomes more challenging as models must generalize to completely unseen document structures, revealing their true capability to extract information from novel layouts.

Design tradeoffs: Larger test sets vs. complete template separation; representativeness vs. zero overlap
Failure signatures: Performance drops indicate successful overlap removal; minimal drops suggest remaining overlap

## Limitations
- Resampling methodology may create unrepresentative test sets that don't reflect real-world document diversity
- Limited scope to only two benchmarks (SROIE and FUNSD) restricts generalizability
- Document similarity metrics used for overlap detection lack transparency and may have false positives/negatives
- Performance drops may be influenced by reduced training data size rather than solely by eliminated overlap

## Confidence
High Confidence: Identification of template overlap as data leakage source is well-supported by experimental evidence
Medium Confidence: Resampled splits provide more realistic evaluation, though representativeness concerns remain
Low Confidence: Generalizability to other benchmarks and long-term impact on model development practices is uncertain
