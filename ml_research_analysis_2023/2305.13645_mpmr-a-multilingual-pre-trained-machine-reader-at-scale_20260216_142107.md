# mPMR: A Multilingual Pre-trained Machine Reader at Scale

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2305.13645
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.13645
- Reference count: 18
- Improves XLM-R by up to 12.6 F1 on TyDiQA and 8.7 F1 on WikiAnn

## Executive Summary
mPMR introduces multilingual MRC-style pre-training to guide multilingual language models in both sequence classification and span extraction across languages. By constructing MRC data from Wikipedia hyperlinks and jointly pre-training with sequence classification and span extraction tasks, mPMR enables direct inheritance of multilingual NLU capability from pre-training to downstream tasks. The method also provides a unified solver for cross-lingual span extraction and sequence classification, allowing extraction of rationales to explain classification decisions. Experiments show mPMR improves over XLM-R by up to 12.6 F1 on TyDiQA and 8.7 F1 on WikiAnn, with faster convergence and better cross-lingual generalization.

## Method Summary
mPMR retrofits multilingual pre-trained language models with multilingual MRC-style pre-training to enable both sequence classification and span extraction across languages. The method constructs MRC data from Wikipedia hyperlinks using a Wiki Anchor Extraction (WAE) objective, then jointly pre-trains encoder and extractor modules with both sequence classification and span extraction tasks. Key innovations include Unified Q/C Construction and Stochastic Answer Position strategies to improve scalability and robustness across multiple languages. The pre-trained model can be fine-tuned on downstream XLU tasks using a unified MRC formulation that handles both classification and extraction tasks.

## Key Results
- Improves over XLM-R by up to 12.6 F1 on TyDiQA and 8.7 F1 on WikiAnn
- Achieves faster convergence during fine-tuning compared to baseline models
- Demonstrates better cross-lingual generalization across multiple languages
- Provides a unified solver for both span extraction and sequence classification tasks

- Core assumption: The multilingual MRC data constructed from Wikipedia hyperlinks provides sufficient coverage and quality to learn robust NLU capabilities across multiple languages

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: The Unified Q/C Construction and Stochastic Answer Position strategies improve scalability and robustness of mPMR across multiple languages
- Mechanism: By not relying on sentence segmentation tools and allowing the answer to appear at any position within the context, mPMR becomes more robust to position bias and can better generalize to low-resource languages
- Core assumption: The position of the answer within the context is not a strong predictor of the correct answer, and the model can learn to focus on content rather than position

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map: Encoder (from XLM-R) -> Extractor (randomly initialized) -> MRC Head
- Critical path:
  1. Pre-training: Load XLM-R encoder, initialize extractor, continually pre-train with multilingual MRC data using WAE objective
  2. Fine-tuning: Load pre-trained mPMR, fine-tune on downstream XLU tasks using MRC formulation
  3. Inference: Use fine-tuned mPMR to perform span extraction or sequence classification on target languages

- Design tradeoffs:
  - Using XLM-R as the encoder provides a strong multilingual representation backbone but may limit the potential improvements from using a more advanced mPLM
  - Constructing MRC data from Wikipedia hyperlinks provides a scalable source of multilingual data but may introduce biases related to the topics and entities covered in Wikipedia

- Failure signatures:
  - If the model fails to converge during pre-training, it may indicate issues with the MRC data quality or the WAE objective
  - If the model shows poor performance on low-resource languages, it may suggest that the multilingual MRC data is insufficient for those languages

- First 3 experiments:
  1. Train mPMR on a small subset of the multilingual MRC data and evaluate on a single downstream task to verify the basic functionality
  2. Compare the performance of mPMR with XLM-R on a diverse set of XLU tasks to quantify the improvements from the MRC-style pre-training
  3. Analyze the extracted rationales for sequence classification tasks to verify that they accurately represent the classification decisions

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

- Does the stochastic answer position strategy improve performance for all languages equally, or are there specific language groups that benefit more from this approach?
- What is the optimal balance between the number of answerable and unanswerable examples in the pre-training data, and how does this ratio affect downstream performance?
- How does mPMR's performance compare when using alternative knowledge sources beyond Wikipedia hyperlinks, such as Wikidata triples or other structured knowledge bases?
- Can the rationale extraction capability of mPMR be quantitatively evaluated to determine how often the extracted spans actually correspond to the decision-making process?
- What is the relationship between the amount of pre-training data and performance improvements, and is there a point of diminishing returns for adding more languages?

## Limitations

- The quality and generalizability of the automatically constructed multilingual MRC data is uncertain
- The paper lacks ablation studies that isolate the specific contributions of each proposed strategy
- The rationale extraction capability lacks quantitative validation and empirical evidence
- The method may not perform well on languages with few Wikipedia hyperlink annotations

## Confidence

- **High confidence**: The overall improvement claims over XLM-R (up to 12.6 F1 on TyDiQA and 8.7 F1 on WikiAnn) are well-supported by experimental results across multiple tasks and languages
- **Medium confidence**: The mechanism explanation for why MRC-style pre-training improves cross-lingual transfer is plausible but could benefit from more rigorous ablation studies
- **Medium confidence**: The unified solver capability for both span extraction and sequence classification is demonstrated, but the quality of extracted rationales needs further validation

## Next Checks

1. **Ablation study on MRC construction strategies**: Conduct experiments removing either the Unified Q/C Construction or Stochastic Answer Position strategy individually to quantify their individual contributions to overall performance improvements.

2. **Rationale quality evaluation**: For sequence classification tasks, compare the extracted spans against human-annotated rationales (if available) or conduct a human evaluation study to assess whether the extracted spans accurately represent the classification reasoning.

3. **Low-resource language analysis**: Perform detailed analysis of model performance across different resource levels of languages in the training data to identify any performance gaps and determine if additional data augmentation strategies are needed for truly low-resource languages.
