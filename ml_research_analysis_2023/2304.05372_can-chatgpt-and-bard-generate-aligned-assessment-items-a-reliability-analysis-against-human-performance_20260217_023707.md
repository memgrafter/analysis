# Can ChatGPT and Bard Generate Aligned Assessment Items? A Reliability Analysis against Human Performance

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2304.05372
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2304.05372
- Reference count: 6
- Primary result: ChatGPT (ICC=0.22) and Bard (ICC=0.05) show low inter-rater reliability with human complexity ratings, compared to human-human reliability (ICC=0.84)

## Executive Summary
This study evaluates whether OpenAI ChatGPT and Google Bard can reliably rate the complexity of IELTS writing prompts compared to human experts. Using intraclass correlation as the primary metric, the research finds that both AI tools demonstrate poor alignment with human judgments of prompt complexity. While human raters show excellent inter-rater reliability (ICC=0.84), both AI tools perform significantly worse, with ChatGPT achieving ICC=0.22 and Bard only ICC=0.05. The findings suggest that despite their capabilities in generating assessment items, current LLMs struggle to match human perceptions of writing prompt complexity, particularly in understanding semantic and pragmatic nuances.

## Method Summary
The study used 20 IELTS Academic Writing Task II prompts randomly selected from official past papers (1996-2022, excluding 2012 and 2014). Nineteen human raters with formal education/training in teaching writing rated each prompt on a 1-8 complexity scale. ChatGPT and Bard were then manually presented with the same prompts and asked to provide complexity ratings. Inter-rater reliability was computed using two-way random effects absolute agreement multiple raters intraclass correlation (ICC2/k model). The study compared ICC values between human raters, between human raters and each AI tool, and between AI tools.

## Key Results
- Human raters achieved excellent inter-rater reliability (ICC=0.84)
- ChatGPT showed moderate but limited alignment with human ratings (ICC=0.22)
- Bard demonstrated very poor alignment with human ratings (ICC=0.05)
- Both AI tools were capable of generating assessment items but failed to align with human perceptions of prompt complexity

## Foundational Learning

- **Concept**: Intraclass Correlation (ICC)
  - Why needed here: ICC is the primary metric used to quantify agreement between raters (both human and AI) on ordinal complexity ratings.
  - Quick check question: What does an ICC value of 0.84 indicate about human rater agreement?
    - Answer: It indicates excellent inter-rater reliability, suggesting strong consistency among human raters.

- **Concept**: Two-way random effects model in ICC
  - Why needed here: The study uses this model to account for both raters and items as random effects, appropriate when generalizing to other raters/items.
  - Quick check question: When would a two-way random effects ICC be preferred over a one-way model?
    - Answer: When both raters and items are considered random samples from a larger population.

- **Concept**: Ordinal data analysis
  - Why needed here: Complexity ratings are on an ordinal scale (1-8), requiring appropriate statistical methods that respect the ordered but non-interval nature of the data.
  - Quick check question: Why can't we use Pearson correlation for ordinal data?
    - Answer: Pearson correlation assumes interval-level data and linear relationships, which ordinal data doesn't satisfy.

## Architecture Onboarding

- **Component map**: Data collection (Qualtrics survey) → Human rating processing → AI tool interaction → ICC computation → Results interpretation
- **Critical path**: Human ratings must be reliable (ICC ≥ 0.7) before meaningful comparison with AI tools can occur
- **Design tradeoffs**: Single rater comparison (lower ICC precision) vs. averaging human ratings (introduces potential bias but increases reliability)
- **Failure signatures**: Low human inter-rater reliability would invalidate the gold standard; AI tools providing non-numeric responses would break the analysis pipeline
- **First 3 experiments**:
  1. Test ICC computation with synthetic data where true agreement is known to validate the statistical approach
  2. Run a small pilot with 3-5 human raters on 5 prompts to check rubric clarity and inter-rater reliability
  3. Compare single human rater vs. averaged human ratings on ICC values to assess the impact of aggregation

## Limitations

- Manual interaction with AI tools introduces potential variability in how prompts were presented and interpreted
- Single ratings from AI tools rather than multiple trials limits assessment of AI consistency
- The study assumes human ratings represent the gold standard without examining potential systematic biases among raters

## Confidence

- **High confidence**: Human raters demonstrate excellent inter-rater reliability (ICC = 0.84), establishing a robust gold standard for comparison
- **Medium confidence**: AI tools show measurable but weak alignment with human complexity ratings, with ChatGPT outperforming Bard
- **Low confidence**: The specific reasons for AI-human divergence remain unclear, and the manual rating process introduces potential variability

## Next Checks
