# VIFS: An End-to-End Variational Inference for Foley Sound Synthesis

## Executive Summary
This paper proposes VIFS, an end-to-end variational inference method for Foley sound synthesis. The problem addressed is generating diverse sound clips for specific categories in the DCASE 2023 Challenge Task 7. The core method idea is to adapt VITS, a text-to-speech model with variational inference, to the category-to-sound problem. The key modification is a heavily modified prior encoder that introduces an additional Gaussian distribution to promote variance within categories while maintaining consistency within each audio clip. The primary result is that VIFS achieved lower Frchet Audio Distance (FAD) than the baseline for four categories (dog bark, footstep, gunshot, rain), with an average FAD of 7.497 for the ensemble model when excluding the outlier category "moving motor vehicle".

## Method Summary
VIFS is a VITS-based variational inference model that generates diverse sound clips for specific categories in Foley sound synthesis. The model uses category embeddings instead of speaker embeddings, and modifies the prior encoder to handle longer sound events while maintaining temporal coherence. The modified prior encoder introduces an additional Gaussian distribution to promote variance within categories, and uses learnable positional embeddings and large-kernel frame prior networks to capture temporal structure. The model is trained with a GAN to improve perceptual quality and reduce artifacts. The key innovation is the adaptation of VITS to the category-to-sound problem, with modifications to the prior encoder to handle the unique challenges of Foley sound synthesis.

## Key Results
- VIFS achieved lower Frchet Audio Distance (FAD) than the baseline for four categories (dog bark, footstep, gunshot, rain)
- The ensemble model achieved an average FAD of 7.497 when excluding the outlier category "moving motor vehicle"
- VIFS still requires improvement to outperform the baseline for categories like keyboard, moving motor vehicle, and sneeze & cough

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: The additional Gaussian distribution in the prior encoder introduces intra-category variance while maintaining intra-clip coherence.
- Mechanism: By sampling from a standard normal distribution for the compressed posterior latent vector (via SAP) and adding L2 regularization, the model learns to generate diverse sound examples within a category while preserving the temporal consistency of each clip.
- Core assumption: The category embedding alone cannot capture the full range of acoustic diversity within a category, necessitating an additional source of stochasticity.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract]: "This introduced additional Gaussian on the prior encoder which promotes variance within the category."
  - [section 2.3]: "Second, to enhance diversity, we tried adding time-expanded Gaussian noise to expanded category embedding... we give another condition from the posterior latent variables to the prior encoder instead."
- Break condition: If the L2 regularization on the compressed vectors is too weak, the model may generate sounds that deviate too far from the training distribution, leading to poor FAD scores.

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: Learnable positional embedding and large-kernel frame prior networks provide temporal coherence for longer sound events.
- Mechanism: By conditioning the prior encoder on learnable positional embeddings and using large-kernel frame prior networks, the model can better capture the temporal structure of sound events that span several seconds, unlike phonemes in TTS which are much shorter.
- Core assumption: Sound events in Foley synthesis require modeling of temporal dependencies that are not present in the short pronunciation generation of TTS.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract]: "To compensate for the difference while maintaining consistency within each audio clip, we heavily modified the prior encoder..."
  - [section 2.3]: "To generate sounds with clip-level consistency, the prior encoder needs to model latent variables considering temporal position. Thus, we add learnable positional embedding along time axis..."
- Break condition: If the positional embeddings are not properly learned, the model may generate sounds with abrupt transitions or inconsistent temporal structure.

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: The self-attentive pooling (SAP) of posterior latent variables provides a compressed representation that guides the prior encoder during training.
- Mechanism: By compressing the posterior latent variables using SAP and expanding them to match the size of the category embedding, the prior encoder can learn to generate sounds that are consistent with the training data's latent space distribution.
- Core assumption: The posterior latent variables contain information about the sound event that can be used to guide the generation of diverse yet coherent sounds during training.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [section 2.3]: "The posterior latent variables z are compressed to a single vector by self-attentive pooling (SAP) and expanded in time dimension to match the size of category embedding."
- Break condition: If the SAP layer fails to capture the relevant information from the posterior latent variables, the prior encoder may generate sounds that are inconsistent with the training data's latent space distribution.

- Design tradeoffs:
  - Using a VAE framework allows for diverse sound generation within categories but may introduce some loss of fidelity compared to autoregressive models.
  - Incorporating a GAN improves the perceptual quality of the generated sounds but adds complexity to the training process.
  - Modifying the prior encoder to handle longer sound events improves temporal coherence but may reduce the diversity of the generated sounds.

- Failure signatures:
  - High FAD scores indicate that the generated sounds deviate significantly from the training data distribution.
  - Poor subjective quality (e.g., audible artifacts, lack of realism) suggests issues with the GAN training or decoder architecture.
  - Inconsistent temporal structure or abrupt transitions in the generated sounds may indicate problems with the prior encoder or positional embeddings.
