# AudioPaLM: A Large Language Model That Can Speak and Listen

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID**: 2306.12925
- **Source URL**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.12925
- **Reference count**: 25
- **Primary result**: AudioPaLM unifies speech and text processing in a single model, achieving state-of-the-art speech-to-speech translation and competitive speech recognition performance.

## Executive Summary
AudioPaLM presents a novel approach to unified speech and text processing by extending a text-only large language model with audio tokens. The model achieves impressive results across multiple speech tasks including speech-to-speech translation, speech-to-text translation, and speech recognition. By leveraging the linguistic knowledge from text pretraining and combining it with discrete audio tokenization, AudioPaLM demonstrates strong zero-shot translation capabilities and voice preservation across languages. The unified architecture enables handling of arbitrarily interleaved speech and text sequences through a joint vocabulary and task-based prefixes.

## Method Summary
AudioPaLM extends a pre-trained text-only PaLM-2 model by expanding its embedding matrix to include audio tokens, enabling unified processing of speech and text. The model uses discrete audio tokenization (w2v-BERT or USM-v2) to convert speech into token sequences that can be processed by the Transformer decoder. A joint vocabulary combining audio and text tokens, along with task prefixes, allows training a single decoder-only model on a mixture of ASR, AST, and S2ST tasks. The model generates outputs autoregressively and can decode audio tokens to raw waveforms using AudioLM or SoundStorm. Training involves initializing from PaLM-2 weights and finetuning on speech tasks with a carefully designed task mixture.

## Key Results
- Achieves state-of-the-art results on speech-to-speech translation benchmarks
- Demonstrates competitive performance on speech recognition tasks
- Shows strong zero-shot translation capabilities for unseen language pairs
- Preserves speaker identity across languages using audio prompting

## Architecture Onboarding

- **Component map**: Input (mixed text/audio tokens + task prefixes) -> Modified PaLM-2 decoder -> Output (text/audio tokens) -> Post-processing (detokenization/audio decoding)

- **Critical path**: 1) Tokenize input audio/text 2) Prepend task prefix tokens 3) Pass through modified decoder 4) Generate output tokens autoregressively 5) Detokenize text or decode audio tokens to waveforms

- **Design tradeoffs**: Tokenization method (w2v-BERT vs USM-v1 vs USM-v2), model size (larger = better but more compute), training mixture (ASR helps AST but S2ST slightly hurts ASR/AST)

- **Failure signatures**: Poor tokenization leading to low performance, catastrophic forgetting of text capabilities, task confusion from ineffective prefixes

- **First 3 experiments**: 1) Compare training from scratch vs. finetuning text-pretrained checkpoint 2) Compare different tokenization methods on same dataset 3) Train with/without combined tasks to measure intermediate supervision impact

## Limitations
- Unclear contribution of PaLM-2 pretraining versus architecture to zero-shot capabilities
- Limited human evaluation sample size (50 sentences) for voice preservation assessment
- Small model variants (1.2B) used in ablations may not represent full-scale performance

## Confidence
- **High Confidence**: Core architectural innovation of expanding embedding matrix for audio tokens
- **Medium Confidence**: Initializing from text-pretrained weights significantly improves speech performance
- **Medium Confidence**: Zero-shot translation results, though impressive, depend on undisclosed PaLM-2 pretraining details
