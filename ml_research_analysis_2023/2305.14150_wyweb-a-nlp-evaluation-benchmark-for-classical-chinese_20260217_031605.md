# WYWEB: A NLP Evaluation Benchmark For Classical Chinese

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2305.14150
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.14150
- Reference count: 40
- Current state-of-the-art models struggle with classical Chinese tasks

## Executive Summary
This paper introduces WYWEB, a comprehensive benchmark for evaluating NLP models on classical Chinese. The benchmark includes nine carefully designed tasks covering sentence classification, sequence labeling, reading comprehension, and machine translation. The authors demonstrate that current state-of-the-art models struggle with these tasks, highlighting the need for better classical Chinese language models. The benchmark uses high-quality classical Chinese texts from authoritative sources and provides evaluation tools and a leaderboard to support further research in this domain.

## Method Summary
WYWEB consists of nine tasks across four categories: sequence classification (genre classification, time period classification, filial piety classification), sequence labeling (punctuation restoration, named entity recognition), sentence pair tasks (synonym detection), reading comprehension (passage selection, idiom reasoning), and sequence-to-sequence tasks (translation). The benchmark provides pre-processed datasets, evaluation metrics (F1, Accuracy, BLEU, chrF2, TER, ROUGE), and fine-tuning scripts for pre-trained models including GuwenBERT, RoBERTa-classical-chinese, SikuBERT, SikuRoBERTa, DeBERTa-base, and RoBERTa-wwm-ext.

## Key Results
- Current models show significant performance gaps compared to human baselines across all tasks
- Sequence labeling tasks (PUNC, GLNER) prove particularly challenging for models
- Reading comprehension tasks (WYWRC, IRC) require implicit knowledge and reasoning beyond current model capabilities
- Machine translation between classical and modern Chinese remains difficult for existing models

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
WYWEB tasks reveal fundamental limitations in classical Chinese language models due to the language's distinct linguistic features. By requiring models to handle tasks like sentence segmentation without punctuation and named entity recognition in texts without modern punctuation, the benchmark exposes the gap between models pre-trained on modern Chinese versus classical Chinese. The core assumption is that classical Chinese has significantly different linguistic characteristics than modern Chinese, making direct transfer of pre-trained models ineffective.

### Mechanism 2
WYWEB's task diversity exposes models' weaknesses in handling various classical Chinese text styles and periods. The benchmark includes texts from different historical periods and different genres, forcing models to generalize across diverse linguistic phenomena. The core assumption is that classical Chinese texts vary significantly across time periods and genres, requiring models to adapt to different linguistic patterns.

### Mechanism 3
The human performance gap indicates that WYWEB tasks require implicit knowledge and reasoning abilities beyond current model capabilities. Tasks like WYWRC and IRC require understanding cultural context, historical references, and idiomatic expressions that are not explicitly stated in the text. The core assumption is that classical Chinese texts contain implicit knowledge and cultural references that require human-like reasoning to interpret correctly.

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map: WYWEB consists of nine tasks divided into four categories: sequence classification (GJC, TLC, FSPC), sequence labeling (PUNC, GLNER), sentence pair tasks (XuCi), reading comprehension (WYWRC, IRC), and sequence-to-sequence tasks (WYWMT). Each task has its own dataset and evaluation metrics.

- Critical path: The most challenging task is WYWRC, which requires models to understand context, select correct answers from multiple choices, and sometimes apply additional knowledge. Improving performance on this task will likely improve performance on other tasks as well.

- Design tradeoffs: WYWEB prioritizes task diversity and linguistic coverage over task quantity. The benchmark includes fewer tasks than some other NLP benchmarks, but each task is carefully designed to test specific aspects of classical Chinese language understanding.

- Failure signatures: Models may perform well on some tasks (e.g., GJC) but struggle on others (e.g., WYWRC, IRC). This suggests that the model has learned some linguistic patterns but lacks the broader understanding required for more complex tasks.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- Question: How well do current models handle the translation of classical Chinese to modern Chinese, and what are the key challenges in this process?
- Basis in paper: The paper introduces WYWMT, a task for evaluating machine translation between classical and modern Chinese, and presents baseline results showing the models' performance.
- Why unresolved: While the paper provides initial results, it does not fully explore the nuances of translation quality or the specific linguistic features that pose challenges for models.
- What evidence would resolve it: Detailed analysis of translation errors, comparison with human translations, and exploration of linguistic features unique to classical Chinese that affect translation quality.

### Open Question 2
- Question: What are the limitations of using classical Chinese corpora for training models, and how can these be addressed to improve model performance?
- Basis in paper: The paper mentions the use of various classical Chinese corpora and highlights the challenges in handling classical Chinese text, but does not delve into the limitations of these corpora.
- Why unresolved: The paper does not provide a comprehensive analysis of the limitations of the corpora used or propose solutions to overcome these limitations.
- What evidence would resolve it: Comparative studies of different corpora, analysis of corpus size and diversity, and proposed methods for corpus augmentation or improvement.

### Open Question 3
- Question: How can the performance of models on classical Chinese tasks be improved, particularly for tasks like reading comprehension and machine translation?
- Basis in paper: The paper shows that current models struggle with tasks like WYWRC and WYWMT, indicating a need for improvement.
- Why unresolved: The paper does not provide specific strategies or techniques for improving model performance on these challenging tasks.
- What evidence would resolve it: Development and testing of new model architectures, incorporation of domain-specific knowledge, and exploration of advanced fine-tuning techniques tailored to classical Chinese.

## Limitations

- Limited evaluation to six pre-trained models without extensive architecture exploration
- Incomplete documentation of text distribution across historical periods and genres
- Human performance baselines lack detailed expertise level specification and inter-rater reliability measures

## Confidence

- **Medium confidence**: Claims about fundamental limitations of current models in classical Chinese understanding
- **Medium confidence**: Claims about task diversity and linguistic coverage
- **Low confidence**: Claims about the benchmark's ability to drive future research progress

## Next Checks

1. **Model architecture ablation study**: Test whether performance gaps persist when using larger models (e.g., LLaMA, GPT-style architectures) or different architectural approaches (e.g., encoder-decoder vs. encoder-only) to determine if the limitations are fundamental or architecture-dependent.

2. **Cross-period generalization analysis**: Evaluate model performance when trained on texts from one historical period and tested on another to quantify the actual impact of temporal variation in classical Chinese.

3. **Expert annotation validation**: Conduct a more rigorous human evaluation with multiple classical Chinese experts, including inter-rater reliability measurements and expertise level documentation, to establish more robust human performance baselines.
