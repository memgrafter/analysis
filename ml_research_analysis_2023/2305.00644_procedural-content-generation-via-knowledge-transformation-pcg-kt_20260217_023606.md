# Procedural Content Generation via Knowledge Transformation (PCG-KT)

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2305.00644
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.00644
- Reference count: 40
- One-line primary result: Introduces a framework for Procedural Content Generation via Knowledge Transformation (PCG-KT) that characterizes methods where content generation is enabled by transforming knowledge between domains

## Executive Summary
This paper introduces the PCG-KT framework, which provides a new perspective on procedural content generation by characterizing methods where content generation is enabled through knowledge transformation between domains. The framework defines key features including knowledge structure, derivation function, transformation context, and transformation properties to describe PCG-KT approaches. The authors survey existing works that fall under this framework, categorizing them into combinational creativity and transfer learning approaches, and discuss various techniques such as conceptual blending, conceptual expansion, domain adaptation, and style transfer.

## Method Summary
The PCG-KT framework involves transforming knowledge between domains to enable procedural content generation. The method follows a pipeline where raw knowledge (game content in native format) is processed through a derivation function to extract structured knowledge, then transformed via a transformation function that maps knowledge between domains, and finally outputs transformed knowledge in the target domain format. The approach uses machine learning models like variational autoencoders (VAEs), Markov chains, and conditional VAEs combined with transformation functions such as conceptual blending, domain adaptation, and style transfer to generate novel content that blends or transfers characteristics from source domains to target domains.

## Key Results
- Introduces the PCG-KT framework as a new lens for understanding and categorizing PCG methods
- Categorizes existing PCG-KT approaches into combinational creativity and transfer learning types
- Demonstrates how knowledge transformation enables content generation for games with limited training data
- Highlights open problems including evaluation methods, multi-genre extension, hybrid approaches, and controllability tools

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: Knowledge transformation enables PCG systems to generate content for games with limited training data by transferring and adapting existing knowledge from related games.
- Mechanism: The PCG-KT framework defines a transformation function that maps knowledge structures from a source domain to a target domain, allowing models trained on one game to be adapted for content generation in another game with minimal retraining.
- Core assumption: There exists a meaningful mapping between the knowledge structures of different games that can be learned and applied algorithmically.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "Such works have involved, for example, performing transfer learning on models trained on one game's content to adapt to another game's content"
  - [section] "We identify a number of potential, longstanding challenges in PCG that can be addressed by answering the above questions... If we could transform knowledge from existing games towards the production of new games, we could sidestep this issue"
  - [corpus] Weak evidence - related papers focus on general PCG but do not specifically address knowledge transformation between domains
- Break condition: If the knowledge structures between source and target domains are too dissimilar, the transformation function cannot produce meaningful mappings, resulting in poor quality generated content.

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: Compositional creativity approaches within PCG-KT enable the generation of entirely novel content by recombining existing knowledge in innovative ways.
- Mechanism: Conceptual blending and conceptual expansion techniques transform extracted knowledge by finding equivalence points between different domains and projecting them into a blended space to generate new content types.
- Core assumption: Existing knowledge from multiple games contains sufficient diversity and complementarity to create meaningful novel combinations when transformed.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [section] "In [13], Guzdial and Riedl employed conceptual blending on learned level design models... combining two generative level design models that produced underwater and castle Mario levels to create a model that could generate underwater-castle levels"
  - [section] "In [18], Guzdial and Riedl demonstrated the production of novel games from the same input knowledge... They evaluated these games in a human subject study compared to other combinational creativity approaches"
  - [corpus] No direct evidence - corpus focuses on general PCG approaches rather than specific combinational creativity methods
- Break condition: If the transformation function cannot find meaningful equivalence points between input domains, the blending process produces incoherent or uninteresting content that fails to leverage the complementary aspects of the source knowledge.

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: Domain adaptation approaches within PCG-KT enable style transfer between games while preserving gameplay affordances and structural properties.
- Mechanism: The transformation function learns mappings between tile representations and affordance-based representations, allowing levels to be converted between games while maintaining their functional properties.
- Core assumption: Gameplay mechanics and level structure can be decoupled from visual presentation, allowing style to be transferred independently of core gameplay elements.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [section] "Sarkar and Cooper introduced tile2tile [37], a method for transferring style between levels of different platformer games... translating levels from a source game into an affordance-based representation using a pre-defined mapping"
  - [section] "In [9], Snodgrass and Onta˜n´on presented an approach aimed at automatically defining a mapping between the representations of multiple platforming game level domains"
  - [corpus] Weak evidence - corpus contains general PCG papers but lacks specific research on style transfer mechanisms
- Break condition: If the affordance-based representation cannot capture all relevant gameplay properties, the style transfer process may preserve visual style but lose essential gameplay characteristics, resulting in non-functional or unbalanced content.

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map:
  - Raw Knowledge: Game content in native format (levels, sprites, rulesets)
  - Derivation Function: ML model or hand-authored rules that extract structured knowledge from raw content
  - Transformation Function: Algorithm that maps knowledge between domains (transfer learning, blending, style transfer)
  - Transformed Knowledge: Output content in target domain format
  - Controllability Interface: User-facing controls for guiding the transformation process

- Critical path: Raw Knowledge → Derivation Function → Transformation Function → Transformed Knowledge
  - The transformation function is the core component that distinguishes PCG-KT from standard PCG approaches

- Design tradeoffs:
  - Representational vs Content Distance: Balancing between preserving core gameplay properties while achieving novel transformations
  - Automation vs Control: Tradeoff between fully automated transformation and user-guided creative processes
  - Generality vs Specificity: Balancing between general transformation frameworks and game-specific optimizations

- Failure signatures:
  - Poor quality generated content: Indicates issues with the derivation or transformation functions
  - Mode collapse in VAEs: Suggests insufficient diversity in the learned latent space
  - Overfitting to source domain: Transformation function too closely tied to source domain characteristics

- First 3 experiments:
  1. Implement a simple tile-to-tile mapping system between two platformer games and evaluate generated content quality
  2. Train a VAE on levels from multiple games and perform interpolation between latent vectors to generate blended levels
  3. Apply style transfer between two games using affordance-based representations and evaluate preservation of gameplay properties

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- Question: How can we develop robust and informative evaluation techniques for knowledge transformation in PCG-KT, beyond just assessing the goodness and functionality of generated outputs?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper explicitly discusses the need for better evaluation methods, stating "It is unclear how to best evaluate knowledge transformation in a generative process" and "more robust and informative evaluation techniques need to be developed."
- Why unresolved: Current evaluation methods focus on output quality rather than the properties of the transformation itself, and even content evaluation methods need improvement for blended content.
- What evidence would resolve it: Development and validation of specific metrics capable of assessing various properties of knowledge transformation, potentially utilizing features from the PCG-KT framework.

### Open Question 2
- Question: How can knowledge transformation approaches be extended to multiple game genres, enabling the generation of new types and varieties of content beyond platformer games?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper highlights that most PCG-KT works have focused on platformer games and poses the question "Could we design transformation functions able to combine extracted knowledge from a platformer such as Mario with extracted knowledge from an adventure game like Zelda?"
- Why unresolved: Working with knowledge from multiple genres poses new challenges in developing techniques for reasoning with such knowledge and applying transformations meaningfully.
- What evidence would resolve it: Demonstration of successful knowledge transformation approaches that combine knowledge from different genres, resulting in novel gameplay experiences and potentially new game genres.

### Open Question 3
- Question: How can hybrid knowledge transformation processes be developed that combine multiple models and techniques, such as Markov chains, Bayesian networks, and VAEs, to inform and influence the transformation process?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper suggests exploring "hybrid knowledge transformation processes that combine the use of these models in some meaningful way" and asks "could the models used for conceptual expansion be combined with the Markov models used for domain adaptation or with VAEs?"
- Why unresolved: While existing works use single models or architectures, the potential of combining different models to create hierarchical knowledge transformation remains unexplored.
- What evidence would resolve it: Successful implementation and evaluation of hybrid knowledge transformation systems that combine multiple models, demonstrating improved performance or new capabilities compared to single-model approaches.

## Limitations
- The framework provides theoretical grounding but lacks extensive empirical validation across diverse game domains
- Many mechanisms rely on assumptions about knowledge mappings between domains that haven't been rigorously tested
- Effectiveness for games beyond platformer and puzzle genres remains largely unexplored

## Confidence
- High Confidence: The conceptual framing of PCG-KT as a knowledge transformation approach and its categorization of existing methods into combinational creativity and transfer learning
- Medium Confidence: The specific mechanisms described for domain adaptation and style transfer, as these are supported by concrete examples but limited empirical evidence
- Low Confidence: Claims about evaluation methods and the framework's applicability to "multiple game genres" without specific validation studies
