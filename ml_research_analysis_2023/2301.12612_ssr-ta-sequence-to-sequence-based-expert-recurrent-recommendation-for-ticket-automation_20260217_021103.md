# SSR-TA: Sequence to Sequence based expert recurrent recommendation for ticket automation

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2301.12612
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2301.12612
- Reference count: 16
- Key outcome: SSR-TA outperforms several baselines in resolution rate, mean step to resolver, and mean reciprocal rank for expert recommendation in ticket automation

## Executive Summary
This paper addresses the challenge of efficiently assigning experts to resolve IT tickets in software systems. The proposed solution, SSR-TA, combines a sequence-to-sequence model that translates ticket descriptions into resolutions with a recurrent recommendation network that generates sequences of expert recommendations. By assuming that previous experts in the recommendation sequence cannot solve the ticket, the model iteratively refines its recommendations. Experiments on two real-world datasets demonstrate that SSR-TA achieves higher resolution rates and faster expert identification compared to several baseline approaches.

## Method Summary
SSR-TA uses a sequence-to-sequence architecture with a bidirectional GRU encoder that transforms ticket descriptions into hidden states, and a decoder that generates corresponding resolutions. The model incorporates an attention-based recurrent recommendation network that generates a sequence of expert recommendations, with each recommendation conditioned on the assumption that previous experts cannot solve the ticket. The model is trained using a joint objective function that balances description translation accuracy, recommendation accuracy, and recommendation diversity. The approach is evaluated on two real-world datasets using metrics including resolution rate, mean step to resolver, and mean reciprocal rank.

## Key Results
- SSR-TA outperforms baselines in resolution rate, achieving 1-3% higher performance on both datasets
- The model reduces mean step to resolver compared to one-time recommendation approaches
- SSR-TA shows faster convergence in reciprocal rank metrics, especially for shorter recommendation sequences (N<3)

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: The sequence-to-sequence model captures ticket description features by translating them into resolutions, improving expert recommendation accuracy.
- Mechanism: SSR-TA uses a bidirectional GRU encoder to transform ticket descriptions into hidden states, then a decoder generates corresponding resolutions. This forces the model to learn meaningful representations of ticket content that correlate with expert solutions.
- Core assumption: The resolution of a ticket contains complementary information to its description that, when combined, creates a more complete representation of the problem.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "The sequence to sequence model transforms the ticket description into the corresponding resolution for capturing the potential and useful features of representing tickets."
  - [section 3.1] "For training SSR-TA, the description of solved tickets is translated into the corresponding resolution, where the potential features of ticket description and resolution are captured to improve the representation of the ticket description."
- Break condition: If ticket descriptions and resolutions are semantically disjoint or if the resolution doesn't meaningfully reflect the problem characteristics.

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: The recurrent recommendation network with attention mechanism improves the ranking of true resolvers by assuming previous experts cannot solve the ticket.
- Mechanism: After each recommendation, the model updates its hidden state based on which experts were previously recommended and failed, then generates the next recommendation conditioned on this updated state.
- Core assumption: Expert solving ability is context-dependent - knowing which experts previously failed provides information about which types of experts are more likely to succeed.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "The recurrent recommendation network recommends the appropriate expert based on the assumption that the previous expert in the recommendation sequence cannot solve the expert."
  - [section 3.2] "SSR-TA is designed to generate the next expert based on the assumption that the previous expert in the recommended sequence failed to solve tickets."
  - [section 4.6] "Notice that the RR of SSR-TA and DeepRouting increases faster than other baselines on TDS-a, especially when the length of the recommendation sequence is less than 3."
- Break condition: If expert solving ability is independent of previous recommendations or if the assumption that previous experts failed is incorrect.

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: The joint objective function balancing description translation, recommendation accuracy, and recommendation diversity improves overall model performance.
- Mechanism: The model optimizes three objectives simultaneously - making the generated resolution match the true resolution, ensuring recommended experts are likely to solve the ticket, and ensuring diversity in the recommendation sequence.
- Core assumption: Optimizing these three objectives together creates a model that both understands tickets well and recommends appropriate experts efficiently.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [section 3.3] "SSR-TA has three objectives, including the description translation, the accuracy of recommendation, and the disparity of recommendation."
  - [section 4.4] "As observed from Fig. 3 and Table 5, the models without the resolution decoder (i.e., x and x+z) perform worse than the seq2seq-based models (i.e., x+y and x+y+z)."
  - [section 4.6] "SSR-TA outperforms other baselines" in resolution rate, MRR, and MSTR metrics.
- Break condition: If the three objectives conflict in ways that prevent convergence or if the weighting parameters α1, α2, α3 cannot be properly tuned.

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map:
  Input layer: Ticket description words embedded as vectors → Description encoder: Bidirectional GRU network → Resolution decoder: Standard GRU network (training only) → Attention mechanism: Computes weights for recurrent recommendation → Expert recommendation network: Generates sequence of experts → Output layer: Probability distribution over experts for each recommendation step

- Critical path: Description → Encoder → Hidden state → Decoder (training only) → Attention-based recurrent recommendation → Expert sequence

- Design tradeoffs:
  - Using seq2seq vs direct classification: seq2seq forces richer representation learning but is more computationally expensive
  - Attention mechanism complexity vs performance gain: adds parameters but improves recommendation quality
  - Recommendation sequence length vs efficiency: longer sequences improve accuracy but increase resolution time

- Failure signatures:
  - Poor resolution generation indicates encoding issues or insufficient training data
  - Stagnant recommendation performance suggests attention mechanism not learning meaningful weights
  - High variance in recommendations across similar tickets indicates overfitting or lack of generalization

- First 3 experiments:
  1. Compare performance with and without the resolution decoder to verify its contribution to feature learning
  2. Test different sequence lengths (N=1,3,5,10) to find optimal balance between accuracy and efficiency
  3. Evaluate on a held-out test set with only user-generated tickets vs system-generated tickets to understand model limitations

## Limitations
- The assumption that previous experts cannot solve a ticket may not hold in all organizational contexts, particularly in smaller teams with limited expert availability.
- Model performance on system-generated tickets is questionable due to lack of contextual richness in descriptions.
- The seq2seq approach requires substantial training data and computational resources compared to traditional methods.

## Confidence
- High: SSR-TA architecture design and basic methodology
- Medium: Performance improvement claims over baselines (1-3% margins may not justify increased complexity)
- Low: Cross-domain generalization beyond the two tested datasets
