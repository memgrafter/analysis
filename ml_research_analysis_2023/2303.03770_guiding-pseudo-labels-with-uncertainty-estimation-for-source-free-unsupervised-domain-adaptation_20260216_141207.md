# Guiding Pseudo-labels with Uncertainty Estimation for Source-free Unsupervised Domain Adaptation

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID**: 2303.03770
- **Source URL**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.03770
- **Reference count**: 40
- **Primary result**: Achieves state-of-the-art accuracy in source-free unsupervised domain adaptation with +1.8% gains on VisDA-C and DomainNet, and +12.3% on PACS

## Executive Summary
This paper addresses the challenging problem of source-free unsupervised domain adaptation (SF-UDA), where only a pre-trained source model and unlabeled target data are available. The authors propose a method that combines pseudo-label refinement through neighbor knowledge aggregation with uncertainty-weighted classification loss and a temporal queue-based negative pairs exclusion strategy for contrastive learning. Their approach demonstrates significant performance improvements across three major domain adaptation benchmarks (VisDA-C, DomainNet, and PACS), with the most substantial gain of +12.3% accuracy on PACS in the single-source setting. The method is particularly effective in handling noisy pseudo-labels by reweighting the loss based on estimated uncertainty and excluding incorrect negative pairs through historical pseudo-label analysis.

## Method Summary
The method iteratively refines pseudo-labels using knowledge aggregation from nearest neighbors in feature space, where the probability outputs from K neighbors are averaged to create more reliable pseudo-labels. The classification loss is reweighted based on the entropy of these refined pseudo-labels, with lower entropy (higher reliability) receiving higher weight. A temporal queue stores features and pseudo-labels from past T epochs, allowing the contrastive learning framework to identify and exclude negative pairs that shared the same class at any point in the history. The method uses a negative learning loss that treats the correct class as an "avoided" class rather than the target, making it more robust to noisy pseudo-labels. The approach is implemented with standard ResNet backbones and trained using self-learning with joint optimization of the uncertainty-weighted classification loss and contrastive loss.

## Key Results
- Achieves +1.8% accuracy improvement over state-of-the-art methods on VisDA-C and DomainNet datasets
- Demonstrates +12.3% accuracy gain on PACS dataset in single-source setting
- Shows robustness to noisy pseudo-labels through uncertainty estimation and reweighting
- Outperforms existing methods on all three benchmark datasets across various domain shifts

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: Reweighting classification loss by estimated uncertainty of refined pseudo-labels reduces the impact of noisy labels during adaptation.
- Mechanism: Entropy of averaged neighbor predictions is used to estimate pseudo-label reliability; unreliable labels receive lower weight in the loss.
- Core assumption: Lower entropy in averaged neighbor predictions indicates higher consensus and thus higher reliability of the refined pseudo-label.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "The classification loss is reweighted based on the reliability of the pseudo-labels that is measured by estimating their uncertainty."
  - [section] "we reweight the classiﬁcation loss in Eq. (7) computing a weight w that puts more importance on pseudo-labels obtained from p̂t with low entropy and less importance to pseudo-labels obtained from p̂t with high entropy."
- Break condition: If neighbor predictions are consistently wrong due to severe domain shift, low entropy could falsely indicate high reliability.

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: Temporal queue-based negative pairs exclusion strategy identifies and excludes same-class sample pairs even when pseudo-labels are noisy.
- Mechanism: Queue stores features and past pseudo-labels; negative pairs are excluded if samples shared the same pseudo-label at any point in the past T epochs.
- Core assumption: Correct class labels will appear in the history of pseudo-labels even if current labels are noisy, allowing correct identification of same-class pairs.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "A novel negative pairs exclusion strategy is proposed to identify and exclude negative pairs made of samples sharing the same class, even in presence of some noise in the pseudo-labels."
  - [section] "By looking at the history of pseudo-labels, we have a higher probability to observe, at least one time, the correct label in the history rather than by looking only at the current pseudo-label."
- Break condition: If noise is persistent across all T epochs, history-based exclusion may fail to identify same-class pairs.

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: Knowledge aggregation from nearest neighbors refines pseudo-labels by leveraging feature space structure.
- Mechanism: Refined pseudo-labels are obtained by averaging probability distributions from K nearest neighbors in feature space.
- Core assumption: Similar samples lie close in feature space and are likely to belong to the same class.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [section] "the pseudo-label of xt is reﬁned by aggregating knowledge from the selected neighbours... the probability outputs from the selected neighbours are averaged to perform a soft-voting strategy."
  - [corpus] "Key Design Choices for Double-Transfer in Source-Free Unsupervised Domain Adaptation" suggests neighbor-based refinement is effective in SF-UDA.
- Break condition: If feature space is not well-structured (e.g., due to poor adaptation), nearest neighbors may not share the same class.

## Foundational Learning

- Concept: Entropy as uncertainty measure
  - Why needed here: Entropy quantifies disagreement among neighbor predictions, enabling uncertainty estimation for pseudo-label reweighting.
  - Quick check question: If a sample has neighbors voting for different classes with equal probability, what is the entropy of their average distribution?

- Concept: Contrastive learning with negative pairs
  - Why needed here: Contrastive framework enforces feature space structure where same-class samples are close, enabling effective neighbor-based refinement.
  - Quick check question: What happens to features from same-class samples if negative pairs are not properly excluded in contrastive learning?

- Concept: Negative learning loss
  - Why needed here: Negative learning loss is robust to noisy pseudo-labels by treating the correct class as an "avoided" class rather than the target.
  - Quick check question: How does negative learning loss differ from standard cross-entropy loss in handling incorrect pseudo-labels?

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map: Feature extractor -> Classifier head -> Temporal queue -> Neighbor bank -> Entropy-based uncertainty estimator -> Negative learning loss module
- Critical path:
  1. Generate initial pseudo-labels from source model
  2. Maintain temporal queue and neighbor bank
  3. For each target sample: find neighbors, aggregate predictions, estimate uncertainty, refine pseudo-label
  4. Compute contrastive loss with temporal queue-based exclusion
  5. Compute classification loss with uncertainty-based reweighting
  6. Update model parameters
- Design tradeoffs:
  - Queue length T: longer queues provide more history but increase memory usage
  - Number of neighbors K: more neighbors improve refinement but increase computation
  - Uncertainty weighting function: exponential vs linear affects sensitivity to entropy
- Failure signatures:
  - Accuracy plateaus early: likely feature space not well-structured
  - Accuracy decreases over epochs: overfitting to noisy pseudo-labels
  - No improvement from uncertainty weighting: entropy estimation not capturing reliability
- First 3 experiments:
  1. Verify neighbor-based pseudo-label refinement improves accuracy over initial pseudo-labels
  2. Test contrastive loss with vs without temporal queue-based exclusion
  3. Compare exponential vs linear uncertainty weighting functions

## Limitations
- The method's performance relies heavily on specific hyperparameter configurations (K, T, momentum rates) that are not fully specified
- Neighbor-based refinement assumes meaningful feature space structure throughout adaptation, which may break down under severe domain shifts
- The substantial accuracy gains may partly stem from improved hyperparameter tuning rather than solely the novel components

## Confidence
- **High confidence**: The mechanism of entropy-based uncertainty weighting for pseudo-label reweighting is well-supported by ablation studies and aligns with established uncertainty estimation principles
- **Medium confidence**: The temporal queue-based negative pairs exclusion strategy shows theoretical soundness, but the claim that looking at T epochs of history improves exclusion accuracy requires more empirical validation
- **Medium confidence**: The 12.3% accuracy improvement on PACS is substantial, but the paper doesn't adequately address whether this stems from the novel components or improved hyperparameter tuning

## Next Checks
