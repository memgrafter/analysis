# A Web-based Mpox Skin Lesion Detection System Using State-of-the-art Deep Learning Models Considering Racial Diversity

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2306.14169
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.14169
- Reference count: 40
- Primary result: Transfer learning with HAM10000 weights achieves 83.59% accuracy in mpox lesion classification

## Executive Summary
This study addresses the clinical challenge of mpox skin lesion diagnosis by developing a deep learning-based detection system. The authors created MSLD v2.0, a dataset containing mpox and five other skin diseases, with careful attention to reducing racial bias through HSV color space augmentation. Using transfer learning with pre-trained models like DenseNet121, the system achieves 83.59% accuracy. A web application enables users to upload skin images for mpox prediction, demonstrating the potential of AI-assisted tools for public health screening and early detection.

## Method Summary
The study developed a deep learning pipeline using transfer learning from pre-trained models, specifically leveraging weights from the HAM10000 skin lesion dataset. Images were collected via web-scraping, verified by expert dermatologists, and augmented using both standard transformations and HSV color space variations to reduce racial bias. The models were trained and evaluated using 5-fold cross-validation, with the best-performing DenseNet121 architecture achieving 83.59% accuracy. A Streamlit web application was created to allow user image uploads and mpox predictions.

## Key Results
- Transfer learning with HAM10000 weights achieved 83.59 ± 2.11% accuracy, outperforming ImageNet initialization
- HSV color space augmentation increased dataset universality and aimed to reduce racial bias across skin tones
- Expert dermatologist verification ensured dataset quality by validating disease labels for all images
- Web application successfully deployed for user-friendly mpox skin lesion detection and screening

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: Transfer learning from HAM10000 improves mpox classification over generic ImageNet pre-training
- Mechanism: HAM10000's domain-specific skin lesion features (texture, color, shape patterns) transfer effectively to mpox classification, reducing data requirements and improving feature extraction
- Core assumption: Low-level visual features relevant to skin lesions transfer across dermoscopic and clinical image modalities
- Evidence anchors: Abstract states "best overall accuracy of 83.59 ± 2.11%" with HAM10000 pre-training; Section shows improved performance metrics with HAM10000 initialization

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: HSV color space augmentation reduces racial bias in classification
- Mechanism: Transforming images into 180 HSV variations forces the model to learn texture-based features rather than color-dependent patterns that correlate with skin tone
- Core assumption: Color information is a primary source of racial bias in skin lesion classification
- Evidence anchors: Abstract mentions "color space data augmentation method to increase skin color variability"; Section describes HSV transformation to increase dataset universality

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: Expert dermatologist verification reduces misclassification risk
- Mechanism: Web-scraped images may contain incorrect labels; dermatologist verification ensures accurate disease identification and improves dataset reliability
- Core assumption: Automated or crowd-sourced labeling is insufficient for medical image datasets
- Evidence anchors: Abstract and section both state "an expert dermatologist verified the disease label of each skin image"

## Foundational Learning

- Concept: Transfer learning in deep neural networks
  - Why needed here: Limited mpox lesion images make training from scratch impractical; transfer learning leverages pre-trained models for good performance with smaller datasets
  - Quick check question: What are the two main components of transfer learning, and why is it particularly useful for medical image analysis with limited data?

- Concept: Color space augmentation (HSV)
  - Why needed here: Skin lesion datasets often exhibit racial bias due to overrepresentation of certain skin tones; HSV augmentation increases skin tone variability to improve model generalizability
  - Quick check question: How does HSV augmentation differ from traditional RGB augmentation, and why is it more effective at reducing skin tone bias?

- Concept: Cross-validation for model evaluation
  - Why needed here: Ensures consistent performance across data splits and prevents overfitting, critical for medical applications
  - Quick check question: What is the difference between k-fold cross-validation and a simple train/test split, and why is it preferred for small datasets?

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map: Web scraping → Quality filtering → Expert verification → Augmentation (standard + HSV) → Train/validation/test split → Pre-trained CNN → Custom FC layers → Softmax output → Web application
- Critical path: Dataset preparation → Model selection with HAM10000 weights → Training with cross-validation → Performance evaluation → Web app integration
- Design tradeoffs: HAM10000 provides domain-specific features but modality mismatch vs ImageNet's general features; HSV augmentation reduces bias but may remove diagnostic color cues; Streamlit enables rapid development but may not scale
- Failure signatures: Low accuracy with high variance indicates overfitting or insufficient augmentation; poor performance on darker skin tones suggests inadequate HSV augmentation; all-prediction "healthy" indicates class imbalance or initialization issues
- First 3 experiments: 1) DenseNet121 with ImageNet weights and standard augmentation only; 2) DenseNet121 with HAM10000 weights and standard augmentation; 3) DenseNet121 with HAM10000 weights and both standard + HSV augmentation

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- Question: What is the impact of training the model on a more diverse dataset collected from multiple geographical locations on its generalizability and accuracy?
- Basis in paper: Explicit mention of need for large-scale mpox data collection and international collaboration
- Why unresolved: Current dataset lacks crucial meta-data like clinical history, duration of sickness, and disease stage
- What evidence would resolve it: Experiments with geographically diverse datasets compared to current model

### Open Question 2
- Question: How does the performance of the proposed model compare to that of human dermatologists in diagnosing mpox?
- Basis in paper: Explicit mention of need for professional dermatologist scrutiny to reduce mislabeling risk
- Why unresolved: Study does not provide direct comparison between model and human dermatologist performance
- What evidence would resolve it: Study comparing model predictions to panel of human dermatologists on identical image sets

### Open Question 3
- Question: What is the effect of incorporating clinical meta-data on model performance?
- Basis in paper: Explicit acknowledgment of missing meta-data and its importance for diagnosis
- Why unresolved: Current dataset lacks clinical history, duration, and disease stage information
- What evidence would resolve it: Experiments with meta-data-rich datasets compared to current model

## Limitations

- Dataset size (800 images across 6 classes) remains relatively small despite augmentation strategies
- HAM10000 pre-training introduces modality mismatch between dermoscopic and clinical mpox lesion images
- Performance disaggregated by skin tone is not reported, making racial bias reduction claims difficult to verify

## Confidence

- High Confidence: Transfer learning from skin lesion datasets improves performance over ImageNet initialization
- Medium Confidence: HSV augmentation reduces racial bias (mechanism plausible but not directly measured)
- Medium Confidence: Expert verification improves dataset quality (logical assumption but not empirically validated)
