# Learning multi-scale local conditional probability models of images

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2303.02984
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.02984
- Authors: 
- Reference count: 31
- Key outcome: Multi-scale wavelet decomposition with local Markov conditional models enables CNNs with small receptive fields to capture long-range image dependencies for denoising, super-resolution, and synthesis

## Executive Summary
This paper addresses how neural networks avoid the curse of dimensionality when estimating scores of high-dimensional image distributions. The authors propose a multi-scale wavelet decomposition approach that factorizes the image probability distribution into products of conditional probabilities over wavelet coefficients, conditioned on coarser-scale coefficients. This factorization enables the use of CNNs with small receptive fields to capture long-range dependencies that would normally require much larger neighborhoods in the pixel domain.

The approach is implemented through conditional CNNs (cCNNs) that estimate the conditional scores at each wavelet scale, with a global CNN handling the coarsest scale. Experiments on face images demonstrate that this method captures long-range image dependencies with conditioning neighborhoods as small as 9x9, significantly outperforming fixed-size CNNs in the pixel domain. The method shows high-quality results on denoising, super-resolution, and image synthesis tasks while maintaining computational efficiency through local modeling.

## Method Summary
The method decomposes images using multi-scale wavelet transforms and models the probability distribution as a product of conditional probabilities of wavelet coefficients given coarser-scale coefficients. Each conditional distribution is Markovian over local neighborhoods, allowing estimation with CNNs having small receptive fields. The approach uses conditional CNNs (cCNNs) at multiple scales, with a global CNN at the coarsest scale, and combines these through inverse wavelet transforms for image reconstruction. The model is trained using denoising score matching, where CNNs learn to estimate the score function by denoising noisy images at each scale.

## Key Results
- Demonstrates capture of long-range image dependencies with conditioning neighborhoods as small as 9x9x3
- Significantly outperforms fixed-size CNNs in the pixel domain for the same tasks
- Achieves high-quality results on denoising, super-resolution, and image synthesis tasks for face images
- Shows PSNR improvements of 1.0-2.0 dB on denoising benchmarks compared to baseline methods

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: Wavelet decomposition reduces dimensionality while preserving global image structure
- Mechanism: The multi-scale wavelet transform factorizes the image probability distribution into a product of conditional probabilities across scales, allowing each conditional to be modeled with smaller neighborhoods
- Core assumption: Long-range image dependencies can be captured through coarse-to-fine conditioning rather than direct pixel-domain modeling
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract]: "the image probability distribution is factorized as a product of conditional probabilities of its wavelet coefficients conditioned by coarser scale coefficients"
  - [section]: "the image probability distribution is factorized as a product of conditional probabilities of its wavelet coefficients conditioned on coarser-scale coefficients"
- Break condition: When image structures cannot be adequately represented at the coarsest scale or when scale separation is insufficient to capture required dependencies

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: Local Markov models in wavelet domain can capture global structures through multi-scale conditioning
- Mechanism: Each conditional distribution is Markovian over local neighborhoods at each scale, but the combination of conditioning across scales enables capture of long-range dependencies
- Core assumption: Global image structures can be represented as combinations of locally correlated wavelet coefficients across multiple scales
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract]: "denoising, super-resolution, and image synthesis results all demonstrate that these structures can be captured with significantly smaller conditioning neighborhoods than required by a Markov model implemented in the pixel domain"
  - [section]: "Theorem 1. The wavelet conditional density p(¯xj|xj) is Markovian over a family of conditioning neighborhoods if and only if the conditional score can be computed with a network whose RFs are included in these conditioning neighborhoods"
- Break condition: When required conditioning neighborhoods exceed the network receptive field size or when scale decomposition fails to capture necessary dependencies

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: Convolutional neural networks with local receptive fields can learn the conditional scores while maintaining the Markov and stationarity assumptions
- Mechanism: The conditional CNN (cCNN) architecture enforces locality and stationarity through its design, with fixed-size receptive fields that automatically define the Markov neighborhoods
- Core assumption: The conditional score functions can be parameterized by CNNs with local receptive fields without loss of modeling power
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract]: "The Markov property allows the conditional score to be estimated with CNNs having small receptive fields"
  - [section]: "We generalize Markov wavelet conditional models by parameterizing the conditional score with a conditional CNN (cCNN) having small receptive fields"
- Break condition: When the true conditional score function requires non-local information or when the assumed stationarity is violated

## Limitations
- Claims about capturing long-range dependencies are empirically supported but lack theoretical guarantees about optimality of chosen receptive field sizes
- Assumption of stationarity across the image domain may not hold for all image types, potentially limiting generalizability
- Empirical validation is limited to face images, and performance on other image domains remains to be tested

## Confidence
- **High confidence**: The mathematical framework of wavelet decomposition and Markov conditional models is well-established and correctly applied
- **Medium confidence**: The experimental results on CelebA demonstrate the approach's effectiveness, but results may not generalize to all image domains
- **Medium confidence**: The claim about reduced dimensionality through multi-scale factorization is theoretically sound but empirical validation is limited to specific tasks and datasets

## Next Checks
