# Evaluating the Stability of Semantic Concept Representations in CNNs for Robust Explainability

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2304.14864
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2304.14864
- Reference count: 40
- The paper proposes a framework for combining supervised and unsupervised concept analysis methods for object detection and classification models, introducing metrics for measuring concept retrieval and attribution stability.

## Executive Summary
This paper investigates the stability of semantic concept representations in CNNs for robust explainability. The authors propose a framework that combines supervised (TCAV) and unsupervised (ICE) concept analysis methods for object detection and classification models. They introduce two novel metrics - SLk for concept retrieval stability (considering concept separation and consistency) and CAD for concept attribution stability - to evaluate how reliably concepts can be extracted and attributed across different runs and conditions.

## Method Summary
The authors propose a unified framework combining ICE (unsupervised concept mining) and TCAV (supervised concept activation vectors) for concept analysis in CNNs. They introduce SLk as a stability metric that evaluates both concept separation (using F1-score) and consistency (using cosine similarity) across multiple runs. CAD measures concept attribution stability by quantifying the variance in attribution values across perturbed inputs. The framework is tested on six diverse CNN models including VGG, ResNet, DenseNet, and EfficientNet architectures across classification and object detection tasks.

## Key Results
- 1D concept activation vectors (CAVs) offer the best overall stability across all tested metrics
- Gradient smoothing techniques like SmoothGrad significantly improve concept attribution stability in shallow layers where gradient instability is more pronounced
- Increasing training samples per concept improves CAV stability by reducing underfitting and improving consistency across runs
- Different CNN architectures show varying stability profiles, with EfficientNet models demonstrating more stable concept representations than VGG models

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: 1D-CAVs achieve better stability because they have fewer parameters and use translation-invariant aggregation, reducing noise sensitivity.
- Mechanism: Dimensionality reduction via mean aggregation across spatial dimensions creates translation-invariant representations. Fewer parameters mean less overfitting to noise in small training sets.
- Core assumption: Concept information is encoded in linear combinations of convolutional filters rather than spatial locations.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract]: "1D concept activation vectors (CAVs) offer the best overall stability"
  - [section]: "1D-CAVs achieve the best overall CAV-stability due to their (mostly) best consistency (cos) and good concept separation (f1)"
  - [corpus]: Weak evidence - corpus neighbors focus on concept explainability but not CAV dimensionality tradeoffs.

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: Gradient smoothing (SmoothGrad) improves concept attribution stability in shallow layers where gradient instability is more pronounced.
- Mechanism: Averaging gradients from multiple noisy copies of the input reduces variance in gradient estimates, mitigating the effect of small input perturbations that cause large gradient fluctuations.
- Core assumption: Gradient instability is more severe in shallow layers due to amplification through backpropagation.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract]: "gradient smoothing techniques are beneficial for concept attribution stability in shallow layers where gradient instability is more pronounced"
  - [section]: "gradient smoothing techniques, such as SmoothGrad, can have a higher impact on concept attribution values in shallow layers, where the gradient instability is higher"
  - [corpus]: Weak evidence - corpus neighbors discuss gradient-based methods but not gradient stability specifically.

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: Increasing training samples per concept improves CAV stability by reducing underfitting and improving consistency across runs.
- Mechanism: More training samples provide better coverage of concept variations, leading to more robust linear classifiers that generalize across different initialization conditions.
- Core assumption: Concept representations follow a distribution that can be learned with sufficient samples, and small sample sizes lead to high variance in learned CAVs.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract]: "stability of concept retrieval can be enhanced through dimensionality reduction via data aggregation"
  - [section]: "increasing the number of training concept images has a positive impact on the stability of CAV"
  - [corpus]: Weak evidence - corpus neighbors discuss concept learning but not the relationship between sample size and CAV stability.

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map:
  Concept Pool -> ICE (Concept Miner) -> TCAV (Concept Extractor) -> Stability Evaluator -> Gradient Stabilizer

- Critical path:
  1. Mine concepts using ICE on CNN activations
  2. Validate and label mined concepts
  3. Train CAVs using TCAV on labeled concepts
  4. Compute stability metrics on test set
  5. Apply gradient smoothing if needed

- Design tradeoffs:
  - CAV dimensionality vs stability: 1D-CAVs are more stable but may lose spatial information; 3D-CAVs preserve more information but are less stable
  - Sample size vs labeling cost: More samples improve stability but increase labeling effort
  - Gradient smoothing vs computation: SmoothGrad improves stability but increases computation by N-fold

- Failure signatures:
  - Low SLk values: Poor concept separation or consistency, possibly due to insufficient training samples or inappropriate layer selection
  - High CAD values: Gradient instability affecting attribution, especially in shallow layers
  - Inconsistent CAVs across runs: Insufficient training samples or high concept variability

- First 3 experiments:
  1. Run ICE on a CNN layer to mine concepts and visualize the extracted patches
  2. Train 1D, 2D, and 3D CAVs on a small concept set and compare SLk values
  3. Apply SmoothGrad to gradient-based attribution and measure CAD reduction in shallow layers

## Limitations
- The corpus analysis yielded minimal relevant neighbors, suggesting this work addresses a relatively novel problem space
- The stability metrics SLk and CAD are proposed but not independently validated against established benchmarks
- The analysis assumes linear separability of concepts in CNN latent space, which may not hold for complex semantic concepts

## Confidence
- Mechanism 1 (1D-CAV stability): Medium confidence - limited validation that 1D-CAVs don't lose critical spatial information for all concept types
- Mechanism 2 (Gradient smoothing benefits): Medium confidence - analysis doesn't quantify the exact layer-dependent gradient instability that SmoothGrad addresses
- Mechanism 3 (Sample size effects): Medium confidence - insufficient evidence about optimal sample-to-concept ratios

## Next Checks
1. Test 1D-CAVs on spatially-dependent concepts (e.g., "object on left side") to verify they don't lose critical spatial information
2. Quantify gradient variance across layers with and without SmoothGrad to establish the exact relationship between layer depth and gradient instability
3. Perform ablation studies varying sample sizes systematically to identify the minimum effective training set size per concept
