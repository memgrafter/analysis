# Logic for Explainable AI

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2305.05172
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.05172
- Reference count: 40
- Key outcome: Formalizes explainable AI through symbolic logic, identifying necessary/sufficient reasons for decisions and minimal changes to flip them

## Executive Summary
This tutorial presents a theoretical framework for explainable AI using symbolic logic to answer fundamental questions about classifier decisions. It introduces complete and general reasons to identify necessary and sufficient conditions for decisions, along with minimal sufficient conditions and minimal necessary changes. The framework applies to various classifier types including Bayesian networks, decision trees, random forests, and certain neural networks by compiling them into tractable logical forms. The theory enables generation of explanations with hard guarantees about decision reasons, feature irrelevance, and minimal perturbations needed to flip decisions.

## Method Summary
The method compiles classifiers into logical class formulas represented as or-decomposable NNFs, then applies symbolic logic operations to extract explanations. Complete reasons are computed by universally quantifying away inconsistent states from instances, while general reasons use a weaker quantification allowing non-simple literals. Sufficient reasons are derived as prime implicants of complete reasons, and necessary reasons as prime implicates. The framework handles both discrete and continuous features through discretization when needed, providing theoretical guarantees about explanation quality.

## Key Results
- Complete reasons provide necessary and sufficient conditions for decisions using only states from the instance
- General reasons offer more informative abstractions than complete reasons when features are non-binary
- Sufficient reasons (prime implicants) identify minimal sets of features guaranteeing a decision
- The framework enables explanations with hard guarantees about decision reasons and minimal perturbations to flip decisions

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: Complete reasons provide necessary and sufficient conditions for a decision by constructing abstractions using only states from the instance
- Mechanism: The complete reason is computed by universally quantifying away states from the instance that are inconsistent with the class formula, resulting in an NNF formula using only the instance's states
- Core assumption: The class formula is represented in a tractable form (e.g., or-decomposable NNF) that allows efficient quantification
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "complete and general reasons based on symbolic logic"
  - [section] "The complete reason for the decision on instance I is the weakest NNF Γ whose literals are in I and that satisfies I| = Γ|= ∆i."
  - [corpus] Weak or missing - no direct corpus evidence for complete reason computation
- Break condition: If the class formula cannot be compiled into a tractable form, complete reason computation becomes intractable

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: General reasons provide more informative abstractions than complete reasons when features are non-binary by allowing non-simple literals
- Mechanism: General reasons are computed by quantifying away states from the instance using a weaker operator that permits non-simple literals, capturing broader justifications for decisions
- Core assumption: Non-binary features exist in the classifier, making general reasons potentially more informative than complete reasons
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "general reasons providing more informative abstractions than complete ones when features are non-binary"
  - [section] "The only difference between the complete reason and the general reason is that the former requires literals to appear in the instance, while the latter only requires that they are implied by the instance."
  - [corpus] Weak or missing - no direct corpus evidence for general reason computation
- Break condition: If all features are binary, general reasons reduce to complete reasons and provide no additional information

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: Sufficient reasons identify minimal aspects of an instance that guarantee a decision by computing prime implicants of the complete reason
- Mechanism: Sufficient reasons are derived as prime implicants of the complete reason, representing minimal sets of characteristics that ensure the decision holds
- Core assumption: Prime implicant computation is tractable for the given class formula representation
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "minimal conditions that are sufficient for a decision"
  - [section] "The sufficient reasons (SRs) for the decision on instance I are the prime implicants of the complete reason ∀I· ∆i."
  - [corpus] Weak or missing - no direct corpus evidence for sufficient reason computation
- Break condition: If prime implicant computation is intractable for the class formula representation, sufficient reasons cannot be efficiently computed

## Foundational Learning

- Concept: Discrete logic with non-binary variables
  - Why needed here: The tutorial uses discrete logic instead of Boolean logic to handle non-binary features directly, which is critical for the semantics of explainability theory
  - Quick check question: How does a literal for a ternary variable differ from a literal for a binary variable?

- Concept: Quantified logic operators (∀ and ∀)
  - Why needed here: These operators are used to compute complete and general reasons respectively by quantifying away states from instances
  - Quick check question: What is the difference between ∀ and ∀ quantification operators in terms of the literals they produce?

- Concept: Prime implicants and prime implicates
  - Why needed here: These notions capture minimality for sufficient and necessary reasons respectively, identifying the smallest sets of features that guarantee or undermine decisions
  - Quick check question: How does the definition of prime implicant differ between Boolean and discrete logic?

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map: Classifier -> Class formula compilation -> Complete reason computation -> Prime implicant/implicate extraction -> Explanations
- Critical path: Instance → Class formula compilation → Complete reason computation → Prime implicant/implicate extraction → Sufficient/necessary reasons
- Design tradeoffs: Using or-decomposable NNFs enables linear-time reason computation but may require more complex compilation; using simpler forms may limit tractability
- Failure signatures: If reason computation is slow, check class formula representation; if explanations are too complex, consider discretization or binarization tradeoffs
- First 3 experiments:
  1. Implement complete reason computation for a simple Naïve Bayes classifier with binary features
  2. Extend to general reason computation for a classifier with ternary features
  3. Add sufficient reason extraction and verify minimality properties on a decision tree classifier

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- Question: How can the theory of explainable AI be extended to handle continuous feature spaces beyond simple discretization?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper discusses discretization as a solution for handling numeric features but notes limitations when features have thousands of values
- Why unresolved: Discretization loses information and may not be suitable for all classifier types or applications
- What evidence would resolve it: Development of theoretical framework that directly handles continuous variables while maintaining guarantees about explanations

### Open Question 2
- Question: What is the computational complexity of generating general sufficient reasons for arbitrary classifiers represented as tractable circuits?
- Basis in paper: [inferred] The paper discusses tractable circuits but notes that general reasons require additional computational considerations beyond complete reasons
- Why unresolved: The paper shows that complete reasons can be computed efficiently for certain circuit types, but general reasons require more complex operations
- What evidence would resolve it: Formal complexity analysis and efficient algorithms for general reason computation on various circuit representations

### Open Question 3
- Question: How can the theory be extended to provide explanations that are both faithful to the model and comprehensible to end users?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper discusses hard guarantees but acknowledges the challenge of human interpretability when reasons become complex
- Why unresolved: There's a fundamental tension between theoretical guarantees and practical usability that hasn't been resolved
- What evidence would resolve it: User studies demonstrating effective communication of complex explanations and metrics for measuring interpretability-quality trade-offs

### Open Question 4
- Question: What are the relationships between different explanation methods (complete reasons, general reasons, sufficient reasons, necessary reasons) in terms of information content and computational complexity?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper establishes various relationships but notes that further investigation is needed
- Why unresolved: The paper shows some relationships but doesn't provide a comprehensive framework for comparing these methods
- What evidence would resolve it: Formal framework quantifying the trade-offs between different explanation types and their relative merits for various applications

## Limitations
- The theoretical framework assumes classifiers can be compiled into tractable logical forms, but compilation complexity for real-world classifiers remains unclear
- Scalability to high-dimensional feature spaces with many non-binary variables is not addressed
- The tutorial does not provide empirical validation on real datasets

## Confidence
- **High confidence**: The theoretical foundations for complete reasons and sufficient reasons (prime implicants) are well-established in discrete logic
- **Medium confidence**: The extension to general reasons and their relationship to complete reasons is sound but less empirically validated
- **Low confidence**: The practical implementation details for compiling complex classifiers (especially neural networks) into tractable class formulas are not fully specified

## Next Checks
