# Revealing Weaknesses of Vietnamese Language Models Through Unanswerable Questions in Machine Reading Comprehension

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2303.13355
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.13355
- Authors: 
- Reference count: 19
- Primary result: Vietnamese monolingual models underperform on high-level semantic reasoning required to identify unanswerable questions in machine reading comprehension.

## Executive Summary
This paper investigates weaknesses of Vietnamese language models using unanswerable questions in machine reading comprehension. The authors analyze monolingual and multilingual models on Vietnamese MRC datasets, finding that monolingual models like PhoBERT perform well on lexical/grammatical aspects but struggle with high-level semantic reasoning required to identify unanswerable questions. To enable more fine-grained analysis, the authors propose a method to create higher-quality unanswerable questions by having annotators first answer the original question before transforming it. Experiments on both in-domain and out-of-domain Vietnamese MRC datasets show monolingual models underperform on certain question types like antonyms and relation reversal, indicating limitations in semantic reasoning. The results suggest Vietnamese monolingual models need more pretraining data and tasks targeting high-level reasoning to match multilingual models. The authors also highlight that current Vietnamese MRC benchmarks may overestimate model capabilities due to biases.

## Method Summary
The paper proposes a novel method for creating high-quality unanswerable questions by having annotators first answer the original question before transforming it. The authors fine-tune both monolingual (PhoBERT, WikiBERT) and multilingual (mBERT, XLM-RoBERTa) models on the UIT-ViQuAD 2.0 training set and evaluate them on development sets and newly annotated benchmarks. They analyze performance across different question types and compare results on in-domain (UIT-ViQuAD 2.0) and out-of-domain (UIT-VinewsQA) data to reveal model weaknesses and benchmark biases.

## Key Results
- Monolingual models like PhoBERT achieve high performance on lexical/grammatical aspects but struggle with high-level semantic reasoning required for unanswerable questions
- The proposed method for creating unanswerable questions improves question quality and reveals weaknesses in current Vietnamese MRC benchmarks
- Monolingual models significantly underperform on specific question types like antonyms and relation reversal compared to multilingual models
- Current Vietnamese MRC benchmarks overestimate model capabilities due to biases in the annotation process

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- **Claim:** Vietnamese monolingual models struggle more with high-level semantic reasoning than multilingual models.
- **Mechanism:** Monolingual models rely heavily on lexical/grammatical features for prediction, while multilingual models encode broader semantic relationships through multilingual training.
- **Core assumption:** Pretraining data size and multilingual exposure are key determinants of semantic reasoning ability.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract]: "monolingual models like PhoBERT perform well on lexical/grammatical aspects but struggle with high-level semantic reasoning required to identify unanswerable questions"
  - [section 4.2]: "monolingual models show poor performances when encountering unanswerable questions that require the ability to comprehend a bigger 'picture'"
  - [corpus]: Weak - The corpus does not directly provide evidence about training data size differences, only mentions that PhoBERT and WikiBERT have smaller pre-training corpora than multilingual models.
- **Break condition:** If monolingual models are trained on much larger corpora or with tasks specifically targeting semantic reasoning, this mechanism may not hold.

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim:** The unique linguistic features of Vietnamese pose significant challenges for multilingual models.
- **Mechanism:** Vietnamese uses post-nominal adjectives, unlike many resource-rich languages, making it difficult for multilingual models to learn correct syntactic patterns.
- **Core assumption:** Multilingual models are biased toward syntactic patterns common in resource-rich languages.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [section 4.2]: "Vietnamese monolingual models acquire a better ability in low-level lexical and grammatical features of Vietnamese than multilingual counterparts do"
  - [section 4.2]: "If an adjective is used as a noun modifier in Vietnamese, the adjective must go after the main noun instead of before, as in English and many other resource-rich languages"
  - [corpus]: Weak - The corpus does not provide direct evidence about syntactic pattern learning in multilingual models.
- **Break condition:** If multilingual models are trained with explicit syntactic supervision or with balanced multilingual corpora, this mechanism may not hold.

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim:** Current Vietnamese MRC benchmarks overestimate model capabilities due to biases in annotation process.
- **Mechanism:** Models learn shortcuts from biased training data rather than truly understanding context, as evidenced by poor performance on newly created unanswerable questions.
- **Core assumption:** Benchmark datasets contain artifacts that enable shortcut learning.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract]: "current Vietnamese MRC benchmarks may overestimate model capabilities due to biases"
  - [section 5.2]: "UIT-VinewsQA does not fully reveal the existing weaknesses of MRC systems"
  - [section 5.2]: "PhoBERT and XLM-RoBERTa achieve high performances on the UIT-VinewsQA development set, but our unanswerable questions reveal that these two models do not truly understand the context"
- **Break condition:** If new benchmarks are created without biases and models still perform well, this mechanism may not hold.

## Architecture Onboarding

- **Component map:** Model training pipeline includes pretraining (monolingual/multilingual), fine-tuning on MRC datasets, evaluation on answerable and unanswerable questions, analysis by question type
- **Critical path:** Pretraining → Fine-tuning on UIT-ViQuAD 2.0 → Evaluation on UIT-ViQuAD 2.0 dev set → Creation of Parallel UIT-VinewsQA → Evaluation on Parallel UIT-VinewsQA → Analysis by question type
- **Design tradeoffs:** Using out-of-domain data for evaluation vs. in-domain data for fine-tuning; balancing between answerable and unanswerable question types; choosing between monolingual and multilingual models
- **Failure signatures:** High performance on in-domain answerable questions but poor performance on out-of-domain unanswerable questions; significant gap between monolingual and multilingual model performance on specific question types
- **First 3 experiments:**
  1. Fine-tune both monolingual and multilingual models on UIT-ViQuAD 2.0 training set and evaluate on development set to establish baseline performance
  2. Create Parallel UIT-VinewsQA with answerable/unanswerable question pairs and evaluate all models to reveal benchmark biases
  3. Analyze performance by question type (e.g., Antonym, Entity Swap, Relation Reverse) to identify specific weaknesses of monolingual vs. multilingual models

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- Question: What specific unsupervised pre-training tasks could be designed to improve high-level semantic reasoning abilities in Vietnamese language models?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper suggests introducing new unsupervised tasks targeting high-level semantic and factual knowledge during pre-training.
- Why unresolved: While the authors propose this direction, they don't provide specific task designs or empirically evaluate potential tasks.
- What evidence would resolve it: Experimental results comparing the performance of PhoBERT when pre-trained with different semantic reasoning tasks on Vietnamese MRC benchmarks.

### Open Question 2
- Question: How does the quality of Vietnamese MRC benchmarks compare to benchmarks in other languages, and what specific artifacts/biases exist?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The authors reveal that current Vietnamese MRC benchmarks overestimate model capabilities due to biases and artifacts.
- Why unresolved: The paper identifies the existence of these issues but doesn't provide a detailed analysis of specific benchmark artifacts or a comparative analysis with benchmarks in other languages.
- What evidence would resolve it: A comprehensive error analysis of model predictions on Vietnamese MRC benchmarks to identify specific patterns of bias, and a comparison of these patterns with artifacts found in English/French MRC benchmarks.

### Open Question 3
- Question: What are the optimal corpus sizes for pre-training Vietnamese monolingual language models to achieve state-of-the-art performance on MRC tasks?
- Basis in paper: [inferred] The paper suggests that the small pre-training corpus size of PhoBERT may be the main reason for its poor performance on high-level semantic reasoning tasks.
- Why unresolved: While the authors hypothesize about the importance of corpus size, they don't provide empirical evidence or specific recommendations for optimal corpus sizes.
- What evidence would resolve it: A systematic study pre-training Vietnamese language models on corpora of varying sizes and evaluating their performance on different MRC tasks, particularly those requiring high-level semantic reasoning.

## Limitations
- The findings are constrained by the specific Vietnamese language models and datasets analyzed
- The generalizability of the proposed method for creating unanswerable questions to other languages or MRC tasks remains untested
- The paper does not explore the impact of different pretraining strategies or model architectures on semantic reasoning capabilities
- The assumption that poor performance on certain question types indicates fundamental weakness in semantic reasoning may not account for other factors like annotation quality or model bias

## Confidence
- High confidence: The finding that Vietnamese monolingual models struggle with high-level semantic reasoning compared to multilingual models
- Medium confidence: The claim that current Vietnamese MRC benchmarks overestimate model capabilities due to biases
- Low confidence: The assertion that Vietnamese linguistic features pose unique challenges for multilingual models
