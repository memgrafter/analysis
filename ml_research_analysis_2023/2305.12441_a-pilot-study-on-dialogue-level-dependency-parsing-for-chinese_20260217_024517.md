# A Pilot Study on Dialogue-Level Dependency Parsing for Chinese

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2305.12441
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.12441
- Reference count: 19
- Introduces first Chinese dialogue-level dependency parsing dataset with 850 dialogues and 199,803 dependencies

## Executive Summary
This paper introduces the first Chinese dialogue-level dependency parsing dataset, extending both syntactic and RST dependencies to cover inner-EDU and inter-EDU dependencies. Due to annotation costs, the work focuses on zero-shot and few-shot learning scenarios. The proposed signal-based method transforms syntactic dependencies into inter-EDU dependencies using masked language modeling to detect discourse signals. Single-view and multi-view data selection methods are applied to utilize pseudo-labeled data from large-scale unlabeled dialogue data, achieving reasonable performance in zero-shot settings and significant improvements in few-shot settings.

## Method Summary
The method involves transforming syntactic dependencies into inter-EDU dependencies using a signal-based approach with masked language modeling to detect discourse signals. The Chinese ELECTRA base discriminator serves as the encoder, with a biaffine attention decoder for arc and label prediction. Single-view and multi-view pseudo-labeled data filtering with confidence thresholds are used to select high-quality training samples from unlabeled dialogue data.

## Key Results
- Signal-based transformation achieves reasonable performance in zero-shot settings
- Multi-view filtered pseudo-labeled data provides significant improvements in few-shot settings
- Best performance achieved by combining transformed treebank with filtered pseudo-labeled data from multiple views

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
Signal-based dependency transformation assigns inter-EDU labels by detecting discourse signals via masked language modeling. Certain words reflect EDU semantic roles (e.g., "if" signals condition), and an MLM model recovers these dropped signal words from template prompts. The predicted signal word distribution is grouped by predefined categories and assigned to EDU's inter-EDU arcs. Break condition: ambiguous signal words or inaccurate MLM recovery leads to incorrect inter-EDU labels.

### Mechanism 2
Pseudo-labeled data filtering improves parsing performance by selecting high-confidence samples from unlabeled dialogue data. A trained parser assigns pseudo labels, with confidence computed as average of highest arc and label probabilities. Samples above threshold are retained as additional training data. Break condition: low threshold includes noisy samples, high threshold retains too few samples.

### Mechanism 3
Multi-view data selection using multiple parsers improves performance by leveraging complementary information. Two parsers independently assign pseudo labels, with high-confidence samples from both views merged and de-duplicated by confidence score. Break condition: if both parsers make similar errors, multi-view selection may reinforce those errors.

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map: Syntactic treebank → Inner-EDU parser → Signal detection → Inter-EDU transformation → Multi-view data filtering → Final parser
- Critical path: Syntactic treebank → Inner-EDU parser → Signal transformation → Multi-view pseudo-labeling → Fine-tuning
- Design tradeoffs: Syntactic base vs. building from scratch, simple EDU segmentation vs. complex discourse segmentation, single-view vs. multi-view pseudo-labeling, high vs. low confidence threshold
- Failure signatures: Low inner-EDU scores indicate syntactic base parser issues; poor inter-EDU scores despite good inner-EDU scores suggest signal detection problems; performance drops with added pseudo-data indicate noisy filtering
- First 3 experiments:
  1. Train baseline parser on syntactic treebank only, evaluate inner-EDU performance
  2. Apply signal-based transformation to baseline, evaluate inter-EDU performance
  3. Add single-view filtered pseudo-data, evaluate impact on both inner and inter-EDU parsing

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
How effective are the proposed methods in more diverse dialogue domains beyond customer service? The paper focuses on customer service dialogue data, making generalizability to other domains like social media or medical consultations unexplored.

### Open Question 2
What's the impact of more sophisticated inter-utterance dependency parsing methods on overall performance? The current simplistic method linking root nodes between utterances may miss complex semantic relationships across turns.

### Open Question 3
How does iterative pseudo-labeled data selection affect performance compared to single iteration? The paper only uses one round of confidence-based filtering, but multiple iterations might refine pseudo-labeled data quality.

### Open Question 4
What's the contribution of each individual signal word to the overall performance of the MLM-based signal detection method? The paper doesn't analyze individual signal word contributions or their frequency distribution in the corpus.

## Limitations

- Small annotated dataset (50 training dialogues) constrains few-shot learning evaluation
- Signal-based transformation relies on manually curated word-signal dictionary without validation details
- Pseudo-labeled data filtering assumes high-confidence samples are higher quality without rigorous testing
- Focus on zero-shot and few-shot settings leaves full-data scenario performance unexplored

## Confidence

- High Confidence: Overall experimental methodology and performance improvements are well-supported
- Medium Confidence: Signal-based transformation effectiveness depends on word-signal dictionary quality
- Low Confidence: Pseudo-labeled data filtering sensitivity to confidence threshold (0.98) is not thoroughly explored
