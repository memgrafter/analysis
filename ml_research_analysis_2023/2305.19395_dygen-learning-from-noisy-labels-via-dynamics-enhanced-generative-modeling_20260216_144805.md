# DyGen: Learning from Noisy Labels via Dynamics-Enhanced Generative Modeling

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2305.19395
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.19395
- **Reference count:** 40
- **Key outcome:** DyGen achieves an average accuracy improvement of 3.10% on two synthetic noise datasets and 1.48% on three real-world noise datasets compared to the previous state-of-the-art.

## Executive Summary
This paper proposes DyGen, a method for fine-tuning language models with noisy labels that leverages dynamic patterns in the embedding space during training. The approach uses a variational auto-encoding framework to infer true labels from noisy labels and training dynamics, combined with a co-regularization mechanism across multiple model branches. DyGen demonstrates significant performance improvements over existing methods on both synthetic and real-world noisy label datasets.

## Method Summary
DyGen is a two-stage method that addresses noisy label learning by capturing training dynamics in the embedding space. First, it fine-tunes a PLM with noisy labels to obtain training trajectories, then computes dynamics-based priors using KNN algorithms. Second, it employs a deep generative model (VAE) with co-regularization across multiple branches to infer true labels from noisy labels and training dynamics. The method treats true labels as latent variables and learns to reconstruct noisy labels conditioned on these latent variables and the training trajectories.

## Key Results
- Achieves 3.10% average accuracy improvement on synthetic noise datasets (20newsgroup, AG NEWS, WOS)
- Demonstrates 1.48% average accuracy improvement on real-world noise datasets (ChemProt, TREC, SemEval)
- Outperforms state-of-the-art baselines across varying noise ratios up to 50%
- Shows effectiveness on both symmetric, asymmetric, and instance-dependent noise types

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- **Claim:** Training dynamics in embedding space can distinguish noisy from clean samples during fine-tuning.
- **Mechanism:** Noisy samples gradually shift away from their true-label cluster towards their assigned-label cluster during training, creating larger mean and standard deviation in distances to assigned label cluster centroids over epochs.
- **Core assumption:** The memorization effect causes models to first fit clean label patterns and then overfit noise, creating observable dynamic patterns.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract]: "we propose the Dynamics-Enhanced Generative Model (DyGen), which uses dynamic patterns in the embedding space during the fine-tuning process of language models to improve noisy label predictions."
  - [section]: "When fine-tuning PLMs with noisy labels, noisy samples gradually shift away from the true-label cluster towards their assigned-label cluster in the embedding space, leading to a larger Euclidean distance between the noisy samples and their assigned label cluster centroids across epochs."
- **Break condition:** If noise patterns are systematic and dominate the clean data signal, or if the noise ratio is so high that random noise overpowers the collective signal of clean data.

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim:** The variational auto-encoding framework can infer true labels from noisy labels and training dynamics.
- **Mechanism:** True labels are treated as latent variables, and the VAE learns to reconstruct noisy labels conditioned on these latent true labels and training dynamics, enabling posterior inference of true labels.
- **Core assumption:** The relationship between true labels, noisy labels, and training dynamics can be modeled as a generative process where true labels are latent variables.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract]: "DyGen uses the variational auto-encoding framework to infer the posterior distributions of true labels from noisy labels and training dynamics."
  - [section]: "To model distribution 𝑝(𝑦|ˆ𝑦, w), we introduce a deep generative model that can encode and reconstruct noisy labels ˆ𝑦, conditioned on the training trajectories w."
- **Break condition:** If the VAE cannot learn meaningful representations from the training dynamics, or if the reconstruction loss dominates and prevents proper posterior inference.

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim:** Co-regularization across multiple model branches improves robustness to noisy labels.
- **Mechanism:** Multiple branches with identical structures but different initializations are trained, and a co-regularization loss encourages consensus among these branches for posterior predictions, reducing reliance on potentially corrupted labels.
- **Core assumption:** Different initializations will lead to diverse predictions, and consensus among these predictions indicates higher confidence in the true labels.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract]: "Additionally, a co-regularization mechanism is used to minimize the impact of potentially noisy labels and priors."
  - [section]: "To enhance robustness, we employ multiple branches that co-regularize each other to reach consensus for the posterior 𝑝(𝑦|ˆ𝑦, w)."
- **Break condition:** If the number of branches is insufficient to capture diversity, or if the co-regularization loss is too strong and prevents individual branches from learning useful representations.

## Architecture Onboarding

- **Component map:** Fine-tune PLM with noisy labels -> Compute dynamics-based priors -> Train VAE with co-regularization -> Average predictions across branches
- **Critical path:**
  1. Fine-tune PLM with noisy labels to obtain training trajectories
  2. Compute dynamics-based priors using KNN algorithm
  3. Train VAE with co-regularization to infer true labels
  4. Average predictions across branches for final output
- **Design tradeoffs:**
  - More model branches increase robustness but also computational cost
  - Stronger co-regularization may prevent individual branches from learning useful representations
  - Higher noise ratios require more sophisticated prior computation
- **Failure signatures:**
  - If the co-regularization loss dominates, individual branches may not learn meaningful representations
  - If the prior computation is inaccurate, the VAE may learn incorrect relationships between true and noisy labels
  - If the training dynamics patterns are not distinct enough, the method may not improve over baseline approaches
- **First 3 experiments:**
  1. Verify that training dynamics patterns can distinguish noisy from clean samples on a simple dataset
  2. Test the accuracy of the dynamics-based prior computation using KNN algorithm
  3. Evaluate the impact of co-regularization on the robustness of the VAE to noisy labels

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- **Question:** How does the performance of DyGen scale with extremely large noise ratios (e.g., 80-90%) compared to other methods?
- **Basis in paper:** [inferred] The paper shows performance curves under large noise ratios (≥ 50%) but doesn't test extreme cases beyond 50%.
- **Why unresolved:** The experimental results only go up to 50% noise ratio, leaving uncertainty about performance at higher noise levels where the method might break down or excel.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Systematic experiments testing DyGen against baselines on datasets with 60%, 70%, 80%, and 90% noise ratios, measuring accuracy and calibration metrics.

### Open Question 2
- **Question:** Can the dynamics-based prior be effectively applied to other types of models beyond BERT-based architectures?
- **Basis in paper:** [explicit] The paper only tests DyGen on BERT and BioBERT architectures, with BioBERT used only on ChemProt dataset.
- **Why unresolved:** The method relies on specific embedding space dynamics that may or may not transfer to other model architectures like CNNs, RNNs, or newer transformer variants.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Experiments applying DyGen to alternative architectures (e.g., RoBERTa, XLNet, or even non-transformer models) on the same benchmark datasets.

### Open Question 3
- **Question:** What is the computational overhead of DyGen compared to baseline methods during both training and inference?
- **Basis in paper:** [inferred] While the paper describes the algorithmic components, it doesn't provide runtime comparisons or computational complexity analysis.
- **Why unresolved:** The method involves multiple model branches, dynamic pattern encoding, and generative modeling, but the paper doesn't quantify the practical training time or inference latency implications.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Detailed timing experiments comparing training wall-clock time and inference latency of DyGen against baselines across different dataset sizes and hardware configurations.

## Limitations
- Performance heavily depends on quality of training dynamics patterns, which may not be as distinct in real-world scenarios
- Computational overhead of co-regularization across multiple branches is not quantified, potentially limiting scalability
- Assumes noisy samples consistently shift away from true-label clusters during training, which may not hold for all noise distributions

## Confidence
- **High Confidence:** The core mechanism of using training dynamics for noisy label detection is well-supported by empirical evidence and aligns with established principles of model memorization during training
- **Medium Confidence:** The VAE framework for inferring true labels from noisy labels and training dynamics is theoretically sound, but its effectiveness may vary depending on the specific noise characteristics
- **Low Confidence:** The co-regularization mechanism's impact on robustness is promising but requires further validation across diverse noise scenarios
