# Compressing Sentence Representation with maximum Coding Rate Reduction

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2304.12674
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2304.12674
- Reference count: 29
- Key outcome: MCR2 compression achieves comparable performance to full-size embeddings on semantic tasks while reducing dimensionality by 75%

## Executive Summary
This paper introduces Maximum Coding Rate Reduction (MCR2) as a method to compress Sentence-BERT embeddings while preserving semantic information. The approach adds a projection layer trained on the MCR2 objective to a frozen pre-trained Sentence-BERT model, enabling significant dimensionality reduction without substantial performance loss. The compressed embeddings show promising results across semantic retrieval, textual similarity, and classification tasks, demonstrating that high-dimensional sentence representations can be effectively compressed for deployment in resource-constrained environments.

## Method Summary
The paper proposes using MCR2 to compress Sentence-BERT embeddings by adding a projection layer trained on the MCR2 objective function. This projection layer is trained while keeping the pre-trained Sentence-BERT model frozen. The MCR2 objective encourages the compressed representations to maintain both the diversity and clustering structure of the original embeddings, preserving semantic relationships while reducing dimensionality. The method is evaluated on semantic retrieval, textual similarity, and classification tasks, comparing compressed representations against full-size embeddings and other compression baselines like k-means clustering.

## Key Results
- Semantic retrieval: MCR2 achieves 0.645 accuracy with 200-dim embeddings vs 0.648 with k-means clustering on 768-dim embeddings
- Textual similarity: Relative error up to 13% with only 6% of original dimension
- Classification: Maintains comparable performance to full-size embeddings across tested datasets

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The MCR2 objective function explicitly optimizes for both the rate (compression efficiency) and distortion (preservation of semantic structure) in the compressed representations. By encouraging the compressed embeddings to maintain the diversity of the original representations while preserving their clustering structure, MCR2 effectively captures the essential semantic information needed for downstream tasks. The frozen Sentence-BERT backbone ensures that the compressed representations inherit the rich semantic knowledge from the pre-trained model, while the projection layer learns to distill this information into a lower-dimensional space without losing task-relevant features.

## Limitations
- Relies heavily on k-means clustering as a baseline, which may not be the strongest comparison
- Assumes frozen Sentence-BERT models, limiting applicability when fine-tuning is feasible
- Does not address potential degradation in multilingual or domain-specific settings
- Lacks empirical validation of deployment efficiency on actual resource-constrained devices

## Confidence
**High**: The MCR2 methodology is technically sound and well-established in representation learning
**Medium**: Performance claims against k-means clustering are reasonable but dataset-dependent
**Low**: Deployment efficiency claims lack hardware-specific validation
