# Smart ROI Detection for Alzheimer's disease prediction using explainable AI

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2303.10401
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.10401
- Reference count: 0
- Achieves 98.6% accuracy and 1.0 AUC in MCI-to-AD progression prediction

## Executive Summary
This paper proposes an automatic ROI detection method for Alzheimer's disease prediction using explainable AI and 3DCNN. The approach extracts disease-specific brain regions per patient using Grad-CAM, then classifies AD progression based on these ROIs. Tested on 176 MCI patients from ADNI, the method achieves 98.6% accuracy and 1.0 AUC, significantly outperforming whole-brain approaches while reducing computational complexity.

## Method Summary
The method uses a two-stage 3D CNN architecture. First, a 3DCNN is trained on whole brain MRI images to classify MCI progression, with Grad-CAM extracting feature importance heatmaps. These heatmaps are thresholded to create binary ROI masks, which are then applied to the original images. A second 3DCNN is trained on these extracted ROIs for final classification. The approach is evaluated using 5-fold cross-validation on 176 MCI patients from ADNI, comparing ROI-based classification against whole-brain classification.

## Key Results
- Achieves 98.6% accuracy and 1.0 AUC on MCI-to-AD progression prediction
- ROI-based method significantly outperforms whole brain-based approach
- Demonstrates reduced computational complexity through focused ROI classification
- Automatic ROI extraction eliminates need for manual expert intervention

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: Automatic ROI detection using Grad-CAM increases classification accuracy by identifying disease-specific regions per patient.
- Mechanism: Grad-CAM generates feature importance heatmaps from the last convolutional layer of a 3DCNN. High-weight regions are thresholded and used as ROIs, focusing the classifier on disease-relevant areas rather than the entire brain.
- Core assumption: The last convolutional layer's activations capture the most semantically relevant features for AD classification, and these weights reflect true disease-related regions.

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: Using ROI-based classification reduces computational complexity and improves accuracy compared to whole-brain classification.
- Mechanism: Extracting ROIs reduces input volume, allowing a smaller, faster model to focus only on regions most informative for AD prediction. This avoids noise from unaffected brain areas.
- Core assumption: ROI-based inputs contain sufficient information for accurate AD prediction and exclude irrelevant regions.

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map:
  1. Preprocessing pipeline: skull stripping, artifact correction, resizing (256×256×256 → 128×128×32)
  2. Initial 3DCNN: Feature extraction and ROI detection using Grad-CAM
  3. ROI extraction: Thresholding heatmaps to create binary masks
  4. Final 3DCNN: Classification using extracted ROIs
  5. Evaluation: 5-fold cross-validation with multiple metrics

- Critical path:
  Raw MRI → Preprocessing → Initial 3DCNN → Grad-CAM → ROI Mask → Final 3DCNN → Classification

- Design tradeoffs:
  - Whole brain vs ROI: Whole brain captures all information but includes noise; ROI reduces noise but may miss regions
  - Threshold selection: Higher thresholds reduce input size but risk missing important regions; lower thresholds capture more but increase complexity
  - Model complexity: Deeper networks may capture more features but require more data and training time

- Failure signatures:
  - Low AUC despite high accuracy: Class imbalance or poor threshold selection
  - Similar performance for whole brain vs ROI: ROIs not capturing disease-specific regions
  - High variance across folds: Insufficient data or unstable ROI extraction

- First 3 experiments:
  1. Test Grad-CAM on a simple 3DCNN with synthetic data to verify heatmap generation works as expected
  2. Run initial 3DCNN on whole brain images and examine Grad-CAM heatmaps to ensure disease-relevant regions are highlighted
  3. Implement ROI extraction with varying thresholds (0.5, 0.7, 0.9) and measure impact on classification performance

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

- Question: How does the proposed automatic ROI detection method perform when predicting Alzheimer's disease 10 years before clinical conversion, rather than just 24 months as in the current study?
  - Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper acknowledges this as a limitation, stating "the most important issue is predicting AD in early stages for example 10 years earlier than conversion."
  - Why unresolved: The current study only uses MCI patients who convert (or not) to AD after 24 months, limiting its ability to detect very early-stage disease.
  - What evidence would resolve it: Testing the method on longitudinal data that includes patients from CN to AD conversion over a 10-year period would provide evidence of the method's effectiveness for very early prediction.

- Question: How would incorporating additional biomarkers such as age, gender, and education alongside MRI images affect the performance of the proposed method?
  - Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper mentions this as a limitation, stating "the second limitation is that in our proposed method MRI images are used only while metadata like age, gender, education and so on are important biomarkers."
  - Why unresolved: The current method only uses MRI images without considering other potentially important clinical and demographic factors.
  - What evidence would resolve it: Integrating additional biomarkers into the model and comparing performance metrics (accuracy, AUC, etc.) with the current MRI-only approach would demonstrate the impact of multimodal data.

- Question: Can the proposed method be generalized to other neurodegenerative diseases beyond Alzheimer's disease?
  - Basis in paper: [inferred] The method focuses on Alzheimer's disease specifically, but the general approach of automatic ROI detection using explainable AI could potentially be applied to other conditions.
  - Why unresolved: The paper only tests the method on Alzheimer's disease data from the ADNI dataset.
  - What evidence would resolve it: Applying the method to MRI data from patients with other neurodegenerative diseases (e.g., Parkinson's, Huntington's) and evaluating its performance would demonstrate generalizability.

## Limitations

- Sample size of 176 patients is relatively small for deep learning applications
- ROI extraction threshold of 0.7 is arbitrarily chosen without systematic optimization
- Method's dependence on accurate initial 3DCNN training creates error propagation
- Computational overhead of two-stage 3DCNN approach not adequately addressed

## Confidence

- High Confidence: The mechanism of using Grad-CAM for feature importance visualization is well-established in the literature.
- Medium Confidence: The claim that ROI-based classification outperforms whole-brain approaches, though supported by results, needs validation on larger, independent datasets.
- Low Confidence: The assertion that the method can be used for both classification and diagnosis requires clinical validation beyond the current experimental setup.
