# CILIATE: Towards Fairer Class-based Incremental Learning by Dataset and Training Refinement

## Executive Summary
This paper addresses fairness issues in class-based incremental learning (CIL) where models suffer from dataset bias (imbalanced or under-representative training data) and algorithm bias (learning only easy-to-learn features). The authors propose Ciliate, a framework that uses differential analysis to identify samples with unique features overlooked by traditional CIL methods. By prioritizing these samples through refined dataset and training strategies combining knowledge distillation with dropout-based learning, Ciliate significantly improves both fairness metrics and accuracy across three benchmark datasets.

## Method Summary
Ciliate addresses fairness in CIL through a two-pronged approach: dataset refinement and training refinement. The method first performs differential analysis between a base model and incrementally trained model using Jensen-Shannon divergence to identify samples that were overlooked during incremental learning. These samples are then prioritized in a refined dataset and training process. During training, Ciliate applies balanced distillation (knowledge distillation for correctly classified samples) and dropout-based learning (forcing different neurons to learn diverse representations) for important samples, while using ordinary training for others. The framework also includes a dataset verifier that checks neuron coverage to ensure sufficient feature representation.

## Key Results
- Improves fairness performance (CWV and MCD) by 17.03%, 22.46%, and 31.79% compared to state-of-the-art methods on CIFAR-100, Flowers-102, and Stanford Cars respectively
- Improves accuracy by 11.75%, 14.70%, and 1.56% on the same datasets
- Dataset refinement contributes more to performance gains than training refinement according to ablation studies
- Shows that dataset refinement has greater impact than training refinement on model performance

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
Ciliate uses differential analysis to identify samples with unique features that are overlooked by traditional CIL methods. By comparing model outputs before and after incremental training, it calculates Jensen-Shannon divergence to measure how much each sample influenced the model's learning. Samples with high divergence values are considered to carry unique features that were forgotten or not properly learned during CIL.

### Mechanism 2
Ciliate uses dropout training to enforce the model to learn unique features from important samples. For samples identified as important (high divergence), Ciliate applies dropout during training, which forces different neurons to learn different representations of the same sample. This prevents the model from relying on the same easy-to-learn features and encourages learning of more diverse and robust features.

### Mechanism 3
Ciliate uses balanced distillation to prevent forgetting of old knowledge while learning new classes. Ciliate applies knowledge distillation loss to correctly classified samples to retain old knowledge, while using cross-entropy loss for misclassified samples to correct errors. This balanced approach helps maintain performance on old classes while adapting to new ones.

- **Design tradeoffs:**
  - Dataset refinement vs. training refinement: The paper shows dataset refinement has a greater impact on performance
  - Dropout rate vs. learning effectiveness: Too much dropout prevents learning, too little doesn't enforce diversity

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- Question: What is the optimal size of the sampled dataset X_s that balances fairness and accuracy in class-based incremental learning?
- Basis in paper: The paper states that "the performance improvement of the model for the CIFAR-100 dataset is not readily apparent once the number of old samples is raised to 5000, which is 10% of the total training sample size" and that "increasing the size of the sampled dataset can help improve model performance, but there is a marginal effect."
- Why unresolved: The paper does not provide a definitive answer to the optimal size of X_s, and it may vary depending on the specific dataset and model used.
- What evidence would resolve it: Experiments comparing model performance with different sizes of X_s on various datasets and models would help determine the optimal size.

### Open Question 2
- Question: How does the performance of Ciliate change when applied to vision transformers (ViTs) compared to traditional convolutional neural networks?
- Basis in paper: The paper mentions that "Vision transformer (ViT) has recently been an emerging visual model" and that "Limited by hardware, we did not test our method on ViT, but we believe dataset refinement can still be applied since the approach does not depend on the model architectures."
- Why unresolved: The paper does not provide any results or comparisons of Ciliate's performance on ViTs, and the potential differences in performance between ViTs and traditional CNNs remain unknown.
- What evidence would resolve it: Experiments comparing Ciliate's performance on ViTs and traditional CNNs using the same datasets and settings would help determine any differences in performance.

### Open Question 3
- Question: What are the long-term effects of using Ciliate on model performance and fairness as the number of incremental learning steps increases?
- Basis in paper: The paper states that "the more steps taken for incremental learning, the more serious degradation of the model performance" and that "we plan to explore ways to improve its efficiency in future work."
- Why unresolved: The paper does not provide any results or analysis of the long-term effects of using Ciliate on model performance and fairness, and it is unclear how the model's performance and fairness would change as the number of incremental learning steps increases.
- What evidence would resolve it: Experiments tracking the performance and fairness of Ciliate over multiple incremental learning steps on various datasets and models would help determine the long-term effects of using the method.

## Limitations
- Effectiveness depends on the assumption that Jensen-Shannon divergence can reliably identify overlooked samples with unique features
- Performance may degrade on datasets with different characteristics than the three tested (CIFAR-100, Flowers-102, Stanford Cars)
- Critical hyperparameter 𝜂 was tuned on validation sets, raising concerns about potential overfitting to specific dataset characteristics

## Confidence

- **High Confidence**: The overall framework design and the improvement in fairness metrics (CWV and MCD) over baseline methods. The ablation studies clearly demonstrate that dataset refinement contributes more than training refinement to performance gains.
- **Medium Confidence**: The specific implementation details of differential analysis and dropout training. While the paper describes the approach conceptually, the exact implementation nuances are not fully specified.
- **Low Confidence**: Generalization to real-world scenarios where data distributions may change over time or where catastrophic forgetting patterns differ from controlled incremental learning settings.
