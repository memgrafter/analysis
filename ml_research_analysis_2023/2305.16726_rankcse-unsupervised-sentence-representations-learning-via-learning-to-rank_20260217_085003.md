# RankCSE: Unsupervised Sentence Representations Learning via Learning to Rank

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2305.16726
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.16726
- Reference count: 40
- This paper proposes RankCSE, an unsupervised sentence representation learning method that incorporates ranking consistency and ranking distillation with contrastive learning.

## Executive Summary
RankCSE is an unsupervised sentence representation learning method that addresses the limitation of existing contrastive learning approaches in capturing fine-grained ranking information among sentences. The method combines contrastive learning with two novel ranking objectives: ranking consistency between dropout-masked representations and ranking distillation from pre-trained teacher models. By enforcing these ranking constraints, RankCSE learns more discriminative sentence representations that outperform several state-of-the-art baselines on both semantic textual similarity and transfer tasks.

## Method Summary
RankCSE extends the SimCSE framework by incorporating two ranking objectives alongside the standard InfoNCE contrastive loss. The method processes each sentence through a BERT/RoBERTa encoder with two different dropout masks to obtain two representations. It then enforces ranking consistency between these representations using Jensen-Shannon divergence of top-one probability distributions derived from similarity scores. Additionally, RankCSE distills listwise ranking knowledge from pre-trained teacher models (SimCSE) using ListNet or ListMLE losses. The combined objective optimizes for both semantic similarity preservation and fine-grained ranking discrimination.

## Key Results
- RankCSE outperforms several state-of-the-art baselines on semantic textual similarity tasks
- The method achieves significant improvements in both semantic textual similarity and transfer tasks
- RankCSE effectively balances alignment and uniformity compared to pure contrastive methods

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: RankCSE captures fine-grained ranking information by enforcing ranking consistency between two dropout-masked representations.
- Mechanism: The method computes similarity scores between a query sentence and other sentences from two differently masked encoder outputs. It then minimizes the Jensen-Shannon divergence between the top-one probability distributions derived from these similarity lists, ensuring that rankings are preserved across both representations.
- Core assumption: The two dropout-masked representations of the same sentence provide complementary views that should yield consistent rankings when compared to other sentences.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "learn semantically discriminative sentence representations by simultaneously ensuring ranking consistency between two representations with different dropout masks"
  - [section 4.3] "For each sentence xi, two lists of similarities with other sentences can be naturally obtained from the two representations... We then enforce the ranking consistency between these two similarity lists"
  - [corpus] Weak - no direct neighbor papers discuss dropout mask consistency in ranking
- Break condition: If dropout masks produce highly divergent representations that don't preserve semantic similarity, the ranking consistency loss could degrade performance rather than improve it.

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: RankCSE generalizes fine-grained ranking information by distilling listwise ranking knowledge from pre-trained teachers.
- Mechanism: The model uses pre-trained SimCSE models as teachers to provide pseudo ranking labels. It then minimizes listwise ranking losses (ListNet or ListMLE) between the student's similarity scores and the teacher's scores, transferring coarse-grained ranking knowledge into fine-grained distinctions.
- Core assumption: Teacher models, despite only learning coarse rankings, contain useful signal that can be refined through listwise distillation into more discriminative representations.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "distills listwise ranking knowledge from the teacher"
  - [section 4.4] "we obtain the similarity score list from the teacher model, which is then served as pseudo ranking labels in the ranking distillation"
  - [section 5.3] "RankCSE is able to preserve ranking knowledge from multiple teachers, and generalize fine-grained ranking information from multiple coarse-grained representations"
- Break condition: If teacher models provide inaccurate or overly coarse rankings, the distillation process could reinforce incorrect ranking information rather than refine it.

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: RankCSE achieves better alignment-uniformity tradeoff than pure contrastive methods.
- Mechanism: By pulling similar negatives closer through ranking consistency and distillation losses while maintaining contrastive separation, RankCSE balances alignment (bringing similar items together) and uniformity (scattering representations on the hypersphere).
- Core assumption: The combination of ranking-based losses with InfoNCE creates a more optimal distribution of representations than either approach alone.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [section 5.3] "RankCSE effectively improves both alignment and uniformity compared with average BERT embeddings, while SimCSE and DiffCSE only improve uniformity and alignment respectively"
  - [section H] Mathematical definitions of alignment and uniformity showing how RankCSE balances both metrics
  - [corpus] Weak - no direct neighbor papers analyze alignment-uniformity tradeoff in this specific way
- Break condition: If the ranking consistency and distillation losses overpower the contrastive component, representations could collapse into clusters rather than maintaining uniform distribution.

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map:
  Encoder (BERT/RoBERTa) → Two dropout-masked representations → InfoNCE contrastive loss module → Ranking consistency module (JS divergence) → Ranking distillation module (ListNet/ListMLE) → Multi-teacher aggregation

- Critical path: Forward pass through encoder → Compute similarities for both representations → Calculate three losses → Backpropagate combined loss

- Design tradeoffs: The ranking consistency loss adds computation by requiring two forward passes with different dropout masks, but provides more discriminative representations. Using multiple teachers improves ranking quality but increases inference time for pseudo-label generation.

- Failure signatures: Poor STS/TR performance despite good training loss could indicate ranking consistency or distillation components are misaligned with the contrastive objective. Large variance in similarity scores across dropout masks suggests instability in the consistency mechanism.

- First 3 experiments:
  1. Train RankCSE with only InfoNCE loss to establish baseline performance
  2. Add ranking consistency loss and measure improvement on STS ranking metrics (KCC/NDCG)
  3. Incorporate ranking distillation with single teacher and compare against multi-teacher setup

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- Question: What is the optimal combination of teacher models to achieve the best performance in RankCSE?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper mentions that using a multi-teacher approach (weighted average of SimCSE base and large models) outperforms single teacher models, but does not explore the upper bound of improvements.
- Why unresolved: The paper only experiments with a combination of SimCSE base and large models, but does not explore other potential combinations or the theoretical upper limit of improvements.
- What evidence would resolve it: Systematic experiments with various combinations of different teacher models (e.g., SimCSE, DiffCSE, other contrastive learning methods) and their weighted combinations to determine the optimal teacher configuration.

### Open Question 2
- Question: How does the ranking consistency loss (Lconsistency) perform when applied to different types of sentence pairs beyond positive pairs?
- Basis in paper: [inferred] The paper mentions that Lconsistency does not explicitly distinguish positives from negatives, and models with only Lconsistency show significant performance drops, suggesting potential issues with how it handles negative pairs.
- Why unresolved: The paper only tests Lconsistency within the contrastive learning framework where it's combined with LinfoNCE, not in isolation or with different types of sentence pairs.
- What evidence would resolve it: Experiments testing Lconsistency with different sentence pair types (hard negatives, easy negatives, neutral pairs) and in isolation from other loss components to understand its behavior across different semantic relationships.

### Open Question 3
- Question: What is the relationship between the temperature hyperparameters (τ2, τ3) and the semantic granularity of the learned representations?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper shows that the performance of RankCSE ListNet is sensitive to the ratio between τ2 and τ3, with optimal performance at τ2:τ3 = 2:1, but does not explore the semantic implications of this relationship.
- Why unresolved: The paper only tests temperature effects on performance metrics, not on the actual semantic properties of the learned representations or how different temperature ratios affect the model's ability to capture fine-grained vs. coarse-grained semantic distinctions.
- What evidence would resolve it: Systematic analysis of how different temperature settings affect the semantic granularity captured by the model, including correlation analysis between temperature settings and performance on tasks requiring different levels of semantic discrimination.

## Limitations
- The reliance on pre-trained teacher models (SimCSE) for ranking distillation introduces a dependency that may not generalize to domains where such pre-trained models are unavailable
- The computational overhead of maintaining ranking consistency with two dropout-masked representations doubles the forward pass requirements, potentially limiting scalability
- The experiments focus primarily on English Wikipedia data, raising questions about cross-lingual and cross-domain generalization

## Confidence

- High confidence: The core mechanism of combining InfoNCE with ranking consistency and distillation losses is well-supported by experimental results and mathematical formulations
- Medium confidence: The claims about alignment-uniformity tradeoff improvements are supported by metrics but lack detailed ablation studies to isolate the contribution of each component
- Medium confidence: The generalization claims from ranking consistency and distillation are demonstrated empirically but could benefit from more rigorous theoretical analysis
