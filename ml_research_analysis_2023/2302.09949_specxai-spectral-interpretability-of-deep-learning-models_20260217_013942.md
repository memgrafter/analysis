# SpecXAI -- Spectral interpretability of Deep Learning Models

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2302.09949
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2302.09949
- Reference count: 40
- Key outcome: Novel explainable AI framework that generates interpretable explanations by decomposing deep neural networks into spectral components

## Executive Summary
SpecXAI is an explainable AI framework that leverages spectral decomposition of deep neural networks to generate interpretable explanations. The core innovation is viewing the network as an input-dependent operator and applying Singular Value Decomposition (SVD) to decompose its action into interpretable components. The method was tested on image classification models (AlexNet and VGG11) trained on ImageNet, as well as 3D data models for car drag coefficient and wind turbine stress prediction. Results demonstrate that SpecXAI produces spatially localized and geometrically meaningful singular vectors that highlight important features.

## Method Summary
SpecXAI decomposes deep neural networks into interpretable components by applying Singular Value Decomposition to the network's action as an input-dependent operator. The framework splits the network at an arbitrary layer, applies SVD to the resulting operator, and generates singular vectors and coefficients. These components are then used to create linear symbolic expressions of network outputs and feature-wise contractions that produce heatmaps highlighting important features. The method was validated on multiple datasets including ImageNet for image classification and 3D datasets for physical property prediction.

## Key Results
- Generated spatially localized and geometrically meaningful singular vectors that focus on central objects in images
- Demonstrated that VGG11 produces less background prominence than AlexNet in singular vector visualizations
- Showed ability to generate linear symbolic expressions of network outputs through spectral decomposition
- Revealed global properties of networks by analyzing singular vectors across entire datasets

- Break condition: If network's action cannot be effectively decomposed into spectral components, linear symbolic expression generation would fail

- Break condition: If singular vectors from different examples don't provide insights into global properties, framework's ability to reveal such properties would be limited

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map: Input → Operator decomposition via SVD → Singular vectors → Linear symbolic expression → Feature-wise contraction → Heatmaps
- Critical path: Input → Operator decomposition via SVD → Singular vectors → Linear symbolic expression → Feature-wise contraction → Heatmaps
- Design tradeoffs:
  - SVD provides principled decomposition but may be computationally expensive for large networks
  - Choice of layer to split operator is arbitrary and affects interpretability
  - Feature-wise contraction allows intuitive explanations but may lose fine-grained details
- Failure signatures:
  - Singular vectors not spatially localized or geometrically meaningful
  - Linear symbolic expression doesn't accurately represent network's output
  - Feature-wise contraction doesn't highlight important features

  3. Analyze singular vectors across multiple examples to understand global network properties

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- Question: How do learned singular vectors relate to ground truth features in datasets with known underlying structure?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] Paper compares singular vectors to data matrix SVD in toy rotated squares dataset but lacks quantitative analysis across diverse datasets
- Why unresolved: Paper shows visual similarity between learned and data-derived singular vectors but lacks quantitative alignment analysis
- What evidence would resolve it: Systematic quantitative comparison of alignment between network-derived singular vectors and data matrix SVD across multiple datasets with known ground truth structure

### Open Question 2
- Question: Does order of singular vectors correlate with their actual contribution to model predictions across all layers?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] Paper notes order of singular vectors is not necessarily reflective of their contribution to output
- Why unresolved: Paper provides examples showing disconnect but doesn't establish if this is systematic or isolated
- What evidence would resolve it: Comprehensive analysis across multiple models and datasets measuring correlation between singular value magnitude and actual contribution to predictions

### Open Question 3
- Question: How does presence of bias terms affect interpretability and performance of neural networks compared to bias-free architectures?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] Paper observes bias terms in car dataset are responsible for bulk of reconstructed shape
- Why unresolved: Paper demonstrates bias terms' significant role but doesn't explore removing or modifying bias terms
- What evidence would resolve it: Controlled experiments comparing bias-containing and bias-free models on same tasks, measuring both performance and interpretability metrics

## Limitations

- Computationally expensive SVD decomposition for large networks may limit scalability and real-time applications
- Qualitative comparisons with other XAI methods lack comprehensive quantitative evaluation across multiple metrics
- Arbitrary choice of layer for operator decomposition affects interpretability but implications are not rigorously explored

## Confidence

- High confidence: Core mechanism of using SVD to decompose network operators into interpretable components is mathematically sound
- Medium confidence: Claim of producing more fine-grained explanations than Grad-CAM, LIME, and SHAP supported by qualitative comparisons but lacks comprehensive quantitative evaluation
- Low confidence: Assertion that singular vectors reveal global properties of network weakly supported with limited analysis across dataset
