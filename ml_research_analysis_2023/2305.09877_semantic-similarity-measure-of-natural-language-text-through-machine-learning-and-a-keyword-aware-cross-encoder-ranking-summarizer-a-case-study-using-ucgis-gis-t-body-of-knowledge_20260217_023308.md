# Semantic Similarity Measure of Natural Language Text through Machine Learning and a Keyword-Aware Cross-Encoder-Ranking Summarizer -- A Case Study Using UCGIS GIS&T Body of Knowledge

## Executive Summary
This study addresses the challenge of automating semantic similarity analysis among topics in the GIS&T Body of Knowledge (BoK), which currently relies on manual curation that can be incomplete and effort-intensive. The research evaluates multiple natural language processing (NLP) techniques, including traditional and deep learning models, to measure semantic similarity between BoK topics. A novel text summarization method, KACERS (Keyword-Aware Cross-Encoder-Ranking Summarizer), is introduced to generate concise, semantically rich summaries of long text documents by leveraging keywords and cross-encoder ranking. Experimental results demonstrate that RoBERTa, when applied to semantic summaries generated by KACERS, achieves a recall@10 of 0.6927 and a balanced accuracy of 0.8161, outperforming other models. The findings provide guidance for automating topic navigation and cross-referencing in the GIS&T BoK and highlight the potential of advanced NLP techniques for analyzing scientific publications.

## Method Summary
The study implements a pipeline combining keyword-aware text summarization with multiple NLP models for semantic similarity measurement. The KACERS summarizer extracts sentences containing specified keywords and ranks them using a cross-encoder to generate semantic summaries. These summaries are then processed by various embedding models (LSA, GloVe, RoBERTa, SciBERT, SPECTER, Doc2vec) and compared using cosine similarity. The system is evaluated against a human-curated list of related topics from the GIS&T BoK, measuring performance using recall@K, precision, F-score, and balanced accuracy metrics.

## Key Results
- RoBERTa applied to KACERS-generated semantic summaries achieves recall@10 of 0.6927 and balanced accuracy of 0.8161
- Semantic summaries enable efficient processing by models with token limits while preserving core semantics
- Pre-trained transformer models (RoBERTa, SciBERT, SPECTER) adapted to scientific texts outperform traditional models (TF-IDF, LSA, GloVe) on GIS&T BoK similarity tasks

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
KACERS improves semantic summary quality by leveraging keyword-aware cross-encoder ranking to capture contextual relationships between keywords and sentences. For each keyword-sentence pair, a cross-encoder computes similarity scores based on simultaneous attention to both elements, enabling richer semantic representation than isolated keyword or sentence embeddings. This works because cross-encoders can model the semantic context of a sentence given a keyword better than a bi-encoder by jointly encoding both elements. The approach may break down if keywords are too sparse or ambiguous, preventing meaningful semantic links.

### Mechanism 2
Semantic summaries enable efficient and accurate semantic similarity measurement by reducing input length while preserving core semantics. By summarizing long documents into concise representations, models like RoBERTa (with token limits) can process full content without truncation, improving similarity accuracy. This relies on the assumption that the summarization step retains essential semantic features needed for similarity comparison. The mechanism may fail if summarization overly compresses content, losing important semantic distinctions between topics.

### Mechanism 3
Pre-trained models (RoBERTa, SciBERT, SPECTER) adapted to scientific texts yield higher semantic similarity accuracy than traditional models. Fine-tuning on large scientific corpora allows these models to capture domain-specific semantics and contextual relationships better than TF-IDF, LSA, or GloVe. This works under the assumption that pre-trained transformer models generalize well to GIS domain semantics despite not being specifically trained on GIS literature. The approach may be less effective if domain specificity is critical and general scientific pre-training cannot capture specialized GIS terminology nuances.

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map: Web scraping -> Preprocessing -> Summarization -> Embedding -> Similarity -> Evaluation
- Critical path: Web scraping → Preprocessing → Summarization → Embedding → Similarity → Evaluation
  - Summarization must precede embedding for models with token limits
- Design tradeoffs:
  - KACERS vs. full-text: Shorter input enables broader model use but risks semantic loss
  - Multiple models vs. single best: Provides robustness but increases complexity
  - Keyword selection: High-quality keywords improve KACERS but require domain expertise
- Failure signatures:
  - Low recall@10 despite high token similarity: Possible semantic mismatch or poor summarization
  - Inconsistent results across models: Possible preprocessing or segment selection issues
  - Keywords produce unrelated summaries: Keywords may be too generic or misaligned with content

## Limitations
- Evaluation relies on a single case study (GIS&T BoK), limiting evidence about performance across different domains or document types
- Cross-encoder approach in KACERS lacks direct experimental comparison to bi-encoder alternatives within this work
- Semantic summaries' quality depends heavily on keyword selection quality, which may vary across domains and require significant manual effort

## Confidence
- **High Confidence**: Experimental results showing RoBERTa achieving recall@10 of 0.6927 and balanced accuracy of 0.8161 on semantic summaries are directly supported by reported metrics
- **Medium Confidence**: Claim that KACERS generates semantically rich summaries is supported by architectural description but lacks direct human evaluation or ablation studies
- **Medium Confidence**: Assertion that pre-trained transformer models outperform traditional approaches is consistent with general NLP literature but needs broader comparative studies
- **Low Confidence**: Claim that KACERS's cross-encoder mechanism inherently provides superior semantic capture compared to bi-encoders is theoretical rather than empirically validated
