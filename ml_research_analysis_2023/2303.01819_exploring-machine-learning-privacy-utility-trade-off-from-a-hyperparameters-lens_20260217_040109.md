# Exploring Machine Learning Privacy/Utility trade-off from a hyperparameters Lens

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2303.01819
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.01819
- Reference count: 38
- This work investigates how hyperparameters of deep learning models affect privacy-utility trade-offs under differential privacy.

## Executive Summary
This paper systematically examines how hyperparameters impact privacy-utility trade-offs in deep learning models trained under differential privacy. The authors investigate activation functions, learning rates, batch normalization, and dropout on model robustness to membership inference attacks and performance under DPSGD. The key contribution is demonstrating that bounded activation functions, specifically BoundedRELU with optimally tuned thresholds, significantly improve accuracy under the same privacy budget, achieving state-of-the-art results on MNIST, FashionMnist, and CIFAR-10. The study also shows that small learning rates and batch normalization improve privacy-preserving properties, while dropout and bounded activations reduce membership inference attack success rates.

## Method Summary
The authors employ DPSGD with Opacus for training differentially private models, using the moment accountant method for privacy budget calculation. They implement membership inference attacks using shadow models and SVM classifiers to evaluate privacy leakage in unprotected models. The experimental framework involves parameter selection, network modification, model training, privacy evaluation, and comparison with baseline models. Hyperparameter variations include bounded versus unbounded activation functions, batch normalization presence/absence, learning rate adjustments, and dropout rates. The evaluation metrics include attack success rate for unprotected models and accuracy versus privacy budget (ε) for DPSGD-protected models across MNIST, FashionMNIST, and CIFAR-10 datasets.

## Key Results
- BoundedRELU activation with optimally tuned threshold achieves state-of-the-art results: MNIST (96.02%), FashionMnist (84.76%), and CIFAR-10 (44.42%)
- Small learning rates and batch normalization improve privacy-preserving properties of models
- Dropout and bounded activations reduce membership inference attack success rates in unprotected models
- Theoretical analysis explains why bounded activations reduce gradient clipping loss in DPSGD

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: Bounded activation functions reduce gradient clipping loss in DPSGD, leading to improved utility under the same privacy budget.
- Mechanism: BoundedRELU clips gradient values naturally at a threshold, preventing large gradients that would be clipped by the DPSGD algorithm. This preserves more information during training, reducing the accuracy loss typically associated with gradient clipping in differentially private training.
- Core assumption: The threshold value for BoundedRELU can be optimized to minimize gradient clipping while maintaining model capacity.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "Theoretical analysis explains why bounded activations reduce gradient clipping loss in DPSGD."
  - [section] "To highlight the impact of BoundedRELU on the gradient size we perform an analytical comparison between RELU and BoundedRELU" showing that ∂L/∂wi = 0 when x > a for BoundedRELU, versus ∂L/∂wi = yi · γi+1 for RELU.
  - [corpus] No direct evidence in corpus papers, but this mechanism aligns with the paper's claim of new state-of-the-art results (MNIST 96.02%, FashionMnist 84.76%, CIFAR-10 44.42%).

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: Small learning rates improve privacy-preserving properties of models by limiting overcorrection and improving generalization accuracy.
- Mechanism: Smaller learning rates reduce the magnitude of parameter updates, making the model less sensitive to individual training examples. This inherently improves privacy as the model's outputs become less dependent on specific training data points.
- Core assumption: The relationship between learning rate and model sensitivity to training data is monotonic and predictable.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "The study also shows that small learning rates... improve privacy-preserving properties"
  - [section] "This is may be due to the fact that when learning rate is too large often moves too far in the correct direction resulting in an instability of the convergence of our model which causes poor generalization accuracy"
  - [corpus] No direct evidence in corpus papers, but this aligns with Wilson & Martinez (2001) cited in the paper.

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: Batch normalization improves privacy-utility trade-off by stabilizing learning and reducing overfitting, which in turn reduces membership inference attack success rates.
- Mechanism: Batch normalization reduces internal covariate shift and overfitting by normalizing layer inputs. Since overfitting increases vulnerability to membership inference attacks (Yeom et al., 2018), batch normalization indirectly improves privacy by making models less distinguishable between training and test data.
- Core assumption: The connection between overfitting and membership inference attack effectiveness is causal rather than merely correlational.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "The study also shows that... batch normalization improve privacy-preserving properties"
  - [section] "we notice improvement for those activation functions in robustness to the attack for the large model" and "except for the slight drop in robustness for a number of shadows equal to 6 and 8 in Figure 9 compared to the baseline model"
  - [corpus] No direct evidence in corpus papers, but this aligns with Yeom et al. (2018) cited in the paper.

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map: Baseline model training -> Membership inference attack evaluation -> DPSGD protected model training with hyperparameter variations -> Privacy budget calculation -> Accuracy evaluation -> Comparison
- Critical path: For protected models: parameter selection → network modification → DPSGD training with gradient clipping and noise addition → privacy budget calculation using moment accountant → accuracy evaluation. For unprotected models: parameter selection → network modification → standard training → membership inference attack → attack success rate evaluation.
- Design tradeoffs: Bounded activation functions improve privacy-utility trade-off but may limit model capacity if threshold is too restrictive. Small learning rates improve privacy but may slow convergence. Batch normalization improves privacy and utility but adds computational overhead.
- Failure signatures: If bounded activation functions are set with thresholds too low, accuracy will degrade significantly. If learning rates are too small, models may not converge within reasonable epochs. If batch normalization is applied incorrectly, it may not provide the intended regularization effect.
- First 3 experiments:
  1. Implement membership inference attack on baseline model with RELU activation to establish baseline attack success rate.
  2. Replace RELU with BoundedRELU (threshold = 2) and compare attack success rates on unprotected model.
  3. Train protected model with DPSGD using BoundedRELU and compare accuracy vs privacy budget with baseline RELU model.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- Question: How does the optimal threshold parameter for BoundedRELU vary across different model architectures and datasets beyond MNIST, FashionMNIST, and CIFAR-10?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper tunes BoundedRELU's threshold parameter using a genetic algorithm for specific datasets and mentions "we set the threshold of BoundedRELU a = 2 for a protected model" on MNIST, a = 3 on FashionMnist, and a = 4 on CIFAR-10, but calls for broader exploration.
- Why unresolved: The study only examines three benchmark datasets and one LeNet-style architecture, leaving uncertainty about threshold optimization for diverse architectures and more complex datasets.
- What evidence would resolve it: Systematic experiments varying the BoundedRELU threshold parameter across multiple model architectures (e.g., ResNet, VGG) and diverse datasets (e.g., ImageNet, medical imaging) to establish general principles for optimal threshold selection.

### Open Question 2
- Question: What is the theoretical relationship between gradient clipping frequency in DPSGD and the choice of activation function boundedness?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper provides theoretical analysis showing that bounded activation functions reduce gradient clipping loss in DPSGD, stating "bounded activation function outperform unbounded activation functions and this is due to the fact of reducing the impact of clipping gradient."
- Why unresolved: While the paper demonstrates empirical benefits of bounded activations, it doesn't provide a complete theoretical framework quantifying the relationship between activation function boundedness and gradient clipping frequency under different privacy budgets and dataset characteristics.
- What evidence would resolve it: A formal mathematical analysis deriving the probability distribution of gradient norms under different activation functions and establishing the expected reduction in clipping events for bounded versus unbounded activations.

### Open Question 3
- Question: How do hyperparameters interact with each other in their effects on privacy-utility trade-offs, beyond pairwise combinations?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper mentions "we can also see how several parameters might be combined to increase model privacy" and discusses interactions between batch normalization and BoundedRELU, but calls for broader investigation.
- Why unresolved: The study primarily examines individual hyperparameters and limited combinations, without exploring higher-order interactions or establishing a comprehensive framework for understanding how hyperparameters jointly affect privacy and utility.
- What evidence would resolve it: Systematic experiments using factorial design or automated hyperparameter optimization techniques to quantify three-way and higher-order interactions between multiple hyperparameters, along with a theoretical framework explaining these interactions.

## Limitations

- The choice of threshold value for BoundedRELU appears arbitrary without systematic sensitivity analysis across a broader range of values
- The generalizability to more complex real-world scenarios beyond standard benchmark datasets remains untested
- The membership inference attack framework may not capture all possible privacy leakage vectors

## Confidence

- High confidence: The empirical results showing improved accuracy under DPSGD with bounded activations are well-supported by the experimental data.
- Medium confidence: The theoretical explanation for why bounded activations reduce gradient clipping loss is plausible but requires further mathematical rigor.
- Medium confidence: The claims about batch normalization and learning rates improving privacy-utility trade-offs are supported by experiments but lack deep theoretical grounding.
