# Counterfactual Identifiability of Bijective Causal Models

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2302.02228
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2302.02228
- Reference count: 40
- Key outcome: Establishes counterfactual identifiability for bijective causal models and demonstrates practical learning methods using structured generative networks

## Executive Summary
This paper introduces Bijective Generation Mechanisms (BGMs), a class of causal models where each endogenous variable is generated through a bijective mapping from its exogenous counterpart. The authors prove that BGMs enable counterfactual identifiability under three common causal structures with unobserved confounding: Markovian, Instrumental Variable, and Backdoor Criterion settings. They propose a practical learning approach that frames BGM estimation as structured generative modeling, leveraging conditional normalizing flows to satisfy the necessary conditional independence constraints. The method is evaluated on both synthetic and real-world tasks, showing improved counterfactual estimation accuracy compared to baseline approaches.

## Method Summary
The authors propose learning bijective generation mechanisms through structured generative modeling. The approach constructs directed graphical models (DGMs) that encode the required conditional independence constraints for counterfactual identifiability. Conditional normalizing flows serve as the building blocks, providing tractable density estimation with the needed properties of invertibility and differentiability. The training objective becomes standard density modeling while the DGM architecture ensures the theoretical identifiability conditions are satisfied by construction. For counterfactual estimation, the learned BGM can be efficiently inverted to recover the exogenous state, enabling exact counterfactual computation.

## Key Results
- Establishes counterfactual identifiability theorems for BGMs under Markovian, Instrumental Variable, and Backdoor Criterion causal structures
- Demonstrates that combining IV and BC conditions reduces estimation error by 38% compared to BC alone
- Shows practical implementation using conditional normalizing flows achieves accurate counterfactual estimation on both synthetic and real-world video streaming tasks

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: Bijective generation mechanisms (BGM) enable counterfactual identifiability by ensuring information preservation during transformation from exogenous to endogenous variables.
- Mechanism: The bijective property guarantees that each endogenous variable contains all information from its corresponding exogenous variable. This allows exact inversion of the generation process to recover the original exogenous state, which is essential for counterfactual estimation.
- Core assumption: The generation function fi is bijective for each realization of Pa(Vi), ensuring no information is lost in the transformation.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "We study counterfactual identifiability in causal models with bijective generation mechanisms (BGM)"
  - [section] "We assume that the function fi is a bijective mapping from Ui to Vi, for each realization of Pa(Vi), hence the name Bijective Generation Mechanism (BGM)"
- Break condition: The generation function is not bijective, leading to information loss and inability to recover the original exogenous state

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: Conditional independence constraints embedded in structured generative networks guarantee the distributional requirements for counterfactual identifiability.
- Mechanism: By constructing directed graphical models that encode the necessary conditional independencies (e.g., U⊥⊥X|Z for backdoor criterion), the learning process is guided to satisfy the theoretical identifiability conditions by construction.
- Core assumption: The structured generative network can be designed to satisfy the conditional independence constraints specified by the causal structure.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [section] "To efficiently encode the conditional independence constraints, we make use of directed graphical models (DGM) for building structured generative networks"
  - [section] "This has two benefits: i) (Conditional) independence is guaranteed by construction. ii) Training objective simply becomes density modeling"
- Break condition: The DGM structure cannot encode the required conditional independencies, or the learning process fails to satisfy them despite the architectural constraints

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: The combination of instrumental variables and backdoor criterion conditions provides stronger identifiability guarantees than either condition alone.
- Mechanism: When both IV and BC conditions are satisfied simultaneously, the structured generative network can exploit both paths to exogenous variables, reducing estimation error compared to using either condition alone.
- Core assumption: The causal structure allows for simultaneous satisfaction of both IV and BC conditions, enabling the combined approach.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [section] "It is possible to combine settings explored in §5 to further improve accuracy provided the problem has the appropriate causal structure"
  - [section] "IV+BC decreases the average error compared to the BC scheme by almost 38%"
- Break condition: The causal structure doesn't support simultaneous satisfaction of both IV and BC conditions, or the combined approach doesn't provide additional benefits over individual conditions

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- Question: How robust are the theoretical assumptions (e.g., strict monotonicity, differentiability) required for identifiability, and can they be relaxed while maintaining identifiability?
- Basis in paper: [inferred] The authors note that linear rational splines used in their experiments are not necessarily twice differentiable, and ReLU activations make CGMs non-differentiable with respect to x, yet the experiments perform well.
- Why unresolved: The experiments suggest the assumptions may not be tight, but the paper does not provide a formal relaxation or analysis of these assumptions.
- What evidence would resolve it: Experiments with alternative spline types (e.g., quadratic splines) and activation functions (e.g., GELU) demonstrating maintained identifiability and performance.

- Why unresolved: The paper only establishes identifiability for three specific causal structures, leaving open the question of whether similar results hold for other structures.
- What evidence would resolve it: Proving identifiability theorems for additional causal structures, such as those involving mediation or cycles.

## Limitations
- Theoretical assumptions may be too restrictive in practice, particularly regarding differentiability requirements
- Empirical evaluation limited to synthetic and semi-synthetic datasets, with limited real-world validation
- The method's performance depends on correct specification of the causal structure, which may be challenging to determine in practice
