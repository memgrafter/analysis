# Data Augmentation Alone Can Improve Adversarial Training

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2301.09879
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2301.09879
- Reference count: 31
- Key outcome: A new data augmentation scheme (IDBH) with Cropshift transformation achieves state-of-the-art accuracy and robustness in adversarial training while mitigating robust overfitting.

## Executive Summary
This paper investigates how data augmentation can improve adversarial training by focusing on two key properties: diversity and hardness. The authors find that diversity generally improves both accuracy and robustness, while hardness can boost robustness at the cost of accuracy within a certain limit and degrade both metrics beyond that limit. They propose a new crop transformation, Cropshift, which has improved diversity compared to the conventional Padcrop method. The authors then develop a new data augmentation scheme, IDBH, which combines Cropshift with other transformations and an optimal hardness-diversity balance. Empirically, IDBH achieves state-of-the-art performance for data augmentations in adversarial training, matching or exceeding the performance of the best contemporary regularization methods for alleviating robust overfitting.

## Method Summary
The authors propose a new data augmentation scheme, IDBH, which consists of four augmentation layers: Cropshift, Random Erasing, Flip, and a color/shape transformation schedule. Cropshift crops a region and shifts it to a random location, introducing more varied patterns compared to Padcrop. The IDBH schedule optimizes the hardness and diversity of augmentations by adjusting the probability and strength of each transformation during training. The authors train Wide ResNet34-1, PreAct ResNet18, and Wide ResNet34-10 on CIFAR-10, SVHN, and Tiny ImageNet using PGD-10 adversarial training with IDBH. They evaluate the models' accuracy and robustness against PGD-50 and AutoAttack, and compare their results to state-of-the-art regularization methods for robust overfitting.

## Key Results
- Cropshift significantly improves diversity compared to Padcrop, leading to better robustness and accuracy in adversarial training.
- The hardness of data augmentation has a U-shaped relationship with both accuracy and robustness, with an optimal level that balances the two metrics.
- IDBH achieves state-of-the-art accuracy and robustness for data augmentations in adversarial training, and when combined with weight averaging, it matches or exceeds the performance of the best contemporary regularization methods for alleviating robust overfitting.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- Question: How does the hardness of data augmentation interact with model architecture and capacity in adversarial training?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The authors demonstrate that the optimal hardness of data augmentation is highly sensitive to the model's capacity, with different augmentations performing dramatically differently across architectures like Wide ResNet34-1 and PreAct ResNet18.
- Why unresolved: While the paper shows this sensitivity, it doesn't provide a clear framework or theory for predicting how hardness should scale with model capacity. The relationship appears empirical and dataset-dependent rather than theoretically grounded.
- What evidence would resolve it: A systematic study mapping model capacity (e.g., number of parameters, FLOPs) to optimal hardness levels across multiple architectures and datasets, potentially revealing underlying scaling laws.

### Open Question 2
- Question: What is the relationship between diversity in data augmentation and robust generalization beyond just reducing overfitting?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The authors show that diversity in data augmentation generally improves both accuracy and robustness, but the paper doesn't explore deeper mechanisms beyond the empirical observation.
- Why unresolved: The paper demonstrates correlation but doesn't establish causation or explain why diversity improves robust generalization. Is it through better coverage of the input space, more stable gradients, or some other mechanism?
- What evidence would resolve it: Experiments isolating different types of diversity (spatial, strength, type) and measuring their specific effects on robust generalization metrics, combined with theoretical analysis of how diversity affects the loss landscape.

### Open Question 3
- Question: Can the Cropshift transformation be theoretically justified as superior to Padcrop for adversarial training?
- Basis in paper: [inferred] The authors claim Cropshift improves diversity compared to Padcrop and empirically show it outperforms Padcrop in their augmentation framework, but don't provide theoretical justification for why this should be the case.
- Why unresolved: The paper demonstrates empirical superiority but doesn't explain the underlying mathematical or statistical reasons why shifting cropped regions provides better diversity for adversarial training specifically.
- What evidence would resolve it: Mathematical analysis of the transformation distributions, showing how Cropshift better covers the input space or provides more effective regularization for adversarial robustness, potentially supported by information-theoretic arguments.

### Open Question 4
- Question: What is the optimal balance between hardness and diversity for adversarial training across different threat models?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The authors find that optimal data augmentation requires both diversity and balanced hardness, but their experiments focus primarily on l∞ bounded perturbations with ϵ=8/255.
- Why unresolved: The paper doesn't explore how the optimal balance changes for different perturbation norms (l2, l0), larger ϵ values, or adaptive attacks. The balance might need to shift depending on the threat model.
- What evidence would resolve it: Systematic experiments varying the threat model (perturbation type, magnitude, attack method) and measuring how the optimal hardness-diversity balance shifts, potentially revealing a general principle for robust training across threat models.

## Limitations

- The IDBH augmentation schedule was only fully optimized for WRN34-1 on CIFAR-10, with results for other architectures and datasets extrapolated from these optimizations.
- The paper provides theoretical justification for Cropshift but limited empirical comparison under identical conditions to Padcrop.
- The precise IDBH schedule parameters were determined through grid search with computational constraints, potentially missing optimal configurations.

## Confidence

**High Confidence** in the general findings about diversity and hardness trade-offs, supported by extensive ablation studies across multiple datasets and architectures.

**Medium Confidence** in the specific Cropshift implementation details and its comparative advantage over Padcrop, as the paper provides theoretical justification but limited empirical comparison under identical conditions.

**Low Confidence** in the precise IDBH schedule parameters, as these were determined through grid search with computational constraints, potentially missing optimal configurations.

## Next Checks

1. **Cross-architecture validation**: Test IDBH with the exact optimized schedule from WRN34-1 on WRN34-10 and PRN18 to verify that performance gains transfer as claimed, particularly for the robustness-overfitting gap reduction.

2. **Ablation of augmentation layers**: Systematically remove or modify individual components of IDBH (e.g., test Cropshift alone, then add Random Erasing, then add color/shape transformations) to quantify their independent contributions to the reported improvements.

3. **Robustness calibration**: Compare PGD-50 and AutoAttack evaluations at the same checkpoints to verify that the claimed reduction in robust overfitting is not an artifact of using different evaluation methods at different stages of training.
