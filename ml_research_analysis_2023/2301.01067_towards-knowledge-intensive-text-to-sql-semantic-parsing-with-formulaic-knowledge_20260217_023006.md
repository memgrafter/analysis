# Towards Knowledge-Intensive Text-to-SQL Semantic Parsing with Formulaic Knowledge

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2301.01067
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2301.01067
- **Reference count:** 31
- **Primary result:** 28.2% overall improvement on Chinese KnowSQL benchmark for knowledge-intensive text-to-SQL parsing

## Executive Summary
This paper introduces knowledge-intensive text-to-SQL semantic parsing, where domain knowledge is necessary to parse expert questions into SQL queries over domain-specific tables. The authors build a new Chinese benchmark KnowSQL consisting of domain-specific questions covering finance, estate, and transportation domains. To address this problem, they propose formulaic knowledge as a domain knowledge base and a three-stage framework (ReGrouP) to leverage this knowledge during parsing. Experimental results demonstrate a significant 28.2% improvement overall on KnowSQL, showing that formulaic knowledge can effectively bridge the gap between expert questions and SQL queries without requiring additional data annotations.

## Method Summary
The paper presents a three-stage framework called ReGrouP for knowledge-intensive text-to-SQL parsing. The approach first retrieves relevant formulaic knowledge items from a knowledge bank using a DPR model with Chinese BERT-wwm-ext encoder. Second, it grounds formulaic knowledge concepts to schema elements using an ETA model with a confidence threshold. Finally, it parses the question into SQL using the UniSAr parser with mBART-CC25, incorporating the grounded knowledge. The method is evaluated on the KnowSQL benchmark, which includes 23,157 training examples and 2,580 test examples across three domains. The formulaic knowledge bank contains 1,954 items across 19 domains, providing domain-specific knowledge that helps parse expert questions requiring specialized understanding.

## Key Results
- 28.2% overall improvement on KnowSQL benchmark compared to vanilla baselines
- Significant performance gains across all three domains: finance (29.3%), estate (24.1%), and transportation (25.6%)
- ReGrouP successfully handles questions requiring domain-specific knowledge like "annual return" in finance or "total floor area" in estate

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The approach works by introducing formulaic knowledge as an intermediate representation between natural language questions and SQL queries. When expert questions contain domain-specific terminology or require understanding of industry conventions, formulaic knowledge provides the necessary semantic mapping. For example, when a user asks about "annual return" in finance, the system retrieves relevant formulaic knowledge that maps this concept to specific table columns and operations. This bridging mechanism allows the parser to understand expert-level questions without requiring extensive training data for every possible domain-specific phrase. The three-stage pipeline (retrieve, ground, parse) systematically incorporates this knowledge while maintaining modularity and interpretability.

## Architecture Onboarding

**Component Map:** DPR Retriever -> ETA Grounder -> UniSAr Parser

**Critical Path:** Question -> DPR Retrieval (top-3 formulaic knowledge) -> ETA Grounding (H=0.6 threshold) -> SQL Generation (UniSAr with grounded knowledge)

**Design Tradeoffs:** Uses formulaic knowledge instead of data augmentation to avoid annotation costs, but requires maintaining knowledge bank; weak supervision for grounding reduces labeling effort but may introduce errors

## Limitations
- Does not specify exact preprocessing pipeline for converting matrix tables from annual reports into relational format
- Negative sampling strategy during DPR training is not clearly described
- Evaluation focuses solely on exact set match accuracy without component-level ablation studies

## Confidence
- **High confidence:** 28.2% overall improvement on KnowSQL benchmark, three-stage framework architecture (ReGrouP), use of formulaic knowledge for bridging expert questions to SQL queries
- **Medium confidence:** Effectiveness of specific grounding threshold H=0.6, claim that formulaic knowledge is superior to annotation-based approaches
- **Low confidence:** Scalability to domains beyond finance, estate, and transportation; long-term maintenance requirements for formulaic knowledge bank
