# Bias mitigation techniques in image classification: fair machine learning in human heritage collections

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2303.11449
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.11449
- Reference count: 0
- Primary result: Combined bias mitigation techniques with transfer learning achieved near-zero demographic parity differences in gender classification of historical photographs

## Executive Summary
This work evaluates bias mitigation techniques for image classification in human heritage collections, where data scarcity and diversity present challenges. Using gender classification as a case study, the authors assess transfer learning, re-weighting, image augmentation, and threshold change with Xception and EfficientNet models on 19th-20th century photographs. Transfer learning from the FairFace dataset improves accuracy from 78.5% to 84.5% for EfficientNet. The fairest classifier combines transfer learning, re-weighting, image augmentation, and threshold change, achieving near-zero demographic and proportional parity differences. Individual bias mitigation techniques showed limited effectiveness, but their combination with transfer learning yielded significant fairness improvements.

## Method Summary
The authors conducted a comprehensive evaluation of bias mitigation techniques using a dataset of 19th-20th century photographs (CHDS) with binary gender labels. They tested four bias mitigation strategies individually and in combination: transfer learning from FairFace dataset, re-weighting, image augmentation, and threshold change. Two CNN architectures (Xception and EfficientNet) were evaluated using demographic parity difference (DPD) and proportional parity difference (PPD) as fairness metrics, alongside standard accuracy measures. The study compared baseline models against those incorporating various combinations of bias mitigation techniques to identify the most effective approach for achieving both accuracy and fairness in cultural heritage collections.

## Key Results
- Transfer learning from FairFace dataset improved EfficientNet accuracy from 78.5% to 84.5%
- Individual bias mitigation techniques showed limited effectiveness when applied separately
- The combined approach (transfer learning + re-weighting + image augmentation + threshold change) achieved near-zero demographic parity differences
- Re-weighting, augmentation, and threshold change together without transfer learning showed no significant fairness improvement

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The study demonstrates that bias mitigation in image classification requires addressing both data scarcity and inherent dataset biases simultaneously. Transfer learning from a bias-aware dataset (FairFace) provides a foundation of diverse, representative features that compensates for the limited size and potential bias in historical photograph collections. The combination of multiple mitigation techniques addresses different aspects of bias: transfer learning introduces fairness-aware features, re-weighting adjusts for class imbalance, augmentation increases dataset diversity, and threshold change optimizes decision boundaries for fairness. This multi-pronged approach is necessary because individual techniques only address specific aspects of bias, while their combination creates synergistic effects that substantially improve both accuracy and fairness metrics.

## Foundational Learning
- **Demographic parity difference**: Measures fairness by comparing prediction rates across demographic groups; needed to quantify bias in classification outcomes; quick check: calculate DPD across protected groups in your dataset
- **Proportional parity difference**: Evaluates whether classification accuracy is proportional across groups; needed to ensure balanced performance; quick check: verify PPD is near zero for fair classifiers
- **Transfer learning**: Using pre-trained models on related tasks; needed to overcome data scarcity in heritage collections; quick check: test accuracy improvement when using pre-trained vs random initialization
- **Re-weighting**: Adjusting sample weights during training to address class imbalance; needed to prevent majority group dominance; quick check: compare performance with and without class weight adjustments
- **Image augmentation**: Creating synthetic variations of training images; needed to increase dataset diversity and size; quick check: measure accuracy changes with different augmentation intensities
- **Threshold adjustment**: Modifying decision boundaries for different groups; needed to optimize fairness metrics; quick check: experiment with group-specific threshold values

## Architecture Onboarding

Component map:
Pre-processing (face extraction) -> Transfer learning (FairFace) -> Model training (Xception/EfficientNet) -> Bias mitigation (re-weighting, augmentation, threshold) -> Fairness evaluation (DPD, PPD)

Critical path:
Face extraction → Transfer learning initialization → Model training with bias mitigation → Fairness evaluation

Design tradeoffs:
- Accuracy vs fairness: The fairest model (combined approach) achieved near-zero parity differences but may sacrifice some accuracy compared to baseline
- Computational cost: Combining multiple mitigation techniques increases training complexity and resource requirements
- Dataset dependency: Transfer learning effectiveness depends on the quality and bias characteristics of the pre-training dataset

Failure signatures:
- Persistent demographic parity differences despite mitigation indicate inadequate transfer learning or biased face extraction
- Accuracy drops with fairness improvements suggest over-correction or insufficient data
- Inconsistent results across time periods reveal temporal bias or dataset fragmentation issues

First experiments:
1. Compare transfer learning from FairFace vs ImageNet on a subset of CHDS to establish baseline improvement
2. Test individual bias mitigation techniques (re-weighting, augmentation, threshold) on baseline model to measure standalone effectiveness
3. Evaluate face extraction accuracy across demographic groups to identify potential pre-processing bias

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- Question: How do bias mitigation techniques perform across different intersectionalities (e.g., age, ethnicity, gender) in cultural heritage collections?
- Basis in paper: The authors note that the CHDS data set is diverse concerning age, ethnicity, culture, and period but only has gender labels, limiting intersectional analysis.
- Why unresolved: The study only evaluated gender-based bias, preventing investigation of intersectional fairness.
- What evidence would resolve it: Experiments using labeled data across multiple demographic dimensions (age, ethnicity, gender) with intersectional bias metrics would clarify performance differences.

### Open Question 2
- Question: How does face extraction bias affect gender classification fairness in historical photographs?
- Basis in paper: The authors acknowledge potential bias in their pre-trained face extraction model but did not investigate its impact on the final gender classification results.
- Why unresolved: The study did not analyze whether the face detection module systematically failed to detect faces from certain demographic groups.
- What evidence would resolve it: Analysis of face detection accuracy across demographic groups in the CHDS would reveal extraction bias.

### Open Question 3
- Question: How do different transfer learning strategies (e.g., fine-tuning, feature extraction) compare for fairness and accuracy in small cultural heritage datasets?
- Basis in paper: The authors mention that fine-tuning was not included in their transfer learning experiments, despite noting it could potentially improve performance.
- Why unresolved: The study only tested limited transfer learning approaches without exploring fine-tuning strategies.
- What evidence would resolve it: Experiments comparing various transfer learning strategies (feature extraction, fine-tuning, unfreezing different layers) would identify optimal approaches for fairness and accuracy.

## Limitations
- Study focuses exclusively on binary gender classification, limiting conclusions about broader demographic fairness
- Specific dataset composition and image characteristics may not represent all human heritage collections
- Individual bias mitigation techniques showed limited standalone effectiveness, suggesting the combination approach might be necessary rather than sufficient

## Confidence
- Transfer learning accuracy improvement: High confidence (84.5% achieved, well-documented improvement)
- Combined bias mitigation fairness: Medium confidence (near-zero parity differences but single task validation)
- Individual mitigation techniques: Low confidence (limited standalone effectiveness demonstrated)

## Next Checks

3. Conduct temporal validation by splitting the heritage collection into distinct time periods to assess if fairness improvements persist across different photographic eras
