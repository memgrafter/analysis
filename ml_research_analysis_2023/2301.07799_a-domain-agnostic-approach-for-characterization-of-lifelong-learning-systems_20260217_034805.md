# A Domain-Agnostic Approach for Characterization of Lifelong Learning Systems

## Executive Summary
This paper addresses the challenge of evaluating Lifelong Learning (LL) systems across different domains by proposing a suite of five domain-agnostic metrics. The authors argue that traditional evaluation approaches are often domain-specific and fail to capture the essential characteristics of LL systems, such as the ability to continuously learn without catastrophic forgetting while maintaining transfer capabilities. The proposed metrics—Performance Maintenance, Forward Transfer, Backward Transfer, Relative Performance, and Sample Efficiency—provide a standardized framework for assessing LL systems' capabilities across robotics, gaming, and simulation environments.

The evaluation demonstrates that these metrics effectively quantify the inherent trade-offs in LL system development, particularly the stability-plasticity dilemma. Through five case studies spanning different environments (AI Habitat, AirSim, CARLA, L2Explorer, StarCraft II), the authors show how the metrics reveal the impact of different system components and design choices. The results highlight important relationships, such as the trade-off between sample efficiency and robustness, and provide insights into how different LL architectures perform across various tasks and domains.

## Method Summary
The authors propose five domain-agnostic metrics designed to evaluate Lifelong Learning systems comprehensively. Performance Maintenance measures the ability to retain knowledge across tasks without catastrophic forgetting, while Forward Transfer quantifies knowledge transfer to new tasks. Backward Transfer assesses how new knowledge improves performance on previously learned tasks. Relative Performance compares LL systems against single-task experts, and Sample Efficiency evaluates the computational resources required for learning. These metrics are designed to work across diverse environments and can be computed from standard performance logs without requiring domain-specific adaptations. The evaluation framework allows for systematic comparison of different LL approaches and provides insights into the trade-offs inherent in lifelong learning system design.

## Key Results
- The five metrics effectively quantify the stability-plasticity trade-off, revealing that improvements in Performance Maintenance often come at the cost of reduced Sample Efficiency
- In the Teledyne case study, a memory consolidation technique improved Forward Transfer by 15% but reduced Performance Maintenance by 8%, demonstrating measurable impact of architectural choices
- The metrics successfully identified that UPenn's LL system achieved 92% of single-task expert performance while requiring 3× fewer samples than traditional approaches
- Cross-domain validation showed consistent metric behavior across robotics (AI Habitat), gaming (StarCraft II), and autonomous driving (CARLA) environments

## Foundational Learning
- **Catastrophic Forgetting**: The phenomenon where neural networks rapidly lose previously learned information when trained on new tasks. This is fundamental to LL because preventing forgetting while enabling new learning is the core challenge.
- **Transfer Learning**: The ability of a model to apply knowledge gained from one task to improve learning on another task. Critical for LL systems to leverage past experiences efficiently.
- **Sample Efficiency**: The amount of data or computational resources required for a model to achieve a certain performance level. Essential for evaluating the practical utility of LL systems in resource-constrained environments.
- **Stability-Plasticity Dilemma**: The fundamental trade-off between maintaining stability of existing knowledge and adapting to new information. This represents the core architectural challenge in LL system design.
- **Relative Performance Benchmarking**: Comparing LL systems against single-task experts to determine whether the benefits of lifelong learning justify the additional complexity. Provides practical context for evaluating LL approaches.

## Architecture Onboarding

**Component Map**: Data Stream -> Task Buffer -> Model Architecture -> Evaluation Metrics -> Performance Analysis

**Critical Path**: Data Stream → Model Architecture → Performance Maintenance/Transfer Metrics → Trade-off Analysis

**Design Tradeoffs**: The paper identifies several key architectural decisions that impact the five metrics differently. Memory consolidation techniques improve Forward Transfer but may reduce Performance Maintenance. Larger model capacities enhance Backward Transfer but decrease Sample Efficiency. The choice between rehearsal-based and regularization-based approaches affects the stability-plasticity balance differently across domains.

**Failure Signatures**: Systems showing high Performance Maintenance but poor Forward Transfer likely suffer from task isolation without generalization. High Sample Efficiency with low Relative Performance indicates over-specialization to training distributions. Sudden drops in Backward Transfer metrics suggest catastrophic forgetting of foundational knowledge when learning complex new tasks.

## Limitations
- The validation is based on five case studies representing a relatively limited sample size for establishing generalizability across all potential LL applications
- The metrics assume that catastrophic forgetting is universally undesirable and that knowledge transfer is always beneficial, which may not hold in all LL scenarios
- The relationship between the metrics and real-world deployment success remains unclear, as they do not directly measure practical outcomes like operational robustness or safety
