# Analyzing Syntactic Generalization Capacity of Pre-trained Language Models on Japanese Honorific Conversion

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2306.03055
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.03055
- Reference count: 4
- Pre-trained language models show better performance on Japanese honorific conversion when fine-tuned versus using prompt-based approaches

## Executive Summary
This paper introduces a Japanese honorific conversion task that requires both syntactic and pragmatic knowledge, evaluating GPT-3 models on their ability to convert sentences to appropriate honorific forms based on social relationships. The authors construct a template-based dataset to systematically test syntactic generalization across simple and complex sentence structures. Their results demonstrate that fine-tuned GPT-3 models significantly outperform prompt-based models, with the fine-tuned models showing good syntactic generalizability except for cases involving direct speech honorific conversion.

## Method Summary
The authors create a template-based dataset for Japanese honorific conversion by filling placeholders with 23 verbs and 19 common Japanese names to generate controlled test cases. They evaluate two approaches: fine-tuning GPT-3 (davinci) models with SIMPLE_TRAIN datasets of 117 problems repeated 3 or 7 times, and prompt-based evaluation using text-davinci-003. The evaluation involves converting non-honorific sentences to appropriate honorific forms based on explicitly provided social relationship contexts, with manual correctness checking of the outputs. They test both simple structures (SIMPLE_TEST with 108 examples) and complex structures including scrambling, center embedding, and direct speech (COMPLEX_TEST with 408 examples).

## Key Results
- Fine-tuned GPT-3 models achieve significantly higher accuracy than prompt-based models on Japanese honorific conversion tasks
- Fine-tuned models demonstrate syntactic generalization on compound honorific sentences across most complex structures
- Prompt-based models show much lower performance (<25% accuracy) and tend to default to polite forms rather than appropriate honorifics
- Both model types struggle with direct speech honorific conversion, incorrectly applying speaker-based honorific rules to quoted content

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: Fine-tuned GPT-3 models perform better on Japanese honorific conversion than prompt-based models because fine-tuning allows the model to learn task-specific syntactic rules and pragmatic social relationship patterns.
- Mechanism: The fine-tuning process provides explicit training data with input-output pairs showing correct honorific conjugations based on context. This allows the model to learn the mapping between social relationship information and appropriate honorific forms.
- Core assumption: GPT-3 has sufficient capacity to learn honorific rules from the relatively small dataset when fine-tuned, and these rules generalize to new sentence structures.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract]: "Our results showed that the fine-tuned GPT-3 performed better in a context-aware honorific conversion task than the prompt-based one."
  - [section 4.2.1]: "The scores plummeted when the models were tested with COMPLEX_TEST compared to SIMPLE_TEST. When we increased the data size, the scores increased in most cases"
- Break condition: If the training dataset lacks sufficient coverage of complex sentence structures (like direct speech) or social relationship variations, the fine-tuned model may fail to generalize to those cases.

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: Prompt-based models perform poorly because zero-shot learning lacks the explicit training needed to learn the complex mapping between social relationships and honorific conjugations.
- Mechanism: Without fine-tuning, the model relies on pre-existing knowledge encoded during pre-training. The prompt may not provide sufficient guidance for the model to understand the specific task requirements and apply the correct honorific rules.
- Core assumption: The pre-training data contains enough Japanese honorific examples for the model to perform the task in zero-shot settings, but the prompt doesn't effectively activate this knowledge.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract]: "The prompt-based models showed much lower performance than the fine-tuned ones."
  - [section 4.2.2]: "The scores were relatively higher when tested with SIMPLE_TEST than with COMPLEX_TEST, but the scores under all of our settings were lower than 25%."
- Break condition: If the prompt could be improved to better guide the model or if few-shot examples were added to the prompt, performance might improve significantly.

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: GPT-3 fails on direct speech honorific conversion because it treats direct speech differently than indirect speech, applying speaker-based honorifics to quoted content incorrectly.
- Mechanism: The model appears to generalize indirect speech honorific rules to direct speech cases, ignoring the quotation marks that indicate the speech belongs to another person. This suggests the model isn't properly distinguishing between quoted and unquoted content.
- Core assumption: The model has learned that honorifics can be applied to indirect speech based on the speaker's relationship with the people mentioned, and incorrectly applies this same logic to direct speech.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [section 4.2.1]: "In Table 2, the model failed to convert a direct speech sentence... The verb kaeru should not be in a subject honorific form (okaerininaru) because Takahashi does not use honorifics for Kimura, given their relationships."
  - [section 4.2.1]: "Based on this characteristic, we suppose that the model applied the same honorific knowledge as indirect speech to direct speech, ignoring the role of brackets."
- Break condition: If the model could be explicitly trained on the distinction between direct and indirect speech, or if special handling for quotation marks was implemented, this failure mode might be resolved.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- Question: Can Japanese honorific conversion models effectively handle complex syntactic structures like scrambling and center embedding?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper shows that fine-tuned GPT-3 models struggled with complex syntactic structures, particularly with direct speech sentences, while demonstrating some success with simpler structures.
- Why unresolved: The study found that while the models performed better on simple syntactic structures, they did not generalize well to complex structures like direct speech, indicating a gap in the models' ability to handle such complexity.
- What evidence would resolve it: Testing the models on a larger and more diverse dataset that includes a wider range of complex syntactic structures would provide clearer insights into their generalization capabilities.

### Open Question 2
- Question: How can prompt-based models be improved to better differentiate between subject honorifics (SH), object honorifics (OH), and polite forms in Japanese?
- Basis in paper: [inferred] The paper mentions that prompt-based models often defaulted to converting sentences into polite forms rather than applying the correct honorifics, suggesting a need for better prompt design.
- Why unresolved: The current prompt setting may not be sufficiently detailed to guide the model in distinguishing between different types of honorifics, leading to suboptimal performance.
- What evidence would resolve it: Developing and testing new prompts that explicitly differentiate between SH, OH, and polite forms, possibly using few-shot learning, would help determine if model performance improves.

### Open Question 3
- Question: What role do social relationships play in the accuracy of Japanese honorific conversion, and how can models better incorporate this contextual information?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper highlights the importance of social relationships in determining the correct use of honorifics and suggests that models need to effectively utilize this information for accurate conversion.
- Why unresolved: While the study provided explicit social relationship information, it remains unclear how well models can extract and apply such contextual information from more implicit sources.
- What evidence would resolve it: Conducting experiments where models must infer social relationships from context rather than being explicitly provided could reveal how well they can integrate this information into their conversions.

## Limitations
- Template-based dataset generation may not fully capture the diversity of natural Japanese honorific usage
- Findings are specific to GPT-3 models and may not generalize to other language models
- Manual evaluation introduces potential subjectivity in correctness assessment
- Prompt-based evaluation lacks detailed description of prompt format and task instructions

## Confidence
- Fine-tuning vs. Prompt Performance: Medium
- Direct Speech Failure Pattern: High
- Syntactic Generalization: Medium
