# Grokking of Hierarchical Structure in Vanilla Transformers

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2305.18741
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.18741
- Reference count: 11
- Key finding: Transformers exhibit "structural grokking" - gradually learning to generalize hierarchically long after in-domain accuracy saturates

## Executive Summary
This paper investigates whether vanilla transformers can learn hierarchical structure in language processing tasks. The key finding is that transformers exhibit "structural grokking" - they gradually learn to generalize hierarchically, but this occurs extremely slowly, long after in-domain accuracy saturates. This phenomenon shows an inverted U-shaped scaling with model depth: intermediate-depth models generalize best, while very shallow and very deep models struggle. The optimal model depth correlates with functional tree-structuredness, which measures how well the transformer's computation approximates a tree-structured encoding. In contrast, weight norms and attention sparsity do not predict grokking success. Overall, the results suggest that with sufficient training, transformers can discover and utilize hierarchical structure, contrary to some prior claims.

## Method Summary
The authors conduct synthetic tree transduction experiments using recursive synthetic tree transduction tasks. They systematically vary transformer depth while keeping width constant to study how depth affects generalization. The experiments track the relationship between in-domain accuracy (training accuracy) and out-of-domain accuracy (test accuracy) over time, revealing the phenomenon of "structural grokking" where generalization to hierarchical structure occurs extremely slowly. They introduce a metric called functional tree-structuredness to measure how well the transformer's computation approximates a tree-structured encoding, and correlate this with generalization performance across different model depths.

## Key Results
- Transformers exhibit "structural grokking" - gradually learning to generalize hierarchically long after in-domain accuracy saturates
- Intermediate-depth models generalize best, showing an inverted U-shaped scaling with model depth
- Functional tree-structuredness correlates with generalization success, while weight norms and attention sparsity do not predict grokking

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The mechanism behind structural grokking appears to involve transformers gradually discovering and utilizing hierarchical structure in the data through extended training. The inverted U-shaped scaling suggests that there's an optimal depth where transformers can best capture hierarchical relationships without being too shallow (lacking capacity) or too deep (potentially overfitting or losing structure). The correlation between functional tree-structuredness and generalization indicates that successful grokking involves the model developing a tree-like computational structure that aligns with the hierarchical nature of the task.

## Foundational Learning
- Structural grokking: The phenomenon where transformers learn hierarchical generalization extremely slowly after in-domain accuracy saturates. This is needed to understand how transformers develop compositional reasoning capabilities.
- Functional tree-structuredness: A metric measuring how well transformer computations approximate tree-structured encoding. This is needed to quantify the relationship between model architecture and hierarchical structure learning.
- Inverted U-shaped scaling: The observation that intermediate-depth models generalize best while very shallow and very deep models struggle. This is needed to understand the optimal architectural configurations for hierarchical learning.

## Limitations
- Results are based on synthetic tree transduction tasks, raising questions about generalization to natural language processing
- The extremely long training times required (up to 10^6 steps) may not reflect realistic training regimes
- The metric of functional tree-structuredness relies on assumptions about optimal parsing that may not fully capture how transformers represent structure

## Confidence
- High: The finding that intermediate-depth models generalize best within the experimental scope
- High: The assertion that weight norms and attention sparsity don't predict grokking success
- Medium: The claim that transformers can discover hierarchical structure with sufficient training, as this requires validation on natural language tasks

## Next Checks

3. Test whether functional tree-structuredness correlates with downstream performance on linguistic tasks, or if it's merely a proxy for some other computational property.
