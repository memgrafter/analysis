# Ranking & Reweighting Improves Group Distributional Robustness

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2305.05759
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.05759
- Reference count: 40
- Primary result: Ranking-based methods improve worst-group accuracy on out-of-distribution test data

## Executive Summary
This paper addresses the challenge of group distributional robustness in machine learning, where models often perform poorly on underrepresented groups due to spurious correlations. The authors propose a ranking-based approach inspired by information retrieval techniques to improve both model selection and training. They introduce the Discounted Cumulative Gain (DCG) metric for model selection, which considers multiple poorly-performing groups rather than just the worst one, leading to better hyperparameter choices. Building on this, they develop a novel training method called Discounted Rank Upweighting (DRU) that iteratively upweights groups based on their accuracy ranking during training. Experiments on synthetic and real-world datasets show that both DCG-based model selection and DRU-based training significantly outperform existing methods in worst-group accuracy on out-of-distribution test data, without sacrificing average performance.

## Method Summary
The paper proposes two main techniques: (1) DCG-based model selection using a soft-minimax approach that weights multiple poorly-performing groups, and (2) DRU-based training that iteratively upweights groups based on their accuracy rankings during training. The methods are implemented using DistilBERT as the base learner on grouped text datasets, with experiments comparing against baselines like ERM, Group DRO, and JTT. The approach leverages concepts from information retrieval and learning-to-rank literature to address group distributional robustness.

## Key Results
- DCG-based model selection outperforms worst-group selection by considering multiple poorly-performing groups simultaneously
- DRU improves worst-group accuracy by iteratively upweighting poorly-performing groups during training
- The soft-minimax approach generalizes better than hard minimax by smoothing the optimization landscape

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: DCG-based model selection outperforms worst-group selection by considering multiple poorly-performing groups rather than a single worst group
- Mechanism: The DCG metric assigns decreasing weights to groups based on their accuracy ranking, allowing the model selection process to capture performance across multiple underrepresented groups simultaneously
- Core assumption: The validation set contains groups that are representative of the distribution shift present in the test set
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "DCG allows us to consider the validation performance across more than one group while choosing hyperparameters"
  - [section 4.1] "DCG weights multiple poorly-performing groups (instead of considering just the group with the worst performance)"
  - [corpus] Weak - corpus neighbors focus on similar DRO approaches but don't directly validate DCG metric effectiveness
- Break condition: If validation groups are not representative of test distribution shift, or if spurious features vary dramatically across groups in ways not captured by ranking

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: DRU improves worst-group accuracy by iteratively upweighting poorly-performing groups during training
- Mechanism: At each epoch, groups are ranked by accuracy, and samples from lower-ranked groups receive higher weights based on a logarithmic discount function
- Core assumption: Groups with poor accuracy in one epoch will remain poorly-performing and benefit from increased attention in subsequent epochs
- Evidence anchors:
  - [section 4.2] "DRU iteratively ranks the groups by their accuracy and then upweights poorly performing groups"
  - [section 6.2] "qDRU+M provides the highest worst group accuracy (target metric) on the OOD test dataset on average"
  - [corpus] Weak - corpus neighbors focus on DRO and reweighting but don't specifically validate the ranking-based approach
- Break condition: If accuracy rankings change dramatically between epochs, or if upweighting creates instability in model training

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: The soft-minimax approach generalizes better than hard minimax by smoothing the optimization landscape
- Mechanism: Instead of optimizing only for the worst group, the approach considers a distribution of poorly-performing groups, reducing overfitting to specific spurious correlations
- Core assumption: Multiple poorly-performing groups share some underlying vulnerabilities that can be addressed simultaneously
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "our proposed approach can be seen as a soft-minimax strategy, which smooths the predictive signal from multiple poorly-performing groups"
  - [section 4] "we propose a soft-minimax approach that weights the errors from several poorly performing groups"
  - [corpus] Moderate - corpus neighbors discuss DRO approaches but don't explicitly validate the soft vs hard minimax distinction
- Break condition: If groups have completely independent failure modes, or if worst-group optimization is actually optimal for the specific dataset

## Limitations
- The effectiveness of DCG-based model selection depends on validation sets containing representative distribution shifts, which may not hold in practice
- The logarithmic discounting function in DRU introduces hyperparameters that could affect stability
- Empirical validation relies heavily on synthetic datasets with controlled spurious correlations

## Confidence
- **High confidence**: The theoretical motivation for soft-minimax approach and the general framework of ranking-based group selection
- **Medium confidence**: Empirical results showing DCG outperforms worst-group selection and DRU improves worst-group accuracy, though sample sizes are modest
- **Medium confidence**: The claim that ranking-based methods provide better generalization without sacrificing average accuracy, though ablation studies are limited
