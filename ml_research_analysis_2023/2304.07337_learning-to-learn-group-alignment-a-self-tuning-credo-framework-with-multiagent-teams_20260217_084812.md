# Learning to Learn Group Alignment: A Self-Tuning Credo Framework with Multiagent Teams

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2304.07337
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2304.07337
- Reference count: 28
- Agents dynamically adjust reward function weightings to discover more effective team alignments during training.

## Executive Summary
This paper introduces a self-tuning framework for multi-agent learning systems that enables agents to dynamically adjust their team alignment and reward-sharing structures during training. Building on the "credo" model, where agents optimize for individual, team, and system-level goals, the framework allows agents to update their credo parameters online rather than fixing them beforehand. Using a two-level policy architecture, the approach demonstrates that agents initialized with system-focused objectives can self-tune to discover more effective team alignments, achieving up to 21% higher mean population reward compared to static configurations while maintaining reward equality.

## Method Summary
The framework extends the credo model by introducing online credo-tuning capabilities. Agents employ a two-level policy architecture: a low-level behavioral policy that acts in the environment, and a high-level credo-tuning policy that adjusts the agent's reward function weighting parameters every fixed number of episodes. This allows agents to adapt their optimization targets dynamically during training. The credo parameters control the balance between individual rewards, team rewards, and system-wide rewards, and are updated through reinforcement learning to optimize long-term performance. The framework was evaluated in a Cleanup Gridworld Game where agents needed to coordinate their actions to achieve optimal division of labor.

## Key Results
- Credo-tuning agents achieved up to 21% higher mean population reward compared to static system-focused agents
- Agents maintained high reward equality while converging to the same division-of-labor joint policy as fully team-focused agents
- Self-tuning enabled discovery of more effective team alignments starting from system-focused initialization

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The framework works by enabling agents to dynamically adjust their optimization targets based on observed performance. The two-level policy architecture separates the immediate action selection (low-level behavioral policy) from the strategic adjustment of reward weightings (high-level credo-tuning policy). This allows agents to experiment with different reward structures and settle on configurations that balance individual and collective objectives optimally for the given task. The self-tuning capability helps agents escape suboptimal fixed reward configurations and discover team alignments that better suit the environment's coordination requirements.

## Foundational Learning

Multi-agent reinforcement learning: Understanding how multiple agents can learn to cooperate in shared environments while balancing individual and collective objectives. This is needed to frame the problem of team alignment in cooperative settings.

Credo model: A framework where agents optimize for individual, team, and system-level goals through weighted reward functions. This provides the foundation for understanding how different reward structures affect team behavior.

Division of labor: The emergence of specialized roles among team members to improve collective performance. This concept is central to understanding optimal team alignments in multi-agent tasks.

Policy architecture: The design of learning systems that determine agent behavior, including how different levels of decision-making interact. This is crucial for understanding how the two-level approach enables credo-tuning.

Reward shaping: The process of modifying reward functions to guide learning toward desired behaviors. This underlies how credo-tuning adjusts reward weightings to influence team alignment.

## Architecture Onboarding

Component map: Environment -> Low-level behavioral policy (acts) -> High-level credo-tuning policy (adjusts reward weights) -> Credo parameters (updated)

Critical path: Credo parameters -> Low-level policy behavior -> Environment interaction -> Reward signals -> High-level credo-tuning updates

Design tradeoffs: Fixed vs. adaptive reward structures; computational overhead of maintaining two policy levels; stability vs. adaptability in team alignment; potential for overfitting to specific environments.

Failure signatures: Poor convergence if credo-tuning frequency is too high; getting stuck in local optima if tuning is too conservative; instability in team alignment if credo updates are too aggressive; failure to discover optimal alignments if initial credo parameters are poorly chosen.

First experiments: (1) Compare static vs. adaptive credo parameters in simple coordination tasks; (2) Test different credo-tuning frequencies to find optimal update intervals; (3) Evaluate performance across multiple initialization points to assess robustness.

## Limitations
- Results are demonstrated only in the Cleanup Gridworld domain, raising questions about generalizability to more complex, real-world scenarios
- The framework introduces additional hyperparameters (credo-tuning frequency, parameter update rules) whose sensitivity is not thoroughly explored
- Limited evidence that the framework would identify beneficial alignments in tasks without predefined optimal strategies
