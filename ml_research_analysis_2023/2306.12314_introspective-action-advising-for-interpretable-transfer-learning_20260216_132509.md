# Introspective Action Advising for Interpretable Transfer Learning

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2306.12314
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.12314
- Reference count: 40
- Primary result: Introspective Action Advising (IAA) enables interpretable transfer learning by allowing a teacher to selectively advise a student based on value function similarity

## Executive Summary
This work proposes Introspective Action Advising (IAA), an alternative approach to transfer learning between reinforcement learning tasks. Instead of directly transferring policies through fine-tuning, IAA employs a teacher trained in a source task to actively guide a student's exploration in a target task through selective action advising. The key innovation is the introspective mechanism that allows the teacher to determine when advice is beneficial by comparing state-value estimates between tasks, enabling knowledge transfer that is both effective and interpretable.

## Method Summary
IAA involves a pre-trained teacher policy that actively guides a student policy in a target task through selective action advising. The teacher maintains value function estimates for both source and target tasks, using introspection to compare these estimates and determine when advice should be given. Advice is issued with decaying probability over time, and off-policy correction is applied to maintain unbiased learning from mixed-policy trajectories. The approach is evaluated on Gridworld and Atari environments, showing improved convergence rates compared to baseline transfer methods.

## Key Results
- IAA leads to improved convergence rates in Gridworld and Atari environments
- The introspective mechanism enables interpretable transfer by revealing what knowledge is transferred
- Performance is robust across different source-target task pairs with varying similarity
- IAA outperforms fine-tuning, standard action advising, and policy distillation baselines

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: The introspective teacher identifies transferable actions by comparing state-value estimates between source and target tasks.
- Mechanism: The teacher maintains two value function estimates: V_Src for the source task and V_new for the target task. At each state, it computes the difference between these estimates. If the difference is below threshold ε, the action is deemed transferable and advice is issued.
- Core assumption: Similar expected returns across tasks indicate transferable knowledge.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract]: "Through introspection, the teacher is capable of identifying when advice is beneficial to the student and should be given, and when it is not."
  - [section 4]: "Introspection: We address this issue by having the teacher itself directly estimate the state-value function in the target task, with the assumption that refining the teacher's existing estimate of the state-values in the source task will lead to quicker convergence to V*πT than estimating it from scratch."
- Break condition: If the source and target tasks have fundamentally different reward structures or dynamics, the value function estimates will diverge significantly, leading to false negatives where beneficial advice is suppressed.

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: The decay parameter λ controls when advice is given, balancing transfer benefits with exploration needs.
- Mechanism: Advice is issued with probability λ^t, where t is the current timestep. This creates a temporal annealing schedule where advice is more likely early in training and less likely as the student gains experience.
- Core assumption: Early in training, the student's policy is worse than random, so teacher advice is beneficial. Later, the student needs to explore independently.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [section 4]: "Correspondingly, our approach involves accurately estimating V_Tar^πT in the target task. However, we assume advice is only beneficial in the early stages of training when t < τ..."
  - [algorithm 2]: "p ~ Bern(λmax(0,t-δ))"
- Break condition: If λ is set too high, advice continues too long, preventing the student from developing independent capabilities. If too low, beneficial early advice is suppressed.

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: Off-policy correction enables learning from mixed-policy trajectories.
- Mechanism: When actions come from the teacher's policy, importance sampling ratios are applied to adjust the loss for both teacher and student updates, maintaining unbiased gradient estimates.
- Core assumption: The policy distribution shift between teacher and student can be corrected through importance sampling.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [section 4]: "A consequence of introspective action advising is that the samples collected for training updates are no longer generated purely by rolling out the student's policy πS. Actions produced by the student's policy are interspersed with advised actions from the teacher's policy, meaning that the resulting trajectory samples will violate assumptions for on-policy algorithms and as such, need an off-policy correction applied to their losses."
- Break condition: If the teacher's policy differs dramatically from the student's, importance sampling ratios can become unstable with high variance, leading to poor gradient estimates.

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map: Teacher policy → Introspective module → Advice decision → Student action → Environment → Returns → Teacher critic update → Student policy update

- Critical path: Teacher policy → Introspective module → Advice decision → Student action → Environment → Returns → Teacher critic update → Student policy update. The introspective decision happens at each timestep based on current state and value estimates.

- Design tradeoffs: Fine-tuning transfers all knowledge but may include harmful information, while IAA selectively transfers but requires additional computation for introspection. The threshold ε controls selectivity vs. transfer volume.

- Failure signatures: High variance in student returns after burn-in period may indicate poor threshold selection. Consistently negative transfer across tasks suggests the introspection mechanism isn't filtering harmful advice effectively.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

- Question: How does the introspection threshold (epsilon) affect the performance of Introspective Action Advising (IAA) across different tasks and environments?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper discusses the effect of the introspection threshold on IAA's performance, showing that overly high thresholds can cause large performance drops for certain tasks, and that there is a negative correlation between performance and distribution shift between source and target observation distributions.
- Why unresolved: The paper does not provide a comprehensive analysis of how the introspection threshold should be tuned for different tasks and environments, and how it affects the amount of advice issued.
- What evidence would resolve it: A detailed study of the introspection threshold's effect on IAA's performance across a wide range of tasks and environments, with varying levels of similarity between source and target tasks.

### Open Question 2

- Why unresolved: The paper does not explore the possibility of extending IAA to handle tasks with different state and action spaces, which would be a significant limitation in practical applications.
- What evidence would resolve it: An experimental study demonstrating the effectiveness of IAA in transferring knowledge between tasks with different state and action spaces, and a theoretical analysis of the challenges and potential solutions for such scenarios.

## Limitations
- The introspection threshold ε is not fully specified, leaving ambiguity about how to tune it for different task pairs
- The approach assumes source and target tasks have the same state and action spaces, limiting practical applicability
- Limited ablation studies on the importance of individual components (introspection, off-policy correction, temporal decay)
