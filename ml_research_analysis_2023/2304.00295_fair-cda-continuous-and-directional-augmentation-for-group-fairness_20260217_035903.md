# Fair-CDA: Continuous and Directional Augmentation for Group Fairness

- arXiv ID: 2304.00295

## Executive Summary
This paper addresses the challenge of achieving group fairness in machine learning models when dealing with sensitive attributes. The proposed Fair-CDA method employs feature disentanglement to extract features related to sensitive attributes and applies continuous, directional augmentation to these features. By adjusting perturbation strength along transition paths between groups, Fair-CDA achieves controllable and auditable fairness improvements. The method introduces a calibrated model to impute labels for augmented data, mitigating accuracy degradation. Experiments demonstrate significant performance improvements over state-of-the-art methods across multiple datasets including Adult, CelebA, and MovieLens, with additional validation in an online recommendation system.

## Method Summary
Fair-CDA tackles group fairness through a novel combination of feature disentanglement and continuous directional augmentation. The approach first disentangles features into sensitive-attribute-related and other components, then applies augmentation along carefully calculated transition paths between groups. A calibrated imputation model generates labels for augmented data to maintain accuracy. The method's key innovation lies in its ability to control fairness through continuous adjustment of perturbation strength, providing both controllability and auditability. This framework allows for systematic exploration of fairness-accuracy tradeoffs while maintaining model performance.

## Key Results
- Achieves 86.3% relative improvement in fairness on Adult dataset while maintaining accuracy
- Outperforms state-of-the-art methods across Adult, CelebA, and MovieLens datasets
- Validated effectiveness in online recommendation system deployment

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
Fair-CDA's effectiveness stems from its systematic approach to feature manipulation. By disentangling sensitive attributes from other features, the method can isolate the components that contribute to fairness disparities. The directional augmentation then modifies these sensitive features along calculated transition paths, effectively simulating data points that would reduce group-based performance differences. The calibrated imputation model ensures that augmented samples receive appropriate labels, preventing accuracy degradation that often accompanies fairness interventions. This three-component approach allows for precise control over the fairness-accuracy tradeoff while maintaining model performance.

**Directional Augmentation**: Applying perturbations along specific transition paths allows controlled movement between groups. Quick check: Confirm that augmentation directions align with theoretical transition paths.

**Calibrated Imputation**: Generating appropriate labels for augmented data prevents accuracy degradation. Quick check: Validate that imputed labels match expected distributions for augmented samples.

## Architecture Onboarding

**Component Map**: Data -> Feature Disentanglement -> Directional Augmentation -> Calibrated Imputation -> Augmented Dataset -> Model Training

**Critical Path**: The feature disentanglement step is most critical as it directly affects the quality of subsequent augmentation. If this step fails to properly isolate sensitive features, the entire fairness intervention becomes ineffective.

**Design Tradeoffs**: The method trades computational complexity (additional disentanglement and imputation steps) for improved fairness control. This represents a reasonable tradeoff when fairness is a primary concern.

**Failure Signatures**: Poor disentanglement leads to ineffective augmentation, while incorrect transition path calculation results in suboptimal fairness improvements. Calibration errors in imputation can cause accuracy degradation.

## Limitations
- Assumes sensitive attributes can be meaningfully disentangled from other features, which may not hold in all domains
- Effectiveness depends on accurate estimation of transition path directions
