# Generalist: Decoupling Natural and Robust Generalization

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2303.13813
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.13813
- Reference count: 40
- Key outcome: Achieves 91.03% clean accuracy and 52.91% robust accuracy on CIFAR-10 with WRN-32-10

## Executive Summary
This paper introduces Generalist, a novel framework that decouples natural and robust generalization in adversarial learning. The approach trains two specialized base learners—one for natural examples and one for adversarial examples—and aggregates their parameters to form a global learner. The framework addresses the trade-off between clean accuracy and adversarial robustness by allowing each learner to focus on its respective task while periodically leveraging knowledge transfer through re-initialization from the global learner. Theoretical analysis guarantees that the global learner's risk decreases as base learners are well trained, and extensive experiments demonstrate state-of-the-art performance on benchmark datasets.

## Method Summary
The Generalist framework operates by training two base learners in parallel: one specialized for natural examples and another for adversarial examples. These learners are periodically re-initialized from a global learner that aggregates their parameters. This decoupling allows each base learner to focus on its specific task without compromising the other's performance. The theoretical analysis proves that the global learner's risk is guaranteed to decrease as the base learners are well trained. The framework is evaluated on CIFAR-10 and CIFAR-100 datasets, demonstrating significant improvements in both clean accuracy and robustness compared to existing methods.

## Key Results
- Achieves 91.03% clean accuracy and 52.91% robust accuracy on CIFAR-10 with WRN-32-10
- Significantly improves upon existing methods in the trade-off between clean accuracy and adversarial robustness
- Demonstrates state-of-the-art performance across various benchmark datasets

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The Generalist framework works by decoupling the training of natural and robust generalization into separate base learners. By allowing each learner to specialize in its respective task, the framework avoids the typical trade-off between clean accuracy and adversarial robustness. The periodic re-initialization from the global learner enables knowledge transfer between the base learners, ensuring that both aspects of generalization benefit from each other's progress. The theoretical analysis provides guarantees that the global learner's risk decreases as the base learners are well trained, ensuring convergence to a solution that balances both natural and robust generalization.

## Architecture Onboarding

### Component Map
Global Learner -> Base Learner 1 (Natural) and Base Learner 2 (Robust) -> Re-initialization -> Global Learner

### Critical Path
1. Initialize global learner
2. Initialize base learners from global learner
3. Train base learners on their respective tasks (natural or adversarial)
4. Aggregate base learners' parameters to update global learner
5. Periodically re-initialize base learners from updated global learner
6. Repeat steps 3-5 until convergence

### Design Tradeoffs
- Balancing the frequency of re-initialization to ensure effective knowledge transfer without destabilizing training
- Choosing the appropriate architecture for base learners to ensure they can specialize effectively
- Determining the optimal aggregation method for combining base learners' parameters

### Failure Signatures
- Base learners fail to specialize, leading to poor performance on their respective tasks
- Global learner's risk does not decrease, indicating issues with the aggregation or re-initialization process
- Training instability due to inappropriate re-initialization frequency

### First Experiments
1. Train base learners separately on natural and adversarial examples to verify their specialization
2. Evaluate the impact of different re-initialization frequencies on the overall performance
3. Test the aggregation method by combining pre-trained base learners and assessing the global learner's performance

## Limitations
- Scalability to larger and more complex datasets remains untested
- Optimal re-initialization frequency for base learners is unclear
- Long-term stability and convergence over extended training periods are not investigated

## Confidence
- High confidence in the theoretical analysis and framework's ability to decouple natural and robust generalization
- Medium confidence in generalizability to other datasets and model architectures
- Low confidence in long-term stability and convergence of the method
