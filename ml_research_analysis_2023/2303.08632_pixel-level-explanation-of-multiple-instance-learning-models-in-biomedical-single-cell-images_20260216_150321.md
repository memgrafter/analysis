# Pixel-Level Explanation of Multiple Instance Learning Models in Biomedical Single Cell Images

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2303.08632
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.08632
- Reference count: 33
- Key outcome: MILPLE provides pixel-level explainability for MIL models in AML diagnosis, showing varying performance across attribution methods and datasets

## Executive Summary
This paper introduces MILPLE, a multiple instance learning framework that extends pixel-level explainability to biomedical single cell image classification. The method adapts four attribution techniques—GradCAM, LRP, IBA, and InputIBA—to MIL architectures for acute myeloid leukemia diagnosis. Through evaluation on two large-scale AML datasets, the authors demonstrate how different attribution methods highlight distinct cellular features, though no single method consistently outperforms others across datasets.

## Method Summary
MILPLE combines a ResNeXt backbone with attention-based MIL pooling and four attribution methods adapted for the MIL context. The IBA method is applied at a third convolutional layer where Gaussian distribution assumptions hold, while LRP uses ZBox, gamma, and epsilon rules for different network components. The framework is trained on single white blood cell images extracted from blood smears, with model performance evaluated using standard metrics and explanation quality assessed through insertion/deletion and ROAR experiments.

## Key Results
- GradCAM achieves highest performance on DeepAPL dataset, while LRP and IBA excel on the larger AML dataset
- Attribution methods highlight different cellular features—IBA focuses on cytoplasm while LRP emphasizes nucleus regions
- ROAR experiments show varying effectiveness across methods, with LRP showing poor performance despite good visual explanations
- MILPLE maintains strong classification accuracy while providing interpretable pixel-level explanations

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: MILPLE adapts pixel-level attribution methods to MIL by addressing the additional bag-instance dimension through architectural modifications.
- Mechanism: The MIL structure introduces an extra dimension (multiple instances per bag) that standard attribution methods cannot directly handle. MILPLE overcomes this by applying IBA at the third convolutional layer of the ResNet backbone, where the Gaussian distribution approximation of activation values is most valid, and using adversarial optimization to match distributions between the induced and optimal bottlenecks.
- Core assumption: The optimal bottleneck location for IBA in MIL is at a deeper convolutional layer where feature distributions approximate Gaussian distributions.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "MILPLE leverages four different attribution methods to provide explanations for instances: GradCAM, Layer-Wise Relevance Propagation (LRP), Information Bottleneck Attribution (IBA), and InputIBA."
  - [section] "We had to overcome an obstacle of additional dimension introduced by the bag instances compared to conventional neural networks to apply InputIBA to the MIL structure."
  - [corpus] Weak evidence - no corpus papers directly address MIL-specific attribution challenges.
- Break condition: If the Gaussian distribution approximation fails at the chosen bottleneck layer, the IBA method's theoretical foundation breaks down.

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: LRP with different rule combinations (ZBox, gamma, epsilon) provides effective pixel-level explanations in MIL architectures.
- Mechanism: LRP redistributes relevance backward through the network layers using specific rules. In MILPLE, ZBox rule is applied to the input pixel space, gamma rule to the feature extractor, and epsilon rule to the attention mechanism and final classifier. This combination accounts for the unique MIL architecture structure.
- Core assumption: The combination of LRP rules can be effectively mapped to different architectural components in MIL.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "We investigate the use of four attribution methods to explain a multiple instance learning models: GradCAM, Layer-Wise Relevance Propagation (LRP), Information Bottleneck Attribution (IBA), and InputIBA."
  - [section] "We tested different combinations of rules. Based on the results and suggestions introduced by Montavon et al. [18], we decided to apply ZBox rule on the first layer for every instance, gamma rule for the feature extractor femb and epsilon rule on the attention mechanism and final classifier."
  - [corpus] Weak evidence - no corpus papers provide MIL-specific LRP rule combinations.
- Break condition: If the rule combinations fail to properly distribute relevance through the MIL architecture, the explanations become unreliable.

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: Quantitative evaluation methods (Insertion/Deletion and ROAR) provide objective assessment of attribution quality in MIL.
- Mechanism: These methods perturb features identified as important and measure the impact on model output. Insertion gradually adds pixels while deletion removes them, with AUC calculated for each. ROAR retrains the model after removing important features and measures accuracy degradation.
- Core assumption: The quality of feature attributions can be measured by how much they affect model performance when perturbed.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "We observe how each attribution method performs on the multiple instance learning architecture focusing on different properties of the white blood single cells."
  - [section] "In order to evaluate the quality of different methods, we performed Insertion/Deletion and ROAR experiments on each of the GradCAM, LRP, IBA, and InputIBA methods as shown in Fig. 5."
  - [corpus] Weak evidence - no corpus papers discuss quantitative evaluation of MIL attributions.
- Break condition: If the perturbations don't accurately reflect the true importance of features, the quantitative metrics become misleading.

## Foundational Learning

- Concept: Multiple Instance Learning (MIL)
  - Why needed here: MIL is the core architecture being explained, where bags of instances are classified without instance-level labels.
  - Quick check question: How does attention pooling in MIL provide instance-level explainability while MILPLE extends this to pixel-level?

- Concept: Information Bottleneck Principle
  - Why needed here: IBA and InputIBA rely on information bottleneck theory to restrict information flow and identify predictive features.
  - Quick check question: What is the trade-off controlled by β in the information bottleneck optimization?

- Concept: Attribution Method Diversity
  - Why needed here: Different attribution methods (GradCAM, LRP, IBA, InputIBA) capture different aspects of model decision-making, providing complementary explanations.
  - Quick check question: Why might IBA focus on cytoplasm while LRP focuses on the nucleus in the same cell image?

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map: Input → Backbone (ResNeXt) → MIL Pipeline (embedding, attention, classifier) → Classification → Attribution (GradCAM, LRP, IBA, InputIBA) → Evaluation
- Critical path: Input → Backbone → MIL Pipeline → Classification → Attribution → Evaluation
- Design tradeoffs:
  - Attribution method selection: Each method has different strengths (GradCAM for localization, LRP for relevance distribution, IBA for information-theoretic focus, InputIBA for input-space attribution)
  - IBA bottleneck placement: Must balance between layer depth and Gaussian distribution validity
  - Quantitative metrics: Insertion/Deletion vs ROAR provide complementary but sometimes conflicting assessments
- Failure signatures:
  - GradCAM focusing on background rather than cells
  - LRP showing uniform relevance distribution (indicates rule misconfiguration)
  - IBA/InputIBA producing scattered or non-focused attributions
  - Quantitative metrics showing poor performance across all methods
- First 3 experiments:
  1. Apply all four attribution methods to a simple MIL model on synthetic data to verify basic functionality
  2. Test IBA bottleneck placement at different ResNet layers to find optimal location
  3. Run Insertion/Deletion and ROAR on a single cell to understand metric behavior and correlation with visual inspection

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- Question: Which pixel-level explainability method (GradCAM, LRP, IBA, or InputIBA) performs best for identifying morphological features relevant to AML subtype classification across different datasets?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper compares four methods quantitatively using ROAR and insertion/deletion experiments, finding that performance varies by dataset and method.
- Why unresolved: The paper shows that no single method consistently outperforms others across both datasets, with GradCAM excelling on DeepAPL while LRP and IBA perform better on the AML dataset.
- What evidence would resolve it: Systematic testing of these methods across multiple additional biomedical datasets with known morphological features and expert annotations would establish consistent performance patterns.

### Open Question 2
- Question: How can pixel-level explainability methods be systematically evaluated against expert annotations to identify novel morpho-genetic correlations in blood cancer?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The authors note the need for "more work on systematically comparing and quantifying clinical expert annotations with explainability predictions."
- Why unresolved: The current study only compares explanations with expert annotations for a small subset of cells, and lacks a rigorous framework for quantifying agreement or identifying novel correlations.
- What evidence would resolve it: Development of standardized metrics for comparing explainability maps with expert annotations across large datasets, combined with validation of discovered correlations through independent studies.

### Open Question 3
- Question: What architectural modifications to MIL models would improve the effectiveness of pixel-level explainability methods?
- Basis in paper: [inferred] The authors mention that MIL architectures present challenges for applying standard explainability methods due to complex gradient flows and the bag structure, requiring adaptations like applying IBA on earlier layers.
- Why unresolved: The paper applies existing explainability methods to MIL with some adaptations, but doesn't explore fundamental architectural changes that might better support pixel-level explanations.
- What evidence would resolve it: Comparative studies of MIL architectures designed with explainability in mind versus standard MIL architectures, measuring both classification performance and explanation quality.

### Open Question 4
- Question: How can the ROAR metric be improved to better evaluate pixel-level explanations in MIL models, given its limitations with methods like LRP?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The authors observe that "the ROAR results for LRP are not very informative" and suggest "a problem with the ROAR metric."
- Why unresolved: ROAR removes important pixels and measures accuracy degradation, but this approach may not capture all aspects of explanation quality, particularly for methods that distribute relevance differently.
- What evidence would resolve it: Development and validation of alternative metrics that better capture the quality of pixel-level explanations in MIL contexts, potentially incorporating measures of explanation consistency, interpretability, and alignment with known biological features.

## Limitations
- IBA and InputIBA adaptations to MIL lack complete methodological details, particularly regarding adversarial optimization scheme
- The LRP rule combinations may not generalize well to other MIL architectures beyond the specific implementation
- Correlation between quantitative metrics and visual inspection of attribution maps is not systematically validated

## Confidence
- **High Confidence**: Classification performance metrics (accuracy, F1 score, ROC AUC) as these are standard evaluations with clear implementation paths.
- **Medium Confidence**: GradCAM and LRP attribution methods as their implementations are well-documented in computer vision literature, though MIL-specific adaptations need verification.
- **Low Confidence**: IBA and InputIBA implementations due to the specialized MIL adaptations and limited methodological details provided.
