# Explainable Data Poison Attacks on Human Emotion Evaluation Systems based on EEG Signals

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2301.06923
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2301.06923
- **Reference count:** 40
- **Key outcome:** Two label-flipping attack scenarios drastically reduce accuracy of 6 ML classifiers (AdaBoost most resilient) on EEG-based emotion recognition, with XAI revealing Pz electrode features most vulnerable

## Executive Summary
This study demonstrates how data poisoning attacks using label-flipping can compromise human emotion evaluation systems based on EEG signals. The authors propose two attack scenarios that inject poisoned training samples with deliberately incorrect labels, causing significant degradation in classification accuracy across six different ML models. Using XAI techniques including SHAP, LIME, and Permutation Importance, the paper analyzes how these attacks affect feature importance and model decision-making, revealing that the Pz electrode features are most critical for emotion classification and most vulnerable to poisoning.

## Method Summary
The paper performs explainable data poisoning attacks on EEG-based emotion recognition systems using label-flipping during training. EEG signals from 5 electrode positions are preprocessed into 25 features across 5 frequency bands, then used to train 6 ML classifiers. Two label-flipping attack scenarios are applied at poisoning rates of 5%, 25%, 50%, and 75%, with XAI techniques analyzing the impact on model performance and feature importance. The attacks systematically replace correct labels with incorrect ones to degrade model accuracy while XAI methods reveal which features and attack patterns cause the most damage.

## Key Results
- AdaBoost achieved highest accuracy (96.77%) under 5% poisoning, while MLP performed worst (43.55%)
- Under 50% poisoning, AdaBoost accuracy dropped to 50.32% while MLP fell to 39.68%
- Pz electrode features (Pz_ALPHA, Pz_LOW_BETA, Pz_THETA) consistently ranked highest in importance across XAI analyses
- Scenario 1 attacks caused more damage than Scenario 2 at equivalent poisoning rates

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- **Claim:** Label-flipping attacks degrade ML model accuracy by injecting poisoned samples with deliberately incorrect labels during training.
- **Mechanism:** The attack replaces correct labels in a subset of training samples with opposite class labels, causing the model to learn wrong decision boundaries. As poisoning rate increases, the classifier misclassifies more test samples, reducing accuracy and F1-score.
- **Core assumption:** Attackers have access to and can modify the training dataset before model training completes.
- **Evidence anchors:** [abstract] "data poison attacks using label-flipping during the training stage" [section III.B] "Label-flipping technique has been offered as the DP attack on the training sets of EEG signal data"

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim:** Different ML models show varying resilience to label-flipping attacks due to their inherent structure and training algorithms.
- **Mechanism:** AdaBoost and ExtraTrees maintain higher accuracy under poisoning because their ensemble structures and robust feature selection mitigate the impact of poisoned samples, while simpler models like MLP degrade faster.
- **Core assumption:** Ensemble methods inherently average out noise from poisoned samples better than single-model approaches.
- **Evidence anchors:** [section IV.B] "Although, the six ML models show different resilience to the DP attacks" [abstract] "AdaBoost and ExtraTree models archived relatively higher accuracy compared to the other ML models"

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim:** XAI techniques (SHAP, LIME, Generated Trees, Permutation Importance) reveal which EEG signal features are most vulnerable to poisoning and how attacks alter feature importance.
- **Mechanism:** By analyzing feature contribution scores before and after poisoning, XAI identifies that Pz electrode features (Pz_ALPHA, Pz_LOW_BETA, Pz_THETA) consistently rank highest in importance, and shows how poisoning shifts these rankings.
- **Core assumption:** XAI methods can accurately quantify feature importance changes caused by poisoned training data.
- **Evidence anchors:** [section III.C] "This paper will use XAI techniques including Shapley Additive Explanation (SHAP), Local Interpretable Model-agnostic Explanations (LIME), and Generated Decision Trees" [section IV.C] "Permutation Importance of Random Forest under DP attack Scenario 1 illustrates that Pz_ALPHA, Pz_LOW_BETA, and Pz_THETA are the most significant features"

## Architecture Onboarding

- **Component map:** Data collection -> Preprocessing -> Train Clean Model -> Inject Poison -> Retrain -> Evaluate -> Explain with XAI
- **Critical path:** Data → Preprocessing → Train Clean Model → Inject Poison → Retrain → Evaluate → Explain with XAI
- **Design tradeoffs:**
  - Poisoning rate vs. detection: Higher rates cause more damage but are more detectable
  - Attack scenario choice: Scenario 1 causes broader damage; scenario 2 is stealthier
  - XAI method selection: SHAP is computationally expensive but theoretically sound; Permutation Importance is faster but model-specific
- **Failure signatures:**
  - Accuracy drops without corresponding changes in feature importance patterns
  - XAI methods fail to converge (e.g., Permutation Importance fails at 75% poisoning)
  - Log loss increases disproportionately compared to accuracy degradation
- **First 3 experiments:**
  1. Train all 6 models on clean data, record baseline accuracy and feature importance
  2. Apply 5% scenario 1 poisoning, retrain models, compare performance degradation
  3. Apply 25% scenario 2 poisoning, retrain models, analyze XAI explanations for feature vulnerability patterns

## Limitations

- The study relies on a single dataset from Emotiv Insight with 17 participants, limiting statistical power and generalization
- The label-flipping scenarios are simplified binary or ternary risk classifications, which may not capture the complexity of real-world emotion assessment
- The XAI analyses are limited to post-hoc interpretation rather than proactive attack detection mechanisms

## Confidence

- **High confidence:** AdaBoost shows superior resilience to poisoning (supported by experimental results)
- **Medium confidence:** Specific feature vulnerability (Pz electrode importance) requires additional validation
- **Low-Medium confidence:** Broader applicability across different EEG datasets and emotion recognition tasks

## Next Checks

1. **Dataset Generalization Test:** Apply the same attack methodology to multiple EEG emotion datasets (DEAP, DREAMER) to verify if attack patterns hold across different recording conditions and participant pools.

2. **Multi-label Complexity Test:** Extend the attack scenarios beyond ternary risk classification to multi-dimensional emotion models (valence-arousal-dominance) to assess attack robustness in more complex labeling schemes.

3. **Real-time Detection Evaluation:** Implement and evaluate runtime detection mechanisms that can identify label-flipping attacks during model training, measuring false positive and false negative rates.
