# Topic-Guided Sampling For Data-Efficient Multi-Domain Stance Detection

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2306.00765
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.00765
- Reference count: 35
- Primary result: Achieves 3.5 F1 point average in-domain and 10.2 F1 points out-of-domain improvements over baselines while using ≤10% of training data

## Executive Summary
This paper introduces TESTED, a method for multi-domain stance detection that addresses data inefficiency through topic-guided diversity sampling and contrastive learning. The approach tackles the challenge of imbalanced and diverse stance detection datasets by selecting representative samples while mitigating label imbalances across domains. TestedED demonstrates substantial performance improvements over state-of-the-art baselines, achieving significant F1 score gains while requiring minimal training data.

## Method Summary
TESTED employs a two-pronged approach to improve multi-domain stance detection efficiency. First, it uses topic-guided diversity sampling to select representative samples across domains while addressing both inter-domain and per-topic class imbalances. This sampling strategy ensures coverage of diverse perspectives and stances within each topic. Second, TESTED incorporates a contrastive learning objective that helps the model differentiate between similar and divergent inputs, improving its ability to segment samples with varying labels. The method was evaluated across 16 stance detection datasets, demonstrating superior performance with minimal training data requirements.

## Key Results
- Achieves 3.5 F1 point average improvement in-domain compared to state-of-the-art baselines
- Delivers 10.2 F1 points out-of-domain improvement while using ≤10% of training data
- Effectively mitigates both inter- and per-topic class imbalances through sampling strategy

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The method's effectiveness stems from addressing two key challenges in stance detection: data efficiency and class imbalance. The topic-guided diversity sampling ensures that the training data represents the full spectrum of stances within each topic, preventing model bias toward dominant classes. The contrastive learning objective forces the model to learn more discriminative features by explicitly teaching it to distinguish between similar and dissimilar inputs. This combination allows the model to learn more efficiently from fewer examples while maintaining robustness across different domains.

## Architecture Onboarding

**Component map:** Data preprocessing -> Topic-guided sampling -> Contrastive learning module -> Stance classification

**Critical path:** The core workflow processes raw text through topic identification, applies diversity sampling to select training instances, and feeds them through a BERT-based model with contrastive loss optimization for stance classification.

**Design tradeoffs:** The approach prioritizes data efficiency and robustness over raw model size/complexity. While contrastive learning adds training overhead, it reduces the overall data requirements significantly.

**Failure signatures:** The model may struggle with extremely rare stance expressions or domains with highly specialized vocabulary not well-represented in the sampled training data.

## Limitations
- Limited to stance detection domain, with unclear generalizability to other NLP tasks
- Evaluation restricted to stance detection datasets, preventing assessment of cross-domain applicability
- Does not compare against recent transformer-based methods that may have emerged since initial experiments

## Confidence
- High confidence in topic-guided diversity sampling effectiveness for stance detection
- Medium confidence in contrastive learning objective's contribution to sample segmentation
- Low confidence in scalability and transferability to non-stance detection domains
