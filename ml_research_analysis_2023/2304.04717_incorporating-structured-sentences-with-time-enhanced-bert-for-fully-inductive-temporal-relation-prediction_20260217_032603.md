# Incorporating Structured Sentences with Time-enhanced BERT for Fully-inductive Temporal Relation Prediction

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID**: 2304.04717
- **Source URL**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2304.04717
- **Reference count**: 40
- **Primary result**: SST-BERT achieves state-of-the-art performance on both transductive and fully-inductive temporal relation prediction benchmarks

## Executive Summary
This paper introduces SST-BERT, a novel model for temporal relation prediction in fully-inductive temporal knowledge graphs (TKGs). The key innovation is incorporating structured sentences consisting of relation paths and historical descriptions into a time-enhanced BERT architecture. By converting symbolic edges into natural language form and using a specialized time masking pre-training task, SST-BERT can capture temporal reasoning patterns and achieve robust performance even when training and test entities are completely disjoint. Experiments demonstrate significant improvements over existing baselines in both transductive and fully-inductive settings.

## Method Summary
SST-BERT converts relation paths and historical descriptions from TKGs into structured natural language sentences, which are then processed by a pre-trained BERT model. The model uses a time masking MLM pre-training task on TKG-specific corpus to enhance temporal sensitivity. During training, structured sentences are generated for target entities by extracting relation paths and treating edges not in paths as historical descriptions. The encoded representations are combined with temporal encoding (t2v) and aggregated with exponential weighting based on temporal proximity. The model is trained using self-adversarial loss and evaluated on temporal relation prediction tasks.

## Key Results
- SST-BERT achieves state-of-the-art performance on both transductive datasets (ICEWS14, ICEWS05-15, YAGO11k, Wikidata12k) and newly generated fully-inductive benchmarks
- Significant improvements over baseline models in fully-inductive setting where training and test entities are completely disjoint
- The time masking pre-training task demonstrates effectiveness in enhancing temporal sensitivity compared to standard random masking

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- **Claim**: SST-BERT captures temporal reasoning patterns by encoding relation paths and historical descriptions into structured sentences that can be processed by a pre-trained language model.
- **Mechanism**: The model constructs natural language sentences from symbolic edges (relation paths and historical facts), allowing BERT to learn semantic rules hidden in the graph structure rather than relying on symbolic logic.
- **Core assumption**: Relation paths and historical descriptions provide sufficient and relevant information for predicting temporal relations without needing external knowledge sources.
- **Evidence anchors**:
  - [abstract]: "Our model can obtain the entity history and implicitly learn rules in the semantic space by encoding structured sentences"
  - [section 3.2]: "we convert relation paths connecting the subject and object into natural language form as a part of the structured sentences"
  - [corpus]: Weak evidence - no direct corpus statistics provided for structured sentences
- **Break condition**: If relation paths are too sparse or noisy, the structured sentences may fail to capture meaningful patterns for temporal reasoning.

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim**: The time masking pre-training task enhances BERT's sensitivity to temporal information in TKGs.
- **Mechanism**: Instead of random masking, the model masks 25% of time tokens in a TKG-specific corpus, forcing BERT to focus on temporal relationships and order of events.
- **Core assumption**: Temporal tokens in the generated corpus are frequent and meaningful enough to improve time-sensitive understanding.
- **Evidence anchors**:
  - [abstract]: "We propose to use a time masking MLM task to pre-train BERT in a corpus rich in temporal tokens specially generated for TKGs"
  - [section 3.3]: "we present a new MLM pre-training task, time masking, which replaces the random masking in the original MLM task with a time tokens targeted masking strategy"
  - [corpus]: No direct evidence of token frequency distribution in corpus
- **Break condition**: If the corpus lacks sufficient temporal diversity or the masking rate is inappropriate, the pre-training may not improve temporal sensitivity.

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim**: SST-BERT achieves robust performance in fully-inductive settings by using internal historical descriptions instead of external knowledge bases.
- **Mechanism**: The model treats edges not in relation paths as historical descriptions, providing emerging entities with relevant background information directly from TKGs.
- **Core assumption**: Historical descriptions inside TKGs are more relevant and less noisy than external entity definitions or attributes.
- **Evidence anchors**:
  - [abstract]: "we treat one single edge around the subject or object which is not included in the generated paths as background description texts"
  - [section 3.2]: "the edges that happened before the target quadruple can serve as directly relevant information and can be converted into ideal historical description texts of entities"
  - [corpus]: No corpus evidence provided for internal vs external knowledge comparison
- **Break condition**: If historical descriptions become irrelevant or introduce noise, the model's inductive reasoning capability may degrade.

## Architecture Onboarding

- **Component map**: Structured sentences → TempBERT encoding → t2v time encoding → score aggregation → loss computation
- **Critical path**: Structured sentences → TempBERT encoding → t2v time encoding → score aggregation → loss computation
- **Design tradeoffs**:
  - Using structured sentences provides semantic understanding but requires careful template design
  - Internal historical descriptions avoid external dependencies but may be less comprehensive
  - Time masking focuses on temporal sensitivity but requires TKG-specific corpus generation
- **Failure signatures**:
  - Poor performance on emerging entities indicates insufficient historical description coverage
  - Degradation on time-sensitive tasks suggests time masking pre-training was ineffective
  - High variance across benchmarks suggests sensitivity to test set composition
- **First 3 experiments**:

## Limitations
- The effectiveness of time masking pre-training lacks direct empirical validation due to limited corpus statistics
- The superiority of internal historical descriptions over external knowledge bases is asserted without comparative studies
- The model's performance may be sensitive to the specific templates used for converting relations into natural language

## Confidence

**High Confidence**: The core architectural design of SST-BERT (structured sentences + pre-trained BERT + temporal encoding) is well-specified and the experimental methodology is sound. The reported improvements over baselines in both transductive and fully-inductive settings are statistically significant.

**Medium Confidence**: The effectiveness of time masking pre-training in enhancing temporal sensitivity is plausible given the masking strategy, but the lack of corpus statistics and ablation studies makes it difficult to definitively attribute performance gains to this component.

**Low Confidence**: The claim that internal historical descriptions are superior to external knowledge bases is asserted without empirical comparison, making this a speculative rather than evidence-based conclusion.

## Next Checks

1. **Time Masking Ablation**: Run SST-BERT with standard random masking MLM pre-training versus time masking pre-training on the same dataset to quantify the specific contribution of temporal sensitivity enhancement.

2. **Internal vs External Knowledge Comparison**: Implement an alternative SST-BERT variant that uses external entity descriptions (from Wikipedia or similar sources) instead of internal historical descriptions, and compare performance on fully-inductive benchmarks.

3. **Template Robustness Test**: Create multiple alternative prompt templates for converting relation paths into natural language and evaluate SST-BERT's performance variance across different template choices to assess sensitivity to template design.
