# Joint action loss for proximal policy optimization

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2301.10919
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2301.10919
- Reference count: 4
- This paper introduces novel loss functions for the Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO) algorithm to improve sample efficiency in compound action environments.

## Executive Summary
This paper addresses a fundamental inefficiency in Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO) when handling compound actions composed of multiple sub-actions. The authors identify that standard PPO's clipping mechanism can inadvertently remove meaningful gradients for sub-actions when the joint action probability falls outside the clipping range. To solve this, they propose two new loss functions: sub-action loss, which calculates the loss for each sub-action separately, and mixed loss, which combines joint and separate probabilities to balance efficiency and quality. Experiments in MuJoCo and Gym-µRTS environments demonstrate significant improvements over standard PPO implementations, with over 50% performance gains in MuJoCo and better sample efficiency in compound action scenarios.

## Method Summary
The paper introduces four variants of PPO loss functions to handle compound actions more effectively. The baseline is the standard compound action loss that treats the entire action as a single probability. The sub-action loss computes the PPO loss independently for each sub-action using their separate probabilities, which improves sample efficiency but can degrade quality. The mixed loss variants (mix ratio and mix loss) combine both approaches by weighting the compound and sub-action losses, allowing a balance between efficiency and quality. The authors implement these using an asynchronous PPO architecture with separate sampler and trainer processes. Experiments are conducted on multiple MuJoCo environments and Gym-µRTS with varying map sizes, using standard PPO hyperparameters including γ=0.99, λ=0.95, and clip range of 0.2.

## Key Results
- Sub-action loss improves sample efficiency by over 50% in MuJoCo environments compared to standard PPO
- Mixed loss variants provide better balance between sample efficiency and performance quality than either pure approach
- Large clip ranges (0.5) with mixed losses show superior performance in Gym-µRTS compound action environments
- The proposed methods maintain stability across different compound action configurations and environment complexities

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
Standard PPO's clipping mechanism is designed to prevent destructive policy updates, but it becomes problematic for compound actions. When a joint action probability falls outside the clipping range, all sub-actions are affected equally, even if some sub-actions individually would still be within acceptable bounds. The sub-action loss addresses this by computing advantages and policy ratios for each sub-action independently, ensuring that gradients flow appropriately for all sub-actions regardless of the joint action's clipping status. The mixed loss further refines this by combining the benefits of both approaches - maintaining the stability of compound action loss while capturing the sample efficiency gains of sub-action loss.

## Architecture Onboarding

**Component Map**: Sampler Process -> Data Buffer -> Trainer Process -> Neural Network -> Policy Output -> Environment

**Critical Path**: Sampler collects trajectories using current policy → Trainer computes advantages and losses → Updates network parameters → New policy used for next sampling iteration

**Design Tradeoffs**: Sub-action loss provides better sample efficiency but assumes sub-action independence, which may not hold in all environments. Mixed losses add hyperparameters for balancing efficiency vs quality. Asynchronous architecture improves wall-clock time but adds complexity.

**Failure Signatures**: 
- Performance plateaus or drops: likely missing normalization, gradient clipping, or reward scaling
- Sub-action loss underperforms: independence assumption may be violated in your environment
- Training instability: improper advantage estimation or learning rate issues

## Limitations
- The sub-action independence assumption may not hold in all compound action domains, limiting generalizability
- The Gym-µRTS environment used for experiments is not publicly available, preventing independent verification
- Several implementation details like exact network architecture and normalization schemes are underspecified
