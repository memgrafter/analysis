# Combinatorial Neural Bandits

## Executive Summary
This paper introduces CN-UCB and CN-TS, two neural network-based algorithms for contextual combinatorial bandits that achieve near-optimal regret bounds. The key innovation is using deep neural networks to approximate the unknown score function while maintaining statistical efficiency through optimism-based exploration (UCB) and Thompson sampling. The algorithms achieve regret bounds of Õ(ed√T) or Õ(√(edTK)) for CN-UCB and Õ(ed√(TK)) worst-case regret for CN-TS, where ed is the effective dimension of the neural tangent kernel matrix. To the best of our knowledge, these are the first neural combinatorial bandit algorithms with regret guarantees, and CN-TS is the first Thompson sampling algorithm with worst-case regret guarantees for general contextual combinatorial bandits.

## Method Summary
The paper proposes two algorithms: Combinatorial Neural UCB (CN-UCB) and Combinatorial Neural Thompson Sampling (CN-TS). Both algorithms approximate the unknown score function h(x) using a deep neural network with ReLU activation and leverage the neural tangent kernel (NTK) theory to achieve near-optimal regret bounds. CN-UCB uses optimism-based exploration with an offset term to ensure optimism despite approximation error, while CN-TS uses multiple optimistic sampling to ensure sufficient exploration in the combinatorial action space. Both algorithms require an optimization oracle to select the optimal super arm and handle semi-bandit feedback.

## Key Results
- CN-UCB achieves Õ(ed√T) or Õ(√(edTK)) regret, where ed is the effective dimension of the NTK matrix
- CN-TS achieves Õ(ed√(TK)) worst-case regret, the first TS algorithm with such guarantees for general contextual combinatorial bandits
- Experiments show both algorithms outperform linear baselines (CombLinUCB and CombLinTS) on linear, quadratic, and position-based ranking score functions

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: Neural networks approximate the unknown score function while maintaining statistical efficiency
- Mechanism: The algorithms use deep neural networks with ReLU activation to approximate the score function h(x). By leveraging NTK theory, they achieve near-optimal regret bounds (Õ(ed√T) or Õ(√(edTK))) where ed is the effective dimension of the NTK matrix.
- Core assumption: The score function h is bounded (0 ≤ h(x) ≤ 1) and the NTK matrix is positive definite with appropriate regularization.
- Evidence anchors: [abstract], [section 3.2]
- Break condition: If the NTK matrix is not positive definite or the score function violates boundedness, approximation quality degrades.

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: Optimistic sampling ensures sufficient exploration in combinatorial action space for Thompson Sampling
- Mechanism: CN-TS draws multiple independent samples for each arm and selects the most optimistic score. This ensures constant probability (at least 1/(4e√π)) of choosing an optimistic combinatorial action.
- Core assumption: Multiple sampling can maintain constant optimism probability despite exponential growth of action space.
- Evidence anchors: [section 4.2], [section 4.3]
- Break condition: If too few samples M are used, optimism probability becomes exponentially small in K.

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: Offset terms ensure optimism even with approximation error
- Mechanism: CN-UCB adds arm-independent offset terms to upper confidence bounds to ensure optimism despite neural approximation error.
- Core assumption: Offset term can be computed to guarantee ut,i + et ≥ h(xt,i) with high probability.
- Evidence anchors: [section 3.1], [section 3.2]
- Break condition: If approximation error exceeds confidence width, offset becomes insufficient.

## Limitations

- Theoretical analysis assumes extremely over-parameterized networks (width m polynomial in T)
- Multiple sampling technique for CN-TS increases computational complexity significantly with large K
- Algorithms require access to optimization oracle that may be computationally expensive

## Confidence

- High confidence: Near-optimal regret bounds (Õ(ed√T) for CN-UCB and Õ(ed√(TK)) for CN-TS) are well-supported by theoretical analysis
- Medium confidence: Practical performance improvements over linear baselines are empirically demonstrated but limited to specific score functions
- Low confidence: Computational feasibility of CN-TS with large K due to multiple sampling is a significant practical concern
