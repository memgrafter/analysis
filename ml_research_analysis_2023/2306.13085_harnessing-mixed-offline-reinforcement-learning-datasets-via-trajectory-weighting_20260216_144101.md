# Harnessing Mixed Offline Reinforcement Learning Datasets via Trajectory Weighting

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2306.13085
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.13085
- Reference count: 40
- One-line primary result: Proposed trajectory re-weighting improves offline RL performance in mixed datasets by up to 70% probability of improvement over uniform sampling in high-RPSV settings.

## Executive Summary
This paper addresses the challenge of learning from offline datasets that mix mostly low-return trajectories with a few high-return ones. The proposed solution is to re-weight trajectory sampling during training using Boltzmann distributions based on return or advantage, effectively biasing the implicit behavior policy toward higher-performing trajectories. The method is theoretically justified for deterministic MDPs and empirically validated across three state-of-the-art offline RL algorithms (CQL, IQL, TD3+BC) and behavior cloning on 62 Atari datasets from D4RL. Results show significant improvements in high-RPSV (return per sampled trajectory variance) datasets, with gains up to 70% probability of improvement over uniform sampling, while maintaining performance on regular datasets.

## Method Summary
The core approach involves re-weighting trajectory sampling during offline RL training using Boltzmann distributions based on either trajectory returns or estimated advantages. This re-weighting shifts the implicit behavior policy toward higher-performing trajectories, mitigating the negative impact of low-return data. The method is implemented as a wrapper around existing offline RL algorithms and is theoretically grounded in deterministic MDPs, where it is shown to improve performance bounds. Empirically, it is tested across 62 Atari datasets from D4RL, using three state-of-the-art algorithms (CQL, IQL, TD3+BC) and behavior cloning, demonstrating consistent gains in high-RPSV datasets.

## Key Results
- Return-based and advantage-based re-weighting methods improve performance in high-RPSV datasets, with up to 70% probability of improvement over uniform sampling.
- The proposed methods match or exceed performance of top-x% and half-sampling baselines across all tested algorithms and datasets.
- In regular datasets, the re-weighting methods perform comparably to baselines, demonstrating robustness across different dataset characteristics.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The mechanism relies on shifting the implicit behavior policy toward higher-performing trajectories during training. By re-weighting trajectory sampling using Boltzmann distributions based on return or advantage, the method reduces the influence of low-return data, which can degrade policy learning. This is particularly effective in high-RPSV datasets, where the variance in trajectory returns is high, and a small fraction of high-return trajectories can dominate learning if properly emphasized.

## Foundational Learning
- **Boltzmann distributions**: Used to assign sampling probabilities to trajectories based on their returns or advantages, allowing emphasis on higher-performing data.
- **Implicit behavior policy**: The distribution over trajectories induced by the sampling strategy during offline RL training.
- **RPSV (Return per Sampled Trajectory Variance)**: A metric quantifying the variance in trajectory returns, used to characterize dataset difficulty.
- **Deterministic MDPs**: Theoretical setting where the method is proven to improve performance bounds, though real-world applications may involve stochasticity.
- **Trajectory weighting**: The process of assigning different importance weights to trajectories during training to influence policy learning.
- **State-of-the-art offline RL algorithms**: CQL, IQL, and TD3+BC are used as base algorithms to validate the re-weighting approach.

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map**: Trajectory storage -> Boltzmann weighting -> Offline RL algorithm (CQL/IQL/TD3+BC/BC) -> Policy update
- **Critical path**: Trajectory sampling → Weight computation (return/advantage-based) → Weighted batch formation → Algorithm-specific update
- **Design tradeoffs**: Balancing emphasis on high-return trajectories vs. maintaining exploration; temperature hyperparameter controls weighting strength.
- **Failure signatures**: Over-emphasis on few high-return trajectories may lead to overfitting; under-weighting may fail to improve performance.
- **First experiments**: 1) Test on a high-RPSV dataset with return-based weighting; 2) Compare advantage-based vs. return-based weighting; 3) Sweep temperature hyperparameter to find optimal setting.

## Limitations
- Empirical evaluation is limited to Atari games within D4RL, limiting generalizability to other domains.
- Theoretical analysis is confined to deterministic MDPs, leaving questions about performance in stochastic environments.
- The study does not explore the effects of dataset mixing ratios or contamination levels, which are critical in practical applications.
- Hyperparameter sensitivity, particularly to the temperature parameter, is not thoroughly investigated.

## Confidence
- **High confidence** in the empirical finding that return-based and advantage-based re-weighting consistently improves performance in high-RPSV datasets across all three tested algorithms.
- **Medium confidence** in the theoretical justification for deterministic MDPs, as it does not directly address stochastic environments or generalization beyond the provided assumptions.
- **Medium confidence** in the robustness claim, given the limited scope of domains and lack of systematic ablation on mixing ratios or contamination.

## Next Checks
1. Test the method on continuous control benchmarks (e.g., D4RL locomotion tasks) to assess cross-domain effectiveness.
2. Conduct experiments with varying proportions of high-return trajectories in mixed datasets to understand sensitivity to contamination.
3. Perform a systematic hyperparameter sweep for the temperature parameter in the Boltzmann weighting to determine optimal settings across datasets.
