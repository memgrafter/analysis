# Speeding Up EfficientNet: Selecting Update Blocks of Convolutional Neural Networks using Genetic Algorithm in Transfer Learning

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2303.00261
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.00261
- Reference count: 36
- This paper presents a genetic algorithm-based method for automatically selecting blocks of layers to update during transfer learning, reducing training time while maintaining or improving accuracy.

## Executive Summary
This paper introduces a genetic algorithm approach for selecting which blocks of layers to update during transfer learning with EfficientNet models. The method significantly reduces training time (from hours to minutes) while maintaining or improving accuracy compared to updating all layers. The authors apply their approach to EfficientNetB0 pre-trained on ImageNet using three target datasets: Food-101, CIFAR-100, and MangoLeafBD. Their experiments show that the proposed block selection algorithm outperforms a baseline layer selection method in terms of runtime while achieving competitive accuracy.

## Method Summary
The authors frame transfer learning as an optimization problem where blocks of layers are selected for updating using a genetic algorithm. The algorithm searches for the optimal subset of blocks to update by treating block selection as a binary optimization problem (1 = update block, 0 = freeze block). Fitness is evaluated by training for one epoch on the target dataset and measuring accuracy. The process uses a population of 7 candidates with elite-plus-roulette selection, 1% mutation probability, and runs for 100 epochs. After finding the optimal blocks, the model is fine-tuned on the target dataset. The method also introduces a block importance metric based on Optimal Transport Dataset Distance (OTDD) to analyze which blocks are most effective for transfer learning.

## Key Results
- The genetic algorithm-based block selection reduces training time from hours to minutes while maintaining or improving accuracy
- The approach outperforms the baseline layer selection algorithm in terms of runtime while achieving similar or better accuracy
- Blocks with lower OTDD scores are more effective for transfer learning, indicating that OTDD can predict block transferability
- The method requires fewer parameters to be learned compared to updating all layers, further reducing computational cost

- Core assumption: Feature map similarity correlates with transferability of learned features.
- Evidence anchors:

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map:
  - Genetic Algorithm Engine -> EfficientNet Model -> Training Loop -> Evaluation Metrics
  - OTDD Calculator -> Block Importance Analysis

- Critical path:
  1. Initialize population of block selection candidates.
  2. For each candidate, evaluate fitness by training for one epoch on target dataset.
  3. Select parents based on fitness.
  4. Apply crossover and mutation to generate new population.
  5. Repeat until convergence or maximum generations reached.
  6. Fine-tune the best candidate for full training.

- Design tradeoffs:
  - Population size vs. computational cost: Larger populations provide better exploration but increase evaluation time.
  - Number of generations vs. solution quality: More generations may find better solutions but increase runtime.
  - Block granularity: Treating blocks as atomic units simplifies the problem but may miss optimal combinations of individual layers within blocks.

- Failure signatures:
  - Premature convergence to suboptimal block selections.
  - High variance in fitness evaluations due to single-epoch training.
  - Inefficient search if block interactions are not captured by the genetic algorithm.

- First 3 experiments:
  1. Verify that the genetic algorithm can find a non-trivial block selection that outperforms random selection on a simple dataset.
  2. Compare the performance of the block selection algorithm against the layer selection baseline on CIFAR-100.
  3. Analyze the correlation between OTDD scores and transfer learning performance for individual blocks.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

- **Question:** How does the proposed block selection algorithm scale to even larger EfficientNet models (B1-B7) and other CNN architectures?
  - Basis in paper: [explicit] The authors mention that their method is generic and expect similar results on other pre-trained networks, but only test on EfficientNetB0.
  - Why unresolved: The authors only evaluate their method on EfficientNetB0 and do not test on larger models or other architectures to confirm scalability.
  - What evidence would resolve it: Experimental results applying the block selection algorithm to EfficientNetB1-B7 and other CNN architectures like ResNet or DenseNet, comparing accuracy and runtime to baseline methods.

- **Question:** What is the optimal population size and genetic algorithm parameters (mutation rate, selection method) for different datasets and CNN architectures?
  - Basis in paper: [explicit] The authors set population size to 7, mutation probability to 1%, and use elite-plus-roulette selection, but do not explore the impact of varying these parameters.
  - Why unresolved: The authors use fixed genetic algorithm parameters without investigating how different values affect performance across datasets and architectures.
  - What evidence would resolve it: A systematic study varying population size, mutation rate, and selection method across multiple datasets and architectures to determine optimal parameter settings.

- **Question:** How does the block importance metric (BI) correlate with transfer learning performance across different source-target dataset pairs?
  - Basis in paper: [explicit] The authors calculate BI for each block but do not analyze the correlation between BI values and transfer learning accuracy across different dataset pairs.
  - Why unresolved: While the authors calculate BI, they do not empirically validate whether blocks with lower BI consistently lead to better transfer learning performance across various source-target combinations.
  - What evidence would resolve it: Statistical analysis correlating BI values with transfer learning accuracy across multiple source-target dataset pairs to establish the predictive power of the BI metric.

## Limitations

- The paper lacks comparison against state-of-the-art transfer learning methods, limiting context for the proposed approach's effectiveness.
- The computational overhead of OTDD calculation wasn't quantified relative to the training time savings achieved.
- The small population size (7) may limit the genetic algorithm's exploration capability and lead to suboptimal solutions.

## Confidence

- Genetic algorithm runtime improvements: Medium confidence based on reported speedups and competitive accuracy
- OTDD block importance metric: Low confidence due to limited validation across different dataset pairs
- Scalability to larger models: Low confidence as only EfficientNetB0 was tested
