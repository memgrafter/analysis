# Tighter Lower Bounds for Shuffling SGD: Random Permutations and Beyond

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2303.07160
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.07160
- Reference count: 40
- Key outcome: Establishes tighter lower bounds that close the gap between existing lower and upper bounds for without-replacement SGD methods

## Executive Summary
This paper studies lower bounds for without-replacement SGD methods for smooth (strongly)-convex finite-sum optimization. The key contribution is establishing tighter lower bounds that close the gap between existing lower and upper bounds in terms of the condition number κ, number of components n, and number of epochs K. The authors prove lower bounds for weighted average iterates of SGD with Random Reshuffling and arbitrary permutation-based SGD, showing that these bounds match corresponding upper bounds. The main technical approach involves constructing carefully designed objective functions that demonstrate slow convergence for any permutation-based SGD algorithm.

## Method Summary
The paper defines specific function classes with different assumptions (smoothness, convexity, bounded gradient errors) and constructs worst-case functions for different scenarios (Random Reshuffling vs arbitrary permutations, with/without component convexity). The authors then prove lower bounds that match known upper bounds, verifying tightness by comparing to existing results. The technical approach involves analyzing convergence behavior for different step size regimes and aggregating results to demonstrate the dependence on the condition number κ.

## Key Results
- Lower bounds for SGD with Random Reshuffling match upper bounds up to logarithmic factors
- Dropping component convexity assumptions yields sharper lower bounds with respect to κ
- The dependence on κ in the lower bounds matches the dependence in the upper bounds, closing the existing gap
- For arbitrary permutation-based SGD, a lower bound of Ω(Lν²/µ²n²K²) exactly matches the upper bound of the GraB algorithm

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- Question: Can we establish lower bounds for weighted average iterates of SGD-RR when the step size is larger than 1/(Ln)?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper states "One might wonder why the lower bound becomes a constant for small K ≲ κ. This is because in the η = O(1/Ln) regime, K < c2κ implies η < 1/(µnK), i.e., the step size is too small for SGD to reach the optimum in K steps." It also notes that "The difficulty of extending the η = Ω(1/Ln) regime in Theorem 1 to arbitrary weighted average iterates originates from our proof strategy: for small enough η, we can show for our worst-case examples that all xk0's (in expectation) stay on the positive side bounded away from zero, thereby proving that any weighted average also stays sufficiently far from zero. However, for larger η, the iterates may oscillate between positive and negative regions, making it possible for an average iterate to converge faster than individual xk0's."
- Why unresolved: The authors explicitly identify this as a limitation of their current proof technique and note that "it is still an open problem whether the same rate can also be achieved for the final iterate xK n or not."
- What evidence would resolve it: A proof establishing tight lower bounds for weighted average iterates in the regime η = Ω(1/Ln) would resolve this question. Alternatively, a counterexample showing that faster convergence is possible for larger step sizes would also provide resolution.

### Open Question 2
- Question: Does the GraB algorithm achieve the optimal convergence rate for permutation-based SGD when the number of epochs K is less than the condition number κ?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper notes "We finally note that the power of general permutation-based SGD is not yet well-understood for the regime when the number of epochs is less than the condition number. Safran and Shamir (2021) show that SGD-RR does not enjoy faster convergence than with-replacement SGD in this regime, and it is still unclear whether the same restriction holds for permutation-based SGD as well."
- Why unresolved: The paper's lower bounds in Theorem 9 require K ≥ max{κ²/n, κ^(3/2)n^(1/2)}, which means they don't apply to the regime K < κ. The authors explicitly state this remains an open question.
- What evidence would resolve it: A lower bound proof showing that permutation-based SGD cannot converge faster than with-replacement SGD when K < κ would resolve this. Alternatively, an algorithm that achieves faster convergence than with-replacement SGD in this regime would provide resolution.

### Open Question 3
- Question: Can we find a permutation-based SGD algorithm that achieves dimension-free convergence rates while maintaining the same dependency on other factors (n, K, κ)?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper discusses dimension dependency in existing results, noting that "Both Proposition 8 and Proposition 10 contain a dimension term in their convergence rates" and that "It is an open problem whether there exists a permutation-based SGD algorithm that gives a dimension-free upper bound while maintaining the same dependency on other factors."
- Why unresolved: The paper identifies that current upper bounds for permutation-based SGD (like GraB) have dimension-dependent terms due to the herding bound H, which is at least √d. The authors explicitly call this out as an open problem.
- What evidence would resolve it: A proof of a dimension-free upper bound for some permutation-based SGD algorithm, or a lower bound showing that dimension dependency is unavoidable, would resolve this question.
