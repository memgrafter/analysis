# Are VAEs Bad at Reconstructing Molecular Graphs?

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2305.03041
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.03041
- Authors: 
- Reference count: 19
- Key outcome: State-of-the-art graph-based VAEs achieve surprisingly low reconstruction accuracy (<66%) on diverse molecular datasets, worse than SMILES-based models, with reconstruction errors primarily in motif assembly rather than selection.

## Executive Summary
This work examines reconstruction capabilities of graph-based generative models of molecules, finding surprisingly low accuracy (below 66%) for state-of-the-art VAEs. The authors evaluate JT-VAE, HierVAE, and MoLeR on a large and diverse molecular dataset, showing that reconstruction errors primarily occur during motif assembly rather than motif selection. Failed reconstructions tend to be chemically similar to inputs, sharing motifs and properties like solubility. The study reveals that improving reconstruction accuracy doesn't directly lead to better sampling or optimization performance, suggesting current VAEs may be optimized for different objectives than faithful reconstruction.

## Method Summary
The authors evaluate three graph-based VAE architectures (JT-VAE, HierVAE, and MoLeR) on reconstruction accuracy using the Guacamol dataset. They systematically vary the β coefficient in the VAE loss function to study the trade-off between reconstruction accuracy and latent space organization. The evaluation includes measuring Frechet ChemNet Distance (FCD) for sampling quality, optimization performance using the molecule-set optimization (MSO) method, and detailed error analysis of reconstruction failures. Chemical similarity between original molecules and failed reconstructions is assessed using Tanimoto similarity of Morgan fingerprints and motif overlap.

## Key Results
- Graph-based VAEs achieve reconstruction accuracy below 66%, worse than SMILES-based models (95%) on the same dataset
- Improving reconstruction accuracy doesn't directly improve sampling quality or optimization performance
- Failed reconstructions are chemically similar to inputs, with Tanimoto similarity of 35-48% compared to ~0.25% for random pairs
- More than 90% of reconstruction errors occur during motif assembly (attachment points, bond types) rather than motif selection

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- **Claim**: The low reconstruction accuracy is not primarily due to posterior collapse.
- **Mechanism**: The authors test distinguishability of latent space encodings between original molecules and their reconstructions. High distinguishability (>97.5%) in most cases indicates that the encoder is producing meaningfully different posteriors for inputs and outputs, contradicting the posterior collapse hypothesis.
- **Core assumption**: If posterior collapse were dominant, the latent distributions of inputs and their reconstructions would be statistically indistinguishable.
- **Evidence anchors**:
  - [abstract] "the input molecule and its failed reconstruction are usually mapped by the different encoders to statistically distinguishable posterior distributions, hinting that posterior collapse may not fully explain why VAEs are bad at reconstructing molecular graphs."
  - [section B.2] "we observe that 55% (89%) are more than 97.5% distinguishable for MoLeR (HierVAEpoly)"
  - [corpus] weak evidence; related papers focus on diffusion models or OOD detection, not posterior collapse analysis.

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim**: The dominant source of reconstruction failure is incorrect motif assembly, not motif selection.
- **Mechanism**: Error analysis on MoLeR reconstructions shows >90% of errors occur during motif assembly steps (attachment points, bond types), while only 5% involve selecting a motif not present in the original molecule. This suggests the model can identify correct motifs but struggles with how to connect them.
- **Core assumption**: The error taxonomy accurately captures the sequence of generation steps and correctly isolates assembly errors from selection errors.
- **Evidence anchors**:
  - [section B.3] "more than 90% of the errors are related to a failure in assembling the motifs, whereas it is rare that the model picks a motif that is not contained in the original molecule."
  - [section] "We conclude that motifs which are not part of the original molecule are typically only added after an erroneous step has already occurred."
  - [corpus] no direct evidence; related works focus on other aspects of VAEs.

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim**: Failed reconstructions still preserve significant chemical similarity to original molecules.
- **Mechanism**: Despite low reconstruction accuracy, Tanimoto similarity between Morgan fingerprints of failed reconstructions and original molecules is significantly higher than random pairs (35-48% vs ~0.25%). This suggests the models are capturing meaningful structural information even when exact reconstruction fails.
- **Core assumption**: Tanimoto similarity on Morgan fingerprints and motif overlap are valid measures of chemical similarity relevant to molecular function.
- **Evidence anchors**:
  - [section] "the failed reconstructions are significantly more similar than random pairs of molecules for both HierVAEpoly and MoLeR... A similarity of 35% might not seem high, however, 99.7% of random pairs are less than 35% similar."
  - [section B.1] "we observe that 12% of the MoLeR reconstructions have exactly the same set of motifs as the original molecule"
  - [corpus] weak evidence; related papers don't discuss similarity preservation in failed reconstructions.

## Architecture Onboarding

- **Component map**: Input molecule → GNN encoder → latent mean/covariance → sampling (or deterministic mean) → sequential decoder → reconstructed graph → SMILES comparison
- **Critical path**: Input molecule → GNN encoder → latent mean/covariance → sampling (or deterministic mean) → sequential decoder → reconstructed graph → SMILES comparison
- **Design tradeoffs**: High β encourages disentangled latent space but reduces reconstruction accuracy; low β improves reconstruction but may collapse latent space. Graph-based generation allows fine-grained constraints but is more complex than string-based approaches.
- **Failure signatures**: Posterior collapse (decoder ignores encoder), motif selection errors, motif assembly errors (wrong attachment points, bond types), and failure to generate chemically valid structures.
- **First 3 experiments**:
  1. Train MoLeR on Guacamol with β=0.02 and evaluate reconstruction accuracy on 10k test samples.
  2. Compare Tanimoto similarity between Morgan fingerprints of original molecules and their failed reconstructions.
  3. Analyze error types in MoLeR's stepwise generation process to identify where failures occur most frequently.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- What specific architectural or training modifications could significantly improve molecular graph reconstruction accuracy in VAEs beyond simple loss weighting adjustments?
- How do reconstruction errors in molecular VAEs correlate with specific chemical properties beyond solubility, particularly with drug-likeness and bioactivity predictions?
- What is the relationship between the chemical diversity of the training dataset and reconstruction accuracy in molecular graph VAEs?
- How does the reconstruction accuracy of VAEs compare to non-variational autoencoder approaches for molecular graph generation when controlling for other factors?
- What is the optimal trade-off between reconstruction accuracy and latent space organization for molecular optimization tasks, and how does this vary across different optimization objectives?

## Limitations
- The error analysis methodology relies heavily on manual inspection and subjective categorization of reconstruction failures
- The study focuses primarily on three specific VAE architectures, potentially limiting generalizability to other graph-based generative models
- The chemical similarity analysis uses Morgan fingerprints, which may not fully capture functional similarity between molecules

## Confidence
- **Mechanism 1 (Posterior collapse not dominant)**: Medium confidence - supported by statistical distinguishability tests, but could benefit from additional validation using alternative metrics like mutual information between latent variables and reconstructions.
- **Mechanism 2 (Motif assembly errors dominant)**: Medium confidence - error analysis provides strong evidence, but manual categorization introduces potential bias and may miss systematic patterns.
- **Mechanism 3 (Chemical similarity preservation)**: Medium confidence - statistical evidence is compelling, but the relevance of Tanimoto similarity for functional chemical properties requires further validation.

## Next Checks
1. Develop an automated system to classify reconstruction errors using graph edit distance metrics and structural similarity measures to validate the manual error analysis findings and reduce subjective bias.

3. Test the identified mechanisms (posterior collapse, motif assembly errors, similarity preservation) on additional graph-based generative models such as GraphRNN, MolGAN, or diffusion-based approaches to assess the generality of the findings beyond the three studied VAE architectures.
