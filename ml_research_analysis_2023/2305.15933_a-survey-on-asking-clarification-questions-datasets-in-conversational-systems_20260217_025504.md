# A Survey on Asking Clarification Questions Datasets in Conversational Systems

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2305.15933
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.15933
- Reference count: 40
- Key outcome: Highlights lack of standard benchmarks and inconsistent experimental setups across ACQ studies

## Executive Summary
This paper surveys 12 public datasets for asking clarification questions (ACQs) in conversational systems, identifying critical gaps in standardization and evaluation. The authors introduce a t-SNE-based visualization to cluster semantically similar clarification questions across datasets, aiming to guide dataset selection. They analyze three core tasks—clarification need prediction, question ranking, and user satisfaction—and benchmark representative models, revealing inconsistent performance across datasets. The study emphasizes the urgent need for unified evaluation frameworks, large-scale human-machine interaction data, and multi-modal ACQs datasets to advance the field.

## Method Summary
The survey methodology involves systematic comparison of 12 ACQ datasets across size, domain, collection method, and semantic similarity. A t-SNE visualization is introduced to encode clarification questions into a shared semantic space, enabling visual clustering for dataset selection. Automatic evaluation metrics (MAP, NDCG, Precision, Recall, F1, MRR, MSE) and human evaluation (relevance, usefulness, naturalness, clarification) are applied to benchmark models across three tasks. The study synthesizes findings to highlight the absence of standard benchmarks and inconsistent experimental setups.

## Key Results
- t-SNE visualization clusters similar clarification questions across datasets, aiding semantic comparison
- Lack of standard benchmarks prevents meaningful cross-study model comparison
- Doc2Query query expansion improves performance on short conversational search queries via BM25

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: t-SNE visualization clusters similar clarification questions across datasets, revealing semantic overlap and aiding dataset selection.
- Mechanism: By embedding clarification questions into a shared semantic space, t-SNE reduces dimensionality and visually clusters questions that are semantically similar, enabling comparison across datasets.
- Core assumption: Clarification questions from different datasets can be meaningfully embedded into a shared vector space where semantic similarity is preserved.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "We introduce a visualised semantic encoding strategy to explain dataset suitability when selected for their corresponding experiments."
  - [section] "In Figures 1a and 1b, we use the t-distributed Stochastic Neighbor Embedding (i.e., t-SNE) method to visualize the semantic representation of clarification questions (semantic embeddings) for Conv. Search and Conv. QA datasets."
  - [corpus] Weak: no specific corpus citations provided for t-SNE application.
- Break condition: If clarification questions lack semantic coherence or the embedding model fails to capture task-specific nuances, clusters will be meaningless.

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: Standard benchmarks are missing, making it hard to compare ACQ models across inconsistent datasets and evaluation setups.
- Mechanism: Without a common benchmark, researchers use different datasets, evaluation metrics, and baselines, leading to incomparable results and unclear state-of-the-art.
- Core assumption: Inconsistent experimental setups across studies prevent meaningful performance comparison.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "highlighting a lack of standard benchmarks and comparable experimental setups across studies."
  - [section] "Many newly introduced models apply customised evaluation strategies even while using an identical dataset for addressing a specific asking clarification task."
  - [corpus] Weak: corpus does not provide direct evidence of benchmark absence.
- Break condition: If a widely accepted benchmark is adopted, this mechanism breaks.

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: Clarification question ranking improves when queries are expanded using Doc2Query on short conversational search queries.
- Mechanism: Doc2Query generates query expansions from relevant documents, enriching short queries in conversational search and improving retrieval performance when combined with BM25.
- Core assumption: Short conversational queries benefit from document-based expansion more than longer QA-style queries.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [section] "we observe that the query expansion via Doc2Query can be effective for most of the conversational search datasets, due to their shorter queries."
  - [section] "For the results, we observe that the query expansion via Doc2Query can be effective for most of the conversational search datasets, due to their shorter queries."
  - [corpus] Weak: no corpus citation confirming query length differences.
- Break condition: If queries are already long or expansion introduces noise, performance may degrade.

  - Why needed here: Different ACQ tasks require different metrics; ranking uses MAP, NDCG; generation uses BLEU, ROUGE.

  - Why needed here: Some datasets treat clarification need as binary classification, others as continuous regression.

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map: Data loader → t-SNE visualizer → Model trainer (classification/regression/ranking) → Evaluator (metrics) → Benchmark aggregator
- Critical path: 1. Load dataset → 2. Embed questions → 3. Visualize clusters → 4. Select appropriate task → 5. Train model → 6. Evaluate → 7. Compare results
- Design tradeoffs: Use of large-scale embedding models vs. computational cost; classification vs. regression for clarification need prediction; ranking vs. generation for clarification question selection
- Failure signatures: t-SNE clusters show no clear separation → poor embedding quality; inconsistent model performance across datasets → overfitting or dataset bias; low human evaluation scores despite high automatic metrics → misalignment with user satisfaction
- First 3 experiments: 1. Run t-SNE on a subset of datasets to confirm semantic clustering; 2. Compare BM25 vs. Doc2Query + BM25 on a single conversational search dataset; 3. Train BERT for clarification need classification on ClariQ and evaluate on a held-out test set

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- Question: How can a standardized benchmark dataset be developed for evaluating clarification question techniques across different domains and tasks?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper highlights the lack of a standard benchmark and inconsistent use of datasets across studies, leading to incomparable results and difficulty in identifying the true state-of-the-art.
- Why unresolved: The paper identifies the need for a benchmark but does not provide a concrete methodology for developing one that can accommodate diverse domains and tasks.
- What evidence would resolve it: A proposed methodology for creating a standardized benchmark dataset, along with an evaluation framework that can be applied across different domains and tasks, would resolve this question.

### Open Question 2
- Question: What is the impact of incorporating user-system interactions in the evaluation of clarification question models?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper notes that only a few datasets, such as MIMICS, include user-system interaction data, which is essential for evaluating models in realistic scenarios.
- Why unresolved: The paper highlights the importance of user-system interactions but does not provide empirical evidence on how their inclusion affects model performance and evaluation outcomes.
- What evidence would resolve it: Experimental results comparing model performance and evaluation outcomes with and without user-system interaction data would resolve this question.

### Open Question 3
- Question: How can multi-modal information be effectively incorporated into clarification question datasets and models?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper mentions the lack of multi-modal datasets for clarification questions, despite the growing interest in multi-modal conversational systems.
- Why unresolved: The paper identifies the gap in multi-modal datasets but does not propose a methodology for creating such datasets or integrating multi-modal information into clarification question models.
- What evidence would resolve it: A proposed methodology for creating multi-modal clarification question datasets, along with experimental results demonstrating the effectiveness of incorporating multi-modal information into models, would resolve this question.

## Limitations
- Core claims limited by absence of standardized benchmarks, making cross-study comparisons uncertain
- t-SNE visualization effectiveness depends on quality of semantic embeddings, which may not capture task-specific nuances
- Inconsistent model performance across datasets suggests potential dataset bias or overfitting issues not thoroughly investigated

## Confidence
- High confidence: Observation that standard benchmarks are missing and inconsistent experimental setups exist across ACQ studies
- Medium confidence: Effectiveness of t-SNE visualization for dataset selection, as approach is demonstrated but not validated against ground truth semantic relationships
- Low confidence: Generalizability of Doc2Query's effectiveness for query expansion, as claim is based on limited dataset characteristics without systematic ablation studies
