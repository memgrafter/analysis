# Selective Guidance: Are All the Denoising Steps of Guided Diffusion Important?

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2305.09847
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.09847
- Reference count: 40
- Optimizing last 20% of denoising iterations reduces inference time by 8.2% with minimal quality loss

## Executive Summary
This study proposes optimizing the Stable Diffusion guided inference pipeline by selectively eliminating unconditional noise computation in later denoising iterations. The method reduces computational complexity by 50% in optimized iterations while maintaining visual quality. Experiments show that optimizing the last 20% of iterations reduces inference time by 8.2% with minimal quality loss, while optimizing 50% of iterations achieves 20.3% speedup with still visually acceptable results. The study finds that later iterations are less sensitive to optimization, validating the approach. Users can tune guidance scale after optimization to restore lost details when applying aggressive optimization.

## Method Summary
The method modifies the Stable Diffusion denoising loop to skip unconditional noise computation for selected iterations. Using 50 total iterations, the optimization is applied to the last N% of iterations (0% to 50%). The unconditional noise term is removed from the noise prediction formula, reducing per-iteration computation by 50%. The approach is tested on a Tesla V100 GPU with CUDA 11.6, using 60 different text prompts. Quality is evaluated through visual inspection and timing measurements.

## Key Results
- Optimizing last 20% of iterations reduces inference time by 8.2% with minimal quality loss
- Optimizing 50% of iterations achieves 20.3% speedup with visually acceptable results
- Later denoising iterations are less sensitive to optimization, making them ideal candidates for computational savings

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: Later denoising iterations are less sensitive to optimization, making them ideal candidates for eliminating unconditional noise computation
- Mechanism: As denoising progresses, the image structure becomes increasingly established, reducing the impact of conditional guidance perturbations on final image quality
- Core assumption: The conditional noise dominates the unconditional noise in later iterations when guidance scale > 1
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract]: "later iterations of the SD are less sensitive to optimization"
  - [section]: "we propose that the later iterations are comparatively less sensitive to optimization than the early ones"
  - [corpus]: Weak evidence - corpus papers focus on guidance optimization but don't specifically address iteration sensitivity
- Break condition: If guidance scale approaches 1, the conditional and unconditional noise contributions become comparable, eliminating the optimization benefit

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: Eliminating unconditional noise computation reduces computational complexity by 50% per optimized iteration
- Mechanism: The noise computation in guided diffusion involves calculating both conditional and unconditional terms; removing one term halves the computational load
- Core assumption: Unconditional noise computation is independent of conditional noise and can be safely removed
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract]: "limiting the noise computation to conditional noise and eliminating unconditional noise computation, thereby reducing the complexity of the target iterations by 50%"
  - [section]: "we suggest eliminating the unconditional noise in some denoising iterations to cut the computational complexity of that iteration in half"
  - [corpus]: Weak evidence - corpus papers discuss acceleration but don't specifically analyze unconditional noise elimination
- Break condition: If unconditional noise contributes significantly to image quality in later iterations, its removal would degrade results

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: Guidance scale tuning can restore lost details when aggressive optimization is applied
- Mechanism: Increasing guidance scale amplifies the conditional noise contribution, compensating for the removed unconditional noise
- Core assumption: Guidance scale is an independent parameter that can be adjusted post-optimization
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract]: "Users can tune guidance scale after optimization to restore lost details when applying aggressive optimization"
  - [section]: "by adjusting the GS (from 7.5 to 9.6 in this case), we were able to restore the lost details"
  - [corpus]: No direct evidence in corpus papers
- Break condition: If guidance scale tuning introduces artifacts or cannot fully compensate for missing unconditional noise

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- Question: How does the quality degradation vary across different types of images (e.g., abstract vs. realistic) when applying optimization to 50% of iterations?
- Basis in paper: [inferred] The paper only tests prompts with varied content but doesn't systematically categorize image types or analyze degradation patterns across different visual domains.
- Why unresolved: The study uses a diverse set of prompts but doesn't classify images by type or analyze whether certain visual styles are more sensitive to optimization than others.
- What evidence would resolve it: A systematic study categorizing generated images by visual style (abstract, realistic, architectural, natural scenes, etc.) and measuring quality degradation for each category when applying optimization to different percentages of iterations.

### Open Question 2
- Question: What is the optimal guidance scale tuning strategy for recovering lost details after aggressive optimization, and how does it vary with different optimization levels?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper mentions tuning guidance scale can restore lost details but only provides one demonstration example without systematic analysis.
- Why unresolved: The paper only demonstrates one example of GS tuning (from 7.5 to 9.6) for recovering one specific detail, without exploring the relationship between optimization level and required GS adjustment.
- What evidence would resolve it: Systematic experiments testing various GS values for different optimization levels (20%, 30%, 40%, 50%) across multiple prompts to establish guidelines for optimal GS tuning.

### Open Question 3
- Question: How does the optimization technique affect image generation time for different hardware configurations (e.g., GPUs with different memory capacities or CPUs)?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper only reports timing results for Tesla V100 GPU with CUDA 11.6.
- Why unresolved: The paper doesn't explore how optimization performance scales across different hardware setups or whether the 50% computation reduction holds true across different platforms.
- What evidence would resolve it: Timing experiments on multiple hardware configurations (different GPU models, CPUs) to determine if the computational benefits are consistent across platforms and how memory constraints affect optimization effectiveness.

### Open Question 4
- Question: What is the relationship between denoising iteration count and optimal optimization percentage?
- Basis in paper: [inferred] The paper fixes denoising iterations at 50 but doesn't explore how optimal optimization percentages might change with different iteration counts (e.g., 100 or 200 iterations).
- Why unresolved: The study only tests with 50 iterations and doesn't investigate whether the optimal optimization window shifts when using different iteration counts, which could affect the trade-off between quality and speed.
- What evidence would resolve it: Experiments testing various iteration counts (50, 100, 150, 200) with different optimization percentages to determine if there's a relationship between total iterations and optimal optimization window.

## Limitations
- Results only validated on Tesla V100 GPU architecture, limiting generalization to other hardware
- Mechanism for why later iterations are "less sensitive" lacks quantitative sensitivity analysis
- Does not address potential quality degradation in specific image types or content domains

## Confidence
**High Confidence** - The core computational claim that eliminating unconditional noise computation reduces complexity by 50% per iteration is mathematically sound and directly verifiable through profiling. The claim about maintaining visual quality with 20% optimization also appears well-supported by the experimental setup.

**Medium Confidence** - The finding that later iterations are less sensitive to optimization is supported by results but the underlying mechanism could benefit from more rigorous analysis. The guidance scale tuning compensation approach is demonstrated but not extensively validated across different optimization levels.

**Low Confidence** - The generalization of results to different GPU architectures, diffusion model variants, and real-world deployment scenarios remains largely untested. The paper does not address how the optimization behaves with different guidance scales or in multi-GPU distributed settings.
