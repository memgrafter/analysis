# Enhancing Translation for Indigenous Languages: Experiments with Multilingual Models

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2305.17406
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.17406
- Reference count: 18
- Primary result: mBART50 fine-tuned on Spanish-indigenous pairs outperformed baseline for 3 languages (Bribri, Asháninka, and Quechua) in AmericasNLP 2023 Shared Task

## Executive Summary
This paper presents CIC NLP's submission to the AmericasNLP 2023 Shared Task on machine translation for indigenous languages. The authors experimented with three models - Helsinki NLP Spanish-English, M2M-100, and mBART50 - using different transfer learning setups across 11 low-resource indigenous languages. The best setup fine-tuned mBART50 on Spanish-indigenous pairs, which outperformed the baseline for three languages. The results demonstrate that multilingual pretraining can improve translation quality for low-resource indigenous languages when appropriately fine-tuned.

## Method Summary
The authors fine-tuned three different multilingual models on 11 low-resource indigenous languages using Spanish as the source language. They employed three approaches: direct fine-tuning of Helsinki NLP Spanish-English model on each language pair, direct fine-tuning of mBART50 on each language pair, and a two-step approach where M2M-100 was first fine-tuned on a combined Spanish-All dataset containing all 11 languages before individual language fine-tuning. All models were evaluated using chrF2 metric.

## Key Results
- mBART50 fine-tuned directly on Spanish-indigenous pairs achieved the best average performance across languages
- The model beat the strong baseline in three languages: Bribri, Asháninka, and Quechua
- Direct fine-tuning of mBART50 on Spanish-indigenous pairs was more effective than the Spanish-All intermediate approach
- Multilingual pretraining with mBART50 provided consistent benefits across the 11 languages tested

- Claim: Multilingual pretraining on diverse languages improves low-resource translation performance.

- Core assumption: The multilingual pretraining process captures transferable linguistic knowledge that benefits low-resource languages during fine-tuning.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- Question: How do the results of fine-tuning multilingual models on a Spanish-All dataset compare to fine-tuning on individual Spanish-Indigenous pairs when scaled to more languages?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The authors mention they combined all eleven languages to create a Spanish-All dataset and fine-tuned M2M-100 before fine-tuning for individual languages, but only tested this approach with one multilingual model.
- Why unresolved: The paper only tested this approach with M2M-100 and didn't compare it systematically against individual language fine-tuning across all models.
- What evidence would resolve it: Systematic experiments comparing Spanish-All pre-training followed by individual fine-tuning versus direct individual fine-tuning across all three models (M2M-100, mBART50, and Helsinki NLP) with more languages.

### Open Question 2
- Question: What is the optimal number of pre-training epochs on the Spanish-All dataset before fine-tuning on individual languages?
- Basis in paper: [inferred] The authors used 5 epochs for pre-training on Spanish-All but didn't explore whether this was optimal or if different numbers of epochs would yield better results.
- Why unresolved: The paper fixed the number of pre-training epochs at 5 without exploring how varying this hyperparameter affects final performance.
- What evidence would resolve it: Experiments varying the number of pre-training epochs (e.g., 1, 3, 5, 10) on Spanish-All followed by fine-tuning on individual languages, measuring performance differences.

### Open Question 3
- Question: How would including monolingual indigenous language data in pre-training affect translation quality compared to using only parallel data?
- Basis in paper: [inferred] The authors only used parallel data for fine-tuning but didn't explore incorporating monolingual indigenous language data, which is common in low-resource MT research.
- Why unresolved: The paper didn't experiment with monolingual data incorporation techniques like back-translation or self-training that could potentially improve results.
- What evidence would resolve it: Experiments comparing models trained only on parallel data versus models that incorporate monolingual indigenous language data through techniques like back-translation or self-training.

### Open Question 4
- Question: What linguistic factors (e.g., language family, morphological complexity, word order) most strongly correlate with translation performance across these indigenous languages?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The authors mention linguistic diversity and divergent properties from mainstream languages but don't analyze which specific linguistic factors correlate with performance differences.
- Why unresolved: While the paper provides results across 11 languages, it doesn't systematically analyze which linguistic characteristics predict better or worse translation performance.
- What evidence would resolve it: Correlation analysis between linguistic features (family membership, morphological typology, syntactic alignment, etc.) and translation performance metrics across the 11 languages studied.

## Limitations
- The chrF2 metric may not fully capture translation quality for morphologically complex indigenous languages
- The study lacks ablation experiments to determine which specific aspects of multilingual pretraining drive improvements
- The paper doesn't report statistical significance testing for performance differences between models

## Confidence
- **High confidence**: The finding that mBART50 showed better average performance across languages compared to Helsinki-NLP and M2M-100 is well-supported by the reported results.
- **Medium confidence**: The claim that multilingual pretraining specifically improves low-resource indigenous language translation is plausible but not definitively proven.
- **Low confidence**: The effectiveness of the Spanish-All intermediate fine-tuning approach remains unclear.

## Next Checks
1. **Statistical significance testing**: Perform paired statistical tests (e.g., bootstrap resampling) on the chrF2 scores between mBART50 and baseline models for each language to determine if performance differences are statistically significant.

2. **Linguistic analysis validation**: Have native speakers or linguistic experts evaluate a sample of translations from mBART50 and the baseline for at least two languages to verify that chrF2 improvements correspond to actual translation quality gains.

3. **Ablation study on pretraining**: Fine-tune a monolingual Spanish-indigenous model with similar architecture to mBART50 but without multilingual pretraining, and compare performance to both mBART50 and the baseline to isolate whether improvements stem from multilingual pretraining specifically.
