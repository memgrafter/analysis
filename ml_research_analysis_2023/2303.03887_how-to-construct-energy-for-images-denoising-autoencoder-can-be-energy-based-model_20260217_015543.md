# How to Construct Energy for Images? Denoising Autoencoder Can Be Energy Based Model

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2303.03887
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.03887
- Reference count: 40
- Introduces Denoising-EBM, an energy-based model that achieves state-of-the-art performance on image generation tasks using semantic and texture energy decomposition

## Executive Summary
This paper proposes Denoising-EBM, a novel energy-based model that decomposes image energy into semantic and texture components. The approach leverages denoising autoencoders to learn high-level semantic representations in the latent space while capturing pixel-level details through reconstruction error. By utilizing multi-scale noisy samples and outputting vectors instead of scalars, the model explores a larger function space during optimization. The training process employs fast MCMC sampling in the low-dimensional latent space followed by pixel-level refinement. The framework achieves state-of-the-art performance on CIFAR-10 and CelebA datasets, outperforming most energy-based models in FID and Inception Score metrics.

## Method Summary
The method introduces Denoising-EBM, which combines denoising autoencoder (DAE) structure with energy-based modeling. A U-net style DAE learns to reconstruct images from multi-scale Gaussian noise, creating a latent space that captures semantic information. The model decomposes energy into two components: semantic energy computed in the latent space and texture energy based on pixel-level reconstruction error. During training, maximum likelihood estimation is performed using a two-stage MCMC sampling process - first in the low-dimensional latent space using semantic energy, then in the data space using texture energy. The model outputs a vector (reconstruction) rather than a scalar energy value, allowing exploration of a larger function space during optimization.

## Key Results
- Achieves state-of-the-art FID and Inception Score on CIFAR-10 and CelebA datasets among energy-based models
- Demonstrates superior out-of-distribution detection performance compared to other EBMs
- Shows efficient sampling through two-stage MCMC process in latent and data spaces

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: The model achieves faster sampling by performing MCMC in the low-dimensional latent space before refining in pixel space
- Mechanism: Semantic energy is optimized in the DAE's latent space (Z), which is lower dimensional than the data space (X). MCMC mixing is more efficient in Z, and the initial state in X is close to the model distribution, reducing the number of steps needed in the second stage
- Core assumption: The latent space of a well-trained DAE preserves semantic information and has a lower effective dimensionality than the data space
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "After training, the semantics are first synthesized by fast MCMC through 'semantic energy', and then the pixel-level refinement of semantic image will be performed to generate perfect samples based on 'texture energy'"
  - [section 4.2] "Performing fast MCMC sampling first in the low-dimensional latent space can effectively save the computational resources of training and generation"
  - [corpus] Weak evidence - corpus papers mention diffusion models and multi-scale texture loss but don't directly address latent space MCMC efficiency
- Break condition: If the DAE's latent space does not preserve semantic information or has high effective dimensionality, the efficiency gain from latent space MCMC will be minimal or negative

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: Decomposing image energy into semantic and texture components allows the model to capture both high-level representations and fine-grained details separately
- Mechanism: Semantic energy models high-level semantic information in the latent space, while texture energy models pixel-level reconstruction error. This separation allows each component to specialize in its respective domain
- Core assumption: Image energy can be meaningfully decomposed into semantic and texture components that can be modeled separately and combined
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "We propose a Denoising-EBM which decomposes the image energy into 'semantic energy' and 'texture energy'"
  - [section 4.1] "We decompose the image energy by leveraging the structure of DAE... The 'semantic energy' models the semantics in the latent space, and 'texture energy' model the detail information of image in data space"
  - [corpus] Weak evidence - corpus papers discuss multi-scale texture loss and diffusion models but don't directly address energy decomposition into semantic and texture components
- Break condition: If the semantic and texture components are not independent or if one component dominates the other, the decomposition may not provide benefits over a unified energy model

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: Using vector output instead of scalar output allows the model to explore a larger set of functions during optimization
- Mechanism: The model outputs a vector (the reconstruction) rather than a scalar energy value. This allows the optimization to explore a larger function space than traditional EBMs with scalar outputs
- Core assumption: The relationship between input and output can be better captured by a vector function than a scalar function
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "it outputs a vector instead of a scalar for exploring a larger set of functions during optimization"
  - [section 4.1] "In this way, we vectorize the output of EBM and combine the U-net structure for capturing more detail information"
  - [corpus] Weak evidence - corpus papers mention diffusion models and multi-scale texture loss but don't directly address vector vs scalar output in energy-based models
- Break condition: If the additional degrees of freedom from vector output lead to overfitting or if the optimization becomes unstable, the benefits may be outweighed by the drawbacks

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map: DAE with U-net structure (fα → gβ) → Semantic decoder (hγ) → Energy functions (Uc_ϕ, Us_γ) → MCMC samplers (latent space Z, data space X)
- Critical path: Training → Latent space MCMC → Data space MCMC → Generation
- Design tradeoffs:
  - Latent space dimension vs. semantic representation quality
  - Number of MCMC steps in each space vs. sampling efficiency
  - Noise levels in denoising objective vs. score estimation quality
- Failure signatures:
  - Poor generation quality: Check if latent space MCMC is converging properly
  - Unstable training: Check if texture energy is dominating semantic energy or vice versa
  - Slow sampling: Check if MCMC in data space is taking too many steps
- First 3 experiments:
  1. Train the DAE with U-net structure and verify that it can denoise corrupted images effectively
  2. Implement the latent space MCMC sampler and verify that it can generate reasonable semantic samples

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- Question: What is the theoretical limit of the "semantic energy" and "texture energy" decomposition in capturing all image information, and are there image types or domains where this decomposition becomes insufficient?
- Basis in paper: [inferred] The paper decomposes image energy into "semantic energy" and "texture energy" but doesn't explore the theoretical limits or completeness of this decomposition across different image domains
- Why unresolved: The paper demonstrates success on standard datasets (CIFAR-10, CelebA) but doesn't systematically investigate where this decomposition might fail or what types of images it cannot adequately represent
- What evidence would resolve it: Systematic experiments across diverse image domains (medical imaging, satellite imagery, abstract art) showing where the decomposition performs well or breaks down, along with theoretical analysis of the representational capacity

### Open Question 2
- Question: How does the choice of latent space dimensionality in the denoising autoencoder affect the quality of the semantic energy model, and is there an optimal dimensionality for different data types?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper mentions the latent space of DAE is used for semantic energy but doesn't explore how the dimensionality of this space affects performance
- Why unresolved: The paper uses a fixed architecture without analyzing the sensitivity of results to latent space size or investigating whether different image datasets require different latent dimensionalities
- What evidence would resolve it: Ablation studies varying latent space dimensionality across multiple datasets, showing how generation quality and training stability change with different dimensions

### Open Question 3
- Question: Can the two-stage MCMC sampling approach be extended to other generative modeling frameworks beyond EBMs, and what modifications would be necessary?
- Basis in paper: [inferred] The paper introduces a novel two-stage MCMC sampling approach that leverages DAE structure, but doesn't discuss its potential application to other generative models
- Why unresolved: The paper focuses on demonstrating the effectiveness of this approach within their specific framework without exploring its generalizability or the theoretical requirements for applying it to other models like GANs or diffusion models
- What evidence would resolve it: Implementation and comparison of the two-stage sampling approach applied to other generative frameworks, along with analysis of what components are essential versus specific to EBMs

## Limitations
- The vector output mechanism introduces additional complexity without clear theoretical justification for superiority over scalar energy functions
- The multi-scale noise approach requires careful tuning of noise levels for different datasets
- Evaluation focuses primarily on FID and IS metrics with limited analysis of sample diversity and mode coverage

## Confidence

High confidence in the DAE training methodology and its role in semantic representation learning
Medium confidence in the two-stage sampling approach, pending more rigorous analysis of convergence properties
Low confidence in the scalability of the approach to higher resolution images and more complex datasets
