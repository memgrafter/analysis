# Prompting for Multimodal Hateful Meme Classification

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2302.04156
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2302.04156
- Authors: 
- Reference count: 10
- Key outcome: PromptHate achieves 90.96 AUC on Hateful Memes dataset and 84.47 on Harmful Memes dataset, outperforming state-of-the-art multimodal baselines

## Executive Summary
This paper introduces PromptHate, a prompt-based approach for hateful meme classification that converts meme images into textual descriptions and uses pre-trained language models with carefully designed prompts and demonstrations. The method leverages RoBERTa's implicit knowledge through masked language modeling objectives rather than traditional fine-tuning. PromptHate demonstrates superior performance on two benchmark datasets, achieving state-of-the-art results while maintaining the parameter efficiency of prompt-based learning approaches.

## Method Summary
PromptHate processes meme images by first extracting text with EasyOCR, inpainting images to remove text, generating captions using ClipCap trained on Conceptual Captions, and extracting demographic information with Google Vision API and FairFace classifier. The model constructs prompts using positive (non-hateful) and negative (hateful) demonstrations with an "It was [MASK]" template, using "good" and "bad" as label words. During inference, a multi-query ensemble strategy averages predictions from 4 demonstration pairs. The model is trained with RoBERTa-large using a masked language modeling objective, cross-entropy loss on masked word prediction, learning rate 1.3e-5 for FHM and 1.0e-5 for HarM, batch size 16, and 10 epochs.

## Key Results
- Achieves 90.96 AUC and 84.47 accuracy on Hateful Memes dataset
- Achieves 84.47 AUC and 77.17 accuracy on Harmful Memes dataset
- Outperforms state-of-the-art multimodal baselines while using fewer parameters

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: PromptHate achieves high AUC by leveraging implicit contextual knowledge embedded in RoBERTa during pre-training.
- Mechanism: The model uses carefully designed prompts and in-context demonstrations to elicit this implicit knowledge, allowing RoBERTa to generate predictions based on nuanced understanding of hateful memes rather than just surface features.
- Core assumption: RoBERTa's large-scale pre-training data contains sufficient implicit cultural and contextual information about hate speech.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "PromptHate, a simple yet effective prompt-based model that prompts pre-trained language models (PLMs) for hateful meme classification"
  - [section 1] "the prompt-based approach is that PLMs will tap into the implicit and unstructured knowledge in their large-scale pretraining data"
  - [corpus] Weak evidence - only mentions related works on multimodal hateful meme detection, no direct support for implicit knowledge mechanisms
- Break condition: If the implicit knowledge in RoBERTa does not cover the cultural or contextual nuances required for hateful meme detection, performance will degrade significantly.

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: The prompt-based approach outperforms traditional fine-tuning by better aligning with RoBERTa's pre-training objectives.
- Mechanism: Instead of adding task-specific classification heads, PromptHate uses masked language modeling (MLM) objectives that match RoBERTa's original training task, allowing more efficient use of the pre-trained parameters.
- Core assumption: Maintaining alignment with original pre-training objectives leads to better performance than fine-tuning with new classification heads.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [section 1] "prompt-based tuning does not have additional parameters beyond those in the PLMs, and the MLM task does not deviate from PLM's pre-training objectives"
  - [section 5.2] "The MLM training objective is designed to align with the PLMs' training objectives. This plays a significant role in enabling PromptHate to better utilize the embedding implicit knowledge in the PLMs"
  - [corpus] Weak evidence - mentions prompt-based learning but not specific to MLM objective alignment
- Break condition: If the task requires significant deviation from RoBERTa's original MLM objective, the alignment benefit disappears.

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: Multi-query ensemble strategy improves performance by reducing target-specific bias in demonstration selection.
- Mechanism: Instead of carefully selecting demonstrations similar to the inference instance, the model uses multiple pairs of demonstrations, averaging predictions to mitigate bias from any single demonstration pair.
- Core assumption: Different demonstrations capture different aspects of the hateful meme classification task, and averaging predictions reduces variance.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [section 1] "we adopt a multi-query ensemble strategy to predict the inference instance using multiple pairs of demonstrations"
  - [section 4.4] "The multi-query ensemble will result in a set of prediction scores for the inference instance: {ym}M m=1, where ym∈ R2 is the predicted scores with the m-th pair of demonstration"
  - [corpus] Weak evidence - only mentions related works on multimodal hateful meme detection, no direct support for ensemble mechanisms
- Break condition: If the variance between demonstration pairs is too high, ensemble averaging may actually hurt performance.

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map: Image preprocessing → Caption generation → Prompt construction → RoBERTa prediction → Ensemble aggregation
- Critical path: The sequence from image caption generation through prompt construction to RoBERTa prediction is the core pipeline that determines performance
- Design tradeoffs: Using captions instead of raw visual features simplifies input to RoBERTa but may lose fine-grained visual information; ensemble approach adds robustness but increases computational cost
- Failure signatures: Poor caption quality leads to incorrect predictions; suboptimal prompt design results in RoBERTa not leveraging the right implicit knowledge; insufficient demonstrations cause high variance

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- Question: How do different prompt templates and label words affect the performance of PromptHate on hateful meme classification?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper mentions experimenting with different label words and templates, showing that semantic alignment between label words and classes improves performance, especially in few-shot settings.
- Why unresolved: While the paper tests a few combinations of label words and templates, it does not exhaustively explore the space of possible prompts or provide a systematic method for optimizing them.
- What evidence would resolve it: Conducting a comprehensive study with a wider variety of templates and label words, potentially using automated methods for prompt generation and selection, could provide insights into the optimal prompt design for this task.

### Open Question 2
- Question: How can the performance of PromptHate be improved by incorporating external knowledge bases or reasoning modules?
- Basis in paper: [inferred] The paper acknowledges that hateful meme classification requires contextual background knowledge, and while PromptHate leverages implicit knowledge in PLMs, it may still struggle with memes requiring deep multi-modal reasoning or external context.
- Why unresolved: The paper's error analysis suggests that some incorrect predictions stem from missing contextual information or the need for advanced reasoning, but it does not explore methods for integrating external knowledge or reasoning capabilities into the model.
- What evidence would resolve it: Experiments with PromptHate augmented by external knowledge bases, reasoning modules, or other techniques for capturing contextual information could demonstrate whether these additions improve classification accuracy.

### Open Question 3
- Question: How does the size of the pre-trained language model affect the performance of PromptHate, and what is the optimal model size for this task?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper mentions that PromptHate with RoBERTa-large outperforms baselines with smaller models, and that model size plays a critical role in performance. However, it also notes that PromptHate-RB (with RoBERTa-base) still outperforms some baselines, suggesting diminishing returns with larger models.
- Why unresolved: The paper does not provide a detailed analysis of how model size affects performance or determine the point of diminishing returns. It also does not explore other large language models beyond RoBERTa.
- What evidence would resolve it: A systematic study comparing PromptHate's performance with various model sizes, including other large language models, could identify the optimal model size and architecture for this task.

## Limitations
- Lack of detailed implementation guidance for prompt templates and demonstration selection criteria
- Heavy dependency on caption quality without ablation studies quantifying this impact
- No empirical validation that RoBERTa's pre-training contains sufficient implicit knowledge about hate speech
- Multi-query ensemble hyperparameter sensitivity not explored

## Confidence
- High confidence: The prompt-based approach using MLM objectives is technically sound and aligns with established prompt-based learning literature
- Medium confidence: The claim that PromptHate achieves state-of-the-art performance is credible based on reported metrics but needs independent reproduction
- Low confidence: Mechanism claims about implicit knowledge elicitation and multi-query ensemble benefits lack sufficient empirical evidence

## Next Checks
1. **Caption ablation study**: Compare PromptHate performance using ground truth captions vs. ClipCap-generated captions vs. captions from different vision-language models to quantify the impact of caption quality on final classification accuracy.

2. **Demonstration quantity analysis**: Systematically vary the number of demonstration pairs (1, 2, 4, 8) in the multi-query ensemble to determine the optimal ensemble size and whether the 4-query choice is dataset-specific or generalizable.

3. **Knowledge coverage test**: Evaluate PromptHate on a curated subset of memes where the hateful content relies on cultural or contextual knowledge unlikely to be present in RoBERTa's pre-training data, measuring performance degradation to validate the implicit knowledge mechanism assumption.
