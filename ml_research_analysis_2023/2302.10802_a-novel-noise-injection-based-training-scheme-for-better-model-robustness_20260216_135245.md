# A Novel Noise Injection-based Training Scheme for Better Model Robustness

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2302.10802
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2302.10802
- Reference count: 40
- Key outcome: Proposed noise injection method achieves up to 162.5% higher accuracy under PGD attacks and 108.9% under BIM attacks on MNIST compared to conventional training methods.

## Executive Summary
This paper introduces a novel noise injection-based training scheme that improves the robustness of artificial neural networks (ANNs) and spiking neural networks (SNNs) against adversarial attacks. The method uses a likelihood ratio approach to estimate gradients with respect to both weights and noise levels, enabling efficient stochastic gradient descent training. By injecting noise into neuron activations during training, the network learns to be less sensitive to small input perturbations, resulting in significantly improved adversarial robustness while maintaining or slightly improving accuracy on clean inputs.

## Method Summary
The method employs a likelihood ratio (LR) technique to compute unbiased gradient estimates for both synaptic weights and noise levels during training. This allows the network to optimize noise injection parameters alongside weights using stochastic gradient descent. To reduce computational overhead, a sign-based approximation is introduced that stores only the sign of noise terms rather than full activation-noise products. The approach is applied to SNNs with Leaky Integrate-and-Fire neurons for classification tasks on MNIST and Fashion-MNIST datasets, demonstrating superior adversarial robustness compared to conventional training methods like STBP.

## Key Results
- SNNs trained with the proposed method show up to 162.5% higher accuracy under PGD attacks on MNIST
- 108.9% higher accuracy under BIM attacks compared to conventional STBP training
- Slight improvement in accuracy on original (clean) samples
- Simplified sign-based approximation offers further efficiency gains while maintaining strong robustness performance

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: The likelihood ratio (LR) method provides unbiased gradient estimates for both weights and noise levels.
- Mechanism: By computing the expectation of the product of the loss and the scaled noise term, the LR method captures how changes in noise affect the loss, enabling optimization of noise levels alongside weights.
- Core assumption: The noise added is Gaussian and independent across neurons, allowing analytical gradient computation via the LR trick.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "we first develop a likelihood ratio method to estimate the gradient with respect to both synaptic weights and noise levels"
  - [section 3.1] "By an LR method, we have an unbiased gradient estimation of E [Ln(θ,σ )] with respect to ANN parameters as follows"
- Break condition: If the noise distribution deviates significantly from Gaussian or becomes correlated across neurons, the LR estimator loses its unbiasedness and convergence guarantees.

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: The sign-based approximation reduces memory and computation cost while maintaining convergence.
- Mechanism: Instead of storing full input-noise products, the method stores only the sign of the noise term, reducing memory from O(n²) to O(n) and enabling parallelization.
- Core assumption: The magnitude of the noise term is less critical than its sign for gradient direction in early training stages.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [section 3.2] "Then we propose the following surrogate ascent direction: ˜gk = 1/b ∑ n∈Bk Ln(θk,σk)(sign(Zθn),Zσn)"
  - [section 3.2] "Due to the existence of extra term |Zn,d|−1 in the first D0 components, the objective function is different from the original one"
- Break condition: If the training dynamics require precise magnitude information (e.g., in late-stage fine-tuning), the sign approximation may slow convergence or get stuck in suboptimal minima.

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: Noise injection acts as a regularizer that improves generalization and adversarial robustness.
- Mechanism: By forcing the network to learn from noisy activations, the model becomes less sensitive to small input perturbations and better generalizes to unseen variations.
- Core assumption: The added noise level is appropriately scaled so it regularizes without overwhelming the signal.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "Noise injection-based method has been shown to be able to improve the robustness of artificial neural networks"
  - [section 1] "Noise injection-based methods can effectively improve the robustness of ANNs under adversarial attacks"
- Break condition: If noise level is too high, the network may underfit and lose accuracy on clean inputs; if too low, the regularization effect vanishes.

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map: Forward pass → noise injection → loss → LR gradient estimation → parameter update → (optional sign approximation)
- Critical path: Forward pass → noise injection → loss → LR gradient estimation → parameter update. Bottlenecks occur in memory usage for storing intermediate activations and noise terms.
- Design tradeoffs: Memory vs. accuracy (storing full noise terms improves gradient quality but increases memory), computational efficiency vs. convergence speed (sign approximation reduces compute but may slow convergence), noise level vs. robustness (higher noise improves robustness but may hurt clean accuracy)
- Failure signatures: High variance in gradient estimates (unstable training), memory overflow during long sequences (OOM errors), degradation in clean accuracy (over-regularization), slow convergence with sign approximation (inadequate gradient information)
- First 3 experiments:
  1. Train a simple fully connected network on MNIST with noise injection using exact LR gradients; compare convergence and robustness to baseline.
  2. Replace exact gradients with sign-based approximation; measure memory usage, training speed, and impact on final accuracy.
  3. Apply the method to a small SNN on MNIST; verify that the temporal integration and noise injection work together without instability.

## Limitations

- The method assumes Gaussian noise distribution, which may not be optimal for all network architectures or datasets
- Theoretical guarantees for the sign-based approximation's convergence are limited
- Effectiveness on deeper architectures and more complex datasets beyond MNIST/Fashion-MNIST remains untested

## Confidence

- **High confidence**: The likelihood ratio method for unbiased gradient estimation in the presence of noise injection is mathematically sound and correctly implemented
- **Medium confidence**: The sign-based approximation provides computational benefits while maintaining robustness, though convergence guarantees are weaker
- **Low confidence**: The claim that this method generalizes well to deeper architectures and more complex datasets is not yet validated
