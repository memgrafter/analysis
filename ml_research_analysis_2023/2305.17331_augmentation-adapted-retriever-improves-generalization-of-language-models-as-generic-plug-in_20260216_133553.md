# Augmentation-Adapted Retriever Improves Generalization of Language Models as Generic Plug-In

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2305.17331
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.17331
- **Authors:** 
- **Reference count:** 23
- **Key outcome:** AAR trained with small source LM significantly improves zero-shot performance of larger target LMs (250M-175B parameters) on MMLU and PopQA datasets

## Executive Summary
This paper introduces Augmentation-Adapted Retriever (AAR), a method to improve language model generalization by training retrievers to learn LM preferences rather than human preferences. The key innovation is using FiD attention scores from a source LM to identify "LM-preferred" documents that contain complementary information useful for LM reasoning. AAR can be trained once with a small source LM and then serve as a generic plug-in for various target LMs without fine-tuning, addressing the challenge of black-box APIs and real-world deployment constraints.

## Method Summary
AAR fine-tunes a pre-trained retriever using documents preferred by a source LM, identified through FiD attention scores during inference. The retriever is trained with a contrastive loss combining LM-preferred documents (top-K attention scores) and human-preferred documents from MS MARCO QA as positive examples, with ANCE negatives. This augmentation-adapted retriever can then retrieve documents for unseen target LMs without further fine-tuning, improving their zero-shot performance on tasks like MMLU and PopQA.

## Key Results
- AAR trained on Flan-T5Base improves InstructGPT accuracy from 60.2% to 62.2% on MMLU
- The method works across a wide range of model sizes (250M Flan-T5 to 175B InstructGPT)
- Different LMs share overlapping document preferences, enabling one AAR to serve multiple target LMs
- Performance is sensitive to choice of retrieval corpus (MS MARCO for MMLU, KILT-Wikipedia for PopQA)

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- **Claim:** LM-preferred documents contain complementary information that differs from human-preferred documents, improving LM performance.
- **Mechanism:** The retriever learns to select documents based on the LM's internal attention patterns (FiD cross-attention scores) rather than human judgment, capturing evidence the LM finds more useful for reasoning.
- **Core assumption:** LM attention patterns during fusion-in-decoder inference correlate with the type of evidence most useful for that LM's reasoning process.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract] "Further analysis indicates that the preferences of different LMs overlap, enabling AAR trained with a single source LM to serve as a generic plug-in for various target LMs."
  - [section 5.3] "We observe that the human-preferred document can always present the gold answer at the beginning of the text, while the LM-preferred document may not contain the exact answer. However, an LM-preferred document can (1) deliver a new perspective to answer the given question... and (2) give a specific explanation instead of an abstract definition..."
  - [corpus] Weak evidence - the corpus contains related works on retrieval-augmented LMs but doesn't directly validate the LM vs human preference distinction.
- **Break condition:** If LM attention patterns don't correlate with reasoning utility, or if LMs consistently prefer the same types of documents as humans, the mechanism fails.

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim:** A small source LM can provide training signals that generalize to larger target LMs.
- **Mechanism:** The retriever learns LM-preference patterns from a smaller model, and these patterns transfer because larger LMs share overlapping preferences with smaller ones.
- **Core assumption:** Different-sized LMs share significant overlap in their document preferences, making training signals from a small LM transferable to larger ones.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract] "Further analysis indicates that the preferences of different LMs overlap, enabling AAR trained with a single source LM to serve as a generic plug-in for various target LMs."
  - [section 5.2] "LMs with near capacities tend to yield closer preferred document sets... LMs with similar sizes tend to yield closer document sets."
  - [corpus] Moderate evidence - the corpus includes works on zero-shot generalization and instruction-finetuning that support the idea of transferability, but doesn't directly test the specific claim about LM-preference overlap.
- **Break condition:** If LM preferences are highly idiosyncratic and don't overlap across sizes, or if the source LM's preferences are too divergent from target LMs, the mechanism fails.

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim:** Fine-tuning the retriever separately from the LM enables it to serve as a generic plug-in for black-box LMs.
- **Mechanism:** The retriever learns to identify documents useful for a source LM without requiring joint fine-tuning with target LMs, making it applicable to unseen LMs that can't be fine-tuned.
- **Core assumption:** A retriever can learn general document preference patterns from one LM that transfer to others without requiring task-specific fine-tuning.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract] "Experiments on the MMLU and PopQA datasets demonstrate that our AAR trained with a small source LM is able to significantly improve the zero-shot generalization of larger target LMs ranging from 250M Flan-T5 to 175B InstructGPT."
  - [section 3.2] "Due to the emerging real-world demands and the limitations of black-box APIs, fine-tuning retrieval-augmented LM for each possible downstream task can be infeasible. Hence, we introduce Augmentation-Adapted Retriever (AAR) as a generic plug-in for black-box LMs."
  - [corpus] Moderate evidence - the corpus includes works on zero-shot retrieval augmentation that support the concept of retriever-only approaches, but doesn't directly validate the plug-in claim for black-box APIs.
- **Break condition:** If target LMs have drastically different retrieval needs that aren't captured by the source LM's preferences, or if the retriever overfits to the source LM, the mechanism fails.

## Foundational Learning

  - Why needed here: The retriever operates by embedding queries and documents in a shared vector space and using dot product similarity for matching
  - Quick check question: How does the retriever represent queries and documents for matching, and what similarity function does it use?

  - Why needed here: The method uses FiD attention scores to determine LM preferences for documents, requiring understanding of how FiD aggregates multiple documents
  - Quick check question: What does FiD do differently from simple concatenation when handling multiple documents, and how does it compute attention across them?
- **Concept:** Contrastive learning for retrieval fine-tuning
  - Why needed here: The retriever is trained using a contrastive loss with positive and negative document samples, requiring understanding of this training paradigm
  - Quick check question: What loss function is used to train the retriever, and how are positive and negative examples selected?

## Architecture Onboarding

- **Component map:** Query → Retriever → Document set → LM input → Answer generation
- **Critical path:** Query → Retriever → Document set → LM input → Answer generation
- **Design tradeoffs:**
  - Source LM size vs. effectiveness: Smaller source LMs require less compute but may provide weaker preference signals
  - Retrieval corpus choice: MS MARCO works better for MMLU while KILT-Wikipedia works better for PopQA
  - Number of augmentation documents: More documents help larger LMs but increase computation
- **Failure signatures:**
  - Retriever performance drops on MS MARCO after augmentation-adapted training (distribution shift)
  - Target LMs show minimal improvement despite successful retriever fine-tuning
  - Performance degrades significantly when switching retrieval corpus
- **First 3 experiments:**
  1. Verify that FiD attention scores can be extracted from the source LM and correlate with document utility
  2. Test retriever fine-tuning with LM-preferred documents vs. human-preferred documents on a validation set
  3. Evaluate zero-shot performance of fine-tuned retriever on a small target LM before scaling to larger models

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- **Question:** How does the size of the source LM affect the quality of LM-preferred documents and the subsequent performance of AAR on target LMs?
- **Basis in paper:** [inferred] The paper mentions that using a small source LM (Flan-T5Base) is effective and efficient, but also notes that the performance of AAR varies with different source LMs.
- **Why unresolved:** The paper does not provide a systematic comparison of AAR performance using source LMs of different sizes.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Experiments comparing AAR performance using source LMs of various sizes (e.g., Flan-T5Base, Flan-T5Large, Flan-T5XL) on the same target LM and task.

### Open Question 2
- **Question:** How do different types of knowledge sources (e.g., structured vs. unstructured) impact the performance of AAR on knowledge-intensive tasks?
- **Basis in paper:** [explicit] The paper uses MS MARCO and KILT-Wikipedia as retrieval corpora, which are both unstructured knowledge sources.
- **Why unresolved:** The paper does not explore the use of structured knowledge sources (e.g., knowledge graphs) in AAR.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Experiments comparing AAR performance using structured and unstructured knowledge sources on the same target LM and task.

### Open Question 3
- **Question:** How does the number of positive documents (K) in the LM-preferred document set affect the performance of AAR on target LMs?
- **Basis in paper:** [explicit] The paper sets K=2 in the main experiments but does not explore the impact of different values of K.
- **Why unresolved:** The paper does not provide a systematic analysis of how varying K affects AAR performance.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Experiments comparing AAR performance using different values of K (e.g., 1, 2, 5, 10) on the same target LM and task.

### Open Question 4
- **Question:** How does the performance of AAR on decoder-only LMs (e.g., GPT-3, GPT-4) compare to its performance on encoder-decoder LMs (e.g., Flan-T5)?
- **Basis in paper:** [explicit] The paper mentions that AAR is evaluated on InstructGPT (a decoder-only LM) but does not provide a comprehensive comparison with encoder-decoder LMs.
- **Why unresolved:** The paper does not provide a detailed analysis of AAR performance on different LM architectures.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Experiments comparing AAR performance on decoder-only and encoder-decoder LMs of similar sizes on the same target task.

## Limitations
- The method relies on FiD attention patterns correlating with reasoning utility, which isn't fully validated
- Performance is sensitive to retrieval corpus choice, requiring task-specific corpus selection
- Limited testing on truly black-box models with different architectures beyond the Flan-T5 family

## Confidence
- **High Confidence:** The technical feasibility of fine-tuning retrievers using FiD attention scores and the empirical observation that this improves performance on benchmark datasets
- **Medium Confidence:** The claim that LM-preferred documents contain complementary information distinct from human-preferred documents
- **Low Confidence:** The claim that AAR can serve as a universal plug-in for any black-box LM

## Next Checks
1. **Cross-Architecture Generalization Test:** Evaluate AAR trained on Flan-T5Base with a completely different LM architecture (e.g., OPT, BLOOM, or LLaMA) to test whether the plug-in claim extends beyond Flan-T5 family models.

2. **Causal Mechanism Validation:** Conduct ablation studies that systematically vary the quality of FiD attention scores (e.g., by using corrupted attention patterns) to establish whether the attention-based document preference signal is truly necessary for the observed improvements.

3. **Domain Shift Robustness:** Test AAR performance when the source task distribution (MS MARCO) differs substantially from the target task (e.g., using legal or medical corpora) to evaluate whether the retriever can generalize beyond the training distribution overlap.
