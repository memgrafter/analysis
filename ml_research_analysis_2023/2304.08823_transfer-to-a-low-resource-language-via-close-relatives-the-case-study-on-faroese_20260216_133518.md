# Transfer to a Low-Resource Language via Close Relatives: The Case Study on Faroese

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2304.08823
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2304.08823
- Reference count: 34
- Key outcome: Cross-lingual transfer with massively multilingual transformers fails for low-resource languages like Faroese due to limited per-language representation quality. This work demonstrates that leveraging phylogenetically related high-resource languages (Danish, Norwegian, Swedish, Icelandic) instead of MMTs improves downstream transfer performance.

## Executive Summary
This paper addresses the challenge of cross-lingual transfer for low-resource languages by leveraging phylogenetically related high-resource languages. The authors demonstrate that training language models on Scandinavian languages and then fine-tuning them on Faroese data significantly outperforms using general-purpose multilingual models like XLM-R across multiple NLP tasks. The work includes the release of new Faroese datasets and shows that excluding Faroese from initial pretraining can actually improve downstream performance when fine-tuned on limited Faroese data.

## Method Summary
The authors train new language models on Scandinavian languages (Danish, Norwegian, Swedish, Icelandic) and compare their performance to XLM-R when fine-tuned on Faroese task-specific datasets. They create several model variants, including models that exclude Faroese from pretraining, and evaluate them on POS tagging, NER, dependency parsing, and semantic similarity tasks. The method involves collecting and preprocessing Scandinavian language corpora, training language models on these related languages, fine-tuning on small Faroese datasets, and evaluating the transfer performance against XLM-R and other baselines.

## Key Results
- Models trained on Scandinavian languages outperform XLM-R across POS tagging, NER, dependency parsing, and semantic similarity tasks for Faroese
- The best results come from models pretrained on all Scandinavian languages except Faroese, then fine-tuned on small Faroese corpora
- New Faroese datasets released: web corpus (FC3), named entity recognition (FoNE), and semantic text similarity (Fo-STS)

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: Training language models on phylogenetically related high-resource languages improves cross-lingual transfer to a low-resource sibling language.
- Mechanism: Language models trained exclusively on related high-resource languages capture shared linguistic features and subword structures that transfer effectively to the low-resource target language.
- Core assumption: Linguistic similarity and shared vocabulary across related languages enables effective knowledge transfer.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "by leveraging abundant resources of other Scandinavian languages (i.e., Danish, Norwegian, Swedish, and Icelandic) for the benefit of Faroese"
  - [section 5.1] "The best-performing model for POS, as evaluated on the Sosialurin POS corpus, is ScandiBERT-fc3, outperforming ScandiBERT by more than 1 point in terms of F1"
  - [corpus] Evidence from Faroese being closely related to other Scandinavian languages suggests shared linguistic features.
- Break condition: If the target language has no closely related high-resource siblings or the linguistic distance is too large, this mechanism would fail.

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: Excluding the target language from initial pretraining, then adapting with limited target corpus data, can improve downstream performance.
- Mechanism: Pretraining on related languages without the target language forces the model to rely on shared subword tokenization patterns, which then transfers better when adapted to the target language.
- Core assumption: Shared tokenization patterns across related languages are more beneficial than language-specific tokenization for transfer learning.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [section 5.1] "the ScandiBERT-no-fo model that is not trained on Faroese outperforms the model that has Faroese included, when fine-tuned further on the FC3 dataset"
  - [abstract] "we can substantially improve the transfer performance to Faroese by exploiting data and models of closely-related high-resource languages"
  - [corpus] Limited Faroese data makes it difficult to learn optimal tokenization, supporting the use of related languages' tokenization patterns.
- Break condition: If the target language has sufficient monolingual data to train its own tokenizer effectively, this mechanism would not provide additional benefit.

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: Cross-lingual transfer from monolingual models of closely related languages outperforms massively multilingual transformers for low-resource languages.
- Mechanism: Monolingual models of closely related languages provide more focused and higher-quality representations than general-purpose multilingual models.
- Core assumption: Focused representation learning for a group of related languages is more effective than broad multilingual pretraining for cross-lingual transfer.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "by leveraging the phylogenetic information and departing from the 'one-size-fits-all' paradigm"
  - [section 5.1] "Different variants of ScandiBERT outperform XLM-R without any Faroese data across the board in all evaluation tasks"
  - [corpus] Faroese shares many linguistic features with other Scandinavian languages, supporting focused transfer.
- Break condition: If the target language is not closely related to any high-resource language, or if the high-resource languages are not well-represented in general multilingual models, this mechanism would fail.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- Question: How does the quality of the Wechsel method for cross-lingual transfer compare between closely related languages versus distantly related languages, and what specific factors contribute to any observed differences?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper compares the Wechsel method for cross-lingual transfer between closely related languages (Faroese and Icelandic) and distantly related languages (English-dominant models to French, German, Chinese, etc.) and finds that the Wechsel method performs significantly worse for closely related languages.
- Why unresolved: The paper does not provide a detailed analysis of the factors contributing to the observed differences in Wechsel method performance between closely and distantly related languages.
- What evidence would resolve it: A detailed analysis of the Wechsel method's performance on a range of language pairs with varying degrees of relatedness, along with an investigation of the factors contributing to any observed differences.

### Open Question 2
- Question: How does the inclusion of a target language's data in the initial pretraining of a language model affect its downstream performance, and what are the underlying mechanisms driving these effects?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper observes that including Faroese data in the initial pretraining of ScandiBERT leads to slightly worse performance compared to ScandiBERT-no-fo, which is then fine-tuned on Faroese data. The authors hypothesize that this might be due to the lower subword overlap between Faroese and other Scandinavian languages when Faroese is included in the vocabulary.
- Why unresolved: The paper does not provide a comprehensive investigation of the effects of including target language data in initial pretraining on downstream performance, nor does it explore the underlying mechanisms driving these effects.
- What evidence would resolve it: A systematic study of the effects of including target language data in initial pretraining on downstream performance across a range of languages and tasks, along with an investigation of the underlying mechanisms driving these effects.

### Open Question 3
- Question: How can the benefits of leveraging phylogenetically related high-resource languages for cross-lingual transfer be maximized, and what are the optimal strategies for selecting and combining these languages?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper demonstrates that using language models trained on phylogenetically related high-resource languages improves cross-lingual transfer performance to Faroese compared to using massively multilingual transformers (MMTs). However, the paper does not provide a comprehensive investigation of the optimal strategies for selecting and combining these languages.
- Why unresolved: The paper does not explore the full potential of leveraging phylogenetically related high-resource languages for cross-lingual transfer, nor does it provide a comprehensive investigation of the optimal strategies for selecting and combining these languages.
- What evidence would resolve it: A systematic study of the effects of different strategies for selecting and combining phylogenetically related high-resource languages on cross-lingual transfer performance across a range of languages and tasks.

## Limitations

- The paper focuses exclusively on Faroese and Scandinavian languages, providing no evidence about how the phylogenetic transfer approach would work for languages from different families or with varying degrees of relatedness to high-resource languages
- The extreme data imbalance (6.8B tokens of pretraining data vs. 5.5K-18K sentences of Faroese fine-tuning data) raises questions about whether improvements reflect genuine transfer learning or simply effects of training on vastly more data
- The experimental design lacks negative controls and ablations that would isolate the effects of language relatedness versus other factors like model architecture choices or training procedures

## Confidence

**High Confidence**: The empirical observation that models trained on Scandinavian languages outperform XLM-R on Faroese downstream tasks is well-supported by the experimental results presented across four different tasks.

**Medium Confidence**: The mechanism explaining why excluding Faroese from initial pretraining improves transfer performance has theoretical plausibility but limited empirical validation, with evidence from only one model variant.

**Low Confidence**: The broader claim that phylogenetic transfer is a general solution for low-resource language NLP problems lacks sufficient evidence, as the paper focuses exclusively on Faroese and Scandinavian languages.

## Next Checks

1. Conduct quantitative measurements of linguistic similarity between Faroese and each of the other Scandinavian languages (lexical overlap, syntactic divergence, morphological similarity scores) to establish the foundation for the phylogenetic transfer hypothesis.

2. Systematically vary the size of the Faroese fine-tuning datasets while keeping the pretraining data constant to determine whether the observed improvements are robust to different data availability scenarios.

3. Apply the same methodology to a low-resource language from a different family (e.g., a Baltic language or a Slavic language with high-resource relatives) to test whether the phylogenetic transfer approach generalizes beyond the Scandinavian context.
