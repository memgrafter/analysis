# Learning to diagnose cirrhosis from radiological and histological labels with joint self and weakly-supervised pretraining strategies

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2302.08427
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2302.08427
- Reference count: 0
- Primary result: Improved cirrhosis classification using combined weakly-supervised and self-supervised pretraining, achieving AUC of 0.84 on 106 histologically labeled CT-scans

## Executive Summary
This paper proposes a method to improve automatic cirrhosis diagnosis by leveraging large amounts of radiological annotations as weak supervision alongside self-supervised learning. The approach combines weakly-supervised and self-supervised pretraining through a weighted loss function, allowing the model to benefit from both label-rich but noisy radiological data and representation learning from unlabeled data. When fine-tuned on a small dataset with histological labels, the method outperforms both supervised baselines and pure self-supervised approaches, demonstrating the value of hybrid pretraining strategies for medical image classification with limited annotations.

## Method Summary
The method involves pretraining a CNN backbone on a large dataset of CT-scans with radiological labels (weak supervision) and unlabeled data (self-supervision), then fine-tuning on a small dataset with histological labels. The pretraining uses a hybrid loss combining weak supervision (binary cross-entropy with radiological labels) and self-supervision (SimCLR contrastive loss) with a tunable weighting parameter β. The architecture consists of five convolutional layers followed by a projection head, with data augmentation including crops, flips, and rotations. Evaluation is performed through 5-fold cross-validation on the histological dataset, reporting patient-level AUC and balanced accuracy.

## Key Results
- The hybrid weak-SimCLR pretraining method achieved AUC of 0.84 and improved balanced accuracy compared to baseline
- Outperformed two state-of-the-art self-supervised methods (SimCLR and SupCon) in cirrhosis classification
- Demonstrated the effectiveness of combining weakly-supervised and self-supervised approaches when histological annotations are scarce

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
Combining weakly-supervised and self-supervised pretraining improves cirrhosis classification when labeled histology data is scarce. The method leverages large amounts of weakly-labeled radiological data to pretrain feature extractors, then fine-tunes on the small histology-labeled dataset. This combines the label richness of weak supervision with the representation learning power of self-supervision.

### Mechanism 2
The weighted combination loss (weak-SimCLR) balances supervised and self-supervised learning to optimize feature representations. The loss function Lweak-SimCLR = βLweak + (1-β)LSimCLR allows tuning the contribution of radiological supervision versus self-supervised contrastive learning during pretraining.

### Mechanism 3
Transfer learning from weakly-labeled data improves performance on the histology-labeled target task. Pretraining on the large Dradio dataset with radiological labels, then fine-tuning on the small Dhisto dataset with histological labels, transfers useful features learned from weak supervision.

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map:
  - CT scan slices (512x512, portal venous phase) -> Backbone CNN (5 conv layers, 512-dim output) -> Projection head (2 dense layers, 128-dim for self-supervised, 2-dim for supervised) -> Data augmentation (crops, flips, rotations) -> Loss computation (Lweak-SimCLR) -> Sampling strategy (weighted sampling for class imbalance)

- Critical path:
  1. Load and preprocess CT scan slices from Dradio and Dhisto
  2. Apply data augmentations
  3. Compute backbone features
  4. Compute projection head outputs
  5. Calculate combined loss (Lweak-SimCLR)
  6. Backpropagate and update weights
  7. Evaluate on histology-labeled data

- Design tradeoffs:
  - Using 2D slices instead of 3D volumes reduces computational complexity but may lose spatial context
  - Binary classification simplifies the task but loses granularity of fibrosis staging
  - Weighted sampling addresses class imbalance but may introduce bias

- Failure signatures:
  - Poor performance on the histology-labeled test set despite good performance on weakly-labeled pretraining set
  - High variance in cross-validation results indicating instability
  - Minimal improvement from pretraining compared to training from scratch

- First 3 experiments:
  1. Baseline: Train CNN directly on Dhisto from random initialization
  2. Weakly-supervised: Pretrain on Dradio with radiological labels, then fine-tune on Dhisto
  3. Self-supervised: Pretrain on Dradio with SimCLR, then fine-tune on Dhisto

## Limitations
- Performance gain over baseline is moderate (AUC improvement from 0.80 to 0.84)
- Comparison limited to two self-supervised methods rather than broader benchmark
- Effectiveness depends critically on optimal β hyperparameter which was fixed at 0.5 without extensive validation

## Confidence

- Mechanism 1 (Weak+Self pretraining improves cirrhosis classification): Medium
- Mechanism 2 (Weighted loss balances supervision): Medium
- Mechanism 3 (Transfer learning from radiological to histological): Low
