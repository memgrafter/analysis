# DEGREE: Decomposition Based Explanation For Graph Neural Networks

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2305.12895
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.12895
- Reference count: 14

## Executive Summary
DEGREE introduces a novel decomposition-based approach to explain Graph Neural Networks (GNNs) by tracking how specific graph components contribute to model predictions. The method decomposes GNN message-passing into target and background portions, allowing isolation of information flows at each layer. Through an agglomeration process that leverages structural information, DEGREE provides subgraph-level interpretations that reveal complex node interactions. The approach demonstrates superior faithfulness compared to baseline methods while identifying important graph structures in both synthetic and real-world datasets.

## Method Summary
DEGREE works by decomposing each GNN layer into target and background portions that separately track information flow from specific node groups. For GCN layers, it computes target and background portions using normalized adjacency matrices and competes for bias terms. The method employs contextual contribution scores based on random walk sampling to compare target node group contributions against various backgrounds. An agglomeration algorithm iteratively constructs important subgraphs by selecting nodes that maximize contribution impact. The approach applies to both node and graph classification tasks without requiring surrogate models or input perturbations.

## Key Results
- Demonstrates superior faithfulness compared to baseline explanation methods
- Successfully identifies important subgraphs in synthetic BA-Shapes dataset
- Achieves strong performance on real-world node and graph classification tasks
- Provides interpretable subgraph-level explanations that capture non-linear interactions

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: DEGREE can decompose the message-passing mechanism of GNNs to isolate target and background information flows.
- Mechanism: The method decomposes each GNN layer into target and background portions that track the contribution of specific graph components through the model's feed-forward propagation. - Core assumption: The information flow in GNN's message propagation mechanism is decomposable, meaning target and background portions can be isolated without breaking the overall information flow.

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: DEGREE provides subgraph-level interpretation by revealing complex interactions between nodes through an agglomeration process.
- Mechanism: The method uses a contextual contribution score φ(Vγ) that measures the relative contribution of a target node group compared to its contexts, sampled within the neighborhood using random walks. An agglomeration algorithm then constructs important subgraphs by iteratively selecting nodes that most significantly affect the contribution of existing subgraphs.
- Core assumption: Important subgraph structures can be identified by comparing the contribution of node groups to the target class against various contextual backgrounds.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "A subgraph-level interpretation algorithm is designed to reveal complex interactions between nodes using an agglomeration process that leverages structural information"

- Break condition: If the random walk sampling fails to capture relevant contexts or if the agglomeration process gets stuck in local optima.

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: DEGREE guarantees faithfulness by directly analyzing GNN feed-forward propagation rather than relying on perturbation or surrogate models.
- Mechanism: The method tracks information flow through actual model computation without modifying the input graph or training additional explanation models. This is achieved by decomposing each layer's computation to maintain separate target and background portions throughout the forward pass.
- Core assumption: Direct analysis of the actual model computation provides more faithful explanations than methods that approximate or perturb the model.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "DEGREE guarantees explanation fidelity by directly analyzing GNN feed-forward propagation, instead of relying on input perturbation or the use of alternative models"

## Limitations
- Decomposition assumption may break when target and background information flows interact significantly
- Random walk sampling introduces stochasticity that may lead to inconsistent explanations
- Agglomeration algorithm's greedy nature risks converging to local optima
- Computational complexity scales poorly with graph size, particularly for dense graphs

## Confidence

**Major Limitations:**
The decomposition assumption may break when target and background information flows interact significantly during propagation, potentially limiting DEGREE's applicability to certain GNN architectures. The random walk sampling for context selection introduces stochasticity that may lead to inconsistent explanations across runs. The agglomeration algorithm's greedy nature risks converging to local optima rather than globally optimal subgraph explanations. Computational complexity scales poorly with graph size, particularly for dense graphs where neighborhood sampling becomes expensive.

**Confidence Labels:**
- Mechanism 1 (Decomposition of message passing): Medium - While the theoretical framework is sound, empirical validation across diverse GNN architectures is limited
- Mechanism 2 (Subgraph-level interpretation): Low - The agglomeration process lacks rigorous guarantees about finding optimal subgraphs
- Mechanism 3 (Faithfulness guarantee): Medium - Direct analysis of model computation is promising, but the decomposition itself may introduce approximation errors
