# Compositional Probabilistic and Causal Inference using Tractable Circuit Models

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2304.08278
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2304.08278
- Reference count: 40
- Primary result: MDNets achieve lower error than counting approaches for backdoor estimation on datasets with higher-dimensional adjustment sets, with computation time increasing only linearly in dimension.

## Executive Summary
This paper introduces md-vtrees, a novel structural formulation of marginal determinism in structured decomposable probabilistic circuits (PCs). The framework unifies previously proposed PC classes and provides tractability conditions for advanced inference queries expressed as compositions of basic probabilistic operations. The authors propose MDNets, a new PC architecture using md-vtrees, and derive efficient algorithms for causal inference queries like backdoor adjustment. Empirically, MDNets show promise for handling higher-dimensional adjustment sets in causal inference tasks.

## Method Summary
The paper presents a compositional framework for tractable probabilistic circuits using md-vtrees, which specify marginal determinism constraints on sum nodes. MDNets are introduced as a practical architecture that enforces these constraints through node groups with disjoint marginalized support. The MD-calculus provides backward reasoning rules to derive tractability conditions for composed inference queries. The approach is validated on Bayesian network models from bnrepository, comparing MDNets to counting-based methods for backdoor adjustment estimation.

## Key Results
- MD-vtrees unify scope decomposition and support properties in structured decomposable circuits
- MD-calculus enables backward reasoning from desired tractability to required marginal determinism constraints
- MDNets enforce md-vtrees by construction through node groups with disjoint marginalized support
- Lower error than counting approaches for backdoor estimation on datasets with higher-dimensional adjustment sets
- Computation time increases only linearly in dimension for MDNets

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: md-vtrees unify scope decomposition and support properties in structured decomposable circuits.
- Mechanism: md-vtrees integrate a labeling function ψ with a vtree to specify which marginal determinism properties are enforced on each sum node, enabling a single framework to represent PSDDs, deterministic circuits, and more general classes.
- Core assumption: Marginal determinism can be specified by a single set Q per vtree node without restricting support more than necessary.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "md-vtrees, a novel structural formulation of (marginal) determinism in structured decomposable PCs"
  - [section] "Proposition 2 (Superset Q-Determinisms for Sum Nodes)"
  - [corpus] "On the Hardness of Approximating Distributions with Tractable Probabilistic Models" (shows importance of unified tractability frameworks)
- Break condition: If two non-nested Q-determinism constraints are required on the same sum node, the circuit cannot have full support.

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: The MD-calculus enables backward reasoning from desired tractability to required marginal determinism constraints.
- Mechanism: By propagating md-vtree conditions backward through a composition of basic operations, one can derive minimal sets of Q-determinism constraints that ensure tractability.
- Core assumption: The input-output conditions in the MD-calculus are sufficient for tractability.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "derive tractability conditions and algorithms for causal inference queries"
  - [section] "Theorem 3 (MD-calculus). The conditions in T able 3 hold."
  - [corpus] "A Compositional Atlas for Algebraic Circuits" (similar compositional reasoning approach)
- Break condition: If the backward propagation leads to conflicting Q-determinism requirements, tractability cannot be achieved without restricting support.

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: MDNets enforce md-vtrees by construction through node groups with disjoint marginalized support.
- Mechanism: Node groups partition the domain of ψ(m) for each vtree node, ensuring that the required marginal determinism properties are satisfied throughout the circuit structure.
- Core assumption: Regular md-vtrees (where labels are either inherited or unioned) can be enforced through mixing and synthesizing layers.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "As a practical instantiation of the framework, we propose MDNets"
  - [section] "The key component of MDNets is the node group"
  - [corpus] "Soft Learning Probabilistic Circuits" (learning approaches for tractable models)
- Break condition: If the learned parameters violate the disjoint marginalized support invariant within node groups.

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map:
  - md-vtree: vtree + labeling function ψ mapping vtree nodes to marginal determinism constraints
  - MDNet: Architecture with node groups enforcing disjoint marginalized support
  - MD-calculus: Backward propagation rules for deriving tractability conditions
  - Learning: Random structure generation + parameter learning (MLE or EM)

- Critical path: md-vtree specification → MDNet structure → parameter learning → inference query execution

- Design tradeoffs:
  - More restrictive ψ(m) labels → fewer circuits respect the md-vtree but more inference queries become tractable
  - Less restrictive ψ(m) labels → more expressive circuits but fewer tractable inference queries
  - Regular md-vtrees offer optimal tradeoff between expressivity and tractability

- Failure signatures:
  - Inference queries fail → check if required marginal determinism constraints are satisfied
  - Learning produces poor models → verify that node group invariant is maintained
  - MD-calculus derivation fails → check for conflicting Q-determinism requirements

- First 3 experiments:
  1. Implement md-vtree class with label propagation for MARG and PROD operations
  2. Build MDNet with node groups enforcing disjoint marginalized support for a simple vtree
  3. Apply MD-calculus backward to derive tractability conditions for a simple causal inference query

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- Question: How can we learn the vtree and MDNet structure from data while maintaining tractability?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper mentions this as an open problem in the conclusion, stating "For more challenging datasets, how can we also learn the vtree and/or MDNet structure from data while maintaining tractability?"
- Why unresolved: Learning the optimal vtree structure is computationally hard and can impact the tractability of inference. Current methods rely on randomized structures, which may not be optimal for all datasets.
- What evidence would resolve it: An algorithm that can learn the vtree structure from data with provable guarantees on tractability and comparable performance to randomized structures would resolve this question.

### Open Question 2
- Question: For what other inference queries can we derive tractability conditions using the MD-calculus?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper concludes by asking "For what other inference queries can we derive tractability conditions using the MD-calculus?"
- Why unresolved: The MD-calculus provides a general framework for deriving tractability conditions, but its applicability to a wider range of inference queries remains to be explored.
- What evidence would resolve it: Successful application of the MD-calculus to derive tractability conditions for a diverse set of inference queries, such as maximum likelihood estimation or structure learning, would demonstrate its broader utility.

### Open Question 3
- Question: How does the expressiveness of MDNets compare to other probabilistic circuit architectures for causal inference tasks?
- Basis in paper: [inferred] The paper introduces MDNets as a new PC architecture but does not provide a comprehensive comparison to other architectures in terms of expressiveness for causal inference.
- Why unresolved: While the paper shows MDNets can handle backdoor adjustment, it's unclear how they perform compared to other architectures on a wider range of causal inference tasks.
- What evidence would resolve it: A thorough empirical comparison of MDNets to other PC architectures (e.g., PSDDs, SDDs) on various causal inference tasks would provide insights into their relative expressiveness and performance.

## Limitations
- Limited experimental validation on real-world datasets beyond synthetic data sampled from Bayesian networks
- Lack of discussion on scalability challenges for larger, more complex models
- Limited handling of missing data or unobserved variables

## Confidence
- High confidence: The theoretical framework of md-vtrees and the MD-calculus rules are sound and well-established based on the paper's rigorous proofs and the foundational work in tractable probabilistic circuits.
- Medium confidence: The practical implementation of MDNets and the learning algorithm are likely to work as described, but the lack of detailed experimental results on real-world data introduces some uncertainty.
- Low confidence: The paper's claims about the superiority of MDNets over existing approaches for causal inference in high-dimensional settings are not fully supported by the limited experimental evidence provided.
