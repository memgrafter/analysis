# Co-Learning Empirical Games and World Models

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2305.14223
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.14223
- Reference count: 40
- Primary result: Dyna-PSRO computes lower regret solutions than PSRO while requiring substantially fewer experiences

## Executive Summary
This paper introduces Dyna-PSRO, a novel algorithm that co-learns world models and empirical games for multi-agent decision-making. The approach leverages strategic diversity from empirical games to train more robust world models, which in turn reduce the computational cost of policy construction through planning. The method was evaluated on three partially observable general-sum games, demonstrating superior performance compared to the baseline PSRO algorithm in terms of both solution quality and sample efficiency.

## Method Summary
Dyna-PSRO combines model-based reinforcement learning with the Policy Space Response Oracle (PSRO) framework to create a synergistic learning system. The algorithm alternates between constructing empirical games from interaction data and using these games to train world models that can simulate future interactions. These learned world models then guide the policy improvement process, reducing the need for costly real-world interactions while maintaining strategic diversity in the policy population.

## Key Results
- Computes lower regret solutions compared to baseline PSRO algorithm
- Requires substantially fewer experiences for training
- Demonstrates mutual benefits between world model learning and empirical game construction

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The algorithm works by creating a feedback loop between empirical game construction and world model learning. Strategic diversity from empirical games provides rich training signals for world models, while learned world models enable more efficient policy construction through planning. This co-learning approach reduces the sample complexity of multi-agent reinforcement learning by replacing some real interactions with simulated ones guided by the learned world model.

## Architecture Onboarding

**Component Map**: Data Collection -> Empirical Game Construction -> World Model Training -> Policy Planning -> Data Collection

**Critical Path**: The algorithm iterates through data collection from policy interactions, empirical game construction from collected data, world model training on these games, and policy planning using the world model to generate new policies for data collection.

**Design Tradeoffs**: Balances between real interaction data collection and simulated planning. More aggressive world model use reduces sample complexity but risks model bias. The algorithm must carefully manage the exploration-exploitation tradeoff between discovering new strategies and exploiting known good policies.

**Failure Signatures**: 
- Degraded world model accuracy leading to poor policy recommendations
- Insufficient strategic diversity in empirical games causing policy collapse
- Computational overhead from world model training outweighing benefits
- Mode collapse where the policy population converges too quickly
