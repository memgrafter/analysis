# Susceptibility to Influence of Large Language Models

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2303.06074
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.06074
- Reference count: 0
- One-line primary result: GPT-3 exhibits Illusory Truth Effect and can model populist framing influence effects, though with systematic differences from human responses

## Executive Summary
This paper demonstrates that large language models, specifically GPT-3, can serve as models for understanding how influence affects human beliefs and values. Two studies show that GPT-3 exhibits the Illusory Truth Effect (believing repeated statements more than novel ones) in a pattern similar to humans, though with quantitative differences in truth ratings. The second study shows GPT-3 can model the influence of populist framing on news articles, affecting persuasion and mobilization similarly to humans, though with higher mobilization ratings. These findings suggest LLMs have potential applications for understanding psychological influence, ranging from identifying disinformation risks to developing beneficial applications like improved education and policy-making.

## Method Summary
The study employed GPT-3 (text-davinci-003) via API to simulate human responses to influence effects. For the Illusory Truth Effect experiment, 100 novel statements were exposed to simulated participants with varying attributes (truth, interest, sentiment, importance) using a Latin square design, collecting 1000 simulated ratings per statement. The Populist Framing study simulated participant demographics and relative deprivation based on Bos et al. (2020), exposing simulated participants to four versions of news articles with different framings. Ratings for persuasion and mobilization were collected and analyzed using regression models matching the human studies. Bootstrap resampling provided confidence intervals for comparisons.

## Key Results
- GPT-3 exhibits Illusory Truth Effect similar to humans, believing repeated statements more than novel ones, though GPT-3 rates statements as "definitely true" more frequently than humans
- GPT-3 models populist framing effects on persuasion comparably to humans, but shows nearly 2-unit higher mobilization ratings on 7-point scales
- Both studies demonstrate LLMs can simulate influence effects, supporting their potential as models of psychological influence despite quantitative differences

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: GPT-3 exhibits the Illusory Truth Effect (ITE) when exposed to statements and later tested for truthfulness, mirroring human responses.
- Mechanism: Repeated exposure to a statement increases the perceived truthfulness in both GPT-3 and humans due to a fluency effect where prior exposure facilitates easier processing of the statement later.
- Core assumption: GPT-3's internal processing and weighting can simulate a human-like fluency effect when statements are repeated.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract]: "64 ratings per participant were collected, using all exposure-test combinations of the attributes: truth, interest, sentiment and importance. The results for human participants reconfirmed the ITE, and demonstrated an absence of effect for attributes other than truth, and when the same attribute is used for exposure and test. The same pattern of effects was found for LLM-simulated participants."
  - [section]: "We first compare the ratings given by GPT-3 and humans to unexposed statements. Figure 4 shows that the distributions of ratings produced by humans and GPT-3 are similar, except for truth where humans are much less likely than GPT-3 to rate a statement as 6=’definitely true’."
  - [corpus]: Weak evidence - no direct studies in corpus on fluency effect simulation in GPT-3.
- Break condition: GPT-3 fails to exhibit the ITE when exposed statements are tested for truthfulness, or when other attributes are used for exposure and test.

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: GPT-3 can model the effect of populist framing on persuasion and political mobilization similar to human responses.
- Mechanism: GPT-3's responses to populist framing are influenced by the framing of news articles, affecting both persuasion and mobilization, similar to human responses.
- Core assumption: GPT-3's processing of language allows it to respond to populist framing in a manner analogous to human psychological processing.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract]: "The second demonstrates that GPT-3 can model the influence effect of populist framing of news articles on persuasiveness and political mobilization, although the modelling is not perfect."
  - [section]: "Table 7 shows that there are substantial differences between the ratings of probe statements produced by Humans and GPT-3. The mean persuasion ratings match well, but GPT-3 mobilization ratings are on average nearly 2 units (on a 7-point scale) higher than humans."
  - [corpus]: Weak evidence - limited studies in corpus directly comparing GPT-3's response to populist framing with human responses.
- Break condition: GPT-3 does not exhibit the same pattern of increased persuasion or mobilization in response to populist framing as humans.

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: GPT-3's responses to influence can be modeled and predicted based on its training and processing of natural language.
- Mechanism: GPT-3's architecture and training allow it to simulate human-like responses to influence through learned patterns in language.
- Core assumption: The transformer architecture and training data enable GPT-3 to replicate human-like influence effects.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract]: "Together the two studies support the view that LLMs have potential to act as models of the effect of influence."
  - [section]: "The capabilities of LLMs have been explored in a flurry of ‘prompt engineering’ , including the useful discovery that LLMs can make sense of instructions written in loose pseudo-code [16] – presumably because of the inclusion of code in their training corpus."
  - [corpus]: Moderate evidence - corpus includes studies on GPT-3's ability to process and respond to prompts in a human-like manner.
- Break condition: GPT-3 fails to replicate human-like influence effects in a significant number of cases, indicating limitations in its modeling capabilities.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- Question: What is the precise mechanism by which LLMs exhibit the Illusory Truth Effect? Is it a fluency-like effect, a meaning-based effect, or an accidental correlation in the training data?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper discusses various possible explanations, including fluency effects, meaning-based explanations, and parrot/accidental explanations based on training data.
- Why unresolved: The paper acknowledges that understanding the internal mechanisms of LLMs is difficult and requires further research. It suggests that different studies may be needed to distinguish between these explanations.
- What evidence would resolve it: Experiments that manipulate LLM training data, probe internal LLM representations, or compare LLM responses to different types of influence could help distinguish between these mechanisms.

### Open Question 2
- Question: How do correlations between demographic traits and relative deprivation scores in human populations affect the modeling of influence effects? Would simulating these correlations in LLM models improve the accuracy of influence modeling?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper notes that they did not simulate correlations between traits and relative deprivation in their PFN study, and suggests this could explain some mismatches between human and LLM results.
- Why unresolved: The paper did not simulate these correlations and so cannot determine their impact on the results.
- What evidence would resolve it: Repeating the PFN study with LLM models that include simulated correlations between traits and relative deprivation, and comparing the results to human data.

### Open Question 3
- Question: To what extent do the limitations of LLMs as models of human psychology (e.g., lack of emotional response, different cognitive architecture) affect their ability to model influence effects? Are there specific types of influence effects that LLMs are particularly ill-suited to model?
- Basis in paper: [inferred] The paper discusses the potential limitations of LLMs as models of human psychology and notes that some human effects were not reproduced by LLMs.
- Why unresolved: The paper does not systematically investigate the limitations of LLMs as models of human psychology or identify specific types of influence effects that may be difficult to model.
- What evidence would resolve it: Comparative studies of LLM and human responses to a wide range of influence effects, with careful consideration of the potential limitations of LLMs.

## Limitations

- GPT-3 shows systematic quantitative differences from human responses, particularly rating statements as "definitely true" more frequently than humans
- The model exhibits nearly 2-unit higher mobilization ratings compared to humans in the populist framing study
- LLM responses are based on simulations rather than actual human psychological processes, limiting direct inference about human cognition

## Confidence

- **High confidence**: GPT-3 exhibits the Illusory Truth Effect in a pattern that matches human responses for truthfulness ratings (though with quantitative differences)
- **Medium confidence**: GPT-3 can model the directional effects of populist framing on persuasion and mobilization, but with systematic biases in magnitude
- **Low confidence**: GPT-3 can serve as a comprehensive model of human susceptibility to influence across diverse psychological phenomena

## Next Checks

1. **Prompt sensitivity analysis**: Systematically vary prompt phrasing, temperature settings, and completion parameters to quantify how sensitive GPT-3's influence effect measurements are to prompt engineering choices.

2. **Cross-model comparison**: Repeat both studies with multiple LLM architectures (e.g., GPT-4, Claude, LLaMA) to determine whether observed influence effects are consistent across different model families or specific to GPT-3's architecture.

3. **Temporal stability test**: Conduct repeated measurements over time with the same prompts and statements to assess whether GPT-3's influence effect responses remain stable or drift, indicating potential training data contamination or model evolution.
