# Evaluating and Improving Tool-Augmented Computation-Intensive Math Reasoning

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2306.02408
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.02408
- Reference count: 40
- This paper introduces CARP, a dataset of 4,886 computation-intensive algebra problems with formalized annotations of intermediate steps. It finds that four tested LLMs make most errors in early reasoning steps, even when using chain-of-thought prompting. To address this, the authors propose DELI, an iterative framework that alternates tool-based verification (via SymPy interfaces) and natural-language solution revision, refining each step until convergence. DELI outperforms baselines on CARP and six other datasets, achieving 9.35% higher accuracy on CARP, and improves over standard CoT and ReAct variants.

## Executive Summary
This paper introduces CARP, a dataset of 4,886 computation-intensive algebra problems with formalized annotations of intermediate steps, and DELI, an iterative framework that alternates tool-based verification and natural-language solution revision to improve reasoning accuracy. The study finds that LLMs make most errors in early reasoning steps, even with chain-of-thought prompting, and demonstrates DELI's superior performance over baselines across multiple datasets.

## Method Summary
The authors propose DELI, an iterative framework that alternates tool-based verification and natural-language solution revision. DELI uses SymPy interfaces for tool-based verification of intermediate steps and refines solutions through iterative revision until convergence. The framework is evaluated on CARP and six other datasets, demonstrating improved performance over baselines including standard CoT and ReAct variants.

## Key Results
- DELI achieves 9.35% higher accuracy on CARP compared to baselines
- LLMs make most errors in early reasoning steps, even with chain-of-thought prompting
- DELI outperforms baselines on CARP and six other datasets

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The iterative refinement process in DELI addresses the fundamental challenge of early-stage errors in LLM reasoning. By alternating between tool-based verification and natural-language revision, the framework catches and corrects mistakes before they compound through subsequent reasoning steps. The tool-based verification provides objective correctness checks while the revision component maintains the natural language reasoning flow.

## Foundational Learning
- Symbolic computation tools (SymPy) - Why needed: Provides reliable mathematical verification independent of LLM reasoning; Quick check: Can correctly evaluate algebraic expressions
- Chain-of-thought prompting - Why needed: Establishes baseline reasoning approach for comparison; Quick check: Generates step-by-step solutions
- Iterative refinement - Why needed: Allows progressive improvement of solutions; Quick check: Each iteration improves solution accuracy

## Architecture Onboarding

**Component map:** Problem input -> LLM reasoning -> Tool verification -> Revision module -> (loop back to LLM reasoning)

**Critical path:** The core loop of LLM generation → tool verification → revision is the critical path for accuracy improvement.

**Design tradeoffs:** The framework trades computational efficiency (multiple iterations) for accuracy gains. The reliance on external tools introduces dependency on tool availability and correctness.

**Failure signatures:** Early reasoning errors cascade through subsequent steps; tool verification failures indicate either incorrect reasoning or tool limitations; revision module failures suggest inability to correct identified errors.

**First experiments:** 1) Test single-step vs multi-step verification effectiveness; 2) Compare different revision strategies (automatic vs human-assisted); 3) Evaluate impact of verification frequency on final accuracy

## Limitations
- Evaluation limited to algebra problems within CARP dataset
- Effectiveness depends heavily on quality of tool-based verification interface
- May not generalize to broader mathematical domains or real-world applications

## Confidence
- High confidence in observation that LLMs make most errors in early reasoning steps
- High confidence in DELI's improved performance metrics
- Medium confidence in iterative refinement being the key driver of success

## Next Checks
1. Test DELI's performance on non-algebra mathematical domains (calculus, geometry, statistics) to assess generalizability beyond the CARP dataset's scope.
2. Conduct ablation studies comparing DELI's full framework against variants that use only tool verification without iterative revision, or vice versa, to isolate the contribution of each component.
3. Evaluate DELI's robustness when using alternative symbolic computation tools or when the tool interface has limited coverage of mathematical operations to understand dependency on specific implementation choices.
