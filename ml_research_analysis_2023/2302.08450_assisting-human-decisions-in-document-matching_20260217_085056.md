# Assisting Human Decisions in Document Matching

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2302.08450
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2302.08450
- Reference count: 25
- Participants (N=271) showed 26% accuracy improvement and 20% faster completion using task-specific highlighting methods versus control

## Executive Summary
This paper investigates how different types of assistive information affect human performance in document matching tasks. Through a crowdsourced study with 271 participants, the research compares three assistive methods—black-box explanations (SHAP), extractive summarization (BERTSum), and task-specific highlighting (co-occurrence and semantic similarity)—against a control group. Task-specific methods significantly improved both accuracy and speed, while SHAP and BERTSum decreased performance. Notably, participants perceived all highlighted information as helpful despite objective metrics showing otherwise, revealing a disconnect between perceived and actual utility.

## Method Summary
The study used CNN/DailyMail dataset summaries and articles to create a document matching task where participants selected relevant articles from candidate sets. Four assistive methods were tested: SHAP for black-box model explanations, BERTSum for extractive summarization, and two task-specific methods using co-occurrence (ROUGE-L) and semantic (Sentence-BERT) similarity measures. The methods highlighted sentences and phrases in candidate articles based on similarity to summary sentences. 271 U.S. adult participants were randomly assigned to five treatment groups (including control) and completed 16 questions each (4 easy, 12 hard) with 3-minute time limits and bonus payments for accuracy and speed.

## Key Results
- Task-specific highlighting methods improved accuracy by 26% and speed by 20% compared to control
- SHAP and BERTSum decreased accuracy, with BERTSum also increasing completion time
- Participants perceived all highlighted information as helpful despite objective performance metrics showing otherwise
- The disconnect between perceived and actual utility was particularly notable for SHAP and BERTSum

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: Task-specific highlighting methods improve accuracy by guiding attention to document details that distinguish correct matches from distractors
- Mechanism: The methods compute similarity between each summary sentence and candidate article sentences, then highlight the most relevant sentences and matching phrases. This hierarchical highlighting helps users quickly identify precise information confirming a match
- Core assumption: Users can effectively utilize highlighted information when it points to distinguishing details
- Evidence anchors: Task-specific methods help participants be quicker and more accurate compared to control; methods operate at sentence and phrase-level information
- Break condition: If similarity measures fail to identify truly distinguishing information, or if users ignore the hierarchical structure

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: Black-box explanations (SHAP) decrease accuracy by highlighting model-centric importance rather than user-relevant information
- Mechanism: SHAP highlights tokens based on attribution to model predictions, but these may not correspond to specific details users need to distinguish correct matches
- Core assumption: Information highlighted by SHAP is not aligned with what users need for accurate decisions in this task
- Evidence anchors: SHAP decreases participants' matching performance compared to control; SHAP highlights important input tokens for model predictions
- Break condition: If SHAP highlights align with distinguishing details by chance, or if users adapt their strategy to use SHAP effectively

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: Extractive summarization (BERTSum) decreases performance by providing too much or too little information
- Mechanism: BERTSum extracts key sentences that may not contain specific details needed to distinguish correct matches from distractors, while potentially overwhelming users
- Core assumption: Extracted sentences from BERTSum do not provide precise details users need for accurate matching decisions
- Evidence anchors: BERTSum decreases accuracy and increases completion time; BERTSum highlights key sentences to reduce cognitive load
- Break condition: If BERTSum extracts contain distinguishing details by chance, or if users adapt their strategy to use BERTSum highlights effectively

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- Question: Does the effectiveness of task-specific methods generalize to longer, more complex documents beyond summary-article matching?
- Basis in paper: The paper suggests the proxy task can be used as a first-pass test, but notes complex and lengthy documents may require different adaptations
- Why unresolved: Current study uses relatively short documents; longer documents may need different visualization approaches
- What evidence would resolve it: Testing same methods on longer documents like academic papers with appropriate interface adaptations

### Open Question 2
- Question: How does the amount and specificity of highlighted information affect task performance?
- Basis in paper: Participants using SHAP most strongly agreed there were too many highlights; this is noted as open research question
- Why unresolved: Study used fixed number of highlights but didn't systematically vary amount to find optimal levels
- What evidence would resolve it: Experiments varying number of highlighted sentences/phrases and measuring impact on accuracy and completion time

### Open Question 3
- Question: Why does BERTSum decrease accuracy and increase completion time despite being designed to reduce cognitive load?
- Basis in paper: Found BERTSum decreased accuracy and increased completion time, contrary to expectations
- Why unresolved: Paper doesn't investigate specific mechanisms by which BERTSum negatively impacts performance
- What evidence would resolve it: Qualitative analysis of which types of information BERTSum highlights versus omits

### Open Question 4
- Question: Can the disconnect between perceived and actual utility of assistive information be mitigated?
- Basis in paper: Participants overwhelmingly perceived all highlighted information as helpful despite objective metrics showing otherwise
- Why unresolved: Study didn't test interventions to align user perceptions with actual performance
- What evidence would resolve it: Testing whether guidance on interpreting highlights or adaptive highlighting systems can improve alignment

## Limitations

- Uses proxy task (summary-article matching) rather than target application (paper-reviewer assignment), limiting external validity
- Manual classification of questions as "easy" or "hard" introduces potential subjectivity
- Findings may not generalize to other document matching domains like legal or medical literature review

## Confidence

- **High Confidence**: Task-specific highlighting methods significantly improve both accuracy (26% increase) and speed (20% faster) compared to control
- **Medium Confidence**: Negative effects of SHAP and BERTSum, though effect sizes are modest and disconnect between perceived and actual utility warrants caution
- **Low Confidence**: Generalizability of findings to other document matching domains beyond summary-article matching

## Next Checks

1. Replicate with domain-specific data: Conduct same experiment using actual paper-reviewer assignment data to validate task-specific highlighting benefits in target application domain

2. Test alternative similarity metrics: Compare current co-occurrence and semantic similarity methods against other metrics (TF-IDF, BM25) to determine if benefits represent broader principle

3. Investigate perception-performance gap: Design follow-up studies to understand why participants perceive SHAP and BERTSum as helpful despite negative/neutral performance effects, potentially through think-aloud protocols or post-task interviews
