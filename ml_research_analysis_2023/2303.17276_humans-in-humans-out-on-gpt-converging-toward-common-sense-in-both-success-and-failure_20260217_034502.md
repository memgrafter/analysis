# Humans in Humans Out: On GPT Converging Toward Common Sense in both Success and Failure

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2303.17276
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.17276
- Reference count: 25
- Key outcome: GPT models increasingly produce human-like reasoning patterns, including fallacies, as they scale up

## Executive Summary
This study investigates whether large language models approximate human reasoning patterns, including systematic errors, when trained on human-generated text. Using the ETR61 benchmark - 61 inference and judgment problems from the Erotetic Theory of Reason - researchers tested GPT-3, GPT-3.5, and GPT-4 against both correct and fallacious human responses. The findings reveal a clear trend: as models scale, they produce more human-like reasoning outputs, including the very fallacies that characterize human cognitive biases. GPT-3 achieved 59% ETR-predicted outputs, increasing to 77% in GPT-3.5 and 75% in GPT-4, with fallacious judgments rising from 18% to 33% to 34% respectively. ETR-inspired prompt engineering showed promise in reducing these mistakes by encouraging broader question consideration.

## Method Summary
The researchers employed the ETR61 benchmark, a carefully constructed set of 61 inference and judgment problems based on the Erotetic Theory of Reason. They tested three model versions - GPT-3, GPT-3.5, and GPT-4 - against both correct and fallacious human responses to measure convergence toward human-like reasoning patterns. The study used quantitative metrics to track the percentage of ETR-predicted outputs and fallacious judgments across model versions, with additional experiments testing ETR-inspired prompt engineering approaches to mitigate reasoning errors.

## Key Results
- GPT-3 produced ETR-predicted outputs for 59% of examples, increasing to 77% in GPT-3.5 and 75% in GPT-4
- Human-like fallacious judgments rose from 18% in GPT-3 to 33% in GPT-3.5 and 34% in GPT-4
- ETR-inspired prompt engineering showed promise in reducing reasoning mistakes by encouraging broader question consideration

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The convergence toward human-like reasoning patterns, including systematic errors, emerges from the fundamental training approach of large language models. When trained on human-generated text at scale, models learn to reproduce not just correct reasoning but also the patterns, biases, and fallacies inherent in human cognition. This creates a paradox where increased model capability and scale lead to more human-like outputs, including human weaknesses. The ETR-inspired prompt engineering approach works by explicitly guiding models to consider questions more broadly and systematically, counteracting some of the implicit biases learned from training data.

## Limitations
- The sample size of 61 problems may not capture the full range of human reasoning patterns or provide sufficient statistical power
- The ETR61 benchmark may not generalize to other reasoning paradigms or domains
- The study assumes human fallacies represent a meaningful target rather than errors to be avoided
