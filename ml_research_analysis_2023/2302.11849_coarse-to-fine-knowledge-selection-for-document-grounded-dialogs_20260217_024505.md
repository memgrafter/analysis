# Coarse-to-Fine Knowledge Selection for Document Grounded Dialogs

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID**: 2302.11849
- **Source URL**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2302.11849
- **Reference count**: 0
- **Key outcome**: Re3G optimizes both coarse-grained passage retrieval and fine-grained span extraction for document-grounded dialogues, outperforming baselines on DialDoc Shared Task.

## Executive Summary
This paper addresses the challenge of knowledge selection for document-grounded dialogue systems by proposing Re3G, a method that optimizes both coarse-grained passage retrieval and fine-grained span extraction in a unified framework. The approach consists of three components: a Retriever for efficient passage retrieval, a Reranker for capturing deeper interactions between context and passages, and a joint Knowledge Refinement and Generation model for extracting relevant spans and generating responses. Experiments on the DialDoc Shared Task demonstrate that both coarse and fine-grained knowledge selection are essential for effective response generation, with early fusion outperforming FiD for incorporating retrieved knowledge.

## Method Summary
Re3G is a three-component system for document-grounded dialogue response generation. The Retriever uses a BERT-base bi-encoder architecture trained in three phases (negative log likelihood loss, vanilla generation guidance, and teacher-student learning with the Reranker) to efficiently retrieve relevant passages. The Reranker employs RoBERTa to capture deeper interactions between dialogue context and candidate passages using InfoNCE loss with dynamic negative sampling. Finally, the Knowledge Refinement and Generation component jointly models span extraction and response generation using prompt-based multi-task learning with a T5 sequence-to-sequence model, incorporating retrieved passages through early fusion concatenation.

## Key Results
- Re3G achieves state-of-the-art performance on the DialDoc Shared Task, demonstrating the effectiveness of coarse-to-fine knowledge selection
- Both coarse-grained passage retrieval and fine-grained span extraction are essential for response generation, with ablation studies showing significant performance drops when either component is removed
- Early fusion outperforms Fusion-in-Decoder (FiD) for incorporating retrieved knowledge into generation, allowing richer interactions via self-attention
- Using a larger pre-trained model (T5-3b) further improves performance compared to T5-large

- Core assumption: Relevant knowledge can be decomposed into passage-level and span-level granularities, with different retrieval strategies optimal for each level.

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map: Retriever (BERT-base bi-encoder) -> Reranker (RoBERTa) -> Knowledge Refinement and Generation (T5-large)
- Critical path: Retriever → Reranker → Knowledge Refinement → Generation
  - Each component must complete successfully for the next to function
- Design tradeoffs:
  - Retriever efficiency vs. Reranker accuracy (24 candidates vs. 100 candidates at inference)
  - Joint training complexity vs. potential performance gains
  - Early fusion interaction richness vs. FiD computational efficiency
  - Pre-trained model size (T5-large vs. T5-3b) vs. training/inference costs
- Failure signatures:
  - Low retrieval recall: Retriever not finding relevant passages
  - Poor reranking: Top passages from Retriever not improved by Reranker
  - Knowledge refinement failure: Extracted spans not relevant or too long/short
  - Generation issues: Responses not grounded in extracted knowledge
- First 3 experiments:
  1. Ablation study: Test Re3G with only Retriever (no Reranker, no refinement) to establish baseline
  2. Retrieval quality analysis: Measure passage recall@5 before and after Reranking
  3. Knowledge refinement evaluation: Assess extracted span quality using ROUGE or span-level F1 metrics

## Limitations
- The coarse-to-fine approach assumes relevant information can be effectively decomposed into passage and span levels, which may not hold for all document-grounded dialogue scenarios
- The method's generalization to other domains or document types remains uncertain, as experiments were limited to the DialDoc Shared Task dataset
- The computational overhead of maintaining three separate models (Retriever, Reranker, and Refinement/Generation) may limit scalability for production systems with large document collections

## Confidence
- **High confidence**: The effectiveness of joint training for knowledge refinement and generation (supported by ablation results showing significant improvement when refinement is included)
- **Medium confidence**: The superiority of early fusion over FiD for incorporating retrieved knowledge (supported by performance comparisons, but limited to this specific task and dataset)
- **Medium confidence**: The importance of coarse-to-fine knowledge selection for document-grounded dialogues (demonstrated on DialDoc, but needs validation on other datasets)

## Next Checks
1. **Cross-domain validation**: Test Re3G on other document-grounded dialogue datasets beyond DialDoc (e.g., QReCC or HotpotQA) to assess generalization of the coarse-to-fine approach across different domains and document types.

3. **Knowledge granularity sensitivity analysis**: Systematically vary the passage length and span extraction parameters to determine how sensitive the model's performance is to these granularity choices, identifying optimal configurations for different document types.
