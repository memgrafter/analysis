# Noisy Positive-Unlabeled Learning with Self-Training for Speculative Knowledge Graph Reasoning

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2306.07512
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.07512
- **Reference count:** 35
- **Primary result:** 10.3% relative improvement over 11 baselines in speculative reasoning on knowledge graphs with noise

## Executive Summary
This paper addresses the challenge of speculative reasoning on real-world knowledge graphs that contain both false negative (missing true facts) and false positive (unreliable facts) issues. The authors formulate this as a noisy Positive-Unlabeled (nPU) learning problem, where collected facts are treated as noisy positive samples and uncollected facts as unlabeled samples. The proposed nPUGraph framework uses a variational approach to jointly estimate the correctness of both collected and uncollected facts while updating model parameters during training. This approach improves robustness against false positive links and identifies missing facts to provide high-quality reasoning grounds, unified through a self-training procedure.

## Method Summary
nPUGraph is a variational framework that simultaneously estimates label posteriors and updates model parameters through self-training. The framework treats collected facts as noisy positive samples and uncollected facts as unlabeled samples, enabling it to handle both false negatives and false positives in knowledge graphs. The variational approach allows for joint optimization of label posterior estimation and model training, while the self-training procedure iteratively refines both the estimated correctness of facts and the reasoning model. This creates a feedback loop where improved label posteriors lead to better model training, which in turn produces more accurate label posteriors.

## Key Results
- nPUGraph outperforms eleven state-of-the-art baselines with 10.3% relative improvement on average
- Demonstrated effectiveness across three benchmark knowledge graphs (FB15K-237, WN18RR, YAGO3-10) and one Twitter dataset
- Shows robust performance across various degrees of false negative/positive cases

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The framework works by simultaneously learning two interdependent tasks: estimating the correctness of facts (label posterior) and performing knowledge graph reasoning. The variational formulation allows for principled handling of uncertainty in the label estimation process. The self-training procedure creates a virtuous cycle where improved label estimates lead to better reasoning, which provides more accurate labels for the next iteration. This joint optimization is particularly effective because it addresses both sources of noise in the data simultaneously rather than treating them as separate problems.

## Foundational Learning

**Noisy Positive-Unlabeled (nPU) Learning**: A learning paradigm where positive samples are corrupted with noise and unlabeled samples exist alongside them. Needed because real-world KGs have both missing true facts and unreliable facts. Quick check: Verify that the noise model assumptions align with the actual error patterns in the KG.

**Variational Inference**: A framework for approximating intractable probability distributions. Needed to jointly estimate label posteriors and model parameters in a principled probabilistic manner. Quick check: Ensure the variational family is flexible enough to capture the true posterior distributions.

**Self-Training**: A semi-supervised learning technique where a model iteratively refines its own training data. Needed to create a feedback loop between label estimation and model training. Quick check: Monitor for self-training divergence or overfitting to noisy labels.

## Architecture Onboarding

**Component Map:** KG Encoder -> Label Posterior Estimator -> Model Parameter Updater -> Self-Training Loop

**Critical Path:** Input facts → Graph encoder produces embeddings → Label posterior estimation identifies reliable/unreliable facts → Model parameters updated based on refined labels → Self-training iteration → Improved predictions

**Design Tradeoffs:** The variational approach provides principled uncertainty handling but adds computational complexity compared to deterministic methods. The self-training loop improves accuracy but requires careful monitoring for convergence and stability.

**Failure Signatures:** Model divergence in self-training iterations, overfitting to noisy labels, or poor performance on KGs with different noise patterns than training data.

**First Experiments:** 1) Ablation study removing self-training to measure its contribution, 2) Sensitivity analysis of noise levels on performance, 3) Comparison of variational vs. non-variational label estimation approaches.

## Limitations

- **Generalizability to Different KG Domains:** The evaluation focuses on standard benchmarks and one Twitter dataset; effectiveness on biomedical, financial, or other domain KGs remains uncertain.
- **Computational Complexity:** The paper lacks detailed analysis of the overhead introduced by the variational framework and self-training, which could be problematic for very large-scale KGs.
- **Hyperparameter Sensitivity:** Performance improvements are reported with specific settings, but sensitivity to these choices is not thoroughly explored.
