# Offline Reinforcement Learning with Additional Covering Distributions

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID**: 2305.12679
- **Source URL**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.12679
- **Reference count**: 38
- **Primary result**: Introduces a method for sample-efficient offline RL using covering distributions that trade off prior knowledge of optimal policies against dataset coverage requirements

## Executive Summary
This paper addresses the challenge of learning optimal policies from logged datasets in offline reinforcement learning with function approximation. The key innovation is leveraging an additional "covering distribution" that encodes prior knowledge about optimal trajectories, enabling sample-efficient learning under weak assumptions. By assuming the covering distribution covers all non-stationary near-optimal policies, the authors demonstrate that sample-efficient offline RL becomes possible even with partial dataset coverage and weak realizable function classes. The proposed VOPR algorithm achieves finite-sample guarantees for general Markov decision processes under these weak assumptions, representing a significant advance over existing methods that require strong assumptions like exploratory data coverage or Bellman-completeness.

## Method Summary
The paper proposes VOPR, an algorithm based on marginalized importance sampling (MIS) that uses a covering distribution to trade off between prior knowledge of optimal policies and dataset coverage requirements. The method estimates the optimal value function Q⋆ using MIS with both the dataset and covering distribution, then derives an optimal policy using a policy ratio function class. The approach relies on weak realizability assumptions and near-optimal concentrability, which are significantly weaker than standard offline RL assumptions. The covering distribution allows the algorithm to relax the requirement that the dataset covers all possible trajectories, instead only requiring coverage of trajectories induced by an optimal policy from the covering distribution.

## Key Results
- VOPR achieves finite-sample guarantees for general MDPs under weak realizability assumptions and partial dataset coverage
- The method demonstrates that sample-efficient offline RL is possible by trading off prior knowledge (via covering distribution) against data coverage requirements
- First finite-sample result showing sample-efficient offline RL with only partial coverage datasets and weak realizable function classes given additional side information of a covering distribution

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- **Claim**: A covering distribution allows trade-off between prior knowledge of optimal trajectories and dataset coverage requirements.
- **Mechanism**: The covering distribution dc is designed to cover all non-stationary near-optimal policies. By choosing dc carefully, the algorithm can relax the requirement that the dataset covers all possible trajectories, instead only requiring coverage of trajectories induced by an optimal policy from dc.
- **Core assumption**: The covering distribution covers all non-stationary near-optimal policies, and the dataset covers trajectories induced by an optimal policy from the covering distribution.
- **Evidence anchors**: The authors state that "the covering distribution trades off prior knowledge of the optimal trajectories against the coverage requirement of the dataset."

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim**: All near-optimal concentrability allows policy optimization under weak realizability assumptions.
- **Mechanism**: Instead of requiring the dataset to cover all possible policies, the algorithm only requires that the covering distribution covers all non-stationary near-optimal policies. This weaker assumption, combined with the ability to estimate Q⋆ accurately under the covering distribution, enables policy optimization without strong function class assumptions.
- **Core assumption**: The covering distribution covers all non-stationary near-optimal policies (Assumption 1).
- **Evidence anchors**: "we provide finite-sample guarantees for a simple algorithm based on marginalized importance sampling (MIS), showing that sample-efficient offline RL for general MDPs is possible with only a partial coverage dataset and weak realizable function classes given additional side information of a covering distribution."

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim**: MIS with policy ratio class enables accurate estimation of Q⋆ under the covering distribution.
- **Mechanism**: The algorithm uses marginalized importance sampling with a policy ratio function class B to estimate Q⋆. By optimizing over both the value function class Q and the policy ratio class B, the algorithm can accurately estimate Q⋆ under the covering distribution, even with weak realizability assumptions.
- **Core assumption**: The policy ratio class B can realize the optimal policy ratio (Assumption 4).
- **Evidence anchors**: "We provide finite-sample guarantees for a simple algorithm based on marginalized importance sampling (MIS), showing that sample-efficient offline RL for general MDPs is possible..."

## Architecture Onboarding

- **Component map**: Dataset D -> VOPR Algorithm (MIS-based Q⋆ estimation) -> Policy Ratio Class B -> Optimal Policy π⋆

- **Critical path**:
  1. Choose a covering distribution dc based on prior knowledge of optimal policies.
  2. Estimate Q⋆ using MIS with the dataset D and covering distribution dc.
  3. Derive the optimal policy using the estimated Q⋆ and policy ratio class B.

- **Design tradeoffs**:
  - Trade-off between prior knowledge of optimal policies and dataset coverage: More refined covering distributions allow weaker dataset coverage assumptions but require more prior knowledge.
  - Trade-off between function class complexity and estimation accuracy: More complex function classes may lead to better approximations but also increase the risk of overfitting and computational complexity.

- **Failure signatures**:
  - Inaccurate estimation of Q⋆: This can lead to suboptimal policy learning.
  - Poor choice of covering distribution: If the covering distribution does not accurately represent the optimal policy space, the algorithm may fail to find a near-optimal policy.
  - Insufficient dataset coverage: Even with a good covering distribution, if the dataset does not contain sufficient coverage of trajectories from the covering distribution, the algorithm may fail.

- **First 3 experiments**:
  1. Implement Algorithm 1 with a simple MDP and a covering distribution that covers a subset of near-optimal policies. Verify that the algorithm can learn a near-optimal policy with less dataset coverage than required by standard offline RL methods.
  2. Vary the complexity of the covering distribution (e.g., linear combination of near-optimal policies) and measure its impact on the required dataset coverage and the learned policy's performance.
  3. Compare the performance of Algorithm 1 with other offline RL methods (e.g., pessimistic approaches) on MDPs with different levels of dataset coverage and prior knowledge of optimal policies.

- The paper does not provide concrete methods for constructing or estimating the covering distribution from real datasets
- Lack of empirical validation leaves uncertainty about the algorithm's performance relative to existing methods in practical settings
