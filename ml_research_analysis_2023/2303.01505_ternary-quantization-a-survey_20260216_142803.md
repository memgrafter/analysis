# Ternary Quantization: A Survey

## Executive Summary
This survey provides a comprehensive overview of ternary quantization methods for deep neural networks, where weights are constrained to three values: -1, 0, and 1. The paper systematically categorizes projection methods into direct (fully or partially discrete) and indirect (gradual or stochastic discrete) approaches, while examining optimization techniques including estimation-based, optimization-based, and greedy search-based methods. The authors establish theoretical connections between various quantization methods through proximal optimization and reveal intrinsic relationships among different ternary quantization approaches. This work serves as a valuable reference for understanding the evolution and current state of ternary quantization in neural network compression.

## Method Summary
The survey systematically reviews ternary quantization approaches by categorizing them based on their projection mechanisms and optimization strategies. Direct projection methods apply explicit rounding functions to convert continuous weights to ternary values, while indirect methods gradually transform weights through stochastic sampling or gradual conversion processes. The optimization component focuses on determining appropriate scaling factors and thresholds, with three main approaches: estimation-based (using statistical properties), optimization-based (formulating as optimization problems), and greedy search-based (iterative refinement). The paper establishes theoretical foundations through proximal optimization, demonstrating connections between Straight-Through Estimator, dual-averaging, and proximal operators, providing a unified framework for understanding various ternary quantization methods.

## Key Results
- Ternary quantization reduces model size and computational complexity by constraining weights to three values (-1, 0, 1)
- Direct projection methods include weight grouping, enlarging, stochastic rounding, and temperature adjusting
- Indirect projection methods use gradual conversion or stochastic sampling approaches
- Three optimization techniques exist for determining scaling factors and thresholds: estimation-based, optimization-based, and greedy search-based
- Proximal optimization reveals intrinsic relationships among various ternary quantization methods

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
Ternary quantization works by reducing the representational capacity of neural network weights to three discrete values, which dramatically reduces memory requirements and computational complexity. The mechanism relies on careful projection functions that map continuous weight values to the ternary space while preserving as much information as possible. The optimization techniques ensure that scaling factors and thresholds are appropriately chosen to minimize information loss during quantization. The proximal optimization framework provides theoretical guarantees for convergence and reveals how different quantization approaches are fundamentally related through their underlying optimization objectives.

## Foundational Learning
- Proximal operators: Mathematical tools for solving optimization problems involving non-smooth functions; needed for understanding the theoretical foundation of quantization methods; quick check: verify gradient descent converges to the same solution as proximal gradient descent for L1 regularization.
- Straight-Through Estimator (STE): A gradient approximation technique that allows backpropagation through discrete operations; needed for training quantized networks; quick check: compare STE gradients with exact gradients for simple quantization functions.
- Stochastic rounding: A technique that randomly rounds values based on probability distributions; needed for reducing quantization bias; quick check: measure variance reduction when using stochastic vs deterministic rounding.
- Temperature-based scaling: A method for controlling the sharpness of quantization boundaries; needed for fine-tuning quantization sensitivity; quick check: plot quantization error vs temperature parameter.
- Greedy search optimization: Iterative refinement technique for finding optimal parameters; needed for efficient threshold and scaling factor selection; quick check: measure convergence speed compared to grid search.
- Dual-averaging methods: Optimization techniques that accumulate historical gradients; needed for stable training of quantized networks; quick check: compare training stability with and without dual-averaging.
