# Causal isotonic calibration for heterogeneous treatment effects

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2302.14011
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2302.14011
- Reference count: 40
- Primary result: Proposed method achieves fast doubly-robust calibration rates under weak conditions

## Executive Summary
This paper introduces causal isotonic calibration, a nonparametric method for calibrating heterogeneous treatment effect (HTE) predictors. The approach ensures that the average treatment effect among individuals with identical predictions matches their shared prediction value. By using isotonic regression on a doubly-robust pseudo-outcome, the method can calibrate any black-box HTE predictor while maintaining theoretical guarantees. The authors also propose cross-calibration, which eliminates the need for hold-out calibration sets by using cross-fitted predictors and all available data.

## Method Summary
The authors develop a framework for calibrating HTE predictors using isotonic regression on a doubly-robust pseudo-outcome. The method takes any existing HTE predictor and adjusts its predictions to ensure that the average treatment effect among individuals with identical predicted values is close to that value. The cross-calibration variant further improves data efficiency by avoiding separate calibration sets through cross-fitting. Under weak conditions, the approach achieves fast doubly-robust calibration rates, requiring only that either the propensity score or outcome regression be estimated accurately.

## Key Results
- Causal isotonic calibration achieves fast doubly-robust calibration rates under weak conditions
- Cross-calibration variant eliminates need for hold-out calibration sets while maintaining performance
- Simulation studies show improved calibration and preserved/enhanced predictive performance, particularly for tree-based methods

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The method works by leveraging isotonic regression on a doubly-robust pseudo-outcome to adjust HTE predictions. The doubly-robust construction ensures that calibration is achieved as long as either the propensity score or outcome regression is estimated accurately, providing robustness to model misspecification. The isotonic regression step enforces the calibration constraint that predicted values equal average treatment effects for groups with identical predictions.

## Foundational Learning
1. Isotonic regression - non-decreasing function fitting for calibration; needed to enforce monotonic constraints on predictions
2. Doubly-robust estimation - combines propensity score and outcome regression; needed for robustness to model misspecification
3. Cross-fitting - sample splitting for variance reduction; needed to avoid overfitting while using all available data
4. Heterogeneous treatment effects - individual-level causal effects; needed as the target of prediction
5. Calibration curves - graphical assessment of prediction accuracy; needed to evaluate method performance
6. Super learner - ensemble machine learning for prediction; needed as potential base HTE predictors
