# Rectifying Group Irregularities in Explanations for Distribution Shift

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2305.16308
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.16308
- Reference count: 20
- Primary result: GSE improves feasibility (up to 28%) and robustness (up to 42%) compared to state-of-the-art methods while maintaining or enhancing overall explanation quality.

## Executive Summary
This paper addresses group irregularities in distribution shift explanations, where standard methods fail to preserve subpopulation structures when explaining how to transform source data to match target distributions. The authors introduce Group-aware Shift Explanations (GSE), a framework that leverages worst-group optimization to ensure explanations preserve group membership across demographic or hierarchical subpopulations. By maximizing the minimum PercentExplained (PE) across pre-specified groups, GSE achieves significantly better feasibility and robustness metrics while maintaining overall explanation quality.

## Method Summary
GSE is a general framework that wraps around existing shift explanation methods (K-cluster transport, optimal transport, DiCE) and optimizes them using worst-group objectives. The method takes source and target distributions along with group labels, then learns a mapping function that maximizes the minimum PE across all groups while still achieving good overall distribution alignment. For non-tabular data, GSE uses a featurization-reverse featurization pipeline to apply the method to language and image datasets.

## Key Results
- GSE achieves up to 28% improvement in feasibility metrics compared to baseline methods
- GSE improves robustness by up to 42% while maintaining overall explanation quality
- The method successfully preserves group structures across tabular, language, and image datasets

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: Group-aware shift explanations preserve subpopulation structures by optimizing worst-group PE instead of overall PE.
- Mechanism: The method maximizes the minimum PercentExplained (PE) across pre-specified groups using worst-group optimization, which implicitly improves PE for all groups while still maintaining focus on overall distribution shift.
- Core assumption: The worst-group PE maximization will naturally improve PE for all other groups without explicitly optimizing for each one.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract]: "GSE leverages worst-group optimization to preserve group structures"
  - [section 3.2]: "We propose Group-aware Shift Explanations (GSE), an explanation method for distribution shift that preserves groups in the data"
- Break condition: If group definitions are poorly chosen or don't capture meaningful subpopulations, the worst-group optimization may optimize for irrelevant or noisy group boundaries.

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: GSE improves both feasibility and robustness by avoiding explanations that cross group boundaries.
- Mechanism: By constraining the explanation mapping to preserve group membership, GSE avoids changes that would be considered unfeasible (like changing gender in the Adult dataset) and reduces sensitivity to perturbations that might cause group-crossing modifications.
- Core assumption: Group-crossing explanations are inherently less feasible and robust than explanations that preserve group membership.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [section 2.2.1]: "Shift explanations which break apart groups of the data are not only problematic because they degrade on the subpopulation level, but they can also be overall infeasible"
  - [section 3.4]: "GSE takes groups into account, we can enhance an explanation's feasibility by constructing groups using the unactionable attributes"
- Break condition: If all feasible changes within groups are already exhausted, the method may still need to cross groups to achieve good overall shift explanation quality.

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: The unified framework allows GSE to generalize across different explanation methods and data types.
- Mechanism: By parameterizing the mapping function M(x; θ) and objective function L, GSE can be applied to K-cluster transport, optimal transport, and DiCE methods, and extended to language and image data through featurization and reverse featurization steps.
- Core assumption: Different shift explanation methods can be expressed in a common parameterized framework that GSE can optimize over.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [section 3.3.1]: "Generalizing to other shift explanation methods" and discussion of M(x; θ) and L(θ; M, P, Q)
  - [section 3.3.2]: "Generalizing to language and image data" with featurization pipeline
- Break condition: If a particular shift explanation method cannot be expressed in the required parameterized form, GSE cannot be directly applied.

## Foundational Learning

- Concept: Wasserstein distance and its role in measuring distribution shift
  - Why needed here: The PercentExplained metric relies on Wasserstein-2 distance to quantify how well the explanation reduces the gap between source and target distributions
  - Quick check question: If the Wasserstein distance between source and target is 10, and after applying an explanation it becomes 4, what is the PercentExplained?

- Concept: Counterfactual explanations and their adaptation for distribution shift
  - Why needed here: DiCE is adapted from counterfactual explanation methods to explain distribution shift by finding counterfactual examples that would be classified as target samples
  - Quick check question: How does finding counterfactual examples for a source vs target classifier differ from finding counterfactual examples for a regular classification model?

- Concept: Group DRO (Distributionally Robust Optimization) and worst-group optimization
  - Why needed here: GSE uses group DRO to optimize the worst-group PE, which is the key mechanism for preserving group structures
  - Quick check question: In what way does optimizing for worst-group performance differ from optimizing for average performance across all groups?

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map: Data preprocessing -> Featurization (if needed) -> Base explanation method -> GSE wrapper -> Evaluation
- Critical path: Featurization → Base explanation method → GSE optimization → Evaluation
- Design tradeoffs:
  - More groups provide better structure preservation but increase computational cost
  - Tighter worst-group optimization (lower λ) better preserves groups but may reduce overall PE
  - Featurization quality directly impacts explanation quality for non-tabular data
- Failure signatures:
  - Low WG-PE despite high overall PE indicates group irregularities
  - Feasibility much lower than 100% suggests the explanation is making unfeasible changes
  - Large gap between robustness and worst-case robustness indicates sensitivity to specific perturbations
- First 3 experiments:
  1. Run vanilla K-cluster on Adult dataset and compare PE vs WG-PE to observe group irregularities
  2. Apply GSE to the same dataset and verify WG-PE improvement while maintaining reasonable overall PE
  3. Test robustness by perturbing source features and comparing explanation stability between vanilla and GSE versions

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- Question: How does the choice of aggregation function F in Equation 5 affect the performance of GSE across different datasets and explanation methods?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper mentions experimenting with different aggregation functions like max X and ∑x∈X x, but notes that F(X) = ∑x∈X x is only applicable to certain loss functions.
- Why unresolved: The paper doesn't provide a comprehensive analysis of how different aggregation functions impact GSE's performance across various datasets and explanation methods. This choice could significantly influence the balance between worst-group performance and overall performance.
- What evidence would resolve it: A systematic study comparing GSE performance with different aggregation functions (max, mean, weighted combinations, etc.) across multiple datasets and explanation methods, showing how this choice affects feasibility, robustness, and overall explanation quality.

### Open Question 2
- Question: How does GSE perform when groups are not predefined but need to be discovered from the data?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper mentions that group labels may not always be available and discusses using pretrained models or unsupervised methods for grouping data, with a brief experiment on language data using K-means clustering.
- Why unresolved: The paper provides limited exploration of unsupervised group discovery methods. It's unclear how well GSE performs when groups need to be automatically discovered, especially in complex high-dimensional datasets.
- What evidence would resolve it: Extensive experiments comparing GSE performance using different unsupervised grouping methods (clustering algorithms, contrastive learning, etc.) across various datasets, showing how the quality of discovered groups affects GSE's effectiveness.

### Open Question 3
- Question: What is the impact of group granularity on GSE's performance, and how can we determine optimal group sizes?
- Basis in paper: [inferred] The paper mentions that groups are highly context-specific and shows experiments with different numbers of groups (e.g., 3 groups for Breast dataset, 2 groups for Adult), but doesn't explore how varying group granularity affects results.
- Why unresolved: The paper doesn't investigate how the number and size of groups affect GSE's ability to preserve group structures while maintaining overall explanation quality. Too few groups might miss important subpopulations, while too many might overfit to noise.
- What evidence would resolve it: A systematic study varying the number of groups across different datasets and measuring the impact on WG-PE, feasibility, and robustness, potentially revealing an optimal balance between group granularity and explanation quality.

## Limitations
- The effectiveness of worst-group optimization depends heavily on proper group definition, but the paper provides limited guidance on when and how to define groups for different data types.
- The unified framework assumes all shift explanation methods can be parameterized in the same way, but this may not hold for more complex or non-parametric methods.
- The trade-off between overall PE and WG-PE is controlled by a single hyperparameter λ, but the sensitivity of results to this choice is not thoroughly explored.

## Confidence
- **High**: The core mechanism of worst-group optimization for improving group-aware shift explanations is well-supported by the experimental results
- **Medium**: The framework's ability to generalize across different explanation methods and data types is demonstrated but not exhaustively tested
- **Medium**: The improvements in feasibility and robustness are clearly shown for the Adult dataset but less conclusively for other datasets
