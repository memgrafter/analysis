# Visual-Aware Text-to-Speech

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2306.12020
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.12020
- Reference count: 0
- This work proposes a new task called Visual-Aware Text-to-Speech (VA-TTS) that synthesizes speech conditioned on both textual inputs and sequential visual feedback of the listener in face-to-face communication.

## Executive Summary
This work introduces Visual-Aware Text-to-Speech (VA-TTS), a novel task that synthesizes speech conditioned on both textual inputs and sequential visual feedback from the listener in face-to-face communication. Unlike traditional TTS, VA-TTS incorporates the listener's visual feedback (e.g., head motion, facial expression) to dynamically adjust the speaker's speech prosody (pitch, energy, duration) in real-time. The authors propose a baseline model that fuses phoneme linguistic information and listener visual signals for speech synthesis. Extensive experiments on the ViCo-X multimodal conversation dataset show that VA-TTS generates more natural audio with scenario-appropriate rhythm and prosody compared to traditional TTS.

## Method Summary
The method involves finetuning a FastSpeech2 model on the ViCo-X dataset to incorporate listener visual feedback. Listener visual features are extracted using 3DMM coefficients from video frames, encoded with LSTM, and fused with phoneme features via multi-head self-attention to predict refined prosody features (pitch, energy, duration). The encoder of FastSpeech2 is frozen during training to preserve baseline TTS quality while only the variance adaptor and decoder are updated.

## Key Results
- VA-TTS generates more natural audio with scenario-appropriate rhythm and prosody compared to traditional TTS
- Objective metrics (GPE, VDE, FFE, MCD13) demonstrate effectiveness of the proposed method
- Qualitative analysis shows synthesized interactive speech better responds to listener feedback

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
Visual feedback from the listener can directly influence the prosody of the generated speech by modifying pitch, energy, and duration. The system extracts listener visual features (head motion, facial expression) and encodes them as time-aligned representations. These features are fused with phoneme-level acoustic and semantic features via multi-head self-attention, producing refined prosody predictions that override default TTS outputs.

### Mechanism 2
Pre-trained TTS backbone can be fine-tuned to incorporate listener-derived prosody refinement without catastrophic forgetting. The encoder of the FastSpeech2 model is frozen; only the variance adaptor and decoder are updated using listener visual features and phoneme features. This preserves TTS speech quality while allowing prosody adaptation.

### Mechanism 3
Phoneme-level alignment between listener video frames and speech output is feasible using forced alignment and simple temporal indexing. Montreal Forced Aligner aligns phonemes to audio; visual frames are indexed by phoneme boundaries with a fixed look-ahead window to account for synthesis latency.

## Foundational Learning

- **Concept: Forced alignment of phonemes to audio**
  - Why needed here: To obtain precise phoneme-level timestamps so that listener visual feedback can be correctly associated with the corresponding speech segment
  - Quick check question: How does Montreal Forced Aligner use audio and transcript to produce phoneme-level time stamps?

- **Concept: Multi-head self-attention for modality fusion**
  - Why needed here: To learn cross-modal interactions between listener visual features and phoneme/acoustic features for prosody refinement
  - Quick check question: What is the role of the [FUSION] token in the attention-based prosody modeling?

- **Concept: 3D Morphable Model (3DMM) coefficients for face reconstruction**
  - Why needed here: To extract identity-independent, dynamic facial features (expression, pose) from listener video frames that inform prosody changes
  - Quick check question: Which 3DMM components (α, β, δ, p, γ) are used for listener feedback and why are identity components dropped?

## Architecture Onboarding

- **Component map**: Text preprocessing → phoneme sequence → FastSpeech2 encoder (frozen) → variance adaptor (fine-tuned) → decoder → HiFi-GAN vocoder; parallel path: video frames → 3DMM extraction → LSTM encoder → attention fusion → prosody prediction → variance adaptor
- **Critical path**: Text → phonemes → TTS backbone → prosody refinement (visual-aware) → final waveform
- **Design tradeoffs**: Freezing the encoder preserves TTS quality but limits adaptation; using simple LSTM for video encoding is efficient but may miss long-range dependencies; phoneme-level alignment simplifies timing but may ignore sub-phoneme prosodic nuances
- **Failure signatures**: If visual features are noisy, prosody predictions may oscillate; if look-ahead window is too short, timing misalignment occurs; if fine-tuning is unstable, baseline TTS quality degrades
- **First 3 experiments**:
  1. Ablation: Remove listener visual input; confirm TTS baseline performance matches original FastSpeech2 fine-tuned on ViCo-X
  2. Latency test: Measure phoneme-to-synthesis latency; adjust φ to ensure visual features are available before needed
  3. Modality fusion test: Compare prosody refinement with and without the [FUSION] token; assess impact on MCD13 and MAE metrics

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
How does the performance of VA-TTS vary across different listener feedback types (e.g., nodding vs. smiling vs. head shaking) and does the model weight certain visual cues more heavily than others? The paper describes using visual feedback like nodding, smiling, and head shaking but does not analyze how different types of feedback affect the synthesized speech differently.

### Open Question 2
What is the optimal frame rate and temporal resolution for capturing listener feedback, and how does this affect the quality and latency of the synthesized speech? The paper mentions using 30 FPS video and a fixed lookahead of 1 frame (33ms) but doesn't explore whether this is optimal or how it affects performance.

### Open Question 3
How well does VA-TTS generalize to unseen speakers and listener types, and what is the impact of listener identity on the synthesis quality? The paper uses a dataset with 10 speakers but doesn't report cross-speaker evaluation or analyze how listener identity affects synthesis performance.

## Limitations

- Limited empirical evidence showing how robustly visual features predict appropriate prosodic adjustments across diverse conversational scenarios
- Fixed look-ahead window assumes constant synthesis latency, but real-time systems often face variable delays
- Fine-tuning approach preserves TTS quality by freezing the encoder, but this constraint may limit learning complex cross-modal relationships

## Confidence

- **High Confidence**: The technical framework for fusing visual features with phoneme representations using multi-head self-attention is well-established and technically sound
- **Medium Confidence**: The claim that listener visual feedback can improve speech naturalness and scenario-appropriate prosody is supported by objective metrics (GPE, VDE, FFE, MCD13) and qualitative analysis
- **Low Confidence**: The assumption that phoneme-level alignment with a fixed look-ahead window can reliably handle real-time synthesis latency variations across different hardware and environmental conditions

## Next Checks

1. **Latency Robustness Test**: Measure actual phoneme-to-synthesis latency across multiple hardware configurations and verify whether the current φ parameter maintains alignment under varying conditions
2. **Visual Feature Ablation Study**: Systematically remove or randomize specific visual feature components to quantify their individual contributions to prosody refinement
3. **Cross-Scenario Generalization**: Evaluate the model on conversational scenarios not present in ViCo-X to test whether visual feedback consistently improves prosody across diverse contexts
