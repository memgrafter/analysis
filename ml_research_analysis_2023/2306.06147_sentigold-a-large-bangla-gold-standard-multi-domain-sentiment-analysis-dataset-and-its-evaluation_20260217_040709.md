# SentiGOLD: A Large Bangla Gold Standard Multi-Domain Sentiment Analysis Dataset and its Evaluation

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2306.06147
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.06147
- Reference count: 40
- Introduces SentiGOLD: 70,000 Bangla samples from 30 domains with 5 sentiment classes

## Executive Summary
This paper presents SentiGOLD, a comprehensive Bangla sentiment analysis dataset comprising 70,000 samples across 30 diverse domains. The dataset was created through a rigorous annotation process involving a gender-balanced team of linguists, achieving a high Inter-Annotator Agreement score of 0.88. Data sources include social media, blogs, news, and YouTube comments, with extensive preprocessing and deduplication steps ensuring quality. The work establishes a new benchmark for Bangla sentiment analysis, with the top-performing BanglaBERT model achieving a macro F1 score of 0.62 on the 5-class task.

## Method Summary
The dataset was constructed using a systematic approach involving data collection from multiple sources including social media platforms, blogs, news articles, and YouTube comments. An annotation management system was developed to facilitate quality control, with data preprocessed and deduplicated before annotation. The annotation team consisted of linguists with gender balance, and an iterative improvement process was employed to refine the dataset. The methodology emphasized adherence to national linguistic conventions and included comprehensive preprocessing steps to ensure data quality.

## Key Results
- SentiGOLD dataset contains 70,000 Bangla samples across 30 domains with 5 sentiment classes
- Top-performing BanglaBERT model achieves 0.62 macro F1 score on 5-class sentiment task
- Cross-dataset testing shows 0.61 macro F1 score, demonstrating strong generalization capabilities
- High Inter-Annotator Agreement (IAA) score of 0.88 using Fleiss' kappa

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The success of SentiGOLD stems from its rigorous annotation protocol and diverse data sources. The gender-balanced team of linguists ensures multiple perspectives in sentiment interpretation, while the extensive domain coverage captures the complexity of Bangla sentiment expression. The high IAA score indicates consistent annotation quality, and the multi-source data collection provides representative samples of natural language usage across different contexts.

## Foundational Learning
- **Fleiss' kappa**: Measures inter-annotator agreement across multiple raters, essential for assessing annotation quality and reliability
- **Macro F1 score**: Evaluates model performance across all classes equally, critical for imbalanced sentiment datasets
- **Data deduplication**: Removes redundant samples to prevent model overfitting and ensure dataset diversity
- **Cross-dataset validation**: Tests model generalization on unseen data, fundamental for real-world applicability
- **Linguistic preprocessing**: Cleans and normalizes text data, necessary for handling Bangla's complex morphology
- **Annotation management systems**: Facilitates quality control and iterative improvement, crucial for large-scale dataset creation

## Architecture Onboarding
Component map: Data Collection -> Preprocessing -> Annotation Management -> Quality Control -> Model Training
Critical path: Data collection from diverse sources → Preprocessing and deduplication → Annotation by linguists → Quality validation → Model training and evaluation
Design tradeoffs: Larger dataset size vs. annotation quality, domain diversity vs. specialized coverage, computational efficiency vs. model performance
Failure signatures: Low IAA scores indicate annotation inconsistency, domain bias reveals limited generalization, preprocessing errors cause data quality issues
First experiments: 1) Verify data distribution across domains, 2) Test model performance on individual domains, 3) Evaluate annotation consistency across different annotator pairs

## Limitations
- Limited demographic representation beyond gender balance in annotation team
- Potential coverage gaps in niche Bangla language domains
- Uncertainty about temporal stability of sentiment classifications

## Confidence
High confidence: Dataset creation methodology is well-documented with established NLP metrics
Medium confidence: Model performance benchmarks are credible but limited comparison data
Low confidence: True demographic representativeness and long-term stability remain uncertain

## Next Checks
1. Conduct demographic diversity analysis of the annotation team to assess potential biases in sentiment interpretation across age groups, regional backgrounds, and educational levels
