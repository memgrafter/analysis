# Robust Representation Learning by Clustering with Bisimulation Metrics for Visual Reinforcement Learning with Distractions

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2302.12003
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2302.12003
- Reference count: 23
- Key outcome: The paper addresses the challenge of learning robust visual representations for reinforcement learning in environments with task-irrelevant distractions. The authors propose a clustering-based method, Clustering with Bisimulation Metrics (CBM), which learns robust representations by grouping visual observations in the latent space according to their bisimulation distances to learned prototypes. This approach enables CBM to capture task-relevant information and filter out distractions. ## Executive Summary
This paper tackles the problem of learning robust visual representations for reinforcement learning (RL) in environments with task-irrelevant distractions. The authors propose a novel clustering-based method called Clustering with Bisimulation Metrics (CBM) that groups visual observations in the latent space according to their bisimulation distances to learned prototypes. This approach enables the agent to capture task-relevant information and filter out distractions, leading to improved sample efficiency and asymptotic performance. Experiments on the Distracting Control Suite demonstrate that CBM significantly enhances the performance of popular visual RL algorithms like DrQ and DrQ-v2, achieving state-of-the-art results in both multiple and single distraction settings.

## Method Summary
The paper introduces Clustering with Bisimulation Metrics (CBM), a method that learns robust visual representations for reinforcement learning in distracting environments. CBM employs a clustering approach to group visual observations in the latent space based on their bisimulation distances to learned prototypes. The method consists of a convolutional encoder that maps observations to a latent space, a latent dynamics model based on contrastive predictive coding, and a clustering module that groups observations using bisimulation metrics. CBM is integrated into popular visual RL algorithms like DrQ and DrQ-v2, resulting in significant improvements in sample efficiency and asymptotic performance on the Distracting Control Suite benchmark.

## Key Results
- CBM significantly improves the sample efficiency and asymptotic performance of DrQ and DrQ-v2 on the Distracting Control Suite benchmark.
- CBM achieves state-of-the-art results in both multiple and single distraction settings, outperforming other visual RL methods.
- The method is effective in filtering out task-irrelevant distractions and capturing task-relevant information in the latent space.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The proposed method, CBM, works by leveraging the concept of bisimulation metrics to group visual observations in the latent space. Bisimulation metrics quantify the similarity between states based on their long-term behavior, allowing CBM to identify and cluster task-relevant observations while filtering out distractions. By learning prototypes in the latent space and assigning observations to clusters based on their bisimulation distances to these prototypes, CBM effectively captures the essential features required for decision-making while ignoring irrelevant visual information. This robust representation learning approach enables the agent to focus on the task at hand and improve its performance in distracting environments.

## Limitations
- The scalability of CBM to more complex, high-dimensional visual domains beyond the Distracting Control Suite remains unexplored.
- The computational overhead introduced by the clustering process and its impact on sample efficiency in real-world applications is not thoroughly discussed.
- The method's performance in environments with stochastic dynamics or partial observability is not addressed, and its comparison to alternative metric learning approaches is limited.

## Confidence
- **Sample Efficiency Improvement**: Medium - The experimental results on the Distracting Control Suite are compelling, but the study lacks ablation studies to isolate the contribution of the clustering component from other factors.
- **Asymptotic Performance**: Medium - While CBM achieves state-of-the-art results, the comparison with other state-of-the-art methods for visual reinforcement learning in distracting environments is limited.
- **Scalability to Complex Domains**: Low - The paper does not explore the performance of CBM in more complex visual domains or environments with multiple types of distractions.
