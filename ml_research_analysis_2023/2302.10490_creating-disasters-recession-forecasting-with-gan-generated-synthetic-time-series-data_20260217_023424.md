# Creating Disasters: Recession Forecasting with GAN-Generated Synthetic Time Series Data

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2302.10490
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2302.10490
- Reference count: 6
- Primary result: GAN-generated synthetic Treasury yield data improves recession prediction accuracy and short-term yield forecasting performance compared to models trained only on real data.

## Executive Summary
This paper addresses the challenge of forecasting rare economic events like recessions, where limited historical data hinders model training and testing. The authors propose using DoppelGANger, a generative adversarial network designed for time series data, to generate synthetic Treasury yield samples with associated recession indicators. By augmenting real data with synthetic samples, the paper demonstrates improved performance in both short-term Treasury yield forecasting (reducing RMSE from 0.077 to 0.046 for 1-year yields) and recession prediction accuracy (increasing LSTM classifier AUC from 0.69 to 0.86).

## Method Summary
The paper trains DoppelGANger on historical Treasury yield data to generate synthetic samples that maintain statistical properties of the original series. These synthetic samples include recession metadata, allowing models to train on more recession examples than exist in historical data. The synthetic data is then used to train LSTM models for both forecasting (predicting future yields) and classification (predicting recession probability). Performance is evaluated by comparing models trained on synthetic data, real data, and combined datasets against baselines.

## Key Results
- LSTM classifiers trained on synthetic recession samples achieve AUC of 0.86 versus 0.69 for real-data-only models
- Short-term Treasury yield forecasting RMSE improves from 0.077 to 0.046 for 1-year yields when using synthetic training data
- Synthetic data maintains yield correlations (0.946 in real data vs 0.945 in synthetic) while expanding training sample size

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- **Claim**: Synthetic recession samples improve LSTM classifier performance by increasing exposure to rare events during training.
- **Mechanism**: The GAN generates synthetic Treasury yield series with recession metadata, including a "future recession" indicator. By augmenting the training set with synthetic samples that simulate recession conditions, the LSTM classifier learns patterns that precede recessions more robustly, leading to higher AUC scores.
- **Core assumption**: The synthetic data maintains distributional fidelity to real data so that learned patterns generalize to unseen real recessions.
- **Evidence anchors**:
  - [abstract] "training models on synthetic recessions can improve a model's ability to predict future recessions over a model trained only on real data."
  - [section 5.3.2] "The ROC curves for the LSTM models can also be found in Figure 10. Here the AUC is clearly much higher for the model trained on synthetic data (0.86) and the model trained on combined data (0.81) than the AUC for the model trained only on real data (0.69)."
  - [corpus] "no related citations found" (weak evidence, relies on paper's internal results)
- **Break condition**: If synthetic data quality degrades (e.g., mode collapse, poor autocorrelation capture), the classifier will overfit to unrealistic patterns and performance will drop.

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim**: Synthetic data improves short-range Treasury yield forecasting by expanding the training set size without introducing significant noise.
- **Mechanism**: By generating 1000 synthetic 125-day samples, the LSTM forecasting model trains on 100,000 synthetic samples versus 13,710 real samples. This larger dataset reduces variance in parameter estimates and improves RMSE for 1-day-ahead forecasts (RMSE 0.077 → 0.046 for 1-year yields).
- **Core assumption**: The synthetic samples are sufficiently realistic that the model learns generalizable patterns rather than noise.
- **Evidence anchors**:
  - [abstract] "short-range forecasting performance for Treasury yields is improved for models trained on synthetic data relative to models trained only on real data."
  - [section 4] Table 1 shows RMSE improvement from 0.077 to 0.046 for 1-year yields when trained on synthetic data.
  - [corpus] "no related citations found" (weak external validation)
- **Break condition**: If synthetic data introduces artifacts (e.g., negative yields, poor long-term autocorrelation), forecast errors will increase despite larger sample size.

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim**: DoppelGANger's batch generation and metadata normalization reduce mode collapse and improve synthetic data fidelity for time series.
- **Mechanism**: DoppelGANger generates S steps ahead per RNN pass (S=5 here), reducing the number of passes needed. Metadata normalization by local min/max per series preserves scale diversity, preventing the generator from collapsing to a single mode. This yields synthetic samples with similar correlations (0.946 → 0.945) and distributions as real data.
- **Core assumption**: The architectural tweaks effectively mitigate mode collapse in the Treasury yield context.
- **Evidence anchors**:
  - [section 2.3] "DoppelGANger was designed with the intent of producing synthetic networked systems data... The model uses a unique kind of normalization for time series exhibiting a wide range of values across samples and series."
  - [section 3.3] "The correlation between 1-year and 10-year Treasury bond yields was 0.946. In the generated samples, the correlation was 0.945."
  - [corpus] "no related citations found" (no external validation of DoppelGANger's effectiveness)
- **Break condition**: If batch size S is poorly chosen or normalization fails, mode collapse will produce degenerate synthetic samples.

## Architecture Onboarding

- **Component map**: FRED Treasury yields -> Rolling window samples -> DoppelGANger GAN -> Synthetic samples -> LSTM forecaster/classifier -> Performance metrics
- **Critical path**: Collect and preprocess Treasury yield data into rolling windows -> Train DoppelGANger on real data (125-day or 30-day samples) -> Generate synthetic samples with associated recession metadata -> Train forecasting/classification models on real, synthetic, or combined datasets -> Evaluate performance on held-out test periods
- **Design tradeoffs**:
  - Sample length vs. number of samples: Longer samples improve long-term autocorrelation capture but reduce training sample count
  - GAN stability vs. fidelity: More training epochs may improve fidelity but risk mode collapse
  - Model complexity vs. overfitting: Complex LSTMs may overfit synthetic noise if data quality is poor
- **Failure signatures**:
  - Synthetic samples with constant or straight-line yields (mode collapse)
  - Forecast RMSE worse than real-only model (synthetic noise dominates)
  - Classifier ROC AUC near random (synthetic recession patterns don't match real ones)
  - Negative yield generation (violation of economic constraints)
- **First 3 experiments**:
  1. Train DoppelGANger on 125-day samples, generate 1000 synthetic samples, compare yield correlations to real data
  2. Train LSTM forecaster on synthetic data only, evaluate 1-day-ahead RMSE on real test set
  3. Train LSTM classifier on synthetic recession samples, evaluate ROC AUC on real test recessions

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

- **Open Question 1**: How does the performance of DoppelGANger compare to other GAN architectures (e.g., TimeGAN, GANite) for generating synthetic time series data in economic forecasting applications?
- **Open Question 2**: How sensitive are the improvements in forecasting and recession prediction to the choice of hyperparameters in the LSTM models and the GAN training process?
- **Open Question 3**: How well do the synthetic data generated by DoppelGANger generalize to other economic time series data beyond Treasury yields and recession indicators?

## Limitations
- Limited external validation of synthetic data quality and model improvements
- Mode collapse remains a concern for time series generation, particularly with longer sample lengths
- Small number of historical recessions constrains statistical significance of classification results

## Confidence
- Mechanism 1 (recession prediction improvement): **Medium** - Internal results show clear AUC improvement, but lack external validation and the mechanism assumes synthetic recession patterns are realistic
- Mechanism 2 (yield forecasting improvement): **Medium** - RMSE reductions are demonstrated, but synthetic data quality verification is limited to basic statistical comparisons
- Mechanism 3 (DoppelGANger architecture benefits): **Low** - No external validation of DoppelGANger's effectiveness; improvements are attributed to architectural choices without ablation studies

## Next Checks
1. Generate synthetic data using DoppelGANger with different random seeds and evaluate whether performance improvements are consistent across seeds, indicating robustness rather than lucky initialization
2. Test classifier performance on synthetic recession samples from a hold-out period not seen during GAN training to assess whether the model generalizes beyond the training distribution
3. Compare DoppelGANger-generated synthetic data against simpler baselines (e.g., Gaussian noise with matched autocorrelation, bootstrap resampling) to establish whether the GAN architecture provides meaningful advantages for this specific task
