# HandMeThat: Human-Robot Communication in Physical and Social Environments

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2310.03779
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.03779
- Reference count: 40
- Key outcome: A benchmark dataset and evaluation framework for human-robot instruction understanding that combines physical context and social reasoning

## Executive Summary
HandMeThat is a benchmark for instruction understanding and following in physical and social environments. It addresses the challenge of resolving ambiguous human instructions by considering both physical context (object states and relations) and social context (human actions and goals). The benchmark contains 10,000 episodes where a robot observes a human's action trajectory toward an internal goal, receives an ambiguous instruction, and must take actions to accomplish a subgoal. The evaluation shows that current baseline models perform poorly on this task, indicating significant room for advancement in physical and social human-robot communication.

## Method Summary
The HandMeThat benchmark uses a text-based environment where robots observe human actions and receive ambiguous instructions. Episodes are generated using a Rational Speech Acts model to create instructions that balance informativeness and efficiency. The benchmark is divided into four hardness levels that systematically increase the complexity of required reasoning. Several baseline models are evaluated including random action selection, heuristic approaches, sequence-to-sequence models, and deep reinforcement learning algorithms. The evaluation considers both fully-observable and partially-observable settings.

## Key Results
- All baseline models perform poorly on HandMeThat tasks, with success rates significantly below human performance
- Performance degrades substantially as task hardness increases from Level 1 to Level 4
- Even with oracle goal information, models struggle to resolve ambiguous instructions effectively
- Partially observable settings are significantly more challenging than fully observable ones

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: The robot can infer a human's subgoal from ambiguous instructions by combining physical context (object states) and social context (human actions and goals).
- Mechanism: The benchmark generates episodes where a human performs a sequence of actions toward an internal goal, then asks for help with an ambiguous instruction. The robot must use both the observed action trajectory and the instruction to identify the most useful subgoal for the human.
- Core assumption: The human's instruction is a subgoal that is useful for their overall goal, and this usefulness can be quantified by the reduction in remaining cost-to-go.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract]: "considers the resolution of human instructions with ambiguities based on the physical (object states and relations) and social (human actions and goals) information"
  - [section]: "The key challenge in HandMeThat tasks is the recognition of human’s subgoal from her historical actions and ambiguous instructions"
  - [corpus]: Weak - no direct evidence from corpus papers that address this specific combination of physical and social reasoning for ambiguous instruction resolution
- Break condition: If the instruction is truly ambiguous with no grounding in either physical or social context, the robot cannot resolve it without additional information gathering.

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: The benchmark uses a Rational Speech Acts (RSA) model to generate human instructions that are intentionally ambiguous to varying degrees.
- Mechanism: When generating an instruction, the model iteratively considers how a rational listener would interpret different possible utterances, balancing informativeness against communication cost (number of specifiers).
- Core assumption: Humans naturally make this tradeoff between accuracy and efficiency of communication when giving instructions to robots.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract]: "human makes trade-offs between accuracy and efficiency of communication"
  - [section]: "Our utterance generation process follows the Rational Speech Acts (RSA) models"
  - [corpus]: Weak - while RSA is well-established in linguistics, the corpus doesn't show direct application to human-robot instruction generation in physical environments
- Break condition: If the RSA model parameters are poorly tuned, the generated instructions may be either too ambiguous to be useful or too specific to require social reasoning.

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: The benchmark's four hardness levels systematically isolate different reasoning challenges (grounding, social reasoning, pragmatic reasoning, and information gathering).
- Mechanism: Each level increases the complexity of required reasoning by controlling how much the instruction overlaps with what can be inferred from the human's goal and actions alone.
- Core assumption: These reasoning capabilities can be evaluated independently by controlling the relationship between the true subgoal, the instruction, and the human's goal.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [section]: "we split HandMeThat into four hardness levels, and the gaps between levels correspond to different challenges"
  - [section]: "Level 1: A(m) = A(u). The utterance has no ambiguity" through "Level 4: A(m) ⊂ A(r). In this case, the human utterance u is inherently ambiguous"
  - [corpus]: Weak - the corpus papers discuss related challenges but don't present a systematic evaluation framework with controlled difficulty levels
- Break condition: If the distinction between levels is not maintained in the generated data, the evaluation will not accurately measure the targeted reasoning capabilities.

## Foundational Learning

- Concept: Reinforcement Learning
  - Why needed here: The robot learns to map observations (human actions, object states, instructions) to actions through trial and error in the environment
  - Quick check question: What is the difference between the reward signal in standard RL and the reward structure used in this benchmark?

- Concept: Goal Recognition
  - Why needed here: The robot must infer the human's underlying goal from their action trajectory to properly interpret ambiguous instructions
  - Quick check question: How does the cost-to-go calculation help quantify the usefulness of a potential subgoal?

- Concept: Pragmatic Reasoning
  - Why needed here: The robot must understand why the human chose a particular (possibly ambiguous) instruction given their goal and context
  - Quick check question: What role does the listener's prior distribution play in the RSA model for utterance generation?

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map: Environment simulator -> Instruction generator (RSA model) -> Agent (observation encoder, action decoder) -> Evaluation module (goal checker, cost calculator)
- Critical path: Observation → Goal inference → Pragmatic reasoning → Action selection → Environment transition → Reward/termination check
- Design tradeoffs: Fully observable vs partially observable environments (ease of learning vs realism), Text interface vs visual interface (simplicity vs real-world applicability), Fixed vs variable instruction ambiguity (controllability vs natural variation)
- Failure signatures: Poor performance on Level 2+ tasks indicates inability to integrate social reasoning, random action selection indicates exploration problems, high action costs indicate inefficient planning
- First 3 experiments:
  1. Test baseline performance on Level 1 tasks to establish grounding capability without social reasoning
  2. Test with oracle goal information provided to isolate the impact of goal recognition on performance
  3. Test with oracle subgoal information provided to isolate the impact of pragmatic reasoning on performance

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- Question: How does the complexity of the HandMeThat tasks scale with the number of objects and locations in the environment?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper mentions that each scene contains more than 200 movable objects and 14 locations.
- Why unresolved: The paper does not provide a detailed analysis of how task complexity changes with scene size.
- What evidence would resolve it: Experiments showing performance degradation as scene size increases, or theoretical analysis of state space growth.

### Open Question 2
- Question: How would the performance of models change if the human's internal goal G was partially observable rather than completely hidden?
- Basis in paper: [inferred] The paper discusses the challenge of inferring the human's internal goal from actions, but assumes the goal is completely hidden.
- Why unresolved: The paper does not explore scenarios where partial goal information is available.
- What evidence would resolve it: Experiments comparing model performance with varying levels of goal observability.

### Open Question 3
- Question: What is the impact of different pragmatic reasoning models on the HandMeThat benchmark performance?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper uses a Rational Speech Acts (RSA) model for utterance generation and evaluation.
- Why unresolved: The paper only uses one pragmatic reasoning model and does not compare alternatives.
- What evidence would resolve it: Experiments comparing performance using different pragmatic reasoning models (e.g., Gricean Maxims, game-theoretic approaches).

### Open Question 4
- Question: How does the performance of models change when the robot is allowed to ask clarifying questions to resolve ambiguities?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper mentions this as a potential extension but does not implement it.
- Why unresolved: The paper focuses on one-shot instruction following without clarification.
- What evidence would resolve it: Experiments comparing performance with and without question-asking capabilities, and analysis of when questions are most beneficial.

### Open Question 5
- Question: How does the distribution of task types (bring-me, move-to, change-state) affect the difficulty of the HandMeThat benchmark?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper mentions these three task types and their relative frequencies.
- Why unresolved: The paper does not provide a detailed analysis of how task type distribution affects overall difficulty.
- What evidence would resolve it: Experiments isolating the performance on each task type and analysis of their relative contributions to overall benchmark difficulty.

## Limitations

- The benchmark uses a text-based environment rather than real visual perception, limiting direct applicability to real-world scenarios
- The evaluation focuses on one-shot instruction following without allowing for clarification questions, which may be unrealistic in practical applications
- The generated instructions, while controlled for ambiguity, may not fully capture the complexity and variability of natural human communication

## Confidence

- RSA instruction generation mechanism: Low - while RSA is well-established, its application to physical human-robot interaction lacks direct corpus support
- Four-level hardness framework: Medium - theoretically sound but needs empirical validation of independence between reasoning challenges
- Overall benchmark design: Medium - combines established concepts but the specific integration has limited direct evidence
