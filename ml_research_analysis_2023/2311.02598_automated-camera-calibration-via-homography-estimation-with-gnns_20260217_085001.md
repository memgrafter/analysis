# Automated Camera Calibration via Homography Estimation with GNNs

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2311.02598
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2311.02598
- Reference count: 40
- Primary result: GNN-based homography estimation achieves 95.96% IoU on synthetic datasets and 87.89% on real-world cameras

## Executive Summary
This paper presents an automated camera calibration method that leverages Graph Neural Networks (GNNs) to estimate homography matrices from intersection viewpoint images. The approach generates synthetic intersection viewpoint images from bird's-eye-view images, framing the problem as a graph of virtual cameras. By learning relationships within this graph structure, the method estimates homography matrices that enable retrieval of extrinsic calibration parameters. The framework demonstrates superior performance compared to existing methods on both synthetic and real-world datasets, achieving state-of-the-art results.

## Method Summary
The proposed method treats camera calibration as a graph-based homography estimation problem. It generates synthetic intersection viewpoint images from bird's-eye-view images and constructs a graph where nodes represent virtual cameras and edges encode spatial relationships. GNNs are employed to learn the underlying structure of this graph, enabling the model to estimate homography matrices by leveraging neighborhood representations. This approach exploits multiple images rather than relying on single image matches, improving robustness. The homography matrices are then used to retrieve extrinsic calibration parameters. The method is trained on synthetic data and evaluated on both synthetic and real-world camera datasets, demonstrating superior performance compared to baseline methods.

## Key Results
- Achieves 95.96% average IoU score on synthetic datasets, outperforming previous approaches by up to 10%
- Achieves 87.89% average IoU score on real-world cameras, outperforming baseline models by up to 8%
- Demonstrates superior performance compared to state-of-the-art methods on both synthetic and real-world datasets
- Shows computational efficiency advantages over previous approaches while maintaining high accuracy

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The method's effectiveness stems from its graph-based representation of camera relationships. By framing camera calibration as a graph problem, the GNN can learn spatial relationships and dependencies between multiple viewpoints simultaneously. The synthetic data generation provides rich training data that captures diverse intersection scenarios. The homography estimation benefits from neighborhood information in the graph, allowing the model to leverage context from multiple cameras rather than relying on isolated image pairs. This multi-view approach reduces sensitivity to noise and occlusions that commonly affect traditional single-image calibration methods.
