# Maintaining Stability and Plasticity for Predictive Churn Reduction

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2305.04135
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.04135
- Reference count: 40
- Primary result: Proposes Accumulated Model Combination (AMC) that reduces churn by up to 85% while maintaining or improving accuracy

## Executive Summary
Predictive churn occurs when updated ML models incorrectly predict samples that original models predicted correctly, eroding user trust. This paper reveals that even single models are prone to churn during training due to incompatible gradients, making churn reduction more challenging than previously thought. The authors propose AMC, which maintains both base and new models and selects between their predictions using confidence scores. AMC requires no retraining of the original model and adds minimal overhead. Experiments across diverse datasets show AMC outperforms state-of-the-art churn reduction methods including distillation and ensembles.

## Method Summary
AMC addresses predictive churn by maintaining both the original deployed model and the updated model, then choosing between their predictions based on confidence scores. The framework extracts prediction confidence and average confidence over training for each sample, using these as meta-features to select which model's output to trust. Unlike distillation methods that try to make the new model mimic the old one, AMC explicitly acknowledges that each model may be better on different subsets of data. The approach works across image, text, and tabular datasets without requiring retraining of the original model, making it computationally efficient compared to ensemble methods.

## Key Results
- AMC reduces churn by up to 85% compared to baseline methods
- Combining average prediction confidence with final model prediction confidence yields better churn reduction than using either score individually
- AMC maintains or improves accuracy while substantially reducing negative flips
- AMC outperforms distillation and ensemble methods on diverse datasets including CIFAR, FashionMNIST, and text classification tasks

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: Negative flips arise when gradient updates are incompatible with individual samples, causing loss to increase even as overall training accuracy improves.
- Mechanism: During training, the average batch gradient can point in a direction that increases loss for some individual samples. This "incompatible gradient" problem becomes more prominent as the model becomes more accurate, because correctly classified samples are more sensitive to perturbations that could flip their predictions.
- Core assumption: The incompatible gradient problem is a fundamental source of negative flips during training, and reducing these incompatible gradients can reduce negative flips.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "We motivate the need for AMC by showing the difficulty of making a single model consistent with its own predictions throughout training thereby revealing an implicit stability-plasticity tradeoff when training a single model."
  - [section 4.1] "Reducing incompatible gradients can be formulated as a quadratic programming problem as follows min_˜g 1/2 ||g - ˜g||^2 s.t. ⟨˜g, gj⟩ ≥ 0 ∀j ∈ [B]"
  - [corpus] "Flashbacks to Harmonize Stability and Plasticity in Continual Learning" - while not directly about churn, addresses the same stability-plasticity tradeoff
- Break condition: If the model's loss landscape is sufficiently smooth or the learning rate is sufficiently small, incompatible gradients may not be a significant source of negative flips.

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: AMC reduces churn by maintaining both the base and new model, and choosing between their predictions based on confidence scores.
- Mechanism: AMC bypasses the stability-plasticity tradeoff by not requiring the new model to match the base model's predictions. Instead, it uses a meta-model to choose between the base and new model's outputs based on confidence scores like prediction confidence and average confidence over training.
- Core assumption: The base model is at least as good as the new model on some samples, and choosing the higher-confidence model will reduce negative flips while maintaining or improving accuracy.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "To address the limitations of existing approaches, we make the following contributions: 1. Show that completely eliminating negative flips between epochs when training a single model is infeasible even on small datasets."
  - [section 5.1] "AMC does not require retraining the original model and adds little overhead."
  - [corpus] "Balanced Online Class-Incremental Learning via Dual Classifiers" - uses dual models but for different purpose
- Break condition: If both models are poorly calibrated or if the confidence scores are not reliable indicators of correctness, AMC may not effectively reduce churn.

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: The combination of prediction confidence and average confidence over training is more effective at reducing churn than either score alone.
- Mechanism: Prediction confidence alone may not be sufficient because it doesn't capture stability over time. Average confidence over training provides information about which samples are consistently predicted correctly, complementing the information from prediction confidence.
- Core assumption: Samples that are consistently predicted correctly over training are less likely to be negative flips, and this information can be captured by average confidence over training.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "We show superior churn reduction performance compared to distillation and ensembles. In particular, when combining average prediction confidence throughout training with final model prediction confidence to choose between models, churn is decreased substantially compared to using either score individually."
  - [section 5.1] "AvgConf as the average confidence of a model across training epochs for the final predicted class"
  - [corpus] "New Insights for the Stability-Plasticity Dilemma in Online Continual Learning" - addresses stability but not in context of churn
- Break condition: If the training process is highly unstable or if the average confidence over training is not a reliable indicator of sample difficulty, the combination of scores may not be effective.

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map:
  - Base model (fb) -> New model (fn) -> Meta-model (ψ)
  - Scoring functions -> Confidence scores -> Model selection

- Critical path:
  1. Train fb on initial training data
  2. Train fn on combined initial and additional data
  3. Extract confidence scores for both models
  4. Use meta-model to choose between fb and fn outputs
  5. Deploy the meta-model for inference

- Design tradeoffs:
  - Computational cost: AMC requires inference with both models, doubling inference cost
  - Model selection: Different scoring functions have different strengths and weaknesses
  - Accuracy vs churn: AMC aims to reduce churn without sacrificing accuracy, but this may not always be possible

- Failure signatures:
  - Both models are poorly calibrated: AMC's confidence-based selection may not be effective
  - High model instability: Average confidence over training may not be a reliable indicator of sample difficulty
  - Significant distribution shift: The base and new models may be making predictions on very different data distributions

- First 3 experiments:
  1. Implement AMC with just prediction confidence score and evaluate churn reduction on a small dataset
  2. Add average confidence over training score and evaluate if combination improves churn reduction
  3. Compare AMC's churn reduction performance to distillation and ensembles on a larger dataset

## Limitations
- The incompatible gradient problem may not represent a fundamental tradeoff, as alternative optimization techniques could potentially eliminate negative flips entirely
- AMC's performance on highly imbalanced datasets and extreme domain shifts remains unclear
- The method doubles inference cost by requiring predictions from both models

## Confidence
- Empirical effectiveness of AMC: High
- Theoretical claims about incompatible gradients: Medium
- Generality of stability-plasticity tradeoff explanation: Medium
