# STT4SG-350: A Speech Corpus for All Swiss German Dialect Regions

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2305.18855
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.18855
- Reference count: 13
- Primary result: 343 hours of Swiss German speech across 7 dialect regions with 74.7 BLEU score on test set

## Executive Summary
STT4SG-350 is a large-scale speech corpus for Swiss German dialects covering all seven regional varieties with 343 hours of speech data from 316 speakers. The corpus was created through a web application where participants translated and recorded Standard German sentences in their local dialect. It includes speaker metadata (dialect region, age, gender) and is balanced across regions despite population disparities. The test set uses identical sentences across dialects for fair evaluation. An ASR model trained on this corpus achieved 74.7 BLEU score and outperformed previous models on two other Swiss German datasets.

## Method Summary
The corpus was collected using a web application where participants translated Standard German sentences into their local Swiss German dialect and recorded them. The dataset includes 343 hours of audio with speaker metadata covering age, gender, and dialect region. The corpus is split into training, validation, and test sets, with the test set containing the same 3,515 sentences recorded in all seven dialect regions. An XLS-R (1B) wav2vec2.0 model was fine-tuned on the training data using specific hyperparameters including batch size of 1,600 seconds, learning rate of 3e-5, and evaluation across 5 random seeds.

## Key Results
- 343 hours of speech data from 316 speakers across 7 Swiss German dialect regions
- ASR model achieved 74.7 BLEU score on the test set
- Model outperformed previous approaches on two other Swiss German datasets
- Balanced speaker distribution across dialect regions (differing by factor of 1.2 maximum)

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
Balanced dialect representation improves ASR model generalization across Swiss German dialects. By ensuring roughly equal amounts of speech data from each dialect region (Basel, Bern, Graubünden, Innerschweiz, Ostschweiz, Wallis, Zurich), the ASR model is exposed to a diverse phonetic and lexical inventory. This prevents overfitting to dominant dialects and encourages the model to learn shared acoustic-phonetic patterns across dialects.

### Mechanism 2
Standard German transcripts paired with Swiss German speech enable end-to-end speech-to-text translation without requiring Swiss German orthography. The corpus sidesteps the problem of Swiss German's lack of standardized orthography by using Standard German as the target transcription. This allows the use of existing ASR models and training pipelines designed for Standard German, simplifying the development of speech-to-text systems for Swiss German.

### Mechanism 3
The test set with identical sentences across dialects enables fair comparison of ASR performance. By recording the same 3,515 sentences in all 7 dialect regions, the corpus allows for a controlled comparison of ASR performance across dialects. This eliminates variability due to sentence difficulty and focuses on the impact of dialectal differences on ASR accuracy.

## Architecture Onboarding

- **Component map**: Audio recordings (FLAC training/validation, MP3 test) -> Standard German transcripts -> Speaker metadata (dialect region, age group, gender) -> Train/val/test splits
- **Critical path**: 1) Download corpus, 2) Preprocess audio and text data, 3) Train ASR model on training set, 4) Evaluate on test set
- **Design tradeoffs**: Uses Standard German as target transcription (simplifies development but may introduce errors); balanced across dialects but may underrepresent certain demographics (children, elderly)
- **Failure signatures**: Poor ASR performance on certain dialects or demographic groups; inaccuracies in transcribed text due to Swiss German/Standard German differences
- **First 3 experiments**: 1) Train and evaluate ASR model on validation set, 2) Fine-tune pre-trained multilingual ASR model and evaluate on test set, 3) Train separate models per dialect region and compare to unified model

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
How does the ASR model performance vary across different age groups in the STT4SG-350 corpus? The paper mentions demographic imbalance (1.3% children, 3.5% elderly) and potential performance issues for these groups, but provides no specific performance metrics.

### Open Question 2
What is the impact of the lack of spontaneous speech data on the ASR model's performance in real-world applications? The corpus only covers read speech, and the model hasn't been tested on spontaneous speech, which is expected to perform significantly worse.

### Open Question 3
How does the ASR model's performance differ between speakers from high-population and low-population dialect regions? While the corpus is balanced across regions, the paper doesn't provide specific performance metrics for different dialect regions.

## Limitations
- Uses Standard German transcripts which may not accurately represent spoken Swiss German content
- Demographic imbalance with only 1.3% child speakers and 3.5% elderly speakers
- Only covers read speech, not spontaneous conversational speech
- Non-commercial license restricts certain applications

## Confidence

**High Confidence**: Corpus size (343 hours), dialect region coverage (7 regions), and balanced speaker distribution across regions.

**Medium Confidence**: ASR model performance (74.7 BLEU score) and superiority over previous models - depends on specific datasets and evaluation protocols.

**Low Confidence**: Effectiveness for applications beyond ASR (dialect identification, speaker recognition) - mentioned as possibilities but limited evidence provided.
