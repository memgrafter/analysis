# Modeling Graphs Beyond Hyperbolic: Graph Neural Networks in Symmetric Positive Definite Matrices

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2306.14064
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.14064
- Reference count: 40
- Introduces SPD4GNNs library implementing popular GNNs in SPD matrix space

## Executive Summary
This paper introduces a novel approach to graph neural networks by embedding graph data in the space of symmetric positive definite (SPD) matrices, a rich Riemannian symmetric space. The authors leverage gyro-calculus tools to implement five popular graph neural network architectures (GCN, GAT, Cheb, SGC, GIN) in SPD space, enabling them to handle complex graphs with mixed geometric and topological features more effectively than traditional Euclidean or hyperbolic embeddings. Experimental results demonstrate significant performance improvements on node and graph classification tasks for complex datasets, achieving up to 20% higher accuracy than Euclidean and hyperbolic baselines.

## Method Summary
The method implements graph neural networks in the space of symmetric positive definite (SPD) matrices using gyro-calculus operations. The approach involves mapping graph features to SPD space, performing message passing using gyro-addition and gyro-inversion in the tangent space, and applying Riemannian exponential/logarithm maps to switch between SPD space and its tangent space. The authors implement five popular GNN architectures in SPD space and compare them against Euclidean and hyperbolic baselines on multiple node and graph classification datasets using three different classifier types.

## Key Results
- SPD-based GNNs achieve up to 20% higher accuracy than Euclidean and hyperbolic counterparts on complex datasets
- Citeseer node classification improves from ~70% (Euclidean) to ~90% (SPD)
- AIDS graph classification improves from ~70% (Euclidean) to ~90% (SPD)
- SPD models show competitive runtimes despite increased computational complexity

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
SPD matrices provide a richer geometric space that can simultaneously represent both Euclidean-like grid structures and hyperbolic-like tree structures within a single embedding space. The space of SPD matrices contains multiple totally geodesic submanifolds that are isometric to Euclidean and hyperbolic spaces of various dimensions, allowing graphs with mixed geometric features to embed naturally without distortion.

### Mechanism 2
Performing message passing in the tangent space of SPD while maintaining SPD embeddings enables more effective information propagation. The Riemannian exponential and logarithm maps allow switching between SPD space and its tangent space, where standard linear operations can be performed while preserving the geometric properties of SPD.

### Mechanism 3
Gyro-calculus operations (gyro-addition and gyro-inversion) provide a principled way to perform vector space-like operations directly on SPD matrices. Gyro-addition P ⊕ Q = √(PQ)√P provides a way to "add" points in SPD space while preserving the geometric structure, enabling operations like bias addition and neighbor aggregation.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
How exactly does the presence of Euclidean and hyperbolic subspaces within SPDn contribute to improved performance on mixed-topology graphs? The authors hypothesize that SPDn's ability to contain both Euclidean and hyperbolic subspaces is an important factor in superior performance, but this is not fully understood.

### Open Question 2
What is the optimal classifier architecture for graph neural networks in SPD space, and under what conditions do different classifiers (Linear-XE, SVM-MM, NC-MM) perform best? The authors note that determining the optimal classifier is challenging and presents an avenue for continued improvement.

### Open Question 3
What is the computational overhead of SPD-based graph neural networks compared to Euclidean and hyperbolic alternatives when scaling to larger graphs and higher dimensions? The authors note that SPD models take longer to train but suggest the benefits outweigh this drawback.

## Limitations
- Optimal SPD dimension remains unclear, with no systematic exploration of the dimension-selection problem
- Computational overhead compared to specialized geometric GNNs lacks detailed complexity analysis
- Limited understanding of how Euclidean and hyperbolic subspaces within SPDn are leveraged during operations

## Confidence

- **High confidence**: The theoretical foundation of SPD as a Riemannian manifold with embedded Euclidean and hyperbolic subspaces
- **Medium confidence**: The implementation of gyro-calculus operations and their effectiveness in practice
- **Low confidence**: Claims about computational efficiency relative to specialized geometric GNNs

## Next Checks

1. **Dimensionality ablation study**: Systematically evaluate SPD4GNNs performance across different SPD dimensions (n=2,3,4,5) on the same datasets to quantify the trade-off between geometric flexibility and performance.

2. **Mixed-topology dataset creation**: Construct synthetic graphs with controlled mixtures of grid-like and tree-like substructures to directly test the claim that SPD can handle mixed geometric features better than specialized spaces.

3. **Computational complexity benchmarking**: Measure wall-clock time and memory usage for gyro-calculus operations versus standard Euclidean operations on graphs of increasing size to quantify the practical overhead of SPD embeddings.
