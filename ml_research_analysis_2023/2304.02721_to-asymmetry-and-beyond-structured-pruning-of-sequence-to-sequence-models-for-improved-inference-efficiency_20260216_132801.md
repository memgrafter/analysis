# To Asymmetry and Beyond: Structured Pruning of Sequence to Sequence Models for Improved Inference Efficiency

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2304.02721
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2304.02721
- **Reference count:** 40
- **Primary result:** Asymmetric pruning (removing more decoder layers than encoder layers) achieves up to 2.7× faster inference with minimal loss in Rouge-2 scores for summarization.

## Executive Summary
This paper investigates structured pruning of sequence-to-sequence models to improve inference efficiency for abstractive summarization. The authors demonstrate that asymmetric pruning—removing more decoder layers than encoder layers—yields significantly better speed-accuracy trade-offs than symmetric pruning. Their experiments across three summarization datasets (CNN/DM, XSUM, QIWS) and three model sizes (FLAN-T5-small, base, large) show that decoder-only pruning provides up to 2.7× speedup with minimal ROUGE-2 degradation. Larger models are more compressible, experiencing smaller relative accuracy drops when pruned. The authors release their code and trained models for reproducibility.

## Method Summary
The authors fine-tune pre-trained FLAN-T5 models on summarization datasets, then apply structured pruning by removing entire encoder and/or decoder layers. After pruning, models are fine-tuned again until convergence. They compare asymmetric pruning (e.g., pruning only decoder layers) versus symmetric pruning (pruning equal numbers of layers in both encoder and decoder). Performance is measured using ROUGE-1/2/L scores and inference latency on A10 GPU with batch size 1. The pruning ratios range from 1/6 to 5/6 of total layers.

## Key Results
- Decoder-only pruning achieves up to 2.7× speedup with minimal ROUGE-2 loss (often <1 point)
- Larger models (base, large) maintain accuracy better when pruned compared to smaller models
- Asymmetric pruning consistently outperforms symmetric pruning across all tested configurations
- Batch size affects speedup advantage, with non-uniform benefits diminishing as batch size grows

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: Asymmetric pruning yields higher speedup with lower accuracy loss because decoder execution dominates latency in seq2seq models.
- Mechanism: Decoder runs iteratively token-by-token until EOS, while encoder runs once per batch. Removing decoder layers directly reduces inference steps; removing encoder layers has minimal impact since encoder cost is amortized.
- Core assumption: Inference time is dominated by decoder computation, and encoder depth is more tightly coupled to summarization quality.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "We show that model accuracy is tied to the encoder size while inference efficiency is connected to the decoder."
  - [section 4.2] "While detailed inference results can be found in the appendix A.4 on this focused set of results, we can see that pruning only the encoder leads to no more than 30% improvement in inference efficiency at a sizable loss in accuracy."
- Break condition: If encoder-to-decoder ratio is inverted (e.g., in long-context tasks where encoder dominates), or if task requires heavy encoder-side reasoning.

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: Larger models are more compressible with smaller relative accuracy loss.
- Mechanism: Larger models have more parameters and richer representations, so pruning a fraction of layers reduces capacity less sharply; small models have less redundancy and are more sensitive to layer removal.
- Core assumption: Model capacity scales sublinearly with parameter count, and redundancy increases with model size.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [section 4.1] "Looking at abridged results in 5, 6, and 7, there is a clear scaling law as smaller models see much larger drops in performance when compressed to the same degree."
  - [section 4.1] "For example, on the QIWS dataset, compression to 1/6 of the layers on the encoder and decoder cause an 80% drop in R-2 on a small model but only 40% on the larger model."
- Break condition: If larger model overfits or pruning removes critical cross-attention components.

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: Uniform pruning gives consistent latency gains across batch sizes, but non-uniform speedup advantage diminishes as batch size grows.
- Mechanism: With large batch sizes, encoder computation is fully amortized and decoder per-token cost is less dominant; thus, removing decoder layers yields smaller relative speedup versus uniform pruning.
- Core assumption: Encoder cost scales with input length only; decoder cost scales with output length and batch size.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [section 5.3] "When the batch size is minimal, the difference in the type of non-uniformity has a significant impact on inference efficiency. As batches scale, the speedup from encoder only or decoder only becomes much closer and becomes minor when compared to uniform methods."
- Break condition: If output sequences are very short or batch sizes are small; or if streaming/infinite-generation scenarios where decoder cost always dominates.

## Architecture Onboarding

- **Component map:** Source text (max_seq_len=1024) -> Encoder stack (l_enc layers) -> Decoder stack (l_dec layers) -> Target summary (max_sum_len=256)
- **Critical path:** Pre-training → fine-tune full model → prune layers (asymmetric or symmetric) → fine-tune pruned model until convergence → evaluate ROUGE-2 and latency
- **Design tradeoffs:**
  - Asymmetric pruning gives best latency/accuracy balance but may hurt generation length or coherence
  - Symmetric pruning easier to implement but yields smaller speedup
  - Batch size tuning can mitigate or amplify speedup differences
- **Failure signatures:**
  - Large R-2 drop after pruning: likely removed too many decoder layers or encoder layers critical for context
  - Minimal latency gain after encoder-only pruning: confirm decoder dominates inference cost
  - Batch-size-dependent speedup variance: indicates asymmetric benefit is batch-size sensitive
- **First 3 experiments:**
  1. Prune only encoder (e.g., 6→3) → measure latency gain vs. R-2 drop (expect <30% speedup, ~5-10% R-2 loss)
  2. Prune only decoder (e.g., 6→3) → measure latency gain vs. R-2 drop (expect ~2× speedup, ~1-2 R-2 loss)
  3. Prune both equally (e.g., 6→3 each) → measure speedup vs. R-2 drop (expect ~5× speedup, ~5-8 R-2 loss)

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
The paper does not explicitly call out open questions, but the following areas remain unexplored based on the study's scope:
- How asymmetric pruning affects generation quality metrics beyond ROUGE (e.g., coherence, factual consistency)
- Whether the findings generalize to other transformer architectures beyond FLAN-T5
- The impact of different fine-tuning schedules after pruning on accuracy recovery

## Limitations
- The study is limited to FLAN-T5 models and may not generalize to other transformer architectures
- Evaluation focuses exclusively on ROUGE metrics, potentially missing quality aspects like coherence or factual consistency
- Batch-size-dependent speedup scaling claims are based on limited experimental evidence

## Confidence
- **High confidence:** Decoder-only pruning yields superior latency gains with minimal accuracy loss, well-supported by direct experimental evidence
- **Medium confidence:** Larger models are more amenable to pruning with smaller relative accuracy loss, but limited to three model sizes from one architecture family
- **Low confidence:** Batch-size-dependent speedup scaling claims lack theoretical grounding and broader empirical validation

## Next Checks
1. **Cross-architecture validation:** Apply asymmetric pruning to BART or Pegasus models and verify whether decoder-only pruning consistently yields 2× speedup with <2 ROUGE-2 degradation across multiple datasets.

2. **Task diversity test:** Evaluate pruning strategy on non-summarization seq2seq tasks (e.g., translation, question answering) to determine if decoder-dominance assumption holds universally.

3. **Quality metric expansion:** Measure generation quality using BERTScore, factual consistency checks, and length control to identify quality degradation modes that ROUGE scores might miss.
