# Improving Defensive Distillation using Teacher Assistant
## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2305.08076
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.08076
- Reference count: 20
- Primary result: Adding teacher assistant network improves defensive distillation robustness against adversarial attacks with minimal accuracy loss

## Executive Summary
This paper introduces an enhancement to defensive distillation by incorporating a teacher assistant network between the teacher and student models. The approach builds on knowledge distillation principles to improve model robustness against adversarial attacks while maintaining high accuracy. Experiments on MNIST and CIFAR10 datasets demonstrate that the teacher assistant method consistently outperforms baseline defensive distillation across multiple attack types, with particularly strong improvements in L0 attack resistance.

## Method Summary
The method extends defensive distillation by adding an intermediate teacher assistant model in the knowledge transfer pipeline. The standard distillation process trains a student model using soft labels from a teacher, but this approach introduces an assistant network trained on the teacher's soft labels, which then provides soft labels to the student. This creates a smoother gradient between teacher and student capacities. The approach is evaluated using three adversarial attack types (L0, L2, L∞) across multiple distillation temperatures, measuring both accuracy and robustness to perturbations.

## Key Results
- Adding teacher assistant improves model robustness against adversarial attacks in most cases
- Very little impact on accuracy compared to baseline models
- Multi-step distillation further improves robustness
- On CIFAR10, L0 attack requires 0.072 perturbation for baseline teacher vs 0.075 for student with teacher assistant

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- **Claim**: The teacher assistant model improves adversarial robustness by smoothing the probability distribution between the teacher and student, creating a more generalizable intermediate representation.
- **Mechanism**: By introducing an assistant network of intermediate capacity between the teacher and student, the distillation process creates a more gradual knowledge transfer. This reduces the gap between teacher and student, allowing the student to better capture the relative likelihood relationships encoded in the teacher's soft labels.
- **Core assumption**: The teacher-student gap creates a bottleneck in knowledge transfer that limits the student's ability to learn robust representations.
- **Evidence anchors**:
  - [abstract]: "we derive inspiration from teacher assistant knowledge distillation and propose that introducing an assistant network can improve the robustness of the distilled model"
  - [section I-B]: "the performance of the student is often far than ideal when there is a large difference between the capacity of the student and the teacher"
  - [corpus]: Weak - corpus contains related papers on distillation defense but no direct evidence about teacher assistant specific mechanisms
- **Break condition**: If the assistant network is too similar to either the teacher or student, it provides no additional smoothing benefit. If it's too dissimilar, it may introduce noise rather than useful intermediate knowledge.

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim**: Multi-step distillation progressively increases model robustness by iteratively smoothing gradients and reducing sensitivity to adversarial perturbations.
- **Mechanism**: Each distillation step reduces the magnitude of gradients in the model (as shown in the sensitivity analysis), making the model less responsive to small input changes. This creates a compounding effect where multiple distillation steps yield progressively smoother decision boundaries.
- **Core assumption**: Gradient magnitude directly correlates with model sensitivity to adversarial attacks, and reducing gradients increases robustness.
- **Evidence anchors**:
  - [section III-B]: "increasing distillation temperature will lead to decreasing the magnitude of the model gradients"
  - [section VI]: "robustness against the attacks increases as we carry out distillation through multiple steps"
  - [corpus]: Weak - corpus papers discuss distillation and robustness but don't specifically address multi-step effects
- **Break condition**: After sufficient distillation steps, additional steps may yield diminishing returns or could over-smooth the model, reducing its ability to capture important decision boundaries.

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim**: Higher distillation temperatures improve robustness by creating softer probability distributions that encode relative class relationships rather than hard binary labels.
- **Mechanism**: Higher temperatures produce probability vectors where the relative differences between classes are preserved but absolute differences are smoothed. This additional entropy in the probability vector provides structural information about the data that helps the student generalize better to adversarial examples.
- **Core assumption**: The entropy in soft labels contains structural information about class relationships that hard labels cannot capture.
- **Evidence anchors**:
  - [section I-A]: "The output of the teacher network thus contains relatively similar probabilities for these two digits... This extra entropy in the probability vector contains structural information about the data"
  - [section III-A]: "Using probabilities Fj(X) ensures that the training algorithm constrains the output neurons Fd j (X) proportionally to their likelihood"
  - [corpus]: Weak - corpus papers discuss temperature effects but don't specifically address the entropy-based mechanism
- **Break condition**: If temperature is set too high, the probability distribution approaches uniform (1/N for N classes), losing discriminative information entirely.

## Architecture Onboarding

- **Component map**:
  Teacher Network -> Teacher Assistant -> Student Network -> Adversarial Attack Module -> Evaluation Pipeline

- **Critical path**:
  1. Train teacher network on dataset
  2. Train TA on teacher's soft labels (temperature T)
  3. Train student on TA's soft labels (temperature T)
  4. Evaluate student against adversarial attacks
  5. Compare robustness with baseline (no TA) and varying temperatures

- **Design tradeoffs**:
  - Higher temperatures improve robustness but may reduce accuracy slightly
  - Adding more distillation steps increases robustness but adds computational cost
  - Assistant network capacity must balance between teacher and student for optimal transfer
  - Attack evaluation time increases with dataset size and number of attack types

- **Failure signatures**:
  - Accuracy drops significantly with increasing temperature (>1-2% degradation)
  - Robustness doesn't improve with added TA (teacher and student perform similarly)
  - Sensitivity analysis shows no gradient reduction with higher temperatures
  - Adversarial attacks succeed with minimal perturbation despite distillation

- **First 3 experiments**:
  1. Baseline comparison: Train teacher and student directly (no TA) at temperature T=1, measure accuracy and L2 attack robustness
  2. Single TA experiment: Train with TA at T=10, compare accuracy, sensitivity (gradient magnitude), and all three attack types against baseline
  3. Multi-step distillation: Train with 3-step distillation (teacher→TA1→TA2→student) at T=20, measure if robustness continues to improve beyond single TA

- What evidence would resolve it: Conducting experiments with varying distillation temperatures and measuring both sensitivity and robustness would help determine the optimal temperature for a given task.

- What evidence would resolve it: Testing defensive distillation against a wider range of adversarial attack methods would help determine its overall effectiveness and limitations.

## Limitations

- Core mechanisms linking teacher assistant networks to improved robustness remain partially theoretical
- Effectiveness of multi-step distillation beyond two assistant layers is unexplored
- Approach's generalizability to larger, more complex architectures remains unknown
- Computational overhead of training multiple assistant networks versus robustness gains has not been quantified

## Confidence

- **Mechanism Claims**: Medium - The smoothing and gradient reduction mechanisms are supported by experiments but lack theoretical guarantees
- **Empirical Results**: High - The MNIST and CIFAR10 results are reproducible with the provided specifications
- **Generalizability**: Low - Limited to small-scale datasets and simple architectures
