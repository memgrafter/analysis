# Summarizing Indian Languages using Multilingual Transformers based Models

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2303.16657
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.16657
- Reference count: 22
- Key outcome: Data augmentation significantly improves ROUGE scores for low-resource Indian language summarization, with IndicBART outperforming mT5-small in most cases

## Executive Summary
This paper evaluates multilingual transformer models (IndicBART and mT5-small) on abstractive summarization for low-resource Indian languages (English, Hindi, and Gujarati) using data augmentation. The authors demonstrate that data augmentation (3x and 5x) significantly improves ROUGE scores across all three languages. IndicBART, despite being smaller than mT5-small, shows competitive performance due to its language-specific pretraining on Indic languages. The study uses the FIRE 2022 ILSUM dataset and fine-tunes models using HuggingFace's Transformers library with PyTorch.

## Method Summary
The study fine-tunes IndicBART and mT5-small models on the FIRE 2022 ILSUM dataset for abstractive summarization in English, Hindi, and Gujarati. Models are trained with data augmentation at 3x and 5x levels to address the low-resource nature of these languages. The training uses a learning rate of 2e-5, max input length of 1024 tokens, and max output length of 100 tokens. ROUGE-1, ROUGE-2, ROUGE-3, and ROUGE-4 metrics are used for evaluation across 5-10 epochs.

## Key Results
- Data augmentation (3x and 5x) significantly improves ROUGE scores across English, Hindi, and Gujarati
- IndicBART outperforms mT5-small in most cases despite being smaller and more efficient
- Maximum performance achieved with 5x data augmentation on IndicBART for all three languages

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: Data augmentation significantly improves ROUGE scores for low-resource Indian language summarization
- Mechanism: Creates additional training samples through augmentation, exposing the model to more diverse linguistic patterns, reducing overfitting, and improving generalization
- Core assumption: Augmented data maintains linguistic quality and diversity relevant to the summarization task
- Evidence anchors: Experiments show data augmentation significantly improves ROUGE scores across all three languages
- Break condition: If augmented data introduces noise or irrelevant patterns, it could degrade model performance

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: IndicBART outperforms mT5-small for Indian language summarization tasks
- Mechanism: IndicBART's architecture is specifically optimized for Indic languages through pretraining on 11 Indic languages and English
- Core assumption: Language-specific pretraining captures linguistic nuances that mT5-small misses
- Evidence anchors: IndicBART outperforms mT5 in most cases despite being smaller
- Break condition: If test data contains significant code-mixing with English, mT5's broader multilingual pretraining might perform better

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: Abstractive summarization produces more human-like output compared to extractive methods
- Mechanism: Abstractive models generate summaries from scratch rather than selecting existing sentences, requiring more complex language understanding
- Core assumption: The complexity of abstractive summarization is justified by the quality of the output summaries
- Evidence anchors: Abstractive summarization is more human-like but more difficult to perform than extractive summarization
- Break condition: If ROUGE metrics don't adequately capture quality differences between abstractive and extractive methods

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map: Data preprocessing -> Data augmentation -> Model fine-tuning -> Evaluation pipeline
- Critical path: Load and preprocess dataset -> Apply data augmentation -> Fine-tune selected model -> Evaluate using ROUGE metrics -> Compare results
- Design tradeoffs:
  - IndicBART vs mT5-small: IndicBART is smaller but language-optimized; mT5-small is larger but general-purpose
  - Augmentation level: 3x provides moderate improvement; 5x provides maximum improvement but increases computational cost
  - Sequence length: 1024 input/100 output balances context with memory constraints
- Failure signatures:
  - Poor ROUGE scores despite high training accuracy (overfitting to augmented data)
  - Disproportionate degradation in ROUGE-2/3/4 scores (loss of semantic coherence)
  - Performance drops for code-mixed text (insufficient handling of English-Indic language mixing)
- First 3 experiments:
  1. Baseline: Fine-tune IndicBART on original dataset without augmentation (en_ibart/gu_ibart/hi_ibart)
  2. Moderate augmentation: Fine-tune IndicBART with 3x augmented data (da_en_ibart/da_gu_ibart/da_hi_ibart)
  3. Maximum augmentation: Fine-tune IndicBART with 5x augmented data (da5_en_ibart/da5_gu_ibart/da5_hi_ibart)

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- Question: How would larger models like mbart-large or mt5-base/mt5-large perform compared to IndicBART and mT5-small on low-resource Indian language summarization tasks?
- Basis in paper: Due to computational constraints, larger models like mbart-large and mt5-base could not be used
- Why unresolved: Computational limitations prevented experimentation with larger models
- What evidence would resolve it: Experimental results comparing mbart-large or mt5-base/mt5-large with IndicBART and mT5-small on the same datasets

### Open Question 2
- Question: What is the optimal amount of data augmentation needed to maximize performance on low-resource Indian language summarization?
- Basis in paper: Only 3x and 5x augmentation levels were tested
- Why unresolved: Only two augmentation levels were tested, leaving uncertainty about the optimal augmentation factor
- What evidence would resolve it: Systematic experiments testing multiple augmentation levels to identify diminishing returns

### Open Question 3
- Question: How do these multilingual transformer models handle code-mixing and script-mixing in Indian language text?
- Basis in paper: The dataset presents a special problem of code- and script-mixing
- Why unresolved: The paper reports overall performance metrics but doesn't analyze how models handle mixed-language content
- What evidence would resolve it: Analysis comparing model performance on purely monolingual segments versus code-mixed segments

## Limitations
- Lack of detailed explanation of data augmentation methodology makes it difficult to assess quality of augmented data
- No ablation studies to determine which specific components of data augmentation drive performance improvements
- Evaluation limited to ROUGE metrics without human evaluation or qualitative analysis of generated summaries
- Substantial computational resources required for 5x data augmentation may not be feasible for all research teams

## Confidence
- High confidence: Data augmentation improves ROUGE scores across all three languages
- Medium confidence: IndicBART outperforms mT5-small due to its language-specific pretraining
- Low confidence: Abstractive summarization produces more human-like output
