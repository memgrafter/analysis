# BERT-based Authorship Attribution on the Romanian Dataset called ROST

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2301.12500
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2301.12500
- Authors: 
- Reference count: 36
- Primary result: BERT-based authorship attribution achieved 87.4% macro-accuracy on the ROST Romanian dataset, surpassing earlier AI techniques

## Executive Summary
This paper presents BERT-based authorship attribution on the Romanian text dataset ROST, achieving state-of-the-art performance with 87.4% macro-accuracy. The dataset contains 400 texts from 10 Romanian authors spanning different periods, genres, and mediums. Texts were preprocessed into 200-token segments to fit BERT's input requirements while maintaining computational efficiency. A pre-trained Romanian BERT model was fine-tuned on three shuffled versions of the dataset, with evaluation using micro- and macro-accuracy metrics alongside precision, recall, and F1-score per author.

## Method Summary
The study fine-tuned a pre-trained Romanian BERT model (dumitrescustefan/bert-base-romanian-cased-v1) on the ROST dataset, which contains 400 Romanian texts from 10 authors. Texts were divided into 200-token segments to meet BERT's input constraints while maintaining sufficient context for author discrimination. The dataset was shuffled into three versions, each split into training and test sets. The HuggingFace Transformers library was used with AutoTokenizer, AutoModelForSequenceClassification, Trainer, and TrainingArguments. Performance was evaluated using micro- and macro-accuracy, precision, recall, and F1-score per author, with macro-accuracy showing the most robust results at 87.4%.

## Key Results
- Achieved 87.4% macro-accuracy, surpassing previous AI techniques on the same dataset
- Best performance on authors: Ion Creangă (1), Barbu Șt. Delavrancea (2), and Emilia Plugaru (7)
- Notable confusion between authors from similar periods, particularly Mihai Eminescu (3) and Nicolae Filimon (4)
- Lower performance on specific authors: Emil Gârleanu (4), Mihai Oltean (6), and Liviu Rebreanu (8)

- Mechanism: BERT dynamically generates token representations conditioned on both left and right context in all layers, allowing the model to capture nuanced stylistic patterns beyond static features.
- Core assumption: Contextual embeddings encode author-specific stylistic patterns that remain discriminative even when texts are shortened to 200 tokens.

- Core assumption: The linguistic and stylistic patterns in ROST are sufficiently similar to the general Romanian text distribution the model was pre-trained on.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- Question: How would preserving sentence integrity instead of arbitrary 200-token segments affect BERT-based authorship attribution performance on ROST?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper notes that texts were divided into 200-word segments without preserving sentence boundaries, and suggests future tests to examine if maintaining sentence integrity would impact results.
- Why unresolved: The current experiments used fixed-length segments that may have broken sentences, potentially disrupting contextual coherence for BERT.
- What evidence would resolve it: Comparing attribution accuracy between fixed-length segments versus sentence-boundary-preserving segments on the same dataset would provide direct evidence.

### Open Question 2
- Question: Would using longer input sequences beyond 200 tokens improve BERT performance on the ROST dataset, given the dataset contains texts up to 39,195 words?
- Basis in paper: [inferred] The paper limited inputs to 200 tokens due to BERT's constraints, but acknowledges this was a preprocessing choice that might affect performance compared to using full texts.
- Why unresolved: The truncation to 200 tokens was a practical necessity rather than an optimal choice, and longer contexts might provide better author characterization.
- What evidence would resolve it: Testing BERT with various input lengths (e.g., 200, 400, 512 tokens) while maintaining the same preprocessing approach would reveal the impact of sequence length.

### Open Question 3
- Question: How does BERT's performance on ROST compare to the original AI techniques when both use the same 200-token preprocessing approach?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper states that ROST was modified for BERT experiments, making direct comparison with previous AI techniques (which used full texts) impossible without recreating those experiments.
- Why unresolved: The preprocessing changes mean the datasets are no longer directly comparable, preventing fair evaluation of BERT against earlier methods.
- What evidence would resolve it: Re-running all previous AI techniques (SVM, MEP, C5.0, etc.) on the 200-token version of ROST would enable direct, apples-to-apples comparison with BERT results.

### Open Question 4
- Question: Could incorporating the Prediction by Partial Matching (PPM) technique with BERT improve authorship attribution accuracy on the ROST dataset?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper mentions PPM as a related technique from the literature and suggests exploring it as a future direction.
- Why unresolved: While PPM has been used for authorship attribution in compression-based approaches, its combination with BERT-based transformer models remains unexplored.
- What evidence would resolve it: Implementing a hybrid approach that combines PPM-based features with BERT embeddings and evaluating on ROST would demonstrate whether the combination provides complementary benefits.

## Limitations

- The ROST dataset is highly unbalanced with 400 texts from 10 authors, potentially inflating performance metrics
- Persistent confusion between authors from similar periods suggests temporal features rather than authorial style may be driving classifications
- Lower performance on specific authors (Emil Gârleanu, Mihai Oltean, Liviu Rebreanu) indicates certain authorial styles remain challenging for BERT to capture

## Confidence

- **High confidence**: BERT's overall superior performance compared to traditional methods on this dataset
- **Medium confidence**: The 200-token segment length being optimal for balancing context and computational efficiency
- **Medium confidence**: Fine-tuning pre-trained Romanian BERT transferring general language understanding to authorship attribution
- **Low confidence**: Claims about BERT's bidirectional architecture being the primary driver of improvement over static features, as no direct comparison was provided
