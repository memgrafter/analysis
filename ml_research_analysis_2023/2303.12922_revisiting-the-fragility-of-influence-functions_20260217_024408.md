# Revisiting the Fragility of Influence Functions

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2303.12922
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.12922
- Reference count: 31
- Key outcome: Investigates the validity of influence functions in deep learning, finding that current validation methods may be flawed rather than the methods themselves being inaccurate

## Executive Summary
This work examines the reliability of influence functions as a tool for understanding deep learning model behavior, particularly focusing on their performance in larger models and datasets. The authors conduct experiments across different datasets (Iris, MNIST, CIFAR10) and model architectures (fully connected, LeNet, VGG13) to evaluate how well influence functions approximate the true effect of training points on model predictions. Their findings challenge some previously held assumptions about the fragility of influence functions in deep learning contexts. The research suggests that while traditional validation approaches may be inadequate, influence functions themselves may still be valid tools for model interpretability.

## Method Summary
The authors evaluate influence functions by comparing approximate and true loss differences using Spearman correlation as the primary metric. They conduct experiments across three datasets of increasing complexity (Iris, MNIST, CIFAR10) with varying model architectures. The validation framework involves calculating influence estimates using the full Hessian matrix and comparing these to ground truth values obtained through re-training procedures. They also investigate the effect of model size (both depth and width) on influence function accuracy, and compare Bayesian Neural Networks against traditional methods. The analysis includes examining eigenvalues of the Hessian matrix to understand the stability of influence function approximations.

## Key Results
- No significant effect of increasing model size on influence function estimates for small datasets, contradicting previous work
- Bayesian Neural Networks outperform other methods in influence function accuracy for small datasets
- Spearman correlation may be an inadequate metric for evaluating influence function performance due to potential non-monotonic relationships
- Re-training procedures for obtaining true loss differences may be invalid for large datasets due to extreme curvature of the loss function

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
Influence functions approximate how the model's predictions would change if a training point were upweighted infinitesimally. The method relies on the inverse of the Hessian matrix to capture second-order information about the loss landscape. The mechanism works by using the curvature of the loss function around the optimum to estimate the sensitivity of model predictions to individual training examples. The accuracy of this approximation depends on how well the quadratic approximation around the optimum captures the true loss landscape, which is influenced by factors like dataset size, model architecture, and the flatness of the loss surface.

## Foundational Learning
- **Hessian Matrix**: Captures second-order derivatives of the loss function, representing the curvature of the loss landscape. Needed to understand how sensitive model predictions are to changes in training data. Quick check: verify eigenvalues of Hessian are finite and well-conditioned.
- **Influence Functions**: A technique from robust statistics that approximates the effect of removing a training point on model predictions. Needed as the core method being evaluated. Quick check: verify influence estimates have reasonable magnitude and sign.
- **Spearman Correlation**: A non-parametric measure of rank correlation that assesses how well the relationship between two variables can be described by a monotonic function. Needed as the primary evaluation metric. Quick check: plot true vs. estimated loss differences to visually inspect monotonicity.
- **Bayesian Neural Networks**: Neural networks that incorporate uncertainty by placing distributions over weights rather than point estimates. Needed to compare alternative approaches to traditional influence functions. Quick check: verify posterior distributions are well-calibrated.

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component Map**: Data → Model Training → Influence Estimation (Hessian-based) → Ground Truth (Retraining) → Evaluation (Spearman Correlation)
- **Critical Path**: The most critical computational step is computing the inverse Hessian or approximations thereof, as this dominates the computational complexity and determines the accuracy of influence estimates.
- **Design Tradeoffs**: Full Hessian computation provides accuracy but is computationally prohibitive for large models; approximations like LiSSA or conjugate gradient offer scalability at potential cost to accuracy.
- **Failure Signatures**: High condition numbers in the Hessian indicate numerical instability; non-monotonic relationships between true and estimated loss differences suggest the quadratic approximation is breaking down.
- **First Experiments**: 1) Verify influence functions work on a small, well-conditioned problem like logistic regression on Iris; 2) Compare influence estimates using full vs. approximate Hessian on a small neural network; 3) Test whether Bayesian methods provide more stable influence estimates on the same small network.

## Limitations
- Analysis primarily based on small-scale experiments with Iris, MNIST, and CIFAR-10 datasets
- Limited architectural variations tested may not generalize to all model families
- Re-training procedure for ground truth may not be valid for large datasets due to extreme curvature
- No clear alternative to Spearman correlation is proposed for evaluating influence functions

## Confidence
- Model size effects finding: Medium confidence (supported by experiments but limited architectural variations)
- Bayesian Neural Network performance: Medium confidence (promising but requires further validation)
- Spearman correlation inadequacy: Medium confidence (compelling arguments but alternative metrics needed)
- Overall conclusion about validation methods: Medium confidence (reasonable but leaves open questions)
