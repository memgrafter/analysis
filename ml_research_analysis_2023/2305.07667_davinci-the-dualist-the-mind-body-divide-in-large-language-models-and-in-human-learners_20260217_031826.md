# Davinci the Dualist: the mind-body divide in large language models and in human learners

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID**: 2305.07667
- **Source URL**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.07667
- **Reference count**: 0
- **Key result**: GPT-3.5 exhibits mind-body Dualist bias when probed about psychological traits' manifestation in brain or persistence after death

## Executive Summary
This study investigates whether large language models can acquire the mind-body Dualism bias from human narratives rather than through innate core knowledge. Researchers tested two GPT variants (GPT-3 and GPT-3.5) using structured prompts about psychological traits, finding that both models showed Dualist tendencies. The GPT-3.5 version demonstrated a stronger and more robust effect, considering thoughts less likely to show up in the brain while not assuming they persist after death. The results suggest that the mind-body divide is partly learnable from experience, as LLMs acquire not only knowledge but also human cognitive biases from exposure to human narratives.

## Method Summary
The researchers probed Davinci (GPT-3) and text-davinci-003 (GPT-3.5) using structured prompts about whether psychological traits would manifest in the brain or persist after death. The experimental design systematically varied phrasing and syntactic constraints to test the robustness of responses. Both models were evaluated on their reasoning about thoughts versus emotions/actions, with particular attention to whether responses aligned with human Dualist tendencies. The study compared model outputs across different phrasings and assessed sensitivity to linguistic variations.

## Key Results
- Both GPT-3 and GPT-3.5 exhibited mind-body Dualist bias in responses about psychological traits
- GPT-3.5 showed stronger and more robust Dualist effects than GPT-3
- The Dualist bias increased with the model's inductive potential and correlated with human behavior
- GPT-3.5's responses were sensitive to phrasing and syntactic constraints

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The study demonstrates that LLMs can learn cognitive biases through exposure to human narratives during training. When trained on human-generated text that reflects Dualist reasoning patterns, the models internalize these patterns and reproduce them in relevant contexts. The mechanism operates through statistical pattern matching rather than conceptual understanding, where the model associates certain phrasings and contexts with Dualist responses based on their prevalence in the training corpus.

## Architecture Onboarding

**Component map**: Training corpus -> Statistical pattern extraction -> Response generation -> Dualist bias manifestation

**Critical path**: Human narratives about mind-body relationships in training data → Model learns statistical associations → Prompts trigger learned patterns → Dualist responses generated

**Design tradeoffs**: The model trades computational efficiency for statistical approximation of human reasoning, sacrificing true understanding for pattern matching. This enables rapid learning of human biases but makes responses vulnerable to phrasing artifacts and limits generalization beyond learned patterns.

**Failure signatures**: Sensitivity to syntactic variations, inconsistent responses across similar prompts, correlation-based rather than conceptual reasoning, and artifactual patterns reflecting training corpus rather than genuine understanding.

## Limitations
- GPT-3.5's responses showed sensitivity to phrasing and syntactic constraints, suggesting the bias may be partly artifactual
- The study relied solely on GPT variants, limiting generalizability to other LLM architectures
- Binary experimental design may oversimplify complex psychological constructs involved in mind-body reasoning
- Correlation between human and model responses does not establish causation

## Confidence
- **LLMs exhibit mind-body Dualist bias**: High confidence - consistent results across two model versions and multiple prompt variations
- **Bias emerges from learning rather than innate knowledge**: Medium confidence - demonstrates learnability but cannot fully exclude pre-training influences
- **Results generalize to broader Dualist reasoning**: Low confidence - model-specific sensitivity to phrasing and limited scope of testing

## Next Checks
1. Test additional LLM architectures (Claude, LLaMA, etc.) with identical prompts to assess whether the Dualist bias generalizes beyond GPT models
2. Conduct ablation studies varying training corpus composition to determine which types of human narratives most strongly influence the Dualist bias
3. Design prompts that explicitly distinguish between conceptual understanding and linguistic pattern-matching to better isolate genuine Dualist reasoning from superficial correlations
