# Evaluating the feasibility of using Generative Models to generate Chest X-Ray Data

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID**: 2305.18927
- **Source URL**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.18927
- **Reference count**: 0
- **Primary result**: PG-GANs and Stable Diffusion can generate visually convincing synthetic chest X-ray images with acceptable classification performance (0.73 accuracy using synthetic data vs 0.81 with real data only)

## Executive Summary
This paper investigates the feasibility of using generative models to create synthetic chest X-ray images, addressing the critical challenge of limited medical data availability due to ethical constraints. The authors explore two approaches: Progressive Growing GANs (PG-GANs) and fine-tuned Stable Diffusion models trained on the Chest X-ray 14 dataset. While the classification accuracy decreased when incorporating synthetic images (from 0.81 to 0.73), the results remain promising for enhancing medical diagnosis through deep learning. The study demonstrates that generative models can produce visually convincing synthetic chest X-rays, though concerns about overfitting and limited disease-specific training data highlight areas requiring further investigation.

## Method Summary
The authors employ two generative modeling approaches to synthesize chest X-ray images. First, they use Progressive Growing GANs (PG-GANs) trained on the Chest X-ray 14 dataset, which contains over 100,000 labeled frontal chest radiographs. Second, they fine-tune pre-trained Stable Diffusion models on the same dataset to generate synthetic chest X-rays. Both models are evaluated qualitatively through visual inspection and quantitatively by training a classification model on combined real and synthetic datasets. The performance comparison focuses on classification accuracy for pneumonia detection and other common chest X-ray abnormalities.

## Key Results
- Visual inspection confirms PG-GAN and Stable Diffusion models generate realistic-looking chest X-ray images
- Classification accuracy drops from 0.81 (real data only) to 0.73 when synthetic images are included
- Synthetic data integration shows potential for augmenting limited medical datasets while maintaining reasonable diagnostic performance
- Overfitting concerns identified during generative model training require attention for clinical applications

**Component Map:**

**Critical Path:**
PG-GAN training on Chest X-ray 14 → Synthetic chest X-ray generation → Classification model training → Performance evaluation (0.81 real vs 0.73 real+synthetic)

**Design Tradeoffs:**
The choice between PG-GAN and Stable Diffusion involves balancing training stability (PG-GANs are known for stable high-resolution generation) against leveraging pre-trained knowledge (Stable Diffusion requires less training data but may introduce artifacts from the original training domain).

**Failure Signatures:**
Classification accuracy significantly lower than baseline (>0.08 drop) indicates synthetic data quality issues; visually unrealistic generated images suggest training instability or mode collapse in GANs; overfitting manifests as poor generalization to unseen samples.

## Limitations
- Classification accuracy decreases when incorporating synthetic data (0.81 → 0.73), suggesting potential quality or distribution mismatch issues
- Limited disease-specific training data may produce less reliable synthetic samples for rare conditions
- Single dataset evaluation (Chest X-ray 14) constrains generalizability to other medical imaging contexts
