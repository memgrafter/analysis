# From high-dimensional & mean-field dynamics to dimensionless ODEs: A unifying approach to SGD in two-layers networks

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2302.05882
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2302.05882
- Reference count: 40
- Primary result: Low-dimensional ODEs describe SGD dynamics in two-layer networks, independent of input dimension in classical regime

## Executive Summary
This work develops a low-dimensional deterministic description of one-pass stochastic gradient descent (SGD) for two-layer neural networks trained on Gaussian data with synthetic labels. The approach leverages sufficient statistics of the network and teacher activations to derive ordinary differential equations (ODEs) that govern the dynamics. A key finding is that these ODEs are independent of the input dimension d in the classical gradient-flow regime, exhibiting a dimension-free property. The framework unifies several existing limits—mean-field, high-dimensional, and classical—and extends to over-parameterized networks through a mean-field PDE approximation. Numerical results validate the theoretical predictions across regimes, including explicit low-dimensional ODEs for quadratic activations.

## Method Summary
The paper derives low-dimensional ODEs by decomposing network weights into projections onto teacher vectors and orthogonal components, tracking sufficient statistics matrices M and Q. These ODEs capture SGD dynamics across classical (small learning rate), high-dimensional (large d), and mean-field (large width) regimes. The classical regime yields dimension-independent ODEs through Gaussian data assumptions, while high-dimensional settings include variance terms proportional to learning rate over model size. The mean-field regime approximates the empirical measure with a PDE, exploiting invariance properties of the teacher-student setup.

## Key Results
- Low-dimensional ODEs describe SGD dynamics independent of input dimension d in classical gradient-flow regime
- High-dimensional limit introduces variance term proportional to γ/p that affects limiting risk
- Mean-field PDE approximation extends analysis to over-parameterized networks with rotation-invariant initializations
- Framework unifies classical, high-dimensional, and mean-field limits through common sufficient statistics approach

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map:
  - Gaussian data generation -> Two-layer student network -> Teacher network with synthetic labels -> One-pass SGD optimization -> Sufficient statistics tracking -> Low-dimensional ODEs/PDEs

- Critical path:
  1. Generate Gaussian data and synthetic labels using the teacher network
  2. Initialize the student network parameters
  3. Run one-pass SGD and track the sufficient statistics matrix \(\Omega(t)\)
  4. Derive the low-dimensional ODEs or PDEs that describe the dynamics in the appropriate regime
  5. Analyze the convergence and generalization properties of the network

- Design tradeoffs:
  - Computational efficiency vs. accuracy: The low-dimensional ODEs and PDEs offer a computationally efficient way to analyze the SGD dynamics but may not capture all the nuances of the full high-dimensional system.
  - Generality vs. specificity: The analysis assumes a specific data generation process (Gaussian data with synthetic labels) and network architecture (two-layer neural network), which may not generalize to all settings.

- Failure signatures:
  - If the data is non-Gaussian or the network architecture deviates significantly from the assumed two-layer structure, the low-dimensional ODEs and PDEs may not accurately describe the SGD dynamics.
  - If the SGD iterates escape the bounded region assumed in the analysis, the convergence properties of the network may be affected.

- First 3 experiments:
  1. Verify the dimension-independence property of the ODEs in the classical regime by running simulations with different input dimensions \(d\) but fixed initial conditions.
  2. Investigate the effect of the variance term in the high-dimensional regime by comparing the dynamics with and without this term in the ODEs.
  3. Test the mean-field approximation in the over-parameterized regime by comparing the dynamics of the full network with the low-dimensional PDE.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- Question: How robust is the dimension-independence property of the ODEs in the classical regime when data deviates from Gaussian assumptions?
- Basis in paper: [inferred] The dimension-independence is explicitly derived under Gaussian data assumptions, with a note that dimensionality still plays a crucial role through initialization.
- Why unresolved: The analysis relies on Gaussianity to derive the ODEs and show their independence from dimension d, but real-world data rarely follows perfect Gaussian distributions.
- What evidence would resolve it: Numerical experiments comparing the ODEs' predictions with SGD simulations using non-Gaussian data distributions (e.g., heavy-tailed, multimodal) would test the robustness of the dimension-independence property.

### Open Question 2
- Question: What are the precise conditions under which the mean-field approximation (MF-ODE) remains valid as d approaches infinity, especially for non-rotation-invariant initializations?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] Theorem 3.2 assumes rotation-invariant initializations (A3), and the high-dimensional mean-field limit (HDMF-ODE) is derived by neglecting off-diagonal terms of a random matrix Ξ.
- Why unresolved: The paper assumes rotation invariance to simplify the mean-field equations, but this assumption might be too restrictive for practical scenarios where initializations are not perfectly rotation-invariant.
- What evidence would resolve it: Analyzing the convergence of MF-ODE to HDMF-ODE as d increases for different types of initializations (e.g., with correlations, non-uniform distributions) would clarify the role of rotation invariance in the high-dimensional limit.

### Open Question 3
- Question: How does the presence of label noise affect the long-term dynamics and convergence properties of SGD in the high-dimensional and mean-field regimes, beyond the first-order correction term Ψ(noise)?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper derives a first-order correction term Ψ(noise) for the GF-ODE to account for label noise in the classical regime, but it is noted that this term vanishes in the true γ/p → 0+ limit.
- Why unresolved: The analysis of label noise is limited to the classical regime and only considers the first-order correction. The impact of noise on the high-dimensional and mean-field dynamics, where the variance term is explicitly contributing to the limiting dynamics, is not explored.
- What evidence would resolve it: Extending the analysis of Ψ(noise) to the SS-ODE, MF-ODE, and HDMF-ODE, and comparing the predicted dynamics with SGD simulations under different noise levels, would reveal the role of label noise in the high-dimensional and mean-field regimes.

## Limitations

- The analysis critically depends on Gaussian data assumptions that rarely hold in practical scenarios
- Dimension-independence requires extremely small learning rates to be observable in practice
- The variance term in high-dimensional settings introduces potentially problematic dependence on γ/p ratio

## Confidence

- **High confidence**: The mathematical framework connecting sufficient statistics to low-dimensional ODEs is rigorously established. The decomposition approach using W = MP⁻¹W* + W⊥ is mathematically sound.
- **Medium confidence**: The dimension-independence property in the classical regime is proven theoretically but may be difficult to observe in practical settings due to numerical constraints.
- **Low confidence**: The practical relevance of the variance term in high-dimensional settings and its impact on generalization remains largely theoretical without extensive empirical validation.

3. **Robustness to non-Gaussian data**: Extend the analysis to non-Gaussian data distributions (e.g., sub-Gaussian or heavy-tailed) to assess the sensitivity of the dimension-independence property and determine the range of data distributions for which the framework remains valid.
