# Multimodal Composite Association Score: Measuring Gender Bias in Generative Multimodal Models

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2304.13855
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2304.13855
- Reference count: 4
- Introduces MCAS as a proposal for examining bias across both text and image modes for large scale multimodal generative models and provides the preliminary demonstration of its effectiveness when used to evaluate models for gender bias

## Executive Summary
This paper introduces the Multimodal Composite Association Score (MCAS) as a comprehensive methodology for quantifying gender bias in multimodal generative models. The approach builds on established bias measurement frameworks like WEAT and extends them to multimodal contexts, measuring bias through three components: Text-Text, Image-Image, and Cross-modality association scores. The paper demonstrates the methodology's effectiveness by evaluating DALL-E 2 and Stable Diffusion models, revealing measurable gender bias in both systems.

## Method Summary
MCAS measures gender bias in multimodal generative models through a composite scoring system that evaluates bias across three dimensions: text-to-text associations, image-to-image associations, and cross-modality associations between text and images. The methodology leverages established bias measurement techniques and adapts them for multimodal contexts, allowing for both internal bias measurement within models and external evaluation against standards. The approach can be applied to different model stages and extended to other modalities beyond gender bias, potentially measuring biases related to race, ethnicity, and geography.

## Key Results
- Measured bias shows the presence of traditional gender bias in both DALL-E 2 and Stable Diffusion
- MCAS provides a comprehensive score for quantifying bias in multimodal models
- The methodology can measure both internal bias and provide ways to measure bias using external data or standards

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
MCAS works by extending established bias measurement frameworks to multimodal contexts through a systematic evaluation of associations across text and image modalities. The methodology leverages the proven WEAT framework while adapting it to capture cross-modal relationships that exist in generative models. By measuring bias at multiple stages and through multiple associations, MCAS provides a more complete picture of how gender bias manifests in multimodal systems.

## Architecture Onboarding

**Component Map:** Text processing -> Image generation -> Cross-modal association -> Composite scoring

**Critical Path:** Input text → Text-Text bias measurement → Image generation → Image-Image bias measurement → Cross-modality evaluation → MCAS calculation

**Design Tradeoffs:** The methodology balances comprehensiveness (measuring multiple bias dimensions) against complexity (requiring multiple evaluation stages), and generalizability (can extend to other biases) against specificity (currently focused on gender).

**Failure Signatures:** If Text-Text scores show high bias but Image-Image scores don't, this suggests bias is primarily textual rather than visual. If cross-modality scores dominate, this indicates strong interactions between modalities amplify bias.

**First 3 Experiments:**
1. Run MCAS on a baseline model with known gender associations to verify scoring works
2. Compare MCAS scores across different prompt formulations to test sensitivity
3. Measure individual component scores (Text-Text, Image-Image, Cross-modality) separately to identify which stage contributes most to overall bias

## Limitations
- The paper acknowledges it focused only on gender bias, leaving other biases (race, ethnicity, geography) unexplored
- Validation is primarily based on two models, which may limit generalizability
- The relationship between MCAS scores and real-world impacts remains unclear

## Confidence
**High confidence:** The technical implementation of MCAS components (Text-Text, Image-Image, and Cross-modality scores) appears sound and follows established methodologies. The demonstration of bias in DALL-E 2 and Stable Diffusion is robust within the scope tested.

**Medium confidence:** The proposed extensions to other modalities, applications in MLOps, and use for bias mitigation are plausible but not yet validated. The scoring system's sensitivity to prompt variations and hyperparameter changes needs more empirical support.

**Low confidence:** Claims about using MCAS for understanding bias formation during training or for comprehensive drift analysis lack sufficient methodological detail or validation.

## Next Checks
1. Test MCAS across a broader range of multimodal models beyond just DALL-E 2 and Stable Diffusion to establish generalizability.

2. Validate the proposed extensions by implementing MCAS in an MLOps pipeline and measuring its utility for bias monitoring during model deployment.

3. Conduct systematic experiments varying prompts, hyperparameters, and model stages to quantify how these factors affect MCAS scores and identify potential bias amplification patterns.
