# Leveraging GPT-4 for Food Effect Summarization to Enhance Product-Specific Guidance Development via Iterative Prompting

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID**: 2306.16275
- **Source URL**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.16275
- **Reference count**: 0
- **Primary result**: GPT-4 generates more accurate food effect summaries than ChatGPT for NDA review documents, with 85% consistency with FDA professional ratings

## Executive Summary
This study introduces iterative prompting, a multi-turn conversational approach that leverages ChatGPT and GPT-4 to generate food effect summaries from NDA review documents. The method refines summary quality progressively through keyword-focused and length-controlled prompts. Evaluations using 100 NDA review documents show GPT-4 outperforms ChatGPT in summary accuracy, as rated by FDA professionals (43% vs. 12%) and GPT-4 consistency assessment (64% vs. 35%). Importantly, FDA professionals rated 85% of GPT-4-generated summaries as factually consistent with reference summaries, with GPT-4 evaluation confirming 72% consistency. These results demonstrate GPT-4's strong potential to assist FDA professionals in drafting accurate food effect summaries, thereby improving efficiency in product-specific guidance development.

## Method Summary
The method employs three-turn iterative prompting using OpenAI API to generate summaries from NDA review documents with annotated food effect sections. Turn 1 provides task instruction, Turn 2 adds keyword constraints (AUC, Cmax, Tmax), and Turn 3 enforces length constraints (2-3 sentences). Generated summaries are evaluated using ROUGE metrics, human pairwise comparison by FDA assessors, and GPT-4 consistency scoring against reference summaries from FDA drug labeling.

## Key Results
- GPT-4 achieves 85% consistency with FDA professional ratings for generated summaries
- FDA professionals rated GPT-4 summaries as accurate 43% of the time vs 12% for ChatGPT
- GPT-4 consistency evaluation shows 72% agreement with human ratings (69% overlap)

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: Iterative prompting progressively improves summary quality by refining keyword inclusion and length control across turns
- Mechanism: Each turn adds a constraint (keywords → length) that forces the model to incorporate previously missing details while maintaining conciseness
- Core assumption: The model can retain earlier turn information in its working memory and adjust output accordingly
- Evidence anchors: [abstract] "the summary quality is progressively improved throughout the iterative prompting process"; [section] "Turn 2 (keyword-constrained prompt): Add facts of AUC, Cmax, and Tmax in the summary. Turn 3 (length-constrained prompt): Summarize it in 2-3 sentences and keep the detail of AUC, Cmax, and Tmax."

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: GPT-4 outperforms ChatGPT in summarization due to superior adherence to length constraints and inclusion of key metrics
- Mechanism: GPT-4 better follows explicit length and content instructions, producing more consistent and concise summaries
- Core assumption: GPT-4 has improved instruction-following capabilities over ChatGPT
- Evidence anchors: [abstract] "GPT-4 performs better than ChatGPT, as evaluated by FDA professionals (43% vs. 12%) and GPT-4 consistency assessment (64% vs. 35%)"; [section] "GPT-4 demonstrates better adherence to the given length constraints than ChatGPT. All the summaries generated by GPT-4 meet the requirement in the length-constraint prompt, while only 89% of the summaries generated by ChatGPT follow the constraint."

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: GPT-4 can be used for automated consistency evaluation, providing a cheaper alternative to human raters
- Mechanism: GPT-4 compares generated summaries against reference summaries to determine factual consistency
- Core assumption: GPT-4 can reliably judge factual consistency between two texts
- Evidence anchors: [abstract] "GPT-4 rating of 72% consistency" and "human majority vote indicating they are consistent with the reference summary"; [section] "GPT-4 evaluation shows a consistency rate of 72%, albeit low relative to human rating, there is rather strong consistency of rating between human and GPT-4 as both share 69% overlap."

## Architecture Onboarding

- **Component map**: Input (NDA review document sections) → Processing (Three-turn iterative prompting via OpenAI API) → Output (Generated summary) → Evaluation (ROUGE metrics, human ratings, GPT-4 scoring)

- **Critical path**: 1. Load annotated NDA section 2. Send first-turn prompt (task instruction) 3. Send second-turn prompt (keyword inclusion) 4. Send third-turn prompt (length constraint) 5. Collect generated summary 6. Evaluate using ROUGE, human ratings, and GPT-4 scoring

- **Design tradeoffs**: Model choice (gpt-3.5-turbo cheaper vs gpt-4 better quality), Prompt length (detail vs token limits), Human vs automated evaluation (cost vs accuracy)

- **Failure signatures**: Summaries missing key metrics (AUC, Cmax, Tmax), GPT-4 failing to follow length constraints, Automated metrics disagreeing with human judgments, GPT-4 biased toward its own outputs in consistency evaluation

- **First 3 experiments**: 1. Compare single-turn vs three-turn prompting on a subset of documents 2. Test temperature=0 vs temperature=0.7 for GPT-4 consistency evaluation 3. Evaluate impact of prompt wording variations on summary quality

  - What evidence would resolve it: Experimental data comparing pharmacokinetic parameters (AUC, Cmax, Tmax) across different meal types for the same drug would clarify the impact of meal composition

- **Open Question 2**: What is the impact of iterative prompting on the summarization of non-food effect related pharmacokinetic information?
  - Basis in paper: [inferred] The study demonstrates iterative prompting's effectiveness for food effect summarization, but does not explore its application to other pharmacokinetic domains
  - Why unresolved: The iterative prompting approach is validated only for food effect summaries, leaving its generalizability to other pharmacokinetic domains untested
  - What evidence would resolve it: Applying iterative prompting to summarize other pharmacokinetic aspects (e.g., absorption, distribution) and comparing results with human-generated summaries would determine its broader applicability

- **Open Question 3**: How does the inclusion of irrelevant information in the input text affect the quality of the generated summary?

  - Why unresolved: The study acknowledges the potential issue but does not measure the extent to which irrelevant information impacts summary quality
  - What evidence would resolve it: Systematic experiments varying the amount of irrelevant information in the input and assessing the impact on summary quality metrics would quantify this effect

- **Open Question 4**: What are the limitations of GPT-4's evaluation capabilities in summarization tasks, and how can they be mitigated?
  - Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper identifies issues such as inconsistency, ordering effects, and bias in GPT-4's evaluation, but does not propose specific mitigation strategies
  - Why unresolved: While limitations are identified, the study does not explore potential solutions to improve GPT-4's evaluation reliability

## Limitations
- Limited evaluation scope: FDA professional ratings based on 30 randomly selected summaries may not capture full performance variability
- Potential bias in automated evaluation: GPT-4's consistency assessment may be influenced by its own output patterns
- Uncontrolled variables: The study does not report temperature settings or other generation parameters that could affect results

## Confidence
- **High confidence**: GPT-4 outperforms ChatGPT in following length constraints and producing concise summaries
- **Medium confidence**: Iterative prompting improves summary quality through progressive refinement
- **Medium confidence**: FDA professional ratings validate GPT-4's superiority over ChatGPT for food effect summarization
