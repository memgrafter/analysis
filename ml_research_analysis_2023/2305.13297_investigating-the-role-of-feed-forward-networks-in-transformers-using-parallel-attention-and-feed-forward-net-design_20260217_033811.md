# Investigating the Role of Feed-Forward Networks in Transformers Using Parallel Attention and Feed-Forward Net Design

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2305.13297
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.13297
- Reference count: 8
- PAF design achieves 1.5x-2x speed gains while maintaining GLUE benchmark performance

## Executive Summary
This paper introduces a Parallel Attention and Feed-Forward Net Design (PAF) that runs the attention block and feed-forward network (FFN) in parallel at each transformer layer, as opposed to the standard series approach. The authors claim this architectural modification can provide 1.5x to 2x speed improvements while maintaining model performance. They validate their approach by pre-training RoBERTa-large and Bert-Large-Uncased variants using PAF and evaluating them on the GLUE benchmark, demonstrating near-identical performance to their series counterparts. The work also provides theoretical justification for why FFN primarily serves to prevent token embedding degeneration rather than contribute to representational power.

## Method Summary
The PAF design modifies the standard transformer architecture by running the multi-head attention and FFN blocks in parallel within each layer, rather than sequentially. Both blocks receive the same input from the previous layer and their outputs are combined through residual connections before layer normalization. This requires careful initialization and normalization to ensure stable training. The authors pre-train two large language models (RoBERTa-large and Bert-Large-Uncased) using this parallel design and compare their performance to standard series implementations on the GLUE benchmark tasks.

## Key Results
- PAF variants achieve near-identical GLUE benchmark performance compared to SAF counterparts
- Theoretical analysis shows FFN's primary role is preventing token embedding degeneration
- Residual norms from attention blocks are sufficiently smaller than input token embedding norms
- Parallel execution can provide 1.5x to 2x speed improvements

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The paper demonstrates that in standard transformers, the feed-forward network's primary function is to prevent the degeneration of token embeddings by maintaining their norm, rather than contributing significant representational power. When attention and FFN are run in parallel, each block can independently process the input while maintaining stability through proper initialization and normalization. The residual connection ensures that both pathways contribute to the final output, with the attention mechanism handling contextual relationships and the FFN maintaining embedding stability.

Design tradeoffs: Speed vs implementation complexity, where parallelization offers computational gains but requires careful synchronization and initialization.

## Limitations
- Performance validation limited to GLUE benchmark tasks only
- Theoretical assumptions about token embedding behavior may not generalize across all datasets
- Speed gain claims are based on theoretical analysis rather than comprehensive empirical measurement
- No exploration of PAF in non-language domains like vision or multi-modal transformers

## Confidence
- High confidence in experimental methodology and GLUE benchmark results
- Medium confidence in theoretical analysis of token embedding behavior
- Medium confidence in efficiency claims due to potential implementation variations
