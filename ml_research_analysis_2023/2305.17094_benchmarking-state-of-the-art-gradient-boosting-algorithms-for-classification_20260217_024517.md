# Benchmarking state-of-the-art gradient boosting algorithms for classification

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2305.17094
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.17094
- Reference count: 33
- Primary result: LightGBM with randomized search tuning achieved highest average ranks across accuracy, F1, and AUC metrics on 12 diverse classification datasets

## Executive Summary
This study benchmarks four gradient boosting algorithms—GBM, XGBoost, LightGBM, and CatBoost—on 12 diverse datasets for classification tasks. The research focuses on comparing randomized search with Bayesian optimization using Tree-structured Parzen Estimators (TPE) for hyperparameter tuning, evaluating performance using accuracy, F1-score, and AUC metrics. Statistical validation through Wilcoxon signed-ranks and Nemenyi tests confirms significant performance differences between algorithms.

The findings demonstrate that LightGBM consistently outperforms other algorithms when tuned with randomized search, while XGBoost and CatBoost show strong baseline performance with minimal improvement from tuning. The study provides practical guidance on algorithm selection based on dataset characteristics, with LightGBM emerging as the fastest overall despite CatBoost's slower performance on high-dimensional data.

## Method Summary
The research evaluates four gradient boosting algorithms (GBM, XGBoost, LightGBM, CatBoost) across 12 diverse classification datasets using both baseline and tuned configurations. Hyperparameter tuning employs two strategies: randomized search and Bayesian optimization with Tree-structured Parzen Estimators (TPE), each running for 1,000 iterations. Performance is assessed using accuracy, F1-score, and AUC metrics, with statistical validation through Wilcoxon signed-ranks and Nemenyi tests to determine significant differences between algorithms and tuning methods.

## Key Results
- LightGBM with randomized search achieved highest average ranks across all three metrics
- Statistical tests confirmed LightGBM's significant improvement over baseline GBM
- XGBoost and CatBoost showed strong baseline performance with minimal tuning gains
- LightGBM demonstrated fastest overall training times across datasets

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The superior performance of LightGBM stems from its histogram-based learning approach and gradient-based one-side sampling (GOSS), which efficiently handles large datasets by reducing computational complexity. The algorithm's leaf-wise growth strategy with depth constraints allows for more precise model fitting compared to level-wise approaches used by other implementations. Randomized search tuning proved particularly effective for LightGBM because its histogram-based optimization is less sensitive to specific hyperparameter combinations, allowing broader exploration of the search space to find robust configurations.

## Limitations
- Small sample of 12 datasets may not represent full diversity of classification problems
- Hyperparameter tuning procedures may not have explored full parameter space optimally
- Study does not account for computational cost of hyperparameter optimization
- Limited to three performance metrics without considering model interpretability or resource requirements

## Confidence
High confidence: Relative algorithm rankings (LightGBM > XGBoost ≈ CatBoost > GBM) consistent across metrics and statistical tests; computational efficiency findings well-supported.

Medium confidence: Magnitude of tuning improvements varies considerably across datasets, suggesting sensitivity to data characteristics.

Low confidence: Practical guidance for algorithm selection based on dataset size and dimensionality requires further validation across broader problem types.
