# Evaluation of drain, a deep-learning approach to rain retrieval from gpm passive microwave radiometer

## Quick Facts

- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.01220

## Executive Summary
This paper introduces DRAIN, a deep learning approach for rain retrieval from GPM passive microwave radiometer data. The method uses a U-net architecture trained on co-located GMI brightness temperatures and DPR surface rain rates to process images rather than individual pixels. Using only four brightness temperature channels (37 and 89 GHz, horizontal and vertical polarizations), DRAIN achieves strong performance with Probability of Detection values of 0.75 over ocean and 0.74 over land, and R²=0.29 when validated against Météo-France ground radar data.

The evaluation demonstrates that DRAIN provides reliable confidence intervals for rain estimates up to approximately 10 mm/hr and shows fewer coastal artifacts than the operational GPROF algorithm. The approach is particularly effective over land where it outperforms GPROF, and the image-based processing approach provides better spatial coherence than pixel-wise methods. The algorithm's ability to handle coastal regions and provide uncertainty estimates represents significant improvements over existing operational precipitation retrieval methods.

## Method Summary
DRAIN employs a U-net architecture trained on quantile regression loss function with 99 quantiles to retrieve rain rates from GMI brightness temperatures. The model uses four input channels: 37 and 89 GHz brightness temperatures in both horizontal and vertical polarizations. Training data consists of 70,000 images from 2014-2018, with 33,000 images for validation, and testing performed on 2019 data. The approach processes entire GMI swaths as images rather than individual pixels, allowing the model to capture spatial patterns and correlations. The quantile regression framework provides confidence intervals for each rain rate estimate, with the 99-quantile model capturing the upper bound of precipitation uncertainty.

## Key Results
- DRAIN achieves POD of 0.75 over ocean and 0.74 over land with FAR of 0.17 and 0.21 respectively
- Performance comparable to or slightly better than GPROF, particularly over land regions
- R² value of 0.29 against Météo-France ground radar validation versus GPROF's 0.20
- Reliable confidence intervals provided up to approximately 10 mm/hr rain rate
- Fewer coastal artifacts compared to GPROF algorithm

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The success of DRAIN stems from its ability to capture complex, non-linear relationships between microwave brightness temperatures and surface precipitation through deep learning. Unlike traditional physical models, the neural network can learn statistical patterns from large volumes of training data without requiring explicit physical parameterization. The image-based processing approach leverages spatial context and patterns that single-pixel methods miss, improving both detection accuracy and spatial coherence of rain estimates.

## Foundational Learning
**Quantile Regression Loss Function**: Needed to provide confidence intervals and handle precipitation's skewed distribution. Quick check: Verify the 99-quantile model captures extreme precipitation events without excessive false alarms.

**U-net Architecture**: Needed for image-to-image translation with skip connections preserving spatial resolution. Quick check: Confirm receptive field covers appropriate spatial scales for precipitation features.

**Brightness Temperature Preprocessing**: Needed to normalize multi-channel inputs across varying surface conditions. Quick check: Validate input scaling maintains physical relationships between channels.

**Co-located Training Data**: Needed to establish ground truth relationships between microwave observations and surface precipitation. Quick check: Ensure sufficient training samples across all precipitation regimes.

## Architecture Onboarding

**Component Map**: GMI brightness temperatures (37/89 GHz, H/V) -> U-net encoder -> bottleneck -> U-net decoder -> Rain rate estimates + confidence intervals

**Critical Path**: Input brightness temperatures flow through convolutional layers, bottleneck representation, then up-convolutional layers to produce rain rate maps with associated uncertainty estimates.

**Design Tradeoffs**: The choice of four channels balances computational efficiency with information content, while the U-net architecture trades parameter efficiency for spatial resolution preservation. The quantile regression approach increases model complexity but provides valuable uncertainty quantification.

**Failure Signatures**: Coastal artifacts appear as systematic biases where land-ocean transitions occur. Performance degradation over complex terrain suggests limitations in handling heterogeneous surface conditions. Underperformance on heavy rain (>10 mm/hr) indicates saturation in learned relationships.

## Limitations
- Geographic validation limited to single region (Météo-France), restricting generalizability to other climate zones
- Performance degradation over complex terrain like the Amazon lacks detailed diagnostic analysis
- Confidence interval reliability claims based on internal validation without external verification
