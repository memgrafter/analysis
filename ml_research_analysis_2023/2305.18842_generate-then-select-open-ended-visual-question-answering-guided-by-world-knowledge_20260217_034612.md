# Generate then Select: Open-ended Visual Question Answering Guided by World Knowledge

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2305.18842
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.18842
- Reference count: 16
- Key outcome: Achieves state-of-the-art 58.5% accuracy on OK-VQA, improving previous best by 4.1 percentage points

## Executive Summary
This paper introduces RASO, a generate-then-select approach for open-ended visual question answering that leverages world knowledge. Unlike prior methods that either directly generate answers or rely on knowledge retrieval, RASO first uses frozen language models to generate multiple answer choices, then trains a lightweight model to select the correct answer. This approach addresses the limitations of low knowledge coverage in retrieval-based methods and the narrow focus of direct generation approaches. The authors demonstrate significant improvements on the OK-VQA benchmark, achieving 58.5% accuracy and expanding knowledge coverage by over 20% compared to previous methods.

## Method Summary
RASO converts images to textual context using captioning and object tagging, then employs few-shot prompting with multiple PLMs to generate diverse answer choices. Chain-of-thought rationales are generated to guide the selection process, and lightweight answer selection models (KAT, UnifiedQA, ClipCap, IterPLM) are trained to choose the correct answer from the generated candidates. The method emphasizes knowledge coverage expansion through ensembling multiple PLMs and uses a two-stage pipeline that separates answer generation from selection, allowing for specialized training of each component.

## Key Results
- Achieves state-of-the-art 58.5% accuracy on OK-VQA, improving previous best by 4.1 percentage points
- Expands knowledge coverage by over 20% compared to prior approaches
- Multiple PLM ensembling consistently outperforms single PLM approaches
- Chain-of-thought rationales consistently boost answer selection performance across different selection models

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: Using multiple answer choices from PLM generates higher knowledge coverage than direct answer generation
- Mechanism: The generate-then-select approach creates a ranked list of candidate answers, increasing the probability that the correct answer appears in the top N choices
- Core assumption: The PLM has sufficient world knowledge to generate the correct answer, even if it doesn't have high confidence in its top prediction
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract]: "Rather than following the de facto standard to train a multi-modal model that directly generates the VQA answer, RASO first adopts PLM to generate all the possible answers, and then trains a lightweight answer selection model for the correct answer."
  - [section 3.1]: "The outputs are combined together to form the final answer choices ˆAi = { ˆai0, ˆai1, ˆai2, ...} for the current image-question pair. Our goal is to have ai ∈ ˆAi."
  - [corpus]: Weak evidence - only 8 corpus papers found, none specifically addressing this mechanism
- Break condition: If the PLM lacks the necessary world knowledge to generate the correct answer, no amount of selection can recover

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: Chain-of-thought rationales improve answer selection by providing reasoning context
- Mechanism: The CoT generation creates an intermediate reasoning step that helps the answer selector understand why certain answers are more appropriate than others
- Core assumption: The CoT generation is sufficiently aligned with human reasoning patterns to provide useful guidance
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract]: "By plugging in the previous SOTA method KAT (Gui et al., 2022) as the answer selector, we achieve the new SOTA performance 58.5% (+4.1%) on the OK-VQA dataset"
  - [section 4.4]: "We first generate chain-of-thought (CoT) (Wei et al., 2022) style rationales to help guide the selection process"
  - [section 4.6]: "input with CoT rationales consistently boosts the answer selection performance of KAT, UnifiedQA, and ClipCap"
- Break condition: If the CoT generation is misaligned with the reasoning needed for the task, it may introduce noise rather than helpful context

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: Ensembling across multiple PLMs provides more diverse knowledge coverage than single PLM approaches
- Mechanism: Different PLMs have different knowledge bases and biases, so combining their outputs captures a broader range of possible answers
- Core assumption: The PLMs have complementary rather than redundant knowledge
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract]: "we propose RASO, a new VQA pipeline that expands world knowledge retrieval by requesting PLMs to generate multiple answer choices"
  - [section 3.1]: "The outputs are combined together to form the final answer choices"
  - [section 4.6]: "We can see that using all choices is consistently better than using Top 1 choice"
- Break condition: If PLMs share the same biases or knowledge gaps, ensembling provides little benefit

## Foundational Learning

- Concept: Chain-of-thought prompting
  - Why needed here: Provides intermediate reasoning steps that help models understand the relationship between visual context, question, and answer choices
  - Quick check question: Can you explain how CoT differs from direct answer generation in terms of the model's internal reasoning process?

- Concept: Few-shot in-context learning
  - Why needed here: Enables the PLM to understand the task format and generate relevant answer choices without fine-tuning
  - Quick check question: What are the key differences between few-shot prompting and zero-shot prompting when using PLMs for generation tasks?

- Concept: Knowledge coverage evaluation
  - Why needed here: Critical for understanding whether the approach is actually retrieving more diverse knowledge versus just generating more answers
  - Quick check question: How would you measure knowledge coverage in a VQA task, and what baseline would you compare against?

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map: Image → Caption + Tags → PLM Prompting → Answer Choices → CoT Generation → Answer Selection → Final Answer
- Critical path: Image preprocessing → Caption generation + Object tagging → Answer choice generation → Answer selection → Evaluation
- Design tradeoffs: PLM choice vs computation cost, number of answer choices vs selection model complexity, CoT generation quality vs selection performance
- Failure signatures: Low knowledge coverage despite multiple PLMs, CoT generation that doesn't align with answer selection, selection model that can't distinguish between similar answer choices
- First 3 experiments:
  1. Compare knowledge coverage of single PLM with Top-1 generation vs Top-N generation
  2. Evaluate impact of CoT rationales on selection performance across different PLMs
  3. Test ensemble performance with different combinations of PLMs to find optimal coverage-diversity tradeoff

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- Question: How does the quality of generated CoT rationales impact the performance of the answer selection model?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper mentions that CoT rationales consistently boost the performance of KAT, UnifiedQA, and ClipCap in the answer selection step, but notes that this conclusion fails for iterative prompting.
- Why unresolved: The paper does not provide a detailed analysis of the quality of generated CoT rationales and their direct impact on answer selection performance.
- What evidence would resolve it: A detailed analysis of the quality of CoT rationales generated by different PLMs and their correlation with answer selection performance would help understand the impact of CoT quality on the overall VQA system.

### Open Question 2
- Question: What are the limitations of using multi-modal models like ClipCap for answer selection in VQA tasks?
- Basis in paper: [inferred] The paper mentions that transformer-based text-only models like UnifiedQA and KAT significantly outperform the multi-modal ClipCap model by around 20% on average, suggesting limitations in the use of multi-modal models for answer selection.
- Why unresolved: The paper does not provide a detailed explanation of why multi-modal models like ClipCap underperform compared to text-only models in answer selection tasks.
- What evidence would resolve it: A detailed analysis of the strengths and weaknesses of multi-modal models like ClipCap in answer selection tasks, including comparisons with text-only models, would help understand the limitations of multi-modal approaches in VQA tasks.

### Open Question 3
- Question: How does the size and diversity of the training data impact the performance of the generate-then-select VQA pipeline?
- Basis in paper: [inferred] The paper mentions that the answer selectors used in the experiments are not good enough, and increasing the number of choices leads to lower accuracy scores. This suggests that the quality and diversity of the training data may impact the performance of the answer selection model.
- Why unresolved: The paper does not provide a detailed analysis of how the size and diversity of the training data impact the performance of the generate-then-select VQA pipeline.
- What evidence would resolve it: A study on the impact of training data size and diversity on the performance of the answer selection model in the generate-then-select VQA pipeline would help understand the importance of data quality and quantity in training effective VQA systems.

## Limitations
- The paper doesn't clearly specify what baseline is used for the "20% knowledge coverage expansion" claim
- Limited statistical significance testing on performance improvements across different selection models
- The methodology doesn't fully explain how CoT generation quality impacts answer selection performance

## Confidence

**Confidence: Low** on CoT rationales consistently improving performance across all models - limited ablation study with no statistical testing
**Confidence: Medium** on knowledge coverage expansion - claim is made but baseline comparison is unclear
**Confidence: Medium** on PLM ensemble benefits - results show improvement but don't account for redundancy or identify optimal combinations

## Next Checks

1. **Knowledge Coverage Validation**: Conduct a controlled experiment comparing knowledge coverage between (a) Top-1 generation from a single PLM, (b) Top-N generation from the same PLM, and (c) Top-1 generation from an ensemble of PLMs. This would isolate whether coverage improvements come from the number of choices or the diversity of PLMs.

2. **CoT Ablation with Statistical Testing**: Perform a more comprehensive ablation study of CoT rationales across all selection models with statistical significance testing (e.g., paired t-tests) to validate the claim that CoT consistently improves performance. Include analysis of when CoT helps versus when it may introduce noise.

3. **Knowledge Gap Analysis**: Analyze the specific types of questions where RASO succeeds versus fails by categorizing OK-VQA questions by knowledge domain (e.g., science, history, geography, etc.). This would validate whether the approach truly expands knowledge coverage or just improves performance on certain question types.
