# Hedges in Bidirectional Translations of Publicity-Oriented Documents

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2305.12146
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.12146

## Executive Summary
This corpus-based study examines hedging devices in Chinese-English political text translation across three sub-corpora: Chinese government reports (2000-2004, 2018-2022), their English translations, and UN annual reports with Chinese translations (2018-2022). The research reveals that hedges appear more frequently in English political texts overall, with Chinese-English translations containing significantly more hedging devices than their English-Chinese counterparts. A diachronic analysis shows a marked increase in hedging devices in English translations of Chinese government reports over two decades, suggesting a shift from faithfulness-oriented to target-oriented translation norms aimed at improving international reception of Chinese political discourse.

## Method Summary
The study constructed three parallel sub-corpora to compare hedging device usage across translation directions. The analysis examined Chinese government reports and their English translations from two distinct periods (2000-2004 and 2018-2022) alongside UN annual reports and their Chinese translations from 2018-2022. Hedging devices were identified and quantified using corpus linguistics methodology, with frequency comparisons made between source and target texts across different translation directions and time periods. The diachronic comparison between the two government report periods enabled tracking of changes in hedging patterns over time.

## Key Results
- English political texts contain significantly more hedging devices than Chinese political texts
- Chinese-English translations show higher hedging frequencies than English-Chinese translations
- Diachronic analysis reveals a substantial increase in hedging devices in English translations of Chinese government reports between 2000-2004 and 2018-2022 periods

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The study's findings suggest that translators actively employ hedging strategies as part of a target-oriented translation approach, moving away from traditional faithfulness-oriented norms. This shift reflects changing translation practices that prioritize the target audience's reception and understanding over strict source text fidelity. The increased use of hedging in Chinese-English translations likely serves to soften assertions and make Chinese political discourse more palatable to international audiences by aligning with Western rhetorical conventions that favor indirectness and mitigated statements.

## Limitations
- Limited temporal scope (20 years for government reports, 5 years for UN reports) may not capture longer-term translation practice evolution
- Focus on only two document types from government sources may not generalize to other political or institutional genres
- Attribution of hedging frequency differences to translator agency versus source text changes remains speculative without more granular analysis
- Diachronic increase could be influenced by broader changes in international diplomatic discourse not controlled for in the analysis

## Confidence
- Diachronic increase in hedging devices in Chinese-English translations: High
- Frequency differences between translation directions: Medium
- Attribution to translation norm shift: Medium
- Claims about international reception improvement: Low
