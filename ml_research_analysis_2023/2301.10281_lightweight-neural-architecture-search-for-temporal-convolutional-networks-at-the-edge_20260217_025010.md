# Lightweight Neural Architecture Search for Temporal Convolutional Networks at the Edge

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2301.10281
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2301.10281
- Authors: 
- Reference count: 40
- This work proposes PIT, a novel NAS tool for optimizing TCNs, which are widely used for edge-relevant time-series processing tasks. PIT explores architectural parameters like dilation, receptive field, and number of features in each layer, finding Pareto-optimal architectures with 15.9-152× fewer parameters than the seed network while maintaining the same accuracy. PIT outperforms state-of-the-art NAS tools and reduces latency and energy by up to 5.5× and 3.8× on edge devices compared to hand-tuned models.

## Executive Summary
This paper introduces PIT, a lightweight Neural Architecture Search (NAS) tool specifically designed for optimizing Temporal Convolutional Networks (TCNs) at the edge. The approach focuses on exploring key architectural parameters such as dilation, receptive field, and the number of features in each layer to find Pareto-optimal architectures that maintain accuracy while significantly reducing model complexity. The method demonstrates substantial improvements in efficiency, achieving 15.9-152× fewer parameters than the baseline while maintaining comparable accuracy.

PIT's approach is particularly relevant for edge devices where computational resources and energy consumption are critical constraints. The tool outperforms existing NAS approaches and provides practical benefits in terms of latency (5.5× reduction) and energy consumption (3.8× reduction) when deployed on edge hardware, making it a promising solution for time-series processing tasks in resource-constrained environments.

## Method Summary
PIT employs a specialized NAS framework tailored for TCNs that explores architectural parameters including dilation rates, receptive fields, and feature counts across network layers. The search process is designed to identify Pareto-optimal architectures that balance accuracy with efficiency metrics such as parameter count, latency, and energy consumption. The method leverages a systematic exploration of the architectural search space, evaluating candidate architectures against performance benchmarks to identify optimal configurations that maintain accuracy while minimizing resource requirements.

## Key Results
- PIT finds Pareto-optimal TCN architectures with 15.9-152× fewer parameters than the seed network while maintaining the same accuracy
- Outperforms state-of-the-art NAS tools for TCN optimization
- Achieves 5.5× latency reduction and 3.8× energy reduction on edge devices compared to hand-tuned models

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
PIT's effectiveness stems from its focused search space that targets the most impactful architectural parameters for TCNs. By systematically exploring dilation rates, receptive fields, and feature dimensions, the method can identify configurations that maintain temporal modeling capacity while eliminating redundancy. The Pareto-optimal search ensures that efficiency gains do not come at the cost of accuracy, making the approach particularly suitable for edge deployments where both performance and resource constraints must be balanced.

## Limitations
- Search space limited to specific architectural parameters (dilation, receptive field, features) which may not capture all relevant optimizations
- Performance comparisons made against a single seed network rather than multiple baselines
- Edge device metrics reported but implementation details are limited
- Generalizability to other neural architectures beyond TCNs is unclear
