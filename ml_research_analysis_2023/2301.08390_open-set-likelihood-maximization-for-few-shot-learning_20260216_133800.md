# Open-Set Likelihood Maximization for Few-Shot Learning

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2301.08390
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2301.08390
- Reference count: 40
- Primary result: Introduces OSLO for Few-Shot Open-Set Recognition, achieving up to 82.6% AUROC and 86.4% accuracy

## Executive Summary
This work tackles Few-Shot Open-Set Recognition (FSOSR), where the goal is to classify instances from a few known classes while detecting those from unknown classes. Existing transductive few-shot methods fail in open-set scenarios by matching prediction confidence for outliers and inliers, making outlier detection difficult. The authors propose Open-Set Likelihood Optimization (OSLO), which generalizes the maximum likelihood principle by introducing latent inlierness scores to down-weight potential outliers. OSLO co-optimizes these scores and the parametric model via block-coordinate descent, providing interpretable and modular solutions applicable to any pre-trained model. Extensive experiments show OSLO consistently surpasses both inductive and existing transductive methods on open-set detection (AUROC up to 82.6%) while maintaining strong closed-set accuracy (up to 86.4%).

## Method Summary
OSLO addresses FSOSR by treating outlierness as a latent variable co-optimized with the parametric model. The method uses block-coordinate descent to alternately update three components: latent inlierness scores (ξ) that down-weight potential outliers, soft class assignments (z), and prototype centroids (µ). Unlike existing methods that use heuristics for outlier detection, OSLO provides closed-form solutions within each iteration. The approach is modular and can be applied to any pre-trained feature extractor, with experiments showing consistent improvements across five datasets, multiple architectures, and different pre-training strategies.

## Key Results
- OSLO achieves up to 82.6% AUROC for open-set detection, significantly outperforming existing methods
- Maintains strong closed-set accuracy up to 86.4% while improving outlier detection
- Consistent improvements across five datasets (mini-ImageNet, tiered-ImageNet, CUB, FGVC-Aircraft, Fungi)
- Model-agnostic performance across different architectures and pre-training strategies
- Ablation studies confirm the importance of latent inlierness variable for handling outliers

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: Latent inlierness scores ξi allow OSLO to distinguish outliers from inliers during transductive inference.
- Mechanism: OSLO treats outlierness as a latent variable optimized alongside model parameters. Samples with low likelihood under the current model get low ξi, reducing their influence on prototype updates and class assignment probabilities.
- Core assumption: Outliers can be identified by their low likelihood under the current parametric model and should be down-weighted in the likelihood objective.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract]: "OSLO provides highly interpretable and closed-form solutions within each iteration for both the soft assignments, outlierness variables, and the parametric model."
  - [section 4]: "Our idea is simple but powerful: instead of finding heuristics to assess the outlierness of each unlabelled query sample, we treat this score as a latent variable of the problem."
  - [corpus]: Weak evidence - related works discuss outlier logits but do not explicitly treat outlierness as a latent variable co-optimized with the model.
- Break condition: If the feature extractor embeds outliers and inliers similarly (poor representation quality), ξi cannot distinguish them effectively.

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: Block-coordinate descent alternates closed-form updates for ξ, z, and µ, enabling mutual refinement.
- Mechanism: At each iteration, ξ is updated based on current z and µ, z is updated using ξ as a sample-wise temperature, and µ is updated as a weighted average of features where weights are ξ·z.
- Core assumption: The optimization problem is strictly convex in each variable when others are fixed, enabling efficient alternating optimization.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [section 4]: "We proceed with a block-coordinate ascent, which alternates three iterative steps, each corresponding to a closed-form solution for one of the variables."
  - [section 4, Proposition 1]: Provides explicit closed-form update equations for ξ, z, and µ.
  - [corpus]: No direct evidence - related methods use heuristics rather than closed-form alternating updates.
- Break condition: If the objective is not strictly convex in any block, updates may not converge to a good solution.

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: OSLO maintains competitive closed-set accuracy while improving outlier detection by down-weighting outliers in prototype computation.
- Mechanism: Outliers (low ξi) contribute less to prototype µk updates, preventing the model from being pulled toward outlier distributions while still fitting inliers.
- Core assumption: Reducing outlier influence on prototypes improves both closed-set accuracy (by focusing on true class structure) and outlier detection (by keeping prototypes class-pure).
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract]: "OSLO consistently surpasses both inductive and existing transductive methods on open-set detection (AUROC up to 82.6%) while maintaining strong closed-set accuracy (up to 86.4%)."
  - [section 4]: "ξi acts as a sample-wise temperature in the update of zi, encouraging outliers (ξi ≈ 0) to have a uniform distribution over closed-set classes. Additionally, those samples contribute less to the update of closed-set prototypes µ."
  - [corpus]: Weak evidence - related works improve either closed-set accuracy or outlier detection, but not both simultaneously via this mechanism.
- Break condition: If the support set is too small or ambiguous, down-weighting may hurt closed-set accuracy by discarding useful information.

## Foundational Learning

- Concept: Transductive inference vs. inductive inference
  - Why needed here: OSLO is a transductive method that leverages all query samples simultaneously, unlike inductive methods that classify each query independently.
  - Quick check question: What key assumption does transductive inference make about the query set that inductive inference does not?

- Concept: Open-set recognition
  - Why needed here: FSOSR requires detecting samples from unknown classes (outliers) while classifying known classes (inliers), which is the core challenge OSLO addresses.
  - Quick check question: In open-set recognition, what distinguishes an outlier from an inlier in terms of model behavior?

- Concept: Maximum likelihood estimation with latent variables
  - Why needed here: OSLO generalizes maximum likelihood by introducing latent inlierness scores ξi, requiring understanding of how latent variables modify the objective.
  - Quick check question: How does introducing a latent variable ξi change the standard maximum likelihood objective in OSLO?

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map:
  - Feature extractor φθ (frozen, pre-trained)
  - Parametric model p(x,k;µ) = mixture of Gaussians with centroids µ
  - Latent variables: inlierness scores ξi, class assignments zi
  - Optimization loop: block-coordinate descent updating ξ, z, µ
  - Centering: Base centering for inductive, Task centering for transductive

- Critical path:
  1. Extract features φθ(x) for support and query sets
  2. Initialize µ (e.g., from support set centroids)
  3. Alternate updates: ξ → z → µ until convergence
  4. For each query, use z as class probabilities and 1-ξ as outlier score

- Design tradeoffs:
  - Modularity: Can plug OSLO onto any pre-trained feature extractor vs. methods requiring end-to-end training
  - Transduction: Requires query set upfront vs. inductive methods that work sample-by-sample
  - Centering choice: Base centering preserves feature space geometry vs. task centering adapts to current task but may overfit

- Failure signatures:
  - Poor outlier detection: Indicates ξ updates not distinguishing low-likelihood samples
  - Degraded closed-set accuracy: Suggests over-aggressive outlier down-weighting
  - Slow convergence: May indicate poor initialization or non-convexity in updates

- First 3 experiments:
  1. Apply OSLO to mini-ImageNet 1-shot tasks with ResNet-12, compare accuracy and AUROC to SimpleShot + k-NN baseline
  2. Vary query set size NQ to observe performance scaling, confirming Figure 3 trends
  3. Test model-agnostic capability: apply OSLO to features from ViT-B/16 on Fungi dataset, measure improvement over strong baseline

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- Question: How does OSLO's performance degrade when the number of query samples per class is very small (e.g., 1-2 samples)?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper states "fewer query samples tend to decrease OSLO's performance" and shows performance drops in the 1-shot scenario.
- Why unresolved: The paper only shows results down to 1 query per class in experiments. The exact rate and nature of performance degradation at extremely low query counts remains unclear.
- What evidence would resolve it: Systematic experiments varying query count from 1 to 5 samples per class, showing performance curves for both accuracy and outlier detection metrics.

### Open Question 2
- Question: Would incorporating stronger regularization on the parametric model improve OSLO's closed-set accuracy when feature representations are poor?
- Basis in paper: [inferred] The paper notes "OSLO's closed-set accuracy stands more than 6% above the baseline in the in-domain tiered→tiered scenario but reduces to 2% in the most challenging-domain scenario tiered→Aircraft," suggesting feature quality affects performance.
- Why unresolved: The paper uses only basic L2 regularization on centroids. More sophisticated regularization strategies (e.g., weight decay, dropout, contrastive learning objectives) were not explored.
- What evidence would resolve it: Experiments comparing OSLO with various regularization schemes on cross-domain tasks, measuring both closed-set accuracy and outlier detection performance.

### Open Question 3
- Question: How would OSLO perform if open-set query samples were distributed across all remaining classes in the test set rather than just 5 classes?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper states "we also report results in supplementary materials for a more general setting in which open-set query instances are sampled indifferently from all remaining classes in the test set" and shows performance changes.
- Why unresolved: The paper only provides supplementary results for this broader setting without detailed analysis of why performance changes occur or whether the distribution of open-set classes affects OSLO differently than other methods.
- What evidence would resolve it: Controlled experiments varying the number of open-set classes and their distribution, analyzing how this affects both closed-set and open-set performance across different methods.

## Limitations
- The latent variable formulation may fail when outliers are semantically close to inliers or when feature extractors have poor generalization
- Block-coordinate descent relies on strict convexity assumptions that may not hold in practice
- Task centering may overfit to current tasks and hurt generalization
- Requires access to full query set upfront, limiting streaming applications
- Strong open-set detection performance may partly stem from favorable dataset characteristics

## Confidence

- **High confidence**: OSLO's modular architecture and claim to work with any pre-trained model (extensive experiments across five datasets and multiple architectures)
- **Medium confidence**: The mechanism by which latent inlierness scores enable simultaneous improvement in both closed-set accuracy and open-set detection (experiments show this occurs, but theoretical understanding is incomplete)
- **Medium confidence**: The closed-form update equations for block-coordinate descent (Proposition 1 provided, but strict convexity assumption not thoroughly validated)
