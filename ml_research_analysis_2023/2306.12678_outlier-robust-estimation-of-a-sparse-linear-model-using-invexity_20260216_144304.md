# Outlier-robust Estimation of a Sparse Linear Model Using Invexity

## Quick Facts

## Executive Summary
This paper addresses the challenge of estimating a sparse linear regression vector in the presence of outlier samples, a scenario where standard Lasso methods are known to fail. The authors propose a combinatorial outlier-robust Lasso formulation that simultaneously identifies clean samples and estimates the regression vector. To make this tractable, they introduce a novel invex relaxation and provide provable theoretical guarantees for both estimation of the sparse vector and exact support recovery. The method involves solving a non-convex yet invex optimization problem to identify clean samples, followed by using these to estimate the regression vector.

## Method Summary
The core method proposed in this paper is a two-step approach for outlier-robust sparse linear regression. First, a novel invex relaxation is used to solve a combinatorial problem that identifies the clean samples in the presence of outliers. This non-convex optimization problem is designed to be invex, ensuring that local optima are global optima. Once the clean samples are identified, the sparse regression vector is estimated using standard Lasso on the identified clean data. The key theoretical result shows that with only Ω(k^3 log^2 p) clean samples, the method can achieve exact support recovery and good estimation of the true regression vector.

## Key Results
- Introduces a novel invex relaxation for outlier-robust sparse linear regression
- Proves that with only Ω(k^3 log^2 p) clean samples, exact support recovery and good estimation are achievable
- Experimental results show superior performance compared to standard Lasso, Adaptive Lasso, and Robust Lasso methods, especially in high outlier proportions

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The proposed method works by leveraging the invexity property of the optimization problem. Invex functions have the property that every local minimum is a global minimum, which makes the non-convex problem tractable. By formulating the outlier detection problem as an invex optimization, the method can efficiently identify the clean samples even in the presence of outliers. Once the clean samples are identified, the sparse regression vector can be accurately estimated using standard Lasso, as the influence of outliers is removed.

## Architecture Onboarding
Component map: Data -> Outlier detection (invex relaxation) -> Clean sample identification -> Sparse regression (Lasso) -> Estimated regression vector
Critical path: The critical path involves solving the invex relaxation to identify clean samples, followed by applying Lasso on the identified clean data to estimate the sparse regression vector.
Design tradeoffs: The method trades off computational complexity for robustness to outliers and provable recovery guarantees. The invex relaxation allows for efficient outlier detection but may be more computationally intensive than standard convex relaxations.
Failure signatures: The method may fail if the invex relaxation does not correctly identify the clean samples, leading to biased estimates of the sparse regression vector. This can occur if the number of clean samples is insufficient or if the outliers have structured patterns that are not captured by the invex relaxation.

## Limitations
- Theoretical sample complexity Ω(k^3 log^2 p) is derived under specific assumptions about noise and outlier distribution; practical requirements may be higher
- Computational complexity of solving the invex relaxation is not explicitly analyzed, raising concerns about scalability to large-scale problems
- Experiments are limited to synthetic data with i.i.d. Gaussian covariates; performance on real-world datasets with correlated features and structured outliers is not demonstrated

## Confidence
- Theoretical guarantees: High for stated conditions, Medium for practical scenarios
- Computational efficiency: Low
- Practical performance: Medium
