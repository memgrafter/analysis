# Mitigating Spurious Correlations in Multi-modal Models during Fine-tuning

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2304.03916
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2304.03916
- Reference count: 10
- Worst-group accuracy improved by 23% on CLIP with ResNet-50 and 32% with ViT backbone while maintaining average accuracy

## Executive Summary
This paper addresses the challenge of spurious correlations in multi-modal models during fine-tuning, focusing on the CLIP architecture. The authors propose a novel approach that leverages the different modalities in CLIP to detect and explicitly separate spurious attributes from the affected class. The method introduces a multi-modal contrastive loss function that expresses spurious relationships through language, enabling the model to improve accuracy when spurious attributes are absent and direct attention to the actual class when they are present. Experimental results on the Waterbirds dataset demonstrate significant improvements in worst-group accuracy while maintaining overall performance.

## Method Summary
The proposed method introduces a multi-modal contrastive loss function that operates on both visual and textual representations. During fine-tuning, the model learns to distinguish between genuine class attributes and spurious correlations by expressing these relationships through language. The approach works by encouraging the model to maintain class-relevant features while suppressing spurious attribute activations, effectively separating the two during the fine-tuning process.

## Key Results
- Achieved 23% higher worst-group accuracy than ERM on CLIP with ResNet-50 backbone
- Achieved 32% higher worst-group accuracy than ERM on CLIP with ViT backbone
- Maintained the same average accuracy as ERM while improving worst-group performance
- Demonstrated improved model explanation maps with higher AIoU scores

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The method works by exploiting the inherent structure of multi-modal models like CLIP, which have separate pathways for processing different modalities. By introducing a contrastive loss that explicitly considers language expressions of spurious relationships, the model can learn to disentangle class-relevant features from spurious correlations. This approach leverages the complementary information available in different modalities to create a more robust representation that is less sensitive to spurious patterns.

## Foundational Learning
- **Contrastive Learning**: A training approach that learns representations by comparing similar and dissimilar pairs
  - Why needed: Enables the model to learn discriminative features between genuine class attributes and spurious correlations
  - Quick check: Verify that the contrastive loss effectively separates class and spurious representations

- **Multi-modal Representation Learning**: Techniques for learning joint representations across different data modalities
  - Why needed: CLIP's strength comes from its ability to align visual and textual representations
  - Quick check: Confirm that the method maintains alignment between modalities while separating spurious correlations

- **Group Robustness**: The ability of models to perform well on underrepresented or minority groups in the data
  - Why needed: Addresses the core problem of spurious correlations where certain groups are systematically misclassified
  - Quick check: Measure worst-group accuracy to validate improvements

- **Spurious Correlation Detection**: Identifying and mitigating unintended correlations between class labels and background attributes
  - Why needed: The fundamental problem being addressed by the proposed method
  - Quick check: Validate that the model correctly identifies and handles spurious attributes

## Architecture Onboarding

**Component Map**: Visual Encoder -> Multi-modal Contrastive Loss -> Language Expression Module -> Fine-tuned Visual Encoder

**Critical Path**: Input image → Visual features → Contrastive loss with language representation → Updated visual encoder parameters

**Design Tradeoffs**: The method balances between maintaining overall accuracy and improving worst-group performance, requiring careful tuning of the contrastive loss weight

**Failure Signatures**: 
- If the language expressions are poorly defined, the model may fail to identify spurious correlations
- Overly aggressive contrastive loss may harm overall performance
- The approach may not generalize to spurious correlations that cannot be easily expressed in language

## Limitations
- Experimental validation limited to a single dataset (Waterbirds)
- Effectiveness of language-based spurious attribute representation may vary
- Computational overhead during fine-tuning not fully characterized
- Scalability to larger models and more complex spurious patterns unclear

## Confidence
- **High confidence**: Core mechanism of using multi-modal contrastive loss is technically sound
- **Medium confidence**: Claims about aligning model explanation maps supported by AIoU scores
- **Low confidence**: Scalability and robustness across diverse datasets not yet established
