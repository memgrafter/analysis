# GAR: Generalized Autoregression for Multi-Fidelity Fusion

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2301.05729
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2301.05729
- Reference count: 40
- Primary result: GAR and CIGAR achieve up to 6x improvement in RMSE over state-of-the-art multi-fidelity fusion methods with only a few high-fidelity samples.

## Executive Summary
This paper proposes GAR (Generalized Autoregression), a novel approach for multi-fidelity fusion of high-dimensional outputs using tensor algebra and latent features. GAR generalizes classic autoregression to handle arbitrary dimensional outputs and complex multi-fidelity data structures while maintaining fully tractable likelihood and posterior. The key innovation is organizing outputs as tensors and using a Tucker decomposition to capture inter-output correlations through latent features. The paper also introduces CIGAR, a simplified version that achieves exact predictive mean accuracy with cubic computational reduction, and proves the autokrigeability theorem that enables this simplification. Empirical results on canonical PDEs and real scientific examples demonstrate consistent improvements over existing methods.

## Method Summary
GAR generalizes autoregression for multi-fidelity fusion by representing outputs as tensors and using latent coordinate features to capture inter-output correlations. The method employs tensor-variate Gaussian processes with a Tucker decomposition that maps low-fidelity tensor functions to high-fidelity ones. For non-subset data structures, GAR uses an imaginary subset approach that treats missing low-fidelity data as latent variables with tractable Gaussian posteriors. CIGAR is a simplified version that exploits the autokrigeability theorem to achieve exact predictive mean accuracy while reducing computational complexity by a factor of d^3. Both models admit closed-form inference without requiring approximate methods.

## Key Results
- GAR and CIGAR consistently outperform state-of-the-art methods with up to 6x improvement in RMSE on multi-fidelity problems
- CIGAR achieves exact predictive mean accuracy while reducing computation by factor of d^3 compared to full GAR
- The methods scale well to high-dimensional outputs (tested up to 5000 dimensions) and handle non-subset multi-fidelity data structures
- Performance is robust across diverse applications including canonical PDEs, topology optimization, SOFC simulations, and plasmonic nanoparticle arrays

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: Tensor formulation with latent features generalizes AR to arbitrary dimensional outputs.
- Mechanism: Outputs are reshaped into multi-array tensors and indexed via latent coordinate features. A Tucker decomposition maps low-fidelity tensor functions to high-fidelity ones, capturing inter-output correlations via latent features while preserving closed-form tractability.
- Core assumption: The tensor-variate GP framework admits tractable likelihood and posterior via Kronecker algebra, even for high-dimensional outputs.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "generalize the classic autoregression (AR)... using tensor formulation and latent features"
  - [section 3.1] "We organize the low-fidelity output as a M-mode tensor... a tensor-variate GP (TGP) prior"
  - [corpus] Weak: No direct neighbor papers discuss tensor algebra in AR generalization; likely novel.
- Break condition: If latent features cannot adequately capture output correlations or tensor dimension is too large for Kronecker algebra.

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: The autokrigeability theorem enables CIGAR with exact predictive mean accuracy and cubic complexity reduction.
- Mechanism: Under subset data structure, the predictive mean of GAR depends only on low-fidelity GP mean and residual GP mean, not on output covariance matrices. This allows removing output correlations (setting them to identity) without losing mean prediction accuracy.
- Core assumption: Subset data structure ensures independence of predictive mean from high-dimensional output covariance matrices.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "develop CIGAR, a simplified GAR with the exact predictive mean accuracy with computation reduction by a factor of d3"
  - [section 3.4] "Notice that the mean prediction... does not depend on any output covariance matrixes"
  - [corpus] Weak: No neighbor papers discuss autokrigeability; appears to be novel derivation.
- Break condition: If data are non-subset or if output correlations are critical for accurate mean predictions.

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: GAR handles non-subset multi-fidelity data via imaginary subset and tractable likelihood decomposition.
- Mechanism: Missing low-fidelity data points are treated as latent variables with Gaussian posterior given observed low-fidelity data. The joint likelihood decomposes into tractable low-fidelity likelihood plus a Gaussian integral over imaginary subset.
- Core assumption: The imaginary subset approach preserves the closed-form tractability of GAR likelihood and posterior.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [section 3.3] "we propose a fast inference method based on imaginary subset"
  - [section 3.3] "The integral can be calculated using Gaussian quadrature or other sampling methods"
  - [corpus] Weak: No neighbor papers discuss imaginary subset for multi-fidelity fusion; likely novel approach.
- Break condition: If Gaussian quadrature or sampling methods are too expensive or inaccurate for the problem.

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map: Data → Tensorization → Model fitting → Prediction → Uncertainty quantification
- Critical path: Data → Tensorization → Model fitting → Prediction → Uncertainty quantification
- Design tradeoffs:
  - Full GAR vs CIGAR: CIGAR is faster (O(N^3) vs O(∑(d_m^3 + N^3))) but ignores output correlations
  - Subset vs non-subset: Subset allows parallel computation, non-subset requires imaginary subset trick
  - Tensor vs vectorization: Tensor preserves structure but may be harder to interpret
- Failure signatures:
  - Poor performance on non-subset data with CIGAR (ignores important output correlations)
  - Numerical instability in Kronecker algebra for very large dimensions
  - Overfitting with too many latent features relative to data size
- First 3 experiments:
  1. Verify tensor algebra complexity reduction on synthetic high-dimensional data
  2. Test GAR vs CIGAR on a simple multi-fidelity PDE with known solution
  3. Evaluate performance degradation when removing subset assumption (non-subset case)

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- The paper does not explicitly call out open questions, but the methodology raises several natural research directions regarding scalability to extremely high dimensions, handling non-linear fidelity relationships, and optimal tensorization strategies.

## Limitations
- The tensor-based approach may face scalability challenges for extremely high-dimensional outputs beyond tested cases, particularly when d_m values become large.
- The autokrigeability assumption (subset data structure) is restrictive; while the paper addresses non-subset cases via imaginary subset, this adds computational overhead and approximation error.
- Performance heavily depends on appropriate latent feature dimensionality selection, which is not discussed in detail for practical hyperparameter tuning.

## Confidence
- **High Confidence:** Tensor algebra framework enabling tractable GP inference for high-dimensional outputs (well-established mathematical foundation).
- **Medium Confidence:** The autokrigeability theorem and CIGAR derivation (novel theoretical contribution but relies on specific data structure assumptions).
- **Medium Confidence:** Empirical performance improvements over baselines (robust across diverse test cases but limited by available public multi-fidelity datasets).
