# IGB: Addressing The Gaps In Labeling, Features, Heterogeneity, and Size of Public Graph Datasets for Deep Learning Research

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID**: 2302.13522
- **Source URL**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2302.13522
- **Reference count**: 40
- **Key outcome**: IGB provides 162× more labeled nodes than existing datasets, enabling significant GNN accuracy improvements through semantic embeddings

## Executive Summary
Illinois Graph Benchmark (IGB) addresses critical limitations in existing graph datasets for deep learning research by providing a large-scale, flexible benchmark with significantly higher labeling rates and richer features. The benchmark includes both homogeneous and heterogeneous academic graphs with over 40% labeled nodes, compared to less than 1% in traditional datasets. Through extensive ablation studies, IGB demonstrates that using RoBERTa NLP embeddings with higher dimensions significantly improves GNN accuracy, achieving up to 12.96% boost in performance. The benchmark is open-sourced and supports both DGL and PyG frameworks, making it accessible for comprehensive GNN research.

## Method Summary
IGB provides multiple dataset variants (tiny, small, medium, large) of academic graphs with varying node counts and edge densities. The benchmark uses pre-trained RoBERTa embeddings generated from paper titles and abstracts, with options for dimensionality reduction via PCA. Three GNN models (GCN, GraphSAGE, GAT) are evaluated on homogeneous graphs, while RGCN, RSAGE, and RGAT are used for heterogeneous graphs. Training follows standard supervised learning procedures with 60/20/20 train/validation/test splits, learning rate of 0.01, batch size of 10K, and 3 epochs (20 for smaller datasets). The framework supports both in-memory training and mmap-based storage for datasets exceeding GPU memory.

## Key Results
- IGB provides 162× more labeled nodes than existing datasets, with over 40% of nodes labeled
- RoBERTa embeddings with 1024 dimensions achieve up to 12.96% accuracy improvement over other language models
- PCA reduction from 1024D to 384D saves 2.67× memory with only 3.55% accuracy loss
- mmap approach enables training on datasets exceeding host memory but achieves only 25% of storage bandwidth

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: IGB's high labeling rate (>40%) directly improves GNN model accuracy compared to datasets with <1% labeled nodes.
- Mechanism: More labeled nodes provide richer supervisory signals during training, reducing the model's reliance on heuristics and allowing it to better generalize to unseen data.
- Core assumption: Supervised GNN training benefits from increased labeled data density in the same graph structure.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract]: "Compared to the largest graph datasets publicly available, the IGB provides over 162× more labeled data"
  - [section]: "Increasing the classification task complexity from 19-class to 2983-class shows a similar trend in the performance. This is expected behavior as these models are trained as supervised learning tasks and more labeled data should help in boosting the model's performance."
  - [corpus]: Weak - no direct citations, but the FMR score (0.60) indicates moderate relevance of the related paper on labeled data.
- Break condition: If labeling quality degrades (e.g., noisy or incorrect labels), the accuracy gains plateau or reverse.

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: Using RoBERTa embeddings initialized via Sentence-BERT yields higher GNN accuracy than random or other language model embeddings.
- Mechanism: RoBERTa's pre-trained language understanding captures richer semantic features from text (titles/abstracts), which GNNs can leverage alongside structural signals to improve node classification.
- Core assumption: The downstream GNN task benefits from embeddings that encode both semantic and syntactic nuances rather than purely structural or random vectors.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [section]: "we find that using the Roberta NLP embeddings provides better accuracy on GNN models and that a larger embedding dimension further assists in the GNN model accuracy."
  - [section]: "From Figure 5, both mpnet [56] and RoBERTa [38] models outperform the other language models while training GNN."
  - [corpus]: Weak - no explicit citations, but the FMR score (0.60) suggests some overlap in graph/representation learning topics.
- Break condition: If the text data is too sparse or domain-specific, RoBERTa's general embeddings may underperform specialized embeddings.

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: Memory-mapped embeddings (mmap) allow scaling GNN training to datasets exceeding host memory, albeit with performance penalties.
- Mechanism: mmap provides a virtual address space for embeddings stored on NVMe SSDs, enabling the framework to access large graphs without fitting them entirely in RAM.
- Core assumption: Modern SSDs and OS page caching can sustain sufficient I/O throughput to support iterative GNN training workloads.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [section]: "If the graph and embeddings do not fit in the host memory of a single system, as is the case of the IGB dataset, then embeddings can be memory-mapped (mmap) and be stored in fast storage medium like NVMe SSDs."
  - [section]: "Profiling reveals that the mmap approach can only achieve up to 25% of storage bandwidth (1GBps) and is mainly limited by the system’s page fault handler and page-cache throughput."
  - [corpus]: Weak - no citations, but the FMR score (0.60) suggests partial relevance.
- Break condition: If the I/O bandwidth drops below the iteration rate required by the GNN training loop, GPU utilization collapses.

  - Why needed here: IGB maintains consistent homophily across different dataset sizes to ensure fair ablation studies.
  - Quick check question: Why is homophily important for GNN generalization? (It allows the model to learn smoother representations)

  - Why needed here: IGB demonstrates that PCA can reduce RoBERTa embeddings from 1024D to 384D with minimal accuracy loss, saving memory.
  - Quick check question: What trade-off does PCA introduce when reducing dimensions for GNN embeddings? (Memory savings vs. potential accuracy loss)

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map: Dataset -> Graph structure + Node embeddings + Labels; Framework (DGL/PyG) -> Graph storage + Mini-batch sampling + Message passing; Training loop -> Sampling -> Aggregation -> Computation; System -> GPU memory + Host memory + Storage (NVMe SSDs)
- Critical path: Graph + embeddings -> Mini-batch sampling -> Message passing -> Loss computation -> Backpropagation -> Parameter update
- Design tradeoffs: Large embeddings improve accuracy but strain memory; mmap enables larger datasets but hurts performance; multiple language models offer flexibility but increase complexity
- Failure signatures: Low GPU utilization -> sampling or aggregation bottleneck; Out-of-memory errors -> embeddings or graph too large for GPU; Degraded accuracy -> insufficient labeled data or poor embeddings
- First 3 experiments:
  1. Train a small GNN (e.g., GCN) on IGB-tiny with 1024D RoBERTa embeddings; verify GPU utilization >50%
  2. Repeat with PCA-reduced 384D embeddings; compare accuracy and memory usage
  3. Train on IGB-small with mmap enabled; profile time distribution across sampling/aggregation/compute stages

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- Question: How does the performance of GNN models change when using domain-specific embeddings (e.g., SPECTER for scientific text) compared to general-purpose embeddings like RoBERTa?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper mentions that SPECTER embeddings provide a 1.5% F1 score improvement over Sentence-BERT for the 19-class task but chose RoBERTa as the default to benchmark general performance.
- Why unresolved: The paper only briefly mentions this comparison and doesn't provide systematic evaluation of different embedding types across various tasks and datasets.
- What evidence would resolve it: Comprehensive ablation studies comparing different domain-specific and general-purpose embeddings across multiple IGB datasets and classification tasks.

### Open Question 2
- Question: What is the optimal embedding dimensionality for different GNN architectures and dataset sizes?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper shows that reducing dimensions from 1024 to 384 results in 2.67× memory savings with 3.55% accuracy loss for RoBERTa embeddings, but doesn't explore the full space of embedding dimensions across different models and tasks.
- Why unresolved: The study only examines a limited range of dimensions (384, 512, 768, 1024) and doesn't systematically analyze the trade-offs for different GNN architectures or dataset characteristics.
- What evidence would resolve it: Detailed performance analysis across a broader range of embedding dimensions, GNN models, and IGB dataset variants to identify optimal dimensionality trade-offs.

### Open Question 3
- Question: How do GNN models perform on non-English datasets, and what is the impact of using multilingual versus monolingual language models for embedding generation?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper conducted a limited study on Japanese, Spanish, and French datasets using multilingual models, finding no significant performance difference between multilingual and English-only embeddings.
- Why unresolved: The study only examined three languages and a limited set of models, and didn't explore the potential benefits of using language-specific embeddings or the impact on different GNN architectures.
- What evidence would resolve it: Extensive evaluation of GNN performance across multiple languages and language model types, including language-specific embeddings and their impact on various classification tasks and GNN architectures.

## Limitations
- Performance gains are primarily demonstrated on academic graph structures and may not generalize to other domains
- mmap-based scaling is limited by I/O bottlenecks, achieving only 25% of storage bandwidth
- The benchmark focuses on node classification tasks, limiting applicability to other GNN use cases

## Confidence
- Mechanism 1 (labeled data density): High confidence - well-established in supervised learning literature
- Mechanism 2 (RoBERTa embeddings): Medium confidence - ablation studies show consistent improvements, but domain transferability untested
- Mechanism 3 (mmap scaling): Low confidence - demonstrated on NVMe SSDs but limited by I/O bottlenecks in current implementation
