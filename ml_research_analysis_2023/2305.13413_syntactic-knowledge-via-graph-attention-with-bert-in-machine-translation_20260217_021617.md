# Syntactic Knowledge via Graph Attention with BERT in Machine Translation

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2305.13413
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.13413
- Authors: 
- Reference count: 15
- Primary result: SGB model improves translation quality across Zh→En, Ru→En, De→En without sacrificing BLEU scores

## Executive Summary
This paper proposes incorporating syntactic knowledge into machine translation through Graph Attention Networks (GAT) combined with BERT. The authors introduce Syntax-Guided BERT (SGB) models that explicitly represent dependency relations as graph structures and learn weighted representations of word relationships. The approach improves translation fluency for short and medium-length sentences while maintaining or improving BLEU scores across three language pairs.

## Method Summary
The method combines pre-trained BERT encoders with Graph Attention Networks that process syntactic dependency graphs. Source sentences are parsed to obtain dependency relations, which form graph structures where nodes represent tokens and edges represent syntactic dependencies. GAT layers learn attention weights over these relationships, and the resulting features are fused with BERT representations through either concatenation (SGBC) or additional decoder attention (SGBD). The model is trained on large-scale parallel corpora and evaluated on the PUD corpus with gold syntactic annotations.

## Key Results
- SGB models achieve better BLEU scores than baseline BERT models across all three language pairs
- Quality Estimation (QE) scores improve significantly, indicating better translation fluency
- RSA analysis shows syntactic knowledge leads to new modeling of syntactic aspects in BERT's lower and middle layers
- GAT learning of specific dependency relations correlates with improved translation quality for sentences containing those relations

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- Question: How does the performance of SGB models compare across different resource levels (low, medium, high) in terms of translation quality and efficiency?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper mentions experiments with different training set sizes (0.1M, 0.5M, 1M) and states that SGB models perform better than the baseline, but does not provide a comprehensive comparison across all resource levels.
- Why unresolved: The paper does not provide a detailed analysis of how the SGB models perform across a wide range of resource levels, making it difficult to assess their scalability and efficiency.
- What evidence would resolve it: Conducting experiments with a broader range of resource levels (e.g., 0.01M, 0.1M, 0.5M, 1M, 5M) and comparing the performance of SGB models against the baseline in terms of translation quality and efficiency metrics (e.g., BLEU score, QE score, training time, inference time) would provide a more comprehensive understanding of their performance across different resource levels.

### Open Question 2
- Question: What is the impact of using different graph attention network (GAT) architectures on the performance of SGB models?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper mentions that the GAT in SGB engines has 2 layers and 6 attention heads for Zh, 4 attention heads for Ru and De, but does not explore the impact of different GAT architectures on model performance.
- Why unresolved: The paper does not investigate how different GAT architectures (e.g., varying number of layers, attention heads, or attention mechanisms) affect the performance of SGB models, making it difficult to determine the optimal GAT configuration.
- What evidence would resolve it: Conducting experiments with various GAT architectures and comparing their performance against the baseline and the current SGB models in terms of translation quality metrics (e.g., BLEU score, QE score) and efficiency metrics (e.g., training time, inference time) would provide insights into the impact of different GAT configurations on model performance.

### Open Question 3
- Question: How does the SGB model handle out-of-domain sentences, and what is its generalization ability?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper mentions that the PUD corpus contains out-of-domain sentences and that SGBD can achieve better translation results for out-of-domain sentences without sacrificing BLEU performance, but does not provide a detailed analysis of the model's generalization ability.
- Why unresolved: The paper does not provide a comprehensive evaluation of the SGB model's performance on out-of-domain sentences or its ability to generalize to new domains, making it difficult to assess its practical applicability.
- What evidence would resolve it: Conducting experiments with a diverse set of out-of-domain sentences and evaluating the SGB model's performance in terms of translation quality metrics (e.g., BLEU score, QE score) and domain adaptation metrics (e.g., domain accuracy, domain perplexity) would provide insights into its generalization ability and practical applicability.

## Limitations
- The dependency parser accuracy is not explicitly evaluated, which is critical since GAT performance depends entirely on the quality of gold syntactic annotations
- The study uses a single parser (HanLP) without examining parser robustness across different domains or languages
- The RSA analysis methodology lacks detailed explanation of how representational similarity changes specifically indicate syntactic knowledge integration

## Confidence

- **High Confidence**: The experimental results showing BLEU and QE score improvements across three language pairs are reproducible given the detailed training setup. The baseline vs. SGB comparison methodology is sound.
- **Medium Confidence**: The claim about GAT learning specific dependency relations correlating with translation quality improvements requires more rigorous statistical validation. The correlation analysis could be strengthened with significance testing.
- **Low Confidence**: The interpretation of RSA similarity drops as evidence of syntactic knowledge refactoring in BERT layers needs additional validation, as similar similarity changes could arise from other factors during fine-tuning.

## Next Checks

1. **Parser Robustness Analysis**: Evaluate how parser error rates affect GAT performance by comparing results using gold vs. automatic syntactic parses across different domains and sentence complexities.
2. **Statistical Correlation Validation**: Perform rigorous statistical testing (e.g., permutation tests) to verify that the correlation between GAT dependency prediction accuracy and translation quality improvements is significant and not due to chance.
3. **Controlled RSA Experiment**: Compare RSA similarity changes when incorporating different types of auxiliary knowledge (syntax vs. semantic vs. random graphs) to isolate whether the observed BERT layer changes specifically reflect syntactic knowledge integration.
