# Uncertainty-Aware Reward-based Deep Reinforcement Learning for Intent Analysis of Social Media Information

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2302.10195
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2302.10195
- Reference count: 4
- Primary result: 95% multi-class classification accuracy while reducing input sequence length through selective word removal guided by uncertainty-aware rewards

## Executive Summary
This work introduces a novel approach for intent classification of fake and true news spreaders using deep reinforcement learning (DRL) with uncertainty-aware rewards. The method optimizes text structure by selectively removing noisy words from input sequences, guided by a policy gradient DRL model. A key innovation is the integration of multidimensional uncertainty estimates—vacuity and dissonance—derived from Subjective Logic to formulate an immediate reward that enhances decision-making. Experimental results on 600K training episodes demonstrate that the proposed framework achieves 95% multi-class classification accuracy while significantly reducing the number of words in the input sequence.

## Method Summary
The method employs an LSTM-based intent classifier and a DRL agent with an actor network that makes word-level keep/mask decisions. The LSTM processes sequences of word embeddings, with its hidden states serving as states for the DRL agent. The actor uses policy gradients (REINFORCE) to update word selection decisions, receiving delayed rewards based on classification accuracy and word count reduction, plus immediate rewards from uncertainty estimates (vacuity and dissonance) derived from Subjective Logic. The LSTM classifier is frozen during DRL training to stabilize learning.

## Key Results
- Achieves 95% multi-class classification accuracy on annotated dataset
- DRL-CV and DRL-CD uncertainty-aware models outperform standard DRL in gold intent prediction
- Significant reduction in input sequence length while maintaining high accuracy, especially for fake news classification

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map: Word embeddings → LSTM cells → Actor network (keep/mask decisions) → Mask if needed → LSTM intent classifier → Reward (delayed + immediate) → Policy Gradient Update

- Critical path: Word → Embedding → LSTM → Actor (decide keep/mask) → Mask if needed → Next word → Final sequence → LSTM Intent Classifier → Reward (delayed + immediate) → Policy Gradient Update

- Design tradeoffs:
  - Masking vs. reweighting: The design chooses hard masking of words rather than soft attention, which is simpler but may lose nuanced information.
  - Immediate vs. delayed reward: Adding immediate uncertainty rewards aims to speed up learning but adds complexity and requires careful tuning of β.
  - Fixed LSTM vs. joint training: The LSTM classifier is fixed during DRL training to stabilize learning, but this may limit end-to-end optimization.

- Failure signatures:
  - If actor policy collapses to all "mask" or all "keep" actions, indicating reward hacking or poor reward shaping.
  - If classification accuracy drops significantly after masking, indicating that relevant words are being removed.
  - If training becomes unstable with high variance in rewards, suggesting poor credit assignment or reward scaling issues.

- First 3 experiments:
  1. Verify that the LSTM intent classifier achieves reasonable accuracy on the annotated dataset before adding DRL.
  2. Run DRL with only delayed reward (λ > 0, β = 0) and check if it improves classification and reduces word count.
  3. Add vacuity-based immediate reward (λ > 0, β small) and compare convergence speed and final performance to the baseline DRL.

## Limitations
- Manual annotation of 1,500 tweets with five intent classes introduces potential subjectivity and scalability concerns
- Fixed LSTM classifier during DRL training may prevent full end-to-end optimization
- Specific implementation details of Subjective Logic vacuity maximization and word embedding preprocessing are not fully specified

## Confidence
- High Confidence: The core claim that DRL can optimize text structure through word selection and improve classification accuracy while reducing input length
- Medium Confidence: The effectiveness of multidimensional uncertainty rewards (vacuity and dissonance) in accelerating DRL convergence and improving decision quality
- Low Confidence: The scalability and generalization of the approach to larger, noisier social media datasets beyond the LIAR-2015 corpus used in experiments
