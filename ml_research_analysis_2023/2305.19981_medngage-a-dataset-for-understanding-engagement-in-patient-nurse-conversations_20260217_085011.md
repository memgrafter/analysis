# MedNgage: A Dataset for Understanding Engagement in Patient-Nurse Conversations
## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2305.19981
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.19981
- Reference count: 11
- Dataset: MedNgage contains 3.1K socio-affective and 1.8K cognitive spans across 68 patient-nurse conversations about cancer symptom management

## Executive Summary
MedNgage introduces a novel dataset and framework for understanding patient engagement in nurse-patient conversations about cancer symptom management. The study develops the SACe framework, which captures both cognitive and socio-affective dimensions of engagement through 3.1K annotated spans. The dataset demonstrates that higher patient engagement correlates with improved symptom control outcomes. Transformer models fine-tuned on this data achieve strong performance in predicting engagement categories, suggesting practical applications for clinical decision support.

## Method Summary
The MedNgage dataset was created by annotating patient-nurse conversations about cancer symptom management with cognitive and socio-affective engagement categories using the SACe framework. This framework draws from discourse theories and was grounded in actual conversation data. The annotation process identified 3.1K socio-affective and 1.8K cognitive spans across 68 patients. Statistical analysis was performed to examine correlations between engagement levels and symptom control outcomes. Transformer models were then fine-tuned on the annotated data to predict engagement categories, with LIME analysis used to identify model limitations and decision-making patterns.

## Key Results
- Transformer models fine-tuned on MedNgage achieve F1 scores above 78% for engagement category prediction
- Higher patient engagement correlates with improved symptom control outcomes in cancer symptom management conversations
- LIME analysis reveals specific model limitations in predicting certain engagement categories

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The MedNgage framework works by capturing the complex interplay between cognitive and socio-affective engagement in healthcare conversations. By grounding the SACe framework in actual patient-nurse dialogue data, the approach captures authentic patterns of engagement that are clinically meaningful. The transformer models leverage contextual understanding of language to identify engagement markers that correlate with symptom management outcomes, suggesting that engagement can be quantified and predicted from conversation patterns.

## Limitations
- Small dataset size (68 patients) may limit generalizability across diverse patient populations and cancer types
- SACe framework has not been independently validated outside the study context, raising questions about universal applicability
- Symptom control outcomes correlation may be influenced by unmeasured confounding variables such as disease severity or treatment phase
