# CeFlow: A Robust and Efficient Counterfactual Explanation Framework for Tabular Data using Normalizing Flows

## Executive Summary
CeFlow addresses the limitations of existing counterfactual explanation methods that use variational autoencoders (VAEs), which are slow and produce unstable results due to randomness. The framework leverages normalizing flows to achieve exact density modeling of mixed-type tabular data, avoiding the lower-bound approximation errors of VAEs. By integrating variational dequantization for categorical features and a conditional normalizing flow with Gaussian mixture models for continuous features, CeFlow generates counterfactual samples that are both efficient and robust. Experimental results on three datasets (Law, Compas, Adult) demonstrate competitive or better performance compared to state-of-the-art methods in terms of success rate, l1-norm, and log-density, while significantly reducing running time by approximately 90%.

## Method Summary
CeFlow is a counterfactual explanation framework that uses normalizing flows to generate robust and efficient counterfactual samples for tabular data with mixed continuous and categorical features. The method addresses the limitations of existing approaches that use variational autoencoders, which are slow and produce unstable results due to randomness. CeFlow integrates variational dequantization for categorical features and a conditional normalizing flow with Gaussian mixture models for continuous features. The framework is trained by maximizing log-likelihood, and counterfactuals are generated by adding a scaled vector to the latent representation. Experiments on three datasets (Law, Compas, Adult) show that CeFlow achieves competitive or better performance than state-of-the-art methods in terms of success rate, l1-norm, and log-density, while significantly reducing the running time (around 90% reduction).

## Key Results
- CeFlow achieves competitive or better performance than state-of-the-art methods in terms of success rate, l1-norm, and log-density.
- The framework significantly reduces running time by approximately 90% compared to VAE-based methods.
- CeFlow produces more stable counterfactual samples with the lowest l1-variance across all tested datasets.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: Normalizing flows enable exact density modeling of mixed-type tabular data, avoiding the lower-bound approximation errors of VAEs.
- Mechanism: The proposed conditional normalizing flow architecture combines variational dequantization for categorical features and Gaussian mixture models for continuous features, allowing exact log-likelihood computation via the change of variables formula.
- Core assumption: The invertible and differentiable transformation preserves the structure of mixed-type data distributions sufficiently to enable meaningful counterfactual generation.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract]: "CeFlow integrates variational dequantization for categorical features and a conditional normalizing flow with Gaussian mixture models for continuous features."
  - [section 4.3]: "We then perform merge operation on continuous representation zcat and continuous feature xcon to obtain values (zcat,xcon) ↦→ xfull. Thereafter, we apply flow Gaussian mixture model [14] which is a probabilistic generative model for training the invertible function fθ."
  - [corpus]: Found 25 related papers, average neighbor FMR=0.552, suggesting moderate but not strong novelty in the normalizing flow approach for counterfactual explanations.
- Break condition: If the Gaussian mixture model assumptions about class-conditional distributions are violated, the conditional normalizing flow would fail to capture the true data distribution.

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: Direct latent space perturbation is faster than iterative optimization because it eliminates repeated sampling and gradient descent steps.
- Mechanism: The framework computes the mean latent vectors for each class (μk) and uses the scaled difference between original and target class means as the perturbation vector, then applies a single inverse transformation to generate counterfactuals.
- Core assumption: The latent space is structured such that class means are well-separated and moving along the line connecting them produces valid counterfactuals.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract]: "CeFlow achieves competitive or better performance than state-of-the-art methods in terms of success rate, l1-norm, and log-density, while significantly reducing the running time (around 90% reduction)."
  - [section 4.4]: "Therefore, inspired by the study [13], we add the scaled vector as the perturbation from the original instance xorg to counterfactual onexcf."
  - [corpus]: The approach is novel enough to warrant comparison with multiple baselines, but no direct evidence about the speed improvement mechanism beyond runtime claims.
- Break condition: If the latent space is not linearly separable by class or if the mean-based perturbation doesn't reach valid counterfactual regions, the approach would fail.

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: The exact log-likelihood computation ensures counterfactual samples are drawn from high-density regions, making them more plausible and actionable.
- Mechanism: By maximizing exact likelihood (not lower bound), the generated counterfactuals naturally follow the data distribution, avoiding unrealistic samples that VAEs might produce.
- Core assumption: High-density regions correspond to plausible data points, and the classifier's decision boundary is aligned with these density regions.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract]: "CeFlow also produces more stable counterfactual samples with the lowest l1-variance."
  - [section 4.2]: "The density of the transformed random variable xfull =f−1θ (zfull) is given by: pX (xfull) = log(pZ(fθ(xfull))) + log(|det(∂fθ/∂xfull)|)"

- Break condition: If the classifier's decision boundary doesn't align with density regions, or if high-density regions contain implausible samples, the approach would produce non-actionable counterfactuals.

- Component map:
  - Data preprocessing → Variational dequantization for categorical features → Continuous feature processing → Merge operation → Conditional normalizing flow → Mean vector computation → Counterfactual generation
  - Each component has clear input/output interfaces, with the normalizing flow as the central invertible transformation

- Critical path: The path from original sample through encoder to latent space, perturbation calculation, and inverse transformation to counterfactual sample is the core execution sequence
  - Understanding this path is essential for debugging and optimization

- Design tradeoffs: Exact likelihood vs. model complexity, speed vs. stability, linear perturbation vs. more sophisticated navigation in latent space
  - The choice of Gaussian mixture models trades flexibility for computational efficiency
  - The mean-based perturbation trades optimality for speed

- Failure signatures: Low success rate indicates the perturbation doesn't reach target class region; high l1-variance indicates instability in the generative process; poor log-density indicates samples fall in low-probability regions
  - Monitoring these metrics helps identify which component is failing

## Limitations
- The framework assumes linear separability in latent space for counterfactual generation, which may not hold for complex decision boundaries.
- The performance gains are demonstrated primarily on tabular datasets with relatively simple structures, and generalization to high-dimensional or highly non-linear data remains uncertain.
- The computational efficiency gains come at the cost of potentially reduced flexibility compared to iterative optimization methods.
