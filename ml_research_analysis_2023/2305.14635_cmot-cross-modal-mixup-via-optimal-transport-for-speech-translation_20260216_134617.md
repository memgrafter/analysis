# CMOT: Cross-modal Mixup via Optimal Transport for Speech Translation

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2305.14635
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.14635
- Reference count: 25
- Key outcome: CMOT achieves 30.0 average BLEU score across 8 translation directions on MuST-C ST benchmark, outperforming previous methods.

## Executive Summary
This paper addresses the challenge of end-to-end speech translation by proposing CMOT (Cross-modal Mixup via Optimal Transport), a method that bridges the modality gap between speech and text using optimal transport-based alignment and token-level mixup. The approach adaptively aligns speech and text sequences through optimal transport, then creates cross-modal hybrid sequences that serve as intermediaries during training. CMOT was evaluated on the MuST-C ST benchmark, demonstrating significant improvements over previous methods with an average BLEU score of 30.0 across 8 translation directions. The method effectively finds cross-modal alignments and reduces the modality gap between speech and text representations.

## Method Summary
CMOT addresses end-to-end speech translation by using optimal transport to find cross-modal alignment between speech and text sequences, then performing token-level mixup based on this alignment. The method follows a pretraining-finetuning paradigm: first pretraining text embedding and joint transformer with machine translation tasks, then finetuning with multi-task learning (speech translation + machine translation) with KL divergence regularization. During training, CMOT uses relaxed OT with window strategy to align speech and text tokens, creates mixed sequences by combining aligned tokens probabilistically, and regularizes predictions between unimodal and mixed sequences to improve cross-modal transfer learning.

## Key Results
- Achieves 30.0 average BLEU score across 8 translation directions on MuST-C ST benchmark
- Outperforms previous methods in end-to-end speech translation
- Effectively finds cross-modal alignments between speech and text sequences
- Demonstrates improved cross-modal transfer learning through token-level mixup

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
Optimal transport (OT) can find meaningful cross-modal alignment between speech and text sequences by solving a transport problem where speech tokens are matched to text tokens based on their representations. This works under the assumption that speech and text sequences are distributions where token importance can be captured by vector norms, and the cost of transport can be measured using Euclidean distance between token embeddings.

### Mechanism 2
Token-level mixup using OT alignment bridges the modality gap between speech and text representations by creating cross-modal sequences that serve as intermediaries between the two modalities. The mixed sequences created through OT-based token alignment help the model learn better cross-modal representations by exposing it to hybrid examples during training.

### Mechanism 3
Regularizing predictions between unimodal and cross-modal sequences improves cross-modal transfer learning by forcing the model to produce consistent outputs across modalities. KL divergence between prediction distributions of speech, text, and mixed sequences ensures the model maintains consistency between predictions on unimodal and mixed inputs.

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map: Speech Encoder -> Translation Encoder -> CMOT (OT + Mixup) -> Translation Decoder -> Output

- Critical path: Speech/Text → Translation Encoder → CMOT (OT + Mixup) → Translation Decoder → Output

- Design tradeoffs:
  - OT computation vs alignment quality: Relaxed OT with window strategy trades some alignment precision for faster computation
  - Mixup probability vs regularization: Higher p* increases text token proportion but may introduce noise
  - KL weight vs task balance: Higher λ increases regularization but may hurt main translation performance

- Failure signatures:
  - Poor BLEU scores despite training: Check if OT alignment is finding meaningful matches (inspect cost matrices)
  - Mode collapse to one modality: Check mixup probability and KL weight balance
  - Slow convergence: Try different OT window sizes or mixup positions

- First 3 experiments:
  1. Compare relaxed OT vs IPOT with window strategy on alignment quality and BLEU scores
  2. Test different mixup probabilities (p* = 0.2, 0.4, 0.6) to find optimal balance
  3. Vary KL loss weight (λ = 1.0, 2.0, 3.0) to optimize regularization effect

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
Does the CMOT method's performance on speech translation improve when applied during the pretraining phase rather than just during fine-tuning? The paper mentions that CMOT is applied during fine-tuning and suggests that applications on the pretraining phase are needed for exploration in the future.

### Open Question 2
Can the CMOT method be effectively adapted for speech translation tasks in languages without transcribed text? The paper acknowledges that CMOT relies on transcripts during training, which may limit its applicability to languages without transcribed text.

### Open Question 3
How does the CMOT method's performance compare when using different speech encoders, such as wav2vec 2.0, compared to HuBERT? The paper discusses the use of HuBERT as a speech encoder and mentions that experiments with wav2vec 2.0 are conducted, showing that CMOT still performs well with this alternative encoder.

## Limitations

- Evaluation setup makes direct performance attribution difficult due to different data augmentation strategies and training configurations used by compared methods
- Minimal ablation study on CMOT's individual components (OT alignment, mixup, and KL regularization)
- No quantitative analysis of OT alignment quality beyond qualitative discussion
- Method's performance on lower-resource language pairs is not discussed in detail

## Confidence

- High confidence: The mechanism of KL regularization between unimodal and mixed sequence predictions is well-established in multi-task learning literature
- Medium confidence: The overall effectiveness of CMOT in bridging the modality gap is supported by reported BLEU improvements, but lacks detailed ablation studies
- Low confidence: The claim that optimal transport can reliably find meaningful cross-modal alignments is primarily supported by qualitative discussion rather than quantitative metrics
