# Mind the Gap: Offline Policy Optimization for Imperfect Rewards

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID**: 2302.01667
- **Source URL**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2302.01667
- **Reference count**: 40
- **Primary result**: Unified framework for offline RL with imperfect rewards that achieves superior performance across diverse reward imperfection types

## Executive Summary
This paper addresses the challenge of offline reinforcement learning when reward functions are imperfect, proposing a unified framework called Reward Gap Minimization (RGM). The method learns to correct imperfect rewards by minimizing the gap between given rewards and expert demonstrations through a bi-level optimization approach. RGM can handle various types of imperfect rewards including partially correct, completely incorrect, and sparse rewards without requiring online interactions or reward engineering.

## Method Summary
RGM formulates offline RL with imperfect rewards as a bi-level optimization problem. The upper layer learns a reward correction term that minimizes the f-divergence between the optimal policy's visitation distribution under corrected rewards and the expert demonstration distribution. The lower layer solves a pessimistic RL problem with the corrected rewards using Lagrangian duality. By exploiting this duality structure, RGM can be solved in a fully offline manner. The framework is evaluated across D4RL, Robomimic, and grid-world navigation tasks, demonstrating superior performance compared to existing methods under various imperfect reward settings.

## Key Results
- RGM achieves significant performance improvements over baselines (BC, DWBC, SMODICE, TD3+BC, CQL, IQL) on D4RL tasks with flipped rewards
- The method effectively corrects wrong or inconsistent rewards while retrieving useful information from biased rewards
- RGM learns reward corrections that generalize beyond expert data coverage, encoding long-horizon information in sparse reward settings

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: RGM learns a correction term that adjusts imperfect rewards toward expert behavior without requiring online interactions
- Mechanism: Uses bi-level optimization where upper layer minimizes f-divergence between optimal policy distribution under corrected rewards and expert distribution, while lower layer solves pessimistic RL with corrected rewards using Lagrangian duality
- Core assumption: Optimal policy's visitation distribution under true reward can be approximated by expert demonstration's visitation distribution
- Evidence anchors: Abstract mentions upper layer optimizes reward correction term through visitation distribution matching; section 4.2 describes reward gap minimization toward expert behaviors
- Break condition: If expert demonstrations don't sufficiently cover state-action space or are biased, approximation of true reward's optimal distribution will be poor

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: RGM handles diverse imperfect reward types (partially correct, sparse, multi-task data sharing, completely incorrect) within unified framework
- Mechanism: Treats all imperfect reward types through reward gap minimization lens, with correction term learning to distinguish expert from non-expert data regardless of imperfection type
- Core assumption: Reward gap concept is sufficiently general to capture discrepancies in all imperfect reward scenarios
- Evidence anchors: Abstract states RGM effectively corrects wrong/inconsistent rewards and retrieves information from biased rewards; section 5.1 shows RGM alleviates performance degradation when perfect rewards are unattainable
- Break condition: If imperfection type requires fundamentally different handling not captured by reward gap minimization

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: Corrected rewards learned by RGM encode long-horizon information and generalize beyond expert data coverage
- Mechanism: Reward correction term learned through visitation distribution matching captures long-term behavioral patterns, providing dense signals even in sparse reward settings by encoding expert's successful navigation patterns
- Core assumption: Visitation distribution matching captures temporal dependencies and long-term consequences of actions
- Evidence anchors: Section 5.2 shows learned rewards recover correct learning signals on expert path and generalize to regions not covered by expert data; section 5.2 notes agent can navigate to destination by maximizing one-step reward
- Break condition: If task requires explicit credit assignment over long time horizons not captured through visitation distribution matching

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map: Dataset -> Discriminator training -> Bi-level optimization (upper/lower) -> Policy extraction
- Critical path: Dataset → Discriminator training → Bi-level optimization (upper/lower) → Policy extraction
- Design tradeoffs:
  - Using expert demonstrations provides information but requires careful handling to avoid overfitting
  - Two-timescale optimization stabilizes training but requires careful learning rate scheduling
  - KL-divergence vs χ²-divergence choice affects numerical stability and gradient behavior
- Failure signatures:
  - If discriminator fails to distinguish expert from non-expert data, reward correction will be ineffective
  - If α is too large, pessimistic RL will dominate and prevent learning
  - If reward correction term is updated too quickly, training becomes unstable
- First 3 experiments:
  1. Test on simple grid world with sparse rewards to verify reward correction mechanism
  2. Evaluate on D4RL tasks with flipped rewards to test partial correction capability
  3. Test on multi-task data sharing to verify unified framework works across different scenarios

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- Question: How does performance of RGM vary with quality and quantity of expert dataset in real-world applications where expert data is scarce or noisy?
- Basis in paper: Explicit mention that RGM relaxes strong dependencies on online reward tuning but still requires small expert dataset which may not be easily accessible
- Why unresolved: Paper provides empirical results showing effectiveness with small expert dataset (1-200 trajectories) but doesn't explore limits of this requirement or performance degradation with very limited/noisy expert data
- What evidence would resolve it: Experiments testing RGM's performance with varying sizes and qualities of expert datasets, particularly in domains with limited expert availability

### Open Question 2
- Question: Can RGM's reward correction mechanism generalize to settings with dynamic or non-stationary reward functions encountered in evolving real-world tasks?
- Basis in paper: Inferred from paper demonstrating RGM's ability to correct static imperfect rewards without addressing scenarios where reward function changes over time
- Why unresolved: Paper focuses on offline policy optimization with fixed reward functions, doesn't explore applicability to dynamic reward settings
- What evidence would resolve it: Experiments evaluating RGM's performance on tasks with time-varying or context-dependent reward functions

### Open Question 3
- Question: How does RGM's performance compare to other methods when applied to high-dimensional state-action spaces with complex reward structures found in real-world robotics or autonomous systems?
- Basis in paper: Explicit mention that paper shows RGM's effectiveness on tasks like D4RL, Robomimic, and grid-world navigation but doesn't specifically address scalability to extremely high-dimensional or complex reward structures
- Why unresolved: While paper demonstrates success on benchmark tasks, doesn't provide evidence of performance in highly complex real-world scenarios with intricate reward functions
- What evidence would resolve it: Comparative experiments on real-world robotic control tasks or autonomous navigation with complex reward structures

## Limitations
- Method requires expert demonstrations which may not always be available in real-world applications
- Two-timescale optimization introduces additional hyperparameters that need careful tuning
- Computational overhead of bi-level optimization may limit scalability to larger problems

## Confidence
- **High confidence**: Mathematical formulation of RGM and ability to handle partially correct rewards (verified through Theorem 1 and empirical results)
- **Medium confidence**: Generalization to completely incorrect rewards and sparse reward settings, as these require more extrapolation beyond expert data
- **Medium confidence**: Claim about long-horizon information encoding, as this relies on visitation distribution matching capturing temporal dependencies

## Next Checks
