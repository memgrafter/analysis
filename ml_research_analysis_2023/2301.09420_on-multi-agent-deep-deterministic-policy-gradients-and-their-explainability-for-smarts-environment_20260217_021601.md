# On Multi-Agent Deep Deterministic Policy Gradients and their Explainability for SMARTS Environment

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2301.09420
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2301.09420
- Reference count: 29
- MADDPG with prioritized experience replay outperforms MAPPO and achieves competitive results against top challenge submissions in SMARTS autonomous driving environment

## Executive Summary
This paper presents a comparative study of Multi-Agent Proximal Policy Optimization (MAPPO) and Multi-Agent Deep Deterministic Policy Gradients (MADDPG) in the SMARTS autonomous driving simulator. The authors implement both algorithms and evaluate their performance on standard benchmark scenarios. MADDPG with prioritized experience replay demonstrates superior performance, particularly in Humanness metrics, achieving 1362.7 compared to MAPPO's 641.86. The study suggests that prioritized experience replay provides implicit explainability by prioritizing safety-critical experiences. The research identifies key limitations including incomplete training convergence and lack of visual input integration, while proposing future directions for incorporating computer vision and evolutionary computing for hyperparameter optimization.

## Method Summary
The paper implements two multi-agent reinforcement learning algorithms for autonomous driving in the SMARTS environment. MAPPO uses on-policy updates with PPO-style clipping, while MADDPG employs off-policy learning with a centralized critic and decentralized actors. MADDPG utilizes prioritized experience replay to sample experiences based on TD error, with priorities adjusted according to safety metrics. Both algorithms are trained for 100 episodes on standard SMARTS benchmark scenarios. The evaluation focuses on average reward and Humanness score, which measures driving naturalness. The authors attribute MADDPG's superior performance to the priority-based replay buffer's ability to focus learning on safety-critical situations.

## Key Results
- MADDPG with prioritized experience replay achieved Humanness score of 1362.7, significantly outperforming MAPPO's 641.86
- MADDPG demonstrated competitive performance against top challenge submissions in the SMARTS benchmark
- The priority-based replay buffer showed implicit explainability by prioritizing experiences based on safety metrics

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The effectiveness of MADDPG with prioritized experience replay stems from its ability to focus learning on critical experiences that have higher impact on safety and performance. The prioritized sampling mechanism ensures that the agent learns more effectively from near-miss or safety-critical situations by assigning higher priority to experiences with larger TD errors or safety violations. This selective learning approach allows the agent to develop more robust decision-making capabilities in complex driving scenarios where safety is paramount. The centralized critic in MADDPG also enables better coordination between agents by providing access to global state information during training.

## Architecture Onboarding

**Component Map**
Input Sensors -> Local Observation Processor -> MADDPG Actor Network -> Action Output
                         ↓
                   Centralized Critic
                         ↓
                Prioritized Experience Replay Buffer

**Critical Path**
1. Sensor data collection from environment
2. Local observation processing per agent
3. MADDPG actor network inference
4. Action execution in environment
5. Experience storage with priority calculation
6. Batch sampling from prioritized buffer
7. Centralized critic update
8. Actor network updates

**Design Tradeoffs**
The centralized critic approach trades increased computational complexity during training for improved coordination and performance. The prioritized experience replay requires additional memory overhead but provides focused learning on safety-critical experiences. The decentralized execution during deployment ensures scalability but may limit coordination compared to fully centralized approaches.

**Failure Signatures**
Performance degradation when safety-critical experiences are under-sampled due to improper priority calculation. Coordination failures may occur if the centralized critic becomes a bottleneck or if the communication overhead is too high. Training instability can arise from improper prioritization that leads to overfitting to specific experience types.

**3 First Experiments**
1. Test individual agent performance in isolation to establish baseline capabilities
2. Evaluate coordination between two agents in simple merge scenarios
3. Compare performance with and without prioritized experience replay on safety-critical scenarios

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
The paper identifies several limitations that represent open questions for future research. The training convergence was not fully evaluated, with performance remaining constant or only marginally improving after 100 episodes. The evaluation metrics were limited to average reward and Humanness score without comprehensive safety analysis across diverse driving scenarios. The absence of visual feedback integration represents a significant gap, as current inputs are restricted to local sensory readings, position, and velocity without incorporating camera or lidar data. The authors also note that the computational resources used may not reflect real-world deployment constraints.

## Limitations
- Training convergence was not fully evaluated, with performance plateauing after 100 episodes
- Evaluation metrics were limited to average reward and Humanness score without comprehensive safety analysis
- Lack of visual feedback integration, restricting inputs to local sensory readings and positional data
