# Effective Theory of Transformers at Initialization

- **Key outcome:** This work performs an effective-theory analysis of forward-backward signal propagation in wide and deep Transformers, focusing on residual neural networks with multi-head self-attention and multilayer perceptron blocks. The analysis suggests particular width scalings of initialization and training hyperparameters for these models. ## Executive Summary
This paper presents an effective-theory analysis of forward-backward signal propagation in wide and deep Transformers, proposing width scalings of initialization and training hyperparameters. The authors suggest scaling initialization covariances, learning rates, and output rescaling factors with the inverse of the model width to maintain order-one preactivation signals and stable training dynamics. Empirical validation on Vision and Language Transformers demonstrates improved performance and reduced mid-training spikes compared to standard uniform scaling strategies. The work highlights the importance of proper width scaling of hyperparameters in optimizing Transformer architectures.

## Method Summary
The paper proposes an effective-theory analysis of forward-backward signal propagation in wide and deep Transformers. It suggests width scalings of initialization covariances, learning rates, and output rescaling factors to maintain order-one preactivation signals and stable training dynamics. The authors apply these theoretical suggestions by training Vision Transformers on ImageNet-1k and Language Transformers on span denoising tasks, comparing the proposed scaling strategies with standard uniform scaling. The experiments demonstrate improved performance and reduced mid-training spikes for the proposed scaling strategies.

## Key Results
- Proper width scaling of initialization covariances (e.g., 1/n for query/key weights) maintains order-one preactivation signals in wide Transformers.
- Scaling relative learning-rate factors with width (e.g., λQ = ΛQ/n) keeps the neural tangent kernel order-one, ensuring stable training dynamics.
- Scaling the output rescaling factor (Nrescale = 1/√n) prevents output signal explosion in tied-weight architectures.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: Proper width scaling of initialization hyperparameters ensures preactivation signals remain order-one throughout forward propagation in wide Transformers.
- Mechanism: By scaling initialization covariances with inverse width (e.g., 1/n for query/key weights), the effective variance of preactivations remains bounded even as networks grow wider, preventing signal explosion or vanishing.
- Core assumption: Layer normalization effectively normalizes signals such that any linear growth in signal variance due to depth is canceled.

- Break condition: If normalization layers are removed or if the width scaling is not applied correctly, signals may explode or vanish despite proper initialization.

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: Scaling relative learning-rate factors with width keeps the neural tangent kernel (NTK) order-one, ensuring stable training dynamics.
- Mechanism: By scaling learning rates inversely with width for each parameter group (e.g., λQ = ΛQ/n for query weights), the NTK remains stable during training, preventing gradient explosion or vanishing.
- Core assumption: The NTK at initialization dictates the training dynamics at leading order in the width expansion.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract]: "This analysis suggests particular width scalings of initialization and training hyperparameters for these models."
  - [section]: "our objective in §I 3 will be to scale relative learning-rate factors λG's such that the neural tangent kernel H(α1;i1)(α2;i2) – and hence the change in the network function – stays of order one as Transformers get wider."
  - [corpus]: Weak - no direct corpus evidence for this specific mechanism.
- Break condition: If the learning rate scaling is not applied correctly, the NTK may grow or shrink with width, leading to unstable training.

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: Proper width scaling of output rescaling factor (Nrescale = 1/√n) prevents output signal explosion in tied-weight architectures.
- Mechanism: By scaling the output rescaling factor with inverse width, the output signal variance remains bounded, preventing explosion in architectures with tied word embeddings between stem and head blocks.
- Core assumption: The output signal is a sum of n order-one random numbers, requiring rescaling by 1/√n to maintain order-one variance.
- Evidence anchors:

## Limitations

- The analysis relies heavily on the effective theory approach, which assumes infinite width and depth limits and may not fully capture the behavior of finite-width, finite-depth Transformers used in practice.
- The paper proposes specific width scalings for initialization covariances, learning rates, and output rescaling factors, but their optimality and generalizability may depend on specific architectural and dataset choices.
- The empirical validation is limited to a few Vision and Language Transformer architectures and datasets, and further experiments are needed to verify the generalizability of the proposed width scalings across different model families and tasks.
