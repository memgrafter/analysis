# Communicating Uncertainty in Machine Learning Explanations: A Visualization Analytics Approach for Predictive Process Monitoring

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2304.05736
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2304.05736
- Reference count: 27
- Key outcome: Proposed methodology integrates model uncertainty into post-hoc explanations for ML models, specifically PDP and ICE plots, using Monte Carlo dropout and visualized through density plots, box plots, and uncertainty profiles

## Executive Summary
This paper addresses the critical challenge of communicating uncertainty in machine learning explanations for predictive process monitoring. The authors propose a novel methodology that integrates uncertainty quantification into post-hoc explanation techniques, specifically Partial Dependence Plots (PDP) and Individual Conditional Expectation (ICE) plots. By leveraging Monte Carlo dropout to estimate model uncertainty and presenting it through multiple visualization formats, the approach aims to improve the interpretability and trustworthiness of predictive models in manufacturing contexts. The methodology is implemented in a visual analytics interface and evaluated with domain experts, demonstrating improved understanding of model behavior and reliability.

## Method Summary
The methodology combines Monte Carlo dropout with PDP and ICE visualization techniques to quantify and communicate uncertainty in ML explanations. A deep feedforward neural network is trained on manufacturing event log data enriched with customer order information for cycle time prediction. The model incorporates dropout layers during both training and inference, where multiple forward passes generate prediction distributions that capture uncertainty. These distributions are then visualized through density plots showing prediction spread, box plots summarizing uncertainty statistics, and uncertainty profiles categorizing uncertainty levels into low, medium, and high profiles. The approach is integrated into a visual analytics interface designed for domain experts in manufacturing predictive process monitoring.

## Key Results
- Uncertainty-aware visualizations improve interpretability and trustworthiness of model predictions
- Domain experts found the visualizations intuitive and helpful for understanding model behavior and reliability
- The visual analytics interface successfully communicates both the prediction trends and their associated uncertainty levels

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The methodology works by capturing the inherent uncertainty in neural network predictions through Monte Carlo dropout, which approximates Bayesian inference by treating dropout as a variational approximation to the posterior distribution. Multiple forward passes with dropout enabled generate a distribution of predictions for each input, quantifying the model's confidence. This uncertainty information is then integrated into traditional explanation techniques (PDP and ICE) by showing not just the average effect but also the spread and variability of predictions across the uncertainty distribution. The visualization techniques (density plots, box plots, and uncertainty profiles) translate these statistical measures into intuitive visual formats that domain experts can readily interpret and use to assess model reliability.

## Foundational Learning

**Monte Carlo Dropout**
- Why needed: Provides a practical method for uncertainty estimation in neural networks without requiring full Bayesian modeling
- Quick check: Verify that multiple forward passes with different dropout masks produce varying predictions that capture model uncertainty

**Partial Dependence Plots (PDP)**
- Why needed: Shows the marginal effect of features on predictions averaged across the dataset
- Quick check: Confirm that PDP shows expected trends and that uncertainty bands capture natural variation in the feature-prediction relationship

**Individual Conditional Expectation (ICE) plots**
- Why needed: Reveals heterogeneous feature effects by showing individual instance-level predictions rather than averages
- Quick check: Ensure ICE plots display individual variation while uncertainty visualizations capture both individual and population-level uncertainty

## Architecture Onboarding

**Component Map:**
Data preprocessing -> Feature engineering -> Deep feedforward NN with MC dropout -> Uncertainty estimation -> Visualization generation -> Visual analytics interface

**Critical Path:**
The critical path flows from data preprocessing through model training with MC dropout to uncertainty-aware visualization generation. The MC dropout implementation is crucial as it enables uncertainty estimation without requiring significant architectural changes to standard feedforward networks. The visualization component must effectively translate statistical uncertainty measures into intuitive visual formats for domain experts.

**Design Tradeoffs:**
The approach trades computational efficiency for uncertainty quantification, as MC dropout requires multiple forward passes. The choice of visualization techniques balances information richness against interpretability - density plots show full distribution shapes but may be complex, while box plots simplify information but may lose detail. The uncertainty categorization into low/medium/high profiles simplifies communication but introduces arbitrary thresholds.

**Failure Signatures:**
- Poor model performance due to inadequate feature engineering will manifest as uninformative or misleading uncertainty estimates
- Unrealistic uncertainty estimates may indicate incorrect MC dropout implementation or insufficient forward passes
- Visualization issues will appear as cluttered or confusing displays that fail to communicate uncertainty effectively

**First Experiments:**
1. Verify MC dropout implementation by checking prediction variance across multiple forward passes
2. Generate basic PDP and ICE plots without uncertainty to establish baseline visualization functionality
3. Implement simple density plots for prediction distributions before integrating into the full visual analytics interface

## Limitations
- Evaluation relies on a single case study in manufacturing without comparative analysis against other uncertainty visualization methods
- Proposed uncertainty categorization thresholds appear arbitrary without justification for their selection
- Visual analytics interface and specific implementation details are not fully specified, making independent replication challenging
