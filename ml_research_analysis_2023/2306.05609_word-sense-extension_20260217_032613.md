# Word sense extension

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2306.05609
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.05609
- Reference count: 22
- Primary result: Framework partitions polysemous words into pseudo-tokens and predicts plausible novel senses for over 7,500 English words with superior performance over baselines

## Executive Summary
This paper introduces a framework for word sense extension (WSE) that enables words to spawn new senses in novel contexts. The approach partitions polysemous words into pseudo-tokens marking different senses, then uses chaining-based models to predict whether a source pseudo-token can extend to express the meaning of a target pseudo-token. The framework combines episodic learning with BERT-based language modeling to transform the semantic space, enabling prediction of rare word senses with few or zero mentions in training data. Results show the WSE framework outperforms transformer-based WSD models on rare senses.

## Method Summary
The WSE framework partitions polysemous English words from Wikitext-103 into pseudo-tokens marking different senses. For each word type with multiple senses, the method creates source-target pseudo-token pairs by randomly selecting one sense as target and the remaining as source. Usage sentences are annotated with WordNet sense IDs using the Bevilacqua and Navigli (2020) algorithm. BERT-base-uncased models are trained from scratch on a masked language modeling task using replaced usage sentences, then fine-tuned through episodic learning to create a sense-extensional semantic space. Two chaining-based models are evaluated: WSE-Prototype (using global average embeddings) and WSE-Exemplar (using individual usage embeddings), compared against BERT-MLM and BERT-STS baselines using mean precision and MRR-100 metrics.

## Key Results
- Framework predicts plausible novel senses for over 7,500 English words
- WSE models show superior performance compared to BERT-MLM and BERT-STS baselines
- Framework improves transformer-based WSD models on predicting rare word senses with few or zero mentions in training data

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The framework works by creating a specialized semantic space where polysemous words are partitioned into pseudo-tokens representing distinct senses. Through episodic learning, the model learns to identify chains of semantic similarity between source and target pseudo-tokens. The chaining mechanism allows the model to leverage contextual similarity patterns to predict when a source sense can extend to express a target sense, even when the target sense is rare or unseen. The episodic learning algorithm specifically transforms the embedding space to support this type of sense extension reasoning.

## Foundational Learning
- Polysemy partitioning: Splitting polysemous words into pseudo-tokens marking distinct senses - needed to create discrete units for sense extension prediction; quick check: verify each word type has ≥2 senses with ≥10 mentions each
- Episodic learning: Learning algorithm that samples source-target pairs and computes similarity scores - needed to transform embedding space for sense extension; quick check: validate N source-target pairs are properly sampled during training
- Chaining models: Prototype and Exemplar approaches for computing sense similarity - needed to measure semantic relationships between pseudo-tokens; quick check: confirm global average vs individual usage embedding selection

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component map:** Wikitext-103 corpus → WordNet sense annotation → Pseudo-token partitioning → BERT MLM training → Episodic learning → WSE-Prototype/WSE-Exemplar models → Evaluation

**Critical path:** The episodic learning algorithm is the critical component that transforms the BERT embedding space to support sense extension. This algorithm samples source-target pseudo-token pairs and computes similarity scores that guide the semantic space transformation.

**Design tradeoffs:** The framework trades computational complexity for accuracy by training BERT models from scratch on replaced usage sentences rather than fine-tuning pre-trained models. This prevents information smuggling but increases training time.

**Failure signatures:** Poor performance may result from inadequate partitioning of polysemous words, improper implementation of the episodic learning algorithm, or insufficient training data for rare senses. Low precision scores indicate the chaining mechanism isn't capturing semantic relationships effectively.
