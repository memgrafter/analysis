# ITALIC: An Italian Intent Classification Dataset
## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2306.08502
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.08502
- Reference count: 0
- Primary result: First large-scale Italian intent classification dataset with 16,521 crowdsourced audio samples from 70 speakers

## Executive Summary
This paper introduces ITALIC, the first large-scale speech dataset specifically designed for Italian intent classification. The dataset comprises 16,521 crowdsourced audio samples collected from 70 speakers across various Italian regions, making it a significant contribution to Italian natural language processing research. The authors provide comprehensive metadata including speaker demographics, recording conditions, and regional origin, enabling detailed analysis of model performance across different conditions.

The research demonstrates that language adaptation improves speech model performance, monolingual text models outperform multilingual ones, and ITALIC presents greater challenges than existing Italian benchmarks. The dataset, annotation scheme, and evaluation code are released to support future research on Italian spoken language understanding and language-specific datasets, addressing the current gap in resources for Italian SLU tasks.

## Method Summary
The ITALIC dataset was created through a crowdsourcing approach, collecting 16,521 audio samples from 70 speakers representing different Italian regions. Each sample was labeled with intent categories and accompanied by detailed metadata including speaker demographics, recording conditions, and regional origin. The dataset was designed to capture the linguistic diversity of Italian while maintaining consistent intent classification labels. State-of-the-art speech and text models were evaluated on this dataset, with particular attention to language adaptation effects and model architecture choices. The evaluation framework compared performance across different model types and investigated the relative difficulty compared to existing Italian benchmarks.

## Key Results
- Language adaptation significantly improves speech model performance on Italian intent classification
- Monolingual text models consistently outperform multilingual models for Italian language tasks
- ITALIC demonstrates higher difficulty levels compared to existing Italian SLU benchmarks
- Comprehensive metadata enables detailed analysis of demographic and regional performance variations

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The dataset's effectiveness stems from its diverse speaker pool and comprehensive metadata collection, which enables models to learn robust representations across different Italian dialects and speaking styles. The crowdsourcing approach captures real-world variability in recording conditions and speaker characteristics, making models trained on ITALIC more generalizable to practical deployment scenarios. The intent classification task structure aligns well with the linguistic properties of Italian, while the metadata enables targeted improvements through demographic-aware modeling approaches.

## Limitations
- Crowdsourced data collection introduces uncontrolled recording conditions and potential demographic biases
- Evaluation focuses on intent classification accuracy without comprehensive error analysis across dialects and proficiency levels
- Dataset size remains modest compared to large-scale English SLU benchmarks, potentially limiting model generalizability
- Study does not address out-of-scope intent detection or provide detailed analysis of failure cases across demographic groups
