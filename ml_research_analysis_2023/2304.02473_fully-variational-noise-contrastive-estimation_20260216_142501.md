# Fully Variational Noise-Contrastive Estimation

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2304.02473

## Executive Summary
This paper introduces fully variational noise-contrastive estimation (NCE), a method that extends noise-contrastive estimation to latent variable models by applying variational lower bounds to both terms in the NCE loss. The authors construct a family of proper scoring rules satisfying the "double ELBO property," enabling tractable estimation of unnormalized latent variable models. Variational autoencoders are shown to be a particular instance of this framework, and the method is demonstrated on toy examples showing improved out-of-distribution detection and control over reconstruction quality.

## Method Summary
The method extends standard noise-contrastive estimation by applying variational lower bounds to both the positive and negative terms in the NCE loss, creating what the authors call the "double ELBO property." This is achieved by constructing proper scoring rules that satisfy specific mathematical properties, allowing for the estimation of unnormalized latent variable models. The framework provides a unified view of various generative models and establishes connections with variational autoencoders through the scoring rule perspective.

## Key Results
- Demonstrated theoretical framework for applying NCE to latent variable models
- Showed improved out-of-distribution detection on toy examples
- Established connections between fully variational NCE and variational autoencoders
- Provided control over reconstruction quality through the scoring rule framework

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The method works by simultaneously applying variational lower bounds to both the positive and negative terms in the NCE objective, which allows for tractable estimation of unnormalized latent variable models. This "double ELBO" approach overcomes the limitation of standard variational NCE, which can only apply the ELBO to one term. The proper scoring rules ensure that the resulting bound is tight and provides a valid objective for optimization.

## Foundational Learning
- Noise-Contrastive Estimation (NCE): A method for learning unnormalized models by contrasting observed data with noise samples. Why needed: Provides the foundation for the contrastive learning approach used in the paper.
- Variational Autoencoders (VAEs): Generative models that use variational inference to learn latent representations. Why needed: Serves as a special case of the proposed framework and provides context for the connections.
- Proper Scoring Rules: Functions that measure the quality of probabilistic predictions. Why needed: The paper constructs a family of scoring rules that satisfy the double ELBO property.
- ELBO (Evidence Lower Bound): A lower bound on the log-likelihood used in variational inference. Why needed: The core variational technique applied to both terms in the NCE objective.
- Unnormalized Models: Models where the partition function is intractable. Why needed: The primary target for estimation using the proposed method.

## Architecture Onboarding
Component Map: Data -> NCE Objective -> Double ELBO Bound -> Proper Scoring Rule -> Optimization
Critical Path: The optimization process flows from data through the NCE objective, applies the double ELBO bound, evaluates proper scoring rules, and performs parameter updates.
Design Tradeoffs: The choice of variational family affects both expressiveness and computational tractability. More expressive families may provide tighter bounds but increase computational cost.
Failure Signatures: If the variational family is insufficiently expressive, the bounds may become loose, leading to poor optimization and suboptimal models.
First Experiments: 1) Verify the double ELBO property on simple distributions, 2) Test proper scoring rules on synthetic data, 3) Compare fully variational NCE against standard NCE on a simple latent variable model.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
The paper does not explicitly call out open questions, but the limitations section suggests several areas for future work, including scalability analysis, empirical validation on real-world datasets, and investigation of optimization stability.

## Limitations
- Theoretical results rely on strict assumptions about variational family expressiveness
- Empirical validation limited to synthetic toy examples
- Computational overhead of maintaining variational distributions for both positive and negative samples not thoroughly analyzed
- No analysis of optimization stability when applying double ELBO bounds
