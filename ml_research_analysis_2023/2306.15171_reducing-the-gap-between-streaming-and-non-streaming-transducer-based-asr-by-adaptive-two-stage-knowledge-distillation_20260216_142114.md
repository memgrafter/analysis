# Reducing the gap between streaming and non-streaming Transducer-based ASR by adaptive two-stage knowledge distillation

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2306.15171
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.15171
- Reference count: 0
- Key outcome: Achieved 19% relative WER reduction on LibriSpeech by using adaptive two-stage knowledge distillation for conformer transducer models

## Executive Summary
This paper addresses the performance gap between streaming and non-streaming automatic speech recognition (ASR) models by proposing an adaptive two-stage knowledge distillation method. The approach leverages a conformer transducer architecture and introduces a novel technique to transfer knowledge from a non-streaming teacher model to a streaming student model. By combining hidden layer learning with mean squared error (MSE) loss and output layer learning with Kullback-Leibler (KL) divergence enhanced by adaptive temperature smoothing, the method achieves significant improvements in both accuracy and response time for streaming ASR systems.

## Method Summary
The proposed method employs a two-stage knowledge distillation framework designed specifically for conformer transducer models. In the first stage, the student model learns from the hidden representations of the teacher model using MSE loss, capturing intermediate feature transformations. The second stage focuses on the output layer, where KL divergence is used to align the probability distributions of the student and teacher models. A key innovation is the introduction of an adaptive temperature method based on power transformation, which dynamically adjusts the smoothness of the output distribution during training. This adaptive approach helps the streaming model better approximate the non-streaming teacher's behavior while maintaining its real-time capabilities.

## Key Results
- 19% relative reduction in word error rate (WER) compared to the original streaming conformer transducer model on LibriSpeech
- Faster response time for the first token, demonstrating improved streaming performance
- The method effectively bridges the accuracy gap between streaming and non-streaming ASR models while preserving low-latency characteristics

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The two-stage distillation process allows the streaming model to learn both intermediate representations and final output distributions from the non-streaming teacher. The hidden layer learning stage captures the essential feature transformations that occur in the teacher model, while the output layer learning stage ensures the final predictions align closely with the teacher's distributions. The adaptive temperature smoothing is particularly effective because it prevents the student model from being overly confident in its predictions during training, allowing for better exploration of the output space and more robust generalization to streaming scenarios.

## Foundational Learning
- **Knowledge Distillation**: Why needed - to transfer learned representations from a complex teacher model to a simpler student model; Quick check - student WER improves when trained with teacher guidance versus standalone training
- **Conformer Architecture**: Why needed - combines convolutional and transformer components for efficient sequence modeling in ASR; Quick check - model shows strong performance on both local and global context in speech data
- **Streaming vs Non-streaming Models**: Why needed - streaming models process speech incrementally for real-time applications but typically sacrifice accuracy; Quick check - streaming model processes input with fixed look-ahead while non-streaming model has full context
- **KL Divergence**: Why needed - measures the difference between probability distributions for output alignment; Quick check - lower KL divergence between student and teacher outputs correlates with better student performance
- **Power Transformation**: Why needed - provides mathematical framework for adaptive temperature scaling; Quick check - smooth monotonic transformation of temperature values during training
- **MSE Loss**: Why needed - effectively captures differences in hidden representations between models; Quick check - reduced MSE in hidden layers corresponds to improved downstream performance

## Architecture Onboarding

**Component Map**: Teacher (Non-streaming Conformer) -> Student (Streaming Conformer) -> Distillation Process (Stage 1: MSE Loss on Hidden Layers -> Stage 2: KL Divergence with Adaptive Temperature on Output)

**Critical Path**: Speech input → Streaming Conformer encoder → Encoder states → Predictor → Joint network → Output distribution → Token prediction

**Design Tradeoffs**: The method prioritizes accuracy improvement over computational efficiency during training (two-stage process with adaptive temperature calculation), but the resulting student model maintains streaming efficiency during inference. The adaptive temperature adds computational overhead during training but is not required during inference.

**Failure Signatures**: If the adaptive temperature is not properly tuned, the student model may either fail to learn from the teacher (too low temperature) or become overly smoothed and lose discriminative power (too high temperature). Insufficient hidden layer learning can result in the student failing to capture essential intermediate representations.

**First Experiments**: 1) Train streaming model with only Stage 1 (MSE on hidden layers) to isolate its contribution; 2) Train with only Stage 2 (KL divergence without adaptive temperature) to evaluate the impact of the smoothing mechanism; 3) Apply the method to a different conformer configuration to test robustness across model sizes.

## Limitations
- The method is evaluated only on the LibriSpeech corpus, limiting generalizability to other speech datasets and domains
- No analysis of computational overhead introduced by the adaptive smoothness method during training
- The power transformation parameters and their sensitivity to different acoustic conditions remain unclear
- Potential overfitting to the specific streaming architecture is not addressed

## Confidence
High confidence in the 19% WER reduction claim and faster first token response time based on LibriSpeech results. Medium confidence in generalizability due to single dataset evaluation. Low confidence in computational efficiency claims as training overhead is not quantified.

## Next Checks
