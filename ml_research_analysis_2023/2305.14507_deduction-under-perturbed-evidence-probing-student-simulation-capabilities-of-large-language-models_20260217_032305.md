# Deduction under Perturbed Evidence: Probing Student Simulation Capabilities of Large Language Models

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2305.14507
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.14507
- Reference count: 5
- Key outcome: Large language models struggle significantly with reasoning over perturbed evidence, experiencing a 45% drop in accuracy when facts are manipulated to reverse conclusions

## Executive Summary
This paper introduces the DUPE (Deduction under Perturbed Evidence) reasoning paradigm to test whether large language models can reason with manipulated facts to arrive at valid yet false conclusions. The authors create a DUPEd version of the StrategyQA dataset by minimally perturbing facts to reverse original answers, then evaluate GPT3.5 and GPT4 under two prompt settings inspired by student simulation models. Results show both models struggle significantly with the DUPEd dataset, experiencing a 45% drop in accuracy compared to the original dataset. The study reveals that mathematical perturbations are more robust than natural language perturbations, and that student simulation-inspired prompts can partially mitigate the accuracy drop but still leave a substantial 29% drop compared to the original dataset.

## Method Summary
The study creates a DUPEd version of the StrategyQA dataset by manipulating facts to reverse original answers with minimal changes (1-2 words). Two GPT models (GPT3.5 and GPT4) are evaluated under two prompt settings: P1 asks models to answer based on provided evidence, while P2 primes models that evidence may be incorrect, simulating student responses. The researchers measure accuracy drops between the original and DUPEd datasets and analyze differences between language and math perturbations.

## Key Results
- Both GPT3.5 and GPT4 experience a 45% drop in accuracy on the DUPEd dataset compared to the original StrategyQA dataset
- Mathematical perturbations are more robust than natural language perturbations (58.7% vs 32.4% accuracy drop for GPT3.5; 50.3% vs 39.4% for GPT4)
- Student simulation-inspired prompts improve performance but still result in a 29% accuracy drop compared to the original dataset
- GPT4 outperforms GPT3.5 on both original and DUPEd datasets, demonstrating superior reasoning capabilities

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: Large language models rely on their parameters as the dominant source of factual information during reasoning, which causes them to ignore or override manipulated evidence in prompts
- Mechanism: Feed-forward layers in transformer models function as key-value memories that encode factual associations. When presented with manipulated facts, the model's internal parameters dominate the reasoning process, leading to conclusions based on encoded knowledge rather than prompt evidence
- Core assumption: Factual information encoded in model parameters has greater influence on output generation than contextual information in prompts
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "they typically rely on their parameters, which encode mostly accurate information, to reason and make inferences"
  - [section 3.2] "factual information encoded in the parameters of LLMs plays a dominant role in governing the generated output" and "feed-forward layers in transformer models function as key-value memories, which implies that they encode factual information"
- Break condition: If localized editing techniques can successfully modify factual associations to align with manipulated evidence

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: Student simulation-inspired prompts can partially override the parameter dominance by explicitly framing the task as reasoning about student misconceptions
- Mechanism: When primed with a student simulation context, the model shifts from pure fact retrieval to reasoning about knowledge states and misconceptions, allowing manipulated evidence to have greater influence on conclusions
- Core assumption: Prompt framing can significantly alter how LLMs process evidence versus parameter knowledge
- Evidence anchors:
  - [section 2.3] "Prompt P2 inspired by student simulation setting informs/ primes the models that the provided evidence may be incorrect since the evidence reflects the erroneous nature of students' responses"
  - [section 3.1.1] "prompt setting P2 performs significantly better than P1 by a margin of 16.0% for the GPT4 model"
- Break condition: If accuracy improvement from student simulation prompts remains consistently below original accuracy

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: Mathematical perturbations are easier for LLMs to process correctly than natural language perturbations due to their discrete, numerical nature
- Mechanism: Mathematical statements involve explicit numerical relationships that may be more directly encoded in parameters, while natural language requires more complex semantic processing that is more vulnerable to manipulation
- Core assumption: Numerical reasoning pathways in LLMs are more robust to perturbations than linguistic reasoning pathways
- Evidence anchors:
  - [section 3.1.2] "both GPT models are more resilient to math perturbations compared to language perturbations" with specific accuracy drops (58.7% vs 32.4% for GPT3.5; 50.3% vs 39.4% for GPT4)
  - [abstract] "models are more robust to mathematical perturbations than natural language perturbations"
- Break condition: If mathematical perturbations with similar complexity to language perturbations show comparable accuracy drops

## Limitations

- Dataset size of 325 examples may not capture the full spectrum of reasoning challenges
- Focus on minimal perturbations (1-2 word changes) may not represent more complex real-world misinformation scenarios
- Evaluation limited to only two GPT models, restricting generalizability to broader LLM architectures

## Confidence

- High confidence: The 45% accuracy drop on DUPEd dataset compared to original StrategyQA, and differential performance between P1 and P2 prompts
- Medium confidence: Observed difference in robustness between mathematical and natural language perturbations
- Low confidence: Generalizability to other LLM architectures beyond GPT3.5 and GPT4
