# Approximation and Estimation Ability of Transformers for Sequence-to-Sequence Functions with Infinite Dimensional Input

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2305.18699
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.18699
- Reference count: 40
- This paper studies the theoretical aspects of Transformer networks for sequence-to-sequence functions with infinite dimensional inputs, showing they can avoid the curse of dimensionality through feature extraction and parameter sharing.

## Executive Summary
This paper provides theoretical analysis of Transformer networks for approximating and estimating sequence-to-sequence functions with infinite dimensional inputs. The authors derive approximation and estimation error bounds for Transformers with anisotropic and mixed smoothness, demonstrating that these models can avoid the curse of dimensionality. The key insight is that Transformers' feature extraction ability and parameter sharing property allow them to focus on important features while maintaining finite parameter complexity. The paper also extends these results to piecewise smooth functions where the importance of features changes with each input.

## Method Summary
The paper studies non-parametric regression with infinite-dimensional sequence-to-sequence inputs and outputs. The method involves empirical risk minimization (ERM) over a class of Transformer networks with specific hyperparameters for approximating functions with mixed or anisotropic smoothness. The Transformer architecture includes embedding layers with sinusoidal positional encoding, multi-head self-attention with sliding window mechanism, and feed-forward neural networks. The theoretical analysis derives approximation error bounds based on smoothness parameters and estimation error bounds based on sample size, showing that Transformers can achieve optimal rates without suffering from curse of dimensionality.

## Key Results
- Transformers can avoid the curse of dimensionality when approximating anisotropic or mixed smooth functions due to their feature extraction ability and parameter sharing property
- Self-attention mechanism dynamically estimates feature importance for piecewise smooth functions, allowing Transformers to extract important features adaptively
- Sinusoidal positional encoding enables relative position attention in shift-equivariant settings through linear transformation properties

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: Transformers can avoid the curse of dimensionality when approximating anisotropic/mixed smooth functions
- Mechanism: Feature extraction ability and parameter sharing property allow Transformers to focus on important coordinates
- Core assumption: Target function has anisotropic or mixed smoothness with sparse important features
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "Transformers can avoid the curse of dimensionality due to their feature extraction ability and parameter sharing property"
  - [section] "This is in contrast to FNNs, where the number of parameters should increase at least linearly with the input and output length. The parameter sharing property and feature extraction ability of Transformers play an essential role in the proof"
  - [corpus] "Approximation Bounds for Transformer Networks with Application to Regression" - Weak evidence, no specific claim about dimensionality avoidance
- Break condition: If smoothness is not anisotropic/mixed or features are not sparse, the dimensionality advantage disappears

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: Self-attention mechanism dynamically estimates feature importance for piecewise smooth functions
- Mechanism: Attention weights adapt based on input-dependent importance function, allowing selection of relevant tokens
- Core assumption: Importance function is well-separated and has appropriate smoothness
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "Transformers can estimate the importance of features for each input and extract important features dynamically"
  - [section] "Due to the dynamical feature extraction ability of self-attention mechanism, Transformers can approximate Π and achieve similar approximation error results as in the fixed smoothness setting"
  - [corpus] "On Expressive Power of Looped Transformers: Theoretical Analysis and Enhancement via Timestep Encoding" - Weak evidence, focuses on timestep encoding rather than feature importance
- Break condition: If importance function is not well-separated or attention mechanism fails to distinguish features, approximation breaks down

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: Sinusoidal positional encoding enables relative position attention in shift-equivariant settings
- Mechanism: Shift operation on positional encoding corresponds to linear transformation, allowing attention to focus by relative position
- Core assumption: Positional encoding uses sinusoidal pattern as in Vaswani et al. (2017)
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] No direct mention
  - [section] "Due to the shift-equivariance, Transformer networks should extract important tokens for each output by relative position. Since we use absolute positional encoding, it is not trivial to show that Transformers have such capability... To overcome this issue, we adopt the sinusoidal positional encoding since the shift operation pi → pi+j can be represented by linear transformation"
  - [corpus] No direct evidence, but related to "Bridging Smoothness and Approximation" which mentions graph convolutions
- Break condition: If positional encoding is not sinusoidal or attention mechanism cannot utilize relative position information, the approximation fails

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- Question: Can the approximation error rate for Transformers with anisotropic smoothness be improved beyond the current bound of N^(-a†), ignoring poly-log factors?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper states "This is in contrast to FNNs, where the number of parameters should increase at least linearly with the input and output length" and mentions that "the approximation error can be bounded by N^(-a†) ignoring poly-log factor".
- Why unresolved: The paper presents the current best-known rate but does not prove optimality or provide matching lower bounds for this approximation rate.
- What evidence would resolve it: Either a proof that N^(-a†) is the optimal rate for anisotropic smoothness with Transformers, or a construction of a Transformer architecture achieving a better rate would resolve this question.

### Open Question 2
- Question: How does the choice of positional encoding scheme affect the approximation and estimation ability of Transformers for infinite dimensional inputs?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper discusses the importance of positional encoding, stating "Due to the shift-equivariance, Transformer networks should extract important tokens for each output by relative position" and that "fixed length positional encoding is enough for feature extraction by adjusting the scale appropriately inside the self-attention mechanism."
- Why unresolved: While the paper uses sinusoidal positional encoding and shows it's sufficient, it doesn't explore other encoding schemes or prove that sinusoidal encoding is optimal for various function classes.
- What evidence would resolve it: Comparative analysis showing how different positional encoding schemes (e.g., learned, relative, or other fixed encodings) affect approximation rates for various function classes would resolve this.

### Open Question 3
- Question: Can the estimation error bound for piecewise γ-smooth functions be improved when V (the number of partitions) grows polynomially with n?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper states "This convergence rate is the same as in Theorem 5.4 up to poly-log order if V = poly(n)" but doesn't provide tighter bounds for this specific case.
- Why unresolved: The current bound includes a (log V)^3 factor that could potentially be reduced when V has a specific relationship with n.
- What evidence would resolve it: A refined analysis showing that when V = poly(n), the (log V)^3 factor can be eliminated or reduced to a lower-order term would resolve this.

### Open Question 4
- Question: What is the impact of the window size U in the sliding window attention mechanism on the approximation and estimation rates?
- Basis in paper: [inferred] The paper uses U as a hyperparameter in the analysis, with conditions like "log U ~ T" and "Ui = V (i >= 2)", but doesn't provide a detailed study of how different choices of U affect performance.
- Why unresolved: While the paper uses specific values of U in the theorems, it doesn't explore the trade-offs between larger/smaller U and approximation/estimation rates.
- What evidence would resolve it: A systematic study varying U and analyzing its effect on approximation and estimation rates for different function classes would resolve this.

### Open Question 5
- Question: Can the theoretical results be extended to more general function classes beyond the (piecewise) mixed and anisotropic smoothness considered in this paper?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper focuses on (piecewise) mixed and anisotropic smoothness, stating "In this paper, we consider the non-parametric regression problems and study the approximation and estimation ability of Transformers for sequence-to-sequence functions with infinite dimensional inputs."
- Why unresolved: The paper provides a thorough analysis for specific function classes but doesn't explore whether the techniques can be generalized to other function classes that might arise in practical applications.
- What evidence would resolve it: Extending the theoretical framework to other function classes (e.g., hierarchical composition models, functions with different smoothness structures) and deriving corresponding approximation and estimation rates would resolve this.

## Limitations
- Theoretical guarantees rely heavily on idealized assumptions about smoothness and shift-equivariance that may not hold in real-world applications
- The paper assumes infinite-dimensional inputs but practical implementations must discretize, creating potential gaps between theory and practice
- Estimation error bounds depend on unknown constants and require sample sizes that may be prohibitive in practice

## Confidence

**High Confidence**: The core theoretical framework for approximation error bounds under anisotropic/mixed smoothness is mathematically rigorous and well-supported by the proofs. The parameter sharing mechanism and its role in avoiding curse of dimensionality is clearly established.

**Medium Confidence**: The estimation error bounds and their dependence on sample size are theoretically sound, but the practical implications depend on unknown constants and real-world data distributions that may violate theoretical assumptions.

**Low Confidence**: The dynamic feature importance mechanism in piecewise smooth functions, while theoretically plausible, lacks concrete implementation details and empirical validation. The requirement for "well-separated" importance functions is vague and may be difficult to verify in practice.
