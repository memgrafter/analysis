# Act-Then-Measure: Reinforcement Learning for Partially Observable Environments with Active Measuring

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2303.08271
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.08271
- Authors: 
- Reference count: 10
- This paper addresses reinforcement learning in action-contingent noiselessly observable MDPs (ACNO-MDPs) by decoupling control and measurement actions through the act-then-measure heuristic.

## Executive Summary
This paper introduces a novel approach for reinforcement learning in action-contingent noiselessly observable Markov decision processes (ACNO-MDPs), where agents can actively gather information but incur costs for doing so. The authors propose the act-then-measure (ATM) heuristic, which separates the selection of control actions from measurement actions, assuming future state uncertainty can be ignored when choosing control actions. They prove a performance bound for policies following this heuristic and introduce measuring value as a metric to determine when taking measurements is beneficial. The paper develops Dyna-ATMQ, a reinforcement learning algorithm based on this heuristic that outperforms prior methods while remaining computationally tractable for larger problem sizes.

## Method Summary
The method proposes an act-then-measure (ATM) heuristic that decouples control actions (which affect the environment) from measurement actions (which affect observability) in ACNO-MDPs. The key insight is that when the cost of taking measurements is bounded, an agent can ignore future state uncertainty when choosing control actions. The authors introduce measuring value to determine when measurements are beneficial, computed using a full model of the environment. They develop Dyna-ATMQ, which combines Dyna-Q with the ATM heuristic, using R-Max exploration with optimism under uncertainty. The algorithm updates Q-values based on simulated experiences generated from a learned model, incorporating measuring value to decide when to take measurements. The approach is evaluated on both measuring value environments and standard frozen lake environments, demonstrating superior performance compared to AMRL-Q and observe-before-planning methods.

## Key Results
- Dyna-ATMQ outperforms prior methods including AMRL-Q and observe-before-planning on both measuring value environments and standard frozen lake environments
- The algorithm remains computationally tractable for larger problem sizes compared to observe-before-planning
- Empirical results show successful identification of when measurements are valuable, particularly in semi-stochastic environments
- Performance bound is proven for the ATM heuristic under bounded measurement cost assumptions

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The ATM heuristic works by exploiting the structure of ACNO-MDPs where information gathering is action-contingent and noiseless. By decoupling control and measurement actions, the agent can plan control actions assuming full observability while separately deciding when to gather information. The measuring value concept quantifies the expected benefit of information gathering, allowing the agent to make rational decisions about when the cost of measurement is justified by the potential improvement in future rewards. This separation simplifies the planning problem from a complex POMDP to a series of MDPs with different levels of observability.

## Foundational Learning
- ACNO-MDPs (Action-Contingent Noiselessly Observable MDPs): These are MDPs where observations are contingent on actions taken, but when observations are available, they are noiseless. Understanding this problem class is essential because it represents a specific type of partial observability that can be exploited for more efficient planning.

- Measuring Value: A metric that quantifies the expected benefit of taking a measurement action, calculated as the difference in expected return with and without the measurement. This concept is crucial for determining when information gathering is worthwhile given its associated cost.

- Dyna-Q Framework: A model-based reinforcement learning algorithm that interleaves planning (using a learned model to generate simulated experiences) with direct RL updates. This framework is important as Dyna-ATMQ builds upon it by incorporating measurement decisions into the planning process.

- R-Max Exploration: An exploration strategy that assigns optimistic Q-values to state-action pairs that have been visited fewer than a threshold number of times. This is used in Dyna-ATMQ to balance exploration and exploitation in the partially observable setting.

- Performance Bounds for Decoupled Planning: The theoretical guarantee that planning control actions separately from measurement actions yields near-optimal performance when measurement costs are bounded. This mathematical foundation validates the ATM heuristic's practical effectiveness.

## Architecture Onboarding

**Component Map:**
Agent -> Environment -> (Observation, Reward) -> Agent -> (Action, Measurement Decision)

**Critical Path:**
1. Agent observes current state (or lack thereof)
2. Agent computes measuring value for all possible measurement actions
3. Agent decides whether to take a measurement based on measuring value vs cost
4. If measuring, agent takes measurement and updates belief state
5. Agent selects control action using Q-values computed assuming full observability
6. Environment transitions to new state and returns observation/reward
7. Agent updates model and Q-values using Dyna-Q framework

**Design Tradeoffs:**
- The ATM heuristic trades optimality for computational tractability by ignoring future uncertainty when choosing control actions
- Using a full model for measuring value computation requires model knowledge but enables more informed measurement decisions
- The decoupled approach simplifies planning but may miss synergistic effects between control and measurement actions

**Failure Signatures:**
- Poor performance in highly stochastic environments where the ATM assumption is strongly violated
- Suboptimal measurement decisions when the model is inaccurate or incomplete
- Computational intractability if the state space is extremely large despite improvements over prior methods

**First Experiments:**
1. Implement Dyna-ATMQ on a simple grid world with controllable observations to verify basic functionality
2. Test measuring value computation on environments with known models to validate the theoretical metric
3. Compare Dyna-ATMQ against a baseline that always takes measurements to demonstrate the value of selective information gathering

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- Question: What is the precise computational complexity of Dyna-ATMQ compared to AMRL-Q and observe-before-planning in ACNO-MDPs of varying sizes?
- Basis in paper: [inferred] The paper states that Dyna-ATMQ stays computationally tractable for larger environments than observe-before-planning while yielding higher returns than AMRL-Q, but does not provide explicit complexity analysis.
- Why unresolved: The paper provides empirical evidence of scalability but lacks formal complexity analysis comparing the algorithms across different problem sizes.
- What evidence would resolve it: A detailed computational complexity analysis of Dyna-ATMQ versus baseline algorithms, including both time and space complexity as functions of state space size, action space size, and planning horizon.

### Open Question 2
- Question: How does the performance of ATM-based methods degrade in environments with non-noiseless measurements or partially observable states?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper notes that the ATM heuristic relies on the assumption of complete and noiseless observations, and mentions that investigating the ATM heuristic in the more general active measure POMDP setting could be an interesting direction.
- Why unresolved: The paper only evaluates the method in ACNO-MDPs with perfect observations and does not test it in partially observable settings with noisy measurements.
- What evidence would resolve it: Empirical evaluation of Dyna-ATMQ or similar ATM-based methods in environments with noisy or incomplete observations, comparing performance to POMDP solvers and other RL methods for partially observable domains.

### Open Question 3
- Question: What is the optimal exploration strategy for Dyna-ATMQ to balance exploration and exploitation in ACNO-MDPs?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper mentions using an optimistic Q-value with an exploration bonus based on R-Max, but notes this is a heuristic approach and suggests more sophisticated action choices could improve performance.
- Why unresolved: The paper uses a specific exploration strategy but does not investigate alternative exploration methods or theoretically analyze the exploration-exploitation tradeoff in ACNO-MDPs.
- What evidence would resolve it: Comparative analysis of different exploration strategies for Dyna-ATMQ, including theoretical analysis of exploration regret bounds and empirical comparison across diverse ACNO-MDP environments with varying levels of stochasticity.

## Limitations
- The ATM heuristic assumes future state uncertainty can be ignored when choosing control actions, which may not hold in highly stochastic environments
- Performance bound relies on bounded consecutive measurements without control actions, a condition that may not always be satisfied
- Measuring value computation requires a full model of the environment, limiting applicability in model-free settings
- The approach's scalability to extremely large state spaces remains uncertain despite improvements over prior methods
