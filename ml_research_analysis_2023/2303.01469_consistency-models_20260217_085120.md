# Consistency Models

## Executive Summary
Consistency models offer a fast, non-adversarial approach to generative modeling, enabling high-quality sample generation in a single step. By training a model to map noisy data points to their origins along the probability flow ODE trajectory, consistency models avoid iterative sampling while retaining the flexibility for few-step refinement and zero-shot data editing. Experiments show that consistency models outperform existing distillation techniques, achieving state-of-the-art FID scores on CIFAR-10 and ImageNet 64x64. When trained as standalone generative models, they surpass other non-adversarial single-step models on multiple datasets, making them a strong alternative for efficient, high-quality image synthesis.

## Method Summary
Consistency models are trained to map any point on the probability flow ODE trajectory to its origin, enabling one-step generation while preserving the flexibility for iterative refinement. The model can be trained either by distilling pre-trained diffusion models or as standalone generative models, offering flexibility in training approaches. Consistency models support zero-shot data editing tasks, such as image inpainting, colorization, and super-resolution, without requiring explicit training on these tasks. The method involves defining the consistency function and its self-consistency property, choosing a parameterization for the consistency model, implementing the consistency distillation loss or standalone training loss, training the model using the chosen loss and training mode, and evaluating the model's performance on one-step generation and few-step refinement.

## Key Results
- Consistency models achieve state-of-the-art FID scores of 3.55 on CIFAR-10 and 6.20 on ImageNet 64x64 for one-step generation.
