# Sparsity-depth Tradeoff in Infinitely Wide Deep Neural Networks

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2305.10550
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.10550
- Reference count: 40
- This paper investigates the sparsity-depth tradeoff in infinitely wide deep neural networks

## Executive Summary
This paper analyzes the sparsity-depth tradeoff in infinitely wide deep neural networks by deriving a sparse neural network Gaussian process (NNGP) kernel with ReLU activation and predetermined active neuron fractions. The authors find that sparser networks outperform non-sparse networks at shallow depths across multiple datasets, while deeper sparse networks suffer from overly flat eigenspectra. This behavior is explained through analysis of kernel dynamics over layers and the relationship between kernel eigenspectrum and modal error spectrum.

## Method Summary
The authors derive a sparse NNGP kernel for ReLU activation with predetermined sparsity fractions, computing it via numerical integration of the I(θ|τ) function. They perform kernel ridge regression using this kernel on benchmark datasets (MNIST, Fashion-MNIST, CIFAR10, CIFAR10-Grayscale) with varying training set sizes. The theoretical framework analyzes generalization error through the relationship between kernel eigenspectrum and modal error spectrum using first-order perturbation theory.

## Key Results
- Sparse networks outperform dense networks at shallow depths on multiple datasets
- Deep sparse networks suffer poor generalization due to overly flat eigenspectra
- Sparse shallow networks achieve comparable performance to dense deep networks with fewer kernel compositions

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: Sparsity improves generalization at shallow depths by creating a flatter eigenspectrum, reducing modal errors for modes with high target power.
- Mechanism: Sparse ReLU activations reduce cosine similarity between representations (cl < 1), causing the kernel Gram matrix to approach an identity matrix plus a constant offset. This yields a flatter eigenspectrum compared to dense networks, where cl → 1 causes rank-1 collapse.
- Core assumption: Target function powers are concentrated in low-frequency modes and do not vary significantly across kernels.
- Evidence anchors: [abstract] "sparser networks outperform the non-sparse networks at shallow depths"; [section] "a moderately steep eigenspectrum results in an optimal modal error spectrum that results in the minimum Eg"; [corpus] Weak - related work focuses on kernel dynamics but not specifically sparsity-induced eigenspectrum flattening.
- Break condition: If target function powers are spread uniformly across modes, the benefit of a flatter spectrum diminishes.

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: Deep sparse networks suffer poor generalization because their eigenspectrum becomes too flat, increasing modal errors for low-frequency modes.
- Mechanism: As depth increases, sparse kernels decorrelate inputs to cl < 1, producing Gram matrices with nearly flat spectra. This increases modal errors for modes corresponding to low eigenvalues, which dominate when target powers are skewed toward low-frequency modes.
- Core assumption: The relationship between eigenspectrum shape and modal error spectrum is monotonic for small perturbations from a flat spectrum.
- Evidence anchors: [abstract] "at the deeper configurations, denser activations are required to maintain high generalization performance"; [section] "if the eigenspectrum is too steep, the modal error increases too fast over ρ, contributing to increases in Eg"; [corpus] Weak - no direct corpus evidence for depth-induced flatness in sparse kernels.
- Break condition: If target function has significant power in high-frequency modes, a flatter spectrum may be beneficial.

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: Sparse shallow networks achieve comparable performance to dense deep networks with fewer kernel compositions.
- Mechanism: Sparse networks reach optimal eigenspectrum shape (moderately steep) at shallower depths, avoiding the rank-1 collapse of dense networks. This reduces computational cost while maintaining generalization.
- Core assumption: The number of kernel compositions directly correlates with computational time and generalization performance.
- Evidence anchors: [abstract] "the sparse and shallow networks have comparable generalization performances to dense and deep networks"; [section] "a sparse kernel requires a smaller depth, and hence fewer kernel compositions to reach the performance level observed in the deep dense model"; [corpus] Weak - related work discusses kernel composition but not sparsity-depth tradeoffs.
- Break condition: If computational cost is dominated by other factors (e.g., data size), the benefit of fewer compositions may be negligible.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- Question: How does the sparsity-depth tradeoff generalize to different activation functions beyond ReLU?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The authors derive a sparse NNGP kernel specifically for ReLU activation and note that exploring other activation functions is a future research direction.
- Why unresolved: The paper focuses solely on ReLU and does not investigate how different activation functions might affect the sparsity-depth tradeoff.
- What evidence would resolve it: Experiments comparing sparse NNGP kernels with different activation functions (e.g., tanh, sigmoid) across various depths and sparsity levels on benchmark datasets.

- Why unresolved: The paper only considers sparsity in the context of NNGP and does not investigate its effects on the NTK.
- What evidence would resolve it: Theoretical analysis and empirical experiments comparing the performance of NTK models with and without sparsity in their intermediate layers.

### Open Question 3
- Question: How does the sparsity-depth tradeoff change when using different training set sizes and data distributions?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The authors observe the sparsity-depth tradeoff across various datasets and training set sizes, but the exact relationship is not fully characterized.
- Why unresolved: The paper provides empirical observations but does not offer a comprehensive theoretical explanation for how the tradeoff varies with different data regimes.
- What evidence would resolve it: A theoretical framework that predicts the optimal sparsity and depth for a given dataset size and distribution, validated by extensive experiments.

## Limitations
- Analysis assumes infinite-width networks, which may not accurately represent finite-width practical networks
- The relationship between eigenspectrum shape and modal error spectrum is derived for small perturbations and may not hold for extreme sparsity levels
- Computational benefits of sparse networks are not quantified in terms of actual training time or resource usage

## Confidence
- **High confidence**: The basic NNGP framework and kernel ridge regression setup are well-established
- **Medium confidence**: The theoretical explanation linking eigenspectrum shape to generalization performance relies on perturbation theory
- **Low confidence**: The claim that sparse shallow networks achieve comparable performance to dense deep networks with fewer kernel compositions lacks direct empirical validation
