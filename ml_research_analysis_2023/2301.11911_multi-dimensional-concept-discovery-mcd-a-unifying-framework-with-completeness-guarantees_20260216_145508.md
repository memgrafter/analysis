# Multi-dimensional concept discovery (MCD): A unifying framework with completeness guarantees

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2301.11911
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2301.11911
- Reference count: 23
- Key outcome: MCD discovers multi-dimensional linear subspaces as concepts in neural networks, providing global model understanding with completeness guarantees where concept relevances sum to model predictions.

## Executive Summary
Multi-dimensional Concept Discovery (MCD) is a framework for discovering and analyzing model concepts in deep neural networks. MCD addresses the problem of obtaining global model understanding beyond single predictions by identifying multi-dimensional linear subspaces as concepts in intermediate feature layers. The core idea is to use sparse subspace clustering to discover these concepts without imposing orthogonality or dimensionality constraints, enabling true-to-the-model concepts that capture the actual model reasoning. MCD provides two complementary analysis tools: concept activation maps showing where concepts are expressed in input samples, and concept relevance heatmaps decomposing model decisions into concept contributions. The method guarantees a completeness relation where concept relevances sum up to the model prediction.

## Method Summary
MCD uses sparse subspace clustering (SSC) to discover multi-dimensional linear subspaces as concepts in intermediate neural network layers. The method clusters feature vectors from the last hidden layer, determines subspace bases via PCA, and decomposes both feature vectors and model predictions into concept contributions. Concept activation maps are created by upsampling concept expressions to input space, while concept relevance heatmaps show the spatial decomposition of model decisions. The framework guarantees completeness by ensuring that concept relevances sum to the final prediction, providing both local and global interpretability. MCD outperforms previous methods like k-means and PCA-based approaches by avoiding orthogonality constraints and allowing arbitrary multi-dimensional subspaces.

## Key Results
- MCD-SSC discovers meaningful, concise concepts that better reflect model reasoning structure compared to k-means and PCA methods
- Concept activation maps and relevance heatmaps provide complementary views of where and how concepts contribute to predictions
- Smallest Destroying Concepts (SDC) benchmark shows sharper accuracy declines when MCD concepts are removed, indicating better true-to-the-modelness
- Multi-dimensional concepts achieve completeness with fewer concepts than one-dimensional alternatives, delivering more concise explanations

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: Sparse subspace clustering (SSC) discovers true-to-the-model concepts by finding multi-dimensional linear subspaces without imposing orthogonality constraints.
- Mechanism: SSC clusters feature vectors from intermediate neural network layers based on their geometric arrangement in the high-dimensional feature space. By identifying linear subspaces that naturally emerge from the data distribution, SSC captures the actual model reasoning structures rather than artificially constrained concepts.
- Core assumption: The model's reasoning structures in feature space naturally form linear subspaces that SSC can identify without additional regularization or constraints.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract]: "We propose sparse subspace clustering to discover improved concepts and fully leverage the potential of multi-dimensional subspaces."
  - [section 2.2]: "SSC does not take advantage of the spatial proximity of the data, it can be implemented robustly against noise and outliers and does not require specifying the cluster dimensionalities in advance."
- Break condition: If the model's reasoning structures are non-linear or highly entangled, SSC may fail to discover meaningful concepts, requiring alternative clustering approaches.

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: Completeness relation ensures local and global interpretability by guaranteeing that concept relevances sum to the model prediction.
- Mechanism: The method decomposes both feature vectors and weight vectors into concept contributions, creating a linear additive model where concept relevances can be aggregated at both local (per-sample) and global (model-wide) levels.
- Core assumption: The final classification layer is linear or can be locally approximated as linear, allowing decomposition of predictions into concept contributions.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract]: "MCD offers two complementary analysis tools for concepts in input space: (1) concept activation maps, that show where a concept is expressed within a sample, allowing for concept characterization through prototypical samples, and (2) concept relevance heatmaps, that decompose the model decision into concept contributions."
  - [section 2.3]: "Concept relevance heatmaps and completeness relation...We specialize to the last hidden layer, which is only followed by linear operations."
- Break condition: If the model contains significant non-linear operations after the concept layer, the completeness relation breaks down, requiring alternative approximation methods.

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: Multi-dimensional concepts provide more concise explanations than one-dimensional concepts by covering relevant feature space with fewer concepts.
- Mechanism: Higher-dimensional subspaces can capture more variance and model reasoning with fewer concepts, reducing fragmentation of the explanation space while maintaining coverage.
- Core assumption: Model reasoning naturally organizes into multi-dimensional subspaces rather than requiring decomposition into many one-dimensional components.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract]: "Multi-dimensional concept subspaces reach a certain level of completeness with a smaller number of concepts than one-dimensional concepts and thus deliver more concise explanations."
  - [section 1]: "Allowing for arbitrary multi-dimensional subspaces unfolds the most general definition of a linear decomposition. Thus, this general approach enables true-to-the-model concepts as it allows to capture any meaningful linear structure within the hidden feature layer."
- Break condition: If model reasoning is inherently one-dimensional or if multi-dimensional concepts become too complex to interpret, the conciseness benefit diminishes.

## Foundational Learning

- Linear algebra and subspace geometry
  - Why needed here: The entire framework relies on understanding linear subspaces, basis vectors, and their properties. Concepts are defined as linear subspaces, and the completeness relation is derived from linear decomposition properties.
  - Quick check question: Can you explain why any feature vector can be uniquely decomposed into contributions from disjoint linear subspaces that span the entire feature space?

- Clustering algorithms and their properties
  - Why needed here: Different clustering approaches (k-means, PCA, SSC) have different strengths and weaknesses for discovering concept subspaces. Understanding their geometric properties is crucial for method selection.
  - Quick check question: What is the key difference between k-means clustering and sparse subspace clustering when applied to feature vectors?

- Principal angles and Grassmann distance
  - Why needed here: These metrics quantify the similarity and distinctness between concept subspaces, which is essential for evaluating concept quality and ensuring they capture different aspects of model reasoning.
  - Quick check question: How do principal angles between subspaces differ from the angles between individual vectors?

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map:
  Feature extraction -> Concept discovery -> Basis construction -> Concept activation -> Relevance computation -> Visualization

- Critical path:
  1. Extract feature vectors from target layer
  2. Cluster feature vectors using SSC
  3. Determine subspace bases via PCA
  4. Compute concept activation maps
  5. Calculate concept relevance heatmaps
  6. Generate prototypes for concept characterization

- Design tradeoffs:
  - Concept dimensionality vs interpretability: Higher dimensions capture more reasoning but may be harder to interpret
  - Clustering algorithm choice: SSC provides true-to-model concepts but is computationally heavier than k-means
  - Number of concepts: More concepts increase completeness but reduce conciseness
  - Orthogonality constraint: Enforced orthogonality simplifies relevance computation but may force unnatural concept splits

- Failure signatures:
  - Low completeness scores across classes indicate concepts aren't capturing relevant model reasoning
  - Very high average subspace dimensionality suggests concepts are too broad and not meaningful
  - Poor concept separation (low Grassmann distance) indicates concepts overlap significantly
  - SDC benchmark shows no sharp accuracy decline when concepts are removed

- First 3 experiments:
  1. Compare SSC vs k-means on CIFAR-10 subset to evaluate concept quality differences
  2. Vary number of concepts to find optimal completeness vs conciseness tradeoff
  3. Apply MCD to ResNet50 and ResNet50v2 to demonstrate architecture-specific concept differences

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- Question: How do the discovered concepts generalize to out-of-distribution samples, and what happens to their completeness relation when applied to data from different domains or distributions?
- Basis in paper: [inferred] The paper mentions that MCD concepts are "true-to-the-model" and discusses completeness relations, but does not explicitly test generalization to out-of-distribution data.
- Why unresolved: The experiments focus on in-distribution samples from ImageNet classes, leaving the behavior of MCD concepts on OOD data unexplored.
- What evidence would resolve it: Testing MCD on OOD datasets (e.g., corrupted images, different datasets) and measuring concept activation maps and completeness scores would clarify generalization capabilities.

### Open Question 2
- Question: Can MCD concepts be used to improve model robustness by identifying and mitigating spurious correlations or biases in the learned representations?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper mentions MCD's potential for "systematically investigating spurious correlations (model biases)" but does not demonstrate this application.
- Why unresolved: While the theoretical framework is established, empirical validation of MCD's utility in detecting and addressing biases is missing.
- What evidence would resolve it: Applying MCD to datasets with known biases (e.g., gender or racial biases) and showing how concept relevances correlate with spurious features would demonstrate this capability.

### Open Question 3
- Question: How sensitive are MCD concepts to the choice of hidden layer, and what is the optimal layer depth for discovering meaningful concepts?
- Basis in paper: [inferred] The paper focuses on the "last hidden layer" but does not systematically explore other layers or their impact on concept discovery.
- Why unresolved: The methodology assumes a fixed layer but does not investigate how concept quality changes across different depths in the network.
- What evidence would resolve it: Conducting experiments across multiple hidden layers and comparing concept activation maps, completeness scores, and relevance heatmaps would identify optimal layer selection.

## Limitations
- Computational complexity: SSC-based approach scales quadratically with dataset size, limiting practical applicability for very large models or datasets
- Dataset specificity: Performance on non-vision domains (medical imaging, satellite imagery) remains untested
- Interpretability-accuracy tradeoff: Higher-dimensional concepts may become increasingly difficult for humans to interpret meaningfully
