# Dish-TS: A General Paradigm for Alleviating Distribution Shift in Time Series Forecasting

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2302.14829
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2302.14829
- Reference count: 40
- This paper proposes Dish-TS, a general neural paradigm for alleviating distribution shift in time series forecasting, achieving more than 20% improvement on average across multiple datasets.

## Executive Summary
This paper addresses the critical challenge of distribution shift in time series forecasting, where the statistical properties of data change over time or differ between input and output spaces. The authors propose Dish-TS, a general paradigm that systematically categorizes distribution shifts into intra-space (within the input space) and inter-space (between input and output spaces) shifts. By employing a Dual-CONEt framework that separately learns these distributions, Dish-TS provides a robust approach to handling distribution shifts that can be integrated with existing forecasting models.

## Method Summary
Dish-TS introduces a coefficient net (CONET) that maps input sequences into learnable distribution coefficients, allowing the model to adapt to changing distributions over time. The framework incorporates a prior knowledge-induced training strategy to enhance the learning of CONET, making it more effective at capturing distribution characteristics. The Dual-CONEt architecture maintains separate distribution learning modules for input and output spaces, addressing both intra-space and inter-space shifts. This approach can be applied as a general enhancement to existing time series forecasting models, improving their robustness to distribution changes.

## Key Results
- Consistently improves state-of-the-art forecasting models by more than 20% on average across multiple datasets
- Effectively addresses both intra-space and inter-space distribution shifts in time series data
- Demonstrates robustness to various types of distribution changes that commonly occur in real-world time series applications

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
Dish-TS works by explicitly modeling the distribution shifts that occur in time series data through its Dual-CONEt framework. The coefficient net learns to map input sequences to distribution parameters that can adapt as the underlying data distribution changes. By maintaining separate distribution learning for input and output spaces, the method can capture complex relationships between observed and target distributions. The prior knowledge-induced training strategy helps guide the learning process toward more meaningful distribution representations, improving the model's ability to generalize across different distribution scenarios.

## Foundational Learning
- **Distribution Shift**: The phenomenon where statistical properties of data change over time or across different domains. Needed to understand the core problem being addressed; quick check: verify that distribution properties (mean, variance) change between training and testing periods.
- **Coefficient Net (CONET)**: A neural network component that maps input sequences to distribution parameters. Needed to understand how the model adapts to distribution changes; quick check: examine how CONET outputs change with different input distributions.
- **Intra-space vs Inter-space Shifts**: Two categories of distribution shifts - within the input space and between input/output spaces. Needed to grasp the theoretical framework; quick check: identify which type of shift is more prominent in your specific time series problem.

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component Map**: Input sequences -> Coefficient Net -> Distribution Coefficients -> Dual-CONEt modules -> Forecast outputs
**Critical Path**: Input sequences → CONET → Distribution learning modules → Forecast generation
**Design Tradeoffs**: The separate Dual-CONEt modules add computational overhead but provide better distribution modeling; the prior knowledge strategy improves learning but requires domain expertise
**Failure Signatures**: Performance degradation when distribution shifts are minimal or when shifts occur at multiple time scales simultaneously
**First Experiments**: 1) Test on synthetic data with controlled distribution shifts, 2) Apply to a dataset with known temporal distribution changes, 3) Compare with baseline models without distribution shift handling

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
The paper does not explicitly call out open questions, but several areas warrant further investigation: how the method performs with very subtle distribution shifts, the impact of different time scales of distribution changes, and the scalability of the approach to very large time series datasets with high dimensionality.

## Limitations
- The distinction between intra-space and inter-space shifts may not always be clear-cut in practice
- Computational overhead from maintaining separate distribution learning modules
- Limited analysis of failure cases and scenarios where the method may not perform well

## Confidence
- High confidence in the theoretical framework for categorizing distribution shifts
- Medium confidence in the effectiveness of Dual-CONEt given limited ablation studies
- Medium confidence in the 20% improvement claim, pending independent validation
- Low confidence in scalability assessments due to limited dataset diversity
