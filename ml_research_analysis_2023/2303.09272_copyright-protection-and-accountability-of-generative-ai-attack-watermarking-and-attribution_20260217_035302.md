# Copyright Protection and Accountability of Generative AI:Attack, Watermarking and Attribution

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID**: 2303.09272
- **Source URL**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.09272
- **Reference count**: 22
- **Key outcome**: The paper presents a comprehensive evaluation of IPR protection methods for GANs, showing adversarial attacks effectively disrupt outputs, watermarking works better for generation than translation models, and spectral attribution achieves high accuracy, but training set protection remains inadequate.

## Executive Summary
This paper evaluates three key intellectual property rights (IPR) protection methods for generative AI: adversarial attacks, watermarking, and attribution. The authors conduct experiments across diverse GAN architectures (StyleGAN variants, CycleGAN, StarGAN) and datasets (Horse2Zebra, CelebAHQ) to assess the effectiveness of these protection mechanisms. While adversarial attacks successfully disrupt GAN outputs and watermarking shows promise for generation models, the study reveals a significant gap in protecting training sets. The research highlights the need for unified evaluation metrics and further investigation into style transfer applications.

## Method Summary
The evaluation framework tests IPR protection methods across multiple GAN architectures including StyleGAN2, StyleGAN3, StyleSwin, DDGAN, CycleGAN, AttentionGAN-v2, CUT, StarGAN, and StarGANv2 using datasets like Horse2Zebra for translation tasks and CelebAHQ for generation tasks. Adversarial attacks employ Projected Gradient Descent with regularization coefficient 0.05 to measure output disruption via L1/L2 norms and FID scores. Watermarking is implemented using Yu et al.'s encoder-decoder method for dataset watermarking and Ong's backdoor approach for model watermarking, with bit accuracy as the evaluation metric. Attribution uses spectral analysis through Discrete Cosine Transform to create confusion matrices for model identification.

## Key Results
- Adversarial attacks effectively disrupt GAN outputs, particularly on high-resolution images, though less effective for style transfer tasks
- Watermarking proves more successful for generation models than translation models, with better bit accuracy in detection
- Attribution using spectral analysis achieves high accuracy in identifying GAN models from generated images
- Current IPR protection methods fail to provide robust protection for training sets (original images)

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: Adversarial perturbations effectively disrupt GAN outputs by introducing noise that alters the model's internal feature representations.
- Mechanism: Projected Gradient Descent (PGD) algorithm generates imperceptible adversarial noise that is added to input images, causing significant distortions in the GAN's output while preserving the input's visual appearance to humans.
- Core assumption: The adversarial noise remains within the model's decision boundary but shifts the output distribution enough to cause recognizable distortions.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "The results show that adversarial attacks can effectively disrupt GAN outputs, particularly on high-resolution images"
  - [section] "Table 1, the adversarial attack causes significant disruptions in outputs... FID [7] on the original output of the generative network and the output generated with adversarial input examples"
  - [corpus] "weak evidence" - No direct corpus neighbor mentions adversarial attacks specifically
- Break condition: When adversarial perturbations exceed the threshold of human perceptibility or when the GAN architecture has built-in robustness to such perturbations (e.g., through adversarial training).

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: Watermarking can embed ownership information into GAN models that can be later retrieved from generated content.
- Mechanism: Additional watermarking loss terms are added during GAN training, forcing the model to encode specific patterns or information into its outputs that can be detected by a decoder network.
- Core assumption: The watermark embedding process does not significantly degrade the GAN's generation quality while maintaining retrievability under various transformations.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "Watermarking proves more successful for generation models than translation models, with better bit accuracy in detection"
  - [section] "We utilized Yu's method [19] as a representative CNN-based watermarking technique... Figure 5 shows the result of dataset watermarking on different models"
  - [corpus] "weak evidence" - Corpus contains watermarking papers but none directly address GAN-specific watermarking methods
- Break condition: When the watermark is removed through model fine-tuning, pruning, or quantization, or when the detection threshold becomes too stringent for reliable retrieval.

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: Spectral analysis can attribute generated images to specific GAN architectures based on unique frequency domain fingerprints.
- Mechanism: Discrete Cosine Transform (DCT) extracts frequency domain features that are distinct for different GAN architectures, allowing classification through spectral imprints.
- Core assumption: Different GAN architectures produce systematically different frequency domain characteristics that remain consistent across various inputs and training conditions.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "Attribution using spectral analysis achieves high accuracy in identifying GAN models from generated images"
  - [section] "Following the approach and extending the scope outlined in Frank et al.'s work [6], we generated the confusion matrix for attribution spectral analysis"
  - [corpus] "weak evidence" - Spectral analysis papers exist but don't specifically address GAN attribution
- Break condition: When GAN architectures converge to similar frequency domain characteristics or when advanced GANs generate outputs that mask their spectral signatures.

- Failure signatures:
  - Adversarial attacks: Minimal FID increase but visually imperceptible changes
  - Watermarking: Low bit accuracy in detection or significant drop in image quality
  - Attribution: High confusion between similar GAN architectures in confusion matrix

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- Question: How effective are current adversarial attack watermarks in protecting style transfer applications compared to source characteristic preservation?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper states that adversarial attacks are "less effective in applications focusing on style transfer compared to source characteristic preservation in image-to-image translation."
- Why unresolved: The paper mentions the need for further investigation into the relevant loss to confirm the hypothesis about style reconstruction disruption, indicating that the current understanding is incomplete.
- What evidence would resolve it: Detailed analysis of the loss functions and their impact on style transfer versus source characteristic preservation, along with quantitative metrics comparing the effectiveness of adversarial attacks in these two areas.

### Open Question 2
- Question: What are the specific limitations of current watermarking methods in protecting the intellectual property rights of training sets?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper highlights that "IPR protection for training sets remains a prevalent concern" and that "current techniques for protecting the IPR of the training sets (the original copyrighted images) are inadequate."
- Why unresolved: The paper does not provide a detailed breakdown of why current watermarking methods fail to protect training sets, nor does it suggest potential solutions or improvements.
- What evidence would resolve it: Comprehensive studies identifying the weaknesses of existing watermarking techniques in the context of training sets, along with proposed methods to enhance protection.

### Open Question 3
- Question: How can a unified evaluation metric be developed to comprehensively assess the effectiveness of adversarial attack watermarks and IPR protection levels?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper underscores the importance of "developing a unified pipeline to assess the performance of adversarial attack watermarks and measure the IPR protection levels and accountability tracing of generative AI."
- Why unresolved: The paper indicates that current metrics exhibit limitations in accurately reporting distortions and that new metrics are needed, but it does not provide a framework or methodology for creating such unified metrics.
- What evidence would resolve it: A detailed proposal for a unified evaluation metric, including its components, how it addresses current limitations, and validation through experimental results across various GAN architectures and datasets.

## Limitations
- IPR protection for training sets remains largely unaddressed, with current methods proving inadequate for original image protection
- The effectiveness of adversarial attacks is reduced for style transfer applications compared to source characteristic preservation
- Current evaluation metrics exhibit limitations in accurately reporting distortions and measuring IPR protection levels

## Confidence
- **High confidence**: Attribution using spectral analysis is effective for identifying GAN models, with confusion matrices showing clear separation between different architectures
- **Medium confidence**: Watermarking shows better success rates for generation models compared to translation models, though the effectiveness varies significantly based on the specific architecture and dataset
- **Medium confidence**: Adversarial attacks effectively disrupt GAN outputs, particularly on high-resolution images, but their effectiveness is reduced for style transfer tasks
