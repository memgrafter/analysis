# Generalization Bounds for Magnitude-Based Pruning via Sparse Matrix Sketching

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2305.18789
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.18789
- Reference count: 40
- Key outcome: Proposes a compression-based generalization bound for magnitude-based pruning that depends on approximation error and parameter count, achieving bounds 10^7 times tighter than norm-based approaches on CIFAR10

## Executive Summary
This paper develops a new generalization bound for Magnitude-Based Pruning of overparameterized neural networks using a compression-based approach. The key insight is that pruning can be analyzed through its effect on the hypothesis space - the bound depends on both the approximation error introduced by pruning and the number of parameters in the pruned model. The authors introduce a novel pruning algorithm based on assuming trained weights follow a zero-mean Gaussian distribution, which induces small approximation error with high probability. They employ Sparse Matrix Sketching to efficiently represent pruned matrices as smaller dense matrices, dramatically reducing parameter count. Experiments on MNIST and CIFAR10 verify the improved bounds empirically, though the bounds remain vacuous in practical terms.

## Method Summary
The paper develops a compression-based generalization framework for magnitude-based pruning that combines two key components: (1) an approximation error analysis based on Gaussian weight assumptions that bounds the error introduced by pruning, and (2) a sparse matrix sketching technique that efficiently represents the space of pruned matrices using smaller dense matrices. This combination yields a generalization bound that scales with the number of parameters in the pruned model rather than the original overparameterized network. The framework is extended to Iterative Magnitude Pruning (Lottery Tickets) and validated empirically on simple architectures, showing significantly tighter bounds compared to standard norm-based approaches.

## Key Results
- Proposes a compression-based generalization bound for magnitude-based pruning
- Achieves bounds 10^7 times tighter than standard norm-based bounds on CIFAR10
- Introduces Gaussian weight assumption to bound approximation error from pruning
- Uses Sparse Matrix Sketching to efficiently represent pruned matrices
- Extends framework to prove generalization bound for Iterative Magnitude Pruning
- Empirically verifies improved bounds on MNIST and CIFAR10 datasets

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The compression-based approach works by quantifying how much the hypothesis space shrinks when weights are pruned. By bounding both the approximation error (how much the pruned model deviates from the original) and the compressed representation size (via sparse matrix sketching), the framework captures the true generalization capacity of the pruned model. The Gaussian weight assumption allows probabilistic control of approximation error, while the sketching technique provides an efficient parameterization that directly reflects the model's effective complexity.

- Gaussian concentration inequalities: needed to bound approximation error under weight distribution assumptions; quick check: validate the Gaussian assumption empirically on trained weights

## Architecture Onboarding

Component Map: Weight Pruning -> Approximation Error Bound -> Sparse Matrix Sketching -> Compressed Representation -> Generalization Bound

Critical Path: The critical path flows from the pruning decision through error analysis to the final bound. The Gaussian weight assumption enables tractable error bounds, which combine with the sketching efficiency to yield the final compression-based guarantee.

Design Tradeoffs: The framework trades the strong Gaussian assumption for tighter bounds, and computational efficiency (via sketching) for potentially looser representation accuracy. The choice of sketching method affects both the bound quality and practical efficiency.

Failure Signatures: Bounds may become vacuous if the Gaussian assumption is severely violated, if the sketching approximation error dominates, or if the pruned model retains too many parameters relative to its generalization gap.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
The paper does not explicitly call out open questions beyond noting that the bounds remain vacuous and some assumptions are strong. The work primarily focuses on establishing the theoretical framework and its initial empirical validation.

## Limitations
- Gaussian weight assumption represents a significant simplification that may not hold in practice
- Empirical verification relies on relatively simple architectures (MLP on MNIST, small CNNs on CIFAR10)
- The claimed 10^7 improvement over norm-based bounds stems from comparing against potentially loose baseline bounds rather than direct empirical comparison
- The bounds remain vacuous in practical terms, limiting immediate applicability
