# Improving Sentence Similarity Estimation for Unsupervised Extractive Summarization

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2302.12490
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2302.12490
- Reference count: 0
- The paper proposes two novel strategies to improve sentence similarity estimation for unsupervised extractive summarization: contrastive learning and mutual learning.

## Executive Summary
This paper addresses the challenge of unsupervised extractive summarization, where the goal is to rank sentences by salience without labeled data. The authors propose two novel strategies to improve sentence similarity estimation: (1) contrastive learning to optimize a document-level objective that sentences from the same document are more similar than those from different documents, and (2) mutual learning to enhance the relationship between sentence similarity estimation and sentence salience ranking using a Deep Differential Amplifier. Experiments on the NYT and CNNDM datasets show that the proposed method (SimBERT) achieves the best Rouge-1, Rouge-2, and Rouge-L scores compared to baselines like TF-IDF and SimCSE, demonstrating the effectiveness of the strategies.

## Method Summary
The proposed method, SimBERT, consists of two main components: a sentence encoder based on BERT and two novel strategies for improving sentence similarity estimation. The first strategy uses contrastive learning to optimize a document-level objective, encouraging sentences from the same document to have higher similarity than those from different documents. The second strategy employs mutual learning to enhance the relationship between sentence similarity estimation and sentence salience ranking, using an extra signal amplifier called the Deep Differential Amplifier. The salience scores are computed using degree centrality on a sentence similarity graph.

## Key Results
- SimBERT achieves the best Rouge-1, Rouge-2, and Rouge-L scores on the NYT and CNNDM datasets compared to baselines like TF-IDF and SimCSE.
- The proposed method demonstrates the effectiveness of the two novel strategies (contrastive learning and mutual learning) in improving sentence similarity estimation for unsupervised extractive summarization.
- SimBERT outperforms strong baselines like BERT, SimCSE, and BERTSUMEXTABS in terms of Rouge scores.

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: Mutual learning aligns sentence similarity estimation with sentence salience ranking through an auxiliary signal amplifier.
- Mechanism: A Deep Differential Amplifier (DDA) is trained to predict salient sentences using coarse-grained labels (top 40% salient), while the main model's similarity-based ranking is supervised by the DDA's fine-grained predictions (top 3 sentences). - Core assumption: There exists a learnable mapping from similarity-based rankings to actual salience rankings that can be captured by the DDA, and this mapping generalizes across documents.

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map:
  - Sentence Encoder (BERT-based) -> Contrastive Learning Module (document-level similarity objective) -> Degree Centrality Calculator (graph-based salience scoring) -> Deep Differential Amplifier (mutual learning partner) -> Training Loop (joint optimization of all components)

- Critical path:
  1. Input document -> Sentence Encoder -> Sentence embeddings
  2. Embeddings -> Contrastive loss computation (same vs. different document pairs)
  3. Embeddings -> Dot product similarity matrix -> Degree centrality -> Salience scores
  4. Salience scores -> DDA input -> DDA predictions -> Mutual learning supervision
  5. All losses combined -> Encoder update

- Design tradeoffs:
  - Using dot product vs. cosine similarity: Simpler computation but potential stability issues
  - Document-level contrastive objective vs. sentence-level: Captures broader context but requires careful batching
  - Mutual learning vs. direct supervision: Potentially better alignment but adds complexity and training instability risk

- Failure signatures:
  - Contrastive loss dominates other losses -> Embeddings become too discriminative, losing general semantic information
  - DDA predictions diverge from main model -> Mutual learning becomes counterproductive
  - Dot products become too large or too small -> Numerical instability in centrality calculation

- First 3 experiments:
  1. Verify contrastive loss behavior: Check that dot products increase for same-document pairs and decrease for different-document pairs during training
  2. Test mutual learning alignment: Compare salience rankings from main model vs. DDA at different training stages
  3. Ablation on similarity metric: Replace dot product with cosine similarity and measure impact on final ROUGE scores

## Limitations
- The exact implementation details of the Deep Differential Amplifier are not fully specified, making it difficult to assess whether the mutual learning component is truly essential or could be replaced with simpler alternatives.
- No ablations are provided to isolate the individual contributions of contrastive learning versus mutual learning to the final performance gains.
- The paper relies entirely on ROUGE scores without qualitative analysis of the generated summaries or human evaluation.

## Confidence
- High confidence in the basic framework: The overall approach of using contrastive learning for document-level similarity and mutual learning for alignment is technically sound
- Medium confidence in performance claims: While ROUGE improvements are reported, the lack of ablation studies and the strong dependence on hyperparameters create uncertainty about the magnitude of each component's contribution
- Low confidence in generalization: The method is tested only on NYT and CNNDM datasets, with no exploration of its performance on different domains or document types
