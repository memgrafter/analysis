# Decomposition and Interleaving for Variance Reduction of Post-click Metrics

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2306.10024
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.10024
- **Reference count:** 35
- **Primary result:** Efficiently compares post-click metrics of multiple rankings through decomposition and interleaving, reducing sample variance and improving evaluation accuracy.

## Executive Summary
This paper introduces a novel method for comparing post-click metrics across multiple rankings in online experiments. The approach decomposes post-click metric measurement into click model estimation and individual item post-click metric measurement, then uses interleaving to preferentially expose items with high population variance. This reduces sample variance and improves evaluation efficiency. Two stabilization techniques - variance prediction and systematic error correction - are introduced to further improve accuracy. The method demonstrates superior performance over existing approaches in both simulation and real service data, particularly when input rankings share many items.

## Method Summary
The method decomposes post-click metric measurement into two components: click model estimation and item-level post-click metric measurement. It then uses interleaving to preferentially expose items with high population variance, reducing the overall variance of ranking comparisons. The approach estimates item-level post-click metrics by aggregating samples across multiple rankings, and optimizes interleaved rankings to minimize total variance. Two stabilization techniques are applied: variance prediction using regression models to estimate item-level variances early in experiments, and systematic error correction to adjust for mismatches between assumed and actual click models.

## Key Results
- The proposed method outperforms A/B testing and traditional interleaving methods in efficiency and accuracy for post-click metric evaluation.
- Variance reduction is particularly effective when input rankings share many items, with significant improvements in binary error rates.
- The stabilization techniques (variance prediction and error correction) successfully improve evaluation accuracy and efficiency in both simulation and real service settings.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: Decomposing post-click metric measurement into click model estimation and item-level post-click measurement enables variance reduction by aggregating samples across rankings.
- Mechanism: The decomposition allows multiple rankings to share item-level post-click measurements, increasing sample size for each item. This reduces the variance of item-level estimates (/u1D449[/u1D438[/u1D465| /u1D451]]) by combining data from different rankings.
- Core assumption: Post-click behavior is independent of the ranking in which an item appears (/u1D438[/u1D465| /u1D451] is invariant across rankings).
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "The decomposition of the post-click metric measurement enables the free layout of items in a ranking and focuses on the measurement of the post-click metric of each item in the multiple rankings."
  - [section 4.1] "This decomposition can potentially achieve higher efficiency than A/B testing, because the samples for item /u1D451 in any rankings in /u1D445 can be used to estimate /u1D438[/u1D465| /u1D451]."

- Break condition: If post-click behavior depends on ranking context (e.g., item performance varies based on co-presented items), the decomposition assumption fails and variance reduction is compromised.

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: Interleaving rankings to preferentially expose items with high population variance reduces the variance of ranking comparisons.
- Mechanism: The DIRV method optimizes interleaved rankings to increase impressions for items with high variance, thereby increasing sample size for these items and reducing their estimation variance. This follows from the monotonically decreasing relationship between variance and sample size.
- Core assumption: The variance function /u1D719(/u1D45B/u1D456/u1D451, /u1D45B/u1D450/u1D451) decreases as sample sizes increase.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "The interleaving of multiple rankings reduces the sample variance of the items possessing a high population variance by optimizing a ranking to be presented to the users so that those items received more samples of the post-click metric."
  - [section 4.2] "The ranking /u1D45C minimizes the summation of /u1D449[/u1D438[/u1D465| /u1D45F/u1D456]] over all the rankings in /u1D445 when /u1D45C is exposed to a user."

- Break condition: If the variance function does not decrease monotonically with sample size (e.g., due to systematic biases that manifest with more data), the interleaving strategy becomes ineffective.

## Foundational Learning

- Concept: Variance reduction through sample aggregation
  - Why needed here: Understanding how combining samples across multiple rankings reduces estimation variance is fundamental to why DIRV works.
  - Quick check question: If an item appears in 3 different rankings with 10 impressions each, what is the approximate variance reduction compared to measuring it in only one ranking with 30 impressions?

- Concept: Interleaving and credit assignment
  - Why needed here: The interleaving mechanism assigns credit for user interactions to multiple rankings, which is non-trivial when evaluating post-click metrics.
  - Quick check question: How does the credit assignment differ between click-based metrics and post-click metrics in an interleaving framework?

  - Why needed here: The decomposition relies on separating examination probability from attractiveness, requiring understanding of position-based click models.

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map: 
  - Input rankings (/u1D445) -> Click model estimator -> Post-click metric estimator -> Variance predictor -> Ranking optimizer -> Error correction module -> Interleaved rankings

- Critical path:
  1. Receive input rankings
  2. Estimate click model parameters from interaction data
  3. Estimate item-level post-click metrics
  4. Predict item-level variances
  5. Generate interleaved ranking to minimize total variance
  6. Present ranking to users
  7. Collect feedback and update estimates

- Design tradeoffs:
  - Variance prediction vs. empirical variance: Using predicted variance helps early in experiments but may be inaccurate; empirical variance is more accurate but requires more data.
  - Click model complexity vs. estimation error: More complex click models may better capture user behavior but are harder to estimate accurately.
  - Exploration vs. exploitation: Need to balance presenting rankings to reduce variance vs. presenting them to estimate true preferences.

- Failure signatures:
  - High variance even with many impressions: Indicates poor variance reduction or violation of independence assumptions
  - Inconsistent preferences across runs: Suggests high estimation error or insufficient sample size
  - Error correction parameters not converging: Indicates systematic model mismatch

- First 3 experiments:
  1. Compare DIRV against A/B testing on synthetic data with known ground truth
  2. Test variance prediction effectiveness by comparing DIRV with and without variance prediction
  3. Validate error correction by introducing known systematic bias and measuring correction effectiveness

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- Question: How robust is the method to different types of click models beyond the cascade model assumed in the paper?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The authors mention that their theoretical results rely on the cascade click model assumption and note that "the simple assumption for the click model might sacrifice the evaluation error for efficiency." They also demonstrate an error correction technique to address potential mismatches between assumed and actual click models.
- Why unresolved: The paper primarily validates their method using a cascade click model and only briefly mentions the possibility of using other models like position-based click models. There is no comprehensive evaluation of how the method performs under different click model assumptions.
- What evidence would resolve it: Experiments comparing the performance of DIRV under various click models (cascade, position-based, cascade with continuation, etc.) on real-world datasets would provide insight into the robustness of the method.

### Open Question 2
- Question: What is the impact of item duplication rate on the performance of DIRV in real-world scenarios?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The authors note that "the performance was especially remarkable when the input rankings shared many items" and conduct experiments with varying item duplication rates in simulation. However, they don't explore this aspect in their real service setting.
- Why unresolved: The real service dataset used in the paper has a fixed item duplication rate, limiting the ability to assess how different duplication rates affect performance in practical scenarios.
- What evidence would resolve it: Conducting experiments with real service data where the item duplication rate varies across different queries or time periods would provide insights into how this factor influences the method's effectiveness.

### Open Question 3
- Question: How does the choice of variance prediction model affect the performance of DIRV in practice?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The authors propose using a regression model to predict item variances and show that this improves efficiency in simulation. However, they only use a simple gradient boosting model with limited features.
- Why unresolved: The paper doesn't explore the impact of using different types of variance prediction models (e.g., neural networks, different feature sets) or how model performance affects overall evaluation efficiency.
- What evidence would resolve it: Comparing DIRV's performance using different variance prediction models (varying complexity, feature sets, and algorithms) on real-world datasets would clarify the importance of model choice and potential improvements.

## Limitations

- The decomposition assumption that post-click behavior is independent of ranking context is critical but not extensively validated across diverse datasets and ranking scenarios.
- The effectiveness of variance prediction depends heavily on the quality of the regression model, but implementation details are underspecified.
- The systematic error correction assumes a relatively stable relationship between assumed and actual click models, which may not hold in dynamic environments.

## Confidence

- High confidence: The variance reduction mechanism through sample aggregation across rankings is theoretically sound and empirically supported.
- Medium confidence: The interleaving strategy effectively reduces variance in practice, though its optimality depends on accurate variance prediction.
- Medium confidence: The error correction technique improves accuracy, but its effectiveness may vary with the degree of model mismatch.
