# On Knowledge Editing in Federated Learning: Perspectives, Challenges, and Future Directions

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2306.01431
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.01431
- **Reference count:** 18
- **Primary result:** First comprehensive survey of knowledge editing (augmentation/removal) in federated learning, introducing Federated Editable Learning paradigm

## Executive Summary
This survey provides the first comprehensive overview of knowledge editing in federated learning, introducing the Federated Editable Learning (FEL) paradigm that integrates federated increasing learning and federated unlearning. The authors systematically classify existing methods into three main categories: federated continual learning (FCL) for addressing catastrophic forgetting, federated few-shot learning (FFsL) for extracting knowledge from limited data, and federated unlearning (FU) for selective amnesia to protect privacy. The survey covers verification and auditing mechanisms while identifying key challenges including catastrophic forgetting, data scarcity, high computational costs, and auditing needs. Future directions highlighted include flexible unified frameworks, knowledge disentanglement and reassembly, and trustworthy FL communities.

## Method Summary
The authors conducted a systematic survey of knowledge editing in federated learning by first establishing the FEL paradigm that unifies knowledge augmentation and removal. They classified existing approaches into three main categories: federated continual learning for handling new tasks without forgetting previous knowledge, federated few-shot learning for learning from limited data, and federated unlearning for selective knowledge removal. For federated unlearning specifically, they further categorized methods into exact approaches (model retraining-based) and approximate methods (gradient recovery, parameter updating, architecture modification, and noise perturbation). The survey methodology involved comprehensive literature review across these domains, analysis of technical challenges, and identification of future research directions.

## Key Results
- Introduced Federated Editable Learning (FEL) paradigm as the first unified framework for knowledge editing in FL
- Classified federated unlearning methods into exact (model retraining) and approximate (gradient recovery, parameter updating, architecture modification, noise perturbation) approaches
- Identified catastrophic forgetting, data scarcity, high computational costs, and auditing needs as primary challenges
- Proposed future directions including flexible unified frameworks and knowledge disentanglement techniques

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The survey works by providing a structured framework that connects knowledge editing operations with specific federated learning challenges. The FEL paradigm creates a unified perspective where knowledge can be both added and removed systematically. Federated continual learning addresses catastrophic forgetting through rehearsal-based and regularization-based approaches. Federated few-shot learning leverages limited data through meta-learning and metric-based methods. Federated unlearning provides mechanisms for selective knowledge removal through exact model retraining or approximate gradient-based approaches, ensuring privacy compliance and model adaptability.

## Foundational Learning
- **Federated Learning basics** - Why needed: Understanding the distributed learning paradigm where clients train models locally and share parameters with a central server
- **Catastrophic forgetting** - Why needed: Knowledge of how models lose previously learned information when trained on new tasks, critical for FCL methods
- **Knowledge unlearning principles** - Why needed: Understanding how to selectively remove information from trained models while maintaining performance on retained knowledge
- **Meta-learning concepts** - Why needed: Framework for few-shot learning where models learn how to learn from limited examples efficiently
- **Privacy-preserving mechanisms** - Why needed: Ensuring unlearning operations effectively protect sensitive information while maintaining model utility
- **Gradient-based optimization** - Why needed: Understanding how parameter updates work in federated settings for both learning and unlearning operations

Quick checks: Verify understanding of FL communication protocols, rehearsal buffer mechanisms, differential privacy concepts, and gradient accumulation methods.

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component Map:** Clients (local data, local models) -> Central Server (global model aggregation) -> Knowledge Editing Module (FCL, FFsL, FU operations)

**Critical Path:** Local training → Global aggregation → Knowledge editing → Model verification → Audit verification

**Design Tradeoffs:** Exact unlearning provides stronger guarantees but requires high computational resources; approximate methods are efficient but may have residual knowledge traces. Continual learning balances new task learning with retention of old knowledge. Few-shot methods trade off between data efficiency and model accuracy.

**Failure Signatures:** Catastrophic forgetting when FCL methods fail to preserve old knowledge; privacy breaches when FU methods don't completely remove sensitive information; performance degradation when knowledge editing disrupts model convergence.

**First Experiments:** 1) Test federated continual learning on sequential task learning benchmarks 2) Evaluate federated few-shot learning on limited data scenarios 3) Assess federated unlearning effectiveness through membership inference attacks
