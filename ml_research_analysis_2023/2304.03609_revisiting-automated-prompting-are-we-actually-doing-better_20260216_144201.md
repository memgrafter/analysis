# Revisiting Automated Prompting: Are We Actually Doing Better?

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2304.03609
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2304.03609
- Reference count: 40
- Automated prompt generation methods (AutoPrompt and Differential Prompt) do not consistently outperform simple manual prompts for few-shot learning with GPT-3.5

## Executive Summary
This paper challenges the prevailing assumption that automated prompt generation is necessary for effective few-shot learning with large language models. Through systematic comparison across six NLP datasets and various K-shot settings, the authors demonstrate that manually crafted prompts consistently match or exceed the performance of automated methods like AutoPrompt and Differential Prompt. The study reveals that automated prompts can sometimes perform worse than fine-tuning and may fail catastrophically on certain tasks, suggesting that the complexity of automated methods may not justify their use when simpler manual approaches often suffice.

## Method Summary
The authors conducted comprehensive experiments comparing three prompting approaches: manual prompt creation, AutoPrompt, and Differential Prompt. They evaluated these methods across six diverse NLP datasets using GPT-3.5 in multiple K-shot settings (ranging from few to more examples). The manual prompts were carefully crafted through an iterative process, while the automated methods used their original implementations with standard hyperparameters. Performance was measured against both fine-tuning baselines and across different data amounts to assess robustness. The study controlled for prompt template consistency while varying only the selection method, allowing direct comparison of the underlying prompt generation strategies.

## Key Results
- Manual prompts consistently matched or outperformed automated prompt generation methods across all six datasets
- Automated prompts sometimes performed worse than fine-tuning and exhibited catastrophic failures on specific tasks
- Manual prompts demonstrated superior robustness when varying the amount of training data available
- On several tasks, manual prompts achieved performance comparable to fine-tuning while being significantly simpler to implement

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The paper does not explicitly detail the mechanisms behind why manual prompts succeed, but the results suggest that human intuition in crafting prompts captures task-relevant linguistic patterns that automated methods miss or cannot optimize effectively within their search constraints.

## Foundational Learning

**Few-shot learning with LLMs**: Training models with minimal examples through prompt engineering. Needed because traditional fine-tuning requires extensive data and computational resources.

**Prompt optimization**: Systematic search for effective prompt formulations. Critical for understanding how prompt structure affects model performance.

**Manual vs automated prompt design**: Human-crafted prompts versus algorithmically generated ones. Important for evaluating whether automation provides meaningful advantages.

**GPT-3.5 architecture**: The specific model family used for evaluation. Relevant because results may not generalize to other model sizes or architectures.

**K-shot learning**: The number of examples used for demonstration in prompts. Fundamental for understanding few-shot learning paradigms and their limitations.

## Limitations
- Limited to six NLP datasets, potentially missing edge cases or task types where automated methods excel
- Focus exclusively on GPT-3.5, limiting generalizability to other model families or sizes
- Manual prompt creation process lacks detailed documentation of iteration criteria and evaluation methods
- Automated methods may not have received equal optimization opportunities compared to manual approaches

## Confidence
- Finding that manual prompts match/exceed automated methods: Medium (consistent results but limited task diversity)
- Recommendation to use manual prompts as baseline: Medium (solid empirical support but needs broader validation)
- Generalizability across model families: Low (only tested GPT-3.5)
