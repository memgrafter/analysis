# PED-ANOVA: Efficiently Quantifying Hyperparameter Importance in Arbitrary Subspaces

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2304.10255
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2304.10255
- Reference count: 40
- This paper introduces PED-ANOVA, a novel method for quantifying hyperparameter importance (HPI) in arbitrary subspaces of hyperparameter optimization (HPO) problems.

## Executive Summary
PED-ANOVA addresses the critical limitation of existing functional ANOVA (f-ANOVA) methods in analyzing specific subspaces of hyperparameter optimization problems. By reformulating f-ANOVA for local hyperparameter importance using a Lebesgue split to define subspaces based on performance thresholds, PED-ANOVA enables efficient analysis of high-performance regions. The method leverages Pearson divergence to compute hyperparameter importance in closed form, dramatically reducing computational requirements compared to Monte Carlo sampling approaches.

The key innovation lies in extending functional ANOVA to arbitrary subspaces while maintaining computational tractability. PED-ANOVA achieves this by exploiting the mathematical properties of Pearson divergence to approximate the importance measures without expensive sampling. This enables rapid analysis of hyperparameter importance in both global and local spaces, making it particularly valuable for identifying which hyperparameters drive performance in high-performing regions of the search space.

## Method Summary
PED-ANOVA reformulates functional ANOVA for local hyperparameter importance quantification using a Lebesgue split to define subspaces based on performance thresholds. The method employs Pearson divergence to efficiently compute HPI in closed form, avoiding computationally expensive Monte Carlo sampling. By leveraging the mathematical properties of Pearson divergence, PED-ANOVA approximates importance measures while maintaining computational efficiency. The approach enables analysis of arbitrary subspaces, including high-performance regions, with significantly reduced computational overhead compared to existing methods.

## Key Results
- Demonstrates accurate identification of important hyperparameters in both global and local spaces
- Achieves computational efficiency handling up to 10^8 data points in under a minute
- Shows performance superiority over existing methods requiring weeks for 10^5 data points on benchmark datasets
- Validated on toy functions and real-world datasets (JAHS-Bench-201)

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
PED-ANOVA works by reformulating functional ANOVA to handle arbitrary subspaces through Lebesgue splitting based on performance thresholds. The key mechanism involves using Pearson divergence to compute importance measures in closed form, which eliminates the need for expensive Monte Carlo sampling. This mathematical reformulation allows the method to efficiently analyze local subspaces, such as high-performance regions, while maintaining accuracy in identifying important hyperparameters.

## Foundational Learning
1. **Functional ANOVA (f-ANOVA)**: Decomposition of variance in function spaces to attribute importance to different variables
   - Why needed: Provides the theoretical foundation for quantifying hyperparameter importance
   - Quick check: Verify variance decomposition correctly captures main effects and interactions

2. **Lebesgue Split**: Mathematical technique for partitioning measure spaces based on performance thresholds
   - Why needed: Enables definition of arbitrary subspaces for local analysis
   - Quick check: Confirm subspace definitions correctly capture target regions

3. **Pearson Divergence**: Statistical measure of difference between probability distributions
   - Why needed: Provides computationally efficient approximation for importance quantification
   - Quick check: Validate divergence calculations match known importance structures

4. **Monte Carlo Sampling**: Traditional computational approach for estimating integrals and expectations
   - Why needed: Serves as baseline for comparing computational efficiency
   - Quick check: Compare sampling-based estimates with closed-form solutions

5. **Hyperparameter Importance (HPI)**: Quantification of how individual hyperparameters affect model performance
   - Why needed: Core objective of the method being developed
   - Quick check: Ensure importance rankings align with known sensitivity structures

## Architecture Onboarding

**Component Map:**
Response Surface -> Lebesgue Split -> PED Computation -> Importance Scores

**Critical Path:**
Data Collection → Response Surface Estimation → Subspace Definition → PED Calculation → Importance Quantification

**Design Tradeoffs:**
The method trades some approximation accuracy for dramatic computational efficiency gains. While Pearson divergence provides closed-form solutions, it may introduce small approximation errors compared to exact Monte Carlo methods. The tradeoff is justified by the ability to handle extremely large datasets and high-dimensional spaces that would be computationally intractable with sampling-based approaches.

**Failure Signatures:**
- Importance scores show unexpected patterns when response surfaces have discontinuities
- Computational benefits diminish when hyperparameter spaces have very few dimensions
- Accuracy degradation in subspaces with very few data points

**3 First Experiments:**
1. Validate importance quantification on synthetic functions with known sensitivity structures
2. Compare computational time and accuracy against Monte Carlo-based f-ANOVA on small datasets
3. Test subspace analysis capabilities on benchmark HPO datasets with established performance regions
