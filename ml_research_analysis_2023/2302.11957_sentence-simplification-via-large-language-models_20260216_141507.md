# Sentence Simplification via Large Language Models

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2302.11957
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2302.11957
- Authors: 
- Reference count: 19
- Key outcome: Large Language Models (LLMs) such as ChatGPT and GPT-3.5 can effectively perform sentence simplification, outperforming current state-of-the-art methods and achieving results comparable to human annotators.

## Executive Summary
This paper investigates the effectiveness of Large Language Models (LLMs) for sentence simplification, a task of rephrasing complex sentences into simpler ones while retaining meaning. The authors employ zero-shot and few-shot learning approaches with carefully crafted prompts to guide LLMs like ChatGPT and GPT-3.5 without requiring task-specific fine-tuning. They demonstrate that LLMs can achieve state-of-the-art performance on benchmark datasets for English, Portuguese, and Spanish, often surpassing specialized sentence simplification models and even matching human annotators in quality. The study highlights the strong multilingual generalization capabilities of LLMs and their proficiency in removing non-essential information while preserving semantic meaning.

## Method Summary
The authors use zero-shot and few-shot learning with GPT-3.5 and ChatGPT to perform sentence simplification. They design various prompt templates that frame the simplification task as a natural language understanding and generation problem. For few-shot learning, they provide one or more examples of complex-to-simple sentence transformations. The models are evaluated on multiple datasets: TURKCORPUS and ASSET (English), ASSET (Portuguese), and SIMPLEXT (Spanish). Automatic metrics including SARI (primary) and FKGL (English) or FRES (Spanish) are used to assess performance, along with human evaluation for subjective quality assessment.

## Key Results
- ChatGPT achieved new state-of-the-art performance on the ASSET dataset, improving SARI score by +3.44 points over previous best methods
- LLMs demonstrated strong multilingual generalization, outperforming specialized models on Portuguese and Spanish simplification tasks
- Human evaluation showed ChatGPT's simplifications were on par with human-written simplifications in terms of meaning preservation and simplicity
- LLMs excelled at deleting non-essential information and adding new information while maintaining semantic equivalence

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: LLMs excel at sentence simplification by leveraging their learned linguistic knowledge from massive pretraining corpora.
- Mechanism: The model implicitly learns simplification patterns (deletion, reordering, paraphrasing) during pretraining, which can be activated through carefully designed prompts without requiring task-specific fine-tuning.
- Core assumption: Pretraining on diverse text corpora encodes generalizable linguistic transformation capabilities that transfer to simplification tasks.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract]: "LLMs have demonstrated the ability to perform a variety of natural language processing tasks"
  - [section 4.2]: "LLMs boast a superior deletion score in comparison to other SS methods"
  - [corpus]: Weak evidence - no direct analysis of pretraining corpus composition or its relation to simplification capability
- Break condition: If the pretraining corpus lacks diverse simplification-relevant patterns, or if the simplification task requires domain-specific knowledge not covered in pretraining.

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: Prompt engineering enables zero-shot and few-shot learning for sentence simplification by providing explicit task guidance.
- Mechanism: Structured prompts with examples (few-shot) or clear instructions (zero-shot) guide the LLM to generate simplified outputs by framing the task as a natural language understanding and generation problem.
- Core assumption: LLMs can interpret natural language instructions and examples as task specifications, and their internal representations align with human notions of simplicity.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract]: "We empirically analyze the zero-/few-shot learning ability of LLMs"
  - [section 3]: "We test multiple prompts to analyze the results" and "we employ the {Guidance-Complex-Simple} mapping"
  - [section 4.5]: "prompt T1 surpasses prompt T2 in terms of efficacy for both Zero-Shot and Single-Shot SS"
- Break condition: If prompts cannot effectively communicate the simplification objective, or if the model fails to generalize from provided examples.

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: LLMs demonstrate strong multilingual generalization capabilities for sentence simplification due to their large-scale multilingual pretraining.
- Mechanism: The same LLM can be prompted in different languages, leveraging cross-lingual knowledge transfer from pretraining to perform simplification in languages not seen during fine-tuning.
- Core assumption: Multilingual pretraining creates shared representations across languages that enable transfer of simplification skills.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract]: "ChatGPT is a monolithic model capable of supporting multiple languages"
  - [section 4.2]: "ChatGPT outperform the SS results of MUSS-US on both Portuguese and Spanish testset"
  - [corpus]: Weak evidence - only tested on Portuguese and Spanish, limited analysis of cross-lingual transfer mechanisms
- Break condition: If the model's performance degrades significantly on low-resource languages or languages with different syntactic structures.

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map: Prompt templates → LLM API (ChatGPT/GPT-3.5) → Output processing → Automatic metrics (SARI, FKGL, FRES) → Human evaluation interface → Result aggregation
- Critical path: Prompt construction → LLM API call → Output processing → Metric calculation → Result aggregation and analysis
- Design tradeoffs: Zero-shot vs. few-shot prompting (simplicity vs. performance), prompt template design (specificity vs. generalization), language support (monolingual vs. multilingual)
- Failure signatures: Poor performance on complex syntactic structures, inability to maintain semantic equivalence, inconsistent output formatting, high computational costs for large-scale evaluation
- First 3 experiments:
  1. Test zero-shot prompting with the basic T1 template on a small subset of the English test set to verify basic functionality.
  2. Implement few-shot prompting with single reference examples and compare performance against zero-shot on the same subset.
  3. Evaluate multilingual prompting by translating the T1 template to Portuguese and running it on the Portuguese test set.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- Question: How do LLMs perform on sentence simplification tasks for languages other than English, Portuguese, and Spanish?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The authors mention that ChatGPT outperformed state-of-the-art methods in multilingual sentence simplification tasks for Portuguese and Spanish.
- Why unresolved: The paper only tested ChatGPT on three languages, so it is unclear how well it would perform on other languages.
- What evidence would resolve it: Conducting experiments to test ChatGPT's performance on sentence simplification tasks for various languages would provide insights into its multilingual generalization capabilities.

### Open Question 2
- Question: What is the impact of the number of manual simplified references on the performance of LLMs in sentence simplification tasks?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The authors mention that they tested the performance of LLMs with single and multiple manual simplified references, and observed differences in performance.
- Why unresolved: The paper does not provide a detailed analysis of the impact of the number of manual simplified references on the performance of LLMs.
- What evidence would resolve it: Conducting experiments with varying numbers of manual simplified references and comparing the performance of LLMs would provide insights into the impact of reference quantity on performance.

### Open Question 3
- Question: How do LLMs handle more complex sentence simplification tasks, such as those involving domain-specific jargon or highly technical content?
- Basis in paper: [inferred] The authors mention that LLMs excel at deleting non-essential information and adding new information while preserving meaning, but do not provide examples of complex sentence simplification tasks.
- Why unresolved: The paper does not provide examples of LLMs handling complex sentence simplification tasks, so it is unclear how well they would perform in such scenarios.
- What evidence would resolve it: Conducting experiments to test LLMs on sentence simplification tasks involving domain-specific jargon or highly technical content would provide insights into their ability to handle complex simplification tasks.

## Limitations

- The study's evaluation was limited to only Portuguese and Spanish beyond English, raising questions about performance on typologically distinct languages
- The research does not address computational costs or environmental impact of using large LLMs for simplification tasks
- Prompt engineering effectiveness may not generalize well to specialized domains or technical vocabulary without additional adaptation

## Confidence

**High Confidence**: The claim that LLMs can perform sentence simplification without task-specific fine-tuning is well-supported by extensive empirical results across multiple datasets and languages. The superiority of ChatGPT over previous state-of-the-art methods is consistently demonstrated.

**Medium Confidence**: The assertion that prompt engineering is the key driver of performance gains, while supported by the results, lacks ablation studies that would definitively prove this mechanism. The multilingual generalization capability, though impressive, is based on limited language coverage.

**Low Confidence**: The study's claims about the mechanism of how pretraining enables simplification capabilities are largely speculative, with minimal direct evidence linking pretraining corpus composition to simplification performance.

## Next Checks

1. **Cross-Lingual Generalization Test**: Evaluate the same prompt templates on a third, typologically distinct language (e.g., Finnish or Mandarin) to assess the limits of multilingual transfer.

2. **Domain Adaptation Analysis**: Test the zero-shot/few-shot approach on domain-specific simplification tasks (medical, legal, technical) to determine if prompt engineering alone suffices for specialized vocabulary.

3. **Cost-Benefit Assessment**: Quantify the computational resources required for LLM-based simplification versus traditional methods, including inference costs and latency, to evaluate practical feasibility.
