# Will Affective Computing Emerge from Foundation Models and General AI? A First Evaluation on ChatGPT

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2303.03186
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.03186
- Reference count: 26
- ChatGPT performs decently on affective computing tasks but not as well as specialized models trained for those tasks.

## Executive Summary
This paper evaluates ChatGPT's capabilities on three affective computing tasks: personality prediction, sentiment analysis, and suicide tendency detection. The study compares ChatGPT against specialized models (RoBERTa, Word2Vec, and Bag-of-Words) using zero-shot prompting without task-specific training. Results show ChatGPT provides decent performance, being comparable to simpler baselines but inferior to task-specific models. The evaluation reveals ChatGPT's robustness to noisy data and its effectiveness as a generalist model, though not optimal for specific affective computing applications.

## Method Summary
The study evaluates ChatGPT on three affective computing tasks using three datasets: FI dataset for personality (509 test examples), Sentiment140 for sentiment (359 examples), and Suicide and Depression dataset from Reddit (496 examples). ChatGPT was manually queried with specific prompt formats for each task, with responses parsed using regex. Three baselines were trained: RoBERTa-base fine-tuned for each task, Word2Vec with SVM, and Bag-of-Words with linear SVM. Hyperparameters for baselines were optimized using SMAC. Evaluation metrics included classification accuracy and Unweighted Average Recall (UAR), with statistical significance tested via randomized permutation tests.

## Key Results
- ChatGPT performs better than simple baselines (Word2Vec, BoW) on sentiment analysis but worse than specialized models (RoBERTa)
- ChatGPT shows robustness to noisy Twitter data that causes word-based models to perform worse
- Task-specific performance varies significantly: best on sentiment analysis, worst on personality assessment, average on suicide detection

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: ChatGPT performs decently on affective computing tasks but not as well as specialized models trained for those tasks.
- Mechanism: ChatGPT uses its general language understanding capabilities to interpret affective computing questions and provide responses, while specialized models have been explicitly trained on domain-specific data.
- Core assumption: The zero-shot or few-shot prompting approach can leverage general language knowledge for domain-specific classification tasks.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "ChatGPT provides decent results, and is relatively comparable to the Word2Vec and BoW baselines."
  - [section] "Results show that the RoBERTa trained for a specific downstream task generally has a superior performance."
- Break condition: If the task requires deep domain-specific knowledge or nuanced understanding that general language models haven't encountered during pretraining.

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: ChatGPT shows robustness to noisy data that causes specialized word-based models to perform worse.
- Mechanism: The subword tokenization and contextual understanding in ChatGPT allows it to handle noisy text (like Twitter data) better than static word embeddings.
- Core assumption: Subword tokenization provides resilience to typos and non-standard language in social media text.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "ChatGPT further shows robustness against noisy data, where Word2Vec models achieve worse results due to noise."
  - [section] "One of the potential reasons of the inferiority of Word2Vec and BoW on the sentiment dataset is not using subword encodings."
- Break condition: When noise levels exceed the subword tokenization's ability to reconstruct meaningful representations.

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: ChatGPT's performance varies significantly across different affective computing tasks.
- Mechanism: The effectiveness depends on how well the task aligns with patterns and knowledge present in ChatGPT's pretraining data.
- Core assumption: Different affective computing tasks have different levels of overlap with general language patterns in pretraining corpora.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "ChatGPT has the best performance in sentiment analysis, and poor performance on personality assessment, and average performance on suicide assessment."
  - [section] "The performance of ChatGPT on the personality assessment is inferior to the three baselines on all traits."
- Break condition: When task requirements diverge significantly from general language understanding.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- Question: Does ChatGPT's performance on affective computing tasks generalize to other domains beyond sentiment analysis, personality prediction, and suicide detection?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper evaluates ChatGPT on three specific affective computing tasks and concludes it is a generalist model but not as effective as specialized models. It suggests expanding experiments to more complex tasks in the future.
- Why unresolved: The current study only covers a limited set of tasks, and the authors explicitly call for further research to test ChatGPT's capabilities on a broader range of affective computing problems.
- What evidence would resolve it: Testing ChatGPT on additional affective computing tasks like sarcasm detection, metaphor understanding, and emotion recognition, and comparing its performance to specialized models on those tasks.

### Open Question 2
- Question: How do the computational costs and efficiency of ChatGPT compare to specialized models when performing affective computing tasks?

- What evidence would resolve it: Benchmarking future versions of ChatGPT or similar models against specialized models on the same tasks, after implementing suggested improvements in training or architecture.

### Open Question 4
- Question: How does ChatGPT's performance on affective computing tasks vary with different prompt formulations and preprocessing techniques?

- Why unresolved: The study uses a fixed set of prompts and does not explore how variations in prompt engineering might affect ChatGPT's performance or consistency across tasks.
- What evidence would resolve it: Systematic experimentation with different prompt structures, instructions, and examples to determine optimal prompt engineering strategies for affective computing tasks.

## Limitations

- Manual querying process with rate limits (25-35 requests/hour) makes large-scale evaluation impractical
- Evaluation only covers three specific affective computing tasks, limiting generalizability
- No statistical significance testing performed on ChatGPT vs. baseline comparisons

## Confidence

- High Confidence: ChatGPT performs better than simple baselines (Word2Vec, BoW) on sentiment analysis but worse than specialized models (RoBERTa)
- Medium Confidence: ChatGPT shows robustness to noisy data compared to word-based models, though this needs verification with more comprehensive noise testing
- Medium Confidence: Task-specific performance variations are real but the exact causes (data characteristics, prompt sensitivity) require further investigation
