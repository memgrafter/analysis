# On Logic-Based Explainability with Partially Specified Inputs

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2306.15803
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.15803
- Reference count: 40
- One-line primary result: Computing logic-based explanations (AXp/CXp) with partially specified inputs preserves complexity and duality while enabling smaller, more intuitive explanations.

## Executive Summary
This paper addresses the problem of computing logic-based explanations when input features are partially specified, i.e., some feature values are missing. The core method idea is to generalize existing algorithms for computing prime implicant (PI) explanations and contrastive explanations to handle partially specified inputs by treating unspecified features as universally quantified. The primary results show that the complexity of computing one AXp/CXp remains unchanged, and the duality between AXp's and CXp's is preserved. Experiments on public datasets illustrate novel explainability use cases, such as explaining predictions with missing values and detecting unrealistic predictions in decision trees. The method also enables computing smaller, more intuitive explanations.

## Method Summary
The paper extends existing algorithms for computing abductive (AXp) and contrastive (CXp) explanations to handle partially specified inputs. The key insight is to treat unspecified features as universally quantified, which allows the algorithms to compute explanations that are valid for all possible completions of the unspecified features. The method involves extending the feature space to include a distinguished value 'u' for unspecified features, and generalizing the classification function to map partially specified inputs to sets of target classes. The adapted algorithms use seeds that exclude unspecified features, and preserve the complexity and duality properties of the original algorithms.

## Key Results

- **Design tradeoffs**: The main tradeoff is between the size and interpretability of the explanations, and the complexity of the algorithms. Smaller explanations are more interpretable, but may be harder to compute.
- **Failure signatures**: Failure modes include incorrect handling of unspecified features, failure to preserve the duality between AXp's and CXp's, and incorrect use of the SAT solver.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- **Open Question 1**: Can the proposed framework be extended to handle non-monotonic classifiers? The paper mentions the possibility of generalizing to non-monotonic classifiers, but does not provide a concrete extension or discuss the potential challenges and limitations.
- **Open Question 2**: How does the choice of target classes affect the quality and interpretability of the explanations, and can we develop methods to automatically select the most informative target classes? The paper discusses the flexibility in choosing target classes, but does not provide a systematic analysis of the impact or propose methods for optimal selection.
- **Open Question 3**: Can the proposed framework be used to identify and mitigate potential biases in ML models, by analyzing the explanations obtained for different subgroups of the population with varying levels of feature specification? The paper mentions the potential use of logic-based explanations to reveal issues with induced ML models, but does not explicitly discuss the use of the framework for bias detection and mitigation.

## Limitations
- The reliance on a SAT solver for consistency checks, whose implementation details are unspecified.
- The limited empirical validation on a small number of datasets and classifiers.
- The potential challenges and limitations of extending the framework to non-monotonic classifiers and handling incompatible target classes.

## Confidence
- **High confidence**: The complexity of computing one AXp/CXp remains unchanged when handling partially specified inputs, and the duality between AXp's and CXp's is preserved.
- **Medium confidence**: The method enables computing smaller, more intuitive explanations for predictions with missing values, and can detect unrealistic predictions in decision trees.
- **Low confidence**: The generality of the approach to other types of classifiers beyond decision trees and the scalability to larger datasets with many unspecified features.
