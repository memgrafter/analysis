# Contrastive Language-Image Pretrained Models are Zero-Shot Human Scanpath Predictors

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2305.12380
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.12380

- Reference count: 32

## Executive Summary
This paper introduces NevaClip, a zero-shot method for predicting human visual scanpaths that combines CLIP models with biologically-inspired neural visual attention algorithms. The method generates scanpaths through gradient-driven exploration that aligns foveated visual stimuli with associated captions, outperforming existing unsupervised models in scanpath plausibility for both captioning and free-viewing tasks. By demonstrating that CLIP's pretrained semantic alignment can guide visual exploration without task-specific training, the work advances understanding of task-driven visual attention and enables more sophisticated computational approaches to scanpath prediction.

## Method Summary
NevaClip simulates human scanpaths by aligning the representation of foveated visual stimuli with associated captions using gradient-driven visual exploration. The method employs CLIP's visual encoder to maximize cosine similarity between foveated image embeddings and caption embeddings at each fixation point. A Gaussian foveation model mimics human retinal acuity falloff, creating biologically-plausible visual input. The algorithm operates in a zero-shot manner, requiring no task-specific training while generating scanpaths optimized for caption alignment through iterative fixation updates.

## Key Results
- NevaClip outperforms existing unsupervised models in scanpath plausibility for both captioning and free-viewing tasks
- Conditioning NevaClip with incorrect captions leads to random behavior, demonstrating the importance of accurate guidance
- The method achieves zero-shot performance by leveraging CLIP's pretrained semantic alignment between images and captions

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: The cosine similarity loss between CLIP image and text embeddings serves as a task-specific guidance signal that directs visual exploration to image regions semantically aligned with the caption.
- Mechanism: At each fixation, the method foveates the image around the current gaze location, encodes it with CLIP's image encoder, and compares the resulting embedding to the caption embedding via cosine similarity. The loss is backpropagated to update the next fixation location, effectively performing gradient-based optimization toward better caption alignment.
- Core assumption: CLIP's shared embedding space preserves semantic correspondence between images and captions such that maximizing similarity corresponds to attending to caption-relevant image regions.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "NevaClip simulates human scanpaths by aligning the representation of the foveated visual stimulus and the representation of the associated caption, employing gradient-driven visual exploration to generate scanpaths."
  - [section 3.3] "Let S be a visual stimulus and C be its associated caption. We use CLIP's visual encoder fI θ as the task model, where the downstream task is, at each step, to maximize the cosine similarity between the foveated image embedding and caption embeddings"
  - [corpus] Weak evidence: no neighbor papers explicitly describe this gradient-based alignment mechanism in CLIP.
- Break condition: If CLIP's image and text embeddings do not align semantically for the task domain, the guidance signal will mislead exploration, causing scanpaths to diverge from caption-relevant content.

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: The biologically-inspired foveation constraint ensures that the method's scanpaths resemble human-like visual exploration by limiting high-resolution perception to a small visual field.
- Mechanism: Each fixation produces a Gaussian-blurred representation of the image centered at the fixation point, combining high-resolution foveal input with low-resolution peripheral input, mimicking the human retina's acuity falloff.
- Core assumption: Human scanpaths are constrained by retinal acuity limits, and imposing this constraint on a computational model produces human-plausible scanpaths even without explicit training on human data.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "NevaClip simulates human scanpaths by aligning the representation of the foveated visual stimulus and the representation of the associated caption, employing gradient-driven visual exploration to generate scanpaths."
  - [section 3.2] "Let ξ = {ξt}t∈{1,...,N } be a sequence of fixation points...π (S,ξt) =Gσξ(ξt) ·S + (1 −Gσξ(ξt)) · ˜S"
  - [corpus] Weak evidence: while related work exists on foveation in scanpath prediction, no neighbor paper explicitly validates this specific biologically-inspired constraint.
- Break condition: If the Gaussian blur kernel parameters (σξ) are poorly chosen, the foveation will either be too narrow (missing relevant context) or too broad (losing the foveal advantage), degrading scanpath plausibility.

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: Zero-shot operation is enabled by CLIP's pretrained semantic alignment, eliminating the need for task-specific training data while maintaining performance.
- Mechanism: The method leverages CLIP's existing image-text alignment without fine-tuning, using the pretrained encoders to compute embeddings and similarity scores directly on the target dataset (CapMIT1003).
- Core assumption: CLIP's pretraining on large-scale image-text pairs generalizes to the captioning task domain, providing meaningful embeddings for novel images and captions without domain adaptation.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "We introduce NevaClip, a novel zero-shot method for predicting visual scanpaths that combines contrastive language-image pretrained (CLIP) models with biologically-inspired neural visual attention (NeVA) algorithms."
  - [section 3.3] "Let S be a visual stimulus and C be its associated caption. We use CLIP's visual encoder fI θ as the task model... This approach provides an efficient method for generating scanpaths that are optimized to minimize the distance between the foveated versions of the stimulus"
  - [corpus] No neighbor paper explicitly discusses zero-shot CLIP usage for scanpath prediction.
- Break condition: If the target domain (captioned images) differs substantially from CLIP's pretraining distribution, the embeddings will be misaligned, causing poor guidance and random scanpaths.

## Foundational Learning

- Concept: Contrastive learning and shared embedding spaces
  - Why needed here: Understanding how CLIP aligns images and text in a common feature space is essential to grasp why cosine similarity serves as an effective task guidance signal.
  - Quick check question: If two images have embeddings e1 and e2, and two captions have embeddings c1 and c2, what does a high cosine similarity between e1 and c1 (relative to e1 and c2) indicate about the semantic relationship?

- Concept: Gradient-based optimization in continuous parameter spaces
  - Why needed here: The method uses backpropagation to update fixation locations, requiring understanding of how gradients flow through the foveation and embedding operations.
  - Quick check question: Given a loss function L(ξ) that depends on fixation location ξ, what does the gradient ∇L(ξ) represent in the context of visual exploration?

- Concept: Foveated vision and Gaussian blurring
  - Why needed here: The biologically-inspired constraint requires understanding how to construct foveated stimuli by blending high and low-resolution image regions.
  - Quick check question: If a Gaussian kernel with standard deviation σ is applied to an image, what happens to spatial frequency content at scales much smaller than σ?

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map: CLIP model -> Gaussian foveation module -> Cosine similarity loss -> Gradient optimization loop -> Scanpath sequence

- Critical path:
  1. Load image and associated caption
  2. Initialize fixation at image center
  3. For each of N fixations:
     - Apply Gaussian foveation centered at current fixation
     - Encode foveated image with CLIP image encoder
     - Compute cosine similarity with caption embedding
     - Backpropagate loss to update fixation location
  4. Output scanpath sequence

- Design tradeoffs:
  - Zero-shot vs. fine-tuned: Avoids annotation cost but may underperform on domain-shifted data
  - Fixed Gaussian blur vs. learned foveation: Simpler implementation but less adaptive to task
  - Markovian vs. history-dependent: Computational efficiency vs. potential for richer exploration strategies
  - Number of optimization steps M: More steps improve accuracy but increase computation time

- Failure signatures:
  - Random or center-biased scanpaths indicate broken CLIP alignment or incorrect caption conditioning
  - Extremely rapid fixation changes suggest learning rate α too high
  - Scanpaths stuck in local regions indicate loss landscape issues or insufficient exploration
  - Performance drop with ViT vs. ResNet may indicate backbone sensitivity

- First 3 experiments:
  1. Verify cosine similarity gradients flow correctly by checking fixation updates on a simple synthetic image-caption pair
  2. Test different Gaussian blur standard deviations (σξ) to find optimal foveation scale for human-like scanpaths
  3. Compare scanpath plausibility when conditioning on correct vs. random captions to validate guidance mechanism

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- Question: How do different CLIP model architectures (e.g., ResNet vs. ViT) impact the quality of generated scanpaths for different types of images?
  - Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper compares ResNet50, ResNet101, and ViT-B/32 backbones for NevaClip, showing performance differences
  - Why unresolved: The paper only provides aggregate performance metrics across all images without analyzing how different architectures perform on specific image types or content categories
  - What evidence would resolve it: A detailed breakdown of scanpath plausibility scores by image type (e.g., natural scenes, faces, objects, text-heavy images) for each CLIP backbone

- Question: What is the relationship between caption length/complexity and scanpath plausibility when using captions as guidance?
  - Basis in paper: [inferred] The paper shows that conditioning with random captions leads to random behavior, but doesn't analyze how caption characteristics affect performance
  - Why unresolved: The paper only tests with "correct" captions versus random captions, without exploring how caption properties (length, vocabulary complexity, descriptive detail) influence the generated scanpaths
  - What evidence would resolve it: An analysis correlating caption linguistic features (word count, syntactic complexity, semantic density) with scanpath plausibility scores

- Question: How does the number of fixations in the generated scanpath relate to caption-guided performance?
  - Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper mentions humans clicked up to ten times and shows results for different subpath lengths (k=1 to 10)
  - Why unresolved: While the paper shows scanpath plausibility decreases with more fixations, it doesn't explain whether this is due to task complexity or limitations in the guidance mechanism
  - What evidence would resolve it: A systematic study varying the number of fixations allowed and analyzing the trade-off between exploration completeness and scanpath plausibility for different caption guidance strategies

## Limitations
- Zero-shot performance depends heavily on CLIP's generalization to the captioning domain, which wasn't thoroughly tested across diverse datasets
- The biologically-inspired foveation constraint lacks empirical validation showing that the chosen Gaussian parameters produce truly human-like visual behavior
- The comparison with supervised models is limited, making it unclear whether the zero-shot advantage justifies potential performance gaps in real-world applications

## Confidence
- **High Confidence**: The core mechanism of using CLIP cosine similarity as a guidance signal is well-founded, given CLIP's established performance on image-text alignment tasks. The scanpath plausibility results on both CapMIT1003 and MIT1003 datasets are reproducible and statistically significant.
- **Medium Confidence**: The zero-shot performance claims are credible but require broader domain testing. The comparison with unsupervised baselines is robust, but the absence of supervised comparisons limits generalizability claims.
- **Low Confidence**: The biological plausibility of the foveation parameters and their optimization remains speculative without psychophysical validation or systematic parameter sensitivity analysis.

## Next Checks
1. **Cross-domain robustness test**: Evaluate NevaClip on datasets from different domains (e.g., medical imaging, satellite imagery) to assess CLIP's generalization limits and identify failure modes in domain-shifted scenarios.

2. **Parameter sensitivity analysis**: Systematically vary the Gaussian foveation standard deviation (σξ) and learning rate (α) across multiple orders of magnitude to identify optimal ranges and understand how parameter choices affect scanpath plausibility and biological plausibility.

3. **Ablation study with supervised fine-tuning**: Fine-tune CLIP on the CapMIT1003 dataset and compare performance against zero-shot NevaClip to quantify the trade-off between annotation cost and scanpath prediction accuracy, providing clearer guidance for practical deployment decisions.
