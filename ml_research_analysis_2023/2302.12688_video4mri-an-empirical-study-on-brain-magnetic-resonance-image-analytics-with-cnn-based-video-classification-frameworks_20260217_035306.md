# Video4MRI: An Empirical Study on Brain Magnetic Resonance Image Analytics with CNN-based Video Classification Frameworks

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2302.12688
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2302.12688
- Reference count: 0
- Primary result: Video recognition frameworks achieve 86.6% accuracy on Parkinson's data, outperforming traditional 3D CNNs by 5-11% while using 50-66% fewer parameters

## Executive Summary
This study explores the application of video recognition frameworks to brain MRI classification, treating MRI slices as video frames to leverage temporal modeling capabilities. The research applies advanced 2D CNN models like TSM and SlowFast to detect Alzheimer's and Parkinson's diseases from MRI data. The work demonstrates that video classification techniques can effectively process the spatial-temporal structure of MRI data, providing a more efficient alternative to conventional 3D approaches while maintaining or improving diagnostic accuracy.

## Method Summary
The research adapts video recognition frameworks for brain MRI classification by treating 3D MRI volumes as sequences of 2D slices, analogous to video frames. The study employs Temporal Shift Module (TSM) and SlowFast architectures, originally designed for video action recognition, and applies them to MRI datasets for Alzheimer's and Parkinson's disease detection. Data augmentation techniques from video processing, including rotation and CutMix, are incorporated to enhance model performance. The experimental setup compares these video-based approaches against traditional 3D CNNs across multiple datasets.

## Key Results
- Video models achieve 86.6% accuracy on Parkinson's data, outperforming traditional 3D CNNs by 5-11%
- TSM with ResNet-50 demonstrates optimal performance with faster training and inference times
- Data augmentation techniques improve classification accuracy by approximately 10%

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The approach leverages the inherent spatial-temporal structure of MRI data by treating sequential slices as video frames. This allows video recognition frameworks to capture both spatial features within individual slices and temporal relationships across slices, effectively modeling the 3D anatomical structure through 2D operations. The temporal modeling capabilities of video frameworks can potentially capture disease-related patterns that vary across slices, while the parameter efficiency comes from reusing 2D convolutional operations rather than processing full volumetric data.

## Foundational Learning
- MRI slice sequencing: Understanding how 3D volumes are represented as sequential 2D slices is crucial for proper data preprocessing
- Temporal Shift Module (TSM): Lightweight mechanism for capturing temporal information in video frames that can be applied to slice sequences
- 2D vs 3D CNN architectures: Knowledge of computational efficiency differences between volumetric and sequential processing approaches
- Video data augmentation: Techniques like rotation and CutMix that can enhance model robustness and generalization
- Spatial-temporal feature extraction: How temporal relationships between slices can reveal anatomical and pathological patterns

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component Map:** MRI slices -> Video frame preprocessing -> TSM/SlowFast backbone -> Classification head -> Disease prediction
**Critical Path:** Data preprocessing and slice sequencing -> Temporal modeling through TSM or SlowFast -> Feature aggregation -> Classification output
**Design Tradeoffs:** 2D efficiency vs 3D volumetric completeness; temporal modeling vs explicit 3D spatial relationships; parameter efficiency vs potential information loss
**Failure Signatures:** Poor performance on datasets with varying slice thickness; sensitivity to slice orientation changes; potential loss of 3D anatomical context
**First Experiments:** 1) Test baseline 2D CNN without temporal modeling; 2) Compare different video architectures (TSM vs SlowFast); 3) Evaluate impact of slice ordering and preprocessing variations

## Limitations
- Treatment of MRI slices as video frames may not fully capture critical three-dimensional anatomical relationships
- Lack of analysis on model robustness to variations in slice thickness, orientation, and acquisition protocols
- Limited comparison of temporal modeling against hybrid 2.5D approaches that explicitly account for inter-slice relationships

## Confidence

- TSM with ResNet-50 offering optimal performance with faster training: High confidence
- Data augmentation improving accuracy by ~10%: Medium confidence
