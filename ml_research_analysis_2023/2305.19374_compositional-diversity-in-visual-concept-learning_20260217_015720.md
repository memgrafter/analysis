# Compositional diversity in visual concept learning

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2305.19374
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.19374
- Authors: 
- Reference count: 18
- Primary result: Bayesian program induction best matched human classification judgments and generation patterns, with neuro-symbolic models capturing additional behavioral nuances.

## Executive Summary
This paper investigates how humans learn compositional visual concepts from few examples using a novel alien figures domain. Participants demonstrated inductive biases like orientation invariance and "complete-the-pattern" tendencies when classifying and generating these figures after seeing only a few exemplars. The authors developed a Bayesian program induction model that searches for structured generative programs to explain exemplars, outperforming lesioned versions and exemplar models on human data. To capture additional behavioral patterns, they introduced a generative neuro-symbolic model combining symbolic program control with neural modules, which better matched human generation behavior and captured key inductive biases.

## Method Summary
The study used synthetic alien figures composed of shape primitives in various compositional configurations. Two behavioral experiments were conducted: a few-shot classification task where participants categorized test figures after seeing exemplars, and a generation task where participants created new examples from exemplars. The primary computational approach was Bayesian program induction using a probabilistic context-free grammar to represent concepts as programs. Alternative models included lesioned Bayesian variants and exemplar models. A generative neuro-symbolic model was also developed, combining symbolic program control with neural network modules for flexible statistical learning. Models were evaluated against human responses using correlation for classification and log-likelihood for generation.

## Key Results
- Bayesian program induction best matched human classification judgments compared to lesioned versions and exemplar models
- The neuro-symbolic GNS model outperformed the Bayesian model on held-out human generation data
- Fitted Bayesian parameters revealed interpretable human assumptions about compositionality and inductive biases

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: Bayesian program induction enables strong generalization by searching for the most probable generative programs that explain observed exemplars.
- Mechanism: The model defines a hypothesis space of programs using a probabilistic grammar, and computes posterior probabilities using Bayes' rule to select the best programs for classification and generation.
- Core assumption: Human concept learning follows Bayesian inference, where people evaluate programs based on prior simplicity and likelihood of generating the observed data.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "The authors developed a Bayesian program induction model that searches for structured generative programs to explain exemplars"
  - [section 1.2.1] "Under the Bayesian program induction framework, learning a new visual concept amounts to a search for the best programs for explaining the examples"
  - [corpus] Weak evidence - no direct neighbor paper addresses Bayesian program induction specifically.
- Break condition: If human learners do not follow Bayesian inference principles, or if the hypothesis space is too restrictive to capture the relevant concepts.

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: Neuro-symbolic modeling captures additional behavioral patterns by combining symbolic program control with neural modules for statistical structure.
- Mechanism: The generative neuro-symbolic (GNS) model uses neural networks to learn flexible statistical relationships between parts and tokens, going beyond the rigid parametric assumptions of the Bayesian model.
- Core assumption: Human concept learning involves both structured symbolic representations and flexible statistical learning that cannot be fully captured by symbolic models alone.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "To capture additional behavioral nuances missed by the Bayesian model, they introduced a generative neuro-symbolic (GNS) model combining symbolic program control with neural modules"
  - [section 3.1.2] "Our GNS decoder (Fig. 8, right) generates new tokens by sampling a sequence of symbolic primitives which together specify a unique instance of an alien figure concept"
  - [corpus] Moderate evidence - neighbors like "Compositional Generative Modeling: A Single Model is Not All You Need" suggest hybrid approaches are valuable.
- Break condition: If the neural components overfit to the training data or fail to generalize to new concept types.

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: Meta-learning enables effective training of GNS models by exposing them to diverse episodes of support-query pairs.
- Mechanism: The GNS model is trained using episodes where it must generate new tokens given support exemplars, learning patterns that connect queries to support through repeated exposure.
- Core assumption: Learning to learn from few examples is a key aspect of human concept learning that can be captured through meta-learning.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [section 3.2] "As with any statistical estimator that uses neural networks, our GNS model calls for a sizeable training dataset to avoid overfitting and ensure adequate generalization"
  - [section 3.2] "Figure 11. Meta-learning episodes. Each episode consists of 1) a support set X of 1-6 examples that demonstrate the concept, and 2) a query set of additional tokens for evaluation"
  - [corpus] Weak evidence - no direct neighbor paper addresses meta-learning for concept generation specifically.
- Break condition: If the meta-learning episodes do not adequately represent the diversity of human concept learning tasks.

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map: Probabilistic grammar → MCMC inference → program selection (Bayesian model); CNN encoder → transformer aggregator → neural network subroutines (GeneratePart, GenerateRelation, GenerateOrientation, Terminate) → symbolic renderer (GNS model)
- Critical path: For few-shot generation, the critical path is: encode support exemplars → generate new token one part at a time using neural subroutines → terminate when complete. Each neural subroutine conditions on current program state and support embedding.
- Design tradeoffs: The Bayesian model offers interpretability and strong compositional generalization but struggles with flexible statistical patterns. The GNS model captures more behavioral nuances but requires more training data and is less interpretable. The meta-learning approach enables effective training but relies on synthetic data that may not fully capture human behavior.
- Failure signatures: Bayesian model fails when concepts require flexible statistical learning or when the hypothesis space is too restrictive. GNS model fails when it overfits to training data or cannot generalize to new concept types. Both models may struggle with concepts that involve complex functional relationships beyond visual composition.
- First 3 experiments:
  1. Implement the Bayesian program induction model with a simple probabilistic grammar and test on a small set of alien figure concepts.
  2. Extend the Bayesian model to handle more complex concepts by expanding the grammar and testing on a wider range of composition types.
  3. Implement the GNS model with neural network subroutines and test on the same concepts, comparing performance to the Bayesian model and analyzing failure modes.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- Question: How do human visual concept learning strategies differ between classification and generation tasks, and what factors drive these differences?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper reports divergence in behavioral patterns between categorization and generation experiments, with participants showing distinct inductive biases like "complete-the-pattern" only in generation tasks.
- Why unresolved: The paper notes these differences but does not provide a complete theoretical account of why participants behave differently across tasks or what additional reasoning processes might be involved.
- What evidence would resolve it: Detailed analysis of participant behavior patterns in both tasks, possibly including eye-tracking or think-aloud protocols to understand decision-making processes.

### Open Question 2
- Question: How sensitive are humans to visual "motifs" or gestalt patterns in composition, and how does this sensitivity influence their generalization behavior?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper presents preliminary evidence that participants show sensitivity to symmetrical and visually salient compositions, generating more tokens consistent with the underlying concept when exemplars contain familiar forms.
- Why unresolved: While the paper observes this phenomenon, it does not quantify the extent of this sensitivity or how it interacts with other factors in visual concept learning.
- What evidence would resolve it: Systematic manipulation of visual salience in stimuli and measurement of its effect on human generalization behavior across different concept types.

### Open Question 3
- Question: Can the Generative Neuro-Symbolic (GNS) model be trained directly on human behavior data without relying on Bayesian model-generated synthetic data?
- Basis in paper: [inferred] The paper notes that the current GNS model relies on synthetic data from the Bayesian model for training, and suggests that scaling up the human experiment could allow direct learning from human behavior.
- Why unresolved: The current dataset of 155 trials is insufficient for training the neural network components, and the paper does not explore alternative approaches to data collection or model architecture.
- What evidence would resolve it: A larger-scale human experiment with sufficient trials to train the GNS model directly, or development of alternative model architectures that require less training data.

## Limitations

- Behavioral experiments used a novel alien figures domain that may not generalize to natural visual concepts
- Meta-learning approach depends on synthetic data generation that may not perfectly match human concept structures
- The GNS model requires substantial training data and is less interpretable than the Bayesian approach

## Confidence

- High confidence: Bayesian program induction best matched human classification judgments
- Medium confidence: GNS models captured additional behavioral nuances missed by Bayesian models
- Medium confidence: Fitted Bayesian parameters revealed interpretable human inductive biases
