# GANs and alternative methods of synthetic noise generation for domain adaption of defect classification of Non-destructive ultrasonic testing

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2306.01469
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.01469
- Reference count: 40
- This work improves defect classification F1 scores from 0.394 (pure simulation) to 0.843 using CycleGAN for synthetic data augmentation.

## Executive Summary
This paper addresses the challenge of insufficient training data for deep learning in non-destructive ultrasonic testing of composites by developing four synthetic data generation methods. Physics-based simulations of defect responses lack experimental noise, reducing classifier performance. The authors evaluate a modified CycleGAN architecture alongside three alternative noise simulation approaches to augment simulated data. The methods are tested using a CNN optimized via genetic algorithm, trained on synthetic data and evaluated on real experimental images.

## Method Summary
The study uses simulated ultrasonic defect responses from CIVA software combined with experimental ultrasonic data from composite samples with flat-bottom holes. Four synthetic data generation methods are implemented: (1) a modified CycleGAN that maps simulated to experimental noise profiles using mid-cycle activation map loss, (2) superposition of real clean experimental images with simulated defects, (3) fully simulated C-scan noise, and (4) fully simulated A-scan noise. Each method is evaluated by training a CNN optimized via genetic algorithm on the synthetic data and testing on real experimental images, with performance measured by F1 score, precision, recall, and accuracy.

## Key Results
- CycleGAN approach achieved highest F1 score of 0.843
- A-scan noise simulation method achieved F1 score of 0.738 with better scalability
- Real noise superposition method achieved F1 score of 0.688
- C-scan noise simulation method achieved F1 score of 0.629
- All synthetic methods significantly outperformed pure simulation baseline (0.394 F1)

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: A modified CycleGAN can learn to map simulated ultrasonic defect responses to experimentally realistic noise profiles while preserving defect signal integrity.
- Mechanism: The modified CycleGAN introduces a mid-cycle activation map loss that focuses gradient updates on defect response regions, preventing mode collapse and maintaining defect signal fidelity while allowing flexible noise synthesis.
- Core assumption: The defect response in simulated data is spatially distinct from background noise and can be isolated via normalized activation maps without losing critical information.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "A modified CycleGAN architecture to learn the mapping from physics-based simulations of defect indications to experimental indications"
  - [section] "the introduction of a mid-cycle activation map loss... help to encourage accurate defect signal reproduction whilst allowing for the addition of experimental noise"
  - [corpus] Weak evidence - no similar GAN-based defect-to-noise mapping found in neighbor papers
- Break condition: If the defect response is not spatially separable from background noise, the activation map normalization will fail and defect information will be lost.

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: Combining real clean experimental images with simulated defect responses preserves realistic noise distribution while maintaining defect accuracy.
- Mechanism: Pixel-level summation of clean experimental noise patterns with simulated defect responses creates hybrid images where noise characteristics match experiments while defect geometry remains simulation-accurate.
- Core assumption: The noise distribution in clean experimental images is independent of defect presence and can be superimposed onto defect responses without interaction effects.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [section] "The second method was based on combining real experimental defect free images with simulated defect responses"
  - [section] "The experimental data required is from defect-free images which are more accessible and considerably easier to acquire"
  - [corpus] Weak evidence - neighbor papers discuss data augmentation but not this specific clean+defect combination approach
- Break condition: If experimental noise has depth-dependent characteristics that interact with defect signals, simple pixel summation will produce unrealistic artifacts.

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: Simulating noise at the A-scan level captures both structural and random noise components that occur physically in ultrasonic testing.
- Mechanism: The method separates structural noise (from ply interactions) and random noise components, models their distributions from experimental data, then generates realistic noise patterns that are added to simulated A-scans before C-scan formation.
- Core assumption: Structural noise patterns remain constant within B-scans while random noise varies per A-scan, allowing statistical modeling and generation.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [section] "An approach of fully generating a simulated noise profile at an A scan level was also investigated which is better aligned to how noise occurs from the physical response of ultrasonic signals"
  - [section] "For each A scan in each B scan, it is then possible to work out the random noise component from the differences between each A scan and the structural noise component"
  - [corpus] Weak evidence - no neighbor papers address A-scan level noise simulation in composites
- Break condition: If the structural noise assumption fails (e.g., noise varies significantly within B-scans), the statistical model will not capture realistic noise patterns.

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map:
  - Data generation pipeline: Simulation → Processing → Synthetic augmentation
  - Model training pipeline: Dataset preparation → CNN training with HPO → Evaluation
  - Synthetic generation modules: CycleGAN, noise superposition, C-scan noise, A-scan noise
  - Analysis tools: Guided Grad-CAM for interpretability, confusion matrix analysis

- Critical path:
  1. Generate or obtain simulated defect responses
  2. Process experimental data to create clean noise patterns
  3. Apply chosen synthetic generation method
  4. Train CNN with HPO on synthetic data
  5. Evaluate on experimental test set
  6. Analyze results with Grad-CAM

- Design tradeoffs:
  - CycleGAN: High classification performance but complex training and limited scalability
  - Real noise superposition: Simple but requires experimental clean images
  - C-scan noise: Fully simulated but depth-agnostic noise patterns
  - A-scan noise: Most physically accurate but computationally intensive

- Failure signatures:
  - Mode collapse in CycleGAN (all outputs look identical)
  - Classification performance below 0.5 F1 score (random guessing)
  - Grad-CAM showing attention on non-defect regions
  - Noise patterns that obscure defect responses completely

- First 3 experiments:
  1. Train baseline CNN on pure simulated data and verify poor performance (~0.4 F1)
  2. Implement and test CycleGAN with mid-cycle activation map loss on a small dataset
  3. Compare classification performance of all four synthetic methods on the same experimental test set

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- Question: How does the proposed CycleGAN architecture generalize to different defect types beyond Flat-Bottom Holes?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The authors note that the CycleGAN approach showed the greatest improvement in classification performance but acknowledge that it may struggle to generalize to other defect types without significantly broader examples in training.
- Why unresolved: The study only evaluated the CycleGAN on Flat-Bottom Holes, and the complexity of naturally occurring defects was not addressed.
- What evidence would resolve it: Testing the CycleGAN on a diverse set of defect types and evaluating its classification performance across these types would provide evidence of its generalization capabilities.

### Open Question 2
- Question: Can the synthetic data generation methods be extended to three-dimensional volumetric data analysis?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The authors mention that the A-scan noise approach allows for noising of the full volumetric data, which could provide opportunities in three-dimensional data analysis.
- Why unresolved: The study only evaluated the methods on two-dimensional C-scan images, and the potential for three-dimensional analysis was not explored.
- What evidence would resolve it: Applying the synthetic data generation methods to three-dimensional volumetric data and evaluating their impact on classification performance would demonstrate their potential for 3D analysis.

### Open Question 3
- Question: How can the synthetic data generation methods be optimized for scalability and computational efficiency?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The authors discuss the scalability of different methods, noting that the real noise approach is superseded by the A-scan and C-scan synthetic approaches due to their greater robustness and computational efficiency.
- Why unresolved: The study does not provide a detailed comparison of the computational costs and scalability of the different methods, nor does it explore optimization techniques.
- What evidence would resolve it: Conducting a thorough analysis of the computational complexity and scalability of each method, along with exploring optimization techniques, would provide insights into their efficiency and scalability.

## Limitations
- Modified CycleGAN implementation details are not fully specified beyond the mid-cycle activation map loss concept
- Physical validity of noise simulation methods relies on assumptions about noise independence and distribution stability
- Genetic algorithm-based CNN optimization may not have found the global optimum due to fixed population size and iteration limits

## Confidence
- **High**: Classification performance improvements over baseline simulation (F1 score increase from 0.394 to 0.843)
- **Medium**: Physical plausibility of A-scan noise simulation methodology
- **Medium**: Effectiveness of CycleGAN modifications for defect preservation
- **Low**: Scalability assessment of CycleGAN versus A-scan noise generation
