# Towards Computationally Efficient Responsibility Attribution in Decentralized Partially Observable MDPs

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2302.12676
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2302.12676
- Reference count: 40
- The paper proposes an efficient Monte Carlo Tree Search method, RA-MCTS, to approximate agents' degrees of responsibility in decentralized partially observable MDPs under computational budget constraints.

## Executive Summary
This paper addresses the challenge of efficiently determining the responsibility of agents in decentralized, partially observable Markov decision processes (Dec-POMDPs) under uncertainty. Building on prior work connecting Dec-POMDPs and structural causal models (SCMs), it formalizes responsibility attribution in this setting and proposes an efficient Monte Carlo Tree Search (MCTS) method, RA-MCTS, to approximate the degrees of responsibility under a computational budget. The method introduces a novel search tree, pruning techniques, and a child selection policy based on linear scalarization, with backpropagation that accounts for minimality. The approach is evaluated on three card games (Euchre, Spades, and a team variation of Goofspiel) and outperforms baselines such as random search and brute-force methods.

## Method Summary
The method, RA-MCTS, is a Monte Carlo Tree Search variant designed for responsibility attribution in Dec-POMDPs. It uses a novel search tree structure where nodes represent interventions at specific time steps by agents, with leaves corresponding to candidate actual causes. The algorithm includes pruning techniques based on minimality and candidate cause-witness detection, a child selection policy using linear scalarization of responsibility degrees and environment feedback, and backpropagation that enforces minimality constraints. For unknown contexts, the method samples from the posterior distribution over contexts and averages the responsibility assignments. The approach is evaluated against baselines like random search and brute-force methods on three card games, showing significant computational efficiency gains while maintaining accuracy.

## Key Results
- RA-MCTS outperforms random search and brute-force methods in approximating responsibility degrees under computational budget constraints.
- In known-context scenarios, RA-MCTS converges to exact responsibility assignment within orders of magnitude fewer environment steps than exhaustive search.
- In unknown-context scenarios, RA-MCTS reliably approximates responsibility under context uncertainty, though exact attribution may be impossible if the posterior over contexts is non-informative.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: The search tree structure aligns simulation paths with deterministic state transitions under a fixed causal setting, avoiding redundant computation.
- Mechanism: Every node in the tree represents a deterministic function of the causal setting, so once a path is simulated, its outcomes are cached and never recomputed.
- Core assumption: The structural equations and context are fixed for a given causal setting, making all intermediate computations deterministic.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [section] "Simulation Phase... the entire simulation path is added to the search tree... under a fixed causal setting, the state transitions, observations generation and other such functions are deterministic."
  - [corpus] Weak corpus support for deterministic simulation reuse; no direct mention in neighbors.
- Break condition: If the causal setting or context changes mid-search, the determinism assumption fails and caching becomes invalid.

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: Pruning based on minimality and candidate cause-witness detection dramatically reduces the search space without losing optimality.
- Mechanism: Nodes are pruned if their intervention set is non-minimal with respect to found causes or if descendants cannot improve responsibility attribution; this uses structural properties of actual causality and responsibility definitions.
- Core assumption: AC3 minimality and the Chockler-Halpern responsibility function have exploitable monotonicity properties.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [section] "Pruning can take place... a node needs not be further visited if it becomes apparent that the evaluation of any leaf node reachable from that node cannot in any way influence the final responsibility assignment."
  - [section] Lists specific pruning conditions tied to AC3 and candidate cause-witness pairs.
- Break condition: If the responsibility function is non-monotonic or the minimality check is approximate, pruning may discard optimal branches.

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: Linear scalarization of multi-objective scores in RA-MCTS balances exploration across agents and environment feedback.
- Mechanism: Child selection uses weighted sum of responsibility degrees and environment-specific signals, cycling through agents to guide search toward diverse high-impact interventions.
- Core assumption: The environment-specific function qenv reliably signals proximity to winning outcomes, complementing responsibility scores.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [section] "the child selection policy... follows a linear scalarization approach... At iteration k, a pre-defined weight b_{k,j} is assigned... the only simulated responsibility degrees that guide the search at iteration k are those of agent i."
  - [section] Mentions the complementary role of the last score dimension.
- Break condition: If qenv is uninformative or highly correlated with responsibility scores, the scalarization provides no diversification benefit.

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map:
  - Search tree builder (TimeStep → Agent → Action → Leaf)
  - Pruning engine (minimality checks, candidate cause-witness detection)
  - MCTS controller (selection, expansion, simulation, backpropagation)
  - Evaluation function (responsibility degree + environment feedback)
  - Context sampler (posterior sampling under uncertainty)
  - Brute-force baselines (BF-DT, BF-ST, RANDOM) for comparison

- Critical path:
  1. Build initial search tree from causal setting.
  2. Run MCTS iterations: select node → expand if needed → simulate to leaf → evaluate → backpropagate.
  3. Prune nodes that cannot improve the solution.
  4. Output responsibility assignment (exact if context known, averaged if sampled).

- Design tradeoffs:
  - Deterministic simulation reuse vs. flexibility for changing contexts.
  - Aggressive pruning vs. risk of discarding optimal branches.
  - Linear scalarization vs. Pareto-based multi-objective methods.
  - Sampling-based uncertainty handling vs. exact posterior inference.

- Failure signatures:
  - Search tree growth stalls: likely pruning too aggressively or evaluation function uninformative.
  - Convergence to suboptimal responsibilities: insufficient exploration or qenv not guiding search.
  - High variance in unknown-context results: posterior samples too dispersed or insufficient samples.

- First 3 experiments:
  1. Verify deterministic simulation caching by running two identical simulations and checking identical node expansions.
  2. Test pruning by constructing a known non-minimal intervention set and confirming it is pruned before evaluation.
  3. Compare RA-MCTS against RANDOM and BF-ST on a small TeamGoofspiel instance with known context, measuring steps to exact solution.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- Question: Can the proposed search tree structure be extended or adapted to continuous action spaces in Dec-POMDPs?
- Basis in paper: [inferred] The paper discusses a search tree tailored for discrete action spaces and mentions that extending the approach to continuous models is an interesting future direction.
- Why unresolved: The current search tree is specifically designed for discrete actions, and adapting it to continuous spaces would require significant modifications to the tree structure, child selection policy, and evaluation function.
- What evidence would resolve it: A modified version of RA-MCTS that successfully handles continuous action spaces in Dec-POMDPs, with experimental validation showing comparable or improved performance.

### Open Question 2
- Question: How does the choice of the exploration parameter C and the weight B in the child selection policy affect the convergence and performance of RA-MCTS?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper mentions that RA-MCTS parameters are fixed to C=2 and B=0.5 in experiments, but does not explore the sensitivity of the algorithm to these choices.
- Why unresolved: The paper does not provide an analysis of how different values of C and B impact the algorithm's efficiency, exploration-exploitation balance, or final responsibility assignments.
- What evidence would resolve it: A systematic study varying C and B across a range of values, showing their impact on convergence speed, accuracy of responsibility attribution, and computational efficiency.

### Open Question 3
- Question: Can the pruning techniques be further optimized or generalized to reduce the search space more aggressively without losing accuracy in responsibility attribution?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper introduces pruning techniques based on structural properties of the actual causality definition and responsibility attribution, but notes that these are specific to the problem and could potentially be improved.
- Why unresolved: The paper does not explore alternative pruning strategies or provide theoretical guarantees on the completeness or optimality of the proposed pruning.
- What evidence would resolve it: Development and testing of more aggressive or adaptive pruning strategies, with empirical results showing reduced computational cost while maintaining or improving accuracy.

### Open Question 4
- Question: How does the non-informativeness of the posterior distribution over contexts impact the reliability of responsibility attribution in real-world applications?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper identifies that when the posterior over contexts is non-informative, exact responsibility attribution may be impossible even with unlimited computational budget.
- Why unresolved: The paper does not quantify the threshold of posterior informativeness required for reliable attribution, nor does it propose methods to detect or mitigate this issue in practice.
- What evidence would resolve it: Empirical or theoretical analysis linking posterior entropy or KL divergence to attribution accuracy, and/or proposed techniques for improving posterior informativeness in high-uncertainty settings.

### Open Question 5
- Question: Can the framework be extended to handle non-stationary or dynamic environments where the underlying SCM or policies change over time?
- Basis in paper: [inferred] The paper focuses on static Dec-POMDP SCMs and does not address scenarios where the causal model or agent policies evolve.
- Why unresolved: Real-world systems often exhibit non-stationarity, and the current framework assumes a fixed causal structure and policy set.
- What evidence would resolve it: A dynamic extension of the RA-MCTS framework that adapts to changing SCMs or policies, with experimental validation in non-stationary Dec-POMDP environments.

## Limitations

- The pruning conditions rely heavily on the monotonicity of the responsibility function; if this assumption is violated in edge cases, the search may discard optimal branches (Medium confidence).
- Linear scalarization assumes the environment feedback function qenv is complementary to responsibility scores; if qenv is uninformative or highly correlated, search efficiency may degrade (Medium confidence).
- In unknown-context scenarios, responsibility attribution may be impossible if the posterior over contexts is non-informative, limiting the applicability of RA-MCTS (High confidence).

## Confidence

- **Search tree determinism and reuse**: High confidence; supported by explicit statement that "state transitions, observations generation and other such functions are deterministic" under fixed causal settings.
- **Pruning efficacy**: Medium confidence; pruning rules are clearly specified but empirical validation is limited to relative performance gains.
- **Multi-objective scalarization**: Medium confidence; the linear combination is justified but no ablation study isolates its contribution from other factors.
