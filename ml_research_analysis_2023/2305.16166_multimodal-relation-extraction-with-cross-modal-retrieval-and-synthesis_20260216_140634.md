# Multimodal Relation Extraction with Cross-Modal Retrieval and Synthesis

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2305.16166
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.16166
- Reference count: 11
- Primary result: F1 score of 84.64% on MNRE benchmark, 2.79% improvement over state-of-the-art

## Executive Summary
This paper addresses multimodal relation extraction (MRE) by proposing a method that retrieves and synthesizes textual and visual evidence from object, sentence, and whole image levels. The approach uses cross-modal retrieval to obtain relevant multimodal evidence and cross-modal synthesis to combine this evidence for better relational reasoning. The model encodes visual and textual content, selects and compares evidence across modalities, and ensures consistency between retrieved and original evidence. Experiments on the MNRE benchmark demonstrate that this method significantly outperforms existing models.

## Method Summary
The proposed method involves cross-modal retrieval to obtain textual and visual evidence at multiple levels (object, sentence, image), followed by cross-modal synthesis to combine this evidence for relation extraction. The model first encodes the original multimodal input, then retrieves additional relevant evidence using Google Vision APIs and Google Custom Search API. Multi-head attention is used to select and compare evidence across modalities, while a consistency module evaluates the alignment between retrieved and original evidence. The final classifier predicts relation labels based on the synthesized representations.

## Key Results
- Achieves F1 score of 84.64% on MNRE benchmark
- 2.79% improvement over best-reported model
- Outperforms state-of-the-art models on multimodal relation extraction task

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: Cross-modal retrieval provides complementary evidence that helps resolve ambiguity in relation extraction.
- Mechanism: The model retrieves textual evidence from both the entire image and specific objects, as well as visual evidence based on the text content. This creates a richer context for identifying semantic relationships between entities.
- Core assumption: The retrieved evidence is relevant and provides meaningful additional context beyond what's available in the original input.

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: Cross-modal synthesis effectively combines evidence from different levels (object, sentence, image) to improve relational reasoning.
- Mechanism: The model uses multi-head attention to select and compare evidence across modalities, then evaluates consistency between retrieved and original evidence to update representations.
- Core assumption: The attention mechanism can effectively identify which pieces of evidence are most relevant for the specific relation being extracted.

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: Fine-grained alignment between visual and textual objects improves relation extraction performance.
- Mechanism: The model aligns visual objects detected in images with textual entities mentioned in sentences, creating a more precise mapping between modalities.
- Core assumption: There exists meaningful correspondence between visual objects and textual entities that can be leveraged for relation extraction.

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map: Visual Encoder -> Textual Encoder -> Cross-Modal Selection -> Cross-Modal Consistency -> Classifier -> Output
- Critical path: Input → Visual/Textual Encoding → Cross-Modal Selection → Cross-Modal Consistency → Classifier → Output
- Design tradeoffs:
  - Retrieval vs. content-only: Retrieval provides additional context but introduces latency and potential noise
  - Evidence quantity: More evidence can provide better context but may introduce irrelevant information
  - Multi-level vs. single-level: Different levels of evidence capture different aspects but require more complex integration
- Failure signatures:
  - Poor performance on tail relations: May indicate insufficient evidence for rare relation types
  - Inconsistent predictions across runs: Could suggest instability in the retrieval or synthesis components
  - Degradation when adding evidence: Might indicate the retrieved evidence is too noisy or the consistency module isn't effective
- First 3 experiments:
  1. Ablation study: Remove each evidence type (object, image, visual) and measure performance impact
  2. Evidence quantity analysis: Vary the number of retrieved evidence items and plot F1 score
  3. Consistency module evaluation: Compare with and without the consistency module to measure its contribution

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- Question: How can the proposed method be extended to incorporate other types of heterogeneous evidence, such as tables, info lists, and knowledge graphs, to improve relation extraction performance?
- Basis in paper: The paper mentions that evidence may be presented in different forms other than texts and images, such as structural information like tables, info lists, and knowledge graphs, which also provide important contexts for identifying semantic relations.
- Why unresolved: The current method only models and reasons over textual and visual evidence, while humans can extract relevant information from heterogeneous sources for inference.
- What evidence would resolve it: An experimental evaluation of the proposed method's performance on relation extraction tasks when incorporating heterogeneous evidence sources, such as tables, info lists, and knowledge graphs, alongside textual and visual evidence.

### Open Question 2
- Question: How can the retrieval process be optimized to reduce the impact of noisy evidence on the relation extraction performance?
- Basis in paper: The paper mentions that using a search engine to obtain related knowledge can bring the issue of noisy evidence, as unrelated visual and textual evidence returned by the search engine may lead to incorrect predictions from the model.
- Why unresolved: The current method does not explicitly address the issue of noisy evidence and its impact on relation extraction performance.
- What evidence would resolve it: An experimental evaluation of the proposed method's performance on relation extraction tasks when applying techniques to filter out or reduce the impact of noisy evidence on the retrieval process.

### Open Question 3
- Question: How can the retrieval-augmented method be made more efficient to address time-sensitive scenarios?
- Basis in paper: The paper mentions that retrieval-augmented methods are slower than content-based counterparts, since retrieving evidence from the Internet requires extra time, which may not satisfy some of the time-sensitive scenarios.
- Why unresolved: The current method does not explicitly address the efficiency of the retrieval process and its impact on the overall performance of the relation extraction system.
- What evidence would resolve it: An experimental evaluation of the proposed method's performance on relation extraction tasks when optimizing the retrieval process for efficiency, such as by using faster retrieval algorithms or pre-computing some of the evidence.

- Computational overhead makes system less suitable for real-time applications
