# The notion of role in conceptual modelling
## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2302.09863
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2302.09863

## Executive Summary
This paper examines the fundamental concept of roles in conceptual modeling, exploring how they connect domain knowledge to problem-solving methods. The analysis focuses on characterizing roles through five key dimensions: modeling process, definition, description, localization, and level of formalisation. The research demonstrates that roles serve as critical organizational elements that guide knowledge acquisition, enable model testing, and facilitate reuse of generic components across different knowledge engineering contexts.

The paper advocates for a dual characterization approach that combines both syntactic and semantic properties of roles, arguing this provides a more comprehensive framework for understanding their utility. The findings highlight how role-based modeling approaches can enhance model-driven development by providing clear guidelines for knowledge organization and reuse, while acknowledging that roles vary in their level of abstraction and formality across different domains and applications.

## Method Summary
The paper employs a theoretical analysis approach, examining existing literature and knowledge engineering practices to develop a comprehensive framework for understanding knowledge roles. The methodology involves systematic classification of role characteristics and their applications across various modeling paradigms. Through comparative analysis of different knowledge engineering approaches, the paper identifies common patterns and variations in how roles are defined, described, and utilized in conceptual modeling processes.

## Key Results
- Roles serve as crucial connectors between domain knowledge and problem-solving methods, enabling systematic knowledge acquisition and model testing
- The five-characteristic framework (modeling process, definition, description, localization, formalization) provides a comprehensive approach to characterizing knowledge roles
- Double characterization combining syntactic and semantic properties enhances the utility of roles in both bottom-up and top-down modeling contexts

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The role-based approach works because it provides a structured framework for organizing knowledge that bridges the gap between abstract problem-solving methods and concrete domain knowledge. By defining clear interfaces between generic components and specific domain content, roles enable systematic knowledge acquisition and facilitate the reuse of proven modeling patterns. The dual characterization approach works by capturing both the formal structure of roles (syntax) and their intended meaning and function (semantics), making them more adaptable to different modeling contexts while maintaining consistency.
