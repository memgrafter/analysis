# On the convergence of the MLE as an estimator of the learning rate in the Exp3 algorithm

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2305.06660
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.06660
- Reference count: 40
- Primary result: Constant learning rates lead to logarithmic convergence of MLE estimation error, while polynomially decreasing learning rates enable polynomial convergence rates.

## Executive Summary
This paper analyzes the consistency of Maximum Likelihood Estimation (MLE) for learning rate parameters in the Exp3 algorithm, a model used in cognitive science to fit individual learning data. The authors demonstrate that constant learning rates lead to poor estimation performance with error rates decreasing no faster than logarithmically with the number of observations. In contrast, when the learning rate decreases polynomially with sample size, both prediction error and estimation error decrease at polynomial rates. The analysis employs martingale concentration inequalities and truncated log-likelihoods to handle the dependencies and non-stationarity inherent in learning data. Simulation results support these theoretical findings, showing estimation error decreasing in n^{-1/4} for prediction error and n^{-1/8} for estimation error in a simplified two-arm setting.

## Method Summary
The paper studies MLE estimation of learning rates in the Exp3 algorithm through both theoretical analysis and simulation experiments. For constant learning rates, the authors analyze the log-likelihood function and derive logarithmic lower bounds on estimation error. For decreasing learning rates following a polynomial decay schedule η_n = η_0/n^α, they implement truncated MLE by removing uninformative observations where selection probabilities become negligible. The estimation procedure uses optimization algorithms to maximize the truncated log-likelihood, with evaluation metrics including prediction error (difference between true and estimated probabilities) and estimation error (difference between true and estimated learning rates). Simulations are conducted with 2-4 arm bandit problems using specified loss vectors and learning rate schedules.

## Key Results
- Constant learning rates lead to MLE estimation error decreasing no faster than logarithmically with the number of observations
- Polynomially decreasing learning rates enable polynomial convergence with prediction error ~ n^{-1/4} and estimation error ~ n^{-1/8} (for K=2)
- Truncated MLE with polynomially decreasing learning rates successfully avoids numerical instability and achieves consistent estimation
- The analysis uses martingale concentration inequalities to bound prediction error and derive convergence rates

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: When the learning rate is constant, the maximum likelihood estimator (MLE) suffers from logarithmic convergence due to the "tetration behavior" of the selection probabilities.
- Mechanism: The Exp3 algorithm updates arm selection probabilities recursively. For a constant learning rate, these probabilities decay extremely rapidly after a few steps, making most observations uninformative and causing the log-likelihood to become numerically unstable (producing -Inf values). This leads to poor distinguishability between close parameter values.
- Core assumption: The experiment setup involves a fixed loss vector and the subject follows the Exp3 algorithm exactly.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract]: "constant learning rates lead to poor estimation, with error rates decreasing no faster than logarithmically with the number of observations"
  - [section 3]: "After I(η), the probability of pulling the worst arm is so small that it is negligible from a numerical point of view and almost uninformative from an estimation point of view"
  - [corpus]: Weak - related papers discuss MLE but not the tetration-specific failure mode
- Break condition: If the learning rate decreases polynomially with sample size, this mechanism is avoided.

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: A decreasing learning rate enables polynomial convergence of the MLE by maintaining informative observations throughout the experiment.
- Mechanism: When the learning rate decreases as η_n = η_0 / n^α, the selection probabilities remain bounded away from zero for a polynomial number of steps (Υ_n), ensuring sufficient data for estimation. Truncating the log-likelihood at this stopping time removes uninformative tail observations while preserving statistical power.
- Core assumption: The learning rate follows a known polynomial decay schedule.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract]: "when the learning rate decreases polynomially with the sample size, both prediction error and estimation error... decrease at a polynomial rate"
  - [section 4.2]: "the prediction error seems indeed to be decreasing in n^(-1/4)"
  - [corpus]: Weak - no direct mention of polynomial learning rate effects on MLE
- Break condition: If the true learning rate doesn't follow the assumed polynomial decay, estimation performance degrades.

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: The truncated MLE achieves better estimation by controlling the martingale concentration of the log-likelihood differences.
- Mechanism: By analyzing the martingale structure of the truncated log-likelihood, the authors derive concentration inequalities that bound the prediction error. This analysis uses a specific distance metric on the parameter space that controls the differences in selection probabilities.
- Core assumption: The parameter space Θ is compact and the true parameter lies within it.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [section 4.3.3]: "we turn to the work of (Baraud, 2010), which provides a bound on supremum of the type Z = supδ∈Θ Yδ"
  - [section 4.2]: "the rate is polynomial in O(n^(-α/2))"
  - [corpus]: Moderate - related work on martingale inequalities for statistical estimation
- Break condition: If the martingale concentration fails (e.g., extreme parameter values), the bounds may not hold.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- Question: Is the logarithmic lower bound for the estimation error of constant learning rates tight, or could it be even slower?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper shows that estimation error decreases no faster than logarithmically with the number of observations for constant learning rates (Theorem 3.2), but suggests this bound might be pessimistic.
- Why unresolved: The authors state they "have not been able to prove it" but suspect the bound might hold even for smaller distances between parameters.
- What evidence would resolve it: Proving a tighter lower bound on the estimation error rate for constant learning rates would confirm whether the logarithmic bound is indeed tight or if an even slower rate applies.

### Open Question 2
- Question: Are the polynomial convergence rates for the prediction error (n^(-1/4)) and estimation error (n^(-1/8) for K=2) in the decreasing learning rate case tight?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper derives these rates theoretically (Theorems 4.2 and 5.2) and shows they are confirmed by simulations, but acknowledges uncertainty about whether they are tight.
- Why unresolved: The authors state "we do not know if this rate is tight or not" for both the prediction and estimation error rates.
- What evidence would resolve it: Proving matching lower bounds on the convergence rates for both prediction and estimation errors in the decreasing learning rate case would establish their tightness.

### Open Question 3
- Question: Does the phenomenon of poor estimation for constant learning rates extend to other realistic cognitive learning models beyond Exp3?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The authors suggest that similar phenomena "appear in a large variety of models well used in practice" but limit their theoretical analysis to Exp3.
- Why unresolved: The paper only provides theoretical guarantees for Exp3 and does not extend the analysis to other cognitive models like (Kruschke, 1992) or (Gluck & Bower, 1988).
- What evidence would resolve it: Extending the theoretical analysis to other popular cognitive learning models and demonstrating similar poor estimation properties for constant learning rates would support the generalizability of the findings.

## Limitations

- The theoretical analysis relies heavily on the assumption that the learning rate follows a specific polynomial decay schedule, which may not reflect real-world learning behavior.
- The truncation mechanism requires careful tuning of the precision parameter ε, with no clear guidance provided for practical implementation.
- Simulation results are limited to relatively small-scale problems (2-4 arms) and may not generalize to more complex multi-arm bandit scenarios.

## Confidence

- High confidence: The logarithmic convergence of MLE under constant learning rates is well-supported by both theoretical analysis and simulation evidence.
- Medium confidence: The polynomial convergence under decreasing learning rates is theoretically sound but relies on specific assumptions about the learning rate decay schedule.
- Medium confidence: The effectiveness of truncated MLE is theoretically justified through martingale concentration inequalities, but practical implementation details are not fully explored.
