# Linear Classifier: An Often-Forgotten Baseline for Text Classification

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2306.07111
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.07111
- Reference count: 16
- Primary result: Linear classifiers on TF-IDF features achieve competitive accuracy with BERT while being much faster and using fewer parameters on legal document datasets.

## Executive Summary
This paper advocates for using linear classifiers as a simple baseline when applying advanced text classification models like BERT. Through experiments on benchmark legal document datasets, the authors demonstrate that linear methods on TF-IDF features can achieve competitive performance with BERT in terms of accuracy while being significantly faster and requiring fewer parameters. The experiments also reveal that directly applying BERT without proper handling of long documents can lead to poor performance. The results emphasize the value of simple baselines for validating advanced methods and understanding their limitations.

## Method Summary
The authors compare linear classifiers (one-vs-rest linear SVM) with BERT-based models on six legal document classification datasets from LexGLUE. Linear methods use TF-IDF features with uni-gram tokenization, while BERT experiments include standard BERT and hierarchical BERT approaches for long documents. The study evaluates micro-F1 and macro-F1 scores, training times, and model sizes to assess both performance and efficiency.

## Key Results
- Linear SVM is competitive with BERT on four of six datasets, outperforming BERT on the SCOTUS dataset
- Linear methods are hundreds of times faster than BERT and use significantly fewer parameters (2M vs 110M-149M)
- Directly applying BERT without proper long document handling can result in poor performance
- Linear methods provide robust baselines to validate proper use of advanced approaches

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
Linear classifiers on TF-IDF features can achieve competitive performance with BERT for many text classification tasks. TF-IDF features capture term importance, and linear classifiers efficiently separate classes in this high-dimensional sparse space without requiring deep learning. This works when documents are sufficiently long that TF-IDF features are informative and class separation is linearly separable in this feature space.

### Mechanism 2
Simple baselines help validate whether advanced methods like BERT are properly applied. By comparing simple methods to advanced methods, practitioners can detect if poor performance stems from improper use of advanced methods rather than task difficulty. This works when the simple method is implemented correctly and efficiently, providing a reliable reference point.

### Mechanism 3
Linear methods are much faster and require fewer resources than BERT. Linear classifiers have low computational complexity and small model sizes compared to deep neural networks. This advantage is most apparent when computational resources are limited and efficiency is a key concern.

## Foundational Learning

- Concept: TF-IDF feature extraction
  - Why needed here: Linear classifiers require numerical feature vectors, and TF-IDF transforms text into a weighted term frequency representation suitable for linear separation.
  - Quick check question: What is the difference between term frequency and TF-IDF, and why is IDF important for text classification?

- Concept: One-vs-rest strategy for multi-class classification
  - Why needed here: Linear classifiers naturally handle binary problems, so one-vs-rest decomposes multi-class into multiple binary problems.
  - Quick check question: How does the one-vs-rest approach handle prediction for multi-class problems, and what are its limitations?

- Concept: Handling class imbalance in multi-label classification
  - Why needed here: Real-world text classification often has imbalanced label distributions, requiring techniques like thresholding and cost-sensitive learning.
  - Quick check question: What is the difference between thresholding and cost-sensitive approaches for handling label imbalance?

  BERT comparison: Standard BERT with sequence length handling → hierarchical BERT for long documents → parameter tuning

- Critical path:
  1. Preprocess text and generate TF-IDF features
  2. Train one-vs-rest linear SVM baseline
  3. Evaluate baseline performance
  4. Train BERT with appropriate document length handling

- Design tradeoffs:

  Linear methods underperforming significantly on short documents

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
What specific quantitative metric could predict when to use linear classifiers versus PLMs for text classification? The authors note that having a simple quantitative measurement to pre-determine when to use which remains a challenging future research problem. This remains unresolved because while the paper demonstrates that linear methods can be competitive on some datasets, it doesn't provide a general rule for when to choose each approach. The decision appears to depend on multiple factors including document length, label distribution, and data size. A comprehensive study across diverse datasets measuring performance trade-offs could identify patterns or develop a decision framework.

### Open Question 2
How do different feature engineering choices (e.g., uni-gram vs bi-gram vs tri-gram) impact the performance gap between linear methods and PLMs? The authors mention that various settings of bag-of-words features such as bi-gram or tri-gram can be considered, but advocate starting with uni-gram TF-IDF features. This remains unresolved because the paper only experiments with uni-gram TF-IDF features for linear methods, leaving open the question of whether richer feature representations could close the performance gap with PLMs on certain tasks. Systematic experiments comparing linear classifiers with different feature engineering approaches against PLMs would reveal the impact of feature complexity.

### Open Question 3
What is the impact of re-training BERT on combined training and validation sets versus using validation only for early stopping? The authors discuss this issue in Appendix H, noting that while re-training sometimes improves performance, the outcome may be data-dependent. The paper presents mixed results from re-training experiments and concludes that the impact varies by dataset. The general effectiveness of this approach remains unclear. Large-scale experiments across diverse text classification datasets measuring the consistent impact of re-training BERT on combined datasets would clarify when this practice is beneficial.

## Limitations

- The findings are primarily based on legal document classification tasks, which may not generalize to other domains
- The paper doesn't fully explore edge cases where BERT's complexity would be necessary
- Exact TF-IDF preprocessing parameters used in comparison studies are not specified

## Confidence

- High confidence: BERT vs. linear classifier performance comparison, computational efficiency metrics, parameter count comparisons
- Medium confidence: Generalizability of findings to non-legal domains, boundary conditions for when linear methods fail
- Medium confidence: The specific TF-IDF preprocessing parameters used, as exact values aren't specified

## Next Checks

1. **Domain Transfer Validation**: Replicate the experiments on non-legal text classification datasets (e.g., news categorization, product reviews) to verify if the linear classifier competitiveness holds across domains.

2. **Edge Case Analysis**: Systematically test document length boundaries where linear methods transition from competitive to non-competitive, specifically examining very short documents (<100 words) and documents at various truncation thresholds.

3. **Hyperparameter Sensitivity**: Conduct ablation studies on TF-IDF parameters (ngram range, max features, IDF smoothing) to determine how sensitive the linear method performance is to preprocessing choices, and whether the competitive advantage persists across parameter variations.
