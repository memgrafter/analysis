# A Heat Diffusion Perspective on Geodesic Preserving Dimensionality Reduction

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2305.19043
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.19043
- Reference count: 40
- Primary result: HeatGeo preserves geodesic distances better than PHATE, Diffusion Maps, and t-SNE on synthetic data; shows superior interpolation on single-cell RNA-seq datasets

## Executive Summary
HeatGeo is a manifold learning method that uses Varadhan's formula to approximate geodesic distances through heat kernel diffusion. The method constructs a graph from data, computes heat kernel approximations, and applies weighted MDS to obtain embeddings that preserve manifold structure. HeatGeo outperforms existing methods in preserving geodesic distances on synthetic datasets and shows strong performance on single-cell RNA-seq data for interpolation tasks.

## Method Summary
HeatGeo computes geodesic distances by applying Varadhan's formula to the heat kernel on a data-derived graph. The method first constructs a graph (typically kNN), approximates the heat kernel using Chebyshev polynomials, selects an appropriate diffusion time via knee-point detection, computes a heat-geodesic dissimilarity with optional triplet distance denoising and Harnack regularization, and finally applies weighted MDS to obtain the low-dimensional embedding. The method can be configured to produce results similar to PHATE and SNE while providing better geodesic preservation.

## Key Results
- On Swiss roll and Tree datasets, HeatGeo achieves higher Pearson and Spearman correlations with ground truth distance matrices compared to Diffusion Maps, PHATE, and Shortest Path
- HeatGeo enables interpolation of withheld timepoints in single-cell RNA-seq datasets with lower Earth Mover Distance than UMAP and t-SNE
- The method preserves continuous manifold structure better than baselines while maintaining competitive clustering quality metrics

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- **Claim:** HeatGeo uses Varadhan's formula to directly approximate geodesic distances from heat kernel diffusion
- **Mechanism:** The method computes a heat kernel $H_t$ on a graph built from data, then defines a dissimilarity $d_t(x,y) = [-4t \log(H_t)_{ij} - \sigma 4t \log(V_t)_{ij}]^{1/2}$ that converges to the squared geodesic distance as $t \to 0$
- **Core assumption:** The data lies on or near a Riemannian manifold where Varadhan's formula applies
- **Evidence anchors:** [abstract] "Varadhan's formula, which relates the heat kernel to the geodesic distance on a manifold"; [section] "Varadhan's formula is a powerful tool in differential geometry that establishes a connection between the heat kernel and the shortest path (geodesic) distance on a Riemannian manifold"

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim:** HeatGeo's triplet-based distance computation acts as a denoising step that improves robustness
- **Mechanism:** Instead of using direct pairwise heat kernel entries, the method compares heat kernel rows: $D_T(x_i,x_j) = \|d_t(x_i,\cdot) - d_t(x_j,\cdot)\|_2^2$, which reduces sensitivity to local noise
- **Core assumption:** Noise affects pairwise heat kernel entries independently, but triplet distances average out this effect
- **Evidence anchors:** [section] "Empirically, we notice that this step acts as a denoiser for distances"; [section] "Proposition 4.3. Denote the perturbed triplet distance by $gDT(x_i, x_j) = ||\tilde{d}_t(x_i, \cdot) - \tilde{d}_t(x_j, \cdot)||_2$... the triplet distance $DT$ is robust to perturbations"

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim:** The weighted MDS step promotes preservation of local manifold structure while maintaining global distances
- **Mechanism:** By minimizing $(H_t)_{ij}(d_t(x_i,x_j) - \|\phi(x_i) - \phi(x_j)\|^2)^2$, the method emphasizes reconstruction accuracy for high-affinity pairs, balancing local detail and global layout
- **Core assumption:** The heat kernel's affinity values reflect meaningful local neighborhood relationships
- **Evidence anchors:** [section] "In Sec. 5, we will show how this modification relates our method to the embedding defined by SNE"; [section] "The heat-geodesic method also relates to the Stochastic Neighbor Embedding (SNE)... In SNE, the similarity between points is encoded via transition probabilities $p_{ij}$"

## Architecture Onboarding

- **Component map:** Graph construction (kNN + kernel) -> Heat kernel approximation (Chebyshev or Euler) -> Diffusion time selection (knee-point detection) -> Dissimilarity computation (log + Harnack regularization) -> Optional triplet distance (denoising) -> Weighted MDS (embedding)

- **Critical path:** 1. Build graph -> 2. Approximate heat kernel -> 3. Select t -> 4. Compute dissimilarity -> 5. Apply triplet (optional) -> 6. Run weighted MDS

- **Design tradeoffs:** Exact vs approximate heat kernel: accuracy vs speed; Using triplet distance: denoising vs computational cost; Harnack regularization: robustness vs bias in distance estimation; Diffusion time selection: automatic vs manual tuning

- **Failure signatures:** Disconnected graph -> poor heat kernel -> meaningless distances; Too small t -> numerical instability in log computation; Too large t -> loss of local structure; Poor kNN choice -> wrong neighborhood structure

- **First 3 experiments:** 1. Swiss roll with varying noise levels: validate geodesic preservation via Pearson correlation; 2. Synthetic cluster dataset: test clustering quality (homogeneity, aMI); 3. Single-cell temporal data: evaluate interpolation capability (EMD between interpolated and true timepoints)

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

The paper does not explicitly call out open questions, but based on the methodology and results, several areas warrant further investigation:

1. How to optimally choose the Harnack regularization parameter σ for different types of manifolds and noise levels
2. The impact of different graph construction methods on HeatGeo's performance across diverse datasets
3. Extending HeatGeo to handle non-Euclidean data structures such as graphs or trees

## Limitations

- The method's theoretical guarantees depend on the manifold satisfying parabolic Harnack conditions, which may not hold for real-world data
- Computational complexity can be high for large datasets due to heat kernel approximation and weighted MDS steps
- The method requires careful tuning of multiple parameters including diffusion time, kernel bandwidth, and regularization terms

## Confidence

- **High confidence:** Core algorithmic framework and synthetic dataset results
- **Medium confidence:** Real-world dataset performance claims
- **Low confidence:** Theoretical guarantees under realistic noise conditions
