# Clinical BERTScore: An Improved Measure of Automatic Speech Recognition Performance in Clinical Settings

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID**: 2303.05737
- **Source URL**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.05737
- **Reference count**: 0
- **Key outcome**: Clinical BERTScore (CBERTScore) better aligns with clinician preferences on medical transcripts than traditional metrics like WER, BLEU, and METEOR by incorporating medical domain knowledge into BERTScore evaluation.

## Executive Summary
This paper introduces Clinical BERTScore (CBERTScore), a novel metric designed to evaluate Automatic Speech Recognition (ASR) performance specifically in clinical settings. Traditional metrics like Word Error Rate (WER) treat all recognition errors equally, failing to account for the critical importance of medical terminology in clinical contexts. CBERTScore addresses this limitation by combining the semantic similarity capabilities of BERTScore with domain-specific knowledge from medical knowledge graphs, allowing it to penalize clinically-relevant errors more heavily than non-clinical ones.

The authors created the Clinician Transcript Preference (CTP) dataset, consisting of 149 realistic medical sentences evaluated by 13 clinicians to establish ground truth preferences. Using this dataset, they tuned CBERTScore's clinical penalty weight to best match human clinician judgments. Experimental results demonstrate that CBERTScore more closely aligns with clinician preferences than existing metrics, sometimes showing substantial improvements. The metric also maintains reasonable performance on non-medical transcripts, suggesting potential for broader application beyond clinical settings.

## Method Summary
The Clinical BERTScore method combines the semantic similarity scoring of BERTScore with clinical domain knowledge to create a more appropriate evaluation metric for ASR systems in healthcare settings. BERTScore computes neural word embeddings and cosine distances to measure semantic similarity between reference and hypothesis texts, addressing limitations of exact string matching approaches. The key innovation involves integrating a clinical penalty term that increases the score penalty when medically-relevant terms are misidentified or substituted.

The method works by first identifying clinical terms using an external medical knowledge graph, then applying a weighted penalty to BERTScore calculations when errors involve these terms. The weight of this clinical penalty (λ) is tuned using the Clinician Transcript Preference dataset, where human experts evaluated pairs of ASR outputs. By optimizing λ to best match clinician preferences, the authors ensure that CBERTScore reflects the actual priorities of medical professionals when assessing ASR accuracy. The resulting metric provides a more nuanced evaluation that distinguishes between clinically critical errors and less consequential mistakes.

## Key Results
- CBERTScore more closely aligns with clinician preferences on medical transcripts than traditional metrics (WER, BLEU, METEOR, BERTScore), sometimes by wide margins
- The metric successfully incorporates clinical domain knowledge to penalize medically-relevant mistakes more heavily than general errors
- CBERTScore demonstrates reasonable performance on non-medical transcripts, suggesting potential for general-purpose use beyond clinical settings

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The effectiveness of Clinical BERTScore stems from its dual approach: leveraging BERTScore's semantic understanding while incorporating domain-specific knowledge about medical terminology importance. BERTScore provides robust semantic similarity measurement through contextual word embeddings and cosine distance calculations, capturing meaning beyond surface-level text matching. By adding a clinical penalty term weighted by domain knowledge, the metric specifically accounts for the critical nature of medical terminology in clinical contexts.

This mechanism works because it aligns the evaluation metric with how clinicians actually assess ASR quality - they care more about accurate transcription of medical terms than general vocabulary. The external medical knowledge graph provides authoritative identification of clinical terms, while the tunable penalty weight allows the metric to be calibrated to human preferences. This combination creates an evaluation framework that better reflects the practical requirements of clinical ASR systems, where errors in medical terminology can have serious consequences for patient care and documentation.

## Foundational Learning

**BERTScore**: A semantic similarity metric that computes neural word embeddings and cosine distances between reference and hypothesis texts. *Why needed*: Traditional metrics like BLEU and WER use exact string matching, failing to capture semantic meaning. *Quick check*: Verify that BERTScore can identify semantically similar words that are not exact matches.

**Medical Knowledge Graphs**: Structured representations of medical terminology and relationships between clinical concepts. *Why needed*: Provides authoritative identification of clinical terms for the penalty mechanism. *Quick check*: Confirm the knowledge graph contains relevant medical terms used in the evaluation dataset.

**Clinician Preference Datasets**: Collections of human evaluations where clinicians rank or rate ASR output quality. *Why needed*: Provides ground truth for tuning the clinical penalty weight. *Quick check*: Ensure the dataset covers diverse medical specialties and error types.

**Cosine Similarity**: A measure of similarity between two vectors based on the cosine of the angle between them. *Why needed*: Used in BERTScore to compute semantic similarity between word embeddings. *Quick check*: Verify cosine similarity ranges from -1 to 1 and correctly identifies similar versus dissimilar vectors.

**Semantic Embeddings**: Dense vector representations of words that capture their meaning in context. *Why needed*: Enable semantic comparison beyond exact string matching. *Quick check*: Test that semantically similar words have similar embeddings while unrelated words have dissimilar embeddings.

## Architecture Onboarding

**Component Map**: CTP Dataset -> Clinical Term Identification -> BERTScore Calculation -> Clinical Penalty Application -> Tuned CBERTScore

**Critical Path**: The evaluation pipeline flows from clinician preferences through clinical term identification, semantic similarity computation, penalty application, and final score calculation. The most critical components are accurate clinical term identification and proper tuning of the penalty weight to match human preferences.

**Design Tradeoffs**: The choice to use an external knowledge graph provides authoritative clinical term identification but may miss domain-specific or emerging terminology. Using BERTScore provides semantic understanding but adds computational complexity compared to simpler metrics. The tunable penalty weight allows optimization to human preferences but requires sufficient preference data for effective calibration.

**Failure Signatures**: Poor clinical term identification from the knowledge graph will result in inappropriate penalty application. Insufficient or biased clinician preference data will lead to poorly tuned penalty weights. Over-reliance on semantic similarity may miss critical factual errors that don't affect semantic similarity but are clinically important.

## Limitations
- The evaluation is based on a relatively small human preference dataset (149 sentences from 13 clinicians), potentially limiting generalizability
- Reliance on a single external medical knowledge graph may miss important terms or domain-specific terminology not captured in that source
- The clinical penalty weight (λ=0.5) was tuned to match preferences on this specific dataset, raising questions about generalizability to other clinical contexts
