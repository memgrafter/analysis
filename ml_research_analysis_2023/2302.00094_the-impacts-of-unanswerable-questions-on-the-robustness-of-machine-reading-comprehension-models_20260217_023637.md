# The Impacts of Unanswerable Questions on the Robustness of Machine Reading Comprehension Models

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID**: 2302.00094
- **Source URL**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2302.00094
- **Reference count**: 34
- **Key outcome**: Training on unanswerable questions provides "hidden robustness" to adversarial attacks, revealed by forcing non-empty answers

## Executive Summary
This paper investigates whether training MRC models with unanswerable questions improves their robustness to adversarial attacks. The authors fine-tune BERT, RoBERTa, and SpanBERT on SQuAD 1.1 and SQuAD 2.0, then evaluate their performance under adversarial attacks. While SQuAD 2.0-trained models initially appear less robust, they exhibit "hidden robustness" that becomes apparent when forcing non-empty answers. This improved robustness extends to out-of-domain datasets. The authors also introduce a new adversarial attack to reveal artifacts in SQuAD 2.0 that current MRC models learn.

## Method Summary
The authors fine-tune three state-of-the-art transformer models (BERT, RoBERTa, SpanBERT) on either SQuAD 1.1 or SQuAD 2.0. They evaluate performance on the original SQuAD 2.0 dev set and generate adversarial attacks using AddSent and C2U methods. A new adversarial attack is introduced to test model reliance on target sentences with lexical overlap. Models are evaluated under normal conditions and when forced to output non-empty answers. Out-of-domain generalization is tested on five additional MRC datasets.

## Key Results
- Models trained on SQuAD 2.0 initially appear less robust but reveal hidden robustness when forced to output non-empty answers
- v2 models significantly outperform v1 models on four out of five out-of-domain datasets
- SQuAD 2.0-trained models show improved robustness against adversarial attacks when non-empty answers are forced
- A new adversarial attack reveals that v2 models learn to rely on target sentences with significant lexical overlap for answerability determination

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
Training on unanswerable questions provides "hidden robustness" to adversarial attacks on answerable questions. Models trained on SQuAD 2.0 learn to recognize and handle confusion-inducing patterns, but initially express this as "unanswerable" responses instead of corrected answers. The models' second-best answer often contains the correct response, indicating latent understanding.

### Mechanism 2
Unanswerable questions train models to better detect and reject adversarial perturbations in answerable questions. Exposure to unanswerable questions teaches models to identify target sentences with significant lexical overlap as unreliable, improving their ability to resist confusion-based attacks.

### Mechanism 3
Additional training on unanswerable questions doesn't harm generalization to out-of-domain datasets. The robustness gained from unanswerable questions transfers across different question-answering datasets and contexts.

## Architecture Onboarding

- **Component map**: Pre-trained transformer models (BERT, RoBERTa, SpanBERT) → Fine-tuning on SQuAD 1.1 or 2.0 → Evaluation on original and adversarial test sets
- **Critical path**: Data preprocessing → Model fine-tuning → Adversarial attack generation → Performance evaluation → Analysis of response categories
- **Design tradeoffs**: Using unanswerable questions improves robustness but may initially mask this advantage; forcing non-empty answers reveals hidden robustness but may introduce other biases
- **Failure signatures**: Performance drops on adversarial attacks, high percentage of "C2U" responses, inability to transfer robustness to out-of-domain datasets
- **First 3 experiments**:
  1. Fine-tune each model on SQuAD 1.1 and SQuAD 2.0, evaluate on original SQuAD 2.0 dev set
  2. Generate AddOneSent adversarial attacks on answerable questions from SQuAD 2.0 dev set, evaluate both v1 and v2 models
  3. Force v2 models to output non-empty answers on attacked answerable questions, compare with v1 models' performance

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
How can we accurately measure the robustness of MRC models on both answerable and unanswerable questions? The paper identifies that current frameworks may mask performance losses on unanswerable questions due to shifts between incorrectly answerable and correctly unanswerable categories.

### Open Question 2
What are the underlying reasons for v2 models' reliance on target sentences with significant lexical overlap to determine answerability? The paper identifies this as an undesirable behavior but does not explore the root causes or potential solutions.

### Open Question 3
How can we prevent MRC models from learning the undesirable behavior of relying on target sentences with lexical overlap for answerability determination? The paper suggests this as a direction for future research but does not provide specific approaches or solutions.

### Open Question 4
To what extent does the improved robustness of v2 models against adversarial attacks generalize to other types of robustness challenges beyond those tested in this paper? The paper tests robustness against specific types of adversarial attacks and out-of-domain datasets, but does not explore other robustness challenges.

## Limitations
- The robustness gains appear conditional on forcing non-empty answers, which may not reflect practical deployment scenarios
- Evaluation covers only five additional datasets, potentially limiting generalizability claims
- The specific mechanisms by which unanswerable questions confer robustness remain somewhat speculative

## Confidence

- **High Confidence**: The observation that v2 models show better performance on out-of-domain datasets and that forcing non-empty answers reveals hidden robustness
- **Medium Confidence**: The mechanism explaining how unanswerable questions train models to detect lexical overlap patterns
- **Medium Confidence**: The generalizability of robustness gains across different MRC architectures

## Next Checks

1. **Cross-dataset robustness validation**: Test whether the hidden robustness phenomenon persists when models are evaluated on entirely new, unseen adversarial attack patterns beyond AddSent and C2U, including semantic and syntactic perturbations.

2. **Mechanism isolation experiments**: Design ablation studies that systematically remove target sentence identification capabilities to determine whether lexical overlap detection is truly the primary mechanism of robustness.

3. **Deployment scenario testing**: Evaluate model performance when the "force non-empty answer" constraint is removed, simulating real-world scenarios where models must decide answerability autonomously.
