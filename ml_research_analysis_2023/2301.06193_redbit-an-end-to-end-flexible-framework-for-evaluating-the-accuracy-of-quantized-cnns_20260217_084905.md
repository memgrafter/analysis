# RedBit: An End-to-End Flexible Framework for Evaluating the Accuracy of Quantized CNNs

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2301.06193
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2301.06193
- Reference count: 40
- This paper presents RedBit, a PyTorch-based open-source framework that enables systematic evaluation of quantized CNNs

## Executive Summary
RedBit is a PyTorch-based open-source framework designed to systematically evaluate the accuracy of quantized convolutional neural networks (CNNs). It provides a transparent, extensible, and user-friendly interface for assessing the effectiveness of various quantization algorithms and parameters on network accuracy. The framework was employed to evaluate five state-of-the-art quantization methods across MNIST, CIFAR-10, and ImageNet datasets, resulting in over 2300 unique network models trained. The study highlights that models can achieve up to 32× memory savings while maintaining minimal accuracy degradation, particularly for 1-bit quantization.

## Method Summary
The RedBit framework offers a systematic approach to evaluate quantized CNNs by providing a transparent, extensible, and easy-to-use interface. It was used to assess five state-of-the-art quantization methods across three datasets: MNIST, CIFAR-10, and ImageNet. The framework's design allows for comprehensive experimentation, enabling the training of over 2300 unique network models to analyze the impact of different quantization algorithms and parameters on network accuracy.

## Key Results
- For 1-bit quantization, accuracy losses for MNIST, CIFAR-10, and ImageNet datasets range between [0.26%, 0.79%], [9.74%, 32.96%] and [10.86%, 47.36%] top-1, respectively.
- Models can be quantized with memory savings of up to 32× while maintaining minimal accuracy degradation.
- The framework and all results are publicly available, encouraging community contributions to expand the repository of quantized CNN configurations.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The RedBit framework's effectiveness stems from its systematic approach to evaluating quantized CNNs, providing a transparent and extensible interface that allows for comprehensive experimentation. By enabling the assessment of various quantization algorithms and parameters, it facilitates a deeper understanding of their impact on network accuracy. The framework's design supports extensive experimentation, as evidenced by the training of over 2300 unique network models, which contributes to a robust analysis of quantization effects across different datasets.

## Limitations
- The study focuses on a specific set of quantization methods and datasets, potentially limiting generalizability to other architectures or applications.
- The evaluation primarily measures accuracy degradation without extensive analysis of other performance metrics such as latency or energy consumption.
- The extensive experimentation required over 2300 models, which may not be accessible to all researchers due to computational resource constraints.
