# Comparative Analysis of Deep Learning Models for Brand Logo Classification in Real-World Scenarios

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID**: 2305.12242
- **Source URL**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.12242
- **Reference count**: 15
- **Primary result**: DaViT small achieved 99.60% accuracy while DenseNet29 delivered 366.62 FPS inference speed for brand logo classification

## Executive Summary
This study evaluated deep learning models for brand logo classification using a dataset of 3,717 labeled images from ten prominent brands. The investigation compared Convolutional Neural Networks (DenseNet29, MobileNetV3 small/large) and Vision Transformer models (DaViT small). The DaViT small achieved the highest accuracy of 99.60%, while DenseNet29 delivered the fastest inference speed at 366.62 FPS. The results indicate that transformer-based models like DaViT small offer superior accuracy for offline applications, whereas CNNs provide better computational efficiency.

## Method Summary
The study used 3,717 labeled RGB images (300×300 resolution) of ten brand logos, split 80% training and 20% validation. Four models were evaluated: DenseNet29, MobileNetV3 small/large (CNNs), and DaViT small (Vision Transformer). Training used AdamW optimizer with 5-epoch warmup, SoftCrossEntropy loss, mixup augmentation, and AutoAugment. DaViT small was pretrained and fine-tuned on misclassified validation samples, while CNNs were trained from scratch. Evaluation measured accuracy and inference speed (FPS) on NVIDIA A100 GPU with CUDA 11.7, PyTorch 2.0.0, FP32.

## Key Results
- DaViT small achieved the highest accuracy of 99.60% on validation set
- DenseNet29 delivered the fastest inference speed at 366.62 FPS
- MobileNetV3 variants showed intermediate performance between DaViT small and DenseNet29
- Data augmentation strategies (mixup, AutoAugment) were applied to improve generalization

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: DaViT small achieves higher accuracy than CNNs by leveraging dual attention mechanisms that capture both local and global spatial and channel features.
- Mechanism: The dual attention blocks in DaViT alternate spatial window attention (local features) and channel group attention (global features), allowing the model to capture fine-grained patterns and long-range dependencies simultaneously.
- Core assumption: Brand logos contain both local discriminative patterns and global contextual relationships that benefit from multi-scale attention.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "The ViT model, DaViT small, achieved the highest accuracy of 99.60%"
  - [section] "The spatial window attention is capable of extracting local features within windows, while channel group attention can learn global features"
  - [corpus] Weak evidence - related papers focus on different domains (RL localization, visual reasoning, etc.) without direct comparative analysis of dual attention for logos
- Break condition: If logo images are too small or highly compressed, spatial attention windows may lose critical local features, reducing accuracy advantage.

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: DenseNet29 achieves fastest inference speed due to its dense connectivity pattern and efficient parameter utilization.
- Mechanism: DenseNet29's dense connectivity allows gradient flow improvement while maintaining smaller network structure, reducing computational overhead during inference.
- Core assumption: Computational efficiency is prioritized over accuracy for real-time applications.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "DenseNet29 delivered the fastest inference speed at 366.62 FPS"
  - [section] "DenseNet's dense connectivity pattern allows it to improve gradient flow while maintaining a smaller network structure"
  - [corpus] No direct corpus evidence - related papers don't compare FPS metrics
- Break condition: If the dataset complexity increases significantly, DenseNet29's efficiency advantage may diminish as it requires deeper architectures for comparable accuracy.

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: Data augmentation strategies (mixup, AutoAugment) significantly improve model generalization by artificially increasing dataset diversity.
- Mechanism: Mixup combines features and labels from two images, while AutoAugment applies learned augmentation policies to create more varied training examples, helping models handle real-world variations.
- Core assumption: Brand logo classification benefits from robustness to variations in lighting, scale, and partial occlusion.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [section] "Data augmentation is an important step in the process of training neural networks for computer vision. It enhances the variability of data and helps to restrain the overfitting problem"
  - [section] "Several techniques were applied to augment the training dataset in our study" (mixup and AutoAugment mentioned)
  - [corpus] Weak evidence - related papers don't detail specific augmentation techniques for logo classification
- Break condition: If augmentation policies are too aggressive, they may introduce unrealistic variations that harm model performance on actual logo images.

## Limitations

- Limited dataset size (3,717 images) may not represent broader real-world scenarios with more extreme logo variations
- Evaluation focused on ten prominent brands, limiting insights into performance with lesser-known or highly similar brand logos
- All testing conducted on single NVIDIA A100 GPU, making direct performance comparisons with other hardware configurations uncertain

## Confidence

- **High Confidence**: DaViT small achieving highest accuracy (99.60%) and DenseNet29 achieving fastest inference speed (366.62 FPS) - these are direct experimental measurements from the study
- **Medium Confidence**: The superiority of transformer-based models for offline applications and CNN models for computational efficiency - this conclusion depends on the specific dataset characteristics and may vary with different logo datasets
- **Low Confidence**: The claim that dual attention mechanisms specifically account for DaViT's accuracy advantage - while mentioned, the study lacks ablation experiments isolating the attention mechanisms' contribution
