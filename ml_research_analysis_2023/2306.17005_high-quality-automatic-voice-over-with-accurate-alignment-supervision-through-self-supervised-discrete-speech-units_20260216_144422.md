# High-Quality Automatic Voice Over with Accurate Alignment: Supervision through Self-Supervised Discrete Speech Units

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2306.17005
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.17005
- Authors: 
- Reference count: 0

## Executive Summary
This paper introduces DSU-AVO, a novel approach to automatic voice over that leverages self-supervised discrete speech units to improve lip-speech synchronization. The method predicts discrete speech units from text-video context rather than reconstructing acoustic features, providing more direct supervision for alignment learning. Experimental results on the Chem dataset demonstrate significant improvements in both synchronization accuracy and synthetic speech quality compared to existing methods.

## Method Summary
DSU-AVO uses self-supervised discrete speech units from HuBERT as the learning objective for automatic voice over. The model architecture consists of a video encoder, text encoder, text-video aligner, and unit predictor. It predicts discrete speech units from the text-video context and synthesizes speech directly from these units using a pretrained unit vocoder. The system is trained using cross-entropy loss for discrete speech unit prediction and diagonal constraint loss to shape diagonal attention for better alignment. Experiments are conducted on the Chem dataset, a single-speaker audio-visual English speech corpus.

## Key Results
- DSU-AVO achieves LSE-C of 6.81, LSE-D of 7.56, and FD of 3.23, outperforming baselines in lip-speech synchronization
- Speech quality measured by MOS reaches 3.98, demonstrating high-quality synthetic speech generation
- The method effectively improves alignment learning and synthetic speech quality through discrete speech unit supervision

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: Discrete speech units provide more direct supervision for alignment learning than acoustic features
- Mechanism: By predicting discrete speech units from text-video context, the model learns to map linguistic content directly to speech units, bypassing the indirect acoustic feature reconstruction step
- Core assumption: Discrete speech units better capture linguistic content than mel-spectrogram
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "discrete speech unit prediction... provides more direct supervision for the alignment learning"
  - [section] "Since the context is formed by aligning multi-modal representations, alignment learning is an integral part of context modeling"
  - [corpus] Weak evidence; neighbor papers focus on voice conversion rather than alignment learning
- Break condition: If discrete units fail to capture linguistic content accurately, the alignment supervision becomes ineffective

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: Using unit vocoder alleviates mismatch between context and acoustic features
- Mechanism: By synthesizing speech directly from discrete units rather than through acoustic feature reconstruction, the model eliminates the representation gap between text-video context and speech output
- Core assumption: Unit vocoder can produce high-quality speech directly from discrete units
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "synthesize speech directly from discrete speech units with a pretrained unit vocoder, thus alleviating mismatch"
  - [section] "Unit HiFi-GAN models the mapping from discrete speech units to speech waveform without any spectrogram estimation"
  - [corpus] Weak evidence; neighbor papers focus on voice conversion rather than vocoder architecture
- Break condition: If unit vocoder quality degrades or cannot handle the discrete unit representation

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: Diagonal constraint loss shapes attention to be more diagonal, improving alignment
- Mechanism: The diagonal constraint loss encourages the attention matrix to have higher weights on diagonal elements, promoting monotonic alignment between text and video
- Core assumption: Monotonic alignment is optimal for text-video synchronization
- Evidence anchors:
  - [section] "We adopt the diagonal constraint loss Ldiag following [10, 30] to shape diagonal attention"
  - [section] "the attention weight matrix. Hv is added through residual connection to enhance alignment learning"
  - [corpus] No direct evidence; requires domain knowledge about attention mechanisms
- Break condition: If the video and text are not naturally aligned or have non-monotonic relationships

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map: Unit tokenizer → Video encoder → Text encoder → Text-video aligner → Unit predictor → Unit vocoder → Speech

- Critical path: Video/Text → Align → Predict Units → Vocode → Speech

- Design tradeoffs:
  - Discrete units vs continuous features: Discrete units provide better alignment supervision but may lose fine-grained acoustic information
  - Frozen components vs trainable: Freezing HuBERT and Unit HiFi-GAN reduces training complexity but limits adaptation
  - Attention-based alignment vs other methods: Attention is flexible but may struggle with long sequences

- Failure signatures:
  - Poor LSE scores: Indicates alignment issues between lip and speech
  - Low MOS scores: Indicates quality problems in synthetic speech
  - High WER scores: Indicates content prediction errors

- First 3 experiments:
  1. Verify unit tokenizer correctly converts ground truth speech to discrete units by comparing predictions to ground truth
  2. Test text-video aligner independently by checking attention weight matrix patterns
  3. Validate unit vocoder by synthesizing speech from ground truth discrete units and comparing to original audio

## Limitations
- Experimental validation relies on a single-speaker Chem dataset, limiting generalizability to multi-speaker scenarios
- Comparison with only HuBERT-AVO as a baseline reduces the strength of performance claims
- Use of frozen pretrained components may constrain the model's ability to adapt to the specific alignment task

## Confidence
- High confidence in the general approach of using discrete speech units for alignment learning
- Medium confidence in the specific implementation details and hyperparameter choices
- Medium confidence in the relative performance improvements over baselines
- Low confidence in the method's generalization to different speakers, languages, and speaking styles

## Next Checks
1. Evaluate DSU-AVO on multi-speaker datasets to verify the method's generalization beyond the single-speaker Chem dataset used in the paper.

2. Test the impact of unfreezing HuBERT and Unit HiFi-GAN components to assess whether end-to-end training could further improve performance.

3. Test the model on extended video sequences (beyond the dataset's scope) to evaluate its robustness to varying context lengths and potential drift in alignment accuracy over time.
