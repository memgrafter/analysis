# Abstractive Text Summarization using Attentive GRU based Encoder-Decoder

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2302.13117
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2302.13117
- Reference count: 17
- Primary result: GRU-based encoder-decoder with Bahdanau attention achieves ROUGE-1 F1 of 35.29 and ROUGE-L F1 of 35.25 on CNN/Daily Mail

## Executive Summary
This paper presents an abstractive text summarizer using a GRU-based encoder-decoder architecture with Bahdanau attention mechanism. The model was trained on 10,000 examples from the CNN/Daily Mail dataset, employing a bidirectional GRU encoder and unidirectional GRU decoder. The attention mechanism helps focus on key parts of the input sequence during summary generation. After training for 100 epochs with Adam optimizer and sparse categorical cross-entropy loss, the model achieved competitive ROUGE scores of 35.29 (ROUGE-1) and 35.25 (ROUGE-L) F1, outperforming several baseline models including Words-lvt5k-1sent, Words-lvt2k-temp-att, ABS+, and RAS-Elman.

## Method Summary
The model architecture consists of a bidirectional GRU encoder and unidirectional GRU decoder with Bahdanau attention. Input text undergoes preprocessing including lowercasing, contractions handling, punctuation removal, and lemmatization. The attention mechanism computes alignment scores between encoder and decoder states, allowing the decoder to focus on relevant parts of the input sequence. The model was trained using Adam optimizer with a learning rate of 0.001 for 100 epochs on a subset of the CNN/Daily Mail dataset.

## Key Results
- ROUGE-1 F1 score: 35.29
- ROUGE-L F1 score: 35.25
- Outperforms Words-lvt5k-1sent baseline (28.61, 25.42)
- Outperforms ABS+ baseline (28.18, 23.81)
- Outperforms RAS-Elman baseline (33.78, 31.15)

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The bidirectional GRU encoder captures context from both forward and backward directions, creating richer representations of the input text. The Bahdanau attention mechanism computes alignment scores between encoder and decoder states, allowing the decoder to dynamically focus on the most relevant parts of the input sequence during summary generation. This combination enables the model to generate more coherent and contextually appropriate summaries compared to models without attention.

## Foundational Learning

**GRU (Gated Recurrent Unit)**: A type of recurrent neural network that uses gating mechanisms to control information flow. Why needed: Handles sequential data and captures temporal dependencies. Quick check: Verify gates properly regulate information flow.

**Bahdanau Attention**: Computes alignment scores between encoder and decoder states to focus on relevant input parts. Why needed: Allows decoder to dynamically attend to important input regions. Quick check: Ensure attention weights highlight semantically important tokens.

**Bidirectional Encoding**: Processes input sequence in both forward and backward directions. Why needed: Captures context from both directions for better representation. Quick check: Confirm bidirectional states combine effectively.

**Sparse Categorical Cross-Entropy**: Loss function for multi-class classification with sparse labels. Why needed: Suitable for word prediction in vocabulary with many classes. Quick check: Monitor loss convergence during training.

## Architecture Onboarding

**Component map**: Input text -> Bidirectional GRU Encoder -> Bahdanau Attention -> Unidirectional GRU Decoder -> Summary

**Critical path**: The attention mechanism is critical, as it enables the decoder to focus on relevant input parts. The bidirectional encoder provides rich input representations that the attention mechanism can leverage.

**Design tradeoffs**: Uses GRU instead of LSTM for simpler gating mechanism; unidirectional decoder vs bidirectional for faster inference; limited training data due to computational constraints.

**Failure signatures**: Poor attention alignment indicates input representation issues; high loss suggests optimization problems; low ROUGE scores indicate generation quality problems.

**First experiments**: 1) Test attention weight visualization to verify focus on relevant tokens; 2) Evaluate with different sequence lengths to check truncation impact; 3) Compare unidirectional vs bidirectional encoder performance.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- Question: How would the model's performance change if trained on the full CNN/Daily Mail dataset instead of just 10,000 examples?
- Basis in paper: The authors mention they used only 10,000 examples due to low computational power, stating "Due to low configuration of our system, 10000 examples from CNN/Daily Mail dataset has been used."
- Why unresolved: The authors acknowledge hardware limitations prevented using the full dataset, so comparative results are unavailable.
- What evidence would resolve it: Training the same model architecture on the complete CNN/Daily Mail dataset and comparing ROUGE scores would demonstrate the impact of dataset size on performance.

### Open Question 2
- Question: Would incorporating BERT-based pre-training improve the model's ability to generate more meaningful summaries compared to the current GRU-based approach?
- Basis in paper: The authors state "In future, we will use BERT based pre-training model to enhance model's performance and to generate more meaningful summary."
- Why unresolved: This represents planned future work that hasn't been implemented yet.
- What evidence would resolve it: Implementing a BERT-based summarization model and comparing its ROUGE scores and summary quality against the current GRU-based model would provide a direct comparison.

### Open Question 3
- Question: How would the model perform on summarizing scientific articles, particularly COVID-19 related papers, compared to news articles?
- Basis in paper: The authors mention "We will try to create summary of Covid-19 related scientific articles which can help the medical community by providing a clean and meaningful high-quality knowledge base of the pandemic."
- Why unresolved: The current model is trained only on news data, so its performance on scientific text is unknown.
- What evidence would resolve it: Training or fine-tuning the model on scientific article datasets and evaluating its performance on COVID-19 research papers would demonstrate domain transfer capabilities.

## Limitations
- Trained on only 10,000 examples from CNN/Daily Mail, representing a small fraction of available training data
- Uses outdated baseline models that may not reflect current state-of-the-art transformer-based approaches
- Limited to single-sentence headline generation, reducing applicability to comprehensive summarization tasks
