# Towards Coding Social Science Datasets with Language Models

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2306.02177
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.02177
- Authors: 
- Reference count: 16
- Primary result: GPT-3 matches or exceeds human coder performance on social science text coding tasks using only 1-3 examples

## Executive Summary
This paper demonstrates that large language models, specifically GPT-3, can serve as effective automated coders for social science text data. Through experiments across four diverse datasets, the authors show that GPT-3 achieves inter-coder agreement levels comparable to human coders while requiring minimal examples (1-3) to learn coding schemes. The study compares GPT-3's performance to human coders and traditional supervised machine learning methods, finding that GPT-3 offers a low-cost alternative with benefits in efficiency, scalability, and reduced training requirements.

## Method Summary
The study evaluates GPT-3's coding capabilities across four datasets: Pigeonholing Partisans (7,675 texts), New York Times Headlines (560 headlines), Congressional Hearing summaries (326 summaries), and The Guardian Populism data (1,300 texts). GPT-3 (175B parameters) was prompted with 1-3 labeled examples and minimal instructions to classify texts into predefined categories. The authors measured inter-coder agreement using ICC, joint probability of agreement, and Fleiss' Kappa, comparing GPT-3's codes to human-coded labels. Performance was also compared to a supervised machine learning baseline trained on 3,000 labeled examples.

## Key Results
- GPT-3 achieved 79% accuracy on populism coding versus 86% for a supervised method, requiring only 4 examples versus 3,000
- GPT-3's inter-coder agreement metrics matched or exceeded those between human coders across all datasets
- Using 1-3 exemplars, GPT-3 demonstrated few-shot learning capabilities without requiring extensive training data

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: GPT-3 can perform coding tasks with few-shot learning using minimal examples
- Mechanism: GPT-3's pre-training on massive amounts of human-created text provides it with an extensive understanding of language patterns, allowing it to infer coding schemes from just 1-3 labeled examples without requiring extensive fine-tuning or large training datasets
- Core assumption: The coding task can be expressed as a language prediction problem where GPT-3 can match patterns between examples and new texts
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "Using minimal examples (1-3) to teach GPT-3 the coding scheme, the study finds that GPT-3 matches or exceeds human coder performance on average"
  - [section] "We discovered that the LM responded quite similarly across various versions of our guidance, and that it required only two or three coded examples to perform well on these tasks"
- Break condition: Tasks requiring deep domain expertise or complex reasoning beyond pattern matching in text

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: GPT-3 achieves inter-coder agreement levels comparable to human coders
- Mechanism: GPT-3's language understanding enables it to consistently apply coding rules across different texts, achieving reliability metrics (ICC, Fleiss' kappa) similar to or better than human coder agreement
- Core assumption: The coding task has sufficient structure and consistency that a well-trained model can achieve human-level reliability
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "GPT-3 matches or exceeds human coder performance on average, achieving inter-coder agreement levels comparable to those between human coders"
  - [section] "Both our intercoder agreement metrics tell the same story, and imply a finding that holds across metrics: GPT-3 correlates with each human just as well as or better than the humans correlate with each other"
- Break condition: Tasks with highly subjective interpretations or ambiguous boundaries between categories

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: GPT-3 provides efficiency and scalability advantages over traditional coding methods
- Mechanism: By eliminating the need for extensive training data and reducing the human effort required for coding, GPT-3 enables faster, cheaper analysis of large text corpora while maintaining accuracy
- Core assumption: The cost savings from reduced training requirements and faster processing outweigh any marginal accuracy differences compared to human coding
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "GPT-3 matched the human populism codes 79 percent of the time... the SML baseline outperforms GPT-3 by about 7 percentage points, it does so at the cost of 3000 labeled examples"
  - [section] "Given the drastically lower costs of coding with GPT-3 - in the case, the requirement of hiring, training, and supervising coders to classify 4,000 texts - we again see this as evidence of the value of GPT-3 as a coding tool"
- Break condition: Tasks where the marginal accuracy loss from automation significantly impacts research conclusions

- Concept: Language model prompting techniques
  - Why needed here: The paper's methodology depends on effective prompt engineering to teach GPT-3 coding tasks, requiring understanding of how different prompt structures affect performance
  - Quick check question: What is the purpose of providing exemplar examples in the prompt?

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map: GPT-3 model -> Prompt engineering layer -> Input processing -> Output interpretation -> Evaluation framework
- Critical path: Prompt creation -> GPT-3 inference -> Output parsing -> Accuracy calculation -> Comparison with human codes
- Design tradeoffs: Accuracy vs. efficiency (GPT-3 requires fewer examples but may be slightly less accurate than SML with extensive training), flexibility vs. specificity (few-shot learning allows broad application but may struggle with highly specialized domains)
- Failure signatures: Poor performance on highly subjective coding tasks, inconsistent results across different prompt formats, inability to handle out-of-distribution text
- First 3 experiments:
  1. Test GPT-3 performance with 0, 1, 2, and 3 exemplars to establish the learning curve
  2. Compare GPT-3 accuracy across different prompt formats (question vs. statement format)
  3. Evaluate GPT-3 performance on texts with varying levels of ambiguity or complexity

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- Question: Can GPT-3 be conditioned or fine-tuned to emulate specific heterogeneous groups of coders, potentially addressing bias concerns?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The authors suggest that if GPT-3 can be conditioned to hold specific biases rather than others, it could emulate specific heterogeneous groups of coders for a richer, more diverse, and representative coding.
- Why unresolved: The paper mentions this as a possibility but does not explore or demonstrate it empirically.
- What evidence would resolve it: Empirical testing showing GPT-3's ability to be conditioned to emulate different groups of coders, with results compared to actual human coders from those groups.

### Open Question 2
- Question: How does the performance of GPT-3 as a coder vary across different languages and cultural contexts?
- Basis in paper: [inferred] The paper primarily focuses on English-language datasets from the US and UK. The authors mention that bias in coders is an omnipresent problem in social sciences, suggesting that cultural and linguistic differences might affect coding performance.
- Why unresolved: The study does not include datasets from diverse linguistic or cultural backgrounds to test GPT-3's performance in these contexts.
- What evidence would resolve it: Comparative studies using GPT-3 to code datasets in multiple languages and from various cultural contexts, with results analyzed for consistency and accuracy across these variations.

### Open Question 3
- Question: What is the optimal number and type of exemplars for GPT-3 to achieve the best coding performance across different tasks?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The authors experimented with different numbers of exemplars and found that one exemplar performs much better than none, but there is little gain in accuracy achieved by providing more than 2 or 3 exemplars. They also tested different classes of exemplars (prototypical, ambiguous, tricky) but found no significant difference in performance.
- Why unresolved: While the paper provides some insights, it does not conclusively determine the optimal number and type of exemplars for all possible coding tasks.
- What evidence would resolve it: Systematic testing of GPT-3's performance with varying numbers and types of exemplars across a wide range of coding tasks, identifying patterns or rules for optimal exemplar selection.

## Limitations

- The study uses only four datasets, which may not fully represent the diversity of social science coding tasks
- Comparison with human coders is limited to single human coders rather than multiple independent coders for each dataset
- The paper does not address potential bias in GPT-3's training data that could affect coding decisions for sensitive political topics

## Confidence

- **High confidence**: Core finding that GPT-3 can match or exceed human coder performance with minimal examples
- **Medium confidence**: Efficiency claims regarding reduced training requirements and faster processing
- **Low confidence**: Generalizability of results across all social science coding tasks

## Next Checks

1. Conduct a replication study using datasets with multiple human coders to establish more robust inter-coder reliability baselines
2. Test GPT-3's performance on coding tasks requiring complex reasoning or deep domain expertise beyond pattern matching
3. Evaluate the consistency of GPT-3's coding over time by conducting the same coding tasks across multiple weeks/months to identify potential drift in performance
