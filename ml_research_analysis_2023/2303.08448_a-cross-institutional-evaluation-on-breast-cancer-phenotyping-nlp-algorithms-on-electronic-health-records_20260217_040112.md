# A Cross-institutional Evaluation on Breast Cancer Phenotyping NLP Algorithms on Electronic Health Records

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2303.08448
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.08448
- Reference count: 0
- Key outcome: CancerBERT achieved best cross-institutional performance (micro-F1: 0.925) compared to CRF and BiLSTM-CRF models for breast cancer phenotype extraction

## Executive Summary
This study evaluates the generalizability of BERT-based clinical NLP models across different institutions for breast cancer phenotype extraction from electronic health records. Three model types (CRF, BiLSTM-CRF, and CancerBERT) were developed using data from the University of Minnesota and Mayo Clinic. The CancerBERT model demonstrated superior performance and robustness when evaluated on a permutation test set, with fine-tuning on target institution data yielding performance close to models trained from scratch.

## Method Summary
The study developed and evaluated three types of NLP models (CRF, BiLSTM-CRF, and CancerBERT) for extracting eight breast cancer phenotype categories from clinical text. Models were trained on data from one institution and evaluated on data from another institution to assess cross-institutional generalizability. The evaluation framework included standard F1 metrics, entity coverage ratio (ECR), and permutation testing to assess contextual learning. CancerBERT models were fine-tuned on target institution data to evaluate transfer learning effectiveness.

## Key Results
- CancerBERT achieved micro-F1 score of 0.925 when evaluated across institutions
- Fine-tuned CancerBERT models performed comparably to locally trained models (0.925 vs 0.932)
- CancerBERT demonstrated better robustness on permutation test set compared to CRF and BiLSTM-CRF models
- Entity coverage ratio analysis showed CancerBERT's superior ability to handle unseen entities

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: CancerBERT outperforms traditional CRF and BiLSTM-CRF models in cross-institutional generalizability.
- Mechanism: CancerBERT learns contextual embeddings from large pretraining corpora, allowing it to generalize better to new institutions with similar entity patterns but different surface forms.
- Core assumption: Contextual embeddings capture entity semantics robustly across syntactic variations in clinical texts.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract]: "CancerBERT model achieved the best performance among the three types of models...demonstrated better robustness when evaluated on a permutation test set"
  - [section]: "CancerBERT models were developed based on the BERT language model and showed superior performance compared to the CRF and BiLSTM-CRF models"
  - [corpus]: Weak evidence. The study compared corpora but did not directly show CancerBERT's embeddings are more robust than GloVe/Word2Vec embeddings across institutions.
- Break condition: If entity surface forms differ drastically (e.g., completely different clinical terminology or language), contextual embeddings may not generalize without fine-tuning.

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: Similarity of target entities between corpora correlates with model performance drops.
- Mechanism: Models perform better when test corpora share more entity surface forms and patterns with training corpora, as this reduces the distributional shift.
- Core assumption: Entity surface form overlap is a major driver of performance drop when transferring models between institutions.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract]: "The generalizability of the models was found to be correlated with the similarity of the target entities between the corpora."
  - [section]: "The similarities among breast cancer phenotypes are significantly higher than the similarity between the two corpora...It indicates that clinicians may use similar clinical language to describe specific medical concepts"
  - [corpus]: Moderate evidence. The study calculated TF-IDF cosine similarity for phenotype entities and overall corpora, showing higher phenotype similarity (0.9088) vs overall (0.5411).
- Break condition: If target entities are semantically similar but use completely different surface forms, similarity metrics may overestimate actual transferability.

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: Fine-tuning pretrained CancerBERT on target institution data yields performance close to training from scratch.
- Mechanism: Pretrained CancerBERT captures general medical language patterns; fine-tuning adapts it to specific institutional variations with minimal data.
- Core assumption: Pretraining on large medical corpora provides a strong foundation that can be efficiently specialized.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract]: "The CancerBERT model developed in one institute and further fine-tuned in another institute achieved reasonable performance compared to the model developed on local data (micro-F1: 0.925 vs 0.932)."
  - [section]: "Although the CancerBERTMC_397 model trained from scratch using MC data obtained the best overall performances, the performances of the CancerBERTMC_refined model is comparable...indicates that BERT-based models can be transported to another clinical institute with keeping relatively high level of performance using a very low cost"
  - [corpus]: Weak evidence. The study compared fine-tuned vs scratch models but did not provide detailed analysis of the minimal data required for effective fine-tuning.
- Break condition: If institutional variations are too extreme or if fine-tuning data is insufficient, performance may not approach locally trained models.

  - Large performance drop on permutation test indicates poor learning of entity contexts

  - Minimal improvement after fine-tuning suggests institutional variations exceed model capacity

  1. Evaluate source-trained models on target test set to establish baseline portability

## Limitations

- Limited to two institutions with similar clinical documentation practices, limiting generalizability to more diverse healthcare settings
- Does not characterize pretraining data composition or assess sensitivity to different pretraining approaches
- Entity coverage ratio metric doesn't capture semantic equivalence when entities have different surface forms
