# Hierarchical shrinkage Gaussian processes: applications to computer code emulation and dynamical system recovery

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2302.00755
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2302.00755
- Reference count: 34
- Primary result: Novel Gaussian process model with structured shrinkage priors for improved emulation of sparse response surfaces

## Executive Summary
This paper introduces the Hierarchical Shrinkage Gaussian Process (HierGP), a novel emulator model that incorporates structured sparsity principles within a Gaussian process framework. The method uses cumulative shrinkage priors to embed the principles of effect sparsity, hierarchy, and heredity - widely used in experimental design but not captured by standard GPs. The authors develop efficient Gibbs sampling algorithms and prove posterior consistency results. Numerical experiments demonstrate improved predictive performance and uncertainty quantification over existing methods, particularly when the response surface exhibits structured sparsity.

## Method Summary
The HierGP model extends standard Gaussian processes by placing cumulative shrinkage priors on the basis coefficients of a Karhunen-Loève expansion. This prior structure explicitly encodes effect hierarchy and heredity principles, favoring main effects over interactions. The authors develop a Gibbs sampling algorithm with data augmentation for efficient posterior inference, along with theoretical guarantees of posterior consistency. The method is applied to both computer code emulation and dynamical system recovery, with extensions to capture weaker forms of heredity.

## Key Results
- HierGP achieves improved predictive performance on sparse response surfaces compared to standard GPs
- The model provides better uncertainty quantification, with coverage probabilities closer to nominal levels
- In dynamical system recovery applications, HierGP outperforms SINDy and other GP-based methods

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
Structured shrinkage priors capture effect hierarchy and heredity, improving predictive performance in sparse response surfaces. The cumulative shrinkage prior assigns decreasing probability to higher-order basis coefficients, embedding the principle that main effects dominate interactions. Core assumption: The true response surface exhibits sparsity consistent with effect hierarchy and heredity principles.

### Mechanism 2
Data augmentation trick enables efficient Gibbs sampling by introducing latent variables. Latent indicators z_k determine whether each coefficient uses a spike (inactive) or slab (active) distribution, enabling closed-form conditionals. Core assumption: The spike-and-slab prior structure allows marginalization over the latent indicators to yield tractable sampling distributions.

### Mechanism 3
Posterior consistency guarantees the model learns the true sparse structure as data increases. Under mild assumptions on sparsity and basis representation, the posterior concentrates around the true function in appropriate topologies. Core assumption: The true function has finite sparsity and belongs to the function space spanned by the chosen basis with controlled smoothness.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
What is the posterior contraction rate for the HierGP model, and does it improve upon standard GP contraction rates under structured sparsity? The paper establishes consistency but defers contraction rates to future work due to the complex hierarchical structure. Mathematical proof of posterior contraction rates would resolve this.

### Open Question 2
How does the HierGP perform under different forms of hierarchical sparsity beyond effect hierarchy and heredity, such as weak heredity? The current formulation is designed for strict hierarchy, but alternative sparsity structures remain unexplored. Numerical experiments with modified shrinkage priors would address this.

### Open Question 3
What is the optimal choice of basis functions for the HierGP in various application domains, and how does basis choice affect model performance? While non-orthonormal bases can be integrated, guidance on optimal selection is lacking. Systematic comparison across basis types would resolve this.

## Limitations
- Method effectiveness depends on assumption of structured sparsity following effect hierarchy principles
- Computational complexity scales as O(Bdn∥K∥ + ∥K∥³), potentially prohibitive for very high-dimensional problems
- Performance in cases where true function violates assumed sparsity structure remains unclear
