# ShapeWordNet: An Interpretable Shapelet Neural Network for Physiological Signal Classification

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID**: 2302.05021
- **Source URL**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2302.05021
- **Reference count**: 36
- **Primary result**: Significantly outperforms four categories of cutting-edge TSC methods on three real-world physiological datasets, especially in multi-class classification with label sparsity

## Executive Summary
This paper introduces ShapeWordNet, a novel time series classification method for physiological signals that combines interpretable shapelet discretization with multi-scale contrastive learning. The method extracts shapelets as discriminative local patterns, clusters them into shapewords, and discretizes continuous signals into ShapeSentences to preserve both local and global information. To address label sparsity, it employs a multi-scale transformation strategy and cross-scale contrastive learning mechanism to learn scale-invariant features. Extensive experiments on three real-world datasets demonstrate superior performance compared to state-of-the-art methods, with visualization analysis showing interpretability of the shapelet-based discretization.

## Method Summary
ShapeWordNet transforms continuous physiological signals into discrete token sequences through shapelet-based discretization. It first extracts shapelets using F-statistic evaluation, clusters them into shapewords via K-means, and discretizes each signal into multi-scale ShapeSentences. Each scale is encoded by a dilated causal CNN, and representations are fused through channel-wise concatenation followed by a 1D convolution. The model is trained with a combination of classification loss and cross-scale contrastive loss, where different-scale ShapeSentences of the same sample form positive pairs. This approach preserves interpretability while leveraging self-supervised learning to mitigate label sparsity issues.

## Key Results
- ShapeWordNet significantly outperforms four categories of cutting-edge TSC methods on three physiological signal datasets
- The method shows particular strength in multi-class classification tasks with severe label sparsity
- Visualization analysis demonstrates the interpretability of shapelet-based discretization in capturing discriminative patterns

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- **Claim**: ShapeWord discretization reduces high-dimensional physiological signals to discriminative token sequences while preserving both local patterns and global context
- **Mechanism**: Raw multivariate time series are segmented into overlapping windows of size equal to a ShapeWord, and each segment is assigned the nearest ShapeWord (cluster centroid) by Euclidean distance
- **Core assumption**: The cluster centroids (ShapeWords) represent prototypical patterns that are discriminative across classes and that nearest-neighbor assignment preserves class-relevant semantics
- **Evidence anchors**: [abstract] "we discretize the continuous signal into a sequence of shapewords (ShapeSentence) to preserve both local and global information"; [section] "we segment the original signals into non-overlapping subsequences via a sliding window of the ShapeWord size. Then, we assign each segment the cluster label token that refers to its nearest ShapeWord according to the Euclidean Distance"
- **Break condition**: If ShapeWords do not capture discriminative patterns (e.g., low inter-class variance in sDist), discretization may discard useful continuous information and hurt performance

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim**: Multi-scale ShapeSentence transformation acts as data augmentation to alleviate label sparsity by providing self-supervised signals for contrastive learning
- **Mechanism**: The same raw signal is transformed into ShapeSentences of multiple window sizes (e.g., 10, 25, 50 time steps). These different scales are treated as positive pairs in a contrastive loss, encouraging the model to learn scale-invariant representations
- **Core assumption**: Different-scale ShapeSentences of the same sample share latent invariance that the encoder can capture, and this invariance is useful for downstream classification
- **Evidence anchors**: [abstract] "to alleviate the label sparsity issue, a multi-scale transformation strategy is adaptively designed to augment data and a cross-scale contrastive learning mechanism is accordingly devised to guide the model training"; [section] "we make the best of the large shapelet candidate space to generate different sizes of ShapeWords and transform each sample into multiple scales of ShapeSentences, which are further utilized to construct self-supervised signals in the contrastive learning mechanism"
- **Break condition**: If the invariance assumption fails (e.g., different scales encode unrelated information for the task), contrastive loss may mislead training

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim**: Scale-aware feature integration via channel-wise concatenation and 1D convolution fuses multi-scale representations better than simple pooling
- **Mechanism**: Each scale's encoded ShapeSentence is concatenated along the channel dimension, forming a tensor that is fed into a single 1D convolution layer whose filters are learned per scale, allowing adaptive fusion
- **Core assumption**: Different scales contribute complementary information, and a learned fusion can extract better joint features than fixed operations like averaging
- **Evidence anchors**: [abstract] "a scale-aware feature integrator is devised to fuse the representations of multi-scale ShapeSentences for class label prediction"; [section] "we regard each scale's representation as complementary to the raw data's invariant features and concatenate them by channel. We then input the concatenated representation tensor into the Scale-aware Feature Integrator (SFI) for feature fusion"
- **Break condition**: If scale contributions are redundant or conflicting, concatenation without proper gating may hurt performance

## Architecture Onboarding

- **Component map**: Raw signal → ShapeWord discretization (multi-scale) → multi-scale encoder → contrastive loss → concatenated features → SFI → classification
- **Critical path**: Raw signal → ShapeWord discretization (multi-scale) → multi-scale encoder → contrastive loss → concatenated features → SFI → classification
- **Design tradeoffs**:
  - Shapelet clustering vs. learned shapelets: clustering is interpretable but may miss task-specific patterns; learned shapelets are task-specific but less interpretable
  - Multi-scale vs. single-scale: multi-scale increases data diversity and robustness but adds complexity and training cost
  - Channel-wise concatenation vs. pooling: concatenation preserves scale-specific information but may increase dimensionality
- **Failure signatures**:
  - Poor shapelet discrimination (low F-statistic) → ShapeWords do not represent class-relevant patterns → discretization fails
  - Imbalanced contrastive pairs → loss minimization does not reflect true invariance → contrastive learning misguides training
  - Over-parameterized SFI → overfitting on small datasets → poor generalization
- **First 3 experiments**:
  1. **Shapelet quality check**: Compute F-statistic for selected shapelets vs. generated ShapeWords; ensure cluster centroids retain or improve discrimination
  2. **Ablation of contrastive loss**: Train with and without CCLM on a small subset; measure impact on accuracy and robustness to label sparsity
  3. **Scale number sensitivity**: Vary number of scales in MST (e.g., 1, 2, 3) and measure classification performance and training stability

## Limitations
- Shapelet discretization quality depends on cluster centroid representativeness, which may not capture task-specific patterns in heterogeneous physiological signals
- Multi-scale contrastive learning assumes scale-invariant features exist across different ShapeSentence transformations, which may not hold for all signal types
- The scale-aware feature integrator's effectiveness compared to simpler fusion methods remains largely unverified through ablation studies

## Confidence
- **High Confidence**: The overall framework design (shapelet clustering → discretization → multi-scale encoding) is sound and well-motivated by existing time series classification literature
- **Medium Confidence**: The specific implementation details (encoder architecture, contrastive loss parameters, shapelet selection criteria) are described but lack sufficient detail for exact replication
- **Low Confidence**: The empirical validation is limited to three datasets, and the visualization analysis, while promising, does not provide quantitative measures of interpretability or shapelet relevance to clinical outcomes

## Next Checks

2. **Ablation of Multi-scale Strategy**: Train ShapeWordNet with only a single scale and compare performance to the multi-scale version on each dataset. Include a variant that uses simple average pooling instead of the scale-aware feature integrator to assess whether learned fusion provides benefits.
