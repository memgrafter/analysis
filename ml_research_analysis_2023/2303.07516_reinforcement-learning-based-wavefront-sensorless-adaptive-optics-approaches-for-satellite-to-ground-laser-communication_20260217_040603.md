# Reinforcement Learning-based Wavefront Sensorless Adaptive Optics Approaches for Satellite-to-Ground Laser Communication

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2303.07516
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.07516
- Reference count: 12
- Achieves Strehl ratio over 60% using PPO after hundreds of training episodes

## Executive Summary
This paper presents a reinforcement learning approach for wavefront sensorless adaptive optics in optical satellite-to-ground communication systems. The method eliminates the need for traditional wavefront sensors by directly controlling a deformable mirror from focal plane measurements. Three RL algorithms (Soft Actor-Critic, DDPG, and PPO) are evaluated, with PPO achieving a Strehl ratio exceeding 60% after hundreds of training episodes, approaching the performance of an idealized Shack-Hartmann sensor reference.

## Method Summary
The approach uses reinforcement learning to control a deformable mirror based on focal plane intensity measurements (Strehl ratio) rather than traditional wavefront sensor data. The system learns to optimize the mirror shape to compensate for atmospheric turbulence and other distortions in real-time. The RL agent receives the Strehl ratio as reward signal and adjusts the mirror actuators to maximize this metric. The method is tested through simulations comparing three different RL algorithms: Soft Actor-Critic, Deep Deterministic Policy Gradient, and Proximal Policy Optimization.

## Key Results
- PPO algorithm achieves Strehl ratio over 60% after hundreds of training episodes
- Performance approaches that of an idealized Shack-Hartmann wavefront sensor
- System claims potential 30-40% cost and latency reduction by eliminating wavefront sensor hardware
- SAC and DDPG algorithms show inferior performance compared to PPO in this application

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The RL approach works by directly optimizing the observable output metric (Strehl ratio) rather than inferring the underlying wavefront structure. By treating the deformable mirror control as a sequential decision problem, the agent learns to make incremental adjustments that cumulatively improve the focal plane intensity. This end-to-end optimization bypasses the need for explicit wavefront reconstruction, which can introduce errors and latency.

- Adaptive Optics fundamentals: Why needed - to understand atmospheric distortion compensation; Quick check - system should correct low-order aberrations effectively
- Strehl ratio measurement: Why needed - primary reward signal for optimization; Quick check - ratio should increase as correction improves
- Deformable mirror control: Why needed - physical actuator interface for wavefront correction; Quick check - mirror should respond predictably to control signals
- Focal plane sensing: Why needed - provides direct feedback without wavefront sensors; Quick check - intensity measurements should correlate with correction quality

## Architecture Onboarding
- Component map: Satellite laser -> Atmospheric turbulence -> Deformable mirror -> Focal plane sensor -> RL controller
- Critical path: Laser transmission → Atmospheric distortion → Mirror correction → Focal plane measurement → RL update → Mirror adjustment
- Design tradeoffs: Hardware complexity (no wavefront sensor) vs. potential performance limitations vs. traditional AO systems
- Failure signatures: Plateaued Strehl ratio, high variance in corrections, inability to track rapid turbulence changes
- First experiments: 1) Test RL training convergence with synthetic turbulence patterns, 2) Compare single vs. multiple actuator control granularity, 3) Evaluate performance under varying turbulence strength levels

## Limitations
- Performance falls short of idealized Shack-Hartmann sensor benchmark
- Real-world atmospheric turbulence and satellite motion not validated in simulation
- No analysis of computational requirements for on-board satellite implementation
- Limited to three RL algorithms without exploring broader algorithm space

## Confidence
- RL algorithm performance claims: Medium
