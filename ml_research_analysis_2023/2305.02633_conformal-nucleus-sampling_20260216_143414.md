# Conformal Nucleus Sampling

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2305.02633
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.02633
- Reference count: 7
- Key outcome: OPT language models are overconfident in nucleus sampling, with calibration showing inverse scaling with model size; conformal prediction improves calibration while maintaining generation quality.

## Executive Summary
This paper investigates whether nucleus (top-p) sampling in language models is calibrated, meaning the probability p associated with the top-p set accurately reflects the likelihood of the correct word being included. Using conformal prediction, the authors calibrate the parameter p as a function of the entropy of the next word distribution, revealing that OPT models are systematically overconfident. The proposed conformal top-p decoding algorithm ensures the sampling has a meaningful probabilistic interpretation, improving calibration across model sizes while preserving generation quality metrics.

## Method Summary
The authors employ conformal prediction to calibrate nucleus sampling by computing minimal prediction sets that achieve desired coverage levels. They collect next-word distributions from OPT language models (125M to 30B parameters) on Wikipedia data, compute entropy for each distribution, and bin examples by entropy percentile. Using a validation set, they calculate conformal scores that measure the minimum probability mass needed to include the true word, then determine calibration thresholds per entropy bin. During generation, these calibrated thresholds replace the nominal p values to ensure the top-p set contains the correct word with the intended probability.

## Key Results
- OPT language models are overconfident, with effective confidence consistently below nominal p values
- Calibration shows moderate inverse scaling with model size - larger models exhibit worse calibration
- Conformal top-p decoding maintains generation quality (MAUVE, BERTScore) while achieving better calibration
- Entropy-dependent calibration significantly outperforms global calibration, correcting for context-dependent overconfidence

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: The calibration procedure adjusts the p threshold so that the effective coverage probability matches the nominal p value.
- Mechanism: The conformal prediction framework computes a threshold q^ such that the prediction set C_q^(x) contains the correct word with probability at least p over the validation set. This threshold is chosen as the p-quantile of the conformal scores st computed from the validation data.
- Core assumption: The validation set is exchangeable with the test data (or at least approximately so for natural language).
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "We employ conformal prediction, a calibration procedure that focuses on the construction of minimal prediction sets according to a desired confidence level, to calibrate the parameter p as a function of the entropy of the next word distribution."
  - [section 2] "Denote the prediction set obtained by taking the most probable classes until the total mass just exceeds a value q, by Cq(x)... Let ˆq be the smallest threshold value that p(y∈ Cˆq(x))≥ 1−α"
  - [corpus] Weak - only shows general related work on conformal prediction, no direct evidence for language model calibration
- Break condition: If the validation set is not representative of the test distribution, or if the exchangeability assumption is severely violated, the coverage guarantee may not hold.

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: The calibration threshold varies with the entropy of the next-word distribution, correcting for overconfidence in low-entropy (high certainty) contexts.
- Mechanism: By binning examples by entropy percentile and calibrating each bin separately, the method accounts for the fact that the model's overconfidence is entropy-dependent. Lower entropy (more peaked distributions) leads to higher thresholds q^ to maintain coverage.
- Core assumption: Entropy of the next-word distribution is a reliable proxy for the model's uncertainty in a given context.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "calibration shows a moderate inverse scaling with model size" (implies entropy-dependent behavior)
  - [section 3] "we calibrated the examples belonging to each equally-sized percentile independently... the degree of calibration varies significantly with the entropy of the model's distribution"
  - [corpus] Weak - no direct evidence that entropy is used for calibration in related work
- Break condition: If entropy does not capture the true uncertainty (e.g., due to model artifacts), the per-entropy calibration may not achieve proper coverage.

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: The conformal scores st are designed to measure the minimum threshold needed to include the true word, enabling efficient calibration.
- Mechanism: The conformal score st for example t is defined as the sum of probabilities of words that are at least as probable as the true word: st = sum{i | pt(i)≥pt(yt)} pt(i). This directly measures the minimal set mass needed to cover the true word.
- Core assumption: The model's probability estimates are well-calibrated enough that the true word's rank and probability are meaningful for calibration.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [section 2] "Deﬁne the conformal scores to be: st = sum{i|pt(i)≥pt(yt)}pt(i)"
  - [section 2] "We next look for a minimal threshold ˆq such that the correct labelyt is included in the prediction set Cˆq(xt) for at least (1−α)n points of the validation set"
  - [corpus] Weak - no evidence in related work about this specific conformal score for language models
- Break condition: If the model's probabilities are severely miscalibrated (e.g., due to training artifacts), the conformal scores may not accurately reflect the true coverage needed.

## Foundational Learning

- Concept: Exchangeability of data
  - Why needed here: Conformal prediction theory relies on the assumption that the validation and test data are exchangeable to provide coverage guarantees.
  - Quick check question: If we permute the order of examples in the validation set, does the coverage guarantee still hold? (Answer: Yes, by definition of exchangeability)

- Concept: Quantile computation
  - Why needed here: The calibration threshold q^ is computed as a quantile of the conformal scores over the validation set.
  - Quick check question: If we have 100 validation examples and want 90% coverage, what is the rank of the threshold in the sorted list of conformal scores? (Answer: The 90th score)

- Concept: Entropy as uncertainty measure
  - Why needed here: The method bins examples by entropy percentile to account for context-dependent overconfidence.
  - Quick check question: For a deterministic distribution (one word with probability 1), what is the entropy? (Answer: 0)

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map:
  Language model (OPT variants) -> Entropy computation module -> Entropy binning module -> Conformal prediction module -> Validation set processor -> Generation interface

- Critical path:
  1. Compute next-word distributions from language model
  2. Calculate entropy for each distribution
  3. Assign examples to entropy bins
  4. Compute conformal scores on validation set
  5. Determine per-bin thresholds q^
  6. During generation, apply appropriate q^ based on current entropy

- Design tradeoffs:
  - Per-entropy-bin calibration vs. global calibration: Per-bin allows better correction for context-dependent overconfidence but requires more validation data and computation
  - Threshold computation frequency: Computing thresholds online vs. pre-computing and storing for common entropy ranges
  - Entropy binning strategy: Equal-width vs. equal-count bins for handling skewed entropy distributions

- Failure signatures:
  - Calibration threshold q^ much larger than nominal p indicates severe overconfidence
  - High variance in q^ across entropy bins suggests context-dependent miscalibration
  - Coverage below nominal p even after calibration indicates exchangeability violation or insufficient validation data

- First 3 experiments:
  1. Compute conformal scores on validation set and plot their distribution to understand the magnitude of miscalibration
  2. For a small set of examples, manually verify that the calibrated thresholds q^ do indeed achieve the target coverage
  3. Generate text with both standard p-sampling and calibrated conformal p-sampling and qualitatively compare the outputs

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
The paper identifies several open questions for future research: (1) How does calibration performance vary across different language domains and datasets beyond Wikipedia? (2) What other measures of uncertainty beyond entropy could be used to calibrate nucleus sampling, and how would they compare in effectiveness? (3) How does the inverse scaling relationship between model size and calibration performance manifest across different model architectures and training objectives?

## Limitations
- The calibration approach relies on the exchangeability assumption between validation and test data, which may not hold for domain-shifted text
- The method only tested on Wikipedia data, limiting generalizability to other domains and datasets
- Entropy is used as the sole proxy for uncertainty without exploring alternative measures that might capture different aspects of model miscalibration

## Confidence

**High Confidence**: The methodology for computing conformal prediction thresholds is clearly described and follows established procedures. The observation that OPT models are overconfident is well-supported by the empirical results showing effective confidence below nominal p values across all model sizes.

**Medium Confidence**: The entropy-dependent calibration mechanism is supported by the observation that miscalibration varies with entropy, but the paper does not establish a causal relationship or rule out alternative explanations. The effectiveness of the calibration in improving generation quality (MAUVE, BERTScore) is demonstrated but with modest effect sizes.

**Low Confidence**: The generalizability of the calibration approach to other model architectures (beyond OPT), domains (beyond Wikipedia), or decoding strategies remains unproven. The paper does not investigate whether the calibration is robust to different tokenization schemes or subword vocabulary sizes.

## Next Checks

1. **Coverage Stability Analysis**: Run the calibration procedure multiple times with different random seeds for validation set splits and measure the variance in computed thresholds q^ across runs. This would quantify the stability of the calibration and identify whether the method is sensitive to the particular validation set chosen.

2. **Domain Transfer Experiment**: Apply the conformal calibration trained on Wikipedia data to generate text from a different domain (e.g., news articles or scientific papers) and measure whether the coverage guarantees transfer. This would test the exchangeability assumption and reveal domain-specific miscalibration patterns.

3. **Alternative Uncertainty Measures**: Replace entropy with alternative uncertainty metrics (e.g., mutual information, variance of predictive distribution, or ensemble disagreement) and compare the calibration quality achieved. This would validate whether entropy is the most effective proxy for model uncertainty in this context.
