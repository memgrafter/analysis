# Extending Kernel PCA through Dualization: Sparsity, Robustness and Fast Algorithms

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2306.05815
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.05815
- **Reference count:** 40
- **Primary result:** Reformulates KPCA as a difference of convex functions to avoid expensive Gram matrix SVD, achieving 3.7-9.2× speedups over standard methods with simultaneous robustness and sparsity capabilities.

## Executive Summary
This paper introduces a dual formulation of Kernel PCA (KPCA) that reformulates the optimization as a difference of convex functions, enabling efficient gradient-based optimization that avoids the expensive SVD of the Gram matrix. The approach naturally extends KPCA to incorporate robustness and sparsity objectives through Moreau envelope losses (Huber and ε-insensitive). Experimental results demonstrate significant computational speedups over standard SVD and randomized SVD methods, with the framework achieving 40% sparsity with only 10% increase in reconstruction error while maintaining robustness to outliers.

## Method Summary
The method reformulates KPCA as a difference of convex functions (DC) problem by introducing a dual variable H, which transforms the original problem into minimizing Tr√(H^T G H) subject to rank constraints. This dual formulation allows gradient-based optimization using LBFGS, avoiding the cubic complexity of Gram matrix SVD. For Moreau envelope objectives, the DC algorithm alternates between gradient steps on the convex part and proximal steps on the concave part. The framework supports Huber loss for robustness and ε-insensitive loss for sparsity, with closed-form proximal operators for common norms.

## Key Results
- Achieves 3.7-9.2× speedups over standard SVD and randomized SVD methods for moderate accuracy requirements
- Demonstrates 3.4-4.2× speedups for high accuracy requirements compared to randomized SVD
- Achieves 40% sparsity with only 10% increase in reconstruction error using ε-insensitive loss
- Robust Huber losses show lower reconstruction error on contaminated datasets compared to standard KPCA

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- **Claim:** The DC formulation of KPCA allows efficient gradient-based optimization by avoiding the expensive SVD of the Gram matrix.
- **Mechanism:** By reformulating KPCA as the minimization of a difference of convex functions, the dual problem involves optimizing over a finite-dimensional matrix H, whose gradient can be computed efficiently without full SVD.
- **Core assumption:** The Gram matrix G is full rank, which ensures the gradient expression for π(H) is well-defined and differentiable.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract]: "This allows to naturally extend KPCA to multiple objective functions and leads to efficient gradient-based algorithms avoiding the expensive SVD of the Gram matrix."
  - [section]: "The main motivation for going from the primal problem to the dual in the KPCA case is that the dual variable H is a finite dimensional matrix, suitable to gradient-based optimization schemes."
  - [corpus]: No direct evidence. Related works like "Invertible Kernel PCA with Random Fourier Features" focus on reconstruction but not on avoiding SVD.
- **Break condition:** If the Gram matrix is rank-deficient, the gradient computation for π(H) fails due to non-positive eigenvalues, making the optimization unstable.

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim:** Moreau envelope structure enables simultaneous robustness and sparsity in KPCA objectives.
- **Mechanism:** Using Moreau envelopes of norms (Huber, ε-insensitive) in the primal leads to Fenchel-Legendre transforms that constrain the dual variables, promoting robustness or sparsity.
- **Core assumption:** The choice of norm in the Moreau envelope aligns with the desired property (e.g., ℓ1 for sparsity, ℓ2 for robustness).
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract]: "Particularly, we consider objective functions that can be written as Moreau envelopes, demonstrating how to promote robustness and sparsity within the same framework."

- **Break condition:** If the norm choice does not align with the desired property, the induced constraints may not effectively promote robustness or sparsity.

- **Claim:** The DC algorithm with proximal operators efficiently solves the modified KPCA objectives.
- **Mechanism:** Alternating gradient steps on π(H) and proximal steps on Ψ* allow solving the DC problem, with closed-form projections for common norms.

## Limitations
- The method assumes the Gram matrix is full rank for gradient computation, which may not hold for small datasets or inappropriate kernels
- No theoretical convergence guarantees provided for the non-convex DC optimization problem
- The speedups demonstrated are primarily on specific datasets where RSVD performs poorly due to slowly decaying eigenspectra
