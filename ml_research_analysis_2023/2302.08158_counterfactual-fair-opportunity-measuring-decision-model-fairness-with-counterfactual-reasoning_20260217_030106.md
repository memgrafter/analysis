# Counterfactual Fair Opportunity: Measuring Decision Model Fairness with Counterfactual Reasoning

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2302.08158
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2302.08158
- Reference count: 31
- Primary result: Introduces CFlips and nDCCF metrics to quantify discrimination in models trained without sensitive features, revealing that proxy features enable biased decisions

## Executive Summary
This paper addresses the challenge of detecting discrimination in machine learning models that intentionally exclude sensitive attributes (fairness under unawareness). The authors propose a counterfactual fairness framework that generates alternative samples with flipped decision outcomes and evaluates whether these also flip the predicted sensitive attribute. Two novel metrics, CFlips and nDCCF, quantify the degree to which models use proxy features to encode sensitive information. Experiments across multiple datasets demonstrate that even when sensitive features are removed, classifiers can infer demographic information from other correlated features, leading to discriminatory outcomes.

## Method Summary
The method involves training a black-box Decision Maker classifier without sensitive features, then generating counterfactual samples that flip negative predictions to positive ones using the DiCE framework. A separate Sensitive-Feature Classifier predicts the omitted sensitive attribute from non-sensitive features. The CFlips metric measures the percentage of counterfactuals that flip the sensitive prediction, while nDCCF provides a distance-aware ranking-based measure. The approach is evaluated on four datasets (Adult, Adult-debiased, Crime, German) using various classifiers including logistic regression, SVM, decision trees, random forests, XGBoost, and debiasing models. Results are analyzed using both the proposed metrics and traditional fairness metrics like DSP, DEO, and DAO.

## Key Results
- Removing sensitive features does not prevent discrimination, as proxy features enable classifiers to infer sensitive information
- The proposed CFlips and nDCCF metrics effectively reveal discriminatory behavior in fairness under unawareness settings
- Debiasing models perform best when sensitive information is well-represented in the data, while traditional models like SVM achieve lower discrimination in other cases
- The metrics are robust to different counterfactual generation strategies and classifier choices

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
Counterfactual Fair Opportunity definition allows detection of bias when sensitive attributes are removed. By generating counterfactual samples that flip the decision outcome, the method checks if sensitive feature predictions also flip, revealing proxy feature discrimination. Core assumption: Sensitive-Feature Classifier can infer omitted sensitive attributes from non-sensitive features. Evidence anchors: [abstract] "proposes a counterfactual fairness method for detecting discrimination when sensitive attributes are removed"; [section 4] "each counterfactual (CF) sample is evaluated by the Sensitive-Feature Classifier that predicts the value of the (omitted) sensitive feature"; [corpus] Weak - related papers discuss counterfactual fairness but not this specific unawareness setting. Break condition: If Sensitive-Feature Classifier accuracy is too low, metric values become unreliable.

### Mechanism 2
CFlips metric quantifies discrimination by measuring percentage of counterfactuals with flipped sensitive predictions. For each negatively-predicted sample, generates counterfactuals with positive outcomes and counts how often sensitive predictions change. Core assumption: Counterfactuals maintain similar characteristics except for decision outcome. Evidence anchors: [section 4] "Counterfactual Flips indicate the percentage of counterfactual samples belonging to another demographic group"; [section 6.2] "CFlips metric indicates how often a change of result for the Decision Maker corresponds to a change in the classification of the sensitive feature"; [corpus] Weak - no direct corpus evidence for this specific metric formulation. Break condition: If counterfactual generation produces samples too dissimilar from originals, metric loses interpretability.

### Mechanism 3
nDCCF metric provides distance-aware fairness measurement by ranking counterfactuals and discounting based on position. Ranks counterfactuals by proximity to original sample and calculates cumulative gain, penalizing flips in top positions. Core assumption: Counterfactuals closer to original sample are more informative about decision boundary behavior. Evidence anchors: [section 4] "nDCCF metric gives more importance to counterfactuals with highest positions in the ranking"; [section 6.2] "DCCF rewards the CF samples in the ranking that did not flip"; [corpus] Weak - related papers discuss counterfactual ranking but not this specific normalized formulation. Break condition: If ranking criterion poorly captures meaningful similarity, metric becomes misleading.

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map:
  Decision Maker -> Counterfactual Generator (DiCE) -> Sensitive-Feature Classifier -> Metric Calculator (CFlips and nDCCF)

- Critical path:
  1. Decision Maker predicts negative outcome
  2. Counterfactual Generator creates positive-outcome samples
  3. Sensitive-Feature Classifier predicts sensitive attributes
  4. Metrics compute discrimination scores

- Design tradeoffs:
  - Genetic strategy explores new space but may create unrealistic samples
  - KDtree uses existing samples for realism but limited exploration
  - More counterfactuals improve stability but increase computation

- Failure signatures:
  - Low Sensitive-Feature Classifier accuracy → unreliable metrics
  - All counterfactuals fail to flip outcome → insufficient exploration
  - Very high CFlips on privileged group → potential implementation error

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
How does the performance of nDCCF vary with different counterfactual ranking criteria beyond the absolute difference in expected predictions? The paper mentions that nDCCF could be affected by the ranking criterion of the counterfactual generator and suggests investigating alternative ranking strategies. The paper only tests one alternative ranking criterion (absolute difference in expected predictions) and doesn't compare it to the original utility function used by the counterfactual generator. Comparative experiments showing nDCCF performance with multiple counterfactual ranking criteria (e.g., Euclidean distance, Mahalanobis distance, model-specific utility functions) across different datasets and decision models would resolve this.

### Open Question 2
What is the relationship between the number of counterfactuals generated and the stability of CFlips and nDCCF metrics across different decision boundaries? The ablation study shows that CFlips and nDCCF stabilize after 20 counterfactuals for most models, but LFERM shows increasing values with more counterfactuals, suggesting distance-based effects. The paper doesn't explore the mathematical relationship between counterfactual distance, decision boundary curvature, and metric stability, nor does it test this across diverse model types with varying decision boundary complexities. Theoretical analysis and empirical validation showing how decision boundary properties (e.g., smoothness, curvature) affect the convergence behavior of counterfactual-based fairness metrics would resolve this.

### Open Question 3
Can the proxy feature detection method be extended to identify intersectional discrimination patterns across multiple sensitive attributes simultaneously? The paper analyzes individual sensitive features (gender, race) separately and identifies features that correlate with flips in single sensitive attributes, but doesn't explore combinations of sensitive attributes. The methodology focuses on univariate analysis of sensitive features, and the Pearson correlation approach used for detecting proxy features would need modification to handle multi-dimensional sensitive attribute interactions. Experimental results demonstrating the methodology's effectiveness in detecting intersectional proxy features across datasets with multiple sensitive attributes, along with statistical validation of the findings, would resolve this.

## Limitations
- Sensitive-Feature Classifier accuracy directly affects metric reliability, but comprehensive validation is lacking
- Counterfactual generation may produce unrealistic samples, particularly with Genetic strategy
- The claim that debiasing models perform best when sensitive information is well-represented lacks strong empirical support

## Confidence
- **High Confidence**: The mathematical formulation of CFlips and nDCCF metrics is clearly defined and the experimental methodology is reproducible
- **Medium Confidence**: The claim that proxy features enable discrimination even when sensitive attributes are removed is well-supported by experiments
- **Low Confidence**: The assertion that debiasing models perform best when sensitive information is well-represented lacks strong empirical support

## Next Checks
1. **Classifier Performance Validation**: Verify that the Sensitive-Feature Classifier achieves AUC > 0.8 on held-out data for each dataset before computing fairness metrics
2. **Counterfactual Realism Assessment**: Manually inspect feature distributions for a subset of generated counterfactuals to ensure they remain realistic and within valid ranges
3. **Metric Sensitivity Testing**: Create synthetic datasets with known proxy feature discrimination patterns to verify that CFlips and nDCCF metrics correctly identify the presence and degree of bias
