# Strategy Extraction in Single-Agent Games

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2305.12623
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.12623
- Authors: Multiple authors (specific names not provided)
- Reference count: 26
- Primary result: A novel method combining event frequency analysis with modified Smith-Waterman sequence alignment to extract transferable strategies from single-agent game trajectories.

## Executive Summary
This paper introduces a novel approach for extracting transferable knowledge from agent behavior in single-agent environments by leveraging observed event frequencies and a modified Smith-Waterman sequence alignment algorithm. The method identifies partial plans or strategies from trajectory data by comparing positive and negative event sequences, then clustering and aligning similar trajectories. Demonstrated across three video game environments, the approach successfully identifies plausible strategies such as collecting power-ups to defeat enemies, showing robustness and reasonable strategy candidates. Performance depends on sample size and balanced trajectory collection.

## Method Summary
The method extracts strategies from single-agent game trajectories by first training reinforcement learning agents to generate positive trajectories containing events of interest, and negative trajectories from random policies. Event frequencies are calculated in both trajectory sets, and negative trajectory lengths are normalized to match positive distributions. A modified Smith-Waterman local sequence alignment algorithm is then applied to identify high-scoring subsequences that form strategy candidates. Clustering by shared events ensures multiple distinct strategies are uncovered for the same goal. The approach is demonstrated on Pacman, Dungeon Crawler, and Bank Heist environments.

## Key Results
- Successfully extracted strategies like "collect power-up → kill ghost" from Pacman trajectories
- Method shows robustness across different sample sizes, though performance improves with more trajectories
- Clustering approach enables discovery of multiple distinct strategies for the same event of interest
- Length normalization improves frequency-based likelihood estimation accuracy

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: Modified Smith-Waterman local sequence alignment can identify partial plans from event trajectories that capture strategy-relevant sequences.
- Mechanism: The algorithm aligns two trajectories and scores matches, mismatches, and gaps based on event frequency weights. High-scoring subsequences are retained as strategy candidates, allowing the method to ignore irrelevant events and focus on goal-relevant ones.
- Core assumption: Events that occur more frequently in positive (goal-achieved) trajectories than negative (goal-not-achieved) trajectories are likely part of the strategy.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract]: "Our method combines observed event frequency information with local sequence alignment techniques to find patterns of significance that form a strategy."
  - [section 4.4]: "If 𝑒 occurs considerably more often in the positive samples than in the negative samples, its likelihood of appearing in the strategy is very high."
  - [corpus]: Weak; no direct citations, only algorithm similarity.
- Break condition: If event frequency differences are small or noisy, likelihood estimates become unreliable and the alignment cannot distinguish meaningful strategy components.

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: Normalizing negative trajectory lengths to match positive trajectory lengths reduces bias in frequency-based likelihood estimation.
- Mechanism: By sampling subsequences of equal length from negative trajectories, the method ensures that long, random exploration paths do not inflate the frequency of low-importance events, making the likelihood difference calculation more accurate.
- Core assumption: Without length normalization, longer negative trajectories would contain more total events, artificially increasing the frequency of common events in the negative set.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [section 4.3]: "To solve this problem, 𝜏𝑁 is normalised to ensure its length distribution matches that of 𝜏𝑃."
  - [section 4.4]: "Depending on the environment and event of interest, there could be significant variation between the length distributions of 𝜏𝑃 and 𝜏𝑁."
  - [corpus]: Weak; normalization is standard in comparative sequence analysis but not cited.
- Break condition: If the environment has highly variable episode lengths unrelated to strategy, normalization may remove meaningful variation and obscure strategy cues.

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: Clustering trajectories by contained events before pairwise alignment allows extraction of multiple distinct strategies for the same goal.
- Mechanism: Each cluster contains trajectories sharing a common event; within each cluster, the shortest trajectory is aligned against all others. This ensures different strategy variants (e.g., using different tools or orders) are not merged into one overly general strategy.
- Core assumption: Different successful trajectories to the same goal can be meaningfully grouped by their intermediate events, and strategies are localized within such groups.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [section 4.5]: "The aim of clustering is to ensure we uncover patterns when there are many different versions of trajectories that achieve the same 𝐸."
  - [section 5]: "In the Dungeon Crawler environment... multiple possible strategies were obtained for the 'unlock door' event of interest."
  - [corpus]: Weak; clustering for strategy diversity is inferred, not directly cited.
- Break condition: If trajectories are too diverse or share few common events, clustering may produce many tiny clusters with insufficient samples for meaningful alignment.

## Foundational Learning

- Concept: Reinforcement Learning policy training in discrete action spaces
  - Why needed here: The method assumes access to a pre-trained RL agent whose trajectories are used to extract strategies; understanding RL training is essential for generating valid trajectories.
  - Quick check question: What is the difference between on-policy and off-policy RL, and why would on-policy trajectories be preferable for strategy extraction?

- Concept: Sequence alignment algorithms (Smith-Waterman)
  - Why needed here: The core novelty is adapting Smith-Waterman for strategy extraction; understanding its scoring and traceback mechanics is necessary to modify it for event sequences.
  - Quick check question: How does the Smith-Waterman algorithm differ from Needleman-Wunsch, and why is local alignment more suitable for finding partial plans?

- Concept: Frequency-based likelihood estimation
  - Why needed here: The method uses event occurrence frequencies in positive vs. negative trajectories to compute likelihood scores; understanding this statistical approach is key to interpreting the algorithm's filtering step.
  - Quick check question: If an event appears in 80% of positive trajectories and 20% of negative trajectories, what is its likelihood value according to the paper's formula?

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map:
  - Event of interest detection (reward-based heuristic) -> Trajectory collection (positive from trained policy, negative from random policy) -> Length normalization module -> Likelihood calculation module -> Clustering module (by shared events) -> Strategy extraction module (pairwise Smith-Waterman alignment) -> Result aggregation and filtering
- Critical path:
  1. Train PPO agent → collect 𝜏𝐻
  2. Identify events of interest → select 𝐸
  3. Generate 𝜏𝑃 and 𝜏𝑁
  4. Normalize 𝜏𝑁 → compute likelihoods
  5. Filter 𝜏𝑃 → cluster → pairwise align → extract strategies
- Design tradeoffs:
  - Length normalization vs. preserving full trajectory context
  - Fixed likelihood threshold vs. adaptive thresholding per environment
  - Clustering granularity vs. risk of over-fragmentation
- Failure signatures:
  - All events have low likelihood → poor separation between positive/negative trajectories
  - Very few or no clusters formed → trajectories too diverse or too similar
  - Extracted strategies contain mostly gaps or irrelevant events → alignment scoring mis-specified
- First 3 experiments:
  1. Run on Pacman with a small, fixed map and collect 50 positive/50 negative trajectories; verify strategy "collect power-up → kill ghost" appears.
  2. Vary sample size (10, 50, 100) in Dungeon Crawler; observe changes in event likelihood distribution and extracted strategies.
  3. Disable normalization step and compare extracted strategies; confirm that normalization improves strategy quality.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- Question: How does the performance of the strategy extraction method scale with increasing trajectory sample size, and is there an optimal sample size that balances computational cost with extraction accuracy?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper investigates the effect of trajectory sample size on the strategies extracted, observing that the sample size influences event likelihood values and that there is a positive correlation between the average time taken to run the extraction approach and the sample size of the trajectories.
- Why unresolved: The paper only examines sample sizes of 10, 20, 50, 100, and 200 trajectories. The impact of sample sizes beyond 200 or between these values is not explored. Additionally, the trade-off between computational cost and accuracy is not quantified.
- What evidence would resolve it: Experiments varying the sample size across a wider range, measuring both the accuracy of extracted strategies and the computational time, would help identify an optimal sample size. Statistical analysis of the relationship between sample size and performance metrics would be beneficial.

### Open Question 2
- Question: How robust is the strategy extraction method to variations in the event representation and reward structure across different environments?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper mentions that the proposed method is not limited to reinforcement learning agents and that the form of strategies can be flexible depending on the type of learning agent. It also notes that the method is designed to be game-agnostic, using likelihood values instead of rewards to avoid dependence on the reward scheme of the game.
- Why unresolved: The experiments are conducted on a limited set of custom video game environments with specific event representations and reward structures. The method's performance on environments with different event representations (e.g., continuous states) or reward structures (e.g., sparse rewards) is not evaluated.
- What evidence would resolve it: Applying the strategy extraction method to a diverse set of environments with varying event representations and reward structures, and comparing the extracted strategies to ground truth or human-generated strategies, would assess its robustness.

### Open Question 3
- Question: What are the most effective techniques for generalizing and transferring extracted strategies to new domains with differing action and state spaces and environmental dynamics?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper acknowledges that future work will focus on utilizing the extracted strategies and exploring generalization techniques for transfer to domains with differing action and state spaces and environmental dynamics. It mentions considering generalization via abstraction and using ontologies to determine how environment-specific events within the strategies can be lifted to a high-level representation.
- Why unresolved: The paper does not provide any concrete methods or experiments for generalizing and transferring strategies. The challenges of mapping between different state and action spaces, and adapting strategies to new environmental dynamics, are not addressed.
- What evidence would resolve it: Developing and evaluating specific techniques for generalizing and transferring strategies, such as using ontologies, reward shaping, or policy reuse, and testing them on a variety of target domains, would provide insights into the most effective approaches.

## Limitations
- Event definition sensitivity: The method relies heavily on accurate identification of events of interest, and noisy or ambiguous event detection can make likelihood calculations unreliable.
- Sample size dependency: Strategy extraction quality degrades significantly with small trajectory samples, limiting applicability to data-scarce domains.
- Algorithm adaptation specificity: The modified Smith-Waterman algorithm is custom-built for this application, making exact reproduction challenging without open-source implementation details.

## Confidence
- **High confidence**: The core mechanism of combining event frequency analysis with sequence alignment is sound and theoretically grounded. The length normalization approach is standard in comparative sequence analysis.
- **Medium confidence**: The clustering approach for handling multiple strategies is reasonable but lacks empirical validation across diverse environments. The likelihood calculation formula is plausible but not rigorously tested against alternative statistical methods.
- **Low confidence**: The generalization claims to unseen environments are based on only three game domains. The paper does not demonstrate transfer to real-world or continuous control tasks.

## Next Checks
1. **Cross-environment robustness test**: Apply the method to a continuous control environment (e.g., LunarLander) and verify whether the strategy extraction remains meaningful when event boundaries are less discrete.
2. **Ablation study on normalization**: Systematically vary the normalization ratio between positive and negative trajectory lengths and measure impact on strategy quality using precision/recall metrics.
3. **Sample size sensitivity analysis**: Create a systematic grid of trajectory sample sizes (10, 25, 50, 100, 200) and plot strategy extraction quality metrics to identify the minimum viable sample size for each environment.
