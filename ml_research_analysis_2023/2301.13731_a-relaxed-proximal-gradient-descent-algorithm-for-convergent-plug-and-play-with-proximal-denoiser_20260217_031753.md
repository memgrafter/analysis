# A relaxed proximal gradient descent algorithm for convergent plug-and-play with proximal denoiser

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2301.13731
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2301.13731

## Executive Summary
This paper introduces a new Plug-and-Play (PnP) algorithm for solving image inverse problems by minimizing the sum of a data-fidelity term and a regularization term. The key idea is to use a relaxed version of the Proximal Gradient Descent (PGD) algorithm, called αPGD, which allows for a wider range of regularization parameters compared to the standard PGD. The authors show that when plugged with a relaxed proximal denoiser, the proposed PnP-αPGD algorithm converges for any regularization parameter, thus enabling more accurate image restoration. The method is evaluated on both image deblurring and image super-resolution tasks, demonstrating improved performance over existing PnP algorithms.

## Method Summary
The proposed method introduces a relaxed version of the Proximal Gradient Descent (PGD) algorithm, called αPGD, which incorporates a relaxation parameter α to allow for a wider range of regularization parameters. The αPGD algorithm is then integrated into the Plug-and-Play (PnP) framework, where the proximal operator is replaced with a denoiser. The authors demonstrate that when using a relaxed proximal denoiser, the PnP-αPGD algorithm converges for any regularization parameter, thus enabling more accurate image restoration. The method is evaluated on both image deblurring and image super-resolution tasks, showing improved performance over existing PnP algorithms.

## Key Results
- The proposed PnP-αPGD algorithm converges for any regularization parameter when paired with a relaxed proximal denoiser.
- Improved performance over existing PnP algorithms in image deblurring and super-resolution tasks.
- Enables more accurate image restoration compared to standard PGD-based methods.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The success of the PnP-αPGD algorithm stems from the introduction of a relaxation parameter α in the proximal gradient descent step. This relaxation allows for a wider range of regularization parameters, making the algorithm more flexible and adaptable to different inverse problems. By using a relaxed proximal denoiser, the method ensures convergence for any regularization parameter, overcoming a key limitation of traditional PnP methods. The combination of αPGD and the relaxed denoiser provides a robust and convergent framework for image restoration tasks.

## Foundational Learning
- Proximal Gradient Descent (PGD): A first-order optimization method for minimizing the sum of a smooth and a non-smooth function. Why needed: Forms the basis for the proposed αPGD algorithm. Quick check: Understand the update rule and convergence properties of PGD.
- Relaxed Proximal Operator: A modification of the proximal operator that introduces a relaxation parameter. Why needed: Enables a wider range of regularization parameters and ensures convergence. Quick check: Verify the definition and properties of the relaxed proximal operator.
- Plug-and-Play (PnP) Framework: A method that replaces the proximal operator in iterative algorithms with a denoiser. Why needed: Allows the use of pre-trained denoisers for image restoration tasks. Quick check: Understand how the PnP framework is applied to PGD and its convergence properties.

## Limitations
- The theoretical framework assumes access to a well-defined relaxed proximal denoiser, which may not always be readily available or straightforward to implement in practice.

- The evaluation is limited to image deblurring and super-resolution tasks, leaving questions about generalizability to other inverse problems.
