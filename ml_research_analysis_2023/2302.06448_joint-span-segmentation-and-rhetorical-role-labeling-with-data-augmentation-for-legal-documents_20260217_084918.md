# Joint Span Segmentation and Rhetorical Role Labeling with Data Augmentation for Legal Documents

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2302.06448
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2302.06448
- Reference count: 34
- Primary result: Semi-Markov CRF improves span-level RRL performance when documents contain multi-sentence spans, with data augmentation providing additional gains

## Executive Summary
This paper addresses rhetorical role labeling (RRL) in legal documents by proposing a joint span segmentation and labeling approach using semi-Markov Conditional Random Fields (CRFs). Unlike prior sentence-level classification methods, this approach treats RRL as a span-level sequential classification task, allowing the model to capture semantically coherent multi-sentence spans with the same rhetorical role. The method employs a hierarchical architecture combining BERT encoding, BiLSTM layers, and attention pooling, with data augmentation strategies to address data scarcity. Experiments show that the semi-Markov CRF model outperforms standard CRF baselines on span-level metrics, particularly when documents contain multi-sentence spans, and that data augmentation further improves performance at both span and sentence levels.

## Method Summary
The proposed method casts rhetorical role labeling as a span-level sequential classification task using semi-Markov CRFs. The approach employs a hierarchical architecture with BERT encoding at the word level, followed by BiLSTM layers and attention pooling to generate sentence-level representations. These representations are then processed by the semi-Markov CRF, which jointly learns span segmentation and labeling. The model handles the conditional independence assumption violation in standard CRFs by allowing spans to have variable lengths, enabling it to predict labels at the span level rather than individual sentences. Three data augmentation strategies are explored: word deletion, back-translation, and sentence swapping, aimed at mitigating data scarcity issues in legal document RRL.

## Key Results
- Semi-Markov CRF model improves span-level prediction metrics compared to standard CRF baseline, particularly for documents with multi-sentence spans
- Data augmentation strategies (word deletion, back-translation, sentence swapping) further improve performance at both span and sentence levels
- Performance benefits of span-level modeling are contingent on the presence of multi-sentence spans, as demonstrated through comparison of two datasets with different span characteristics

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The semi-Markov CRF approach works because legal documents often contain large sections of consecutive sentences with the same rhetorical role, which cannot be effectively captured by sentence-level classification methods. By allowing variable-length spans, the model can better represent the semantic coherence of legal document structures. The hierarchical architecture enables rich contextual representations at both word and sentence levels, while the semi-Markov formulation directly optimizes for the desired output structure. Data augmentation helps address the scarcity of annotated legal documents by artificially expanding the training data, though care must be taken to preserve legal domain characteristics during augmentation.

## Foundational Learning

**Semi-Markov CRFs**: Why needed - to handle variable-length spans in sequential data; Quick check - can model spans of arbitrary length rather than fixed-size segments

**Hierarchical document representation**: Why needed - legal documents require both local (word-level) and global (sentence-level) context; Quick check - attention pooling effectively combines local features into sentence representations

**Data augmentation in NLP**: Why needed - legal RRL datasets are typically small; Quick check - augmentation strategies preserve semantic meaning while increasing data diversity

**BERT embeddings**: Why needed - provide rich pre-trained contextual representations for legal text; Quick check - domain-specific fine-tuning improves performance on legal RRL task

**Rhetorical role labeling**: Why needed - essential for legal document understanding and information extraction; Quick check - accurate span segmentation improves downstream legal applications

## Architecture Onboarding

Component map: BERT -> BiLSTM -> Attention Pooling -> Semi-Markov CRF

Critical path: The critical path flows from BERT word embeddings through BiLSTM layers to generate contextualized representations, then through attention pooling to create sentence-level representations, which are finally processed by the semi-Markov CRF for joint span segmentation and labeling.

Design tradeoffs: The semi-Markov CRF trades computational complexity for the ability to model variable-length spans, which is crucial for legal documents but may be unnecessary for domains with predominantly single-sentence spans. The hierarchical architecture adds complexity but enables richer representations compared to flat models.

Failure signatures: The model may struggle with documents containing many short spans or highly variable span lengths, where the semi-Markov assumption becomes less effective. It may also be sensitive to the quality of sentence boundaries and could be affected by domain shift in legal terminology.

## Limitations

- The superiority of semi-Markov CRFs is primarily demonstrated on datasets with multi-sentence spans, with limited analysis of scenarios where sentence-level classification might be equally effective
- Data augmentation strategies may not generalize well across different legal domains or languages, potentially disrupting domain-specific legal language patterns
- The analysis focuses on two datasets without exploring the broader distribution of span lengths across various legal document types
