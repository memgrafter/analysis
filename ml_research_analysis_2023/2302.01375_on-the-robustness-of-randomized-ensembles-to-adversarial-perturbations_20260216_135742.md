# On the Robustness of Randomized Ensembles to Adversarial Perturbations

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2302.01375
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2302.01375
- Reference count: 40
- This paper provides theoretical results on the adversarial robustness of randomized ensemble classifiers (RECs) and proposes BARRE, a boosting algorithm for training robust RECs.

## Executive Summary
This paper investigates the theoretical foundations and practical implementation of randomized ensemble classifiers (RECs) for adversarial robustness. The authors establish fundamental limits on REC robustness and introduce BARRE, a novel boosting algorithm that trains ensembles by sequentially creating diverse, robust classifiers. Theoretical analysis reveals that randomization helps when individual classifiers have similar robustness levels and when perturbations that fool one classifier don't easily fool others. Empirical results on CIFAR-10 and CIFAR-100 show BARRE-trained RECs achieve competitive robustness to state-of-the-art deterministic ensembles while requiring 2-4x less computation.

## Method Summary
The paper proposes BARRE, a boosting algorithm for training randomized ensemble classifiers that combines adversarial training with ensemble diversity. BARRE initializes each new classifier from the previous one and trains it on adversarial examples generated from the current ensemble, forcing different decision boundaries while maintaining robustness. The algorithm alternates between classifier training using APGD attacks and optimal sampling probability (OSP) updates to minimize adversarial risk. Evaluation uses the ARC attack with randomized class order loop against ℓ∞ norm-bounded adversaries (ϵ=8/255) on CIFAR-10 and CIFAR-100 datasets using ResNet and MobileNet architectures.

## Key Results
- BARRE-trained RECs achieve robust accuracy within ~0.5% of MRBoost-trained deterministic ensembles on CIFAR-10/100
- RECs require 2-4x less computation than deterministic ensembles for comparable robustness
- Theoretical bounds show randomization helps when classifiers have similar individual risks and when adversarial perturbations don't easily transfer between classifiers
- Optimal sampling probability can be computed exactly for small ensembles (M≤3) but requires sub-gradient methods for larger ensembles

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: Randomized ensemble classifiers (RECs) improve adversarial robustness when individual classifier risks are similar and perturbations that fool one classifier don't easily fool others.
- Mechanism: RECs randomly select one classifier from an ensemble during inference. This randomization reduces the adversary's ability to predict which classifier will be used, making it harder to craft effective adversarial examples.
- Core assumption: The ensemble contains diverse classifiers with different decision boundaries, and no single classifier is significantly more vulnerable than others.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "Recent methods, such as RobBoost [AAK21] and MRBoost [ZZC+22], formulate the design of robust ensembles from a margin boosting perspective, achieving state-of-the-art robustness for deterministic ensemble methods."
  - [section] "Theorem 1 states that randomization is guaranteed to help when the condition in (8) is satisfied, i.e., when the probability of data-points z (P {z ∈ R1}) for which it is possible to find adversarial perturbations that can fool f1 or f2 but not both (see configuration 1 in Fig. 3), is greater than the absolute difference (|η1 −η2|) of the individual classifiers' adversarial risks."
- Break condition: If one classifier has significantly worse robustness than others, or if adversarial perturbations transfer easily between classifiers, randomization provides minimal benefit.

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: BARRE training algorithm creates diverse classifiers by training subsequent classifiers to be robust against adversarial examples of the current REC.
- Mechanism: BARRE initializes each new classifier from the previous one and trains it on adversarial examples generated from the current ensemble, forcing it to develop different decision boundaries while maintaining robustness.
- Core assumption: Training on adversarial examples of the ensemble creates diversity without sacrificing robustness.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [section] "Including f(·|θm) in the attack (Line (8)) is crucial, as it ensures that the robustness of f(·|θm) is not completely compromised, thereby improving the bounds in Theorem 3."
  - [section] "BARRE does so by initially adversarially training a robust classifier f1 (minimizing η1), then training f2 (initialized from f1 to minimizes η2) on the adversarial examples of the REC of f1 and f2."
- Break condition: If the initialization from previous classifiers creates too much similarity, or if the training attack fails to generate diverse adversarial examples.

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: Optimal sampling probability calculation ensures the ensemble achieves minimum adversarial risk.
- Mechanism: The OSP algorithm finds the sampling probability that minimizes the piece-wise linear convex adversarial risk function, either through enumeration for small ensembles or sub-gradient optimization for larger ones.
- Core assumption: The adversarial risk function is convex and can be optimized to find the minimum.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [section] "Proposition 1 allows us to re-write the adversarial risk in (3) using the analytic form in (4), which is much simpler to analyze and work with."
  - [section] "The convexity of η implies that any local minimum α∗ is also a global minimum."
- Break condition: If the adversarial risk function is not accurately estimated (e.g., due to insufficient training data or weak attack during optimization), the optimal sampling probability may be suboptimal.

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map:
  Classifiers (f1, f2, ..., fM) -> Attack oracle -> OSP algorithm -> BARRE training loop

- Critical path:
  1. Initialize f1 with adversarial training
  2. For each subsequent classifier fm:
     - Initialize from fm-1
     - Train using adversarial examples from current REC
     - Update sampling probability via OSP every Eo epochs
  3. Evaluate final REC using strong attack (ARC)

- Design tradeoffs:
  - Number of classifiers (M): More classifiers can improve robustness but increase memory/compute
  - Training attack strength: Stronger attacks create more robust classifiers but may slow training
  - OSP frequency: More frequent updates can find better sampling probabilities but add computational overhead

- Failure signatures:
  - All classifiers have similar decision boundaries: REC provides minimal benefit
  - One classifier is significantly less robust: Optimal sampling probability may ignore it
  - Training overfits to specific attack: Poor generalization to new adversarial examples

- First 3 experiments:
  1. Train REC with M=2 using BARRE and compare to single adversarially trained model
  2. Vary the number of classifiers (M=2,3,4) and measure robustness/compute tradeoff
  3. Replace BARRE training with independent adversarial training and compare results

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- Question: What is the theoretical limit on adversarial robustness for randomized ensembles with more than 3 classifiers?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper derives bounds for M=2 and M=3 classifiers, but states that for larger M the optimal sampling probability becomes intractable to compute exactly
- Why unresolved: The paper only provides theoretical bounds for small ensemble sizes (M≤3) and states that finding optimal sampling probability for larger ensembles requires sub-gradient methods
- What evidence would resolve it: Mathematical derivation of tight bounds for arbitrary M, or empirical validation showing how close practical solutions come to theoretical limits

### Open Question 2
- Question: Why does using ARC as the training attack in BARRE lead to poor generalization performance compared to APGD?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper observes that ARC leads to significantly more accurate RECs on clean images but much lower robust accuracy on test sets compared to APGD
- Why unresolved: The paper hypothesizes that adversarial samples generated via ARC during training don't generalize well but doesn't provide a complete explanation
- What evidence would resolve it: Empirical studies comparing the distribution of adversarial examples generated by ARC vs APGD and their effects on training dynamics

### Open Question 3
- Question: How do the theoretical bounds on REC robustness change when considering different perturbation sets beyond ℓ∞ norm-bounded adversaries?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper's theoretical results hold for any closed and bounded perturbation set S, not just ℓ∞ balls
- Why unresolved: The paper only empirically evaluates robustness against ℓ∞ norm-bounded adversaries
- What evidence would resolve it: Theoretical analysis of bounds under different perturbation sets and/or empirical evaluation against other attack types (e.g., ℓ₂, ℓ₁, or non-ℓp bounded attacks)

## Limitations

- Theoretical analysis is limited to small ensemble sizes (M≤3) where optimal sampling probability can be computed exactly
- Empirical evaluation is restricted to CIFAR-10 and CIFAR-100 datasets with specific network architectures
- The paper doesn't explore alternative initialization strategies for BARRE beyond using previous classifier parameters
- ARC attack as training method shows poor generalization compared to APGD, limiting its practical utility

## Confidence

**High confidence**: Claims about BARRE's computational efficiency compared to deterministic ensembles, supported by FLOPs measurements showing 2-4x reduction in training cost. Claims about the piece-wise linear convex nature of adversarial risk function, backed by Proposition 1.

**Medium confidence**: Claims about REC robustness improvements relative to MRBoost, based on single experiment showing ~0.5% robust accuracy difference. Claims about the theoretical bounds on REC effectiveness, which depend on assumptions that may not hold empirically.

**Low confidence**: Claims about BARRE's performance on larger ensembles (M>2) and with stronger attacks during training, as these configurations were not thoroughly evaluated.
