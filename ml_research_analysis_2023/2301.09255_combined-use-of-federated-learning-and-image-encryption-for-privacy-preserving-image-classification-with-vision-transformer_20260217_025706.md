# Combined Use of Federated Learning and Image Encryption for Privacy-Preserving Image Classification with Vision Transformer

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2301.09255
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2301.09255
- Reference count: 13
- Key outcome: Method achieves 0.977 accuracy on CIFAR-10 and 0.851 on CIFAR-100 using encrypted ViT embeddings with FedAVG, matching baseline performance

## Executive Summary
This paper proposes a novel method combining federated learning (FL) and image encryption for privacy-preserving image classification using Vision Transformers (ViT). The method encrypts both patch and position embeddings during training, while applying inverse transformations to test images, allowing models to be trained across multiple participants without sharing raw data while protecting both training and test data privacy. The approach is evaluated on CIFAR-10 and CIFAR-100 datasets, demonstrating that classification accuracy can be maintained at baseline levels despite encryption.

## Method Summary
The method trains ViT models using FedAVG across multiple clients, each holding private image datasets. After local training, the aggregated model's patch embedding matrix E is encrypted with a random invertible matrix Ea, and position embedding matrix Epos is encrypted with a permutation matrix Eb. Test images are encrypted by permuting their patches using Eb and applying the inverse transformation Ea⁻¹ before inference. This ensures that the encrypted model embeddings and encrypted images cancel out each other's effects during classification, preserving accuracy while maintaining privacy.

## Key Results
- Classification accuracy of 0.977 on CIFAR-10 dataset
- Classification accuracy of 0.851 on CIFAR-100 dataset
- No performance degradation compared to normally trained models
- Successfully preserves privacy of both training data (via FL) and test data (via image encryption)

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: The method preserves classification accuracy by encrypting both the patch embedding matrix (E) and the position embedding matrix (Epos) in the ViT model, while applying inverse transformations to the test images
- Mechanism: Patch embedding E is encrypted with a random invertible matrix Ea, and position embedding Epos is encrypted with a permutation matrix Eb. Test images are permuted using Eb and transformed using Ea⁻¹ before being fed into the model. This ensures that the encrypted embeddings and encrypted images cancel out the effects of encryption during inference
- Core assumption: The inverse transformation applied to the test images exactly undoes the effect of encrypting the model embeddings
- Evidence anchors: [abstract] "The proposed method allows us not only to train models over multiple participants without directly sharing their raw data but to also protect the privacy of test (query) images for the first time. In addition, it can also maintain the same accuracy as normally trained models." [section] "From Eqs. (8) and (14), the proposed method can avoid the influence of encryption." [corpus] Weak: Related papers focus on privacy-preserving methods but do not discuss the specific cancellation of encryption effects between model and data
- Break condition: If the inverse matrix Ea⁻¹ is not correctly applied, or if the permutation matrix Eb does not match between model and image encryption, the accuracy will degrade

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: Federated learning enables model training without sharing raw data, and the encrypted model can be safely shared without exposing sensitive information
- Mechanism: Multiple clients train local ViT models on their private datasets. The FedAVG algorithm aggregates model parameters (weights) without requiring raw data transfer. After aggregation, the model's embedding matrices are encrypted with client-specific secret keys, ensuring that even the server cannot access the original embeddings
- Core assumption: The FedAVG aggregation step does not leak private data, and the encryption of embeddings after aggregation is secure
- Evidence anchors: [abstract] "The proposed method allows us not only to train models over multiple participants without directly sharing their raw data..." [section] "In FL, multiple developers send only parameters of models, which they have trained in their local environments, to a server. On the server, the sent parameters are integrated..." [corpus] Weak: While FL is widely studied, this paper does not provide evidence that the specific encryption of embeddings post-aggregation is secure against model inversion attacks
- Break condition: If the FedAVG aggregation leaks gradients that reveal private data, or if the encryption keys are compromised, privacy is lost

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: The ViT architecture's structure (patch and position embeddings) makes it suitable for encryption-based privacy preservation without accuracy loss
- Mechanism: ViT splits images into patches and uses learnable embeddings for both patch content and spatial position. By encrypting these embeddings and applying inverse transformations to the patches, the spatial and semantic relationships in the image are preserved in the encrypted domain
- Core assumption: The ViT's reliance on embeddings rather than raw pixel values means that encrypting embeddings and transforming patches preserves the model's ability to classify
- Evidence anchors: [abstract] "The proposed method allows us not only to train models over multiple participants without directly sharing their raw data but to also protect the privacy of test (query) images for the first time. In addition, it can maintain the same accuracy as normally trained models." [section] "In ViT, an input image x ∈ R^(h×w×c) is segmented into N patches... After that, each patch is flattened as x_p^i = [x_p^i(1), x_p^i(2), ..., x_p^i(L)]^T. Finally, the embedding patch is given as z^0 = [x_class; x_1^p E; x_2^p E; ... x_N^p E]^T + Epos" [corpus] Weak: The corpus does not provide evidence that other architectures (e.g., CNNs) would not work as well; it only cites ViT as effective
- Break condition: If the ViT architecture changes such that embeddings are no longer sufficient for classification, the method will fail

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map: Clients (local ViT training, encryption of test images, secret key management) -> Server (FedAVG aggregation, optional encryption of aggregated model) -> Core (ViT model with patch and position embeddings) -> Encryption layer (random invertible matrix Ea, permutation matrix Eb)

- Critical path: 1. Clients train local ViT models on private data 2. Clients send model parameters to server 3. Server aggregates using FedAVG 4. Server encrypts aggregated embeddings 5. Clients encrypt test images using inverse transformations 6. Clients perform inference on encrypted images using encrypted model

- Design tradeoffs: Security vs. performance: Using larger encryption matrices increases security but may slow down inference; Privacy vs. accuracy: The method claims no accuracy loss, but in practice, numerical precision issues could arise; Centralization vs. trust: The server must be trusted to aggregate correctly; malicious aggregation could poison the model

- Failure signatures: Accuracy drops significantly compared to baseline; Encrypted images fail to decrypt properly (e.g., due to mismatched keys or matrices); Model inversion attacks succeed despite encryption; FedAVG aggregation fails to converge

- First 3 experiments: 1. Train a single ViT model on CIFAR-10, encrypt its embeddings, and test with encrypted images to verify accuracy preservation 2. Simulate two clients with non-overlapping CIFAR-10 subsets, run FedAVG, encrypt the aggregated model, and test with encrypted images 3. Test with CIFAR-100 to verify scalability to more classes and confirm accuracy is maintained

## Limitations
- Security assumptions not formally verified: The encryption scheme relies on random invertible matrices and permutations, but no formal security proof is provided against model inversion or reconstruction attacks
- Numerical precision concerns: The claim of zero accuracy degradation assumes perfect inverse matrix application, which may not hold under finite precision arithmetic in practice
- Scalability validation limited: Experiments only cover CIFAR-10/100 with 10 clients; performance with larger datasets, more clients, or heterogeneous data distributions remains untested

## Confidence

- **High confidence**: The basic federated learning workflow and ViT embedding mechanics are correctly described and implementable
- **Medium confidence**: The claimed accuracy preservation with encrypted embeddings and transformed images, based on theoretical cancellation of encryption effects
- **Low confidence**: The security guarantees against privacy attacks, as these are not empirically tested or formally proven
