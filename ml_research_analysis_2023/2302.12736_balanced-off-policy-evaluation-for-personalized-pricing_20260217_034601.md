# Balanced Off-Policy Evaluation for Personalized Pricing

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2302.12736
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2302.12736
- Reference count: 40
- Key outcome: Balanced approach minimizes worst-case MSE for personalized pricing with limited overlap

## Executive Summary
This paper addresses off-policy evaluation for personalized pricing where traditional methods fail due to limited overlap between target and logging policies. The authors propose a balanced approach that computes optimal weights by minimizing worst-case mean squared error or maximizing a worst-case lower bound on policy performance. The method leverages reproducing kernel Hilbert spaces to define plausible revenue functions and provides both theoretical guarantees and empirical improvements over standard IPW and doubly robust methods.

## Method Summary
The approach computes weights that optimize a worst-case objective over a set of plausible revenue functions defined by an RKHS ball. This trades off bias and variance more effectively than traditional IPW when overlap is limited. The method decomposes the revenue function into reference and perturbation components, assuming the perturbation lies in an RKHS with a chosen kernel. Weights are computed through optimization problems that either minimize worst-case MSE or maximize Bernstein-based lower bounds on revenue, providing safe performance guarantees.

## Key Results
- Outperforms IPW, doubly robust, and other baselines in terms of mean squared error
- Provides better safe revenue guarantees through worst-case optimization
- Effectively handles continuous pricing decisions through RKHS smoothness assumptions
- Demonstrates advantages using real-world pricing datasets

- Mechanism: The method computes weights that optimize a worst-case MSE objective over a set of plausible revenue functions defined by an RKHS ball. This balances the bias-variance tradeoff more effectively than traditional IPW methods when overlap is limited.
- Core assumption: The true revenue function can be well-approximated by functions in a reproducing kernel Hilbert space with bounded norm.

  - [section]: "we use weights that either (i) minimize the worst-case mean squared error of our estimated revenue or (ii) maximize a worst-case lower bound on the unknown target revenue"
  - [corpus]: Weak evidence - related papers focus on off-policy evaluation but don't specifically address the worst-case MSE optimization framework.
- Break condition: If the RKHS assumption is violated or the kernel is poorly chosen, the worst-case optimization may not capture the true revenue function.

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: The balanced approach provides better safe revenue guarantees than traditional methods through Bernstein-based bounds.
- Mechanism: By formulating a worst-case optimization problem for the Bernstein bound, the method computes weights that maximize a lower bound on the true revenue with high probability, providing operational safety guarantees.
- Core assumption: The demand follows a Bernoulli distribution and the revenue function is bounded between 0 and price.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract]: "maximize a worst-case lower bound on policy performance"
  - [section]: "we next provide a high-conﬁdence lower bound on the true revenue R in terms of the estimate ˆR(w)"

- Break condition: If the demand distribution significantly deviates from Bernoulli or if the revenue bounds are violated, the Bernstein-based bounds may become unreliable.

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: The method handles continuous action spaces effectively by enforcing smoothness through RKHS assumptions.
- Mechanism: By assuming the revenue function belongs to an RKHS with a suitable kernel, the method enforces smoothness across prices and can handle continuous pricing decisions, unlike methods limited to discrete actions.
- Core assumption: The revenue function is smooth enough to be represented in an RKHS with the chosen kernel.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [section]: "By suitably choosing the kernel K(·, ·), we can enforce structural constraints on ∆(·, ·), e.g., that ∆(·, ·) is linear in price or Sobolev smooth in the covariates"

- Break condition: If the revenue function has discontinuities or is too complex for the chosen RKHS, the method may fail to capture important revenue patterns.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- Question: How robust is the balanced approach to off-policy evaluation for personalized pricing when the assumed kernel K(·, ·) is misspecified?
- Basis in paper: [inferred] The authors assume the perturbation function is in a reproducing kernel Hilbert space (RKHS) and use a Gaussian kernel, but do not test robustness to kernel misspecification.
- Why unresolved: The paper focuses on a specific Gaussian kernel and does not explore how performance changes with different kernels or when the true revenue function does not lie in the assumed RKHS.
- What evidence would resolve it: Numerical experiments comparing the performance of the balanced approach with different kernel choices (e.g., linear, polynomial, Matérn) and when the true revenue function is not smooth or lies outside the assumed RKHS.

### Open Question 2
- Question: How does the balanced approach perform when the logging policy has some exploration but not sufficient overlap with the target policy?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The authors motivate their approach by noting that traditional methods based on inverse propensity weighting can perform poorly when there is limited overlap between the target and logging policies, which is common in pricing applications. However, they do not study the intermediate case where there is some exploration but not full overlap.
- Why unresolved: The theoretical results and experiments focus on the extremes of no overlap or sufficient overlap, but do not investigate the intermediate regime.
- What evidence would resolve it: Numerical experiments comparing the performance of the balanced approach and traditional methods across a range of overlap scenarios, from no overlap to full overlap.

## Limitations
- Performance heavily depends on kernel and RKHS parameter selection
- Theoretical guarantees assume specific conditions (Bernoulli demand, bounded revenue) that may not hold in all scenarios
- Computational complexity of worst-case optimization could be prohibitive for large datasets
- RKHS smoothness assumptions may not capture revenue functions with discontinuities
