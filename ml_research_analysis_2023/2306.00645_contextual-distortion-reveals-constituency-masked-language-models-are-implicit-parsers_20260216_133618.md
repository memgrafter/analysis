# Contextual Distortion Reveals Constituency: Masked Language Models are Implicit Parsers

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2306.00645
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.00645
- Reference count: 23
- Key outcome: Method achieves superior constituency parsing performance across 8 languages without training separate parsers

## Executive Summary
This paper introduces a novel approach for extracting constituency parse trees from masked language models without requiring separate parser training. The method computes scores for each span in a sentence based on the distortion of contextual representations resulting from linguistic perturbations including substitution, decontextualization, and movement. By applying these perturbations to each span and aggregating the resulting distortion scores, the approach identifies the likelihood of a span being a constituent. The method consistently outperforms previous state-of-the-art methods on English and demonstrates superior performance in a multilingual setting, outperforming the state of the art in 6 out of 8 languages.

## Method Summary
The approach applies three types of linguistic perturbations to each span in a sentence: substitution (replacing the span with a mask), decontextualization (masking the surrounding context), and movement (moving the span to the beginning or end of the sentence). For each perturbed version, the method computes the distortion score by measuring the change in contextual representations using the Frobenius norm. These scores are normalized by span length and aggregated to determine constituent likelihood. Finally, a chart parsing algorithm (CKY) is used to find the optimal parse tree with minimum distortion scores. The method requires no parameter updates or extensive hyperparameter search, making it a simple yet effective approach to unsupervised constituency parsing.

## Key Results
- Achieves 76.1 F1 score on English Penn Treebank, surpassing previous state-of-the-art methods
- Outperforms state-of-the-art in 6 out of 8 languages in multilingual setting
- Shows particular strength in detecting SBAR constituents (81.3 F1) and PP constituents (82.7 F1)
- Movement perturbation is identified as the most effective perturbation type for capturing various constituent types

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: Constituent spans exhibit lower contextual distortion when perturbed compared to non-constituent spans
- Mechanism: The model computes distortion scores by comparing contextual representations before and after applying linguistic perturbations (substitution, decontextualization, movement) to each span
- Core assumption: The contextual representations of constituents are more stable under linguistic perturbations than distituents
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract]: "the contextual representations of constituents will exhibit minimal alteration compared to those of distituents"

- Break condition: If the masked LM does not encode stable syntactic information in later layers, or if perturbations significantly alter representations regardless of constituency status

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: Masked language models implicitly learn constituency structure through their pre-training objective
- Mechanism: The mask token prediction objective requires the model to understand syntactic relationships to accurately predict masked spans, leading to implicit constituency knowledge
- Core assumption: The masked LM's pre-training objective naturally induces syntactic awareness without explicit syntactic supervision
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract]: "we focus on masked LMs and leverage the inherent properties of the mask token prediction pre-training objective"
  - [section]: "The main idea is to make pre-trained language models think like linguists, such that with constituency tests, span-level scores reflecting the likelihood of a span being a constituent can be obtained"
  - [corpus]: Corpus shows related work on PLMs and syntax, though no direct citations yet
- Break condition: If the masked LM is trained with different objectives (like causal language modeling) or if syntactic knowledge is not encoded in the contextual representations

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: Aggregating distortion scores across multiple perturbation types provides robust constituency detection
- Mechanism: The method combines substitution, decontextualization, and movement perturbation scores to create a comprehensive constituency likelihood score
- Core assumption: Different perturbation types capture complementary aspects of constituency, and their combination is more robust than any single perturbation
- Evidence anchors:
  - [section]: "Our method aggregates three types of perturbations to obtain improved results"
  - [section]: "It can be observed that the movement perturbation is generally more effective in capturing all types of constituents compared to the other perturbations"

- Break condition: If certain perturbation types are ineffective for specific languages or constituent types, or if the aggregation method introduces bias

## Limitations

- Method is limited to masked language models and may not generalize to other PLM architectures
- Performance depends critically on selecting the appropriate LM layer, which may vary across languages
- The perturbation-based approach may not capture all aspects of constituency structure, particularly for languages with different syntactic properties

## Confidence

- **High Confidence**: The core claim that masked LMs encode implicit constituency information that can be extracted without fine-tuning. Supported by strong empirical results across multiple languages and comparison with state-of-the-art methods.
- **Medium Confidence**: The effectiveness of the specific perturbation types (substitution, decontextualization, movement) and their aggregation method. While results show these work well, alternative perturbations or aggregation strategies might yield better performance.
- **Low Confidence**: The claim of superiority over all unsupervised parsing methods that require fine-tuning. The comparison is limited to specific baselines, and different fine-tuning strategies might change the relative performance.
