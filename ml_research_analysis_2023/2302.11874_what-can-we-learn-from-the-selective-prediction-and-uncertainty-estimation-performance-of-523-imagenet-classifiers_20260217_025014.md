# What Can We Learn From The Selective Prediction And Uncertainty Estimation Performance Of 523 Imagenet Classifiers

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2302.11874
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2302.11874
- Reference count: 40
- Primary result: Comprehensive evaluation of uncertainty estimation in 523 ImageNet classifiers, focusing on selective prediction and calibration metrics

## Executive Summary
This paper presents the first large-scale empirical study examining uncertainty estimation across 523 ImageNet classifiers, evaluating both selective prediction and calibration metrics. The research systematically compares various architectures and training regimes to identify which methods most effectively improve uncertainty estimation. Knowledge distillation emerges as the most consistent improvement method, outperforming other approaches like adversarial training or pretraining across all evaluated metrics.

The study reveals that a subset of Vision Transformer models trained with specific regimes achieve superior uncertainty estimation performance, with some models reaching 99% top-1 selective accuracy at 47% coverage. Temperature scaling is shown to benefit both ranking and selective prediction, not just calibration. The research also highlights that correlations between uncertainty metrics and model attributes vary by architecture family, and that MC dropout underperforms compared to softmax-based confidence scoring.

## Method Summary
The study evaluates uncertainty estimation across 523 ImageNet classifiers using a comprehensive set of metrics including selective prediction accuracy, calibration error, and ranking quality. Models span multiple architectures (ResNet, DenseNet, EfficientNet, ViT, ConvNeXt) and training regimes, with various techniques applied including knowledge distillation, adversarial training, pretraining, and temperature scaling. The evaluation framework measures both ranking performance (how well uncertainty estimates order predictions) and selective prediction (how accurately models can abstain from uncertain predictions). A subset of ViT models trained with specific configurations achieves particularly strong results, reaching 99% top-1 selective accuracy at 47% coverage.

## Key Results
- Knowledge distillation consistently improves uncertainty estimation across all metrics, outperforming other methods
- Vision Transformer architectures achieve superior uncertainty estimation performance, with some models reaching 99% top-1 selective accuracy at 47% coverage
- Temperature scaling benefits both ranking and selective prediction, not just calibration
- MC dropout underperforms compared to softmax-based confidence scoring
- Correlations between uncertainty metrics and model attributes vary by architecture family

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
Knowledge distillation improves uncertainty estimation by transferring the teacher model's predictive distribution to the student, which captures more nuanced confidence estimates than hard label training. Vision Transformers benefit from their attention-based architecture that better captures global context and hierarchical feature representations, leading to more reliable uncertainty estimates. Temperature scaling adjusts the sharpness of softmax outputs, allowing better calibration of confidence scores across different prediction scenarios. The selective prediction framework works by setting confidence thresholds that allow models to abstain from uncertain predictions, directly improving accuracy on the predictions made.

## Foundational Learning
- Selective prediction: Models choose when to abstain from predictions based on uncertainty estimates, improving accuracy on predictions made
  - Why needed: Risk-sensitive applications require models to know when they don't know
  - Quick check: Verify that coverage drops while accuracy increases on remaining predictions
- Uncertainty calibration: Ensuring predicted probabilities match empirical accuracy
  - Why needed: Mis-calibrated models make overconfident wrong predictions
  - Quick check: Expected calibration error should be low across confidence bins
- Ranking quality: How well uncertainty estimates order predictions from most to least certain
  - Why needed: Good ranking enables effective selective prediction
  - Quick check: AUROC between confidence scores and correctness labels
- Knowledge distillation: Training student models to mimic teacher model outputs
  - Why needed: Soft targets provide richer learning signals than hard labels
  - Quick check: Student should match teacher's predictive distribution

## Architecture Onboarding
- Component map: Input -> Backbone (CNN/ViT) -> Classification head -> Softmax -> Confidence scoring -> Uncertainty estimation -> Selective prediction
- Critical path: Input features → Model backbone → Softmax confidence → Uncertainty quantification → Decision threshold
- Design tradeoffs: Accuracy vs. uncertainty quality, computational cost vs. calibration quality, model size vs. selective accuracy
- Failure signatures: Overconfident predictions on OOD data, poor calibration under distribution shift, high selective accuracy but low coverage

## Limitations
- Results focus on ImageNet classification, limiting generalizability to other domains like medical imaging
- Study primarily evaluates softmax-based confidence scoring, potentially underrepresenting alternative uncertainty methods
- Computational constraints of evaluating 523 models may have limited depth of analysis for individual training variants

- Medium confidence: Vision Transformer architectures show superior uncertainty estimation performance
- Medium confidence: Temperature scaling benefits both ranking and selective prediction
