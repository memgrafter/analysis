# Retrosynthesis Prediction with Local Template Retrieval

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2306.04123
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.04123
- Reference count: 6
- Primary result: State-of-the-art top-1 accuracy of 57.2% on USPTO-50K and 60.6% on USPTO-MIT

## Executive Summary
This paper addresses the challenge of retrosynthesis prediction in drug discovery by proposing RetroKNN, a method that combines neural network predictions with non-parametric local template retrieval using k-nearest neighbors (KNN). The approach leverages a trained graph neural network (GNN) to generate hidden representations, which are then used to retrieve relevant reaction templates from atom and bond stores during inference. A lightweight adapter network adaptively combines the GNN and KNN predictions, resulting in improved accuracy on two benchmarks. The method achieves new state-of-the-art results with 57.2% and 60.6% top-1 accuracy on USPTO-50K and USPTO-MIT datasets, respectively.

## Method Summary
RetroKNN combines neural network predictions with non-parametric local template retrieval using k-nearest neighbors (KNN). During inference, templates are retrieved from atom and bond stores based on hidden representations from a trained graph neural network (GNN). A lightweight adapter network is used to adaptively combine GNN and KNN predictions. The method is evaluated on two benchmarks, USPTO-50K and USPTO-MIT, achieving new state-of-the-art results with 57.2% and 60.6% top-1 accuracy, respectively.

## Key Results
- Achieves state-of-the-art top-1 accuracy of 57.2% on USPTO-50K
- Achieves state-of-the-art top-1 accuracy of 60.6% on USPTO-MIT
- Combines neural network predictions with non-parametric local template retrieval using KNN

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The combination of neural network predictions with non-parametric local template retrieval using KNN allows RetroKNN to leverage both the learned representations from the GNN and the relevant reaction templates from the stores. The lightweight adapter network effectively combines these predictions, resulting in improved accuracy. The KNN-based template retrieval process during inference helps to capture local chemical patterns and similarities, which are crucial for accurate retrosynthesis prediction.

## Architecture Onboarding

### Component Map
Trained GNN -> Hidden Representations -> KNN-based Template Retrieval -> Adapter Network -> Combined Predictions

### Critical Path
The critical path involves the trained GNN generating hidden representations, which are then used for KNN-based template retrieval from the atom and bond stores. The retrieved templates are combined with the GNN predictions using the adapter network to produce the final retrosynthesis predictions.

### Design Tradeoffs
The main design tradeoff is between the complexity of the GNN and the efficiency of the KNN-based template retrieval process. A more complex GNN may lead to better learned representations but could also increase computational costs during inference. Similarly, a larger number of nearest neighbors (k) may improve the quality of template retrieval but could also increase the computational overhead.

### Failure Signatures
Potential failure modes include:
- Insufficient or irrelevant templates in the atom and bond stores, leading to poor KNN-based retrieval
- Ineffective combination of GNN and KNN predictions by the adapter network, resulting in suboptimal final predictions
- Inadequate generalization of the trained GNN to diverse chemical compounds or reaction types
