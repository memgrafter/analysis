# Perceive and predict: self-supervised speech representation based loss functions for speech enhancement

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2301.04388
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2301.04388
- **Reference count:** 0
- **Primary result:** Self-supervised speech representations as loss functions improve speech enhancement performance

## Executive Summary
This paper investigates the use of self-supervised speech representations as loss functions for speech enhancement tasks. The authors analyze how distances in speech representations correlate with psychoacoustic metrics and human MOS ratings. They demonstrate that distances computed using earlier feature encoder layers of self-supervised models show stronger correlation with speech quality and intelligibility metrics than distances from final output layers or spectrogram distances. Experimental results show improved performance in speech enhancement models trained with these distances as loss functions.

## Method Summary
The authors conduct correlation analysis between distances in speech representations of clean and noisy speech with psychoacoustically motivated metrics and human MOS ratings. They compare distances computed using earlier feature encoder layers versus final output layers of self-supervised speech representation models. The distances are then used as loss functions for training speech enhancement models, with performance evaluated using objective metrics like PESQ and STOI.

## Key Results
- Distances from earlier feature encoder layers show stronger correlation with psychoacoustic metrics than final layer distances
- Speech enhancement models trained with early layer distances outperform models trained with spectrogram distances
- The "perceive" feature encoder layers preserve noise and distortion better than "predict" layers, making them more suitable for enhancement tasks

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The earlier feature encoder layers of self-supervised speech representation models preserve more noise and distortion information compared to deeper layers. This preservation makes them more suitable for speech enhancement tasks, as the loss functions can better capture the differences between clean and noisy speech in perceptually relevant ways.

## Architecture Onboarding

**Component map:** Input speech -> Spectrogram extraction -> Self-supervised model (encoder + decoder) -> Layer selection -> Distance computation -> Speech enhancement model training

**Critical path:** The most critical components are the self-supervised model architecture and the selection of appropriate layers for distance computation, as these directly impact the quality of the loss function.

**Design tradeoffs:** The choice between using early encoder layers versus deeper layers involves balancing noise preservation against semantic information. Early layers preserve more noise but less semantic content, while deeper layers capture more meaning but may lose noise-related information.

**Failure signatures:** Poor correlation with psychoacoustic metrics, degradation in speech enhancement performance, or computationally expensive training could indicate issues with layer selection or distance computation methods.

**First experiments:** 1) Correlation analysis between layer distances and psychoacoustic metrics, 2) Layer ablation study to identify optimal layers, 3) Comparative training of speech enhancement models with different layer-based loss functions.

## Limitations
- Analysis primarily based on English speech data, limiting generalizability
- Correlation analysis relies on objective measures that may not fully capture subjective speech quality
- Computational cost of using self-supervised representations as loss functions not thoroughly explored
- Limited exploration of different self-supervised model architectures
