# Hyperparameters in Reinforcement Learning and How To Tune Them

## Executive Summary
This paper addresses reproducibility issues in deep reinforcement learning caused by inconsistent hyperparameter tuning practices across research papers. The authors demonstrate that hyperparameter choices significantly affect both final performance and sample efficiency, with results varying substantially across random seeds. They propose adopting AutoML best practices, including separating tuning and testing seeds, and using principled hyperparameter optimization (HPO) across broad search spaces. The study compares multiple state-of-the-art HPO tools (DEHB, PB2, BGT) against hand-tuned baselines on various RL algorithms and environments, showing that HPO approaches often achieve higher performance with lower computational overhead than traditional methods.

## Method Summary
The study compares hyperparameter optimization (HPO) methods for reinforcement learning (RL) algorithms across multiple environments (Pendulum, Acrobot, Ant, Halfcheetah, Humanoid, MiniGrid) and algorithms (PPO, DQN, SAC). The researchers implement random search, DEHB, and PBT variants, applying them with controlled budgets to tune RL hyperparameters. They use separate seeds for tuning and testing to prevent overfitting and ensure fair comparisons. The hyperparameter search spaces are designed to be broad and representative of typical RL tuning ranges, with specific ranges detailed in appendices.

## Key Results
- Hyperparameter choices in RL significantly affect agent performance and sample efficiency, with results varying substantially across random seeds
- HPO approaches often have higher performance and lower compute overhead than traditional hyperparameter sweeps or grid searches
- Using separate tuning and testing seeds prevents overfitting and ensures fair algorithm comparisons

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: Hyperparameter choices in RL significantly affect both final performance and sample efficiency, with results varying substantially across random seeds
- Mechanism: Hyperparameters directly control learning dynamics such as learning rate, entropy regularization, and policy clipping. These settings determine how quickly and stably an agent learns optimal behaviors. Different random seeds generate different initial conditions and environment trajectories, causing the same hyperparameter configuration to yield different learning curves and final returns
- Core assumption: The RL optimization landscape is sensitive to both hyperparameter values and random initialization, so small changes in either can produce large performance differences
- Evidence anchors: [abstract] "hyperparameter choices in RL can significantly affect the agent's final performance and sample efficiency, and that the hyperparameter landscape can strongly depend on the tuning seed"

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: Standard RL tuning practices (grid search, limited hyperparameters) are suboptimal compared to principled HPO methods
- Mechanism: Grid search evaluates a fixed, sparse set of hyperparameter combinations, missing potentially better configurations in the search space. Principled HPO methods like DEHB and PBT systematically explore the space, often finding configurations with better performance using fewer total runs by leveraging multi-fidelity evaluations or adaptive population-based refinement
- Core assumption: The hyperparameter space contains configurations that significantly outperform hand-tuned or grid-searched ones, and these can be found efficiently with modern HPO algorithms
- Evidence anchors: [abstract] "HPO approaches often have higher performance and lower compute overhead"

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: Using separate seeds for tuning and testing prevents overfitting and ensures fair algorithm comparisons
- Mechanism: When hyperparameters are tuned on the same seeds used for final evaluation, the tuning process can inadvertently select configurations that overfit to those specific seeds. By holding out distinct test seeds, the evaluation measures generalization to unseen conditions, providing a more reliable comparison across algorithms
- Core assumption: RL performance is sensitive to random seeds, so configurations tuned on one set of seeds may not generalize to others
- Evidence anchors: [abstract] "separation of tuning and testing seeds, as well as principled hyperparameter optimization (HPO) across a broad search space"

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map: RL environments (Pendulum, Acrobot, Ant, etc.) -> RL algorithms (PPO, SAC, DQN) -> HPO methods (DEHB, PB2, BGT, RS) -> evaluation infrastructure (seed management, performance metrics)
- Critical path: For each environment-algorithm pair, run HPO to find best hyperparameters on tuning seeds, then evaluate the incumbent configuration on separate test seeds to measure generalization
- Design tradeoffs: Larger search spaces and more seeds improve coverage and reliability but increase computational cost. Multi-fidelity methods trade off evaluation accuracy for speed, while population-based methods adaptively refine promising configurations
- Failure signatures: Poor HPO performance may indicate an overly large or poorly chosen search space, insufficient budget, or high seed sensitivity causing overfitting to tuning seeds
- First 3 experiments:
  1. Tune PPO on Pendulum using DEHB with a small search space (learning rate only) on 5 tuning seeds, then evaluate on 5 test seeds
  2. Repeat with the full PPO search space to observe the impact of broader exploration
  3. Compare DEHB results to random search on the same problem to quantify HPO benefits

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- Question: How do hyperparameter interactions vary across different RL environments and algorithms beyond the observed lack of complex patterns in Pendulum and Acrobot?
- Basis in paper: [inferred] The paper mentions that Partial Dependency Plots on Pendulum and Acrobot show almost no complex interaction patterns between hyperparameters, but doesn't explore other environments extensively
- Why unresolved: The analysis was limited to specific environments (Pendulum and Acrobot) for PPO, and the authors acknowledge that more comprehensive analysis across environments is needed
- What evidence would resolve it: Systematic analysis of interaction effects across a broader range of environments and algorithms using methods like fANOVA or SHAP values to quantify interaction strengths

### Open Question 2
- Question: What is the optimal number of tuning seeds needed to balance generalization and computational efficiency in RL hyperparameter optimization?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The authors observe that performance estimation across more than 3 seeds leads to decreased test performance and increased variance, suggesting a trade-off exists
- Why unresolved: The paper only tested up to 10 seeds and found diminishing returns, but didn't systematically explore the optimal range for different environments and budgets
- What evidence would resolve it: Systematic experiments varying the number of tuning seeds (e.g., 1, 3, 5, 10, 20) across multiple environments and algorithms to find the sweet spot for generalization

### Open Question 3
- Question: How do population-based methods like PB2 and BGT compare to multi-fidelity methods like DEHB when given larger budgets and more sophisticated variants?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The authors note that PB2 and BGT struggle with overfitting and generalization on small budgets, but suggest they might perform better with larger budgets or more advanced variants
- Why unresolved: The experiments used limited budgets (16-64 runs) and basic variants of PB2 and BGT, leaving open questions about their scalability and potential with more resources
- What evidence would resolve it: Head-to-head comparisons of PB2, BGT, and DEHB across a range of budgets (e.g., 16, 64, 256, 1024 runs) and with advanced variants (e.g., with run initialization, configuration racing) to determine scalability and effectiveness

## Limitations

- The computational overhead comparisons between HPO methods and traditional approaches may be sensitive to specific implementation details and hardware configurations not fully disclosed in the paper
- The findings hinge on the assumption that seed sensitivity is representative across diverse RL settings, though it remains unclear whether these findings generalize to all RL domains
- Conclusions about HPO methods outperforming traditional tuning approaches may be algorithm- and environment-specific, requiring broader validation

## Confidence

- **High Confidence**: Claims about the importance of separating tuning and testing seeds for fair evaluation are well-supported by empirical results showing performance drops when configurations are evaluated on unseen seeds
- **Medium Confidence**: Conclusions about HPO methods outperforming traditional tuning approaches are supported but may be algorithm- and environment-specific, requiring broader validation
- **Medium Confidence**: The recommendation for broad hyperparameter search spaces is supported but may not apply universally across all RL applications, particularly those with constrained resources

## Next Checks

1. **Cross-environment validation**: Test whether seed sensitivity and HPO benefits observed in the paper's environments (Pendulum, Acrobot, Ant, etc.) extend to other RL domains like Atari games or continuous control benchmarks

2. **Implementation reproducibility**: Replicate the key experiments using different RL frameworks (e.g., StableBaselines3 vs. RLlib) to verify that performance differences are robust to implementation choices

3. **Budget sensitivity analysis**: Systematically vary the computational budget for HPO methods to determine the point at which their overhead outweighs their benefits compared to simpler approaches like random search
