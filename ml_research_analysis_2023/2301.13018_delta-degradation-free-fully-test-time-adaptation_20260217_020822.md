# DELTA: degradation-free fully test-time adaptation

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2301.13018
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2301.13018
- Reference count: 40
- The paper identifies two critical flaws in current test-time adaptation methods: (1) inaccurate normalization statistics derived from small test batches, and (2) biased optimization favoring dominant classes.

## Executive Summary
DELTA addresses two critical flaws in current test-time adaptation methods: inaccurate normalization statistics from small test batches and biased optimization favoring dominant classes. The paper introduces Test-time Batch Renormalization (TBR) to correct normalization using moving averages and Dynamic Online re-weighTing (DOT) to re-balance class contributions during optimization. Evaluated across four test scenarios on three standard datasets and a real-world video dataset, DELTA consistently improves the performance of state-of-the-art methods like TENT and Ent-W, achieving new state-of-the-art results and robust adaptation across all scenarios.

## Method Summary
DELTA consists of two components: Test-time Batch Renormalization (TBR) and Dynamic Online re-weighTing (DOT). TBR uses test-time moving averaged statistics to correct normalization, addressing the issue of inaccurate batch normalization statistics derived from small test batches. DOT assigns weights to samples based on predicted class frequencies to address class bias during optimization. The method is evaluated on three standard datasets (CIFAR100-C, ImageNet-C/-R) and one real-world video dataset (YTBB-sub), using pre-trained models including ResNeXt-29 for CIFAR100-C and ResNet-50 for ImageNet datasets. Performance is measured by class-wise accuracy averaged over all classes, with results averaged over 15 corruption types for CIFAR100-C and ImageNet-C.

## Key Results
- DELTA consistently improves performance of state-of-the-art methods like TENT and Ent-W across all four test scenarios (IS+CB, DS+CB, IS+CI, DS+CI)
- Achieves new state-of-the-art results on CIFAR100-C, ImageNet-C, and ImageNet-R datasets
- Maintains robust adaptation performance even with very small batch sizes (batch size 1 on CIFAR100-C achieves 69.1% accuracy)

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: Normalization statistics in test-time batch normalization are inaccurate because they are derived solely from small test batches, leading to poor adaptation performance.
- Mechanism: Test-time batch renormalization (TBR) uses exponential-moving-average (EMA) estimates of normalization statistics to correct normalization, providing more stable and accurate statistics for adaptation.
- Core assumption: The EMA estimates of normalization statistics from test batches are better approximations of the true population statistics than statistics from a single small batch.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract]: "First, we reveal that the normalization statistics in test-time BN are completely affected by the currently received test samples, resulting in inaccurate estimates."
  - [section 3.2]: "We observe that the statistics used in BN adapt is inaccurate in per batch compared to the actual population statistics."
  - [corpus]: Weak evidence. The corpus does not contain papers specifically discussing normalization statistics inaccuracy in test-time adaptation.
- Break condition: If the test stream distribution changes rapidly, the EMA estimates may lag behind and become inaccurate.

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: Test-time optimization is biased towards dominant classes, leading to inferior performance.
- Mechanism: Dynamic online re-weighting (DOT) assigns weights to samples based on their pseudo labels and a momentum-updated class-frequency vector, balancing the contributions of each class during adaptation.
- Core assumption: The predictions of the pre-trained model on out-of-distribution data are imbalanced, and this imbalance can be exacerbated by self-learning-based adaptation methods.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract]: "Second, we show that during test-time adaptation, the parameter update is biased towards some dominant classes."
  - [section 3.3]: "We notice that the model predictions are extremely imbalanced on out-of-distribution data, which can be exacerbated by the self-learning-based adaptation methods."
  - [corpus]: Weak evidence. The corpus does not contain papers specifically discussing class bias in test-time optimization.
- Break condition: If the test stream is truly class-balanced, the benefits of DOT may be minimal.

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: Combining TBR and DOT can address both inaccurate normalization statistics and biased optimization, leading to improved adaptation performance across all scenarios.
- Mechanism: TBR corrects the normalization statistics, while DOT re-balances the contributions of each class during optimization. Together, they provide a comprehensive solution to the two main issues in test-time adaptation.
- Core assumption: The two issues (inaccurate normalization statistics and biased optimization) are independent and can be addressed separately.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract]: "We propose two powerful tools. Specifically, to handle the inaccurate normalization statistics, we introduce test-time batch renormalization (TBR)... We further propose dynamic online re-weighting (DOT) to tackle the biased optimization."
  - [section 3.2, 3.3]: Both mechanisms are introduced separately and their effectiveness is demonstrated individually.
  - [corpus]: Weak evidence. The corpus does not contain papers specifically discussing the combination of TBR and DOT.
- Break condition: If one of the issues is not present in a specific scenario, the corresponding mechanism may not provide significant benefits.

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map:
  Pre-trained model with Batch Normalization layers -> Test-time Batch Renormalization (TBR) module -> Dynamic Online re-weighting (DOT) module -> Optimizer for updating model parameters

- Critical path:
  1. Receive test mini-batch
  2. Perform forward pass with TBR
  3. Compute loss with DOT weights
  4. Perform backward pass and update model parameters
  5. Update EMA estimates and class-frequency vector

- Design tradeoffs:
  - TBR vs. using current batch statistics: TBR provides more stable estimates but may lag behind rapid distribution changes.
  - DOT vs. no re-weighting: DOT balances class contributions but may introduce additional hyperparameters.

- Failure signatures:
  - Inaccurate normalization statistics: Large fluctuations in the estimates of mean and standard deviation.
  - Biased optimization: Predictions are dominated by a few classes, and the model performs poorly on minority classes.

- First 3 experiments:
  1. Evaluate the performance of TBR on a simple dataset with known distribution shifts.
  2. Evaluate the performance of DOT on a dataset with class imbalance.
  3. Combine TBR and DOT and evaluate their performance on a challenging dataset with both distribution shifts and class imbalance.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- Question: How does the performance of DELTA scale with batch size and what are the theoretical limits of its effectiveness in extremely small batch regimes?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper shows DELTA maintains reasonable performance even with batch size 1 on CIFAR100-C (69.1% vs 1.6% for TENT), but does not investigate theoretical bounds or performance degradation curves for varying batch sizes.
- Why unresolved: The paper demonstrates DELTA works better than alternatives in small batch regimes but doesn't establish whether there's a minimum batch size below which DELTA cannot function or if performance asymptotically approaches a limit.
- What evidence would resolve it: Systematic experiments varying batch size from 1 to 128 on multiple datasets, theoretical analysis of the normalization statistics' variance as a function of batch size, and identification of the batch size threshold where performance degradation becomes prohibitive.

### Open Question 2
- Question: What is the impact of different initialization strategies for the moving normalization statistics (using first test batch vs inherited from pre-trained model) across different domains and data distributions?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper mentions two initialization strategies for TBR's normalization statistics but only provides ablation studies on CIFAR100-C, finding "First" initialization superior for larger batch sizes while "Inherit" works better for very small batch sizes.
- Why unresolved: The paper only investigates this on one dataset and doesn't explore how initialization affects adaptation across different types of domain shifts, corruption types, or data distributions.
- What evidence would resolve it: Cross-domain experiments comparing both initialization strategies on multiple datasets with varying degrees of domain shift, analysis of how initialization affects convergence speed and final accuracy across different corruption types.

### Open Question 3
- Question: How does DELTA perform when the test stream contains concept drift or temporal changes in the underlying data distribution?
- Basis in paper: [inferred] The paper evaluates on dependent streams where samples of the same class cluster together, but doesn't investigate scenarios where the class distribution changes over time or where the semantic content of classes evolves.
- Why unresolved: The paper focuses on static test distributions and doesn't address the more challenging scenario where the test distribution itself changes during adaptation, which is common in real-world applications like video streams or time-series data.
- What evidence would resolve it: Experiments with synthetic concept drift (gradual or sudden shifts in class frequencies), evaluation on datasets with known temporal dynamics, and analysis of how the moving average statistics and DOT weighting adapt to changing distributions.

## Limitations
- The effectiveness of TBR relies on the assumption that EMA estimates will track true population statistics, which may break down with rapid distribution shifts
- DOT's reliance on pseudo-label-based class frequency estimation introduces potential feedback loops, especially in early adaptation stages
- The claim of being "degradation-free" may be overstated as the paper doesn't extensively analyze failure scenarios or provide theoretical guarantees

## Confidence
- **High Confidence**: The core observation that normalization statistics in test-time BN are batch-dependent and can be inaccurate, and that class imbalance affects test-time optimization, is well-supported by empirical evidence across multiple datasets and scenarios.
- **Medium Confidence**: The proposed solutions (TBR and DOT) are shown to improve performance, but the extent of improvement varies across scenarios. The analysis of failure modes and limitations could be more comprehensive.
- **Low Confidence**: The claim that DELTA is "degradation-free" may be overstated, as the paper doesn't extensively analyze scenarios where the method might fail or provide theoretical guarantees against degradation.

## Next Checks
1. **Distribution Shift Robustness**: Test TBR's performance on datasets with non-stationary distribution shifts where the underlying distribution changes rapidly over time, rather than the static corruptions used in the current evaluation.

2. **Early Adaptation Behavior**: Analyze the adaptation trajectory of DOT in the first few batches when pseudo-labels are least reliable, and assess the impact of different initialization strategies for the class-frequency vector.

3. **Ablation on Hyperparameters**: Conduct a systematic study of the sensitivity to α in TBR and λ in DOT across different datasets and scenarios, providing practical guidance for hyperparameter selection.
