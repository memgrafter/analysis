# Probing Taxonomic and Thematic Embeddings for Taxonomic Information

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2301.10656
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2301.10656
- Reference count: 29
- Key outcome: Taxonomic embeddings outperform thematic embeddings on hypernym-hyponym prediction tasks, with taxonomic information encoded in both dimensions and norms, while thematic embeddings only encode in dimensions.

## Executive Summary
This paper investigates how taxonomic and thematic embeddings encode hierarchical taxonomic information through a novel probing framework with noise ablation. The authors compare embeddings trained on natural corpora versus those generated from WordNet random walks, examining whether the norm of word vectors can encode taxonomic hierarchy depth. Their results show that taxonomic embeddings carry taxonomic information in both their dimensions and norms, while thematic embeddings only encode this information in dimensions. This suggests that the underlying data distribution significantly influences what information embeddings encode and where it is stored.

## Method Summary
The study uses pre-trained static embeddings (SGNS and GloVe) trained on either natural corpora (Google News, Common Crawl) or taxonomic pseudo-corpora generated by random walks over WordNet. A novel probing framework with noise ablation ablates either the norm or dimensions of concatenated word vectors before passing them to an MLP classifier that predicts hypernym-hyponym relationships. The experiments measure AUC-ROC performance across different ablation conditions to determine where taxonomic information is encoded.

## Key Results
- Taxonomic SGNS embeddings outperform thematic embeddings on hypernym-hyponym prediction tasks
- Only taxonomic embeddings carry taxonomic information in their vector norm
- Deleting half of each word vector in the concatenated input results in surprisingly small performance drops, suggesting redundancy in dimension encoding

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: The norm of a word embedding vector can encode hierarchical depth information in a taxonomy.
- Mechanism: In taxonomic embeddings, words deeper in the hierarchy (hyponyms) have larger vector norms than their hypernyms, creating a mapping where distance from origin reflects taxonomic position.
- Core assumption: Random walk generation of pseudo-corpora preserves hierarchical structure in word frequency distributions.
- Evidence anchors: [abstract] suspicion about norm encoding hierarchical structure; [section] median norm measurements showing hypernyms have larger norms than hyponyms; [corpus] weak evidence with no direct corpus data.
- Break condition: If pseudo-corpus generation doesn't preserve hierarchical structure or embedding training doesn't learn the norm-hierarchy mapping.

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: Taxonomic embeddings use the norm to supplement encoding of taxonomic information beyond what's available in dimensions.
- Mechanism: After ablating dimension information, taxonomic GloVe embeddings maintain high performance, indicating the norm contains unique taxonomic information.
- Core assumption: Embedding training learns to utilize norm as separate information container for taxonomic relationships.
- Evidence anchors: [abstract] finding only taxonomic embeddings carry taxonomic information in norm; [section] taxonomic SGNS embeddings use norm more than thematic ones; [corpus] no direct evidence but WordNet random walks designed to reflect taxonomic co-occurrences.
- Break condition: If embedding training doesn't learn to utilize norm or norm becomes correlated with non-taxonomic factors.

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: There is redundancy within dimensions of embeddings, where small subsets can encode same linguistic features.
- Mechanism: Deletion experiments show removing half dimensions still allows probe to maintain high performance, indicating not all dimensions are necessary for encoding hypernym-hyponym relationships.
- Core assumption: Embedding dimensions are not uniformly distributed in their contribution to encoding target linguistic feature.
- Evidence anchors: [section] probe infers relevant relationship from concatenated vectors; [section] consistent with Durrani et al. finding small neuron subsets sufficient for linguistic tasks; [corpus] no direct evidence but WordNet structure implies encoding redundancy.
- Break condition: If linguistic feature requires all dimensions for accurate prediction or probe is memorizing patterns rather than learning relationships.

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map: Embedding generation (WordNet random walks for taxonomic, natural corpora for thematic) -> Probing framework with noise ablation -> MLP classifier for prediction -> Evaluation using AUC-ROC scores
- Critical path: Training embeddings → generating probing dataset from WordNet → running probing experiments with various noise conditions → analyzing performance differences → interpreting results based on norm vs dimension contributions
- Design tradeoffs: Using concatenated vectors as input simplifies task to binary classification but may lose word order information; using AUC-ROC accounts for class imbalance but may be less interpretable than accuracy
- Failure signatures: If random baselines perform too close to vanilla embeddings, indicates no meaningful signal encoded; if ablating both norm and dimensions still yields above-random performance, suggests probe is memorizing patterns
- First 3 experiments:
  1. Run vanilla probing experiment on both taxonomic and thematic embeddings to establish baseline performance differences
  2. Apply norm ablation to both embedding types to measure norm's contribution to taxonomic information encoding
  3. Apply dimension ablation to both embedding types to measure dimensions' contribution and compare with norm ablation results

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- Question: Does the amount of taxonomic information encoded in the norm vary depending on the size and domain of the training corpus?
- Basis in paper: [inferred] Authors note taxonomic GloVe embeddings trained on small pseudo-corpus underperform compared to thematic GloVe on large natural corpus, but opposite is true for SGNS embeddings
- Why unresolved: Study only examined limited set of embedding models on specific corpora; systematic investigation of corpus size/domain effects on norm-based encoding needed
- What evidence would resolve it: Probing experiments on embeddings trained on corpora of varying sizes and domains with careful control of other factors

### Open Question 2
- Question: Is the mapping between taxonomic hierarchy and distance from origin in vector space a general property of taxonomic embeddings or specific to certain architectures/training procedures?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] Authors find in taxonomic embeddings, norm of hypernyms is larger than norm of hyponyms, indicating hierarchy-distance mapping not observed in thematic embeddings
- Why unresolved: Study only examined limited set of taxonomic embeddings; unclear whether this property is universal or depends on specific factors
- What evidence would resolve it: Probing experiments on wider range of taxonomic embeddings generated by different methods and trained on different corpora

### Open Question 3
- Question: What is the nature of the redundancy observed within dimensions of embeddings where deleting half vector results in small performance drop?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] Authors observe deleting half of each word vector in concatenated input results in performance drop significantly above random but much smaller than expected
- Why unresolved: Study doesn't investigate underlying cause of redundancy; could be due to probe architecture, probing task nature, or inherent embedding properties
- What evidence would resolve it: Further analysis of embedding dimensions using dimensionality reduction and visualization to understand structure and information content; probing experiments with different architectures and tasks

## Limitations

- The findings are constrained by artificial nature of WordNet random walk pseudo-corpora which may not capture full complexity of natural language use
- Probing method's reliance on concatenated vectors rather than contextualized representations limits generalizability to modern language models
- Experimental setup cannot distinguish whether norm encoding is intentional feature of embedding learning algorithm or emergent artifact of training data distribution

## Confidence

- **High confidence**: Taxonomic SGNS embeddings outperform thematic embeddings on hypernym-hyponym prediction tasks; norm ablation results showing taxonomic embeddings retain more information than thematic ones
- **Medium confidence**: Specific claim that taxonomic embeddings "carry taxonomic information in their norm" - supported but alternative explanations cannot be fully ruled out
- **Medium confidence**: Interpretation that norm encoding reflects hierarchical depth in taxonomy, as this mapping is observed but not explicitly validated against WordNet structure

## Next Checks

1. Conduct ablation studies on WordNet structure itself to verify norm values correlate with actual taxonomic depth rather than arbitrary training artifacts
2. Test probing framework on contextualized embeddings (BERT, RoBERTa) to determine if norm encoding phenomenon extends beyond static embeddings
3. Generate alternative pseudo-corpora with controlled hierarchical properties to isolate whether norm encoding emerges from taxonomy structure or distributional properties of random walk generation
