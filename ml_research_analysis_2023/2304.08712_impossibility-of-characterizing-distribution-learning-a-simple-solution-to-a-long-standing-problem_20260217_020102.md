# Impossibility of Characterizing Distribution Learning -- a simple solution to a long-standing problem

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2304.08712
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2304.08712
- Reference count: 4
- Primary result: No parameter or dimension can characterize PAC learnability of distribution classes

## Executive Summary
This paper resolves a fundamental question in learning theory by proving that distribution learning cannot be characterized by any parameter or dimension. The authors demonstrate this impossibility through two complementary approaches: first, by showing that sample complexity functions form a cofinal set under eventual dominance, and second, by proving that no finitary characterization can distinguish learnable from non-learnable classes. The results extend beyond distribution learning to classification learning and real-valued function learning with continuous losses, establishing a broader impossibility result for characterizing learnability across multiple learning paradigms.

## Method Summary
The authors construct specific distribution classes with controlled sample complexity properties to prove their impossibility results. They use classes of the form Pη,n = {(1 - η)δ0 + ηUA : A ⊂ {1, ..., n}} and union classes Q¯η,¯n = ∪∞i=1 P¯η(i),¯n(i) to create learnable classes whose sample complexities can be precisely controlled. The proofs involve analyzing upper and lower bounds on sample complexity, verifying cofinality conditions, and applying impossibility theorems that show no weak sample complexity dimension or finitary characterization can exist for these learning tasks.

## Key Results
- There is no weak sample complexity dimension for distribution learning
- Learnability of distribution classes cannot be characterized by a finitary characterization for either realizable or agnostic PAC learnability
- These impossibility results extend to classification learning for distribution classes and learning of real-valued functions with continuous losses

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: The impossibility of characterizing distribution learning arises from the cofinality of sample complexity functions under eventual dominance ordering
- Mechanism: The construction of learnable distribution classes with sample complexities that form a cofinal set in the eventual dominance ordering of functions shows that no fixed sample complexity dimension can exist. For any proposed dimension function, one can construct a class whose sample complexity eventually dominates all sample complexities associated with that dimension value
- Core assumption: The set of sample complexities for learnable classes is cofinal in the set of all functions from natural numbers to natural numbers under eventual dominance
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "Using constructions of distribution classes with controlled sample complexity, the authors prove that there is no weak sample complexity dimension for distribution learning"
  - [section 3.1] "Lemma 1. Let C denote the family of all learnable classes w.r.t. some learning task. For any given learnable class Q consider the function mQ(1/·, 1/7) : N → N that maps a natural number k to mQ(1/k, 1/7). If the set {mQ(1/k, 1/7) : k ∈ N, Q ∈ C} is cofinal in NN (under the eventual dominance ordering) then there exists no weak sample complexity dimension for that task"

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: The learnability of distribution classes cannot be characterized by any finitary characterization due to the existence of infinite sequences of non-learnable classes that can be ǫ-approximated by a single learnable class
- Mechanism: By constructing a sequence of non-learnable classes {Hk} where each Hk can be ǫk-approximated by a fixed learnable class H0, and showing that the union of finite subsets of these classes is learnable, the authors demonstrate that no finitary characterization can distinguish learnable from non-learnable classes
- Core assumption: The learning task satisfies closure under finite unions of learnable classes and allows for ǫ-approximations that converge to zero
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "They also show that the learnability of distribution classes cannot be characterized by a finitary characterization, neither for realizable nor 3-agnostic PAC learnability"
  - [section 3.2] "Theorem 2. The learnability of any learning task that satisfies the following two properties cannot be characterized by a finitary characterization"
  - [section 4] "Proof of Theorem 4. We only need to show that the two conditions from Theorem 2 are fulfilled by the problem of distribution learning"

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: The impossibility results extend to other learning tasks beyond distribution learning, including classification learning for distribution classes and learning of real-valued functions with continuous losses
- Mechanism: The authors use similar constructions of distribution classes with controlled sample complexity to show that no dimension can characterize the learnability of these other tasks, applying the same cofinality arguments and finitary characterization impossibility results
- Core assumption: The other learning tasks share structural properties with distribution learning that make them subject to the same impossibility results
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "The results are extended to other learning tasks, such as classification learning for distribution classes and learning of real-valued functions with continuous losses, demonstrating the impossibility of characterizing their learnability as well"

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- Question: Can the characterization of polynomial sample complexity distribution learning be improved to a uniformly bounded finitary characterization?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper shows that there is no uniformly-bounded finitary characterization of polynomial distribution learning, but this is a partial result that does not rule out non-uniformly bounded characterizations.
- Why unresolved: The proof in the paper uses a uniformly bounded characterization assumption, so the result does not directly apply to non-uniformly bounded characterizations. It remains open whether such characterizations exist.
- What evidence would resolve it: Either a proof that no non-uniformly bounded finitary characterization exists, or a construction of such a characterization for polynomial distribution learning.

### Open Question 2
- Question: Does the equivalence between weak learning and PAC learning for most tasks imply that these tasks are characterized?
- Basis in paper: [inferred] The paper mentions that for most tasks with known characterizations, there is an equivalence between weak learning and PAC learning. This suggests a potential connection between this equivalence and the characterizability of the learning problem.
- Why unresolved: The paper does not provide a formal proof or explanation of this connection. It remains open whether the equivalence is a necessary or sufficient condition for characterization.
- What evidence would resolve it: A proof showing that for tasks with an equivalence between weak and PAC learning, there exists a characterization, or a counterexample of a task with this equivalence that is not characterized.

### Open Question 3
- Question: Can the techniques and definitions proposed in this paper be applied to characterize other learning tasks beyond those discussed?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The authors state in the discussion section that they believe their approach and techniques are also applicable to more learning tasks beyond those covered in the paper.
- Why unresolved: The paper only provides results for a limited set of learning tasks. It remains open whether the proposed definitions and techniques can be successfully extended to other learning problems.
- What evidence would resolve it: Successful application of the proposed framework to characterize additional learning tasks, or a proof that certain classes of learning problems cannot be characterized using these techniques.

## Limitations

- The impossibility results rely on specific constructions of distribution classes that may not generalize to all learning paradigms
- The relationship between these results and undecidability in ZFC set theory is mentioned but not rigorously established
- The paper does not explore whether weaker forms of characterization (beyond finitary characterizations) might still be possible for certain learning tasks

## Confidence

- **High confidence**: The construction of distribution classes with controlled sample complexity and the basic impossibility results for weak sample complexity dimensions
- **Medium confidence**: The extension of results to other learning tasks beyond distribution learning, as these rely on unstated structural assumptions
- **Medium confidence**: The characterization of learnability as impossible through finitary characterizations, as this requires careful verification of the closure properties
