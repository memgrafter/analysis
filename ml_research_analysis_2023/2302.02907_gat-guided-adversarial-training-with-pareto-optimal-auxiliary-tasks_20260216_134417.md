# GAT: Guided Adversarial Training with Pareto-optimal Auxiliary Tasks

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2302.02907
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2302.02907
- Reference count: 40
- Key outcome: GAT improves adversarial robustness by augmenting single-task models with auxiliary tasks and gradient curvature regularization, achieving up to 4× improvement in robust accuracy on CIFAR-10 and 83% robust AUC on CheXpert.

## Executive Summary
Guided Adversarial Training (GAT) enhances single-task models with self-supervised and domain-knowledge auxiliary tasks to improve adversarial robustness. It introduces a gradient curvature regularization to guide training toward less vulnerable loss landscapes and employs a Pareto-optimal multi-objective optimization strategy (MGDA) to balance clean and robust performance across tasks. Experimental results demonstrate GAT significantly outperforms eight state-of-the-art adversarial training methods, increasing robust accuracy on CIFAR-10 up to 4× and boosting CheXpert robust AUC from 50% to 83%.

## Method Summary
GAT extends single-task adversarial training by adding auxiliary task decoders to a shared encoder and using multi-task learning with gradient curvature regularization. The method employs MGDA to dynamically balance clean and robust losses across multiple tasks, seeking Pareto-stationary points rather than weighted-sum optima. Auxiliary tasks include self-supervised (rotation, jigsaw) and domain-knowledge tasks, which expand the training signal and improve representation quality without requiring additional labeled data.

## Key Results
- GAT increases robust accuracy on CIFAR-10 from 11% to 42% (4× improvement) and boosts CheXpert robust AUC from 50% to 83%.
- Outperforms eight state-of-the-art adversarial training methods by up to 26.4% robust accuracy.
- Achieves 56.21% robust accuracy with ResNet-50 on CIFAR-10 and demonstrates effectiveness on larger architectures, adaptive attacks, and transfer attacks.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: Gradient curvature regularization flattens the loss landscape, reducing adversarial vulnerability.
- Mechanism: Penalizing the multi-task curvature measure guides the model toward regions where task gradients are more aligned and the loss surface is smoother, making it harder for small perturbations to cause large loss increases.
- Core assumption: A flatter loss landscape correlates with higher adversarial robustness in both single-task and multi-task settings.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract]: "drives the loss optimization with a regularization of the gradient curvature across multiple tasks."
  - [section 3]: "the gradient curvature measure can be a good surrogate to study the robustness of MTL models, especially with AT. The negative correlation between the gradient curvature measure and the robust accuracy suggests that a flatter multitask loss landscape leads to more robust models."
- Break condition: Over-constraining the loss landscape could hurt clean accuracy or cause optimization instability.

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: Pareto-optimal multi-objective optimization (MGDA) balances clean and robust performance across tasks.
- Mechanism: MGDA dynamically computes a descent direction common to all objectives (clean and adversarial losses) at each training step, ensuring no task's loss degrades without improving another's.
- Core assumption: Adversarial training involves conflicting gradients between clean and robust objectives, and a Pareto-stationary point is preferable to a weighted-sum optimum.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract]: "uses a Pareto-optimal multi-objective optimization strategy (MGDA) to balance clean and robust performance across tasks."
  - [section 4.3]: "Instead of achieving the minimization of the whole loss, we seek to reach a Pareto-stationary point where we cannot improve the loss of one task without degrading the loss of another task."
- Break condition: MGDA's convergence could be too slow or unstable, preventing the model from reaching a good Pareto front within training budget.

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: Auxiliary tasks expand the training signal, improving representation quality and robustness.
- Mechanism: Adding self-supervised or domain-knowledge tasks helps the model learn richer features shared across tasks, acting as a form of data augmentation without requiring additional labeled data.
- Core assumption: Multi-task learning induces a beneficial inductive bias that improves generalization and robustness to adversarial perturbations.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract]: "GAT increases the robust AUC of CheXpert medical imaging dataset from 50% to 83% and On CIFAR-10, GAT outperforms eight state-of-the-art adversarial training and achieves 56.21% robust accuracy with Resnet-50."
  - [section 5.2]: "For Atelectasis (blue), age task augmentation leads to lower results than the baseline. However, all remaining task augmentations outperform the baseline both on the clean and robust AUC."
- Break condition: Poorly chosen or noisy auxiliary tasks may introduce conflicting gradients that degrade main-task performance.

## Foundational Learning

- Concept: Multi-task learning (MTL) and shared encoders.
  - Why needed here: GAT relies on transforming a single-task model into a multi-task one by adding decoders for auxiliary tasks while sharing the encoder.
  - Quick check question: How does adding an auxiliary task affect the gradient flow during backpropagation?

  - Why needed here: GAT extends standard AT by incorporating multiple tasks into the inner maximization and outer minimization loops.
  - Quick check question: What is the difference between crafting adversarial examples using only the target task loss vs. using a joint loss?

- Concept: Pareto optimization and gradient alignment.
  - Why needed here: GAT uses MGDA to navigate conflicting gradients between clean/adv and main/auxiliary tasks without collapsing to a suboptimal weighted sum.
  - Quick check question: What is a Pareto-stationary point in the context of multi-task learning?

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map:
  - Shared encoder (e.g., ResNet-50) -> multiple decoders (one per task)
  - Auxiliary task decoders: classification for rotation/jigsaw, regression for age, etc.
  - Loss functions: standard cross-entropy/regression + curvature regularization
  - Optimizer: SGD with cosine annealing; MGDA for dynamic weighting

- Critical path:
  1. Extend single-task model with auxiliary decoders
  2. During each batch, generate clean and adversarial examples
  3. Compute losses for all tasks on both clean and adversarial data
  4. Apply MGDA to find Pareto-optimal weights
  5. Backpropagate weighted gradients and update shared encoder

- Design tradeoffs:
  - Adding auxiliary tasks increases model size and compute, but may reduce data requirements
  - Curvature regularization can hurt clean accuracy if over-applied
  - MGDA adds overhead per batch but avoids suboptimal static weighting

- Failure signatures:
  - Degraded clean accuracy: likely over-regularization or conflicting auxiliary tasks
  - Unstable training: MGDA convergence issues or poorly scaled losses
  - No robustness gain: auxiliary tasks not aligned with main task or curvature term ineffective

- First 3 experiments:
  1. Train GAT with only rotation auxiliary task on CIFAR-10; compare clean/robust accuracy to standard AT
  2. Disable curvature regularization; observe impact on robustness vs. clean accuracy trade-off
  3. Replace MGDA with equal weighting; measure Pareto front quality and robustness difference

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- Question: How does the choice of auxiliary task impact the trade-off between clean and robust accuracy in multi-task adversarial training?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper evaluates different auxiliary tasks (Jigsaw, Rotation, Macro) and their impact on robust accuracy, noting that Jigsaw task causes significant degradation of robustness.
- Why unresolved: The paper does not provide a comprehensive analysis of the trade-off between clean and robust accuracy for each auxiliary task, leaving the question of optimal task selection open.
- What evidence would resolve it: A systematic study comparing the impact of various auxiliary tasks on both clean and robust accuracy, potentially including additional tasks beyond those evaluated in the paper.

### Open Question 2
- Question: What is the optimal weighting strategy for combining clean and robust losses across multiple tasks in adversarial training?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper compares different weighting strategies (Equal, IMTL, MGDA, GradVac, PCGrad) and finds that MGDA yields the best clean and robust accuracy.
- Why unresolved: While MGDA performs well, the paper does not explore the possibility of optimizing the weighting strategy specifically for the adversarial training setting or investigate the impact of different weighting strategies on the trade-off between clean and robust accuracy.
- What evidence would resolve it: A detailed analysis of the impact of different weighting strategies on the trade-off between clean and robust accuracy, potentially including the development of a new weighting strategy specifically tailored for adversarial training.

### Open Question 3
- Question: How does the number of auxiliary tasks affect the robustness of multi-task adversarial training models?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper shows that the robust accuracy peaks at three tasks using the combination of Macro and Rotation auxiliary tasks, while combinations involving the Jigsaw task are less effective.
- Why unresolved: The paper does not provide a comprehensive analysis of the impact of the number of auxiliary tasks on robustness, leaving questions about the optimal number of tasks and their combination open.
- What evidence would resolve it: A systematic study evaluating the impact of varying numbers of auxiliary tasks on robustness, potentially including the development of a method for selecting the optimal number and combination of tasks.

## Limitations
- The exact contribution of each component (MGDA vs. curvature regularization vs. auxiliary tasks) is not fully isolated in ablation studies.
- The mechanism connecting curvature regularization to robustness relies on the assumption that a flatter loss landscape universally improves robustness.
- The generalizability to datasets outside medical imaging and CIFAR-10 is not fully established.

## Confidence
- Overall effectiveness of GAT: High
- Specific mechanisms (curvature regularization, MGDA): Medium
- Generalizability to other datasets: Low
