# Towards End-to-end Speech-to-text Summarization

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2306.05432
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.05432
- Reference count: 31
- Primary result: E2E model outperforms extractive baseline but lags behind cascade system in ROUGE scores

## Executive Summary
This paper introduces an end-to-end (E2E) speech-to-text (S2T) abstractive summarization model for French broadcast news. The model leverages transfer learning from a pre-trained text-to-text (T2T) summarizer and a pre-trained speech representation model (Wav2Vec 2.0). The E2E model is pre-trained on ASR data and fine-tuned on broadcast news (BNews) data. Experimental results show that both the E2E model and a cascade system outperform an extractive baseline, but the E2E model still lags behind the cascade model in automatic evaluation metrics (ROUGE scores). Human evaluation reveals that the cascade system performs better in terms of factual consistency, relevance, and fluency.

## Method Summary
The proposed E2E model uses Wav2Vec 2.0 to extract speech features, which are then processed by a cross-modal adapter before being fed into a decoder transferred from a pre-trained T2T summarizer. The cross-modal adapter is pre-trained in three stages: first on ASR data (masked speech feature prediction), then on BNews (encoder-decoder training), and finally on EOS prediction. The model is fine-tuned on the BNews dataset. A cascade system is also implemented for comparison, consisting of an ASR model and a T2T abstractive summarizer. Both models are evaluated on the BNews dataset using ROUGE scores and human evaluation metrics.

## Key Results
- E2E model and cascade system outperform extractive baseline on ROUGE scores
- E2E model lags behind cascade system in automatic evaluation (ROUGE scores)
- Human evaluation shows cascade system performs better in factual consistency, relevance, and fluency
- Fine-tuning the S2T abstractive summarizer after cross-modal adapter pre-training significantly improves ROUGE scores (25-50% relative increase)

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: Transfer learning from a T2T abstractive summarizer improves S2T performance by leveraging external text summarization data.
- Mechanism: The decoder from a pre-trained T2T summarizer is transferred to the E2E model, allowing the model to benefit from the large amount of training data in the MLSUM corpus without requiring paired speech/summary data.
- Core assumption: The decoder's language modeling capabilities are transferable across modalities (text to speech-to-text).
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "Our novel E2E model leverages external data by resorting to transfer learning from a pre-trained T2T summarizer."
  - [section 4.2] "As is illustrated on Fig. 2, the decoder from the T2T summarizer is transferred to the S2T summarizer, which allows leveraging the MLSUM training data."
- Break condition: If the cross-modal adapter fails to effectively map speech features to the decoder's expected input space, the transfer learning benefit is negated.

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: Pre-training the cross-modal adapter on ASR and BNews data significantly improves E2E model performance.
- Mechanism: The cross-modal adapter is pre-trained in three stages: first on ASR data (masked speech feature prediction), then on BNews (encoder-decoder training), and finally on EOS prediction. This staged pre-training helps the adapter learn to map speech features to textual embeddings effectively.
- Core assumption: Staged pre-training with increasingly task-specific data allows the cross-modal adapter to learn robust cross-modal representations.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [section 5.2] "The pre-training of the cross-modal adapter encompasses three controlled steps... The cross-modal adapter is trained to minimise the mean squared error (MSE) between the reference embeddings y(i) and the ones predicted from the masked sequence ˆy(i)."
  - [section 6.1] "Regarding the ablation studies, by comparing the performance of the E2E and E2E (nFT) models, it is found that fine-tuning the S2T abstractive summarizer after the pre-training of the cross-modal adapter significantly improves the ROUGE scores with a relative increase on the interval of 25% - 50%."
- Break condition: If the pre-training stages are not properly tuned (e.g., masking probability, learning rates), the adapter may not learn effective cross-modal mappings.

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: The E2E model can leverage non-verbal and acoustic information from speech that cascade systems cannot access.
- Mechanism: By processing raw speech features directly, the E2E model has access to acoustic cues (tone, pauses, emphasis) that are lost in automatic transcripts used by cascade systems.
- Core assumption: Non-verbal and acoustic information contains summarization-relevant cues that can improve summary quality.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "End-to-end (E2E) modelling of S2T abstractive summarization is a promising approach that offers the possibility of generating rich latent representations that leverage non-verbal and acoustic information, as opposed to the use of only linguistic information from automatically generated transcripts in cascade systems."
  - [section 2] "Cascade systems used for S2T summarization fail to utilize non-verbal and acoustic information that could be useful for summarization [22]."
- Break condition: If the cross-modal adapter cannot effectively encode acoustic information into useful textual features, the theoretical advantage is not realized in practice.

## Foundational Learning

- Concept: Speech feature extraction using pre-trained Wav2Vec 2.0 models
  - Why needed here: The E2E model requires high-quality speech representations as input. Wav2Vec 2.0 provides state-of-the-art speech representations that capture both acoustic and semantic information.
  - Quick check question: Why does the paper choose the 7th transformer layer of Wav2Vec 2.0 for feature extraction?

- Concept: Cross-modal representation learning
  - Why needed here: The cross-modal adapter must learn to map speech features (audio space) to textual embeddings (text space) so the transferred decoder can process them.
  - Quick check question: What attention mechanisms are used in the cross-modal adapter to facilitate cross-modal mapping?

- Concept: Teacher forcing and decoding strategies in sequence-to-sequence models
  - Why needed here: Both the cross-modal adapter and the T2T summarizer use Seq2seq architectures that require careful training and inference strategies.
  - Quick check question: How does the "peeling back" algorithm differ from standard teacher forcing, and why is it used during cross-modal adapter training?

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map: Speech → Wav2Vec features (layer 7) → Cross-modal adapter → Text decoder → Summary
- Critical path: Speech → Wav2Vec features → Cross-modal adapter → Text decoder → Summary
- Design tradeoffs:
  - Using pre-trained Wav2Vec 2.0 vs. training from scratch: Pre-trained provides better representations but may not be optimal for the specific domain.
  - Transfer learning the decoder vs. training from scratch: Transfer learning leverages external data but may introduce mismatch between modalities.
  - Staged pre-training vs. direct training: Staged pre-training helps with convergence but adds complexity.

- Failure signatures:
  - E2E performance significantly worse than cascade despite theoretical advantages → cross-modal adapter not effectively mapping speech to text features
  - E2E model generates repetitive or incoherent summaries → text decoder not handling speech-derived features well
  - E2E model fails to generate EOS → EOS prediction mechanism not working

- First 3 experiments:
  1. Compare E2E model performance with and without cross-modal adapter pre-training on a small validation set
  2. Ablation study: Replace cross-modal adapter with a simple linear projection layer to quantify its contribution
  3. Test E2E model on gold transcripts (bypassing ASR) to isolate the impact of acoustic information

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- Question: How can the performance gap between the E2E and cascade models be further reduced?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper states that the E2E model's performance is hindered by the limited amount of abstractive summarization training data available for pre-training the cross-modal adapter.
- Why unresolved: The paper suggests potential future directions, such as transferring the text encoder from the T2T abstractive summarizer and using augmented data from T2T summarization corpora, but does not provide concrete solutions or experimental results.
- What evidence would resolve it: Experimental results comparing the performance of the E2E model with different pre-training strategies, such as transferring the text encoder or using augmented data, would provide insights into the most effective approach for reducing the performance gap.

### Open Question 2
- Question: How does the cross-modal adapter's performance compare to the text encoder of the T2T abstractive summarizer in terms of generating rich textual latent representations?
- Basis in paper: [inferred] The paper suggests that the large T2T summarization corpus (MLSUM) played a significant role in the text encoder's ability to generate rich textual latent representations, while the cross-modal adapter only had access to the BNews corpus during its development.
- Why unresolved: The paper does not provide a direct comparison between the cross-modal adapter and the text encoder in terms of their ability to generate rich textual latent representations.
- What evidence would resolve it: A comparative analysis of the textual latent representations generated by the cross-modal adapter and the text encoder, using metrics such as the Projection Weighted Canonical Correlation Analysis (PWCCA) score, would provide insights into their relative performance.

### Open Question 3
- Question: How does the use of augmented data from T2T summarization corpora through speech synthesis affect the E2E model's performance?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper suggests that using augmented data from T2T summarization corpora through speech synthesis could be a potential future direction for improving the E2E model's performance.
- Why unresolved: The paper does not provide any experimental results or analysis on the impact of using augmented data through speech synthesis on the E2E model's performance.
- What evidence would resolve it: Experimental results comparing the performance of the E2E model with and without the use of augmented data from T2T summarization corpora through speech synthesis would provide insights into the effectiveness of this approach.

## Limitations

- Performance gap: E2E model underperforms cascade system in automatic evaluation metrics (ROUGE scores)
- Pre-training bug: Cross-modal adapter was not pre-trained on the full MLSUM dataset as intended
- Untested hypothesis: Paper does not experimentally verify whether E2E model can leverage acoustic information better than cascade system

## Confidence

- High Confidence: Transfer learning from pre-trained T2T summarizers improves E2E performance (25-50% relative increase in ROUGE scores)
- Medium Confidence: Staged pre-training of cross-modal adapter improves performance, but exact contribution of each stage is not isolated
- Low Confidence: Hypothesis that E2E models can leverage acoustic and non-verbal information remains speculative without direct experimental evidence

## Next Checks

1. Test acoustic advantage hypothesis: Run the E2E model on gold transcripts (bypassing ASR) and compare performance with the cascade system on the same gold transcripts to isolate whether the E2E model can leverage acoustic information.

2. Complete MLSUM pre-training: Reproduce the experiments with the cross-modal adapter pre-trained on the full MLSUM dataset to quantify the impact of the pre-training bug and validate the authors' hypothesis about data limitations.

3. Cross-modal adapter ablation: Replace the cross-modal adapter with a simpler projection layer (e.g., linear layer) and compare performance to quantify the adapter's specific contribution beyond basic feature transformation.
