# Generalized Weak Supervision for Neural Information Retrieval

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2304.08912
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2304.08912
- Authors: Multiple
- Reference count: 40
- Key outcome: GWS framework achieves 42.7% and 20.8% relative NDCG@10 improvements over BM25 and weakly supervised BERT respectively on WikiPassageQA

## Executive Summary
This paper introduces a generalized weak supervision (GWS) framework for training neural ranking models without manual labels by iteratively relabeling training data using the current model itself. The framework generalizes weak supervision through four implementations: self-labeling, cross-labeling, joint cross- and self-labeling, and greedy multi-labeling. The paper establishes a theoretical connection between self-labeling and the Expectation-Maximization algorithm, and demonstrates substantial performance improvements over both weak supervision and zero-shot baselines on two passage retrieval benchmarks.

## Method Summary
The GWS framework iteratively relabels training data using the current model, enabling the model to play the role of weak labeler. Four implementations are presented: self-labeling (model relabels its own training data), cross-labeling (multiple models relabel each other), joint cross- and self-labeling (combination approach), and greedy multi-labeling (incremental improvement strategy). The framework is theoretically connected to the EM algorithm, and incorporates query performance prediction models to reduce noise in generated training data. Experiments demonstrate significant improvements over traditional weak supervision approaches.

## Key Results
- Best BERT model achieves 42.7% relative NDCG@10 improvement over BM25 on WikiPassageQA
- Same model achieves 20.8% relative NDCG@10 improvement over weakly supervised BERT
- All four GWS implementations show substantial improvements over weak supervision and zero-shot baselines
- Query performance prediction models effectively reduce noise in generated training data

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The framework works by iteratively refining training labels through self-referential learning, where the model progressively improves its own supervision. Each iteration uses the current model's predictions to generate better training signals, creating a bootstrapping effect that refines ranking quality. The theoretical connection to EM provides mathematical grounding for the iterative improvement process, while the multiple implementation variants allow flexibility in how models interact during the relabeling process.

## Architecture Onboarding

Component Map: Weak labeler -> GWS framework -> Neural ranking model -> Improved retrieval performance

Critical Path: Initial weak supervision -> Iterative relabeling -> Model training -> Performance evaluation

Design Tradeoffs: The framework trades computational overhead (multiple training iterations) for improved ranking performance without manual labeling costs. The self-referential nature enables continuous improvement but may introduce overfitting risks.

Failure Signatures: Poor weak supervision quality can lead to degraded performance through iterative amplification of errors. Insufficient training iterations may not fully realize performance gains.

## Limitations
- Limited to two passage retrieval datasets (WikiPassageQA and AN antique), raising generalizability concerns
- Theoretical connection to EM algorithm mentioned but not rigorously established
- Potential overfitting to weak labels not adequately addressed
- Computational overhead of multiple training iterations not thoroughly analyzed
