# MG-ShopDial: A Multi-Goal Conversational Dataset for e-Commerce

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID**: 2304.12636
- **Source URL**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2304.12636
- **Reference count**: 40
- **Primary result**: Novel dataset of 64 English multi-goal e-commerce conversations (2,196 utterances) across four product categories

## Executive Summary
This paper introduces MG-ShopDial, a novel dataset of English multi-goal conversations in the e-commerce domain. Unlike existing conversational datasets that focus on single goals like search, recommendation, or question answering, MG-ShopDial captures realistic scenarios where users mix multiple goals within a single conversation. The dataset was collected using a coached human-human protocol across four product categories, resulting in 64 high-quality dialogues with utterance-level annotations for both intents and goals.

## Method Summary
The authors developed a coached human-human data collection protocol with a specialized tool to facilitate the collection of multi-goal conversations. This approach was necessary because natural conversations rarely contain multiple goals, making it difficult to collect such data through traditional methods. The protocol guided participants to create realistic multi-goal interactions while maintaining natural conversational flow. The resulting dataset contains 64 dialogues with 2,196 utterances across Sports and Outdoors, Books, Office Products, and Cell Phones and Accessories categories, with each utterance annotated for both intent and goal.

## Key Results
- MG-ShopDial contains 64 dialogues with 2,196 utterances across four product categories
- Conversational patterns consistently show two or three phases: recommendation, information seeking, and secondary recommendation
- Meta-communication is frequently used to maintain naturalness and facilitate goal transitions
- Clear distinction exists between recommendation and search/QA goals, but less distinction between search and QA

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The coached human-human data collection protocol enables the creation of realistic multi-goal conversations that would rarely occur naturally. By providing structured guidance while allowing natural conversational flow, the protocol captures authentic user behavior patterns when multiple goals are present simultaneously.

## Architecture Onboarding

**Component map**: Data collection tool -> Human coaches -> Participants -> Conversation annotation pipeline

**Critical path**: Collection protocol execution → Conversation generation → Utterance-level annotation → Dataset validation → Analysis

**Design tradeoffs**: Coached collection ensures multi-goal scenarios but may introduce artificial elements vs. natural collection that rarely produces multi-goal conversations.

**Failure signatures**: Conversations lacking clear goal transitions, unnatural meta-communication, or participants failing to engage with multi-goal structure.

**First experiments**: 1) Analyze goal transition frequency and patterns across all conversations. 2) Compare intent distribution between single-goal and multi-goal segments. 3) Evaluate annotator agreement on goal identification across different product categories.

## Limitations
- Dataset size (64 dialogues, 2,196 utterances) is relatively small compared to other conversational datasets, potentially limiting diversity representation
- Coached collection protocol may introduce biases that don't reflect natural user behavior patterns
- Analysis focuses on common conversational patterns but doesn't thoroughly examine edge cases or more complex goal combinations

## Confidence

**Dataset representativeness**: Medium - Sound methodology but small scale and potential coaching artifacts limit confidence
**Conversational pattern characterization**: Low-Medium - Observed phases appear logical but lack rigorous statistical validation and comprehensive edge case analysis

## Next Checks
