# MemeGraphs: Linking Memes to Knowledge Graphs

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID**: 2305.18391
- **Source URL**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.18391
- **Reference count**: 36
- **One-line primary result**: MemeGraphs outperforms ImgBERT on MultiOFF dataset for hatefulness detection using structured visual and knowledge graph representations

## Executive Summary
This paper introduces MemeGraphs, a novel method for classifying memes by incorporating scene graphs and knowledge graphs into a Transformer-based architecture. The approach automatically generates scene graphs using a pre-trained model and links detected entities to background knowledge from Wikidata. The resulting structured representations are serialized as text and fed into a text-based Transformer model for classification. Experiments on the MultiOFF dataset show that MemeGraphs outperforms ImgBERT, a multimodal model using learned representations, achieving better F1 scores in detecting hateful content in memes.

## Method Summary
MemeGraphs combines scene graphs and knowledge graphs as structured representations for meme classification. Scene graphs are generated automatically from meme images using the Schemata model, capturing objects and their visual relations as triplets. Named entities extracted from meme text are linked to Wikidata entries, with their descriptions concatenated to provide external context. The combined structured data is serialized as text and fed into a BERT model for classification, allowing the model to reason about both visual structure and contextual knowledge without requiring multimodal fine-tuning.

## Key Results
- MemeGraphs achieves higher F1 scores than ImgBERT on the MultiOFF dataset for hatefulness detection
- Automatic augmentation outperforms manual augmentation by linking more entities and relations
- The combination of scene graphs and knowledge graphs provides complementary information for classification

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: Scene graphs improve meme classification by making visual relations explicit and consumable by text-only models.
- Mechanism: The Schemata model detects objects and their relations in meme images and serializes these as triplets like "0-man has 11-eye". These triplets are concatenated into a text sequence that can be processed by BERT alongside the meme's original text.
- Core assumption: Visual relations captured in scene graphs contain discriminative information for hatefulness classification that is not redundant with textual cues.
- Evidence anchors: [abstract] "we propose to use scene graphs... and knowledge graphs as structured representations for meme classification with a Transformer-based architecture."

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: Knowledge graphs improve meme classification by linking detected entities to background knowledge that aids contextual interpretation.
- Mechanism: Named entities extracted from meme text are linked to Wikidata entries, and their descriptions are concatenated into the model input. This provides the classifier with external context that helps disambiguate entity references.
- Core assumption: Entity descriptions from knowledge bases contain context that is necessary for correctly interpreting the meme's intent.
- Evidence anchors: [section 3.2] "This model detects named entities in a text... It can provide useful information that assists to natural language understanding."

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: Automatic augmentation outperforms manual augmentation because it captures more entities and relations, providing richer structured representations.
- Mechanism: The automatic pipeline processes all memes uniformly and links more entities than human annotators, who may miss entities or be inconsistent.
- Core assumption: The quantity and consistency of structured data from automatic methods outweighs the quality gains from human corrections.
- Evidence anchors: [abstract] "Analysis shows that automatic methods link more entities than human annotators and that automatically generated graphs are better suited for hatefulness classification in memes."

## Foundational Learning

- **Concept: Scene graph generation and serialization**
  - Why needed here: To convert visual content into a text format that BERT can process without requiring multimodal fine-tuning.
  - Quick check question: What is the format of a scene graph triplet, and how are they concatenated into a single text input?

- **Concept: Named entity recognition and knowledge base linking**
  - Why needed here: To augment meme text with external context that clarifies ambiguous references and supports interpretation.
  - Quick check question: How does the NER model output map to Wikidata lookups, and what happens if no entry exists?

- **Concept: Transformer text classification with multimodal concatenated input**
  - Why needed here: To leverage the strengths of BERT in understanding complex textual inputs that now include structured visual and knowledge graph data.
  - Quick check question: How does the model distinguish between original meme text and augmented graph/knowledge text in the input sequence?

## Architecture Onboarding

- **Component map**: Image → Schemata → Serialized triplet text; Text → NER → Entity list → Wikidata lookup → Serialized descriptions; Concatenated text → BERT → Classification head
- **Critical path**: Image → Schemata → Text serialization → BERT input assembly → Prediction
- **Design tradeoffs**: Using structured text representations allows leveraging pre-trained text models but may lose spatial layout information; automatic augmentation scales but may introduce noise.
- **Failure signatures**: Low F1 scores despite correct data flow suggest either the structured data is not discriminative or the serialization loses critical information.
- **First 3 experiments**:
  1. Run scene graph generation on a small subset and inspect serialized output for correctness and coverage.
  2. Test NER + Wikidata lookup on meme texts to verify entity detection and description retrieval quality.
  3. Train BERT on original text only and measure baseline F1, then incrementally add scene graph and knowledge inputs to measure improvements.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- Question: How does the performance of MemeGraphs compare to multimodal models that incorporate additional features like visual attributes, CLIP representations, or automatic captions?
- Basis in paper: [inferred] The paper compares MemeGraphs to ImgBERT but does not explore combining structured representations with additional multimodal features.
- Why unresolved: The paper focuses on comparing structured visual and knowledge graph representations to learned image embeddings without exploring complementary approaches.
- What evidence would resolve it: Experiments comparing MemeGraphs to multimodal models incorporating visual attributes, CLIP, or captions on the same dataset.

### Open Question 2
- Question: How does the performance of MemeGraphs generalize to other meme datasets beyond MultiOFF?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper evaluates MemeGraphs on the MultiOFF dataset containing memes from the 2016 U.S. Presidential Election but does not report results on other datasets.
- Why unresolved: The paper demonstrates effectiveness on a specific dataset but lacks evidence of generalizability to different meme types and topics.
- What evidence would resolve it: Evaluating MemeGraphs on other meme datasets like Hateful Memes or MAMI.

### Open Question 3
- Question: What is the impact of the quality of the scene graph generation model and the NER model on the performance of MemeGraphs?
- Basis in paper: [inferred] The paper uses off-the-shelf models but does not investigate how model quality affects final performance.
- Why unresolved: The paper assumes pre-trained models provide adequate quality without exploring sensitivity to component accuracy.
- What evidence would resolve it: Experiments using different scene graph generation and NER models with varying levels of accuracy.

## Limitations

- Reliance on off-the-shelf models (Schemata, spaCy NER, Wikidata) introduces variability that may not transfer to other meme datasets or languages.
- 67 out of 743 memes could not be processed automatically, raising questions about robustness in real-world deployment.
- Limited comparison to multimodal baselines, only evaluating against ImgBERT without exploring additional feature combinations.

## Confidence

- **High confidence**: The core methodology of converting visual and knowledge graph data into text for BERT processing is technically sound and well-documented.
- **Medium confidence**: The claim that automatic augmentation outperforms manual annotation is supported by reported results but lacks detailed analysis of specific differences.
- **Low confidence**: The assertion that scene graphs and knowledge graphs provide complementary information is plausible but not empirically validated through ablation studies.

## Next Checks

1. **Ablation study on structured components**: Run experiments that isolate the contribution of scene graphs, knowledge graphs, and their combination by training separate models on each input type.

2. **Error analysis of automatic vs manual annotation**: Conduct a detailed comparison of entities and relations captured by automatic methods versus human annotators on a small subset of memes.

3. **Cross-dataset generalization test**: Apply the MemeGraphs pipeline to a different meme classification dataset without retraining the scene graph or NER models to measure performance degradation.
