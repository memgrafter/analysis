# BOMP-NAS: Bayesian Optimization Mixed Precision NAS

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2301.11810
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2301.11810
- **Reference count:** 19
- **Primary result:** Achieves 67.36% accuracy on CIFAR-10 with only 4.57 kB model size, outperforming JASQ by 1.4 percentage points at similar size.

## Executive Summary
BOMP-NAS introduces a novel neural architecture search (NAS) method that integrates mixed-precision quantization with Bayesian optimization, enabling efficient discovery of compact, high-performance networks. By leveraging quantization-aware fine-tuning during the search loop, the approach achieves strong performance under low-precision quantization, resulting in nearly 50% model size reduction on CIFAR-10. The method combines early training, quantization-aware fine-tuning, and multi-objective Bayesian optimization to find networks that outperform state-of-the-art results with up to 6× shorter search times. BOMP-NAS produces a Pareto front of trade-offs between accuracy and model size, supporting flexible deployment on resource-constrained edge devices.

## Method Summary
BOMP-NAS combines mixed-precision quantization with Bayesian optimization within a neural architecture search framework. The method uses quantization-aware fine-tuning during the search loop, which is essential for achieving strong performance under low-precision quantization. By integrating early training, quantization-aware fine-tuning, and multi-objective Bayesian optimization, BOMP-NAS efficiently discovers compact networks that balance accuracy and model size. The search process is accelerated by up to 6× compared to previous methods, while achieving significant model size reductions and high accuracy.

## Key Results
- Achieves 67.36% accuracy on CIFAR-10 with only 4.57 kB model size, outperforming JASQ by 1.4 percentage points at similar size.
- Enables nearly 50% model size reduction on CIFAR-10 through mixed-precision quantization.
- Outperforms state-of-the-art results with up to 6× shorter search times.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
BOMP-NAS works by integrating mixed-precision quantization directly into the neural architecture search process, guided by Bayesian optimization. This allows the search to consider both architectural choices and quantization parameters simultaneously, leading to compact networks that maintain high accuracy. Quantization-aware fine-tuning during search ensures that the discovered architectures are robust to quantization effects, which is crucial for achieving strong performance under low-precision constraints.

## Foundational Learning

**Bayesian Optimization**
- *Why needed:* Efficiently navigates the high-dimensional, non-convex search space of neural architectures and quantization parameters.
- *Quick check:* Verify that the acquisition function balances exploration and exploitation during search.

**Quantization-Aware Fine-Tuning**
- *Why needed:* Ensures discovered architectures are robust to quantization effects, critical for low-precision deployment.
- *Quick check:* Confirm that fine-tuning occurs during search, not just after.

**Mixed-Precision Quantization**
- *Why needed:* Allows different layers to use different bit-widths, optimizing the accuracy-size trade-off.
- *Quick check:* Inspect the bit-width distribution across layers in discovered architectures.

## Architecture Onboarding

**Component Map**
Search Space -> Bayesian Optimizer -> Quantization-Aware Trainer -> Performance Evaluator -> Pareto Front

**Critical Path**
Bayesian optimizer selects candidate architectures, which are trained and evaluated with quantization-aware fine-tuning; results update the surrogate model for the next iteration.

**Design Tradeoffs**
- Balancing search efficiency (speed) with solution quality (accuracy/size).
- Choosing between uniform and mixed-precision quantization.
- Deciding the extent of fine-tuning during search versus post-search.

**Failure Signatures**
- Search converges to architectures with poor accuracy under quantization.
- Search time remains high despite optimizations.
- Discovered architectures are not robust to distribution shifts or adversarial attacks.

## Limitations
- Evaluation is limited to two datasets (CIFAR-10 and Tiny-ImageNet) and a narrow set of NAS baselines.
- Search cost and memory requirements for multi-objective Bayesian optimization are not detailed.
- Does not address robustness to distribution shifts or adversarial attacks.

## Confidence

**High confidence:**
- Novelty and practical relevance of combining mixed-precision quantization with Bayesian optimization in NAS.

**Medium confidence:**
- Reported efficiency gains (search time, model size reduction), given the limited scope of experiments and absence of comparisons with the most recent NAS methods.

**Low confidence:**
- Generalizability and robustness of discovered architectures beyond the tested settings.
