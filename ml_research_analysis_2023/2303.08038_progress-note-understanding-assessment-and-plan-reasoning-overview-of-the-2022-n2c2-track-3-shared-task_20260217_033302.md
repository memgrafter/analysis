# Progress Note Understanding -- Assessment and Plan Reasoning: Overview of the 2022 N2C2 Track 3 Shared Task

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2303.08038
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.08038
- Reference count: 24
- Primary result: The best-performing system achieved a Macro F1 score of 0.8212

## Executive Summary
This paper presents the overview of the 2022 National NLP Clinical Challenges (N2C2) Track 3 Shared Task, which focused on predicting causal relations between the Assessment section and Plan subsections in daily progress notes. The task aimed to identify and prioritize diagnoses as first steps in diagnostic decision support to find the most relevant information in long documents like daily progress notes. Participants used transformer-based models, with the top-performing system using an ensemble of BERT models fine-tuned on both progress notes and discharge summaries.

## Method Summary
The shared task required participants to develop NLP systems that automatically predict causal relations between the overall status of the patient contained in the Assessment section and its relation to each component of the Plan section. The task used progress notes from the MIMIC-III database, with 4670 training pairs, 594 development pairs, and 677 test pairs. Participants submitted their predictions and survey responses about their methods. The primary evaluation metric was Macro F1 score, with 95% confidence intervals calculated using bootstrapping.

## Key Results
- The best-performing system achieved a Macro F1 score of 0.8212
- The average Macro F1 score across all submitted teams was 0.7949
- Performance was notably lower on the "Indirect" and "Neither" relation labels

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- **Claim:** The task succeeds because it aligns with real clinical reasoning patterns where physicians implicitly connect Assessment diagnoses to Plan treatments.
- **Mechanism:** By modeling the causal link between Assessment section summaries and Plan subsection diagnoses as a classification task, the system learns to mimic physician prioritization logic.
- **Core assumption:** The explicit labels of "Direct," "Indirect," "Neither," and "Not Relevant" capture clinically meaningful distinctions in reasoning that can be generalized from training data.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract]: "The goal of the task was to develop and evaluate NLP systems that automatically predict causal relations between the overall status of the patient contained in the Assessment section and its relation to each component of the Plan section."
  - [section]: "The Assessment section summarizes the patients' active health problems or diseases for that day. The Plan section consists of multiple subsections, each addressing a specific health issue or diagnosis followed by a detailed treatment plan."
- **Break condition:** If the physician reasoning patterns are too implicit or context-dependent to be captured in labeled pairs, the model will misclassify indirect or complex relationships.

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim:** Pre-trained biomedical language models (BERT, ClinicalBERT, GatorTron, etc.) provide strong base representations for clinical text understanding, enabling effective fine-tuning for this relation prediction task.
- **Mechanism:** Transformer models pre-trained on large EHR and biomedical corpora encode medical terminology and semantic relationships, which are then adapted to the specific relation classification via supervised learning on the MIMIC-III labeled data.
- **Core assumption:** The domain-specific pretraining captures enough medical context and terminology that fine-tuning on the task data is sufficient to learn the relation prediction patterns.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract]: "We received 29 submissions for the survey with the response rate for each question shown in Table 5. None of the submissions used other datasets besides MIMIC or involved multi-modal models."
  - [section]: "We observed that models trained on EHR data showed a subtle advantage over the other pre-trained models, as two of the top 3 systems were trained on MIMIC dataset."
- **Break condition:** If the medical reasoning required goes beyond what can be captured in token-level embeddings (e.g., requires cross-note or multi-modal reasoning), the models will plateau in performance.

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim:** Incorporating structured medical knowledge (e.g., UMLS, MetaMap) into the model pipeline improves relation prediction accuracy by providing explicit concept mappings and hierarchies.
- **Mechanism:** Feature extraction from medical knowledge bases (concept codes, semantic types) augments the text representations, allowing the model to better distinguish between related diagnoses and treatments.
- **Core assumption:** The knowledge base concepts align well with the language used in the notes and the reasoning patterns required for the task.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [section]: "Three of eight teams utilized the Unified Medical Language System (UMLS), a large medical vocabulary for medical concepts [14]. The best-performing system developed by team CMU designed an ensembled BERT method and trained the models on both progress notes and discharge summaries."
  - [abstract]: "Four teams answered 'Yes' to the question of whether the entire progress note was being used or other parts of the notes were used; however, the additional sections or notes were only utilized in pre-training and continuous training."
- **Break condition:** If the medical terminology or reasoning patterns in the notes deviate significantly from the knowledge base structure, the features may introduce noise or misalignment.

## Foundational Learning

- **Concept:** SOAP note structure and clinical reasoning patterns
  - **Why needed here:** The task is built on understanding the relationship between Assessment (diagnoses/problems) and Plan (treatments) subsections, which mirrors how physicians document and reason about patient care.
  - **Quick check question:** Can you identify the Assessment and Plan sections in a sample SOAP note and explain their clinical purpose?

- **Concept:** Named Entity Recognition (NER) and medical concept extraction
  - **Why needed here:** Many top systems used NER taggers (e.g., Stanza) to identify medical entities before relation prediction, improving the model's ability to focus on relevant concepts.
  - **Quick check question:** Given a sentence from an Assessment section, can you extract the diagnoses/problems and their semantic types using a medical NER tool?

- **Concept:** Pre-trained language models and fine-tuning for classification
  - **Why needed here:** All submissions used transformer models (BERT, ClinicalBERT, etc.) fine-tuned on the labeled data; understanding how to prepare inputs, train, and evaluate is critical for replicating or extending the work.
  - **Quick check question:** How would you format an Assessment-Plan pair as input to a BERT model for relation classification?

## Architecture Onboarding

- **Component map:** Tokenization (BERT tokenizer) of concatenated Assessment + Plan + special tokens -> Optional NER tagging, UMLS concept extraction, or structured features -> Transformer-based classifier (BERT, ClinicalBERT, GatorTron, etc.) or ensemble -> Softmax over four relation classes (Direct, Indirect, Neither, Not Relevant) -> Macro F1 evaluation with bootstrapping for confidence intervals

- **Critical path:**
  1. Data preparation: Parse SOAP notes, extract Assessment-Plan pairs, label relations
  2. Feature engineering: Optional NER, concept extraction, or ordering features
  3. Model training: Fine-tune transformer on labeled pairs, possibly ensemble
  4. Evaluation: Test on held-out set, report Macro F1 and CIs

- **Design tradeoffs:**
  - Single vs. ensemble models: Ensembles (e.g., CMU's Bayesian combination) achieved higher performance but at increased complexity
  - Full note vs. subsection input: Full note could provide more context but was rarely used; most models focused on the Assessment-Plan pair
  - Knowledge integration: Adding UMLS features helped some teams but required extra preprocessing and alignment

- **Failure signatures:**
  - High error on "Indirect" and "Neither" labels suggests the model struggles with non-obvious or absent causal links
  - Performance plateaus despite model complexity may indicate the task requires more nuanced reasoning or longer context than provided
  - Overfitting to specific physician styles or note templates if training data is not diverse enough

- **First 3 experiments:**
  1. Train a baseline ClinicalBERT model on the Assessment-Plan pairs and evaluate Macro F1; compare to random baseline.
  2. Add NER-tagged features (e.g., using Stanza) as input to the model and measure impact on performance, especially for "Indirect" relations.
  3. Create an ensemble of two transformer models (e.g., ClinicalBERT + GatorTron) and combine predictions via majority vote or Bayesian inference; evaluate if Macro F1 improves over single models.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- Question: What is the optimal sample size needed for future N2C2 shared tasks to detect meaningful differences in model performance?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper states "no formal sample size calculations were performed" and suggests "future directions in shared tasks should include sample size calculations a priori"
- Why unresolved: The current test set may be underpowered to detect performance differences between systems, as noted by the authors
- What evidence would resolve it: Conducting a priori power analysis to determine required sample size, then comparing results with the current test set

### Open Question 2
- Question: How would multi-center sampling across different EHR systems and clinical disciplines affect model performance?
- Basis in paper: [inferred] The paper mentions limitations including single health system sampling and suggests "multi-center sampling across more disciplines is another future direction"
- Why unresolved: The current task used data from a single ICU setting, limiting generalizability
- What evidence would resolve it: Conducting the same task using multi-center data from different EHR vendors and clinical settings

### Open Question 3
- Question: Would incorporating full progress notes and multi-modal data (vital signs, labs, imaging) significantly improve model performance?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper notes that "important details were available in the Subjective and Objective sections that were missed in all model inputs" and suggests "future research may include working on long document processing and multi-modal cNLP models"
- Why unresolved: None of the participants used the full progress note or multi-modal data
- What evidence would resolve it: Re-running the task with models that incorporate full progress notes and structured clinical data as input

## Limitations

- The current test set may be underpowered to detect performance differences between systems
- The task used data from a single ICU setting, limiting generalizability
- None of the participants used the full progress note or multi-modal data, which may have provided additional context

## Confidence

- **High Confidence:** The task design and evaluation metrics are clearly specified, and the use of transformer-based models is well-supported by the results.
- **Medium Confidence:** The effectiveness of incorporating structured medical knowledge (UMLS, MetaMap) is supported by some teams' results, but the specific feature engineering details are not provided.
- **Low Confidence:** The exact ensemble strategies and model combinations used by top teams are not fully disclosed, making it difficult to replicate their exact performance.
