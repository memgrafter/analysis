# A Scalable Walsh-Hadamard Regularizer to Overcome the Low-degree Spectral Bias of Neural Networks

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2305.09779
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.09779
- Reference count: 40
- Primary result: HASH WH regularizer significantly improves generalization, especially in low-data regime, by encouraging sparsity in hashed Walsh-Hadamard spectrum

## Executive Summary
This paper addresses the low-degree spectral bias of neural networks, where models tend to learn lower-degree Fourier coefficients earlier during training, which can hurt generalization. The authors propose a novel regularizer called HASH WH that hashes the Walsh-Hadamard transform of the neural network and imposes an L1 norm on the buckets to encourage sparsity. This regularizer helps the network avoid learning erroneous low-degree frequencies and learn relevant high-degree frequencies. Experiments on synthetic and real-world datasets show that HASH WH significantly improves generalization performance, especially in the low-data regime, compared to standard neural networks and other relevant baselines. The regularizer is also computationally efficient and scales well with the input dimension.

## Method Summary
The proposed method, HASH WH, is a scalable regularizer that addresses the low-degree spectral bias of neural networks. It works by hashing the Walsh-Hadamard transform of the neural network into buckets and imposing an L1 norm on these buckets to encourage sparsity. The hashing matrix is a uniform random binary matrix that is re-sampled each epoch. The regularizer is integrated into the training loop of a 5-layer fully connected neural network with LeakyReLU activations. The trade-off between computational complexity and regularization quality is controlled by adjusting the hashing matrix size b. Experiments are conducted on synthetic datasets with pseudo-Boolean functions and real-world datasets, comparing the performance of HASH WH with standard neural networks and other baselines like EN-S, XGBoost, Random Forest, and Lasso.

## Key Results
- HASH WH significantly improves generalization performance, especially in the low-data regime, compared to standard neural networks and other baselines.
- The regularizer helps the network avoid learning erroneous low-degree frequencies and learn relevant high-degree frequencies.
- HASH WH is computationally efficient and scales well with the input dimension.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
Neural networks exhibit low-degree spectral bias, where they tend to learn lower-degree Fourier coefficients earlier during training, which can hurt generalization. HASH WH addresses this by hashing the Walsh-Hadamard transform into buckets and imposing an L1 norm on these buckets to encourage sparsity. This sparsity promotes the learning of higher-degree frequencies while avoiding overfitting to lower-degree ones. The hashing mechanism allows the regularizer to scale efficiently with the input dimension.

## Foundational Learning
- **Walsh-Hadamard Transform**: A fast orthogonal transform that decomposes a function into its frequency components in the Fourier domain. Why needed: It provides the frequency representation of the neural network's function, which is crucial for the spectral regularization. Quick check: Verify that the transform correctly decomposes a known function into its Fourier coefficients.
- **Spectral Bias**: The tendency of neural networks to learn lower-degree Fourier coefficients earlier during training. Why needed: Understanding this bias is key to developing effective regularization techniques. Quick check: Analyze the learning curves of lower and higher-degree frequencies during training.
- **L1 Regularization**: A sparsity-inducing norm that encourages many coefficients to be exactly zero. Why needed: It helps the network focus on the most relevant features and avoid overfitting. Quick check: Compare the sparsity of the hashed Walsh-Hadamard spectrum with and without L1 regularization.
- **Hashing**: A technique to map high-dimensional data to a lower-dimensional space using a hash function. Why needed: It allows the regularizer to scale efficiently with the input dimension. Quick check: Verify that the hashing preserves the essential structure of the Walsh-Hadamard spectrum.

### Component Map

### Critical Path
The critical path for HASH WH is the integration of the regularizer into the training loop. The Walsh-Hadamard transform and hashing operations are performed on the network's output, and the L1 norm of the hashed spectrum is added to the loss function. This path ensures that the regularizer effectively encourages sparsity in the frequency domain during training.

### Design Tradeoffs
The main design tradeoff in HASH WH is the choice of the hashing matrix size b. A larger b provides more fine-grained control over the frequency spectrum but increases computational complexity. A smaller b is more efficient but may not capture the full spectrum effectively. The tradeoff is problem-dependent and should be tuned based on the input dimension and the desired level of regularization.
