# On the Initialisation of Wide Low-Rank Feedforward Neural Networks

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2301.13710
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2301.13710
- Reference count: 40
- This paper analyzes the edge-of-chaos dynamics of randomly initialized low-rank feedforward neural networks

## Executive Summary
This paper extends the edge-of-chaos analysis of random neural network initialization to the low-rank setting. The authors derive optimal initialization schemes for low-rank weight matrices that maintain critical dynamics (neither exploding nor vanishing) during forward propagation. By analyzing how singular values and rank constraints affect network dynamics, they provide principled guidance for initializing low-rank networks that balances expressivity with stable training behavior.

## Method Summary
The authors extend the mean-field theory framework to analyze low-rank weight matrices. They derive analytical expressions for the optimal weight and bias variances that maintain edge-of-chaos dynamics, showing these should be scaled by the rank-to-width ratio. The analysis includes depth scales and gradient scaling behaviors as functions of rank-to-width ratio, with numerical experiments validating theoretical predictions across different network configurations.

## Key Results
- Weight and bias variances should be scaled by the rank-to-width ratio to maintain edge-of-chaos dynamics
- The variance of the input-output Jacobian increases as the rank-to-width ratio decreases, leading to greater variability in training
- Depth scales and training gradient scalings are derived as functions of the rank-to-width ratio

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
Low-rank weight matrices constrain the network's expressivity while maintaining critical dynamics when properly initialized. The singular value decomposition structure allows controlled scaling of the effective weight variance through the rank parameter. By scaling initialization variances with rank-to-width ratio, the network maintains signal propagation properties similar to full-rank networks despite having fewer parameters.

## Foundational Learning
- Edge-of-chaos dynamics: The critical point where neural network signals neither explode nor vanish during propagation. Understanding this is essential for stable training initialization.
- Mean-field theory for neural networks: Framework analyzing infinite-width networks through deterministic evolution of activation distributions. Needed to derive analytical initialization conditions.
- Low-rank matrix initialization: How to properly scale singular values when initializing low-rank weight matrices. Critical for maintaining signal propagation properties.
- Jacobian spectrum analysis: How the distribution of singular values affects the variance of network Jacobians. Important for understanding training stability.
- Depth scales in neural networks: Characteristic distances over which signals meaningfully propagate. Determines effective network depth for a given initialization.

## Limitations
- The analysis is asymptotic, relying on infinite-width limit assumptions
- Practical implications for finite-width networks remain uncertain
- No clear thresholds specified for when low-rank approximations break down

## Confidence
- Weight and bias variance scaling rules (High confidence)
- Increased Jacobian variance with lower rank (Medium confidence)
