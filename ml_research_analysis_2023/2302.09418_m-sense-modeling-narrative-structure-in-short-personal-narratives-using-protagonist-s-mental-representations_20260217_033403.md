# M-SENSE: Modeling Narrative Structure in Short Personal Narratives Using Protagonist's Mental Representations

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2302.09418
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2302.09418
- Reference count: 22
- F1 scores: 0.694 for climax, 0.743 for resolution (20% improvement over baselines)

## Executive Summary
This paper introduces M-SENSE, a model for identifying key narrative structure elements—climax and resolution—in short personal narratives. The approach combines protagonist mental state embeddings (intent and emotion) with contextual semantic embeddings using a multi-feature fusion strategy. The model achieves significant improvements over prior supervised baselines, demonstrating that mental state representations are crucial for narrative structure identification. The method also shows effectiveness on out-of-domain movie plot synopsis data.

## Method Summary
M-SENSE employs an ensemble of sentence encoders including token-level BERT for semantic embeddings and the NEMO model for protagonist intent and emotion embeddings. These embeddings are fused using a Transformer-based layer to create unified sentence representations. A story encoder with Transformer layers extracts narrative-level features, and an interaction layer computes similarity metrics between sequential context windows. The final classification layer predicts climax, resolution, or none. The model is trained on annotated Reddit personal narratives and evaluated using F1 scores for climax and resolution classification.

## Key Results
- M-SENSE achieves F1 scores of 0.694 for climax and 0.743 for resolution
- Represents absolute improvements of approximately 20% over prior supervised baselines
- Mental state embeddings (particularly intent and emotion) are shown to be crucial for performance gains
- Model demonstrates effectiveness on out-of-domain movie plot synopsis dataset

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
**Claim**: Protagonist's mental state embeddings capture pivotal narrative events more effectively than semantic embeddings alone.
**Mechanism**: The M-SENSE model fuses protagonist intent and emotion embeddings with semantic embeddings to identify climax and resolution. This integration captures both the linguistic and psychological trajectory of the protagonist.
**Core assumption**: The protagonist's mental state (intent and emotion) correlates strongly with the narrative's dramatic arc, particularly at key turning points.
**Evidence anchors**: [abstract] "Our model is able to achieve significant improvements in the task of identifying climax and resolution." [section] "Experiments that analyze the impact of our modeling choices... we gauge the influence of incorporating mental state embeddings and report an improvement in F1 scores of ~11% and ~13% over the base model for predicting climax and resolution respectively."

### Mechanism 2
**Claim**: The Transformer-based fusion layer effectively combines multiple types of embeddings to create a unified representation.
**Mechanism**: The fusion layer treats multiple latent vectors as a sequence of features by concatenating them and applying a Transformer layer to derive a unified sentence embedding.
**Core assumption**: Different types of embeddings capture complementary aspects of the narrative, and their combination provides a richer representation than any single type alone.
**Evidence anchors**: [section] "We apply a fusion strategy to derive a unified sentence embedding for our classification task." [section] "The final hidden representation of [FUSE] token obtained after feeding them to a Transformer layer is the fused output sentence representation."

### Mechanism 3
**Claim**: The story encoder captures inter-sentence dependencies and narrative context, improving identification of narrative structure elements.
**Mechanism**: The story encoder applies Transformer layers on top of sentence representations to extract narrative-level features, focusing on different sentences in the narrative and producing context-aware sentence embeddings.
**Core assumption**: Narrative structure elements are not determined by individual sentences in isolation but by their context within the entire narrative.
**Evidence anchors**: [section] "We apply Transformer layers on the top of the sentence representations to extract narrative-level features." [section] "The transformer layer focuses on possibly different sentences in the narrative, and produces context-aware sentence embeddings."

## Architecture Onboarding

- **Component map**: Input Narrative → Ensemble Sentence Encoders → Fusion Layer → Story Encoder → Interaction Layer → Classification Layer
- **Critical path**: Ensemble Sentence Encoders → Fusion Layer → Story Encoder → Interaction Layer → Classification Layer
- **Design tradeoffs**:
  - Token-level BERT vs. sentence-level BERT: Token-level provides more fine-grained information but requires more processing
  - Mental state embeddings vs. only semantic embeddings: Mental state embeddings improve performance but add complexity
  - Transformer-based fusion vs. other fusion methods: Transformers capture complex relationships but are computationally expensive
- **Failure signatures**:
  - Poor performance on climax/resolution detection: May indicate issues with mental state embeddings or story encoder
  - Overfitting: May indicate need for more regularization or data augmentation
  - Slow training: May indicate need for optimization or hardware upgrade
- **First 3 experiments**:
  1. Compare performance with and without mental state embeddings (M-SENSE vs. M-SENSE-Fusion)
  2. Evaluate different sentence encoders (token-level BERT vs. sentence-level BERT vs. USE)
  3. Test the impact of the story encoder by removing it and comparing performance

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
**Question**: How do the mental state embeddings specifically contribute to identifying climax versus resolution in narratives?
**Basis in paper**: [explicit] The paper notes that intent embeddings are more helpful for climax prediction while emotion embeddings aid resolution prediction, but does not provide detailed analysis of this difference.
**Why unresolved**: The paper provides overall performance improvements but lacks granular analysis of how mental state embeddings differentially impact climax versus resolution identification.
**What evidence would resolve it**: Detailed analysis comparing performance on climax versus resolution when using only intent embeddings, only emotion embeddings, or different combinations of mental state embeddings.

### Open Question 2
**Question**: How does the model perform on longer narratives beyond 14 sentences?
**Basis in paper**: [inferred] The paper mentions that token-level BERT outperforms sentence-level BERT for narratives up to 14 sentences, but performance degrades beyond this length, suggesting potential limitations.
**Why unresolved**: The paper only analyzes performance up to 14 sentences and does not explore model behavior on longer narratives.
**What evidence would resolve it**: Performance analysis of the model on narratives significantly longer than 14 sentences, including comparison of different sentence encoders.

### Open Question 3
**Question**: What is the impact of the protagonist assumption when applying the model to movie plot synopses?
**Basis in paper**: [explicit] The paper notes that they assume the protagonist is the top character from IMDb cast information when applying the model to movie plot synopses, acknowledging this may not always be true.
**Why unresolved**: The paper does not analyze how often this assumption is incorrect or how it affects model performance.
**What evidence would resolve it**: Analysis of model performance when the protagonist assumption is correct versus incorrect, including identification of cases where the assumption fails.

## Limitations

- The exact implementation details and training procedure for the NEMO model (used for extracting mental state embeddings) are not fully specified
- Model performance on longer narratives or narratives with more complex structures remains untested
- The 20% absolute improvement is based on a relatively small dataset (STORYES), raising questions about generalizability

## Confidence

**High Confidence**: The architectural approach of combining semantic and mental state embeddings is well-grounded in narrative theory and NLP literature. The reported F1 scores and ablation studies are supported by experimental results.

**Medium Confidence**: The claim that mental state embeddings specifically capture narrative structure elements better than semantic embeddings alone is supported by results, but the underlying mechanism could benefit from further qualitative analysis.

**Low Confidence**: The exact contribution of each component (sentence encoder choice, fusion layer architecture, story encoder depth) to overall performance is difficult to isolate definitively from the ablation studies presented.

## Next Checks
