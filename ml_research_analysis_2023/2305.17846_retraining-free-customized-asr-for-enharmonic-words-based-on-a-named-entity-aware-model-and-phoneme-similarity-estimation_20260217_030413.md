# Retraining-free Customized ASR for Enharmonic Words Based on a Named-Entity-Aware Model and Phoneme Similarity Estimation

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2305.17846
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.17846
- **Reference count:** 0
- **Primary result:** 35.7% relative reduction in target named entity character error rate on Japanese personal names using a retraining-free ASR approach

## Executive Summary
This paper introduces a retraining-free method for handling enharmonic words—Japanese personal names that share pronunciation but differ in spelling—in end-to-end automatic speech recognition (E2E-ASR). The approach leverages a named-entity-aware ASR model to extract candidate named entities, followed by phoneme similarity estimation against a dictionary of known enharmonic word pairs. When the similarity exceeds a threshold, the system suggests or applies corrections. Experiments demonstrate a 35.7% relative improvement in target named entity character error rate compared to a conventional E2E-ASR model, with robustness maintained as dictionary size increases. The dual-encoder named-entity-aware model is identified as the most effective architecture for this task.

## Method Summary
The proposed method operates without retraining the ASR model, relying instead on post-processing and dictionary-based correction. First, a named-entity-aware E2E-ASR model identifies named entities in the ASR output. Next, the extracted named entities are compared to a precompiled dictionary of enharmonic words using phoneme similarity metrics. If the similarity exceeds a defined threshold, the system corrects the recognized word to the correct variant from the dictionary. The approach is evaluated specifically on Japanese personal names, which are a common source of homophonic ambiguity in ASR. The dual-encoder architecture for named-entity-aware ASR is shown to outperform single-encoder alternatives in this context.

## Key Results
- 35.7% relative reduction in target named entity character error rate compared to baseline E2E-ASR.
- The method is retraining-free and relies on dictionary lookup and phoneme similarity.
- The dual-encoder named-entity-aware model outperforms single-encoder models for this task.
- Robustness is maintained as the dictionary size increases.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The method exploits the fact that many Japanese personal names are pronounced identically but written differently, a common source of error in ASR. By integrating named-entity awareness into the ASR model, the system can more accurately identify candidate named entities. Phoneme similarity estimation then allows the system to disambiguate between homophonic alternatives using a dictionary, enabling correction without the need for costly retraining. The dual-encoder architecture further enhances named entity recognition accuracy, directly contributing to the observed performance gains.

## Foundational Learning

- **End-to-end ASR (E2E-ASR)**: Direct mapping from speech to text without intermediate components. Why needed: Enables efficient, integrated modeling for modern ASR. Quick check: Model outputs are final transcriptions, not intermediate representations.
- **Named-entity awareness**: ASR models explicitly trained or adapted to recognize and handle named entities. Why needed: Improves accuracy for names and entities that are frequent error sources. Quick check: Model distinguishes named entities from general vocabulary during decoding.
- **Phoneme similarity estimation**: Quantification of how closely two words' pronunciations match. Why needed: Enables automated disambiguation of homophonic words. Quick check: Similarity scores are computed for all dictionary candidates against recognized names.
- **Dictionary-based correction**: Use of a precompiled list of word variants for post-processing error correction. Why needed: Provides ground truth for known ambiguous words without model retraining. Quick check: Dictionary entries are complete for the tested named entity set.

## Architecture Onboarding

**Component map:** ASR model (dual-encoder) → Named entity extraction → Phoneme similarity estimation → Dictionary lookup → Correction (if threshold met)

**Critical path:** The core pipeline is ASR recognition → named entity extraction → similarity scoring → conditional correction. Each step is necessary; failure at any point results in no improvement for that instance.

**Design tradeoffs:** Retraining-free approach trades off adaptability for efficiency and ease of deployment. Dictionary dependency limits coverage to known word pairs; unknown or new names cannot be corrected. Dual-encoder architecture increases model complexity but yields higher named entity recognition accuracy.

**Failure signatures:** Errors occur when the named entity is not in the dictionary, the similarity threshold is too high (missing corrections) or too low (false corrections), or the ASR model fails to recognize the named entity in the first place.

## Limitations
- Method is specialized for Japanese personal names and may not generalize to other languages or entity types.
- Performance is contingent on the completeness and quality of the precompiled dictionary of enharmonic words.
- Cannot handle out-of-vocabulary or unseen named entities not present in the dictionary.
- Experimental validation is limited to the Japanese language and does not explore robustness beyond the tested dictionary sizes.

## Confidence

**Major claim clusters and confidence:**
- **Effectiveness of the retraining-free approach**: High confidence, supported by controlled experiments and quantitative results.
- **Robustness to dictionary size**: Medium confidence; while results indicate stability, the range of dictionary sizes tested is not fully disclosed, and edge cases are not explored.
- **Generalizability to other languages or entity types**: Low confidence; current validation is limited to Japanese personal names, and cross-linguistic or broader entity type experiments are absent.
