# SlideVQA: A Dataset for Document Visual Question Answering on Multiple Images

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2301.04883
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2301.04883
- Reference count: 12
- Introduces SlideVQA dataset with 2.6k slide decks, 52k slide images, and 14.5k questions requiring complex reasoning

## Executive Summary
This paper introduces SlideVQA, a novel dataset for document visual question answering over multiple slide images. It contains 2.6k slide decks with 52k slide images and 14.5k questions requiring complex reasoning, including multi-hop and numerical reasoning. To tackle this task, the authors propose M3D, an end-to-end multi-modal model that jointly performs evidence selection and question answering in a unified sequence-to-sequence format, enhanced with arithmetic expression generation for numerical reasoning. Experiments show that M3D outperforms existing state-of-the-art models on SlideVQA, but human performance remains significantly higher, highlighting the dataset's challenging nature and its potential to advance research in document VQA and multi-image reasoning.

## Method Summary
The paper proposes M3D (Multi-Modal Multi-image Document VQA), an end-to-end model using T5 backbone that jointly performs evidence selection and question answering through multi-task learning in a unified sequence-to-sequence format. The model incorporates arithmetic expression generation for numerical reasoning tasks and integrates text, layout, and visual embeddings to handle document understanding. The model is trained on the SlideVQA dataset using AdamW optimizer with learning rate 5e-5 and batch size 32.

## Key Results
- M3D outperforms existing state-of-the-art models on SlideVQA dataset
- Human performance remains significantly higher than M3D, indicating dataset difficulty
- Arithmetic expression generation module significantly improves numerical reasoning performance
- Model demonstrates effectiveness in handling multi-hop reasoning across multiple slide images

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: M3D's multi-task learning of evidence selection and QA in a unified sequence-to-sequence format enables better performance than pipeline approaches.
- Mechanism: The model jointly learns to predict evidence pages and answers in a single forward pass, sharing the same encoder-decoder architecture. This allows cross-task information sharing and reduces error propagation from separate stages.
- Core assumption: Joint optimization of evidence selection and QA leads to better feature representations than training separate models.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "M3D, an end-to-end multi-modal model that jointly performs evidence selection and question answering in a unified sequence-to-sequence format"
  - [section] "Multi-modal Encoder-Decoder" and "Evidence selector" sections show the shared architecture
  - [corpus] Weak - no direct comparison to pipeline approaches in corpus
- Break condition: If the tasks are too different, joint training may hurt performance due to conflicting gradients.

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: Predicting arithmetic expressions as intermediate reasoning steps improves numerical reasoning ability.
- Mechanism: Instead of directly predicting numerical answers, the model generates arithmetic expressions (e.g., "11% - 6%") which are then evaluated by a calculator. This provides a more interpretable reasoning trace.
- Core assumption: Decomposing numerical reasoning into expression generation and evaluation is easier than direct numerical prediction.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "enhanced with arithmetic expression generation for numerical reasoning"
  - [section] "Answer/Arithmetic-expression decoder" describes the expression generation approach
  - [corpus] Weak - no quantitative comparison of expression vs direct numerical prediction
- Break condition: If the calculator makes errors or the expression space is too large, performance may degrade.

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: Incorporating layout and visual embeddings in addition to text embeddings improves performance on document VQA tasks.
- Mechanism: The input embeddings combine token, segment, layout, and visual features to provide rich multimodal representations of document images.
- Core assumption: Document understanding requires integration of textual, layout, and visual information.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "It also provides bounding boxes around textual and visual elements for understanding document layout"
  - [section] "Multi-modal Task-Specific Input" describes the layout and visual embeddings
  - [corpus] Weak - no ablation study on individual modalities in corpus
- Break condition: If visual features are noisy or layout information is unreliable, adding these embeddings may hurt performance.

  - Why needed here: SlideVQA requires finding answers that span multiple slides, not just a single image
  - Quick check question: Can you trace the reasoning path from question to answer across multiple slides in a sample?

- Concept: Numerical reasoning with arithmetic expressions
  - Why needed here: Many questions require calculations like differences, sums, or comparisons of numerical values
  - Quick check question: Given two numbers and an operation, can you generate the correct arithmetic expression?

- Concept: Multi-modal information fusion
  - Why needed here: Document images contain text, layout, and visual elements that must be integrated
  - Quick check question: Can you identify how text, layout, and visual features complement each other in understanding a document?

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map: Encoder (text + layout + visual features) -> Decoder (evidence selector + answer generator)
- Critical path: Input encoding -> cross-attention in decoder -> evidence selection + answer generation
- Design tradeoffs: Joint vs. separate training of evidence selection and QA; expression generation vs. direct numerical prediction
- Failure signatures: Poor evidence selection -> wrong answers; expression generation errors -> wrong calculations
- First 3 experiments:
  1. Train M3D with only text features, measure performance drop
  2. Train with joint evidence selection and QA, compare to pipeline
  3. Test arithmetic expression generation vs. direct numerical prediction

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- Question: What is the optimal architecture for evidence selection in multi-image document VQA when dealing with large numbers of input images (e.g., open-domain QA setting)?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper states "our model uses cross-attention on all evidence candidates, which may cause a computational problem when there are a lot of input images (e.g., as in the open-domain QA setting like DocCVQA)."
- Why unresolved: Current models using cross-attention on all evidence candidates face computational challenges with large input image sets.
- What evidence would resolve it: Performance comparison between models using different evidence selection strategies (e.g., two-stage selector vs. cross-attention on all candidates) on datasets with varying numbers of input images.

### Open Question 2
- Question: How can multi-hop reasoning be improved in document VQA models to better handle questions requiring complex cross-slide reasoning?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper shows a "huge gap compared with human performance" on multi-hop reasoning tasks and notes that "better multi-hop reasoning over multiple images is needed."
- Why unresolved: Current models struggle with multi-hop reasoning across multiple images, as evidenced by the significant performance gap with humans.
- What evidence would resolve it: Experimental results showing improved multi-hop reasoning performance on multi-image document VQA datasets using novel architectures or training approaches.

### Open Question 3
- Question: What is the most effective approach for generating answers involving arithmetic operations in document VQA tasks?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper states that "non-span answers to questions involving arithmetic operations have to be improved" and shows that the arithmetic expression generation module significantly improved performance.
- Why unresolved: Current models still struggle with generating correct numerical answers, particularly for arithmetic operations.
- What evidence would resolve it: Comparative performance analysis of different approaches to handling numerical reasoning in document VQA, including various arithmetic expression generation and evaluation methods.

## Limitations
- Evaluation relies heavily on automated metrics without extensive human evaluation of answer quality
- No comparison with specialized pipeline approaches that might handle multi-image reasoning differently
- Limited ablation studies on the contribution of individual multimodal features to overall performance

## Confidence
- High confidence: Dataset construction methodology and scale
- Medium confidence: M3D model's superiority over baselines
- Low confidence: Practical utility of arithmetic expression generation without quantitative comparisons
