# FinRED: A Dataset for Relation Extraction in Financial Domain

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID**: 2306.03736
- **Source URL**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.03736
- **Reference count**: 26
- **Primary result**: State-of-the-art joint entity and relation extraction models achieve significantly lower performance on FinRED (F1: 87.80%) compared to general domain datasets (FB-NYT: 92.30%, WebNLG: 91.80%)

## Executive Summary
This paper introduces FinRED, a relation extraction dataset specifically for the financial domain, created by mapping Wikidata triplets to financial entities using distance supervision. The dataset contains 5,699 training examples and 1,068 test examples with 29 financial-specific relations. When evaluated on state-of-the-art joint entity and relation extraction models (SPN, TPLinker, CasRel), the models achieved significantly lower performance on FinRED compared to general domain datasets like FB-NYT and WebNLG. The authors interpret this performance gap as evidence that better models are needed to capture financial domain-specific relations.

## Method Summary
The authors created FinRED by mapping Wikidata triplets to financial domain entities through a multi-step process. First, they extracted triplets from Wikidata and identified financial entities within these triplets. They then matched these entities to sentences from a financial corpus, using the presence of both entities in a sentence as evidence for the specified relation (distance supervision). This automatic annotation approach generated 6,767 total examples with 29 financial-specific relations. The dataset was split into 5,699 training examples and 1,068 test examples. The financial entities were sourced from a pre-existing financial entity dataset, ensuring domain relevance.

## Key Results
- SPN model achieved F1 score of 87.80% on FinRED compared to 92.30% on FB-NYT and 91.80% on WebNLG
- TPLinker and CasRel models showed similar performance drops when evaluated on FinRED versus general domain datasets
- The performance gap indicates current models struggle with financial domain-specific relations
- FinRED contains 29 financial-specific relations compared to the broader relation sets in general domain datasets

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The paper does not explicitly explain why the dataset creation methodology works, but the approach leverages distance supervision to efficiently create domain-specific relation extraction data. The mechanism relies on the assumption that if two entities appear together in a sentence and have a relation in Wikidata, this relation likely holds in the sentence context. However, this assumption may introduce noise when entities co-occur but do not actually have the specified relationship in that particular context.

## Foundational Learning

**Distance Supervision**: A technique where relations between entities are automatically labeled based on their co-occurrence in knowledge base triples, used here to map Wikidata relations to financial sentences.

*Why needed*: Manual annotation of relation extraction datasets is extremely time-consuming and expensive, especially for specialized domains like finance.

*Quick check*: Verify that entities co-occurring in sentences actually have the specified relationship in the knowledge base.

**Joint Entity and Relation Extraction**: Models that simultaneously identify entities and their relations in text, as opposed to separate entity recognition and relation classification steps.

*Why needed*: Joint models can leverage shared information between entity and relation identification, potentially improving performance on both tasks.

*Quick check*: Evaluate entity extraction accuracy alongside relation extraction to understand performance bottlenecks.

**Domain Adaptation**: The process of adapting models trained on general domain data to perform well on specialized domain data like financial texts.

*Why needed*: Financial texts contain domain-specific terminology, abbreviations, and relation patterns that differ from general language.

*Quick check*: Compare model performance on in-domain versus out-of-domain test sets.

## Architecture Onboarding

**Component Map**: Financial Corpus -> Entity Extraction -> Wikidata Triplet Mapping -> Distance Supervision -> Annotated Dataset -> Joint RE Models (SPN/TPLinker/CasRel)

**Critical Path**: The most critical component is the distance supervision mapping step, as errors here propagate directly to model training and evaluation.

**Design Tradeoffs**: Automatic annotation via distance supervision trades annotation quality for scalability, potentially introducing noise that affects model training.

**Failure Signatures**: Performance degradation on financial domain data relative to general domain data, indicating domain shift or annotation noise.

## Limitations
- Automatic annotation via distance supervision may introduce noise from false positive relations
- Dataset size (6,767 examples) is relatively small compared to general domain benchmarks
- Evaluation focuses only on joint entity and relation extraction models without exploring alternative approaches
- Performance gap interpretation assumes domain specificity rather than other factors like dataset size or annotation quality

## Confidence
**Dataset Utility**: Medium - The methodology is sound and addresses a real need for financial domain benchmarks, but automatic annotation and limited evaluation scope reduce confidence in specific performance conclusions.

**Performance Gap Interpretation**: Medium - The observation that state-of-the-art models perform worse on FinRED than general domain datasets is well-supported, though the interpretation of this gap requires caution.

**Generalizability**: Medium - The dataset creation approach may not generalize well to other specialized domains with different entity distributions or relation patterns.

## Next Checks
