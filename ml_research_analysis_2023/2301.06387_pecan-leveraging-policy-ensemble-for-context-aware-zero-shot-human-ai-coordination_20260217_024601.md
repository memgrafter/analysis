# PECAN: Leveraging Policy Ensemble for Context-Aware Zero-Shot Human-AI Coordination

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2301.06387
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2301.06387
- Authors: [Not specified in source]
- Reference count: 40
- Key outcome: PECAN achieves up to 26.8% higher performance than state-of-the-art baselines for zero-shot human-AI coordination in Overcooked

## Executive Summary
This paper addresses the challenge of zero-shot human-AI coordination, where an AI agent must learn to collaborate with novel human partners without access to human data during training. The key issue is that existing methods train agents with a finite population of partners, limiting diversity and leading to poor generalization with unseen humans. To overcome this, the authors propose PECAN, which introduces two key innovations: (1) a policy ensemble method that generates diverse partners by combining multiple policies with random weights, increasing behavioral diversity without increasing population size, and (2) a context-aware module that identifies the partner's skill level from past trajectories, allowing the agent to learn level-specific coordination strategies rather than a one-size-fits-all approach. Evaluated on the Overcooked environment with both behavior-cloned human proxies and real humans, PECAN significantly outperforms state-of-the-art baselines, achieving up to 26.8% higher performance in certain scenarios.

## Method Summary
PECAN addresses zero-shot human-AI coordination by generating diverse partners through policy ensemble methods and learning context-aware coordination strategies. The method first trains a population of diverse policies using Maximum Entropy Population-based training (MEP), then splits this population into skill-level groups. During training, PECAN creates policy-ensemble partners by randomly weighted averaging of policy primitives from these groups. A context encoder is trained to identify partner skill levels from past trajectories using self-attention and classification. The ego agent conditions its policy on the predicted context to learn level-specific coordination skills. Training uses group-level prioritized sampling based on coordination performance, ensuring the agent learns to handle partners of all difficulty levels.

## Key Results
- PECAN achieves up to 26.8% higher performance than state-of-the-art baselines in zero-shot human-AI coordination
- Policy ensemble method generates more diverse partners than vanilla population-based training
- Context-aware module enables learning of level-specific coordination strategies
- PECAN outperforms baselines including Self-play PPO, Population-based Training, Fictitious Co-Play, TrajeDi, and MEP

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: Policy ensemble generates more diverse partners than vanilla population-based training by mixing multiple policies with random weights
- Mechanism: Instead of sampling a single policy from the population, PECAN creates a new partner by taking a weighted average of multiple policies with randomly assigned weights. This creates partners whose behavior is a blend of different policies, making them distinct from any single policy in the population
- Core assumption: Random weights ensure the ensemble partner's behavior differs from all population members
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "a policy ensemble method that generates diverse partners by combining multiple policies with random weights"
  - [section 4.2] "Instead of directly using policy πᵢ, i ∈ [1,n] in a policy set G : {π₁, π₂, ..., πₙ}, we use an ensembled policy πₚ over this set G as the training partner"
  - [section 4.2] "In each training iteration, the weights for each policy primitive are assigned at random"
- Break condition: If random weights consistently converge to the same pattern or if the policy space is too constrained

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: Context-aware module enables the ego agent to learn level-based common best responses rather than partner-specific responses
- Mechanism: The context encoder identifies the partner's skill level from past trajectories and conditions the ego agent's policy on this context. This allows the ego agent to learn different coordination strategies for different partner levels rather than a single best response for all partners
- Core assumption: Partner skill level is identifiable from trajectory patterns and correlates with effective coordination strategies
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "a context-aware module that identifies the partner's skill level from past trajectories, allowing the agent to learn level-specific coordination strategies"
  - [section 4.3] "we devise a context encoder f to help the ego agent analyze and predict the level of its partner as policy context"
  - [section 4.3] "By conditioning on the predicted context ĉ indicating the partner's level, the ego agent's policy πₑ₉ₒ(·|s, ĉ) learns level-specific coordination skills"
- Break condition: If trajectory patterns don't reliably indicate skill level or if skill level doesn't affect optimal coordination strategy

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: Group-level prioritized sampling stabilizes training and ensures the ego agent can coordinate with all partner levels
- Mechanism: Instead of uniform sampling, PECAN samples partner groups based on the ego agent's average performance with each group. Groups that are harder to coordinate with get sampled more frequently, ensuring the ego agent learns to handle all difficulty levels
- Core assumption: Partner difficulty can be measured by ego agent performance and sampling harder partners improves generalization
- Evidence anchors:
  - [section 4.2] "we employ a group-level prioritised sampling strategy that selects groups based on the average performance of the ego agent with each group J(Gᵢ)"
  - [section 4.2] "group-level prioritized sampling helps the ego agent learns to coordinate with partners at the level with the worst coordination performance"
- Break condition: If performance metrics don't correlate with actual coordination difficulty or if sampling harder partners prevents learning from easier ones

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map:
  Population of diverse policies -> Policy ensemble generator -> Context encoder -> Ego agent -> Training loop

- Critical path:
  1. Generate diverse population via MEP
  2. Split population into skill level groups
  3. Sample group via prioritized sampling
  4. Generate policy ensemble from selected group
  5. Collect trajectories with ensemble partner
  6. Update context encoder with true skill labels
  7. Update ego agent policy conditioned on predicted context
  8. Update group performance metrics for next iteration

- Design tradeoffs:
  - Random vs learned ensemble weights: Random weights provide more diversity but may be less optimal than learned combinations
  - Level-based vs continuous skill representation: Discrete levels simplify context prediction but may miss nuance
  - Context prediction from full trajectories vs recent windows: Full trajectories provide more information but increase latency

- Failure signatures:
  - Context encoder consistently predicts wrong skill levels
  - Policy ensemble weights converge to same values each iteration
  - Ego agent performance plateaus despite continued training
  - Group performance metrics show no differentiation between groups

- First 3 experiments:
  1. Compare partner diversity (measured by action distribution divergence) between vanilla MEP and PECAN with policy ensemble
  2. Test context encoder accuracy on held-out trajectory data with known skill levels
  3. Evaluate zero-shot coordination performance with partners from each skill level group

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- Question: How can we analyze and identify human players' behavior patterns as context for the ego agent without using human data during training?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper mentions this as a future work direction: "Our future work is to study how to analyze and identify the human player's behavior pattern as the ego agents' context (rather than the level-based context in the current method) with the population and no human data during training."
- Why unresolved: This is identified as a challenging research subject because it requires comprehending human behaviors using only a population of AI agents, without any human data during training.
- What evidence would resolve it: Developing a method that successfully identifies human behavior patterns as context using only AI agents in the population, and demonstrating improved human-AI coordination performance in experiments.

### Open Question 2
- Question: Can the PECAN method be trained in an end-to-end manner rather than the current two-stage approach?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper states: "And since PECAN is a two-stage method, another future direction is to study how to train it in an end-to-end manner."
- Why unresolved: The current PECAN method uses a two-stage approach (first training the population, then training the ego agent), and the paper suggests exploring an end-to-end training approach as a future direction.
- What evidence would resolve it: Developing an end-to-end training algorithm for PECAN and comparing its performance to the current two-stage approach in terms of coordination performance and computational efficiency.

### Open Question 3
- Question: How does the performance of PECAN scale with the size of the population of policy primitives?
- Basis in paper: [inferred] While the paper demonstrates PECAN's effectiveness with a finite population, it does not explore how performance scales with population size. This could be an important consideration for practical applications.
- Why unresolved: The paper does not provide experiments varying the population size or discussing scalability issues.
- What evidence would resolve it: Conducting experiments with different population sizes and analyzing the relationship between population size and coordination performance, including computational costs.

## Limitations

- Partner diversity is constrained by the MEP training process, potentially limiting the true diversity achievable
- Performance evaluation relies on behavior-cloned human proxies, which may not accurately reflect real human-AI coordination
- The method hasn't been tested for cross-domain generalization beyond the Overcooked environment

## Confidence

- Policy ensemble diversity mechanism: High
- Context-aware module effectiveness: Medium
- Zero-shot coordination with real humans: Low
