# Linking Streets in OpenStreetMap to Persons in Wikidata

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2302.12907
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2302.12907
- Authors: 
- Reference count: 10
- Key outcome: StreetToPerson achieves 0.95 precision on German street-to-person linking, outperforming baselines by 26 percentage points

## Executive Summary
This paper introduces StreetToPerson, a method for linking streets in OpenStreetMap to the persons in Wikidata they are named after. The approach uses street name truncation to isolate person names, retrieves candidate persons from Wikidata, and classifies the correct match using 30 knowledge graph and spatial features. Evaluated on German streets, it achieves 0.95 precision and identifies over 180,000 street-to-person relations. The method bridges geographic data sources with knowledge graphs, enabling use cases in tourism and cultural analysis.

## Method Summary
StreetToPerson links streets in OpenStreetMap to persons in Wikidata through a three-stage process: (1) street names are truncated by removing German suffixes and prefixes to isolate the person name portion, (2) candidate persons are retrieved from Wikidata based on name matching, and (3) a binary classifier selects the correct person using 30 features including Wikipedia link counts, name matching indicators, occupation matches, and spatial relation scores. The method achieves high precision by filtering candidates through classification rather than relying solely on popularity ranking.

## Key Results
- Achieves 0.95 precision on German street-to-person linking task
- Outperforms existing approaches by 26 percentage points
- Identifies over 180,000 street-to-person relations when applied to all German OSM streets

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
Street-to-person linking works by truncating street names to remove suffixes/prefixes, leaving only the person-identifying term. The approach leverages a manually curated list of 80 German suffixes and 34 prefixes to strip common street affixes ("straße", "weg", etc.) from street names. This truncation isolates the person's name portion, which can then be used to retrieve candidate persons from Wikidata via an index. The core assumption is that street names consistently follow a pattern where the person's name is embedded and can be separated from common street affixes.

### Mechanism 2
Classification of candidate persons is enabled by extracting features from knowledge graph relations and spatial dependencies. For each street-candidate pair, 30 features are extracted including Wikipedia link count (popularity), name matching indicators (full, first, last name, alias), occupation matches, and spatial relation scores (born, died, buried, educated at, work location). These features capture both the candidate's relevance and spatial proximity to the street location. The core assumption is that the combination of knowledge graph features and spatial relationships provides discriminative information for identifying the correct person.

### Mechanism 3
The approach achieves high precision by filtering candidates through a classifier rather than relying solely on popularity ranking. Instead of selecting the candidate with the highest Wikipedia link count, a binary classifier is trained on positive examples (streets named after persons from Wikidata) and negative examples (other candidates). This allows consideration of multiple features beyond just popularity. The core assumption is that a classifier trained on feature-rich examples can outperform simple ranking approaches that rely on single metrics.

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map: Street name → Truncation → Candidate retrieval → Feature extraction (30 features) → Classification → Output

- Critical path: Street name → Truncation → Candidate retrieval → Feature extraction (30 features) → Classification → Output

- Design tradeoffs:
  - Precision vs recall: High precision (0.95) comes at the cost of lower recall (0.91), meaning some correct links may be missed.
  - Manual vs automatic affix list: Using a manually curated affix list ensures quality but limits scalability to other languages.
  - Feature complexity: 30 features provide rich information but increase computational cost and potential overfitting.

- Failure signatures:
  - Low precision: Incorrect candidates being selected, possibly due to noisy features or classifier overfitting
  - Low recall: Missing correct candidates, possibly due to incomplete spatial dependency data or insufficient training examples
  - High candidate count: Many candidates returned for truncation, indicating ambiguous street names

- First 3 experiments:
  1. Test truncation accuracy: Run street names through truncation module and manually verify that person names are correctly isolated.
  2. Validate feature extraction: For known street-candidate pairs, verify that the 30 features are correctly computed and meaningful.
  3. Baseline comparison: Implement and run the PopRank baseline on the same test set to confirm the 26 percentage point improvement claim.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
How does StreetToPerson's performance change when applied to countries other than Germany, particularly those with different naming conventions or languages? The authors mention future work to extend the approach to further countries but do not provide empirical results for non-German contexts.

### Open Question 2
What is the impact of using graph-based embeddings instead of the current feature extraction method on classification accuracy? The authors state in the conclusion that future work plans to "enrich the feature space through the utilisation of graph-based embeddings."

### Open Question 3
How does the precision-recall tradeoff change when relaxing the classifier's confidence threshold to increase recall? The authors report high precision (0.95) but do not discuss the effects of threshold tuning on recall.

## Limitations
- Reliance on German street naming conventions may not generalize to other languages
- Performance depends heavily on completeness of spatial dependency data in Wikidata
- Assumes streets are named after single persons, potentially missing cases with multiple honorees

## Confidence
- High Confidence: The 0.95 precision claim on German streets
- Medium Confidence: The 26 percentage point improvement over baselines
- Medium Confidence: The scalability claim of identifying 180,000+ street-to-person relations
