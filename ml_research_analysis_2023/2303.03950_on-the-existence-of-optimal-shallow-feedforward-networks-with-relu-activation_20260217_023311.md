# On the existence of optimal shallow feedforward networks with ReLU activation

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2303.03950
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.03950
- Reference count: 7
- Proves existence of global minima for ReLU networks approximating continuous functions under Lp-loss

## Executive Summary
This paper establishes the existence of optimal solutions for shallow feedforward neural networks with ReLU activation when approximating continuous target functions under Lp-loss. The authors extend the search space to include discontinuous generalized responses and prove that minimizers exist in this extended space through compactness arguments. Crucially, they demonstrate that under mild conditions on the loss function and measure, these discontinuous responses perform worse than representable ReLU networks. This fundamental property distinguishes ReLU activation from other commonly used activation functions and may explain its practical success in applications.

## Method Summary
The authors employ compactness arguments to extend the search space to include discontinuous generalized responses, proving that minimizers exist in this extended space. They then establish that under mild conditions on the loss function and measure, these discontinuous responses perform worse than representable ReLU networks. This approach combines functional analysis techniques with optimization theory to prove existence results for a specific class of neural networks.

## Key Results
- Existence of global minima for ReLU networks under Lp-loss is proven through extension to discontinuous responses
- Compactness arguments guarantee existence of minimizers in the extended function space
- Discontinuous responses perform worse than representable ReLU networks under mild conditions

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The proof works by first extending the optimization problem to include discontinuous generalized responses, where compactness arguments guarantee the existence of minimizers. The key insight is that under specific continuity and convexity conditions on the loss function and measure, any discontinuous optimal response can be replaced by a continuous ReLU network with equal or better performance. This separation between continuous and discontinuous responses leverages the specific properties of ReLU activation, which can approximate continuous functions while maintaining computational efficiency.
