# Contrastive Meta-Learning for Partially Observable Few-Shot Learning

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2301.13136
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2301.13136
- Authors: 
- Reference count: 40
- Key outcome: POEM achieves state-of-the-art performance on PO-Meta-Dataset while maintaining comparable performance on Meta-Dataset, demonstrating effective learning from partial observations

## Executive Summary
This paper addresses the challenge of learning representations from partial observations where useful features may be present in only some views. The proposed method, Partial Observation Experts Modelling (POEM), uses a probabilistic formalism to map views to representations with different levels of uncertainty in different components. These views are then integrated through marginalisation over the uncertainty, allowing POEM to meta-learn consistent representations from partial observations. The authors evaluate POEM on an adapted version of the Meta-Dataset benchmark, called PO-Meta-Dataset, designed to assess representation learning from partial observations, and demonstrate its effectiveness through both few-shot classification and environment recognition tasks.

## Method Summary
POEM addresses few-shot learning under partial observability by using a product of experts formalism with Gaussian embeddings. Each view is mapped to a Gaussian distribution over the representation space with learnable mean and precision parameters. The product of these Gaussians across views for a given sample yields a combined representation where components with high precision (low uncertainty) from individual views dominate, while components with low precision are downweighted. The model is trained using a negative log marginal likelihood objective that encourages representations where the integrated support representation has high overlap with query views belonging to the same sample. The architecture consists of a ResNet-18 backbone with dual MLP heads that output mean and precision vectors, followed by Gaussian product computation and classification layers.

## Key Results
- POEM outperforms standard few-shot learning baselines like Prototypical Networks and MAML on PO-Meta-Dataset while maintaining comparable performance on the original Meta-Dataset
- POEM achieves higher environment recognition accuracy compared to baselines like Prototypical Networks and Recurrent Networks when learning representations from partial views observed by an agent exploring the environment
- The method demonstrates effective integration of partial observations through uncertainty-weighted components, enabling consistent representation learning even when individual views contain only partial information

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- **Claim**: POEM's product of experts formalism allows it to integrate partial views by weighting components according to their uncertainty.
- **Mechanism**: Each view is mapped to a Gaussian distribution over the representation space with learnable mean and precision (inverse variance). The product of these Gaussians across views for a given sample yields a combined representation where components with high precision (low uncertainty) from individual views dominate, while components with low precision are downweighted. This enables coherent integration of partial observations that inform different parts of the representation.
- **Core assumption**: The partial observability can be modeled as each view providing information about only some components of the full representation, with uncertainty indicating informativeness.
- **Evidence anchors**:
  - [abstract] "POEM uses a probabilistic formalism to map views to representations with different levels of uncertainty in different components."
  - [section] "POEM instead maximises consistency between multiple views, by utilising representation uncertainty to learn which features of the entity are captured by a view, and then combining these representations together by weighting features by their uncertainty via a product of experts model."
  - [corpus] Weak - no direct corpus evidence found for product of experts in partial observability setting
- **Break condition**: If all views have high uncertainty for a particular component, the integration would fail to produce informative estimates for that component.

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim**: POEM learns to distinguish between components that should be shared across views versus those that are view-specific through the precision parameters.
- **Mechanism**: During training, the precision parameters for each component in each view's Gaussian are learned. When views consistently provide high-precision information for the same component (indicating it's shared), that component's precision increases across views. When views provide inconsistent information for a component, its precision decreases. This selective precision learning enables POEM to identify which features are consistently present versus partially observable.
- **Core assumption**: The learning process can effectively distinguish shared versus partial features through the optimization of precision parameters.
- **Evidence anchors**:
  - [abstract] "views can then be integrated with one another through marginalisation over that uncertainty"
  - [section] "Given multiple views of the data, the product of experts component in POEM combines the representations, weighting by the variance, to get a maximally informative and consistent representation from the views."
  - [corpus] Missing - no corpus evidence found for precision learning in contrastive meta-learning
- **Break condition**: If the optimization gets stuck in local minima where precisions don't properly reflect true informativeness, the integration could fail.

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim**: POEM's objective function encourages learning representations that maximize the likelihood of correctly matching query views to their source samples, even when query views are also partial.
- **Mechanism**: The training objective computes the marginal predictive distribution for each query view given the support set, then maximizes the likelihood of the correct match. This encourages the model to learn representations where the integrated support representation (combining partial views) has high overlap with query views that belong to the same sample, while having low overlap with query views from other samples. The partial observability is handled naturally through the uncertainty-weighted integration.
- **Core assumption**: The partial observability in the query set can be effectively handled by the same uncertainty-weighted integration used for the support set.
- **Evidence anchors**:
  - [abstract] "POEM then enables us to meta-learn consistent representations from partial observations."
  - [section] "We wish to maximize the likelihood for the correct match of query point to support set, accumulated across all support/query selections indexed with t from the dataset."
  - [corpus] Weak - limited corpus evidence for contrastive meta-learning with partial observability
- **Break condition**: If query views are too partial to provide discriminative information, the matching performance could degrade significantly.

## Architecture Onboarding

- **Component map**: ResNet-18 backbone -> Dual-headed MLP (mean and precision outputs) -> Gaussian product computation layer -> Cross-entropy loss layer

- **Critical path**: View encoding → Gaussian embedding generation → Product of experts computation → Classification prediction
  - The view encoding through the backbone and MLP heads is critical as it determines the quality of the Gaussian embeddings
  - The product of experts computation is the core innovation that handles partial observability

- **Design tradeoffs**:
  - Adding uncertainty (precision) parameters increases model capacity but also computational cost
  - Using Gaussian distributions enables analytic computation but may not capture all types of uncertainty
  - The approach assumes partial observability is structured (each view informs specific components) rather than arbitrary

- **Failure signatures**:
  - If precisions don't learn meaningful values (remain near uniform), the model degrades to standard Prototypical Networks
  - If the product of experts computation becomes numerically unstable (extreme precisions), the model may fail
  - If the backbone doesn't capture view-specific features well, the integration will be based on poor embeddings

- **First 3 experiments**:
  1. Run POEM on standard Meta-Dataset without partial observability to verify it matches baseline performance (confirming the model degrades appropriately when partial observability isn't needed)
  2. Gradually increase the level of partial observability in PO-Meta-Dataset and measure performance degradation compared to baselines
  3. Visualize the learned precision values across components and views to verify they capture meaningful uncertainty patterns

## Limitations

- The evaluation is limited to synthetic partial observability scenarios (image crops/augmentations) rather than real-world applications with naturally occurring partial views
- The MiniGrid experiment uses a simplified 2D gridworld that may not capture the complexity of real robotic or autonomous navigation scenarios
- The computational overhead of maintaining multiple precision parameters per component increases model complexity without clear quantification of the performance-cost tradeoff

## Confidence

- **High confidence**: The mathematical formulation and core mechanism of POEM's product of experts approach
- **Medium confidence**: The empirical results, as the evaluation datasets are synthetic adaptations rather than naturally occurring partial observability scenarios
- **Medium confidence**: The generalizability claim, as the paper only demonstrates effectiveness on image-based tasks and simple gridworld environments
