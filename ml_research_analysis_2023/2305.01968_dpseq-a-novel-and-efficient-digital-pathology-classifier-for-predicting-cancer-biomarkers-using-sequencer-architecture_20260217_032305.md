# DPSeq: A Novel and Efficient Digital Pathology Classifier for Predicting Cancer Biomarkers using Sequencer Architecture

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2305.01968
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.01968
- Reference count: 6
- Introduced DPSeq, a novel and efficient digital pathology classifier for predicting cancer biomarkers using a sequencer architecture

## Executive Summary
This study presents DPSeq, a novel digital pathology classifier that integrates horizon and vertical bidirectional long short-term memory (BiLSTM) networks for predicting cancer biomarkers from histopathological images. The model was evaluated on H&E-stained colorectal cancer images from TCGA and MCO datasets, demonstrating exceptional performance in predicting MSI status, hypermutation, CIMP status, BRAF mutation, TP53 mutation, and chromosomal instability. DPSeq outperformed multiple state-of-the-art models including ResNet, MobileNet, EfficientNet, ViT, and Swin-T architectures while requiring less training and prediction time.

## Method Summary
DPSeq employs a sequencer architecture that combines horizon and vertical BiLSTM networks to process histopathological images. The model was trained and validated on two international datasets of colorectal cancer (TCGA and MCO) using H&E-stained images. The architecture was specifically designed to capture spatial dependencies in tissue samples while maintaining computational efficiency. The model was evaluated against four CNN architectures (ResNet18, ResNet50, MobileNetV2, and EfficientNet) and two transformer models (ViT and Swin-T) under identical experimental conditions.

## Key Results
- DPSeq achieved the highest AUROC and AUPRC values in predicting MSI status, BRAF mutation, and CIMP status compared to all tested models
- The model demonstrated exceptional performance in predicting multiple biomarkers including hypermutation, CIMP status, and chromosomal instability
- DPSeq required less time for both training and prediction due to its simpler architecture compared to competing models

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The sequencer architecture leverages bidirectional LSTM networks to capture both horizontal and vertical spatial dependencies in histopathological images. This approach allows the model to effectively learn tissue patterns and architectural features that are indicative of specific cancer biomarkers. The integration of horizon and vertical BiLSTM networks enables comprehensive analysis of tissue morphology while maintaining computational efficiency through the simpler architectural design.

## Architecture Onboarding

**Component Map**: Image Input -> Horizon BiLSTM -> Vertical BiLSTM -> Feature Fusion -> Prediction Layer

**Critical Path**: The critical path involves processing the histopathological image through both horizon and vertical BiLSTM networks, followed by feature fusion and prediction. This path is critical because it determines how spatial information is captured and utilized for biomarker prediction.

**Design Tradeoffs**: The model trades off some representational capacity compared to deeper CNNs in exchange for computational efficiency and reduced training time. The BiLSTM-based architecture provides better spatial context understanding while maintaining simpler implementation.

**Failure Signatures**: Potential failures may occur when tissue architecture is severely disrupted or when biomarkers are not associated with clear morphological patterns. The model may also struggle with rare biomarker subtypes due to limited training data.
