# The Law of Parsimony in Gradient Descent for Learning Deep Linear Networks

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2306.01154
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.01154
- Reference count: 40
- This paper shows that gradient descent in deep linear networks only updates minimal invariant subspaces when data has low-dimensional structure, enabling more efficient training through network compression.

## Executive Summary
This paper introduces the "law of parsimony" in gradient descent training of deep linear networks, demonstrating that when data exhibits low-dimensional structure, gradient descent only updates a minimal invariant subspace of each weight matrix while leaving other singular subspaces unaffected. The phenomenon emerges from the interaction between orthogonal initialization and low-rank or wide data structure, causing weight matrices to evolve within 2r-dimensional subspaces despite all parameters being updated. This parsimonious behavior enables constructing smaller equivalent networks that train faster without sacrificing performance, as validated in deep matrix completion tasks.

## Method Summary
The paper analyzes gradient descent dynamics in deep linear networks with orthogonal initialization, focusing on how weight matrices evolve when the cross-correlation matrix YX^T has low-dimensional structure. The method involves tracking singular value decomposition of weight matrices throughout training, showing that right singular vectors align with a common nullspace of gradient updates and remain fixed. The analysis extends to weight decay regularization and provides theoretical insights into progressive feature collapse. For practical applications, the paper demonstrates network compression by constructing smaller DLNs that preserve the same learning dynamics as their wider counterparts, particularly in deep matrix completion tasks.

## Key Results
- Under orthogonal initialization, gradient descent only affects minimal portions of singular vector spaces across weight matrices when data has low-dimensional structure
- Smaller equivalent deep linear networks can be constructed that maintain the same learning dynamics as wider networks, enabling faster training
- Progressive feature collapse in deep networks follows a linear decay pattern, with separation measures decreasing linearly across layers
- In deep matrix completion, compressed networks converge significantly faster than original over-parameterized versions

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: When cross-correlation matrix has low-dimensional structure, gradient descent updates only a minimal invariant subspace of each weight matrix while leaving the remaining singular subspaces unaffected.
- Mechanism: The key is that gradient updates across all iterations share a common nullspace. Since weights are initialized orthogonally, right singular vectors can be chosen to align with this nullspace, keeping them fixed throughout training.
- Core assumption: Cross-correlation matrix possesses low-dimensional structure (low-rank or wide matrix) and orthogonal initialization is used.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "Specifically, we show that the evolution of gradient descent starting from orthogonal initialization only affects a minimal portion of singular vector spaces across all weight matrices."
  - [section] "we show that the dynamics of GD tend towards parsimonious solutions. By examining the evolution of GD started from orthogonal initialization, we unveil a surprising, yet common phenomenon"
  - [corpus] Weak evidence - corpus papers focus on general deep learning dynamics but don't specifically address this parsimonious subspace phenomenon
- Break condition: If initialization is not orthogonal or cross-correlation matrix lacks low-dimensional structure, the phenomenon may not occur.

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: The law of parsimony enables constructing smaller equivalent deep linear networks for more efficient training without sacrificing performance.
- Mechanism: Since learning occurs only within a small invariant subspace (2r-dimensional), we can compress wider networks to smaller ones by optimizing only the relevant subspaces while maintaining equivalent learning dynamics.
- Core assumption: The low-dimensional structure identified in Mechanism 1 persists throughout training and can be exploited for network compression.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "we can construct smaller, equivalent deep linear networks without sacrificing the benefits associated with the wider counterparts."
  - [section] "We can construct and train significantly smaller DLNs that share the same learning dynamics as their wider counterparts."
  - [corpus] Moderate evidence - papers on deep linear networks and matrix completion discuss optimization efficiency but don't specifically address this compression approach
- Break condition: If the low-dimensional structure changes during training or the compressed network fails to capture essential dynamics, compression may not work effectively.

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: The law of parsimony provides theoretical insights into progressive feature collapse in representation learning, explaining linear decay of separation measures across layers.
- Mechanism: The minimal invariant subspace structure causes features to progressively collapse toward class means in a linear fashion across layers, creating the observed separation decay pattern.
- Core assumption: The weight matrices satisfy certain optimality and balancedness conditions that allow the parsimonious structure to manifest in feature representations.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "it allows us to better understand deep representation learning by elucidating the linear progressive separation and concentration of representations from shallow to deep layers."
  - [section] "we can precisely characterize the linear progressive separation and concentration of representations from shallow to deep layers"
  - [corpus] Weak evidence - corpus papers discuss feature collapse but don't specifically connect it to this parsimonious subspace mechanism
- Break condition: If the network architecture or data distribution significantly deviates from the assumed conditions, the linear decay pattern may not emerge.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- Question: Does the law of parsimony phenomenon extend beyond gradient descent to other optimization algorithms like Adam, AdaGrad, and RMSprop?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper states "we believe our result can be generalized to other optimization methods beyond GD, such as Adam [33], AdaGrad [34], and RMSprop [35]."
- Why unresolved: The authors note this as a potential direction but haven't proven it yet.
- What evidence would resolve it: Mathematical proof showing similar low-rank structure preservation in the weight matrices when using these optimization algorithms.

### Open Question 2
- Question: How robust is the law of parsimony to different data distributions beyond the whitened data assumption?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper mentions "empirical evidence suggests that our main results should approximately hold for any well-conditioned X, not necessarily restricted to whitened X – we leave this study for future work."
- Why unresolved: The theoretical analysis relies on whitened data, and the authors acknowledge this limitation.
- What evidence would resolve it: Experimental validation showing similar parsimonious behavior on various non-whitened data distributions, and theoretical analysis extending the proof to more general data conditions.

### Open Question 3
- Question: What is the relationship between the initialization scale ε and the rate of progressive feature collapse in deep networks?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper shows experimental results in Figure 11 demonstrating that "decreasing the initialization scale ε leads to a steeper decay in the measure Dl" and mentions "there is indeed a necessary dependence on ε in the upper bound."
- Why unresolved: While the paper shows the relationship empirically, the exact mathematical relationship between ε and the collapse rate remains unclear.
- What evidence would resolve it: Theoretical analysis deriving the precise relationship between initialization scale and feature collapse rate, along with comprehensive experimental validation across different network architectures and depths.

## Limitations

- Analysis relies heavily on orthogonal initialization and low-dimensional data structure, limiting practical applicability when these conditions aren't met
- Weight decay formulation is included in theory but lacks extensive empirical validation
- Progressive separation decay phenomenon may be sensitive to network depth and data distribution beyond controlled experimental settings

## Confidence

**High confidence**: The characterization of parsimonious subspace evolution under orthogonal initialization and low-rank data structure (Mechanisms 1 and 2). The mathematical derivations for gradient flow and weight matrix decomposition are rigorous and well-supported.

**Medium confidence**: The practical effectiveness of network compression and the linear separation decay phenomenon (Mechanisms 2 and 3). While theoretically justified, these claims rely on assumptions about data structure and initialization that may not hold universally in practice.

**Low confidence**: The extension to non-orthogonal initialization scenarios and the robustness of the law of parsimony to various architectural choices beyond deep linear networks.
