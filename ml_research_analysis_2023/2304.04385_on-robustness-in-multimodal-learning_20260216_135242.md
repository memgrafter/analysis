# On Robustness in Multimodal Learning

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2304.04385
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2304.04385
- Reference count: 39
- Primary result: Propose MASD and WiseFT interventions that improve multimodal robustness by 1.5x-4x, achieving 44.2 mAP on AudioSet 20K

## Executive Summary
This paper presents a systematic analysis of multimodal robustness—how models perform when the type and availability of modalities differ between training and deployment. The authors introduce new metrics to capture robustness in multimodal settings and characterize the performance of common multimodal representation learning methods across these metrics. They propose two intervention techniques: Modality Augmented Self-Distillation (MASD) and WiseFT, which significantly improve robustness by encouraging consistency across modalities and ensembling fine-tuned with pretrained weights, respectively. The work demonstrates state-of-the-art results on AudioSet-20K and provides insights into the design of more robust multimodal systems.

## Method Summary
The authors analyze multimodal robustness through contrastive learning and Masked Autoencoder (MAE) pretraining on datasets like AudioSet, Kinetics-400, and ImageNet-Captions. They introduce two intervention techniques: MASD, which applies self-distillation during downstream training to enforce consistency across labeled and unlabeled modalities, and WiseFT, which computes a weighted average of fine-tuned and pretrained weights to balance task adaptation with generalization. The methods are evaluated using novel robustness metrics that measure worst-case performance across different modality scenarios (missing, added, or transferred modalities). The pretraining uses a shared encoder architecture with modality-specific and modality-shared parameters, followed by downstream fine-tuning with the proposed interventions.

## Key Results
- Increasing the number of modalities during pretraining improves downstream performance and robustness
- MASD improves robustness by 1.5x-4x by encouraging consistency across modalities through self-distillation
- WiseFT further enhances robustness by ensembling fine-tuned and pretrained weights
- Achieve state-of-the-art 44.2 mAP on AudioSet-20K by leveraging text as an additional pretraining modality
- MAE models struggle with transferability compared to contrastive models when modalities differ between training and testing

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: Modality Augmented Self-Distillation (MASD) improves robustness by enforcing consistency across labeled and unlabeled modalities.
- Mechanism: During downstream training on a subset of modalities, MASD applies self-distillation from a teacher model (trained on labeled modalities) to a student model (same weights, but evaluated on the unlabeled modalities). This encourages the model to behave consistently across all modalities present during pretraining, even when some are missing at training time.
- Core assumption: The model can produce meaningful outputs for modalities not seen during downstream training because its pretraining has learned a shared representation space that is modality-invariant.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "We introduce two approaches to improve representation learning in a multimodal setting... Modality Augmented Self-Distillation (MASD), encourages consistency in the learned representations across labeled and unlabeled modalities."
  - [section 4.2.1] "Since both the student and teacher models share the same weights, but have different input modalities, we call this loss self-distillation"

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: WiseFT improves robustness by ensembling fine-tuned and pretrained weights to balance task-specific adaptation with general multimodal knowledge.
- Mechanism: WiseFT computes a weighted average of the MASD fine-tuned weights and the linear probe (pretrained) weights. This creates a new set of weights that retain the generalization benefits of pretraining while incorporating task-specific knowledge from fine-tuning.
- Core assumption: A linear combination of fine-tuned and pretrained weights can capture the best of both worlds - task adaptation and multimodal generalization.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "The second approach, derived from WiseFT (Wortsman et al., 2022), leverages a weighted combination of finetuned downstream weights and the initialization pretrained weights to induce robustness."
  - [section 4.2.2] "We compute our new weights by taking a weighted average: θwise = αθmasd + (1-α)θlp"

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: Increasing the number of modalities during pretraining improves downstream performance by providing richer cross-modal interactions.
- Mechanism: Pretraining on more modalities (e.g., audio, video, and text) allows the model to learn more comprehensive shared representations that capture relationships across all modalities, which benefits downstream tasks even when not all modalities are present.
- Core assumption: More modalities during pretraining lead to richer cross-modal interactions and better generalization.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [section 6.1] "Performance consistently improves as a model trains and tests on additional modalities (Fig. 4). Furthermore, the models benefit from more modalities at both pretraining and downstream training time."
  - [abstract] "Increasing the number of modalities used to learn a representation improves downstream performance. In particular, we obtain SOTA results (44.2 mAP) on AudioSet-20K by leveraging text as an additional pretraining modality."

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map: Modality-specific encoders (audio, video, text) -> Shared representation layer (MAE) or pairwise contrastive loss -> Modality-specific heads for contrastive learning -> Downstream task classifiers. MASD adds self-distillation loss, WiseFT adds weight averaging step.
- Critical path: Pretraining -> Linear probing -> Fine-tuning with MASD -> Optional WiseFT -> Downstream evaluation
- Design tradeoffs: Contrastive learning explicitly enforces modality invariance through pairwise losses, while MAE relies on the final shared encoder layer to capture cross-modal information.
- Failure signatures: If robustness doesn't improve with MASD, it may indicate that the shared representation space isn't modality-invariant enough. If WiseFT doesn't help, the linear probe weights may not be representative of the pretrained knowledge.
- First 3 experiments:
  1. Run contrastive learning pretraining on AudioSet with audio and video, then evaluate robustness when text is added at test time.
  2. Apply MASD to the model from experiment 1 and measure robustness improvement when modalities are added or dropped.
  3. Apply WiseFT to the MASD model from experiment 2 and measure any additional robustness gains.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- Question: How does the performance of multimodal models change when tested on modalities not seen during pretraining?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper shows that MAE models struggle with transferability when modalities are completely different between training and testing, while contrastive models exhibit some transferability properties.
- Why unresolved: The paper only tests transferability on a limited number of modality combinations and does not explore the impact of different pretraining objectives on transferability across a wider range of modalities.
- What evidence would resolve it: Experiments testing transferability on a larger number of modality combinations and comparing the performance of different pretraining objectives (e.g., contrastive learning, masked autoencoders, and other methods) would help resolve this question.

### Open Question 2
- Question: What is the optimal number of shared layers in the multimodal encoder for MAE models?
- Basis in paper: [inferred] The paper mentions that downstream training on top of a MAE trained model decreases performance and robustness as the number of modality-shared encoder layers increases.
- Why unresolved: The paper does not explore the impact of different numbers of shared layers on the performance and robustness of MAE models.
- What evidence would resolve it: Experiments testing the performance and robustness of MAE models with different numbers of shared layers would help resolve this question.

### Open Question 3
- Question: How does the choice of pretraining data affect the robustness of multimodal models?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper uses AudioSet-2M as pretraining data for both contrastive and MAE models, but does not explore the impact of using different pretraining datasets.
- Why unresolved: The paper does not compare the performance and robustness of models pretrained on different datasets.
- What evidence would resolve it: Experiments comparing the performance and robustness of models pretrained on different datasets (e.g., AudioSet, Kinetics-400, ImageNet-Captions) would help resolve this question.

## Limitations

- The paper focuses primarily on modality addition/deletion scenarios but does not extensively explore more complex modality transformations or noise patterns that might occur in real-world deployment.
- The robustness metrics, while novel, are evaluated on a relatively limited set of datasets and may not generalize to all multimodal domains.
- The analysis does not explore the impact of different pretraining objectives on transferability across a wide range of modalities.

## Confidence

- **High confidence** in the empirical observation that increasing pretraining modalities improves downstream performance, supported by consistent results across multiple experiments
- **Medium confidence** in MASD's effectiveness, as the mechanism relies on the shared representation space being sufficiently modality-invariant, which isn't directly validated
- **Medium confidence** in WiseFT's contribution, as the optimal weighting α is determined empirically rather than through theoretical justification

## Next Checks

1. Test MASD on scenarios where pretraining modalities are highly heterogeneous to assess robustness boundaries
2. Conduct ablation studies varying α in WiseFT to map the sensitivity of the ensembling approach
3. Evaluate the proposed metrics on additional multimodal datasets (e.g., AV-MNIST, Kinetics-TPS) to assess generalizability
