# Counterfactual reasoning: Testing language models' understanding of hypothetical scenarios

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2305.16572
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.16572
- Reference count: 19
- Models can generally override factual knowledge in counterfactual contexts, but most rely on lexical cues rather than linguistic nuance

## Executive Summary
This work tests how well language models can reason about hypothetical scenarios, particularly when their predictions must override known facts. Using counterfactual conditionals from psycholinguistic studies, it probes five pre-trained models on tasks where the correct answer conflicts with real-world knowledge but aligns with a hypothetical premise. Results show that while models can generally override factual knowledge in counterfactual contexts, most rely heavily on simple lexical cues rather than linguistic nuance. GPT-3 is the only model to consistently use deeper structural cues, though it is still affected by lexical associations. Interestingly, stronger world knowledge correlates with better counterfactual reasoning.

## Method Summary
The study tests five pre-trained language models (GPT-2, GPT-3, BERT, RoBERTa, MPNet) using psycholinguistic stimuli from Ferguson & Sanford (2008) and Ferguson (2012), along with synthetic datasets generated using controlled templates. Models receive counterfactual contexts and predict target continuations, with log-probabilities compared for counterfactual-congruent vs real-world continuations. The analysis examines model preferences across different conditions (CW, RW, CR, CWC, RWCA) and correlates world knowledge strength with counterfactual reasoning performance.

## Key Results
- Models can generally override factual knowledge in counterfactual contexts
- GPT-3 shows unique sensitivity to linguistic nuances of counterfactuals beyond lexical triggers
- Stronger world knowledge correlates with better counterfactual reasoning

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: Models can override factual knowledge when presented with counterfactual contexts.
- Mechanism: Counterfactual language (e.g., "if...were") provides structural cues that shift models' reasoning from real-world associations to hypothetical scenarios.
- Core assumption: Models encode world knowledge and can access it contextually when linguistic markers indicate a shift to a hypothetical world.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract]: "models are consistently able to override real-world knowledge in counterfactual scenarios"
  - [section 2]: "models can increase their preference for counterfactual completions given counterfactual context"
  - [corpus]: Weak evidence - only 2 related papers on counterfactual reasoning, suggesting this is a niche area
- Break condition: If models rely solely on shallow lexical triggers (e.g., "vegetarians" → "cabbages") without deeper structural understanding, they fail when lexical cues are neutralized or conflict.

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: Stronger world knowledge correlates with better counterfactual reasoning.
- Mechanism: Models with more robust factual representations have more opportunities to encounter and learn from counterfactual variants during training, enabling better separation of real and hypothetical.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract]: "this effect is more robust in case of stronger baseline world knowledge"
  - [section 2]: "we find a significant correlation between the robustness of world knowledge encoding and strength of counterfactual preference"
  - [corpus]: Limited - no direct corpus evidence on correlation between knowledge strength and counterfactual reasoning
- Break condition: If models use memorization of counterfactual patterns rather than reasoning, the correlation may be spurious and break when lexical triggers are controlled.

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: GPT-3 shows unique sensitivity to linguistic nuances of counterfactuals beyond lexical triggers.
- Mechanism: GPT-3's massive training data allows it to learn fine-grained associations between counterfactual markers ("if/had" vs "because") and the likelihood of subsequent words, enabling it to parse hypothetical implications even when lexical cues conflict.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract]: "only GPT-3 shows sensitivity to these nuances, though this sensitivity is also non-trivially impacted by lexical associative factors"
  - [section 4]: "only GPT-3 shows substantial difference between CWC and RWCA... indicating finer-grained sensitivity to counterfactual structures"
  - [section A.4]: "GPT-3 does show real sensitivity to linguistic indicators of counterfactuals, but the effect of superficial lexical cues remains strong"
- Break condition: If GPT-3's performance relies on linear distance effects or cue cancellation rather than true structural understanding, it will fail when these factors are controlled.

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map: Five pre-trained models (GPT-2, GPT-3, BERT, RoBERTa, MPNet) process counterfactual contexts and predict target continuations
- Critical path: 1) Generate counterfactual contexts with controlled lexical cues and world knowledge, 2) Feed contexts to models, 3) Compare log-probabilities of counterfactual-congruent vs real-world continuations, 4) Analyze results across conditions
- Design tradeoffs: Using synthetic data allows precise control of variables but may lack naturalness. Small-scale psycholinguistic items have more complex lexical interactions but less systematic control.
- Failure signatures: If models show high preference for counterfactual continuations in real-world contexts, they're relying on lexical triggers rather than structural understanding. If performance drops when lexical cues conflict, they're using shallow associations.
- First 3 experiments:
  1. Test baseline preference for counterfactual continuations in Counterfactual-World (CW) vs Real-World (RW) conditions
  2. Add Counterfactual-to-Reality (CR) condition to isolate effects of lexical triggers
  3. Control world knowledge and lexical cues in Counterfactual-World Context (CWC) vs Real-World Context Alternative (RWCA) conditions

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- Question: Does the success of GPT-3 in counterfactual reasoning reflect genuine understanding of counterfactuals or just sophisticated memorization of linguistic patterns from training data?
- Basis in paper: [inferred] The paper suggests that GPT-3's performance could be explained by either robust logical reasoning or by having developed lower-level knowledge of how linguistic cues mediate associations between words.
- Why unresolved: The paper presents both possibilities but doesn't definitively distinguish between them through additional experiments or analysis.
- What evidence would resolve it: Controlled experiments that test GPT-3's ability to handle novel counterfactual scenarios with previously unseen lexical triggers would help determine if it truly understands the concept or is relying on pattern matching.

### Open Question 2
- Question: Why does stronger world knowledge correlate with better counterfactual reasoning in language models?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper finds a significant correlation between the robustness of world knowledge encoding and strength of counterfactual preference across all language models.
- Why unresolved: While the correlation is established, the paper only speculates about possible explanations without testing them.
- What evidence would resolve it: Systematic experiments varying the strength of world knowledge for specific facts while keeping counterfactual structures constant would help determine if increased exposure to facts in counterfactual contexts enables memorization-based performance.

### Open Question 3
- Question: How do different linguistic factors (conflicting lexical triggers, linear distance, discourse connectives, tense, sentence boundaries) interact to affect language models' counterfactual reasoning abilities?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper conducts a follow-up analysis on GPT-3 testing sensitivity to these factors, but notes the need for additional systematic analysis.
- Why unresolved: The analysis shows effects of individual factors but doesn't explore how they interact or combine to influence model performance.
- What evidence would resolve it: Controlled experiments systematically varying multiple linguistic factors simultaneously while measuring their combined effects on model predictions would clarify their interactions.

## Limitations

- Limited scope of psycholinguistic stimuli - Reliance on small set of controlled stimuli may not capture full range of counterfactual reasoning scenarios
- Reliance on lexical cues - All models except GPT-3 show significant dependence on shallow word associations rather than true structural understanding
- Correlation vs causation ambiguity - Finding that stronger world knowledge correlates with better counterfactual reasoning could reflect multiple mechanisms

## Confidence

- High confidence: Models can generally override factual knowledge in counterfactual contexts
- Medium confidence: GPT-3 shows unique sensitivity to linguistic nuances of counterfactuals beyond lexical triggers
- Medium confidence: Stronger world knowledge correlates with better counterfactual reasoning

## Next Checks

Probe for causal understanding vs pattern matching - Design targeted test cases where lexical cues conflict with structural markers (e.g., "if she had eaten meat, she would have been healthy" when the subject is known to be vegetarian) to determine whether models truly understand counterfactual logic or rely on cue cancellation.

3. Fine-grained analysis of GPT-3's success - Conduct ablation studies on GPT-3's performance by systematically varying linguistic features (tense, modal verbs, conditional markers) to isolate which aspects of counterfactual structure drive its superior performance.
