# The Monge Gap: A Regularizer to Learn All Transport Maps

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2302.04953
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2302.04953
- Reference count: 40
- Key outcome: Novel Monge gap regularizer enables flexible learning of optimal transport maps without architectural constraints, outperforming ICNNs and vanilla MLPs on synthetic and genomics datasets

## Executive Summary
This paper introduces the Monge gap as a novel regularizer for learning optimal transport (OT) maps between probability measures. Rather than constraining network architectures like input convex neural networks (ICNNs) to enforce OT map properties, the authors propose a more flexible approach that minimizes a distance between pushforward measures regularized by the Monge gap. The Monge gap measures how far a candidate map deviates from optimality by comparing the expected cost under a reference measure to the actual OT distance. The authors demonstrate that this regularizer is convex for squared Euclidean costs and characterize when maps are optimal, providing theoretical foundations for the approach.

## Method Summary
The proposed method learns optimal transport maps by minimizing a composite objective: a distance between the pushforward of the source measure and target measure, plus a regularization term based on the Monge gap. The Monge gap is defined as the difference between the expected cost under a reference measure and the optimal transport distance between the reference measure and its pushforward. Two learning procedures are proposed: one for general costs and another for structured costs of the form c(x,y) = h(x-y) with h strictly convex. The method drops architectural constraints on the transport map, allowing the use of standard neural network architectures like MLPs, while the Monge gap regularization enforces desirable properties of optimal transport maps during training.

## Key Results
- The Monge gap regularizer outperforms both ICNNs and vanilla MLPs on synthetic datasets across various dimensions and sample sizes
- On single-cell genomics data, the method achieves better alignment of gene expression distributions compared to baseline approaches
- The method demonstrates robustness to initialization and requires less hyperparameter tuning than architecture-constrained approaches

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The Monge gap regularizer works by providing a principled way to enforce optimality conditions for transport maps without constraining the network architecture. By measuring the deviation from the ideal property that optimal maps should minimize the expected cost, the regularizer guides learning toward maps that satisfy the first-order optimality conditions. The convexity of the Monge gap for squared Euclidean costs ensures a favorable optimization landscape, while the flexibility to use arbitrary network architectures allows the method to capture complex, nonlinear transport relationships. The approach effectively trades architectural guarantees for representational flexibility, with the regularizer compensating for the loss of hard constraints.

## Architecture Onboarding

**Component Map**: Data → Neural Network (Transport Map) → Pushforward Measure → Monge Gap Regularization → Loss Function → Optimization → Trained Transport Map

**Critical Path**: The critical computational path involves sampling from the source measure, applying the transport map, computing the pushforward measure, evaluating the Monge gap, and backpropagating through the entire computation graph. The Monge gap evaluation requires computing expectations and OT distances, which can be computationally intensive depending on the dimensionality and the method used for OT computation.

**Design Tradeoffs**: The method trades architectural constraints (like those in ICNNs) for representational flexibility and computational simplicity. While ICNNs enforce convexity through architecture, the Monge gap approach allows arbitrary architectures but requires careful regularization strength tuning. The choice of reference measure ρ introduces another hyperparameter, though the paper suggests simple choices like ρ = µ work well in practice.

**Failure Signatures**: The method may fail when the cost function is highly non-convex or when the reference measure choice is poor, leading to unstable training or maps that satisfy the Monge gap but not the actual OT objective. High-dimensional problems may suffer from the curse of dimensionality in Monge gap computation. If the regularization strength is too low, the method may degenerate to vanilla MLP performance; if too high, it may over-regularize and prevent learning complex maps.

## Limitations
- The theoretical guarantees for the Monge gap regularizer are primarily established for squared Euclidean costs, with limited analysis for more general cost functions
- Experimental validation is concentrated on synthetic datasets and a single real-world genomics application, limiting generalizability claims
- Computational complexity of evaluating the Monge gap in high dimensions is not thoroughly analyzed, raising scalability concerns

## Confidence
- **High confidence**: The Monge gap provides a valid measure of map optimality when the cost is squared Euclidean distance; the empirical improvements over baseline methods are reproducible from the presented results.
- **Medium confidence**: The method's flexibility and general applicability beyond squared Euclidean costs; the computational efficiency claims relative to ICNNs.
- **Low confidence**: Theoretical guarantees for non-convex cost functions; scalability to very high-dimensional problems; performance on diverse real-world datasets beyond the demonstrated genomics application.
