# Masked Images Are Counterfactual Samples for Robust Fine-tuning

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2303.03052
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.03052
- Authors: 
- Reference count: 40
- Primary result: Method achieves better ID-OOD trade-off using masked images as counterfactual samples with feature-based distillation

## Executive Summary
This paper proposes a fine-tuning method that improves out-of-distribution (OOD) robustness by using masked images as counterfactual samples. The approach masks either semantics-related or semantics-unrelated patches of images based on class activation maps and refills them with patches from other images. These counterfactual samples are then used in feature-based distillation with the pre-trained model, helping the fine-tuned model break spurious correlations between domain-relevant and semantic features. Experiments show this method achieves a better trade-off between in-distribution and OOD performance, surpassing previous methods on OOD metrics.

## Method Summary
The method fine-tunes pre-trained models using counterfactual samples created by masking and refilling image patches. Based on class activation maps (CAM), patches are either masked that are most relevant to the label (semantics-related, Hs) or least relevant (semantics-unrelated, Hd). Masked patches are refilled with content from other images to create counterfactual samples that break spurious correlations. These counterfactual samples are then used in feature-based distillation with the pre-trained model, where the fine-tuned model learns to match the pre-trained model's feature representations. The method is evaluated on ImageNet fine-tuning with five OOD datasets, demonstrating improved robustness compared to vanilla fine-tuning and state-of-the-art methods.

## Key Results
- Achieves better trade-off between in-distribution and out-of-distribution performance compared to vanilla fine-tuning
- Surpasses previous state-of-the-art methods (WiSE-FT, Model Soup) on OOD performance metrics
- Shows effectiveness across multiple masking strategies (object-mask, context-mask) and refilling approaches (single-fill, multi-fill)

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: Masked images break spurious correlations between domain-relevant and semantic features during fine-tuning
- Mechanism: By masking patches identified as most relevant to the label (semantics) or least relevant (context), the method removes parts of the image that contain spurious correlations. Refilling masked patches with content from other images creates counterfactual samples that force the fine-tuned model to rely on true semantic features rather than domain-specific cues.
- Core assumption: The pre-trained model has learned to distinguish semantic from domain-relevant features, and can serve as a teacher through feature-based distillation
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract]: "we mask either the semantics-related or semantics-unrelated patches of the images based on class activation map to break the spurious correlation"
  - [section 3.2.1]: "we investigate two complementary strategies: (1) masking the patches that are most relevant to the label (i.e., Hs, usually the main object); (2) masking the patches that are least relevant to the label (i.e., Hd, usually the context)"
  - [corpus]: Weak evidence - no direct citations about spurious correlation breaking in fine-tuning

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: Counterfactual samples created through masking and refilling improve OOD robustness by forcing the model to learn invariant features
- Mechanism: The counterfactual samples created by masking and refilling create situations where the model must rely on the semantic features (Hs) to match the pre-trained model's predictions, rather than learning to use spurious correlations between domain features (Hd) and labels
- Core assumption: Distribution shifts are sparse in the underlying causal factorization, and masking can isolate these changes
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract]: "use masked images as counterfactual samples that help improving the robustness of the fine-tuning model"
  - [section 3.1.1]: "distribution shifts are shown to be usually sparse in the underlying causal factorization of data generation process"
  - [section 3.2]: "masked images, which serve as counterfactual samples breaking the spurious correlation"
  - [corpus]: Moderate evidence - related works on counterfactual samples for robustness but not specifically for masked image fine-tuning

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: Feature-based distillation from the pre-trained model on counterfactual samples preserves generalizable knowledge during fine-tuning
- Mechanism: By requiring the fine-tuned model to match the pre-trained model's feature representations on counterfactual samples, the method constrains the fine-tuned model to maintain the pre-trained model's ability to distinguish semantic from domain features
- Core assumption: The pre-trained model's feature representations contain robust semantic information that can guide the fine-tuning process
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract]: "The resulting counterfactual samples are used in feature-based distillation with the pre-trained model"
  - [section 3.2]: "we seek an alternative solution in which these samples are used in distillation. This way, the fine-tuning model learns to mimic the pre-trained model in feature representation"
  - [section 3.2.1]: "we require the fine-tuning model to learn from the pre-trained model on these counterfactual samples"
  - [corpus]: Weak evidence - limited direct citations about feature-based distillation for OOD robustness in fine-tuning

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map:
  Pre-trained model (CLIP ViT-B/32) -> CAM-based masking module -> Refilling module -> Counterfactual samples -> Fine-tuned model
  Fine-tuned model -> Feature-based distillation loss + Standard classification loss -> Updated model parameters

- Critical path:
  1. Input image → CAM-based masking → masked image
  2. Masked image + patches from other images → counterfactual sample
  3. Original image + counterfactual sample → compute losses
  4. Losses → update fine-tuned model parameters
  5. Repeat for all training images

- Design tradeoffs:
  - Masking rate vs information preservation: Higher masking rates break more spurious correlations but may lose too much semantic information
  - Single-fill vs multi-fill: Single-fill provides more dramatic distribution shifts but may introduce more noise; multi-fill is more stable but potentially less effective
  - Object-mask vs context-mask: Object-mask targets the main semantic content but may be harder to mask effectively; context-mask is easier but may not break the most critical spurious correlations

- Failure signatures:
  - OOD accuracy drops significantly while ID accuracy remains high (model learned spurious correlations)
  - Both ID and OOD accuracy decrease (masking is too aggressive or pre-trained model knowledge is not being properly transferred)
  - No improvement over vanilla fine-tuning (masking strategy not effective or distillation not working)

- First 3 experiments:
  1. Baseline comparison: Run vanilla fine-tuning on CLIP ViT-B/32 on ImageNet and measure ID/OOD performance
  2. Simple masking test: Implement random masking with no refilling and compare against baseline
  3. CAM-based masking: Implement object-mask strategy with single-fill refilling and compare against baseline and random masking

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
The paper does not explicitly call out open questions, but several implicit questions arise from the work:

1. How does the choice of masking rate (percentage of patches masked) affect the trade-off between in-distribution and out-of-distribution performance across different pre-trained models?

2. Does the effectiveness of CAM-based object masking generalize to datasets with significantly different object scales or layouts compared to ImageNet?

3. What is the theoretical relationship between the masking strategy (object vs. context) and the preservation of causal features during fine-tuning?

## Limitations
- Limited evaluation to CLIP pre-trained models, raising questions about generalizability to other pre-training methods
- No systematic analysis of computational overhead introduced by masking and refilling process
- Does not address scenarios where the pre-trained model itself may have learned spurious correlations

## Confidence
- Effectiveness of spurious correlation breaking: Medium
- Feature-based distillation preserving generalizable knowledge: Medium
- Generalizability across different pre-trained models: Low

## Next Checks
1. **Ablation Study on Refilling Strategies**: Conduct experiments comparing single-fill versus multi-fill approaches across different masking rates to quantify their impact on OOD robustness and computational efficiency.

2. **Pre-trained Model Dependency Analysis**: Evaluate the method's performance when using different pre-trained models (e.g., ResNet, Swin Transformer) to assess the dependency on CLIP's specific feature representations.

3. **Spurious Correlation Attribution**: Perform controlled experiments where known spurious correlations are introduced or removed to directly measure the method's ability to break these correlations versus learning genuine semantic features.
