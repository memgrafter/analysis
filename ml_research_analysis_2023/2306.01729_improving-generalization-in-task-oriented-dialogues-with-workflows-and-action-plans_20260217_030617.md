# Improving Generalization in Task-oriented Dialogues with Workflows and Action Plans

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2306.01729
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.01729
- Authors: 
- Reference count: 40
- Key outcome: Fine-tuning PLMs on ABCD dataset with augmented context (workflow names, action plans) improves generalization to unseen workflows and actions, achieving 44-69% action accuracy for unseen actions and ~70% for unseen workflows.

## Executive Summary
This paper addresses the challenge of generalization in task-oriented dialogue systems, specifically the inability of pre-trained language models to handle unseen workflows and actions. The authors propose augmenting dialogue context with explicit action plans and workflow names, enabling models to better generalize to novel task structures. Experiments on the ABCD dataset demonstrate that this approach significantly improves performance on unseen elements, with action prediction accuracy improving from 0% to 44-69% for unseen actions when action plans are provided.

## Method Summary
The authors fine-tune T5 text2text models on the ABCD dataset using various augmentation strategies. The core approach involves adding workflow names, action plans, and legal workflow lists to the dialogue context before feeding it to the model. The model is trained to predict either the next action or complete action sequence given the augmented context. Different generalization splits are used to evaluate performance on unseen workflows, action sequences, and individual actions. The models are trained with teacher-forcing and evaluated using beam search.

## Key Results
- Without augmentation, models achieve near 0% accuracy for unseen actions
- Providing action plans improves unseen action accuracy from 0% to 44-69%
- Unseen workflow accuracy reaches ~70% with both workflow names and action plans
- Legal workflow lists improve flow prediction accuracy to ~46-57% for unseen flows

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: Providing action plans as prompts improves zero-shot generalization to unseen workflows and actions.
- Mechanism: The model uses the explicit action sequence from the plan to guide its predictions, bypassing the need to infer the correct sequence from context alone.
- Core assumption: The action plan accurately reflects the correct sequence of steps needed for the task.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "Our approach improves action prediction accuracy from 0% to 44-69% for unseen actions and achieves ~70% action accuracy for unseen workflows."
  - [section] "Models which are given the plan, however, (i.e. +F+P, +L+F+P) have action accuracies of ~45% and ~70% for actually unseen actions. Models without plans predict the never before seen actions with ~0% accuracy."
  - [corpus] Weak evidence - corpus neighbors do not discuss action plan prompting specifically.
- Break condition: If the action plan is incorrect or incomplete, the model's predictions will be wrong. If the plan does not capture all necessary branching logic (e.g., if-else conditions), the model may follow an incorrect sequence.

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: Providing a list of legal workflow names improves flow prediction accuracy for unseen flows.
- Mechanism: The model uses the provided list to constrain its predictions to valid workflow names, improving accuracy when predicting unseen flows.
- Core assumption: The list of legal workflow names is complete and accurate.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "We also observe that providing legal workflow names improves flow prediction accuracy for unseen flows."
  - [section] "With the list of flows (+L), it is ~57% for Split 1 and ~46% for Split 3 when even the flow prefix itself is unseen during training."
  - [corpus] Weak evidence - corpus neighbors do not discuss flow prediction or workflow name lists.
- Break condition: If the list of legal workflow names is incomplete or contains errors, the model's predictions will be constrained incorrectly. If the model relies too heavily on the list and does not use context, it may predict an incorrect flow that is in the list.

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: Augmenting the context with workflow names and action plans allows the model to better handle unseen action sequences and actions.
- Mechanism: The additional context provides the model with explicit information about the task structure, allowing it to generate more accurate predictions even for unseen elements.
- Core assumption: The model can effectively process and utilize the additional context information.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "We propose augmenting the dialogue context with action plans and workflow names, enabling PLMs to better generalize to unseen tasks and actions."
  - [section] "For Split 3, when all action sequences in the test are unseen in training and entire flow-prefix groups are also unseen, action accuracy is much higher for models given plans (i.e. +F+P, +L+F+P) at ~70% as compared to models with only the flow (i.e. +F, +L+F) at ~61-61.5%."
  - [corpus] Weak evidence - corpus neighbors do not discuss context augmentation for task-oriented dialogue.
- Break condition: If the model cannot effectively process the additional context, or if the context is too complex or ambiguous, the model's performance may not improve or may even degrade.

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map: Dialogue context -> Augmentation (workflow names, action plans, legal flow lists) -> T5 model (encoder-decoder) -> Predicted action/utterance, workflow name -> Evaluation (action accuracy, flow accuracy, etc.)
- Critical path: Input context -> Augmentation -> Model prediction -> Output parsing -> Evaluation
- Design tradeoffs:
  - Using a smaller T5 model (small/base/large) allows for more extensive experimentation but may limit performance.
  - Providing ground-truth oracle flow to the model ensures correct action plans but may not be realistic in practice.
  - Training with teacher-forcing is efficient but does not account for error propagation.
- Failure signatures:
  - Low action accuracy for unseen actions/flows suggests the model is not effectively using the provided action plans.
  - Low flow accuracy for unseen flows suggests the model is not effectively using the provided legal flow lists.
  - High Levenshtein distance suggests the model is not accurately predicting the action sequence.
- First 3 experiments:
  1. Train a T5-small model on the standard split with no additional context and evaluate action and flow accuracy.
  2. Train a T5-small model on Split 3 with only the legal flow list (+L) and evaluate action and flow accuracy.
  3. Train a T5-small model on Split 3 with the legal flow list and action plan (+L+F+P) and evaluate action and flow accuracy.

## Limitations
- The approach relies on oracle action plans and workflow names during training, which may not reflect real-world deployment scenarios.
- The dataset size (55 workflows, ~22 turns per conversation) may limit the robustness of generalization claims, particularly for larger model variants.
- The paper does not address how the model would perform when action plans contain errors or when workflow names are ambiguous or missing.

## Confidence
- High confidence: The core finding that fine-tuned PLMs struggle with zero-shot generalization to unseen workflows and actions is well-supported by experimental results showing near-zero accuracy for unseen elements without augmentation.
- Medium confidence: The improvement claims for action prediction accuracy (44-69% for unseen actions) are supported by results but depend on the oracle assumption for action plans. The flow prediction improvements (~46-57% accuracy for unseen flows) are more modest and may be influenced by the specific evaluation methodology.
- Low confidence: The mechanism explanations for why action plans and workflow names improve performance are largely intuitive rather than empirically validated. The paper does not provide ablation studies showing whether the benefits come from the plan structure itself versus simply providing more context.

## Next Checks
1. **Noise robustness test**: Evaluate model performance when action plans contain 10-30% errors or when workflow names are partially correct, to assess real-world applicability beyond oracle conditions.
2. **Ablation on context components**: Systematically remove individual elements (workflow name, action plan, legal flow list) to quantify their relative contributions and determine whether simpler context augmentation could achieve similar results.
3. **Cross-dataset generalization**: Test the trained models on a different task-oriented dialogue dataset (such as MultiWOZ) without fine-tuning to assess whether the learned generalization patterns transfer to completely different domains and workflow structures.
