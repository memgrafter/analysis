# Debiasing the Cloze Task in Sequential Recommendation with Bidirectional Transformers

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2301.09210
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2301.09210
- Reference count: 40
- Key outcome: This paper addresses the problem of exposure bias in sequential recommendation, specifically in BERT4Rec models trained with the Cloze task. The authors prove that the common Inverse Propensity Scoring (IPS) debiasing approach does not extend to sequential recommendation due to the temporal nature of the problem. They propose a novel Inverse Temporal Propensity Scoring (ITPS) framework and loss function that theoretically provides an unbiased estimator for the ideal Cloze task loss. Experiments on semi-synthetic and real-world datasets demonstrate the effectiveness of ITPS in capturing true relevance, its robustness to increasing exposure bias, and its ability to mitigate popularity bias in both short and long terms.

## Executive Summary
This paper addresses a critical limitation in sequential recommendation systems: exposure bias in BERT4Rec models trained with the Cloze task. The authors demonstrate that standard IPS debiasing approaches fail in sequential settings due to temporal dependencies, and propose ITPS as a theoretically sound alternative. Their framework introduces temporal exposure propensity scoring that accounts for how item exposure varies across timesteps in sequences. Through semi-synthetic and real-world experiments, ITPS shows superior performance in capturing true item relevance and mitigating popularity bias, particularly in long-term feedback loops.

## Method Summary
The paper proposes Inverse Temporal Propensity Scoring (ITPS) as a novel debiasing framework for the Cloze task in sequential recommendation. ITPS extends traditional IPS by incorporating temporal exposure propensities, which vary across timesteps in a sequence. The authors prove that ITPS provides an unbiased estimator for the ideal Cloze task loss, addressing the inadequacy of IPS in sequential settings. The method involves estimating temporal exposure propensities using models like Tensor Factorization, then training BERT4Rec with an ITPS-based loss function that weights each item prediction by its temporal exposure propensity. The framework is evaluated using both semi-synthetic data (generated from MovieLens 100K with varying exposure bias levels) and real-world datasets (MovieLens 1M, MovieLens 20M, Amazon Beauty), with performance measured using NDCG@k, Recall@k, and Expected Free Discovery (EFD@10) in feedback loop simulations.

## Key Results
- ITPS-BERT4Rec outperforms both BERT4Rec and IPS-BERT4Rec baselines on semi-synthetic data with varying exposure bias levels
- The proposed method demonstrates robustness to increasing levels of exposure bias, maintaining performance where other methods degrade
- In real-world feedback loop experiments, ITPS-BERT4Rec shows superior ability to mitigate popularity bias over both short and long time horizons
- Unbiased evaluation reveals that standard LOO evaluation can be misleading, with ITPS-BERT4Rec showing better alignment with true relevance

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: The proposed Inverse Temporal Propensity Scoring (ITPS) estimator is unbiased for the ideal Cloze task loss.
- Mechanism: ITPS adjusts the loss weighting for each item prediction by the reciprocal of its temporal exposure propensity at the specific timestep, rather than using a static propensity. This accounts for the fact that an item's exposure likelihood varies with its position in the sequence.
- Core assumption: Temporal exposure propensities vary across timesteps and must be estimated accurately for each (sequence, item, timestep) tuple.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract]: "we propose a novel propensity scoring mechanism, which can theoretically debias the Cloze task in sequential recommendation."
  - [section 4]: Proposition 3 proves that ITPS-based Cloze task loss is unbiased for the ideal Cloze task loss.
  - [corpus]: Weak evidence - the neighboring papers discuss exposure bias in sequential recommendation but don't provide specific evidence for ITPS's unbiasedness.
- Break condition: If temporal exposure propensities cannot be estimated accurately, the ITPS estimator becomes biased.

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: The interaction random variable representation in ITPS is adequate for sequential recommendation.
- Mechanism: ITPS models interaction as a product of Choice, Exposure, and Relevance random variables, ensuring only one item can be interacted with per timestep in a sequence. This is adequate because a user can only interact with one item at a time.
- Core assumption: In sequential recommendation, a user can only interact with one item at a given timestep.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [section 3.4]: "the IPS-based framework for recommendation with user profiles does not extend to sequential recommendation because it fails to account for the temporal nature of the problem."
  - [section 4]: "we address the issue of the inadequacy of the interaction random variable representation by including the outcome of the Choice random variable."
  - [corpus]: No direct evidence in neighboring papers for this specific mechanism.
- Break condition: If multiple items can be interacted with at the same timestep in the dataset, this mechanism fails.

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: ITPS is robust to increasing levels of exposure bias.
- Mechanism: By weighting each item prediction by its temporal exposure propensity, ITPS ensures that items with lower exposure are not unfairly penalized in the loss function. This makes the model more robust to skewed exposure distributions.
- Core assumption: The temporal exposure propensities can be estimated and used to adjust the loss function appropriately.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [section 5.1.6]: "ITPS-BERT4Rec shows the best performance overall in approximating the Oracle" and "demonstrates the robustness of our proposed approach to increasing levels of exposure bias."
  - [corpus]: Weak evidence - neighboring papers discuss exposure bias but don't provide specific evidence for ITPS's robustness to increasing bias levels.
- Break condition: If the exposure distribution becomes extremely skewed, the estimated temporal exposure propensities may become unreliable, breaking this mechanism.

## Limitations
- The theoretical guarantees rely heavily on accurate temporal exposure propensity estimation, which is not directly verified in experiments
- The semi-synthetic data generation process depends on Tensor Factorization models with unspecified implementation details
- The evaluation methodology assumes popularity-based negative sampling adequately represents unbiased evaluation without thorough validation

## Confidence
- **High Confidence**: The theoretical framework for ITPS and its unbiasedness (Proposition 3) is well-established and clearly presented
- **Medium Confidence**: The empirical validation shows consistent improvements across datasets, but the semi-synthetic data generation introduces uncertainty about real-world applicability
- **Low Confidence**: The claim about ITPS robustness to increasing exposure bias levels relies on a single semi-synthetic experiment without extensive sensitivity analysis
