# Low-Variance Gradient Estimation in Unrolled Computation Graphs with ES-Single

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID**: 2304.11153
- **Source URL**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2304.11153
- **Reference count**: 40
- **Primary result**: ES-Single is a low-variance evolution strategies gradient estimator for unrolled computation graphs that reuses perturbations across partial unrolls, yielding unbiased estimates with constant variance independent of truncation length.

## Executive Summary
This paper introduces ES-Single, a novel gradient estimation technique for unrolled computation graphs that addresses the high variance problem of existing methods like Persistent Evolution Strategies (PES). The key innovation is reusing the same outer parameter perturbations across all partial unrolls within an inner problem, rather than resampling them at each unroll. This approach yields unbiased gradient estimates with constant variance that doesn't increase with the number of truncations, making it particularly well-suited for long-horizon problems with short truncations. Experiments on synthetic and real-world tasks demonstrate that ES-Single consistently outperforms PES in terms of convergence speed and final performance across multiple domains including hyperparameter optimization, RNN training, and learned optimizer meta-training.

## Method Summary
ES-Single is an evolution strategies-based gradient estimator designed specifically for unrolled computation graphs where inner problems are solved through truncated backpropagation. Unlike PES which resamples perturbations at each partial unroll, ES-Single generates a single perturbation per particle that is kept fixed throughout the entire inner problem. This yields an unbiased gradient estimator that is mathematically equivalent to full-unroll ES for quadratic inner problems. The algorithm maintains separate states for each particle across unrolls, executes partial unrolls using the same fixed perturbations, and aggregates losses to form gradient estimates. This design results in variance that remains constant regardless of the number of truncated unrolls, addressing a key limitation of PES where variance increases linearly with the number of unrolls.

## Key Results
- ES-Single consistently outperforms PES on convergence speed and final performance across hyperparameter optimization, RNN training, and learned optimizer meta-training tasks
- The variance of ES-Single is constant with respect to the number of truncated unrolls, while PES variance increases linearly with truncation count
- ES-Single successfully solves a chaotic 2D regression toy task where PES fails to converge to the optimal meta-objective value
- On the character-level Penn TreeBank dataset, ES-Single demonstrates lower variance than PES when using many short truncations (K ∈ {1, 2, 5, 10, 20, 50, 100, 200, 500, 1000})

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- **Claim**: Reusing the same outer parameter perturbations across all partial unrolls within an inner problem yields lower variance than resampling perturbations at each unroll.
- **Mechanism**: By fixing perturbations for the duration of an inner problem, ES-Single avoids the covariance terms that arise in PES between gradients of each loss term Lt with respect to per-timestep parameters θτ. This constant variance is independent of truncation length K.
- **Core assumption**: The loss landscape of the inner problem is sufficiently smooth that reusing perturbations doesn't introduce significant bias, and the perturbations themselves are independent across particles.
- **Evidence anchors**:
  - [abstract] "ES-Single samples a single perturbation per particle, that is kept fixed over the course of an inner problem (e.g., perturbations are not re-sampled for each partial unroll). Compared to PES, ES-Single is simpler to implement and has lower variance: the variance of ES-Single is constant with respect to the number of truncated unrolls"
  - [section 3.1] "the variance of ES-Single does not depend on the number of partial unrolls per inner problem. We measured the empirical variance on the same task used by Vicol et al. (2021): we consider a tiny LSTM trained on the character-level Penn TreeBank dataset (Marcus et al., 1993). The full inner problem consists of a sequence of length T = 1000, which we split into truncated unrolls of lengths K ∈ {1, 2, 5, 10, 20, 50, 100, 200, 500, 1000}. When measuring the variance of each estimator, we keep the parameters θ fixed—that is, we do not updateθ after each partial unroll—and accumulate the gradient for the full problem by summing the estimates over the truncated unrolls. This allows us to avoid any hysteresis effects."
- **Break condition**: If the loss landscape is highly chaotic with strong local discontinuities, fixed perturbations across unrolls could lead to inconsistent gradient directions that hurt convergence.

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim**: ES-Single is mathematically equivalent to full-unroll ES, making it unbiased by construction for quadratic inner problems.
- **Mechanism**: The gradient estimator in ES-Single is defined as 1/σ² Eϵ [ϵ(L(θ +ϵ) - L(θ -ϵ))], which is the same as full-unroll ES. This equivalence ensures unbiasedness under the assumption of quadratic inner problems.
- **Core assumption**: The inner problem's loss function is quadratic or can be well-approximated as quadratic in the region of interest.
- **Evidence anchors**:
  - [abstract] "Similarly to the recently-proposed Persistent Evolution Strategies (PES), ES-Single is unbiased, and overcomes chaos arising from recursive function applications by smoothing the meta-loss landscape."
  - [section 3] "Proposition 3.1 (ES-Single is unbiased). Assume that L(θ) is quadratic and ∇θL(θ) exists. Then, the ES-Single gradient estimator using antithetic sampling is unbiased, that is, bias(ˆgES-Single) = Eϵ [ˆgES-Single] − ∇θL(θ) = 0."
- **Break condition**: For highly non-quadratic inner problems, the unbiasedness guarantee no longer holds, though the estimator may still be approximately unbiased in practice.

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim**: ES-Single's low variance makes it particularly effective for long-horizon inner problems with short truncations.
- **Mechanism**: Since ES-Single's variance is constant with respect to the number of truncated unrolls, it avoids the linear increase in variance that PES experiences as the number of unrolls increases. This makes ES-Single well-suited for problems where T >> K.
- **Core assumption**: The benefits of low variance outweigh any potential downsides of reusing perturbations across unrolls.
- **Evidence anchors**:
  - [abstract] "ES-Single is simpler to implement and has lower variance: the variance of ES-Single is constant with respect to the number of truncated unrolls, removing a key barrier in applying ES to long inner problems using short truncations."
  - [section 3.1] "In contrast, the variance of PES includes terms in T, the number of unrolls per inner problem (Vicol et al., 2021); depending on the correlation between gradients at each partial unroll, its variance either decreases slightly with increasingT, or increases linearly withT."
- **Break condition**: For short-horizon problems or when using a moderate number of unrolls per inner problem, PES may have slightly lower variance than ES-Single.

## Architecture Onboarding

- **Component map**: Perturbation generation -> State maintenance for each particle -> Partial unroll execution -> Gradient estimation -> Parameter update

- **Critical path**: The critical path in ES-Single is: generate perturbations → maintain separate states for each particle → execute partial unrolls using the same perturbations → aggregate losses to form gradient estimate → update parameters. This path must be efficient to make ES-Single competitive.

- **Design tradeoffs**: ES-Single trades off the potential benefits of adaptive perturbations (as in PES) for the stability of fixed perturbations. This leads to lower variance but may miss opportunities for more adaptive exploration. The choice between ES-Single and PES depends on the specific problem characteristics.

- **Failure signatures**: ES-Single may fail if the inner problem's loss landscape is too chaotic, causing fixed perturbations to consistently point in suboptimal directions. It may also underperform on short-horizon problems where PES's adaptive perturbations are beneficial.

- **First 3 experiments**:
  1. Reproduce the synthetic influence balancing task from the paper to verify unbiasedness and compare variance with PES.
  2. Apply ES-Single to a simple hyperparameter optimization task (e.g., tuning learning rate for linear regression) to compare convergence speed and final performance with PES and vanilla ES.
  3. Test ES-Single on a long-sequence RNN task (e.g., the copy task) to evaluate its performance with short truncations compared to PES and truncated BPTT.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- **Question**: What is the precise relationship between ES-Single's smoothing effect and its performance on chaotic meta-loss landscapes?
- **Basis in paper**: [inferred] The paper notes that ES-Single "smooths the outer loss landscape" and that this allows it to "cross the chaos and reach the optimal meta-objective value" in the toy 2D regression task, but does not provide a quantitative analysis of the smoothing effect.
- **Why unresolved**: The paper demonstrates that ES-Single outperforms PES on chaotic tasks but does not isolate or measure the specific contribution of the smoothing effect to this performance improvement.
- **What evidence would resolve it**: Experiments comparing ES-Single with varying perturbation scales on a spectrum of chaotic vs. non-chaotic meta-loss landscapes, coupled with quantitative measures of landscape smoothness.

### Open Question 2
- **Question**: How does ES-Single's performance scale with increasing inner problem dimensionality?
- **Basis in paper**: [explicit] The paper notes that ES-Single's variance "increases linearly in the number of outer parameters" but does not empirically test performance on high-dimensional inner problems.
- **Why unresolved**: While the theoretical variance analysis provides insight, practical performance may be affected by other factors not captured in the analysis, such as the curse of dimensionality in particle sampling.
- **What evidence would resolve it**: Experiments on inner problems with increasing state and parameter dimensionality, measuring both variance and convergence speed of ES-Single.

### Open Question 3
- **Question**: Can ES-Single be effectively combined with other meta-learning techniques, such as learning rate schedules or adaptive optimizers?
- **Basis in paper**: [inferred] The paper focuses on ES-Single as a standalone gradient estimator and does not explore its integration with other meta-learning approaches.
- **Why unresolved**: The paper demonstrates ES-Single's effectiveness on a range of tasks but does not investigate whether combining it with other techniques could further improve performance.
- **What evidence would resolve it**: Experiments combining ES-Single with various meta-learning techniques and comparing their performance to ES-Single alone.

## Limitations

- The theoretical unbiasedness guarantees only hold for quadratic inner problems, with behavior for non-quadratic problems remaining less certain
- Fixed perturbations across unrolls may be suboptimal when the loss landscape changes significantly between partial unrolls
- Performance may degrade on highly chaotic inner problems where fixed perturbations consistently point in suboptimal directions

## Confidence

- **High Confidence**: The variance reduction mechanism and constant variance property are well-established mathematically and empirically demonstrated. The equivalence to full-unroll ES for quadratic problems is rigorously proven.
- **Medium Confidence**: The practical performance improvements over PES are well-demonstrated across multiple tasks, though some experiments rely on previously established baselines. The algorithm's behavior in extreme edge cases (highly chaotic loss landscapes) is not fully characterized.
- **Low Confidence**: The theoretical guarantees for non-quadratic problems and the algorithm's behavior under extreme parameter configurations remain partially unexplored.

## Next Checks

3. **Long-Horizon Stress Test**: Design a synthetic long-horizon task (T >> K) with known optimal solution to quantitatively compare ES-Single's convergence speed and final accuracy against both PES and full-unroll methods, measuring the trade-off between truncation length and total computational cost.
