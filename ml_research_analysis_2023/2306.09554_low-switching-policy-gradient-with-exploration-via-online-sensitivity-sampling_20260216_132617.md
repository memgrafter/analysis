# Low-Switching Policy Gradient with Exploration via Online Sensitivity Sampling

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2306.09554
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.09554
- Reference count: 40
- Key outcome: ε-optimal policy with eO(poly(d)/ε³) samples using low-switching policy gradient with exploration via online sensitivity sampling

## Executive Summary
This paper introduces a novel policy optimization algorithm that combines low-switching policy gradient updates with exploration via online sensitivity sampling. The method bridges policy-based and value-based approaches to achieve efficient learning with general non-linear function approximation. By measuring the importance of new data points through online sensitivity sampling, the algorithm only updates the policy when necessary, significantly reducing the number of policy updates. The approach incorporates a reward bonus that encourages exploration in both known and unknown state-action spaces, achieving theoretical sample complexity of eO(poly(d)/ε³) for obtaining an ε-optimal policy.

## Method Summary
The algorithm operates within an actor-critic framework, using online sensitivity sampling to measure the importance of new data points. When the sensitivity score exceeds a threshold, the policy is updated; otherwise, it remains unchanged, reducing switching frequency. The method creates optimistic MDPs with reward bonuses based on a width function and known/unknown state-action sets, then solves these with policy gradient updates. This approach combines the benefits of both policy-based and value-based methods while maintaining computational efficiency through selective policy updates.

## Key Results
- Achieves ε-optimal policy with eO(poly(d)/ε³) samples, where d is the eluder dimension of the function class
- Demonstrates significant improvement over previous approaches in sample efficiency
- Shows effectiveness on sparse-reward MuJoCo environments with deep neural networks
- Reduces policy switching frequency while maintaining exploration through reward bonuses

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The method works by strategically balancing exploration and exploitation through online sensitivity sampling. When new data points are sufficiently "surprising" (measured by the sensitivity score), the policy updates to incorporate this information. The reward bonus encourages exploration by making unknown state-action pairs more attractive. This selective updating reduces computational overhead while ensuring the policy adapts to important changes in the environment, leading to efficient sample usage and improved learning performance.

## Architecture Onboarding

**Component Map**: MDP Environment -> Online Sensitivity Sampler -> Policy Update Decision -> Actor-Critic Updates -> Reward Bonus Calculator -> Optimistic MDP

**Critical Path**: MDP data → Sensitivity sampling → Policy update decision → Actor-critic training → Policy execution

**Design Tradeoffs**: 
- Sensitivity threshold: Higher values reduce switching but may miss important updates; lower values increase responsiveness but computational cost
- Reward bonus magnitude: Larger bonuses encourage more exploration but may slow exploitation; smaller bonuses may lead to premature convergence
- Function approximation class complexity: More complex classes capture better policies but increase sample complexity

**Failure Signatures**:
- Poor exploration: Low state-action space coverage, getting stuck in local optima
- Excessive switching: Frequent policy updates with minimal improvement, high computational cost
- Sensitivity miscalibration: Updates triggered too often or too rarely relative to information gain

## Limitations
- Online sensitivity sampling procedure and sensitivity score calculation are not fully specified, making direct implementation challenging
- Neural network architecture and hyperparameters for the deep RL implementation are not detailed
- Empirical evaluation is limited to sparse-reward MuJoCo environments, which may not generalize to other domains

## Confidence

**High Confidence**: Theoretical analysis and sample complexity bounds (ε-optimal policy with eO(poly(d)/ε³) samples) are well-founded and build upon established techniques in reinforcement learning.

**Low Confidence**: The empirical results on sparse-reward MuJoCo environments cannot be fully verified due to missing implementation details and hyperparameters.
