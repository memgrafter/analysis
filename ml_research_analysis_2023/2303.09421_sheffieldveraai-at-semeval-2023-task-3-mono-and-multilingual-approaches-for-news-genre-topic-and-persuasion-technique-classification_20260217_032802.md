# SheffieldVeraAI at SemEval-2023 Task 3: Mono and multilingual approaches for news genre, topic and persuasion technique classification

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID**: 2303.09421
- **Source URL**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.09421
- **Reference count**: 27
- **Key outcome**: Monolingual and multilingual transformer ensembles achieved top rankings across all subtasks and languages, including first place in framing detection for three languages.

## Executive Summary
This paper presents systems for SemEval-2023 Task 3, which involves detecting news genre, framing, and persuasion techniques in a multilingual setting across six languages. The authors employ ensemble approaches combining monolingual and multilingual transformer models, achieving strong performance across all subtasks. Their systems demonstrate that both monolingual and multilingual approaches have distinct advantages depending on the language and task, with monolingual models excelling for English framing detection while multilingual models show superior cross-lingual generalization.

## Method Summary
The authors developed ensemble systems using both monolingual and multilingual transformer models for three subtasks. For subtask 1 (News Genre), they used mBERT ensembles with and without class weighting. For subtask 2 (Framing), they employed two separate ensembles: a monolingual RoBERTa-MUPPETLARGE trained on translated English articles and a multilingual XLM-RoBERTaLARGE with adapters and task-adaptive pretraining (TAPT). For subtask 3 (Persuasion Techniques), they used monolingual RoBERTa-Base for English and mBERT ensembles for other languages, with the key innovation of including non-labeled paragraphs as zero vectors during training. The systems addressed class imbalance through oversampling and class weighting techniques.

## Key Results
- Achieved joint-first place for German in subtask 1 (News Genre) and highest mean rank among multilingual teams
- Secured first place in three languages and best average rank across all languages in subtask 2 (Framing)
- Achieved top-10 rankings across all languages in subtask 3 (Persuasion Techniques), including second place for English
- Demonstrated strong zero-shot performance for low-resource languages including Georgian

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- **Claim**: Monolingual models with translation outperform multilingual models for English in Subtask 2.
- **Mechanism**: Training monolingual RoBERTa-MUPPETLARGE on translated articles gives the model richer, language-specific representations than joint multilingual training, especially when English is the target.
- **Core assumption**: Translation preserves task-relevant features well enough for English-based classification, and monolingual models benefit more from language-specific pretraining.
- **Evidence anchors**:
  - [abstract]: "For Subtask 2 (Framing), we achieved first place in 3 languages, and the best average rank across all the languages, by using two separate ensembles: a monolingual RoBERTa-MUPPETLARGE and an ensemble of XLM-RoBERTaLARGE with adapters and task adaptive pretraining."
  - [section]: "When comparing across monolingual and multilingual models, we see that XLM-R models are unable to compete with the performance of monolingual MUPPET for English."
  - [corpus]: Weak evidence; corpus lists only related task entries without confirming mechanism.
- **Break condition**: If translations distort key linguistic cues (e.g., sarcasm markers, stylistic framing) or if the monolingual model cannot handle the linguistic diversity in the translated text.

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim**: Adapter-based fine-tuning with task-adaptive pre-training (TAPT) improves multilingual performance.
- **Mechanism**: Adapters add a small trainable bottleneck that modifies internal representations without altering the full model, enabling efficient multilingual fine-tuning; TAPT further adapts the base model to the specific task domain.
- **Core assumption**: The pretrained multilingual model's general representations are close enough to the target domain that fine-tuning only the adapter is sufficient, and TAPT improves cross-lingual generalization.
- **Evidence anchors**:
  - [abstract]: "For Subtask 2 (Framing), we achieved first place in 3 languages, and the best average rank across all the languages, by using two separate ensembles: a monolingual RoBERTa-MUPPETLARGE and an ensemble of XLM-RoBERTaLARGE with adapters and task adaptive pretraining."
  - [section]: "We used XLM-RoBERTa Large (Conneau et al., 2020) for our multilingual model. Our system uses two techniques to improve performance: TAPT and adapter layers."
  - [corpus]: Weak evidence; no explicit results for adapter+TAPT vs baseline in corpus entries.
- **Break condition**: If the task requires large shifts from the pretraining domain or if adapters cannot capture task-specific nuances.

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim**: Including non-labelled paragraphs improves overall F1micro for Subtask 3.
- **Mechanism**: Assigning a zero vector to unlabelled paragraphs allows the model to learn a "no-label" class, reducing false positives and improving class balance.
- **Core assumption**: Unlabelled paragraphs carry distributional cues about non-persuasion content that help the model distinguish between classes.
- **Evidence anchors**:
  - [section]: "In contrast to the organiser baseline, which discards paragraphs without a label, we assigned them a class vector of zeros and included them in the training set. This method lead to a significant improvement in performance across all languages."
  - [section]: "Table 14 displays the sizes of the train set for each language without adding non-labelled examples vs. adding them. Table 15 shows the F1 micro results of both approaches..."
  - [corpus]: Weak evidence; no direct mention of this technique in corpus entries.
- **Break condition**: If the dataset size is too small or the unlabelled data introduces noise that outweighs the benefits.

## Foundational Learning

- **Concept**: Multilingual transformer models (e.g., mBERT, XLM-R) can handle multiple languages simultaneously but may underperform monolingual models for high-resource languages.
  - **Why needed here**: The task requires multilingual classification; understanding when to use multilingual vs. monolingual is critical for performance.
  - **Quick check question**: In which subtask did the monolingual approach outperform the multilingual one for English, and why?

- **Concept**: Adapter modules allow efficient fine-tuning by modifying only a small portion of the model's parameters.
  - **Why needed here**: Subtask 2 uses adapters for XLM-R to balance performance and computational cost; knowing how adapters work helps in model selection and tuning.
  - **Quick check question**: What is the reduction factor used for the bottleneck adapter, and how does it affect training speed?

- **Concept**: Task-adaptive pre-training (TAPT) fine-tunes the base model on in-domain data before task-specific fine-tuning.
  - **Why needed here**: TAPT is used in Subtask 2 to improve cross-lingual generalization; understanding its role helps in replicating or extending the approach.
  - **Quick check question**: How many epochs of TAPT were used, and on what data?

## Architecture Onboarding

- **Component map**: Article preprocessing (cleaning, truncation, optional translation) -> Base model (mBERT / XLM-R / RoBERTa-MUPPET) -> Adapter layers (only for multilingual models in Subtask 2) -> Task-adaptive pre-training (TAPT: Subtask 2) -> Fine-tuning head (classification layer with class weighting or oversampling) -> Ensemble voting (3 models per subtask)

- **Critical path**: Data preprocessing -> Model selection (monolingual vs multilingual) -> Fine-tuning (with adapters/TAPT as applicable) -> Ensemble voting -> Prediction

- **Design tradeoffs**:
  - Monolingual vs multilingual: Monolingual gives better English performance but requires translation; multilingual generalizes better across languages but may lag on English.
  - Adapters vs full fine-tuning: Adapters are parameter-efficient but may underperform on complex tasks; full fine-tuning is more expressive but costly.
  - Including vs excluding non-labelled paragraphs: Including improves F1micro but may introduce noise; excluding is cleaner but risks poorer generalization.

- **Failure signatures**:
  - Low F1 on low-resource languages: Likely need more data or zero-shot strategies.
  - High variance across languages: Imbalanced training or inadequate multilingual representation.
  - Overfitting on English: Model too focused on English specifics; consider translation augmentation.

- **First 3 experiments**:
  1. Train a baseline mBERT on all languages without adapters or TAPT; measure F1micro per language.
  2. Add Pfeiffer adapters and TAPT to XLM-R; compare performance to baseline.
  3. Compare monolingual RoBERTa-MUPPET (with translation) vs multilingual XLM-R on English only.

## Limitations
- Evaluation is limited to the SemEval-2023 Task 3 dataset, and claimed improvements may not generalize to other domains or tasks.
- The paper does not provide detailed ablation studies to isolate the exact contribution of each technique (adapters, TAPT, translation, ensemble voting).
- The use of translation for English data introduces potential bias and may not be feasible for all target languages.
- The paper does not discuss the computational cost of the ensemble approach or its scalability to larger datasets.

## Confidence
- **High Confidence**: The ensemble approach combining monolingual and multilingual models achieves strong performance across subtasks, particularly for framing detection. The improvement from including non-labeled paragraphs in subtask 3 is well-supported by quantitative results.
- **Medium Confidence**: The superiority of monolingual models with translation for English in subtask 2 is plausible but not conclusively proven, as the comparison is indirect and relies on relative rankings. The effectiveness of adapters and TAPT for multilingual models is supported but lacks detailed ablation.
- **Low Confidence**: The generalizability of these techniques to other tasks or languages beyond the SemEval dataset is uncertain, as the paper does not test on external data or provide domain transfer results.

## Next Checks
1. **Ablation study**: Remove adapters, TAPT, and translation one at a time to measure their individual contribution to the ensemble's performance, particularly for English subtask 2.
2. **Zero-shot evaluation**: Test the best-performing multilingual model on an unseen language or domain to assess cross-lingual generalization beyond the SemEval test sets.
3. **Computational analysis**: Measure the training and inference time for the ensemble versus individual models to quantify the cost-benefit tradeoff.
