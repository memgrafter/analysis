# Farspredict: A benchmark dataset for link prediction

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2303.14647
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.14647
- Authors: Not specified in source
- Reference count: 12
- Key outcome: Farspredict dataset improves link prediction performance through standardization, outperforming Freebase in many KGE models despite being sparser

## Executive Summary
This paper addresses the lack of a proper Persian knowledge graph for link prediction by developing Farspredict from Farsbase. The authors identify shortcomings in Farsbase for link prediction tasks and implement a three-step standardization process: content modification, graph structural modification, and content modification in the knowledge graph. After standardization, Farspredict demonstrates superior performance to Freebase across multiple knowledge graph embedding models, with significant improvements in mean rank and Top@10 metrics. The study emphasizes that graph connectivity and uniform distribution of triples are critical factors for effective link prediction.

## Method Summary
The researchers developed Farspredict through a three-step standardization process applied to the Farsbase knowledge graph. First, they performed content modification by removing images, URLs, and non-Persian entities/relations. Second, they conducted graph structural modification by eliminating small subgraphs, single nodes, and relations with fewer than 100 triples. Third, they removed entities with fewer than five connections to reduce chain formations. The standardized Farspredict dataset was then used to implement various knowledge graph embedding models including TransE, TransH, TransR, TransD, DistMult, ComplEx, RESCAL, and deep learning models using the OpenKE framework. Performance was evaluated using Mean Rank and Hits@10 metrics and compared against Freebase benchmarks.

## Key Results
- Farspredict outperforms Freebase in many KGE models despite being sparser
- Mean rank and Top@10 metrics improved significantly after standardization
- Graph connectivity and uniform triple distribution are critical for effective link prediction
- TransE model showed approximately 1311 difference in Raw MRR after removing relations with fewer than 100 triples and entities with fewer than 5 connections

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: Farspredict's three-step standardization improves link prediction performance
- Mechanism: Standardization removes noise (images, URLs, non-Persian content), reduces graph sparsity (removes small subgraphs, single nodes, low-degree entities), and regularizes triple distribution across relations
- Core assumption: Link prediction quality depends on graph connectivity, uniform triple distribution, and removal of sparse/low-quality triples
- Evidence anchors: [abstract] "mean rank and Top@10 metrics improving significantly after standardization"; [section] "graph connectivity as an essential factor statistically significantly reduces the mean rank of KGE models"
- Break condition: If standardization removes too much content, reducing graph size below KGE training threshold

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: Removing entities with fewer than five connections reduces chain formation and improves accuracy
- Mechanism: Low-degree entities contribute little to connectivity and often form chains that hinder inference; removing them improves structural quality and mean rank
- Core assumption: Very low-degree entities are often noise or isolated fragments that don't contribute to meaningful link prediction
- Evidence anchors: [section] "removed entities with fewer than five degrees...to reduce graph chains"; [section] "relations with a few triples are private ones used in a particular situation"
- Break condition: If legitimate but sparsely connected entities are removed, critical knowledge may be lost

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: Farspredict outperforms Freebase despite being sparser due to better graph structure
- Mechanism: Better connectivity and more uniform triple distribution lead to superior KGE performance metrics, even with fewer entities and triples
- Core assumption: Graph quality matters more than sheer size for KGE link prediction
- Evidence anchors: [abstract] "Implementation results in the KGE models on Farspredict outperforming Freebase in many cases"; [section] "Raw MRR in the TransE model differed by about 1311"
- Break condition: If graph becomes too small or loses too much diversity, structural advantages may be outweighed by lack of data

## Limitations
- Proprietary nature of Farsbase dataset prevents independent verification of standardization process
- Performance comparison with Freebase may be confounded by language, domain, and size differences
- Limited ablation studies showing which specific standardization steps contribute most to performance gains
- Does not explore hyperparameter tuning or compare against other non-English knowledge graphs

## Confidence

- **High confidence**: Farspredict improves link prediction metrics compared to raw Farsbase data
- **Medium confidence**: Three-step standardization process is effective for link prediction
- **Low confidence**: Farspredict outperforms Freebase due to better graph structure

## Next Checks
1. **Ablation study validation**: Independently verify which standardization steps contribute most to performance improvements by testing each step individually
2. **Dataset size sensitivity analysis**: Evaluate how Farspredict's performance scales with different dataset sizes by systematically removing triples and measuring impact on link prediction metrics
3. **Cross-lingual comparison**: Test Farspredict against other non-English knowledge graphs (e.g., Japanese, Chinese) using same KGE models to determine if improvements are specific to Persian or generalizable to low-resource languages
