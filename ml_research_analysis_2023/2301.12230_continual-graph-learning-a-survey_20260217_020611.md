# Continual Graph Learning: A Survey

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2301.12230
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2301.12230
- Reference count: 26
- Primary result: Comprehensive survey of Continual Graph Learning (CGL) introducing two novel challenges and categorizing recent approaches

## Executive Summary
This survey paper provides a systematic overview of Continual Graph Learning (CGL), an emerging field addressing catastrophic forgetting when learning from evolving graph-structured data. The authors identify two novel challenges unique to CGL: sample-level dependency (connections between nodes within a graph) and graph-level dependency (connections between previous and new subgraphs). They categorize CGL approaches into three families - regularization-based, replay-based, and architecture-based methods - and analyze their strategies for handling these unique challenges. The survey highlights that existing CGL methods are heavily dependent on Graph Neural Networks (GNNs) and suggests several promising directions for future research.

## Method Summary
The paper surveys CGL approaches by first introducing the unique challenges posed by graph-structured data in continual learning settings. It then systematically reviews and categorizes recent state-of-the-art approaches according to the conventional CL taxonomy (regularization-based, replay-based, and architecture-based), analyzing how each family adapts to handle graph-specific dependencies. The survey discusses main concerns within each family and offers potential solutions. Through this structured analysis, the paper provides a comprehensive framework for understanding current CGL methods and their limitations.

## Key Results
- CGL introduces two novel challenges: sample-level dependency (intra-graph edges) and graph-level dependency (inter-graph links between old and new subgraphs)
- CGL methods follow conventional CL taxonomy but require graph-specific adaptations to preserve topological information
- Current CGL approaches are highly dependent on GNNs, limiting their applicability to other graph learning models

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
Graph-structured data brings two new challenges in CGL: sample-level dependency and task(graph)-level dependency. The graph topology creates conditional dependencies that violate the i.i.d assumption, requiring models to preserve both feature information and structural/topological information across incremental updates. This mechanism assumes that graph-structured data inherently contain topological relationships that carry task-relevant information beyond individual node features.

### Mechanism 2
CGL methods follow the same taxonomy as conventional CL (regularization-based, replay-based, architecture-based) but must adapt strategies to handle graph-specific dependencies. Regularization-based methods preserve sample-level dependency by encoding topological importance; replay-based methods select representative nodes while accounting for both dependencies; architecture-based methods isolate parameters for dissimilar tasks while sharing for similar ones. This assumes the three CL families can be effectively extended to graph settings by incorporating graph topology into their core mechanisms.

### Mechanism 3
Graph-level dependency creates new pattern emergence and perturbation effects that require specialized sampling and rehearsal strategies in replay-based CGL methods. New subgraphs perturb existing patterns on old graphs; methods must identify and rehearse both representative nodes and nodes susceptible to perturbation to accommodate novel patterns without forgetting. This assumes graph evolution causes localized perturbations that can be detected and managed through targeted sampling strategies.

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map: Graph data → GNN processing → Memory selection strategy (replay-based) / Topological importance encoding (regularization-based) / Architecture management (architecture-based) → Parameter update → Knowledge preservation
- Critical path: For replay-based CGL: Graph data → GNN processing → Memory selection strategy → Experience replay → Parameter update → Knowledge preservation. For regularization-based CGL: Graph data → GNN processing → Topological importance encoding → Regularization loss → Parameter update → Knowledge preservation.
- Design tradeoffs: Memory-based methods trade storage space for knowledge preservation accuracy; regularization methods trade computational overhead for avoiding memory management; architecture methods trade model complexity for task isolation.
- Failure signatures: Catastrophic forgetting (performance degradation on previous tasks), memory overfitting (poor generalization from limited samples), architectural bloat (excessive parameter growth), and topological information loss (degraded structural representation).

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- Question: How can CGL methods effectively handle graph heterogeneity in complex heterogeneous graphs with intricate meta-paths?
- Basis in paper: The paper identifies "Graph Heterogeneity" as an open issue, noting that existing CGL works on heterogeneous graphs "hardly explore interactions between nodes from different types" and that "studies of CGL on complex heterogeneous graphs with intricate meta-paths are lacking."
- Why unresolved: Current CGL methods focus on homogeneous graphs or simple heterogeneous graphs (knowledge graphs, bipartite graphs). The complexity of handling multiple node/edge types and their interactions in dynamic settings remains unexplored.
- What evidence would resolve it: Development and evaluation of CGL methods specifically designed for heterogeneous graphs with complex meta-path structures, demonstrating effective handling of node/edge type interactions and dynamic evolution.

### Open Question 2
- Question: How can CGL methods be made compatible with various GL models beyond GNNs, such as DeepWalk or other graph embedding techniques?
- Basis in paper: The paper states that "CGL methods are highly dependent on GNNs" and identifies "Compatibility of GL Models and beyond" as an open issue, noting that CGL strategies should be evaluated for compatibility with various GL models.
- Why unresolved: Current CGL approaches are tightly coupled with GNN architectures, limiting their applicability to other graph learning methods that may have different structural learning mechanisms.
- What evidence would resolve it: Implementation and evaluation of CGL strategies on non-GNN graph learning models, demonstrating effective catastrophic forgetting mitigation while preserving the unique characteristics of each model.

### Open Question 3
- Question: What strategies can be developed to effectively preserve and utilize inter-modal relationships in multi-modal graph data within CGL frameworks?
- Basis in paper: The paper identifies "Graph Multi-modality" as an open issue, noting that current approaches "merely consider the inner-modal interaction, while inter-modal relation also plays a pivotal role" and that "CGL approaches for preserving inter-modal interplay on multi-modal graphs deserve future research."
- Why unresolved: Current multi-modal CGL approaches treat different modalities independently before fusion, neglecting the important cross-modal interactions that could enhance learning and prevent forgetting.
- What evidence would resolve it: Development and evaluation of CGL methods that explicitly model and preserve inter-modal relationships during incremental learning, demonstrating improved performance compared to modality-independent approaches.

## Limitations

- Limited empirical validation of the two novel challenges (sample-level and graph-level dependencies) and their impact on learning outcomes
- Assumes graph-specific adaptations of conventional CL approaches are sufficient without exploring fundamentally different approaches
- Effectiveness of proposed solutions for addressing graph-level dependency through perturbation detection and targeted sampling remains largely theoretical

## Confidence

- High confidence: The categorization of CGL approaches into regularization-based, replay-based, and architecture-based families is well-established in the literature and follows conventional CL taxonomy
- Medium confidence: The identification of sample-level and graph-level dependencies as novel challenges is theoretically sound but lacks empirical validation in the paper itself
- Low confidence: The effectiveness of proposed solutions for addressing graph-level dependency through perturbation detection and targeted sampling remains largely theoretical without extensive experimental support
