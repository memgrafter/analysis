# ChatGPT may Pass the Bar Exam soon, but has a Long Way to Go for the LexGLUE benchmark

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2304.12202
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2304.12202
- Authors: 
- Reference count: 8
- Key outcome: ChatGPT achieves 49.0% average micro-F1 on LexGLUE tasks, outperforming random baselines but falling short of fine-tuned models by ~30%.

## Executive Summary
This study evaluates OpenAI's ChatGPT (gpt-3.5-turbo) on the LexGLUE benchmark for legal text classification tasks using zero-shot and few-shot instruction-following prompts. The model achieves an average micro-F1 score of 49.0% across tasks, significantly exceeding random guess baselines but falling approximately 30% short of fine-tuned LegalBERT models. Performance varies considerably across datasets, with exceptional results on ECtHR B (62.8%) and LEDGAR (70.1%), while struggling with datasets having large label sets like EURLEX and LEDGAR in few-shot settings.

## Method Summary
The study evaluates ChatGPT on LexGLUE using zero-shot instruction-following prompts with no training examples. Researchers sampled 1,000 test examples from each dataset, converted them to templated instruction-following format, and sent them through OpenAI's ChatCompletion API with temperature=0 for deterministic outputs. Model responses were matched to labels using exact matching first, then cosine similarity with SentenceBERT embeddings for similar responses. Micro-F1 and macro-F1 scores were calculated and compared to random/biased baselines and fine-tuned LegalBERT models.

## Key Results
- ChatGPT achieves 49.0% average micro-F1 across LexGLUE tasks, significantly exceeding random guess baselines
- Model performs exceptionally well on ECtHR B (62.8%) and LEDGAR (70.1%) datasets
- Few-shot prompting improves performance when label sets are small (e.g., UNFAIR-ToS improves from 39.8% to 55.2%)
- Performance lags behind fine-tuned models by approximately 30% absolute F1 score

## Limitations

- Evaluation uses only 1,000 sampled examples per dataset rather than full test sets, potentially not representing the full benchmark difficulty
- Performance on datasets with large label sets shows bias toward limited demonstration examples in few-shot settings
- Results lag behind fine-tuned models by approximately 30% absolute F1 score, indicating substantial room for improvement before production deployment

## Confidence

- **High Confidence**: ChatGPT's zero-shot performance significantly exceeds random guessing baselines across all LexGLUE tasks
- **Medium Confidence**: ChatGPT performs exceptionally well on specific datasets (ECtHR B and LEDGAR)
- **Medium Confidence**: Few-shot prompting improves performance when label sets are small
