# Do Embodied Agents Dream of Pixelated Sheep: Embodied Decision Making using Language Guided World Modelling

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2301.12050
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2301.12050
- Reference count: 22
- Primary result: DECKARD achieves an order of magnitude improvement in sample efficiency for Minecraft item crafting by using LLM guidance for exploration

## Executive Summary
This paper introduces DECKARD, a method that leverages large language models to guide reinforcement learning exploration in complex environments. The key insight is to use LLMs to generate an Abstract World Model (AWM) representing subgoal dependencies, then verify and correct this model through agent-environment interaction. DECKARD alternates between a Dream phase where it samples subgoals from the AWM and a Wake phase where it executes modular policies to achieve those subgoals. Evaluated on Minecraft item crafting, DECKARD significantly outperforms baseline methods in sample efficiency while remaining robust to errors in the LLM's output.

## Method Summary
DECKARD operates in two phases: Dream and Wake. In the Dream phase, an LLM (Codex) generates an Abstract World Model (AWM) - a directed acyclic graph representing subgoal dependencies for Minecraft item crafting. The agent then samples from the frontier of verified nodes in this AWM to select subgoals for exploration. In the Wake phase, the agent executes modular policies (each a lightweight adapter finetuned from VPT) to achieve the selected subgoal, verifying the AWM through environment interaction and updating it with correct dependencies. This approach combines the exploration guidance of language models with the robustness of interactive verification.

## Key Results
- DECKARD achieves an order of magnitude improvement in sample efficiency compared to baselines on Minecraft crafting tasks
- The method remains robust to significant errors in the LLM's hypothesized AWM, correcting inaccuracies through interaction
- Modular policies trained for individual subgoals can be composed to achieve complex tasks effectively

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
LLM guidance allows DECKARD to prioritize exploration toward useful subgoals, avoiding dead ends in the AWM. During the Dream phase, DECKARD samples only from the frontier of verified nodes and their predicted children on the path to the target, pruning the AWM and focusing exploration. This mechanism assumes LLM predictions are sufficiently accurate to guide exploration better than random discovery.

### Mechanism 2
Modular policies for subgoals enable efficient reuse and faster training than monolithic RL. Each subgoal has a lightweight adapter module finetuned from VPT, allowing independent training and composition. This assumes subgoals are sufficiently independent to be learned in isolation and then composed without interference.

### Mechanism 3
Verification of the LLM-predicted AWM through interaction grounds abstract knowledge in environment dynamics. As the agent explores, it updates the AWM with verified edges and nodes, correcting LLM errors. This assumes environment interactions provide reliable feedback to distinguish correct from incorrect LLM predictions.

## Foundational Learning

- Concept: Directed Acyclic Graphs (DAGs) for representing subgoal dependencies
  - Why needed here: The AWM is a DAG where nodes are subgoals and edges represent dependencies, ensuring no circular dependencies exist
  - Quick check question: Why must the AWM be acyclic? What would happen if cycles were allowed?

- Concept: Modular reinforcement learning and policy composition
  - Why needed here: DECKARD trains separate policies for each subgoal and composes them to achieve complex tasks, reducing sample complexity
  - Quick check question: How does modular RL differ from training a single policy for the entire task? What are the trade-offs?

- Concept: In-context learning and few-shot prompting for knowledge extraction
  - Why needed here: The LLM is prompted to generate structured Minecraft recipes, which are parsed into the AWM without fine-tuning the model
  - Quick check question: What is the difference between few-shot prompting and fine-tuning? Why might few-shot be preferable here?

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map: LLM (Codex) -> generates hypothesized AWM (DAG of Minecraft recipes) -> DECKARD agent -> alternates Dream (sample subgoal) and Wake (execute subgoal, verify/update AWM) phases -> Modular policies (one lightweight adapter per subgoal, finetuned from VPT) -> Environment (Minedojo) -> provides observations, actions, and feedback for verification

- Critical path: 1. LLM generates AWM 2. DECKARD samples frontier node from AWM 3. Executes modular policy for that subgoal 4. Updates AWM with verified nodes/edges 5. Repeats until target item is crafted

- Design tradeoffs: Using LLM vs. learning AWM from scratch (faster exploration but potential for errors); Modular vs. monolithic policies (more efficient training but requires correct subgoal decomposition); Adapter size vs. capacity (small adapters are memory-efficient but may limit expressiveness)

- Failure signatures: Agent stuck sampling from same dead-end nodes (AWM pruning too aggressive or LLM too inaccurate); Modular policies failing to compose (subgoal dependencies not captured correctly); Slow verification (environment feedback too sparse or noisy)

- First 3 experiments: 1. Run DECKARD with no LLM guidance to establish baseline exploration rate 2. Test AWM pruning by comparing exploration with and without frontier sampling 3. Introduce artificial errors in LLM AWM and measure robustness of DECKARD

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
How does DECKARD's sample efficiency scale with task complexity and tree depth in Minecraft? The paper shows DECKARD improves sample efficiency by an order of magnitude compared to baselines on various crafting tasks, but doesn't systematically explore scaling with task complexity.

### Open Question 2
What is the minimum viable LLM accuracy for DECKARD to maintain its performance advantage over baselines? The paper shows DECKARD remains more efficient than the no-LLM baseline even with significant errors in the hypothesized AWM, but doesn't determine the threshold where performance degrades.

### Open Question 3
How does DECKARD's performance generalize to other complex environments beyond Minecraft? The paper demonstrates DECKARD on Minecraft crafting tasks but doesn't evaluate its performance on other complex environments with different characteristics.

## Limitations

- The paper doesn't provide independent validation of LLM prediction accuracy for the AWM
- Limited ablation studies make it difficult to isolate the contribution of language guidance versus the underlying RL architecture
- The modular approach's effectiveness depends on correct subgoal decomposition, which isn't thoroughly analyzed

## Confidence

**High confidence**: Claims about sample efficiency improvements over baselines are well-supported by experimental results showing order-of-magnitude gains in environment steps.

**Medium confidence**: Claims about modular policy composition being more efficient than monolithic RL are supported by results but lack detailed analysis of composition quality and failure modes.

**Low confidence**: The paper's assertion that LLM guidance is the primary driver of exploration efficiency lacks direct comparison to other exploration methods that don't use language models.

## Next Checks

1. **Independent LLM accuracy measurement**: Evaluate the accuracy of the LLM-generated Abstract World Model separately from the RL performance, measuring precision and recall of predicted subgoal dependencies.

2. **Subgoal dependency analysis**: Systematically test modular policy composition by creating tasks with varying degrees of subgoal interdependence, measuring how composition success varies with dependency complexity.

3. **Exploration baseline comparison**: Implement a non-LLM exploration baseline (e.g., count-based exploration or curiosity-driven exploration) to isolate the contribution of language guidance to DECKARD's sample efficiency gains.
