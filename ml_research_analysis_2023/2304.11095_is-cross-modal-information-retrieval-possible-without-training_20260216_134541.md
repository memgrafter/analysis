# Is Cross-modal Information Retrieval Possible without Training?

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2304.11095
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2304.11095
- Reference count: 15
- Key outcome: 77% accuracy on recall@10 for cross-modal retrieval without training deep neural networks

## Executive Summary
This paper investigates whether cross-modal information retrieval can be achieved without training deep neural networks. The authors propose using simple linear mappings to align pretrained embeddings from off-the-shelf models like BERT and ViT. By leveraging least squares and SVD-based Procrustes solutions, they demonstrate that semantic alignment across modalities can be achieved through linear algebra alone. The approach achieves competitive performance compared to more complex, trained models, suggesting that pretrained embeddings contain sufficient semantic structure for cross-modal tasks.

## Method Summary
The method uses pretrained unimodal encoders (BERT/RoBERTa for text, ViT for images) to generate embeddings, which are then aligned using linear mappings computed via least squares or SVD (Procrustes problem). The aligned embeddings enable direct similarity comparison for text-to-image and image-to-text retrieval. The approach is enhanced with optional contrastive learning to bias the encoders and a gMLP layer to capture nonlinear relationships. Experiments are conducted on the Flickr30K dataset using recall@k metrics.

## Key Results
- Achieved 77% accuracy on recall@10 without training deep neural networks
- Linear mappings via least squares and SVD successfully align text and image embeddings
- Contrastive learning and gMLP layers further improve retrieval performance
- Performance comparable to models requiring costly neural network training and fine-tuning

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
Cross-modal mappings can be computed without training by aligning pretrained embeddings using linear algebra. Pre-trained unimodal encoders produce embeddings in high-dimensional spaces. A linear mapping (via least squares or SVD) aligns these spaces, enabling direct similarity comparison across modalities. Core assumption: The embedding spaces from pretrained models contain sufficient semantic alignment that a linear transformation can effectively map one to the other. Break condition: If the pretrained embeddings are too modality-specific or lack semantic overlap, linear alignment will fail to capture necessary relationships.

### Mechanism 2
Contrastive learning can improve cross-modal retrieval by biasing pretrained encoders to better capture fine-grained semantic relationships. By applying contrastive learning on top of pretrained embeddings, the encoders are encouraged to pull together semantically equivalent pairs and push apart non-equivalent pairs, enhancing the quality of the embedding space for retrieval. Core assumption: Contrastive learning on paired data can effectively refine unimodal embeddings to better serve cross-modal tasks without full retraining. Break condition: If the paired data is insufficient or noisy, contrastive learning may not effectively bias the encoders.

### Mechanism 3
Adding a lightweight nonlinear layer (gMLP) after the bimodal encoders can further improve cross-modal retrieval performance. The gMLP layer learns to refine the aligned embeddings from the linear mapping, capturing nonlinear relationships that the simple linear approach cannot. Core assumption: A shallow nonlinear layer can effectively enhance the semantic alignment without requiring extensive training or complex architectures. Break condition: If the gMLP layer is too simple, it may not capture the necessary complexity; if too complex, it risks overfitting with limited data.

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map: Text/Image → Encoder → Alignment → (Optional: gMLP) → Similarity Computation
- Critical path: Text/Image → Encoder → Alignment → (Optional: gMLP) → Similarity Computation
- Design tradeoffs:
  - Simplicity vs. performance: Linear mappings are simple but may miss nonlinear relationships; adding gMLP improves performance but adds complexity.
  - Training vs. no training: Using only pretrained models avoids training but may limit performance; adding contrastive learning improves results but requires paired data.
- Failure signatures:
  - Poor retrieval accuracy: May indicate misalignment in embedding spaces or insufficient semantic overlap.
  - Overfitting with gMLP: Can occur if the dataset is too small relative to the complexity of the gMLP layer.
  - Ineffective contrastive learning: Could result from poor quality or insufficient paired data.
- First 3 experiments:
  1. Implement the linear mapping using least squares on a small paired dataset and evaluate recall@10.
  2. Apply contrastive learning to enhance the encoders and measure improvement in retrieval performance.
  3. Add a gMLP layer after alignment and compare results with and without the gMLP to assess its impact.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- Question: What is the impact of scaling the Procrustes solution for larger datasets or higher-dimensional embeddings?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper uses SVD-based Procrustes solution but does not explore its scalability.
- Why unresolved: Computational complexity of SVD on large matrices is not discussed or benchmarked.
- What evidence would resolve it: Runtime and memory usage analysis of Procrustes solution across varying dataset sizes and embedding dimensions.

### Open Question 2
- Question: How do different negative sampling strategies affect the contrastive learning performance in cross-modal retrieval?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper mentions contrastive learning but only uses in-batch negatives without comparing alternatives.
- Why unresolved: No experiments with hard negatives, semi-hard negatives, or other sampling methods.
- What evidence would resolve it: Comparative results using different negative sampling strategies while keeping other factors constant.

### Open Question 3
- Question: Can the Procrustes-based approach be extended to more than two modalities simultaneously?
- Basis in paper: [inferred] The paper focuses on text-image alignment but does not explore multi-modal extensions.
- Why unresolved: No theoretical framework or experiments for handling three or more modalities.
- What evidence would resolve it: A framework for joint alignment of text, image, and additional modalities (e.g., audio) with experimental validation.

## Limitations
- Limited ablation studies on when linear mappings fail versus when they succeed
- No analysis of computational efficiency compared to trained models
- The effectiveness of gMLP and contrastive learning improvements may depend heavily on dataset characteristics not fully explored

## Confidence
- **High confidence**: The core claim that linear mappings can perform cross-modal retrieval without training is well-supported by the methodology and experimental results.
- **Medium confidence**: The improvements from contrastive learning and gMLP are demonstrated but could benefit from more extensive ablation studies.
- **Low confidence**: The generalizability of these findings to other datasets and modalities remains unexplored.

## Next Checks
