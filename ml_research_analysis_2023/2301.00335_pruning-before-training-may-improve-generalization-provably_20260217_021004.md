# Pruning Before Training May Improve Generalization, Provably

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2301.00335
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2301.00335
- Reference count: 40
- Primary result: Theoretical analysis showing pruning fraction affects generalization bounds in two-layer networks

## Executive Summary
This paper provides the first theoretical analysis of how pruning fraction affects generalization in neural networks. The authors consider a classification task for overparameterized two-layer networks pruned at initialization and trained by gradient descent. They show that for mild pruning (below a threshold), the network can drive training loss to zero and achieve good generalization, with better generalization bounds for larger pruning fractions. This is because mild pruning preserves signal strength while reducing noise variance. In contrast, for over-pruning (above the threshold), the network can still drive training loss to zero by memorizing noise, but generalization performance is no better than random guessing.

## Method Summary
The authors analyze a classification task using overparameterized two-layer neural networks with quadratic activation functions. They study networks pruned at initialization and trained using gradient descent. The theoretical framework considers how different pruning fractions affect the network's ability to learn meaningful features versus memorizing noise. The analysis focuses on the relationship between pruning, signal preservation, and noise reduction in the feature learning process, leading to different generalization behaviors depending on whether the pruning is mild or excessive.

## Key Results
- Mild pruning (below threshold) preserves signal strength while reducing noise variance, leading to improved generalization bounds
- Over-pruning (above threshold) enables zero training loss through noise memorization but yields generalization no better than random guessing
- The optimal pruning fraction balances signal preservation against noise reduction in the feature learning process

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The paper demonstrates that pruning affects generalization through its impact on the feature learning process. When pruning is mild, it reduces the number of parameters that could potentially learn noise while preserving the important signal components. This creates a more regularized learning environment where the network focuses on meaningful features. However, when pruning is excessive, the remaining neurons must compensate by memorizing training data noise, leading to poor generalization despite achieving zero training loss.

## Limitations
- Analysis restricted to two-layer networks with quadratic activations
- Assumes independent neuron pruning rather than correlated feature pruning
- Theoretical results are primarily asymptotic, focusing on large network widths
- Doesn't account for modern iterative pruning and retraining methods

## Confidence

- Medium confidence in the interpretation of how pruning affects feature learning
