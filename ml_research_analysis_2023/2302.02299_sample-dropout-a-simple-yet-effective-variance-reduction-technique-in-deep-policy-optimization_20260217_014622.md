# Sample Dropout: A Simple yet Effective Variance Reduction Technique in Deep Policy Optimization

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2302.02299
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2302.02299
- Reference count: 40
- Primary result: Sample dropout technique reduces variance in importance sampling estimates by dropping samples with high ratio deviations, improving performance of TRPO, PPO, and ESPO across MuJoCo, DMControl, and Atari benchmarks.

## Executive Summary
This paper introduces sample dropout, a simple yet effective variance reduction technique for deep policy optimization algorithms. The method addresses the high variance problem in importance sampling estimates by selectively dropping samples with large policy ratio deviations during training. When applied to TRPO, PPO, and ESPO algorithms, sample dropout consistently improves sample efficiency and final performance across a wide range of continuous control tasks (MuJoCo, DMControl) and discrete action tasks (Atari games). The technique works by bounding the importance ratios through sample disposal, which reduces the variance of surrogate objective estimates while maintaining algorithmic simplicity.

## Method Summary
The sample dropout technique involves filtering training samples during policy optimization based on the deviation of importance ratios from unity. For each sample, if the absolute difference between the new policy's action probability and the old policy's action probability exceeds a threshold δ, that sample is dropped from the training batch. The method can be implemented using either ratio-based dropout (dropping samples where |π_new(a|s)/π_old(a|s) - 1| > δ) or KL-based dropout (dropping samples where |KL(π_new||π_old) - 1| > δ). This selective sample disposal effectively bounds the importance ratios and reduces the variance of the policy gradient estimates while introducing minimal bias.

## Key Results
- Sample dropout consistently improves sample efficiency and final performance across TRPO, PPO, and ESPO algorithms
- The technique demonstrates effectiveness on both continuous control tasks (MuJoCo, DMControl) and discrete action environments (Atari games)
- Ratio ranges during training are more concentrated when using sample dropout compared to baseline algorithms
- The method shows robustness to threshold selection, though optimal values may vary by environment

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: High variance in importance sampling estimates grows quadratically with policy ratios
- Mechanism: The variance bound shows σπ ≤ ξ² · Ea∼π[(˜π(a|s)/π(a|s))²], meaning as policy ratios increase during optimization, the estimation variance grows quadratically
- Core assumption: The surrogate objective is being maximized through importance sampling with off-policy data
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "we show in a principled way that the variance of importance sampling estimate grows quadratically with importance ratios"
  - [section] "Theorem 2. Let µ ≜ Ea∼π[ ˜π(a|s) / π(a|s)Aπ(s,a )], the variance of importance sampling estimate is bounded as follows: σπ ≤ ξ² · Ea∼π[(˜π(a|s) / π(a|s))²] − [Ea∼π[ ˜π(a|s) / π(a|s)Aπ(s,a )]]²"

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: Sample dropout reduces variance by bounding policy ratios through selective sample disposal
- Mechanism: Samples with ratio deviation exceeding threshold δ are dropped using indicator 1{|˜π(a|s)/π(a|s) - 1| < δ}, keeping ratios within controlled bounds
- Core assumption: The variance reduction benefit outweighs the bias introduced by discarding samples
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "We then propose a technique called sample dropout to bound the estimation variance by dropping out samples when their ratio deviation is too high"
  - [section] "We thus propose a strategy by disposing samples whose ratio deviation exceeds the threshold δ"

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: Sample dropout improves trust region enforcement and proximity constraints in policy optimization
- Mechanism: By bounding ratios through dropout, the technique naturally enforces the constraints that trust-region and proximal methods aim to maintain
- Core assumption: Bounded ratios lead to more stable policy updates and prevent divergence
- Evidence anchors:
  - [section] "Retrospecting the theoretical analysis in Section 4, the aim of sample dropout technique is to keep the ratio deviation small and bound the ratios"
  - [section] "we show the ratio range for all algorithms during training... we find that the ratio ranges of algorithms with sample dropout are more concentrated than those without"

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map: Environment interaction -> Advantage estimation (GAE) -> Sample dropout filtering -> Policy/value network update -> Training loop coordination

- Critical path:
  1. Collect trajectory data using current policy
  2. Compute advantages using GAE
  3. Apply sample dropout filtering based on ratio/KL thresholds
  4. Update policy and value networks using filtered data
  5. Repeat

- Design tradeoffs:
  - Threshold selection: Higher thresholds allow more samples but less variance reduction; lower thresholds provide more variance reduction but risk excessive sample loss
  - Ratio-based vs KL-based dropout: Ratio-based works for PPO/ESPO, KL-based aligns with TRPO constraints
  - Computational overhead: Additional filtering step vs benefit of improved training stability

- Failure signatures:
  - Training instability or divergence when thresholds are too high
  - Slow learning or poor performance when thresholds are too low
  - Inconsistent performance across different environments suggesting threshold sensitivity

- First 3 experiments:
  1. Compare training curves of PPO vs SD-PPO on a simple MuJoCo task (e.g., HalfCheetah) to verify performance improvement
  2. Measure variance of surrogate objective estimates with and without sample dropout during training
  3. Test sensitivity to different threshold values (δ) to find optimal range for a given task

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- Question: What is the optimal threshold value for sample dropout that balances variance reduction and bias introduction across different environments and algorithms?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper states that sample dropout is not very sensitive to the threshold hyperparameter but mentions that if the threshold is too small, the policy update could become too conservative and suffer from a performance drop.
- Why unresolved: The paper only provides empirical results for specific threshold values and does not derive a theoretical optimal threshold that generalizes across environments and algorithms.
- What evidence would resolve it: A systematic study varying the threshold value across a wider range of environments and algorithms, combined with theoretical analysis of the bias-variance tradeoff, would help identify the optimal threshold.

### Open Question 2
- Question: How does sample dropout perform when applied to more complex and diverse environments beyond the ones tested in the paper?
- Basis in paper: [inferred] The paper demonstrates the effectiveness of sample dropout in continuous control tasks (MuJoCo, DMControl) and discrete-action environments (Atari games), but does not explore more complex or diverse environments.
- Why unresolved: The paper's experimental results are limited to a specific set of environments, and it is unclear how well sample dropout generalizes to other types of environments or more complex tasks.
- What evidence would resolve it: Applying sample dropout to a wider range of environments, including more complex control tasks, multi-agent settings, or environments with sparse rewards, would provide evidence for its generalizability.

### Open Question 3
- Question: Can the sample dropout technique be extended to other policy optimization algorithms or combined with other variance reduction techniques?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper states that the sample dropout technique is simple to implement and can be easily combined with existing policy optimization algorithms, and mentions that it can be applied to other policy optimization algorithms in the future.
- Why unresolved: While the paper demonstrates the effectiveness of sample dropout when combined with TRPO, PPO, and ESPO, it does not explore its potential when combined with other policy optimization algorithms or other variance reduction techniques.
- What evidence would resolve it: Investigating the performance of sample dropout when combined with other policy optimization algorithms or variance reduction techniques, such as TD(lambda) or generalized advantage estimation (GAE), would provide insights into its broader applicability.

- The computational overhead and scalability implications for large-scale applications need further investigation
