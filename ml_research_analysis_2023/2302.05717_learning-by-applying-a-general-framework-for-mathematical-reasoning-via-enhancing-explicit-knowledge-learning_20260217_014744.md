# Learning by Applying: A General Framework for Mathematical Reasoning via Enhancing Explicit Knowledge Learning

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2302.05717
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2302.05717
- Reference count: 8
- Key outcome: LeAp improves answer accuracy of various backbone solvers while learning accurate knowledge and achieving more interpretable reasoning

## Executive Summary
This paper proposes a general Learning by Applying (LeAp) framework to enhance existing math word problem solvers by learning and applying explicit knowledge. LeAp uses a problem-knowledge-expression paradigm with a Knowledge Encoder to acquire word-word and word-operator relations from problem data, and a Knowledge Decoder with semantics-enhanced and reasoning-enhanced modules to apply this knowledge for better problem comprehension and symbol reasoning. Experiments on three datasets show that LeAp improves answer accuracy of various backbone solvers while learning accurate knowledge and achieving more interpretable reasoning. The proposed autonomous learning mechanism is theoretically analyzed and validated.

## Method Summary
LeAp introduces a problem-knowledge-expression paradigm where a Knowledge Encoder learns explicit word-word and word-operator relations from problem data, and a Knowledge Decoder applies this knowledge through semantics-enhanced and reasoning-enhanced modules to improve both problem comprehension and symbol reasoning. The framework is formalized as a VAE with Bernoulli distributions for knowledge variables, using Gumbel-Softmax reparameterization for discrete variables. The autonomous learning mechanism optimizes MWP solving performance, which implicitly maximizes the posterior probability P(Z|X,Y), whereas traditional approaches optimize isolated prediction P(Z|X).

## Key Results
- LeAp improves answer accuracy across three datasets (Math23K, MAWPS, SVAMP) and multiple backbone solvers
- The framework learns accurate word-word and word-operator relations with high precision@50
- LeAp achieves more interpretable reasoning through explicit knowledge graphs
- Theoretical analysis proves superiority of the autonomous learning mechanism

## Architecture Onboarding

- **Component map**: Problem sentences → Knowledge Encoder → Explicit knowledge graph → Knowledge Decoder (with enhanced modules) → Expressions → Answers

- **Critical path**: Problem sentences → Knowledge Encoder → Explicit knowledge graph → Knowledge Decoder (with enhanced modules) → Expressions → Answers

- **Design tradeoffs**:
  - Explicit vs. implicit knowledge: LeAp trades model complexity for interpretability
  - Knowledge granularity: Binary relations vs. multi-relational knowledge graphs
  - Backbone compatibility: General framework vs. solver-specific optimizations

- **Failure signatures**:
  - Poor knowledge quality: Low Precision@50 on learned relations
  - Ineffective application: No improvement over baseline solvers
  - Training instability: Difficulty optimizing ELBO with discrete variables

- **First 3 experiments**:
  1. **Knowledge quality evaluation**: Train LeAp on Math23K with Seq2Seq backbone, evaluate Precision@50 of learned word-word relations against HowNet/ConceptNet
  2. **Ablation study**: Remove semantics-enhanced or reasoning-enhanced modules, measure impact on answer accuracy across all three datasets
  3. **Knowledge prior analysis**: Vary α (0%, 20%, 40%, 60%) in prior P(Z), measure impact on learning effectiveness and final performance

## Limitations

- The empirical validation of the autonomous learning mechanism's superiority over direct link prediction approaches lacks direct experimental comparison
- The framework's generalizability to other mathematical reasoning domains beyond word problems is untested
- The claim that LeAp achieves more interpretable reasoning is supported by case studies but would benefit from systematic evaluation of knowledge quality and interpretability metrics
