# Progressive Translation: Improving Domain Robustness of Neural Machine Translation with Intermediate Sequences

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2305.09154
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.09154
- Reference count: 15
- Key outcome: Progressive Translation improves out-of-domain BLEU scores by 2.3-3.1 points through intermediate sequences and permutation multi-task learning

## Executive Summary
This paper addresses the challenge of domain robustness in neural machine translation by introducing intermediate sequences that explicitly model the transformation path from source to target language. The method generates word-for-word translations and aligned reordering sequences as supervision signals, then uses full-permutation multi-task learning to prevent spurious causal relationships. Results demonstrate significant improvements in out-of-domain translation quality, with BLEU score gains of 2.3-3.1 points over baseline models, while also reducing hallucination rates by 50-80%.

## Method Summary
The approach introduces two intermediate sequences during training: a lex sequence (word-for-word translation) and an ali sequence (word-for-word translation with aligned reordering). These sequences are prepended to the target in various permutations using full-permutation multi-task learning, which prevents the model from learning spurious causal relationships between intermediate and target sequences. During inference, Minimum Bayes Risk (MBR) decoding dynamically selects the best translation from all permutations. The method is evaluated on multiple domain transfer scenarios including IWSLT→OPUS and IWSLT→Allegra datasets.

## Key Results
- Progressive Translation achieves 2.3-3.1 BLEU point improvements on out-of-domain translation tasks
- The method reduces hallucination rates by 50-80% compared to baseline models
- PTfull with MBR decoding achieves the best overall performance, particularly in low-resource settings
- The approach shows consistent improvements across different domain transfer scenarios

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: Intermediate sequences provide domain-agnostic inductive bias that reduces spurious correlations.
- Mechanism: By introducing lex and ali sequences, the model learns a structured transformation path from source to target, limiting flexibility but preventing domain-specific overfitting.
- Core assumption: Domain-specific spurious correlations are harmful to generalization and can be reduced by constraining the transformation space.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "Such intermediate sequences introduce an inductive bias that reflects a domain-agnostic principle of translation, which reduces spurious correlations that are harmful to out-of-domain generalisation."
  - [section 3.1] "We expect that the introduced intermediate sequences would benefit the domain robustness of NMT. Because the proposed intermediate sequences serve as a supervision signal to provide the model with an explicit path for learning the transformational relations from source to target."
- Break condition: If intermediate sequences become too noisy or domain-specific, they may introduce harmful biases instead of reducing spurious correlations.

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: Full-permutation multi-task learning reduces spurious causal relationships between intermediate and target sequences.
- Mechanism: By training on all permutations of (lex, ali, tgt), the model learns that source is the only reliable information for any target-side sequence, reducing over-reliance on erroneous intermediate predictions during inference.
- Core assumption: NMT models are prone to exposure bias, where they rely too heavily on target history, and this problem is exacerbated under domain shift.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "we introduce a full-permutation multi-task learning to alleviate the spurious causal relations from intermediate sequences to the target, which results from exposure bias."
  - [section 3.2] "To introduce these intermediate sequences as intermediate supervision signals to the model, we prepend them to the output sequence in training. However, simply prepending these produced intermediate sequences to the target would potentially introduce spurious causality relationships from pre-sequence to post-sequence."
- Break condition: If permutation introduces too much noise or if the model cannot effectively learn disentangled causal relationships.

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: MBR decoding improves translation quality by selecting consensus translations from all permutations.
- Mechanism: MBR finds translations that maximize expected utility across the posterior distribution of all permutations, dynamically selecting the best candidate for each input.
- Core assumption: Different test sets prefer different generation orders, and dynamic selection can improve overall quality.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [section 3.3] "We suspect that the translation quality would be further improved if we could dynamically select the best candidate translations from all permutations. Inspired by (Eikema and Aziz, 2021), we use Minimum Bayes Risk (MBR) decoding to select a consensus translation from all permutations."
  - [section 4.3] "MBR ( 7⃝→ 8⃝) could further improve the OOD and ID performances by 0.4 and 0.6 BLEU respectively, and outperforms baseline OOD by 3.1 BLEU at the cost of 1.6 BLEU decrease in ID."
- Break condition: If utility function (BEER) doesn't correlate well with translation quality or if computational cost is prohibitive.

  - Why needed here: The permutation framework creates multiple tasks from the same data, teaching the model that source-to-target mappings are invariant to intermediate sequence order.
  - Quick check question: How does permutation multi-task learning differ from standard multi-task learning in terms of the causal relationships it creates?

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map: Word alignment generator → Intermediate sequence generator (lex/ali) → Permutation controller → Transformer model → MBR decoder
- Critical path: 1) Generate word alignments → 2) Create lex and ali sequences → 3) Apply permutation multi-task learning → 4) Train model → 5) Apply MBR decoding during inference
- Design tradeoffs: Intermediate sequences improve domain robustness but increase target length and computational cost; permutations reduce spurious causality but add training complexity
- Failure signatures: If BLEU improves on in-domain but not out-of-domain, intermediate sequences may be too domain-specific; if training becomes unstable, permutation framework may be introducing too much noise
- First 3 experiments:
  1. Implement lex-only augmentation (simplest form) and compare against baseline on in-domain and out-of-domain data
  2. Add ali sequence without permutation to observe if additional complexity helps or hurts
  3. Implement full permutation framework with MBR decoding to test complete proposed approach

## Limitations

- The experimental setup relies heavily on specific datasets (IWSLT, OPUS, Allegra) and domains (medical, religious), which may not generalize to other translation scenarios
- Manual hallucination evaluation is based on small sample (30 instances) and may not capture full distribution of hallucination types
- Computational overhead introduced by intermediate sequences and MBR decoding is not thoroughly analyzed, leaving questions about practical deployment costs

## Confidence

**High Confidence**: The core empirical finding that Progressive Translation improves out-of-domain BLEU scores by 2.3-3.1 points compared to baseline is well-supported by experimental results across multiple datasets and domains.

**Medium Confidence**: The claim that intermediate sequences reduce hallucinations has strong supporting evidence but relies on small manual evaluation sample that may not capture all failure modes.

**Low Confidence**: The mechanism explanation about spurious correlations and exposure bias, while plausible, lacks direct experimental validation and relies heavily on theoretical arguments rather than controlled ablation studies.
