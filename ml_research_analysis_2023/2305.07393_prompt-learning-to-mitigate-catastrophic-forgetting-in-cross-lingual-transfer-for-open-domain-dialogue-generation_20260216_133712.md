# Prompt Learning to Mitigate Catastrophic Forgetting in Cross-lingual Transfer for Open-domain Dialogue Generation

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2305.07393
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.07393
- **Reference count:** 40
- **Primary result:** Proposed prompt learning approach mitigates catastrophic forgetting in few-shot cross-lingual transfer for dialogue generation across 6 languages

## Executive Summary
This paper addresses catastrophic forgetting in few-shot cross-lingual transfer (FS-XLT) and multitask learning (MTL) for open-domain dialogue generation. Standard approaches suffer from models generating responses in the source language (English) or producing ungrammatical target language responses. The authors propose a prompt learning approach using Fixed-prompt LM Tuning with hand-crafted task-specific prompts to bridge the gap between pre-training and fine-tuning tasks, thereby preserving the multilinguality of mT5.

The method significantly improves dialogue generation quality across six non-English languages (Danish, German, Norwegian, Spanish, Italian, Portuguese) as measured by automatic metrics (BLEU, Distinct-N) and human evaluations. By casting dialogue generation into pre-training tasks using sentinel tokens and formatting prompts, the approach maintains multilingual capabilities during fine-tuning without introducing additional parameters.

## Method Summary
The authors propose a prompt learning approach that mitigates catastrophic forgetting by aligning fine-tuning tasks with mT5's pre-training objectives. They use hand-crafted prompts ("Context:" and "Response:") combined with sentinel tokens (<extra_id_0> and <extra_id_1>) to format dialogue context-response pairs, effectively casting the generation task into span corruption format. The approach employs Fixed-prompt LM Tuning, updating only mT5 parameters without introducing trainable prompt parameters. For multitask learning, they interleave target language few-shot examples with full auxiliary language data. The method is evaluated on the MDIA dataset using automatic metrics (SacreBLEU, Distinct-N) and human fluency assessments across six target languages.

## Key Results
- Prompt learning approach significantly outperforms standard FS-XLT and MTL baselines in all six target languages
- Automatic evaluation shows improved BLEU scores and Distinct-N metrics indicating better fluency and diversity
- Human evaluations confirm generated responses are more fluent and grammatically correct compared to baselines
- The method successfully mitigates catastrophic forgetting, with models generating appropriate target language responses instead of English

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: The prompt learning approach mitigates catastrophic forgetting by aligning fine-tuning with pre-training tasks, preserving multilinguality.
- Mechanism: Hand-crafted prompts cast dialogue generation into pre-training tasks (span-corruption) using sentinel tokens, maintaining the model's multilingual knowledge during fine-tuning.
- Core assumption: Pre-training tasks inherently preserve multilinguality, and bridging the gap between pre-training and fine-tuning maintains this property.
- Evidence anchors: Abstract states approach "preserves the multilinguality of multilingual pre-trained language model (mPLM) in FS-XLT and MTL by bridging the gap between pre-training and fine-tuning"; section describes casting dialogue generation into pre-training tasks to "maintain the multilinguality of mPLM during fine-tuning and mitigating catastrophic forgetting."
- Break condition: If hand-crafted prompts don't accurately represent pre-training tasks or model architecture doesn't support prompt-based fine-tuning.

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: Fixed-prompt LM Tuning preserves multilingual capabilities by updating only mPLM parameters without additional prompt parameters.
- Mechanism: Using fixed prompts and only updating mPLM parameters during fine-tuning leverages pre-existing multilingual knowledge without disrupting learned representations.
- Core assumption: Updating only mPLM parameters during fine-tuning preserves multilingual capabilities without interference from new parameters.
- Evidence anchors: Section describes using sentinel tokens with hand-crafted prompts to format context-response pairs; abstract states experimental results "demonstrate the effectiveness of our approach."
- Break condition: If model requires additional parameters for effective learning or fixed prompts aren't sufficiently expressive.

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: Interleaved data format in MTL helps mitigate catastrophic forgetting by providing regular exposure to multiple languages.
- Mechanism: Evenly interleaving auxiliary language data with few-shot target language examples ensures regular exposure to both languages during fine-tuning.
- Core assumption: Regular exposure to both auxiliary and target languages prevents forgetting while learning the target language.
- Evidence anchors: Section describes interleaving full training data of auxiliary language with few-shot target examples, fine-tuning on interleaved data containing both languages; abstract states experimental results demonstrate effectiveness.
- Break condition: If interleaving ratio isn't optimal leading to insufficient exposure to either language or model architecture doesn't support learning from interleaved data.

- Critical path: 1. Pre-train mT5 on multilingual data 2. Fine-tune on English dialogue data 3. Apply prompt learning with hand-crafted prompts 4. Evaluate on target languages using automatic and human metrics

- Design tradeoffs:
  - Fixed prompts vs. trainable prompts: Fixed prompts are simpler and avoid additional parameters but may be less expressive
  - Interleaving ratio in MTL: Higher target language exposure may improve performance but increase forgetting risk
  - Choice of pre-training tasks: Aligning fine-tuning tasks with pre-training preserves multilinguality but may limit learning new tasks

- Failure signatures:
  - Catastrophic forgetting: Model generates English responses or ungrammatical target language text
  - Overfitting to prompts: Performance degrades on out-of-distribution data or when prompts are modified
  - Imbalance in MTL: Model performs well on one language but poorly on the other due to insufficient exposure

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- Question: How does the performance of prompt learning approaches for cross-lingual dialogue generation vary across different mPLMs beyond mT5?
- Basis in paper: Authors state they experiment with mT5 only and mention it would be interesting to explore soft/trainable prompts for dialogue generation in future work.
- Why unresolved: Paper only evaluates prompt learning on mT5, leaving open how it generalizes to other mPLMs like mBART, mBERT, or other variants.
- What evidence would resolve it: Comparative experiments applying same prompt learning methodology to multiple mPLMs (mBART, mBERT, XLM-R, etc.) on same cross-lingual dialogue tasks would show relative performance differences and generalizability.

### Open Question 2
- Question: What is the optimal number of few-shot examples needed to achieve performance close to fully supervised multilingual training?
- Basis in paper: Authors use exactly 10 examples per target language and observe catastrophic forgetting with this small amount, suggesting there might be a threshold below which performance degrades significantly.
- Why unresolved: Paper fixes few-shot setting at 10 examples but doesn't explore how performance scales with different numbers of examples, leaving uncertainty about minimum effective sample size.
- What evidence would resolve it: Systematic experiments varying few-shot examples (e.g., 1, 5, 10, 50, 100) and measuring performance trade-off would identify critical sample size threshold.

### Open Question 3
- Question: How do soft/trainable prompts compare to fixed hand-crafted prompts in mitigating catastrophic forgetting for cross-lingual dialogue generation?
- Basis in paper: Authors mention in conclusions that it would be interesting to explore soft/trainable prompts for dialogue generation in future work.
- Why unresolved: Paper only evaluates fixed hand-crafted prompts, leaving potential benefits of learnable prompts unexplored.
- What evidence would resolve it: Direct comparison experiments between fixed hand-crafted prompts and soft/trainable prompts (with parameter count and computational cost analysis) would reveal whether additional complexity provides meaningful performance gains.

## Limitations

- Data selection bias: Fixed set of 10 few-shot examples per target language may not represent language diversity or model's ability to generalize to different dialogue styles
- Evaluation scope: Results limited to 6 Indo-European languages; doesn't examine semantic preservation or cultural appropriateness of generated responses
- Implementation details: Critical choices like exact interleaving ratio, specific prompt formatting beyond basics, and hyperparameter tuning procedures not fully specified
- Computational efficiency: Paper doesn't report training times or computational resources required for prompt learning approach compared to baselines

## Confidence

- High confidence: Observation that standard FS-XLT and MTL approaches suffer from catastrophic forgetting is well-supported by experimental results showing English responses or ungrammatical target language outputs in baseline models
- Medium confidence: Claim that prompt learning approach effectively mitigates catastrophic forgetting is supported by improved automatic metrics and human evaluations, but exact mechanism of how prompt alignment preserves multilinguality could be more rigorously tested
- Low confidence: Generalizability of results to languages beyond 6 tested Indo-European languages and to other NLP tasks beyond dialogue generation remains uncertain without additional experimentation
