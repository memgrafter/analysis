# On the Difference of BERT-style and CLIP-style Text Encoders

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2306.03678
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.03678
- Reference count: 11
- This paper compares BERT-style and CLIP-style text encoders through three experiments: (i) general text understanding on GLUE benchmark, (ii) vision-centric text understanding on CxC dataset, and (iii) text-to-image generation.

## Executive Summary
This paper investigates the differences between BERT-style and CLIP-style text encoders through three experiments: general text understanding on GLUE benchmark, vision-centric text understanding on CxC dataset, and text-to-image generation. Experimental results show that CLIP-style text encoders underperform BERT-style ones on general text understanding tasks but achieve higher scores on vision-centric text understanding and generate images with better visual perception when paired with an image decoder. The authors conclude that CLIP-style text encoders have a unique ability, termed synesthesia, to associate a text with its visual appearance, which is more similar to human perception.

## Method Summary
The paper compares BERT-style (BERT-base, BERT-large, RoBERTa-base, RoBERTa-large) and CLIP-style (ViT-B/32, ViT-B/16, ViT-L/14, ViT-L/14@336px) text encoders through three experiments. First, they evaluate the encoders on GLUE benchmark tasks using metrics like Matthew's correlation coefficient, accuracy, F1 score, Spearman correlation, and Pearson correlation. Second, they evaluate the encoders on vision-centric text understanding tasks using the CxC dataset. Third, they generate images from text representations using the Multi-Modal CelebA-HQ dataset and a linear transformation layer to project text representations onto image space, evaluating using Inception Score (IS) and CLIP Score (CLIP-S).

## Key Results
- CLIP-style text encoders underperform BERT-style ones on general text understanding tasks (GLUE benchmark).
- CLIP-style text encoders achieve higher scores on vision-centric text understanding (CxC dataset).
- CLIP-style text encoders generate images with better visual perception when paired with an image decoder.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: CLIP-style text encoders underperform BERT-style encoders on general text understanding but outperform them on vision-centric text understanding.
- Mechanism: CLIP-style encoders learn visual knowledge of textual concepts through image-text contrastive pretraining, enabling them to associate text with visual perception.
- Core assumption: Visual knowledge is complementary to semantic alignment for text understanding tasks involving visual context.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract]: "CLIP-style text encoders achieve higher scores on vision-centric text understanding"
  - [section 4.2]: "CLIP-style text encoders are better at associating texts with images, more similar to human"
  - [corpus]: Weak evidence - no direct comparison of vision-centric performance in corpus results

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: CLIP-style text encoders can generate images with better visual perception when paired with an image decoder.
- Mechanism: CLIP-style text representations capture visual concepts that can be transformed into image prompts through linear projection.
- Core assumption: Text and image representation spaces share conceptual overlap that can be bridged by a simple linear transformation.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [section 5.3]: "CLIP-style text encoders achieve higher scores on the CLIP-S metric that measures the matching of an image-text pair"
  - [section 5.2]: "we train a single linear transformation layer to transfer the two types of text representations as prompts to a frozen image decoder"
  - [corpus]: No direct evidence in corpus results

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: Larger BERT-style models learn better visual perception compared to their smaller counterparts.
- Mechanism: Increased model capacity allows BERT-style encoders to capture more nuanced visual concepts during pretraining.
- Core assumption: Model scale correlates with the ability to learn cross-modal associations even in text-only pretraining.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [section 4.2]: "large-size models (i.e., BERT-large and RoBERTa-large) achieve consistently better performance than their counterparts do on STS-V"
  - [abstract]: "larger BERT learns better visual perception"
  - [corpus]: No direct evidence in corpus results

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- Question: How does the performance of CLIP-style text encoders compare to BERT-style ones when the number of parameters is controlled?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The authors acknowledge that they did not replicate different types of text encoders with the same number of parameters due to computational resource limitations.
- Why unresolved: The comparison between BERT-style and CLIP-style text encoders may be influenced by the difference in model sizes, which was not controlled in the experiments.
- What evidence would resolve it: Conducting experiments with BERT-style and CLIP-style text encoders of the same number of parameters to compare their performance on the same tasks.

### Open Question 2
- Question: How do BERT-style and CLIP-style text encoders perform on tasks that require both textual and visual understanding?
- Basis in paper: [inferred] The paper focuses on the differences between BERT-style and CLIP-style text encoders in general text understanding, vision-centric text understanding, and text-to-image generation. However, it does not explicitly address tasks that require both textual and visual understanding.
- Why unresolved: The paper does not provide experimental results or analysis for tasks that require both textual and visual understanding.
- What evidence would resolve it: Conducting experiments on tasks that require both textual and visual understanding, such as visual question answering or image captioning, using BERT-style and CLIP-style text encoders.

### Open Question 3
- Question: How do the learned representations of BERT-style and CLIP-style text encoders differ in terms of their ability to capture semantic and syntactic properties of text?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The authors mention that CoLA, an English acceptability dataset, shows the most significant performance gap between CLIP-style and BERT-style text encoders, indicating that ITC is worse than MLM on grammatical or syntactic properties.
- Why unresolved: The paper does not provide a detailed analysis of the differences in the learned representations of BERT-style and CLIP-style text encoders in terms of their ability to capture semantic and syntactic properties.
- What evidence would resolve it: Analyzing the learned representations of BERT-style and CLIP-style text encoders using techniques such as probing classifiers or attention visualization to understand their ability to capture semantic and syntactic properties.

## Limitations

- The study focuses primarily on comparing pretrained models without examining how fine-tuning affects the relative performance of BERT-style versus CLIP-style encoders.
- The text-to-image generation experiments use a relatively small dataset (Multi-Modal CelebA-HQ) compared to the large-scale pretraining corpora, which may limit generalizability.
- The evaluation of vision-centric understanding relies on a single dataset (CxC) and specific task types, potentially missing other important dimensions of visual-textual reasoning.

## Confidence

- High Confidence: The finding that CLIP-style encoders underperform BERT-style encoders on GLUE benchmark tasks is well-supported by established results in the literature and the authors' experiments.
- Medium Confidence: The superior performance of CLIP-style encoders on vision-centric tasks is supported by the CxC dataset results, but could benefit from additional datasets and task types to strengthen generalizability.
- Medium Confidence: The claim about CLIP-style encoders generating better images through text-to-image generation is supported by CLIP-S scores, though the small dataset size and single transformation approach limit robustness.

## Next Checks

1. Evaluate both encoder types after fine-tuning on vision-centric downstream tasks to determine if training dynamics affect the observed performance differences.
2. Test the text-to-image generation pipeline on larger, more diverse datasets to verify that CLIP-style advantages extend beyond the CelebA-HQ domain.
3. Conduct ablation studies varying the temperature parameter in CLIP's contrastive loss to quantify its impact on learned visual-textual associations.
