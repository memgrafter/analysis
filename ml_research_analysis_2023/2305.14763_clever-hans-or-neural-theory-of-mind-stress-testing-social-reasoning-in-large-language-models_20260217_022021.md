# Clever Hans or Neural Theory of Mind? Stress Testing Social Reasoning in Large Language Models

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2305.14763
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.14763
- Authors: 
- Reference count: 37
- Key outcome: LLMs show inconsistent performance on Theory-of-Mind tasks and fail on adversarial examples, suggesting they rely on memorization and superficial patterns rather than robust reasoning abilities.

## Executive Summary
This paper investigates whether large language models possess genuine Neural Theory-of-Mind (N-ToM) abilities by evaluating 15 LLMs across 6 diverse benchmarks. The study reveals that while some models achieve near-perfect performance on certain tasks, they struggle significantly with adversarial examples designed to prevent shallow heuristics. The findings indicate that LLMs do not possess robust N-ToM capabilities but rather rely on memorization and superficial correlations. The research emphasizes the importance of using multiple benchmarks and adversarial testing to accurately assess N-ToM capabilities, cautioning against drawing conclusions from anecdotal examples or limited benchmark testing.

## Method Summary
The paper evaluates 15 LLMs (various sizes) on 6 diverse N-ToM benchmarks including Triangle COPA, SocialIQa, ToMi, epistemic reasoning tasks, Adv-CSFB, and FauxPas-EAI. Models are tested using zero-shot evaluation with multiple probing methods (MC-probing, LM-probing, CoT-probing) across both standard and adversarial examples. Performance is measured as accuracy on multiple-choice and generative QA formats, compared to a most-frequent-class baseline. The study examines how model performance varies across different task types and probing strategies.

## Key Results
- LLMs achieve high accuracy on some ToM tasks but show significant performance drops on adversarial examples
- Performance varies substantially across different probing methods, with some models performing well only under specific querying strategies
- The study reveals that models rely on shallow heuristics and memorization rather than genuine ToM reasoning
- No single LLM consistently excels across all benchmarks and probing methods

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: LLMs rely on memorization and shallow heuristics rather than robust ToM abilities
- Mechanism: When models achieve high performance on certain datasets but fail on adversarial examples, it indicates pattern matching over true reasoning. The performance gaps between different question types suggest the models are leveraging spurious correlations rather than understanding mental states
- Core assumption: If models truly understood ToM, they would maintain consistent performance across all variations of a task
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "We find that the performance of LLMs decreases for adversarial examples, suggesting that LLMs don't have robust ToM abilities but rather rely on shallow heuristics"
  - [section 5.2] "We find that the performance of LLMs decreases for adversarial examples, suggesting that LLMs don't have robust ToM abilities but rather rely on shallow heuristics"
- Break condition: If models maintain high performance across all adversarial variations, this mechanism would be invalidated

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: LLMs are sensitive to the probing method used to extract information
- Mechanism: Different prompting strategies (generative QA vs probability distributions) can reveal or obscure latent capabilities. Models may appear to have ToM abilities when using certain probing methods but fail with others
- Core assumption: A truly robust ToM system should perform consistently regardless of how it's queried
- Evidence anchors:
  - [section 4.2] "We examine the effect of the different probing methods... Certain techniques have shown to be superior to others... However, we argue that to claim that a model has N-ToM abilities, it is essential that it performs well across probing techniques"
- Break condition: If models consistently perform well across all probing methods, this mechanism would be invalidated

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: LLMs exhibit the "ELIZA effect" where human-like intelligence is incorrectly ascribed to machines
- Mechanism: The impressive anecdotal examples produced by LLMs in generative settings captivate non-expert individuals, leading to overestimation of their ToM capabilities. This is exacerbated by models being trained to generate text that appears high-quality to human observers
- Core assumption: Human observers are prone to anthropomorphizing and may misinterpret model outputs as genuine understanding
- Evidence anchors:
  - [section 6] "The impressive anecdotal examples produced by LLMs in generative settings (e.g., observed with ChatGPT and GPT4 web-demo), tends to captivate non-expert individuals... This inherent bias in their design can lead to the 'ELIZA effect'"
- Break condition: If systematic evaluation consistently shows robust ToM abilities across diverse tasks, this mechanism would be weakened

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map: Dataset preparation (including adversarial examples) -> Model selection (15 LLMs) -> Prompting strategies (multiple probing methods) -> Response generation -> Evaluation -> Analysis of performance patterns
- Critical path: Data preparation → Model prompting → Response generation → Evaluation → Analysis of performance patterns
- Design tradeoffs: Generative vs discriminative prompting methods trade off between flexibility and controllability; larger models may memorize more but also show more sophisticated patterns
- Failure signatures: Large performance gaps between similar tasks, complete failure on adversarial examples, sensitivity to prompting method changes
- First 3 experiments:
  1. Replicate the basic ToM task performance across all 15 models on the standard datasets
  2. Test the same models on the adversarial variations to identify robustness gaps
  3. Compare performance across different probing methods on the same models and tasks

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- To what extent does the observed performance gap in adversarial examples reflect true limitations in N-ToM abilities versus dataset artifacts or reporting bias?
- How does the performance of LLMs on ToM tasks change when they are exposed to additional training data specifically designed to improve N-ToM abilities?
- What are the specific cognitive mechanisms that enable humans to solve ToM tasks, and how do these mechanisms differ from the computational processes employed by LLMs?

## Limitations
- The study relies on zero-shot evaluation without fine-tuning, which may not fully capture the models' potential
- The evaluation across only 15 LLMs limits generalizability to the broader landscape of models
- The study focuses on English-language benchmarks, which may not translate to multilingual ToM capabilities

## Confidence
**High Confidence**: The core finding that LLMs show inconsistent performance across different ToM tasks and fail on adversarial examples is well-supported by the experimental data.

**Medium Confidence**: The interpretation that poor performance on adversarial examples indicates lack of genuine ToM abilities is reasonable but not definitive.

**Low Confidence**: The extent to which the ELIZA effect influences human perception of LLM ToM capabilities is difficult to quantify.

## Next Checks
1. Cross-linguistic validation: Test the same adversarial ToM benchmarks across multiple languages to determine if performance gaps persist when linguistic and cultural context varies.

2. Controlled data leakage analysis: Create a subset of ToM tasks using completely novel scenarios and entities that are highly unlikely to appear in pretraining data, then compare performance to the standard benchmarks.

3. Fine-tuning intervention study: Fine-tune selected models on a diverse set of ToM tasks including adversarial examples, then retest on held-out adversarial scenarios.
