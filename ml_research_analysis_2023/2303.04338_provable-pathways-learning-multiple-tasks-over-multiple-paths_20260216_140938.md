# Provable Pathways: Learning Multiple Tasks over Multiple Paths

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2303.04338
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.04338
- Reference count: 40
- Key outcome: This work explores the benefits of task-specific representations along supernet pathways for multitask learning (MTL), developing generalization bounds that capture the advantages of compositional MTL representations and hierarchical clustering.

## Executive Summary
This paper formalizes Multipath MTL, where tasks select pathways within a supernet to induce task-specific representations. The authors develop generalization bounds using Gaussian complexity that demonstrate the benefits of learning compositional MTL representations and hierarchical task clustering. The bounds provide theoretical justification for multipath representations, showing superior performance compared to traditional MTL approaches in terms of sample complexity and transfer learning capabilities. Experiments validate the theory, demonstrating the effectiveness of multipath representations in both linear and hierarchical MTL settings.

## Method Summary
The paper proposes three MTL approaches: Vanilla MTL (shared representation), Cluster MTL (cluster-specific representations), and Multipath MTL (hierarchical representations with shared first layer and cluster-specific second layers). Using synthetic linear regression data generated from a hierarchical model, the authors train these algorithms using alternating least-squares optimization with method-of-moments initialization. The comparison focuses on test risk evaluation across different configurations, varying the number of clusters, tasks per cluster, and sample sizes per task.

## Key Results
- Generalization bounds for Multipath MTL expressed in terms of Gaussian complexity that capture benefits of compositional representations
- Theoretical superiority over traditional MTL approaches when tasks can be hierarchically clustered
- Transfer learning guarantees showing when multipath representations enable effective adaptation to new tasks
- Experimental validation demonstrating improved sample efficiency and clustering performance

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- **Claim**: Task-specific pathways reduce effective representation complexity by learning only relevant modules per task.
- **Mechanism**: In multipath MTL, each task selects one module per layer, so the learned representation spans only the utilized modules rather than the full supernet. This reduces Gaussian complexity from O(√K) per layer to O(√K̂) where K̂ is the number of used modules.
- **Core assumption**: The empirical solution naturally selects pathways that only utilize necessary modules, avoiding redundant computation.
- **Evidence anchors**:
  - [abstract] "Our bounds are expressed in terms of Gaussian complexity, lead to tangible guarantees for the class of linear representations, and provide novel insights into the quality and benefits of a multipath representation."
  - [section] "Our bounds are expressed in terms of Gaussian complexity, lead to tangible guarantees for the class of linear representations, and provide novel insights into the quality and benefits of a multipath representation."
- **Break condition**: If the supernet is under-parameterized or pathways are poorly chosen, tasks may require more modules than necessary, negating complexity benefits.

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim**: Hierarchical task clustering enables sample-efficient learning by grouping related tasks and sharing representations at higher layers.
- **Mechanism**: When the supernet has a tree structure, tasks are implicitly clustered at different depths. Tasks sharing modules at higher layers benefit from shared representation learning, reducing the effective sample complexity per task group.
- **Core assumption**: Related tasks naturally form clusters that can be captured by hierarchical pathway assignments.
- **Evidence anchors**:
  - [abstract] "When computation graph is a tree, Multipath MTL hierarchically clusters the tasks and builds cluster-specific representations."
  - [section] "When the supernet graph is hierarchical...our bounds provide insights for the benefits of clustering tasks into similar groups and superiority of multilayer Multipath MTL over using single-layer shallow supernets."
- **Break condition**: If tasks are unrelated or clusters are poorly formed, hierarchical sharing provides minimal benefit and may even hurt performance.

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim**: Multipath representations enable effective transfer learning by maintaining task diversity during MTL training.
- **Mechanism**: The task diversity condition ensures that source tasks provide diverse enough information to learn representations that generalize to new tasks. The transfer learning bound shows that excess risk depends on supernet bias, which is controlled by task diversity.
- **Core assumption**: Source tasks are sufficiently diverse over the target task for the learned supernet to provide good representations for new tasks.
- **Evidence anchors**:
  - [abstract] "In conjunction, we formalize the benefits of resulting multipath representation when adapting to new downstream tasks."
  - [section] "Our bounds are expressed in terms of Gaussian complexity, lead to tangible guarantees for the class of linear representations, and provide novel insights into the quality and benefits of a multipath representation."
- **Break condition**: If source tasks are too similar or not diverse enough, the learned supernet may not generalize well to new tasks, leading to high transfer bias.

## Foundational Learning

- **Concept**: Gaussian complexity as a measure of hypothesis set capacity
  - **Why needed here**: The paper uses Gaussian complexity to bound generalization error for multipath MTL, requiring understanding of how it relates to Rademacher complexity and VC dimension.
  - **Quick check question**: Why does Gaussian complexity scale as √(comp(Q)/n) where comp(Q) is set complexity like VC-dimension?

- **Concept**: Empirical process theory and uniform convergence
  - **Why needed here**: The generalization bounds rely on uniform concentration arguments over the hypothesis space, requiring understanding of how empirical risks converge to population risks.
  - **Quick check question**: How does Talagrand's contraction inequality help in decomposing the Gaussian complexity of composed functions?

- **Concept**: Task diversity and its role in transfer learning
  - **Why needed here**: The transfer learning guarantees depend on task diversity conditions that ensure source tasks provide sufficient information for learning representations useful for new tasks.
  - **Quick check question**: How does the task diversity condition relate to the representation distance between source and target tasks?

## Architecture Onboarding

- **Component map**: Task → Pathway selection → Module composition → Prediction head → Loss computation → Backpropagation through relevant modules only
- **Critical path**: Task → Pathway selection → Module composition → Prediction head → Loss computation → Backpropagation through relevant modules only
- **Design tradeoffs**:
  - Width vs depth: Wider layers increase pathway options but also complexity; deeper hierarchies enable better clustering
  - Module sharing: More shared modules reduce parameters but may limit task-specificity
  - Pathway learning: Fixed pathways simplify training but may miss optimal task groupings
- **Failure signatures**:
  - Poor clustering: Tasks with different characteristics forced to share modules
  - Underutilization: Many modules never selected, wasting parameters
  - Overfitting: Too many pathways relative to data, leading to memorization
- **First 3 experiments**:
  1. Compare vanilla MTL vs multipath MTL on synthetic linear regression with known clusters
  2. Test hierarchical MTL performance as function of cluster size and number of clusters
  3. Evaluate transfer learning from multipath MTL to unseen tasks with varying task diversity

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- **Question**: How does the choice of pathway during the MTL phase impact the performance of the transfer learning phase?
- **Basis in paper**: The paper discusses the importance of finding the right task clusterings during the MTL phase that are useful for transfer learning. It also mentions that not all optimal MTL pathways are good for transfer learning.
- **Why unresolved**: The paper provides a theoretical example showing that some MTL solutions can be poor for transfer learning, but doesn't offer a comprehensive analysis of how pathway choices impact transfer performance.
- **What evidence would resolve it**: Experiments or theoretical analysis showing how different pathway choices during MTL affect the performance of subsequent transfer learning tasks.

### Open Question 2
- **Question**: What are the theoretical guarantees for finding the optimal pathway during the MTL phase?
- **Basis in paper**: The paper mentions the challenge of finding the right task clusterings during the MTL phase and discusses the need for well-populated source tasks in Assumption 5.
- **Why unresolved**: The paper provides assumptions for well-populated source tasks but doesn't offer theoretical guarantees for finding the optimal pathway during the MTL phase.
- **What evidence would resolve it**: Theoretical results or algorithms that guarantee finding the optimal pathway during the MTL phase.

### Open Question 3
- **Question**: How does the depth and width of the supernet graph impact the performance of Multipath MTL?
- **Basis in paper**: The paper discusses the benefits of hierarchical representations and compares the sample complexity of different MTL strategies, but doesn't provide a comprehensive analysis of how the depth and width of the supernet impact performance.
- **Why unresolved**: The paper provides insights into the benefits of hierarchical representations but doesn't offer a detailed analysis of how the depth and width of the supernet impact performance.
- **What evidence would resolve it**: Experiments or theoretical analysis showing how varying the depth and width of the supernet graph affects the performance of Multipath MTL.

## Limitations

- The theoretical analysis assumes access to known task clusters or the ability to learn optimal pathways, which may not hold in all practical scenarios
- The linear representation analysis provides insights but may not directly extend to nonlinear settings without additional assumptions
- The task diversity condition for transfer learning requires careful validation in practice to ensure source tasks provide sufficient information for target tasks

## Confidence

- **Theoretical bounds**: High confidence in the Gaussian complexity bounds and their application to multipath MTL settings
- **Empirical evaluation**: Medium confidence, as synthetic experiments show clear trends but may not fully capture real-world complexity
- **Transfer learning guarantees**: Medium confidence, as they depend on assumptions about task diversity that may be difficult to verify in practice

## Next Checks

1. **Pathway learning validation**: Test whether the learned pathways actually cluster related tasks as predicted by the theory, using real-world datasets with known task relationships.
2. **Non-linear extension study**: Extend the analysis to non-linear representations (e.g., neural networks) and validate whether the theoretical insights about pathway benefits still hold.
3. **Transfer diversity assessment**: Design experiments to empirically verify the task diversity condition by measuring representation distances between source and target tasks in transfer learning scenarios.
