# LLM-empowered Chatbots for Psychiatrist and Patient Simulation: Application and Evaluation

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2305.13614
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.13614
- Reference count: 30
- Key outcome: ChatGPT can simulate realistic patient behavior and conduct professional diagnoses through iterative prompt engineering with psychiatrist feedback

## Executive Summary
This work explores the use of ChatGPT to simulate psychiatrists and patients for psychiatric diagnosis through an iterative design process involving psychiatrists. The researchers developed prompts to guide ChatGPT in conducting empathetic and thorough diagnostic conversations, addressing challenges like excessive repetition and lack of in-depth questioning. The resulting chatbots were evaluated by real psychiatrists and patients, demonstrating that ChatGPT can simulate realistic patient behavior and conduct professional diagnoses while maintaining natural conversation flow.

## Method Summary
The study employed an iterative prompt engineering approach with psychiatrist feedback to develop specialized prompts for ChatGPT-based doctor and patient chatbots. The methodology involved designing initial prompts based on DSM-5 criteria, conducting human evaluation through a web interface with real psychiatrists and patients, and calculating automatic metrics to complement human evaluation. The process focused on improving empathy, in-depth questioning, and realistic patient behavior while addressing issues like excessive repetition and forgetting prescribed behaviors during conversations.

## Key Results
- ChatGPT-powered chatbots can simulate realistic patient behavior and conduct professional psychiatric diagnoses
- Iterative prompt engineering with psychiatrist feedback significantly improves chatbot performance in specialized domains
- Real-time reminders during conversations help maintain consistent patient behavior and adherence to prescribed characteristics

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: Iterative prompt engineering with psychiatrist feedback improves chatbot performance in specialized domains.
- Mechanism: Psychiatrists provide domain-specific feedback on chatbot responses, enabling iterative refinement of prompts to address specific shortcomings like excessive repetition or lack of in-depth questioning.
- Core assumption: Domain experts can identify subtle issues in AI-generated responses that impact real-world usability.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract]: "Through an iterative design process involving psychiatrists, prompts were developed to guide ChatGPT in conducting empathetic and thorough diagnostic conversations."
  - [section 3.1]: "We conducted an experimental study (Phase 2) to design appropriate prompts for ChatGPT-based chatbots and establish an evaluation framework that incorporates both human evaluation and automatic metrics aligned with the objectives from Phase 1. Importantly, the design of prompts and metrics was iterated based on human feedback, with each version evaluated and improved with input from psychiatrists."

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: Adding real-time reminders during conversation helps maintain consistent behavior in patient chatbots.
- Mechanism: By appending brief, contextually relevant reminders to the end of each turn's dialogue history, the chatbot maintains focus on desired behaviors like colloquial language and resistance.
- Core assumption: The LLM's response generation is sensitive to the most recent context, allowing temporary behavioral cues to influence output.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [section 3.2]: "To address the issue of forgetting, we insert new reminders during the conversation... Without the users' awareness, we subtly append the following words at the end of the most recent sentence in the dialogue history."

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: Combining human evaluation with automatic metrics provides comprehensive assessment of chatbot performance.
- Mechanism: Human evaluation captures subjective aspects like empathy and realism, while automatic metrics objectively measure functionality and style consistency.
- Core assumption: Neither human nor automatic evaluation alone is sufficient to capture all relevant aspects of chatbot performance in mental health contexts.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract]: "The resulting chatbots were evaluated by real psychiatrists and patients, showing that ChatGPT can simulate realistic patient behavior and conduct professional diagnoses."
  - [section 4.1.3]: "We mainly measure the expertise of the doctor chatbot using automatic metrics... Then, we evaluate the patient chatbots' style using some linguistic features..."

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map: User input -> Prompt formatting -> ChatGPT API call -> Response processing -> User output
- Critical path: User input → Prompt formatting → ChatGPT API call → Response processing → User output
- Design tradeoffs:
  - Longer prompts provide more guidance but may cause the LLM to lose focus
  - Real-time reminders maintain behavior but add complexity
  - Combining evaluation methods increases comprehensiveness but requires more resources
- Failure signatures:
  - Excessive repetition in responses
  - Deviation from prescribed behaviors (e.g., patient becoming too cooperative)
  - Inconsistent performance across different conversation turns
- First 3 experiments:
  1. Test basic functionality with simple prompts to verify ChatGPT integration
  2. Evaluate performance with different prompt versions to identify optimal instruction balance
  3. Assess the impact of real-time reminders on maintaining consistent patient behavior

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- Question: How do different prompt designs affect the balance between thoroughness and efficiency in questioning during psychiatric diagnosis?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper discusses the trade-off between thoroughness and efficiency in questioning, noting that some prompt designs lead to more comprehensive questioning but longer conversations.
- Why unresolved: The paper identifies this trade-off but does not provide a systematic analysis of how specific prompt elements impact this balance across different psychiatric conditions or severity levels.
- What evidence would resolve it: Comparative studies measuring symptom recall rates against conversation length and user engagement scores for different prompt designs across multiple mental health conditions.

### Open Question 2
- Question: What is the optimal level of patient resistance that maximizes diagnostic accuracy while maintaining user engagement?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper notes that patient chatbots with resistance characteristics perform better in human evaluations but may report more wrong symptoms, suggesting a need to balance resistance levels.
- Why unresolved: The study introduces resistance in prompts but does not systematically vary or optimize the level of resistance to find the ideal balance point.
- What evidence would resolve it: Experiments testing multiple resistance levels with varying degrees of symptom reporting accuracy and user engagement metrics to identify the optimal balance.

### Open Question 3
- Question: How do human doctors' diagnostic behaviors differ from chatbot doctors in multi-disease scenarios beyond depression?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper notes that human doctors conduct more screening for other conditions and have more even distributions of questions across symptoms compared to chatbots.
- Why unresolved: The study focuses on depression diagnosis, leaving open questions about how these differences would manifest when diagnosing multiple or comorbid conditions.
- What evidence would resolve it: Comparative studies of human doctors versus chatbots diagnosing patients with multiple conditions or at risk for various mental health disorders.

## Limitations

- Evaluation sample size is relatively small (8 psychiatrists, 11 patients), limiting generalizability of findings
- Simulation scenarios are limited to four specific psychiatric conditions, not capturing full clinical complexity
- Reliance on ChatGPT API means results may not generalize to other LLM architectures or versions

## Confidence

- **High confidence**: The basic mechanism of using iterative prompt engineering with domain expert feedback to improve specialized chatbot performance
- **Medium confidence**: The effectiveness of real-time reminders for maintaining consistent behavior in patient chatbots
- **Medium confidence**: The combined human and automatic evaluation approach provides comprehensive assessment

## Next Checks

1. **Replication with larger sample sizes**: Conduct the evaluation with a larger, more diverse group of psychiatrists and patients across multiple clinical settings to assess generalizability and identify any demographic or cultural factors affecting chatbot performance.

2. **Longitudinal performance assessment**: Evaluate chatbot performance over extended conversation sequences (multiple sessions) to assess consistency, learning capabilities, and potential degradation in response quality or diagnostic accuracy over time.

3. **Cross-model comparison**: Test the same prompt engineering approach with alternative LLM architectures (e.g., Claude, LLaMA, or fine-tuned open-source models) to determine whether the demonstrated improvements are specific to ChatGPT or represent broader principles of prompt engineering for medical applications.
