# Inducing Point Allocation for Sparse Gaussian Processes in High-Throughput Bayesian Optimisation

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2301.10123
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2301.10123
- Reference count: 15
- Key outcome: Novel DPP-based inducing point allocation improves Bayesian optimization by focusing on local high-fidelity modeling of promising regions

## Executive Summary
This paper introduces a novel approach to inducing point allocation for sparse Gaussian processes in Bayesian optimization. The authors propose using Determinantal Point Processes (DPPs) with quality-diversity decomposition to focus modeling capacity on promising regions rather than achieving global accuracy. This method is specifically designed for high-throughput Bayesian optimization scenarios where computational efficiency and local accuracy are paramount. The framework demonstrates improved performance over established baselines on both synthetic and real-world optimization tasks.

## Method Summary
The method employs a quality-diversity decomposition of Determinantal Point Processes to allocate inducing points for sparse Gaussian processes in Bayesian optimization. The approach uses a greedy MAP estimation algorithm to select inducing points that balance quality (likelihood of containing optima) with diversity (spread across the input space). The quality function is designed to be invariant to scale and shift transformations, ensuring robust performance across different problem settings. This framework provides a general approach for ensuring sparse GPs are accurate in key areas, with applications across various downstream tasks.

## Key Results
- Demonstrates improved Bayesian optimization performance over established baselines
- Shows effective local high-fidelity modeling of promising regions for precise optimization
- Proves flexible framework suitable for broad range of downstream sequential decision-making tasks

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: DPP-based inducing point allocation with quality functions improves BO performance by focusing modeling capacity on promising regions rather than globally uniform coverage.
- Mechanism: The quality-diversity decomposition of DPPs allows encoding a preference for regions likely to contain optima while maintaining diversity. This focuses sparse modeling resources where they matter most for optimization.
- Core assumption: The quality function accurately identifies regions containing optima or high uncertainty.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract]: "Unlike existing methods which seek only to reduce global uncertainty in the objective function, our approach provides the local high-fidelity modelling of promising regions required for precise optimisation."
  - [section 4.1]: "It is clear that a particular Z will occur with high probability only if it contains points that have large quality scores (as measured by q(zi)) and have a diverse spread (as measured by |KZ|)"
- Break condition: If the quality function fails to identify promising regions, the method will not outperform existing approaches.

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: The greedy MAP estimation of DPPs provides an efficient approximation to the optimal inducing point allocation.
- Mechanism: The greedy algorithm iteratively selects points that maximize q(z)σ²ⱼ₋₁(z), where σ²ⱼ₋₁(z) is the conditional variance. This balances quality (q(z)) with diversity (σ²ⱼ₋₁(z)).
- Core assumption: The greedy approximation provides a good estimate of the MAP DPP solution.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [section 4.1]: "This resulting MAP estimate has an intuitive interpretation, as specified in Theorem 1"
  - [section 3]: "This algorithm greedily builds its set of points Z by choosing the jth point from X \Z₁:ⱼ₋₁ as zⱼ = argmax z∈X\Z₁:ⱼ₋₁ |KZ₁:ⱼ₋₁⋃{z}|"
- Break condition: If the greedy approximation significantly deviates from the true MAP solution, the inducing points may not be optimally placed.

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: Scale and shift invariance of the quality function ensures robust inducing point allocation across different problem scales and offsets.
- Mechanism: The quality function is designed to be invariant to linear rescaling and additive offsets, ensuring consistent behavior regardless of data transformations.
- Core assumption: The quality function maintains its discriminative properties under scaling and shifting transformations.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [section 4.2]: "Shift invariance: the resulting IPA should be invariant to adding an offset to the data" and "Scale invariance: the resulting IPA should be invariant to linear re-scaling of the data"
  - [section 4.3]: "A linear rescaling of the data will change q by a multiplicative factor only, and subtracting f̂ makes it positive and shift invariant"
- Break condition: If the quality function is not properly invariant, inducing point allocation may be suboptimal for transformed problems.
