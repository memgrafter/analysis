# Capabilities of GPT-4 on Medical Challenge Problems

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2303.13375
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.13375
- Reference count: 33
- Key outcome: GPT-4 achieves >86% accuracy on USMLE Steps 1-3 without fine-tuning, outperforming specialized medical models

## Executive Summary
This study evaluates GPT-4's performance on medical competency exams and benchmark datasets without any specialized fine-tuning. The model exceeds the passing score on USMLE by over 20 points and significantly outperforms earlier general-purpose models as well as models specifically fine-tuned on medical knowledge. GPT-4 demonstrates strong calibration of its answer probabilities, an important feature for medical applications, and shows ability to handle questions with images through logical reasoning about textual descriptions. While promising for medical education and clinical support, the authors caution that real-world deployment requires careful attention to accuracy, safety, and potential biases.

## Method Summary
The study evaluates GPT-4 on USMLE official practice materials (Steps 1-3) and MultiMedQA benchmark datasets using zero-shot and 5-shot prompting. The model is compared against GPT-3.5 and reported results from Flan-PaLM 540B and Med-PaLM. Evaluation includes accuracy on multiple-choice questions, calibration of answer probabilities, and ability to handle text versus image-based questions. The authors also explore chain-of-thought prompting, interactive case rewriting, and memorization detection using the MELD algorithm.

## Key Results
- GPT-4 achieves >86% accuracy on USMLE Steps 1-3, exceeding passing scores by over 20 points
- Model demonstrates significantly better calibration than GPT-3.5, with high-confidence answers being correct 93% of the time
- Performs well on questions with visual elements (70-80% accuracy) through text-based reasoning alone

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: GPT-4 achieves superior performance without fine-tuning due to increased model scale and emergent reasoning capabilities
- Mechanism: Transformer architecture at sufficient scale develops latent capabilities for medical reasoning through pattern recognition and logical inference
- Core assumption: Scale and diversity of training data enable emergent reasoning without domain-specific fine-tuning
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "GPT-4...exceeds the passing score on USMLE by over 20 points and outperforms earlier general-purpose models...as well as models specifically fine-tuned on medical knowledge"
  - [section 1] "LLMs trained on massive, cross-disciplinary corpora have become potent building blocks in the creation of task-focused systems"
- Break condition: Performance degrades significantly on tasks requiring deep domain expertise not captured in general training data

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: GPT-4 demonstrates superior probability calibration compared to GPT-3.5
- Mechanism: Internal probability estimates better reflect true correctness rates due to improved representation learning at scale
- Core assumption: Calibration improves with model scale and is independent of overall accuracy
- Evidence anchors:
  - [section 5] "GPT-4 exhibits significantly better calibration than its predecessor...datapoints that GPT-4 assigns an average probability of 0.96 tend to be correct 93% of the time"
- Break condition: Calibration degrades on specialized medical domains or when model is prompted in ways that distort confidence estimates

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: GPT-4 can handle questions with visual elements through logical reasoning about textual descriptions
- Mechanism: Uses contextual reasoning and test-taking strategies to infer answers from text descriptions of visual content
- Core assumption: Medical reasoning can be effectively supported by textual reasoning alone
- Evidence anchors:
  - [section 3.1.1] "GPT-4 (no vision) performs best on questions that contain only text, it still performs well on questions with media elements, obtaining 70-80% prediction accuracies"
- Break condition: Performance drops significantly when visual information is essential and cannot be reasonably inferred from text descriptions

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map: Prompt → Model inference → Answer generation → Evaluation → Calibration assessment
- Critical path: Prompt → Model inference → Answer generation → Evaluation → Calibration assessment
- Design tradeoffs:
  - Zero-shot vs. few-shot prompting: Simpler implementation but potentially lower performance vs. better performance but more complex prompt engineering
  - Text-only vs. multi-modal: Broader applicability vs. ability to handle visual medical information
  - Open-world generation vs. constrained multiple-choice: More realistic but harder to evaluate vs. easier to evaluate but less representative of real use cases
- Failure signatures:
  - Poor calibration: High-confidence incorrect answers
  - Hallucinations: Plausible-sounding but factually incorrect medical information
  - Sensitivity to prompt phrasing: Small changes in prompt leading to significantly different outputs
- First 3 experiments:
  1. Compare GPT-4 performance on medical questions with and without chain-of-thought prompting
  2. Test model calibration on different subsets of medical questions (text-only vs. image-referenced)
  3. Evaluate memorization detection by testing model on held-out medical questions vs. known training data

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- Question: Does GPT-4's strong performance on USMLE Step 3 questions indicate it can manage patient care in a dynamic, interactive setting?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper mentions Step 3 includes 13 computer-based case simulations but does not evaluate GPT-4 on these
- Why unresolved: The authors only tested GPT-4 on multiple-choice questions and not the interactive case simulations
- What evidence would resolve it: Direct testing of GPT-4 on the actual computer-based case simulations from recent USMLE Step 3 exams

### Open Question 2
- Question: How sensitive is GPT-4's performance on medical questions to specific prompting techniques?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The authors used simple prompts and found chain-of-thought prompting did not improve performance, but noted more sophisticated prompting could yield better results
- Why unresolved: The paper focused on baseline performance with minimal prompting
- What evidence would resolve it: Systematic testing of various prompting strategies on the same medical datasets

### Open Question 3
- Question: Does GPT-4 exhibit biases in its medical recommendations that could affect patient care for different demographic groups?
- Basis in paper: [inferred] The discussion mentions concerns about biases in healthcare delivery and the need to study fairness
- Why unresolved: The authors did not conduct any analysis of potential biases across different patient demographics
- What evidence would resolve it: Testing GPT-4 on medical questions with patient demographics varied and analyzing differences in recommendations or accuracy

## Limitations
- Training data composition remains undisclosed, making it impossible to fully assess potential memorization effects
- Evaluation relies entirely on multiple-choice formats, which may not represent real-world clinical complexity
- Text-only model cannot directly process medical images, limiting applicability to radiology and pathology tasks

## Confidence

- **High Confidence**: GPT-4 achieves high accuracy (>86%) on USMLE Steps 1-3 without fine-tuning
- **High Confidence**: GPT-4 demonstrates superior calibration compared to GPT-3.5
- **Medium Confidence**: GPT-4's reasoning capabilities represent genuine understanding rather than pattern matching
- **Medium Confidence**: Text-only reasoning can effectively handle questions with visual elements
