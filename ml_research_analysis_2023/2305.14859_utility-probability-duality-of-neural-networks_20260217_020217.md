# Utility-Probability Duality of Neural Networks

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID**: 2305.14859
- **Source URL**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.14859
- **Reference count**: 40
- **Primary result**: Shows that greedy decoding outperforms probability-based methods in language generation despite violating probability principles, and proposes a utility-based explanation with dual probability transformation that improves performance by +14.6 BLEU for sampling and +17.3 for MAP in WMT'14 en2de translation.

## Executive Summary
This paper addresses a paradox in neural language generation where probability-based decision rules like sampling and maximum a-posteriori (MAP) perform worse than greedy decoding, despite neural networks being trained via maximum likelihood estimation. The authors propose that neural networks in supervised learning tasks are better understood as learning ordinal utility functions rather than probability distributions. They show that the MLE training process can be interpreted as a utility-learning algorithm with a small perturbation term, and derive a transformation that converts learned utility values back to a new kind of probability estimation (dual probability) that significantly improves performance when used with probability-compatible decision rules.

## Method Summary
The authors propose interpreting softmax-normalized neural networks as ordinal utility functions rather than probability models, and show that maximum likelihood estimation (MLE) training corresponds to a perturbed variant of a utility-learning algorithm. They derive a dual probability transformation that converts learned utility values back to probability estimations, which performs significantly better than standard softmax probabilities when used with probability-compatible decision rules. The method is evaluated on WMT'14 English→German translation, WMT'17 Chinese→English translation, and CNN/DailyMail summarization tasks.

## Key Results
- Greedy decoding outperforms sampling and MAP decoding in language generation despite violating probability principles
- The dual probability transformation improves sampling performance by +14.6 BLEU and MAP performance by +17.3 BLEU on WMT'14 en2de translation
- MLE training can be decomposed into a utility-learning objective plus a covariance perturbation term
- The utility-probability duality provides a unified framework for understanding neural network behavior in decision-making tasks

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: The neural network learns an ordinal utility function rather than a probability distribution during training.
- Mechanism: During standard MLE training, the softmax-normalized neural network is interpreted as both a probability model and an unnormalized logit sub-model. The logit sub-model learns to encode preferences revealed in training data, functioning as an ordinal utility function that assigns preferential scores to options.
- Core assumption: The utility-based interpretation is valid when the neural network is used for decision-making tasks, particularly sequential decision tasks like language generation.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract]: "We propose an alternative value-based explanation to the standard supervised learning procedure in deep learning... the learned neural network not as a probability model but as an ordinal utility function"
  - [section 3]: "we propose to explain the empirical behaviors of softmax-normalized neural networks from the Q-iteration perspective, in which what the neural network is expected to output are not probabilities, but are just the un-normalized Q-values"
  - [corpus]: Weak - neighboring papers focus on utility-based retrieval and reinforcement learning, but not on the specific duality claim between utility and probability in neural networks.

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: The MLE training process corresponds to a perturbed variant of a utility-learning algorithm (MABE).
- Mechanism: The gradient of the MLE objective can be decomposed into the gradient of a utility-learning objective (JMABE) plus a covariance term. This shows that MLE training is approximately optimizing a utility function, with the covariance term acting as a perturbation.
- Core assumption: The covariance term between the utility predictions and their gradients has limited impact on the learning dynamic for complex neural networks.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract]: "we show that the SGD learning dynamic of maximum likelihood estimation (MLE) can be seen as an iteration process that optimizes the neural network toward an optimal utility function"
  - [section 4]: "Proposition 1 asserts that the gradient of the probability-learning objective (5) differs from the gradient of the utility-learning objective (9) by exactly this covariance"
  - [corpus]: Weak - no direct evidence from corpus about the specific relationship between MLE and utility learning, though related concepts appear in utility-based reinforcement learning.

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: The learned utility function can be transformed back into a probability distribution (dual probability) that provides more accurate probability estimates than the softmax probabilities.
- Mechanism: Using the relationship between utility values and true probabilities at the global maximum of the utility objective, an equation is derived to transform the learned Q-values back to probability estimations. These dual probabilities encode a different probability space from the softmax probabilities and lead to dramatically better performance when used in probability-compatible decision rules.
- Core assumption: The transformation equation provides a reasonable approximation of the true probabilities even when the Q-values are not perfectly learned.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract]: "Our utility-based theory also entails an equation that can transform the learned utility values back to a new kind of probability estimation, called the dual probability, which can be used to improve the performance of probability-compatible decision rules by a large margin"
  - [section 5]: "the dual probability (13) perform much better than the commonly used softmax probabilities, when both are used in probability-compatible decision rules"
  - [corpus]: Weak - neighboring papers focus on utility-based retrieval and reinforcement learning, but not on the specific duality claim between utility and probability in neural networks.

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map: Input sentence -> Transformer encoder -> Transformer decoder -> Linear layer -> Logits (utility sub-model) -> Softmax (probability) -> Dual probability transformation -> Decision rule output

- Critical path: Training: Input -> Encoder -> Decoder -> Output layer (logits) -> Softmax (probabilities) -> MLE loss -> Gradient update; Inference: Input -> Encoder -> Decoder -> Output layer (logits) -> Dual probability transformation -> Decision rule (e.g., greedy, sampling)

- Design tradeoffs: Interpreting the outputs as utility scores vs. probabilities provides better explanation for empirical observations but is less intuitive than probability-based interpretation; using dual probability requires additional transformation step but provides more accurate probability estimates.

- Failure signatures: Poor performance of probability-compatible decision rules when using softmax probability; good performance of greedy rule despite violating probability principles; systematic over-estimation of short, meaningless outputs and under-estimation of desired outputs by softmax probability.

- First 3 experiments:
  1. Train a TransformerBase model on WMT'14 en→de using standard cross-entropy loss for 100,000 steps with dropout=0.1 and label smoothing=0.1.
  2. Apply dual probability transformation to Q-values using formula pdual_i = CLIP(p_i(1 + q_i - p·q))/Z and compare with softmax probabilities using greedy, sampling, and beam search decision rules.
  3. Train the model using MABE(λ) algorithm by computing JMABE objective and adding covariance perturbation term to gradient, then compare performance with MLE-trained model.

## Limitations

- The theoretical connection between MLE training and utility learning relies on a gradient decomposition where the covariance perturbation term is assumed to be small but not rigorously proven to remain negligible throughout training.
- The dual probability transformation is somewhat arbitrary and may distort probability predictions, with limited theoretical justification for why it provides better probability estimates.
- The empirical validation focuses primarily on language generation tasks, leaving open questions about whether the utility-probability duality generalizes to other types of neural network applications.

## Confidence

- **High confidence**: The empirical observation that greedy decoding outperforms probability-based decision rules in language generation tasks is well-established and consistently demonstrated across multiple benchmarks.
- **Medium confidence**: The transformation equation that converts utility values to dual probabilities is mathematically sound, but its practical effectiveness may depend on specific model architectures and training dynamics.
- **Low confidence**: The claim that MLE training fundamentally learns a utility function rather than probability distribution is theoretically compelling but lacks complete mechanistic explanation for why the covariance perturbation doesn't significantly alter the learning trajectory.

## Next Checks

1. **Gradient perturbation analysis**: Quantify the magnitude of the covariance term across different training epochs and model architectures to determine whether it remains sufficiently small to justify the utility-learning interpretation throughout training.

2. **Cross-task generalization**: Test the dual probability transformation on non-sequential tasks (e.g., image classification, structured prediction) to determine whether the utility-probability duality is specific to language generation or represents a broader phenomenon in neural network training.

3. **Alternative utility formulations**: Implement and compare different utility-learning algorithms (beyond MABE variants) to assess whether the observed performance improvements stem specifically from the proposed duality or from general utility-based optimization principles.
