# MOSPC: MOS Prediction Based on Pairwise Comparison

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2306.10493
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.10493
- Reference count: 24
- Primary result: Proposed MOSPC framework improves ranking accuracy of synthesized speech quality using dynamic pairwise comparison and C-Mixup regularization

## Executive Summary
This paper introduces MOSPC (MOS Prediction Based on Pairwise Comparison), a novel framework designed to enhance the ranking accuracy of synthesized speech quality assessment. The key innovation lies in training the model using dynamic pairwise comparisons, which forces the model to focus on relative quality differences between speech samples rather than absolute MOS scores. This approach proves particularly effective when MOS scores are close, addressing a critical limitation of traditional regression-based MOS prediction methods. The framework also incorporates C-Mixup, a regularization technique that improves generalization to unseen systems and multi-language scenarios, demonstrating enhanced robustness in out-of-distribution conditions.

## Method Summary
The MOSPC framework operates on a pairwise comparison paradigm, where the model learns to predict which of two speech samples has higher quality rather than predicting absolute MOS scores directly. This dynamic pairwise training strategy emphasizes relative quality differences, making the model more sensitive to subtle variations in speech quality. The C-Mixup algorithm is integrated as a regularization technique, creating interpolated samples during training to enhance robustness to unseen systems and languages. This combination of pairwise comparison training and mixup-based regularization addresses both ranking accuracy and generalization challenges in MOS prediction tasks.

## Key Results
- MOSPC outperforms baseline models on most correlation coefficient metrics, particularly KTAU (Kendall's Tau), which is directly related to quality ranking performance
- The framework achieves superior ranking accuracy across fine-grained segments, demonstrating consistent improvements in relative quality assessment
- C-Mixup integration enhances robustness to unseen-system and multi-language out-of-distribution (OOD) challenges, improving generalization performance

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The effectiveness of MOSPC stems from its fundamental shift from absolute quality prediction to relative quality comparison. By training on pairwise comparisons, the model develops a more nuanced understanding of quality differences between samples, particularly when MOS scores are close together. This approach forces the model to focus on discriminative features rather than absolute value prediction, which is crucial for accurate ranking. The C-Mixup regularization further enhances this by exposing the model to interpolated samples during training, improving its ability to generalize to unseen conditions and reducing overfitting to specific training distributions.

## Architecture Onboarding

**Component Map:**
Audio Features -> Pairwise Comparator -> Relative Quality Score -> C-Mixup Augmentation -> MOSPC Model

**Critical Path:**
Audio Feature Extraction -> Pairwise Sample Generation -> Relative Quality Prediction -> C-Mixup Integration -> Final MOSPC Output

**Design Tradeoffs:**
- Pairwise vs. Regression: Pairwise improves ranking but increases computational complexity
- Dynamic vs. Static Pairs: Dynamic provides more diverse training but requires careful sampling strategy
- C-Mixup vs. Traditional Augmentation: C-Mixup improves generalization but may introduce synthetic artifacts

**Failure Signatures:**
- Poor performance on very similar quality samples indicates insufficient pairwise training diversity
- Overfitting to training systems suggests inadequate C-Mixup regularization
- Computational bottlenecks during inference may require optimization of pairwise comparison strategy

## Limitations
- Evaluation relies primarily on single dataset (CMU-CS), limiting generalizability claims
- Cross-dataset validation is absent, raising questions about real-world applicability
- Comparison with strong baselines lacks methodological transparency for fair assessment
- Computational costs and scalability for large-scale applications are not addressed
- Multi-language robustness claims need extensive validation across diverse language families
