# Path Planning using Reinforcement Learning: A Policy Iteration Approach

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2303.07535
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.07535
- Reference count: 40
- One-line primary result: The proposed ordinal regression auto-tuning method accelerates policy iteration by 1.48x on average compared to heuristic approaches in complex maze environments.

## Executive Summary
This paper introduces a novel approach to policy iteration in reinforcement learning by incorporating ordinal regression auto-tuning for design space exploration of reward function parameters. The method aims to optimize reward weights to maximize learning agent performance in path planning tasks within complex mazes. By using partial rankings to compare action subsets rather than the entire action space, the approach reduces computational costs while identifying optimal reward parameters. The evaluation demonstrates that the optimal parameters depend on environment characteristics, with the method achieving an average 1.48x acceleration over heuristic approaches in finding optimal policies.

## Method Summary
The approach combines policy iteration with an ordinal regression-based auto-tuner to optimize reward function parameters. Policy iteration operates through repeated policy evaluation and improvement steps using Bellman equation updates until convergence. The auto-tuner explores the high-dimensional design space of reward weights by evaluating different policies across multiple maze environments with varying obstacle configurations. The method adjusts reward parameters based on accumulated rewards achieved by the learning agent, effectively balancing exploration of new policies with exploitation of known successful ones. The ordinal regression framework enables efficient partial ranking comparisons rather than exhaustive full ranking of the action space.

## Key Results
- The proposed method achieves an average 1.48x acceleration in finding optimal policies compared to heuristic approaches
- Optimal reward parameters were shown to depend on specific environmental characteristics, including obstacle types and maze configurations
- The approach successfully handled complex mazes with multiple obstacle types by learning appropriate trade-offs between different penalties and discount factors
