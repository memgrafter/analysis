# Class-Incremental Learning with Repetition

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2301.11396
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2301.11396
- Authors: 
- Reference count: 40
- Primary result: ER-FA outperforms other replay approaches in unbalanced CIR streams, particularly for infrequent classes, while maintaining performance on frequent ones

## Executive Summary
This paper introduces Class-Incremental with Repetition (CIR) scenarios, which allow revisiting previously seen classes in a continual learning stream. The authors propose two generators (Gslot and Gsamp) to create CIR streams with controllable repetition patterns from a single dataset. They conduct a comprehensive evaluation of existing CL strategies under CIR conditions, finding that knowledge accumulation occurs naturally with repetition, reducing the need for replay. The paper also introduces Frequency-Aware Replay (ER-FA), a novel replay strategy that adapts buffer allocation based on class repetition frequencies. ER-FA outperforms other replay approaches in unbalanced CIR streams, particularly for infrequent classes, while maintaining performance on frequent ones. On CIFAR-100 and TinyImageNet, ER-FA achieved higher accuracy and robustness across all classes compared to existing replay methods.

## Method Summary
The paper proposes Class-Incremental with Repetition (CIR) scenarios, where previously seen classes can reappear in the stream. Two stochastic stream generators, Gslot and Gsamp, are introduced to create CIR streams with controllable repetition patterns from a single dataset. Gslot enforces constraints on class occurrences, while Gsamp allows for arbitrarily long streams with controllable repetition via probability distributions. The authors evaluate existing CL strategies (Naive, LwF, EWC, ER-RS, A-GEM) under CIR conditions and propose Frequency-Aware Replay (ER-FA), a novel replay strategy that adapts buffer allocation based on class repetition frequencies. ER-FA is shown to outperform other replay approaches in unbalanced CIR streams, particularly for infrequent classes.

## Key Results
- Natural repetition in CIR scenarios leads to knowledge accumulation, even for naive fine-tuning strategies
- ER-FA outperforms other replay strategies in unbalanced CIR streams, particularly for infrequent classes
- On CIFAR-100 and TinyImageNet, ER-FA achieved higher accuracy and robustness across all classes compared to existing replay methods

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- **Claim:** Natural repetition in the data stream allows knowledge accumulation even for naive fine-tuning strategies.
- **Mechanism:** When classes repeat in the stream, the model's parameters gradually stabilize for previously seen classes. Even when a class is absent in the current experience, the network retains and improves its representation over time due to the repeated exposure of that class in earlier or later experiences.
- **Core assumption:** The stream has sufficient repetition and length to allow gradual parameter consolidation across experiences.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract] "We found out that knowledge accumulation happens naturally in streams with repetition. Even a naive fine-tuning, subjected to complete forgetting in CI scenarios, is able to accumulate knowledge for classes that are not always present in an experience."
  - [section 4.2] "Missing Class Accuracy Increases Over Time... Over time the model accumulate knowledge and the training process stabilizes."
  - [corpus] "Continual Learning in the Presence of Repetition" — related work confirms that repetition can mitigate forgetting even without explicit replay.
- **Break condition:** If repetition probability is too low or the stream is too short, the model will not see classes often enough to stabilize their representations.

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim:** Frequency-aware replay (ER-FA) outperforms standard replay strategies in unbalanced CIR streams by dynamically allocating buffer slots based on repetition frequency.
- **Mechanism:** ER-FA assigns higher buffer quotas to infrequent classes (those with low repetition probability) and fills unused slots with samples from frequent classes. This counteracts the natural imbalance in the stream and ensures infrequent classes are sufficiently rehearsed.
- **Core assumption:** The stream has unbalanced repetition where some classes appear rarely and others frequently.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract] "ER-FA outperforms other replay approaches in unbalanced CIR streams, particularly for infrequent classes, while maintaining performance on frequent ones."
  - [section 4.4] "ER-FA surpasses other Replay strategies in unbalanced streams and provide a more robust performance on infrequent classes without losing accuracy on the frequent ones."
  - [section 3] "We propose Frequency-Aware(FA) storage policy that addresses the imbalance issue in CIR streams by online adjustment of the buffer slots in accordance with the amount of repetition for each class."
- **Break condition:** If the stream is balanced (all classes have similar repetition probability), ER-FA offers no advantage over standard replay methods.

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim:** Knowledge accumulation reduces the gap between naive and replay strategies over time in CIR scenarios.
- **Mechanism:** As the stream progresses, naive fine-tuning improves its accuracy on missing classes due to natural repetition, while replay accuracy saturates. The convergence of the two strategies indicates that repetition alone can provide substantial forgetting mitigation.
- **Core assumption:** The stream is long enough and has non-zero repetition probability for all classes.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [section 4.2] "Accuracy Gap Between Naive and Replay Decreases Over Time... the accuracy of Naive increases, closing the gap between the two strategies from around 25% in experience 100 to 7% in experience 500."
  - [section 4.2] "The Role of Repetition... While the Naive strategy gains a lot from increased repetition, the replay strategy saturates after some point for higher repetitions."
  - [corpus] "Replay-free Online Continual Learning with Self-Supervised MultiPatches" — explores alternatives to replay, suggesting repetition can substitute for replay in some settings.
- **Break condition:** If repetition probability is extremely low or the stream ends before sufficient accumulation, naive strategies will still suffer catastrophic forgetting.

## Architecture Onboarding

- **Component map:**
  - Gslot -> Gsamp -> ER-FA -> Evaluation
  - Gslot: Slot-based generator enforcing constraints on class occurrences; produces CIR streams from a dataset with parameters N (experiences) and K (slots per experience).
  - Gsamp: Sampling-based generator producing arbitrarily long streams with controllable repetition via Pf(S) (first occurrence distribution) and Pr (repetition probabilities).
  - ER-FA: Frequency-aware replay strategy with online buffer quota adjustment based on observation counts per class.
  - ER-CB/ER-RS: Baseline replay strategies (class-balanced and reservoir sampling) for comparison.
  - Naive: Simple fine-tuning baseline to measure knowledge accumulation.

- **Critical path:**
  1. Choose dataset and define CIR scenario parameters (N, K or Pf(S), Pr).
  2. Generate stream using Gslot or Gsamp.
  3. Train model on each experience, applying chosen CL strategy (Naive, ER-CB, ER-RS, ER-FA).
  4. Evaluate accuracy per class and overall metrics (TA, MCA, SCA).
  5. Analyze results and model dynamics (weights, CKA, interpolation).

- **Design tradeoffs:**
  - Gslot limits stream length to the number of classes but guarantees each sample is seen exactly once; Gsamp allows infinite streams but may repeat samples.
  - ER-FA adapts buffer allocation online, improving performance on infrequent classes but adding computational overhead; CB and RS are simpler but suboptimal for unbalanced streams.
  - Longer streams with higher repetition improve knowledge accumulation but increase training time.

- **Failure signatures:**
  - If buffer size is too small, even ER-FA may fail to retain infrequent classes.
  - If repetition probability is too low, knowledge accumulation will be minimal and naive strategies will still forget.
  - If Pr is uniform but the stream is short, the advantage of ER-FA over CB/RS diminishes.

- **First 3 experiments:**
  1. **Transition from CI to DI using Gslot:** Vary K from 1 to C and observe accuracy trends for Naive, LwF, EWC, ER-RS, A-GEM.
  2. **Long stream with fixed repetition (Gsamp):** Set N=500, Pf(S) geometric with p=0.2, Pr=0.2 for all classes; compare Naive vs ER-CB accuracy over time.
  3. **Unbalanced bi-modal stream:** Use Gsamp with Pr=[0.9,0.1] for frequent/infrequent classes, vary fraction of infrequent classes, and compare ER-FA, ER-CB, ER-RS, and Naive on TA and MCA.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- Question: How does the performance of ER-FA compare to other replay strategies when the repetition probabilities of classes are not bi-modal but follow a more complex distribution?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper tests ER-FA on bi-modal unbalanced scenarios but does not explore other distribution types for repetition probabilities.
- Why unresolved: The paper only evaluates ER-FA on bi-modal scenarios with two distinct repetition probability modes (0.1 for infrequent classes and 0.9 for frequent classes).
- What evidence would resolve it: Experiments comparing ER-FA's performance to other replay strategies on scenarios with more complex repetition probability distributions (e.g., multi-modal, exponential, or custom distributions).

### Open Question 2
- Question: What is the optimal buffer size for ER-FA in different CIR scenarios, and how does it scale with the number of classes and experiences?
- Basis in paper: [inferred] The paper mentions that ER-FA outperforms other replay strategies, but does not provide an analysis of how the buffer size affects its performance across different scenarios.
- Why unresolved: The paper does not explore the relationship between buffer size, number of classes, and the number of experiences in determining the optimal buffer size for ER-FA.
- What evidence would resolve it: Experiments varying the buffer size and analyzing its impact on ER-FA's performance across different CIR scenarios with varying numbers of classes and experiences.

### Open Question 3
- Question: How does ER-FA perform when combined with other CL strategies, such as regularization-based methods (e.g., LwF or EWC)?
- Basis in paper: [inferred] The paper focuses on evaluating ER-FA as a standalone replay strategy and does not explore its combination with other CL strategies.
- Why unresolved: The paper does not investigate the potential benefits of combining ER-FA with other CL strategies to further improve performance in CIR scenarios.
- What evidence would resolve it: Experiments combining ER-FA with regularization-based CL strategies and comparing their performance to standalone ER-FA or the combined strategies.

## Limitations

- The study's findings are based on synthetic stream generation, which may not fully capture the complexity and variability of real-world data streams.
- The proposed ER-FA strategy requires careful tuning of buffer allocation parameters and may not generalize seamlessly to all CL scenarios.
- The evaluation focuses on standard image classification datasets, leaving open questions about performance on other modalities or larger-scale problems.

## Confidence

- **High confidence**: The mechanism of knowledge accumulation through natural repetition in CIR scenarios is well-supported by empirical evidence and aligns with related work on repetition in continual learning.
- **Medium confidence**: The effectiveness of ER-FA in unbalanced CIR streams is demonstrated, but its performance relative to other replay strategies may vary depending on stream characteristics and buffer size.
- **Medium confidence**: The claim that naive fine-tuning can accumulate knowledge over time in CIR scenarios is supported, but the extent of this effect depends on repetition probability and stream length.

## Next Checks

1. **Real-world stream validation**: Test the proposed methods on a real-world dataset with inherent repetition patterns (e.g., web crawl data) to validate the effectiveness of natural repetition and ER-FA in practical settings.

2. **Ablation study on buffer size**: Systematically vary buffer size for ER-FA and baseline replay strategies to quantify the trade-off between memory footprint and performance, especially for infrequent classes.

3. **Cross-modality generalization**: Evaluate the proposed methods on non-image datasets (e.g., speech or text) to assess the robustness of knowledge accumulation and ER-FA across different data modalities.
