# IfQA: A Dataset for Open-domain Question Answering under Counterfactual Presuppositions

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2305.14010
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.14010
- Authors: 
- Reference count: 18
- Introduces IfQA, the first large-scale dataset for open-domain QA under counterfactual presuppositions with 3,800+ questions

## Executive Summary
This paper introduces IfQA, the first large-scale dataset for open-domain question answering under counterfactual presuppositions. Each question in the dataset is based on an "if" clause, requiring models to reason about imagined situations that may go against the facts built into their parameters. The dataset contains over 3,800 questions annotated by crowdworkers on relevant Wikipedia passages. The authors evaluate state-of-the-art closed-book and open-book models on the IfQA dataset, finding that while retrieval-augmented approaches outperform closed-book methods, all models lag behind human performance. The best results are achieved by combining passage retrieval with large language model reasoning, demonstrating the importance of both accurate retrieval and strong reasoning capabilities for this task.

## Method Summary
The IfQA dataset is constructed by collecting questions from Wikipedia passages containing "if" clauses, then having crowdworkers paraphrase them into questions about hypothetical scenarios. The dataset contains 3,824 questions on 27,572,699 preprocessed Wikipedia passages. For evaluation, a retrieve-then-read pipeline is used with BM25 or DPR for retrieval and FiD or GPT-3 for reading. GPT-3 is used with chain-of-thought prompting for reasoning. Exact Match (EM) and F1 score are used for end-QA evaluation, while Recall@K is used for retrieval performance.

## Key Results
- Open-book models outperform closed-book models on IfQA, demonstrating the importance of external evidence
- BM25 outperforms DPR for retrieval on IfQA, suggesting dense retrieval struggles with counterfactual language
- GPT-3 with chain-of-thought prompting achieves the best performance when combined with BM25 retrieval
- All models, including GPT-3, perform significantly worse than human performance on the dataset

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: The dataset's design specifically forces models to handle retrieval of facts that are contradicted by the counterfactual presupposition.
- Mechanism: Questions contain an "if" clause that establishes a false premise, requiring models to first retrieve factual evidence about the real world and then reason about the counterfactual scenario.
- Core assumption: Models cannot rely solely on memorized knowledge from pre-training and must engage with external evidence.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "Such questions require models to go beyond retrieving direct factual knowledge from the Web: they must identify the right information to retrieve and reason about an imagined situation that may even go against the facts built into their parameters."
  - [section] "For example, in the question 'If the sea level continues to rise at an accelerated rate, which country is likely to be submerged first?', the targeted passage for retrieval might not directly mention 'sea level', 'rise', and 'submerged', where the question is essentially to ask 'which country is the lowest-lying one in the world'."

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: The dataset exposes the limitations of semantic matching retrieval methods when faced with counterfactual language.
- Mechanism: Dense retrieval methods based on semantic matching struggle with the discrepancy between counterfactual presuppositions and factual evidence, leading to poor retrieval performance.
- Core assumption: Semantic matching cannot adequately capture the semantic shift required when reasoning about counterfactuals.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [section] "When using large language models for in-context learning, more input passages lead to an increase in the number of input tokens, limiting the number of in-context demonstrations."
  - [corpus] "Found 25 related papers (using 8). Average neighbor FMR=0.502, average citations=0.0." (Weak corpus evidence, indicating limited related work on counterfactual QA)

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: Combining retrieval with LLM reasoning provides the best performance on the dataset.
- Mechanism: The entire pipeline can enjoy both the factual evidence provided by the retriever and the powerful deductive reasoning ability of the large language model.
- Core assumption: LLMs have sufficient reasoning capabilities to handle counterfactual scenarios when provided with the right factual context.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "The best results are achieved by combining passage retrieval with large language model reasoning, demonstrating the importance of both accurate retrieval and strong reasoning capabilities for this task."
  - [section] "We found that the combination of BM25 (as retriever) and GPT-3 (as reader) can achieve the best model performance on the IfQA dataset."

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map: Question → Retriever (BM25/DPR) → Reader (FiD/GPT-3) → Answer generation → Evaluation (EM/F1)

- Critical path: Question → Retriever → Reader → Answer generation → Evaluation

- Design tradeoffs:
  - Closed-book vs. open-book models: Closed-book models like GPT-3 struggle without external evidence, while open-book models require efficient retrieval.
  - Dense vs. sparse retrieval: Dense retrieval methods like DPR struggle with counterfactual language, while sparse methods like BM25 perform better.
  - Reader complexity: Simple readers like FiD lag behind LLM readers like GPT-3, but require more computational resources.

- Failure signatures:
  - Poor retrieval performance: Models fail to retrieve relevant passages containing the answer.
  - Incorrect reasoning: Models fail to properly reason about the counterfactual scenario.
  - Hallucination: Models generate incorrect answers based on memorized knowledge rather than retrieved evidence.

- First 3 experiments:
  1. Evaluate the performance of BM25 vs. DPR on the dataset to assess the impact of retrieval method on counterfactual QA.
  2. Compare the performance of FiD vs. GPT-3 as readers to evaluate the impact of reader complexity on counterfactual reasoning.
  3. Analyze the types of errors made by different models (e.g., retrieval errors vs. reasoning errors) to identify areas for improvement.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- Question: How can retrieval methods be improved to better handle counterfactual presuppositions in open-domain QA?
- Basis in paper: Explicit - The paper discusses the challenges of retrieving relevant passages when questions contain counterfactual presuppositions, stating that "current retrieval methods based on keyword matching (e.g., BM25) easier because more keywords are included in the question, but make latent semantic matching (e.g., DPR) methods harder because a single embedding vector cannot well represent enough Information."
- Why unresolved: The paper shows that existing retrieval methods struggle with counterfactual presuppositions, but does not provide a clear solution or direction for improvement.
- What evidence would resolve it: Development and evaluation of new retrieval methods specifically designed to handle counterfactual presuppositions, demonstrating improved performance on the IfQA dataset.

### Open Question 2
- Question: Can large language models be further improved to better reason about counterfactual scenarios in open-domain QA without relying on external evidence?
- Basis in paper: Explicit - The paper evaluates GPT-3 with chain-of-thought prompting, showing that it can improve performance but still lags behind retrieve-then-read models. It states that "LLMs are closed-book models, they still lack non-parametric knowledge. Therefore, their overall performance still lags behind state-of-the-art retrieve-then-read methods."
- Why unresolved: The paper demonstrates that current large language models can be improved with chain-of-thought prompting but still struggle with counterfactual reasoning compared to models with access to external evidence.
- What evidence would resolve it: Development of large language models or prompting strategies that can effectively reason about counterfactual scenarios without relying on external evidence, achieving performance comparable to retrieve-then-read models on the IfQA dataset.

### Open Question 3
- Question: How can the IfQA dataset be expanded to cover more diverse types of questions beyond event-based counterfactuals?
- Basis in paper: Inferred - The paper mentions that "The main limitation of IfQA dataset is that it only covers event-based questions, due to the nature of creating counterfactual presuppositions."
- Why unresolved: The current IfQA dataset is limited to event-based questions, which restricts its applicability for evaluating general open-domain QA models.
- What evidence would resolve it: Creation of new datasets or expansion of IfQA to include counterfactual questions about other domains (e.g., abstract concepts, scientific facts) while maintaining the quality and difficulty level of the existing dataset.

## Limitations

- The IfQA dataset is limited to event-based counterfactual questions, restricting its coverage of counterfactual reasoning scenarios
- The evaluation metrics (EM and F1) may not fully capture the nuances of counterfactual reasoning quality
- The crowdworker annotation process may introduce biases in question selection and answer formulation

## Confidence

- High confidence: The dataset successfully captures the core challenge of counterfactual QA, as evidenced by consistent performance gaps between human and model baselines across multiple model architectures.
- Medium confidence: The conclusion that retrieval-augmented approaches outperform closed-book methods is well-supported, though the specific superiority of BM25 over DPR may depend on implementation details and hyperparameter choices.
- Medium confidence: The claim that GPT-3 with chain-of-thought prompting provides superior reasoning capabilities requires further validation, as the specific prompting strategy details are not fully specified.
