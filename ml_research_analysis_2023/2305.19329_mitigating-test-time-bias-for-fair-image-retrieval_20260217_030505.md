# Mitigating Test-Time Bias for Fair Image Retrieval

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2305.19329
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.19329
- Reference count: 31
- Key outcome: PBM achieves lowest bias (AbsBias@100 ≈ 0.06) while maintaining competitive recall in fair image retrieval

## Executive Summary
This work tackles fairness in text-based image retrieval by addressing the mismatch between model-level debiasing and test-time bias in the retrieval set. The authors propose a post-hoc bias mitigation (PBM) method that balances gender and race representation by selecting top-ranked images from balanced subsets during retrieval. Evaluated on real-world web image search and large-scale image-text datasets, PBM achieves the lowest bias (e.g., AbsBias@100 ≈ 0.06 on Occupation 1) while maintaining competitive recall performance. The approach also generalizes well to complex queries and can be adapted to other information retrieval tasks with accessible demographic attributes.

## Method Summary
The authors propose a post-hoc bias mitigation (PBM) method that addresses fairness in text-based image retrieval by balancing gender and race representation during the retrieval process. Rather than modifying the underlying retrieval model, PBM operates at test time by selecting top-ranked images from balanced demographic subsets. The method identifies demographic attributes for images in the retrieval set and ensures proportional representation across groups during ranking. This approach effectively reduces bias without requiring retraining or model modification, making it applicable to existing retrieval systems.

## Key Results
- PBM achieves the lowest bias scores across multiple datasets, with AbsBias@100 ≈ 0.06 on Occupation 1
- The method maintains competitive recall performance while reducing demographic bias
- PBM generalizes well to complex queries beyond simple demographic categories
- The approach can be adapted to other information retrieval tasks where demographic attributes are accessible

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
PBM works by addressing the fundamental mismatch between debiased models and biased retrieval sets. While retrieval models can be trained to reduce bias, the actual images available for retrieval often contain demographic imbalances. PBM bridges this gap by ensuring that the final ranked list reflects balanced representation across demographic groups. By selecting from pre-balanced subsets during the retrieval process, the method guarantees that users see a fair distribution of images regardless of underlying biases in the image corpus.

## Architecture Onboarding

Component Map: Image Corpus -> Demographic Attribute Detection -> Subpopulation Creation -> Balanced Subset Ranking -> PBM Selection -> Final Rankings

Critical Path: The core pipeline involves detecting demographic attributes for all images, creating balanced subpopulations, ranking within each subpopulation, and then selecting from these balanced pools to construct final results that maintain both relevance and demographic fairness.

Design Tradeoffs: The method trades computational overhead at retrieval time for improved fairness, requiring demographic attribute processing and multiple ranking operations. This post-hoc approach avoids model retraining but depends on accurate attribute detection and balanced subset availability.

Failure Signatures: The method may fail when demographic attributes are missing or incorrectly detected, when balanced subsets lack sufficient high-quality images, or when the demographic distribution in the corpus is extremely skewed, making proportional representation impossible.

## Limitations
- Requires access to demographic attributes (gender and race) for images, which may not always be available or may raise privacy concerns
- Evaluation focuses primarily on binary gender (male/female) and two racial categories (White/Black), potentially oversimplifying demographic complexity
- Effectiveness for complex queries beyond evaluated scenarios remains to be thoroughly validated
- Scalability to extremely large-scale retrieval systems and computational overhead during real-time retrieval are not fully characterized

## Confidence
- High confidence: Empirical results demonstrating reduced bias (AbsBias@100 ≈ 0.06) while maintaining recall performance are well-supported
- Medium confidence: Generalizability claim to other information retrieval tasks is reasonable but based on theoretical reasoning
- Medium confidence: Method's effectiveness for complex queries is suggested but would benefit from more comprehensive testing
