# Beyond Summarization: Designing AI Support for Real-World Expository Writing Tasks

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2304.02623
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2304.02623
- Authors: 
- Reference count: 39

## Executive Summary
This position paper addresses the gap in AI support for expository writing, which involves synthesizing external documents to produce new knowledge rather than merely summarizing existing content. The paper argues that current AI writing assistants are designed primarily for creative and collaborative writing, leaving expository writing tasks underserved. By analyzing the unique characteristics of expository writing - frequent occurrence, cognitive demands, and need for domain expertise - the authors propose a modular approach to AI support that enhances author capabilities while maintaining control over thinking and ideas.

## Method Summary
The paper proposes a modular AI support system for expository writing consisting of three components: reading and evidence collection assistance, information synthesis support, and text composition facilitation. The method involves implementing document discovery and comprehension systems with evidence verification, developing information synthesis modules using language models for automatic grouping and semantic search, and creating text composition interfaces that generate short, evidence-aware text segments. The approach emphasizes maintaining author control while reducing cognitive load through incremental, evidence-aware generation rather than complete paragraph-level outputs.

## Key Results
- Identifies expository writing as a distinct category from summarization with unique AI support needs
- Proposes three modular components for AI support: reading/evidence collection, synthesis, and writing
- Highlights the importance of incremental text generation to reduce hallucination risks and maintain author control

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: Modular AI support components for reading, synthesis, and writing reduce cognitive load while maintaining author control.
- Mechanism: By breaking expository writing into three distinct phases with tailored AI support for each, the system allows authors to focus on high-level thinking while offloading mechanical tasks like evidence extraction and text generation.
- Core assumption: Authors benefit from clear boundaries between AI assistance types and prefer control over when and how AI intervenes.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "We sketch three components for AI support design: 1) assisting reading and evidence collection, 2) supporting information synthesis, and 3) facilitating text composition."
  - [section] "We argue that there are three components: 1) supporting the reading and evidence-seeking for correct and efficient understanding of the source, 2) assisting information synthesis and the production of new knowledge and ideas, and 3) facilitating text composition to communicate relevant evidence and insights."
  - [corpus] Weak evidence - no direct citations found in related papers for this specific three-component modular approach.
- Break condition: If authors find the modular boundaries too rigid or the transitions between components disruptive to their workflow.

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: AI support for reading should focus on evidence extraction and verification rather than full summarization.
- Mechanism: Instead of generating complete summaries that might hallucinate or bias authors, the system extracts key facts and links them back to source documents for author verification.
- Core assumption: Authors need to maintain direct engagement with source material to avoid bias and ensure accuracy.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "Authors' reading and understanding the source still plays a central role, and designing for reading for expository writing should focus on the augmentation of reading and extraction of key information from source documents [16], but at the same time providing support for verifying the extracted evidence."
  - [section] "Ziegler et al. [35] shows that recent instruction-tuned models are merely 'smart copiers' when performing summarization, and overly trusting the model outputs risks biasing authors' views and may lead to authors collecting inaccurate evidence."
  - [corpus] No direct evidence found - this represents a novel design choice not reflected in the corpus neighbors.
- Break condition: If authors find the verification process too time-consuming compared to traditional reading methods.

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: Text generation should be invoked incrementally rather than generating complete paragraphs to reduce errors and maintain author control.
- Mechanism: Authors provide evidence and writing intent to generate short text segments (1-2 sentences) that they can integrate into their writing, reducing hallucination risks and verification costs.
- Core assumption: Short, evidence-aware generations are less likely to hallucinate and easier for authors to verify than long completions.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "An instance of model generation could be relatively short, e.g., one or two sentences, with the authors calling it in multiple turns. Compared to generating long completions at one time, e.g., a whole paragraph, we speculate that this could decrease the possibility of erroneous and biased generation, and decrease the cost of verification by the authors."
  - [section] "The latest language models have demonstrated strong capabilities in generating fluent and high-quality text, and some studies suggest the generations are even on par with freelance writers for certain summarization tasks [34]. It is promising to incorporate language models to support text composition, but several design choices need to be considered to reduce the risk of generation errors and biasing authors' opinions [22]."
  - [corpus] Weak evidence - while related papers mention text generation, none specifically address this incremental generation approach.
- Break condition: If authors find the need to call generation multiple times disrupts their writing flow.

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map: Reading Support (evidence extraction/verification) -> Synthesis Support (information organization/idea generation) -> Writing Support (incremental text generation)
- Critical path: Reading → Synthesis → Writing, with iterative loops back to earlier stages as new insights emerge. AI support intensity varies by stage, with more control in early reading phases.
- Design tradeoffs: Balance between automation and author control, verification overhead vs. efficiency gains, modular boundaries vs. fluid workflow integration.
- Failure signatures: Author frustration with verification requirements, AI suggestions that don't align with author intent, disruption of natural writing flow by modular boundaries.
- First 3 experiments:
  1. Implement basic evidence extraction with source linking and measure author verification time vs. traditional reading
  2. Test incremental text generation (1-2 sentences) with different evidence prompts to find optimal generation triggers
  3. Compare user satisfaction between full summarization support vs. evidence extraction approach for reading tasks

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- Question: How can AI systems effectively support the reading and evidence collection phase of expository writing without overwhelming or biasing the author?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper discusses the challenges of using language models for document summarization due to hallucination and the risk of biasing authors' views.
- Why unresolved: Current language models are prone to hallucination and may not always provide accurate summaries, leading to potential bias in the authors' evidence collection process.
- What evidence would resolve it: Studies comparing different AI support systems for reading and evidence collection in expository writing, measuring their impact on authors' accuracy, efficiency, and bias.

### Open Question 2
- Question: How can AI systems effectively support the information synthesis phase of expository writing, including suggesting connections between evidence and discovering relevant topics?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper mentions the potential for AI to assist in information synthesis by proposing connections between evidence and suggesting relevant topics, but notes the importance of allowing authors to verify and ground model-generated ideas.
- Why unresolved: While AI can assist in organizing and suggesting connections, the challenge lies in ensuring that the generated ideas are accurate, relevant, and do not replace the authors' own thinking and contemplation.
- What evidence would resolve it: Studies evaluating the effectiveness of AI support systems in assisting information synthesis, measuring their impact on authors' ability to generate new ideas and discover connections between evidence.

### Open Question 3
- Question: How can AI systems effectively support the text composition phase of expository writing, ensuring that generated text is accurate, unbiased, and evidence-aware?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper discusses the challenges of using language models for text composition due to the risk of generation errors and biasing authors' opinions, suggesting the need for evidence-aware and explicit generation.
- Why unresolved: While language models have shown strong capabilities in text generation, the challenge lies in ensuring that the generated text is accurate, unbiased, and aligned with the authors' intended message and evidence.
- What evidence would resolve it: Studies comparing different AI support systems for text composition in expository writing, measuring their impact on authors' ability to produce accurate, unbiased, and evidence-aware text.

## Limitations

- Specific technical implementations for the three modular components are not detailed
- No clear evaluation metrics or success criteria are defined for the proposed AI support system
