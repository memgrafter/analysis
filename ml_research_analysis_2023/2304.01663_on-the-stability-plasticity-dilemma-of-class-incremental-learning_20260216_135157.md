# On the Stability-Plasticity Dilemma of Class-Incremental Learning

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2304.01663
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2304.01663
- Reference count: 40
- One-line primary result: Most CIL algorithms show minimal feature improvement across incremental stages, indicating excessive stability at the cost of plasticity.

## Executive Summary
This paper investigates the stability-plasticity trade-off in class-incremental learning (CIL) algorithms by analyzing feature representations across incremental stages. Using ImageNet-1K, the authors evaluate feature extractors from multiple CIL algorithms through classifier retraining and CKA similarity analysis. Surprisingly, most CIL models (LUCIR, SSIL, POD, AANet, AFC) show minimal improvement in feature quality across incremental stages, suggesting they prioritize stability over plasticity. The authors propose two methods, pDER and Exploit, to address this issue, demonstrating that standard CIL metrics may be misleading and that stronger feature representation learning is crucial for true continual learning progress.

## Method Summary
The authors analyze CIL algorithms by freezing feature extractors and retraining classifiers on full ImageNet-1K data, using accuracy as a proxy for feature quality. They measure feature similarity across incremental stages using CKA and visualize feature distributions with t-SNE. The study trains models incrementally on ImageNet-1K with 500 initial classes followed by 5-10 stages of 100-50 classes each. Five CIL algorithms (LUCIR, SSIL, POD, AANet, AFC) are compared against DER and a naive baseline. Two new methods are proposed: pDER (a scalable variant of DER) and Exploit (a simple method that freezes the base feature extractor).

## Key Results
- Most CIL algorithms show minimal feature quality improvement across incremental stages despite maintaining high accuracy.
- Feature extractors trained on initial classes remain sufficiently discriminative for later classes without significant updates.
- Classifier retraining reveals true discriminative power of feature extractors, independent of classifier bias.
- Proposed Exploit method outperforms many CIL algorithms on standard metrics while maintaining a static feature extractor.

- Core assumption: The CIL algorithms employ regularization techniques (e.g., distillation, fixed feature extractors) that explicitly or implicitly enforce feature similarity between old and new models.

## Architecture Onboarding

### Component map:
Feature Extractor (Fi) -> Classifier (Gi) -> CIL Algorithm

### Critical path:
1. Train initial model M0 on D0.
2. For each incremental stage i, initialize Mi with Mi-1 parameters.
3. Train Mi on Di with regularization techniques to manage stability and plasticity.
4. Evaluate feature representations by freezing Fi and retraining Gi on full data.

### Design tradeoffs:
- Stability vs. Plasticity: Enforcing high feature similarity across stages improves stability but limits plasticity.
- Scalability vs. Performance: Methods like DER that add new feature extractors improve performance but reduce scalability.
- Computational Cost vs. Accuracy: Freezing feature extractors reduces computation but may limit accuracy gains.

### Failure signatures:
- High CKA values across stages with minimal accuracy improvement indicate excessive stability and insufficient plasticity.
- Significant accuracy drops on old classes during incremental training indicate catastrophic forgetting.
- Low accuracy on new classes despite high stability indicates insufficient plasticity.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- Question: Can we reach an optimal equilibrium point between forgetting and generalization in CIL without adding new model parameters?
- Basis in paper: The paper references Raghavan et al.'s proof that a stable equilibrium point between forgetting and generalization exists, but notes that current low-plasticity methods may reach suboptimal equilibria. This is explicitly posed as a key question for future work.
- Why unresolved: While theoretical existence of an optimal equilibrium is proven, no practical method has been demonstrated to achieve it without architectural modifications like parameter isolation (DER) or adding new parameters.
- What evidence would resolve it: A CIL algorithm that matches or exceeds the performance of parameter-isolation methods (like DER) while maintaining a fixed model architecture, demonstrated through controlled experiments on standard CIL benchmarks.

### Open Question 2
- Question: How does the stability-plasticity trade-off in feature representations affect CIL performance on more complex, real-world datasets?
- Basis in paper: The analysis is conducted on ImageNet-1K with ResNet-18, showing that most CIL algorithms exhibit minimal plasticity. The paper suggests this might be a dataset-specific phenomenon but doesn't explore more complex scenarios.
- Why unresolved: The study is limited to a single dataset and architecture, and real-world applications often involve more complex data distributions and larger-scale problems.
- What evidence would resolve it: Comparative analysis of CIL algorithms on diverse datasets (e.g., large-scale video datasets, multimodal data) with varying levels of task similarity and complexity, measuring both feature representation quality and final task performance.

### Open Question 3
- Question: Are current CIL evaluation metrics sufficient to capture the true progress in continual learning, or do we need new metrics that better reflect feature representation learning?
- Basis in paper: The authors argue that traditional metrics like average incremental accuracy and final accuracy are misleading, as they don't capture how much the model has actually learned continually. Their "Exploit" method outperforms many CIL algorithms on these metrics despite having a static feature extractor.
- Why unresolved: The paper demonstrates the limitations of current metrics but doesn't propose a comprehensive alternative framework for CIL evaluation.
- What evidence would resolve it: Development and validation of new evaluation metrics that correlate strongly with actual continual learning progress, tested across multiple CIL algorithms and benchmark datasets.

## Limitations

- The analysis focuses primarily on ImageNet-1K, which may limit generalizability to other datasets or domains.
- The CKA similarity metric, while widely used, may not capture all aspects of feature representation quality.
- The study evaluates only a specific set of CIL algorithms, and results may vary with different architectural choices or regularization techniques.

## Confidence

- Confidence in the main claims is High, particularly regarding the excessive stability observed in most CIL algorithms and the effectiveness of the proposed Exploit method. The classifier retraining and CKA analysis provide strong evidence for these conclusions.
- Confidence in the mechanism explanations is Medium, as the exact reasons for the observed stability may vary depending on implementation details of different CIL algorithms.
