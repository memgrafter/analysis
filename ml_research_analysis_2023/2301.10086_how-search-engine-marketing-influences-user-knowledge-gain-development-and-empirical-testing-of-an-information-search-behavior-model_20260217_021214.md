# How search engine marketing influences user knowledge gain: Development and empirical testing of an information search behavior model

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2301.10086
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2301.10086
- Reference count: 22
- This doctoral research project develops and empirically tests an information search behavior model on the influence of search engine marketing (SEM) on user knowledge gain.

## Executive Summary
This doctoral research project aims to develop and empirically test a model examining how search engine marketing (SEM) influences user knowledge gain during information search tasks. The study combines an online experiment with expert evaluation to investigate the relationships between SEM measures (SEO and PSM), information quality, search engine literacy, domain knowledge, and knowledge gain. Using a representative sample of 1,000-2,000 German internet users across health, politics, environment, and consumer protection domains, the research tests six hypotheses about how SEM affects information quality and subsequent learning outcomes.

## Method Summary
The study employs a mixed-methods approach combining an online experiment with expert evaluation. Participants complete search tasks across four domains while their interactions are tracked. Pre- and post-task knowledge tests measure learning outcomes, while expert evaluations assess the representational and intrinsic information quality of search results. The experiment manipulates SERP conditions including organic-only, organic with ads, and varying levels of SEO probability. Data collection includes demographics, domain knowledge, and search engine literacy surveys.

## Key Results
- Development of a novel information search behavior model incorporating SEM influences
- Integration of expert evaluation methodology for assessing information quality
- Comprehensive experimental design testing six hypotheses about SEM's impact on knowledge gain
- Use of representative sample size (N=1,000-2,000) for German internet users

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: SEM measures (SEO and PSM) directly influence the information quality of search results presented to users.
- Mechanism: SEO and PSM professionals optimize content to rank higher in organic or paid search results, altering the visibility of information. This optimization is driven by commercial, political, or other motives, which can bias the information quality of the presented results.
- Core assumption: The optimization techniques used in SEO and PSM inherently affect the intrinsic and representational information quality of the content.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "Due to these motivations, the question arises to what extent these intentions influence the information quality of optimized (SEO) or paid (PSM) results."
  - [section] "SEM measures are driven by commercial, political, or other motives, which suggest an influence on the documents' quality and thus on user knowledge gain."
  - [corpus] Weak evidence - no direct mention of SEM's influence on information quality in related papers.
- Break condition: If expert evaluations find no significant difference in information quality between optimized and non-optimized results.

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: User search engine literacy affects their ability to recognize and select high-quality information in the presence of SEM.
- Mechanism: Users with higher awareness of SEM (both SEO and PSM) are better equipped to critically evaluate search results, potentially mitigating the influence of biased information.
- Core assumption: Increased awareness of SEM practices enables users to make more informed decisions about the credibility and relevance of search results.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "This is particularly true for SEO, as a representative survey of German Internet users shows. While around 68% of users are aware of Google's advertising business model, only 43% know that results can be influenced beyond the placement of ads, i.e., through SEO measures."
  - [section] "Users with higher SEO awareness are expected to be guided less strongly by the position of an (organic) result and thus consider more organic result ranks (e.g., results below the fold)."
  - [corpus] Weak evidence - no direct mention of user search engine literacy in related papers.
- Break condition: If experimental results show no correlation between search engine literacy and selection behavior.

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: The presence of SEM measures creates a feedback loop between information quality and user knowledge gain.
- Mechanism: SEM influences the information quality of search results, which in turn affects what users learn from their search interactions. This learning can then influence future search behaviors and expectations.
- Core assumption: The quality of information encountered during search tasks directly impacts the knowledge gained by users.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "Both questions are not considered by existing models of information behavior. Hence, the doctoral research project described in this paper aims to develop and empirically test an information search behavior model on the influence of SEM on user knowledge gain."
  - [section] "Due to the variety of intentions underlying the use of SEM measures, the question arises to what extent these intentions influence the information quality of optimized (SEO) or paid (PSM) results."
  - [corpus] Weak evidence - no direct mention of feedback loops between SEM, information quality, and knowledge gain in related papers.
- Break condition: If the study finds no significant relationship between information quality and knowledge gain.

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map: Task creation -> Expert evaluation -> Experiment execution -> Data analysis -> Hypothesis testing
- Critical path: Task creation → Expert evaluation → Experiment execution → Data analysis → Hypothesis testing
- Design tradeoffs:
  - Sample size vs. experiment duration and cost
  - Expert evaluation depth vs. scalability
  - Task complexity control vs. real-world relevance
- Failure signatures:
  - Low participant engagement in online experiment
  - Inconsistent expert evaluations of information quality
  - Inability to detect significant effects due to sample size or experimental design
- First 3 experiments:
  1. Pilot study with a small sample to test task clarity and survey effectiveness
  2. Expert evaluation of a subset of search results to refine quality assessment criteria
  3. Full-scale online experiment with representative sample, focusing on data collection and preliminary analysis

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- Question: Does higher search engine literacy (both PSM and SEO awareness) actually lead to selection of more diverse search result ranks and fewer advertisements?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] H1a and H1b in Figure 1 and text: "The higher the PSM awareness, the fewer ads are selected" and "The higher the SEO awareness, the wider the range of selected organic results"
- Why unresolved: This hypothesis is stated but not yet tested - the study is still in preparation phase
- What evidence would resolve it: Experimental results showing selection behavior patterns of users with different levels of search engine literacy awareness

### Open Question 2
- Question: Does domain knowledge reduce knowledge gain, as suggested by previous literature and H2b?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] H2b states "The greater the domain knowledge, the lower the knowledge gain" with citation to Gadiraju et al. [7]
- Why unresolved: This hypothesis is stated but not yet tested - the study is still in preparation phase
- What evidence would resolve it: Pre- and post-task knowledge test results comparing knowledge gain across users with different levels of domain knowledge

### Open Question 3
- Question: How do opposing information qualities (e.g., low intrinsic but high representational quality) interact to affect user knowledge gain?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] RQ6 specifically asks about interaction effects of variables, including "how knowledge gain is affected by documents with opposing qualities"
- Why unresolved: This is an explicitly stated research question that the study aims to investigate but has not yet done so
- What evidence would resolve it: Experimental results showing knowledge gain patterns when users select documents with varying combinations of representational and intrinsic quality ratings

## Limitations
- Reliance on self-reported knowledge gain measurements may introduce response bias
- Expert evaluation of information quality across diverse domains may not capture domain-specific nuances
- Generalizability to non-German populations or different search contexts is uncertain

## Confidence
- High confidence in the experimental methodology and data collection procedures
- Medium confidence in the SEM influence mechanisms due to limited direct evidence in the corpus
- Low confidence in the generalizability of findings to non-German populations or different search contexts
