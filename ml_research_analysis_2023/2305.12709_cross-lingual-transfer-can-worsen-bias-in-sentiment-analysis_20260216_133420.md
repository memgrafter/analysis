# Cross-lingual Transfer Can Worsen Bias in Sentiment Analysis

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2305.12709
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.12709
- **Authors:** Multiple
- **Reference count:** 27
- **Key outcome:** Cross-lingual transfer generally increases gender and racial bias in sentiment analysis compared to monolingual transfer, primarily due to multilingual pre-training data.

## Executive Summary
This paper investigates whether cross-lingual transfer in sentiment analysis increases bias compared to monolingual transfer. Using counterfactual evaluation across five languages (Japanese, Chinese, Spanish, German, and English), the authors compare gender and racial biases between monolingual transfer models and zero-shot cross-lingual transfer models. The results show that cross-lingual transfer generally increases bias, with racial bias increasing dramatically in some cases. The study finds that the increase in bias is primarily attributable to multilingual pre-training data rather than cross-lingual supervision data. Gender bias is influenced by multilingual pre-training in predictable ways based on the presence or absence of syntactic gender agreement in the target language. Distilled models often reduce bias but not always.

## Method Summary
The study uses counterfactual evaluation to measure bias in sentiment analysis models across five languages. The authors fine-tune both monolingual (bert-base-uncased) and multilingual (mbert-base-uncased) models on sentiment analysis tasks using the Multilingual Amazon Reviews Corpus. They then evaluate these models on counterfactual corpora designed to test gender and racial bias. The evaluation measures the difference in sentiment scores between counterfactual pairs and analyzes mean and variance. The study compares bias between monolingual transfer models, zero-shot cross-lingual transfer models, and distilled versions of both.

## Key Results
- Cross-lingual transfer generally increases both gender and racial bias compared to monolingual transfer
- The increase in bias is primarily attributable to multilingual pre-training data rather than supervision data
- Distilled models often reduce bias but not always, with inconsistent effects across demographics
- Gender bias transfer is influenced by the presence or absence of syntactic gender agreement in target languages

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: Cross-lingual transfer introduces demographic biases through multilingual pre-training data rather than supervision data.
- Mechanism: Multilingual pre-training exposes models to more discriminatory content across languages, which transfers to downstream sentiment tasks.
- Core assumption: Pre-training corpora contain discriminatory content that is learnable and transferable to new tasks.
- Evidence anchors: The increase in bias in cross-lingual transfer is primarily attributable to multilingual pre-training data; multilingual models cause large differences in bias.

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: Gender bias transfer is influenced by the presence or absence of syntactic gender agreement in target languages.
- Mechanism: Multilingual models trained on languages with gender agreement transfer stronger gender signals to target languages without such agreement, and vice versa.
- Core assumption: Grammatical gender agreement in pre-training languages affects internal representations that transfer to downstream tasks.
- Evidence anchors: Gender bias is influenced by multilingual pre-training in predictable ways based on syntactic gender agreement; languages with weak gender agreement show increased gender bias when using cross-lingual transfer.

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: Distilled models often reduce bias but not always, with inconsistent effects across demographics.
- Mechanism: Model compression during distillation may remove some bias-related features while preserving task-relevant ones, but the process is not uniformly beneficial.
- Core assumption: Distillation can selectively remove bias-related information without harming model performance.
- Evidence anchors: Distilled models often reduce bias but not always; for gender bias, distillation tends to dampen bias for monolingual models but frequently worsens bias for crosslingual models.

- Concept: Cross-lingual transfer learning
  - Why needed here: To understand how training on one language affects performance on another

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- Question: What specific mechanisms in cross-lingual transfer cause increased bias, and how do they differ from those in monolingual transfer?
- Basis in paper: The paper hypothesizes that multilingual pre-training data introduces more bias than supervision data, but the exact mechanisms are not fully explored.
- Why unresolved: The paper suggests that multilingual pre-training introduces biases but does not provide a detailed causal analysis of how these biases manifest.
- What evidence would resolve it: A detailed analysis of the training process, including how different types of data influence bias, and causal mediation analysis could clarify the mechanisms.

### Open Question 2
- Question: How do the effects of model distillation on bias vary across different languages and demographic groups?
- Basis in paper: The paper notes that distilled models often reduce bias but not always, with varying effects across languages and demographic groups.
- Why unresolved: The paper observes inconsistent effects of distillation on bias, suggesting that language-specific characteristics might play a role, but does not fully explore these interactions.
- What evidence would resolve it: Comparative studies of distilled models across multiple languages and demographic groups, analyzing the interaction between language characteristics and distillation effects.

### Open Question 3
- Question: How do cross-lingual biases transfer between languages with overlapping racial biases versus those with culturally specific biases?
- Basis in paper: The paper suggests that racial biases are less likely to transfer across languages due to their cultural specificity, but does not explore this in depth.
- Why unresolved: The paper hypothesizes that racial biases may not transfer as easily as gender biases but lacks empirical evidence to support this claim across different language pairs.
- What evidence would resolve it: Empirical studies comparing bias transfer in languages with overlapping versus culturally specific racial biases, using a broader range of languages and cultural contexts.

## Limitations

- The study relies on template-based counterfactual constructions that may not fully capture real-world bias manifestations
- The analysis of syntactic gender agreement's role in bias transfer is based on theoretical expectations rather than systematic linguistic analysis of the specific languages studied
- The paper does not investigate whether certain types of bias might be beneficial for sentiment analysis performance or whether bias reduction could negatively impact task accuracy

## Confidence

**High Confidence:** The core finding that cross-lingual transfer generally increases bias compared to monolingual transfer is well-supported by empirical results across multiple languages and bias types.

**Medium Confidence:** The specific mechanisms by which multilingual pre-training increases bias, particularly the relationship between syntactic gender agreement and bias transfer, are plausible but rely on theoretical assumptions.

**Low Confidence:** The claim that bias transfer is primarily attributable to pre-training rather than supervision data is based on correlational evidence without definitively establishing causation.

## Next Checks

1. **Controlled Pre-training Experiments:** Train models with identical architectures but different pre-training corpora (one with bias content removed) to directly test whether pre-training data composition is the causal factor in bias transfer.

2. **Linguistic Analysis Validation:** Conduct systematic linguistic analysis of the specific counterfactual templates and demographic terms used across all five languages to verify that observed bias patterns align with actual linguistic features rather than experimental artifacts.

3. **Performance-Bias Tradeoff Analysis:** Systematically measure whether bias reduction through distillation or other methods correlates with performance degradation across the sentiment analysis task, establishing whether bias mitigation comes at a measurable cost.
