# Toward the Automated Construction of Probabilistic Knowledge Graphs for the Maritime Domain

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2305.02471
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.02471
- Authors: Multiple
- Reference count: 35
- Primary result: Maritime DeepDive framework achieves average F1 scores of 0.81 and ROC-AUC of 0.82 for extracting eight binary relations from maritime piracy reports

## Executive Summary
This paper presents Maritime DeepDive, a framework for automatically constructing probabilistic knowledge graphs from unstructured maritime piracy reports. The approach combines distant supervision with machine learning to extract entities, relations, and their uncertainty from natural language text. By using heuristic rules and secondary databases to generate noisy training labels, the system learns probabilistic models using Markov Logic Networks to handle uncertainty in the extracted knowledge.

## Method Summary
Maritime DeepDive employs a two-stage approach for knowledge graph construction. First, it uses distant supervision to generate training data by applying heuristic rules and leveraging secondary databases to create noisy labels for entity relations in maritime incident reports. Second, it trains Markov Logic Network models on this data to learn probabilistic relations between entities such as aggressors, victims, dates, and locations. The system processes unstructured text reports and outputs a probabilistic knowledge graph where each relation has an associated confidence score.

## Key Results
- Average F1 score of 0.81 across eight binary relations extracted from 75 test incidents
- ROC-AUC score of 0.82 for relation extraction performance
- 45% performance improvement compared to using only secondary databases
- Successfully extracted relations include Victim-Aggressor, Victim-Date, and other maritime incident attributes

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The framework succeeds by combining the scalability of distant supervision with the uncertainty handling capabilities of Markov Logic Networks. Distant supervision allows rapid generation of training data without manual annotation, while MLNs provide a principled way to model the inherent uncertainty in both the training labels and the extracted relations. The probabilistic approach enables reasoning over uncertain information and fusion with other data sources for enhanced maritime situational awareness.

## Foundational Learning
- Distant supervision - needed for scalable training data generation without manual annotation; quick check: verify rule-based label generation captures key entity relations
- Markov Logic Networks - needed for modeling uncertainty in both training data and extracted relations; quick check: validate probability distributions make sense for different relation types
- Natural language entity extraction - needed for identifying key entities in unstructured text; quick check: ensure core entities (ships, dates, locations) are consistently extracted
- Knowledge graph construction - needed for organizing extracted information in a queryable format; quick check: verify graph structure supports required maritime incident queries

## Architecture Onboarding

Component map: Text preprocessing -> Entity extraction -> Relation extraction -> MLN modeling -> Probabilistic knowledge graph

Critical path: Text reports flow through NLP preprocessing, entity recognition, and relation extraction modules before being fed into MLN models that output the final probabilistic knowledge graph.

Design tradeoffs: The system trades off between distant supervision noise (faster but less accurate) versus manual annotation (slower but cleaner). MLNs provide uncertainty handling but may not scale as well as purely neural approaches.

Failure signatures: Performance degrades when reports contain unusual formatting, non-standard language, or when secondary database information is incomplete or outdated. The system may also struggle with relations that have complex or long-range dependencies.

## Limitations
- Evaluation based on only 75 test incidents limits generalizability
- Performance metrics represent averages that may mask significant variation across individual relations
- Reliance on heuristic rules and secondary databases may introduce systematic biases
- MLN approach may struggle with scalability to larger, more complex knowledge graphs

## Confidence

High confidence in the core methodology combining distant supervision with Markov Logic Networks for probabilistic relation extraction.

Medium confidence in the reported performance improvements over baseline approaches, given the limited test set size.

Medium confidence in the system's ability to generalize beyond the specific maritime piracy report corpus used for development.
