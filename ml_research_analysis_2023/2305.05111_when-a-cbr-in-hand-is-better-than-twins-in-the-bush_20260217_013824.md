# When a CBR in Hand is Better than Twins in the Bush

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2305.05111
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.05111
- Reference count: 40
- Primary result: CBR with XGBoost feature importance weights outperforms XGBoost (MAE 5.88 vs 9.22) while maintaining interpretability

## Executive Summary
This paper challenges the conventional wisdom that accuracy and interpretability are trade-offs by demonstrating that a Case-Based Reasoning (CBR) model using XGBoost feature importance as global weights can achieve better predictive accuracy than XGBoost itself. Using flight delay prediction as a testbed, the authors show that CBR achieves MAE of 5.88 compared to XGBoost's 9.22 while maintaining interpretability through case-based explanations. The study also benchmarks SHAP and LIME against CBR, finding that SHAP achieves much higher local accuracy (MAE of 3.3 × 10−6) than LIME (MAE of 8.62), but raises questions about whether local accuracy indicates feature attribution quality.

## Method Summary
The authors use flight data from EUROCONTROL's ETFMS system (2019) to train an XGBoost regression model for predicting flight take-off delays. The model uses 42 features derived from flight plans, weather reports, and airline information, with 5.9M training instances (May-Aug) and 158K testing instances (first 5 days of Sep/Oct). XGBoost is trained with specific hyperparameters (learning_rate=0.1, max_depth=7, subsample=0.5, etc.). CBR uses XGBoost's feature importance as global weights to compute similarities between cases. SHAP and LIME are implemented to explain XGBoost predictions, with their local accuracy measured against XGBoost outputs and feature attributions compared using normalized Discounted Cumulative Gain (nDCG) against CBR's feature weights.

## Key Results
- CBR with XGBoost feature importance weights achieves MAE of 5.88, outperforming XGBoost's MAE of 9.22
- SHAP achieves local accuracy with MAE of 3.3 × 10−6, significantly better than LIME's MAE of 8.62
- Feature importance weights from CBR and feature attributions from additive models (SHAP, LIME) may not align, suggesting they capture different aspects of model behavior
- CBR successfully serves as a benchmark for evaluating explanation model quality, providing a case-based alternative to ground-truth feature importance

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The paper demonstrates that transferring global feature importance weights from ensemble methods to CBR can improve both accuracy and interpretability. By using XGBoost's learned feature importance as similarity weights in CBR, the model leverages ensemble learning's ability to identify relevant features while maintaining case-based interpretability. The study also shows that additive feature attribution methods like SHAP can achieve high local accuracy by approximating model behavior at individual instances, though this doesn't necessarily translate to better feature attribution rankings.

## Foundational Learning
- **Feature importance vs feature attribution**: Understanding that weights derived from global model structure (feature importance) differ from local contributions in additive explanations (feature attribution). Why needed: The paper's central finding that these concepts diverge requires grasping their distinct mathematical foundations. - **Case-based reasoning similarity metrics**: CBR relies on computing similarities between cases using weighted feature differences. Why needed: The paper uses XGBoost feature importance as weights for numeric and categorical similarity calculations. Quick check: Given two flights with different feature values, can you compute their similarity using absolute difference normalized by range?

- **Normalized Discounted Cumulative Gain (nDCG)**: A metric for comparing ranked lists that accounts for position and relevance. Why needed: The paper uses nDCG to compare feature attribution rankings from different methods against CBR's ground truth. Quick check: Can you calculate nDCG for two different feature ranking lists?

- **Local accuracy in explanation models**: The requirement that explanation models must match the original model's predictions at individual instances. Why needed: The paper uses MAE between explanation predictions and XGBoost predictions as a quality metric. Quick check: Given an XGBoost prediction and an explanation model's output for the same instance, can you compute the local accuracy error?

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- Question: Is there a systematic relationship between feature importance weights in CBR and feature attributions from additive models like SHAP and LIME?
- Basis in paper: The paper notes that "the contribution of a feature in an additive feature attribution model is different from feature importance in the sense of weights" and raises questions about whether CBR's example-based explanations support feature attributions from other models.
- Why unresolved: The paper demonstrates that SHAP achieves higher local accuracy than LIME but doesn't clearly establish whether feature importance and feature attribution align, suggesting further research is needed to clarify these relationships.
- What evidence would resolve it: Comparative studies using multiple datasets to empirically test correlations between CBR feature weights and additive model attributions across different problem domains.

### Open Question 2
- Question: Under what conditions does CBR outperform ensemble methods like XGBoost, and can this be generalized beyond flight delay prediction?
- Basis in paper: The paper finds that "the CBR model alone using XGBoost importance factors as global weights, produced a smaller mean absolute error (MAE) than the original XGBoost regression model," challenging the typical accuracy-interpretability trade-off.
- Why unresolved: This observation was made in a single regression problem context (flight delay prediction), and the paper questions whether "CBR models would consistently benefit from learning global weights via ensemble models."
- What evidence would resolve it: Systematic experiments applying the XGBoost-to-CBR weight transfer approach across diverse regression and classification datasets with varying feature types and distributions.

### Open Question 3
- Question: Does local accuracy in explanation models (SHAP, LIME) serve as a reliable indicator of feature attribution quality?
- Basis in paper: The paper notes that "SHAP’s superior performance in local accuracy is not matched" when comparing feature attributions, and recommends investigating whether local accuracy indicates feature attribution quality.
- Why unresolved: The paper observes that SHAP achieves very high local accuracy (MAE of 3.3 × 10−6) but doesn't demonstrate corresponding superiority in feature attribution ranking (nDCG values), creating uncertainty about what local accuracy actually measures.
- What evidence would resolve it: Studies that correlate local accuracy metrics with user comprehension, decision-making quality, or other downstream measures of explanation usefulness across multiple explanation methods.

## Limitations
- The exact feature engineering process from raw ETFMS data to 42 derived features is not fully specified, creating uncertainty in replication
- The study is limited to a single domain (flight delay prediction) and regression task, limiting generalizability
- The relationship between local accuracy and feature attribution quality remains unclear, suggesting the evaluation metrics may not capture all aspects of explanation quality

## Confidence
- **XGBoost training methodology**: High - Hyperparameters and training procedure are clearly specified

- **SHAP and LIME implementation**: Medium - Default parameters provided but perturbation details unclear
- **Feature attribution comparison methodology**: Medium - nDCG metric is standard but ground truth definition is CBR-dependent
