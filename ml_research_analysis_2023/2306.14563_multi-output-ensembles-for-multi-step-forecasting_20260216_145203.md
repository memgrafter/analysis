# Multi-output Ensembles for Multi-step Forecasting

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2306.14563
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.14563
- Reference count: 35
- This paper investigates the performance of dynamic ensembles composed of multi-output models for multi-step ahead forecasting problems.

## Executive Summary
This paper investigates the performance of dynamic ensembles composed of multi-output models for multi-step ahead forecasting problems. Dynamic ensemble methods are typically designed for one-step ahead tasks, making their application to multi-step forecasting unclear. To address this, the authors carried out extensive experiments on 3568 time series from various domains using an ensemble of 30 multi-output models. They discovered that dynamic ensembles based on arbitrating and windowing present the best performance according to average rank. However, as the forecasting horizon increases, most approaches struggle to outperform a static ensemble that assigns equal weights to all models. The experiments are publicly available in a repository, providing a valuable resource for further research in this area.

## Method Summary
The paper proposes a comprehensive experimental framework to evaluate dynamic ensemble methods for multi-step ahead forecasting. The authors test four different strategies for computing ensemble weights along the forecasting horizon: Complete Horizon, Individual Horizon, First Horizon Forward, and Last Horizon Backward. They conduct extensive experiments using 3568 time series from various domains and an ensemble of 30 multi-output models. The performance of different dynamic ensemble approaches is compared against a static ensemble baseline using average rank as the primary metric.

## Key Results
- Dynamic ensembles based on arbitrating and windowing present the best performance according to average rank.
- As the forecasting horizon increases, most dynamic ensemble approaches struggle to outperform a static ensemble that assigns equal weights to all models.
- The choice of weighting strategy along the forecasting horizon (Complete Horizon, Individual Horizon, First Horizon Forward, Last Horizon Backward) does not significantly impact the relative performance of dynamic ensemble methods.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The paper does not provide a clear mechanism explaining why dynamic ensembles work or why certain approaches perform better than others. The empirical results suggest that arbitrating and windowing methods are effective, but the underlying reasons for their superiority are not explored in depth.

## Foundational Learning
1. Multi-output models: Models that can generate forecasts for multiple time steps simultaneously, which is essential for efficient multi-step ahead forecasting.
2. Dynamic ensemble methods: Techniques that adapt the combination of models over time or across forecast horizons, potentially improving performance compared to static weighting schemes.
3. Ensemble weighting strategies: Different approaches for assigning weights to individual models within an ensemble, such as arbitrating and windowing methods.

## Limitations
- The study relies on a single experimental setup using 3568 time series from the M4 competition and an ensemble of 30 multi-output models, which may not generalize to other forecasting scenarios.
- The paper lacks theoretical analysis explaining why certain dynamic approaches perform better than others, making it difficult to predict performance on unseen problems.
- The claim that dynamic ensembles based on arbitrating and windowing "present the best performance according to average rank" carries Medium confidence due to potential variations in performance across different time series characteristics.
