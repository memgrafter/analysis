# Data-Driven Encoding: A New Numerical Method for Computation of the Koopman Operator

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID**: 2301.06542
- **Source URL**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2301.06542
- **Reference count**: 23
- **Primary result**: Presents Data-Driven Encoding (DDE), a numerical method that computes the Koopman operator using direct encoding formulas and numerical integration, avoiding bias issues in least squares-based approaches like EDMD

## Executive Summary
This paper introduces Data-Driven Encoding (DDE), a novel numerical method for computing the Koopman operator that addresses fundamental bias issues in existing least squares-based approaches. The method uses a Direct Encoding formula that computes inner products between observable functions and the nonlinear state transition function through numerical integration, eliminating the data distribution bias inherent in least squares estimation. The approach partitions the data's dynamic range using Delaunay triangulation and computes weighted sums of integrand values to approximate inner product integrals. Numerical experiments on a nonlinear pendulum system demonstrate DDE's superior accuracy and robustness to data distribution compared to EDMD, achieving significantly lower total sum of squared errors (SSE) and reduced variance. The method also shows promise when integrated with deep learning approaches for Koopman operator modeling, yielding substantial improvements in prediction accuracy for high-dimensional systems.

## Method Summary
The Data-Driven Encoding (DDE) method computes the Koopman operator through a direct encoding formula that approximates inner products via numerical integration rather than least squares regression. The algorithm first partitions the data's dynamic range using Delaunay triangulation to create a mesh over the state space. For each observable function, it evaluates the integrand at mesh points and computes weighted sums to approximate the inner product integrals between observable functions and the nonlinear state transition function. This integration-based approach eliminates the bias introduced by data distribution that plagues least squares methods like EDMD. The method includes a convergence proof establishing theoretical guarantees for the approximation. For high-dimensional systems, DDE can be combined with deep learning architectures where the neural network learns the Koopman observables and the DDE framework provides the numerical integration structure for operator computation.

## Key Results
- DDE achieves significantly lower total sum of squared errors (SSE) compared to EDMD on nonlinear pendulum datasets
- The method demonstrates reduced variance in approximation quality across different data distributions
- Integration with deep learning approaches yields substantial improvements in prediction accuracy for high-dimensional systems
- Numerical experiments validate DDE's superior accuracy and robustness to data distribution compared to traditional EDMD

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
DDE works by replacing the least squares estimation problem inherent in EDMD with a direct numerical integration approach. Instead of solving a regression problem where the solution is biased by the data distribution, DDE computes inner products through quadrature rules applied to integrand functions defined by observable functions and the state transition map. The Delaunay triangulation partitions the state space into simplices, allowing efficient evaluation of integrand values at mesh points with appropriate weighting schemes. This integration-based formulation ensures that the approximation quality depends on the smoothness of the integrand and the mesh resolution rather than the density or distribution of training data points. The Direct Encoding formula provides a closed-form expression for each entry of the Koopman operator matrix, computed through these weighted integrals rather than solving an optimization problem.

## Architecture Onboarding

**Component Map**: Data points -> Delaunay triangulation -> Mesh generation -> Integrand evaluation -> Weighted sum integration -> Koopman operator matrix entries

**Critical Path**: The method flows from raw trajectory data through geometric preprocessing (triangulation) to numerical integration (weighted sums), producing the final operator representation. The critical computational bottleneck is the mesh generation and integrand evaluation steps.

**Design Tradeoffs**: The triangulation approach provides uniform coverage and convergence guarantees but requires O(n^d) points in d dimensions. Higher mesh resolution improves accuracy but increases computational cost. The method trades off the bias of least squares for the computational overhead of integration.

**Failure Signatures**: Poor triangulation quality (sliver elements, non-convex regions) leads to inaccurate integrand evaluation. Insufficient mesh resolution causes integration errors, particularly for rapidly varying observables. The method may fail when observable functions are not sufficiently smooth for reliable numerical integration.

## Limitations
- Computational complexity of Delaunay triangulation may become prohibitive for high-dimensional state spaces
- Performance on systems with many degrees of freedom remains largely unvalidated beyond the pendulum example
- Integration parameters (mesh resolution, quadrature rules) require careful tuning for different dynamical regimes
- Limited ablation studies on deep learning integration results, particularly compared to state-of-the-art neural Koopman methods
