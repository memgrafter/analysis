# A Topic-aware Summarization Framework with Different Modal Side Information

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID**: 2305.11503
- **Source URL**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.11503
- **Reference count**: 40
- **Key outcome**: Unified Summarization framework (USS) with multimodal side information outperforms strong baselines, achieving 2.0/1.6/1.7/0.3 improvements on R1, R2, RL, and BERTScore compared to EMSum on CNN dataset.

## Executive Summary
This paper introduces USS, a unified summarization framework that flexibly incorporates various modalities of side information (textual or visual) alongside main documents. The key innovation is a unified topic encoder that jointly discovers latent topics from the document and side information, enabling cross-modal alignment and interaction. The framework also employs a topic-aware graph encoder to model interactions between document and side information, and a triplet contrastive learning mechanism to align representations across modalities. Experiments on three benchmark datasets demonstrate significant performance improvements over strong baselines.

## Method Summary
USS is a transformer-based encoder-decoder architecture that integrates unified topic modeling, a topic-aware graph encoder, and triplet contrastive learning. The unified topic model (UTM) learns topic distributions from the document and side information, predicting the summary's topic distribution to align modalities. The topic-aware graph encoder models interactions through direct and indirect (topic-guided) edges, enabling cross-modal information flow. The summary decoder hierarchically attends to topics and then to document/side information. Triplet contrastive learning aligns paired multimodal representations while pushing apart unpaired ones. The model is trained with negative log likelihood and triplet contrastive loss on CNN, WikiHow, and VMSMO datasets.

## Key Results
- USS achieves 2.0/1.6/1.7/0.3 improvements on R1, R2, RL, and BERTScore compared to EMSum on CNN dataset.
- Significant performance gains observed across all three benchmark datasets (CNN, WikiHow, VMSMO).
- USS outperforms strong baselines including EMSum, especially when incorporating visual side information.
- Human evaluation shows USS summaries are more informative, coherent, and succinct.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: Unified Topic Modeling (UTM) aligns multimodal inputs into a common semantic space by predicting the topic distribution of the target summary.
- Mechanism: UTM encodes the bag-of-words representations of the document and side information into Gaussian-distributed topic variables, then predicts the summary's bag-of-words representation from the combined topic distributions. This forces the model to extract and align the most salient information across modalities.
- Core assumption: The target summary's topic distribution can be effectively predicted from the joint topic distributions of the document and side information, even when the side information is in a different modality.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract]: "we first propose a unified topic encoder, which jointly discovers latent topics from the document and various kinds of side information."
  - [section 3.3]: "In our UTM, instead of reconstruction, we aim to predict the bag-of-word representation of the target summary based on the two inputs."
  - [corpus]: Weak evidence; no directly related work found in corpus.
- Break condition: If the side information contains irrelevant or misleading content, the predicted topic distribution may not align well with the target summary, degrading performance.

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: Topic-aware Graph Encoder models the interaction between document and side information through direct and indirect edges, guided by learned topics.
- Mechanism: The encoder constructs a graph with nodes for document words, side information (textual or visual), and topics. Information flows through direct edges (self-attention within each modality) and indirect edges (cross-attention mediated by topics), allowing cross-modal interaction under topic guidance.
- Core assumption: Topics can serve as effective bridges to relate information from different modalities, and the graph structure can capture complex dependencies.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract]: "The learned topics flexibly bridge and guide the information flow between multiple inputs in a graph encoder through a topic-aware interaction."
  - [section 3.4]: "The document and side graphs communicate with each other through topic-guided and direct interactions."
  - [corpus]: Weak evidence; no directly related work found in corpus.
- Break condition: If the topic-guided interaction does not effectively capture the relevant cross-modal dependencies, or if the graph becomes too complex, the model may fail to properly align information.

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: Triplet Contrastive Learning aligns paired document-side and side-summary representations in the same semantic space, while pushing apart unpaired representations.
- Mechanism: For each case, the model constructs positive pairs (document-side, side-summary, document-summary) and negative pairs (sampled from the batch). A contrastive loss pulls together positive pairs and pushes apart negative pairs in representation space.
- Core assumption: The contrastive loss can effectively learn to align representations across modalities by leveraging large-scale non-paired data.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract]: "We secondly propose a triplet contrastive learning mechanism to align the single-modal or multi-modal information into a unified semantic space."
  - [section 3.6]: "The main idea is to let the representations of the paired images or text close to each other in the semantic space while the non-paired be far away."
  - [corpus]: Weak evidence; no directly related work found in corpus.
- Break condition: If the negative sampling is not informative enough, or if the representations are too similar across pairs, the contrastive loss may not effectively separate the semantic spaces.

## Foundational Learning

- Concept: Topic Modeling
  - Why needed here: Topics serve as a bridge to relate information across different modalities, enabling the model to align and unify them into the same semantic space.
  - Quick check question: How does the model ensure that the learned topics are relevant to the summarization task, and not just generic topics from the corpus?

- Concept: Graph Neural Networks
  - Why needed here: The graph encoder models complex dependencies between document and side information, allowing information to flow through both direct and indirect (topic-guided) edges.
  - Quick check question: What are the advantages and disadvantages of using a graph encoder compared to a simple concatenation of document and side information?

- Concept: Contrastive Learning
  - Why needed here: Contrastive learning aligns the representations of paired multimodal inputs, enhancing the model's understanding of the relationship between the document and side information.
  - Quick check question: How does the choice of negative samples affect the performance of the contrastive learning module?

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map: Unified Topic Modeling (UTM) -> Topic-aware Graph Encoder -> Summary Decoder
- Critical path: UTM → Graph Encoder → Decoder (Contrastive Learning runs in parallel)
- Design tradeoffs:
  - Topic number K: More topics allow finer-grained alignment but increase model complexity.
  - Graph layer number: More layers allow deeper interactions but may lead to overfitting.
  - Contrastive loss temperature: Affects the strength of alignment; too low may cause collapse, too high may not align enough.
- Failure signatures:
  - Poor performance on datasets with only textual side information: UTM may not effectively model textual modality.
  - Inconsistent summaries: Topic-guided interaction may not capture relevant cross-modal dependencies.
  - Slow convergence: Contrastive learning may require careful tuning of negative sampling and loss temperature.
- First 3 experiments:
  1. Ablation study: Remove UTM, graph encoder, or contrastive learning to assess their individual contributions.
  2. Parameter sweep: Vary topic number K and graph layer number to find optimal values.
  3. Qualitative analysis: Visualize topic distributions and attention weights to understand how the model aligns multimodal inputs.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- Question: How does the topic number K and graph layer number L affect the performance of USS across different datasets?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper mentions conducting experiments on VMSMO dataset to probe the impact of K and L, showing that ROUGE scores increase initially and then drop after reaching an upper limit.
- Why unresolved: The paper does not provide a systematic study across all three datasets (CNN, WikiHow, VMSMO) to determine optimal K and L values for each.
- What evidence would resolve it: Conducting comprehensive ablation studies on all three datasets with varying K and L values to identify dataset-specific optimal configurations.

### Open Question 2
- Question: Can the unified topic modeling approach be extended to incorporate more than two modalities of side information simultaneously?
- Basis in paper: [inferred] The paper demonstrates flexibility in handling different modalities (textual and visual) but does not explicitly explore scenarios with more than two modalities.
- Why unresolved: The current framework is validated with at most two modalities (document + one type of side information), leaving the scalability to multiple modalities unexplored.
- What evidence would resolve it: Experiments incorporating three or more modalities (e.g., document + text + images + videos) and evaluating performance improvements over single/multi-modal baselines.

### Open Question 3
- Question: How does the quality of side information affect the summarization performance, and can USS handle low-quality or irrelevant side information?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper acknowledges that side information can contain information from various aspects and the model should recognize useful aspects for summarization.
- Why unresolved: The experiments use curated datasets where side information is presumably relevant and high-quality, not testing robustness to noisy or irrelevant side information.
- What evidence would resolve it: Controlled experiments introducing varying levels of noise or irrelevance in side information and measuring degradation in summarization quality.

### Open Question 4
- Question: What is the computational overhead of the unified topic modeling and graph encoder compared to standard transformer-based summarization models?
- Basis in paper: [inferred] The paper introduces complex components (unified topic model, topic-aware graph encoder, triplet contrastive learning) but does not discuss computational efficiency or training/inference time.
- Why unresolved: While performance improvements are shown, there is no analysis of the trade-off between performance gains and computational costs.
- What evidence would resolve it: Benchmarking USS against baselines in terms of training time, inference speed, and memory usage on the same hardware.

## Limitations

- The effectiveness of the unified topic encoder in aligning different modalities relies heavily on the assumption that topic distributions can effectively bridge semantic gaps between text and visual information, which is not rigorously validated.
- The paper lacks detailed analysis of how the negative sampling strategy in triplet contrastive learning impacts performance, making it difficult to assess the robustness of this mechanism.
- The framework is tested on datasets with curated, presumably high-quality side information, leaving its robustness to noisy or irrelevant side information unexplored.

## Confidence

- **High confidence**: The overall framework architecture is technically sound and the experimental setup follows standard practices for summarization evaluation.
- **Medium confidence**: The claimed improvements over baselines are statistically significant, though the magnitude of improvements may be partially attributable to model complexity rather than the specific innovations.
- **Low confidence**: The mechanism by which topics specifically guide cross-modal interactions is not fully explained, and the paper lacks qualitative analysis of learned topics or attention patterns.

## Next Checks

1. **Component ablation study**: Remove the unified topic modeling component and retrain the model to quantify its specific contribution to performance gains, particularly on datasets with only textual side information.

2. **Topic quality analysis**: Conduct a qualitative analysis of the learned topics by visualizing topic distributions and measuring topic coherence scores to verify that topics are meaningful and relevant to the summarization task.

3. **Cross-modal generalization test**: Evaluate the model on a dataset where side information is known to be partially irrelevant or noisy to test the robustness of the topic-guided interaction mechanism and identify failure conditions.
