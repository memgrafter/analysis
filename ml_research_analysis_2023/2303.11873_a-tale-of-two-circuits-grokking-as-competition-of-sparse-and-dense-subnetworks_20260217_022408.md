# A Tale of Two Circuits: Grokking as Competition of Sparse and Dense Subnetworks

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2303.11873
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.11873
- Reference count: 29
- Primary result: Grokking occurs due to competition between dense and sparse subnetworks, with selective norm growth enabling sparse subnetwork emergence

## Executive Summary
Grokking is a puzzling phenomenon where models overfit initially but then generalize perfectly after prolonged training. This paper proposes that grokking emerges from competition between two subnetworks: a dense subnetwork that dominates early and generalizes poorly, and a sparse subnetwork that emerges later through selective norm growth in specific neurons. The authors demonstrate this mechanism on sparse parity tasks, where the sparse subnetwork size matches the minimal DNF circuit complexity for parity computation. This work provides a mechanistic explanation for grokking as a phase transition driven by sparsification and targeted norm growth.

## Method Summary
The authors study grokking on (n,k)-parity tasks using 1-layer ReLU networks trained with SGD and weight decay on hinge loss. They employ active subnetwork analysis to identify which neurons control predictions at different training stages, tracking neuron norms to observe growth patterns. The key experimental setup involves training on small subsets of data initially, then gradually increasing training data to observe the transition from memorization to generalization. They systematically vary k (number of relevant bits) and weight decay strength to analyze how these parameters affect the emergence of the sparse subnetwork and timing of the grokking phase transition.

## Key Results
- Grokking corresponds to accelerated norm growth in a specific subset of neurons while others decay slowly
- Sparse subnetwork size matches DNF circuit complexity (2^k or 2^(k-1) neurons) for parity computation
- Phase transition occurs when sparse subnetwork becomes functionally dominant over dense subnetwork
- Network implements discrete algorithmic solutions after sparsification rather than continuous function approximation

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: Grokking occurs due to competition between a dense subnetwork (dominant early) and a sparse subnetwork (dominant after), triggered by selective norm growth in specific neurons.
- Mechanism: During training, neurons in the dense subnetwork initially dominate predictions and generalize poorly. A small subset of neurons then undergoes rapid norm growth while others decay slowly. This selective growth sparsifies the network and causes the sparse subnetwork to dominate predictions, enabling generalization.
- Core assumption: The sparse subnetwork emerges through targeted norm growth rather than random weight changes, and this growth is causally linked to the phase transition from memorization to generalization.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "We find that this subnetwork arises when a small subset of neurons undergoes rapid norm growth, whereas the other neurons in the network decay slowly in norm."
  - [section 3.2] "We ﬁnd that the grokking phase transition corresponds to accerelated norm growth in a speciﬁc set of neurons, and norm decay elsewhere."
  - [corpus] Weak evidence - corpus papers discuss related concepts but don't directly support this specific mechanism.
- Break condition: If norm growth is uniformly distributed across neurons rather than targeted to a specific subset, the sparse subnetwork won't emerge and grokking won't occur.

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: The size of the sparse subnetwork corresponds to the minimal DNF circuit size for computing parity, suggesting the network is implementing an efficient algorithmic solution.
- Mechanism: The network discovers a sparse representation that matches the computational complexity of the task. For (n,k)-parity, the sparse subnetwork size matches the DNF circuit construction (2^k or 2^(k-1) neurons), indicating the network has learned an efficient algorithmic solution rather than memorizing.
- Core assumption: The network is optimizing for computational efficiency and discovers the minimal circuit representation for the task.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [section 3.3] "Across 5 runs, the sparse subnetwork has size {6, 6, 6, 8, 8}. This suggests that the network may be computing the parity via a representation resembling disjunctive normal form (DNF)."
  - [section 3.3] "Since our sparse subnetwork always contains either 6 or 8 neuron, we speculate it may always be implementing a variant of these constructions."
  - [corpus] Weak evidence - corpus papers mention related concepts but don't provide direct evidence for this mechanism.
- Break condition: If the sparse subnetwork size doesn't match the theoretical minimal circuit size, or if the network doesn't implement an efficient algorithmic solution.

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: The grokking phase transition is a form of network saturation where the sparse subnetwork becomes functionally dominant, enabling discrete algorithmic behavior.
- Mechanism: Before grokking, the network is in a continuous, high-dimensional space with many active neurons. After selective norm growth, the network becomes effectively sparse and discrete, allowing it to implement clean algorithmic solutions that generalize well to unseen data.
- Core assumption: Sparsification enables the network to behave more like a discrete computational system rather than a continuous function approximator.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "Thus, we suggest that the grokking phase transition can be understood to emerge from competition of two largely distinct subnetworks: a dense one that dominates before the transition and generalizes poorly, and a sparse one that dominates afterwards."
  - [section 3.1] "Noticeably, it becomes orders of magnitude sparser as it starts generalizing to the test set, and crucially, this phase transition happens at the same time as the loss phase transition."
  - [corpus] Weak evidence - corpus papers discuss related concepts but don't directly support this specific mechanism.
- Break condition: If sparsification doesn't lead to improved generalization, or if the network remains effectively dense even after the phase transition.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- Question: Why do neural networks trained on sparse parity tasks consistently find a 6-neuron or 8-neuron DNF-like solution rather than the more efficient 4-neuron threshold-gate construction?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper observes that sparse subnetworks consistently have size 6 or 8, matching DNF constructions, while noting a more efficient 4-neuron construction exists but is not found by the networks.
- Why unresolved: The paper only speculates that the network might be implementing DNF variants but doesn't explain why the learning algorithm favors this less efficient solution.
- What evidence would resolve it: Experiments varying initialization, optimization hyperparameters, or architecture could show whether the DNF-like solution is an inherent bias of SGD or could be avoided with different training procedures.

### Open Question 2
- Question: What determines which specific neurons undergo selective norm growth during the grokking transition?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper observes that a subset of neurons undergoes rapid norm growth while others decay, but doesn't explain what makes certain neurons "chosen" for this growth.
- Why unresolved: The mechanism behind selective norm growth is not investigated beyond observing the phenomenon itself.
- What evidence would resolve it: Analyzing gradients, initializations, or neuron activations during memorization phase could reveal whether certain neurons are pre-selected or whether the growth is purely emergent from the optimization dynamics.

### Open Question 3
- Question: Does the competition between dense and sparse subnetworks during grokking reflect a general principle for understanding phase transitions in other deep learning phenomena?
- Basis in paper: [inferred] The paper suggests grokking as a "controlled test bed for emergence in large language models" and draws parallels to targeted norm growth in pretrained language models, but doesn't test this connection.
- Why unresolved: The analysis is limited to a specific task (sparse parity) and doesn't explore whether similar mechanisms apply to other grokking tasks or emergent behaviors in larger models.
- What evidence would resolve it: Applying the same analytical framework to other grokking tasks (like binary operators) or to emergent capabilities in language models could reveal whether sparse-dense competition is a universal mechanism.

## Limitations
- Task specificity: Findings demonstrated primarily on sparse parity tasks; unclear how well mechanism generalizes to other grokking-prone tasks
- Architecture limitations: Study focuses on 1-layer ReLU networks; mechanism may not extend to deeper networks or transformers
- Causal evidence gaps: Lack of ablation studies showing that disrupting selective norm growth prevents grokking or that inducing norm growth triggers earlier generalization

## Confidence
- High confidence: The observation that grokking involves competition between dense and sparse subnetworks on the parity task, and that this manifests as selective norm growth in specific neurons.
- Medium confidence: The hypothesis that sparse subnetwork size matches DNF circuit complexity for parity, and that this reflects the network discovering an efficient algorithmic solution.
- Low confidence: The broader claim that this circuit competition mechanism explains grokking across diverse tasks and architectures, or that sparsification enables discrete algorithmic behavior.

## Next Checks
1. **Cross-task validation**: Test whether circuit competition and selective norm growth patterns appear in other grokking-prone tasks (e.g., modular addition, small-scale image classification) and whether sparse subnetwork sizes match theoretical circuit complexity bounds.

2. **Intervention experiments**: Use techniques like neuron masking or targeted weight manipulation to test whether disrupting selective norm growth in the identified sparse subnetwork prevents or delays grokking, establishing causal relationships.

3. **Architecture scaling**: Investigate whether the circuit competition mechanism persists as model depth increases, and whether deeper networks show multiple phases of competition between subnetworks at different levels of the architecture.
