# Emergence of a phonological bias in ChatGPT

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2305.15929
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.15929
- Authors: 
- Reference count: 14
- Primary result: ChatGPT exhibits a consonant bias, preferring non-words that preserve consonantal tier over vowel tier (76% vs 24% in English, 74% vs 26% in Spanish)

## Executive Summary
ChatGPT demonstrates a preference for consonants over vowels when identifying words, showing a 76% vs 24% bias in English and 74% vs 26% in Spanish. This phonological bias mirrors human language processing patterns where consonants are prioritized for lexical access. The bias emerged spontaneously from training on natural language data rather than being explicitly programmed, highlighting how statistical regularities in language can induce language-like processing in AI systems.

## Method Summary
The study tested ChatGPT's word recognition preferences using 100 English and 100 Spanish target words with corresponding non-words that differed by either a single consonant or vowel change. Non-words had changes in syllables beyond the first position. The model was manually prompted with each pair and asked to choose which non-word was more similar to the original word. Responses were tallied and analyzed using Wilcoxon signed-rank tests to determine if the consonant preference was statistically significant.

## Key Results
- ChatGPT showed a 76% preference for non-words preserving consonants over vowels in English
- The same bias appeared in Spanish with 74% preference for consonant-preserving non-words
- The bias emerged without explicit phonological programming, suggesting it arose from statistical patterns in training data

## Limitations
- The study only tested two languages (English and Spanish), both of which have more consonants than vowels, limiting generalizability
- The exact mechanism by which the model extracts phonological information without explicit phonological representations remains unclear
- The study cannot distinguish between true phonological processing and statistical correlations at the character level
