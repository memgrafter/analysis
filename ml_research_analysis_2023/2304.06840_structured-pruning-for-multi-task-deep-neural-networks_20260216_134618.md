# Structured Pruning for Multi-Task Deep Neural Networks

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2304.06840
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2304.06840
- Reference count: 40
- This work investigates structured pruning methods for multi-task deep neural networks, showing that random re-initialization of pruned models can outperform fine-tuning from inherited weights.

## Executive Summary
This paper investigates structured pruning methods for multi-task deep neural networks (DNNs), specifically focusing on a new pruning criterion called CosPrune that identifies task-conflicting filters using cosine similarity of task-specific gradients. The authors compare CosPrune against Taylor pruning across three tasks (semantic segmentation, depth estimation, and surface normal prediction) on the NYUv2 dataset. They find that while different pruning methods yield similar performance when controlling for parameter count, iterative pruning with fine-tuning may lead to suboptimal architectures due to poor initialization, whereas random re-initialization with optimal learning rates produces better results.

## Method Summary
The authors propose CosPrune, a structured pruning method for multi-task learning that identifies and removes convolutional filters creating task conflicts. For each filter, they compute pairwise cosine similarity between task-specific gradients during training, summing these similarities to create an importance score. Filters with the lowest scores are pruned iteratively (100 filters every 10 epochs). The study compares CosPrune with Taylor pruning on a VGG-16 backbone with ASPP heads trained on NYUv2 for three tasks. Both methods use iterative pruning with fine-tuning, but the authors also explore random re-initialization of pruned models with optimal learning rates as an alternative to fine-tuning.

## Key Results
- CosPrune and Taylor pruning methods show similar performance when controlling for parameter count across tasks
- Iterative pruning with fine-tuning can lead to suboptimal performance due to inherited weights acting as poor initialization
- Randomly re-initializing pruned models and training from scratch with optimal learning rates achieves better results than fine-tuning
- Performance degradation becomes significant at extreme pruning levels (below 20% of original parameters)

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: CosPrune identifies filters that create task conflicts via cosine similarity of task-specific gradients.
- Mechanism: For each shared filter, accumulate pairwise cosine similarity between normalized task gradients during training. Filters with lowest accumulated scores are pruned.
- Core assumption: Filters with high conflict (negative cosine similarity) between tasks contribute least to joint performance.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "identifies and prunes the convolutional filters that have conflicts between tasks"
  - [section 3] "We define the CosPrune Importance score, as the sum of pairwise cosine similarity between all the tasks for the parameter W"
  - [corpus] Weak: No corpus papers directly discuss cosine similarity for MTL pruning conflicts
- Break condition: If task gradients are highly correlated, cosine similarity may not distinguish useful filters, causing over-pruning of shared features.

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: Iterative pruning with fine-tuning can lead to suboptimal final architectures due to inherited bad initialization.
- Mechanism: Each pruning iteration removes filters and fine-tunes, but inherited weights may not be optimal for the new sparse architecture.
- Core assumption: Fine-tuning from previous weights can trap the model in poor local minima for the new architecture.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "iterative structure pruning may not be the best way to achieve a well-performing pruned model"
  - [section 4.2] "in the case of iterative structured pruning, the inherited trained weights from previous pruning iterations may act as bad initialization for the pruned models"
  - [corpus] Weak: No corpus papers discuss iterative pruning initialization issues for MTL
- Break condition: If the pruned architecture is close to the original, fine-tuning may still work well; break occurs when architecture changes significantly.

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: Randomly re-initializing pruned models and training from scratch can achieve similar or better performance than fine-tuning.
- Mechanism: Discard inherited weights, randomly initialize the pruned architecture, and train with optimal learning rate.
- Core assumption: The pruned architecture itself contains sufficient information; initialization is less critical than architectural structure.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "when the same models are randomly initialized and re-trained, they show better results"
  - [section 4.2] "we observe that they can give relatively better results on all the tasks when compared to the corresponding fine-tuning stage of iterative pruning"
  - [corpus] Weak: Only single-task pruning papers mention re-initialization benefits
- Break condition: If the pruned model is extremely small, random initialization may fail to converge; requires careful learning rate tuning.

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map: VGG-16 backbone (shared) -> ASPP heads (task-specific) -> NYUv2 dataset with 3 tasks
- Critical path: Training → CosPrune/Taylor pruning → Iterative pruning with fine-tuning → Random re-initialization → Optimal learning rate tuning
- Design tradeoffs:
  - Iterative pruning vs single-shot: Iterative allows gradual removal but may trap in local minima
  - Cosine similarity vs weight magnitude: Cosine captures task conflicts but may be noisier
  - Fine-tuning vs re-initialization: Fine-tuning faster but may underperform; re-initialization slower but more stable
- Failure signatures:
  - Extreme parameter reduction causing steep performance drops
  - Learning rate too high/low for re-initialized models
  - Cosine similarity failing to distinguish useful filters when tasks are highly correlated
- First 3 experiments:
  1. Run CosPrune and Taylor pruning on trained model, compare iterative pruning results at moderate pruning levels
  2. Take pruned models from experiment 1, randomly re-initialize, train with multiple learning rates, find optimal settings
  3. Compare re-trained models from both pruning methods at same parameter levels to verify similar performance

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- Question: Does the effectiveness of random channel pruning compared to structured pruning methods like CosPrune and Taylor Pruning generalize across different multi-task learning datasets beyond NYUv2?
- Basis in paper: [inferred] The paper mentions that recent works in single-task neural networks have shown random channel pruning can match dense model performance under appropriate settings, but this has not been explored for multi-task models.
- Why unresolved: The paper only evaluates CosPrune and Taylor Pruning on the NYUv2 dataset, leaving open the question of whether random channel pruning would perform similarly across other multi-task learning tasks and datasets.
- What evidence would resolve it: Empirical comparisons of random channel pruning against CosPrune and Taylor Pruning across multiple multi-task learning datasets and tasks.

### Open Question 2
- Question: What is the optimal learning rate scheduling strategy for re-training pruned multi-task models to achieve the best performance across all tasks?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper found that re-training pruned models with optimal learning rates for each model gave better results than using the same learning rate for all models, but did not explore different learning rate scheduling strategies.
- Why unresolved: While the paper shows the importance of using optimal learning rates, it does not investigate whether different learning rate schedules (e.g., cosine annealing, step decay, etc.) would further improve the performance of re-trained pruned models.
- What evidence would resolve it: Systematic comparison of different learning rate scheduling strategies when re-training pruned multi-task models, evaluating their impact on task performance.

### Open Question 3
- Question: How does the choice of validation metric (e.g., total validation loss vs. individual task metrics) affect the selection of the best pruned multi-task model during training?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper observed that the best total validation loss did not always correspond to the best performance on individual tasks, particularly for semantic segmentation.
- Why unresolved: The paper only considered pixel accuracy as an alternative validation metric, but it is unclear whether other task-specific metrics or combinations thereof would lead to better model selection.
- What evidence would resolve it: Comparative analysis of different validation metrics and their correlation with the final task performance when selecting the best pruned multi-task model.

## Limitations

- The effectiveness of CosPrune is primarily validated on NYUv2 dataset with three specific tasks, limiting generalizability to other datasets and task combinations
- The analysis does not fully explore the relationship between pruning ratios and performance degradation curves, particularly at extreme pruning levels
- No ablation studies are provided to isolate the impact of cosine similarity computation frequency or gradient normalization methods

## Confidence

- **High Confidence:** Claims about iterative pruning potentially leading to suboptimal architectures due to bad initialization
- **Medium Confidence:** Claims that re-initialization with optimal learning rates can achieve similar or better performance than fine-tuning
- **Low Confidence:** Claims that CosPrune specifically identifies task-conflicting filters more effectively than alternative pruning methods
