# xPQA: Cross-Lingual Product Question Answering across 12 Languages

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID**: 2305.09249
- **Source URL**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.09249
- **Reference count**: 31
- **Primary result**: Introduces xPQA dataset with 12 languages for cross-lingual PQA, showing in-domain data is crucial and runtime translation works best for ranking while offline translation excels for generation

## Executive Summary
This paper introduces xPQA, a large-scale cross-lingual product question answering dataset spanning 12 languages. The dataset supports two key tasks: candidate ranking to select the best English candidate to answer a non-English question, and answer generation to produce a natural-sounding answer in the question's language. The authors evaluate three approaches: translate-test, translate-train, and multilingual, using both zero-shot and fine-tuned scenarios. Results show that in-domain data is crucial for performance, with the translate-test approach performing best for candidate ranking and the translate-train approach excelling in answer generation. Despite these findings, there remains a significant performance gap between English and cross-lingual test sets.

## Method Summary
The xPQA dataset was created by sampling 100 English product pages from the ePQA dataset and translating questions into 11 additional languages. For each question, five candidates were annotated as relevant and five randomly sampled from the product's candidate set. Answers were manually written based on the fully answering candidates. The study evaluates three approaches: translate-test (runtime translation to English), translate-train (offline translation to augment training data), and multilingual (direct multilingual training). Both zero-shot and fine-tuned scenarios are tested using BERT-base/T5-base for translate-test and mBERT-base/mT5-base for the other approaches. Candidate ranking uses P@1 while answer generation uses sacreBLEU score.

## Key Results
- In-domain data is essential for cross-lingual PQA; domain transferability outweighs language transferability
- Candidate ranking often prefers runtime-translation approaches while answer generation prefers multilingual approaches
- Translating offline to augment multilingual models helps candidate ranking mainly on languages with non-Latin scripts and helps answer generation mainly on languages with Latin scripts

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: In-domain data is essential as cross-lingual rankers trained on other domains perform poorly on the PQA task
- Mechanism: Fine-tuning on in-domain xPQA data yields better cross-lingual performance than using out-of-domain datasets like XOR-TyDi, even when the latter are cross-lingual
- Core assumption: The product domain has unique question-answer patterns and candidate types that are not captured by general Wikipedia or school exam data
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract]: "In-domain data is essential as cross-lingual rankers trained on other domains perform poorly on the PQA task"
  - [section 5.3]: "Figure 3. Comparison of transfer learning datasets. The English-only in-domain ePQA data is more useful than the cross-lingual out-of-domain XOR-Tydi dataset."
  - [corpus]: No direct corpus evidence; only mentioned in the paper's related work section
- Break condition: If candidate sets and questions in the product domain were similar to Wikipedia or school exams, the domain advantage would disappear

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: Runtime translation approaches perform better for candidate ranking, while offline translation approaches perform better for answer generation
- Mechanism: For ranking, runtime translation allows leveraging the full strength of monolingual English models without corrupting their learned representations. For generation, offline translation enables multilingual models to learn target-language patterns during training, improving fluency
- Core assumption: Multilingual models benefit more from target-language fine-tuning in generation tasks than from post-hoc translation of English outputs
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract]: "Candidate ranking often prefers runtime-translation approaches while answer generation prefers multilingual approaches"
  - [section 5.2]: "Translate-test performs the best, and its advantage is particularly prominent in the zero-shot scenario" for ranking; "In the zero-shot scenario, the translate-test approach often performs the best on languages with non-Latin scripts and the translate-train approach performs the best on languages with Latin scripts" for generation
  - [corpus]: No direct corpus evidence; this is derived from experimental results
- Break condition: If multilingual models could generate high-quality target-language text without training data in that language, the advantage of translate-train for generation would disappear

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: Translating training data helps candidate ranking mainly for non-Latin scripts and answer generation mainly for Latin scripts
- Mechanism: Multilingual models already have good representations for Latin-script languages, so translating training data doesn't help much for ranking. For non-Latin scripts, translation provides crucial training data. For generation, Latin-script languages benefit more from translated training data because multilingual models can better generate Latin scripts after seeing translated examples
- Core assumption: Multilingual models have different levels of pre-training coverage for different script types, affecting their ability to rank and generate in those scripts
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract]: "Translating offline to augment multilingual models helps candidate ranking mainly on languages with non-Latin scripts; and helps answer generation mainly on languages with Latin scripts"
  - [section 5.2]: "The translate-train approach outperforms the multilingual approach mainly for languages that do not use Latin scripts" for ranking; "Interestingly, all the exceptions happen in languages using non-Latin scripts" for generation
  - [corpus]: No direct corpus evidence; this is derived from experimental results
- Break condition: If multilingual models had equal pre-training coverage for all scripts, this script-specific translation benefit would disappear

  - Why needed here: Translation quality varies across languages and affects both runtime and offline translation approaches

## Architecture Onboarding

- **Component map**: Data collection pipeline (questions, candidates, relevance annotations, answer generation) -> Three training approaches (translate-test, translate-train, multilingual) -> Two task modules (candidate ranking, answer generation) -> Evaluation metrics (P@1 for ranking, BLEU for generation)

- **Critical path**: 1. Collect questions and candidates 2. Annotate relevance and generate answers 3. Train models using one of three approaches 4. Evaluate on test set 5. Deploy with appropriate runtime translation strategy

- **Design tradeoffs**: Runtime vs offline translation: Runtime offers flexibility but adds latency; offline improves quality but requires translation infrastructure. Monolingual vs multilingual models: Monolingual models may be stronger in their language but lack cross-lingual capability. Data augmentation vs real annotations: Machine translation can expand training data but may introduce noise

- **Failure signatures**: Low P@1 with high BLEU: Candidate ranking is failing, but when it succeeds, generation is good. Low BLEU with high P@1: Ranking is working, but generated answers are poor quality. Both metrics low: Fundamental issues with the approach or data quality

- **First 3 experiments**: 1. Train a multilingual model on ePQA (multilingual zero-shot) and evaluate on xPQA test set to establish baseline. 2. Train a translate-train model with translated ePQA data and compare to multilingual zero-shot. 3. Fine-tune the best zero-shot model on xPQA training data and compare to non-fine-tuned versions

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

- How would the use of larger, more powerful multilingual models (e.g., mT0, Flan-T5) affect the performance gap between English and cross-lingual test sets?
- What is the impact of answer generation quality on end-user satisfaction compared to the BLEU score metric?
- How does the performance of candidate ranking models change when using a larger candidate set beyond the top-5 selected by the initial English ranker?

## Limitations
- The core ePQA dataset used for English-only experiments is not publicly available, affecting reproducibility
- The study uses base-sized transformer models which may not represent state-of-the-art performance achievable with larger models
- Evaluation focuses on in-scope, answerable questions only, not reflecting real-world scenarios with unanswerable queries

## Confidence
- **High confidence**: In-domain data is essential for cross-lingual PQA
- **Medium confidence**: Script-specific translation benefits (non-Latin for ranking, Latin for generation)
- **Medium confidence**: General superiority of translate-test for ranking and translate-train for generation

## Next Checks

2. Test the translate-train approach across additional languages with different scripts to confirm the observed pattern
