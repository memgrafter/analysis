# Transferring Models Trained on Natural Images to 3D MRI via Position Encoded Slice Models

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2303.01491
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.01491
- Reference count: 0
- 2D-Slice-CNN models with ImageNet-pretrained encoders outperform random initialization and 3D-CNN baselines for brain age prediction and Alzheimer's disease detection from MRI

## Executive Summary
This paper investigates the use of pretrained convolutional neural networks for neuroimaging tasks, specifically brain age prediction and Alzheimer's disease detection from MRI scans. The authors propose using 2D-Slice-CNN models, which process each 2D MRI slice with a pretrained encoder and combine the slice embeddings with permutation-invariant operations. They demonstrate that initializing the 2D encoder with ImageNet pretrained weights outperforms random initialization for both tasks. Additionally, they explore incorporating positional encodings to preserve spatial information, which improved performance in some cases. The best results were achieved using 2D-Slice-CNN models with pretrained ResNet encoders, outperforming a 3D-CNN baseline.

## Method Summary
The paper proposes using 2D-Slice-CNN models to process 3D MRI volumes by extracting 2D slices along the sagittal axis and encoding each slice with a pretrained ResNet-18 or ResNet-50 encoder. The slice embeddings are aggregated using mean or attention operations, with optional positional encodings added to preserve spatial information. The models are trained on brain age prediction and Alzheimer's disease detection tasks using the UK Biobank and ADNI datasets, respectively. The 2D encoders are initialized with ImageNet-pretrained weights, and the models are trained using the ADAM optimizer for 100 epochs.

## Key Results
- ImageNet-pretrained 2D encoders consistently outperform random initialization for both brain age prediction and Alzheimer's disease detection tasks
- 2D-Slice-CNN models with pretrained encoders achieve lower MAE and RMSE than 3D-CNN baselines for brain age prediction
- Incorporating positional encodings improved test performance in some cases, particularly for ResNet-50 encoders

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: Pretraining on natural images improves 2D-slice encoder performance on 3D MRI tasks
- Mechanism: ImageNet-pretrained weights provide better feature extraction than random initialization, even when the domain shifts from natural images to medical images
- Core assumption: The low-level and mid-level features learned from natural images transfer effectively to medical imaging tasks
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "we initialize the 2D encoder with ImageNet pretrained weights that outperform those initialized and trained from scratch"
  - [section] "Initializing with pretrained weights consistently outperforms random initialization in all cases"
- Break condition: If the domain shift is too large (e.g., from natural images to medical images with very different visual characteristics), the pretrained features may not transfer effectively

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: Positional encodings preserve spatial information lost by permutation-invariant operations
- Mechanism: By adding position-specific vectors to slice embeddings before aggregation, the model retains information about which slice is which in the 3D volume
- Core assumption: The spatial location of MRI slices contains predictive information for the target tasks
- Evidence anchors:
  - [section] "we improve the modeling capabilities of 2D-Slice models by incorporating spatial information through position embeddings"
  - [section] "Incorporating positional encodings improved test performance in some cases"
- Break condition: If the predictive features are not spatially localized, or if the MRI alignment is inconsistent, positional encodings may not help and could even hurt performance

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: 2D-Slice-CNN models can effectively process full 3D MRI volumes without losing information
- Mechanism: By encoding each slice with a 2D CNN and aggregating all slice embeddings, the model accesses the complete 3D information while leveraging powerful 2D architectures
- Core assumption: The slice-by-slice processing and aggregation can capture the relevant 3D structure for the target tasks
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "These models can access information from all the slices during training, thus exploiting a richer feature set"
  - [section] "Our approach only substitutes encoders with pretrained counterparts... considers the whole MRI as input"
- Break condition: If the task requires complex 3D spatial relationships that cannot be captured by slice-wise processing and aggregation

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map:
  - Input: 3D MRI volumes (91×109×91)
  - Preprocessing: Reorientation to standard template
  - Slice encoder: 2D CNN (pretrained or random initialization)
  - Positional encoding: Trainable position-specific vectors
  - Aggregation: Mean or attention over slice embeddings
  - Output: Age prediction or disease classification

- Critical path: MRI → Slice encoder → Positional encoding → Aggregation → Output

- Design tradeoffs:
  - 2D vs. 3D CNNs: 2D CNNs are more efficient and can leverage pretrained models, but may lose some 3D information
  - Mean vs. attention aggregation: Mean is simpler and faster, while attention can learn more complex relationships
  - Fixed vs. trainable positional encodings: Fixed encodings are simpler, while trainable encodings can adapt to the task

- Failure signatures:
  - Poor performance: Could indicate insufficient training data, inappropriate architecture, or lack of domain-specific features
  - Overfitting: Could indicate that the model is too complex or that the training data is not diverse enough
  - Sensitivity to alignment: Could indicate that positional encodings are capturing alignment-specific information rather than task-relevant features

- First 3 experiments:
  1. Baseline 3D CNN on the same tasks to establish performance benchmarks
  2. 2D-Slice-CNN with random initialization and mean aggregation to assess the impact of the 2D-slice architecture
  3. 2D-Slice-CNN with ImageNet-pretrained encoder and mean aggregation to assess the impact of transfer learning

## Limitations
- The paper lacks direct empirical validation of transfer learning from natural images to medical imaging tasks within the medical imaging domain
- The comparison with 3D-CNN baselines is limited to a single architecture, preventing broader conclusions about the relative advantages of 2D-Slice-CNN approaches
- The specific benefits of positional encodings are the least substantiated claim, with improvements reported as "in some cases" without systematic analysis

## Confidence
*High Confidence:* The fundamental claim that pretrained weights outperform random initialization is well-supported by both the experimental results and extensive prior literature in transfer learning. The consistent performance improvements across both tasks and multiple model variants provide strong empirical evidence.

*Medium Confidence:* The effectiveness of 2D-Slice-CNN architecture in processing full 3D volumes while leveraging pretrained 2D models is demonstrated but not thoroughly validated. The results show competitive performance against 3D baselines, but the lack of extensive architectural comparisons limits confidence in the generality of these findings.

*Low Confidence:* The specific benefits of positional encodings are the least substantiated claim. The paper reports improvements "in some cases" without systematic analysis of when and why positional encodings help or hurt performance.

## Next Checks
1. **Ablation study on feature representations**: Systematically analyze which layers of the pretrained ResNet contribute most to transfer learning success by freezing different portions of the encoder and measuring performance degradation.

2. **Positional encoding sensitivity analysis**: Conduct controlled experiments varying positional encoding schemes (fixed vs. trainable, different initialization strategies) across datasets with different slice resolutions and anatomical coverage to identify when positional information is beneficial versus detrimental.

3. **Cross-dataset generalization test**: Train models on one dataset (e.g., UK Biobank) and evaluate on another (e.g., ADNI) to assess whether the observed transfer learning benefits generalize across different MRI acquisition protocols and population characteristics.
