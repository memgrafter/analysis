# Introducing Construct Theory as a Standard Methodology for Inclusive AI Models

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2304.09867
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2304.09867
- Reference count: 10
- Primary result: Current facial recognition systems fail on expanded construct elements, demonstrating bias against non-traditional facial variations

## Executive Summary
This paper proposes construct theory as a methodology to reduce bias in AI facial recognition systems by expanding the definition of facial features beyond traditional demographic labels. The authors developed a comprehensive construct framework containing 15 main elements and 50 sub-elements to capture diverse facial variations including cultural markings, lighting conditions, and non-Western features. Testing against Google Cloud Vision and Microsoft Cognitive Services APIs revealed significant performance gaps, particularly for faces with African ethnicity, face painting, cultural tattoos, and beards, demonstrating that current systems are trained on narrow, non-inclusive definitions that perpetuate bias.

## Method Summary
The authors created a construct of a face through literature review and expert consultation, defining 15 main elements (such as skin tone, face shape, cultural markings, and lighting conditions) with 50 corresponding sub-elements. They tested this construct framework against two commercial facial recognition APIs (Google Cloud Vision and Microsoft Cognitive Services) by evaluating performance across the expanded element set. The methodology involved systematic testing of how these APIs handled faces exhibiting various construct elements, measuring failure rates and confidence scores to identify inclusivity gaps in current systems.

## Key Results
- Both Google Cloud Vision and Microsoft Cognitive Services showed poor performance on many expanded construct elements
- Systems demonstrated particularly low confidence and frequent failures for faces with African ethnicity, face painting, cultural tattoos, and beards
- The testing revealed significant inclusivity gaps, with current AI systems missing substantial variations in facial features due to narrow training data definitions

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
Construct theory works by systematically expanding the definition of facial features beyond traditional demographic categories to include culturally diverse and non-Western facial variations. By incorporating a comprehensive set of 15 main elements and 50 sub-elements, the methodology captures facial variations that standard demographic labels miss. This expanded definition ensures that training data and model evaluation include a broader spectrum of human facial diversity, which should theoretically reduce bias by preventing systems from being optimized only for narrow, Western-centric facial features.

## Limitations
- No empirical validation that construct-based training improves model performance
- Construct framework developed through literature review and expert discussion, not empirical validation with diverse populations
- Testing methodology focuses on failure rates rather than examining false positive/negative patterns
- No evidence that the 50 sub-elements are mutually exclusive, collectively exhaustive, or truly representative

## Confidence
- Current systems exhibit bias against expanded facial elements: Medium
- Construct theory methodology will reduce bias in practice: Low
