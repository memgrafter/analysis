# Nonconvex Robust High-Order Tensor Completion Using Randomized Low-Rank Approximation

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2305.11495
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.11495
- Reference count: 40
- This paper develops efficient randomized algorithms for robust high-order tensor completion under the T-SVD framework, achieving 50-70% computational savings while maintaining competitive accuracy.

## Executive Summary
This paper addresses the challenge of robust high-order tensor completion in the presence of missing data and noise/outliers. The authors propose two efficient randomized low-rank tensor approximation algorithms (R-TSVD and RB-TSVD) that significantly reduce computational cost compared to deterministic truncated T-SVD methods. These algorithms are integrated into a double nonconvex optimization framework using weighted tensor Schatten-p norm and weighted ℓq-norm regularizers, solved via ADMM-based algorithms with convergence guarantees. Experimental results on both synthetic and real-world high-order tensor data demonstrate that the proposed methods achieve competitive performance in estimation accuracy while reducing CPU time by approximately 50-70% compared to state-of-the-art approaches.

## Method Summary
The authors develop a comprehensive framework for robust high-order tensor completion by first establishing the order-d tensor singular value decomposition (T-SVD) framework. They then propose two randomized algorithms - R-TSVD and RB-TSVD - that approximate the truncated T-SVD efficiently by leveraging randomized matrix approximation techniques. These randomized methods are designed to maintain computational efficiency while preserving the accuracy of deterministic approaches. The core of the method involves formulating a double nonconvex optimization problem that combines weighted tensor Schatten-p norm regularization with weighted ℓq-norm regularization to handle both low-rank structure and outliers simultaneously. The optimization problem is solved using an ADMM-based algorithm with proven convergence guarantees under certain conditions.

## Key Results
- The proposed R-TSVD and RB-TSVD algorithms achieve 50-70% reduction in CPU time compared to deterministic truncated T-SVD while maintaining similar precision
- The double nonconvex model using weighted tensor Schatten-p and ℓq norms effectively handles both missing data and outliers in high-order tensors
- Experiments on real-world data (color videos, light field images, multi-temporal remote sensing images) demonstrate competitive performance against state-of-the-art tensor completion methods
- The ADMM-based algorithms converge reliably and provide accurate tensor completion results across various tensor orders and dimensions

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The method leverages randomized matrix approximation techniques to efficiently compute truncated tensor SVDs, which are the computational bottleneck in tensor completion. By introducing nonconvex regularization (weighted tensor Schatten-p and ℓq norms), the model can better capture the underlying low-rank structure while simultaneously handling outliers and noise. The ADMM framework provides a systematic way to decompose the complex optimization problem into tractable subproblems, ensuring convergence while maintaining computational efficiency.

**Component Map**: Data Input -> Tensor Unfolding -> R-TSVD/RB-TSVD Approximation -> Double Nonconvex Regularization -> ADMM Solver -> Tensor Completion Output

**Critical Path**: The computational bottleneck is the truncated T-SVD computation, which the randomized algorithms address. The ADMM solver iteratively updates auxiliary variables and primal variables until convergence criteria are met.

**Design Tradeoffs**: The paper trades off determinism for computational efficiency through randomized algorithms, accepting controlled approximation error for significant speedup. The nonconvex regularization provides better model flexibility but requires careful parameter tuning and convergence analysis.

**Failure Signatures**: Poor performance may occur when tensor rank is very high, when noise levels exceed model assumptions, or when the randomized approximation error becomes significant for specific tensor structures. Convergence issues may arise if ADMM parameters are poorly chosen.
