# A Transformer-based representation-learning model with unified processing of multimodal input for clinical diagnostics
## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2306.00864
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.00864
- Reference count: 40
- Primary result: Unified multimodal Transformer (IRENE) achieves 0.924 mean AUROC for pulmonary disease identification, outperforming separate modality models by up to 12%

## Executive Summary
IRENE is a Transformer-based multimodal model that unifies image and clinical text processing for disease diagnosis, eliminating separate modality-specific pipelines and text structuralization. It employs bidirectional multimodal attention to capture intra- and inter-modal token relationships, learning holistic representations from radiographs, chief complaints, laboratory results, and demographics. On pulmonary disease identification, IRENE achieved a mean AUROC of 0.924, outperforming image-only models (0.805) and non-unified fusion methods (0.835–0.826) by up to 12% and 9%, respectively. For COVID-19 adverse outcome prediction, it achieved a mean AUPRC of 0.592, surpassing image-only (0.307) and non-unified methods (0.503–0.521) by up to 29% and 9%, with large gains in death prediction (0.441 vs. 0.192). Attention analysis revealed the model's reliance on radiographic and textual cues aligned with clinical reasoning. Ablation confirmed the critical role of bidirectional attention and multimodal input.

## Method Summary
IRENE uses a unified multimodal Transformer architecture that processes both images and clinical text without separate modality-specific pipelines or text structuralization. The model employs bidirectional multimodal attention to capture relationships between all input tokens across and within modalities. It takes radiographs, chief complaints, laboratory results, and demographic information as input, learning holistic representations for clinical diagnostics. The architecture is trained end-to-end using a single unified objective, allowing it to learn optimal fusion strategies rather than relying on predefined fusion mechanisms.

## Key Results
- Achieved mean AUROC of 0.924 for pulmonary disease identification, outperforming image-only models by 12% (0.805)
- Achieved mean AUPRC of 0.592 for COVID-19 adverse outcome prediction, outperforming image-only models by 29% (0.307)
- Large gains in death prediction (0.441 vs. 0.192) demonstrate superior handling of critical outcomes
- Ablation studies confirmed bidirectional attention and multimodal input are critical for performance

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The unified multimodal Transformer architecture eliminates information loss from modality-specific preprocessing and enables learning of optimal cross-modal attention patterns. Bidirectional attention allows the model to contextualize each token using information from all other tokens across both modalities simultaneously, capturing complex dependencies that separate processing pipelines miss. The end-to-end training enables the model to learn which modality-specific features are most relevant for different clinical tasks, rather than relying on hand-engineered feature selection or predefined fusion strategies.
