# An investigation of the reconstruction capacity of stacked convolutional autoencoders for log-mel-spectrograms

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2301.07665
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2301.07665
- Reference count: 28
- Primary result: Stacked convolutional autoencoders effectively reconstruct log-mel-spectrograms with a novel precision-recall evaluation metric

## Executive Summary
This study investigates stacked convolutional autoencoders for compressing and reconstructing log-mel-spectrograms of monophonic and harmonic musical instrument sounds. The proposed method maps input spectrograms to a lower-dimensional latent space and reconstructs them using a mirrored decoder architecture. Various regularization techniques, pooling strategies, and latent space dimensionality reductions are explored to optimize reconstruction quality. A novel evaluation metric based on precision-recall of detected frequencies is introduced to assess the perceptual similarity between original and reconstructed spectrograms, capturing the significance of harmonics in musical timbre.

## Method Summary
The study employs stacked convolutional autoencoders to compress and reconstruct log-mel-spectrograms. The encoder maps the input spectrogram to a lower-dimensional latent space, while the decoder reconstructs the spectrogram from this compressed representation. Various regularization techniques, including dropout, kernel and activity regularization, are explored to improve reconstruction quality. Different pooling strategies and latent space dimensionality reductions are also investigated. The proposed method is evaluated using a novel precision-recall based metric that assesses the similarity between original and reconstructed spectrograms by detecting and comparing frequencies, capturing the perceptual significance of harmonics in musical timbre.

## Key Results
- L2 kernel regularization and max pooling significantly improve reconstruction performance
- A smaller latent space (3% of original size) yields more accurate reconstructions for the tested dataset
- The absence of a fully connected layer enhances sound quality by preserving spatial information in the spectrograms
- The proposed precision-recall based evaluation metric provides a meaningful assessment of generative audio models' reconstruction quality

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The stacked convolutional autoencoder architecture works by learning hierarchical feature representations of the input log-mel-spectrograms. The encoder progressively reduces the spatial dimensions while increasing the feature depth, capturing increasingly abstract representations of the audio. The decoder then mirrors this process, progressively upsampling the latent representation to reconstruct the original spectrogram. Regularization techniques like L2 kernel regularization prevent overfitting and encourage smoother, more generalizable feature representations. Max pooling helps in capturing the most salient features while reducing computational complexity. The proposed precision-recall metric effectively captures the perceptual similarity by focusing on the presence and prominence of key frequencies, which are crucial for musical timbre perception.

## Architecture Onboarding

**Component Map:**
Input Spectrogram -> Encoder (Conv + Pool) -> Latent Space -> Decoder (Conv + Upsample) -> Reconstructed Spectrogram

**Critical Path:**
The critical path involves the encoder progressively reducing the spatial dimensions while increasing feature depth, the latent space representation, and the decoder mirroring this process to reconstruct the spectrogram.

**Design Tradeoffs:**
- Depth vs. Reconstructability: Deeper networks may capture more abstract features but risk losing fine-grained details crucial for accurate reconstruction.
- Latent Space Size vs. Compression: Smaller latent spaces provide better compression but may lose important information, affecting reconstruction quality.
- Regularization Strength vs. Expressiveness: Stronger regularization prevents overfitting but may limit the model's ability to capture complex patterns in the data.

**Failure Signatures:**
- High-frequency artifacts in the reconstructed spectrograms indicate that the model is struggling to capture fine-grained details.
- Blurriness or loss of sharp transitions suggests that the latent space is too small or the decoder is not effectively upsampling the features.
- Mode collapse, where the model consistently generates similar outputs regardless of input variations, may indicate overfitting or insufficient diversity in the training data.

## Limitations
- The study focuses on log-mel-spectrograms of monophonic and harmonic musical instrument sounds, which may limit generalizability to other types of audio or more complex musical textures.
- The novel precision-recall based evaluation metric, while potentially perceptually relevant, lacks external validation against human perception studies or other established audio quality metrics.
- The claim about the optimal latent space size (3% of original) and the specific benefits of avoiding fully connected layers are based on experiments with a limited dataset and may not generalize to other scenarios without further validation.

## Confidence
- **High confidence:** The general finding that regularization techniques (L2 kernel regularization, max pooling) improve reconstruction quality is well-supported by the results and aligns with established practices in deep learning.
- **Medium confidence:** The effectiveness of the proposed precision-recall based evaluation metric for audio reconstruction quality is supported by the study's results but lacks external validation and comparison to other metrics.
- **Low confidence:** The claim about the optimal latent space size (3% of original) and the specific benefits of avoiding fully connected layers are based on experiments with a limited dataset and may not generalize to other scenarios without further validation.
