# Hyperbolic Contrastive Learning

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2302.01409
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2302.01409
- Reference count: 40
- Key outcome: Hyperbolic Contrastive Learning (HCL) achieves 87.98% accuracy on CIFAR-10 (vs. 86.58% for SimCLR) and RHCL achieves 29.94% adversarial robustness accuracy (vs. 26.57% for RoCL)

## Executive Summary
This paper introduces hyperbolic geometry into contrastive learning to better capture hierarchical structures in image datasets. The authors propose three variants: Hyperbolic Contrastive Learning (HCL) for self-supervised learning, Supervised Hyperbolic Contrastive Learning (SHCL) for supervised classification, and Robust Hyperbolic Contrastive Learning (RHCL) for adversarial robustness. Their experiments demonstrate improved performance on CIFAR-10/100, Tiny ImageNet, and ImageNet compared to standard contrastive learning approaches.

## Method Summary
The method extends contrastive learning to hyperbolic space using the Poincaré ball model. Images are passed through an encoder (ResNet-18/50), projected to a latent space, then mapped to hyperbolic space via an exponential map. The contrastive loss uses hyperbolic distance instead of Euclidean distance. The framework includes three variants: HCL for self-supervised learning, SHCL for supervised classification, and RHCL for adversarial robustness. The curvature parameter c is tuned per dataset, with c=0.6 showing best results on CIFAR-10.

## Key Results
- HCL achieves 87.98% accuracy on CIFAR-10 (vs. 86.58% for SimCLR baseline)
- RHCL achieves 29.94% adversarial robustness accuracy (vs. 26.57% for RoCL baseline)
- SHCL outperforms SupCon on both CIFAR-10 (95.1% vs 93.9%) and CIFAR-100

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: Hyperbolic space provides better representation power for hierarchical image data than Euclidean space.
- Mechanism: Hyperbolic space has exponential volume growth matching tree-like structures, allowing efficient embedding of hierarchical relationships.
- Core assumption: Many image datasets contain inherent hierarchical semantic structures.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "Hyperbolic space is a continuous version of trees that naturally owns the ability to model hierarchical structures"
  - [section] "Different from the Euclidean space Rn that has polynomial volume growth w.r.t. the radius, the hyperbolic space Hn has exponential growth that is suitable for tree-like structure data"
  - [corpus] Weak evidence - only general papers on hyperbolic embeddings, no direct CIFAR/Imagenet hierarchical structure analysis
- Break condition: If dataset lacks hierarchical structure, Euclidean space may be equally or more effective.

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: Contrastive learning in hyperbolic space improves both standard and adversarial accuracy.
- Mechanism: Hyperbolic distance better captures semantic similarity for hierarchical data, leading to better feature alignment during contrastive training.
- Core assumption: The hyperbolic embedding preserves semantic relationships better than Euclidean embedding for the datasets studied.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "we demonstrate that RHCL is more robust to adversarial perturbations compared with general contrastive learning and other baseline methods"
  - [section] "RHCL achieves 29.94% adversarial robustness accuracy (vs. 26.57% for RoCL)"
  - [corpus] No corpus evidence for adversarial robustness specifically
- Break condition: If adversarial robustness gains come primarily from other factors (like data augmentation consistency), hyperbolic space may not be the main driver.

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: Supervised hyperbolic contrastive learning outperforms standard supervised contrastive learning.
- Mechanism: Hyperbolic space allows better separation of classes with hierarchical relationships while maintaining intra-class similarity.
- Core assumption: Class labels in the datasets have some hierarchical relationship (e.g., animal subcategories).
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "SHCL also outperforms SupCon on CIFAR-10 and CIFAR-100"
  - [section] "Our SHCL could reach 95.1 accuracy with 200 epochs pretraining on CIFAR-10 compared with SupCon"
  - [corpus] No corpus evidence for supervised hyperbolic contrastive learning performance
- Break condition: If classes are completely independent without hierarchical relationships, Euclidean space may perform equally well.

  - Sensitivity to curvature parameter c

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- Question: How does the curvature parameter c in hyperbolic space affect the representation quality and downstream task performance?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper shows that different values of c (0.1 to 1.0) were tested on CIFAR-10, with c = 0.6 yielding the best accuracy. The authors note that the model is "slightly affected by c" and that the optimal value may vary across datasets.
- Why unresolved: The paper only provides results for a limited range of c values and a single dataset (CIFAR-10). It's unclear how the optimal c value generalizes to other datasets or tasks.
- What evidence would resolve it: A comprehensive study across multiple datasets and tasks, systematically varying c to find optimal values for each scenario.

### Open Question 2
- Question: Does explicitly incorporating hierarchical information into the contrastive learning process improve performance compared to the unsupervised approach?
- Basis in paper: [inferred] The paper mentions that "hierarchical semantic structures naturally exist in image datasets" and that their method aims to "reveal the underlying hierarchical structure of data." However, they only use unsupervised contrastive learning without explicit hierarchy information.
- Why unresolved: The paper doesn't compare their unsupervised hyperbolic contrastive learning approach with methods that explicitly use hierarchical information.
- What evidence would resolve it: Direct comparison of HCL with a supervised variant that incorporates hierarchy information, measuring performance differences on various datasets and tasks.

### Open Question 3
- Question: How does hyperbolic contrastive learning perform on downstream tasks beyond image classification, such as object detection or semantic segmentation?
- Basis in paper: [inferred] The paper focuses on image classification tasks and mentions that "good image representations" are beneficial to downstream tasks. However, they only evaluate on classification benchmarks.
- Why unresolved: The paper doesn't explore the effectiveness of hyperbolic representations on other computer vision tasks that are commonly used to evaluate representation quality.
- What evidence would resolve it: Evaluation of pre-trained HCL models on object detection, semantic segmentation, and other vision tasks, comparing performance with models pre-trained using Euclidean contrastive learning methods.

## Limitations
- Limited analysis of why hyperbolic space specifically improves performance beyond the general claim about hierarchical structure
- No comparison with other non-Euclidean alternatives (spherical, product spaces)
- Results may be sensitive to hyperparameter tuning, particularly the curvature parameter c
