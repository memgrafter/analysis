# Bayesian calibration of differentiable agent-based models
## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2305.15340
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.15340
- Reference count: 8
- Key outcome: Bayesian inference framework for differentiable ABMs using generalized variational inference with neural spline flows, achieving accurate calibration with 2,500 simulations on COVID-19 model

## Executive Summary
This work introduces a Bayesian inference framework for differentiable agent-based models (ABMs) that combines generalized variational inference with differentiable simulators. The approach leverages automatic differentiation to enable gradient-based inference, providing robust posterior estimates that account for model misspecification. A neural spline flow approximates the posterior, trained with a scoring rule measuring simulation-to-data discrepancy and a divergence term maintaining proximity to the prior. Experiments on a COVID-19 ABM demonstrate accurate parameter inference with significantly fewer simulations compared to traditional ABC methods.

## Method Summary
The framework combines generalized variational inference with differentiable simulators to enable Bayesian calibration of ABMs. It uses automatic differentiation to compute gradients through the ABM, allowing efficient optimization of a neural spline flow that approximates the posterior distribution. The objective function combines a scoring rule that measures discrepancy between simulated and observed data with a divergence term that regularizes the approximation toward the prior. This approach provides uncertainty quantification while requiring substantially fewer simulations than traditional ABC methods.

## Key Results
- Accurate parameter recovery demonstrated on COVID-19 ABM with true parameter values captured within inferred posterior
- Achieved effective calibration with only 2,500 simulations compared to typical ABC requirements
- Neural spline flow successfully approximated the posterior distribution with appropriate uncertainty quantification

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The method exploits differentiability of ABMs through automatic differentiation, enabling gradient-based optimization of the variational approximation. The scoring rule directly measures simulation-to-data discrepancy, while the divergence term ensures regularization toward the prior, creating a balanced objective that handles model misspecification. The neural spline flow provides a flexible, invertible transformation capable of approximating complex posterior geometries efficiently.

## Foundational Learning
- **Generalized variational inference**: Extends standard VI by incorporating scoring rules that measure model fit; needed because standard VI assumptions often fail with complex ABMs; quick check: verify scoring rule properties
- **Automatic differentiation through ABMs**: Enables gradient computation for gradient-based inference; required for efficient optimization of variational parameters; quick check: test gradient correctness on simple models
- **Neural spline flows**: Flexible, invertible transformations for density estimation; necessary for approximating complex posterior distributions; quick check: assess flow expressiveness on known distributions
- **Model misspecification handling**: Framework provides robust inference even when ABM doesn't perfectly match data; critical for real-world ABM applications; quick check: test with deliberately misspecified models
- **Variational inference vs ABC**: Trade-off between computational efficiency and approximation accuracy; VI requires fewer simulations but may struggle with complex posteriors; quick check: compare inference quality across regimes
- **Scoring rules**: Proper scoring rules ensure consistent parameter estimation; essential for valid Bayesian inference; quick check: verify scoring rule properness

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component Map**: Data -> Scoring Rule -> Neural Spline Flow -> Prior Regularization -> Objective Function -> Optimization Algorithm -> Posterior Approximation

**Critical Path**: Observed data → scoring rule computation → gradient calculation through differentiable ABM → neural spline flow optimization → posterior approximation

**Design Tradeoffs**: 
- Differentiability requirement limits applicability to models that can be made differentiable
- Neural spline flow complexity vs approximation accuracy trade-off
- Number of simulations vs inference quality balance
- Scoring rule choice affects robustness to model misspecification

**Failure Signatures**: 
- Poor posterior approximation indicates insufficient flow flexibility or optimization issues
- Gradient explosion suggests numerical instability in differentiable ABM
- Over-regularization toward prior indicates weak data informativeness
- Multimodal posteriors may not be well-captured by single neural spline flow

**First Experiments**:
1. Test gradient computation on simple ABM with known analytical gradients
2. Verify scoring rule properness with synthetic data
3. Assess neural spline flow expressiveness on simple target distributions

## Limitations
- Framework relies on differentiability, constraining applicability to models that can be made differentiable
- Neural spline flow may struggle with highly multimodal posteriors common in ABMs
- Evaluation focuses on parameter recovery rather than predictive performance or model validation
- Computational advantages based on single case study may not generalize across ABM types

## Confidence
- **High**: Mathematical framework combining generalized variational inference with differentiable simulators is sound and well-grounded
- **Medium**: Empirical results showing accurate parameter recovery and computational efficiency are promising but based on single ABM example
- **Medium**: Uncertainty quantification claims are technically valid but practical value for decision-making requires further validation

## Next Checks
1. Test framework on multiple ABMs with varying complexity, including models with known multimodality or hierarchical structures to assess posterior approximation quality

3. Evaluate predictive performance on held-out validation data and compare against established ABM calibration approaches beyond just ABC methods
