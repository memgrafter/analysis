# Transcription free filler word detection with Neural semi-CRFs

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2303.06475
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.06475
- Reference count: 0
- Primary result: 6.4% absolute F1 improvement over CNN baseline on PodcastFillers dataset

## Executive Summary
This paper introduces a transcription-free approach for detecting filler words ("uh" and "um") in spontaneous speech using wav2vec features, an S4 sequence encoder, and a neural semi-CRF layer. The system achieves 6.4% absolute F1 improvement over a previous CNN-based baseline on the PodcastFillers dataset while requiring no manual transcriptions. The neural semi-CRF layer directly models filler events and outputs intervals without requiring post-processing or threshold calibration, addressing a key limitation of frame-level classification approaches.

## Method Summary
The method uses pretrained wav2vec features (10ms hop size from 16kHz audio) as input to an S4 sequence encoder, followed by a neural semi-CRF layer for interval prediction. The S4 backbone captures long-range temporal dependencies in audio features, while the semi-CRF directly models filler events without requiring post-processing. The model is trained end-to-end on 2-second segments with 50ms label resolution using AdamW optimizer (lr=1e-3, weight decay=1e-2, cosine annealing) for 30 epochs. VAD filtering is applied to remove non-voiced regions from the PodcastFillers dataset before training.

## Key Results
- 6.4% absolute F1 improvement (segment level) and 3.1% absolute F1 improvement (event level) over CNN baseline
- Temporal resolution sensitivity: 74.1% F1 at 50ms vs 67.4% at 100ms resolution
- Struggles with short medial fillers and words containing similar phonemes ("a", "the", "uh-huh")

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: S4 sequence encoder effectively captures long-range temporal dependencies in audio features
- Mechanism: S4 uses structured state space models with diagonal plus low-rank matrix parameterization for efficient long convolution kernels
- Core assumption: Filler words are characterized by temporal patterns extending beyond local frames
- Evidence anchors: Abstract states S4 "efficiently captures long-range temporal dependencies"
- Break condition: If fillers are primarily characterized by local acoustic features rather than temporal context

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: Neural semi-CRF layer directly models filler events without post-processing
- Mechanism: Semi-CRFs model conditional probability over non-overlapping intervals rather than frame classifications
- Core assumption: Temporal boundaries of fillers can be learned jointly with classification
- Evidence anchors: Abstract states semi-CRF "directly models filler events and outputs intervals without requiring post-processing"
- Break condition: If optimal threshold varies significantly across audio conditions

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: Wav2vec features provide sufficient low-level acoustic information without transcriptions
- Mechanism: Pretrained wav2vec encoder extracts meaningful acoustic representations capturing phonetic characteristics
- Core assumption: Fillers have distinctive acoustic patterns recognizable without semantic context
- Evidence anchors: Uses wav2vec to encode 16kHz audio into temporal embeddings with 10ms hop size
- Break condition: If fillers are distinguished primarily by semantic context rather than acoustic properties

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map: Raw audio → Wav2vec feature encoder → S4 sequence encoder → Pairwise score networks → Neural semi-CRF layer → Output intervals
- Critical path: Wav2vec → S4 → Semi-CRF (end-to-end training from raw audio to event intervals)
- Design tradeoffs: wav2vec pretrained vs training from scratch (accuracy vs data requirements); S4 vs CRNN (parameter efficiency vs computational complexity); semi-CRF vs frame-level classification (end-to-end learning vs post-processing flexibility)
- Failure signatures: Poor performance on short medial fillers suggests S4 may not capture rapid temporal transitions; confusion with similar phonemes indicates wav2vec features may not capture subtle distinctions; sensitivity to temporal resolution suggests struggles with precise boundary localization
- First 3 experiments: 1) Compare S4 vs CRNN backbones on validation set to quantify improvement from long-range temporal modeling; 2) Test different temporal resolutions (25ms, 50ms, 100ms) to find optimal tradeoff; 3) Evaluate semi-CRF vs frame-level classification + median filtering to measure end-to-end benefit

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- Question: What specific model architecture or training approach could close the gap between transcription-free and transcription-based filler word detection systems?
- Basis in paper: Paper states "Although there is still a large gap from the transcription-based system, VC-50-S4CRF achieves 6.4% absolute improvement..."
- Why unresolved: Paper acknowledges performance gap remains significant but does not propose specific solutions
- What evidence would resolve it: Development and evaluation of transcription-free system achieving performance within 1-2% of transcription-based baseline

### Open Question 2
- Question: How would the system perform on detecting non-filler speech disfluencies without requiring transcriptions?
- Basis in paper: Paper mentions "Future work can concentrate on... extending this framework to the detection of general speech disfluencies without transcriptions"
- Why unresolved: Current system specifically designed for filler words, not tested on other disfluencies
- What evidence would resolve it: Successful adaptation to detect and classify various types of speech disfluencies with comparable accuracy to filler detection

### Open Question 3
- Question: What is the computational efficiency trade-off between S4 and CRNN backbones for real-time applications?
- Basis in paper: Paper states "In S4-based models, the computation of convolution... requires fast Fourier transform... they are not as efficient as CRNN-based models"
- Why unresolved: Paper notes S4 is less efficient but provides no quantitative measurements of latency or computational requirements
- What evidence would resolve it: Comparative analysis of inference time, memory usage, and power consumption between S4 and CRNN models on embedded hardware

## Limitations
- Temporal resolution sensitivity shows significant performance degradation when label resolution changes from 50ms to 100ms
- Phonetic confusion patterns with words containing similar phonemes ("a", "the", "uh-huh") limit generalizability
- Limited cross-domain validation as evaluation is conducted exclusively on PodcastFillers dataset

## Confidence
- High confidence: Architectural contribution of combining S4 with semi-CRFs for filler detection
- Medium confidence: Mechanism claims for S4's long-range modeling and semi-CRF's interval prediction based on specific experimental setup
- Low confidence: Real-time applicability due to lack of timing analysis or latency measurements
