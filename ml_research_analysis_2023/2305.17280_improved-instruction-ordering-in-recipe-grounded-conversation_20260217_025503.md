# Improved Instruction Ordering in Recipe-Grounded Conversation

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2305.17280
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.17280
- Reference count: 30
- Key outcome: Auxiliary subtasks (User Intent Detection and Instruction State Tracking) help reduce incorrect instruction ordering in recipe-grounded conversational agents, though improvements are modest.

## Executive Summary
This paper addresses the challenge of incorrect instruction ordering in recipe-grounded conversational agents. The authors propose two auxiliary subtasks—User Intent Detection and Instruction State Tracking—to improve response generation. User Intent Detection is formulated as a few-shot learning problem, leveraging existing dialogue datasets (MultiWOZ, Schema Guided Dialogue) and natural language descriptions of intents. Instruction State Tracking uses a simple unsupervised approach based on unigram F1 overlap or sentence embeddings to align conversation turns to recipe steps. Experiments on the new ChattyChef dataset show that incorporating user intent and instruction state information helps reduce incorrect order issues, though the improvements are modest. Analysis of ChatGPT outputs reveals it also makes mistakes (10.7% of responses), with about half being out-of-order instructions. The ChattyChef dataset is released to facilitate further research.

## Method Summary
The authors propose two auxiliary subtasks to support response generation in recipe-grounded conversational agents. User Intent Detection is formulated as a few-shot learning problem, where the model is fine-tuned on existing dialogue datasets (MultiWOZ, Schema Guided Dialogue) and then transferred to the recipe-grounded domain using natural language descriptions of intents. Instruction State Tracking uses a simple unsupervised approach based on unigram F1 overlap or sentence embeddings to align conversation turns to recipe steps. The response generation model (GPT-J) is then conditioned on the conversation history, selected recipe knowledge, and the auxiliary information from the two subtasks.

## Key Results
- Incorporating user intent and instruction state information helps reduce incorrect order issues in recipe-grounded conversations.
- ChatGPT makes mistakes in 10.7% of responses, with about half being out-of-order instructions.
- The ChattyChef dataset, containing 267 dialogues and 267 recipes, is released to facilitate further research.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- **Claim**: User intent detection improves instruction ordering by enabling the model to anticipate what type of response is needed.
- **Mechanism**: When the system can classify the user's intent (e.g., "ask for the next instruction" vs. "ask for ingredient substitutions"), it can better decide whether to proceed to the next recipe step or provide information about the current step.
- **Core assumption**: The model's ability to ground responses in the correct recipe context is significantly influenced by understanding the user's intent.
- **Evidence anchors**:
  - [abstract]: "We hypothesize that this is due to the model's lack of understanding of user intent and inability to track the instruction state"
  - [section 3.1]: "we propose to explore two auxiliary subtasks, namely User Intent Detection and Instruction State Tracking, to support Response Generation with improved instruction grounding"
  - [corpus]: Weak evidence - only general citations about intent detection without specific results

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim**: Instruction state tracking improves ordering by providing the model with explicit information about which recipe step the user is currently working on.
- **Mechanism**: By aligning the conversation to specific recipe steps, the model can select only the relevant portion of the recipe to condition on, reducing the likelihood of skipping ahead or repeating steps.
- **Core assumption**: Models perform better when given focused context rather than the entire recipe, especially when they can identify the current step.
- **Evidence anchors**:
  - [abstract]: "We hypothesize that this is due to the model's lack of understanding of user intent and inability to track the instruction state (i.e., which step was last instructed)"
  - [section 4.1]: "we adopt a simple unsupervised approach to track the instruction state. The key idea of our approach is to align the most recent system utterance with its most similar step in the recipe"
  - [corpus]: Weak evidence - only general citations about state tracking without specific results

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim**: Combining intent and state information provides complementary benefits that together improve instruction ordering more than either alone.
- **Mechanism**: Intent detection tells the model what type of response is needed, while state tracking tells it which recipe context to use. Together, they provide both the "what" and "where" of the response.
- **Core assumption**: The two auxiliary tasks address different aspects of the ordering problem, and their effects are additive.
- **Evidence anchors**:
  - [abstract]: "incorporating user intent and instruction state information helps the response generation model mitigate the incorrect order issue"
  - [section 5.3]: "all GPT-J variants that incorporate the intent and/or state information have fewer errors and more responses rated with 4 and 5 for correctness than the base model"
  - [corpus]: Weak evidence - only general citations about combining information without specific results

## Foundational Learning

- **Concept**: Few-shot learning for intent detection
  - **Why needed here**: The recipe-grounded dialogue domain requires specific intents that don't exist in standard dialogue datasets, making it impractical to annotate large amounts of training data.
  - **Quick check question**: How does the model transfer intent knowledge from MultiWOZ to ChattyChef without having seen ChattyChef intents during training?

- **Concept**: Unigram F1 overlap for state alignment
  - **Why needed here**: Simple string matching is effective for aligning conversational responses to recipe steps because cooking instructions contain many specific ingredients and actions that are unlikely to be paraphrased.
  - **Quick check question**: Why might sentence embeddings be less effective than unigram F1 for aligning recipe steps to conversation turns?

- **Concept**: Knowledge selection from recipes
  - **Why needed here**: Large recipes contain many steps, and providing the entire recipe to the model can lead to confusion about which step to focus on. Selecting only relevant portions improves performance.
  - **Quick check question**: What are the tradeoffs between the "Cutoff" approach (selecting from current step forward) and "Center" approach (selecting a window around current step)?

## Architecture Onboarding

- **Component map**: User Intent Detection → Instruction State Tracking → Response Generation → User
- **Critical path**: Conversation History → Intent Detection → State Tracking → Response Generation → User
- **Design tradeoffs**:
  - Simple vs. complex state tracking: The paper uses simple unigram F1 matching rather than more sophisticated methods, trading potential accuracy for simplicity and interpretability
  - Knowledge selection strategies: Cutoff vs. Center approaches balance focus vs. context availability
  - Fine-tuning vs. in-context learning: The paper explores both approaches for intent detection

- **Failure signatures**:
  - Incorrect order instructions: Likely indicates either state tracking failure or model ignoring state information
  - Irrelevant responses: Likely indicates intent detection failure or model ignoring intent information
  - Repetitive responses: Likely indicates state tracking failure to detect step changes

- **First 3 experiments**:
  1. Evaluate intent detection accuracy on ChattyChef test set with different training approaches (in-context, few-shot, cross-dataset)
  2. Compare state tracking accuracy using WordMatch vs. SentEmb approaches on validation and test sets
  3. Compare Response Generation models with different combinations of intent and state information (GPT-J, GPT-J+int, GPT-J+cut, GPT-J+ctr) using automatic and human evaluation metrics

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- **Question**: How can the instruction ordering issue in recipe-grounded dialogue systems be further improved beyond the current methods of User Intent Detection and Instruction State Tracking?
- **Basis in paper**: Explicit - The paper discusses the limitations of current methods in mitigating the incorrect order issue, with only modest improvements observed.
- **Why unresolved**: The current methods do not fully solve the problem, and the paper suggests that more effective ways of incorporating user intent and state information into the Response Generation module need to be investigated.
- **What evidence would resolve it**: Future research could explore alternative methods for tracking user intent and instruction state, or develop new techniques for integrating this information into the response generation process to achieve better results.

### Open Question 2
- **Question**: What is the impact of using different types of large language models (e.g., encoder-decoder models) on the performance of recipe-grounded dialogue systems?
- **Basis in paper**: Explicit - The paper mentions that it has only analyzed the errors of GPT-J and ChatGPT models, and raises the question of whether other models with different architectures have the same issues.
- **Why unresolved**: The study is limited to two specific models, and there is no exploration of how other architectures might perform in this task.
- **What evidence would resolve it**: Conducting experiments with a variety of large language models, including encoder-decoder models, could provide insights into their effectiveness in handling the instruction ordering challenge.

### Open Question 3
- **Question**: How can the performance of the User Intent Detection and Instruction State Tracking subtasks be improved in low-resource settings?
- **Basis in paper**: Explicit - The paper highlights the low performance of the modules for these subtasks and suggests that further research is needed.
- **Why unresolved**: The current methods, while helpful, do not achieve high accuracy, and the paper indicates that more research is required to enhance their effectiveness.
- **What evidence would resolve it**: Developing new techniques for few-shot learning or transfer learning that can better leverage existing datasets could lead to improved performance in low-resource scenarios.

## Limitations

- The ChattyChef dataset, while novel, is relatively small (267 dialogues) which may limit the generalizability of findings.
- The human evaluation was conducted by the authors themselves, which could introduce bias despite efforts to maintain objectivity.
- The evaluation of user intent detection relies on few-shot learning approaches that may not generalize well to more complex or nuanced user intents beyond the basic categories studied.

## Confidence

**High Confidence**: The fundamental observation that large language models make incorrect ordering errors in recipe-grounded conversations (10.7% error rate for ChatGPT) is well-supported by empirical evidence. The correlation between incorporating intent and state information and improved ordering metrics also has strong support from the experimental results.

**Medium Confidence**: The effectiveness of the proposed few-shot learning approach for user intent detection is moderately supported, though the results show that performance is sensitive to the choice of training data and intent descriptions. The unsupervised instruction state tracking method shows promise but has limited quantitative validation compared to more established supervised approaches.

**Low Confidence**: The claim that combining intent and state information provides complementary benefits is weakly supported, as the experimental results show only modest improvements and don't provide clear evidence of synergistic effects between the two approaches.

## Next Checks

1. **Cross-dataset validation**: Test the few-shot intent detection model on a separate recipe-grounded dialogue dataset (if available) or on a held-out portion of ChattyChef to assess generalization beyond the test set.

2. **Ablation study on state tracking**: Compare the proposed unsupervised state tracking approach against a supervised baseline where step alignments are annotated in the training data to quantify the cost of the unsupervised approach.

3. **Real-world deployment test**: Conduct a user study where the system is deployed with real users cooking actual recipes, measuring not just ordering accuracy but also task completion rates and user satisfaction to assess practical effectiveness.
