# Neuro-Symbolic World Models for Adapting to Open World Novelty

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2301.06294
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2301.06294
- Reference count: 40
- Key outcome: WorldCloner improves adaptation efficiency to environmental novelties, outperforming PPO and matching DreamerV2 with fewer policy updates (1.63M vs 3.82E8).

## Executive Summary
This paper addresses the challenge of adapting reinforcement learning agents to sudden environmental changes (novelties) through a neuro-symbolic world model approach called WorldCloner. The method combines a neural policy with a symbolic rule-based world model that learns pre-novelty transition rules using axis-aligned bounding intervals (AABIs). When novelty is detected through rule violations, the system updates the symbolic model and uses it to generate imagined state-action-state transitions that help the policy adapt more efficiently. Results show WorldCloner outperforms standard PPO and matches DreamerV2's adaptive efficiency across three novelty types while requiring significantly fewer policy updates.

## Method Summary
WorldCloner learns symbolic rules representing pre-novelty environment dynamics, then detects novelty when observed transitions violate these rules. Upon novelty detection, the system updates the symbolic rule model and uses it to generate imagined rollouts that populate the policy's experience buffer. This imagination-based adaptation allows the policy to learn from simulated experiences, reducing the need for costly real environment interactions. The symbolic model uses AABIs to represent state-action-state transitions and employs rule relaxation and collision resolution to maintain a compact, non-redundant rule set. The approach is evaluated on grid-world environments with DoorKey and LavaShortcutMaze tasks, testing three novelty types: DoorKeyChange, LavaProof, and LavaHurts.

## Key Results
- WorldCloner outperforms PPO baseline in adaptive efficiency across all three novelty types
- WorldCloner matches DreamerV2's adaptive efficiency while requiring significantly fewer policy updates (1.63M vs 3.82E8)
- The symbolic rule model learns rapidly, achieving near-perfect prediction accuracy within 2000 training steps in simple grid environments
- WorldCloner demonstrates effective novelty detection and adaptation in both pre-planned and shortcut novelty scenarios

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map:
  Neural Policy (PPO/A2C) -> Symbolic Rule Model -> Novelty Detector -> Imagination Engine -> Mixing Module -> Neural Policy

- Critical path:
  1. Learn symbolic rules from pre-novelty interactions
  2. Deploy policy and monitor for rule violations
  3. Upon novelty detection, update symbolic rules
  4. Generate imagined rollouts using updated rules
  5. Update policy using mixed real/imagined experiences
  6. Iterate until asymptotic performance is reached

- Design tradeoffs:
  - Rule complexity vs prediction accuracy: More complex AABIs capture more transitions but increase computational cost
  - Imagination ratio: Higher imagination ratios reduce real interactions but risk propagating model errors
  - Novelty sensitivity: Lower thresholds detect novelty faster but increase false positives

- Failure signatures:
  - Poor pre-novelty rule learning (high prediction error) suggests the environment is too complex for AABIs
  - Delayed novelty detection suggests rules are too general or the novelty is subtle
  - Failure to adapt suggests the rule model cannot capture post-novelty dynamics

- First 3 experiments:
  1. Verify pre-novelty rule learning converges to near-zero prediction error on a simple grid world
  2. Test novelty detection by injecting known novelties and measuring detection time
  3. Compare adaptive efficiency against PPO baseline on a single novelty type

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- Question: How can novelty-aware exploration techniques be designed to detect shortcuts that do not lie on the pre-novelty optimal path?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper notes that "when the pre-novelty optimal path still exists, converged, non-exploring agents will struggle to detect novelty" and that "future research is needed on novelty-aware exploration techniques."
- Why unresolved: Current exploration methods rely on following the optimal policy, which may never encounter the novelty if it's not on the optimal path.
- What evidence would resolve it: An exploration technique that consistently detects shortcut novelties across multiple environments and novelty types, with quantitative comparisons to standard exploration methods.

### Open Question 2
- Question: What is the optimal balance between imagination-based simulation and real environment interactions for policy adaptation in different novelty scenarios?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper uses a mixing ratio of 60% real rollouts to 40% imagined rollouts, determined empirically, and notes "this ratio was determined empirically by looking at the trade-off between asymptotic adaptive performance and adaptive efficiency."
- Why unresolved: The optimal ratio likely varies depending on the environment complexity, novelty type, and world model accuracy.
- What evidence would resolve it: Systematic experiments across multiple environments varying the imagination ratio, measuring both adaptation efficiency and final performance.

### Open Question 3
- Question: How does WorldCloner's performance scale to more complex, high-dimensional environments compared to neural-only world models?
- Basis in paper: [inferred] The experiments were conducted in grid-world environments with simple symbolic features. The paper demonstrates effectiveness in these settings but does not test scalability to more complex domains.
- Why unresolved: Grid worlds have limited state spaces and simple transition dynamics, which may not reflect challenges in more complex environments.
- What evidence would resolve it: Comparative experiments in environments with higher-dimensional observations, continuous state spaces, or more complex dynamics, measuring adaptation efficiency and sample complexity.

## Limitations

- The effectiveness of AABIs for capturing complex state-action transitions in higher-dimensional environments remains unproven, as all experiments were conducted on simple grid worlds with symbolic features
- The computational overhead of maintaining and updating rule sets as they grow with environment complexity is not characterized
- The robustness of novelty detection to false positives/negatives in noisier environments is unclear

## Confidence

- **High Confidence**: Claims about WorldCloner's superior adaptive efficiency compared to PPO in the tested grid environments (DoorKey, LavaShortcutMaze tasks)
- **Medium Confidence**: Claims about matching DreamerV2's performance with fewer policy updates - dependent on specific DreamerV2 implementation details not fully specified
- **Low Confidence**: Claims about scalability to complex, high-dimensional environments - not validated beyond the simple grid world testbed
