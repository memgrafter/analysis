# User-Centric Evaluation of OCR Systems for Kwak'wala

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2302.13410
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2302.13410
- Reference count: 14
- Primary result: OCR reduces transcription time by over 50% for Kwak'wala documents

## Executive Summary
This paper presents a human-centric evaluation of OCR systems for the Kwak'wala language, an endangered language with extensive written documentation. The authors conduct a user study comparing manual transcription speed with and without the use of OCR, including both off-the-shelf tools and post-correction methods. Using a Latin Square design to control for variability, they find that OCR reduces transcription time by over 50% and that post-correction further improves efficiency. While language familiarity was not statistically significant in this small study, participants strongly preferred correcting OCR outputs over typing from scratch, citing speed and reduced cognitive load. The work demonstrates the practical benefits of OCR for language documentation and suggests directions for future research.

## Method Summary
The study employs a controlled user experiment with 10 participants transcribing Kwak'wala documents under different conditions. A Latin Square design is used to control for document difficulty and participant variability. Participants complete transcription tasks using three methods: manual typing, OCR with off-the-shelf tools, and OCR with post-correction workflows. Transcription time is the primary metric, with participants also providing subjective feedback on their experience. The study focuses specifically on Kwak'wala, an endangered language with extensive written documentation, to assess the practical utility of OCR in language documentation workflows.

## Key Results
- OCR reduces transcription time by over 50% compared to manual typing
- Post-correction methods further improve transcription efficiency beyond basic OCR
- Participants strongly preferred correcting OCR outputs over typing from scratch due to reduced cognitive load and faster completion

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The substantial time savings from OCR occur because the technology offloads the visual recognition task from human cognition to computational processing. This allows transcribers to focus on verification and correction rather than character-by-character visual analysis. The cognitive load is shifted from pattern recognition to error detection and correction, which typically requires less mental effort per character. Post-correction methods enhance this effect by providing structured workflows that streamline the verification process, making it more efficient than starting from a blank document.

## Foundational Learning
- **Latin Square experimental design**: Used to control for both participant and document variability in the study. Critical for ensuring that observed effects are attributable to the transcription method rather than confounding factors.
  - Why needed: Prevents systematic bias from document difficulty or individual participant skill differences
  - Quick check: Verify that each participant sees each condition exactly once

- **User-centric evaluation methodology**: Focuses on actual human performance and preferences rather than just technical accuracy metrics
  - Why needed: Ensures that technological improvements translate to real-world workflow benefits
  - Quick check: Confirm that evaluation metrics align with actual user goals and pain points

- **Endangered language documentation challenges**: Understanding the specific constraints of working with low-resource languages
  - Why needed: Context for why transcription efficiency is particularly valuable in this domain
  - Quick check: Review documentation burden statistics for similar endangered languages

## Architecture Onboarding

Component map:
Human participants -> Transcription interface -> (Manual typing | OCR system) -> Output verification -> Time measurement

Critical path:
Document presentation → Transcription method selection → Character input/verification → Error correction → Completion recording

Design tradeoffs:
- Accuracy vs. speed: OCR may introduce errors requiring correction time
- Familiarity vs. efficiency: Experienced typists may initially be slower with new OCR workflows
- Language specificity: General OCR tools vs. language-specific models

Failure signatures:
- High OCR error rates leading to increased correction time
- User resistance to new workflows despite efficiency gains
- Document formatting issues causing OCR system failures

First experiments:
1. Baseline: Measure manual transcription speed on identical documents
2. System comparison: Test multiple OCR systems on the same document set
3. Workflow optimization: Evaluate different post-correction interface designs

## Limitations
- Small sample size (n=10) limits statistical power and generalizability
- Focus on transcription time alone ignores other important factors like accuracy and fatigue
- Single language evaluation (Kwak'wala) restricts broader applicability
- Insufficient detail on post-correction methodology makes replication difficult

## Confidence
High: The finding that OCR reduces transcription time substantially (over 50%) is well-supported by the experimental design and consistent across participants.

Medium: The preference for OCR-based correction over manual typing is strongly indicated but based on subjective participant feedback rather than objective measures of accuracy or quality.

Low: The conclusion that language familiarity does not significantly impact transcription efficiency requires additional data due to the small sample size and limited power.

## Next Checks
1. Replicate the study with a larger sample size (n=30+) to establish statistical power for detecting smaller effect sizes and language familiarity impacts
2. Conduct a within-subjects comparison of transcription accuracy between manual typing and OCR-based approaches across multiple document types and languages
3. Perform a longitudinal study examining transcription quality and participant fatigue over extended sessions (2+ hours) to assess sustainability of OCR benefits
