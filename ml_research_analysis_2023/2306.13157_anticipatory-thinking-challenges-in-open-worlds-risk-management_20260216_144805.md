# Anticipatory Thinking Challenges in Open Worlds: Risk Management

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2306.13157
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.13157
- Reference count: 5
- Key outcome: Introduces two new risk management challenges for AI in open worlds - perception challenges (recognizing rare, high-impact situations) and cognition challenges (dynamically adjusting risk exposure as new risks are identified)

## Executive Summary
This paper addresses the critical challenge of anticipatory thinking in AI systems operating in open-world environments, where uncertainty and risk are inherent. The authors propose two novel challenges - perception and cognition - that focus on enabling AI agents to recognize and adapt to rare, high-impact situations and dynamically adjust their risk exposure as they encounter new risks. Using the rogue-like game Dungeon Crawl Stone Soup as a testbed, the paper outlines how these challenges manifest in both long-term strategy and short-term tactics. The proposed solution involves a hybrid approach combining symbolic reasoning and machine learning to operationalize risk management in open-worlds.

## Method Summary
The method employs a hybrid symbolic-machine learning architecture where symbolic reasoning manages risk profiles and determines optimal capabilities, while machine learning handles few-shot learning for newly observed risks. The approach uses iterative content generation from real-world deployment data to adapt perception systems to rare scenarios. The Dungeon Crawl Stone Soup (DCSS) game environment serves as the primary testbed, featuring procedurally generated content, permanent consequences, and unknown risks that mirror real-world complexity. The methodology involves implementing perception challenges through content generation models and cognition challenges through dynamic risk profile adjustment algorithms.

## Key Results
- Introduces perception challenges for AI agents to recognize and adapt to rare, high-impact situations from imperfect perception
- Defines cognition challenges for agents to dynamically adjust risk exposure when identifying new risks and learning new mitigations
- Proposes hybrid symbolic-machine learning approach as a solution framework for open-world risk management
- Uses DCSS game environment to test long-term strategy (capability enhancement, region transitions) and short-term tactics (engagements, resource management)

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- **Claim:** Iterative content generation from real-world deployment data enables perception systems to adapt to rare, high-impact scenarios without requiring exhaustive pre-training datasets.
- **Mechanism:** When perception systems misclassify objects or scenes in deployment, these errors are fed back into generative models (e.g., VAEs, GANs) that synthesize new test instances with similar characteristics. This creates a closed loop where the system learns from its own mistakes.
- **Core assumption:** Real-world errors capture sufficient diversity to represent the "long tail" of rare but impactful scenarios.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract] "We propose iterative testing where objects mislabelled in deployment are used as input to a content generation model for testing and evaluation"
  - [section] "we propose iterative testing where objects mislabelled in deployment are used as input to a content generation model for testing and evaluation of a perception system's observational biases"
  - [corpus] No direct corpus evidence; this appears to be novel methodology.
- **Break condition:** If real-world deployment data is insufficient to capture the true diversity of rare scenarios, the generative model will create unrealistic or irrelevant test cases.

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim:** Hybrid symbolic-machine learning architectures enable AI agents to dynamically balance risk exposure when encountering new risks and learning new mitigations.
- **Mechanism:** Symbolic reasoning systems maintain a risk profile and determine which new capabilities or assets would produce a desired risk profile, while machine learning models handle the few-shot learning required when observing new risks. This combination allows for both semantic understanding and adaptive learning.
- **Core assumption:** The risk profile can be represented symbolically and new risks can be characterized in terms that symbolic reasoning can process.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract] "Our second contribution are cognition challenges, designed for agents that must dynamically adjust their risk exposure as they identify new risks and learn new mitigations"
  - [section] "A hybrid or 3rd wave approach would reason over the semantics of what new capabilities or assets would produce a desired risk profile"
  - [corpus] Weak evidence; this represents a proposed approach rather than demonstrated mechanism.
- **Break condition:** If the symbolic representation cannot capture the complexity of risk relationships, the system will fail to make appropriate trade-offs between new and existing risks.

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim:** Open-world game environments like DCSS provide realistic testbeds for anticipatory thinking because they feature procedurally generated content, permanent consequences, and unknown risks that mirror real-world complexity.
- **Mechanism:** The stochastic nature of DCSS ensures that agents encounter novel situations that require on-the-fly risk assessment and mitigation, rather than relying on pre-learned patterns. The permanent death mechanic forces careful consideration of risk-reward trade-offs.
- **Core assumption:** The distribution of challenges in DCSS approximates the distribution of challenges in real-world open domains.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [section] "DCSS is one of two rogue-like games with increased interest in recent years and remains an unsolved domain for AI"
  - [section] "DCSS can be considered an open-world for any learning agent who does not start with a complete model of the environment"
  - [corpus] No direct corpus evidence; this appears to be domain-specific justification.
- **Break condition:** If the types of risks in DCSS are fundamentally different from real-world risks, solutions developed in this domain may not transfer effectively.

## Foundational Learning

- **Concept:** Risk profile management and dynamic balancing
  - **Why needed here:** The paper emphasizes that AI agents must dynamically adjust their risk exposure as they identify new risks and learn new mitigations. Understanding how to represent and manipulate risk profiles is fundamental to implementing the cognition challenges.
  - **Quick check question:** If an agent has a risk profile that prioritizes safety over speed, and encounters a new risk that threatens safety, what should the agent do to its risk profile?

- **Concept:** Few-shot learning and meta-learning principles
  - **Why needed here:** The paper proposes using meta-learning approaches to handle small samples of newly observed risks. Engineers need to understand how to train models that can quickly adapt to new, previously unseen situations.
  - **Quick check question:** How does meta-learning differ from traditional transfer learning when dealing with completely new categories of risks?

- **Concept:** Procedural content generation and its implications for AI testing
  - **Why needed here:** The paper uses procedurally generated game environments as testbeds and proposes using generative models for creating rare test scenarios. Understanding how procedural generation creates distributions of challenges is crucial for both evaluation and solution development.
  - **Quick check question:** Why might procedural generation be more effective than hand-crafted test cases for evaluating risk management capabilities?

## Architecture Onboarding

- **Component map:** Perception System → Content Generation Module → Test Suite Generator; Risk Profile Manager (symbolic) ↔ Learning Module (ML) ↔ Action Selection Module; Environment Simulator (DCSS or other) → Agent Interface → Performance Metrics Collector

- **Critical path:** Perception error → Content generation → Test evaluation → Model update (for perception challenges); Risk identification → Profile adjustment → Action selection → Outcome observation (for cognition challenges)

- **Design tradeoffs:** Symbolic reasoning provides explainability and semantic understanding but may struggle with novel situations; ML approaches handle novel situations but lack interpretability. The hybrid approach requires careful integration points.

- **Failure signatures:** Perception systems that fail to generate diverse enough test cases; symbolic components that cannot represent complex risk relationships; ML components that overfit to training distributions; agents that optimize for short-term rewards at the expense of long-term risk management.

- **First 3 experiments:**
  1. Implement the perception challenge 1 loop: deploy a basic vision system, collect misclassifications, generate adversarial examples using a VAE, test the updated system on the generated examples, and measure improvement in handling rare objects.
  2. Create a simplified DCSS environment with 10 monsters and 5 items, implement a basic symbolic risk manager, and evaluate how well it balances risk when encountering new monster types.
  3. Implement a meta-learning component that can learn from 1-3 examples of new risks and evaluate its ability to apply this knowledge to similar but unseen scenarios.

- **Basis in paper:** [explicit] The paper discusses the importance of evaluating AI systems in open-worlds, where new risks and new mitigations require dynamic risk management. It also mentions the need for accurate assessments of a model's ability to identify and manage unknown risks both at the object and scene level.
- **Why unresolved:** Current evaluation methods, such as out-of-distribution evaluation and adversarial filtering datasets, are static and laborious to curate, which limits their effectiveness in real-world risk management.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Developing new evaluation methods that can accurately measure the performance of AI systems in identifying and managing unknown risks in open-world environments, and comparing their effectiveness to current methods.

## Limitations
- The effectiveness of iterative content generation depends on whether real-world deployment errors capture sufficient diversity to represent rare scenarios.
- The assumption that DCSS provides an adequate testbed for real-world risk management capabilities requires empirical validation.
- The hybrid symbolic-ML approach's practical implementation details and performance guarantees are not fully specified.

## Confidence
- **High Confidence:** The identification of perception and cognition challenges as distinct problem areas for AI in open worlds is well-founded and aligns with established AI safety research.
- **Medium Confidence:** The proposed hybrid architecture combining symbolic reasoning and ML is theoretically sound, but practical implementation details and performance guarantees are not fully specified.
- **Low Confidence:** The assumption that DCSS provides an adequate testbed for real-world risk management capabilities requires empirical validation, as the game's risk distribution may not mirror real-world complexity.
